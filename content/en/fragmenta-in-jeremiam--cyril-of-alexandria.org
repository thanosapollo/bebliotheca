* Fragments on Jeremiah
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Truly the hills were for a lie, and the strength of the mountains, etc. He
mentions mountains and hills, since on them in those ancient times, having
built temples and precincts and raised altars, they used to offer most
pleasing sacrifices to the demons. And shame consumed the labors of our
fathers from their youth. They call shame the truly inglorious and most
dishonorable idolatry, and the error in this, which also consumed the
labors of their fathers; for they have spent, sacrificing to the demons, their
sheep and their calves, and they endured together with their possessions a
loss of understanding. Woe is me! for my soul fails over the slain.
Run about in the streets of Jerusalem. You see in these things one in travail
over the slain, and readily wishing to pity Jerusalem, yet somehow held back
by himself, and all but reining in the god-befitting calm because of the
enormity of the sin; yet seeking nevertheless to have compassion on the sins,
and wishing to put an end to the things from wrath upon one? For he was
commanding those who run about to show one man faithful in her. And I said:
Perhaps they
are poor, because they were not able, etc. The prominent and more sacred among
the Jews is reasonably lamented, yet it poured a more violent wrath upon
itself. And this Christ himself will make clear, saying, Woe to you, scribes
and Pharisees, hypocrites, because you go about the sea and the dry land to
make one proselyte, etc. To whom shall I speak and testify? For they were
insulting the word of Christ, saying to those who heard concerning him: He has
a demon and is mad; why do you listen to him? Do you not see what they
themselves do in the cities of Judah? =70.1453= They have therefore been
punished with a holy verdict. For having assigned worship to creation, and
having served the works of their own hands, they themselves poured upon their
own heads the things from divine wrath. For nets are not stretched unjustly
for winged things, according to what is written. The wise were put to shame.
He seems in these things to indicate as wise the chosen ones of the Greeks,
both poets and prose-writers; who have a tongue well-endowed and elaborate,
and trained in the elegances of words toward charm, but without a share in
true understanding as to the heart. For as the all-wise Paul writes, They
became vain in their reasonings, and their foolish heart was darkened.
Saying they were wise, they became fools, and so on. Therefore they are wise,
at least as far as voice and tongue go, but unwise as to the heart. And the
wisdom of this world is foolishness with God, according to what is written.
A fair olive tree, shady in appearance. For it utterly ruins the soul of man
to depart from God and to be wholly without a share of the grace and help from
him. For it is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God. To
you, nations. For even if they have become insolent and disobedient, and have
a mind full of senselessness, nevertheless they are saved and glorified by
Christ.
And the firstfruits of these have been the prize-bearing martyrs, the
brightness of whose glory flashes over the whole under heaven. And he shall be
as the tree. For he does not think highly of wealth, nor indeed, having come
to be outside empty little glories, does he set the loin high (sic); for these
things are vanity, and nothing else. For it is a sure pledge toward salvation
to trust in God who is able in all things; but to set one's hope upon some
others is vain and useless. Behold, as the clay. For he easily reshapes that
which has come from him into whatever he may choose. Are not the words? etc.
He names fire the evangelical and saving preaching, or even the very
participation of the Holy Spirit, which is also likened to fire. And indeed
the all-wise John the Baptist concerning him and concerning the Savior of us
all, Jesus Christ, speaks thus: I baptize you with water unto repentance, etc.
Rightly therefore Christ said: I came to cast fire upon the earth, and what do
I wish if it is already kindled? And you turned back. You see how he does not
endure the types being set aside, or rather the truth itself being outraged as
still in shadows? For the gifts and the calling of God are without repentance,
according to what is written. But the Jews, dragging back those once set free,
and subjecting them again to the yokes of slavery, have not kept that which
is without repentance =70.1456=; and they have given offense through this,
dishonoring the power of the mystery, even if it was still in types.
And we are justified because we have offered no exchange for the life in
Christ freely through grace, nor even bought the glory of freedom, but gaining
the thing by the Master's gentleness and loving-kindness. If you give, etc.
For since, taking courage from the help of the Egyptians, they opposed the
Babylonians, in this way, being provoked after the capture of Jerusalem and
Samaria, they turned against them, and conquered without toil. Of turning
back, etc. When God pursues, no one will save the one in danger; but wherever
one may be, there he will meet the wrath. Behold, I avenge Ammon. Of Cyril the
same also of Apollinarius. And some say Ammon is Alexandria, so called of old,
as also in Nahum he says: Are you better than Ammon, who dwells among the
rivers? Water around her, whose abundance is the sea, and water her walls;
Ethiopia her strength and Egypt, and there was no end of the flight; and the
Libyans became her helpers, and she herself shall go into exile. But the times
in question are older than Alexander's founding. Perhaps therefore before
Alexander a city existed there, and he rebuilt it when it had ceased. And some
say that the Egyptians call Zeus Ammonas, from whom the Ammoniac country is
also named; whom he calls her son as having been deified by her; for the
statue is handmade, which indeed, he says, the Babylonians will take, and the
others of their gods whom he adds. And the phrase upon Pharaoh and Egypt has
in common "I avenge." And he sent messengers to the nations. And he said
"encompassment" for those whom Jeremiah called messengers, and Symmachus
rendered "tidings"; and it could also be understood as a siege. And the divine
nod calls many nations against Idumea, and he called the movement from it
messengers. For being moved by God toward war, they provoked one another, as
Obadiah says; for they invaded together with those from Israel into Idumea and
the neighboring parts of the nations. And turning toward Idumea, he says:
Behold, I have given you small among the nations, yet nevertheless having
become contemptible you thought great upon the difficulties, not knowing that
you will be captured even if you raise your dwelling as an eagle. And as for
"and Idumea shall be a desert," and the things that follow, Obadiah again
spoke in harmony: And the house of Esau shall be stubble, and it shall be
burned up against them, and there shall be no fire-bearer in the house of
Esau.
And the proverb prevails also among those outside: Not even a fire-bearer was
left, concerning those utterly destroyed. For someone went before holding
fire, and purifying the armies before the engagement, and it was a law that no
enemy should lay a hand against this one. =70.1457= We healed Babylon. In
these things they name Jerusalem Babylon, as striving to imitate her things,
and falling short in nothing of the country of the nations, at least as far as
a complete habit goes, and not enduring to honor the law, that is, the benefit
of the prophetic training.
