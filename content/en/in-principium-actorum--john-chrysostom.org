"Many will say to me," says Christ, "on that day: Lord, Lord, did we not
prophesy in your name?" this is a sign and a wonder: "Did we not in your name
cast out many demons, and do many mighty works?" You see signs and wonders
everywhere: let us see what God says. Since the wonders are bare, and nowhere
a way of life, "Depart from me," he says, "I do not know you, you who work
lawlessness." If you do not know them, how do you know them, if they work
lawlessness? That you may learn that the "I do not know you" is not of
ignorance, but of hatred and turning away. "I do not know you." For what
reason, tell me? "Did we not cast out demons in your name?" For this very
reason I hate you and turn from you, he says, because you were not made better
even by the gifts, because having enjoyed so great an honor you remained in
the same wickedness. "Depart from me, I do not know you." What then? In old
times did the unworthy receive gifts, and those corrupted in life display
signs, and enjoy a divine gift, not taking care for the best way of life? They
enjoyed it through the lovingkindness of God, not through their own worth. For
the word of piety had to be sown everywhere, since it was the beginning and
preface of the faith. Just as, then, an excellent husbandman, when he has just
entrusted a newly planted tree to the bosom of the earth, while it is still
tender grants it much care, fencing it on every side, walling it with both
stones and thorns, so that it may not be pulled up by winds, nor harmed by
cattle, nor insulted by any other injury; but when he sees it fixed and run up
to height, he takes away the walls; for the tree itself is enough against
suffering any such thing: so it has also happened in the case of the faith.
When it was newly planted, when it was tender, when it had been recently cast
into the souls of men, it was granted much care from every side; but when it
was fixed and rooted and ran up to height, when it filled the whole inhabited
world, he also took away the


walls, and Christ thereafter removed the safeguards. Therefore at the
beginning gifts were given even to the unworthy; for of old, for the sake of
the faith, there was need of this help; but now they are not given even to the
worthy; for the strength of the faith no longer needs this alliance. And that
you may learn that those men were not lying, but truly did signs, and gifts
were given even to unworthy men, and that besides what has been said something
else also might be accomplished, and that those men, standing in awe of the
gift of God, might put off wickedness, Judas of the twelve is confessed by all
to have done signs, to have cast out demons, to have raised the dead, to have
cleansed lepers; yet he fell away from the kingdom of the heavens. For the
signs did not have power to save him =51.82=, since he became a robber, and a
thief, and a betrayer of the Master. That signs have no power to save apart
from an excellent way of life and a pure and exact life has been shown from
these things; but that a way of life that has not the consolation from signs,
nor has taken that as an ally, but has appeared by itself, has had power to
enter with boldness into the kingdom of the heavens, hear Christ himself
saying: "Come, you blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you
from the foundation of the world." Why? because they raised the dead? because
they cleansed lepers? because they drove out demons? No; but why? "You saw me
hungry," he says, "and you fed me; thirsty, and you gave me drink; naked, and
you clothed me; a stranger, and you took me in." Nowhere wonders, but
everywhere a way of life. As, then, there wonders were everywhere, and
immediately punishment, since the wonders were bare of the way of life; so
also here a way of life is everywhere, and nowhere wonders, and immediately
salvation; since the excellent way of life has power by itself to save those
who possess it. For this reason this blessed and noble and admirable Luke also
titled the book thus, Acts of apostles, not wonders of apostles; although they
also did wonders. But those things happened for a time, and passed away; these
things throughout all time all who are going to be saved must display. Since,
then, our zeal is not toward the signs, but toward the acts of the apostles,
for this reason he titled the book thus. For that you may not say, or rather
that the slothful may not say, whenever we exhort them to the imitation of the
apostles, and say: Imitate Peter, be zealous for Paul, become like John,
follow James; that they may not say, We cannot, we have not the strength, for
those men raised the dead, they cleansed lepers, stopping our shameless
apology, "Be silent," he says, "be muzzled; not wonders, but a way of life
leads into the kingdom of the heavens. Be zealous, then, for the way of life
of the apostles, and you will have nothing less than the apostles. For it was
not the signs that made apostles, but the pure life." And that this is the
mark of the apostolic image, and of the character of the disciples, hear
Christ making this sign clear. For characterizing the images of the disciples,
and showing what ever is a mark of apostleship, he spoke thus: "In this all
will know that you are my disciples." In this, in what? in doing wonders? in
raising the dead? No, he says; but in what? "In this all will know that you
are my disciples, if you love one another." And love is not of wonders, but of
a way of life. For love is the fulfillment of the law. Did you see the mark of
the disciples? did you see the image of apostleship? did you see the form? did
you see the type? seek nothing more; for the Master has declared that love
characterizes the disciples. If, then, you have love, you have become an
apostle, and first of the apostles.

δʹ. Do you wish to learn this from another place? When Christ said to Peter,
"Peter, do you love me more than these?" he says. Nothing is equal to this for
obtaining the kingdom of the heavens, but when we appear loving Christ as one
must love. And he spoke the mark. What, then, is this? and what doing can we
love more than the


apostles? is it by raising the dead? or by displaying some other wonders? By
no means; but by doing what? Let us hear Christ himself who is loved. "For if
you love me," he says, "more than these, shepherd my sheep." See, here too a
way of life is in honor. For the caring, the compassionate, the protective,
the not seeking one's own, but all that one who shepherds must have, all these
things are of a way of life, not of wonders, nor of signs. But those men,
someone says, became such through the wonders. Not through the wonders, but
through the way of life, and from this they shone most of all. For this reason
he says to them: "Let your light shine before men, that men may see" not the
wonders, "but your good works, and glorify your Father who is in the heavens."
You see the way of life shining everywhere, the virtuous life being admired?
Do you wish me to show you Peter himself as well, this chief of the apostles,
both displaying a very great way of life, and doing wonders that surpass human
nature, and both lying side by side, both the wonder and the way of life, and
honored more from the way of life than from the signs? Hear this history:
"Peter and John were going up into the temple at the hour of prayer, the
ninth." Do not run past the narrative simply, but at once stand at the
preface, and learn how great was the love and the harmony and the concord, and
how these men shared with one another everywhere, and bound by the bond of
friendship according to God they did all things, and at table and in prayer
and in walking and in all the rest they appeared with one another. And if
those pillars, those towers, who had much boldness toward God, needed the help
from one another, and were set right by the alliance from one another, how
much more shall we, the weak and wretched and worthy of no account, need the
help of one another? For "a brother helped by a brother is as a strong city";
and again: "See now what is good, or what is pleasant, but that brothers dwell
together?" Peter and John were there, and they had Jesus in the midst; "For
where two or three are gathered," he says, "in my name, there I am in the
midst of them." Did you see how great a thing it is to be together? And they
were not simply in the same place, since even now we are all in the same
place; but one must be in the same place by the bond of love, by the
disposition of choice; and just as our bodies are near one another now, and
are bound together into one, so the hearts also must be bound together. "Peter
and John were going up into the temple." The veil was split, the Holy of
Holies was left desolate, worship in one place was taken away. Paul cries
saying: "In every place lift up holy hands." Why, then, do they themselves run
into the temple to pray? have they come again to the Jewish weakness? God
forbid! but they condescend to the weaker, fulfilling that word of Paul which
says: "I became to the Jews as a Jew." They condescend to the weak, that those
men may not remain weak =51.84=. And otherwise the whole city was also
gathered there. Just as, then, excellent fishermen pursue those bays of the
rivers where all the fish collect, so as to succeed in the catch with ease; so
also these apostles, the spiritual fishermen, were hastening to that bay where
the whole city was gathered, that there, spreading the net of the Gospel, they
might succeed in the catch with ease. And they did this imitating the teacher.
For Christ also says: "Daily I was with you in the temple." Why in the temple?
That he might take those from the temple. So these too were going as if to
pray, but they were going to sow teaching there. "Into the temple to pray at
the hour of prayer, the ninth." Not even the hour has been observed by them
simply. For I have often told you about this hour, that in it paradise was
opened, and the robber entered, in it the curse was taken away, in it the
sacrifice of the inhabited world was offered, in it the darkness was loosed,
in it the light


shone, both the sensible and the intelligible. About the ninth hour. When
others from dinner and drunkenness are sleeping a deep sleep, then those men,
sober and awake and boiling with much longing, were hastening to prayer. And
if those men needed prayer, prayer so extended, prayer so exact, who had such
boldness, who were conscious of nothing evil in themselves, what shall we do,
full of a myriad wounds, yet not applying the medicine of prayer? Prayer is a
great weapon. Do you wish to learn how great a weapon prayer is? The apostles
passed by the protection of the poor, that they might have more leisure for
prayer. "For appoint," he says, "from among you seven men of good report; but
we will continue in prayer and in the ministry of the word."

εʹ. But, as I was saying (for let us not fall away from the subject, that
Peter both displayed a deed and did a wonder, and is praised more from the
deed), he went up then into the temple to pray; and behold a lame man from his
mother's womb, being carried there to the door of the temple. From the very
pangs the maiming of nature was there, and the disease was greater than
medical art, that the grace of God might be displayed the more. This lame man,
then, was lying at the gate of the temple; and seeing them going in, he was
attending to them, seeking to receive alms from them. What then did Peter?
"Look at us," he says. Sufficient from the look is the proof of having no
possessions; there is no need of words, nor of proof, nor of answer, nor of
teaching; the dress shows you the man without possessions. This, then, is the
whole achievement of apostleship, that you may say these things to the poor
man, that you may not set right the poverty only, that you may say, You will
see a greater wealth. "Silver," he says, "and gold I do not have; but what I
have, this I give you: in the name of Jesus Christ rise and walk." Did you see
poverty and wealth? poverty of mo =51.85= ney, but wealth of gifts. He did not
loose poverty of money, but he set right poverty of nature. See the gentleness
of Peter: "Look at us." He did not insult, he did not revile, which we often
do to those who meet us, charging them with accounts, that they sleep. Were
you commanded this, O man? He did not order you to charge idleness, but to set
right poverty; he did not make you an accuser of vice, but he appointed you a
physician of misfortune; not that you may reproach sluggishness, but that you
may stretch out a hand to those who lie; not that you may blame a manner of
life, but that you may loose famine. But we do the opposite; leaving off
comforting those who come by the giving of money, we even grind their wounds
further by the bringing on of charges. But he both apologizes to the poor man
and converses with gentleness; "For incline," it says, "your ear to the poor
without pain, and answer him peaceable things in meekness." "Silver and gold I
do not have; but what I have, this I give you: in the name of Jesus Christ
rise and walk." Two things are here, a way of life and a wonder. A way of
life, the "Silver and gold is not mine"; for it belongs to a way of life to
despise earthly things, to throw away one's goods, to look past the present
vanity; but a wonder is the raising of the lame man, the setting right of the
maimed members. See, then, both a way of life and a wonder. Let us see, then,
from where Peter boasts. What did he say? That he did wonders? although he had
done wonders then; but he did not say this; but what? "Behold, we have left
all and followed you." Did you see the way of life and the wonder, and the way
of life being crowned? What then did Christ? He accepted this man and praised
him. "For I say to you," he says, "that you who have left houses," and the
rest. He did not say, You who have raised the dead, but "You who have left
your goods will sit on twelve thrones; and everyone who has left all that is
his will enjoy this honor." You cannot raise a lame man, as Peter did? But you
can say, as he did: "Silver and gold is not mine." And if you say this, you
have come near Peter; or rather not even if you


say it, but if you do it. You cannot set right a withered hand? But you can
make your own hand, which is becoming withered by inhumanity, be stretched out
through lovingkindness. "Let not," it says, "your hand be stretched out to
take, and in giving contracted." You see that it is not the withering, but
inhumanity also, that contracts the hand? Stretch it out, then, through
lovingkindness and almsgiving. You cannot
cast out a demon? But cast out sin, and you will receive a greater reward. Did
you see how everywhere the way of life and the achievements have greater
praise than the wonders, and more of the recompense? And if you wish, we will
show this from another place as well. "The seventy disciples came to him," it
says, "and were rejoicing, and said: Lord, even the demons are subject to us
in your name." And he says to them: "Do not rejoice that the demons are
subject to you, but rejoice that your names are written in the heavens." You
see the way of life admired everywhere?

6. Come then, let us reckon up what has been said from the beginning: "In
=51.86= this all will know that you are my disciples, if you love one
another." See, from a way of life, not from wonders, the mark of being
disciples has been shown. "Peter, do you love me more than these? shepherd my
sheep." See another mark as well, and this again from a way of life. A third
mark again: "Do not rejoice that the demons are subject to you, but rejoice
that your names are written in the heavens." And this again is an achievement
of a way of life. Do you wish to learn a fourth proof of this as well? "Let,"
he says, "your light shine before men, that they may see your good works, and
glorify your Father who is in the heavens." See, here too works appear. And
when he says again: "Whoever has left house, or brothers, or sisters for my
sake, will receive a hundredfold, and will inherit eternal life," he praises
the way of life, and the exact life. Did you see the disciples recognized from
loving one another, and the one who loves Christ more than the apostles
appearing from shepherding the brothers, and those who ought to rejoice
commanded to be glad not from casting out demons, but from having been written
in heaven, and those who glorify God shown from the brightness of works, and
those who have obtained life and receive a hundredfold obtaining this gift
from the looking past of all present things. Imitate all these, and you will
be able both to be a disciple, and to be numbered among the friends of God,
and to glorify God, and to enjoy eternal life; and there will be no hindrance
for you toward obtaining all the good things, the not doing signs, if you have
an exact way of life. For even this name itself, Peter, he did not receive
from wonders and signs, but from zeal and genuine affection. For he was not so
called because he raised the dead, nor because he set a lame man upright; but
because he displayed genuine faith with the confession, he inherited this
name, "You are Peter, and on this rock I will build my Church." Why? Not
because he did wonders, but because he said: "You are the Christ, the Son of
the living God." You see that even the being called Peter itself took its
beginning not from wonders, but from a kindled zeal. But since I mentioned
Peter, the memory of another Peter also came into me, our common father and
teacher, who succeeding to that man's virtue also inherited that man's seat.
For this too is one advantage of our city, to have received at the beginning
the chief of the apostles as teacher. For the city that before the whole
inhabited world bound on the name of the Christians had to receive the first
shepherd of the apostles. But having received him as teacher, we did not hold
him to the end, but we yielded him to queen Rome; or rather we have had him to
the end; for we do not hold the body of Peter, but we hold the faith of Peter
as Peter; and the faith


of Peter, holding it, we have Peter himself. So also seeing that man's zealot
we seem =51.87= to see him; for Christ also called John Elijah, not because
John was Elijah, but because he came in the spirit and power of Elijah. Just
as, then, John, because he came in the spirit and power of Elijah, was called
Elijah, so also this man, because he has come in the confession and faith of
Peter, would reasonably be counted worthy of that title as well. For kinship
of way of life also makes a sharing of names. And let us all pray that he also
come to the old age of Peter; for the =51.88= Apostle also ended his life in
old age. "For when," he says, "you grow old, then they will gird you, and lead
you where you do not wish." Let us ask, then, for this man too a long way of
life; for his old age being stretched out makes our spiritual youth flourish
the more; which may it be kept always in its prime, by the prayers of that man
and of this Peter, and by the grace and lovingkindness of our Lord Jesus
Christ, to whom be the glory and the power together with the Holy Spirit, now
and always, and unto the ages of the ages. Amen.

That the reading of the Scriptures is useful, and that it makes the one who
attends unconquered by slavery and by a circumstance of affairs, and that the
name of the apostles is a name of many dignities, and that the apostles
possess power and authority far greater than the rulers outside and even than
those who reign; and toward the end, to the newly illumined.

αʹ. When I look to the poverty of my own mind, I grow numb and draw back,
being called to a discourse before so great a people; but when I look up to
your eagerness, and the insatiable character of the hearing, I take courage
and rise, and with eagerness I strip for the stadium of teaching. For you are
able, even if you take a mind of stone, to make it lighter than any wing by
the desire and zeal concerning the hearing. And just as the animals that lurk
in dens, sinking down toward the rocks in the midst of winter, when they see
the summer appear, leave the hiding-place, and herd with the rest of the
animals, and skip in common with us; so also our soul, sinking as into some
den of the weakness of conscience, when it sees the longing of your love,
leaves the den, and

herds with you, leaps in common with you the fair leaps of the Scriptures,
in the spiritual and divine meadow, in the paradise of Scripture. For a
spiritual meadow, and a paradise of delight, is the reading of the divine
Scriptures, and a paradise of delight better than that paradise. This paradise
God planted not in the earth, but in the souls of those who believe; this
paradise he did not set in Eden, nor toward the east, bounding it in one
district, but he spread it out everywhere on the earth, and stretched it to
the ends of the inhabited world. And that he spread the Scriptures everywhere
in the inhabited world, hear the prophet saying: "Their sound went out into
all the earth, and their words to the ends of the inhabited world." And if you
go to the Indians, whom the sun first sees as it rises, and if you go to the
ocean, and if you go to those British islands, and if you sail to the Euxine
sea, and if you go to the southern parts, you will hear all everywhere
philosophizing the things from Scripture, with another voice, =51.88= but not
with another faith, and with a differing tongue, but with a concordant mind.
For the sound of the tongue has been exchanged, but the manner of piety has
not been exchanged; and they barbarianize with the tongue, but they
philosophize with the judgment; and they solecize with the sound,


but they are pious in the manner. Did you see the length of a paradise
stretched to the ends of the inhabited world? Here there is no serpent, the
place is clean of the beasts, and it is walled by the grace of the Spirit.
This paradise also has a spring, as that one did, a spring mother of a myriad
rivers, not of four. For it does not send forth the Tigris, nor the Euphrates,
nor the Egyptian Nile, nor the Indian Ganges, but this spring sends forth a
myriad rivers. Who says this? God himself who granted us the rivers; "For he
who believes in me," he says, "as the Scripture said, rivers of living water
will flow from his belly." Did you see how not four rivers, but unbounded
ones, pour from that spring? And the spring is wonderful not only in
multitude, but also in nature; for the streams are not of water, but the gifts
of the Spirit. This spring is divided in each soul of the faithful, and is not
diminished; divided, and not spent; broken into small parts, and not lessened;
whole in all, and whole in each. For such are the gifts of the Spirit. Do you
wish to learn the abundance of these streams? do you wish to learn the nature
of the waters? how they are not like these, but are better than these and more
wonderful? Hear again Christ himself saying to the Samaritan woman, that you
may learn the abundance of the spring. "The water that I will give," he says,
"to the believer will become in him a spring of water leaping unto eternal
life." He did not say, going out, he did not say, pouring forth, but, leaping,
showing us the plenty through the leaps. Those =51.89= of the fountain waters
are accustomed to leap out and jump from every side, which the springs cannot
hold in their bosoms, but being overcome by the continuity of the inflow they
bubble them up from every side. Wishing, then, to show the abundance of the
streams, he said, leaping, not, going out. Do you wish to learn its nature
thoroughly as well? Learn from the use. For it is useful not unto the present
life, but unto eternal life. Let us spend time, then, in this paradise, let us
sit beside the spring, let us not suffer what Adam suffered, and fall out of
paradise; let us not receive a destructive counsel, let us not admit the
deceit of the devil; let us remain inside; for great is the safety from this;
let us remain in the reading of these Scriptures. For just as those who sit
beside a spring, and enjoy that breeze, and when burning heat lies on them
continually wash the face, drive off the stifling with the streams, and when
thirst troubles them easily heal the passion, having the medicine near from
the spring; so he who sits beside the spring of the divine Scriptures, even if
he sees a flame of unseemly desire troubling him, easily, having washed the
soul from those streams, has driven off the flame; and if burning anger
troubles, making the heart boil like a cauldron kindled underneath, having
dropped a little of the stream, he at once has checked the shamelessness of
the passion; and from all the wicked reasonings, as from the midst of a flame,
the reading of the divine Scriptures snatches the soul.

βʹ. Therefore that great prophet David also, knowing the benefit from the
reading of the Scriptures, likens the one who attends continually to the
Scriptures and enjoys this converse to a plant always flourishing, standing
beside the channels of the waters, speaking thus: "Blessed is the man who has
not walked in the counsel of the ungodly, and has not stood in the way of
sinners, and has not sat on the seat of pestilent men; but his will is in the
law of the Lord, and in his law he will meditate day and night, and he will be
as the tree planted by the outlets of the waters." For just as that tree
planted by the outlets of the waters,

standing beside the very channels, having a continual supply of irrigation,
becomes unconquered by every unevenness of airs, and neither a ray become
hotter


does it fear, nor does it beware of the air turned to drought; for having
sufficient moisture shut up in itself, it at once drives off and thrusts away
every aggression of solar heat that strikes from outside; so also a soul
standing beside the channels of the divine Scriptures, and being watered
continually, gathering in itself those streams and the dew of the Spirit, will
be unconquered toward every circumstance of affairs, even if disease, even if
spite, even if slander, even if revilings, even if jeers, even if all sloth,
even if all the evils of the inhabited world fall upon such a soul, it easily
drives off the burning of the passions, having sufficient consolation from the
reading of the Scriptures. For neither greatness of glory, nor bulk of power,
nor presence of friends, nor anything else of human things will be able to
comfort the one in pain as the reading of the divine Scriptures will. Why
ever? Because =51.90= those things are perishable and corruptible, wherefore
their consolation also is corruptible; but the reading of the Scriptures is
converse with God. When, then, God comforts the one in despondency, what of
the things that are can throw him into despondency? Let us attend, then, to
the reading, not these two hours only (for this bare hearing is not enough for
us unto safety), but continually; and let each, having withdrawn home, take
the Books in his hands, and go over the thoughts of what has been said, if
indeed he is going to have the benefit from Scripture continual and
sufficient. For that tree also, standing beside the channels, does not
converse with the waters two and three hours, but all day, and all night. For
this reason that tree is leafy with leaves, for this reason it is heavy with
fruit, even if no one of men waters it; since it stands beside the channels
itself, it draws the moisture through the roots, and as through certain pores
sends the benefit through to the whole body; so also he who continually reads
the divine Scriptures, and stands beside their channels, even if he has no one
interpreting, through the continual reading, as through certain roots, draws
much benefit. For this reason we too, knowing your cares, the distractions,
the many occupations, lead you gently and little by little to the thoughts of
the Scriptures, making the memory of what is said lasting by the leisure of
the interpretation. For a violent rain, bursting down, floods the surface, and
benefits the depth in nothing; but sitting gently and little by little like
oil on the face of the earth, slipping as through certain veins of its pores
toward the depth, having filled its flanks with moisture, it makes it most
well supplied toward the bearing of fruits. Therefore we too send this
spiritual rain gently into your souls; for the Scriptures are like spiritual
clouds, and the words and thoughts are like rain much better than this; and
for this reason we send this spiritual rain to you little by little, that the
words may go to the very depth. For this reason, having today a fourth day of
interpretation, we have not yet been able to pass one title, but we are still
turning about it. For it is better, digging a small plot, and going down to
the depth, to find a great
treasure, than going over many acres from above, to be distressed simply and
at random and in vain. And yet I know that many are displeased at this
slowness; but I do not care about their accusation, but I care about your
benefit. Let those who can walk more quickly wait for the slower of the
brothers; for these can wait for those, but the weaker have not the strength
to stretch themselves toward those. For this reason Paul also says that we
ought not to force the weak before the time, who cannot stretch toward the
perfection of the strong; but that we who are strong ought the infirmities of
the weak


to bear. We care about your benefit, not about a mere display; for this reason
we spend time on the thoughts.

γʹ. On the first day, then, I was saying that one must not run past the titles
simply, when I also read you the inscription =51.91= of the altar, and
displayed the wisdom of Paul, who transferred the alien soldier standing even
in the array of the enemies to his own phalanx. Into this the whole teaching
ended on the first day; after that, on the second day, we inquired who it was
who wrote the book; and we found by the grace of God Luke the evangelist, and

and how the one is greater, and the other more useful; and how the one, being
by itself, procures a kingdom, and the other, if it does not take the alliance
from the deed, is cast outside those doorways. Today it is necessary to say
the remainder of the title, and to show what ever the name of the apostles is.
For this name is not bare, but it is a title of rule, of a very great rule, of
the most spiritual rule, of the rule above. But rise up. For just as in the
affairs of life there are many rules, yet not all of the same dignity, but
some greater, some lesser; for example, that we may make the count from the
lower, there is the city's advocate; higher than that is the governor of the
nation; after that there is another greater ruler; there is again the general;
there is the prefect; there is a rule higher than these, the rule of the
consuls; and all these are rules, yet not all of the same dignity; so also of
the spiritual things there are many rules, yet not all of the same dignity;
and greater than all is the dignity of apostleship. For we must lead you by
the hand from the sensible to the intelligible. So Christ also did, when
discoursing about the Spirit he mentioned water. "For he who drinks of this
water will thirst again," he says; "but he who drinks of the water that I will
give him will not thirst unto the age." You see him leading the woman by the
hand from the sensible to the intelligible? So we also do, and we go up from
below, that the word may become clearer. For this reason, discoursing about
rule, we did not mention a spiritual rule, but a sensible one, that from this
we might lead you by the hand to that. You heard how many worldly rules we
numbered, and how some are greater, some lesser, and how the rule of the
consuls lies on all as a peak and a head; let us see the spiritual rules as
well. There is a spiritual rule, a rule of prophecy; there is another rule of
evangelizing, there is of a shepherd, there is of a teacher, there is of
gifts, there is of healings, there is of interpretation of tongues. All these
are names of gifts, but facts of rules and authorities. The prophet is a
ruler; among us the one who drives out demons is a ruler; among us the
shepherd and teacher is a spiritual ruler; but greater than all these is the
apostolic rule. And from where is this clear? Because before all these the
apostle is. And just as the consul among the sensible rules, so the apostle
among the spiritual has the presidency. Let us hear Paul himself numbering the
rules, and in the higher place the apostolic


seating. What then does this man say? "God has set some in the Church, first
apostles, second prophets, third teachers, and shepherds; then gifts of

not only lies upon the remaining gifts as a rule and an authority, but also,
having gathered the roots of all in itself, it holds them. And the prophet
cannot be both apostle and prophet; but the apostle is altogether also a
prophet, and has gifts of healings, and kinds of tongues, and interpretation
of tongues; wherefore he is a beginning and a root of the gifts.

δʹ. And that these things are so, let me bring Paul forward to you as a

then are kinds of tongues? Of old the one who was baptized and believed at

since those then were still more weakly disposed, and could not see the
intelligible gifts with the eyes of the flesh, a sensible gift was given, so
that the intelligible might become evident; and the one baptized at once spoke
both in our tongue, and in that of the Persians, and in that of the Indians,
and in that of the Scythians, so that even the unbelievers might learn that he
had been counted worthy of the Holy Spirit. And the sign was sensible, I mean
such a voice; for they heard it with the sense of the body; but this sensible
sign made the intelligible and unseen grace of the Spirit clear to all. And
this sign was called kinds of tongues. For the one who had one tongue from
nature spoke in varied and differing tongues from grace; and one might see a
man one in number, but varied =51.93= in gifts, and having differing mouths,
and differing tongues. Let us see, then, how the Apostle had this gift as
well, and all the others. About this he speaks thus: "I speak in tongues more
than all of you." Did you see how he has the kinds of tongues, and not only
has them, but even with a greater excess than all the remaining faithful? For
he did not say only that I have speaking in tongues, but also, "I speak in
tongues more than all of you." And the prophecy that he had he shows through
those words, speaking thus: "But the Spirit says expressly that in later times
hard times will set in"; and that to speak the things in later times is
prophecy is clear to everyone, I suppose. And again, "But know this, that in
last times hard times will set in"; and again, "And I say to you in a word of
the Lord, that at his coming we who are alive, who are left, will not precede
those who have fallen asleep." And this too is prophecy. Did you see how he
has kinds of tongues and of prophecy? Do you wish to learn how he has gifts of
healings as well? But perhaps this does not even need the proof from words.
when we see not only the apostles, but even their garments having gifts of
healings. And that he was a teacher of the nations, he says this in many
places, and that he took in hand the whole inhabited world, and governed the
Churches. When, then, you hear, first apostles, second prophets, third
shepherds and teachers, gifts of healings, helps, governings, kinds of
tongues, learn that the whole supply of the remaining gifts, as in a head, is
stored in apostleship. Did you not think the name of the apostles bare before
this? See, now you have come to know how great a depth of thought the name
has. And we have said these things, not a display of our own power


making; for what is said is not ours, but of the grace of the Spirit stirring
the sluggishness of the more slothful, so that they may run past nothing
simply. Reasonably, then, we called apostleship a spiritual consulship. For
the apostles are rulers ordained by God; rulers, not receiving differing
nations and cities, but all in common entrusted with the inhabited world. And
that they are spiritual rulers, this too I will try to prove, that after the
proof you may learn that the apostles are as much better than the worldly
rulers as those worldly rulers themselves are better than children at play.
For this rule is far greater than that, and holds our life together more, and
if this is taken away all is gone and dissolved. What, then, is a symbol of
rule, and what must the ruler have? Authority of a prison, so as to bind some,
and loose others, he is master to cast some out, and to throw others in; again
to remit debts of money, and he is master to release some who are liable, and
to order others to pay; again to lead away to death, and to call from death;
or rather this is not of a ruler, but of a king only; or rather not even of a
king is the =51.94= gift complete. For he does not call the one departing from
death, but from the leading away only, and he can loose a sentence, but he
cannot call back from death; and he has the worse, but has been deprived of
the better. And again we test the ruler from the belt, from the voice of the
herald, from the lictors, from the carriage, from the sword; for all these are
symbols of rule. Let us see, then, the rule of the apostles as well, whether
it has these symbols; it has them, yet not such, but far better, And that you
may learn that these are names of facts, and those are truth of facts; that
you may learn the interval between the children who play at rules, and the
rules that have the rules; and, if you wish, we will count first from the
prison. For we have said that the ruler is master of binding and loosing. See,
then, the apostles having this rule. "For as many as you bind on the earth,"
he says, "will be bound in the heavens; and as many as you loose on the earth
will be loosed in heavens." Did you see a prison, and authority of a prison;
and the name is the same, but the fact is not the same. Bonds, and bonds; but
the ones on the earth, the others in heaven. For heaven is their prison.
Learn, then, the greatness of the rule. Sitting on the earth they bring the
vote, and the power of the vote crosses the heavens. And just as kings sitting
in one city vote and legislate, and the power of the votes and of the laws
runs through the whole inhabited world; so also then, the apostles sitting in
one place legislated these things; and the power of the laws, and of these
bonds, not only ran through the inhabited world, but also went up into the
very height of the heavens. Did you see a prison, and a prison; the one on
earth, the other in heaven, the one of bodies, the other of souls; or rather
both of souls and of bodies; for they bound not bodies only, but souls as
well.

Judea they sat, they would have been ignorant of these things. Hereafter he
wishes them to take the experience of both the truth of the prophets and of
his power with the very sights; that if they are well-minded, they may through
these be led by the hand toward the truth; but if they remain in the impiety,
they may have no apology on the fearful day of the judgment. For this reason
he scattered them everywhere in the inhabited world, that we too may gain
something from this, that is, seeing those prophecies spoken about their
dispersion, those about the taking of Jerusalem, which both Daniel, having
mentioned the abomination of desolation, and Malachi having said, "That even
among you gates will be shut," and David and Isaiah and many other prophets
proclaimed these things beforehand, that seeing those who have become
ungrateful about the Master thus being punished, having fallen out of the
paternal freedom and of all their own laws and paternal traditions, we may
learn his power that foretold them and worked them, and the enemies through
our good things may see his strength; but we through the vengeances upon those
may learn his unspeakable lovingkindness and power, and may continue
glorifying him through all, that we may also obtain the eternal and
unspeakable good things, by grace and lovingkindness of our Lord Jesus Christ,
with whom to the Father together with the holy and life-giving Spirit be honor
and power, now and always, and unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
