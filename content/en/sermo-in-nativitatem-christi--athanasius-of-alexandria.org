* Sermon on the Nativity of Christ
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of our father among the saints Athanasius, On the Nativity of Christ.

I see a strange mystery: instead of the sun, the sun of righteousness
contained without circumscription in the Virgin. And do not seek how; for
where God wills, the order of nature is overcome. For he willed, he was able,
he came down, he saved. Run together, all things: God today, the one who is
and who is before, becomes what he was not; for being God, he becomes man, not
departing from being God. For neither by a departure from godhead did he
become man, nor again from among men by progress did he become God; but being
Word, for this reason he became man without passion, the nature remaining
unchanged. And having walked a strange and untrodden way, he came forth from
an untilled =28.961= womb, neither leaving his angels destitute of his
oversight, nor by the incarnation toward us departing from his own godhead.
But kings came to worship the heavenly king, the one begotten ineffably from
the Father, and today being born from the Virgin for my sake; but then indeed
begotten according to nature, and today through his own nature. For having
been begotten before the ages from the Father as the one who begot knows,
today he was born from the Virgin beyond nature, as the grace of the Spirit
knows. The Ancient of Days has become a child; the one seated upon a high
throne is laid in a manger; the intangible and bodiless is wrapped by human
hands; the one who tears apart the bonds of sin is entangled in
swaddling-clothes; and because he wills this. Whom then did the Virgin bear?
The Master of nature. For even if you are silent, nature cries out. For the
Virgin Mary bore as the one born willed to be born. For he was not turned from
nature, but as Master of nature he introduced a strange manner of birth.
Nevertheless, even having become man, he is not born as a man. For if he had
come forth from a common marriage, just as I also, it would have been thought
a lie by the many. Now for this reason the one being born is born from a
Virgin, and thereafter he keeps the mother, and preserves the virginity
unbroken, that the strange manner of the conception may become for me a
procurer of great faith. Hence whether a Greek or a Jew asks me whether Christ
became man according to nature, or contrary to his own nature, I will call as
witness of the word the spotless seal of virginity; for thus God, overcoming
the order of nature. And if you wish, ask the blessed Luke the evangelist, and
he answers you concerning his incarnate economy. For beginning he speaks thus:
"And in the sixth month of the conception of Elizabeth the angel Gabriel was
sent by God to a city of Galilee, whose name was Nazareth, to a virgin
betrothed to a man whose name was Joseph, and the name of the virgin was Mary.
And the angel, having entered in to her, said: Hail, full of grace, the Lord
is with you. Blessed are you among women. And she, having seen, was troubled
at his word, and was considering of what sort this greeting might be. And the
angel said to her: Do not fear, Mary; for you have found favor with God. And
behold, you will conceive in the womb, and will bear a son, and you will call
his name Jesus. This one will be great and will be called Son of the Most
High, and the Lord God will give him the throne of David his father. And he
will reign over the house of Jacob unto the ages, and of his kingdom there
will be no end. And Mary said to the angel: How will this be to me, since I do
not know a man? And the angel answering said to her: The Holy Spirit will come
upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you."

... And he takes it ill. He entered without flesh, he was carried for a
nine-month time in the womb of the Virgin. He entered as he willed, he was
carried as he was well pleased. He came forth as he =28.964= wished. He
entered without flesh, he became man, the godhead being united to the economy.
And we do not say two sons, but we understand one and the same, the Word of
God and our Lord Jesus Christ. He became man and he was, being by nature. He
became what he was not, and remained what he was. For "In the beginning was
the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God." He became flesh,
not putting off what he was. "And he tabernacled among us." The "He
tabernacled among us," that is, he consorted with us, according to the holy
Jeremiah, or rather his minister Baruch: "This is our God; no other shall be
reckoned beside him, who found out every way of knowledge, and gave it to
Jacob his child, and to Israel his beloved. After these things he was seen
upon the earth, and consorted with men." Hear then also the voice of the
Gospel; for the voice of the angel spoken to the holy Virgin brings us great
emphasis. "Behold," he says, "you will conceive in the womb, and will bear a
son, and you will call his name Emmanuel, that is, being interpreted, God with
us." Before being conceived in the womb the child was called God, the God with
us. Therefore the "he consorted with men" and the "he tabernacled among us"
agree with the word of the prophet. Before the conception then the child has
been called God. How then is the holy Virgin Mary not Theotokos? Since
therefore that name does not have the interpretation of a man at all, God is
the child that was born, who did not loose the Virgin's virginity according to
nature; but thus having been born from the Theotokos, God and man; God
according to nature, and man through his own good pleasure. And having become
man, he is also God, the two natures being united. But perhaps the opponent
will say: If the Virgin is Theotokos, you give a beginning to God the Word
from the Virgin. I do not say that he received a beginning from the holy
Virgin; far be it! For he existed before the ages; for he himself also made
the ages, he himself is also creator of all creation, as the Evangelist says:
"All things came to be through him, and without him came to be not even one
thing that has come to be." This one, through whom all things came to be, came
to be in the womb for a nine-month time, and was not alienated from the
paternal bosom. Being in the womb of the Virgin, he was glorified and
worshipped by the host of the powers above. Being in the womb of the Virgin,
he filled heaven and earth, the one who bears heaven and earth not by power,
but by a nod. This one was borne by a womb as man, but was working as God; not
being carried about in the womb of the Virgin, but encompassing and governing
all things as God according to the appellation of the name, "God with us," the
one born from a Virgin. But if you say the one born is a mere child, and not
God, hear the great herald Isaiah crying out and saying toward the ungrateful
synagogue, or rather the leading-out of the Jews: that "A child was born, a
son also was given to us; whose government is upon his shoulder, =28.965= and
his name is called, Angel of great counsel, Wonderful, Counsellor, mighty God,
ruler, prince of peace, father of the age to come. Amen." When did a child
according to nature become God, ruler, as this child, called "God with us"?
But if mighty God, ruler, and prince of peace, and father of the age to come
is the child born from the Virgin, how is the Virgin not Theotokos, but
Theodochos, if she both conceived, and bore, and the one born is God? And
again I say to him, as he willed he entered, as he was well pleased he was
carried, as he wished the one born came forth. Why do you search out his will?
Why do you scrutinize his good pleasure? Why do you track out his counsel?
Hear from Paul, or rather be condemned by him: "For who has withstood his
will?"

Nay rather, O man, who are you who search and track out his generation, when
the prophet says: "Who shall declare his generation?" The prophet flees from
declaring his generation, and you are curious about the nature and busy
yourself with it? But the evangelist Luke, narrating his nativity below,
speaks thus: "There were shepherds in the same country lodging in the fields,
and keeping watches over their flock; and behold an angel of the Lord stood by
them, and the glory of the Lord shone around them, and they feared a great
fear. And the angel said to them: Do not fear; for behold I bring you good
news of a great joy, which will be to all the people; that there was born to
you today a Savior, who is Christ the Lord, in the city of David." The angel
says Christ and Lord, bringing good news to the shepherds, the one born from
the Virgin. If then the one born is Lord, how is the Virgin not Kyriotokos?
But I say the holy Virgin is both Christotokos, and Kyriotokos, and
Soteriotokos, and Theotokos, according to the voice of the angel who brought
good news to the shepherds and the Virgin. If angels proclaim Savior, and
Christ, and Lord, and God, why do we reject their testimony? he entered into
the Virgin without flesh, he was carried bodily and as he was well pleased. He
came out through the maternal nature economically, yet not after being born
was God the Word united to the economy. He came forth unchangeably in nature,
becoming what he was not, remaining what he was. Not being altered in essence,
he emptied himself, that is, as he wished; not emptied by another, he took the
form of a slave; not emptied of the godhead, according to the blessed Paul who
says: "Who, being in the form of God, did not count it robbery to be equal
with God, but emptied himself, taking the form of a slave." He emptied
himself, becoming what he was not; and again I say the same, remaining what he
was, for this Word was God; becoming as a child, he is glorified as Son. He
entered bodiless, he put on a body as he willed, and the mortal was swallowed
up by life. =28.968= Say then Theotokos of the Virgin, and do not say
Theodochos; or rather say Theodochos and Theotokos. If she is Theodochos and
Theotokos: would God the Word not have taken flesh from her? but there the
Evangelist constrains me, crying with a great voice: "The Word became flesh."
And in the other evangelist he speaks thus: "Jesus asked his disciples,
saying: Whom do men say that I the Son of man am? And the apostles answering
said: Some Elijah, and others Jeremiah, or one of the prophets. And Jesus
said: But whom do you say that I am? And Peter answering said: You are the
Christ, the Son of the living God." And he did not say: You are the one who
became by grace Son of God, but, "You are the Christ," with a clear voice,
"the Son of the living God." And Mark the evangelist again has used the same
voice, speaking in accord with the blessed Peter: "The beginning of the Gospel
of Jesus Christ, the Son of God." And in the Gospel according to Matthew, at
the resurrection of our Savior Jesus Christ, the centurion and those with him
keeping Jesus, seeing the earthquake and the signs that came to be, were
greatly afraid, saying, "Truly this was Son of God;" and the angel to the
shepherds: "Behold I bring you good news of a great joy, which will be to all
the people, that there was born to you today a Savior, who is Christ the Lord,
in the city of David." If then the one born is Lord, the Virgin is also
Kyriotokos; and where Lord, there also God; Lord is not separated from God nor
God from Lord, as it says in the Old: "And the Lord rained fire from the
Lord;" and again: "You shall love the Lord your God;" and again: "Hear,
Israel, the Lord your God is one Lord;" and again the Psalmist: "Lord God of
the powers, hear my prayer;" the same again: "God is the Lord, and he has
appeared to us;" and again the same: "Lord God of the powers, who is like
you?"

And Paul in the Letter to Titus writes thus: "The saving grace of God has
appeared to all men, training us; that, having denied ungodliness and worldly
desires, we may live soberly and justly and piously in the present age,
awaiting the blessed hope and appearing of the glory of our great God and
Savior Jesus Christ." This is the one born from the holy Virgin, who is Christ
the Lord. This Christ and Lord the blessed Paul proclaims as great God,
saying: "Of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ." Again in that to the
Romans this one writes: "For I was praying that I myself be anathema from
Christ, for my brothers, my kinsmen according to the flesh, who are
Israelites, whose is the adoption, and the glory, and the covenant, and the
legislation, and the worship, and the promises; whose are the fathers, and
from whom is the Christ according to the flesh, who is God over all." And
again the same Paul: "Every fornicator, and covetous one, and drunkard, and
unclean, =28.969= and idolater, has no inheritance in the kingdom of the Lord
and of God." So that it has been shown that the Lord is also God, and God is
the Lord. But if you will not bear even these, let the demons in the country
of the Gergesenes persuade you, crying: "Ah, what have we to do with you, Son
of God? Have you come here before the time to torment us." Let the demons
persuade you. If you strike out the testimony of Peter, and turn away from the
voice of the Gospel: revere that according to Mark. Fear the angel who said
that the one born from the Virgin is Savior and Christ and Lord. Do you
disbelieve these? Bridle the impulse of your blasphemy, and confess the voice
of the angel spoken in the Gospel, Emmanuel, that is God with us. Before the
conception he is called God; and in his being conceived economically not
according to nature in the womb of the Virgin do you deny that he is God? But
if you confess him to be God in the womb of the Virgin, which also he is, and
that God the Word himself has been united to the incarnate economy, why do you
flee from saying Theotokos of the Virgin? If she is not Theotokos, neither is
she Virgin after the birth. But I say, being in the conception of the virginal
womb, he sat in the bosom of the Father, not being circumscribed. For the
divine nature is simple and uncompounded, not being circumscribed.
Unchangeable is the nature, and unalterable the essence. And this one who is
in the bosom of the Father, this one the Virgin now bore by the economy. As he
willed he entered; as he was well pleased, he was conceived in the womb of the
Virgin; as he wished, he came forth having been born. You, why are you curious
about his generation? Either fear him as God, or revere him as master, or be
devout toward him as maker and creator, or tremble as before Lord, or shudder
as before judge. The demons will put you to shame, who drove the swine and
drowned the herd in the death of the deep. Those, recognizing the master, say:
"What have we to do with you, Son of God? Have you come before the time to
torment us." Those flee the danger and the threat of torment, and you draw
upon yourself the judgment of torment. Those knew the one born from Mary as
Lord and judge of the whole world, and seeing him before the judgment they
trembled; and you, having this judgment before your eyes, despise it, and are
not struck? And again I say these things, and speaking I will not cease,
whitening clean for you the subject of the word; so that in knowledge of
security you may have the faith unshaken and hold firm the confession, the
foundation of the faith. Again we will walk upon the matter at hand, showing
the straight and unswerving way of the faith. For to me to speak is not
irksome, and for you it is safe. This God entered through the hearing of the
Virgin as he willed, was carried as he was well pleased, came forth born as he
wished; he entered bodiless as he willed; the uncontainable was carried in a
containing vessel, in the womb of the Virgin economically, as he was well
pleased. He came forth =28.972= born God and man, as he wished. He became what
he was not, having of unalterable essence what he was. For the Word was God,
and the Word was with God; and yet the Apostle saying: "God sent forth his
Son, become of a woman." This Son God sent forth become of a woman, the one
begotten from the undefiled nature, the one come forth from the ineffable
essence, the one not alienated from the paternal bosom, the one not separated
from the royal throne, but sitting together with the Father as his own and
co-enthroned, not in grace, but in divine nature and paternal essence. For how
has he been separated, tell me, when he says, "I am in the Father, and the
Father in me;" and again: "The Father who remains in me, he does the works"?
The one born from the holy Virgin, this one before the conception the angel
called Emmanuel, that is, "God with us," whom Isaiah the prophet said, this
child who came forth from the Virgin, mighty God, ruler, prince of peace,
father of the age to come. What child born according to nature became mighty
God, ruler? What child born drew a star making known the narrow lodging of the
infant being sought, or rather of the Master? What child called magi from the
east to worship him? What child born according to nature had gifts ever
offered by magi? And let us see the gifts, whether they were offered as to a
weak man, and not to God, king and man; gold as to a king, frankincense as to
God, and myrrh as to a man for burial. Gold as to a king: "O God, give your
judgment to the king, and your righteousness to the son of the king." This
voice David spoke. After these the angel at the greeting of Mary: "And the
Lord God will give him the throne of David his father, and he will reign over
the house of Jacob unto the ages, and of his kingdom there will be no end."
For to him is the glory and the power unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
