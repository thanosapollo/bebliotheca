On the saying, "The Pharisees going out took counsel."
=61.705=

All you who happen to be disciples of Elijah, who did not despise the poor
table of the widow, lodge with us =61.706=. For such a modest table of words
has been prepared among us too, having one loaf upon it: not =61.707= the one
which the mill ground fine, and hands kneaded, and fire finished, but the one
which a virgin field without plow and seed brought to bloom, and the cross
ripened, and the Father finished, and twelve baskets of the apostles
harvested. This bread Mary bore, and the Church received, and every day it is
eaten by us, and remains undiminished. Having set this bread for you upon my
modest table, I wished to bind the instrument of my tongue with silence; but
the treacherous ambush of the God-fighting Jews, stitched together against our
Savior, even forces the stones to speak.

For what did we hear just now being read? And going out, it says, the
Pharisees took counsel against Jesus, how they might destroy him. And what is
the cause for which they plot to destroy the Lord? Because on a sabbath day he
healed a man, and stretched out the idle and withered hand toward work on a
day of idleness. O insensibility of the Jews, who do not understand that the
right hand of Adam in paradise, which touched the tree and was withered by the
transgression, needed to be refashioned again by its own potter! or that the
right hand stained by the devil through the bloodshed of the godless idols
needed again, through its own Creator, to be stretched out bright toward
heaven! But they grieve at a man's benefaction and a soul's salvation, and
they teach the idleness of the sabbath, but they ignore the wonder-working of
Jesus. For why, O Pharisees, were you not grieved, or did you not plot such
things against Manasseh, when he sawed Isaiah asunder, when he sacrificed your
sons and daughters to the demons, and established the houses of God as
precincts of the idols? why did you not bring such a counsel against Herod,
when he unjustly slaughtered the infants, and made mothers childless? when one
could see the infants lying like untimely ears of grain in a single cut of
iron, not yet having the grain of the heart solid, nor yet the awns of the
hair come to youth, but still at the very tenderest nourishment of milk,
murdered by Herod with the violent wrath of hail? when wailings and
lamentations of men and women were heard in the city's squares and lanes,
there fathers with minds unstrung, and mothers with hair let down, terribly
mourning over the unjust slaughter of their untimely children? when Bethlehem
was stained purple with the blood of the infants, and the untimely must of
untimely clusters was mixed with the parents' hot tears? when the faces of
fathers were distorted with wailing, and mothers' bowels were wrung with the
pains, and the milk-bearing springs of breasts were dried up out of season?
when in the mothers' breasts the breath, crowded and whirling in a narrow
space, was carried disorderly about the nostrils? when fathers' and mothers'
eyes imitated springs of waters, and threw silver-colored drops of bubbling
tears from the cheeks to the ground? when women's hair was torn to shreds by
their own nails in the rending, under great frenzy, and the locks they ought
to have hidden they paraded in the drunkenness of grief?

But thinking the contrary, you also do the contrary: and you let the murderers
and those liable to death go free, but you punish the innocent and the just,
though the law says: You shall not kill the innocent and the just. O
disorderly order, and lawless law, sent from the devil, written by demons,
kept by the Jews even until now! What law is this, that the murderers should
be saved, but this one who grants life to the slain should be destroyed? For
they asked Pilate that Barabbas, found in sedition and murder, be released,
but they asked that Jesus, who made alive those murdered by the devil, be
crucified. And going out, it says, the Pharisees took counsel, how they might
destroy him. O ungrateful mind, and counsel filled with want of counsel! The
fountain of good things was gushing benefits upon them like a river, but they,
even while drawing up the grace, belched murderous counsels in their hearts.

Those who had their wounds, being healed, were delivered from the pains; but
they, having no wounds in the body, were in pain, stung by envy. Their hungry
ones in the deserts were fed to fullness; and they, amid plenty of faith, were
being destroyed by unbelief. The water was changed into wine; and they, not
tasting the wine, were carried about drunk with envy =61.708=. They saw the
blood-flows of the woman with the issue of blood stayed; and they, wasting
away with envy, were dripping. Their demoniacs, being freed from the demons,
came to their right mind, and they, moved by envy, raged worse than those
possessed; their blind, not having eyes, were given light, and they, having
eyes, were darkened. Of the deaf, as we heard just now, the coil of hearing
was opened; and their heart, whirling with dizziness, turned about counsels of
murder. The sea, laying down its own waves, prepared itself for the Master's
treading; and their heart foamed through the mouth with death-bearing waves of
thoughts. The heaviest winds calmed the violent tempest of the wind into a
gentle breeze; and they, tossed by the squall of envy, hissed a winter of the
cross. O envy, root of death, the many-tangled disease of the heart, sharpest
nail! for what sharpest nail so stings, as envy wounds the heart that has it?
and what disease so destroys the beauty of the face, as envy withers the
soul's fair-breathing flower, love? Water quenches fire, but a thousand
springs of teachers do not stop envy; one whetstone wipes the rust from iron,
but David's five stones stirred up envy in Saul all the more.

The lizard is said to be green; but it is not greener than the envious. For as
a worm, gnawing the heart of the tree bit by bit, dries it up, so envy, eating
out the heart of the one who has it bit by bit, withers it. And however much
we may say, beloved, about envy, we have not yet begun to tell its wickedness;
even if tongues of orators, even if words of philosophers, even if sayings of
teachers, they will not have strength to speak it out. Fulfillment of the law,
then, is love of the Word, and fulfillment of murder is the wickedness of
envy. O envy, pitch-like ship, tartarean, wreck-bearing! Your shipmaster is
the devil, the serpent is helmsman, and Cain is prow-man. For the devil gave
you the cargo of evils; and the serpent, steering Adam, brought him down to a
shipwreck of death; and Cain is prow-man, because through you, envy, he
himself first worked the murder. Your mast from the beginning in paradise is
the disobedience of the tree, your ropes are the chains of sins, your
passengers are the envious, the sailors the demons, the oars the deceits, the
neck is hypocrisy, the side-rowers the envious. O ship that bears ten thousand
evils!

If you seek hypocrisy, it is found there; if deceit, it is born there. Envy
holds office there, enmities, fights, fraud, contention, railing,
evil-speaking, blasphemy, and whatever we may say, and whatever we may omit,
all these the tartarean ship of envy carries. This ship of envy the flood had
not strength to sink, but Jesus sank it in the power of the Spirit, in the
fountain of baptism. This ship had also iron anchors, but they were recast
into the nails of Christ; this ship had also a sail, but the devil taking it
hewed it into a cross; this ship had also ropes, but Judas taking these hanged
himself. The Jews, embarking on this ship, striking against the rock, were
shipwrecked concerning the faith. Therefore until now the Jews swim in the
depth of ignorance. Yet as many of them as can lay hold of the Christ-bearing
merchant-ship are saved; but the others perish by a salty death of unbelief.
And would that even while they were in the deep they had run out toward Jonah
into the whale; for perhaps, learning from him the piety in Christ, living
with him, they would have been thrown out by the whale in Nineveh.

But now, having shut the eyes of the heart, they neither, seeing the
light-bearer of piety standing in the midst of the earth and flashing forth
the faith, take refuge in him; nor, taking refuge in John the preacher of
repentance, do they live. And going out, it says, the Pharisees took counsel,
how they might destroy him. And going out, it says, the Pharisees. From where?
Not from envy, but from the law. For if they had not gone out from the law,
they would not have insulted the Lawgiver of the law. And the Pharisees going
out. From where? From the law that says: You shall not kill the innocent and
the just. And the Pharisees going out. From where? From the commandment of God
that enlightens the eyes of the heart. For the commandment of the Lord is
far-shining, enlightening the eyes =61.709=. And going out the Pharisees took
counsel, how they might destroy him. And the Pharisees going out. From where?
From the commandment, from the law, from life. For those who plot death for
another themselves first leave the light of life. And going out the Pharisees
took counsel. And the writer did not say from whom, stirring our mind toward
the inquiry. And going out the Pharisees took counsel. From whom? It is clear
that from the devil, the man-slayer from the beginning. And going out the
Pharisees took counsel, how they might destroy him. The "How": in what manner?
by a stone? by a sword? by fire, or by water? But their counselor the devil,
recalling the tree from the beginning, through which he put Adam to death,
takes refuge in it again, and advises that they crucify Jesus with wood. But
he did not know that that wood was going both to undo the ancient
transgression of the tree, and to make life rise upon men. And going out the
Pharisees took counsel, how they might destroy him. O worst counsel, sown
through envy, and convicted through envy! O vain counsel! For they do not know
that God is not destroyed. For they will dissolve the earthen lamp of the
body, but they cannot quench the unquenchable lamp of his Godhead. And going
out the Pharisees took counsel, how they might destroy him. O marvelous deed,
and new mystery! Jesus came to seek and to save that which was lost, and these
seek to destroy the finder of the lost.

For what cause, tell us, Jews, do you plot such things against him? But they
are ashamed to say; we will publish their shame everywhere. For why, they say,
does he raise the dead? and why does he heal the sick? why does he speak good
things? why does he do good things? why does he teach good things? For all
these charges they plot to destroy him. When infant children go out taking
palms, through the palms proclaiming beforehand his victory, and in his praise
saying, Hosanna in the highest, they, as if slaughtered with hunting-spears,
are stung by envy; for the praises of those envied are arrows of those who
envy. And they come to Jesus, and say to him: Do you not hear what these are
saying? And they did not know that what was written was being fulfilled
prophetically: Out of the mouth of babes and sucklings you have perfected
praise. When Jezebel made the forged letters against Naboth, you gladly
endured it; when Pilate writes the good letters on the title, you are
indignant, and you hinder them from being written. For what do they say? Do
not write, that he is The King of the Jews. Do not write, =61.710= that is,
The truth; that is, Let no one be helped, let no one be sound toward piety.
They were certainly not so indignant against those who write the dramas of the
dancers, nor even now if the many hear the songs of the demons. And about
those they feel no pain, but they suffer that the divine teachings are
written.

And is it not beyond every evil, that the fooleries of Philistion should be
written, but the doxologies of Jesus, through which we are led to heaven,
should not be written? And if there were some teacher unskilled in speech, yet
saying the things of God, would it not then be better that his things be
written rather than the demonic mythologies of the Greeks? For it is
profitable for the young to be trained, singing in the sayings of the
theologian teachers, even if they happen to be unskilled, having a blameless
mind, and not, occupying themselves in the teachings of the demons, to be
driven mad into incontinence. Do not write. Why, Do not write? For what harm
does it do you? And of what goods does the writing of the truth deprive you?
Another, in the eagerness of what was being written, did not have a painful
hand, and they were beating their breasts. But even if Pharisees said these
things to Pilate, and hindered him from writing the letters of the truth; yet
he, even though he was a Gentile, said to them: What I have written, I have
written. This teaching also fits us, so that when the heretics counsel us to
move away from the glory, we may say to them: What we have written, we have
written. And going out the Pharisees took counsel, how they might destroy him.
But Jesus knowing it withdrew from there. Knowing, not having learned from
another, but having known of himself; for he was, and is, knower of hearts, as
also John the evangelist says: For he had no need, it says, that anyone should
tell him about man. But Jesus knowing it withdrew from there, not fearing
death, but awaiting the time of the cross.

For he had first to raise the dead, heal the sick, fill many who were hungry,
establish many in the faith, and then come to the celebrated death, or rather
the bridal chamber. He had first to set up the wood of the mystery of the
cross, and then with warm spirit run down upon those lying in Hades. But Jesus
knowing it withdrew from there. Where deceit is, he does not appear; he flees
from deceit. Where murder is being crafted, he withdraws; where love is woven,
both the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit lodge. Let us therefore weave,
beloved, the threefold love, not with deceitful tongues, but with working
hands; that Emmanuel may be with us, to whom be the glory and the power unto
the ages of ages. Amen.
