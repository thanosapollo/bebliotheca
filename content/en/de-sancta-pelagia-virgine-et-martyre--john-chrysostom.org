ENCOMIASTIC HOMILY On the holy martyr Pelagia in Antioch.

=50.579=

αʹ. Blessed be God: women now make sport of death, and girls mock at dying,
and virgins quite young and untried in marriage dance upon the very spear-
points of Hades and suffer nothing dreadful. All these good things have come
to pass for us through the Christ born of a virgin; for after those blessed
pangs and that most dreadful childbirth, the sinews of death were loosened,
the power of the devil was relaxed, and death has become an object of
contempt not for men only, but also for women, and not for women only, but
also for girls. For just as some excellent shepherd, seizing a lion that was
terrifying the flocks and ravaging the whole herd, and knocking out its
teeth, and cutting off its claws, and shaving off its mane, makes it an
object of contempt and ridicule, and hands it over thereafter to the
shepherd-lads and girls to make sport of; so Christ, taking death, which was
fearful by nature to our nature and feared by our whole race, and dissolving
the fear of it, handed it over to all, so that even virgins make sport of
it.

For this reason the blessed Pelagia also ran to it with so much eagerness
that she did not even wait for the executioners' hands, nor enter the
courtroom, but by the excess of her own zeal outran their savagery. She had
prepared herself indeed for tortures, and instruments of punishment, and
every kind of retribution, but she feared lest she lose the crown of her
virginity. And that you may learn that it was the wantonness of the impious
she feared, she anticipates it, and snatches herself away beforehand from
the shameful outrage; a thing which none of men ever attempted, but all
followed into the courtroom, and there displayed their courage; but women,
because of the delicacy of their nature, contrived for themselves this
manner of dying. For if it had been possible both to preserve her virginity
and to attain the crowns of martyrdom, she would not have declined the
entrance into the courtroom; but since it was absolutely necessary that one
of the two fall away, she thought it the height of folly, when it was in her
power to win both victories, to depart crowned with only half of them.

For this reason she was not willing to go away to the courtroom, nor to
become a theater for wanton eyes, nor to allow lecherous gazes to gloat over
the contemplation of her appearance and to outrage that holy body; but from
her chamber and the women's quarters she went to another chamber, heaven.
Great indeed is it also to see the executioners standing round and boring
through one's sides; but this is no less than that. For to those whose
senses have already been worn out beforehand by the variety of tortures,
death no longer appears fearful, but a kind of release and relief from the
evils pressing upon them; but she who had suffered nothing of the kind as
yet, but still had her body intact, and felt no pain whatsoever, needs a
great and courageous spirit, if she is going to bring herself out of the
present life by a violent death. So when you marvel at those for their
endurance, marvel at this one for her =50.580= courage; when you are amazed
at those for their patience, be amazed also at this one for her noble
spirit, that she dared such a death.

And do not pass over what happened in a casual way, but consider how
probable it was that a tender girl, knowing nothing beyond her chamber, with
soldiers suddenly besetting her, standing before her door, calling her to
the courtroom, dragging her to the marketplace over matters of such a kind
and such magnitude; no father within, no mother present, no nurse, no
maidservant, no neighbor, no friend, but alone taken in the midst of those
executioners.

That she was able to go out and answer those executioners, those soldiers;
that she could open her mouth and utter a voice; that she could even see,
stand and breathe; how is this not worthy of astonishment and admiration?
These things were not of human nature; the greater part was contributed by
the inclination of God. Yet neither was she herself standing idle at that
time, but she displayed everything that was her own: the zeal, the purpose,
the noble spirit, the willingness, the choosing, the hastening, the
eagerness; but that all these things came to completion was from the help of
God and the favor from above; so that it is right both to admire her and to
call her blessed; blessed for the alliance of God, admired for her own zeal.
For who would not justly be astonished to hear that in a single turn of the
moment she both took counsel on such a resolution and ratified it and
brought it into deed? Yet surely you all know that the things often
meditated by us over a long time, when the moment comes that demands the
trial of us, and a slight fear seizes our understanding, we all spill out,
terrified all at once by the anguish. But she was able in a single turn of
the moment both to entertain a resolution so dreadful and fearful, and to
ratify it, and to fulfill it in deeds; and neither the terror of the things
present, nor the sharpness of the moment, nor the desolation of her who was
plotted against, nor her being threatened while alone within, nor anything
else of the kind disquieted that blessed one; but as if some friends and
acquaintances were present, so she did everything with permission; and very
reasonably. For she was not alone within, but she had Jesus as her
counselor; he was present with her, he touched her heart, he encouraged her
soul, he alone cast out the fear. But he did not do these things casually,
but because the martyr had first prepared herself worthy of his help.

βʹ. Going out then, she asked the soldiers for leave to go in and change her
dress; and going in she changed her dress, putting on incorruption instead
of corruption, immortality instead of death, life without end instead of a
temporary life. I however, in addition to what has been said, admire this
too: how the soldiers granted the favor, how the woman deceived the men, how
they foresaw nothing of what was going to happen, how they did not perceive
the trick. For it is not possible to say this, that no one ever did such a
thing; for many perhaps have thrown themselves over a precipice, and cast
themselves into the sea, and driven a sword through their breast, and
fastened a noose about themselves, and that era produced many dramas of this
kind; =50.581= but God blinded their hearts, so that they did not perceive
the trick.

Therefore she soared up out of the midst of the nets; and just as a deer,
fallen into the very hands of the hunters, then escaping from there to a
summit of a mountain impassable and inaccessible to the feet of hunters and
the release of arrows, stands thereafter at the end of her run, looking down
with boldness upon those who had plotted against her before; so she too,
fallen into the very hands of the hunters, and held fast within the walls as
if in nets, ran up not to a mountain summit, but to the very summit of
heaven, where it was no longer possible for them to approach her; then
seeing them from there withdrawing with empty hands, she rejoiced, beholding
great shame being wrought for the unbelievers.

Consider how great a thing it was: the judge sitting, the executioners
standing by, the instruments of punishment made ready, the whole crowd
gathered, the soldiers waiting, all drunk with pleasure, expecting to have
their quarry; that those who had gone out for this should come back with
heads bowed, telling the drama that had happened. With how much shame, how
much pain and reproach was it likely that all the unbelievers be put to
flight; how they withdrew with heads bowed and veiled, learning through the
deeds that their war was not against men, but against God! Now Joseph, when
he was being plotted against by his own mistress, leaving the garment seized
by those defiled hands in the hands of the barbarian woman, went out naked;
but she did not even allow her body to be seized by those wanton hands, but
ascending naked in soul, and leaving her holy flesh behind with the enemies,
she cast them into great perplexity; for they no longer had anything they
could make use of in the relic. Such are the achievements of God: to lead
his own servants out of impossible circumstances into great ease, and to
cast those who oppose and war against him, even out of circumstances that
seem full of resources, into the utmost perplexity. For what was harder than
that perplexity into which the girl had then fallen? And what was more
abundantly supplied than the ease in which the soldiers then stood? They had
her alone taken within, shut up in the house as in a prison; and yet they
went away failing of their hunt. Again, the girl being destitute of allies
and helpers, seeing no way of escape anywhere from the dangers, and being
near the mouths of those beasts, snatched, one might say, from the very
throat of them, she escaped the plots, and prevailed over soldiers, judges,
rulers. And while she lived, all these expected to get the better of her;
but when she died, then they fell into greater perplexity, that they might
learn that the death of martyrs is the victory of martyrs. And the same
thing happened as if some freight-ship carrying countless cargo and full of
precious pearls, about to be submerged by the impact of some wave right at
the mouth of the harbor and made to go under, escaping, and being crowded
along by the very rush of the waters toward the harbor, were conveyed
thither with greater speed.

So too the blessed Pelagia. For the assault of the soldiers and the fear of
the expected instruments of punishment and the threat of the judge, falling
upon her more violently than any wave, hastened her to wing her flight to
heaven with greater speed; and the wave about to sink the ship escorted her
to the harbor that lies calm; and her body was carried along thereafter,
brighter than every lightning flash, smiting the =50.582= eyes of the devil.
For not so fearful to us is a thunderbolt hurled from the heavens, as the
body of the martyr, harder to endure than any thunderbolt, terrified the
phalanxes of the demons.

γʹ. And that you may learn that these things did not happen without God, it
is evident most of all from the very display of her zeal, and from the
soldiers' failure to perceive the trick, and from their granting the favor,
and from the deed's reaching its end; but no less than what has been said,
this can be perceived also from the very manner of her death. For many,
carried down from a high roof, suffered nothing dreadful; and others again,
having certain members of the body maimed, lived a long time after the fall;
but in the case of that blessed one, God allowed none of these things to
happen, but at once commanded the body to release the soul, receiving her as
having contended sufficiently and having fulfilled the whole. For her death
was not from the nature of the fall, but from the command of God. The body
lay thereafter not on a bed, but on the ground; yet it did not lie without
honor on the ground, but the ground itself was honored, since it received a
body clothed in such glory.

For this very thing above all, its lying on the ground, made that body more
honored; for outrages endured for Christ yield us an abundance of honor. It
lay then on the ground, in the narrow lane, that virginal body purer than
all gold; and angels encircled it, and all the archangels honored it, and
Christ was present. For if masters escort with honor the more gentle of
their slaves at their death, and are not ashamed, much less would Christ
have been ashamed to honor with his presence her who released her soul for
his sake and undertook so great a danger. It lay then, having as a great
burial garment the martyrdom, adorned for the world by her confession,
clothed in a robe more precious than every royal purple, than every precious
scarlet; and this robe was twofold, that of virginity, and that of
martyrdom; with these burial garments she will stand at the tribunal of
Christ. Let us too strive for some such robe to put on, both living and
having died, knowing that one who has adorned his body with golden garments
has gained nothing, but has even drawn many accusers, as not ceasing from
vainglory even at death; but one who has put on virtue will have many to
praise him even after death. For brighter than the royal courts themselves
will that tomb be to all, wherever there lies a body that has lived together
with virtue and piety. And of this you are witnesses, who run past the tombs
of the rich, though they have golden garments, as if they were caves, but
run with great zeal to this holy one, since the martyr departed, having
clothed herself with martyrdom and confession and virginity instead of
golden garments.

Let us imitate her then according to our power; she despised life, let us
despise luxury, let us laugh at extravagance, let us depart from
drunkenness, let us flee gluttony. It is not simply that I say these things
now, but because I see many, after this spiritual theater was dissolved,
running off to drunkenness and taverns, and the tables in inns, and the
other unseemliness. Therefore I beseech and exhort you always to keep this
saint in memory and mind, and not to disgrace the festival, nor to take away
from us the boldness that comes to us from this feast. For we too,
conversing with the Greeks, are adorned not a little by the multitude of the
feast, shaming them and saying that a whole city and so great a =50.583=
crowd one girl, after her death, draws to herself each year, and after so
many years no length of time has interrupted this succession of honor; but
if they perceive the things done at the festival, the greater part of our
encomiums has been taken from us. For this crowd present today, if it comes
with orderliness, is our greatest ornament; but if with negligence and much
carelessness, a reproach and an accusation.

δʹ. That then we may glory in the multitude of your love, let us depart
homeward with the same orderliness, with such as is fitting for those who
have communed with such a martyr to go away. For if anyone does not depart
home in this way, he has not only gained nothing, but has drawn upon himself
the greatest danger. I know that you are clean of these disorders, but this
is not enough for your defense; but you must also lead and establish the
brothers who behave disorderly into the greatest orderliness, in the seemly
form. Have you honored the martyr with your presence? Honor her also with
the correction of your own members; if you see disorderly laughter, and
unseemly running, and a servile gait, and an unbecoming posture, go up, and
look sharply and terribly upon those doing these things. But do they despise
and laugh the more? Take with you two or three brothers, or even more, so as
to become the more awesome from the number; if even so you do not bring them
round from that folly, make these men known to the priests; or rather, do
this before they arrive at such shamelessness as to be past remedy, so as
not to yield, and be shamed, and abate their disorderly and childish dancing
even when admonished and entreated. Even if you gain ten, or three, or two,
or one only, you will enter into a great bargain.

Long is the length of the road; let us then use this length for the
gathering up of the things said; let us fill the highway with incense-smoke.
For the road will not appear so stately if one, placing censers throughout
it, waves the fragrance through the air, as it will appear stately now, if
all who travel it today, recounting to one another the contests of the
martyr, walk home, each making his own tongue a censer. Do you not see, when
a king enters a city, with what orderliness the soldiers march in file on
either side in armor, encouraging one another =50.584= quietly, and
proceeding with much awe, so as to appear worth seeing to those who watch
them? Let us imitate those men at least; for we too have a king going before
us, a king not sensible, not earthly, but the Lord of the angels. Let us
then also enter in this way, encouraging one another with orderliness to
proceed with measure and order, so that we may astonish those who behold us
not only by our number, but also by our adornment.

For even if no one else were present, and we walked along the road alone, we
ought not so to behave unseemly, because of the sleepless eye, everywhere
present and seeing all things; but now consider that many of the heretics
are mingled among us; and if they see us dancing in this way, laughing,
shouting, playing the drunkard, they will go away passing the last judgment
upon us. And if one who scandalizes a single person undergoes punishment
without release, what punishment shall we give, who scandalize so many? But
God forbid that anyone be found accountable for the things said, after these
words and this exhortation. For if even before this, these things ventured
upon were unpardonable, after this counsel and this rebuke it makes the
punishment much more irremissible, both for those doing these things, and
for those overlooking what is done.

That then you may deliver even those from the punishment, and procure for
yourselves a greater reward, take charge of the guardianship of our
brothers; urge them to the gathering and the narration of the things said,
so that having studied these things through the whole way, and carrying home
the leftovers of this table to those who stayed behind and were left at
home, you may make the feast brilliant there also. For so we shall receive
the greatest fruit of this feast, and shall draw to ourselves the holy
martyr's fuller goodwill, honoring her with true honor. For to come here and
make tumult brings her far less pleasure than that we depart, having
profited and been helped by some spiritual benefit.

May it come to pass, through the prayers of this saint and of those who
contended with her, that you keep an exact memory both of these things and
of the other things said, and, showing forth all things through your deeds,
continue well-pleasing to God in all things, to whom be the glory and the
power unto the ages of ages. Amen.
