On the Ascension of our Lord Jesus Christ. Sermon 4.

=52.799=

... and brightening the hearts of the faithful. Bright also is the subject of
the present feast; and why it is bright, we shall show in the things that will
be said next. In six days, then, God made all his works, as it is written, and
on the seventh he rested. Therefore also in the last days the Word of God,
having been well pleased to seek and save that which was lost, and having
become man, in the same manner according to the number of the days of the
world's making handed over to us the feasts of his own economy. First, then,
and root of the feasts of Christ, is the paradoxical birth according to the
flesh from the holy Virgin and Theotokos Mary after the conception, in which
Christ God came, having bowed the heavens, that he might save a world that was
perishing; in which angels, appearing to the shepherds and making known the
paradox of the wonder, cried: Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace,
among men good will. Therefore it is also justly named the first feast. And
the second feast is the epiphany of Christ our God, according to which, coming
in the Jordan, he showed to all men the condescension of his unspeakable
compassion; in which also a voice of the Father came from heaven, bearing
witness concerning him: This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.

But also the Spirit, having come in the form of a dove, remained upon him, the
consubstantial Trinity having clearly appeared in one moment of time; of which
John, the Forerunner of the Lord, became a worthy eyewitness. Concerning which
epiphany the blessed Paul the apostle also said: The saving grace of God has
appeared to all men. Since, then, it is by nature the salvation of all men, it
is worthily named also a greatest feast by the pious. The third of the feasts
is the holy and worshipful and all-venerable day of the saving passion of
Christ our true God, in which, having been nailed in the flesh to the cross,
he blotted out the bond of sin that stood against us; in which also he
brought Adam into paradise by one word, who had been cast out of paradise
through disobedience. For he says to the thief: Today you shall be with me in
paradise. Whence this too has been established as a greatest feast, and
especially so; for Paul has said: Christ our Passover has been sacrificed for
us. The fourth feast is the all-glorious and peace-making day of the holy
resurrection of Christ our God, according to which, having also been in the
underworld, having crushed the everlasting bars and torn apart the bonds, he
rose from the dead, having won a victory against Hades, having raised up
together the righteous who lay in it, and having entered in to the disciples,
he awarded them the peace. Whence, outside of all contradiction, this
celebrated day has been reckoned by the faithful the mother of feasts. The
fifth feast, then, is the holy ascension of the Lord into the heavens,
concerning which we are now also making our treatment; and it is the fifth of
the feasts, because it was also accomplished on the fifth day of the week.

And for this reason we ought to keep feast, because today Christ has brought
up into the heavens the firstfruits of our lump, that is, the flesh. Therefore
the Apostle also said: He raised us up together and seated us together in the
heavenly places in Christ Jesus. Whence the devil, originator of evil and
inventor of sin, fell through the bulk of his exaltation and the conceit of
his pride, and there, by the greatness of his own loving-kindness, Christ set
in his place the man who had been cast out of paradise through that one's most
wicked counsel. The present feast too, then, has been established as worthy of
honor and of a spiritual choir. But since at the beginning of the discourse we
mentioned a number of seven feasts according to the days of the world's
making, and have shown five feasts only up to the present =52.800=, it is
necessary to bring forward the other two feasts as well, and then in this way
to lead the discourse by the hand to the history before us. The sixth of the
feasts, then, is the sixth all-praised day of the coming of the Holy Spirit,
according to which the gift of the Holy Spirit awaited according to the
promise was sent from the heavens by Christ to the holy apostles in the form
of fiery tongues, which was also named Pentecost because of the completion of
the seven weeks.

Unquestionably, then, this too has been established as a greatest feast for
the pious. The seventh feast is the awaited day of the resurrection of the
dead (for we the faithful look for this hope), in which the lover of mankind
and impartial judge, Christ our God, is about to render to each according to
his works. This is the true rest, on account of which the apostle Paul also
said: Let us be eager to enter into that rest. Then therefore, and especially,
there is a feast filled with joy and gladness and exultation for those who are
about to inherit what eye has not seen, and ear has not heard, and has not
arisen upon the heart of man, the things which God has prepared for those who
love him. So that, beloved, let us too pray to keep feast together with the
righteous in the bridechamber of the kingdom of the heavens, not only for one
day, or two, or three, but unto endless ages. Therefore, the seven feasts
having been shown to us, let us return to the matter at hand. And our
narrative is concerning the present feast in the heavens, I mean that of the
ascension of Christ. For David has said: And he bowed the heavens and came
down, and gloom was under his feet; he signified, that is, the coming of the
Word from the heavens upon the earth. And he said gloom under his feet, making
known the garment of the Godhead through flesh; so that his coming was also
unknown to the many because of his lowliness and meekness. For again the same
prophet says: Lord, your way is in the sea, and your paths in many waters, and
your footsteps shall not be known. For if they had known, he says, they would
not have crucified the Lord of glory. And again he said: He mounted upon
Cherubim, he flew upon the wings of winds. He said upon Cherubim, because he
himself, being also in the heavens with the Father, was borne upon the
Cherubim; for even while living among men upon the earth, he was in no way
separated from the Cherubic and heavenly throne; and by wings of winds he
hints at the clouds, in which he was also taken up, as it is also written in
the Acts of the Apostles: And a cloud received him from their eyes. Then next
it says: And as they were gazing into heaven as he went, wonder and ecstasy
and a stir came upon the apostles; for being mortal in nature, and
unaccustomed to such spectacles, they sought with the mind.

But someone will say that Peter and James and John had also foreseen the
transfiguration. Those men did indeed foresee the Lord transfigured, and a
cloud overshadowing him, but they did not see the cloud snatched into the air,
and taking the Master up into the heavens. That was a wonder, and this too is
a wonder; but a wonder more fearful than a wonder, this higher than that; even
if the power and the mystery belonged to one God. Then Moses and Elijah
appeared to those around Peter, speaking with him; but now a Cherubic throne,
that is, an invisible power, having come suddenly upon the cloud that was
covering, snatched away the Master as he was conversing with the servants.
Then Peter answered with boldness =52.801=: Lord, it is good for us to be
here; let us make three tents here, one for you, and one for Moses, and one
for Elijah. But now not even one of the disciples was able to utter a sound,
nor to open the mouth, but struck with a very great fear they were beside
themselves. And next the Scripture adds, saying: And behold, two men stood by
them in white garments, who also said. Nor is this idle; for the men's being
white-clad is a sign of a greatest feast. And what did they say? Men of
Galilee, why do you stand looking into heaven? The exceeding wonder has struck
you down; you have forgotten him who said: I go to my Father and your Father,
and my God and your God. Or was it not for this that he said to you, I will
not leave you orphans; and again, My peace I give to you, my peace I leave
with you? Did he not promise you the promise of the Paraclete, that is, of the
Holy Spirit? This Jesus, who was taken up from you into heaven, shall so come
in the manner in which you beheld him going into heaven.

So he shall come with the clouds of heaven, with glory and much power; so he
shall come in season, when he is about to judge the inhabited world in
righteousness. For the Father has given him all the judgment. Do you not
remember that, being with you, he said: All authority has been given to me in
heaven and on earth? Then the apostles, coming to themselves, returned,
praising and blessing the Lord, and awaiting the coming of the divine gift,
that is, of the all-holy Spirit. And indeed what was said in the Psalms was
fulfilled: God has gone up in a shout, the Lord in a voice of a trumpet.
Clearly here the prophet signified the duality of the natures of the economy
in Christ; and in the same manner Thomas also, after the Lord's resurrection,
having touched his side, cried saying: My Lord and my God.

God has gone up in a shout, the Lord in a voice of a trumpet. In a shout,
because with unceasing voice they send up the thrice-holy hymn to God; and how
he was taken up upon a Cherubic =52.802= throne, we have already shown. And in
a voice of a trumpet, that is, of an archangel, signifying beforehand his
ascent into the heavens. But the Holy Spirit also proclaimed to the powers
above with the commanding voice: Lift up your gates, O you rulers, and be
lifted up, everlasting gates, and the king of glory shall enter. And the
powers said: Who is this king of glory? Then the Spirit: The Lord strong and
mighty, the Lord mighty in war. For he conquered the Enemy, he armed himself
against the tyranny of the devil in the human body, he quenched his fiery
darts; and having been nailed to the cross, and having tasted death, being
immortal, he despoiled Hades, and having been shown a victor he rose from the
dead; and having turned back the wandering sheep, behold he goes up carrying
it upon his shoulders to the ninety-nine that have not wandered, which feed in
the mountains, that is, in the heavens. The Lord strong and mighty, the Lord
mighty in war.

And again he says: The Lord of the powers, he is the king of glory. The
powers, then, when they heard, The Lord of the powers, crying out with one
voice the accustomed doxology, received the Lord with joy, and sent him as far
as the high and exalted throne. And what was said by David was fulfilled: The
Lord said to my Lord: Sit at my right hand, until I make your enemies a
footstool of your feet. O heretic, it was altogether toward the flesh taken
from us, ensouled from the holy Virgin.

Let those, then, who confess one nature upon Christ be put to shame. For the
divine nature, it is quite clear, was together from eternity with the throne
of majesty. And let the catholic Church be glorified. And may Christ God show
us all worthy of his eternal kingdom; to him be the glory and the might with
the undefiled Father, together with the Holy Spirit, unto the ages of ages.
Amen.
