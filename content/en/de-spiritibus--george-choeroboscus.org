{1General rules concerning Alpha.}1 Alpha, whether it denotes privation, as
ἄφιλος and ἄοικος; or intensification, as Ἄξυλος ὕλη, the wooded (wood); or
something bad, as Ἄφωνος, the ill-voiced; or something little, as Ἀμαθής, the
little-learned; or is redundant, as in στάχυς ἄσταχυς, σταφίς ἀσταφίς, σπαίρω
ἀσπαίρω; or means behind, as Ἀκόλουθος, the one making the path behind; or
together, as Ἄλοχος, the bedfellow, and Ἀκοίτης, the husband; or equal, as
Ἀτάλαντον, of equal weight, takes the smooth breathing. But if it means
gathering, it takes the rough breathing. e.g. ἅπας. Ἁλίζω, i.e. I gather
together. ἅλυσις, however, and ᾅδης, even if they have privative Alpha, take
the rough breathing. for from the privative particle Alpha, and λύω. and from
Alpha, meaning together, and δῶ; for Hades binds all. Alpha in the
prepositions takes the smooth breathing. ἀνά. ἀντί. ἀπό. since every
preposition also takes the smooth breathing. except those beginning from
Upsilon. Alpha before every vowel, in hiatus, takes the smooth breathing.
ἀαρών. ἀηδών. for αἷμα, and such, not being in hiatus, take the rough
breathing. Alpha in the postpositive articles takes the rough breathing. ἅ,
ἅς, αἷς. likewise also in the Doric. ἡμῶν, Ἁμῶν. ἡδύ, Ἁδύ. It should be noted
that Alpha before every vowel, and before the two aspirates Theta Chi, and
before Kappa Xi always takes the smooth breathing. 194

{1General rules concerning the breathing of Epsilon.}1 Epsilon when added, in
words that take the smooth breathing beginning from a vowel, partakes of the
smooth breathing, 199 but in those that take the rough breathing, of the
rough. and of the former an example is ὠνημένος, Ἐωνημένος, the one who
bought. ᾠνοχόει, Ἐῳνοχόει. ᾦξεν, Ἔῳξεν, i.e. opened. ἦν, Ἔην, i.e. existed.
εἴκοσιν, Ἐείκοσι. and of the latter ὥρων, ἑώρων. ὥρακα, ἑώρακα. ἥνδανεν,
ἑήνδανεν. εἷς, ἕεις. Epsilon that takes the rough breathing, if it takes on
Upsilon, takes the smooth breathing. e.g. ἕκηλος, the quiet, Εὔκηλος. ἕωλον,
yesterday's, Εὔωλον. but Εὔαδεν, i.e. pleased, takes the smooth breathing.
because ἔαδεν also does. for those who give it the rough breathing have erred.
Epsilon coming forward and falling away in the past tenses takes the smooth
breathing; and it comes forward in the active and passive of the indicative
verbs, e.g. τύπτω ἔτυπτον. λέγω ἔλεγον. τύπτομαι ἐτυπτόμην. and it falls away
in the other moods. Epsilon in the pronouns before a consonant takes the
smooth breathing. ἐγώ, ἐμοῦ, ἐμοί, ἐμέ, ἐμός, ἐμαυτόν. ἐκεῖνος. and the like.
but ἑαυτόν, and such, not being before a consonant, take the rough breathing.
Epsilon in the numerals takes the rough breathing. ἕν. ἕνδεκα. ἕξ. ἑξήκοντα.
ἑξακόσια. ἑπτά. ἑβδομήκοντα. ἑπτακόσια. ἑκτόν. except ἐννέα. ἐννενήκοντα. and
εἴκοσιν. Epsilon in the prepositions takes the smooth breathing. ἐπί, ἐξ. ἐκ.
ἐν. εἰς. ἐς. as also every vowel in them except upsilon. It should be noted
that Epsilon before three vowels, Epsilon Eta Iota, always takes the smooth
breathing. except Ἑέλδωρ, the desire. and before the three aspirates, Theta
Phi Chi, always takes the smooth breathing. except ἑφθός. and before the
20double20 Psi and before Gamma.

1 Every article, prepositive or postpositive, beginning from a vowel, takes
the rough breathing. prepositive, e.g. ὁ, οἱ, ἡ, αἱ, and postpositive ὅς, i.e.
whoever. οὗ, ᾧ, ὧν. duals, ὥ, οἷν. plurals, οἵ, ὧν, οἷς, οὕς, feminines, ἥ,
i.e. whoever. ἧς, ᾗ, ἥν. duals, ἅ, αἷν. plurals, αἵ, ὧν, αἷς, ἅς. neuters, ὅ,
οὗ, ᾧ, ὅ. duals, ὥ, οἷν. plurals, ἅ, ὧν, οἷς, ἅ. but ὦ of the vocative, taking
the smooth breathing, 212 is in many ways shown not to be an article, but a
vocative adverb. first, that every article beginning from a vowel wants to
take the rough breathing; but this takes the smooth breathing. second, that
every prepositive article admits either a breathing or an accent; but this has
both. third, that every article wants to differ in the three genders and in
the three numbers; in the three genders, e.g. ὁ αἴας, ἡ μοῦσα, τὸ βῆμα; and in
the three numbers, e.g. ὁ αἴας, τὼ αἴαντε, οἱ αἴαντες, ἡ μοῦσα, τὰ μούσα, αἱ
μοῦσαι, τὸ βῆμα, τὼ βήματε, τὰ βήματα; but these do not differ, neither in the
three genders, nor in the three numbers. fourth, that every article wants to
be of the first person or of the third, not of the second; but this is of the
second person. fifth, that every article wants to signify a clear and evident
number. sixth, that it does not keep the rule that says, every vocative wants
to have equal time with its own nominative, or less, but never greater; for
this is greater than its own nominative.
