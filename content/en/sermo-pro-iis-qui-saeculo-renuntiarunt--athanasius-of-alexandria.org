OF OUR FATHER AMONG THE SAINTS ATHANASIUS THE GREAT PATRIARCH OF ALEXANDRIA A
discerning discourse, also on the commandments of God, for those who have
renounced and wish to be saved. Beloved, let us take thought for our
salvation, because the time is shortened henceforth; and no one can take
thought for his own soul, while he takes thought for the things of the body.
For just as no one can look with one eye toward heaven, and with one upon the
earth, so a man cannot take thought for the things of God and the things of
the body. Just as a spring cannot from the same opening bring forth sweet
water and bitter, so a man cannot both please God and fulfill the desires of
the flesh. Just as a thorn cannot put forth fruit of olive, so a man cannot
both seek the heavenly things and long for the earthly, as the Lord says: You
cannot both please God and fulfill the desires of the flesh. Whence also the
apostle Paul, having heard these things, and having cut off from himself all
the thoughts of the flesh, says: "For whose sake I suffer the loss of all
things, that I may gain Christ," and may reign with him. "And having food and
coverings, with these let us be content. For those who wish to be rich fall
into temptation, and a snare of the devil, and unseemly desires." But you, O
brother, do not attend to anything of this world, that you may be able to be
saved, but practice righteousness, and truth, and love, and fear of the Lord,
and meekness, obedience, and self-control, and forgetfulness of wrongs. Hate
worldly words, that your heart may see the Fair One. Love to pray continually,
that your heart may be enlightened. Distribute to those who lack with a rich
eye, that you may not be put to shame before the judgment-seat of Christ; hate
the desire in foods, that Amalek of the passions may not encircle you. Be
swift in the prayers, that the beast may not devour you. Flee wine, that you
may not be deprived of the gladness of God. Love =28.1412= the faithful, that
through them you may show mercy. Long for the saints, that their zeal may draw
you toward virtue. Remember the departure of your soul, and you will not sin
much toward God; always recall the kingdom of the heavens, that desire for it
may draw you toward it; reason upon the gehenna of fire, and the terrible
torments, that you may hate the wicked works. Prepare yourself therefore each
day to meet Christ; for he is about to come invisibly. Each day examine
yourself from the commandments of Christ, in what you have fallen short, and
rebuild it. Let your brothers see your works, and the zeal according to God
will consume them. Search yourself each day, which passion you have put to
death, and give glory to God; for the mercy is his. Do not reckon yourself as
having accomplished the good before God; for he says: "When you have done all
things, then say that We are unprofitable servants." Do not be high-minded,
because from now on you are good; for you cannot entrust yourself to your
enemies. Do not take courage in your own body. He who fears the demons makes
it clear concerning himself that the fear of God is not in him. For he who
fears God from his whole heart is steadfast against them. Not to measure
oneself, but to hold oneself unknown, makes it manifest to the man that he
does not seek to do his own will, but that of God. He who speaks his word
before many makes it clear concerning himself that the fear of God is not in
him. He who wishes to be saved will have leisure for God, and will trample the
passions. To take thought in knowledge drives out the passions; for it is
written: "Care will come upon a wise man." Just as acedia is a help of the
passions, so also long-suffering is a power of the virtues. Not to judge a man
is an unassailable wall; but to blame or to speak against works destruction
for the soul. To hold the tongue makes it clear that he is practical in the
virtues; but the lack of training of the tongue signifies that he has not
acquired virtue within. Almsgiving in knowledge begets foreseeing, and leads
into love; but unmercifulness begets inhumanity. Sympathy is the mother of
virtues; but the fullness of evils is insubordination. Goodness begets
chastity; but irascibility begets the passions. Hard-heartedness begets anger;
but acedia begets want. Satiety of sleep is a disturbance of passions; but
measured vigil is salvation of the soul. Sleep in satiety is a multitude of
fantasies; but the flower of the mind is vigil in prayer. Much sleep thickens
the heart, but vigil thins it; and I mean the vigil that takes place according
to God in psalms, and hymns, and spiritual odes. For sleep in silence is good
of those who keep vigil in vain talk. Mourning drives out the passions, but
laughter separates from God. Not to strike a man begets humility; but the
glory of men begets =28.1413= pride. Freedom from anger begets meekness; but
remembrance of wrongs begets destruction. To love spaciousness drives out the
virtues; but constraint humbles the passions. Desire of foods leads into the
passions; but self-control drives them out. Adornment of the body is overthrow
of the soul; but wasting of the body is renewal of the soul. Contempt of the
body is salvation of the soul; but care of the body is a snare to the soul.
And wasting of the body is illumination of the soul. To keep the mind always
above begets the love toward God; but the care of life drives out the virtues.
To guard the mouth stirs the mind toward God; but much speaking begets acedia
and madness. To do the will of the neighbor signifies that the mind sees the
virtues; but to do one's own will signifies ignorance. Meditation in fear
guards the soul from every evil; but speaking worldly words darkens the soul.
To love the matter of the world separates the mind from God; but
possessionlessness looks with boldness to God. Not to bring out the thoughts
makes it clear that he seeks the shameful and vain glory of this world; but to
make them known to spiritual fathers drives them out. Just as rust eats, the
monk is eaten under the glory of men, if his heart is persuaded together with
it. Just as a worm, that is a bindweed, having twined with the vine, destroys
its fruit, so vainglory destroys the fruit of the monk, if his heart is
persuaded together with it. Before all the virtues is humility; but before all
the passions is gluttony. The end of virtues is love; but the fullness of
evils is to reckon oneself to be righteous. Just as a worm eating wood
destroys it, so remembrance of wrongs destroys the soul of the monk. To cast
oneself down before God begets bearing insult without disturbance; but not to
set oneself at naught begets pride. Do not be confident upon your own power,
and the help of God will sojourn with you. Do not have enmity with anyone,
since your prayer is unacceptable. Love truth; for he who lies sets aside the
Fair One. Guard your eyes, and your heart shall not see evil. For he who looks
at a woman in pleasure commits adultery. Guard your hearing, that you may not
gather to yourself every wicked seed. Flee from slander of the most wicked
demon, and be at peace with all, that you may have boldness when you pray. Do
not desire to hear a hurt of grief of any man, that you may not repay him in
your heart. Work in your handicraft, that a poor man may find your bread; for
idleness is death to the soul. To pray continually ransoms the soul from every
evil. But to be even a little negligent is established as a dwelling-place of
the demons. Sitting in your cell take thought continually of these: of prayer,
and of meditation, and of the handicraft. Each day remember where you are, and
what you are working, and when you depart, where you have to go, and attend to
yourself. If your thought stings you, that someone has something against you,
=28.1416= do not despise him, but put repentance to him. See that you do not
despise to accomplish your services at each hour; for they bring the
illumination in the soul. If you have done good things, do not boast; but if
you have done many evil things, do not grieve beyond measure, but stand, do
them no longer, and you will be saved. If a thought of fornication troubles
you, sacrifice unceasing prayer to God, and he will give you rest. If jealousy
and envy war against your heart, remember that we are all members of Christ,
and that the honor and the reproach of the neighbor is of us all, and it will
flee from you. And discernment, gathering all these things and reasoning them,
will embrace the virtues, and puts the wicked things out of the way. But it is
impossible for discernment to come to you, unless you first accomplish its
virtues, and before all, stillness. Stillness begets ascesis; ascesis begets
compunction and weeping; weeping begets fear; fear begets humility; humility
begets foreseeing; foreseeing begets love; love begets for the soul converse
with angels. Then the man knows at last that he is far from God. He therefore
who wishes, brothers, to come upon all these honorable virtues, must also be
free of care from every affair, and hate this world; and then at last he is
counted worthy to acquire these virtues from the Lord. But learn this,
brother, that every man eating and drinking without measure, or being carried
about in this world, shall not be counted worthy to come into these measures;
for if you have possessions and a vineyard, or dwellings, or anything of this
age, why did you not also marry a wife? Why, deceiving yourself, not knowing
that that which is sown among thorns does not come to full fruit? And he who
has not had leisure cannot know God. And this too I exhort every man wishing
to give repentance to God, to guard himself from much wine; for many are its
falls. Do not therefore cease to give heed to the struggle, as long as you are
in the body, nor trust yourself. Let us stand therefore, brothers, let us
stand in the fear of the Lord, and let us not grow weary in the struggle. "For
our struggle is not against blood and flesh, but against the principalities,
against the powers, against the world-rulers of the darkness of this age,
against the spiritual things of wickedness in the heavenly places." Let us put
on the weapons of the virtues, as hoplites; because the labor and the toil,
and the constraint, and the narrowness, and the calm of the desires not only
guard us from the darts of the enemy, but also establish us heirs of the
kingdom of the heavens. Let us contend therefore, brothers, in this short
time, that we may obtain the kingdom of the heavens. For our teacher Jesus
Christ has spoken thus: "Strive until you come through the narrow gate and the
afflicted way. Watch therefore in =28.1417= every hour, because you do not
know in what hour the Lord comes; lest he enter suddenly, and find you
sleeping. See that your hearts not be weighed down in surfeit and drunkenness,
and cares of living, and the hour come upon you suddenly. Behold, I send you
as sheep in the midst of wolves. Do not acquire gold, nor silver, nor two
tunics; nor fear those who kill the body. For I saw Satan fallen as lightning
from heaven.
Behold, I have given you the authority to tread upon serpents and scorpions,
and upon all the power of the enemy, and they shall not harm you." And not to
the apostles themselves only did the Lord give this, but also to all who wish
to be saved and to follow his commands. And see, you who contradict, the
myriads of the saints. For he gave us the authority to become children of God.
"Fear not, little flock, because your Father has been well pleased to give you
the kingdom of the heavens. Sell your possessions, and give alms, and you will
have purses that do not grow old, an unfailing treasure in the heavens." And
when his holy disciples had done this commandment also, he said to them: "My
peace I give to you, my peace I leave to you." For "he who loves me will keep
my commandments; and I and the Father will come, and we will make a dwelling
with him." And showing us to bear nobly the temptations and the wrestlings, he
said: In the world you will have affliction. But "take courage; I have
overcome the world. And you are those who have remained with me in my
temptations, and I appoint to you, as my Father appointed to me a kingdom,
that you may eat and drink at my table." And he says these things not to all,
but to those who endure the temptations, and the afflictions in
long-suffering. Going away to the holy passion of the cross the Lord spoke
thus: He who wishes to eat and drink at my table, go along with me upon the
cross; that is, let him also be put to death to the world, as I am, through
the practice of my commandments. And the apostle Paul, having heard, and
having cut off from himself all the thoughts of the flesh, dared to say: "I am
crucified with Christ. And I no longer live, but Christ lives in me." For as
long as we walk in fleshly things, we are dead in the sins. For he says:
"Those who are of Christ have crucified the flesh with the passions and the
desires." And if we die with him, we shall also live with him. If we endure,
we shall also reign with him; if we deny, he also will deny us. Who then is
the one who denies, if not the one who walks according to the desires of the
flesh? For this one is he who insults the baptism. And who of us is the one
who has not insulted the baptism through the varied devices of the evil one?
But through his great love, with which our Lord Jesus Christ loved us, he
=28.1420= gave us repentance until the last breath. For if there were not
repentance, all flesh would not have been saved, as the Lord says: "Amen, amen
I say to you, God does not rejoice over ninety-nine righteous, as over one
sinner repenting." Let us be eager, brothers, to repent, while we are in the
body; let us weep, and let us wash the hidden wounds of the heart. For the
terrible things done in us are about to be made manifest upon that fearful
judgment-seat. For the Lord has drawn near, and he comes upon the throne of
his holy glory, and all the nations will be gathered before him. And each is
made manifest from his own lamp; and whoever has not acquired oil, his lamp
will be quenched; and that one shall go in the outer darkness. Let us be eager
therefore, brothers, and let us fill our vessels with oil, that we may not be
cast in the darkness and eternal fire. For the vessel is repentance; and the
oil is the practice of the virtues; and the shining lamp is the holy soul. Let
us contend therefore, brothers, because the day has drawn near. Blessed is the
man who has this care each day and hour. For the fruit having become late-ripe
is a time of the harvest. Blessed is that one who has saved his fruit; the
angels will gather it into the eternal storehouse. But woe to those like me,
that is to the tares, because the fire will inherit them. Let us flee
therefore, O brothers, the world, and all the things in it, that we may
inherit those good things, "which eye has not seen, and ear has not heard, and
which have not arisen upon the heart of man, which God has prepared for those
who love him." For the rest, O brothers, stand in these things, meditate on
these things, and do not neglect, so that the Lord may count you worthy to
keep this commandment day and night, and may save you into his heavenly
kingdom. Whoever reads these commandments, or hears them, and does not do
them, the seed that fell upon the rock has been fulfilled in him, and because
it did not have earth, it was dried up at once. But he who reads and does is
the seed that fell upon the good earth, and it bore fruit thirtyfold, and
sixtyfold, and a hundredfold, in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom is the glory.
Amen.
