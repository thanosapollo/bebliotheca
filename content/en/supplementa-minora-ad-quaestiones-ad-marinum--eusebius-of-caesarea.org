* Lesser Supplements to the Questions to Marinus
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

αʹ. Eusebius in the writings To Marinus says the following: "For the
Church of Christ also legislates two lives and manners: the one
supernatural and beyond the common human way of life, admitting neither
marriages, nor the begetting of children, nor the possession of
property, but wholly and entirely altered from the common and customary
conduct of people who live in the world, and devoted to the service of
God alone, according to an excess of heavenly love. For those who pursue
this manner, seeming to have died, carrying about the mortal life and
the body alone upon the earth, but having transferred the soul in
thought to the heavens, as some heavenly beings they look upon the life
of men, consecrated to the God over all on behalf of the whole race: not
with sacrifices of oxen and blood, nor with libations and the savor of
fat, but with right doctrines of true piety and with a disposition of a
purified soul, and besides with works and words according to virtue; by
these they propitiate the Divine and accomplish the sacred ministry on
behalf of themselves and of their kindred. And the perfect and first
manner of the Christian way of life stands of this kind. But the second
and lower is more human, and altogether incomparable with the first:
which also condescends to temperate marriages and the begetting of
children, and to take care of household management according to justice,
and for those who serve as soldiers, and to prescribe what ought to be
done, and to take thought, with godliness, for fields and commerce and
the rest of the more civic conduct. For these also times of exercises,
and days of instruction and of hearing the divine oracles, have been
appointed, and a second degree of piety has been assigned, furnishing
the benefit that is fitting to such a life."
βʹ. This prophetic saying is of Malachi, not of Isaiah; it is therefore
a scribe's error, as Eusebius of Caesarea says in To Marinus, concerning
the seeming disagreement in the Gospels about the resurrection.
γʹ. For there was no one among the Jews in the city who was worthy, as
Eusebius says in chapter 13 of To Marinus, to bear the trophy against
the devil, the cross; but the one from the countryside, who had no share
in the bloodguilt against Christ.
δʹ. Eusebius of Caesarea, the one surnamed Pamphili, in the Inquiries
and Solutions to Marinus on the Savior's passion and resurrection, also
set these things forth for examination: that the divine evangelist Mark
said it was the third hour at which Christ our God and Savior was
crucified; but the most theological John wrote that at the sixth hour
Pilate sat down upon the judgment seat in the place called Lithostrotos,
the Pavement, and examined Jesus. And he says that this is a scribal
error, overlooked by those who from the beginning copied the Gospels.
For since the letter gamma signifies the third hour, and the episemon
the sixth, and these characters have a great resemblance to one another,
by error the letter gamma, which indicates the third hour, when the
straight line extended in length was bent, passed over into the
signification of the episemon, which indicates the sixth hour. For since
the three evangelists, Matthew and Mark and Luke, say in agreement that
from the sixth hour darkness came over all the earth until the ninth
hour, it is evident that the Lord and God Jesus had been crucified
before the sixth hour, before the darkness came to be, that is, at the
third hour, as Mark recorded; and that John likewise indicated the hour
to be the third, and that those who copied transferred the gamma into
the episemon.
εʹ. He appeared first to Mary Magdalene. Eusebius in To Marinus says
that this Mary is other than the one who saw the young man; or that both
were from Magdala.
ςʹ. Eusebius of Caesarea says that Mary Magdalene and Mary of James
prepared spices; but they are not the ones who came early, when the sun
had risen, but other unnamed women; for there were many who had come up
with the Savior from Galilee. Hence they do not arrive by night, but
early, and having heard that they should tell the disciples and Peter,
they fled, and said nothing to anyone; for they were afraid. For as
having arrived after sunrise they are not even deemed worthy to see the
Savior; nor was it possible that the Magdalene, after so many visions,
when the sun had risen, should be at a loss and not know who would roll
away the stone.
ζʹ. From Eusebius, from the writings To Marinus. We find, then, three
Marys in all at the passion of Christ, together with the other women:
first the Theotokos, second her sister Mary of Clopas, and third the
Magdalene. But some say that two of them are Magdalenes: one, the one
late on the Sabbath in Matthew, and another the one in John who came to
the tomb in the morning, and that this is the one also indicated by
Mark, from whom he had cast out seven demons. For as though there were
also another Magdalene, not of this kind, he says, Mark kept this,
saying: "And having risen early on the first of the Sabbaths he appeared
first to Mary Magdalene, from whom he had cast out seven demons." And
this one perhaps is the one who heard, "Do not touch me," but not the
one in Matthew. For even if that one also came from Magdala, still the
divine Scripture does not allege the same things of her as well. But if
someone should say there is one and the same Magdalene, even so the holy
Gospels would be true, disagreeing in no respect. And the same Mary
Magdalene is introduced as not touching the Lord earlier, when she was
weeping and unbelieving, and as touching him when she is bidden by him,
together with the other Mary, to rejoice; for last she worshipped him as
God, and not as a man, as earlier when she was weeping and unbelieving.
ηʹ. From Eusebius, from the writings To Marinus. And concerning the
further difficulty, how in Matthew the Lord was seen by the disciples in
Galilee on the mountain, but in John by the sea of Tiberias, we shall
say that he was seen not only once and twice, but also many times.
Therefore you would not err in saying that the appearance in Matthew is
a fourth vision, which Matthew recorded as having taken place not by the
sea of Galilee, nor to those who were appearing, but on the mountain to
the eleven, saying: "And the eleven disciples went to the mountain where
Jesus had appointed them; and seeing him they worshipped." And after
this you would not =22.1013= go wrong in saying that he was seen at the
same time by them gathered together, the eleven disciples together also
with the seventy. And the Apostle relates that the Lord was seen after
the resurrection by still more than these, saying: "That he was raised
on the third day according to the Scriptures, and that he appeared to
Cephas, then to the twelve, then he appeared to more than five hundred
brothers at once." Do you see how often and to how many he appeared
after the resurrection? So also to those hiding in Jerusalem for fear of
the Jews, and before all men to Simon Peter, and of women to those about
the Magdalene. Hence Luke also says: "Appearing through forty days and
lodging with them, he delivered the teachings of the kingdom of God."
θʹ. From Eusebius, from To Marinus. And the Holy Spirit breathed by
Christ upon the apostles was such as to loose and forgive every sin; but
the promised [Spirit], that they would be baptized in the Holy Spirit
and partake of a power of operations, operative of the miracles that
were going to be accomplished through them, and also steadfast and
enduring of the dangers that were going to rise against them. For before
this power he did not wish them to preach to the nations, but to wait
and remain until they partook of it from on high. And this gift the
Apostle also, distinguishing it especially from the other operations of
the Holy Spirit, taught, saying: "And to another workings of powers."
And the prophecy says: "The Lord will give a word to those who
evangelize with great power." Of which power, when they did not yet
partake, they all denied him and were scandalized at the time of the
passion; but when they partook of it, they all shone forth in
testimonies and confessions on behalf of Christ. For the Lord, renewing
man, and giving back again the grace which he lost from the inbreathing
of God, breathed into the faces of the disciples, saying, "Receive Holy
Spirit."
ιʹ. And Eusebius Pamphili in the letter To Marinus interprets the matter
set forth thus: that there are divisions of gifts according to the
Apostle, but the same Spirit. And again: to each is given the
manifestation for the common good. "For to one is given through the
Spirit a word of wisdom, and to another a word of knowledge according to
the same Spirit; to another faith in the same Spirit, and to another
workings of powers, and to another prophecies, and to another
discernings of spirits, to another =22.1016= kinds of tongues; and all
these things the one and the same Spirit works, dividing to each one
individually as he wills." For he clearly showed through the one and the
same Spirit that there are many powers, operative of different things.
See, then, whether perhaps here he gave them a certain partial authority
and spiritual grace: not so as to raise the dead and work powers, but so
as to forgive sins; for the gifts of the Spirit are different.
Therefore he also joins on, saying, "If you forgive the sins of any,
they are forgiven them," and what follows, showing that he bestowed on
them this kind of spiritual gifts. But after the ascension the Spirit
himself came down and supplied to them the powers and operations of the
signs and of every other gift. For this reason here he said without the
article, "Receive Holy Spirit," making clear that he gives them a
certain partial operation of the Spirit; but concerning that [later one]
he says: "You shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon
you."
