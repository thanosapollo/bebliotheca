4. And afterwards Judaism, having received the character through circumcision
from the times of Abraham and in Moses, seventh from Abraham, through the
given through him


law widened by God, and from Judah, fourth son of Jacob who was surnamed
Israel, through David the first who reigned from the tribe of the same Judah,
having been allotted the complete name of Judaism. For clearly concerning
=1.165= these four heresies the apostle, rebuking, said "for in Christ Jesus
there is not barbarian, not Scythian, not Greek, not Jew, but a new creation."
Differences of the Greeks:

5. Pythagoreans, or also Peripatetics: Pythagoras dogmatized the monad and
providence and the forbidding of sacrifice to the so-called gods, and not to
partake of living things, and to abstain from wine. And he distinguished at
the same time the things from the moon and above, saying they are immortal,
and the things below mortal; and transmigrations of souls from bodies into
bodies as far as animals and beasts, and he taught to practice silence at the
same time for a time of five years. And at the last he named himself a god.

6. Platonists: God and matter and form, and that the world
is generated and corruptible, but the soul ungenerated and immortal and
divine; and that there are three parts of it, rational, irascible, and
appetitive; and that women become common to all and that no one has one wife
of his own, but that those who wish consort with those who are willing; and
likewise transmigration of souls into differences of bodies as far as beasts,
and they also dogmatized many gods from the one.

7. Stoics, dogmatizing that the all is body and thinking this perceptible
world to be God, and some declared that it has its nature from the substance
of fire. And they define God as mind and =1.166= as soul of the whole existing
vault of heaven and earth; and his body the all, as I said, and the luminaries
as eyes. And that the flesh of all perishes and the soul of all is
transmigrated from body to body.

8. Epicureans supposed atoms and indivisible bodies, both homogeneous and
infinite, to be the beginning of all things, and they dogmatized that the end
of happiness is pleasure, and that neither God nor providence administers
affairs.

9. Samaritanism and the Samaritans from it, which is from Judaism, before
heresies were established among the Greeks and before their doctrines were
constituted, but after the religion of the Greeks existed, and taking its
occasion in the middle of Judaism from the times of Nebuchadnezzar and the
captivity of the Jews; being settlers from the Assyrians into Judaea and
receiving the Pentateuch of Moses alone, the king having sent to them from
Babylon through a priest called Esdras, having all things equal with the Jews,
except for abhorring the nations and not even touching certain things and
except for denying the resurrection of the dead and the other prophecies after
Moses. Four nations of Samaritans:

10. Gorothenes, who keep the feasts at other times than the Sebuaeans.

11. Sebuaeans, differing from the Gorothenes for the same reason of the
feasts.

12. Essenes, opposing neither of the two, but keeping feast with those who
happen to be present indifferently.

13. Dositheans, living by the same customs as the Samaritans also,
circumcision and sabbath and the rest, and using the Pentateuch, but more than
the others keeping abstinence from living things and living in fasts most
continually. And some of them also =1.167= have virginity, and others practice
continence. And they believe the resurrection of the dead, which is foreign to
Samaritans. Seven heresies of the Jews:

14. Scribes, who were lawyers and repeaters of traditions of the


elders among them, keeping by the more excessive will-worship customs which
they have not learned through the law, but patterned for themselves,
observances of a justification of the legislation.

15. Pharisees, interpreted "separated," who live the highest and are
supposedly more approved than the others; among whom there is also
resurrection of the dead as also among the Scribes and the assent concerning
angels and Holy Spirit, that it exists, and a different way of life,
continence for a time and virginity, and fasting twice in the sabbath,
purifications of pints and of plates and of cups, as also among the Scribes,
tithes and firstfruits and continual prayer and will-worshipping figures of
dress through the cloak and the dalmatics, that is the colobia, and the
widening of the phylacteries, that is signs of the purple, and fringes and
little pomegranates on the wings of the cloak, which things were signs of the
continence among them until a time; who also introduced genesis and fate.

16. Sadducees, interpreted "most righteous," who were from the race of
Samaritans and at the same time of a priest named Zadok, denying the
resurrection of the dead and not receiving angel nor spirit, but in all the
rest being Jews.

17. Hemerobaptists: these were Jews in all things, but they said that no one
obtains eternal life, unless he should be baptized every day.

18. Ossenes, who are interpreted "most daring." And these were accomplishing
all things according to the law, but they also used other writings after the
law, and they rejected the greater number of the later prophets.

19. Nasaraeans, interpreted "rebels," who forbid all flesh-eating, do not
partake of living things at all, and as far as Moses and Joshua the son of Nun
they use and believe the holy names of patriarchs in the Pentateuch, I mean
Abraham and Isaac and Jacob and those above and Moses himself and Aaron and
Joshua. But they dogmatize that the writings of the Pentateuch are not of
Moses, and they affirm that they have others besides them.

20. Herodians, who were Jews in all things, but expected Herod as Christ and
assigned to him the honor and name of Christ. This is the first tome,
containing against all these twenty heresies; and in it also the subject
concerning the sojourn of Christ and the confession of the truth. =1.234=
These things are also in this second tome of the first book, in which there
are thirteen heresies as follows:

21. Simonians, those from Simon the magician of the time of Peter the apostle,
of the village Gitthon of Samaria. This man sprang from Samaritans, but
assumed the name of Christ only. And he taught shameful-doing, the mixing of
the defilement of women as indifferent; and he rejects the resurrection of
bodies and says that the world is not of God. And an image of himself as of
Zeus and of the harlot with him, named Helen, a type of Athena * he handed
over to his own disciples for worship. And he said that he himself was the
Father to Samaritans, but the Christ to Jews.

22. Menandrians, those who began from this Simon through a certain Menander,
but differing from the Simonians in some respect; who said that the world had
come to be from angels.

23. Satornilians, those who in Syria strengthened the shameful speech of the
Simonians, but preaching other things besides the Simonians unto a more
excessive =1.235= shock, having had the beginning from Satornilus, who himself
also according to Menander said that the world had come to be by angels, but
of seven only, beside the mind of the upper


father.

24. Basilidians, initiates of the same shameful-working, from Basilides who
together with Satornilus had been a disciple of the Simonians and Menandrians,
thinking the like things, but differing in some respect. And he says there are
three hundred sixty-five heavens and he sets out angelic names for these.
Wherefore also the year is of so many days, and the name Abrasax is of the
same reckoning and is 365. And this, he says, is the holy name.

25. Nicolaitans, from Nicolas who was appointed over the widows by the
apostles, who through jealousy of his own wife taught his disciples to
accomplish the shameful-working together with the others, and introducing
concerning Kaulakau and Prunicus and other barbarian names.

26. Gnostics, succeeding to the same heresies, but working the shamefulness
more than all of them madly, and in Egypt called Stratiotics and Phibionites,
and in the upper parts Secundians, in other parts Socratites, and by others
Zacchaeans; and others call them Coddians, and others call them Borborites.
These boast of Barbelo who is also Barbero.

27. Carpocratians, from a certain Carpocrates of those in Asia, who taught to
accomplish every shameful-working and every practice of sin. And unless one,
he says, should pass through all things and fulfill the will of all demons and
angels, he cannot go up into the highest heaven nor pass by the principalities
and the authorities. And he said that Jesus had received an intellectual soul,
and knowing the things above =1.236= proclaimed them here, and that if anyone
should do things like Jesus, he is according to him. And he forbids the law
together with the resurrection of the dead, as do the heresies from Simon
onward. Of this man was Marcellina in Rome. And having made images in secret
of Jesus and Paul and Homer and Pythagoras he offered incense to these and
worshipped.

28. Cerinthians, who are also Merinthians. These from Cerinthus and Merinthus,
certain Jews boasting circumcision, and saying that the world had come to be
by angels, and that Jesus is called Christ according to progress.

29. Nazoraeans, confessing Christ Jesus son of God, but living in all things
according to the law.

30. Ebionites, similar to the aforementioned Cerinthians and Nazoraeans, to
whom in some respect the heresy of the Sampsaeans and Elkesaites was joined,
who say that Christ was created in heaven and the Holy Spirit, and that Christ
sojourned in Adam first and at times strips Adam himself and puts him on
again; for they say he accomplished this in his incarnate presence. And being
Jews they use gospels, they abhor flesh-eating, and they have the water
instead of God, and that Christ put on a man in the incarnate presence, as I
said. And they are baptized continually in the waters both in summer and in
winter, for purification supposedly, like the Samaritans.

31. Valentinians, who forbid resurrection of flesh, and set aside the old
covenant, and reading the prophets they receive as many things as can be
tropologically likened to their heresy, but introducing certain other
mythologies, saying namings of thirty aeons, male-female, generated together
from the Father of all =1.237=, whom they also consider gods and aeons. And
that Christ brought a body from heaven and passed through Mary as through a
pipe.


32. Secundians, to whom Epiphanes and Isidore are joined, these also using the
same syzygies, having thought the like things as Valentinus, but narrating
other things besides him in some measure. And they add the shameful-working,
teaching it; and these also forbid the flesh.

33. Ptolemaeans, being themselves also disciples of Valentinus, to whom Flora
is joined. And they themselves also say the same things concerning the
syzygies as Valentinus and the Secundians; but they too differ in some
respect. This also is the recapitulation of the second tome of the first book
of the thirteen heresies.

are thirteen heresies:

34. Marcosians. A certain Mark has come to be a fellow-student of Colorbasus,
himself also introducing two principles. And he sets aside resurrection of the
dead, and by cups from an incantation into a dark-blue color and purple †
having changed, he initiates the deceived women. And he himself also wants the
all † to be led by the twenty-four elements similarly to Valentinus.

35. Colorbasusians. And this Colorbasus likewise narrating the same things,
but differing in some respect toward the other heresies, I mean toward those
around Mark and Valentinus, taught the projections and ogdoads otherwise.

36. Heracleonites, they too are carried by the mythology of the ogdoads, but
otherwise than Mark and Ptolemaeus and Valentinus and the others. But also
toward the end they redeem those who die among them similarly to Mark through
oil, opobalsam and water, saying certain invocations with Hebrew words upon
the head of the one supposedly being redeemed.

2.2 37. Ophites, who glorify the serpent and consider this to be Christ, and
having by nature this reptile, a serpent, in a certain chest.

38. Cainites likewise together with the former set aside the law and the one
who spoke in the law and denying resurrection of flesh they glorify Cain,
saying that he is of the stronger power. And at the same time they also deify
Judas, and together also those around Korah and Dathan and Abiram, but also
the Sodomites.

39. Sethians. These again glorify Seth, saying that he is from the Mother
above, who repented over having projected those around Cain, then after Cain
was cast out and Abel was killed having come together with the Father above
and having made Seth a pure seed; from which thereafter the whole race of men
was brought down. And they themselves also dogmatized principalities and
authorities and as many things as the others dogmatize.

40. Archontics. These again refer the all to many rulers and say that the
things that have come to be have come to be from these; and they are also
caught in a certain shamefulness. And they set aside resurrection of flesh and
slander the old covenant. And they use both old and new covenant, twisting
each saying into their own mind.

41. Cerdonians, those from Cerdon who succeeded to the share of the error from
Heracleon, and added to the deceit; who having migrated from Syria to Rome set
out his preaching in the times of bishop Hyginus. And this man preaches two
principles 2.3 opposed to one another, and that Christ is not generated; and
likewise he sets aside resurrection of the dead and the old covenant.

42. Marcionites. Marcion, originating from Pontus, was a son of a bishop,


and having corrupted a virgin he fled because he had been expelled from the
church by his own father, and having gone up to Rome and having asked
repentance those of that time * and not having obtained it, having been lifted
up against the faith he dogmatized, introducing three principles, good and
just and evil, and that the new covenant is foreign to the old and to the one
who spoke in it. He sets aside resurrection of flesh, he gives baptism not
only one, but even two and three after falling; and others among them are
baptized on behalf of the dead catechumens. And without fear he also permits
women supposedly to give a washing.

43. Lucianists. A certain Lucian, an ancient one, not the one who came to be
now in the times of Constantine, dogmatized all things according to Marcion,
but he himself also dogmatizes other things besides Marcion, supposedly more
excessively.

44. Apelleans. And this Apelles likewise to Marcion and Lucian reviles the
whole creation and the one who has made it. But he did not introduce three
principles similarly to these, but one principle and one God, being highest
and unnameable, and that this one himself has made another. And this one who
came to be, having been found wicked, made the world in his own baseness.

45. Severians. A certain Severus again, following Apelles, rejects wine and
the vine, mythologizing that it has grown from the serpent-formed Satan and
earth, they having come together with one another. And 2.4 he also declines
the woman, saying that she is of a left power. And he introduces certain
namings of rulers and certain apocryphal books. And similarly to the others he
sets aside the resurrection of flesh and the old covenant.

46. Tatianists. This man flourished together with Justin the martyr the holy,
who is also philosopher. And after the death of the martyr and philosopher
Justin he was corrupted toward the doctrines of Marcion, having become a
disciple of the same, and he dogmatized the equal things to him and adding
other things besides that man. And he was said to originate from Mesopotamia.
These are the chapters of the first book of the three tomes against forty-six
heresies. =2.211= These things are also in this first tome of the second book,
being fourth according to the sequence of the number of the tomes from the
beginning, in which there are 18 heresies:

47. Encratites, who are a fragment of Tatian, rejecting marriage, saying that
this is of Satan, and forbidding all eating of living things.

48. Cataphrygians, who are also Montanists and Tascodrugites, who receive old
and new covenant, but introduce other prophets after the prophets, boasting a
certain Montanus and Priscilla.

49. Pepuzians, who are also Quintillians, to whom Artotyrites are joined, they
are from these Cataphrygians, but they dogmatize other things besides those,
deifying a certain deserted city Pepuza in the middle of Galatia and
Cappadocia and Phrygia and considering this Jerusalem, and there is also
another Pepuza, and assigning to women both to rule and to priest; and they
initiate by pricking a young child. And they mythologize that to Quintilla or
also Priscilla Christ has been revealed there in Pepuza in the form of a
female. And they use old and new covenant likewise, altering according to
their own mind.

50. Quartodecimans, who accomplish the Pascha on one day of the year, on
whatever day the fourteenth =2.212= of the moon falls, whether on a sabbath
or on a Lord's day, both fasting that day and at the same time keeping vigil.


51. Alogi, so called by us, who throw out the gospel of John and the Word in
it, God always from the Father above, not receiving the gospel itself, the one
according to John, nor his Apocalypse.

52. Adamiani, and by some called Adamizers, having the doctrine as a joke or
rather as truth. For naked as from the mother, men and women gather to the
same place, and so they accomplish the readings and prayers and whatever else,
supposedly being both monastics and continent and not receiving marriage,
considering their church paradise.

53. Sampsaeans, who are also Elkesaites, still to this day dwelling in Arabia,
a country lying above the Dead Sea; who have been deceived from a certain
Elxai, a false prophet, of whose race there still were until now Marthous and
Marthana, two women worshipped by the heresy as goddesses, they have all
things similarly to the Ebionites.

54. Theodotians, those from Theodotus the cobbler from Byzantium. This man had
become supreme in Hellenic education, and being together with others in the
days of the persecution then, having fallen alone, and those having been
martyred, because he was reproached he devised to say that Christ is a mere
man, so that he might not come under a charge of having denied God, so he
taught. 55. Melchizedekians, who honor Melchizedek, saying he is a certain
power and not a mere man and having dared to refer and say all things unto his
name.

56. Bardesanians. This Bardesanes originated from Mesopotamia, at first
belonging to the true faith and distinguished in wisdom =2.213=, but having
turned aside from the truth he dogmatized similarly to Valentinus, apart from
certain few things concerning which he differs toward Valentinus.

57. Noetians. This Noetus was from Smyrna of Asia. And having been carried by
arrogance together with certain others he taught Christ as son-father, saying
that the same is Father and Son and Holy Spirit. And he said that he himself
was Moses and he said that his brother was Aaron.

58. Valesians. These, as we have supposed, who dwell in Bacathus, a
mother-village of Arabia of Philadelphia, who castrate those who happen by and
who are guests

having been cut. And they teach certain other things full of heresy, making to

59. Cathari, who were joined together with Novatus the Roman and who reject

60. Angelics. These have altogether died out; and they were boasting to have

61. Apostolics, who are also Apotactics, and they themselves dwelling about

62. Sabellians, who hold the like things of Noetians, except for not saying

63. Origenists, of a certain Origen. And these are shameful-doers, doing

64. Other Origenists, of Origen who is also Adamantius the composer, who
reject the resurrection of the dead, and Christ as a creature and the Holy
Spirit


introducing, and allegorizing paradise and heavens and all the other things,
and talking nonsense that the kingdom of Christ will cease. These again are
the 18 heresies of the second book, first tome.

3.1 These things are also in the second tome of the same second book, being
fifth according to the aforementioned number, in which there are five heresies
as follows:

65. Paulianists from Paul of Samosata who became bishop of the great Antioch.
This man all but affirms that Christ is non-existent, having fashioned him as
an uttered word, and that he is from Mary onward, having the things said
concerning him in the divine scriptures as announced beforehand, but not
pre-existing, but having come to be from Mary onward through the incarnate
presence.

66. Manichaeans, who are also Acuanites, the disciples of Manes the Persian,
saying Christ in appearance, and worshipping sun and moon, and praying to
stars and powers and demons, introducing two principles, evil and good, always
existing; and that Christ appeared in seeming and suffered in seeming;
blaspheming the old covenant and the God who spoke in it, and defining that
not the whole world, but a part, has come to be from God.

67. Hieracites, those from Hieracas the Leontopolite of Egypt, a certain
exegete, setting aside resurrection of flesh, but using old and new covenant,
and forbidding marriage altogether, and receiving 3.2 monastics and virgins
and the continent and widows. And that children who have not yet come to age
do not share in the kingdom because they have not contended.

68. Melitians, those in Egypt, being a schism but not a heresy. Not having
prayed together with those who fell in the persecution, but now having been
joined to Arians.

69. Arians,
who are also Ariomaniacs, who say the Son of God is a creature and the Holy
Spirit a creature of a creature, affirming that the Savior received flesh only
from Mary and not a soul. And this Arius was a presbyter of Alexander bishop
of Alexandria. This also is the recapitulation of the second book of the
second tome, being fifth from the beginning of the sequence, of five heresies.
=3.230= These things are also in the first tome of the third book, being the
sixth tome according to the number we said before, in which there are seven
heresies together with the schisms as follows:

70. Rebellion and schism of Audians, not however a heresy. These possess a
well-ordered manner of life and living, and they are in all things having the
faith as the catholic church, and the greater number dwell in monasteries. And
they also use many apocrypha to excess. And they do not pray together with us,
blaming the bishops among us, calling them rich and others otherwise. And they
also do the Pascha separately, when the Jews do it. And they also have
something peculiar and contentious, interpreting "according to the image" most
dryly.

71. Photinians. This Photinus, being from Sirmium, was still even until now
going about, who thought the same things as Paul of Samosata. And they differ
toward that man in some respect, and they themselves also affirm Christ from
Mary onward.

72. Marcellians, those from Marcellus from Ancyra of Galatia. This man at the
beginning having thought similarly to Sabellius was reported. And having come
to defenses often and having defended in writing he was accused by many of
remaining in the same things. And perhaps having changed his mind he maybe
corrected himself or his disciples. For on behalf of him and on behalf of


his disciples certain orthodox moderately made defense.

73. Semiarians, who confess Christ as a creature, but by irony saying he is a
creature not as one of the creatures; but, they say, we call him Son, and
because of not attaching passion to the Father through the =3.231= having
begotten we call him created. Likewise also concerning the Holy Spirit they
define it altogether a creature, throwing out the consubstantial of the Son,
and they wish to say like-in-substance. And others of them also threw out the
like-in-substance.

74. Pneumatomachi. These are well concerning Christ, but they blaspheme the
Holy Spirit, defining it created and not being from the Godhead, but rather by
catachresis having been created through an energy, saying it is only a
sanctifying power.

75. Aerians. This Aerius originated from Pontus. And still even until now he
is a trial to life. This man was a presbyter of the bishop Eustathius who was
accused among Arians. And because Aerius himself was not appointed bishop, he
dogmatized many things against the church, being in the faith a most complete
Arian, but he dogmatizes more excessively. He says one must not offer for
those who have fallen asleep before, and he forbids fasting on the fourth day
and the day before sabbath and Lent and Pascha; he preaches renunciation, but
he uses all sorts of flesh-eatings and luxuries without fear. And if anyone of
his should wish to fast, not on appointed days, he says, but when he wishes;
for you are not under law. And he says that a bishop is nothing more different
than a presbyter.

76. Aetians, those from Aetius the Cilician, who became a deacon under George
the bishop of the Arians of Alexandria, who are also called Anomoeans, and by
some Eunomians, through a certain Eunomius who became a disciple of Aetius and
is still living. And with them also was Eudoxius arianizing, but supposedly
through fear toward the emperor Constantius =3.232= he separated himself and
Aetius alone was banished. And Eudoxius remained arianizing, not however
according to Aetius. These the Anomoeans, who are also Aetians, altogether
alienate Christ and the Holy Spirit from God, affirming him created, and they
say he has not even some likeness. For from syllogisms both Aristotelian and
geometrical they wish to present God, and they define by such manners that
Christ supposedly cannot be from God. And those from him called Eunomians
rebaptize all who come to them, and not only, but also those who come from
Arians; and they baptize turning the feet of those being baptized up toward
the head, as a great report is sung. And they say that for someone to have
fallen in fornication or another sin is nothing. For God seeks nothing but
that someone be in this their supposed faith only. And these likewise are also
the heresies of the third book of the first tome, being sixth according to the
sequence, seven heresies. =3.415= These things are also in the second tome of
the same third book, being seventh according to the division of the number of
the tomes which we said, which is tome seven and the end of the whole
treatise, in which there are four heresies:

77. Dimoerites, who are also Apollinarians, who do not confess the incarnation
of Christ as complete; of whom some dared at one time to say the body
consubstantial with the Godhead, and some at one time denied that he had
received a soul, and some, leaning on the saying "the Word became flesh,"
denied that he had received the according-to-flesh from created flesh, that is
from Mary. And they said only contentiously that the Word had become flesh,
unto


afterward, what they have in mind I do not know to say, they say he did not
receive a mind.

78. Antidicomarianites, who say that the holy Mary the ever-virgin after
having begotten the Savior was joined to Joseph.

79. Collyridians, who on a certain day of the year appointed offer a certain
collyris unto the name of the same Mary, on whom we put a name following their
