ON HIS OWN LIFE
The intent of the speech is to recount the course of my evils, or indeed of my
blessings; for some would say thus, others otherwise, according as, I think,
they stand in their leaning. For wishing is not a sure criterion. And metre
plays as a medicine of grief, a lesson and a sweetness for the young at once,
a pleasant consolation. And the speech is toward you, who were once ours, but
now strangers, and as many as are of like mind, or if there is some bastard;
for all are well-disposed to us when we have closed our eyes. Men, the
renowned eye of the inhabited world, who dwell, as I see, in the second world,
clothed in the beauty of earth and sea, new Rome, seat of other nobles, city
of Constantine and pillar of power, hear, men, a man most without falsehood
and one who has toiled much in many turns, from which there is also knowing
more. All things have grown weary; and the good things too have grown weary
with time. Nothing, or a narrow remnant; as when earth has been swept by a
rush of violent rains, pebbles alone are what remain. Not yet a great thing,
if I speak of the many, who even before were not in the rank of the good,
cattle-like and bent downward. But we are the terrible and rough torrent-bed;
our system is loosed, I speak lamenting, as many of us as sit unworthily
high-throned, presidents of the people, teachers of the good, allotted to feed
souls with divine food, yet ourselves starving; physicians of passions,
corpses teeming with ungrudging diseases; guides of paths perhaps over cliffs,
which they never guided nor have travelled; whom it is not even the shortest
dogma to follow, nor the surest teaching of salvation; whose bema is an
accuser of their manner, a lattice fencing off not lives but eyebrows. From
which I was led to give these things to the speech (for it is not dear to me
to rhapsodize many things in vain), let everyone hear, both those now and
those later. And it is necessary to speak my circumstances a little from
above, even if I must be long-winded, that false speeches may not prevail
against us. For the wicked love, of the things they do badly, to turn the
causes upon those who have suffered, that they may harm them still more by the
lies, and slip themselves out from the charges. Let this be the preface of the
speech. I had a father both beautiful and good exceedingly, aged, simple in
manner, a rule of life, truly a patriarch, a second Abraham, being, not
seeming, best, not in the present fashion; a wanderer before, afterward a
friend of Christ, then a shepherd, whatever is the power of shepherds. And a
mother, to speak briefly, yokefellow of so great a man and an unswaying
balance, from the pious before still more pious, female in body, but in manner
beyond a man; both a common speech equally from life. By what is this clear?
How shall I prove the word? I will bring her herself as witness of what I
said, her who bore me, mouth of truth, accustomed rather to hide something of
the manifest than to boast of the unclear for glory's sake. For fear led her,
which is a great teacher. She, longing to see the offspring of a male child in
the house, a thing dear to many, conversed with God and asks to obtain her
longing. And since she was hard to restrain in mind, she gives as a gift him
whom she thought fit to receive, even anticipating the giving by her
eagerness. And so she does not miss her dear prayer, but there came to her a
favorable prelude, a vision bearing a shadow of the things asked. For my form
stands by clearly, and a calling; and the grace of the night was a deed. For I
become to them, if indeed I am worthy of the prayer, the gift of the God who
has given; but if I am to be deprecated, of my own sin. Thus then I came into
this life, mixed with clay (alas, wretched me) and composition, by which we
are mastered or master things scarcely; yet having received a pledge of every
finest thing, the birth itself;

for it is not lawful to be ungrateful. And when I came, at once I become a
stranger with the good estrangement. For I am set before God as some lamb or a
dear calf, a well-born victim and one honored by speech (I shrink from saying,
as some young Samuel, unless I should look to the longing of those who gave).
And reared in all the good things from swaddling-clothes (for I had the best
types from home) already I was taking some reverence of old age, and little by
little, as a cloud out of gloom, the eagerness for the better was gathering
for me. And I went forward with speech also running with me, and I rejoiced in
the books that advocate for God, and I kept company with the men best in
manner. Such then were these things; but the rest I do not know what path of
speech I should cut. Shall I hide the wonders by which God urged me on, having
taken eagerness as the best beginning (for thus he knows how to draw toward
salvation), or shall I set them forth eagerly having spoken them into the
midst? For the one is ungrateful, the other not outside of pride. Better to be
silent (it will suffice that I know), lest also the present things seem to
fight with the speech, falling far short of the eagerness then. What then is
necessary, I will make known to the many. My cheek was downless, and a hot
love of speeches held me. For I sought to give the bastard speeches as helpers
to the genuine, that those who had learned nothing except the vain and empty
fluency, which lies in noises and in throats, might not be lifted up, and that
I might not be in want against the snares of sophisms. But that never came
into my mind, to set anything of my educations further forward. And what the
heat of the young always suffers, easily fanned by unordered impulses, as a
colt darting into courses full of spirit, this I have suffered. For wholly out
of season, the sea no longer being tamed (some tail of Taurus they say is not
without danger, those skilled in these things, a voyage of daring, but not of
mind), then therefore leaving Alexandria (for from there too I plucked
something of speeches) I put out and cut at once the sea of Hellas. The flanks
of Cyprus; and a standing of the winds boiled the ship and all things were one
night: earth, sea, air, heaven growing dark; and thunders answered the
shakings of lightnings, and the ropes roared as the sails were filled. The
mast leaned, and there was no strength of steering-oars; for the necks of the
hand were snatched by force. And the hull was full of water coming over the
walls. And a cry mixed and full of lamentations of sailors, boatswains,
masters, †ἐπηβόλων† calling on Christ from one concord, and of those who
before did not know God; for fear is a more timely teaching. And what was the
most wretched of all evils, the ship was without water; for at once as the
ship was whirled a trough was shattered and was sown in the deep, which held
the sweet treasure of water. And there was a contest of hunger and of storm
and of winds that we become corpses. Of this God saw a swift release. For
merchants of Phoenicia appearing suddenly, though they were in fear, having
learned by prayers the strait of the danger, by props of poles and beatings of
hands having fled the ships (for they were strong) they save us already
corpses of the sea, as fishes leaving the sea or a lamp dying when there is no
food. But the sea grew wild and roaring still more against us for more days,
we neither knowing where we sailed in many turns nor seeing any salvation from
God. And while all feared the common death, the hidden one was more dreadful
to me. For I was being alienated from the purifying waters by which we are
made divine, by stranger-killing waters. This was the lament, this my
disaster, to this I sent cries, stretching out hands, out-sounding the great
roar of the waves, having torn my tunic, lying prone, wretched. And what is
not credible, yet very without falsehood, all leaving their own disaster
gathered with me prayerful outcries, sailors being pious in common evils; so
they shared the pain of my sufferings. You also then were, my Christ, a great
savior, and now freeing me from the waves of life. For since nothing of good
hope was present, no island, no mainland, no peaks of mountains, no beacon, no
stars as lookouts for sailors, nothing small, nothing greater of the things
seen, what do I contrive? What way of the hard things? Having despaired of all
things below

I look toward you, my life, breath, light, power, salvation, who frighten, who
strike, who smile, who heal, always weaving the good with the opposites. And
having reminded you of all the former wonders by which we know your greatest
hand, Egyptians worn out by scourges, the sea split and Israel having
travelled, enemies defeated by a lifting of hands, creation itself enslaved to
generals, walls sacked by trumpets and by running, and having added my things
to those cried of old, "Yours," I said, "I am, both before and still now. You
will take me twice, a possession of those precious to you, a gift of earth and
sea, purified by a mother's prayer and by extraordinary fears. I will live for
you, if I should escape a double danger. You will lose a servant, if you
should cast me away. And now a disciple in the swell; shake sleep from me or
walk the sea, and let fear stand." These things I said; and the standing of
the winds ceased, and the sea falls, and the ship is straight-sailing. And
this too is a merchandise of my prayer; for the whole complement of the ship
went away being pious toward great Christ, having received a double salvation
from God. And having passed Rhodes a little later we put in to an Aeginetan
harbor (for the ship was Aeginetan), sailing with a fair wind. Then Athens and
speeches. But of the things there let others speak, how we conducted ourselves
first in fear of God, knowing first things first; and how in the prime of
youths and a rush of daring, others being stung with other factions, we thus
went through a quiet life (some marine spring, I think, sweet against bitter
waters, as indeed it is believed), neither being dragged by those who carry
toward harm, and ourselves dragging friends toward the better. For God has
done this well for me too: he joined me, bringing me, to the wisest man, the
only one above all in life and in speech. Who is this? You will know very
easily. It was Basil, the great profit of the present life. Him I had as
partner of speech and of roof and of plans. If one must even boast: we were a
pair not obscure to Hellas. All things indeed were common, and one soul
needing the separation of two bodies. And what led us into one especially,
this was: God and longing for the better things. For from when we came to so
much boldness as to speak out even the depths of the heart, we were bound more
to one another by longing. For like-mindedness is faithful toward growing
together. What remains? Fatherland and types of life. For much time had been
worn by speeches; already this was nearly my thirtieth year. There I
recognized what longing toward us and what glory our playmates had. The time
was present, and much toil was present. There was need of embraces and of
gloomy speeches, of the fire-kindlings of memory at departure. Him they
yielded to by force and with difficulty, yet they yielded, when he spoke many
causes of the going-out. But for me even now the tear runs ahead as I remember
the confusion then. All standing round me with much speed, strangers,
familiars, age-mates, teachers, with oaths, lamentations, and mixing in some
force (for loving persuaded them to dare this too) they held me fast, saying
they would not send me from there even if something should happen (for it was
not fitting that we fall out of honored Athens), as if they would give the
power of speeches from a vote; until they bent me (for it was of an oak alone
to stand against so many lamentations and speeches), yet not completely; for
fatherland pulled against, holding by faith nearly those under the sun, in
which it appeared among the good things to philosophize, and parents worn by
old age and time. So still remaining a little time at Athens I stole the
going-abroad almost in secret; I came, I showed speeches, I filled the disease
of some who demanded this of me as some debt. For I have no account of
applauses and noises nor of affectations and twistings, in which the wise
rejoice in a crowd of youths. And first I set this before myself to
philosophize, to throw to God both the other things and the toils of speeches,
as those who have left their properties to sheep-pasture or have gathered gold
into the depths of brine. But what I said before, I danced for the friends.
These were as it were gymnastic exercises of contests or also preliminaries of
greater mysteries.

And there was need thereafter of manly counsels. Within I seat a court of
friends, genuine advisers of my reasonings. A terrible whirl held my mind as I
sought the better among the better things. To throw the things of the flesh
into the depths had long been decided and then pleased still more. But as I
looked at the divine paths themselves it was not very easy to find the better
one, and exceedingly. For for the sake of some another thing appeared good or
bad, as often among things to be done. Such was mine, to liken it to
something, as one thinking of some long going-abroad had fled sailing and
sea-toils, and I was tracking which road was more passable. I had in speech
Elijah the Tishbite and the great Carmel or a strange food, the possession of
the Forerunner, the desert, and the unequipped life of the sons of Jonadab;
and again a longing for divine books prevailed and light of spirit in
contemplation of speech, a thing not a work of desert nor of quiet, until
having leaned often upon both I finally arbitrate for the longings in this way
and I stopped the wandering of the mind moderately. For seeing those whom a
practical life delights, useful to others of those in the midst, but useless
to themselves and whirled by evils, from which the smooth character is waved
out, and those outside somehow more stable and looking toward God with a quiet
mind, but useful to themselves alone by a narrow affection and living an
outlandish and rough life, I came to some middle of the desert-dwellers and
the mixed, bringing the collected mind of the one, the useful of the other.
And there was present something greater also and a grace of the precious, I
mean those who begot me, to whom I am indebted. Their old age (for it is most
pious to assign to parents the first honor from God, from whom there is also
the knowing of God) I warmed, I supported with all strength, I led by the
hand, that I might set old age as a right hand for myself, propitiating old
age. For we reap the very things we also sow. This was a part for me of
philosophic education: not to seem to work out the first life, but to be
rather than seem a friend to God. I thought then that one must also love the
practical, as many as have been allotted some honor from God, leading peoples
in divine rites. But a greater longing of the monastic held me, though I
seemed to have been ranked with more; for the monastery is of manners, not of
bodies. And the bema was holy to me, but to one standing far off, as light of
the sun to weak sights. I would have hoped all things more than to receive
this in many turns of life. Say nothing great, briefly, being a human. Envy
always cuts down exaltations. Take nothing from outside, but look at my
things. To one thinking thus a terrible shaking falls. For my father, though
knowing my mind most exactly, I know not from where, perhaps moved by paternal
affection (and affection with authority is a terrible thing), that he might
hold me with the fetters of the spirit and honor those he had with the better
things, bends me by force into the second of thrones. Thus then I was pained
at the tyranny (for I cannot yet name this otherwise, and let the divine
Spirit forgive me being so), so that loosed at once from all, friends,
begetters, fatherland, race, as those of the cattle struck by a gadfly, I went
to Pontus to set for myself as a medicine of grief the inspired one of my
friends. For there he practiced the company of God, covered with a cloud as
some one of the wise of old; this was Basil, who is now among angels. With him
I softened the grieving of my mind. And when the good father, worn by old age
and by longing, asked much of his child to grant honor to his last breaths,
and time pressed the disaster upon me, as it never should have, I run again
into the deep, fearing a groan of paternal motions, lest the affection fall
out for me into a curse; such a thing is simplicity angered. Some small
interval, and again a third wave, it is not possible to say how much wilder.
And nothing is worse than to make all things known to friends. My brother was
practicing some worldly office (my brother, O most wicked, as much as you are
able), and it was a trust of treasuries. And in the middle of the office he
dies, and a multitude of dogs rises up against the goods and remains of the
dead. Servants tore all things,

strangers, friends. For who does not gather wood when an oak has fallen? But
I, as far as it came to my speech, would never have feared onrushes of
affairs; for I am a bird easily aloft. Yet there was need to bear all things
through with the good father, both the useful and the opposites, being a
partner of affairs, not of moneys. And as those whose footing is not fixed at
first, having slipped once, fall into the depth of a cliff, no longer being in
control of themselves, so for me having tasted of the evils another terrible
thing was raised upon others. There came to us sometime (I will be silent of
the in-between, that I may not seem to bring out a blasphemous speech against
a man whom now I have ceased blessing), but there came to us the dearest of
friends, Basil (alas for the speeches; yet I will speak), another father to me
much more burdensome; for the one I had to endure even when tyrannizing, but
the other not by necessity for the sake of a companionship that brought harm,
not a release from evils. I do not know whether I should blame more my own
sins, which have bitten me many things many times, the thing that fell
together boiling over as always of a young man, or you for the exaltation,
best of men, which the throne has given? As for the rest at least, be gracious
to the speech, perhaps you yourself would not have thought it right to
over-yoke; not then, O best, nor did you think it right; and if you thought it
right, perhaps some well-minded judge of those who know both well would have
held you. What then have you suffered? How have you thrown us so much at once?
May a law of friendship that so reveres friends perish from life. We were
lions yesterday, but today I am an ape; and to you even a lion is a small
thing. If indeed you saw all friends thus (I will utter a high speech), you
ought not me, whom you also led before friends once, before you were above the
clouds having all things below. Why, heart, do you boil? Hold the colt by
force. The speeches again toward the turning-post. He was a liar to me, but in
other things most without falsehood, who having heard me saying these things
often, that now all things are bearable, even if worse should fall, but if
those who begot me should leave life, there is every speech for me too to
leave the affairs, that I might gain something of the hearthless life, being
most easily a citizen of every place: these things then hearing and agreeing
with the speech nevertheless he forced me into a throne of episcopacy, himself
and my father twice tripping me in this. Do not yet be disturbed, before you
learn the whole. If the ill-disposed had planned most time how they might
dishonor, I think they would have found no other than the present manner. Do
you long to hear? Will not all tell you, as many as the thing seemed among the
unjust? What sort of self I furnished to the friend, Pontus knows, and the
city of the Caesareans knows, and all the common friends. For it is
small-minded for me to reproach these things. For it is fitting that the one
well treated remember of what he has been well treated, but the one who did it
in no way. And what sort he was to us, let the facts persuade. There is some
station in the middle of a highway of the Cappadocians, which splits into a
triple road, waterless, without grass, not wholly free, a terribly accursed
and narrow little village. Dust is all things and noises and chariots,
lamentations, groans, tax-collectors, racks, fetters, and a people as many as
are strangers and wanderers. This is the church of my Sasima; to these he gave
me, being straitened by fifty country-bishops (of the good courage), and this,
that when another was seizing by force he might hold the seat by making it
new. For we were to him the first of warlike friends (for we were valiant
once, and blessed wounds are nothing terrible). For besides the other things
which I counted, it was not even without blood to hold the throne; for this
was a no-man's-land of two opposing bishops, and some terrible battle had
clashed, whose craftsman was the cutting of the fatherland, having ordered two
cities as mothers of small ones. Souls a pretext, but the thing is love of
rule; for I shrink from saying, the revenues and the taxes, from which the
whole world is shaken wretchedly. What then was it just for me to do, toward
God? To be content? To receive the

inroads of the evils? To be struck out of season? To be choked in mire? Not
even to have means where I might set this old age always being pushed by force
from shelter? Not even having bread to break for the stranger, a poor man
allotted to steer a poor people, looking at not even one of the things I might
set right, but well supplied with the evils which the cities have, gathering
thorns, not plucking roses, harvesting the terrible naked of the good things?
Demand of me another good courage, which you wish, and hold this out to those
wiser than I. Such were Athens and common toils of speeches, a life of one
roof and one hearth, one mind in both, not two, a wonder of Hellas; and right
hands, to throw the world far, and themselves to live a common life for God
and to give speeches to the only wise Word. All things have been scattered,
thrown on the ground, breezes carry the old hopes. Where is one to wander?
Beasts, will you not receive me, among whom the faithful is more, as at least
it seems to me? These things held thus, to speak briefly. And when I was bent,
not mind, but neck (what am I to say? And from where might I set all my
labor-pain before you?), goads for me again, again some fugitive and a runner
into a mountain stealing a dear way of life, my delight. What is the gain for
me? For I was not a vigorous fugitive, as it seems. And knowing how to endure
all things I am ignoble in this one: I do not bear a father's wrath. First
then my father makes a contest to settle me at Sasima; and when he was weak,
the second voyage but not to remain below, but sharing the toil with him (for
he was already heavy in the flesh) to lighten toils together, stretching out
hands, touching this beard, using such speeches toward me. "A father beseeches
you, O dearest of sons, the old father the young, the master the servant by
nature and by a double law. I do not ask gold of you, nor stones nor silver,
not fields of earth, child, nor as many things as are of luxury. I seek to set
you near Aaron and Samuel, a precious attendant to God. The one who gave holds
you; child, do not dishonor me, that you may obtain the only Father gracious.
The request is good, and if not, it is paternal. You have not yet measured out
so much life as the time of sacrifices that has passed for me. Give the grace,
give (or let another give me to a tomb. This I define as the penalty of
disobedience). Give the brief days to the remnant. And the things thereafter
are to be planned dearly for you." And when I heard these things and the soul
stood a little apart from the weight as a sun from a cloud, what happens?
Where do my passions turn? I admonished myself, that there is no harm until I
fulfill a father's longing of a seat. "For this will not hold me unwilling," I
said, "whom neither a proclamation nor a promise masters." Thus fear having
conquered led me up. And when the parents went out of life having been
allotted a lot, toward which they had long hastened, and I was left not well
free, of the church given I did not take hold at all, not even so much as to
offer one service to God or to pray with a people or to set a hand on any one
of clerics; but of the paternal (for some of the pious did not let up, putting
under oath, falling upon me, announcing departures of many unfinished) for a
brief time as a stranger I had some care of another's, I will not deny, saying
this very thing always to the bishops and asking a gift from the depth of the
heart, to set some man as overseer for the little town, speaking true things,
one, that it had not yet received anyone by a known proclamation, and the
second, that it had long been decided that I flee both friends and affairs.
And when I did not persuade those who from long longing wished to hold, and
those perhaps over-proud, first I went as a fugitive to Seleucia, the
maiden-house of the renowned girl Thecla; "perhaps they would be persuaded but
thus," saying, "worn by time to give reins to someone." And not a brief time
passed for me here. And when standing again upon my evils, of the good things
I thought I found not even one, but of the affairs from which I had fled, a
great throng, having appeared over as from a set term, here then is the
tension of my speech. And I will say what I shall say, though to those who
know well, that not having us you may have this speech, a medicine of grief, a
reproach to enemies, and a witness to friends of the things in which we have
been wronged having wronged nothing. Nature has not given two suns, but two
Romes, lamps of the whole inhabited world, ancient and new power, differing
from one another so much as the one shines before the sun, the other of
evening, and beauty raising beauty against it yoked. And of these the faith of
the one was from more and is still well-running, binding all the west to the
saving word, as is just the

president of the whole, whole revering the concord of God; but the other was
before straight-footed, but now no longer (I mean this one of mine, then not
mine), but it lay in the depths of destruction, from when the light city and
full of all evils, Alexandria, mindless heat, sent Arius, the abomination of
desert, who first said: "the Trinity is not to be revered," and set boundaries
of worth, cutting unequally one nature, the undivided essence, until we were
cut into many paths. Yet even though a most wretched city being so according
to the law from time (for a custom growing old completes into a law) and
having died a pitiable death from unbelief, it had some small seed of a living
breath, souls complete in the word of the faith, a people brief, but more to
God, who does not number a multitude, but hearts, a faithful planting, a most
precious vine. To these (for we seemed in God to be some of the known in life
and speech, though we had always lived a rustic life) the grace of the Spirit
sent us, many shepherds and flocks calling, as helpers of a people and
co-takers of speech, that we might cool with a pious flow thirsty souls still
greening, and that the light might be mixed with oil-food for a lamp, and that
greedy tongues and much-turning weaves, from which the simple of the faith is
gone, and spider-webs, rotten prisons, binding the light things, a laugh to
the strong, might be broken or loosed in firm speeches, and those who had
fallen in might escape the snares. Thus then I came, not willing, but stolen
by violent men, as an advocate of the word. For some gathering also was being
rumored of bishops, a newly come heresy, bringing a word into the dear
churches, which cutting the mixture of the Word of God toward us, which
unturned he received taking a human, ensouled, minded, passionate in the
things of the body, the whole former Adam except sin, this cutting introduces
some mindless God as if fearing lest the mind fight with God (for thus I would
fear also the nature of flesh, for this more, and farthest from God), or of
the other things needing salvation the mind having been decided to perish
completely, which indeed most of all is to be saved by my God, which also most
of all slipped in the first fashioning; for what it had received, it also
overlooked the law, and what was the overlooking, this also is to be assumed.
Let not then a word save me half who have suffered whole, nor let God be
dishonored as not having taken me whole, but clay only, and a mindless soul of
some irrational animal, which has also been saved clearly by your word. And
let every such thing of the pious cease. For they err something equal from the
opposite to those who introduce two sons without aim, the one from God and the
one from the virgin, those cutting the harmony below, some scraping, others
doubling badly. For if two, I fear lest one of the two, either we worship two
gods instead of one, or lest we suffer this being cautious we send the
compound outside of God. For of those of whom is the flesh, God would suffer
nothing. But the nature of a human has shared in God whole, not as a prophet
or some other of the inspired (who did not share God, but the things of God),
but being substantiated as a sun by rays. Let these then perish from the midst
of speech, unless they revere as one a human God, the one who assumed together
with the assumption, the timeless and the thing mixed with time, the one from
a father only and from a mother only, two natures having come into one Christ.
And how and in what manner did our things hold? We met with many evils having
come. First the city boiled against us as introducing more gods instead of
one. No wonder; they had been led thus, so as to be altogether ignorant of a
pious word, how the monad is tripled, the triad again is unified, both being
conceived divinely. And besides those suffering a people is dragged, as a
champion of their then president and shepherd who had taken pity of the
passion, so great a people and full of spirit, for whom not to conquer all
