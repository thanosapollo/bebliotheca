THAT THE ASCETIC OUGHT NOT TO JEST
=48.1055=
The exhortations of the blessed Paul are all-holy, commanding that we work out
our own salvation with fear. For he who does not work the exercise of virtue
with fear and much care undergoes many turnings aside into evil, and as one
wandering from the straight and royal road into the sideways and trackless
ways through thorns and stakes; therefore also unceasing prayers to God become
guides into the good, of those who say, Lead me in your truth; and, Make my
steps firm in your paths. Kindness then is a good, and love is the chief of
goods; but upon these follows much brightness, and laughter exceeding the
measure, and the jesting that humbles solemnity. Terrible indeed is it to be
dissolved in jesting. For laughter loosens the bond of temperance, laughter
drives away solemnity, laughter does not remember the fear of God, laughter
does not fear the threat of Gehenna, laughter is a guide of fornication,
jesting is a proof of the unchaste man, buffoonery makes one be brought down
into slackness, buffoonery is a pretext of contempt. For this reason the
blessed Apostle forbids, saying: Foolish talking or jesting, things that are
not fitting, let them not come out of your mouth, but rather thanksgiving;
saying this, that you should not get cheerfulness as a pretext of slackness,
but as a ground of thanksgiving toward the one who has given. For this reason
Job bears witness concerning himself, saying: If I have also walked with
jesters. And Solomon rebukes the laughter-loving manner: Better, he says, is
sorrow than laughter, because in the sadness of the face the heart will be
made good. The heart of the wise is in the house of mourning, but the heart of
fools in the house of gladness. Better to hear the rebuke of the wise than a
man hearing the song of fools. And proving the unseemly and dishonorable
character of those given over to laughter, he says: As the sound of thorns
under the pot, so is the laughter of fools. But perceive toward what the very
bright and cheerful is fitting: toward the wise quiet and the solemn. And
through fear and care of being seen by God, to strip away the excessive
cheerfulness. For those who are dissolved in unmeasured brightness and jests
seem to be ignorant of their own affairs, their soul weighed down by some
forgetfulness, and not even expecting the Master's presence. Not yet the
Master's goodwill, O man; not yet do you have a sure release from evils; you
do not know what will be a little later, what you yourself will be, and what
will happen to you; then you laugh without care, and you jest, and you carry
yourself without fear, when you ought to be intent upon the supplications
toward God, to be in agony over the things to come, and to tremble for
yourself lest you ever turn aside from virtue, and to be anxious and afraid
concerning the things that will meet you, lest things harder and greater than
your strength should come upon you, you who have been caught in the midst of
so many and so great wars? Let your soul have a contest; demons make war
invisibly, men make war openly, the passions of the soul within make war, the
pleasures raised through the body, and the disasters from without concerning
the body.
For you do not suffer with those near you, you are not weak with those who are
weak, you do not weep with those who weep, you do not fear concerning those
who are not firmly disposed about the faith, lest they be moved and fall; you
do not lament those who have fallen, you do not stret =48.1056= ch out a hand
to those lying down, you do not lay hold of the one who laughs with inward
groanings, since he has forgotten the punishment made ready; the thought of
the time that has been shortened does not overturn your untimely luxury, nor
the presence of the coming fearful day; but you have imitated those who drink
wine with kithara and psaltery, to whom instead of gladness the prophet
threatened the woe. For I, he says, proclaimed weeping and mourning and
shaving of the head, and girding with sackcloth; but they made gladness and
joy, saying: Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die; and these things are
uncovered in the ears of the Lord of Sabaoth, that this sin shall not be
forgiven them until they die. Hearing these things, fear and groan; do not
imitate those who ate and drank, and rose up to play; and that I may say the
more bitter thing, sacrifices and foods and games caused the forgetting of the
fear toward God, and dragged them out toward idolatry. Fear lest while you are
laughing the Master be angry, he who blessed those who weep and called those
who laugh wretched, lest the devil, finding your play and your poured-out
laughter to be fearlessness concerning God and forgetfulness of the things
that profit, should mount upon your soul, and sow his own tares. There was a
time when the seasons had ease more suitably; but even then among the saints
there was much gloom instead of overflowing joy; and the psalmist says: All
the day I went about gloomy, and, I will wash my bed every night, I will wet
my couch with my tears; becoming downcast over his sins, and wailing over the
disasters, and as to the disasters of Jerusalem, the prophet Jeremiah sits
weeping. You then also, do not, using heaviness of head and drunkenness and
jests and jesting, give yourself over; for this reason even the kithara has
been taken from you, and you do not have a tambourine in your hands; and Paul
taught to sing with the heart rather than with the mouth, he who from every
side draws the man together from every slackness and dissolution.
The father Isaac played toward his own wife; but for you even the things
toward a wife have been ordered more solemnly, because The time is shortened,
that those who have wives may be as not having, and those who rejoice as not
rejoicing, and those who buy as not possessing, and those who use this world
as not using it to the full; for the fashion of this world is passing away.
But you do not wish to be drawn together, when all things are being drawn
together, but even before women you pour out tears indifferently, and you
multiply games, and you put forward jests, and you break yourself, bringing
forth shameful speech; for not bearing the burning in your unchaste body, you
then bring the mire outside, and you make yourself manifest; such as you are
through words, such you will altogether be, and much worse in the inner man.
Why do you mix with women without fear? Why do you run down into the snare for
yourself? Why do you throw yourself into the danger? Why do you brighten
toward females, and dare to live with them? For this reason fasts, for this
reason prayers, for this reason a dry diet, for this reason men invent many
kinds of ascetic practices, for this reason they pursue the deserts; and some
are not even filled with bread, nor with water, for no other reason than to
flee those scandals, that they may not, having fallen among females, suffer a
shameful fall. But you, having run past all these things, and not even having
considered the danger after the snare through which the first-formed man fell
from such great blessedness, do you not consider the weapon of the devil,
through which the ancient one, having been wounded, has been cast down? And
the Master forbade even looking at a woman, marking it out as fornication; but
you have gone so mad, and having put feigned names upon the title of love, on
the pretext of kindness you have drawn to yourse =48.1057= lf a housemate, as
Eve did the serpent. Having bound the fire in the bosom, and having set a lamp
in hay, you will have danger from both; for either, having fallen into the
very fall of the pit, you have been in danger concerning temperance; or if you
should not suffer this, you will wrap a shameful reputation around yourself,
you who are commanded to provide things honest, not only before God, but also
before men. Therefore, having an ecclesiastical exhortation, you ought to
guard whatever things are honorable; and not only this, but also whatever
things are of good report.
Measure the kind by the solemn; set fear as a chastener. Why do you stain the
fair name of Christianity? Why do you also snare the souls of others, having
drawn a housemate? For you will give an account not only of the defense
concerning your own soul, but also concerning those who were scandalized
because of you. Cast the serpent out of your bosom, cast the danger away from
yourself; if you long to be served by females, why did you not keep the
ancient law, and join yourself in lawful marriage? Marriage has no punishment;
adultery has inescapable punishment; for cohabitation with women is adultery,
and great adultery. Search out your conscience, whether you were never harmed,
whether neither by night nor by day you received a wound. But I will unfold
your heart, even if you are going to be vexed. But it is no care to me; for I
am in great pain whenever I see an ascetic who has drawn in a woman brought
in; and I say to you that every hour you are wounded, and every hour you
become a prey of wild beasts; for loosening the bond of temperance, you, the
noble warrior, are enervated, you are shown a captive instead of a victor. For
are you a stone? You are a man, subject to the common falls of nature; do you
have fire in the bosom, and are you not burned? And how does it make sense?
Set a lamp in hay, and then see whether you can say to me that the hay is not
burned. And if you deny, saying that the hay is not burned, do not tell me,
but him who knows the hidden things, who comes to gather the thoughts; for he
is a discerner of intentions and thoughts, and he understands the thinking of
hearts; for he says: He who has looked at a woman to desire her has already
committed adultery with her in his heart. See that adultery be not found in
you every hour. You will certainly say: With fasts and ascetic practices and
the dry diet I wall myself, that I may not fall from the thing set before me.
Then your ascetic practice is for nothing else but for your housemate; then
you no longer store up righteousness; for she has undercut your ascetic
practice. Cast out then from yourself the one who is despoiling you of many
goods, lest she cast you out of paradise, lest she make you a plaything for
the devil; do not set her beside yourself, lest having overthrown you she be
brought down with you into Gehenna. Fear the fearful judge; make a covenant
with yourself not even to gaze at a woman; when elders are present, reverence
grey hair, and as one reverencing fathers spare foolish talking and play and
laughter.
Do not play with a younger man, nor show a way of slackness, yourself
beginning it, and producing much more contempt; and reverence a woman even as
far as meeting her glance. Let your life become a pattern for those women;
show yourself such, that those women may reverence you, and that you may
become for them a pattern and a beginning of reverence, because a man is also
more fitted to lead than a woman, and do not add your own easiness to the more
easy female nature. Let a woman be modest in a special degree, inasmuch as the
shamelessness of a woman is also a harder reproach. Even if to speak freely
toward men is very terrible and bold, fear having been added for a woman
toward her own husband rather than to speak freely. For the wife, he says, let
her fear her husband; if then such a law is set before her who has a husband,
what then toward you, the =48.1058= one who has no husband? For you this is
fitting, not even to approach a man at all, if it is possible, nor let a man
see your face at all. For you are a bride of Christ; come forth thus, as a
bride adorned, your face covered with the veil, that you may not, having
wounded someone through the seeing, receive yourself the repayment of that
man's destruction. And we say these things according to the ineffable
teachings of the Scriptures; but many, not even taking the fear of God into
mind, nor fearing their own easy fall, nor foreseeing the punishment laid up,
have borne themselves indifferently, and of their own judgment have come
forward to serve men, not even on equal terms with the rule of her who has
been married and has a husband. But you do not even keep this limit; for you
do not conduct yourself with fear, but with much license and contempt, and you
eat together, and you drink together, and you converse together, and you laugh
together without order; and upon all these things you wish to be called a
virgin, but I will no longer call you a virgin, who have thrown yourself down
in this way, and have not even feared the danger. I will dare to say the
prophetic word: Let the heaven be astonished at this, and let creation
shudder, at how great shamelessness we have come, even beyond the prostitutes
in the workshops. For just as those women, being herded by mimes ... of men,
become bolder toward unseemly acts; so also these, having succeeded in
dwelling with men. God is indignant, men mock, and those women, imitating the
unjust judge, neither fear God, nor regard man; but in all these things they
speak more boldly even than those who dwell solemnly with husbands in
marriage.
These rather dispose arrogantly the affairs of those with whom they live, and
they throw them into confusion, and they insult in return, and they wish to be
masters of all his substance; and if at times he should wish to stretch out
his hand to doing good, she prevents it, and then she is no longer a servant,
but has become a mistress. Withdraw then from the alien cohabitation, O
virgin, and I do not say only from the cohabitation, but take care also not to
converse together or to laugh together. For if to the prudent man it is
scarcely permitted to smile quietly, to the wise woman it scarcely commands
even this. For even to have smiled, for a woman who professes piety, is heavy
and dangerous; for a woman is seen as much better in solemnity than in
brightness and cheerfulness. And indeed, bringing the praises from the woman
onto the spiritual bride, the one who wrote the mystical song, marveling at
her silence, also praises the austerity of her form, naming it amazement; for
this becomes the very solemn and striking thing to those who see. For her
solemnity must be fearful to curious eyes, and fitted to turn away busy
shamelessness; whose adornment for this reason he also took away, that which
is through gold and braiding of hair and costly clothing, that she may not
glitter with the ornaments lying about her, and brighten the eye of those who
see, and call them forth into scandal; for this reason fasts, and continences,
and ascetic practices, and the pale and sullen look, and the virgin ought to
have a cheaper garment because of the slipperiness of nature; for this reason
also Paul, writing to his genuine child Timothy, guards his honorable kindness
toward women by the safety of purity, saying: Honor younger widows as sisters
in all purity. And if the divine writings take away from a woman the adornment
about the body, how much more does the wholly unadorned belong to a man, and
covering only as far as need, that it may be a covering and not an adornment,
as Paul named it: Having food and coverings, with these we shall be content.
For garments unto adornment, let them be counted as belonging to women rather
than to men; for the law also says: A man shall not put on a woman's garment.
Let a man not then be made feminine, where even the female ought not to be
made very feminine before God. John wore a garment of camel's hair, and a
leather belt about his loins; and earlier Elijah, whose spirit John wore, he
too =48.1059= was clothed in a sheepskin only. It belongs to a sluggish and
lazy soul to admire a brightly flowered garment, and one fine according to the
workmanship; but a great-souled soul, which perceives the true and real
adornment, spits upon every soft garment, longing for that which is higher
than it, the unadorned, the more seemly, the most solemn, having preferred
purity above all. Be content then with the reminder through what has been
said, and keep solemnity, not carrying precision beyond measure as if it were
bitter, which becomes sweeter than honey and honeycomb both to the hearer
himself and to those who draw near.
For spiritual joy solemnly and magnificently brings the soul to anchor in the
unity of God, and fills also those who meet with the sweetness. But do not,
taking a pretext from my words, that you were forbidden to live in
cheerfulness, begin to be sullen and harsh toward the brethren who come, nor
be some cruel and unapproachable and inhuman person instead of useful and
loving, nor sullen in the greetings of the brethren who approach, and
unsociable about answer and speech. For to rejoice always is an apostolic
command, and this: Comfort one another, and be kind toward one another; and,
Love, he says, is kind; and the courtesies of the apostles are in their
letters, treating the hearers kindly as children, exhorting those who attend
as brothers; and many things concerning the sacred kindness according to love
one may learn from the holy =48.1060= Scriptures. Nor must one shift toward
unmeasured cheerfulness, nor by buffoonery damage the kindness that is an
imitation of God, but stand in the mean of perfect cheerfulness, neither
grieving those who meet, nor relaxing them and making them slack, nor rebuking
harshly, nor flattering deceptively, but both turning them profitably and
exhorting them kindly. He who spares, he says, to let a hard word go forth, is
intelligent; and good words are honeycombs of honey, and their sweetness is a
healing of the soul; there are who speaking wound as swords, but the tongues
of the wise heal. And it is also good to be devoted to divine songs in
measure; for such brightness sanctifies the soul, according to the Apostle: Be
filled, he says, with the Spirit, singing and making melody in your hearts to
the Lord.
It is good then to approach with zeal toward such gladness, and not, through
some bitter and hard manner, to be reluctant toward the divine song, as if not
deigning to bring a voice to the melody of God. For toward such a divine
spirit, and toward the sacred converse, one must be bright, not being
dissolved into sweetness of voice, nor being disposed in an effeminate manner
toward the divine songs, nor bending them melodiously, and looking around this
way and that, that you may be praised, but delighting in God, and striving to
please him alone, and here especially, that the cheerful may be found mixed
with the solemn, the fear toward God supplying us with solemnity, and the
divine song leading into brightness, and producing an imitation of the eternal
joy, in which those who have lived well as citizens will live the life to come
with the Lord; to whom be the glory and the power unto the ages of ages. Amen.
