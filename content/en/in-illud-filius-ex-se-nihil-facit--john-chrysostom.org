* On that saying, The Son does nothing of himself
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of our father among the saints John Chrysostom. In the great church, a
(bishop) having first spoken a little on the Gospel, on the saying, "The Son
never does anything of himself, unless he sees the Father doing something."

=56.247=

αʹ. O the violence, o the tyranny. The teacher, having the cup filled, having
spoken first, brought us the drink to taste with the tips of the lips; and he
did this not through lack of teaching; for he is accustomed to gush no less
than a fountain; but, as I said before, making manifest the tyranny of your
love, which you have practiced showing toward our smallness; for this reason
he quickly fell silent, and shut up the word, wishing to gratify your desire,
and making us liable for the whole debt. Since therefore he himself has
yielded the word to us, and I see you hanging from our tongue, it is necessary
then to strip for the wrestling; but help me, and stretch out a hand,
awakening our tongue with the prayers, and by the intelligence of the hearing
making the teaching easy.

Since even the prophet seeks not only a marvelous counselor, but also an
intelligent hearer. For it is not some chance contest that lies before us
today, but one needing many prayers, and much vigilance of the hearers, and
much zeal of the speaker, that the things said may both be spoken with
accuracy, and be planted with security in the mind of your love. For I do not
wish you only to hear, but also to be instructed; not only to learn, but also
to teach; not only yourselves to receive, but also to pass it on to others.
For thus our theater will be more splendid, and the assembly greater, when you
through what you hear will be able to bring others also. At the former
assembly, then, bringing forward this evangelical saying, My Father worketh
hitherto, and I work, and showing from it the equality toward the Begetter,
which also before this the evangelist declared, saying, For this cause they
persecuted him the more, not only because he had broken the sabbath, but also
because he said that God was his own Father, making himself equal with God,
thus I ended the word.

But today it is necessary to dissolve the things the heretics bring against us
in answer to these. For even if we contend among friends, we must use such
accuracy that the word be from every side incapable of being deceived and
unconquerable, even if it should come to be in =56.248= the midst of the
enemies. For as I said
before, I do not wish you only to hear, but also to instruct others. Wherefore
with all accuracy I have been eager to put spiritual armor around you, so that
nowhere a member appear naked, nor receive a mortal wound. For the word holds
for us the rank of weapons, fencing the household together, and striking the
adversaries; striking not that it may throw them down, but that it may raise
those who lie fallen. For such is the form of this battle: it sets up a trophy
for the salvation of those who make war. That this then may come to pass,
stretch your mind with me, casting out every worldly care, raise up your
judgment, furnish me a sharp-sighted eye. Let neither the rich be made slack
by ease, nor the poor be crushed by the care of poverty; but casting out all
this unevenness of life, let each furnish himself a prepared hearer; for the
things now set before us are not chance matters. And for this reason


I continually give these commands, since I know the depth which we shall walk.
But do not be troubled having heard of depth; for with the Spirit leading by
the hand, the waters have no gloom, but there will be much ease; only if you
walk this way which I command. And do not be troubled, nor be disturbed. For
the things being moved today at first trouble the hearer who does not attend,
and throw him into perplexity; but if he wait for the end, seeing the fitting
solution, he will enjoy a bright calm, and will be able to bring his own soul
into an unwaved harbor. That these things then may come to pass, do not be
troubled, nor be disturbed, but follow the way with all longsuffering and
endurance, which the teaching of the word shows. What then are the things they
bring against us? The Son cannot, he says, do anything of himself, unless he
sees the Father doing something. And yet Scripture has said these things. Why
then are these things brought against us by them? Not as they have been said
by Scripture are they brought. For what do they wish to construct from this,
he says? Do you see how the Son of God overthrew, he says, that suspicion of
equality? For since the Jews, he says, suspected this, that he makes himself
equal with God, for this reason he brought in the opposite, saying: The Son
cannot do anything of himself.

βʹ. Did I not say, not in vain, that the words have it in them to trouble you,
and spoken from the proems throw the hearer into confusion? But wait, and you
will see them consumed by their own weapons. For first of all, what is said
was not a suspicion of the Jews; which also in the former =56.249= discourse
we demonstrated with all clearness; which, that we may not stir up again,
sending the hearer there, we shall try to dissolve what is now brought
forward, showing that he does not say these things overthrowing that
suspicion, but with much accuracy establishing and confirming it, and
furnishing us a proof of closeness toward the Begetter, and of much
genuineness and conjunction and concord.

For so I am confident in what has been said, that I say this saying especially
is a proof of his kinship toward the Father, and of the unvarying according to
essence. Do not be disturbed by the reasonings of the heretics. For neither
the swords drawn on the walls, nor the spears, nor the points, could trouble
enemies who look with a fearful and sharp gaze. For whatever it is, it is some
shadow and image, not the truth of things. Such are the reasonings of the
heretics, which, that we may refute, let us come near this saying, turning it
round continually, and for now let us ask them how they wish to interpret the
saying. For the reading alone is not enough. For if the reading were enough,
why did Philip say to the eunuch, Understandest thou what thou readest? Whence
that man, reading, knew nothing of the things lying in it; wherefore he also
said: I pray thee, of whom speaketh the prophet this, of himself, or of some
other man? If the reading were enough, how is it that the Jews, reading the
Old, and the things concerning the generation of Christ, and the signs, and
the wonders, and place, and time, and cross, and tomb, and resurrection, and
ascension, and the session at the right hand, and the descent of the Spirit,
and the running forth of the apostles, and the casting out of the synagogue,
and the nobility of the Church, have not believed even to this day? The
reading then is not enough, unless knowledge also be present.

For just as if someone is fed, but does not digest, he will never live; so if
someone reads, but knows nothing of the things lying in it, he will not hit
upon the truth. Do not then furnish me the evangelical saying, but also
interpret it. And this I demand from those men, so that when I have dissolved
their rotten thoughts, then I may lay down the foundation of the truth. Thus
also builders


do, they do not first lay the foundation of the houses, until they have taken
away the rotten things, so that they may build with security. Let us then also
imitate these. Tell me then, can the Son do absolutely nothing of himself? For
he did not say that he can make men, but cannot make angels; nor that he can
make angels, but cannot make archangels; but he said, nothing. Is the word
then of weakness? if indeed he cannot, according to your suspicion, and of
violence and stretched necessity; if indeed he does nothing of himself, but
whatever he sees the Father doing. Behold also teaching added afterward, all
of which are alien to that undefiled and immortal essence, the unspeakable and
ineffable and incomprehensible. And why do I speak concerning Christ?

For not even concerning me, the small and cheap, and come from earth, is it
possible to say this, that I cannot do anything of myself; nor concerning you,
nor concerning any other of men. For if this is true, Gehenna is in vain, and
punishment, and vengeance; crowns are in vain, and prizes, and good things.
For we shall neither undergo those things when we sin, =56.250= nor enjoy
these when we succeed, if indeed we do nothing of ourselves. For the prizes
are laid up not for the deeds simply, but for the will. What I mean is this:
when someone works a good thing of himself, then he is crowned and proclaimed,
not simply when he works, but with will and choice. And that you may learn
that these things are true: There are eunuchs, he says, which were made
eunuchs of men; and there are eunuchs, which have made themselves eunuchs for
the kingdom of heaven's sake. He calls eunuchs here not those who have cut the
members, but those who have cut off the wicked thought full of wantonness, not
with an iron knife, but using reasoning and philosophy, and the help of God
for this. Behold two eunuchs, some having the members cut off by men, others
having cut off the wicked thought by piety. But nevertheless even if they were
made eunuchs differently, both alike abstain from intercourse with women. And
I said alike, not according to will, but according to the nature of the thing.
For neither could a eunuch come together with a woman, nor the monk who has
made himself a eunuch. And the deed is one, but the end is not one. For this
reason, having said concerning those that they were made eunuchs by men, he
set no prize for them on this account; for the thing was of nature, not of
ascesis. But bringing these into the midst, he crowned them with the kingdom,
saying, For the kingdom of heaven's sake. And yet neither this one mixes with
a woman, nor that one; but the one is bound by necessity, the other having
accomplished the thing by choice, and having worked and achieved this of
himself.

Then men can of themselves do such things, and make, and philosophize, and
speak, and work ten thousand other things; but the Master of the angels will
not be able to do of himself neither a small thing nor a great? And who will
endure these things? Do you not hear Paul saying that In a great house there
are not only vessels of gold and of silver, but also of wood and of earth; and
some to honor, and some to dishonor. If a man therefore purge himself from
these, he shall be a vessel unto honor, sanctified, meet for the master's use.

γʹ. Do you see these also correcting themselves of themselves? For this is: If
a man purge himself. What then is the thing said? If our word were only toward
household friends, I would already have brought in the solution; but since it
is toward enemies and adversaries, it is still necessary to pull down their
things; bringing the saying again into the midst, let us thus make it clear.
For that it is possible for us of ourselves both to do and to speak, the word
has sufficiently shown. For if this were not so, we would not have been
crowned when we succeed. But we must ask the heretic again: what is, Unless he
sees the Father doing something, he cannot do anything of himself? For from
this saying,


I say, not from the interpretation; or rather not even from the saying, but
from the distorted interpretation of the heretics, a double creation must
necessarily be introduced. How and in what manner? Unless he sees, he says,
the Father doing something, he cannot do. Therefore of all necessity there
must be finished works of the Father, and also other works of the Son, which
looking toward those he also constructed. For unless he sees him doing
something, he does not do, he says.

In order then =56.251= that he may see, the works must exist. What then? tell
me: we see one sun, will you be able to show me two, that I may suspect the
one of the Father, and the other of the Son? and two moons, and earth, and
sea? and so also all the rest? But you would not be able to say; for the sun
is one. How then, unless he sees him doing something, does he not do? Whose
work do you wish the sun to be? the Father's? Where then is the Son's? But the
Son's? Where then is the Father's, which this one seeing, made another such?
And how will that stand, All things were made through him, and without him was
not anything made that was made? For if all things were through him, what
place would this division have? Do you see the things of the reasonings? how
you pierce yourselves through, and the lie convicts itself? Behold, bringing
their interpretation into the midst, how I showed it struck down by itself.
And I would gladly ask that also: who took up our flesh, and came into a
virgin womb? the Father or the Son? tell me. Is it not altogether clear that
it is the only-begotten Son of God? For Paul also says thus: Let this mind be
in you, which was also in Christ Jesus, who, being in the form of God, thought
it not robbery to be equal with God, but emptied himself, taking the form of a
servant.

And, God sent forth his Son, the only-begotten, made of a woman, made under
the law. And all Scripture, whether old or new, is filled with these
testimonies; and the facts cry out, that the Only-begotten was incarnate, not
the Father. Did the Son then, seeing the Father incarnate, become incarnate?
[For he would not have become incarnate], unless he had seen him. For he
cannot do anything of himself, unless he sees the Father doing something. When
would I see the Father having worked this? You would not be able to say. And
do not think the thing that has happened small. For the chief of our salvation
is the incarnation of the Only-begotten, his condescension. For before he
became man, the things of wickedness were tyrannizing, and a deepest night
held all things, and everywhere altars and temples to the idols, and savor of
fat, and smoke, and torrents of blood, flowing not from sheep and oxen only,
but also from men. For they sacrificed their sons and their daughters unto
demons; and these things the people that had prophets, that had partaken of
the law, that had enjoyed the vision of God, that had been reared in so many
wonders.

And if these were such, consider how the remaining parts of the inhabited
world were disposed, being made frantic by demons, being tyrannized by
wickedness, bowing down to all the passions, serving wood, worshipping stones,
mountains, and hills, and glens, and trees, and lakes, and springs, and
rivers. And why must one speak
the things of the others? For from the evils among the Jews I shall see the
excess among the others. They became horses mad after females, each one
neighed after his neighbor's wife. The ox knoweth his owner, and the ass his
master's crib, but Israel did not know me. Dumb dogs not able to bark. The
forehead of a whore became yours, you were shameless toward all. There is none
that understandeth, =56.252= there is none that seeketh after God; they are
all gone out of the way, they are together become unprofitable. Again another:
In vain [the silversmith] silversmiths; their wickednesses have not melted.
Another again: Curse, and lying, and theft, and murder, and adultery are
poured out upon the earth, and they mix bloods upon bloods. Another again: If
the Ethiopian shall change his skin, and the leopard her spots, this people
also will be able to do judgment, having learned evils. Another again:


Woe is me, my soul, because the godly has perished from the earth, and there
is none that doeth right among men; they all judge for bloods. God again: I
have hated, I have thrust away your feasts, and I will not smell a sacrifice
in your assemblies. Elijah: They have thrown down thine altars, and slain thy
prophets; and I only am left, and they seek my life. God again: I have
forsaken my house, I have left my heritage, I have given my beloved soul into
the hands of her enemies. David again: They sacrificed their sons and their
daughters unto demons, and shed innocent blood, the blood of their sons and
daughters. δʹ. Did you see the tyranny of wickedness? They became dogs and
horses, and more irrational than asses, and more senseless than oxen, and they
raged even against nature itself. But after the incarnation of Christ what
does Scripture say? Our Father, who art in heaven. And before this Scripture
said: Go to the ant, thou sluggard; but after these things we were also
counted worthy of adoption, and were enrolled in heaven, and dance with the
angels, and share the melody with them, and have set the contest toward the
bodiless powers. And hills have been taken away, and temples have been thrown
down. And the stone appeared stone, and the wood, wood, and the trees, trees,
and the springs, springs.

For since the sun of righteousness shone, it showed the nature of things,
which before the night of error covered, and the deep darkness of ignorance,
confounding the sight of those who were deceived. But since the ray of the sun
of righteousness scattered the thick cloud of error, everywhere light and day,
a bright and standing noon. And the Persians who married their mothers now
practice virginity. And those who did not know their own sons and slaughtered
them have become gentler and milder than all. The wolves have changed over to
the gentleness of sheep; or rather even those worse than wolves. For a wolf
does not ignore nature, but recognizes what has been born; but men became even
wilder than these. But after the incarnation of the Only-begotten and the
economy, having cast out the beastliness, they returned to their own nobility;
or rather they went up to the virtue of the angels. And before this the cities
were filled with impiety, but today even the desert philosophizes, and huts of
monks in mountains and glens, imitating the polity of angels, and having
stripped off the present life.

For what need is there of the construction of words, when the facts cry out,
and show more clearly than the sun the good things which, after that marvelous
and spiritual birth-pang of the Virgin, and the economy, and the incarnation,
overtook the whole inhabited world? But nevertheless so great and so vast an
achievement he has done of himself; and Paul cries saying: =56.253= Who, being
in the form of God, thought it not robbery to be equal with God, but emptied
himself, taking the form of a servant. Do you hear, heretic, that he emptied
himself? And again elsewhere: Even as Christ also loved us, and gave himself
for us an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweetsmelling savour. Who also
was crucified of himself, and was slain of himself; wherefore he himself also
said: I have power to lay down my life, and I have power to take it again. No
man taketh it from me; I lay it down of myself. What do you say here, O
heretic, who bringest the evangelical saying that says, The Son can do nothing
of himself? Behold he himself says: I lay it down of myself, and I take it of
myself. And what has been said is not small, but even exceedingly great. And
concerning the Father it has been said, that he has authority of death and of
life. Do you see how you have stepped inside the nets? For what will you say
to this saying? I lay it down of myself, and I take it of myself. How then did
you say that he does nothing of himself? But, as I said, if our word were
toward heretics, having left them in perplexity and bound them, thus


I would have leapt away, having taken a sufficient victory and a splendid
trophy, and having furnished a very great proof of their folly.

But since I do not wish only to muzzle the adversaries, but also to instruct
those who are with us, and to make wise our own members, I will not end the
word here, but I will try to go further, having shown again another work that
convicts their shameless contradiction. For what does he say? The Father
judgeth no man, but the Son judgeth all. εʹ. I ask then the heretic, if the
Father judges no one, but the Son judges, how does he judge? For if he can do
nothing of himself, unless he sees the Father doing something, and the Father
does not judge, but he himself judges all, how can he do what he has not seen?
And do not run past this simply; for this too is not small, but of very great
power. For consider how great it is, to bring all who have come to be from
Adam until the consummation, Greeks, Jews, heretics, those who have missed in
the orthodox faith, all on that day, and to bring out into the midst the
secret things of all, the deeds, the words, the deceits, the plots, the
secrets of the mind, no testimonies being brought, no proofs, no images, no
demonstrations, nor any other of such things, but using their own power toward
the conviction.

But nevertheless so great and so vast an achievement he himself does, not
having first seen the Father, and thus imitated; for the Father judgeth no
man. And see him also elsewhere working all things with authority, both in the
miracles and in the legislations, and in all the rest. For when he went up
into the mountain, and was about to give the new covenant, he says: Ye have
heard that it was said to them of old time, Thou shalt not kill; and whosoever
shall kill shall be in danger of the judgment; and whosoever shall say, Thou
fool, shall be in danger of hell fire. But I say unto you, that whosoever is
angry with his brother without a cause shall be in danger of the judgment. Ye
have heard that it was said, An eye for an eye, and a tooth for a tooth. But I
=56.254= say, resist not evil, but whosoever shall smite thee on thy right
cheek, turn to him the other also. What is this? he who does nothing of
himself both corrects the things of the Father, and leads the legislation to
the better? And when I say, corrects, suspect nothing blasphemous, as if the
Father were weaker. For even if the legislation was lesser, it was not because
of God, but because of those who received the law. But the Old also is of the
Only-begotten, and the New of the Father. How then, tell me, does he do
nothing of himself, adding to the Old, he who displays such authority? Could
anything be weaker than heretics? For the Jews were astonished, because he
taught them as one having authority, and not as the scribes and the Pharisees;
the Jews bear witness to him of authority; but these bear witness that he can
do nothing of himself. And they did not say, as if having authority, but, As
one having authority.

For the authority was not added to him later, but the authority came to be
complete, needing nothing. Wherefore also he said when asked about a kingdom:
For this was I born. Again a paralytic was brought to him, and he says, since
he healed his sins: But that ye may know that the Son of man hath power on
earth to forgive sins, he saith unto him: Take up thy bed, and go unto thine
house. The crowds said that he does all things as one having authority; but he
himself, that The Son of man hath power on earth to forgive sins; and, I have
power to lay down my life, and I have power to take it again. He legislates
with authority, he looses sins with authority, he has authority of death and
of life, and how do you say that he does nothing of himself? Could anything be
clearer than this victory? ϛʹ. But if you wish now, having been freed from the
heretics, let us bring the solution to the things that have been said, having
first taught you this, that the, He cannot, said of God, is not


a proof of weakness, but of power. And if what has been said is new,
nevertheless we shall furnish the proof clear. If I say that it is not
possible for God to sin, I do not accuse him of weakness, but I bear witness
to him of very great power. If I say that it is not possible for God to lie, I
again demonstrate the same. Just as also Paul said: If we suffer, we shall
also reign with him; if we believe not, yet he abideth faithful; for he cannot
deny himself. Do you see the, He cannot, being a sign of power? And why do I
speak concerning God? For I exercise the word also upon these sensible
materials. If I say that adamant cannot be torn apart, do I through the,
cannot, bear witness to it of weakness, or of very great power? When then you
hear that God cannot sin, nor can he lie, nor can he deny himself, do not
think weakness from the not being able to be said, but very great power
indeed, that his essence is unreceptive of all wickedness, untrodden, pure,
higher. Since then this has been distinguished, come, let us bring the
exercise of the word to the matter at hand. The Son cannot do anything of
himself. What then is the, Of himself? For if you learn this with =56.255=
accuracy, then you will know his much closeness toward the Begetter, the
unvarying of the essence, that he is of such an essence as also the Father.
What then is the, He cannot of himself? To do something of his own beside the
Father, he cannot do something foreign and separated beside the Father, nor do
something alienated, nor anything other than what the Father does; for what
that one does, these this one also does. The, He cannot do of himself, then,
is not a taking away of freedom, nor an obliteration of authority, but a proof
of concord, and a testimony of agreement, and a sign of accurate closeness,
and of unvarying. For since he loosed the sabbath, and they accused him of
lawlessness saying: God commanded other things, but you do other things;
stopping this shamelessness of theirs he says: There is not something else
that has come to be by me beside what has come to be by the Father; nor am I
contrary to him and an enemy.

And if he did not speak thus, but shaped the word more humanly and more
coarsely, consider that he was discoursing toward Jews, who thought him an
adversary of God. For this very reason he also quickly added, that no one
might suspect anything such: For the works which he does, these the Son also
does likewise. And if he does nothing of himself, how does he do likewise? For
to do is not great; for this the apostles also did, they raised the dead, they
cleansed lepers, but not Likewise as he. But how did those? Why look ye so
earnestly on us, as though by our own power or authority we had made this man
to walk? And how Jesus? That ye may know that the Son of man hath power on
earth to forgive sins; and again at the resurrection of the dead: For as the
Father raiseth up the dead, and quickeneth them, even so the Son quickeneth
whom he will. And yet the, Even so, was enough; but from abundance checking
the shamelessness of those who wish to contend, he also added the, He will,
clearly with all authority. For this reason he says: For the works which he
does; he did not say, such also the Son does, but those very ones the Son
does. For all things were made through him, and without him was not anything
made that was made. Do you see how he crowds the word into a certain accurate
unity and conjunction and closeness, not saying such, but, The very ones, and,
Likewise, saying, as the Father? For this very reason also in the
condescension of the word shaping it, even there he used the saying with much
security. For he did not say, unless he is taught something by the Father,
lest you introduce him as a disciple; nor did he say, unless he is commanded
something, lest you suspect the rank of slaves; but, Unless he sees the Father
doing something. And this itself also, even so said, is of much closeness
toward the Father. For if he can =56.256= see the Father working, and know how
he works, he is of the same essence.


For often also before we have demonstrated that no one can see essence with
accuracy, nor know it clearly, unless he be of the same nature. An angel at
any rate coming in the naked essence a man was not able to see, and this
Daniel, adorned with much virtue. For this very reason, as being exceptional
of his nature, he set this, saying: No man hath seen God at any time; the only
begotten Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him. And
again: Not that any man hath seen the Father, save he which is of God, he hath
seen the Father. And yet many others also saw, prophets, and patriarchs, and
righteous men, and angels; but he means the accurate knowledge. We will not
however say this, that seeing him he then does; since the, All things were
made through him, and without him was not anything made that was made, how
would it have reason? and the, The works which he does, these the Son also
does likewise? For if he does likewise, how seeing him first does he then do?
For there will be a necessity according to your word, that the Father also,
seeing another working, thus do; which is of extreme madness and folly. But
that we may not, convicting these weak and absurd things, lengthen the word,
we will say this: that since his word was toward Jews, who said he was an
adversary of God and an enemy to the lawgiver, and gathered this from the
things that were happening, for this reason he shaped the word thus more
humanly and more coarsely, leaving it to the intelligent hearer to know the
God-befitting meaning, and correcting those who think something more coarsely;
wherefore he said: For the works which he does, these the Son also does
likewise.

For he does not wait to see the Father working, and then do, nor does he need
discipleship; but he sees his essence, and knows it clearly; For as the Father
knoweth me, he says, even so know I the Father; and he works all things and
creates with the authority that belongs to him, with the intelligence and
wisdom allotted together with him, not needing to learn, not needing to see.
For how, being an accurate image toward the Begetter and doing all things
likewise to that one, and with the same power? For discoursing concerning
power he added these things saying: I and the Father are one.

Knowing then all these things and gathering up the things that have been said,
let us turn away from the assemblies of the heretics, and let us hold
continually to the right faith, and let us display an accurate life and a
polity equal to the dogmas, that we may obtain the good things to come, by the
grace and mercies of our Lord Jesus Christ, to whom be the glory, and the
power, together with the Father and the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto
the ages of ages. Amen.
