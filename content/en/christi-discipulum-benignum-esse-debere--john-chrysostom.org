* That a Disciple of Christ Ought to Be Gentle
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

A DISCOURSE OF THE SAME, that the disciple of Christ must be gentle, and not
quickly angry.
=48.1069=
The divine voice of the Lord in the Gospels teaches us, fulfilling the law,
not abolishing it, not to be angry with our brother, and by this addition of
the word it fulfilled You shall not murder, tearing out the very root of
murder, and cleansing the heart within, and not the hands without. For it laid
on a punishment not such as the law did, the immediate one, but that at the
judgment, saying: For he who is angry with his brother without cause shall be
liable to the judgment. For the punishments from Christ are not swift, as in
the law; but there is much delay of the just repayments, and fearful afterward
is the trial. As he commands the greater things, so also he is more
long-suffering, seeking a cleansing of the hea =48.1070= rt, which it is hard
to work out. Therefore even if we should be led into anger, he does not punish
at once; for if he punished at once, there would be no return through
repentance. For this reason he postpones the punishment, that we may have a
time of repentance, that he who yesterday was angry with his brother may
repent today, and make a covenant with himself no longer to be angry with his
brother; and little by little we may become like God. For he changes us from
the things that alienate our life from the likeness of God, and leads us into
the good according to nature after what is set before us; for we are led into
what is according to nature, and into the communion of the divine Spirit, and
the continual life with God, as we contend for such great things. Let us take
heed to ourselves, and not by contem =48.1071= pt destroy the work of likeness
to God, since the Lord delays to come; let us not give ourselves license to
transgress the oracles of the Lord, nor, despairing as if we could not attain
this according to the likeness of God, abandon the contest, but let hope be
present because of him who commanded and grants power for what was commanded.
But let contempt and negligence be far from us, as we expect a more fearful
punishment because of the long-suffering. For as much as he is long-suffering,
seeking our correction, so much will he be indignant if we are not corrected,
and he will come swiftly upon us as having insulted the grace. Let all the
affairs of this age be despised by us rather than the divine command; for many
are the things that call to anger those who are quick to anger, those who are
easily provoked. But he who fears the command of the fearful king, who ordered
that we not be angry at all, will spit upon all who wish to provoke; and he
will easily endure those who pain him by words, and he will hold out against
those who wish to pain him by deeds, and to him who strikes the cheek he will
turn the other also, and he will bear nobly the one who brings a shameful word
for the sake of insult, having a wondrous consolation in the beatitude, both
that he may be found obedient to the Lord, and that he may quench the anger
toward his brother, and flee the punishment appointed for it. For this reason
Christ commands us to be freed from bodily attachment, both to flee love of
money, and to stand away from love of glory, and not to receive pleasure in
ourselves at all, that we may not through any of these things harm the soul,
and be disposed as strangers to the Holy Spirit. For he who has been taught to
despise money, how shall he be provoked because of money, even if someone
wishes to seize it? and he who does not desire glory, how shall he be provoked
because of glory? Strip off, then, the worldly garment, and put on the
spiritual; strip off the old man, that you may put on the new. For this is why
it is called regeneration, and it has been proclaimed


in the divine writings. For our salvation needs a change of the former things,
that we may become other than what we are, that we may cease from what we are,
and begin another life, which we happened to hold before the transgression.
For you were not such before God as you have become from childhood, nor did
you have from the God-made fashioning such a character as from human
generation. Hear, then, the Lord swearing, and fear; For amen, he says, I say
to you, unless one is born from above, he cannot enter into the kingdom of the
heavens. Therefore begin a newer life, leave the life grown old in sins; let
him who yesterday was a fornicator today become temperate; let him who
yesterday drank without measure today become a faster; throw off what came
later in an evil way, that you may take up again what was given well from the
beginning. It is a small thing to continue undisturbed and without anger. He
who wishes to be according to the image and likeness of God must be despised
by all, spat upon, made cheap, by words, by deeds. For this is why he
commanded not even to repay evils to the one who has wronged you, lest by
repaying the wrongdoer you lose the gentleness according to nature. Do not
then become wise in your own eyes, as if you were just in defending yourself
against the one who wrongs you. Remember the sacred teachings of Paul; Do not
become, he says, wise in your own sight, nor rendering evil for evil. It is
not good to become an adversary, nor to seek justice for yourself, but cast
your cause upon God, who brings defense without anger upon those who do wrong;
For do not avenge yourselves, he says, but give place to wrath; for it is
written, Vengeance is mine, I will repay, says the Lord. Do not say, God
repays, I too will repay; consider what Christ =48.1072= showed you when he
came into the world, that he might show the saving pattern of life, and
emulate this, that you may be saved. For he did not come as a judge, nor
taking vengeance on those who rose against him; and yet he was not wooing a
gain for himself through forbearance, but working salvation for the world. For
the forbearance of Christ has thrown down the boldness of the devil, and the
gentleness of the Lord has wrestled down the savage wrath of the Evil One. You
too, then, set forth the pattern for imitation. He was not angry at those who
insulted him, but hearing Samaritan and one who has a demon, he answers those
who speak gently and meekly: I have no demon, but I honor my Father; and you
dishonor me; but I do not seek my glory; there is one who seeks and judges.
Therefore you too do not avenge yourself, but wait for the judge. For if the
judge himself, having come in human form, did not repay though he was able,
but awaited the judgment of God. For being reviled, he says, he did not revile
in return, suffering he did not threaten, but committed himself to him who
judges justly, you too then do thus, and imitate the Master, who even unto the
cross bore the godless insult, and holds out salvation to those worthy of
destruction. For he also said to the Father, Forgive them this sin; for they
do not know what they are doing. The brother has done wrong, he has been shown
unworthy of the brotherhood; but you, do not destroy the brotherhood. The
brother imitated the brothers of Joseph, he armed himself for fratricide, he
appeared an enemy instead of a brother, he showed an excess of wickedness, he
does wrong though wronged in nothing. But you do the opposite, that you too
may say according to the singer David: I was peaceable with those who hate
peace. Conquer the evil one in the good, call him forth to the good, loose his
wrath with gentleness, show him that he unjustly persecutes the Christ in you,
showing yourself not worthy to be persecuted. But he is incorrigible, and is
not changed toward your mildness. And yet beasts are changed into the mildness
of men, but that one has become wilder than the beasts. Do not contend with
such a one, nor set wrath against wrath; but turn aside as the wise helmsman
does from the harsh rocks; and guard yourself as


a deaf adder that stops her ears, which will not hear the voice of the
charmer. Redeem the time, Because the days are evil; walk in wisdom, knowing
that you are a stranger and a sojourner upon the earth and a pilgrim, as all
your fathers were. You too were sent as a sheep into the midst of wolves, as
also the apostles were; do not become a wolf instead of a sheep; when
contending with wolves, guard with all your power, that you may not lose the
treasure of righteousness; guard against those who plunder; being wise as a
serpent, guard against the plots; and if the wolf should also harm, and if he
should also be about to tear you, prepare yourself to bear it, remembering him
who says: We were accounted as sheep for slaughter; looking to the Lord
himself, Who was led as a sheep to slaughter, and as a lamb silent before its
shearer. The time of your victory too will come, when you conquer by
forbearance with Christ. For the Lord also did not strive nor cry out, nor did
anyone hear his voice in the streets. And the Apostle says he is ready to
avenge all disobedience, when the obedience of those who have obeyed is
fulfilled. And as God is long-suffering, you too be long-suffering; for when
today Christ suffers, as you suffer, the servant of Christ must also endure
for Christ. Cry to him, and he will do the vengeance quickly; he will make an
end of the wickedness of sinners, and God, a just judge, strong and
long-suffering, will direct the just man; for he is long-suffering that we may
turn. To him be the glory unto the ages. Amen.
