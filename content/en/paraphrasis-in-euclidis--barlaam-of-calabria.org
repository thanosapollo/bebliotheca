* Paraphrase on Euclid

Arithmetical demonstration by Barlaam the monk of the things proved linearly
in the second of the Elements. Definitions. I say that a number multiplies a
number, when, as many as are the units in the multiplying number, so many
times the multiplied being added together produces some number, which it also
measures according to the units in the multiplying number. And I call this
one, the one produced from these, a plane number. And I call a square number
the one produced from some number multiplying itself. I call a number a part
of a number, the less of the greater, whether it measures or does not measure
the greater.
1. If there are two numbers and one of them is divided into any numbers
whatever, the plane number from the original two numbers is equal to the plane
numbers produced from the undivided and each of the parts of the divided. Let
there be two numbers AB, C, and let AB have been divided into 352 any numbers
whatever, AD, DE, EB. I say that the plane number from C, AB is equal to the
plane numbers from C, AD, C, DE, C, EB. For let that from C, AB be F, and that
from C, AD GH, and that from C, DE HI, and that from C, EB IK. And since AB
multiplying C produced F, therefore C measures F according to the units in AB.
For the same reasons it also measures GH according to the units in AD, and HE
according to the units in DE, and IK according to the units in EB. Therefore C
measures the whole HK according to the units in AB. But it also measured F
according to the units in AB. Therefore each of F, HK is equimultiple of C.
And equimultiples of the same are equal to one another. Therefore F is equal
to HK. And F is the plane number from C, AB, and HK is the one composed from C
and each of the plane numbers AD, DE, EB. Therefore the plane number from C,
AB is equal to the plane numbers from C and each of AD, DE, EB. If therefore
there are two numbers and one of them is divided into any numbers whatever,
the plane number from the original two numbers is equal to the plane numbers
from the undivided and each of the parts of the divided; which was to be
shown.
2. If a number is divided into two numbers, two plane numbers, those produced
from the whole and each of the parts, both together are equal to the square on
the whole. For let number AB have been divided into two numbers AG, GB. I say
that two plane numbers, both the one from AB, AG and the one from AB, BG,
being added, are equal to the square on AB. 353 Let AB multiplying itself
produce D, and let AG multiplying AB produce EF, and let GB also multiplying
the same AB produce FG. Since then AG multiplying AB produced EF, therefore AB
measures EF according to the units in AG. Again, since GB multiplying AB
produced FG, therefore AB measures FG according to the units in GB. But it
also measured EF according to the units in AG; therefore it measures the whole
EG according to the units in itself. Again, since AB multiplying itself
produced D, it therefore also measures D according to the units in itself.
Therefore AB measures each of D, EG according to the units in itself.
Therefore as many times as D is of AB, so many times also is EG of AB. And
numbers that are equimultiples of the same number are equal to one another;
therefore D is equal to EG. And D is the square on AB, and EG is composed of
two plane numbers, those from AB BG, BA AG. Therefore the square on AB is
equal to that composed of two plane numbers, those from AB BG, BA AG. If
therefore a number is divided into two numbers, two plane numbers, those
produced from the whole and each of the parts, both together are equal to the
square on the whole; which was to be shown.
3. If a number is divided into two numbers, the plane number from the whole
and one of the parts is equal to the plane number from the parts together with
the square on the aforesaid part. 354 For let number AB have been divided into
two numbers AG, GB. I say that the plane number from AB, BG is equal both to
the plane number from AG, GB and to the square on GB. Let AB multiply GB and
produce D, and let AG multiply GB and produce EF, and let GB multiplying
itself produce FG. And since AB multiplying GB produced D, therefore GB
measures D according to the units in AB. Again, since AG multiplying GB
produced EF, therefore GB measures EF according to the units in AG. Again,
since GB multiplying itself produced FG, therefore GB measures FG according to
the units in itself. But it also measured EF according to the units in AG.
Therefore it measures the whole EG according to the units in AB. But it also
measured D according to the units in AB. Therefore GB measures each of D, EG
an equal number of times; and things measured an equal number of times by the
same are equal to one another; therefore D is equal to EG. And D is the plane
number from AB, BG, and EG is the plane number from AG, GB together with the
square on GB. Therefore the plane number from AB, BG is equal both to the
plane number from AG, GB and to the square on GB. If therefore a number is
divided into two numbers taken at random, the plane number from the whole and
one of the parts is equal both to the plane number from the parts together
with the square on the aforesaid part; which was to be shown. 355
4. If a number is divided into two numbers, the square on the whole is equal
to the squares on the parts and twice the plane number from the parts. For let
number AB have been divided into two numbers AG, GB. I say that the square on
AB is equal both to the squares on AG, GB and to twice the plane number from
AG, GB. For let the square on AB be D, that on AG EF, that on GB GH, and from
AG, GB each of FG, HK. Since then AG multiplying itself produced EF, therefore
AG measures EF according to the units in itself. Again, since GB multiplying
GA produced FG, therefore AG measures FG according to the units in GB. But it
also measured EF according to the units in itself. Therefore it measures the
whole EG according to the units in AB. Therefore AB multiplying AG produced
EG. Therefore EG is the plane number from BA, AG. Similarly we shall also show
that GK is the plane number from AB, BG. And the square on AB is D. And if a
number is divided into two numbers, the square on the whole is equal to two
plane numbers from the whole and each of the parts. Therefore D is equal to
EK. But EK is composed both of the squares on AG, GB and of twice the plane
number from AG, GB; and D is the square on AB. Therefore the square on AB is
equal both to the squares on AG, GB and to twice the plane number from AG, GB.
356 If therefore a number is divided into two numbers, the square on the whole
is equal to the squares on the parts and twice the plane number from the
parts; which was to be shown.
5. If an even number is divided in half, and is also divided into unequal
numbers, the plane number from the unequal parts together with the square on
the intermediate is equal to the square on the half. For let AB be an even
number and let it have been divided in half into AG, GB, and unequally into
AD, DB. I say that the square on GB is equal to the plane number from AD, DB
together with the square on GD. For let the square on GB be E, the plane
number from AD, DB FG, and the square on DG GH. And since the number BG has
been divided into two numbers BD, DG, therefore the square on BG, that is E,
is equal to the squares on BD, DG together with twice that from BD, DG. Let
then the square on BD be KL, that on DG NX, and from BD, DG each of LM, MN;
therefore the whole KX is equal to E. And since BD multiplying itself produced
KL, it therefore measures it according to the units in itself. Again, since GD
multiplying DB produced LM, therefore DB measures LM according to the units in
GD. But it also measured KL according to the units in itself; therefore it
measures the whole KM according to the units in GB. And GB is equal to GA.
Therefore DB measures KM according to the units in GA. Again, since GD
multiplying DB produced MN, therefore DB measures MN according to the units in
DG. But it also measured KM according to the units in AG 357; therefore it
measures the whole KN according to the units in AD. But BD also measures FG
according to the units in AD; for this is hypothesized. Therefore FG is equal
to KN; for equimultiples of the same are equal to one another. And GH is also
equal to NX; for each is hypothesized as the square on GD. Therefore the whole
KX is equal to the whole FH. And KX is also equal to E. Therefore FH also is
equal to E. And FH is the plane number from AD, DB together with the square on
DG, and E is the square on GB. Therefore the plane number from AD, DB together
with the square on DG is equal to the square on GB. If therefore an even
number is divided in half, and is also divided into unequal numbers, the plane
number from the unequal parts together with the square on the intermediate is
equal to the square on the half; which was to be shown.
6. If an even number is divided in half, and some number is added to it, the
plane number from the whole together with the added and the added, together
with the square on the half, is equal to the square on that composed of the
half and the added. For let even number AB have been divided in half into
numbers AG, GB, and let some other number BD have been added to it. I say that
the plane number from AD, DB together with the square on GB is equal to the
square on GD. For let the square on GD be E, the plane number from AD, DB FG,
and the square on GB GH. And since that on GD is equal to those on DB, BG
together with twice that from DB, BG, let that on BD be KL, from DB, BG each
of LM, MN, and that on BG NX. Therefore the whole KX is equal to the square on
GD. And the square on GD is E; therefore KX is equal to E. And since BD
multiplying itself has produced KL, therefore BD measures KL according to the
units in itself. And it also measures LM according to 358 the units in BG;
therefore DB measures the whole KM according to the units in GD. And since DB
also measures MN according to the units in GB, and GB is equal to GA; for this
is hypothesized; therefore DB measures the whole KN according to the units in
AD. But DB also measures FG according to the units in AD; for FG is
hypothesized as from AD, DB; therefore FG is equal to KN. And HG is also equal
to NX; for each is the square on GB. Therefore the whole FH is equal to KX.
And KX was shown equal to E; therefore FH also is equal to E. And FH is that
from AD, DB together with the square on GB, and E is that on GD. Therefore
that from AD, DB together with that on GB is equal to the square on GD. If
therefore an even number is divided in half, and some number is added to it,
the plane number from the whole together with the added and the added,
together with the square on the half, is equal to the square on that composed
of the half and the added; which was to be shown.
7. If a number is divided into two numbers, the square on the whole together
with the square on one of the parts is equal to twice the plane number from
the whole and the said part, together with the square on the remaining part.
For let number AB have been divided into numbers AG, GB. I say that the
squares on BA, AG are equal to twice the plane number from BA, AG together
with the square on BG. 359 For since the square on AB is equal to those on BG,
GA and twice that from BG, GA, let the square on AG be added in common;
therefore that on BA together with that on AG is equal to two squares on AG
and one on GB together with twice that from BG, GA. And since once that from
BA, AG is equal to once that from BG, GA together with the square on GA,
therefore twice that from BA, AG is equal to twice that from BG, GA together
with two squares on GA. Let the square on BG be added in common; therefore two
squares on AG and one on GB together with twice that from BG, GA are equal to
twice that from BA, AG together with that on GB. Therefore the square on AB
together with the square on AG is equal to twice that from BA, AG together
with the square on the remaining part GB. If therefore a number is divided
into two numbers, the square on the whole together with the square on one of
the parts is equal to twice the plane number from the whole and the said part,
together with the square on the remaining part; which was to be shown.
8. If a number is divided into two numbers, four times the plane number from
the whole and one of the parts together with the square on the remaining part
is equal to the square on the whole and the aforesaid part as on one. For let
number AB have been divided into two numbers AG, GB. I say that four times
that from AB, BG together with the square on AG is equal to the square on AB,
BG as on one. For let BD be set equal to the number BG. And since that on 360
AD is equal to the squares on AB, BD and twice the plane number from AB, BD,
and BD is equal to BG, therefore the square on AD is equal to the squares on
AB, BG and twice the plane number from AB, BG. And the squares on AB, BG are
equal to twice the plane number from AB, BG and the square on AG; therefore
the square on AD is equal to four times the plane number from AB, BG and the
square on AG. And the square on AD is that on AB, BG as on one; for BD is
equal to BG. Therefore the square on AB, BG as on one is equal to four times
that from AB, BG and that on AG. If therefore a number is divided into two
numbers, four times the plane number from the whole and one of the parts
together with the square on the remaining part is equal to the square on the
whole and the aforesaid part as on one. For let number AB have been divided
into two numbers AG, GB. I say that four times that from AB, BG together with
the square on AG is equal to the square on AB, BG as on one. For let BD be set
equal to the number BG. And since that on 360 AD is equal to the squares on
AB, BD and twice the plane number from AB, BD, and BD is equal to BG,
therefore the square on AD is equal to the squares on AB, BG and twice the
plane number from AB, BG. And the squares on AB, BG are equal to twice the
plane number from AB, BG and the square on AG; therefore the square on AD is
equal to four times the plane number from AB, BG and the square on AG. And the
square on AD is that on AB, BG as on one; for BD is equal to BG. Therefore the
square on AB, BG as on one is equal to four times that from AB, BG and that on
AG. If therefore a number is divided into two numbers, four times the plane
number from the whole and one of the parts together with the square on the
remaining part is equal to the square on the whole and the aforesaid part as
on one; which was to be shown.
9. If a number is divided in half, and is further also divided into unequal
numbers, the squares on the unequal numbers are double of the square on the
half together with the square on the intermediate. For let even number AB have
been divided in half into numbers AG, GB, and into unequal AD, DB. I say that
the squares on AD, DB are double of the squares on AG, GD. For since even
number AB has been divided into equals AG, GB, and into unequals AD, DB,
therefore that from AD, DB together with that on GD is equal to the square on
AG. 361 Therefore twice that from AD, DB together with two squares on GD is
double of the square on AG. And since AB has been divided in half into AG, GB,
therefore the square on AB is quadruple of the square on AG. And since twice
that from AD, DB together with two on DG is double of that on GA, and if there
are two numbers, the one of them quadruple of the same, and the other double,
the quadruple is double of the double, therefore that on AB is double of twice
that from AD, DB together with two on DG. Therefore twice that from AD, DB is
less than half of that on AB by twice that on DG. And since twice that from
AD, DB together with that composed of those on AD, DB is equal to that on AB,
therefore that composed of those on AD, DB is greater than half of that on AB
by twice that on DG. And that on AB is quadruple of that on AG; therefore that
composed of those on AD, DB is greater than double of that on AG by twice that
on DG. Therefore it is double of those on AG, GD. If therefore an even number
is divided in half, and is further also divided into unequal numbers, the
squares on the unequal numbers are double of the square on the half together
with the square on the intermediate; which was to be shown.
10. If an even number is divided in half, and some other number is added to
it, the square on the whole together with the added and the square on the
added, both together, are double of the square on the half and of the square
on that composed of the half and the added as on one. 362 For let AB be an
even number and let it have been divided in half into AG, GB, and let some
other number BD have been added to it. I say that the squares on AD, DB are
double of the squares on AG, GD. For since number AD has been divided into AB,
BD, therefore the squares on AD, DB are equal to twice the plane number from
AD, DB together with the square on AB. And the square on AB is equal to four
squares on AG, GB; for AG is equal to GB. Therefore the squares on AD, DB are
equal both to twice that from AD, DB and to four on BG, GA. And since that
from AD, DB together with that on GB is equal to that on GD, therefore twice
that from AD, DB together with two on GB is equal to two on GD. Therefore the
squares on AD, DB are equal to two on GD and two on AG. Therefore they are
double of those on AG, GD. And the square on AD is that on the whole and the
added, and that on DB is that on the added, and that on GD is that on that
composed of the half and the added. Therefore the square on the whole together
with the added, together with that on the added, is double of that on the half
together with that on that composed of the half and the added. If therefore an
even number is divided in half, and some other number is added to it, the
square on the whole together with the added and the square on the added, both
together, are double of the square on the half and of the square on that
composed of the half and the added as on one; which was to be shown.
