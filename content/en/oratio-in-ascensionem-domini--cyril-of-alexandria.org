has stopped, and he has raised up another, a heavenly one, for us. But let us
take up the saying again. What then does Christ say? I have trodden the
winepress alone. For who else has suffered for us? Was Paul crucified for us,
or were we baptized into the name of Paul? Christ the Son of God alone was
crucified for us, he alone has suffered for us, he alone has saved us: I have
trodden the winepress alone. Do not again, O beloved, cast the Father out of
the economy, nor again the Holy Spirit; do not say that the Father did not
know that the Son was suffering. But he both knew and foresaw and consented;
yet he did not suffer with him, nor did the Spirit; for the Son alone took
upon himself the economy for us. Yet he himself did not suffer in the Godhead
by nature, but he took upon himself the passions of the economy for us; yet
he did not suffer, since he was God. I have trodden the winepress, he says,
all alone, and of the nations there was no man with me. No prophet, no
righteous man, no patriarch, no holy one.
For he says somewhere: My friends and my neighbors drew near from over
against me and stood. I have trodden the winepress all alone. For you have
put my acquaintances far from me, he says, and of the nations there was no
man with me. Do you wish to learn that none of the nations was

with him? Consider the time of the Passion; for when Christ was being
crucified, Peter 135 and John and the others left him and fled and ran again
to the catching of fish. Was there anyone of the nations with him? He trod
the winepress all alone, and of the nations there was no man with me. I
prevailed, he says, in the wrestling alone, I prevailed toward the victory
alone, and having conquered all alone. I grant the victory to my own; for
even if they were enemies according to choice, yet according to essence they
were my work. I have trodden the winepress all alone, and of the nations
there was no man with me, and I trampled them in my wrath, and so on. And at
once he adds the cause of treading the winepress and for what reason he trod
it, and he says: For a day of recompense has come upon them. I was
long-suffering, he says, toward the devil for a long time, I endured him
while he tyrannized, I saw my work crushed and I bore it. But since the day
of recompense had come, since I was well pleased to become gracious over so
many sins of my creatures, since I had mercy on Adam who had insulted me,
the time stood near, and gathering all the demons as clusters into one I
alone trod their winepress, and of the nations there was no man with me. And
what after these things was a day of recompense? Taking this as a riddle,
the heavenly powers, and perceiving in a mystical way what things the Master
is uttering toward them, or rather being led to this truth by the working of
the Holy Spirit, answer to these things and say: I remembered the mercy of
the Lord. For I know, they say, what the Lord was saying through the rest of
the prophets, what he promised to Adam; I know what he was saying then. He
promised to come upon earth and to suffer as a man the things of men. These
things we see with our eyes: I remembered the mercy of the Lord. These
things concerning this present day Isaiah 136 the prophet cries out, he
initiates into this day; for since the lower powers, thus revealed, were
saying: Who is this that comes from Edom, redness of his garments from
Bosor. After this examination and after the remaining inquiries, after this
exactness of the words and after the great clarity of the mysteries, after
Christ had left nothing in silence, they were crying according to David:
Lift up gates, you rulers of yours, and be lifted up, everlasting gates, and
the King of glory shall enter. The higher powers answered them and say: Who
is this that is going up, and why, they say, do you cry, Lift up gates, you
rulers of yours, and be lifted up, everlasting gates, and the King of glory
shall enter. These things
hearing, the nearby powers say: The Lord is strong and mighty, the Lord is
mighty in war. For there are ranks even among the bodiless ones, and many
successions among them; and not all are angels, not all thrones, not all
dominions, but there are also ranks that have gone beyond and powers that
have gone beyond.
Since then the higher powers received an answer to the question, they say:
We have known who this King of glory is. For since they learned who it is
that is going up, they no longer have need of interpretation, they no longer
have need of long speeches, but they say: The Lord of hosts, he is the King
of glory. Up to this point I have followed along with the Master, having the
wing of my mind weak toward those things. Would that, if there were power in
me, I might also go up with him to the mountain of the divine vision, that I
might see Jesus, since he who is joined to the Lord, it says, is one spirit,
and God has also revealed to us through his Spirit. Let us then go up
together with Christ into heaven, brothers; let us not remain upon earth,
let us not again think earthly things, not again about lusts, not again
about robberies, not again about 137 love of money. Do not be lifted up,
since you too fall from heaven as lightning. But may it not be that the one
who follows Christ should suffer this. For we are persuaded concerning you,
beloved, of better things, and we have of salvation, even if we speak thus.
And we say these things for your security, as the apostle says: To me to
speak is not irksome, but for you it is security. And may it be that we all
follow Christ blamelessly and obtain his kingdom; to him be the glory unto
the ages of ages. Amen. Of the same, from a homily spoken in the church of
Cyrinus, on the thirteenth of the month Phamenoth according to the
Egyptians, in which he interprets the saying of Isaiah that says: Death,
having grown strong, swallowed, and again God took away every tear from
every face.
For the God of all, being life and life-giving, willed that man be an
incorruptible living being upon earth, even <if> he is of a corruptible
nature. But he, since he was wicked and envious, plotted against the race,
set himself against the will of the Creator, craftily brought him down into
disobedience, knowing that through it he would fall into death.
