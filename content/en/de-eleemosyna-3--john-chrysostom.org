On Almsgiving.
=62.769=
Divine Scripture says, He who has mercy on the poor lends to God. See what a
debtor the merciful acquire, how great, how well-supplied. If you lend a man
gold, or silver, or grain, you make a bond, and you bind with the writing the
hand that received it; but with God it is not so. You give to the martyrs'
shrines and to the poor man, and God summons and calls out to receive back the
loans which you lent to the poor man; he hastens to loose the surety, and he
does not simply repay, but with much blessing. Likewise the buyer comes to
give little, but the seller more; and so, having set a price in the middle,
they transact what is expedient.
And when with so much toil you acquire what you desire, you do not have
security; for often before it reaches your house, it has either died, or been
lost, or been found worthless, and the toil becomes double. But in the kingdom
of God you suffer nothing of this kind, no toils of travel, no burning heat,
no slackness, no grief of failure, none of these. While you sit inside in the
house, the poor man comes selling paradise, and he says to you: Give bread,
and receive paradise; give a worn garment, and receive the kingdom of heaven.
Yet he does not compel you to give what you do not have, but from what you are
able. But you, not wishing to buy the paradise of God, say, I have no bread.
Give one obol, give a cup of cold water, give what you will, give what you
have; Christ receives all things; only buy paradise; imitate that widow of
Zarephath, who gave the handful of flour and inherited the kingdom of heaven;
and again another widow, the one mentioned in the Gospel, who had nothing else
except two mites only, and casting these into the treasury she bought
paradise. God does not seek a bulk of money, but a merciful soul, and a
well-disposed heart. Give to a poor man, acquire God as debtor: He who has
mercy on the poor lends to God.
And when, he says, does the one who borrowed repay? When you leave behind all
the money here, the houses, the gold, the silver, the clothing, all the rest,
and you go away there, having left all things here; then the one who borrowed
repays with thanksgiving. Send your money on ahead; do not wait for later
times, do not keep it, lest you lose eternal life. Death is uncertain; you do
not know when it comes; you go out at an hour you do not know; and you go
there, but the money stays here, and others, dividing your wealth, do not even
make mention of your name; and often your heirs dig through your estate, even
cursing you. Send your money on ahead into the age that has no end, lest you
go away into the place of the unquenchable fire, as the rich man did because
of Lazarus. Come, you blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for
you from the foundation of the world. For I was hungry, and you gave me to
eat; I was thirsty, and you gave me drink; and what follows. See what the
Maker of the world says. He does not say, I was a king, and you feared me;
nor, A ruler, and you honored me; but, Naked, and you clothed me; sick, and
you visited me.
Truly nothing is equal to almsgiving; a poor man spends, and God repays. God,
having borrowed from the poor and from those who remain at the martyrs'
shrines, does not only repay the loan, but makes the lenders themselves heirs,
saying: Come, you blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you
from the foundation of the world. And when, he says, was it prepared? When you
were supplying the poor with food and clothing, when you were bringing fruit
to the churches and in the =62.770= martyrs' shrines, when you were
celebrating the memorials of the saints, when you were receiving into your
house, when you were visiting the sick and those in prison; when you were
doing these things, I was preparing this kingdom for you. When you are about
to go out of life, do not wait for its hospitality; the master of the house
comes to meet you, saying: Come, you blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom
prepared for you. For I was hungry, and you gave me to eat; I was thirsty, and
you gave me drink. See the greatness of almsgiving, how no virtue is compared
with it or preferred. For he did not say that You kept virginity, or that you
fasted for my sake; he said nothing of that kind, but only, I was hungry, and
you gave me to eat. Great also is fasting, and virginity, and the remaining
virtues, but nothing is equal to almsgiving. Send your world on ahead into
that world there that has no end, and you will find in that age these things
which you provide here, as the Lord also says: Make for yourselves friends
from the mammon of unrighteousness, that when you fail, they may receive you
into the eternal tents. O friendship of the Master, the senselessness of those
who are rich; he himself entreats, and the rich man is in pain. Do you not
understand, O man, what the providence of God is? Could he not have given the
poor man as much money as he has given you, and enrich both you and him? Could
he not have sent down showers of gold, and enrich the poor? Surely God has not
honored you because of wealth? Consider what you are about to become; look in
the tomb at the naked bones; how do you differ there, rich man or poor? Are
not all ash? Are not all dust? Where is a king there? Where a ruler? Where a
mighty man? Where a poor man? Truly all are equal dead, and crawling bones, a
multitude of worms.
Why do you treat the poor man with pride, why do you abhor the sick man? Does
God not look upon him also, and you? Is he not also some likeness of him?
Consider that God calls this very poor man a brother, and makes him more
honorable than you on that day. For God is not unjust; he does not deprive the
poor man of eternal life, and the heavenly kingdom, if indeed he endures the
poverty; but he does not give you two paradises, unless you send on ahead from
the gifts given you by God. For inasmuch as, he says, you did not do it to one
of these my least brethren, you did not do it to me. Come then, let us hear,
and fill the bellies of the poor from what God has supplied you, that you may
find your storehouses full there. For if blessed is he who shall eat bread in
the kingdom of God once, how much more most blessed is he who has been counted
worthy to dwell in it unto the ages that have no end? Do not tell me that the
poor are without means, and have nothing to give me on that day; their surety
is rich.
Do not demand repayment from the poor. God himself will repay you, and he
gives gifts to the poor; he who governs our life here will also save us from
there, if indeed we do what is pleasing to him. For he who has mercy on the
poor lends to God; and according to his gift he will recompense him; according
to the intention, he says. For where there is a good intention, there is
abundant provision. Let us fear the day of the Lord, on which the master of
the house will send into the [...]ria of the wheat, and he will gather the
wheat into the storehouse, but the chaff he will burn with unquenchable fire.
For the master of the house is the Lord, and the reapers are the angels, and
the wheat the righteous, and the chaff the sinners. Let us therefore hasten to
be found ripe wheat through almsgiving, that we may enter into the eternal
tents, which he who was taken up from us prepared; with whom to the Father and
to the Holy Spirit be glory, power, honor, now and always, and unto the ages
of ages. Amen.
