#+BEGIN_QUOTE
Epitaph for Bion edited by J. M. Edmonds
#+END_QUOTE

Wail ailinon for me, glens and Dorian water,
and rivers, weep for lovely Bion.
Now plants, lament for me, and groves now wail,
flowers, now breathe out with gloomy clusters,
now roses, blush your mourning red, now anemones,
now hyacinth, speak your letters and more aiai
babble with your petals; the fair singer is dead.
Begin, Sicilian Muses, begin the dirge.
Nightingales who lament among the dense leaves,
tell the Sicilian streams of Arethusa
that Bion the cowherd is dead, that with him
the song too has died and Dorian music has perished.
Begin, Sicilian Muses, begin the dirge.
Strymonian swans, lament ailinon by the waters,
and with mourning mouths sing a dirge-song,
such as old age sings at your lips,
and tell again the Oeagrian maidens, tell all
the Bistonian Nymphs /the Dorian Orpheus is lost./
Begin, Sicilian Muses, begin the dirge.

That one dear to the herds sings no more,
no more sitting under lonely oaks he sings,
but beside Pluto he sings a Lethean song.
The mountains are voiceless, and the cows among the bulls
wander and wail and will not graze.
Begin, Sicilian Muses, begin the dirge.
Apollo himself wept your swift fate, Bion,
and Satyrs lamented and black-cloaked Priapi;
and Pans groaned your song, and through the wood
the Fountain-nymphs wailed, and the waters became tears.
Echo among the rocks laments, that in silence
she no longer mimics your lips. At your death
trees cast their fruit, and all the flowers withered.
From the sheep no fair milk flowed, nor honey from hives;
it died in the comb, grieving; for it is no longer right
that honey should be gathered, now your honey is dead.
Begin, Sicilian Muses, begin the dirge.
Not so much did the Siren lament by the sea-shores,
nor so much once did the Nightingale sing among the crags,
nor so much did the Swallow wail over the long mountains,
nor so much did Ceyx cry over the sorrows of Alcyone,
nor so much did the cerylus sing in the grey waves,

nor so much in the morning glens did Memnon's bird,
flying about the tomb of Dawn's child, lament,
as they wailed for Bion when he perished.
Begin, Sicilian Muses, begin the dirge.
All the nightingales and swallows, whom he once delighted,
whom he taught to speak, sitting upon the stems
wailed opposite one another; and they cried over
/birds, you mourners, grieve; but we too./
Begin, Sicilian Muses, begin the dirge.
Who ever will play on your pipe, O thrice-desired?
Who will set his mouth to your reeds? Who is so bold?
For still your lips breathe and your breath,
and Echo still feeds on your song in the canes.
Shall I bring the melody to Pan? Perhaps even he would fear
to set his mouth to it, lest he come off second to you.
Begin, Sicilian Muses, begin the dirge.
Galatea too weeps your song, whom you once delighted
as she sat with you by the shores of the sea.
For you did not sing like the Cyclops; from him
fair Galatea fled, but you she looked on more gladly than the sea.
And now, forgetting the wave, she sits on the lonely sands,
and still she tends your cattle.
Begin, Sicilian Muses, begin the dirge.
All the gifts of the Muses have died with you, O herdsman,
the lovely kisses of maidens, the lips of boys,

and gloomy Loves weep about your body.
And Cypris longs for you far more than the kiss
which lately she kissed dying Adonis.
This is a second grief for you, O clearest of rivers,
this, Meles, a new grief. Homer perished for you lately,
that sweet mouth of Calliope, and they say
you lamented your fair son with much-wept streams,
and you filled all the sea with voice; now again another
son you weep, and melt upon a new mourning.
Both were loved by springs, the one drank
of the Pegasis fountain, the other had Arethusa's drink.
And the one sang the fair daughter of Tyndareus
and the great son of Thetis and Atreid Menelaus;
but that one sang not wars, not tears, but Pan,
and piped of herdsmen and herded while he sang,
and made pipes and milked the sweet heifer,
and taught the kisses of boys, and Eros
he nourished in his bosom and provoked Aphrodite.
Begin, Sicilian Muses, begin the dirge.
Every famous city mourns you, Bion, all the towns.
Ascra wails you far more than Hesiod;
the Boeotian woods do not long for Pindar so much;
lovely Lesbos did not lament so much for Alcaeus;
nor so much did the Teian city mourn its singer;
Paros longs for you more than for Archilochus; and instead of Sappho

Mitylene still laments your melody.
Theocritus is among the Syracusans; but I
sing a song of Ausonian grief, no stranger to bucolic
song, but as your pupil whom you taught,
heir of the Dorian Muse, with which honoring me
to others you left your wealth, but to me your song.
Begin, Sicilian Muses, begin the dirge.
Alas, the mallows, when they perish in the garden,
and the green celery and the well-growing curly dill,
later live again and grow into another year;
but we the great and strong, we wise men,
when first we die, unhearing in the hollow earth
sleep a very long, endless, unwaking sleep.
And you then will be packed in silence in the earth,
but it seemed good to the Nymphs that the frog should sing always.
I would not grudge them; for he sings no fair song.
Begin, Sicilian Muses, begin the dirge.
Poison came, Bion, to your mouth, poison you ate:
did it run to such lips and not grow sweet?
What mortal was so savage as to mix for you
or give you, when you called, the poison? Song has expired.
Begin, Sicilian Muses, begin the dirge.
But Justice overtook all. And I upon this grief

pouring tears lament your fate. And if I could,
as Orpheus going down to Tartarus, as Odysseus once,
as Heracles before, I too perhaps would have come to the house
of Pluto, that I might see, and if you sing to Pluto,
that I might hear what you sing. But come, to the Maiden
pipe something Sicilian and bucolicize something sweet.
She too is Sicilian, and played on the Aetnaean
shores, and she knows the Dorian song; not unrewarded
will the singing be. And as before she gave to Orpheus
sweetly playing his lyre Eurydice returning,
so she will send you, Bion, to the mountains. And if I too
could pipe anything, I myself would have sung beside Pluto.
