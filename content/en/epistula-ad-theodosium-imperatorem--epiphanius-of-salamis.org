Letter of Epiphanius, bishop of the Cyprians, to Theodosius the emperor.
19 The devil by his own evil craft contrived idolatry in the world, and
he sowed this in the world and established it and turned men away from
God; but now again, after the heresies and the idols, he has dragged
the faithful down into ancient idolatry and deceived them. 20 He
introduces himself in the letter as having followed from youth the
faith of the fathers at Nicaea, and that his parents were born in this
and had the same confession. 21 For your piety will understand whether
it is fitting for us to have a God painted with colors. 22 I have
heard that some also profess to paint the incomprehensible Son of God;
I do not remember having seen anything of this kind.
23 Which of the ancient fathers, having painted an image of Christ,
set it in a church or in his own house or on door-curtains? Which of
the ancient bishops, having dishonored Christ, painted him? Who thus
made a spectacle of and led in triumph Abraham and Isaac and Jacob,
Moses and the prophets, or Peter or Andrew or James or John or the
rest of the apostles? 24 And at the same time they also lie, forming
the shapes of the saints now one way and now another from their own
thought, sometimes as old men and sometimes as younger the same ones*,
entering into things they have not seen. For they paint the Savior
with hair from a conjecture, because he is called Nazarene, since the
Nazirites have long hair.
But those who attempt to join the types to him are in error; for the
Savior drank wine, which the Nazirites did not drink. 25 It is not
fitting ... for us to have God painted in colors and in limbs. 26 For
even that which they fashion, conceiving it from their own thought,
they lie. For the deceivers paint Peter the holy apostle as an old
man, with the head and the beard shorn; and they paint the holy Paul,
some as bald in front, others as bald and bearded, and the other
disciples as closely shorn. 27 If then the Savior had long hair, and
the other disciples were shorn, and he himself was not shorn and did
not appear equal to them, for what reason did the Pharisees and the
scribes give Judas thirty pieces of silver as a wage, so that by
kissing him he might point him out to them, that "this is the one you
seek," when they could both of themselves and through others have
known by the sign of the hair the one they were seeking to find, and
not have given a wage? 28 He confesses, first that he was set forth as
a laughingstock to the many for the things being raved by him, and
then that even when he advised his fellow bishops and fellow ministers
that these things be taken away, he was not heard. We found at the end
of the letter an emphasis containing something of this kind: that
having often spoken to my fellow ministers that the images be taken
away, I was not received by them, nor did they endure even for a short
time to hear my voice. 29 that the curtains having such painting,
being gathered, might go toward the burial of the poor, and that those
on walls in colors be whitewashed.
30 And those that have already been painted in mosaic, since such
dismantling is difficult: if it is possible for them to be taken down,
well; but if impossible, to be content with what has already been done
and that no one any longer paint in this way.
