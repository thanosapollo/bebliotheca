=96.477= Of Saint Gregory, bishop of Nyssa, from the discourse to Xenodorus
the grammarian, whose beginning is: Nothing is more saving for Christians than
true theology.
For we say that energy is the natural power and movement of each essence,
without which nature neither is nor is known. For of intellectual things it is
intellection, of sensory things sensation (according to which these both
naturally touch things outside and fall under things outside), of winged
things flight, of swimming things swimming, of creeping things creeping, of
walking things walking, of plants growth. And to speak comprehensively, we
call the significant property of each nature a natural energy; of which only
that which is not is deprived. For that which partakes of some essence will
altogether also partake of the power that naturally shows it. For true reason
knows the natural energies of essences as their definitions.
