On Hope.
=60.771=
All human beings are by nature resourceful, because reason is by nature
discerning and intelligent. For each of them commits himself to farming, to
travel, and to some craft under some hope. For all bear labors and sweats,
dangers and afflictions, with a ready will, because they look for goods to
come. The farmer, straining sinews and limbs, bends his back and arms both
hands with spear and plough, and he bears with a ready will the frosts of
winter and the wounds from the clod and the thorns set in it, consoling his
toils with the coming hope of the fruits.
The traveler, setting out on roads hard to pass and steep, while the solid
ground strikes back and his soles are lashed, boiling with abundant drops of
sweat from crown to nails, nobly endures burning heats, thirst for water, and
want of food, because of the gain he hopes from the journey. Physicians, in
cutting out swollen wounds, take the stench upon themselves, and when they
receive from the hand the emptying of bowels and the outpourings of slack
=60.772= humors they do not refuse, for the health of the sick and for their
own prosperity hoped from those they treat. Seafarers on the deep, leaving
land and cutting the salt, dark-blue backs of the sea, endure the violence of
contrary winds and incurable tumults, taking into their sight the stretching
of many-twisted ropes, the fearful creaking of sails and of necks, and the
roaring, fearful looks of wild waves, because of the safety and prosperity
they hope from the voyage. All these, and more than these, endure toils and
dangers for the hope that shows itself for a little while. Much more does the
God-loving race of Christians, I mean both men and women (for not only the
male has been enlisted with God, but the female also, not by strength of body
but by the courage of understanding), both of them =60.773= bearing with a
ready will persecutions and famines, fasting and vigil and sleeping on the
ground, and the other unspeakable afflictions, for the unstained and blessed
hope in the heavens.
Let each therefore stand to his own work, and be braced by hope. You farmer of
the spiritual field, drive the plough of teaching into the receptive plot of
the mind; then, after the heretical seeds already cast, pluck out the shoot of
Jewish and Hellenic sophistries, so that, having cleansed the soul, you may
sow the holy seeds of faith, the gladnesses of hope, the sweetnesses of love.
For he gave not only to Jeremiah authority to pull down the worse and to plant
the better, but to you also, when he said: "Behold, I have put my words in
your mouth; behold, I have set you this day over nations and kingdoms, to root
out and to pull down and to destroy, and to build and to plant." Root out,
then, the wild motions of lust, and plant the holy patterns of chastity; pull
down the planting of the evil one, and plant the citizenship of Christ;
destroy the teachings that must be fled, and set up in public the earnest
thoughts. You builder, pull down the images badly built by the devil's engine
in the souls of men, and build a temple of God, laying as foundation Christ
the Son, the cornerstone, upon whom the fair works of righteousness are built.
Doing these things, if you are pressed, endure; if you are persecuted, do not
grow weary; if you hunger, do not flee; but bear all things with a ready will
for the hoped-for recovery in the delight of paradise. You are a traveler,
according to the one who says, "Order my steps in your paths, that my
footsteps may not be shaken." Flee the broad and spacious way that leads to
destruction. Take the staff of the Spirit, which performs two acts: it kills
the crooked dragon that sits in the way, and it supports you when you are ever
loosened and falling in the trials. Bear the trial as it is brought. If you
thirst, run up to the immortal spring, Christ. If you hunger, seek the living
bread, so that you may not grow weary, nor give in, made slack by pleasures,
but be strengthened, looking to the end of the way, hoping for the life equal
to the angels in the kingdom of heaven. You physician of souls, cut out with
the sword of the Spirit the swollen and ulcerated passions of pride; wash away
the filth of pleasures with the water of teaching; heal the wounds laid by sin
upon the souls of men, using wine and oil after the Lord's example: with wine
astringe them, so that they no longer give in, slackened, to the desires; with
oil loose the pains of the soul, promising them swift forgiveness of the
faults already committed. Do not, with an eye to money, neglect the poor, nor
again, for the sake of contemptible wealth, neglect the rich, but call poor
and rich alike to temperance.
Despair of no one, not even of the great sinner, lest we throw them wholly
into the depth of sin, but give a hand to the fallen =60.774= saying: "The
Lord did not come to save the righteous, but sinners," so that, becoming
eager, they may turn from evil, do the good, and be saved. Do all things for
the health of the sick, being fed by the hope of those who are being saved,
and by the gladness hoped for with God. As an athlete, contend lawfully: for
no one is crowned unless he contends lawfully. The wrestling is not against
blood and flesh, but against the principalities, against the powers, against
the world-rulers of this darkness, against the spiritual things of wickedness
in the heavenly places. Love the ascetic labors; flee the devil's snares. See
Paul running and boxing and wrestling. "I so run," he says, "that I may lay
hold; I so box, not as one beating the air; but I buffet my body, lest I who
have preached should myself be found unapproved." Run, then, that you may lay
hold; box and wrestle, that you may defeat the enemy; anoint yourself with the
oil of piety. You have wrestling-holds left you by the holy fathers: take the
forbearance of Moses, the chastity of Joseph, the meekness of David, the
virginity of Elijah, the fasting of Elisha, the poverty of Isaiah, the faith
of Paul and Peter, the love of Christ, so that, having contended well in all
things, you may receive the prizes of victory from the hand of God. Are you a
shepherd of the rational sheep of Christ? Fold the sheep, turn back the
strayed, bind up the crushed, heal those bitten by wolves, turn the wolves
away; as for the dogs over the flock, they are not sleeping, but barking and
carrying in secret into the Master's flock: drive these away.
Point out the ever-green shoot that sprang from Mary; mark the immortal spring
of baptism; wipe the stained sheep with the water of tears; endure every pain
and toil, so that, when Christ the chief shepherd is revealed, you may receive
the unfading crown of glory. Are you a sheep? Obey your shepherd; do not flee
the fold; love the faith; do not turn aside from the shepherd's rod. For he
does not strike you to kill, but to turn you back from error. Feed without
distinction on the flower that sprang from the rod of Jesse; drink the
immortal spring that bubbles from the hewn rock, so that from here into the
fold of delight. Do not gather wealth; rather, do not love wealth. Flee care
for money; do not hold onto the bites. Do not gather the money from which
charges grow, but sow food richly for the poor, hoping to reap an abundant,
ripe sheaf of the soul. Let the poor man not be faint-hearted, not renounce
life, not blaspheme, but give thanks; endure the short-lived hardship of
poverty; look to your pattern, Lazarus, and be strengthened by hope. For you
too will attain the rest of Abraham's bosom. May the God and Master of all
count us also worthy of that place, in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the
glory and the might with the Holy Spirit, now and always, and to the ages of
ages. Amen.
