* On Sickness and Health
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

From the discourse of Saint Athanasius, patriarch of Alexandria, On Sickness
and Health, which begins: "The letter of your piety was delivered to us."
The teaching about the health of human beings is not set forth in a single
sense in the divine Scriptures; the meaning of it is twofold. For human beings
have their subsistence from two parts, and according to the most blessed
Apostle we are spoken of in two ways, from the inner and from the outer man
(for he says in the Second Letter to the Corinthians: "Though our outer man is
wasting away, yet the inner is being renewed," and in the Letter to the
Ephesians: "For this reason I bow my knees before the Father, from whom every
family in heaven and on earth is named, that he may grant you according to the
riches of his glory to be strengthened with power through his Spirit in the
inner man, that Christ may dwell through faith in your hearts"). So the sense
of these things too must be not single but twofold.
And as, for the outer man, a human being has bodily bread for food, so for the
inner he has the food proper to him, of which Paul said: "Being nourished on
the words of truth." This food has its beginning from the Lord, who says: "I
am the bread that came down from heaven and gives life to men." Again, just as
outwardly he has rivers and fountains and springs for drinking, so inwardly he
has the Spirit, which is given to those who believe and "becomes a spring of
water leaping up to eternal life." And for walking on the earth he has bodily
feet, but for the grace of ascent into the heavens he has the feet which Paul
had when he said: "I run not as one uncertain," and concerning which he also
charged us to "have these feet shod with the readiness of the gospel." He has
hands, too, for bodily touch, and he has them within, so as to touch the Word
as well, such hands as the disciples had when they said: "That which was from
the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, and our
hands have handled, concerning the Word of life." And since the Word is
incorporeal, the hands that touch him must also be incorporeal.
These two are one: the human being made from the beginning by God from body
and soul. By the grace of the Maker they have one natural constitution, so
that the end of each one's action is also one, and they share the same praise
or the same blame. For even if they are parted by death, they will receive one
another again, and once joined they are again one and thereafter remain
undivided. For when we have risen, death will no longer have dominion. And in
short, that I may not linger over what is obvious by setting out each
particular, one must know that the body is composed of members, whereas the
inner man is not composed of bodily things, yet he takes his meaning from the
actions of the members. Feet are the soul's progress in virtue; hands are the
exactness of reasoning; an eye is the discerning mind; a tongue is the very
discrimination of thoughts. It is said to have a womb as well, that the
generative power of its understanding may be shown, as it is written: "From
your fear we conceived in the womb and were in labor and brought forth a
spirit of salvation, which we have wrought upon the earth."
So also it is said to lie down, and then to sleep, when it does nothing. For
whatever it rests in is also its leisure, and they say that it has sleep there
as well. For when it rests from what is base, then it is said to it: "If you
sleep, you will sleep sweetly, and you will not fear a terror coming upon
you." And this is the Savior's grace, who says: "Sleep on now"; fear no
longer. But its idleness and neglect of virtue is a sleep of that kind which
the Holy Spirit blames, saying: "All who mounted horses have grown drowsy,"
and again: "They slept their sleep and found nothing." Hence our Lord Jesus
Christ, wishing our mind not to neglect virtue, commanded: "Watch" and "keep
awake," as also in Proverbs he advises: "Give not sleep to your eyes, nor
drowsiness to your eyelids, that you may be saved as a gazelle from snares and
as a bird from a trap," even if some from the heresies, not understanding
these things and truly having no mind, misinterpret what was written
spiritually. Pretending not to sleep as regards bodily sleep, and stealing the
use of it under cover of necessity, they do not know that they stay awake for
a little while, yet sleep wholly in their understanding, and that, fearing
bodily sleep, they fall away from the soul's watchfulness. This watchfulness
the holy one had even while sleeping in the body, saying: "I sleep, and my
heart is awake." For the sleep of the saints contemplates the better things,
and while their body is at rest on earth the mind travels beyond places and
flies from earth to the heavens. For Elisha the great prophet, sitting bodily
on the mountain, followed Gehazi in the mind, and holy Daniel, sleeping in the
body, was awake in the mind, seeing those great and divine visions.
Thus, then, since the account of human beings has been shown to be twofold,
the sense of their health must also be twofold. For there is health of soul,
and there is health of body. Some are sick in soul only and healthy in body;
others, conversely, are sick in body but healthy in soul; and some have the
sickness in both and are sound neither in body nor in soul. And again the two,
being one human being, suffer with one another, since they also have one
freedom from pain that comes from health, or one torment that comes from
sickness.
And again concerning the same:
Just as the constitution of the body has received from the Creator the five
senses fitted to it, so also the intellectual substance of the soul travels
through the whole working of the commandments in the five senses. The symbol
of its sight is the eyes, as holy David said: "My eyes are ever toward the
Lord." Of hearing: "He added for me an ear to hear," and the Lord: "He who has
ears to hear, let him hear." Of smell: "We will run after the scent of your
ointments." Of taste: "Taste and see that the Lord is good." And: "I opened my
mouth and drew in spirit." And: "Lord, you will open my lips." Of touch: "What
we have beheld, and our hands have handled, concerning the Word of life."
There is also after these another, a sixth sense, according to which we who
are able to touch lay hold of the things that cannot be touched, of which
Solomon spoke. And you will find a divine sense, which often arises in the
compunction of the heart.
