160 I think it is fitting for me also now to speak the words of the prophet,
that "I waited as she who gives birth," always reaching after the progress of
the Holy Spirit in you, which is found not in word and shadows but is
accomplished in the working of "truth" and in "the power of God," according to
the Master's own verdict, that the kingdom is not "in word" "but in" work and
"power," and again: "the kingdom" "is within you." For some philosophize about
Christ in outward form, others also in word, and others even in the work
itself; but those "in the power of God" are exceedingly rare. These are they
upon whom the "Holy Spirit will come" and "the power of the Most High will
overshadow." For it is fitting that every soul that flees the grief that came
from the sentence given to Eve, and that pursues the joy of Mary, be kept
virgin without stain and without blame for the unblemished bridegroom Christ.
162 For just as through the earthy mind and the fleshly passions we recognize
ourselves as sons of Eve and of Adam, so we must recognize the adoption
through the heavenly mind and the sufferings of Christ. For "examine
yourselves," "prove yourselves," "whether you are in the faith," whether
"Jesus Christ" dwells "in you." For if, wearing "the image of the earthy," we
perceived the working of the wicked and shameful and filthy thoughts, how much
more must we, having worn "the image of the heavenly," perceive the working of
the holy and worshipped Spirit. Let the prudent virgin therefore know that she
ought to have Christ in herself just as Mary also did, and as that one in the
womb, you in the heart, and then you will be able to chant with understanding
and say: "Because of your fear, Lord, we conceived in the womb and were in
labor and brought forth a spirit of salvation." So also Ecclesiastes has said:
that "as bones of her who is with child" so "is the way of the spirit." If
then you have "this treasure" in your earthen vessel, if "he who said, Let
light shine out of darkness, has shone" in your heart "for the illumination of
the knowledge" of the gospel, if "the peace of God" rules in your heart
without ceasing, if, having pursued, you have laid hold of "peace" with
holiness, if Christ has dwelt "in" your "inner man," if "the Father" with his
only-begotten has made a "dwelling" with you, if you have been deemed worthy
of the beatitude through purity of heart, if you have become "a temple of God"
"and" his "Spirit" has dwelt in you, if "in full assurance of faith" you have
your heart sprinkled "from an evil conscience," if "the God of peace" has
sanctified you wholly "and" your "whole spirit" and soul "and body
blamelessly," if 164 you are a prudent virgin having the lamp and the oil in
the vessel, if you know what this is: "let your loins be girded and your lamps
burning," if you have put on the "wedding garment," if of all these things you
have received experience through the working in the heart, you will be able
worthily to be wedded to the heavenly bridegroom; but if not, even if I keep
silent, "the stones will cry out."
166 Therefore I bear witness before my Master himself and the worshipped Holy
Spirit, that just as the earthly virgin, even if she receive a pledge, even if
after the pledge many gifts, even if she become mistress of all the substance,
yet if she does not obtain that fleshly communion, she is a stranger to the
man named to her: so also the soul that is kept virgin for Christ, even if she
receive a pledge through baptism (for baptism is a perfect "pledge of the"
coming "inheritance") and immediately after baptism receive many gifts, of
word or of interpretation or of healing or of some other charism, yet if she
is not deemed worthy of union with the incorrupt bridegroom, she is a stranger
to him. For the anointing of "gladness" and the "wedding garment" are not
recognized in charisms, but in the very adoption in which is the unchanging
love. Having then the pledge of baptism you have the "talent" perfect, but if
you have not worked upon it you will be incomplete, and not only that but you
will also be deprived of it. Do not therefore reckon, having fed others with
your "possessions," that you are anything. And if you also have a word,
speaking "with the tongues of angels and of men," do not be lifted up; for you
have not yet received "the perfect." And if you also have "interpretation," do
not remain in it. And if you have also acquired "all faith," come to the
"love" that does not permit you to fall. For "love never fails," being
unchanging it makes those who have longed for it passionless and unshaken.
Therefore the charisms are accomplished for the sake of the divine
dispensation, according to the distribution of the will of God, for those
still infants and incomplete, but love (as I said before) renders those
deemed worthy of it passionless and unchanging, for it "is God," as we have
learned in the Catholic Epistles. Every soul therefore that is acted upon by
filthy thoughts and wicked imaginings is not kept virgin for Christ, for she
is adulterated by the adversary. But she who has purely desired to be
presented to the Lord lives in purity of heart, as the bridesman says: "For I
betrothed you to one husband, to present a pure virgin to Christ; but I fear
lest somehow, as the serpent 167 deceived Eve in his craftiness, so your minds
should be corrupted." Of as many therefore as have their minds corrupted, they
are in the communion of the devil's corruption and not in the communion of the
Savior's incorruption; "for what communion has light with darkness" or "what
agreement has a temple of God with idols?" So let us believe these things,
that we may also read without falsehood: "I live, yet no longer I, but Christ
lives in me." For in those in whom Christ does not dwell, being dead they do
not praise God genuinely, since in the prophet: "the dead shall not praise
you, Lord." Let therefore "the life of Jesus" reign in our mortal flesh. Let
us acquire in ourselves "the Spirit of Christ," that we may be his; for "if
anyone," he says, "does not have the Spirit of Christ, this one is not his."
Let us destroy the nations in us; let us say to God, "scatter the nations that
want wars," "flash lightning and you will scatter them," for we accuse
ourselves, not the Israelites, saying: "they did not destroy the nations which
the Lord told them." We accuse ourselves, not the idols; for we have "ears"
and do not hear, we have "eyes" and do not see, for there is not even a spirit
in us.
So let us pray that "the eyes" of our understanding "be enlightened," that we
may know what this is: "uncover my eyes, and I will perceive your wonders from
your law." Let us cry from the heart "Rabboni," that we may receive our sight.
He who has received his sight, even if he is "in the flesh," does not wage war
"according to the flesh"; "for the weapons of" his "warfare" "are not fleshly
but mighty with God for the pulling down of strongholds, destroying reasonings
and every high thing that is lifted up against the knowledge of God." Let us
lift up "holy hands" in prayers without fail "without wrath and disputings."
Let us become fellow-tribesmen of the Lord who sprang from the tribe of
"Judah." Let us be Jews in secret; "for he is not a Jew who is one outwardly,"
"but he who is one in secret," who through rest from vain thoughts and filthy
notions keeps sabbath always and has received circumcision in his inner man.
168 Therefore let us circumcise the unclean foreskin of the "heart," cutting
away the surplus of the mind, which is "the sting of death, sin," that was
sown alongside through the enemy in the disobedience. Let us receive in our
hearts the law in the Spirit of God, which he promised saying: "giving my laws
in their hearts, and I will write them upon their minds," and from David: "the
law of God is in their hearts, and their steps shall not be tripped." If then
you wish not to be tripped, receive the law of God in secret, which through
holiness establishes you in passionlessness. These are our things, or rather
God's, having a true mind but a simple diction. Having been deemed worthy of
these, let us pray to have "the mind of Christ" and to walk "in newness of
life," not showing one thing in the heart and another in the appearance. For
the false and deceitful character is hateful before God. Therefore the Lord
also clearly and distinctly showed that "the things that defile the man" come
forth from within "out of the heart," and again condemning the Pharisee, or
rather each of us, as being blind according to the inner man to the divine
mysteries, he says: "Blind Pharisee, wash the inside of the cup, that the
outside also may become clean." For he who has set his zeal upon the extreme
brightness of the inward things, that is, who is occupied always about the
mind and the soul, washes the outer also together, plainly. I exhort therefore
that we not remain in the outward manners and forms; let us become a pure
"temple of God"; let us hear him who exhorts us: "that you may become
blameless and harmless, children of God without blemish"; for he is without
blemish who does not work lawlessness in the heart, who has not "spot or
wrinkle or any such thing," who knows what this is: "all the glory of the
king's daughter is within" and not without, who says without falsehood: "you
have given gladness into my heart," who has been marked by the face of Christ,
or rather who has been worked upon, who strives to arrive "at a perfect man,
at the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ," who flees the good
repute among men, 169 but pursues the glory from God that works in the pure
heart. For just as the radiance of the glory of this sun works upon the
fleshly eyes, so the glory of the Holy Spirit works upon the pure eyes
according to the inner man. So he who is eager to be justified through the
achievements of the outer man and his vain power is such as one who has "zeal
of God" "but not according to knowledge," as one who ought to be a teacher
according to "the time" and "again" needs teaching, as one who ought to be
deemed worthy of "solid food" but partakes of "milk," as one who began "in the
Spirit" but "now" finishes "in the flesh." Let such a one therefore know that
"all the righteousness" of the outer man "as a menstruous rag" was reckoned by
the prophet, and by the apostle as "dung."
But may it be granted to us to be subjected to the righteousness of God that
is accomplished in the inner man, in whom the tribunal of Christ stands
together with the unblemished sanctuary. For "this" is "toil before me" (he
says) "until I enter into the sanctuary of God," so that "the testimony of the
conscience" may boast "in the cross of Christ," who redeems our life from
corruption and cleanses our conscience from "dead works," that we may worship
"in the Spirit" of God and not "have confidence in the flesh," that we may
know what we worship according to him who said: "we worship what we know." For
the witness of these things in heaven is faithful, since "we speak in the
presence of God in Christ," thinking nothing else than that "it is good for
the heart to be established by grace" and not to be carried away "by various
and strange teachings." And we pray that you be delivered from the good repute
among men, which, as it seems, craftily and wisely lays a snare so that they
fail to gain the governance that is from the power of God, that you may
neither ever be defeated by the contempt of men, nor again be lifted up by
blessing. Be persuaded by him who leads you "into all" his "truth." Let 170
your soul have communion with Christ, as a bride has communion with a
bridegroom; for "this mystery is great, but I speak concerning Christ and
concerning the" unblemished soul; for no one is enrolled in the "church" of
"the firstborn in heaven" except the unblemished soul, and no one "is crowned
unless he contend lawfully."
