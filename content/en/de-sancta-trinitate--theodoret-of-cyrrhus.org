OF OUR FATHER AMONG THE SAINTS CYRIL ARCHBISHOP OF ALEXANDRIA ON THE HOLY AND
LIFE-GIVING TRINITY.

PROOEMIUM.

PROOEMIUM. It was necessary that all who are made splendid by the name of our
Saviour Jesus Christ, and who glory in being sheep of the shepherd who laid
down his life for us, should hear the voice of the shepherd, and graze the
pasture shown by him, and remain within the bounds and laws of the evangelical
faith, and love the simple teaching of the apostles; but since many, struck
with conceit, and having fallen in love with empty glory, and not knowing
themselves, preferred the offspring of their own wandering mind to the
God-inspired teaching, and having turned aside from the straight road that
leads to the city above, went into many-branched and death-bearing paths, all
wandering alike, yet not following the same road of error in the same way, but
following differently the deceit of reasonings, I think it fitting for those
who travel the royal road worn by the pious both to pity those who wander, and
to lay bare the deceit, and to point out piety, and to guide those who follow,
and to keep them from turning aside either way, until they reach the royal
city.

ΚΕΦΑΛΑΙΟΝ Αʹ. That God makes much of the salvation of men. For this reason
also the Saviour of all sent into the inhabited world the sacred choir of the
apostles, that with the rays of the knowledge of God they might illuminate
those reared in the darkness of ignorance, that they might both gather the
scattered sheep that lay exposed to the wolves and shepherd them fittingly,
that they might change the wild olive into a cultivated olive by the art of
the Spirit, that they might fish with the word of teaching those hidden in the
depth of impiety; since then both to the maker of all men the salvation of men
is most pleasant, and to nature it seems good to help the neighbor in need of
care, let us also put down upon the bankers the talent of knowledge given us
by the Lord, lest we be condemned together with the idle servant; and we shall
set forth the teaching of the divine doctrines, for those who know as a
reminder, and for those who do not know as instruction.

Βʹ. What is the character of the ecclesiastical word. Let the word proclaim
the evangelical faith thus simply and in a teaching manner, not dialectically
nor contentiously, but in a manner fitting the Church of God, moving to
compunction, not ostentatiously, instructively, not forensically, freed from
technicalities, making use of theology; not busying itself with the
unattainable, not searching the inconceivable, not circumscribing with mind
and word the things that cannot be contained, not subjecting the
master-craftsman to technical methods, not leaving faith and demanding
demonstration; following the fishermen, tracing the tentmaker, guided by the
tax-collector, illumined by the prophetic lamp, enlightened by the evangelical
sun, mixing in nothing of one's own reasonings, but weaving all the teachings
of the all-holy Spirit; for this is the bound of divine teaching.

Γ. That the teaching will be toward the pious. The heretical blasphemies we
have already refuted in other writings, taking each separately, and stripping
off the covering of deceit, and showing the impiety naked; but now, God
granting, we shall set forth to the nurslings of the faith the God-given
doctrines of the Church; neither bringing satiety upon the readers by length
of discourse, nor spoiling accuracy by brevity, but choosing the mean of both
extremes; so that we may neither weary the hearers with bulk, and may offer a
clear teaching of the knowledge of God; and I shall begin from above, running
to the very fountain of good things.

Δʹ. What conceptions one must have concerning God and Father. We who are
lovers and worshippers and heralds of the Trinity, both loud-voiced and
high-minded, believe in one God Father without beginning and unbegotten,
always being Father, not acquiring this later, for there was not when he was
not, but from above he was Father; nor did he become first Son then Father,
according to the sequence of bodies; but from the time he is, and he always
is, he both is and is called Father.

Εʹ. What one must think concerning the Son. We believe in one Son co-eternal
with the one who begot him, not taking a beginning of being, but always being,
and being with the Father; for from the time there is Father, and he is always
Father, from him there is Son; for these things have an inseparable relation
to one another, both the names and the realities; but if the Son is not
always, but there was when he was not, neither is the Father always; for from
the time he begot, he has this name; but if God and Father is always (for it
is truly blasphemous to put the maker of times under times, and to declare the
timeless generation beyond time second by a temporal interval), the Son is
always, having been begotten from the Father ineffably, but always being with
the Father, and being known together with the Father. That the Scriptures
teach the Son co-eternal with the Father. "In the beginning," he says, "was
the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God; this one was in the
beginning with God." But he who was in the beginning, when was he not? For he
did not say that in the beginning he came to be, but in the beginning he was;
whatever we may contend in our reasonings to overstep the "was," we shall not
have strength to come beyond the beginning. All things are second to the one
who is in the beginning, both time, and age, and whatever other temporal
interval might be conceived. But if the Son was not always with God and
Father, but was added later at some time, there is necessity that time and age
mediate between Father and Son. But if someone should grant this, the thing
made will be found preexisting the maker; "For all things," says the
evangelist, "came to be through the Son, and without him came to be not even
one thing." But one of all things is the age or time; and the blessed Paul
too: "In the last of the days," he says, "he spoke to us in a Son, whom he
appointed heir of all things, through whom he also made the ages." But if the
ages are products of the Son, they do not preexist the one who made them; and
if ages do not exist, it is quite clear that neither does time, which days and
nights make and measure; but days and nights the rising and setting of light
produce; but the light came to be after the heaven, and the earth, and the
air; and all these things, and the things in them, God =75.1153= the Word by
the good pleasure of the Father created by a word. Therefore, times and ages
having been made together with all the other things by the Word, there would
be nothing between Father and Son, but always God is Father, and always the
Son is with the Father; wherefore also the evangelist cries, "In the beginning
was the Word." And the apostle Paul says: "Who being the radiance of the
glory, and impress of his hypostasis." And from another place: "Who existing
in the form of God, did not count it robbery to be equal with God." And
neither that one permits the "was" to be dropped, nor this one the "being" and
"existing"; since they proclaim the one who always is; for which reason also a
little later the evangelist says, "It was life, and the life was the light of
men." And

again: "It was the true light, which enlightens every man coming into the
world." And again: "The only-begotten Son, who is in the bosom of the Father."
And in the epistle, "That which was," he says, "from the beginning." Thus
those who from the beginning became eyewitnesses and servants of the Word were
taught by the divine Spirit to theologize the only-begotten Word of God;
wherefore they did not number the creator with the creation; they did not rank
the maker with the things made; nowhere did they call the precious offspring
of God a creature; nowhere did they couple "came to be" with the Godhead; but
this one, "In the beginning was the Word"; not in the beginning the Word came
to be. And that one, "As being the radiance of the glory, and impress of the
hypostasis"; not, he became radiance and impress. And again: "Who existing in
the form of God," not becoming form of God, but "Existing in God's form." And
elsewhere: "Who is image of the invisible God," he did not say, who became
image of the invisible God, but, who is.

Ζʹ. Proof from the Old that the Son is eternal. Thus everywhere theology is
coupled with the "was," and the "being," and the "existing," and the "is";
thus conversing with the great Moses he named himself: "I am the one who is."
And again: "Thus shall you say to the sons of Israel: The one who is has sent
me to you." And that these words belong to the Son, even the champions of
blasphemy themselves bear witness, saying that the Father is uncontainable,
but calling the Son mediator of the Father and of the creation, and asserting
that he both appeared and conversed with the patriarchs and the prophets; and
clearly God the Word himself teaches us through Jeremiah the prophet saying:
"In those days, and at that time I will make a new covenant with the house of
Israel, and with the house of Judah, not according to the covenant which I
made with their fathers in the days when I took them by the hand to lead them
out of Egypt." =75.1156= Let us inquire then who gave the new covenant. Or is
it not clear to all that the master Christ is the dispenser of this? For he
himself cries in the holy Gospels: "It was said to the ancients, You shall not
murder; but I say to you, Everyone who is angry with his brother without cause
shall be liable to the judgment; it was said to the ancients, You shall not
swear falsely; but I say to you, not to swear at all." And he sets the other
things likewise, that it was said thus, but I legislate this in this way, not
overturning the law that was laid down, but making the legislation more exact,
and teaching the manner of the keeping. The new covenant then the master
Christ has given us; but the one who furnished this also gave the old to
Israel after the deliverance from Egypt; and the one who gave that, and
delivered from Egyptian slavery, himself clearly sent Moses to Pharaoh; he
himself said: "Thus shall you say to the sons of Israel: The one who is has
sent me to you"; and this the prophet also makes clear elsewhere: "For this,"
he says, "is our God; another shall not be reckoned beside him; he found out
every way of knowledge, and gave it to Jacob his servant, and to Israel his
beloved; afterward he was seen upon earth, and consorted with men." Beginning
then from below, let us search out the prophetic meaning. Who is the one seen
upon the earth, and who consorted with men? It is quite clear, I think, to all
who have sense, that it is God the Word who took up our nature, who did not
count it robbery to be equal with God, but emptied himself, and took the form
of a slave. This one then gave the way of knowledge to Jacob his servant, and
to Israel his beloved, having declared the old law in the wilderness through
Moses. And the one who gave that law, before giving the law, conversing with
Moses said: "Thus shall you say to the sons of Israel. The one who is has sent
me to you." Wherefore the prophet cried: "This is our God, another shall not
be reckoned beside him"; witnessing to him not the lesser, but the
incomparable. Do you see how the prophetic

proclamations resemble the evangelical teachings. Moses proclaims the "who
is"; or rather God the Word himself says concerning himself the "who is"; and
Paul often says the "being," and also adds the "existing," which has the same
force as the "who is"; he also says the "is," equivalent to those in meaning;
and likewise also John the theologian in many places sows the "being" in the
work of the Gospels, and he also adorns the prologue itself with these voices,
not once, nor twice, nor thrice, but often proclaiming the "was."

Ηʹ. That some things fit God the Word, and some the nature taken up. The
heralds of the truth teaching such things, who is so exceedingly bold and
arrogant as instead of =75.1157= the "was" to say the "was not"? and instead
of the "being" and "existing," to call creature the one begotten from the
Father timelessly and without passion, but seated in the bosom of the Father?
for the one who is is not created, the one who exists is not fashioned; for
the "came to be," and the "received," and such things, they said not
theologizing, but proclaiming the economy, those entrusted with the mysteries
of the knowledge of God. And first the blessed John, and "The Word," he said,
"became flesh"; having said, "In the beginning was the Word," and having given
the "was" many times of the Godhead, when he came to the economy of the
incarnation, he necessarily set the "became"; for the firstfruits taken from
us by God the Word was not always, but toward the end of the ages it both came
to be and was taken up by God the Word. This the blessed Paul also does; for
having said, "Existing in the form of God," and having added, "he did not
count it robbery to be equal with God," he brought on "He emptied himself,
taking the form of a slave"; fitting the "taking" to the "form of the slave,"
but coupling the "existing" with the "form of God." Therefore the form of God
preexisting, or rather always existing, took the form of the slave; therefore
the Word of God is not a creature, nor a product, nor from things that are
not, but having been begotten from the Father he is always with the Father,
and receives from the well-disposed the worship with the Father.

Θʹ. Concerning the generation from God. And let no one hearing generation
receive in his reasonings the passions of our generation, cutting, and
flowing, and travail, and the other such things. For these are sufferings of
bodies; but God is bodiless, and impassible, and unchangeable, and
unalterable, always remaining the same; but if anyone thinks generation is not
free from passion, let him receive these reasonings also in the accounts
concerning creation; for as there there is cutting and flowing, so here there
is care, and toil, and sweat, and tools, and matter lying ready, and failures,
and the other things that accompany creatures; but if will alone sufficed God
for the creation of the all, and having willed he immediately showed the
things that were not as being, let the opponent also accept that the
generation of God is free from every passion. And as he did not create
similarly to men, so neither did he beget similarly.

Ιʹ. What is the meaning of the Lord's names. Wherefore also the Word is named
Son as having been begotten without passion, according to the word that has
come to be from the mind without passion. And he is called Son as having come
forth from the Father by generation =75.1160=. And he is addressed as God as
sharing the paternal nature, and being an unvarying image of God who begot.
And Only-begotten, as alone from alone, and begotten in a unique manner;
radiance of glory, as showing the Father in himself, and always being with the
one who begot, as the radiance with the light; impress of hypostasis, so that
you may believe God the Word to be not a bare energy, but a living hypostasis,
showing in itself the whole of the one who begot; but firstborn is not a name
of the divine nature, but of the economy

name; for how is it possible for God the Word to be both only-begotten and
firstborn? For the names are contrary; and the only-begotten signifies the one
alone begotten, but the firstborn the one born before others, and having
primacy over others by birth; but God the Word has no brother, for he is
only-begotten; how then is the one alone begotten from the Father firstborn?
Therefore it is quite clear that "firstborn" is a name of the economy. But if
anyone doubts, let him learn from Paul crying: "That those whom he foreknew,
he also predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son, that he might be
firstborn among many brothers." For of whom by nature are the believers
brothers? Not of God the Word, but of the humanity of like nature; for of this
they are also conformed. And from another place he said: "Who will transform
the body of our humiliation that it may become conformed to the body of his
glory." Therefore he himself is also firstborn who has the many brothers;
concerning whom he himself says in the Psalms: "I will declare your name to my
brothers." And we do not say that the only-begotten is one, and the firstborn
another, but the same, yet not according to the same. For he has been named
only-begotten according to the generation from above, but he has been called
firstborn as first having loosed the pangs of the generation that leads to
life. For this reason he is also named firstborn from the dead, as first
having risen, and having opened the gates of death; and firstborn of all
creation, as first born in the new creation, which he renewed by being born;
concerning which the blessed Paul says: "If anyone is in Christ, a new
creation, the old things have passed away, behold all things have become new."
But if those who prefer strife to persuasion should contend that firstborn of
all creation has been said concerning God the Word, we shall laugh at their
ignorance; yet we shall concede nonetheless, so that even thus we may show
from much that remains with us that the truth is; for he is called firstborn,
but not first-created of all creation; so that it is clear that he himself was
begotten before all creation, and nothing is older than the Son, but he was
always with the Father, and was before all creation; and the whole nature of
created things was later at some time, if it was brought by him to being. So
that nothing will remain from this for those who blaspheme.

ΙΑʹ. That no one knows the Son except the Father, and no one knows the Father,
except the Son. And that we may show the equality of Father and Son, let us
come to the teaching of the Lord himself: "No one," he says, "knows the Son
except the Father; nor does anyone know the Father except the Son, and he to
whom the Son may wish to reveal him." What is clearer than these words? Equal,
he says, is the knowledge to us; for I know the Father, being known by him;
and the Father knows me, being known by me. And all creation has been cast out
of our knowledge. For how is it possible for that which does not share our
nature to share the knowledge? Yet some partake of a certain slight
contemplation; for I reveal as far as possible to those I wish the knowledge
concerning the Father, as in a mirror and in a riddle. These things we have
learned from the Master's teachings; what place does the greater and lesser
have here? What creature knows the creator as it is known by the creator? And
what product receives equality toward the maker? Or do we not know how the
divine Scripture speaks concerning creation? Let us then recall the words of
the Prophet: "In the beginning you, Lord, founded the earth, and the heavens
are works of your hands. They shall perish, but you remain; and all shall grow
old as a garment, and as a covering you shall roll them up, and they shall be
changed; but you are the same, and your years shall not fail." And again: "Who
makes his angels spirits, and his ministers a flame of fire." This is the
difference of creation and creator. Where then there is equality, there is no
longer creature and creator, but Father and Son; wherefore he has also set
these

names, so that from the names we may learn the identity. "For no one," he
says, "knows the Son except the Father, nor does anyone know the Father except
the Son." By saying "no one," he indicated creation; and creation having been
cast out, shows the one who remains superior to created things, but naturally
joined to the one who begot; "No one knows the Son except the Father, nor does
anyone know the Father except the Son, and he to whom the Son may wish to
reveal him." Let the enemies of the truth say how one must read the divine
Scripture, whether to be content with the letter, or to search the meaning. If
then they should say the second, let them learn from what they say to do this
also in their own problems, and to seek the true interpretation of the
realities; but if they should say the letter suffices for exact teaching, from
the things set forth I shall refute their meaning; for the Son is found
unattainable, but the Father attainable; and the one inconceivable, but the
Father visible; for concerning himself the Master Christ having said that "No
one knows the Son except the Father," did not add, and he to whom the Father
may wish to reveal him; but having said, "Nor does anyone know the Father
except the Son," he immediately added, "And he to whom the Son may wish to
reveal him"; and he not only made him contemplable =75.1164= but also hung the
contemplation from his own authority. But even if they should wish ten
thousand times to be impious toward the Son, those to whom this is dear, we
shall not endure to admit a blasphemous thought concerning God and Father; for
we believe that the God of all is unseen and inconceivable; for how would the
incomprehensible Son be of one who can be grasped? Likewise then we say that
Father and Son are uncontainable and unattainable and incomprehensible; but we
believe that the knowledge is revealed through Father and Son to the seeing
faculty of the mind and to the contemplation of faith; "No one knows the Son
except the Father; nor does anyone know the Father except the Son"; and
elsewhere: "As," he says, "the Father knows me, I also know the Father." Not
the one more, the other less, but as I know, I am known.

ΙΒʹ. That the power of the Father and of the Son is equal. Of those then whose
knowledge is equal, of these the power also is equal; and of those whose power
is equal, of these clearly also the essence is one; and that the power of
Father and Son is equal, the Saviour himself again taught us saying thus:
"Those of my sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me, and I
give them eternal life, and they shall not perish forever, nor shall anyone
snatch them out of my hand; the Father, who has given them to me, is greater
than all, and no one is able to snatch out of the hand of my Father. I and the
Father are one." Do you see how he did not simply set "I and the Father are
one," foreseeing the heretical malice, lest they take this voice of purpose
and will; but having first established the equality of power, thus he brought
this on; for to the sheep, he says, who follow me I give eternal life, so that
none of them perish forever; for who is so great as to be able to snatch from
my hand those shepherded by me? for as it is impossible for anyone to prevail
over the right hand of the Father, who is greater than all, so it is
impossible for anyone of those guarded by me to be snatched; "For I and the
Father are one." Where then the greater and the lesser? For if likewise
neither from the hand of the Son, nor from the hand of the Father, can anyone
snatch, the greater has no place; and since the Father is greater than all, no
one can snatch from his hand; therefore the Son is greater than all, since
likewise no one can snatch from his hand; wherefore he added, "I and the
Father are one." For if we follow the letter again, we shall see the Son
placed first; "For I," he said, "and the Father," not the Father and I; and
having shown the duality of the persons, he proclaimed the identity of nature.
For by saying I and the Father, he indicated the number

of the hypostases; but by bringing on, "we are one," he declared the unvarying
of the power; =75.1165= of those then whose knowledge is equal, and the power,
and the will, of these clearly also the nature is one, even if those who speak
ill should be shameless.

ΙΓʹ. That from many places one can learn the equality of Father and Son. And
that [the Son] can do the same things as the Father, one can learn from
another place; for at one time, "My Father," he says, "works until now, and I
work"; and at another: "As the Father raises the dead and makes alive, so also
the Son makes alive whom he will." He said "whom he will," not whom he is
commanded; whom he wishes, not whom he is ordered; for the one is proper to
slavery, the other to mastery. And again from another place: "If I do not do
the works of my Father, do not believe me; but if I do, even if you do not
wish to believe me, believe the works, and know that the Father is in me, and
I in him." Do you see again from here also the equality of Father and Son;
"For the Father," he says, "is in me, and I in him." But in the case of the
greater and the lesser this cannot be found; I mean something of this sort:
All creation together, seen and unseen, the God of all circumscribes in
himself, or rather holds in his hand; "For in his hand," he says, "are the
ends of the earth," And again: "Who holds the circle of the earth, and those
who dwell in it as locusts." And elsewhere: "Who has measured the water with
his hand, and the heaven with a span, and all the earth with a handful?" All
creation then, as I said, the maker of all holds in his hand; but for him to
be encompassed by creation is impossible; therefore it is impossible for
unequals to be contained by one another; but if this is true, as it also is
true, and the Son contains the Father, as the Father the Son, it is quite
clear that the account of inequality has been cast out, and equality is
recognized in Father and Son.

ΙΔʹ. Proof that the Son is equal in honor to the Father. And this again the
Master Christ himself teaches us elsewhere saying: "I am the door. No one
comes to the Father except through me." And from another place: "No one comes
to me the Son, unless my heavenly Father draw him." And we are taught from
this that likewise both the Son leads those being saved to the Father, and the
Father to the Son. Where then is the slavish liturgy of the heretics? where
the ministry fitting creation? where does it show inequality of the paternal
mastery and [of the Son] slavery [cod. but equality]? For we hear that now the
Son guides toward the Father those who desire salvation; and now the Father
does this, and leads to the Son the nurslings of the faith.

ΙΕʹ. That the nature of Father and Son is one. And we learn from this the
equal honor of Father and Son; and this one can learn from another place also,
hearing the Lord himself conversing with the Jews who hear; for having gone
through many things, he later added: "Even if I =75.1168= bear witness
concerning myself, my witness is true, because I am not alone, but I and the
Father who sent me; and in your law it is written that the witness of two men
is true. I am the one who bears witness concerning myself, and the Father who
sent me bears witness concerning me. The Jews then said to him: "Where is your
Father? Jesus answered them: You know neither me nor my Father; if you knew
me, you would know my Father also"; oh how great is the madness of the
heretics! how great a thunderstruck condition do the heirs of the blasphemy of
Arius and Eunomius suffer! besides the madness, one can see much shamelessness
blooming in them; for they mention the sending up and down, and they say the
one who sent is more honorable than the one sent; so great ignorance of the
Scriptures they have; and they do not consider that Jacob, having been sent by
Isaac into

Mesopotamia, is not for this reason less than the one who sent according to
the account of nature; and likewise also Joseph was sent by Jacob to visit his
brothers, and no one would say he does not share the nature of the father,
because obeying the father he accepted the sending. But they say perhaps: If
not according to the account of nature the senders here are greater than those
sent, at least according to paternal dignity they have the primacy of honor.
But we find, O foolish ones, I would say to them, even the greater sent by the
lesser, and we shall not for this reason strip those sent of their own honor;
for Jonathan was sent by David, the king's son by the fugitive, the one
co-reigning with the father by the one who did not dare even to appear in the
rank of a household servant; since then the one sent, and the other was sent,
no longer according to your account is the one sent a king, no longer is the
one who sent a fugitive; but the dignity of the one sent has passed over to
David who sent; and the misfortune of the sender has passed over to Jonathan
who was sent [cod. who sent]; but it is not so. And why must one speak of
human things? For we find God sent, and a man sending; for the one who
wrestled with Jacob says: "Send me away; for the dawn has risen; to whom Jacob
said: I will not send you away unless you bless me; what have the wise
technicians of the faith to say to these things? And otherwise also from the
Master's words we shall find even the Father who sent being with the Son who
was sent; "For I am not," he says, "alone, but I and the Father who sent me";
and elsewhere: "My Father has not left me alone"; and from another place: "My
Father who remains in me, he does the works." If then the one who sent was in
him and with him, where is the cheapness of the one sent? and the one who
fills all things, where and from where was he sent? for sending hints at
change of place; but if Father and Son encompass all things, neither did the
Father, as being far from those =75.1169= to whom he sent, send the Son, nor
did the Son pass from one place to another; therefore it remains to understand
that the sending is of the humanity taken up. But it is time to run back to
the interpretation of the Master's words. "In your law," he says, "it is
written that the witness of two men is true"; then he adds that "I am the one
who bears witness concerning myself, and the Father who sent me bears witness
concerning me." Looking then at the image, let us understand the archetype;
"The witness of two men," he says, "is true"; and of the two men the nature is
one, as would clearly be confessed by all; therefore of Father and Son the
essence is one, one [ita one twice] will be understood from the image and
confessed; for as there two men, so also here God and God, Father and Son,
declaring even by the names themselves the identity of nature; for neither
would the truly God be of another nature besides the one who is truly God; nor
is the Son other besides that one, being Son of God. Hearing these things the
Jews asked him: "Where is your Father?" Jesus then answered: "You know neither
me nor my Father; if you knew me, you would know my Father also." Do you see
again how from this the homoousion is shown; For if you knew me, he says, you
would know my Father also; but from the heteroousion the heteroousion is not
recognized; for things of another tribe and foreign are not indicative of one
another; but things that share nature are known through one another; through
one man one can see the whole nature of men; and through one sheep the whole
race of sheep; yet not through sheep the lions, nor through lions the sheep,
nor angels through men, nor through angels men; for each living thing is
indicative of its own nature. If then the only-begotten Word of God is a
creature, and from things that are not, and has been begotten from some other
nature, how does he show the Father in himself when believed? but if the
Father is known through the Son, and he who

knows the Son also knows the Father, let every blasphemous tongue be bound,
and according to the prophet be glued to the throat; but we the worshippers of
the Trinity receive from this exact knowledge of the homoousion; considering
that the Father would not otherwise have been known in the Son, unless he were
of the same essence; and we worship our Saviour, awaiting the fruit of the
worship, of which the Father himself is dispenser according to the voice of
the Lord; "For if anyone," he says, "serve me, my Father will honor him"; and
the marvelous John who theologizes, "He who believes in the Son," he says,
"has eternal life; but he who disobeys the Son shall not see life, but the
wrath of God remains upon him."

Ιςʹ. That in many places the Lord taught that his essence and the Father's is
one. And that we may show the homoousion from another witness also, let us
hear the Lord himself crying and saying, not to the apostles only, but also to
the Jews: "He who believes in me does not believe in me, but in the one who
sent me. And he who sees me sees the one who sent me; I have come as light
into the world, that he who believes in me may not remain in the darkness." If
then he who sees the Son believes in the Father, where is the greater and the
lesser? for in the lesser the greater is diminished, and is not recognized;
but if the Father is greater, how is he seen in the Son? but if he is seen in
the Son, he is recognized as in an equal, clearly; for equals are indicative
of one another; and again a little later the Lord himself says to the
apostles: "I am the way, and the truth, and the life; no one comes to the
Father except through me; if you had known me, you would have known my Father
also, and from now you know him and have seen him. And Philip says to him:
Lord, show us your Father, and it is enough for us. Jesus says to him: So long
a time I am with you, and you have not known me, Philip? he who has seen me
has seen my Father also; and how do you say, Show us the Father? do you not
believe that I am in the Father, and the Father is in me? The words that I
speak, I do not speak from myself; but the Father who remains in me, he does
the works; believe me that I am in the Father, and the Father in me; but if
not, believe me because of the works themselves." What is clearer than these
words? what more manifest than this teaching? But, as it seems, the veil of
the Jews has passed over to the mind of the heretics; wherefore they do not
wish to see together things clearer than the sun, having drawn upon themselves
the self-chosen mist of ignorance; but let us hear the Lord saying: "If you
had known me, you would have known my Father also; and from now you know him,
and have seen him"; he says this to Thomas who had said to him, We do not know
where you are going, and how can we know the way? and he teaches him, and the
remaining apostles, that he who has believed in him, having seen him with the
eyes of the mind, has also become a seer of the Father, as the Father being
recognized in him. Philip not understanding these things, beseeches him
saying: "Show us your Father, and it is enough for us"; and he is not praised,
as having desired to see the greater according to the heretics; but he is
blamed, as not having seen the Father in the Son; "For so long a time," he
says, "I am with you, and you have not known me, Philip?" And yet it was not
him, but the Father that Philip desired to see; how then is he blamed as not
having known the Son? by the addition of the charge he makes the cause clear;
"For he who has seen me," he says, =75.1173= "has seen the Father." And how do
you say, Show us the Father? I am another, he says, according to the person,
not according to the nature; I carry the whole Father in him; for I am an
unvarying seal of the one who begot, I am impress of the Father's hypostasis,
a natural image coexisting with the one who begot; when then you wish to see
that one, looking at me, you will see both; but you will see not with the eyes
of the body, but with those of faith; and with those of faith so much,

as to know the energies, not the nature, or the essence; for the grasping of
this surpasses every mind; wherefore he added: "Do you not believe that I am
in the Father, and the Father is in me? the words that I speak, I do not speak
from myself, but my Father who remains in me, he does the works. Believe me
that I am in the Father, and the Father in me; but if not, believe because of
the works themselves." If then he utters the same things as the Father, and
the Father remains in him and he in the Father, and he who has seen him has
seen the Father, and he who knows him knows the Father, it is clear to all who
have sense that the nature of the Father and of the Son is one, and the Son
has all the things of the Father; for he would not otherwise have shown the
Father in himself, unless he had all things that the Father has, except the
fatherhood itself; for this is proper to the Father, as sonship is of the Son.

ΙΖʹ. Another proof that the Son is equal to the Father. This equality one can
learn from another place also. For Jesus said, he says: "Now the Son of man
has been glorified, and God has been glorified in him, and God will glorify
him in himself, and will immediately glorify him." And again: "Father, the
hour has come, glorify your Son, that your Son also may glorify you." O the
exceeding folly of the heretics! Greater, he says, is the one who glorifies
than the one glorified; and the Father glorifies, but the Son is glorified;
therefore then the Father is greater than the Son. If then the Son was not
only glorified, but also glorified the one who glorified, what place would the
greater and the lesser have? unless perhaps according to the sequence of the
things said the greater passes over to the one who glorifies, and thus the Son
will be found lesser when he is glorified, but greater when he glorifies; but
to say these things is of madness and frenzy in the extreme; for we are not
taught from this the greater and the lesser, but the equal of Father and Son;
for we hear that both the Father glorifies the Son, and is glorified by the
Son; and the Son is glorified by the Father, and glorifies the Father; because
the one glorified has not received what he did not have, but what he had; in
the same passage he teaches this; "For glorify me," he says, "beside yourself,
you, Father, with the glory which I had, before the world was, beside you."
But if even before the world came to be he had this glory, how does he ask to
receive what he always had?

ΙΗʹ. That the mastery of Father and Son is one. Then showing that he is not
only glorified, but also glorifies, he added: "I have manifested your name to
men." And a little later stopping the mouths of the heretics: "The things that
are mine," he says, "are all yours, and the things that are yours are mine";
not dividing the common mastery, nor wishing to show that other things belong
to the Father; but since those who send forth every blasphemous voice against
the Only-begotten say that he receives, but the Father gives, he shows that he
has the same mastery of all things as the Father; "For the things that are
mine," he says, "are all yours, and the things that are yours are mine";
teaching not the division of the mastery, but the common of the mastery. But I
have stretched the word concerning faith to length, having promised in the
prooemium to go through a definition in a summary; for having wished to show
from the evangelical teaching the dignity of the Only-begotten, I have made
the word longer than the promise; and this though I have been careful of
brevity in the interpretations. Sending then the pious to the evangelical and
prophetic books themselves (for these are full of the theology of the Son), I
shall proceed to the sequence set before us.

ΙΘʹ. Concerning the Holy Spirit. We believe then, as I said, in God Father
without beginning, and in God co-eternal Son by nature, begotten from the
Father, but always being with the Father, according to the voice of the
Gospels; "For in the beginning," he says, "was the Word." And we believe also
in the Holy Spirit, the upright, the princely, the

good, the paraclete, the one who has proceeded from God, not having been
begotten; for there is one Only-begotten; nor indeed having been created; for
nowhere do we find in the divine Scripture numbered with the creation, but
ranked with Father and Son; and we have heard that it proceeds from the
Father, and we do not busy ourselves how it proceeds, but we are content with
the bounds set for us by the theologians and blessed men.

Κʹ. That the Spirit is ranked with Father and Son. For we have been taught by
our Saviour Jesus Christ himself that the Holy Spirit is completive of the
Trinity; "For going," he says, "make disciples of all the nations, baptizing
them into the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit"; and
being witnessed together with the Father and Son, it is superior to all
creation. Wherefore also the blessed Paul continues proclaiming it with Father
and Son; "For the grace," he says, "of our Lord =75.1177= Jesus, and the love
of God and Father, and the communion of the Holy Spirit be with you all."

ΚΑʹ. Proof of the mastery of the Spirit. And again: "And there are divisions
of gifts, but the same Spirit; and there are divisions of ministries, but the
same Lord; and there are divisions of workings, but it is the same God who
works all things in all." And proclaiming its authority he cried: "But all
these things the same and the one Spirit works, dividing privately to each, as
it wills it distributes the gifts to those who believe." For through it we
received deliverance from sins; through it we obtain freedom; through it we
enjoy the gift of adoption; "For we did not receive a spirit," says Paul, "of
slavery again unto fear, but we received a Spirit of adoption, in which we
cry, Abba Father." And elsewhere: "For the law of the Spirit of life has freed
me from the law of sin and of death." And from another place: "And the Lord is
the Spirit; and where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is freedom"; but that
which frees others does not serve; for how would it give to fellow-slaves
things of which it does not partake, which it has no strength to obtain, which
it wishes to enjoy, as is likely, but cannot? But if it gives freedom to those
who believe, and frees those who serve, it clearly does not serve, but
masters, and in a masterly way grants freedom to whom it wishes; wherefore
also the blessed Paul said that "All these things the one and the same Spirit
works, dividing privately to each as it wills." Wherefore also the prophet in
the Old crying proclaimed its authority: "The Lord has sent me, and his
Spirit." And God accusing the Jews: "They made," he says, "counsel, and not
through me, and covenants not through my Spirit"; showing the Holy Spirit
partner of the mastery. And from another place: "Wherefore," he says, "I am
with you, and my Spirit has stood in the midst of you."

ΚΒʹ. That the Paraclete is creator. And Job believing it to be creator and
masterly, but not ministerial, nor a product: "Divine spirit," he says, "that
made me, and breath of the Almighty that teaches me." But if it created human
nature, it has the same essence with Father and Son; for in the making of man
God said: "Let us make man according to our image, and according to likeness."
And of those whose image is one, of these clearly also the essence is one.

ΚΓʹ. That the Holy Spirit is from God. And that the Holy Spirit is of divine
essence, =75.1180= God himself teaches us through the prophet Joel saying: "In
the last days, I will pour out from my Spirit upon all flesh." And the master
Christ teaches us saying to the disciples: "But when they deliver you up, do
not

be anxious how or what you will speak; for it is not you who speak, but the
Spirit of your Father that speaks in you"; and again Paul: "But you are not in
flesh, but in Spirit; if indeed the Spirit of God dwells in you." And a little
later: "For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are sons of God."
And elsewhere: "But to us," he says, "God has revealed through his Spirit; for
the Spirit searches all things even the depths of God"; for who of men knows
the things of the man, except the spirit of the man that is in him? so also
the things of God no one knows, except the Spirit that is from God." From
which it is quite clear that the Holy Spirit is not of another tribe, nor
heteroousion, but of the divine nature; wherefore it also knows the depths of
God, and understands the things of God, as our soul its own. But if anyone
thinks the searching is ignorance, he will find this also lying upon the
Father; "For he who searches," he says, "the hearts, knows what is the mind of
the Spirit." But if the God of all searches not in ignorance, but clearly
understands all things before they come to be, the Holy Spirit of God also
does not search the depths of God through ignorance; for how would it fit the
one who is ignorant, that as the spirit of the man knows the things in him, so
also the things of God no one knows, except the Spirit of God? for knowledge
is contrary to searching; but the soul does not search its own things, but
understands exactly; therefore the Holy Spirit has exact knowledge of God; and
as no one knows the Father except the Son, nor the Son except the Father, so,
he says, no one knows the things of God, except the Spirit of God. And from
the things said we are taught the common of the nature. And since those who
suffer from every shamelessness, the disciples, I mean, of the blasphemy of
Arius and Eunomius, say that the Spirit of God is God himself, the blessed
Paul necessarily shows the person of the Spirit; "For we," he says, "did not
receive the spirit of the world, but the Spirit from God; that we may see the
things granted us by God, which we also speak, not in words taught of human
wisdom, but in those taught of the Holy Spirit, comparing spiritual things
with spiritual; but the soulish man does not receive the things of the Spirit
of God; for it is folly to him, and he cannot know, because it is examined
spiritually; for who has known the mind =75.1181= of the Lord, who will
instruct him? But we have the mind of Christ." Therefore by saying that "We
did not receive the spirit of the world, but the Spirit from God," he taught
that the Holy Spirit is not of the same race as the world, but of the divine
essence. And besides this he taught that he does not have the Word concerning
God and Father, but concerning the Holy Spirit, whose grace those who believe
receive. Wherefore he said the Spirit from God, teaching that it has existence
from the Father, and is of that nature, not indeed generatively, but as he
alone knows who understands the Son, and who alone knows the Father, and the
one that alone understands Father and Son; which we have learned is from God,
but the manner we were not taught. And we are content with the given measures
of knowledge, and we do not busy ourselves ignorantly with the unattainable.

ΚΔʹ. That the great Apostle knows the Holy Spirit to be God. And that the Holy
Spirit is of God, Paul again teaches us; "You were washed," saying, "and you
were sanctified and you were justified in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ,
and in the Spirit of our God." For why are we addressed as temples of God
receiving through baptism the grace of the Spirit, unless the Holy Spirit is
God? and that the faithful are addressed as temples of the Spirit, the same
Apostle teaches us saying: "Do you not know that your bodies are a temple of
the Holy Spirit in you, which you have from God, and you are not your own? for
you were bought with a price." And the temple proclaims the God who dwells;
wherefore also above he said: "Do you not know that you are a temple of God,
and the Spirit of God dwells in you? If anyone

destroys the temple of God, God will destroy this one; for the temple of God
is holy, which you are." If then those who believe receive the grace of the
Spirit through baptism, and being counted worthy of this gift, we are styled a
temple of God, therefore the Holy Spirit is God. For this reason, if those who
enjoy the grace of the Spirit are and are called indwelling and temple of God,
it is clear that the Holy Spirit is of the divine nature, and homoousion with
Father and Son. But if it is a creature, and is of another essence, those who
obtain its gift would not reasonably be addressed as temples of God; but if
those who enjoy a certain partial and slight grace from it are named temples
of God, from the appellation we shall understand the kinship. This also the
crown of the apostles teaches in the Acts, refuting the theft of Ananias.

ΚΕʹ. That the divine Peter also receives things in accord concerning the
Spirit. "Ananias," for, he says, "why has Satan deceived =75.1184= your heart
to lie to the Holy Spirit, and for you to keep back from the price of the
field?" And a little later: "You have not lied to men, but to God." For since
he thought to escape notice as men, the apostles, having taken away of the
price of the property what he wished, the chief of the apostles teaches him
that those who have the grace of the Spirit know clearly all things that
happen in secret. For not us, he says, did you lie to, but the Holy Spirit; do
not then think you have deceived men; for it is God who was lied to by you;
for you have not lied to men, but to God; for not we, he says, were cheated by
you, but the Holy Spirit, which is God, having existence from God, and being
of that nature. This Luke also makes clear in the Acts; for having said above
that while the brothers in Antioch were ministering to the Lord and fasting,
the Holy Spirit said:

Κςʹ. Proof from more that the Holy Spirit is God. "Set apart for me Paul
indeed and Barnabas for the work to which I have called them." And having
added that having been sent out by the Holy Spirit they went down to Seleucia,
and having related how having gone about Cyprus and Lycia, and Lycaonia, and
Pamphylia, and Bithynia, they proclaimed the Gospel, he brings on: "And from
there having sailed away to Antioch those about Barnabas and Paul, from where
they had been delivered to the grace of God for the work which they fulfilled;
and having arrived, and having gathered the Church, they reported how many
things God did with them." Having mentioned from above the Spirit, as having
set apart Paul and Barnabas for the work to which it had called them, this
after the return he names; and twice he sets the appellation of God; first
saying that they sailed away to Antioch, from where they had been delivered to
the grace of God for the work which they fulfilled; and second that, having
gathered the Church, they reported how many things God did with them. And yet
it was the Holy Spirit that worked wonders, that gave wisdom, that furnished
understanding, that strengthened the proclaimers, that inspired in them the
word of teaching; wherefore Paul also said: "For to one is given through the
Spirit a word of wisdom, and to another a word of knowledge according to the
same Spirit, and to another faith in the same Spirit, and to another gifts of
healings in the same Spirit," and the rest. Then teaching that the Holy Spirit
does not distribute these things as ministering, but in a masterly way grants
to whom it wishes, he added: "But all these things the one and the same Spirit
works, dividing privately to each as it wills." If then the Holy Spirit worked
these things through the apostles as it wished, and those about Paul and
Barnabas having gathered the Church reported how many things God did with
them, therefore

the Holy Spirit is God according to the voice of the apostles. =75.1185= This
having happened also in Jerusalem the most divine Luke relates. "For all the
multitude," he says, "was silent, and heard Barnabas and Paul relating how
many signs God did through them, and wonders among the nations." Therefore
then the Holy Spirit is God; for the same works the signs and the wonders.
Wherefore also the Lord in the Gospels said: "But if I by the Spirit of God
cast out the demons, then the kingdom of God has come upon you." And again in
the Acts Luke says that Paul having chosen Silas went out, having been
delivered by the brothers to the grace of God; again having called the Holy
Spirit God, which through the brothers in Antioch set him apart for the work
to which it called him. Again the blessed Paul in the letter to the
Corinthians says: "Whom God set in the Church, some prophets and apostles, and
some pastors and teachers and evangelists, for the equipping of the saints";
but in Miletus taking leave of the brothers, and reminding of the grace given:
"Take heed then to yourselves," he says, "and to all the flock in which the
Holy Spirit set you as overseers to shepherd the Church of the Lord, which he
purchased through his own blood." Do you see again here also the theology of
the Spirit. For there having mentioned God as ordaining pastors, and teachers,
and evangelists, here he says concerning the Spirit: In which the Holy Spirit
set you as overseers to shepherd the Church; teaching that the Holy Spirit is
God, and that it is the same to say God, and Spirit, because of the communion
of nature; for with the things done by God and Father the Son and the Holy
Spirit cooperate; and with the things accomplished by the Son and the Spirit
God and Father likewise consents. And from another place again the blessed
Paul proclaims the Spirit to be God; "For if," he says, "all prophesy, and
some unbeliever or unlearned enter, he is convicted by all, he is examined by
all, the hidden things of his heart become manifest; and thus falling upon his
face he will worship God, announcing that truly God is in you." For since
prophecy is a gift of the Spirit, and through the revelation of the Spirit the
hidden things of the heart become manifest, and it is proper to God to
understand the mind of men, necessarily the one convicted through the
prophecy, being examined will worship God, announcing that truly God is in
you; and those had the grace of the Spirit. But if God was in them, since they
enjoyed the grace of the Spirit, therefore the Holy Spirit is both God and
from God. =75.1188=

ΚΖʹ. That the Holy Spirit is from God uncreatedly, wherefore it is also called
eternal. And the most divine Peter also in the Catholic Epistles: "If you are
reproached," he says, "in the name of Christ, you are blessed, because the
Spirit of glory and of power and of God rests upon you." And the blessed John
in the Epistle: "In this," he says, "we know that we shall remain in him, and
he in us, because he has given us from his Spirit." And that which is
proclaimed from God is not a creature, but existing from the divine essence;
wherefore the blessed Paul also calls it eternal as not having taken a
beginning of being; "For if blood," he says, "of goats and bulls, and ashes of
a heifer sprinkling those who have been defiled sanctifies toward perfection,
how much more the blood of Christ, who through Holy Spirit offered himself?"
If then the Holy Spirit is eternal, and God is eternal, the conclusion is
clear; but let us leave to the industrious to collect all the testimonies
concerning the Holy Spirit, those that proclaim it both God and Lord, and say
it is ranked with Father and Son; but let us hasten to the end of the teaching
set before us.

ΚΗʹ. Recapitulation of the faith. Therefore we believe the nature of the
Trinity to be one, one essence recognized in three properties, the authority
undivided, the kingdom indivisible, one godhead and lordship. For thus both a
monad is shown in the sameness of the essence, and the Trinity is recognized
not in bare names, but in the hypostases. For we do not say one thing with
three names according to the contraction and confusion of Sabellius and
Photinus and Marcellus; nor three of another tribe and heteroousion, unequal
and unlike, set above one another, measured and weighed by mind and tongue,
according to the division and alienation and impious busyness of Arius; but
three hypostases, and one nature of the Trinity, bodiless, unchangeable,
unalterable, endless, immortal, infinite, incorruptible, uncircumscribed,
unencompassed, unseen, unclear, ineffable, unutterable, incomprehensible,
unattainable, inconceivable, self-life, intelligible light, fountain of good
things, treasury of wisdom, creator of all, governing all things, wisdom
steering the ship of creation. This faith we hold; for this we have been
taught by the theologian men. And to those who converse from syllogisms, let
us say: That is your portion, that is your lot, but our portion is the Lord,
whom following, we shall not leave the right =75.1189= road; for we have also
the divine Scripture teaching; wherefore we cry reasonably: "Your law is a
lamp to my feet, and a light to my paths." Illumined by this light, let us
recognize the tracks of the fathers who have gone before, let us follow those
until we all arrive at the resurrection of the dead in Christ Jesus the Lord,
to whom be the glory unto the ages. Amen.
