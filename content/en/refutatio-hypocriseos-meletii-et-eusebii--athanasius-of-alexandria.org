* Refutation of the Hypocrisy of Meletius and Eusebius
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of our father among the saints Athanasius: Refutation of the hypocrisy of
those around Meletius, and of Eusebius of Samosata, against the
consubstantial.  Man has come to be according to the likeness of God, and
man is not God; for that which is like something is not that very thing to
which it is likened. As also, the kingdom of heaven is like a net and
seed, and the kingdom of heaven is none of these; and, he who has been
discipled to the kingdom of heaven is like a man who is a householder, but
this one is not a householder; and many other such things. And there are
also likenesses among bodies: a wolf to a dog, but it is not a dog; and
tin to silver, but it is not silver; bronze to gold, but it is not gold;
and scarlet to blood, but it is not blood; and gypsum to snow, but it is
not snow; and other various such things are observed. The Son therefore,
being God, is not like God, but one, and this; as it is written: «I and
the Father are one.» And he interpreted the unity as divinity; but a
divinity not such as that of those who have been made gods; inasmuch as
he sets it over against that one, as the saying of the Lord has it; for
he is God, not as man who has come to be according to the likeness of
God; and is named God by the likeness, not by nature; having an essence
likened to God; and indeed also advancing in the likeness; according to
which nature already, «We are children of God,» and «If he is manifested,
we shall be like him.» But where the essence is the same, essence does
not advance into the more and the less, but man indeed =28.88= is thus,
while the Lord is the same always; and not at one time lesser, at another
greater, as God: «Jesus,» he says, «yesterday and today the same and unto
the ages.» He therefore who does away with the Son being consubstantial
with the Father, but says that he is like, does away with his being God.
And likewise also he who explains the consubstantial as like in essence
says that the essence is other, but likened to God. Therefore he does not
even fittingly say that he is from the essence, not thinking
consubstantial, as a man is from the essence of man. But if the Son is
not from God as a man from a man according to essence, but as in a
likeness, just as a statue to a man, or as a man to God; it is clear that
such a one, while saying consubstantial, does not think consubstantial;
for he does not wish the consubstantial to be heard according to custom,
which is, of one and the same essence, but contrary to custom; and in
order to slander this, that they have spoken a Hellenic expression, the
word consubstantial of the custom among the Greeks being laid down for
nothing else than to present the same nature; which one must believe of
Father, and Son, and Holy Spirit. At once then this man turns aside the
consubstantial, and names that of the essence spuriously; thinking it as
a work, not as an offspring, and for this reason he also adds
impassibly, persuasively; not in order to take away the slander of
passion; for who even speaks of a passion of God? but in order that he
may say he has come forth made from God, and not begotten from God; as
though such a procession were passible; just as those are accustomed to
say who reject the procession from the essence as a passion, but
establish the making from the essence which they speak of as impassible.
Just as even if making, if it were taken humanly, has motion, and the
toil from the motion; which things are alien to God creating;
nevertheless then, thinking the procession of the Son through such an
explanation as an impassible generation, they alienate the Spirit from
Father and Son; because the procession of this one is not by generation,
but is likened to a breathing through the participation, as of a breath
coming from the mouth and sanctifying the one who participates, just as
indeed it is called of spirit. But it was impossible for the Spirit to be
glorified in the glory of the Trinity, if it is not processively from God
through the Son, but has come to be from God productively, as they say.
For a work is not glorified together with the maker, nor a slave with the
master; nor would the participation of an essence alien to God be of God;
«For in this,» says John, «we know that he is in us, from the Spirit
which he has given us.»
