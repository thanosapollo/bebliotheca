332 But not having understood a difference 333 of visible Judaism and
intelligible Judaism, that is, of "Judaism that is outward" and of "Judaism
that is in secret," those from the godless and most impious heresies
immediately stood apart from Judaism and from the God who gave these
scriptures and they fabricated another god besides the God who has given "the
law and the prophets," besides "the one who made heaven and earth." But it is
not so; rather, the one who has given the law has also given the gospel; the
one who made "the things that are seen" also prepared "the things that are not
seen." And "the things that are seen" have kinship with "the things that are
not seen," and they have kinship in such a way that "the invisible things of
God from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the
things that are made." And "the things that are seen" "of the law and the
prophets" also have kinship toward "the things that are not seen," 334 but are
understood "of the law and the prophets." Since therefore Scripture itself
also has been composed, as it were, of a body which is the thing that is seen,
and of a soul which is understood and grasped in it, and of a spirit according
to "the patterns and shadow of the heavenly things"; come, having called upon
the one who made for Scripture a body and a soul and a spirit, a body for
those before us, a soul for us, and a spirit for those "who in the age to come
shall inherit eternal life" and are going to come upon the heavenly and true
things of the law, let us search not the letter, but the soul for the present;
and if we are able, we shall also ascend to the spirit according to the word
concerning the sacrifices that have been read. 395 For the calling [sc. of a
woman] is of the age of one who is no longer a child, as also that of the man,
even if she does not have the act in sowing. Wherefore Paul also says: "God
sent forth his own son, become of a woman." And Abraham did not say to the
servant sent to Bethuel: "you shall take" a virgin, but: "a wife for my son."
402 For as in the wounds of the body after the healing the scar is a trace of
the part that suffered, so in a soul that has sinned, even if it be healed,
there remains a kind of scar, observed by God and by those who have received
from God to see these things. Hence one must strive to make every trace of the
wound disappear, bringing to it a perfect healing, concerning which wounds
Isaiah says: "from the feet to the head neither wound nor bruise nor festering
blow." And that emollients, as it were, are also applied to some, not reaching
to those who are exceedingly sinful, he shows through: "there is no emollient
to put on, nor oil, nor bandages." And concerning "crushing" and a "painful
blow" of a soul and one "healed with pain in a stern discipline," until it be
wholly healed and the wounds scar over, Jeremiah says: "thus said 403 the
Lord: I have raised up a crushing, painful is your blow; there is none judging
your judgment, you have been healed unto pain" and the following until: "from
a painful blow I will bring you up, says the Lord"; instead of which Aquila
and Symmachus rendered that "I will bring up a cicatrization for you." And
again elsewhere Jeremiah also says clearly according to the Seventy: "behold I
bring up for her a cicatrization and a healing, I will heal them and I will
show them peace and faith; and I will turn back the captivity of Judah and the
captivity of Jerusalem." Therefore after a scar, either one that does not have
so much of the =404.20= things previously sinned as to be a scar, but some
indication, or not even a clear indication, but the so-called "bright," "there
comes to be" at times "in the skin of the flesh a plague of leprosy,"
concerning which the things that follow are legislated. "And if the leprosy
cover all the skin of the plague from head to feet." One of the ancients took
"living flesh" as the word. When this is not present to the soul, whatever may
come to be, the one who sins is without blame, bearing an irrational
condition; but if reason be present, he is liable. What then is "the plague
coming to be throughout the whole body"? Perhaps it indicates figuratively
phrenitides or melancholies? For when reason is not then helping nor 405
sensation being present, every thing done or said is not a sin, but when
sensation is present. "And flesh, if there come to be in his skin an ulcer."
This is a third law concerning leprosy, "if an ulcer be healed and in the
place a white scar be found." For as in a body there are many causes of
diseases, so also in a soul; and when the wounds have ceased, a "white scar"
and leprous is found "or reddish," so that such a condition is "lower" than
the rest of the soul. "A burn of fire." Perhaps a burn is in everyone who has
406 received "a fiery dart of the evil one"? For this one loves flesh and
blood, a woman or a child. Such also are those raging after glory and silver;
for a passionate soul is kindled upon such things. And even if, having been
released from these, it be healed and not so even out the place of the passion
as it was before the passion, but be according to this "lower," it seems
defiled to the law. "In the head or in the beard." See if "leprosy of the
head" can be taken, when instead of having "Christ as head," someone inscribes
some other word as a leprous and unclean head. But also if someone already
"having become a man" and seeming "to have put away the things of the infant"
should sin, not as an "infant," but a sin of a man, perhaps he has "leprosy
upon the beard." And a woman too, a soul that is not sending forth rational
seeds, but is receptive, might at some time have upon the face 407 and "the
beard" in her that which the law named, distinctly beside the former things,
also a "break." 409 And the "two living birds" seem to bear some analogy
toward the two goats of which the one is "of the scapegoat." For there too the
one is sacrificed and the one is sent "unto his dismissal." And both are
clean, just as also the birds now; for not even the one who through his own
wickedness became the scapegoat has been created unclean by nature, they say.
Therefore because of the leprosy from sin the one being cleansed owes
something to the scapegoat, even if he has fled the uncleanness from the
leprosy; but because of the purity the other of the birds is offered
concerning him. However the two appear to be different in kind from those
offered unto the altar, "turtledoves or doves." For the one newly cleansed has
not yet become worthy of the altar, but in order that he may become worthy,
these are taken concerning him, "on whatever day he be cleansed," no
postponement coming to be after the day of the cleansing. And he must also be
cleansed with 410 "cedar wood," which is a symbol of the saving wood, in which
the Savior "triumphed, having stripped" us of "the principalities and the
powers." And the "scarlet" is a symbol of the precious blood through the
color. Such also is that which was "bound" in the birth of Perez and the
"sign" shown from Rahab the harlot to the spies. And "the hyssop" is
cleansing. And the priest himself does not slaughter the bird; for he is not
yet worthy of a priest "on the day of being cleansed"; whence neither is the
blood offered to the altar; but an "earthen vessel of living water" 411
receives this, the water having been put in beforehand, in order that the
cleansing may come to be "with water and blood," which "came out from the
side" of the Savior according to John, who also says in the epistle that the
things that cleanse are "spirit and water and blood"; and here too "blood and
water and the spirit" of the slaughtered bird, through which he is purified in
addition to the purity from the leprosy. For above he said: "on whatever day
he be cleansed," but now: "and he shall be clean." For even having been
cleansed of the leprosy he nevertheless needs the present cleansing for the
pollution of the time that has gone before, or rather even three others. For
one cleansing is the: "on whatever day he be cleansed" and three 412 in
between are indicated by the: "and he shall be clean" being said a third time;
and a fifth and last through: "and he shall be cleansed." For there are as it
were also advances of cleansings before coming to the perfect purity. After
the sending away of the bird, at any rate, first "the one who has been
cleansed washes the garments," then "he shaves every hair" and upon these "he
bathes with water," after which is added: "and he shall be clean," as if apart
from these the thing said upon the sprinkling would not have sufficed: "and he
shall be clean." For even after the sprinkling he had the garments filthy.
Which would be a manner of life not altogether badly woven, since he would
even have put these away. And "hairs" are the dead things that have sprouted
to the soul, to which are contrary those of 413 Samuel and of the Nazirites
and those of the disciples that have been "numbered." For of the Nazirites
"iron does not go up upon the head." "All things, whatever they may do, acting
as things that prosper, so that not even a leaf of them as they bear fruit
falls away." And already also from the body he puts away the pollution with
water. And after becoming "clean" the third time he becomes worthy of the
camp; but he does not enter into his own house until "seven days" and being
shaved again. As the second shaving, more simply spoken, not sufficing, the
second having exceptionally the head being purified because of the more 414
capital things of the doctrines, and a "beard" after it, putting away the sins
of the man, and through "the eyebrows" all conceit, so also he "washes the
garments" a second time and "bathes" a second time and thus already becomes
"clean" a fourth time. "And on the eighth day" of the sojourn in the camp
others will no longer take for him, but he himself "shall send forth for
himself animals and oil and fine flour with oil" so that he may be perfected
in the cleansing; 415 And he who has arrived at the fifth cleansing becomes
worthy of the sacrifice "in a holy place" and his victim is "holy of holies."
The "lamb concerning a trespass," then, is a manly deed destructive of things
that have been sinned; and the one "offered as a whole burnt fruit" is already
a fruit worthy also to be wholly offered to God, becoming food for the fire
"of the altar." After which "the sheep": the fruits that have remained with
the soul of the one being cleansed, being brought up upon the one being
cleansed himself. And unto the three sacrifices the "three tenths" of "the
fine flour" are divided; but upon the fifth cleansing he does not say meal,
but "fine flour," material of 416 clean bread; "and it has been mixed in oil";
and this is food of light, not simply of fire. And through another "cotyle of
oil" again is cleansed the one needing a priest who "shall set him" not only
within the camp but also "beside the very door of the tent of witness before
the Lord."
