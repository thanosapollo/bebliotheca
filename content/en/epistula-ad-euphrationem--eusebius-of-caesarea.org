* Letter to Euphration
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

1 I confess thanks to my master in all things. For we do not say that the Son
coexists with the Father, but that the Father preexists the Son. For if they
coexist, how will the Father be Father and the Son? or how the one first, and
the other second? and the one unbegotten, and the other begotten? For two
coexisting equally similarly to one another would be thought equal in honor and
either both, as I said, unbegotten or each begotten. But neither of these is
true; for neither the unbegotten nor the begotten would be both. But the one
both first and better even in order and honor leads the second, as having also
become cause to the second both of being and of being of such a sort.
2 Only the Son of God himself, knowing more exactly than all, knowing himself
other than the Father and less and having gone under, very piously teaches this
also to us saying: "the Father who sent me is greater than I".
3 And he teaches the same one also to be the only true through the things he
says: "that they may know you the only true God", not as God being one only, but
as being one only true God with a most necessary addition of the true. Since the
Son himself also is God, but not true God. For there is one and only true God
because of not having anyone before him. And if the Son himself also is true,
yet as an image of the true God he would also be God, since "and the Word was
God", not however as the only true God.
4 And surely the image and that of which it is the image are not thought one and
the same, but two essences and two things and two powers, as also so many
appellations.
5 Wherefore most clearly also the divine apostle, handing down to us the
unspeakable and mystical theology, cries and has cried "one is God", then after
the one God he says "one mediator of God and men, a man Christ Jesus".
