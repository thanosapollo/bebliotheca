Of our father among the saints CYRIL, archbishop of Jerusalem
HOMILY ON THE PARALYTIC at the pool.
1 Wherever Jesus appears, there also is salvation. For both if he sees a
tax-collector sitting at the tax-office, he makes this one an apostle and
evangelist, and if he is buried with the dead, he raises dead men. And to the
blind he grants the seeing, and to the deaf the hearing. And he goes about the
pools, not seeking the buildings, but healing those who are sick. 2 For in
Jerusalem there was a sheep-pool having five porticoes, four running around,
and the fifth in the middle, in which a multitude of those being weak was
lying. And there was much unbelief of the Jews. But the physician and healer
of souls and bodies was granting the healing by measure, first healing the
long-time one, that he might more quickly be delivered from the pains. For it
was not one day for him to be lying, as neither a second, nor a first month or
year, but eight and thirty years. Lying with a long-time disease he was known
to those who saw, and showing the energy of the one healing. For the paralytic
was recognized by all because of the time. But the chief physician was showing
the power, yet was being set at nought by those receiving it badly. 3 And
going about the pool he saw, not having learned from a question, but from
divine energy he filled up what was lacking. Seeing, not asking, from how much
time he is lying, but having seen he knew, he who even before the seeing knew.
For if concerning the things according to the heart he had no need that anyone
should ask him concerning the man (for he himself knew what was in the man),
much more concerning the diseases, of which the infliction is from without. 4
He saw a certain one lying and weighed down with a very great disease. For he
had a great burden of sins and a long-time pain of disease. Toward this one he
somehow speaks a question, the thing longed for: Do you wish to become
healthy? And he did not say anything else, but left it to the middleness of
the question. For the question was double. For since not only the body was
sick, but also the soul, (according to what was later said by him, See, you
have become healthy, sin no longer, that something worse may not happen to
you,) he was asking him: Do you wish to become healthy? O great power of a
physician, who places the help upon the wishing. For since from faith is the
salvation, for this reason he heard the Do you wish, that the wishing might
bring on the energy. This is the saying of Jesus alone. For it does not belong
to perceptible chief-physicians. For those who heal perceptible diseases
cannot say to all: Do you wish to become healthy? But Jesus both grants the
wishing, and receives the believing, and grants the gift without pay. 5 Once
the Savior was going about along the way, and two blind men were sitting,
not-seeing with the body, but enlightened as to the mind. For him whom scribes
did not know, this one the blind were showing, crying out. For the Pharisees,
having learned the law and having studied from childhood until old age and
having grown old in ignorance, were saying, that we do not know this one, from
where he is. For he came unto his own, and his own did not receive him. But
the blind were crying out saying, Son of David, have mercy on us. And him whom
those who had learned the law did not know, this one those who have the eyes
that did not serve unto reading knew. Coming to these the Savior was saying:
Do you believe that I am able to do this? and What do you wish that I should
do for you? He did not say, What do you wish that I should say to you, but,
What do you wish that I should do for you. For he was maker and life-giver,
not now beginning to make. For his Father always works, and he himself works
together with the Father, and he was maker of the wholes by a nod of the
Father. And he himself, only from the only, begotten without mediation, asks
the blind, saying: What do you wish that I should do for you? not being
ignorant of what they wish, (for the matter was clear,) but from their



words bringing on the gift, that they might be justified from their words. For
surely he who knows the hearts is not ignorant of what is said, but he was
awaiting the word, that the question might draw the energy. 6 Thus also
standing beside this one and having come over of his own accord toward the
wearied one, the physician (and nothing wonderful if he came toward the one
lying beside the pool, if indeed he came over of his own accord from heaven
toward us) was asking this one: Do you wish to become healthy? from the
question drawing him into the knowledge and raising him up into a question. A
gift of great grace. For it was without pay. For this reason he also had the
healer as one come of his own accord. But he toward him: Yes, Lord. For the
long-time of my diseases makes health longed-for to me, and I indeed long, but
a man I do not have. Do not be downcast, O man, that you do not have a man.
You have God standing beside, according to one thing a man, according to
another God; for both must be confessed. For the confession of the humanity,
the divinity not being confessed together, is not beneficial, but rather a
cause of a curse. For cursed is he who has the hope upon a man. If then we
also, hoping in Jesus, hope in a man, and do not also weave the divinity
together with the hope, we are heirs of a curse. But now we confess both God
and man, and both truly, [God truly] begotten from a true Father, and
worshipping a man not according to fantasy but according to truth begotten we
receive a true salvation. 7 I wish indeed to become healthy, but a man I do
not have. See, whether, where the salvation was lacking, there he gave the
energy. For the many of those who were sick also had houses, and still also
acquaintances, even if certain others. But this one truly pressed in all
poverty and having no power of the help from without, having been left most
alone, had as helper the only-begotten Son of God. Do you wish to become
healthy? Yes, Lord, but a man I do not have, that, when the water is troubled,
he may throw me into the pool. You have the fountain, because with you is a
fountain of life, a fountain-making fountain. And whoever drinks from this
water, rivers will flow from his belly, not of water flowing down, but of
water leaping (for the thing of Jesus does not make a leaping from above
downward, but from the earthly unto the heavenly), of water leaping unto
eternal life. For Jesus is a fountain of good things. 8 And why do you wait
for a pool? You have the one walking upon the waters, the rebuker of the
winds, the charioteer of the sea, who not only has the sea spread under
himself after the manner of a foundation, but also granted to Peter the
like-mannered energy of the walking. For when it was a lightless night, and
the light was present, he was not recognized (for Jesus walking upon the
waters was unknown as far as appearing from the character of the face, but the
peculiarity of the voice made known the one present), and thinking him a
phantom they were afraid. But Jesus said toward them: I am, do not fear. And
Peter says toward him: If you are he whom I know, or rather whom the Father
made known to me, command me to come toward you upon the waters. And he toward
him with most ungrudging sharing says: Come. 9 Therefore the charioteer and
maker of the waters was present beside the waters of the pool. Toward whom the
paralytic says: A man I do not have, that, when the water is troubled, he may
throw me into a pool. The Savior says to him: Why do you wait for a troubling
of waters? Be healed without troubling. And why do you wait for an appearing
movement? By a word the command comes to be, sharper than thought. Only look
upon the energy of the fountain and descry the God appearing in flesh, and do
not look at the appearing one, but the one working through the appearing one.
A man I do not have, that when the water is troubled he may throw me into the
pool. And he toward him: Why do you wait for the small things? Why do you seek
a healing of waters? Rise, the resurrection said to you. For everywhere the
Savior becomes all things to all, bread to those who hunger, water to those
who thirst, resurrection to the dead, physician to the sick, redemption to
sinners. 10 Rise, take up your bed and walk. But first rise, first throw off
the disease. Then take up the



sinew of faith. Be strong first against the bed that carries you, and learn to
carry with a wooden vessel the long-time venerated things. And that Savior was
commanding to carry the wooden litter, concerning whom it has been said: The
king made for himself a litter from woods of Lebanon. He made its pillars
silver, its reclining-place purple, within it a pavement of stone. The symbols
of the passion are stored in the songs, the bridal and sober and chaste ones.
For do not, having taken the words according to the supposition of the many
and having gazed at nothing, think the songs to be erotic and passionate, but
bridal words filled with chastity. And if you do not understand the songs,
come upon the Proverbs, and by a way and by steps go up upon those. Wisdom
built for herself a house, (as the word is concerning a woman,) and she sent
her own servants. And again elsewhere he says: Be in love with her and she
will keep you. Not a love of a woman, but of wisdom that banishes eros. For
where there is an acquisition of wisdom, there also is a banishing of eros.
For the passions are not according to wisdom, but the thoughts are wise.
Horses became mad for females, the impulse is irrational. If then in the songs
you hear the things said as concerning a bridegroom and a bride, do not fall
to the ground, nor hear these passionately; retrain the passions from the
passionless things. 11 Therefore study the divine things of the songs, filled
with chastity, indicative of the passion of Christ. For indeed the things
narrating concerning the passion of the Savior also speak the place: He
entered into a garden, because of the one buried there. And he mentioned the
spices: He took my myrrh full; for the economy was fulfilled. And after the
resurrection he was saying: I ate my bread with my honey. For they gave him
from a honeycomb. And the things concerning the wine mixed with myrrh the
songs were saying again: I will give you to drink from the perfume-maker's
wine. And the things concerning the myrrh poured upon his head elsewhere he
was saying: Until the king was in his reclining, my nard gave its scent. For
while he was reclining in the house of Simon the leper a woman having entered
broke an alabaster of myrrh of costly genuine nard and poured it upon his
head. Thus also concerning the cross. A litter. The wood of the cross, the
carrying one. He made its pillars silver. The beginning of the cross was
silver, the betrayal. For just as a very beautiful house has a golden roof
lying upon it from above, but pillars raise up the whole of the construction,
so also of the being crucified of Christ and rising, silver was the beginning.
For if Judas had not betrayed, he would not have been crucified. For this
reason, as the beginning of a notable passion, he made its pillars silver. 12
His mounting-place is purple. For this reason they also clothed him with
purple, playing indeed, but working prophetically; for he was a king. Even if
they were acting most of all according to mockery, yet they were acting for
the time; and it became the symbol of the kingly dignity. And if a crown of
thorns, yet a crown, and bound by soldiers. For kings are proclaimed by
soldiers. His mounting-place is purple, within it a pavement of stone. The
zealous of the church know Lithostroton, the so-called Gabbatha, being in the
things of Pilate. 13 And these things have been said by me in a digression,
having come from the bed upon the litter. He said then toward him: Rise, take
up your bed and walk. Long-time indeed the disease, but most sharp the
plaster. Many-yeared indeed the paralysis, but most sharp the sinewing. For
the maker of sinews, the one furnishing varied healings to the blind, the one
who through a smearing of clay paradoxically granted the plaster. For if clay
should be poured upon one seeing, it hinders the seeing; but Jesus through
clay was granting seeing to those not seeing. And to others again the power
through other things; for to one the Rise, take up your bed and walk. How
great a shock do you think took those who were seeing? But the thing seen was
wonderful, and the unbelief terrible. And a many-yeared disease is healed, but
a long-time unbelief was not healed. But the Jews were remaining in the
passions, and they were not



willing to be healed. 14 And if it was necessary to be struck at what
happened, it was necessary to worship the physician of souls and bodies. But
they were murmuring. For they were descendants of murmurers, those who twist
the good things into evil, those who say the bitter sweet, and the sweet
bitter. But Jesus was working with all economy on a sabbath, working the
things above sabbath, that the working might persuade. For since word fights
with word, but the work is unfightable, he was healing on a sabbath, teaching,
that the word might not become contradictory, but the working might persuade
those who see. 15 Those were saying: It is sabbath, it is not permitted for
you to take up your bed. A lawgiver was present, and another was saying the It
is not permitted for you. Appoint, Lord, a lawgiver over them, has been said
concerning the Savior. And he who had just been healed both in soul and in
body, having received a wise word from the wisdom, answers at once, not having
to answer legally, but making the answer shortly. You all know, he says, my
long-time disease and the many-yeared of the reclining, the unattended of the
misery. None of you ever showed a caring thing, having taken me and first
thrown me into the pool, that I might be healed. And yet then not having shown
a caring thing, how now have you taken the lawgiving thing, saying, It is not
permitted for you to take up your bed? Therefore I make toward you a most
short answer: He who made me healthy, that one said to me. If you despise me,
take the shock toward the deed. He did not put on a plaster, he did not use
medical devices or helps. He spoke by a word, and the work followed. He
commanded, and I accomplish the command. I have become obedient to the command
of this one who healed through commands. For if the one who commanded toward
the being healed was powerless in the commands, it was not necessary to obey
these. But since by words it has been commanded to stop a clear long-time
suffering, most justly I hear this one, whose disease also having heard was
delivered. He who made me healthy, that one said to me, Take up your bed. 16
The one who was healed did not know who the one healing is. Much the
unvaingloriousness of our Savior is presented. For having healed he turned
aside, that he might not be made known upon the healing. But we do everything
contrary. If it happens sometime to see dreams, or to help through hands, or
to drive away a demon through invocation, so far are we from hiding the
achievement, that even not being asked we boast. But Jesus, teaching us by the
works not to speak concerning himself, furnishing the healing turned aside at
the same time, that he might not be recognized, withdrawing in season and
being present in season. For when it was necessary that the good-report of the
achievement be shut, then he withdraws. But when a withdrawal of the crowds
has come to be, that along with the bodily healing he might also bring on the
healed things of the soul, then being present he was saying: See, you have
become healthy, sin no longer. 17 And he is a varied physician, sometimes
first healing soul, then body, and sometimes conversely. Sin no longer, that
something worse may not happen to you. Teaching many through one. For the word
is not toward that one only, but also toward all of us. For if ever we fall
into sufferings or griefs or necessities, let no one ascribe it to God. For
God is untempted of evils, and he himself tempts no one. And each of us being
bound with cords of sins is scourged. Sin no longer that something worse may
not happen to you. Let every man hear the saying, and let him who was
fornicating before this put away the passion, and he who was a greedy one
before this, become merciful. Let the robber hear, Sin no longer. Great also
is God's unremembering of evil, abundant the grace. But do not take the
beyond-measure of the forbearance as a provision of contempt, nor because God
is long-suffering, for this reason sin. But be healed henceforth of the
fleshly passions, and say also you the thing seasonably read. For when we were
in the flesh, the sufferings of the sins that are through the law were working
in our members. And if the apostle says, When we were in the flesh, he was not
speaking this flesh which we have been clothed with, but the fleshly actions.
Still indeed wearing flesh he was saying, When we were in the flesh. But in
the same way as when a flood was about to



be brought on God was saying, My spirit shall not remain in these men, because
they are fleshes, the spirit being changed into a fleshly choice, so also here
the apostle says, When we were in the flesh. 18 Let no one then be in flesh,
but being in flesh let him not walk according to flesh. For the apostle does
not wish us, having completely led ourselves out of the world, not to work
what is base, but being in flesh that we enslave the flesh, and not be ruled
by it. Let us not be enslaved, but let us rule. Let us use measured food, not
being carried down by gluttony, but bridling the belly, that we may also rule
the things under the belly. Let the body be ruled by the soul, and let not the
soul be dragged by the fleshly pleasures. Sin no longer, that something worse
may not happen to you. The word proclaims to all, and may all the ears hear.
For not every hearing of flesh receiving the word already receives it into the
mind. For this reason the Savior was saying: He who has ears to hear, let him
hear, conversing with those who have ears of flesh. 19 Let everyone then hear
Jesus and sin no longer, but let us run toward the forgiver of sins. And if we
are sick, let us flee to him; whether we are in pain in the soul, let us
follow after the physician of knowledge; whether we hunger, let us receive the
bread; whether we have been made dead, let us take the resurrection. Whether
we have grown old in ignorance, let us ask the wisdom from the Wisdom. But a
word has drawn us out into a length of word. And perhaps we have become a
hindrance of fatherly teaching. And a time calls also to hear greater words,
that having been benefited from greater works we may send up the doxology to
God through the works, to whom is the glory both now and always and unto the
ages of the ages. Amen.
