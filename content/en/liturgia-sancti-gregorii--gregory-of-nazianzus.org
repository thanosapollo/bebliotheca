THE DIVINE LITURGY OF OUR FATHER AMONG THE SAINTS GREGORY. You who have
visited us in mercy and compassions, Master Lord Jesus Christ, and have
granted us boldness, to us the humble and sinful and unworthy servants of
yours, to stand at your holy altar and to offer you the fearful and bloodless
sacrifice, for our own sins and for the ignorances of your people, unto relief
and rest of our fathers and brethren who have fallen asleep before, and unto
the establishment of all your people. Look upon me your unprofitable servant,
and blot out my transgressions through your compassion; and cleanse my lips
and my heart from every stain of flesh and of spirit; and put away from me
every thought both shameful and senseless; and sanctify me by the power of
your Holy Spirit for this liturgy, and receive me through your goodness as I
draw near to your holy altar; and be pleased, Lord, that the gifts about to be
offered to you through our hands become acceptable, condescending to my
weaknesses; and do not cast me away from your face; do not abhor me, my
unworthiness, but have mercy on me, O God, according to your great mercy, and
according to the multitude of your compassions blot out my lawlessness, that
having come without condemnation before your glory I may be counted worthy of
your shelter and of the illumination of your all-holy Spirit; and that I may
not become as a servant of sin, unapproved, but as a servant who may find*
grace and mercy and forgiveness of sins, in the present and in the age to
come. Yes, Master almighty, all-powerful Lord, hear my petition. For you are
the one who works all things in all; and from you we all seek in all things
both help and succor; for you are a lover of mankind, and you are glorified,
Jesus our God, with your unoriginate Father and your Holy Spirit, now and
ever. =36.701= Prayer after the preparation of the holy altar. Master Lord
Jesus Christ, our God, who through your saving presence and the illumination
of your all-holy Spirit have counted us, the humble and sinful and unworthy
servants of yours, worthy to stand at your holy altar and to offer and to
liturgize the undefiled mysteries of your new covenant. Yourself, life-giver
and bestower of good things, make a sign with us for good, and count us worthy
in a pure conscience to worship you all the days of our life, and in holiness
to offer you this divine liturgy, unto forgiveness of sins and unto enjoyment
of the blessedness to come. Remember, O good one, benefactor, king of the
ages, and fashioner of all creation, those who have offered and those through
whom they offered; and keep us without condemnation in the sacred ministry of
your divine mysteries; for blessed and sanctified and glorified is your
all-honorable and magnificent holy name, with the Father and the Holy Spirit
now, and. Prayer of the holy Gospel. Peace to all. Master Lord Jesus Christ
our God, who said to your holy disciples and sacred apostles that many
prophets and righteous men desired to see the things you see, and did not see;
and to hear the things you hear, and did not hear; for blessed are your eyes
because they see, and your ears because they hear. And may we now be counted
worthy to hear and to do your holy Gospels, by the petitions

of your sacred ones. Remember therefore, Master, even now, all who have
charged us the unworthy to remember them in our prayers, and the requests
which we send up to you, Lord our God. Those who have died before, give them
rest; those who are ill, strengthen them; for you are the life of us all, and
the salvation of us all; and the hope of us all, and the healing of us all,
and the proper resurrection of us all; and to you we send up the glory, honor,
and worship, with your almighty and all-seeing Begetter and your all-holy and
life-originating and consubstantial Spirit, now and ever. Prayer of the veil.
No one of those bound by fleshly desires and pleasures is worthy to approach,
or to draw near, or to liturgize you, King of glory. For to minister to you is
great and fearful, and unapproachable even to the heavenly powers themselves.
But nevertheless through your ineffable and unmeasured love of mankind you
became man without change and without alteration, and you were named high
priest for us, =36.704= and you handed over to us the sacred ministry of this
liturgical and bloodless sacrifice, as Master of all things. For you (are*)
master of the heavenly and the earthly and those under the earth; you who are
borne upon a cherubic throne; the Lord of the seraphim, and King of Israel;
the only holy one, who rest in the holy. You therefore I entreat, the only
good and readily-hearing God, look upon me the sinful and unprofitable servant
of yours; and make me sufficient by the power of the Holy Spirit, clothed with
the grace of the priesthood, to stand at this your holy table and to minister
your undefiled body and your precious blood. For I come to you having bowed my
own neck; and I pray you, do not turn your face away from me; nor reject me
from among your children; but count me worthy that these gifts be offered to
you by me the sinful and unworthy servant of yours; for you are the one who
sanctifies and is sanctified; who offers and is offered; who receives and is
received; who gives and is distributed; and to you we send up glory, with the
Father and the Holy Spirit. Another prayer of the veil among the Egyptians.
Lord our God the almighty, who know the mind of men, who search hearts and
reins; who have called me the unworthy to this your liturgy; do not abhor me,
nor turn your face away from me; but blot out all my transgressions, and wash
from me the filth of the body and the stain of the soul, and sanctify me
wholly, that I who entreat you to give forgiveness of sins to others may not
myself become unapproved. Yes, Lord, do not turn away from me, humbled and put
to shame, but send forth to me the grace of your Holy Spirit, and count me
worthy to stand at your holy altar without condemnation, and to offer you this
rational and bloodless offering, with a pure conscience, unto pardon of our
sins and of our transgressions; and unto forgiveness of the ignorances of your
people; unto rest and refreshment of our fathers and brethren who have fallen
asleep before, and unto the establishment of all your people, unto your glory
of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit now. Prayer of the kiss
Peace to all. He who is, and who is before, and who remains unto the ages;
co-eternal with the Father and consubstantial, and co-enthroned and
co-creator. Who through goodness alone

brought man from non-being into being, and set him in a paradise of delight.
And when he had fallen by the deceit =36.705= of the enemy and by disobedience
of your commandment, wishing to renew him and to bring him back to the ancient
dignity, you did not commit our salvation to an angel, not to an archangel,
not to a patriarch, not to a prophet; but yourself becoming flesh without
change, you were made man, and were likened to us in all things except sin
alone; becoming a mediator of us and of the Father, and having destroyed the
middle wall of partition and the long-standing enmity, you joined the earthly
to the heavenly, and gathered both into one and fulfilled the incarnate
economy. And being about to go bodily into the heavens, filling all things
divinely, you said to your holy disciples and apostles: Peace I leave with
you; my peace I give to you. This peace grant us also now, Master; grant a
cleansing of every stain, of every deceit and of every wickedness, and of
craft, and of the death-bearing remembrance of wrongs, and count us worthy to
greet one another with a holy kiss, so as to partake without condemnation of
your immortal and heavenly gift; by the grace of the good pleasure of the
Father, and by the working of the Holy Spirit. For you are the bestower and
giver of all good things, and to you we send up the glory, the eternal
doxology, with your unoriginate Father and your Holy Spirit, now and ever.
Another prayer of the kiss. Christ our God, the fearful and inconceivable
power of God and Father; who sit above the flaming throne of the cherubim and
are escorted by fiery powers, and who exist as a consuming fire as God; and
who through your ineffable condescension and love of mankind did not burn by
your approach the treacherous betrayer, but greeted him as a friend, drawing
him to repentance and recognition of his own daring. Count us worthy, Master,
at this dread hour, in concord and without any twofold wrath, and leftover of
wickedness, to receive one another in a holy kiss. And do not condemn us for
not being wholly, and as is pleasing to your goodness, clean from every dregs
of sin and wickedness and of the death-bearing remembrance of wrongs; but
yourself, by your ineffable and indescribable compassion, knowing our weak and
downward-weighing frame, blot out every stain of our transgressions, that this
divine mystery may not become to us unto judgment or unto condemnation. For
you are the one who is able to forgive every sin and to pass over injustices
and lawlessnesses of wretched men, being the purification of the whole world,
and to you is due from all in concord doxology, honor and worship, together
with your undefiled Father and your life-giving Spirit now, etc. And the kiss
takes place. The deacon says: Let us stand well. The people say: A mercy of
peace. The priest will say aloud: The love of God the Father, and the grace of
the only-begotten =36.708= Son, and Lord and God and Savior of us Jesus
Christ; and the communion and the gift of the Holy Spirit, be with you all.
The people say: And with your spirit. The priest says: Let us lift up our
hearts. The people say: We have them to the Lord. The priest says: Let us give
thanks to the Lord. The people say: It is meet and right. The priest says: It
is meet and right, it is meet and right. Beginning of the proskomide For truly
it is meet and right to praise you, to bless you, to worship you, to glorify
you, the only true God, the lover of mankind, the unspeakable, the invisible,
the uncontained, the unoriginate, the eternal, the timeless, the unmeasured,
the

unchangeable, the inconceivable; the maker of all things, the redeemer of all;
who are propitious toward all our lawlessnesses; who heal all our diseases;
who redeem our life from corruption; who crown us in mercy and compassions.
Angels praise you; archangels worship you; principalities hymn you; dominions
cry out to you; powers proclaim your glory; thrones send up to you the praise;
a thousand thousands stand beside you, and ten thousand times ten thousand
offer you the liturgy. The invisible things hymn you; the things that appear
worship you, all doing your word, Master. The deacon says: You who are seated,
arise. The priest says: You who are, God true Lord from true God; who have
shown us the radiance of the Father; who have granted us the true knowledge of
the Holy Spirit; who have revealed this great mystery of life; who have
established for men the choir-standing of the bodiless ones. Who have handed
over to those on earth the hymnody of the seraphim, receive with the invisible
ones our voice also. Join us to the heavenly powers. Let us also say with
them, having folded away every unseemly thought, let us cry out as they do,
with unsilenced voices, with unresting mouths let us hymn your majesty. The
deacon says: Look toward the east. The priest says: For around you stand the
seraphim, six wings to the one; and with two wings they cover their own faces;
and with two their own feet; and with two they fly, and they cried one to
another. Aloud: The hymn of victory of our salvations, with a glorious voice,
with a bright voice, hymning, singing, crying, glorifying, shouting and
saying. The deacon says: Let us attend. The people say: Holy, holy, holy Lord
of Sabaoth, heaven is full, etc. The priest says: Holy, holy are you, Lord,
and all-holy. Exceptional is the radiance of your essence; =36.709=
unspeakable is the power of your wisdom. No word will measure the sea of your
love of mankind. You made me a man, as a lover of mankind, not yourself in
need of my service, but I rather having need of your mastery. Not existing,
you brought me forth through compassion; you set heaven as a roof for me; you
fixed earth as a base for me; for me you bridled the sea; for me you brought
forth the nature of living things; you subjected all things under my feet; you
left out not one of the deeds of your love of mankind in me. The people say:
Lord, have mercy. The priest says: You fashioned me, and you laid your hand
upon me; of your authority you inscribed the image in me; you placed in me the
gift of the word; you opened paradise for my delight; you handed over the
teaching of your knowledge; you showed me the tree of life* you pointed out to
me a tree; you made known the sting of death; of one plant you forbade me the
enjoyment; from that one alone then you told me not to eat; I ate, I set aside
the law; by choice I neglected the commandment; and I seized the sentence of
death. The people say: Lord, have mercy. The priest says: You, O Master,
changed the punishment for me; as a good shepherd you ran toward the one gone
astray; as a true father you suffered with me who had fallen; with all the
medicines toward life you bound me up. Yourself you sent prophets to me; for
me the sick you gave a law unto help. Yourself to me* the things toward health
that had been transgressed, you ministered; you caused light to rise for those
gone astray; to those who do not

know, you who are always present sojourned; you came upon the virginal womb.
Being God uncontained, you did not count it robbery to be equal with God, but
emptied yourself taking the form of a servant; you blessed my nature in
yourself; for me you fulfilled the law, you prescribed the resurrection of my
fall. You gave release to those held by Hades; you drove away the curse of the
law; in flesh you abolished sin; you made known to me the dominion of your
authority; you gave back seeing to the blind; you raised the dead from tombs;
by a word you restored nature; you pointed out to me the economy of your
compassion; you endured the violence of the wicked; you gave your back to
scourges; and you submitted your cheeks to blows; you did not turn away your
face for me from the shame of spittings. The people say: Lord, have mercy. The
priest says: As a sheep you came to slaughter, as far as the cross you pointed
out my care; by your tomb you put my sin to death; into heaven you brought up
my firstfruits; you announced to me the presence of your coming, in which you
are about to come to judge the living and the dead, and to render to each
according to his works. The people say: According to your mercy, Lord.
=36.712= The priest says: Of this my freedom I offer you the symbols; by your
words I inscribe the deeds. You handed over to me this mystical participation
of your flesh, in bread and wine. The people say: We believe. The priest says:
For on the night in which you were being delivered* yourself yourself, of your
own authority. The people say: We believe. The priest says: Taking bread in
your holy and undefiled and blameless hands, you looked up toward your own
Father, God of us and God of all; you gave thanks † you blessed † you
sanctified † you broke, you gave to your holy disciples and apostles saying:
Take, eat; this is my body, which is broken for you and for many, and
distributed unto forgiveness of sins; do this unto my remembrance. Likewise
after supper, taking a cup, and you mixed it of the fruit of the vine and of
water, you gave thanks † you blessed † you sanctified † you gave to your holy
disciples and apostles, saying: Drink of it, all of you; this is my blood,
that of the new covenant, which is poured out for you and for many unto
forgiveness of sins; do this unto my remembrance. For as often as you eat this
bread, and drink this cup, you proclaim my death, and you confess my
resurrection until I come. The people say: Amen. Thrice. Your death, etc. The
priest says: So then, Master, remembering the condescension upon earth, and
the life-giving death, and your three-day burial, and the resurrection from
the dead, and the ascent into the heavens, and the sitting at the right hand
of the Father, and your second and fearful and glorious presence about to come
from the heavens. Aloud: Offering you your own from your own gifts, according
to all, and through all and in all. The people say: We praise you, we bless
you. The deacon says: Bow to God with fear. The priest says in himself, having
bowed: Yourself then, Master, by your voice transform the things set forth;
yourself being present, complete this mystical liturgy

yourself preserve for us the remembrance of your worship. Yourself send down
your =36.713= all-holy Spirit, that having visited by his holy and good and
glorious presence he may sanctify and transform the things set forth, these
precious and holy gifts, into the very body and the blood of our redemption.
The people say: Let us attend. The deacon says: Amen. The priest will say
aloud: And he will make this bread* become your holy body, of our Lord and God
and Savior and All-King Jesus Christ, unto forgiveness of sins, and unto life
eternal for those who partake of it. The people say: Amen. The priest says:
And this cup, your precious blood, that of your new covenant, of our Lord and
God and Savior and All-King Jesus Christ, unto forgiveness of sins, and unto
life eternal for those who partake of it. The people say: Amen. The priest
says and the people answer the Lord have mercy. We entreat you, Christ our
God. Of your Church, Lord, fix the foundation. Root in us the concord of love.
Increase the truth of the faith. Cut straight for us the way of your piety.
Strengthen the shepherds. Make the shepherded secure. Give to the clergy good
order. To the monks continence. To those in virginity soundness of mind. To
those in honorable marriage well-living. To those in repentance mercy. To
those who are rich goodness. To those in want succor. To the poor help. Gird
the aged. Bring the young to soundness of mind. Convert the unbelieving. Stop
the schisms of the Church. Dissolve the insolences of the heresies. Join us
all toward the concord of your piety. The people say: Lord, have mercy. The
priest says: Remember, Lord, the peace of your holy, only, catholic, and
apostolic Church, from the ends unto the ends of the inhabited world, and of
the orthodox bishops in her who have cut straight the word of truth. Aloud:
Especially of our most holy and most blessed high priest Abba 4., pope and
patriarch of the great city of Alexandria, and for the bishops who remain,
presbyters, deacons, readers, singers, exorcists, monks, ever-virgins,
=36.716= continent, widows, orphans, laypeople, and for the whole fullness of
the holy Church of God of the faithful. The people say: Lord, have mercy. The
priest says: Remember, Lord, those who have reigned piously. Remember, Lord,
those in the palace, our faithful and orthodox brethren, and of the whole
camp. Remember, Lord, those who offer these holy gifts, and those for whom and
through whom they have offered, and grant a heavenly reward to them all.
Remember, Lord, also those in mountains and caves and the holes of the earth.
And of our brethren who are in captivities, and grant peaceful restorations to
their own. The deacon says: Pray for the captives. The people say: Lord, have
mercy. Then the priest bows his own head saying to himself in himself.
Remember, Lord, also my wretched and miserable

soul, my humiliation, and forgive me all my faults and where sin abounded,
make your grace to abound, and do not through my sins and the profanation of
my heart deprive your people of the grace of your Holy Spirit. He will lift
the head and will say aloud. For your people and your Church entreat you, and
through you and with you the Father saying. The people say: Have mercy on us,
O God, our Savior. Thrice. The priest says: Have mercy on us, O God, our
Savior. Thrice. The people say: Lord, have mercy. Thrice. The priest says:
Remember, Lord, the air and the fruits of the earth. Remember, Lord, the due
rising of the river waters. Remember, Lord, the rains and the seed-lands of
the earth. =36.717= Gladden again and renew the face of the earth. Make drunk
her furrows, multiply her produce; present them to us unto seed and unto
harvest, and now blessing bless. Order our life. Bless the crown of the year
of your goodness, for the poor of your people, for the widow and the orphan,
for the stranger and the sojourner, and for us all who hope in you and call
upon your holy name. For the eyes of all hope in you, and you give their food
in due season. Do with us according to your goodness, you who give food to all
flesh. Fill our hearts with joy and gladness, that in all things, always
having all sufficiency, we may abound unto every good work, to do your holy
will. The people say: Lord, have mercy. The priest says, and the people
answer: Lord, have mercy. Grant to your people concord. To the world
stability. To the air a good mixture. To the sick salvation. To those in need
refreshment. To those in exiles relief. To the orphans help. To the widows
succor. Suffice the afflicted unto good. Strengthen those who stand. Raise
those who have fallen. Make those who have stood secure. Remember those who
have fallen asleep. Receive the intercessions of those in confession. Number
those who have sinned and repented with your faithful. Number the faithful
with your martyrs. Make those present in this place imitators of the angels.
And receive us by your grace who have been called to your ministry, being
unworthy. The people say: Lord, have mercy. The priest says: Remember, Lord,
also this our city, and those who dwell in it in orthodox faith, and every
city and country with all their world. And deliver us from famine and
pestilence, earthquake and drowning of fire, and from captivity of barbarians,
and from alien swords, and from uprising of enemies and of heretics. The
people say: Lord, have mercy. The priest says: Remember, Lord, our holy
fathers who have gone before, orthodox bishops, and all who from of old have
been well-pleasing to you, holy Fathers, patriarchs, apostles, prophets,
heralds, evangelists, martyrs, confessors, =36.720= and every righteous spirit
perfected in the faith of Christ. Aloud: Especially of our all-holy,
exceeding-glorious, undefiled, exceeding-blessed lady the Theotokos and
ever-virgin Mary. Of the holy glorious prophet, forerunner, baptist, and
martyr John. Of saint Stephen the first deacon and first martyr. And of our
holy and blessed Father Mark, the apostle and evangelist. And of our father
among the saints

Gregory. And of those whose memorial we make on this present day, and of every
choir of your saints, by whose prayers and intercessions have mercy also on
us, and save us through your holy name which has been invoked upon us. The
deacon says the Diptychs. The priest says in himself. Remember, Lord, our
fathers and brethren who have fallen asleep before in the orthodox faith, and
give rest to their souls with the holy, with the righteous. Nourish, join them
in a place of green pasture, upon water of rest, in a paradise of delight, and
with these whose names we have said. Then he commemorates the living and the
dead, and after the diptychs the priest says. Remember, Lord, those whom we
have remembered and those whom we have not remembered, faithful and orthodox,
with whom also us with them as a good and man-loving God. The people say:
Remit, forgive, pardon. The priest says: For you are our God, merciful, who do
not will the death of the sinner, as that he turn and live. O God, visit us in
your salvation; do with us according to your fairness, you who do exceedingly
above what we ask or understand; that in this also as in all your all-holy and
honorable and blessed name may be glorified and exalted and hymned and blessed
and sanctified, together with your undefiled Father and Holy Spirit. The
people say: As it was, and is, and shall be. The deacon says: Come down, you
deacons. The priest says: Peace to all. The people say: And to your spirit.
Preface of the fraction. The priest says: Jesus Christ, the saving =36.721=
name, who formed these divine and undefiled and heavenly mysteries; who set
the priests in the rank of ministers; and through your invisible power
transformed them. Who appear to the pure in heart, and to those who approach
genuinely through yourself* granting. Who then blessed, now also bless. Who
then sanctified, now also sanctify. Who then broke, now also nourish. Who then
distributed to his own disciples and apostles, now also, Master, distribute to
us and to all your people, O lover of mankind, almighty, Lord our God. The
deacon says: Pray. The people say: Lord, have mercy. The priest says: Peace to
all. The people say: And to your spirit. Prayer of the fraction. He who is,
who was, who came, and who comes again; who sits at the right hand of the
Father; the bread who came down from heaven and gives life to the world; the
great high priest, the pioneer of our salvation; the true light before all
ages. Who being the radiance of the glory and the impress of the hypostasis of
your own Father; who were well-pleased and counted worthy to come down from
the heights of heaven, from the bosom of the unapproachable light and true and
invisible only Father. And having been made flesh of the Holy Spirit and of
our all-glorious, undefiled, holy lady the Theotokos and ever-virgin Mary, and
having been made man perfectly; and according to transfer having changed the
humanity without alteration; having united it to yourself according to
hypostasis, unspeakably and inconceivably, without change and without
confusion, having a soul both rational and intellective, thus you came forth
from her having been made God-man; consubstantial with the Father according to
the divinity, and consubstantial with us according to the humanity. Not two
persons then, nor two forms that is, nor being known in two natures, but one
God, one Lord, one

essence, one kingdom, one mastery, one energy, =36.724= one hypostasis, one
will, one nature of the God Word incarnate, and worshipped. And having been
crucified under Pontius Pilate, and having confessed the good confession;
having suffered and been buried and risen on the third day, and having gone up
into the heavens, and having sat at the right hand of the majesty of the
Father, having trampled death, and having spoiled Hades, having crushed gates
of bronze, and having broken bars of iron, and having called back captive Adam
from corruption, and having freed us from the slavery of the devil. Wherefore
we pray and beseech you, O lover of mankind, good one, count us worthy in a
pure heart to dare fearlessly to cry out to the Master of all, heavenly God,
holy Father, and to say, etc. Another prayer of the fraction. For you are the
Word of the Father, the pre-eternal God, the great High Priest, who for the
salvation of the race of men were made flesh and made man, and called to
yourself from all the nations a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy
nation, a people for possession. Wherefore we pray and beseech you, O lover of
mankind, good Lord, let this sacrifice not become unto reproof and shame, nor
unto judgment, nor unto condemnation of our sins; for on behalf of* our
weaknesses we have offered; but just as you counted these your all-holy
precious gifts worthy to fill with all holiness, through the visitation of
your all-holy Spirit upon them, so also count worthy to sanctify of us the
sinful, your servants, the souls, the bodies, the spirits, the consciences,
that with an enlightened soul, an unashamed face, a pure heart, an unfeigned
conscience, sanctified lips, perfect love, a sure hope, we may dare with
boldness, without fear, to say the holy prayer which you gave to your own holy
disciples and sacred apostles. When you pray, pray thus. Our Father, etc. The
people say: Hallowed be. Another prayer of the fraction. Blessed are you,
Christ the God, the almighty, the redeemer of his own Church; O Word, whom
they foresee, and man, whom they look upon beforehand. Who through his
incomprehensible incarnation prepared for us heavenly bread, this your body,
which you set as a mystery* and all-holy among all. You mixed for us a cup
from the vine of truth, from your divine and undefiled side. Who even after
having given over the spirit pour out from it blood and water, by which there
is sanctification for the whole world. Acquire us, good Lord, your unworthy
servants; make us a peculiar people, a royal priesthood, a holy nation.
Sanctify us also, O God, just as you sanctified these gifts set forth and
holy, and made them invisible from the visible, mysteries of which* they
foresee, these to you, Lord God, our Savior =36.725= Jesus Christ. You then,
Lord, through your much compassion counted us worthy through baptism to become
sons and heirs. You taught us the type of the prayer which is in the mystery,
to pray in it to your undefiled Father. You then also now, Master Lord, count
us worthy in a sanctified conscience, and with a good thought which is fitting
..... with longing and with good boldness to dare to call upon the holy God in
the heavens, your Father, and to say. The people say: Our Father. And after
the Our Father the priest says. Yes, Lord, Lord, who have given us the
authority to tread upon serpents and scorpions, and upon every power of the
enemy, crush and subject the heads of our enemies under the feet

quickly, and scatter all their evil-crafted device against us, for you are
king of our* all, Christ the God; and to you we send up the glory and the
thanksgiving and the worship, each day, with your unoriginate Father and the
