CHAPTER 8. There is a demon, called wanderer, and especially toward dawn he
presents himself to the brothers, who leads the mind of the anchorite around
from city to city, and from house to house, and from village to village,
making first, as it were, bare encounters, and meeting certain acquaintances,
and talking at greater length, and destroying its own state toward those it
meets, and becoming far off, little by little taking forgetfulness of the
knowledge of God, and of virtue, and of the profession. Therefore the one
withdrawing must watch this, both whence it begins and where it ends; for not
idly, nor as it happens, does it work that long circuit, but wishing to
destroy the state of the anchorite, it does these things, so that the mind,
inflamed from these things, and drunk from the many encounters, may
immediately fall in with the demon of fornication, or of anger, or of grief,
who especially spoil the brightness of his state. But we, if we have the aim
of knowing clearly this one's cunning, let us not quickly speak to him, nor
show the things that are happening, how according to the intellect he works
the encounters, and in what manner little by little he drives the mind toward
death, since he will flee from us; =79.1212= for he does not endure to be seen
doing these things; and thereafter we shall know nothing of what we have been
eager to learn. But let us also allow him one more day, or even a second, to
complete the drama, so that having learned his trickery exactly, we may
thereafter refute him with a word and put him to flight.



CHAPTER 9. But since at the time of temptation it happens that the mind, being
troubled, does not see exactly the things that are happening, let this be done
after the withdrawal of the demon: sitting by yourself, remember the things
that happened to you, both whence you began and where you went, and in what
place you were seized by the spirit of fornication, or of anger, or of grief,
and how all the things that happened came to be, and learn these things
clearly, and deliver them to memory so that you may have [means] to refute him
when he approaches, and point out the place hidden by him, and you will no
longer follow him. And if you ever also wish to provoke him to madness, refute
him immediately when he has presented himself, and make manifest the first
place into which he entered, and the second, and the third, for he is greatly
angry, not bearing the shame; and the proof that you have spoken timely toward
him will be the thought's having fled from you; for it is impossible
thereafter for him to stand when openly refuted. And this demon of deception,
having been defeated, is succeeded by heaviest sleep, and a deadening with
much coldness of the eyelids, and infinite yawnings, and shoulders being
weighed down and going numb, which the Holy Spirit will dissolve by intense
prayer.

CHAPTER 10. Hatred against the demons contributes greatly to our salvation,
and is suitable toward the working of virtue; but we are not able to nourish
this in ourselves, as some good offspring, the pleasure-loving spirits
destroying it, and calling the soul back again toward friendship and
familiarity; but this friendship, or rather the hard-to-heal gangrene, the
physician of souls heals through abandonment; for he allows us to suffer
something fearful from them by night or by day, and again the soul runs back
to the archetypal hatred, being taught to say to the Lord, according to David,
"I hated them with perfect hatred; they became enemies to me." For this one
hates the enemies with perfect hatred, who sins neither in act nor in
intellect; which is a proof of the first and greatest passionlessness.

CHAPTER 11. And concerning the demon who makes the soul without sensation,
what need is there to speak? For I have feared even to write concerning him,
how the soul stands outside its own state; for at the time of his visitation,
it strips off both the fear of God and reverence =79.1213=, and it reckons sin
not to be sin, and it does not consider lawlessness to be lawlessness, and it
remembers punishment and eternal judgment as a bare word, and it truly laughs
at a fire-bearing earthquake, and it confesses God, as it were, but what he
has commanded it does not understand; you strike upon the breast, while it is
being moved toward sin, and it does not perceive. You converse from the
Scriptures, and it is wholly hardened, and does not hear; you bring to it the
reproach from men, and it does not reckon the shame among men, and it wholly
does not understand, like a pig that has shut its eyes and is breaking through
the fence; and thoughts of vainglory, having lingered, bring on this demon,
whose days, if they had not been shortened, no flesh would have been saved.
And of those who rarely visit the brothers, the cause also is clear; for upon
the misfortunes of others, either those pressed in diseases, or those
unfortunate in prisons, or those falling into sudden deaths, this one is put
to flight, the soul being pricked little by little, and coming into sympathy,
and the demonic hardness being dissolved, of which we are in want because of
the desert, and the scarcity of those who are sick among us.



CHAPTER 12. The Lord, putting this demon especially to flight, commanded in
the Gospels to see the sick, and to visit those in prison; "For I was sick,"
he says, "and you visited me, and in prison and you came to me." Except this
must be known: if any of the anchorites, having fallen in with this demon, has
not received thoughts of fornication, or has not left the house of acedia,
this one has received chastity and patience come down from the heavens, and he
is blessed who has been deemed worthy of such passionlessness. But as many as
profess piety, and choose to dwell with worldly people, let them guard against
this demon. For I am ashamed to say or write anything more concerning him,
even before men.

CHAPTER 13. All the demons teach the soul to be pleasure-loving; but only the
demon of grief does not endure to do this, but also destroys the thoughts of
those that have entered, cutting off every pleasure and drying it through
grief. If indeed the bones of a grieving man are dried up, and warring
moderately he makes the anchorite proven; for he persuades him to admit
nothing of this world, and to stand aside from every pleasure, but remaining
longer, he begets thoughts counseling the soul to withdraw itself, or
compelling to flee fa =79.1216= r from the places; which the holy Job also
once reckoned and suffered, being troubled by this demon; "For would that I
might," he says, "lay hands on myself, or having begged another, and he will
do this for me." The symbol of this demon is the viper, the beast, whose
nature, being given in a humane way, destroys the poisons of the other beasts,
but being taken without restraint also destroys the animal itself. To this
demon Paul delivered the one who had transgressed in Corinth, wherefore he
also writes again earnestly; "Confirm," saying to the Corinthians, "love
toward him, lest perhaps such a one be swallowed up by the more abundant
grief." But he knows this spirit that afflicts men also to become a procurer
of good repentance for them; whence also holy John the Baptist, to those stung
by this demon and fleeing to God: "Offspring of vipers," he says, "who showed
you to flee from the coming wrath? Produce therefore fruit worthy of
repentance, and do not think to say among yourselves, We have Abraham as
father; for I say to you that God is able from these stones to raise up
children to Abraham." Except every one who has imitated Abraham, and has gone
out from his land and from his kindred, has himself also become stronger than
this demon.

CHAPTER 14. If anyone has mastered anger, this one has mastered demons; but if
anyone has been enslaved to this passion, this one is wholly alien to the
monastic life, and a stranger to the ways of our Savior; if indeed our Lord
himself is said to teach the meek his ways; wherefore also the mind of those
withdrawing becomes hard to hunt, fleeing into the plain of meekness; for the
demons fear almost no virtue so much as meekness; for that Moses also
possessed this, being called meek beyond all men. And holy David also declared
this to be worthy of the memory of God; "Remember, Lord," saying, "David, and
all his meekness." But also the Savior himself commanded us to become
imitators of his meekness; "Learn from me," saying, "that I am meek and humble
in heart, and you will find rest for your souls." But if anyone should abstain
from foods and drinks, yet stirs anger with wicked thoughts, this one is like
a ship sailing the sea and having a demon as helmsman, wherefore one must
attend, as much as power,



to our dog, and teach it to destroy only the wolves, and not to eat the sheep,
showing all meekness toward all men.

CHAPTER 15. Alone among the thoughts, that of vainglory is of many materials,
and encompasses almost the whole inhabited world, and opens the doors to all
the demons, as =79.1217= some wicked traitor of a good city. Wherefore it also
greatly humbles the mind of the one withdrawing, filling it with many words
and affairs, and spoiling his prayers through which he is eager to heal all
the wounds of his soul. All the demons that have been defeated increase this
thought together, and again through it all receive entrance into the souls,
making the last things truly worse than the first. And from this thought is
also born that of pride, which shook down from heaven to earth the seal of
likeness and the crown of beauty. But leap away from this, and do not linger,
lest we betray our life to others, and our life to the merciless. And intense
prayer puts this demon to flight, and doing willingly nothing of the things
that contribute toward the accursed glory. When the mind of those withdrawing
has laid hold of a little passionlessness, then having acquired the horse of
vainglory, it immediately drives through the cities, filling itself with
unmixed praise from the glory, whom, according to economy, the spirit of
fornication having met, and having shut this one into one of the pigsties,
teaches him no longer to leave the bed before complete health, nor to imitate
the disorderly of the sick, who, still carrying about remnants of the weakness
in themselves, give themselves to roads and untimely baths, and fall in with
the diseases from relapse. Wherefore sitting rather let us attend to
ourselves, as, progressing in virtue, we become hard to move toward vice, and
being renewed in knowledge, we take on a multitude of various contemplations,
and being raised again according to prayer we shall more clearly behold the
light of our Savior.

CHAPTER 16. I am not able to write all the wicked devices of the demons, and I
am ashamed to catalogue their evil arts, fearing the simpler of those who will
read. Except hear also the cunning of the spirit of fornication. When someone
has acquired passionlessness of the desiring part, and the shameful thoughts
have thereafter become rather cold, then it introduces men and women playing
with one another, and makes the anchorite a spectator of shameful things and
postures. But this temptation is not of those that linger; for intense prayer,
and a most narrow diet with vigil, and exercise of spiritual contemplations,
drives it like a waterless cloud. And there is a time when it also touches the
flesh, prying it toward irrational burning, and this all-wicked one contrives
also certain other countless things, which it is not necessary to publish and
deliver to writing. And heat of anger moved against the demon contributes
greatly against the thoughts, which anger he especially fears being troubled
at the thoughts, and destroying his =79.1220= intellections; and this is the,
"Be angry, and do not sin." This is a useful drug brought to the soul in such
temptations. And the [demon] of anger also imitates this demon, and he too
shapes some of those who have begotten, or friends, or relatives, or household
members being insulted and struck by the unworthy, and he stirs the anger of
those withdrawing, so as to speak something wicked, or to do toward those
appearing according to the intellect, to whom it is necessary not to attend,
and quickly to snatch the mind from such



idols, lest having lingered in them, it become at the time of prayer like a
smoking firebrand. And the wrathful fall in with these temptations, and
especially those easily kindled toward angers, who are far from pure prayer,
and from the knowledge of our Savior Jesus Christ.

CHAPTER 17. The Lord has delivered the intellections of this age, as certain
sheep, to the good shepherd, the man; for also, he says, he has given the age
in his heart; having yoked anger and desire to him for help, so that, through
anger, he may put to flight the intellections of the wolves, and through
desire he may love the sheep, even when often struck by rains and winds. And
besides these he has also given him a pasture, so that he may shepherd the
sheep, and a place of green grass, and water of rest, and a psaltery, and a
kithara, and a rod, and a staff, so that from this flock he may both be fed
and clothed, and gather mountain grass. "For who," he says, "shepherds a
flock, and does not eat from the milk of the flock?" Therefore the one
withdrawing must guard this flock by night and by day, lest any of the
intellections become prey to wild beasts, or fall in with robbers, and if
indeed some such thing should happen in the glen, immediately to snatch it
from the mouth of the lion or the bear. And the intellection concerning the
brother becomes prey to wild beasts, if it should graze among us with hatred,
and that concerning the woman, if it should turn among us with shameful
desire, and that of silver and of gold, if it should lodge with greed. And the
intellections of the holy gifts, if they should feed according to the
intellect with vainglory; and it will happen likewise also upon the other
intellections, being stolen by the passions.

CHAPTER 18. And one must watch these things not only in the day, but also
guard them by night, keeping vigil. For it happens also that, imagining
shamefully and wickedly, one lose one's own; and this is what is said by holy
Jacob: "I have not brought you a sheep that was prey to wild beasts; I paid
back from myself thefts of day, and the =79.1221= fts of night, and I became
burned with the heat of the day, and with the frost of night, and sleep
departed from my eyes?" And if from the toil acedia should also come upon us,
having run up a little upon the rock of knowledge let us converse with the
psaltery, striking through the virtues of knowledge the strings, and let us
again pasture the sheep under Mount Sinai, so that the God of our fathers may
call us also from the bush, and may grant also to us the words of the signs
and of the wonders. Christ raises a rational nature that has been put to death
by vice through the contemplation of all the ages; but his Father raises this
soul that has died the death of Christ, through knowledge of himself. And this
is what is said by Paul, "If we have died with Christ, we believe that we
shall also live with him." When the mind, having stripped off the old man,
puts on the one from grace, then it will also see its own state at the time of
prayer, resembling sapphire or heavenly color, which Scripture also names a
place of God, seen by the elders under Mount Sinai.

CHAPTER 19. When one of the enemies, having approached, has wounded you, and
you wish to turn his sword, according to what is written, upon his heart, do
thus, as we say. Divide according to yourself the thought that has been thrown
by him, whichever it is, and of how many things it consists, and which this is
especially that afflicts the mind. And what I say is of this sort: Let the
thought of love of money have been sent by him,



divide this into the mind that received it, and into the intellection of gold,
and into the gold itself, and into the money-loving passion; and then ask
which of these is sin: is it the mind? and how? it is an image of God. But the
intellection of gold? and who having a mind would ever say this? but is the
gold itself sin? and for what sake has it come to be? It follows then, that
which is not a thing existing according to essence, nor an intellection of a
thing, nor again an incorporeal mind, but some man-hating pleasure, being born
from free will, and compelling the mind to use the creatures of God badly,
which also the law of God is believed to circumcise. And as you investigate
these things, the thought will be destroyed, being resolved into its own
contemplation, and the demon will flee from you, your intellect being raised
to height by this knowledge.

CHAPTER 20. And if you wish to use that one's sword, and you first desire to
master this one through your sling. Throw also you a stone from your
shepherd's bag, and seek the contemplation of this. How angels and demons
appro =79.1224= ach our world, but we do not approach their worlds; for
neither are we able to join angels more to God, nor do we choose to make
demons more unclean. And how Lucifer who rises at dawn was thrown down to the
earth, and has considered the sea as an ointment-box, and Tartarus of the
abyss as a captive, and makes the abyss boil as a cauldron, troubling all
through his vice, and wishing to rule all? For the contemplation of these
things greatly wounds the demon, and puts all his encampment to flight. But
these things happen only to those who have been somewhat purified, and who see
in some measure the reasons of the things that have come to be. But the
unclean do not know the contemplation of these things, nor if they should sing
them having learned from others will they be heard, much dust and tumult being
constituted through the passions according to the war. For the encampment of
the aliens must altogether be somewhat still, so that Goliath alone may meet
our David. And likewise let us use also the division, and this form of the
war, also upon all unclean thoughts.

CHAPTER 21. Of the unclean demons, some tempt the man as a man, and some
trouble the man as an irrational animal, and the first, approaching, throw
into us intellections of vainglory, or pride, or envy, or accusation, which
touch none of the irrational; but the second, drawing near, move anger or
desire contrary to nature. For these passions are common to us and to the
irrational animals, being covered by the rational nature. Wherefore the Holy
Spirit also says, toward those falling in with the human thoughts, "I said:
You are gods, and sons of the Most High all; but you die as men, and fall as
one of the rulers." And toward those moved irrationally, what does he say? "Do
not become as horse and mule, in which there is no understanding, with bit and
bridle to squeeze their jaws, of those who do not draw near to you." And the
soul that sins, this will die. It is clear that those who die as men as men
will be buried by men, but those being put to death as irrational, or falling,
will be eaten by vultures or ravens, of whom the young, some call upon the
Lord, and some are mixed in bloods. He who has ears to hear, let him hear.

CHAPTER 22. When some of the unclean thoughts are quickly put to flight, let
us seek the cause whence this has happened; whether because of the scarcity of
the



thing, the material being hard to provide, or because of the passionlessness
present to us the enemies have not had strength against us? For example, if
one of those withdrawing should consider, by a demon, to be entrusted with the
spiritual government of the first city =79.1225=, it is clear that this one
will not linger imagining this thought, and the cause becomes known from what
has been said; but if someone of a city, even of a chance one, comes to be,
and likewise thinks, this one is blessed for passionlessness. And likewise
also upon the other thoughts such a manner will be found when examined. And it
is necessary to know these things toward our eagerness and power, so that we
may know whether we have passed the Jordan and are near the city of the palms,
or still we dwell in the desert and are struck by the aliens. For the demon of
love of money appears to me very various, and well-devised toward deception,
who often, being straitened by the extreme renunciation, immediately pretends
the steward and the lover of the poor, and receives more genuinely the
strangers not yet present, and sends service to others who are lacking, and
visits the prisons of a city, and as it were buys those being sold, and
attaches himself to rich women, and points out those who ought to be well
treated, and again admonishes others who have acquired a stout purse to
renounce, and thus having deceived the soul little by little, he subjects it
to thoughts of love of money, and delivers it to the demon of vainglory

CHAPTER 23. as he introduces a multitude of those glorifying the Lord for
these stewardships, and putting forward some talking together little by little
about priesthood he then foretells a quick death of the present priest, and
adds that he would not escape having done countless things, and thus the

are thereafter within, and the thoughts are turning, immediately also the
demon of pride presents himself, forming continuous lightnings according to
the air of the cell, and sending winged dragons, and, the last evil, working
deprivation of wits, but we, having prayed destruction upon these thoughts,
shall live together with poverty with thanksgiving, "For we have brought
nothing into the world, nor is it clear that we are able to carry anything
out; and having foods and coverings, we shall be content with these."
remembering also Paul, who said love of money is a root of all evils. All the
unclean thoughts, lingering in us because of the passions, bring the mind down
to ruin and destruction; for as the intellection of bread lingers in the
hungry because of hunger, and the intellection of water in the thirsty because
of thirst, so also the intellections of possessions and of money linger
because of greed, and the intellections of foods, and of the =79.1228=
shameful thoughts born from the foods, linger because of the passions. But
also upon the thoughts of vainglory, and upon the other thoughts it will
likewise be made manifest. And it is not [possible] for a mind being choked by
such things to stand before God, and to bind on the crown of righteousness.
For from these thoughts that thrice-wretched mind in the Gospels also, being
dragged down, declined the feast of the knowledge of Christ; and again the one
being bound hand and foot and being thrown into the outer darkness had from
these thoughts the garment woven, whom the one who called declared not to be
worthy of such a wedding; wherefore a wedding garment is passionlessness of a
rational soul that has denied worldly desires. What



is the cause of the intellections of sensible things, lingering, destroying
the knowledge has been said in the chapters concerning prayer.

CHAPTER 24. Of the demons opposing the practical life, there are three leading
chiefs, whom all the encampment of the aliens follows, who first stand
according to the war, and call the souls through the unclean thoughts toward
vice: those entrusted with the appetites of gluttony, those suggesting love of
money to us, and those calling us toward the glory of men. Desiring therefore
pure prayer, keep anger, and loving chastity, master the belly, do not give
bread to your belly, and squeeze it in water, keep vigil in prayer, and make
resentment far from you, let words of the Holy Spirit not fail you, and knock
doors with hands of virtues. Then passionlessness of heart will rise for you,
and you will see a starlike mind in prayer. Of the intellections, some stamp
our ruling faculty and shape it, and some furnish knowledge only, not stamping
the mind nor shaping it. For the, "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word
was with God," deposits some intellection in the heart, yet does not shape it,
nor stamp it. The, "having taken bread," shapes the mind, and the, "to break,"
again stamps the mind. And the, "I saw the Lord sitting upon a throne high and
lifted up," stamps the mind apart from the, "I saw the Lord," and the literal
seems to stamp the mind, but that which is signified does not stamp. For he
has seen with a prophetic eye the rational nature raised through practical
life, receiving in itself the knowledge of God. For God is said to sit there
where he is known, wherefore also a pure mind is called a throne of God. And a
woman is also called a throne of dishonor, instead of, a soul that hates just
things, and dishonor of soul is vice and ignorance. The intellection of God,
then, is not in those that stamp the mind, but in the intellections that do
not stamp the mind. Wherefore the one praying must alto =79.1229= gether be
separated from those that stamp the mind. And you will seek, whether, as it is
upon bodies and their thoughts, so it is also upon the incorporeals and their
reasons, and otherwise the mind will be stamped seeing a mind, and otherwise
it will be disposed seeing its reason? And from this we know how spiritual
knowledge withdraws the mind from the intellections that stamp it, and
presents it unstamped to God, because the intellection of God is not in those
that stamp; for God is not a body, but rather in those that do not stamp.
Again of the contemplations that do not stamp the mind, some signify essence
of incorporeals, and some their reasons; and it does not happen as upon
bodies, so also upon the incorporeals; for upon bodies some stamped the mind,
and some did not stamp; but here neither intellection stamps the mind.

CHAPTER 25. When the demon of gluttony, having contended much and often, has
not had strength to destroy the stamped self-control, then he throws the mind
into a desire of most extreme ascesis, from which he also brings into the
midst the things concerning Daniel, that poor life, and the seeds, and he
remembers certain other anchorites who have lived thus through all, or who
have begun, and he compels [one] to become an imitator of these; so that
pursuing the measureless self-control, he may fail even of the symmetry, the
body not sufficing through its own weakness, truly blessing with the body and
cursing with the heart; whom I think it just not to obey in these things, nor
to abstain from bread and oil and water. For the brothers have tried this diet
as very finest, and this not to satiety, and once in the day. For I marvel if
anyone, being filled with bread and water, will be able to receive the crown
of



passionlessness. And I say passionlessness, not the dissolution of sin in act,
for this is called self-control, but that which cuts off the passionate
thoughts according to the intellect, which also holy Paul named a spiritual
circumcision of the hidden Jew. And if anyone is disheartened at what has been
said, let him remember the vessel of election, the Apostle, who finished the
course in hunger and thirst. And the opponent of truth, the demon of acedia,
also imitates this demon, suggesting most extreme withdrawal to the enduring
one, calling into zeal of John the Baptist, and of the first-fruit of the
anchorites, Antony, so that, not having borne the long and inhuman withdrawal,
he may flee with shame, having left the place, and he himself thereafter
boasting may say, "I have had strength against him." =79.1232=

CHAPTER 26. The unclean thoughts receive materials into increase, and extend
together with many things; for they also cross great seas according to the
intellect, and they do not refuse to travel long roads because of much heat of
the passion; but those who have been somehow purified are rather narrower than
these, not being able to extend together with things because of the weakness
of the passion, whence they are also moved more contrary to nature, and,
according to the wise Solomon, wandering outside for some time, and gathering
straw for the lawless brickmaking, so that they may be saved as a gazelle from
snares, and as a bird from a trap. For it is easier to cleanse an unclean
soul, than, having been cleansed and again wounded, to call it back again to
health, the demon of grief not allowing, but always bringing the image of the
sin to the pupils at the time of prayer.

CHAPTER 27. The demons do not know our hearts, as some of men suppose; for
there is only one knower of hearts, who knows the mind of men, and who formed
their hearts one by one; but from the uttered word, and from such-and-such
movements of the body, they know most of the movements in the heart. Which I
now wished to make clear, but the holy priest restrained me, saying it is
unworthy for such things to be published, and thrown into the hearing of the
profane; if indeed, he says, even the one who associates with the menstruous,
according to the law, becomes liable. Except that from such signs they
recognize the things hidden in the heart, and take the starting-points from
these against us, often at any rate we have convicted some who have spoken
