* On the Palm Branches

Of Leontius, presbyter of Constantinople, a discourse on the Palms.
It befits the initiates of piety not only to embrace grace, but also to clasp
the law; for the things which grace confirmed, these the law also proclaimed
beforehand; and the things which the law sketched beforehand, these grace also
sealed. And of this the Lord himself is witness, who both kindled the law as a
lamp and lit grace as a torch, saying to the Jews: If you believed Moses, you
would believe me; for concerning me that man wrote the things he had learned
from me. And that it is one and the same Lord, who both through the prophets
cried out the things to come and through the evangelists confirmed the things
spoken beforehand, take the proof from the things just now read. You were just
now hearing the evangelist John crying out: On the next day a great crowd that
had come to the feast, having heard that Jesus is coming to Jerusalem, took
the palm-fronds of the palms and went out to meet him and cried: Hosanna,
blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord, King of Israel.
Fitting indeed for the Savior is the hymnody, proper for the trophy-bearer is
the blessing. A victory-song is the crowd's melody; for already they have
caught the scent of the life-bearing fragrance of Christ, which makes plain
the defeat of death. Blessed is he who comes to give us authority to tread
upon serpents and scorpions and upon all the power of the enemy, not by
firmness of feet, but by the foundation of faith. Blessed is he who comes in
the name of the Lord, that he may also pay the same wage to those from the
first hour and to those working at the eleventh hour in this spiritual
vineyard of his; for since he is good, a Jewish evil eye does not trouble us.
Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord to dissolve the diabolic
middle-wall and to join the earthly things to the heavenly; for where there is
one kingdom, there also every tyranny is dissolved.
Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord, King of Israel. Why has
Christ been shown as King of Israel only, and not of all the ends of the
earth? Hear with understanding. Israel is interpreted mind seeing God. As many
therefore as see God from the mind, these are ruled by the Master Christ. For
Christ does not call by necessity, but persuades by disposition, as it is
possible to hear him saying: I love those who love me, and those who seek me
shall find grace. Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord. The crowd
took palm-fronds of palms, that they might acclaim Christ the palm of
righteousness. For concerning him the prophet was saying: The righteous shall
blossom as a palm, as the cedar in Lebanon he shall be multiplied.
Why is the Master Christ addressed as palm and cedar? Because of the passion
of the flesh, which he willingly consented to endure through the cross, and
because of the fruitfulness of the godhead. For even when his flesh suffered
the godhead remained impassible, as the prophet says concerning the flesh of
the Lord in the passion: We saw him and he had no form nor beauty; but his
form was dishonored, failing beside all men, he was dishonored and was not
reckoned. And again he is named cedar because of the height of the godhead and
the incorruption of the manhood. For even if he was buried, yet his flesh did
not see corruption; rather he diminished the diabolic moths and remained
sound.
This blessed acclaiming voice of the crowd the Master Christ, having foreseen
from many times past with the unerring gaze of the godhead, was crying through
Isaiah the prophet, saying: All the beasts of the field shall bless me, sirens
and daughters of ostriches. And some will surely say that here the Master
Christ was discoursing about beasts of the field and sirens and ostriches, and
not about men and women and children. Hear with understanding; for nothing
will be said by us without proof. For in many places the divine scripture
knows to call men turning from wickedness beasts, making the name not from
nature but from the deed.
[And of these the Lord himself is witness, saying concerning Herod: Go and say
to this fox, not because Herod was a fox, for he was a king, but he was
receiving the name from his knowledge and intent. Just as the fox goes up one
road and goes about another, making a twisted course of travel, in the same
way also Herod was speaking some things with his lips, but was speaking other,
diabolic things in his heart, accomplishing nothing. And let the blessed Paul
also persuade you, triumphing over Cretans and saying himself: Cretans are
always liars, untamed beasts, idle bellies. Why? A speechless serpent once
having fastened on the hand of the blessed Paul did not even harm him, but the
Cretans, the man-shaped beasts, having run upon the blessed Paul strove to
wrap him in death, and this though they were often being healed by him. Thus
also the prophet Daniel, not being harmed by the lions but being honored as a
friend, showed them to be fasters; and they were honoring him, but wicked men
were taking no satiety of slandering him. Thus also John the Baptist, having
been served by wild beasts in the desert, but having been beheaded in the city
by wicked men, and again triumphing over the beast-souled, was saying: Brood
of vipers, who showed you to flee from the coming wrath? If you have wholly
turned and vomited out the poison of prophet-murder, make fruits worthy of
repentance. For this the Lord said on our behalf: The beasts of the field
shall bless me. He calls beasts the men turning from wickedness, and field the
present world.
And of this the Lord himself is witness, saying: The kingdom of the heavens
was likened to a man, a householder, sowing good seed in his field. And
interpreting the parable the Lord was saying: The field is the world. He calls
beasts the men turning from wickedness, who were the saints as blessing
Christ. And sirens are the souls of the saints, who having crossed the surge
of life cry out each day the song of thanksgiving and say: We went through
fire and water, and you brought us out into refreshment. And daughters of
ostriches he calls the holy women catechized from the prophets, whom the hawk
of perdition, the devil, cannot even snatch, who are Martha and Mary, sisters
of Lazarus, who just now warbled to us with the mouth as with spirit and like
certain nightingales announced the good news of the resurrection of the
Savior. And the word hints that ostriches are the prophets; and of these David
is witness, crying out: I became as a sparrow dwelling alone upon a housetop.
Why? That Saul, the vulture of envy, might not wrong me. And again the same
prophet, answering the diabolic ravens who were counseling vain things to him,
said: How will you say to my soul: Flee upon the mountains as a sparrow? For
behold the great eagle, God the Word, covers with the wings of the
incarnation.]
But that we may not spend the discourse in digressions, come, let us also in
concert, having gathered up the palm-fronds of the palms, that is the robes of
the victory against the devil (for palms are significant of victory), greatly
acclaim with mouth and heart Christ the president of the contest, him who sits
economically upon a colt of an ass, but rests in a manner befitting God upon
the cherubim and the seraphim; for where there is the form of a servant, there
a little ass is the bearer; but where there is the form of God, there the
Father's bosom is the one that warms. You were just now hearing the evangelist
saying concerning this also: And Jesus, having found a little ass, sat upon
it. O the paradoxical things!
For was the Master Christ not able, he who has heaven as a throne and the
earth as a footstool, who by a nod brought all things that are from not-being
into being, to set beside him a multitude of white mules, or pearl-like
gold-inlaid chariots, or a herd of variously caparisoned horses, or a quiet
running-together of fair-formed spear-bearers, or a well-rhythmed going-before
of distinguished rulers, or all the rest of the attendance which the friends
of the world gladly drag about? But he goes after none of these. Why? That he
may not cut off the economy, that the achievement may not be ascribed to
display, that those who believe may run not to the appearance but to the deed,
that the children of Jews and Greeks may not be about to say that the kingly
dignity of the one who arrived, or the honor, or the bodyguard, or the
display, persuaded the more simple, and not the truth of the things. That
therefore every worthless mouth may be stopped and all the world may become
liable to God, for this reason the Master Christ, having sat upon a cheap
little ass and having chosen only twelve cheap disciples, unlearned, poor,
hunters of speechless fish, hooked the whole inhabited world through them, not
having cast a line into the deep, but having sent a word into the world. What
word? Which Lazarus having heard leapt out of the tomb, the dead man having
opened the hearing not by sequence of nature but by energy of godhead.
For you were just now hearing the evangelist saying: Six days before the
Pascha Jesus came to Bethany, where Lazarus was who had died, whom he raised
from the dead. Six days before the Pascha. Hear the day, you who long for the
day of the Lord. Six days before the Pascha. Number the days, that you may
enjoy the day. Near is the coming, the night has advanced, and the day has
drawn near; the winter of ignorance has passed, the spring of knowledge has
peeped in. Finish the course of the Forty Days athletically and wait for the
crown of the feast. For it is good to remind you also of this, that the Forty
Days have been fulfilled with God's goodwill, and the week of the Lord's
passion has been opened from today; for it was not when the Master Christ
fasted that he also suffered. Hear with understanding.
For he fasted after the baptism, when having boxed with the devil he threw him
down and granted the victory to Adam. For our Master Christ did not suffer for
himself, but for the one lying below; nor did he fast for himself, but for the
one who swallowed down the disobedience; nor was he baptized for himself, but
for the one soiled by sin. Therefore our Lord Jesus Christ fasted after the
baptism. But nevertheless let us inquire this too in a digression, why we fast
before baptism, but the Master Christ fasted after the baptism. Why? Because
we, swollen with sins and loaded in the soul's belly, necessarily receive the
fast before baptism as a God-given antidote, that having cleansed ourselves
from every defilement of flesh and spirit we may come into appetite for the
heavenly bread. But the Master Christ as free from sin fasts after the
baptism, granting a type, not lamenting sin. For if our Master Christ was
called curse and sin, yet nevertheless he himself has not been soiled, but he
took up our sins; and the surety is liable for anything whatsoever not as
principal but as mediator.
Whence, having taken up Adam's debt and paid it, he also wiped out the
handwriting of our sins on the cross with the sponge of love of mankind and
did not engrave it. And why did he wipe it out and not engrave it? Why? Hear.
If he had engraved it and not wiped it out, it would have been about to bear
even a small remnant of the former debt. Whence having seen this the blessed
Paul also was crying, saying: Christ has taken the handwriting of our sins out
of the midst, having nailed it to the cross. And why did he nail it to the
cross? That every passer-by may learn its cancellation.
Therefore our Master Christ fasted after the baptism, but he suffered this
voluntary passion not immediately after the baptism, but after a three-year
time, the thing signified as six days before, that which is destructive of
every passion, that which is the bridal-arraying of the church. He suffered
this passion then after having worked many and wonderful signs, after raising
the dead, after binding up paralytics, after giving eyes to the blind and
cleansing lepers. But nevertheless the apostles, having been filled with Holy
Spirit, having joined these two festivals, the fast and the passion, handed
down one public feast to the race of Christians.
Let us exult therefore and be glad, that six days before the Pascha Jesus came
to Bethany. Why? That he might sting the Jews and save the nations. Six days
before the Pascha Jesus came to Bethany. Why? That he might fulfill the
mystery and open the mercy-seat, that he might dine with Lazarus and make the
burial fragrant beforehand, that he might be tried as a servant and be set
free as a son. Six days before the Pascha Jesus came to Bethany. Why? That he
might be scourged for the condemned and as a lover of mankind set them free
from scourges. Six days before the Pascha Jesus came to Bethany. Why? That
having been pierced in the side with a spear he might heal the side of Adam,
that Judas the Iscariot might price the material myrrh at three hundred
denarii, but might agree upon the Master Christ for thirty pieces of silver.
Six days before the Pascha Jesus came to Bethany. Why? That he might root the
cross and make the seal blossom, that he might block up Hades and open
paradise. Six days before the Pascha Jesus came to Bethany. Why? To wear a
crown of thorns and to uproot our sins, that he might take leave of the
faithful and journey to the Father.
Six days before the Pascha Jesus came to Bethany, where Lazarus was who had
died, who cannot be sent off into forgetfulness, the chief citizen of a city,
who having died once was sent a second time into life, who was stolen by
natural death and raised by the Master's grace, who was snatched by the tyrant
Tartarus and torn away by the King Christ, who was defiled by disease and
renewed by a word. For you all know clearly, the friends of Lazarus, or rather
the disciples of the Lord, that when Lazarus had died and been given over to a
tomb, after four days Jesus came to Bethany, that he might wake Lazarus. For
he had fallen asleep in his keeping, for he was also his friend, as doing the
things of friends.
And Martha and Mary the sisters of Lazarus came to the Savior. Mary, being
simple, kept quiet, for the woman was more understanding; but Martha, having
leapt out first and as being in grief, was entreating the Lord, crying: Lord,
if you had been here my brother would not have died; but even now I know that
whatever you ask God, God will give you. And the Lord to Martha: Martha, why
do you utter things that have gone wrong? Why do you not know what you are
saying? You hinder your brother, you do not permit him to rise, you are drunk
with grief. Not yet has Arius sprouted and you bring forth a tare of
weak-godhead, saying: If you had been here my brother would not have died. Was
I not here, Martha? Ask my disciples and learn that even if I was absent in
the body, I was present in the spirit. For as soon as your brother died and
the angel who was with him brought up his translation to me as to a king,
immediately I said to my disciples: Lazarus our friend has fallen asleep, but
I go to wake him. You are speaking to God, Martha: If you had been here my
brother would not have died. Cease then, Martha, I pardon you who are erring,
partly because of nature, partly because of the affliction.
Your brother shall rise. Martha says to him, still stumbling in the faith: I
know, Lord, that my brother shall rise on the last day, when the gift is
universal, and not a particular grace. And Christ to her: Why do you journey
far, O Martha? I am the resurrection and the life. If you believe, entreat;
but if you disbelieve, go away, woman; for I do not permit you to teach. Do
you believe this? Martha says to him: Yes, Lord; for from when you began to
speak to your servant, I have become another instead of another. Then the Lord
answering said to those present: Where have you laid him? They say to him:
Come and see. And Jesus was going as not knowing the tomb, he who was carrying
the soul of Lazarus; for he was going not as being ignorant of the tomb, but
as wishing to draw more witnesses of the resurrection.
Then as Jesus was going Martha again having been baited came to the Savior
saying: Lord, where are you going? Already he stinks, for he is of the fourth
day. Where are you going? You are about to be laughed at, it is not possible
for this man to rise. Already he stinks, for he is of the fourth day. The
hairs have fallen, the fleshes have been diminished, the veins have been
dissolved, the fleshes of the bones have denied their nature. Lord, where are
you going? We are becoming a laughing-stock; worms are feeding, ichors are
pooling. You raised the widow's son, Master, since the dead man was fresh and
still his soul was hovering about the body and the flesh possessed a moisture
of warmth. But this man already stinks, for he is of the fourth day. Even if
you yourself wish it, yet the soul does not consent to enter into a stinking
vessel. Already he stinks, for he is of the fourth day.
And Christ to Martha: Did I not say to you that if you believe you shall see
the glory of God, and you said: Yes, Lord, I have believed that you are the
Christ the Son of God, who is coming into the world? Who baited you again not
to believe? Did you meet with Eve? Did you encounter the serpent? Cease then,
weak-souled Martha, and know that no word is impossible with God. You are
speaking to God: Already he stinks. When does myrrh feel shame before a
stench? A musical man stretches a slackened lyre, and God does not raise a
dissolved dead man?
Having said these things the Lord came, it says, to the cave and Jesus, having
seen the tomb, wept. O the things! He wept, the remover of tears. And why did
the Lord weep for him whom he was about to raise? Why? That he might both
confirm the truth of the incarnation, and present the sympathy, and make known
the authority of the godhead. Jesus wept. Why? That he might fulfill the
saying that declares: To weep with those who weep and to rejoice with those
who rejoice. Jesus wept. But if one must say more exactly why Jesus wept, he
wept not for Lazarus, but for the unbelieving Jews.
Then Jesus says to those present: Roll away the stone from the monument. What
then, Master, are you yourself not able to do this? You raise a dead man and
you do not roll a stone? Did you not yourself say to your disciples: If you
have faith as a grain of mustard, you will say to this mountain: Be taken up
and be cast into the sea? You promise them a moving of mountains and you
yourself are not able to roll away a stone? Yes, I command not from weakness,
but from my own wisdom I order the Jews, so that they may be fully assured not
only by the sight and by the smell, that what is happening is truth and not
display, and that I am the Lord of dead and living.
Then after the rolling away of the stone the Lord cried out: To you I say,
Lazarus, come forth, that even thus Martha may cease saying: "Lord, if you had
been here my brother would not have died." Lazarus, come forth, that those
present may learn that I am not too weak to raise one who has been dissolved.
Lazarus, come forth; I have freed you from the tomb, do you send me forth upon
the cross. Lazarus, come forth, and henceforth as a friend dine with me. For
even if the children of Jews wish to kill you, yet I dine with you. I do not
give you over; for I came not to betray, but to lay down my soul for friends.
And that the children of Jews were wishing to kill Lazarus, they who are
always blood-loving, you were just now hearing the evangelist saying: And the
chief priests and the Pharisees took counsel that they might kill Lazarus
also, because many of the Jews were going away on account of him and were
believing in Jesus. O the madness of the chief priests, or rather of the
un-priests. For him whom Tartarus shuddering gave back, these were wishing to
wrap again in death. Why, O Jews, do you attempt to kill Lazarus? Does he too
loose the sabbath? He announces the resurrection; and why do you wish to kill
him also? For if you kill him, Christ will raise him again. To him belongs the
glory unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
