CHRONOGRAPHIA OF SYMEON MAGISTER AND LOGOTHETE.
Year of the world 307, year of the divine incarnation 807, emperor of the
Romans Leo the Armenian, also called Chamaeleon, the apostate and
iconoclast, 7 years 5 months. He was short in stature (for which he was
also named Chamaeleon), rather stout, well looking, with a beard coming
in, thin of hair, having a voice thundering after the manner of a lion,
both bold and cowardly. He was of mixed race from Assyrians and Armenians;
and the Assyrians were named from Asshur, and the Armenians from a certain
Armenius so called, who came from Armenia, a city of Thessaly, who
campaigned with Jason. 604 2. This Leo, crowned by patriarch Nicephorus,
confirmed to him in writing that he was orthodox in the faith. But the
wolf hidden in the fleece did not escape the good shepherd; for when he
had set the diadem on that polluted head, he seemed to feel pain as from
the rubbing of thorns and burs. And when the customary order had been
completed, the wretch went off to the palace amid acclamations. And when
he came before the gates of the Chalke, on the circular purple marble
that is there to the eye, he rendered thanks to the image of Christ our
God that was there, whether truly or in pretence. There he puts off the
garment which he had put on, shining with rose-coloured hues, and the
colobium which they so name, according to the ancient custom kept among
the magistri militum; and he hands it to Michael of the Amorians, who
was his groom. And when the latter put it on, it was reckoned by those
who knew as an omen of his mounting the imperial power after Leo. Then
coming to the so-called Scyla, he insulted Leo's garment by a heavy
mounting. And Leo, coming to an understanding of this, took it as an omen
that some innovation would come upon him at once. (3) So it came into his
mind concerning the monk at Philomelium, who in the time of Bardanes had
told him the things that would happen. He therefore sends him offerings
for his need according to his prayer. Then, that man having died in the
meantime, it happened that instead of him there was another monk called
Sabbatios, who had also heard the former monk's words 605 by slipping into
his tent, and who set himself against the veneration of the holy images.
And seeing the things sent by the emperor he pretended to send them back
because the emperor venerated the holy images, in return for which he also
foretold by letter that the Lord would overthrow his empire. Deluded in
mind by these things, the emperor summons Theodotus Cassiteras, who even
before Leo laid hold of the empire, having heard a certain woman who had
a Pythonic spirit walking in the marketplace and crying that the
strategos of the Anatolics was about to receive the empire of Michael,
took Leo with him and went off to the church of Saint Paul in the
Orphanage, and having made covenants of protection toward one another,
that he should take heart toward him, that it has been made known to me
concerning you from a divine vision that you will soon attain the empire.
So having summoned this Theodotus he shows him the things sent; and he,
having seized on such a purpose, heaps evil on evil, and concerning a
certain monk who had his dwelling in the quarter of Maurianus he persuades
him that wonders were being worked by him, giving out that he was another
great Antony. Having suggested these things to the emperor he goes off to
the monk, and in a fabricated way says this, that on the coming night I
will bring the emperor to you in a mean dress, whom I advise you to curse
and to declare that he will soon pass from life, unless he should be
formed to the dogma of Leo the Isaurian. And that if he should keep this,
72 606 he would reign, and see children's children on the throne of the
empire, and thirteenth of the apostles



appear; but if not, the contrary, countless dreadful things and a swift
destruction. So when night came on the emperor went off with Cassiteras
to the monk, and at once he is boxed as if the monk had known beforehand
his pretence, saying thus, that you being emperor ought not so to play a
part in a private condition. By which the
emperor, struck with amazement, becomes obedient to him toward the
destruction of the holy images; and immediately vomiting out the venom
against piety that was in him and going mad he began the heresy. 4.
Seeking fellow initiates he finds John the so-called Grammarian, another
Jannes rather than Simon, who was also named Hylzilas, which in Hebrew is
interpreted forerunner and fellow worker of the devil; and he was son of
Pancratius a shadow-painter, and from childhood he had a demon, and he
was notorious for dish-divinations and shameful works. He found also
Constantine, a cobbler's presbyter, who had also been a notary at the
Forakion, and who, fleeing for crimes and becoming a monk, was renamed
Antony, a teller of tales and a jester and living shamefully; who also,
becoming abbot of the monastery of the Metropolitans, taught those under
him these things, and later by God's permission became bishop of Syllaion
as well. And he found also certain others of like mind with him. In the
mean 607 time a comet appeared in the shape of two bright lunar disks,
uniting and again
 dividing into various shapes, so as even to be formed into the fashion
of a headless man, such as also appeared in the time of the emperor
Michael the Curopalates; which certain men interpreting said that the
headless thing was a head, foreshowing Thomas who would afterward set
himself to rebellions, and the insurgents with him. And moreover fearful
earthquakes and plagues and droughts and burnings came to pass in the
days of the god-hated man. 5. In the same year he summons the celebrated
patriarch Nicephorus and the bishops with him before the senate, and
says "You know clearly that certain have risen up saying that the images
ought not to be venerated." To whom first Nicephorus, who is among the
saints, says "The sacred images of the saints, O emperor, are to be
honoured, as having appeared from the beginning by divine nod. For the
holy cloth which Christ once taking laid upon his face, and his form
came to be without hands, is to be honoured and venerable. Then the
images of the holy apostles made with their own hands, of the same
Christ and of her who bore him, and that of the Transfiguration kept
until now in Rome, and Christ appeared with Moses and Elias on Tabor;
which came to be at the bidding of Peter the chief. And also that which
came to pass in the city of Lydda of the East. For there is an ancient
ve 608 nerable temple in the name of the Theotokos raised by the
faithful, in which the form of the Theotokos is depicted; which the Jews
claiming as their own, the Mother of God hearing in Jerusalem said thus,
let the temple be shut and let no one enter, and on the 40th day let it
be opened; and if no sign be seen in it, let the temple be of the Jews.
So after the appointed time they opened and see the form of the
Theotokos holding Christ as an infant in her hands, a form and beauty
wondrous at once and most reverend and most God-befitting, in
translucent slabs, both clearly seen and gone through wholly into the
depth; which the Jews seeing fled. After this many Jews and Greeks
trying to scrape it away failed; and the form without hands remains
likewise until now on the slab to the glory of God. Besides these also
the image of the woman with the issue of blood, healing every passion
through the plant in Paneas and in all the earth." 6. After him the
sacred Euthymius, metropolitan of Sardis, speaking freely stopped the
wretch's mouth. And Theodore, abbot of Studius, said "Do not shake, O
emperor, an ecclesiastical constitution; for to you the constitution of
the state and of the army was entrusted to care for these, but leave the
church." And the tyrant, boiling over with wrath, drove them all out
with insults, and the



patriarch Nicephorus he banished, and 609 Theodore; when it is also said
that as patriarch Nicephorus was being sent into exile, Theophanes, the
so-called of Sigriane and president of the monastery of the Great Field,
saw him from afar with prophetic eyes, and burned incense and sent him
off with prayer, lifting his hands and committing him to God and
stretching out the greeting; and when someone asked, he said the cause,
that behold the great confessor Nicephorus the patriarch is passing in
exile, contending for the holy images. And the sacred Euthymius, as
having uttered the refutation of the heresy more sharply, he crushes
his jaws with blows and tears his back with scourges so much that he
expired under them. These things having happened, he ordains Theodotus
Cassiteras patriarch, an irrational man and more voiceless than fishes,
and fans openly an implacable persecution against the church, trusting
his fellow initiates, who suggested three things for him to do, the
denial of our orthodox faith, the destruction of the divine images, and
the persecution of the pious. And first he takes down every sacred
setting up of images in the churches, and from then on those found
having an image of Christ or of the saints he condemned to terrible
punishments and death, and he murdered many of those in glory and
eminence, and indeed countless monks as well. 7. And when he honoured
Michael of Amorium, whose daughter he had made his child from the 610
spiritual font, as patrician and excubitor, and the latter by the
boldness of his tongue reproached Leo as a destroyer of the holy images,
and that his wife was mixed in unholy marriages, taking him to be
plotting revolt he binds him in the palace bath called Pithekeion, and
orders him to be bound to a pole and given as fuel to the furnace fire;
and when he had been brought forward the emperor stood watching him. But
the empress Theodosia, beseeching with tears, delivered him from the
bloodshed, and the emperor ordered the accused to be let be. And to his
own wife he said "O woman, you have today released my soul from the
judgment there, perhaps you will also shortly deprive me of life; and
you and our children will see what will come of it." For the time of his
killing had been made known to him from a certain advisory book. For it
had a writing of this kind, an image of Leo a complete beast, having on
the head the letter Ch, and behind Ph, and in the middle of the beast
toward the loin itself a sword, that is a blade, stuck in and bloodied;
which showing to Stephen, surnamed Capitolines, he said to him "What do
these things mean?" And he said "Let it be, master, let me not even see
the writing." But he would not accept it. So Stephen, reasoning it out,
says to the emperor "This lion is set for the person of the empire, and
between the letter Ch and Ph some evil happens to him." And the 611
emperor "What is the Ph and the Ch?" And he says "Between the Nativity
of Christ and the Lights." But also from what was said to him through
the patrician Bardanes by the monk at Philomelium, and from the putting
on and mounting of the garments, when he was entering the palace at the
beginning. And besides, his mother's vision also filled him with fear,
which then when spoken was reckoned by him as nothing, but now was lying
heavily on his soul, biting it very much. For there appeared to her, as
she was always going to the divine temple in Blachernae, a maiden
surrounded by many in white, and the whole temple full of blood. So this
maiden said to one of those standing by, that filling a pot with blood
he should give it to Leo's mother to drink. And when she put forward her
great age, through which she had tasted neither meat nor any of things
with blood, and therefore had no need of this pot either, "And how,"
the maiden answered with anger, "does your son not cease filling me with
many bloods and provoking this my son and God?" And from then she often
besought her own son to stand off from the heresy of the image-burners,
making a tragedy of the vision itself. And another night vision
terrified him no



less than the first; for Tarasius the celebrated patriarch having long
since changed his life, he heard him call a certain Michael by name, and
leaping 612 upon him strike a lion a mortal blow and push him down a
precipice that had an abyss. 8. And from the days of the godless Leo the
Bulgarians came, no one meeting or hindering them, as far as the gate;
and Krum, making a sacrifice according to his custom outside the Golden
Gate, sacrificed men and many cattle; and wetting his feet at the
seashore and washing himself round, and sprinkling his people and being
acclaimed by them, he passed through the midst of his concubines,
venerated by them and glorified, and all watching these things from the
wall, and no one daring to hinder him or to let fly a missile against
him. And having done all his desires and whatever he wished, he
surrounded the city and threw a palisade against it, and having spent
certain days and plundered the parts outside the city he began to seek
tribute of gold and a great number of garments and a certain quantity of
choice girls. And Leo, having taken counsel with the officers, sent word
to Krum that come as far as the shore with a few men not carrying arms,
and we will come out through the sea unarmed with a chelandion, and we
will talk together, and whatever you have asked we will do all. And
having arranged these things, bringing out through the night certain
armed men, three, they hid them in certain rooms of Galle outside the
gate of 613 Blachernae, giving them a signal that while we are talking
with Krum, we have a certain sign to make, which seeing come out and
kill him. And on the morrow, the Bulgarians being toward the quarter of
the Holy Unmercenaries outside the city, Krum came down to the sea
according to the agreement with three others, his logothete and
Constantine the so-called of Patzicus who had fled to Bulgaria many
years before, having also his son who was of a sister of Krum; some four
coming out from their people unarmed came down as far as the sea
according to agreement, not knowing the ambush against them. Then those
from the city also came out with a chelandion wishing to talk with Krum,
and taking a pledge they came out of the ship; and Krum, dismounting,
sat on the ground, and Constantine's son held his horse saddled and
bridled. And while they were talking, one of those from the city made
the sign, who was Hexaboules, uncovering his head with his hand. Whom
Krum seeing and taking offence leaped up; and having the horse ready,
those with him lifting him, he mounted. And the people from the wall
cried "The cross has conquered." And he began to flee. And the armed men
coming out from the rooms pursued after Krum, and letting fly their
missiles as they thought they had wounded him, but he fled quite alone
to his people. And the other three were seized by those in the
chelandion. 614 And the logothete was slaughtered at once, but
Constantine and his son were taken alive. 9. So then these things having
happened, Krum in anger ordered on the morrow to burn and overthrow every
place; and beginning to burn they burned all the churches over against
the city, being great, which Irene and Nicephorus and Michael had
renewed; likewise also the monasteries and the palaces and the houses
and the suburbs. And coming to Saint Mamas they burned the palaces there
and the imperial bedchambers, and they broke the columns, and they took
up the leads and the figures of the Hippodrome, and they slaughtered all
the captivity, likewise also the cattle. And passing along the seacoast
of the Strait and the upper parts they burned all the emporia, and
taking much spoil they went off. And seizing the right side of the city
toward



the east, they burned the parts outside the Golden Gate as far as
Rhegium. And coming to Athyras they overthrew the castle there and the
bridge, being extraordinary and very strong. And coming to Selymbria
they overthrew the castle there to the ground, and burned the churches
and the houses. And after this they overthrew the castle of Daonion that
was there. And coming to the city of Heraclea, not being able to come
into it, they burned all the dwellings that were at the harbour and
round the same city. And after this coming to Rhaedestus they overthrew
this castle also, burning 615 all the dwellings in it and the churches
and slaughtering a great people there. And coming to the castle of
Panion they found it secured, having a great people; and having no power
against it, burning and overthrowing all outside they went up to Aspron,
a castle that was itself also, and overthrowing this too and burning it,
and very many other castles, they came down from there through ten days,
and entered the mountains of Ganos; and finding there a great people
hiding and almost all the cattle of Thrace, they slaughtered the men,
and taking captive the cattle being in multitude they sent them to
Bulgaria, and many women and children. And then they went off also to
Hexamilion, and came down as far as Abydos, and turned back to the Hebrus
and the upper country, and overthrew all the castles from small to great
as far as Adrianople. And finding Adrianople strengthened they sat down
against it. And having spent many days and having no power from the
siege, setting up engines they warred against the castle. And being
straitened, having help from nowhere and dying of famine, they gave
themselves up. And the Bulgarians, taking all the captivity being an
innumerable multitude and all their baggage, transplanted them to
Bulgaria beyond the river Ister. And while these things were happening
Leo did not come out of the city, but 616 held the empire tyrannically.
And then, the feasts having arrived, he crowned his son being small and
named Sambates, and he lied saying that he is called Constantine; and
gathering the people saved from the various cities, he gave them pay,
making them acclaim Leo and Constantine, imitating the former emperors
Leo and Constantine the Isaurians, whose heresy he also renewed, wishing
to live many years as they also and to become famous; whose counsel God
put to shame, cutting short his times. 10. And then, the air having
become fair in winter, and the rivers not having much water, the
Bulgarians came out, 30 thousands all in iron; and coming as far as
Arcadiopolis and crossing the Rhegias (and a river is so called) they
found a great people and took them captive. And before they crossed with
the captivity there came a great rain for eight days, and the river
swelling became as a sea. And the Bulgarians not being able to cross
back remained 15 days, holding the captivity. And sending word to Leo
to help, he paid no heed, nor came out of the city, nor sent any others
to help. And then, fair weather coming and the river lessening, they
made the captives themselves 617 cut wood and make a bridge, and so they
crossed with the captivity, being together of men and women and children
50 thousands. Taking also all their belongings, Armenian round cloaks
and upper fleecy rugs and much clothing and bronzes, they loaded all on
the wagons, and taking also their herds of oxen and sheep they entered
Bulgaria. And he who reigned unjustly by God's permission neither
himself came out of the city to help the captivity nor sent others. 11.
So then after this it was announced, they saying that Krum



had marched, gathering a great people and the Avars and all the
Sclavinias; and besides these he prepares engines of various siege-works
and machines and engines of very great size, caltrops and stone-throwers
and tortoises and high ladders, balls and levers and diggers, rams and
arrow-stands, fire-throwers and stone-throwers and scorpions for casting
missiles and slings, all the machines against the battlements for taking
the city. And on the western part, he says, of the city against the wall
of Blachernae he wishes to bring up all these machines, where also he
was shot, stabling ten thousand oxen to carry all the aforesaid engines,
set on wagons, which he ordered 5 thousands to be bound with iron.
Hearing these things Leo, sending scouts and learn 618 ing the truth,
gathering a great people and craftsmen began to build another wall
outside the wall of Blachernae, cutting also the moat broad. And
beginning to build, not yet having finished, that God who scatters the
counsels of nations, setting aside the thoughts of peoples and setting
aside the counsels of rulers, might show that not in their machinery and
strength can men do anything, the spring not yet having arrived, as
certain say who were saved from the captivity from Bulgaria, about Great
Thursday of Pascha the first of Bulgaria, Krum, the famous, who wished
to take the city, made an end of life, being slaughtered invisibly; who
also sent forth streams of bloods through his mouth and his nostrils and
his ears, and so broke off his soul in evils. 12. So Leo, lifted up in
mind, that he himself had brought down the enemy, not God, sent sacrae
into all the countries, announcing that I found the Bulgarians being
near the city, and by my prudence and courage and by my leading, having
shot their first man, I destroyed them all; who also for this cause
dies, he says, our enemy. And these things concerning this; (13) and it
is said also that a voice burst from heaven at that very hour,
proclaiming the dissolution of the godless emperor, and became audible
to many sailing; of which the time 619 and the hour they wrote down (for
it was the tenth hour of the night) and at last found the truth. 14.
Nevertheless prison and bonds held Michael; and since he wished to kill
him after the feast of Christ's Nativity, Michael makes known to his
counsellors, with whom was also the papias of the palace, that unless
you make haste to take me out of the guard, I will denounce us all to
the emperor. And they, gathered and wearing chasubles as priests, and
wearing the swords within, when the palace was opened and the emperor
entered the church, themselves leaping in cut him in pieces limb from
limb and buried his body in the island of Prote. And at that very hour
they loose Michael from the bonds, and bring him in at the feast a
wearer of the crown instead of a wearer of bonds. And the prophetic word
was fulfilled, "In the evening weeping shall lodge, and in the morning
exultation." And they led his wife away to the monastery of the Despots;
and Sambatius the son, renamed Constantine at the crowning, and the
rest, Basil and Gregory together with Theodosius, they tonsured in the
island of Prote and made monks. Where also Constantine, renamed Basil,
seized with voicelessness after the generative parts of his members were
taken away, was begging God, but also Gregory the Theologian set up
there, that his own voice be loosed and his tongue recover its good
sound. Whence also he was heard. 620 For at the festival of the Lights
he sees that divine figure, saying "Take the candle and read." And he
without hesitation going in read with a bright and clear voice "Again
Jesus my own." From then therefore, loathing his hereditary heresy, he
honoured the forms of the saints and embraced them. But these things
later. Year of the world
314, year of the divine incarnation 814, emperor of the Romans Michael
the Amorian the
 
Stammerer and iconoclast, 8 years 9 months. This man took to wife
Euphrosyne, the daughter of Constantine who was blinded, who had accepted
the monastic life. Who, yielding a little from the evil that had
possessed him before, so far as those in prisons and exiles might
dreamingly imagine freedom and relief, still cherished the god-hated
mind of the preceding impious man; for likewise he was caught on the
same hook of the dreadful heresy from extreme folly and lack of
education. Whence in a silentium he said "Those before us who examined
the ecclesiastical ordinance of the dogmas will be required to give
account concerning them, whether they ordained well or ill. In that in
which we found the church walking, in this we also judge it right to
keep her. For to honour the images and not to honour them, let it be
indifferent according to the same." At this 621 time Antony of Syllaion
held the throne of the high priesthood, alien to the veneration of the
holy images. 2. Upon these things Michael crowns Theophilus his son from
Euphrosyne in the Great Church. And Thomas the rebel, setting out from
the eastern parts, dragging a people both rabble and mixed, moved upon
Byzantium, desiring the empire beyond his desert. For being a Roman and
obscure he came to Syria, and renaming himself Constantine he said he
was son of the empress Irene; and from there deceiving many both of
barbarians and of Romans, and gathering an infinite multitude, boasting
in the multitude he came against Constantinople, and having wasted this
for three years, when he saw his ships burned with fire and the picked
men of the enemy destroyed by the citizens, seized with helplessness,
leaving the city he goes to Thrace, plundering it. And Michael, coming
out of the city with a very great force, set out against him, and sitting
down a short time he overcame him without toil; and having cut off his
hands and feet, then also impaling him, he stopped the civil war that
had come to be in three years. 3. While Michael was occupied in these
things, Crete and Sicily and the Cyclades islands were taken from the
empire of the Romans by Africans and Arabs, the beginning having been
taken now first through the sins of the people and the impiety of those
in power. 622 And things being so, the emperor summons Ireneaus the
magister, and says to him "I congratulate you, magister, that Sicily has
revolted." And he said "This is foreign to joy, master." And turning to
one of the great men he said, a beginning of evils will fall upon the
earth, when a dragon ill-tongued altogether and very gold-loving begins
to rule Babylon. (4) For the leader of the Hagarenes, Apokhat, attacked
the promontory of Crete and put in at Charax, ordering the people under
him to leave their ships and plunder the country. And when the army had
moved off toward such an enterprise, ordering it he burned their ships.
And when they murmured about this he said to them that yesterday you
were murmuring against me, O men, seeking a country for habitation, and
for this I brought you here, where there is a flowing of milk and honey."
And they answered him "And where shall we find our children and our
wives?" And he to them "Know that you have brought a very great captivity
for yourselves; from this then fit wives to yourselves." And this pleased
them, and they set a palisade toward fortifications; from which the
place is named Charax until now. And a certain monk afterward coming to
the island said to them that if you should wish to dominate the island,
follow me, and wherever 623 I, taking aim, shall show you fitness of
building, there build a city, through which you will have power over the
island. And he led them to a place called Chandax, in which their city
is established, and they trenched this round. And taking ninety cities
they enslaved them, one monastery alone not being taken but remaining
unbesieged by agreement, its inhabitants



being allowed with freedom to do the things of the Christians; who until
now are called hypotaxioi. And indeed the islands would have been held
by the Hagarenes, unless the emperor Michael had commanded Craterus, the
strategos of the Cibyrrhaeots, to prepare all the ships under him and
set out to Crete; who coming with two hundred ships freed them, and
setting out to Crete became master of all the Hagarenes there, and had
resolved to destroy them, unless certain of the people thinking ill
prevented this, saying that these would in no way array themselves
against us in the night, but in the morning we shall hold them. And being
deluded in mind by their words they returned to their tents, taking no
thought at all for their safety. And in the night the enemies coming
brought an iron sleep upon them all, only their strategos embarking on a
merchant ship and sailing away. Which the leader of the Hagarenes
learning embarked on two merchantmen and pursued after, and seized him
toward the island of Cos, and in it impaled him. Then Oryphas by surname
by the emperor's command 624 gathers a naval levy; to whom giving forty
gold pieces each, they embarking on merchantmen and campaigning against
Crete withdrew unsuccessful from the war, most being slaughtered there;
and those left of them they named the Forty-men. In this island Cyril
the wonder-working bishop of Gortyna of this island was slaughtered by
the Hagarenes for Christ, as a blameless victim, whose precious blood
welling up until now works wonders. And this man had this manner; but
the impious Michael, falling ill grievously from strangury and the pain
of the kidneys, was badly released from living. And his wretched body
being carried off was laid in the church of the Holy Apostles; and
Theophilus his son with his mother Euphrosyne held the empire. Year of
the world 323, year of the divine incarnation 823, emperor of the Romans
Theophilus the son of Michael the Stammerer, iconoclast, 12 years. And
his mother Euphrosyne wishing to give him a wife, brings various girls
incomparable in beauty, among whom one girl most fair was called Icasia
and another named Theodora. Giving him a golden apple the mother said to
give it to her who pleased him. And the emperor Theophilus, 625 struck
by the beauty of Icasia, said that through a woman the base things
flowed. And she with a certain shame answered "But also through a woman
the better things spring." And he, struck in the heart by the word, let
her be, and gave the apple to Theodora, being from Paphlagonia. And both
are crowned in the oratory of Saint Stephen by patriarch Antony on holy
Pentecost, and from there he comes to the Great Church, honouring with
much money both the patriarch and the clergy together with the senate.
And Icasia, missing the empire, prepared a monastery, in which also
being tonsured she continued in a philosophic life pleasing to God until
her end, leaving many writings of her own; and the emperor's mother
Euphrosyne, coming down of her own will from the palace, was quiet in
the monastery of Gastria. 2. And a hippodrome being held, the
polycandelon that was cut with a sword in the slaughter of Leo the
Armenian is brought, and he shows it to all the senate in the kathisma,
and says as those who had dared to make the killing in a temple of the
