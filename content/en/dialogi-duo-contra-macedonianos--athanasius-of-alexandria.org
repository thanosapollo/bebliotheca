OF OUR FATHER AMONG THE SAINTS ATHANASIUS
A discourse in the form of a dialogue with a Macedonian Pneumatomachian.
We sent to your understanding the present composition, so that when you have
read it you may examine both the things said by the heretics who think the
thoughts of Macedonius, and the things set against them by us. The things then
set down by them in the draft are these: If the Holy Spirit is God, he is
Father or Son; but if not, he is
not God. And we to him: You have taken upon yourself this: If the Spirit is
God, he is Father or Son, as if the Father is God for this reason, because he
is Father, and as if the Son is God for this reason, because he is the Son. If
then the Father is God for this reason, because he is Father, the Son is not
God, because he is not Father. And if the Son is God for this reason, because
he is Son, the Father is not God, because he is not Son. Therefore it is not
the case that if the Spirit is God, he is altogether Father or Son. For the
name "God" is indicative of a nature that is contemplated, or rather that runs
through all things; but "Father" is relational, and "Son" likewise. Neither
does the name of the relation signify the nature that runs through, or that is
contemplated; nor does the name of some contemplated nature, or of a nature
that runs through all things, introduce the relation toward a Son. Besides
these things, taking on the persona of an Orthodox, he asked himself: Is he
not then to be worshipped? And then again he himself to himself: The Holy
Spirit falls short of such dignity. For how is that to be worshipped which is
neither Father nor Son? And we to him: If you worship the Father for this
reason, because he is Father, and the Son because he is Son, you worship every
father and every son; but if the Father is not to be worshipped for this
reason, because he is Father, nor the Son for this reason, because he is Son,
but because of the nature itself, this must be sought. And if the Spirit too
should be found of his nature, worship
him; but if he should not be found so, then apart. For the question is not
whether the Spirit is Father, nor whether he is Son, but whether he is of the
same nature as Father and Son. To this again he sets against himself, as said
by an Orthodox: Is it not written that "God is Spirit"? Then he himself says:
God is Spirit, and not the Spirit God. For everything that is God, this is
also Spirit; not everything that is Spirit, this is also God. And we to him:
If you knew the gift of God, and held the memory of the Scriptures, you would
not have declared this. For not everything that is God is also the Spirit. For
Moses too was called god: "For behold, I have made you a god to Pharaoh"; and
also Aaron of the same brother: "For you shall be to him, it says, as a god,
and Aaron your brother shall be to you as a prophet." And the Son in the
Gospels says concerning the saints: "If then he called them gods, to whom the
word of God came, and the =28.1293= Scripture cannot be broken"; and, "God of
gods, the Lord has spoken, and called the earth"; and in others: "The God of
gods shall be seen in Zion"; and, "God stood in the assembly of gods, and in
the midst he will judge gods"; and, "You shall not speak evil of gods," it is
written. According then to your wisdom, if everything that is God is also
Spirit, all the aforementioned men who are also gods are Spirits. But if Moses
is a god, and each of those mentioned, and they are not Spirits, then not
everything that is God is also Spirit; nor is everything that is Spirit also
God. For even the demons of you heretics are spirits, and they are not gods.
Still introducing the persona of an Orthodox, Let us grant, he says, that the
Spirit is not called God, yet he is called Lord; for it is written: "Now the
Lord is the Spirit." Still he says: Speak yourself what follows: "And where
the Spirit of the Lord is, there is freedom." He is speaking, he says,
concerning the Lord, that the Lord is the Spirit. And we to him: Truly the,

"Professing to be wise they became fools," has been said concerning you. For
though what follows plainly shows that it is the Spirit of whom it was said,
"Now the Lord is the Spirit," you deceived yourself, so that a refutation
might come forth from your own mouth, and you might hear that "Out of your own
mouth I will judge you, wicked servant." For what follows is: "And where the
Spirit of the Lord is, there is freedom." And that which is Spirit of the Lord
is the Lord's Spirit, which has been called Lord. For as the Son, being Lord,
is Lord, so the Spirit, being Lord, is Lord. And again making a persona for
himself, he asked himself: Is he not even to be worshipped? Then he himself to
himself: He is not; for it is not even written. Then again taking the persona
of an Orthodox, he says: If men worshipped men, for example, as Nathan the
prophet did David; for it is written: "Nathan the prophet came in before the
face of the king, and worshipped him upon the earth"; why shall we not also
worship the Spirit before all men? And he himself again brings in against
himself: "You shall worship the Lord your God"; not also the Spirit? The
Spirit is not Lord; nor yet God; how then will he be to be worshipped, not
being within the definition of worship? "For no one can say Jesus is Lord
except in the Holy Spirit." For truly the one who is brought to God must be
brought through another to the Holy Spirit. If I then worship the Holy Spirit,
through whom or in whom shall I worship, having been brought to him? What then
do you say to these things? Nathan the prophet will judge you in the day of
judgment, as not having acted contrary to the law even when he worshipped
David. For you brought forward the saying as though you ought to condemn him
=28.1296= as having transgressed. For if he worshipped David (and it is
written: "You shall worship the Lord your God, and him only shall you serve"),
he has acted contrary to the law according to your accusation. But we do not
accuse the prophet; God forbid! But for this very reason we say the Holy
Spirit is to be worshipped. For the prophet would not have worshipped David if
he knew him as a mere man; but having been taught that he was the Lord's
anointed, anointed with holy oil, he worshipped him because of the operation
of the Spirit in the oil. And how, "like a deaf asp that stops her ears, which
will not hear the voice of the charmer," having heard "You shall worship the
Lord your God," did you not listen to what follows, "And him only shall you
serve"? And you do this so that you may not hear "You shall serve only." Whom?
The Father, or the Son? And how will you say "only"? If you are a Christian,
because of the identity of nature. If then the Spirit too is shown to be of
the same nature, "You shall worship the Lord your God, and him only shall you
serve" speaks of Father and Son and Holy Spirit, because of the identity of
nature. But if not, you will be forced, receiving it of one person, by "You
shall worship the Lord your God, and him only shall you serve," either to
disobey the Son or to set aside the Father. If then it is shown that the
Spirit too, poured out from the Father himself through the Son, is of the same
nature and Godhead as the Son and the Father, he will be proved both to be
worshipped and to be served. And we will attempt with the goodwill of the
Spirit himself to show this identity of nature from the divine oracles. And
the, "For no one can say Jesus is Lord except in the Holy Spirit," is not
against the worship of the Spirit. For as, worshipping God the Father himself
in Spirit and truth, we do not deprive the truth of worship, but we know that
the Son himself is the truth, and worshipping God in Spirit and truth, we
worship the truth; so also we worship the Spirit, as the truth. But if,
because we worship in Spirit, you do not worship the Spirit, and because we
worship in truth, you do not worship the truth, and you think the dignity of
the Spirit is diminished because in him we confess the Son as Lord, you will
then also diminish the dignity of the Father, because "no one comes

to the Son, unless the heavenly Father draws him to him." For this is written
in the Gospel; and the Apostle cries: "God is faithful, through whom you were
called into the fellowship of his Son." And the Father himself in the prophets
says to the Son himself: "Behold, proselytes shall pass through to you through
me." And if we are also called through the Father to the Son, the Spirit is
not then diminished in dignity on this account. We are brought to the Spirit.
Read the Scriptures and learn that, just as through the Father to the Son, so
also through the Son to the =28.1297= Father. For it reverses, as has been
previously given. As then through the Father to the Son, so through the Father
and the Son to the Spirit. For it was not after the Father first dwelt in the
apostles that the Son was known; but after they first knew the Son, and were
discipled, and were sent two by two, as it is written, and their names were
written in the heavens, and they were counted worthy of the divine vision of
God in the appearing of the resurrection, then the Holy Spirit was given to
them from God, leading them to the grace of the Spirit. And just as, saying
Jesus is Lord, we have said this having come to know it from the leading of
the Spirit himself; so, being led by the Son into the knowledge of the Spirit,
we were baptized into Father and Son and Holy Spirit; clearly the Son having
revealed the initiation into the Spirit. And how do you not want the Holy
Spirit to be worshipped, into whom you were baptized? For what then is
greater, worship or baptism? And how is it not confessed that baptism is
greater than worship, seeing that even catechumens worship Father and Son, but
they do not have perfection unless they are baptized into the name of the
Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit? And if the catechumens are not
perfect Christians before they are baptized, but once baptized they are
perfected, baptism then is greater than worship, which grants the perfection.
And this is "our reasonable and living service," which the Apostle says:
"Present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, well-pleasing to God, your
reasonable service." And we presented the same as reasonable service, having
been buried with Christ in baptism. Therefore "You shall worship the Lord your
God, and him only shall you serve" introduces the teaching of baptism. Besides
these things he brought in against himself, as if we had said: If he is
neither Lord, nor God, nor to be worshipped, how is he numbered with the
Trinity? And then he sets down his own: He is numbered, he says, with the
name, that of the Spirit, not numbered with the name of the Father, or of God,
or of the Son. Call him thus, not more than he has; for he is sufficient with
his own dignity. For even if you wish to add more than he has, it is not
permitted; for he does not receive addition from the things you glorify, by
the definition of the one who honored him before all creation. And we to him:
Number also all the angels with the Father and the Son, having heard from God
himself: "I said, You are gods, and all sons of the Most High." And the Holy
and good Spirit you do not count worthy of the numbering of the name; but the
men you will be forced, from what you have granted, to number with God? But we
do not number him with the name (for this is nothing great), but with the
nature itself, as we will later demonstrate, having first refuted the vain
chatter in your draft. And truly, as you yourself also unwillingly confessed,
even if you wish to add something to him, I mean to the Holy Spirit, it is not
permitted; and if you wish to take away, you wrong yourself; for he is
sufficient with his own =28.1300= dignity, if indeed it is possible to speak
of dignity in the case of the Spirit. And this also in the case of the Father
and of the Son. For you can neither add anything to the Father and the Son,
nor take anything away, even if you wish to take away; for you wrong yourself;
since all things are perfect in Father and Son and Holy Spirit, and nothing is
incomplete, so that it might also receive from outside. For he has been
honored by the definition of the one before all creation, as you yourself have
written, wishing by this to show

that he is not his Spirit in truth, but by grace, just as also all the other
ministering ones, and they are not by nature his spirits. Besides these things
again he brought in against himself, as said to him by us: Is the Holy Spirit
then not of equal honor with the Father and the Son? And again he declares to
himself: He is not; since it is not even written. For concerning the Father
and the Son it is written: "That they may honor the Son as they honor the
Father." He did not say, and the Spirit. To these things we to him: Blessed
are the ears that have not heard you, and the souls that have been kept
unwounded by your words. And how, not reckoning the Spirit to be of equal
honor with the Father and the Son, are you baptized into him with equal honor?
Or does it not seem to you that the Father and the Son are honored by the
confession of baptism? And if this is not the honor, why are we even baptized?
Why do we also risk every hour to keep the baptism unstained, struggling
against the methods of the devil? And how does it not seem to you that the
presenting of our bodies is above every sacrifice, the Apostle saying: "That
you may present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, well-pleasing to God"?
And every sacrifice comes to pass unto the honor of God. And this we offered
to Father and Son and Holy Spirit even in baptism, not in a different place,
nor in another manner honoring Father and Son and Holy Spirit. And still he
asks himself, as being questioned by us: What then? Do you say the Spirit is a
creature? Then he will be one of the creatures. Then he himself to himself: If
it is written, I say it; but if it is not written, I do not say it. And again
he replies to himself, as brought against him by us, and says: It is written
that "In him all things were created." And he himself to himself: But the Holy
Spirit, being unique, is not made common with all. For the commonness of the
words does not make the natures common; since then there would be equal honor
among the creatures. And we again to these things: If your heart had the fear
of God, it would not have written wickedness, but you would have said straight
out what you thought. But you fear to speak, not fearing God, but the crowd,
as the Pharisees feared to say whence the baptism of John was. And fearing you
do not keep silence, but you cry out; and you cry out to those who know what
"the poison of asps is under their lips" is. For the Pharisees reasoned: "If
we say, From heaven, he will say, Why then did you not believe him? And if we
say, From men, we fear the crowd." This you yourself also reason: If we say,
He is not a creature, they will say: =28.1301= Why then do you not worship
him? If I say, Creature, I fear also the crowd. For the whole people will
stone us, having renounced the creation and enlisted with Father and Son and
Holy Spirit. What then do you do? Saying creature you hide the deceit, and you
construct it itself having said: But the Holy Spirit, being unique, is not
made common with all. For the commonness of the words does not make the
natures common. Since then there will be equal honor among the creatures;
which is: He is a creature, but he is not of equal honor with the creatures.
As if someone were to say: The sun is a unique star, and it is not of equal
honor with the stars. Every star is day-appearing, even if it is not made
common with the other stars. And what is more impious than to say the Holy
Spirit is a creature, when the Son says that "It is not you who speak, but the
Spirit of the Father who speaks in you." When he says the Spirit is of the
Father, how do you say it is a creature? And how do you not hear the Father
saying: "In the last days I will pour out from my Spirit upon all flesh"; and
further: "Behold my servant the chosen, in whom my soul has been well pleased;
I will put my Spirit upon him"? And besides these Peter says: "You know the
word that came to pass throughout all Judea; beginning from Galilee after the
baptism which John preached, Jesus of Nazareth, how God anointed him with the
Holy Spirit and with power." Is the Son then anointed with his own creature?
And how is it not mindless to say this? And the Son himself also says: "The
Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because

he has anointed me. He has sent me to preach good news to the poor." And Peter
also in the Acts of the Apostles says thus: "Having then been exalted by the
right hand of God, and having received from the Father the promise of the Holy
Spirit, he poured out this which you see and hear." Does he then receive from
the Father what he himself created? And did he not have authority over his own
creature? But you will say: Is the Holy Spirit then greater than the Son? We
answer you then: God forbid! None of us says this. But it was necessary that
he, having taken the form of a servant, should be anointed, no longer with oil
prepared according to the ancient commandment of God, but with the oil of
gladness above his fellows. "For this reason, says David, God, your God, has
anointed you with the oil of gladness above your fellows." "This reason,"
which? That, being God and Son, he took the form of a servant. And since we
promised beforehand to show that the Holy Spirit is of the same nature as
Father and Son, accordingly having set up a dialogue from two persons, let us
be eager to show this, with the goodwill of the Spirit himself. Having met one
of those who think the same things as the one who wrote the draft, we spoke
thus: God is said by the Apostle to have immortality alone: "For he alone, it
says, has immortality, dwelling in light =28.1304= unapproachable." Maced.
Yes. Orth. And do the angels also have it, and principalities, and
authorities, thrones and lordships? Maced. Yes. Orth. How then does God alone
have immortality? Maced. Because God himself is immortality; but the angels
are immortal by participation in immortality, and the archangels, and all the
heavenly ones. Orth. And does the Son of God have immortality, or not? Maced.
Very much so. Orth. Thus as the Father, or as the heavenly ones? Maced. As the
Father. Orth. How then does the Father alone have immortality? Maced. Because
the Son is indistinguishable in essence from the Father. Orth. Is the Son then
said to be God alone, as also the Father, because of the indistinguishability
of essence? Maced. I say that the Son has immortality thus similarly to the
Father. Orth. And does the Holy Spirit also have immortality? Maced. He has.
Orth. Does he have it thus as the Father and the Son, or as angels and
principalities and authorities, by participation and grace? Maced. As the
Father and the Son. Orth. The Holy Spirit is then of the same nature as Father
and Son? Maced. God forbid! Orth. God then no longer alone has immortality.
For another nature has been found having it thus as God himself. Maced. I say
again then that the three are one. Orth. Not one in the hypostases, but three.
Maced. How then do I say one nature? Orth. Because the Father and the Son and
the Spirit similarly have the immortality of the Father. And just as Paul and
Peter and Timothy are of one nature, and three hypostases, so I say Father and
Son and Holy Spirit three hypostases, and one nature. Maced. But other men
pre-exist the men; therefore another nature also pre-exists Father and Son and
Holy Spirit. Orth. God forbid! Maced. How then do you say: Thus, as in the
case of men? Orth. Of the men, Paul and Peter and Timothy, men pre-exist, and
of those before them others; but of Adam another does not pre-exist, but God
himself. And of Father and Son and Holy Spirit nothing pre-exists; but the Son
has been begotten from the Father, and the Spirit proceeds. Maced. The Spirit
then is not of the Son? Orth. All things of the Father are also of the Son.
For the Apostle also says the Spirit is both of the Father and of the Son,
speaking thus: "But you are not in flesh, but in spirit, if indeed the Spirit
of God dwells in you. But if anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ, this
one is not his. But if Christ is in you, the body is dead because of sin, but
the spirit is life because of righteousness. But if the Spirit of the one who
raised Christ Jesus from the dead dwells in you, he will also make alive your
mortal bodies through his Spirit that dwells in you." And the Son himself also
said the Spirit is of his Father. Maced. How?

Orth. "For it is not you who speak, but the Spirit of the Father who speaks in
you." And the Father himself also says the Spirit is his own through the
prophet saying: =28.1305= "In the last days I will pour out from my Spirit
upon all flesh." Maced. It is the Son who is speaking. Orth. Read the Apostle,
that you may hear him. For he says thus, writing to his own disciple: "But
when the grace of God our Savior appeared, not from works of righteousness
which we did, but according to his mercy he saved us, through the washing of
regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit, which he poured out upon us
richly, through Jesus Christ our Lord and Savior." Maced. We cannot say one
nature of Father and Son and Holy Spirit. Orth. You yourself also confess the
Father to be holy, of whom it has been said: "Holy is he who rests in the holy
ones." Maced. Yes. Orth. Do you say the angels are holy thus as the Father?
Maced. The angels are holy by participation in sanctification; but the Father
is holy by nature. Orth. Is the Son holy thus as the angels are holy by
participation, or as the Father? Maced. As the Father; for I say him like in
all things. Orth. Do you say the Holy Spirit is holy thus as the angels, or as
the Father and the Son? Maced. As the Father and the Son. Orth. He is then of
the same nature as Father and Son? Maced. And if truly, as you say, there is
one nature; and the Son sits at the right hand of the Father; where does the
Spirit sit? Orth. With Christ himself. For if we, as Paul says, sit together,
it is clear that the Spirit also does; for it is through him that we also do.
Maced. Where is it written that we also sit together? Orth. It is written in
the Letter to the Ephesians: "And we being dead in trespasses, he made us
alive together with Christ, and raised us together, and seated us together in
the heavens." And it is impossible for anyone to sit together with Christ
without having become a temple of the Spirit. And if the temple sits together,
what would you say concerning the indwelling Spirit? Maced. The Spirit is
apostolic; for he is sent. Orth. Is the Son not sent? Maced. Yes. Orth. Was he
sent from place to place? Maced. No; for I say the Son and the Father are
everywhere. Orth. And do you not say the Holy Spirit is everywhere? Maced.
Yes. Orth. If the Father is everywhere, and the Son is everywhere, and the
Spirit everywhere, the Spirit is of the same nature as the Father and the Son.
Maced. Since the devil also is everywhere, is he of the same nature as Father
and Son? Orth. The devil is not everywhere; for he is neither in heaven, nor
in all the earth; but just as some tyrant being in a certain place, sending
subjects through his commands, is said to hold sway everywhere, where the
powers that serve him are; and in hypostasis he is in one place, but by the
operation of the will he is said to be everywhere wherever he holds sway; so
also the devil in hypostasis goes =28.1308= about from place to place, but by
the operation of the will everywhere on earth, his will being operated in "the
sons of disobedience, through the principalities and the authorities, and the
world-rulers of the darkness." But Father and Son and Holy Spirit are
everywhere, and in all, and through all, in essence and power and glory. And
if this, then one essence of Father and Son and Holy Spirit. Maced. Father and
Son are everywhere and in all in essence and will and power and glory; but the
Spirit is there where he is sent. Orth. When then he came down in the upper
room where the disciples were, and sat upon each one of them, was he not also
in heaven? Maced. For when Gabriel was sent to Mary, was he not also in
heaven? Orth. In essence, no; in mind, yes. Maced. Thus I say also of the
Spirit. Orth. And how do you hear the one who says that "The Spirit of the
Lord has filled the inhabited world; and that which holds all things together
has knowledge of a voice"? And what is that which has the knowledge of God,
but the Spirit, of whom it has been said: "Spirit of knowledge and

godliness"? Maced. The Spirit is sanctifying. Orth. Does the Father not
sanctify? Or is it not written in the Gospels: "Father, sanctify them in your
truth"? And through the prophet he says: "I the Lord who sanctify them."
Maced. Yes, the Father also sanctifies and the Son. Orth. If the Father
sanctifies and the Son also sanctifies, and the Spirit also sanctifies, he is
then of the same nature as Father and Son; for those whose operation is the
same, the nature also is the same. Maced. We say the Holy Spirit is leading.
But the one who leads leads toward another. Orth. Does God then not lead?
Maced. Where is it written? Orth. Who is the one who sits upon the cherubim?
Maced. God. Orth. Hear then David saying: "You who shepherd Israel, give heed;
you who lead Joseph like a sheep, you who sit upon the cherubim, appear." Do
you hear that God both shepherds and leads? Maced. Yes, God shepherds and
leads; but he leads toward himself. But the Spirit leads not toward himself,
but toward all the truth. Orth. Does God then not lead toward all truth?
Maced. But he himself is truly all the truth. Orth. He then leads the same
leading which the Spirit leads. And those whose operation is the same, the
nature also is the same. For you cannot say that the Spirit leads otherwise,
and God toward all the truth; the Lord Jesus having said that the Spirit
himself leads us toward all the truth. Maced. But I said that God is all the
truth, and he leads toward himself, but the Spirit not toward himself, but
toward all truth, which is toward God. Orth. And if God leads toward himself,
what need is there of the leading of the Spirit? Maced. First the Spirit
leads, and then God. Orth. If the one who leads leads toward some other,
=28.1309= and the Spirit leads toward God; God too, leading, leads toward some
other. Maced. I said, toward himself. Orth. Then the one who leads does not
lead toward some other, but toward himself. And if this, the Spirit also,
leading, leads both toward himself and toward the Father and toward the Son.
For all the truth toward which we are led is the leading into Father and Son
and Holy Spirit; whether the Father leads, he leads toward himself; whether
the Spirit leads, he leads toward himself. Maced. But I said that first the
Spirit leads, and then God. Orth. But if the Holy Spirit leads the same
leading as God, those whose operation is the same, the nature is the same.
Maced. But this is what is sought, whether first the Spirit leads, and then
God. Orth. I said that it does nothing, whether first the Spirit does it, or
first God, which is not common; for rather God will be found in the more cases
first leading than the Spirit according to your word. For we say the leading
of Father and Son and Holy Spirit is the same and according to identity. But
since you divide the time and the leading, say by whom Adam was first led,
remaining in paradise before the transgression to work it and to keep it; by
the Father, or by the Son, or by the Holy Spirit? Maced. By the Spirit. Orth.
And yet it has been said: "And God placed him in the paradise to work and to
keep." Therefore even unwillingly you confess the Spirit to be God; since you
say he first leads. Maced. God leads in the Spirit. Orth. And how is it
written: "Who led your people like sheep by the hand of Moses and by the arm
of Aaron"? Is the hand of Moses the Holy Spirit? Maced. If there is one nature
of Father and Son and Holy Spirit, the Spirit can both beget and create. Orth.
Do you say there is one nature of Father and Son and Holy Spirit, or do you
not even say this? Maced. I do not say one; but I say similar. Orth. Do you
say similar, as of gold and bronze? Maced. God forbid! Orth. But as of what?
Maced. As of gold and gold. Orth. This then is one nature. And do you say the
Son wholly thus like the Father, as gold to gold? Maced. Yes. Orth. If then
the Son should wish, being of the same nature as the Father, can he beget a
Son? Maced. Yes, he can; but so that we may not be taught a generation of
gods,

he does not do this. Orth. Nothing then prevents the Holy Spirit from being of
the same nature as the Father and the Son, and neither begetting nor creating,
so that we may not be taught a generation of gods, as you said, nor
polytheism. But whatever the Father wills, the Son creates, the Spirit
sanctifies. And this is the, "By the Word of the Lord the heavens were
established, and by the Spirit of his mouth all their power." Neither does the
Father, needing help, create through the Son, nor does the Son, needing help,
create through the Spirit; but so that from the things that come to be the
identity of Father and Son and Holy Spirit may be shown. And that the Holy
Spirit can create has been shown from the =28.1312= Lord's body, the angel
having said: "For that which has been begotten in her is from the Holy
Spirit." Maced. And the Father is Holy Spirit, and the Son; and when he says,
It is from the Holy Spirit, he is speaking of the Son, and not of the Spirit
concerning whom the discourse is. Orth. Read the place of the Gospel, that you
may hear the angel saying to Mary: "The Holy Spirit will come upon you";
which? Concerning whom the discourse is, or concerning the Son, or also
concerning some other holy spirit? For the holy angels also are holy spirits.
Maced. He says Holy Spirit of the Spirit himself, into whom we are baptized.
Orth. From this Spirit then Gabriel says the Lord's body has been begotten.
For it was not another that came upon her, and another that worked the grace;
but that which was begotten in her is from the Holy Spirit, clearly the one
that came upon her. Maced. And how does he say: "Wisdom has built herself a
house"? Orth. For this is what we say, that all the works of the Father are of
the Son, and those of the Son are said to be of the Spirit; since there is one
power of the Father and of the Son operated in the Holy Spirit. Indeed Paul
says: "God has set some in the Church, first apostles, second prophets, third
teachers"; and again the same: "Having sent from Miletus to Ephesus, he
summoned the elders of the Church," and he says to them: "Take heed to
yourselves, and to all the flock, in which the Holy Spirit has set you as
overseers, to shepherd the Church of Christ." Do you see that those whom God
set, the Holy Spirit set, and those whom the Spirit set, God set; and not
others the Spirit, and others God, but those very ones whom God set, the
Spirit set? And if those whom God set, the Spirit set, then the same is the
operation of God and of the Spirit. And those whose operation is the same, the
nature also is the same. Maced. And yet God is the one who operates all things
in all. For it is written: "There are distributions of gifts, but the same
Spirit; and there are distributions of ministries, but the same Lord; and
there are distributions of operations, but it is the same God who operates all
things in all." Orth. Speak what follows, that you may hear: "And to each is
given the manifestation of the Spirit for the common good. For to one is given
through the Spirit a word of wisdom; and to another a word of knowledge
according to the same Spirit; and to another faith in the same Spirit; and to
another gifts of healings in the same Spirit; and to another operations; and
to another prophecy; and to another discernings of spirits; and to another
kinds of tongues. And all these things the one and the same Spirit operates,
distributing to each privately as he wills." And he wills what the Father and
the Son will; and for this reason we say one will. And those whose will is the
same and the operation the same, the nature also is the same. Maced. Is the
Spirit then also God? Orth. =28.1313= This you yourself also confessed from
the very sequence. But know this, that to be God is second to the nature. For
we too, if we become imitators of God, according to Paul become gods; but we
cannot become of the same nature. Maced. Do you then say that the Spirit
himself also is God according to imitation and grace? Orth. God forbid that I
should say anything acquired by the Holy

Spirit; but holy by nature, good by nature, incorruptible by nature, immortal
by nature, as Spirit of God and springing from him, or rather proceeding.
Maced. Does he then always proceed from him? Orth. So that you may not think
that he began to proceed and has ceased, for this reason it has been said: Who
proceeds from the Father; so that you may seek neither beginning nor end of
the procession. This you will also find signified concerning the generation of
the Son. For he did not say, Before all things he begot me, but He begets me;
so that you may seek neither beginning of the generation nor end. Maced. Are
there then two Sons? Orth. God forbid! Maced. If truly both the Son is from
the Father and the Spirit, they are two brothers. Orth. But the Son has been
begotten, and the Spirit proceeds. Maced. And what is the difference of
generation and of procession? Orth. Do not over-busy yourself about the
difference; for it is not graspable, but the things that have been commanded
you, these consider, and do not examine beyond these. And it has been
commanded you to believe that the Son is begotten, and the Spirit proceeds;
but all the other things, heaven, earth, sea, and the things in them rational
and irrational, are creatures, created according to the command of God
himself; "For he spoke, and they were begotten; he commanded, and they were
created." Maced. I too do not say that the Spirit is a creature; but I do not
say he is God. Orth. Know this one thing, that for this reason you yourself
are not God by nature, because you are a creature of God, as if you were not a
creature, you would be God by nature. Maced. But it is nowhere written that
the Spirit is God. Orth. Let us grant that "God" is not written. But you
confess the nature, and the divine operations, and it is enough for me toward
the confession of the Godhead. Maced. Why do you say what is not written?
Orth. It is written, but you confess the nature; and if it is not written, the
nature itself has the name following; for if an hypostatic Spirit,
sanctifying, uncreated, is confessed, he is God, even if you do not wish it.
Maced. Where is it written that the Spirit is God? Orth. Where the like
according to essence is written. Maced. But our Fathers said the
consubstantial according to essence concerning the Son. Orth. Moved from the
meaning of Scripture, or from their own authority, did they say the thing not
written? Maced. Moved from the meaning of Scripture. Orth. And the meaning of
Scripture teaches that the uncreated Spirit, being from God, and =28.1316=
life-giving, and sanctifying, is a divine Spirit. Maced. And we too say he is
divine, but not God. Orth. And the divine Spirit, being uncreated, what else
is he but God? Maced. Divine is one thing, and God another. Orth. If Scripture
says, "The divine Spirit that made me," does it not speak of the Holy Spirit?
Maced. No; for God is the one who made man. Orth. And not the Spirit? Maced.
Is God then a divine Spirit? Orth. Yes. And if God has been called a divine
Spirit in Scripture, and neither angel, nor archangel, nor any other power has
been called divine in Scripture; but God alone and his Spirit have been called
divine; then the divine is not something other besides God. And Paul also in
the Acts says thus: "Being then offspring of God, we ought not to think the
divine is like gold or silver or stone, a carving of art and of the thought of
man." Do you see that God is divine, and the divine is God? And Job also
called the Spirit divine, speaking thus: "The Lord lives, who has thus judged
me, and the Almighty, who has made my soul bitter. If still my breath is in
me, and divine Spirit is in my nostrils, my mouth shall not speak unjust
things"; saying "The Lord lives," the Son; and "the Almighty," the Father;
"the divine Spirit," the Holy Spirit. Maced. Do you then say three equals?
Orth. In fatherhood, no; in sonship, no; in nature, yes. Maced. How is it
possible? Orth. As has been demonstrated. Maced. How has it been demonstrated?
Orth. Because the Spirit similarly to Father and Son is everywhere in essence
and power and will and glory, similarly leads toward all truth, similarly
sanctifies, similarly operates all things in all. And those whose operation is
the same, the nature is the same. Maced. How then has it been said: "He will
not speak from himself,

but whatever he hears, he will speak"? Orth. Because the Son also says: "The
Father who sent me has given me a commandment, what I should say and what I
should speak." And this especially shows the same nature of Father and Son and
Holy Spirit. Maced. How? Orth. Because every generated nature that is rational
also speaks from itself; as when Abraham says: "Let it not be, Lord, if I
speak yet once"; and Moses: "I am weak-voiced and slow of tongue"; and, "I beg
you, Lord, appoint another"; and as Jeremiah: "I am younger, and I cannot
prophesy"; so that God says to him: "Do not say that I am younger." And Jonah,
having deliberated from himself, went up into a ship, to flee to Tarshish from
the face of the Lord. But the Spirit is not thus; for he does not have a
nature of having another will, which is not of God; but all things, whatever
he may speak, are oracles of God. And for this reason it has been said: "He
will not speak from himself." For the same has also been said concerning the
Son; since there is not in him some other will which is not of the Father.
Maced. Does the Spirit then not have a will of his own? Orth. God forbid to
say one alienated from =28.1317= God! Maced. But how does he not have a will
of his own? Orth. The will of God is the will of the Spirit. For he does not
fail to know the will of God; but what the Father wills, this the Spirit also
wills; for he knows all the things of God, as the spirit of man the things of
the man. Maced. And yet the Son also says: "I did not come to do my own, but
the will of the one who sent me." Orth. And what was the will of the one who
sent him, which he came to do? Maced. The salvation of the world. Orth. And
doing this he does not do his own will, but the will of the one who sent him.
Maced. He himself says that I did not come to do my own will, but the will of
the one who sent me. Orth. Then there is every necessity that there be a
contrary will in him, which he prevented, so that he might do that of the one
who sent him. Maced. God forbid! I do not say the will of the Son is contrary
to the will of the Father. Orth. Therefore the same is the will of Father and
Son. For the Son also wills to save our race through cross and death, and the
Father wills this. And if he says, "I did not come to do my own will, but the
will of the one who sent me," he refers all things up to the Father; and if he
says, "The Father who sent me has given me a commandment, what I should say
and what I should speak," he says it honoring the Father as Father. And if
not, how does the Son, in whom are stored all the treasures of wisdom, not
know what he will speak? Maced. This I accept, that the Son as Son has one
will of the Father. But the Spirit, unless he hears and receives, does not
speak from himself. Orth. As then the Son, having all things in the nature, as
Son says, "I do nothing from myself," so that he may refer the beginning of
the good things up to the Father; so also concerning the Father hear him
saying; for having all things in his nature as Spirit of God, he refers it up
to the cause saying: "He will not speak from himself, but whatever he hears,
he will speak, and he will announce to you the things that are coming"; and
the, "He will take from what is mine," clearly from the Father. For he adds:
"All things that the Father has are mine; for this reason I said that he takes
from what is mine and will announce to you." And he said that he takes, not as
not knowing, but as referring the operation and grace of the Spirit up to the
cause. Maced. If thus, I accept this also well; for the Spirit ministers to us
the grace of God. Orth. Again you say ministry, as declining to say the Spirit
is a fellow-worker of God? Maced. You will never persuade me to say the Spirit
is a fellow-worker of God. Orth. Paul the apostle says the apostles are
fellow-workers of God, and you fear to say his Spirit is a fellow-worker? It
is fitting that you hear: "There they feared a fear, where there was no fear."
Maced. Even if I say the Spirit is a fellow-worker with God, still I do not
glorify him together with God. Orth. If then Paul says concerning those who
have =28.1320= believed, that "You are no longer a slave, but a son; and if a
son, also an heir of God, and a fellow-heir of Christ"; do you then consider
yourself worthy of the fellow-heirship, but the Spirit not? And you

hear: "If we suffer together, that we may also be glorified together"; but the
Spirit not? "If we died together, we shall also reign together"; but the
Spirit not? Maced. Do you also call the Spirit king? Orth. "If death reigned
from Adam even until Moses, even over those who did not sin," how does the
Spirit of life not reign for us? Maced. We say that the Father is king, and
the Son; but the Spirit is a servant. Orth. You do not even know your own
heresy. For the servant, even if he is not king, is of the same essence as the
king. You then yourself also say the Spirit is of the same essence as God.
Maced. No. Orth. And that which is not of the same essence is a creature and
changeable and alterable; but it has been previously confessed that he is
unchangeable and unalterable and uncreated; he is then of the same essence as
God and his Son. Maced. Is then one and the same Father and Son and Holy
Spirit? Orth. God forbid to say this! For if it were one and the same Father
and Son and Holy Spirit, it would not have been said by us that they are of
the same essence. For neither is one said to be of the same essence as
himself. Maced. Where is it written that the Spirit is God? Orth. When the
prophet says, "And they shall all be taught of God," concerning whom does he
speak? Maced. Concerning the Son. Orth. And yet the Son says: "Do not be
anxious how or what you shall speak; for the Spirit himself will teach you
what you must do." And if those taught by the Spirit hear "taught of God,"
what else would the Spirit be, ask yourself. Maced. Those taught by the Spirit
hear "taught of God," because God teaches them through the Spirit. Orth. He
did not say: They shall all be taught of the God, but without the article.
Maced. Are there then three Gods? Orth. God forbid! One God the Father, and
one God the Son, and one God the Holy Spirit. Maced. Then three Gods? Orth. Do
you yourself also confess the Father to be God, and the Son God, or not even
this? Maced. I confess God the Father, and the Son. Orth. Do you then say two
Gods? Maced. God forbid! Orth. As then saying God the Father and God the Son,
you do not say two Gods; so, confessing also the Godhead of the Spirit, do not
say three Gods; "For hear, O Israel: the Lord your God, the Lord is one."
Maced. Then there is one God, Father and Son and Holy Spirit. Orth. One God,
not as three-named, but as those perfected in Christ, one by the account of
agreement and of nature. Maced. I too say one agreement. Orth. Say also
nature, that you may become blessed. Maced. I cannot say one nature. Orth.
Then say unlike. Maced. I do not say this either. Orth. Then you are neither
cold, according to what is written, =28.1321= nor hot; for if you were cold,
you would have said the natures unlike; if you were hot, indistinguishable.
Maced. I say the Son is an indistinguishable nature. Orth. And a nature that
is indistinguishable from a nature is the same nature. For he is similarly
immortal, similarly holy, similarly incorruptible, similarly unchangeable. And
Father and Son and Holy Spirit similarly are the things previously said. Then
there is one nature by the account of identity. Maced. But I say the Son is
subjected to the Father, and how can I say one nature? Orth. Do you say the
subjection is a subjection of nature, or of the economy? Maced. Of nature.
Orth. Then you say the nature unlike? Maced. God forbid! Orth. When the
Prophet says: "You have subjected all things under his feet, sheep and all
oxen, and also the beasts of the field," does he say the nature of the
irrational things is subjected to the nature of men? Maced. Yes. Orth. And is
the nature of the beasts unlike the human nature? Maced. Yes. Orth. If he
says, "He subjected peoples to us and nations under our feet," he does not say
nature to nature, but the slavery of sin to the freedom of the faith? Maced.
Yes. Orth. Then the beasts are subjected to us in one way, and the nations in
another? Maced. Yes. Orth. If Scripture says, "The slaves being subjected to
the masters according to the flesh, not only to the good and reasonable, but
also to the crooked," otherwise the beasts, and otherwise the

nations, and otherwise the slaves; for the beasts by nature, the nations by
faith, the slaves by law. For it is possible for the slaves to be better in
virtue than the masters. Maced. It is confessed that subjection is otherwise
and otherwise. Orth. But we too being subjected to one another because of
love, not as the beasts naturally, nor as the nations by faith, nor as slaves
by law. Maced. Neither as beasts, nor as nations, nor as the slaves, but
because of the love of the Lord. Orth. The Son then being subjected to the
Father, as the beasts are subjected to men because of nature, or as slaves
because of the law, or as a sinner because of the faith, or as Son because of
love. And a Son subjected to a Father has the same nature, not by nature of
the Godhead, but by the economy of the manhood. For the one being subjected
has some will, which he chastens, so that he may do that of the one
commanding; but of Father and Son there is one will by the account of the
Godhead. Maced. How then is it written: "Having become obedient unto death";
and, "But when all things have been subjected to him, then the Son himself
also will be subjected to the one who subjected all things to him, that God
may be all things in all"? Orth. For having taken the form of a servant while
existing in the form of God, it is necessary also, having taken up the
subjection of the servant form, that he is subjected to his own Father on our
behalf, not by nature of Godhead, but by union of the servant form which he
took. And why do you also think the nature of the Son is diminished because he
is subjected to the Father? If then you hear that the God Word was also
subjected to men =28.1324=, will you then also set him lesser in nature than
men? Maced. Where is it written that he was also subjected to men? Orth. Mary
was a human being and Joseph, and he was subjected to them, as it is written
in the Gospels. For having found him, it says, in the temple, they said to
him: "Child, why have you done thus to us? Behold I and your father were
seeking you. And he said: Why is it that you were seeking me? Did you not know
that I must be in the house of my Father? And he went down to Nazareth, and
was subjected to them." Was then the Only-begotten of God, the Savior of the
world, also inferior to Joseph and Mary? Maced. No, God forbid! Orth. Thus,
even if he is said to be subjected to the Father, he is not diminished in
nature; but as Son having been well-pleased to take the form of a servant, he
also took the subjection of the servant form. For being in this, he was also
subjected to Mary. Maced. He himself called the Father his own God. And how
can it be the same nature? Orth. The Son of God is both God by nature, and man
by the assumption of the flesh, or not? Maced. Yes. Orth. Therefore by the
Godhead God is his Father, and by the manhood, God. Maced. I say him God of
the compound. Orth. Recall the saying that speaks thus: "I go to my Father and
your Father, and my God and your God." Or is it not thus? Maced. Thus. Orth.
Is he then his Father thus as also ours? Maced. No. Orth. But his by nature,
and ours by grace? For having become partakers of Christ, we who were far off
by nature were found near, so that God is named our Father. Maced. Thus. Orth.
As then we, having become partakers of Christ, have God as Father, so also the
Son, being Word and God, having partaken of blood and flesh, had the Father as
God; so as to say: "From my mother's womb you are my God." And the Word became
from a mother's womb when the Virgin conceived in the womb and bore Emmanuel,
and thus he is subjected having taken the form of a servant. Maced. He himself
says: "My Father is greater than I." Orth. And the "greater" is among the
things said in many ways in the divine Scripture. Maced. And do you say the
"greater" because of the flesh? Orth. Let us see with fear of God the mind of
Scripture, in how many ways it says the "greater." Maced. Let us see. Orth.
The Son says in the Gospels: "The Father who has given to me is greater than
all, and no one can snatch out of the hand of my Father. I and the Father are
one." And concerning Esau it has been said: "That the greater shall serve the
lesser"; and, "Saul was greater

than all men"; and, "Love is greater than all"; and, "The grain of mustard,
when it has grown, becomes greater than all the herbs." Have these things been
written, or not? Maced. They have been written. Orth. Do you then say Esau
greater than Jacob, as Saul than the other men, as love than hope and faith?
Maced. No. Orth. You speak well. For Saul was said greater than the other men
=28.1325= in bulk of body; but love was said greater than hope and faith, as
being the cause of the increase of the other virtues; for having loved we
believe, and having believed we hope. Maced. And this I accept. Orth. Do you
then say the Father greater than all, as love than hope in quality, or as Esau
than Jacob in time, or as Saul than the other men in bulk? Maced. No. For the
Father is greater than all by incomparable superiority. Orth. Therefore, if
the Father is greater than all by incomparable superiority, neither as Esau
than Jacob in time, nor as Saul than the other men in bulk, nor as love than
faith and hope in quality. Maced. But how? Orth. Because the one who is
greater than all by incomparable superiority is similarly and in the same way
greater than all, and not more than one and less than another. Maced. You
speak truly. Orth. Do you then say the Father greater than the Son in the same
way as also than horses, and than dogs, and than all irrational animals, and
than insensible matter? Maced. No, God forbid! Orth. Then he is not greater
than all by incomparable superiority. For than the Son as Father of a Son.
Maced. Then comparatively. Orth. I said as Father of a Son. For excelling the
others by incomparable superiority, he is not greater than the Son by
incomparable superiority, but as Father of a Son. And if he is not greater
than him by incomparable superiority, he is not greater by nature; for he is
not incomparable. And if not greater by nature, neither by bulk; for he is
without bulk; nor by time; for he is without time; nor by quality; for he is
without quality. Then he does not say the Father greater than himself by any
other thing, except as Son having taken the form of a servant and honoring his
own Father. Maced. This I accept. Orth. Then it is the same nature of Father
and Son; since he does not say the Father greater than himself by incomparable
superiority. For he would not even be Son if he did not have the same nature.
And if this, then he is not subjected by nature, but as Son having taken the
form of a servant. Maced. Concerning the Son you speak well; but concerning
the Spirit not well. For even if the Spirit is not created, yet he is
generated. For all things came to be through the Son. And if all things,
clearly the Spirit also. Orth. If he is generated, he is also created. For if
the generated is not created, and all things came to be through him; neither
are all things created. And if this, how shall we understand what is said by
Paul: "All things have been created in him"? Maced. But not all things are of
equal honor. Orth. Even if they are not of equal honor, yet still all things
are creatures. And a creature differs in nothing from a creature, insofar as
it is a creature. Maced. Does Gabriel differ in nothing from the horse, or
also from some other of the irrational things? Orth. Insofar as he is a
creature, he differs in nothing; but insofar as he is an angel, a ministering
spirit, sent for service, not only of the irrational things, but also of us
men, =28.1328= and of the angels, being an archangel. For the eye also is more
honorable than the other members of the body, insofar as it is an eye; yet it
is a body. Maced. We say that he has being from God through the Son. Orth. Are
the other things then, not having being from God through the Son, creatures?
Maced. Yes. Orth. Then the Spirit too is a creature. Maced. I do not say
creature, since it is not written. Orth. Do not say generated, since it is
signified in parallel with it. Maced. But, "All things came to be through
him," is written; and, "All things together are your servants," is written.
Orth. Do you then say the Spirit is a servant? Maced. God forbid! Orth. Then
he is not numbered with the all. Maced. Do you then say three ungenerated?
Orth. But not uncaused, nor without a father. For even if the Spirit is
ungenerated, yet he is not uncaused; for he has as cause God, whose Spirit

he is. And even if the Son is ungenerated, yet he is not without a father; for
he has as Father God, from whom he has also been begotten. But the Father is
both ungenerated and uncaused and without a father. Maced. Did the Spirit then
not come to be through the Son? Orth. God forbid! For how does the Father say
that he puts it upon the Son, if it came to be through him? Maced. Where is it
written that the Father puts the Spirit upon the Son? Orth. "Behold my servant
the beloved, in whom my soul has been well pleased; I will put my Spirit upon
him." Maced. He does not say this Spirit. Orth. Is there then another Spirit
of the Father? Maced. And the Father himself is Spirit; and when he says, "I
will put my Spirit upon him," he is speaking of his power. Orth. When then he
says, "Upon whom you see the Spirit descending and remaining upon him," is he
speaking of his power? Maced. No, but he is speaking of the Spirit himself the
Paraclete. Orth. Then he said to the prophet to put one thing upon him, and
another to John. Maced. What he said to John to put upon him is the Paraclete;
but what he said through the prophet, he is speaking of his own power. Orth.
Does God have two Spirits? Maced. God himself is Spirit; and his power also is
called Spirit. Orth. This power is a hypostatic Spirit. Maced. What hypostatic
is, I do not understand; he said to put his power. Orth. His power, which he
said to put, is hypostatic. Maced. It is God himself. Orth. And yet having
said to John: "Upon whom you see the Spirit descending as a dove, and
remaining upon him"; and John having seen, he brought in the voice signified
in the prophet: "This is my beloved Son, in whom my soul has been well
pleased." Do you see that the same voices are set down; so that we may not
understand another Spirit through the prophet, and another said to John? Then
learn also from Luke what is written. That, the Spirit having come down and
remained upon him, and the voice being spoken, he says: "And Jesus, full of
the Holy Spirit, returned from the Jordan, and was led in the Spirit into the
wilderness to be tempted by the devil." Maced. Is the Holy Spirit then a
filling of Christ? =28.1329= Orth. The evangelist says that Jesus was full of
the Holy Spirit. Maced. He then says the Spirit is greater than the Son. Orth.
God forbid! Maced. If it filled the Son, if he is led in the Spirit, it is
greater than him. Orth. And again I say, God forbid to say this! But just as
if you hear that the Son sits at the right of the Father, you neither think a
place in the case of God, nor consider the Son greater, but from the
surpassing honor you understand the identity of nature; so, if you hear that
Jesus is full of the Holy Spirit, do not think a filling of a lack, nor
consider the Spirit greater; but from the surpassing honor and union
understand the same of nature in three perfect hypostases. And that the Spirit
is upon the Son himself, hear also him saying to the Jews: "The Spirit of the
Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me"; and Peter: "That God anointed
him with the Holy Spirit and with power." Maced. I am unlearned, I bring my
teacher, and he will dialogue with you. Orth. May it be yours to take pious
reasoning as teacher! so that you may not be following men, but God who has
said: "Go, make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them into the name of
the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit." Amen.

OF THE SAME ATHANASIUS
Another dialogue toward a Macedonian, concerning whether the Word of God wore
an ensouled body.
MACEDONIAN. You not only err saying that the Son is consubstantial with the
Father, but also that the Lord when made flesh had a soul. ORTHODOX. We not
only

are pious saying that the Son is consubstantial with the Father, but also that
when made flesh he had a soul. And you do nothing great in denying the
consubstantial, if you deny that he has a soul. For this is proper to those
who say the different-essence and the unlike. For so that they may say the
essence of the Only-begotten is passible, they say it is soulless, and from
this they construct the unlike. Maced. But we do not say unlike, yet we say
soulless. Orth. Do you confess that at the death he began to be grieved and
distressed, as it is written in the Gospel according to Matthew, and in that
according to Mark, He began to be amazed and distressed? Maced. Yes. Orth. And
does Godhead be distressed and be amazed at death, or the soulless body?
Maced. Clearly the Godhead made flesh. Orth. Then the Godhead of the Son is
unlike the Godhead of the Father? Maced. God forbid! Orth. If he is distressed
and amazed at death, how can he be like and indistinguishable from the one who
is neither distressed, nor grieved, nor amazed? Maced. Because he had a human
body, he is distressed. =28.1332= Orth. But a soulless body is not distressed,
is not amazed, is not grieved. Maced. But he had Godhead in place of the soul.
Orth. Did the body make the Godhead be amazed, or the Godhead the body? Maced.
Because of the body the Godhead was amazed. Orthod. Then the Godhead was
troubled by a body, as our governing part. Maced. God forbid to say! Orth.
Then it is necessary to grant a soul that is troubled. Maced. God forbid!
Orth. How then will you hear the angel saying to Joseph: "Take the mother and
the child, and flee into Egypt; for Herod is about to seek the soul of the
child"; and again: "Having arisen take Mary your wife, and come into the land
of Israel; for those who sought the soul of the child have died"; and the Lord
himself says: "I am the good shepherd; the good shepherd lays down his soul
for the sheep"; and again: "When I will, I lay it down, and when I will, I
take it"? Maced. Since the Father also says, "Your new moons and the Sabbaths

Orth. First you did not understand the saying you brought forward. For you
cannot show that it is the Father who is speaking. Maced. But whose is the
voice? Orth. Of the Son of God who has said: "The ox has known his owner, and
the donkey the manger of his lord; but Israel has not known me, and my people
has not understood." Maced. Is this then the one who says: "Your new moons and
the Sabbaths my soul hates"? Orth. Very much so. For he then hated them, when
he became man, and established the New Covenant. Maced. And did he have a
soul, when he appeared to Isaiah? Orth. Just as he did not have bodily hands
and feet, nor yet bodily garments before the becoming-man, when he said, "They
pierced my hands and my feet, and upon my clothing they cast lots," but he was
speaking the things that were going to come to be; so also speaking concerning
a soul he said: "My soul hates." Maced. Let us grant that the voice is of the
Son; is it not also when he said, "Behold my servant my beloved, in whom my
soul has been well pleased; I will put my Spirit upon him," the Son who is
speaking? Orth. It is not the Son who is speaking, but confessedly the Father.
Yet he does not speak these things as himself having a soul; but every holy
soul he calls his own soul. Whichever soul then has been well pleased in the
Son, this, he says, is my soul. For as the righteous man is called a man of
God; so also the righteous soul is called and is a soul of God. Maced. What
then is well-pleasing? Orth. The prophet Isaiah said the "has been well
pleased" as "has accepted," saying: "Jacob my servant, I will take hold of
him; Israel my chosen, my soul has accepted him; I have given my Spirit upon
him." Whichever soul then has accepted this one, whom I show you as my
servant, this is my soul. Maced. We say the Father has spoken the voice
=28.1333= by catachresis. For neither does the Father, having a soul, say: "In
whom my soul has been well pleased"; nor, as you have understood, does he
speak concerning the soul of men. Orth. Let us grant that

it has been said by catachresis concerning the Father; has it then also been
said by catachresis concerning the Son? Maced. Yes. Orth. If then the Father
also is called man, it has been said by catachresis; and if concerning the
Father, shall we then also call the Son man by catachresis? Maced. Where is it
written by catachresis that the Father is man? Orth. The Son himself in the
Gospels calls him man. Maced. Read me where he calls him man. Orth. When he
says, "A certain man made a wedding for his son," what man does he mean? And
when he says: "A certain man planted a vineyard, and let it out to farmers,"
what man do you understand? Maced. In a parable the Father. Orth. Then, since
in parables he calls the Father man, and in the parable itself also himself
Christ a man sent after the slaves, would we also say that the Son himself has
become man by catachresis? Maced. God forbid! Orth. And if we hear a face of
the Father, which the angels of the little ones see, as it is written in the
Gospels; and we hear also a face of the Son, which shone on the mountain, and
which the servant struck; shall we hear the things signified thus? or shall we
name the one a face of a body, and the other a face by catachresis, which is
hypostasis? Maced. It is confessed otherwise in the case of the Father, and
otherwise in the case of the Son. Orth. Then not, even if soul has been said
by catachresis concerning the Father, altogether also concerning the Son; for
this is the madness of the Manichees, to say that the Son of God has not
become man. Maced. We say he has become man; but not that he has taken a soul.
Orth. Do not say only to have become man, but also to have been made flesh.
For if he did not take an ensouled body, he did not even become man. Maced.
Why? Orth. Because a soulless body, that which has not shared in a rational
soul, is not a human body. And how, like "a deaf asp that stops her ears,
which will not hear the voice of a charmer," do you thus hear Peter saying in
the Acts of the Apostles, that "Neither was his soul left in Hades, nor did
his flesh see corruption"? And the prophet Isaiah cries: "If you give your
soul concerning sin, you shall see a long-lived seed." And the Lord will take
counsel in his hand to take away the pain of his soul, to show him light, and
to form by understanding, to justify a righteous one serving many well; and he
himself will bear their sins. For this reason he himself will inherit many,
and will divide spoils of the strong. In return for which his soul was
delivered unto death, and he was numbered among the lawless, and he himself
bore the sins of many, and because of their lawlessness he was delivered.
Maced. And we too know that it has been said in many places in Scripture that
he had a soul; but it has been said by catachresis =28.1336= and not in truth.
Orth. Then all things that have been said concerning him as human, such as
hearing, sight, smell, voice, taste, desire, grief, amazement, distress,
walking, have been said by catachresis, and not in truth? Maced. All these
previously said have been said truly, but soul by catachresis. Orth. And the
one who counted it worthy to be united to a body that grows weary, and
compacted from bones and sinews and veins and arteries and other members and
parts of the body, did not have a soul? Maced. What need did Life have of a
soul? Orth. And what need did he have of the flesh, who without flesh appeared
to our fathers? Maced. For being crucified he had need of flesh. Orth. And for
being grieved, and distressed, and amazed, and being in Hades, he had need of
a soul. For this is the having taken the form of a servant, not only the
appearing in a body, but also the taking the passions of servitude. Maced. He
took the form of a servant, having taken flesh. Orth. Is the form of God a
perfect form of God? Maced. Yes. Orth. Then the form of the servant also is a
perfect form of the servant. For this reason also amazement, and grief, and
distress, because the form of the servant is perfect. And this is the sheep,
which having sought and found, he carries upon the shoulders, not a soulless
wineskin, but

an ensouled living thing, allowing it to have the passions, not those of sin,
but those of nature; so that it may not be thought a phantasm, and a Manichee
go mad. Maced. Let us grant that concerning a soul you speak well, why do you
also say the Son consubstantial with the Father? Orth. Because whichever
account of the essence of the Father you may say, this same account we set
down also of the essence of the Son. Maced. But this is not consubstantial,
but like-essential. Orth. The like-essential, that I may so speak, is rustic
wisdom; as if someone were to say the same-mannered like-mannered, or the
same-kind like-kind, or the same-glory like-glory, or the same-minded
like-minded. Maced. No; but the like-essential in the case of the incorporeal;
and the consubstantial in the case of bodies. Orth. I said that it is rustic
wisdom; for rather the like-essential ought to be said by you in the case of
bodies, in which there is also likeness, and the consubstantial in the case of
the incorporeal, in which there is identity; for the consubstantial is the
same-essential. Maced. But the consubstantial things have a pre-underlying
essence. Orth. You do not speak truly; for Eve is consubstantial with Adam,
and they do not have a pre-underlying essence of men. Maced. But they have the
earth pre-underlying. Orth. But they are not consubstantial for this reason,
because they are from the earth, but because they admit the same account of
the essence. Maced. And I say them consubstantial for this reason, because
they are from the same essence, from the earth. Orth. Then say also the dogs,
and the snakes, and all the other animals, consubstantial with men; and not
only the irrational animals, but also the plants, and if there is anything
else from the earth. For all things are called earthly homonymously; but they
do not admit the same account of the essence. Maced. I do not follow
Aristotle. Orth. Nor do I. Maced. But Aristotle said of homonyms that the
account of the essence is other. Orth. And do you =28.1337= say the account of
the essence of men and of donkeys and of oxen and of horses and of the other
animals is the same? Maced. Yes. Orth. Do you then have the same account of
the essence as the irrational things? Maced. Yes. For we all came to be from
the earth. Orth. I, not following Aristotle, but the truth itself, do not say
of homonyms that the account of the essence is the same, nor that the rational
are the same as the irrational. And every heretic "has been compared to the
mindless beasts, and has been made like them." Maced. Do not insult while
dialoguing. Orth. I do not insult; but your own voice has brought this
together.
