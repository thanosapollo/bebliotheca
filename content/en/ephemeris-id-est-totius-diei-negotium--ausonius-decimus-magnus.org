The Daily Round, That Is, the Business of a Whole Day edited by Hugh G.

Already bright morning unbars the windows,
already the watchful swallow chatters in the nests:
you, as if it were first and middle night,
Parmeno, sleep.
Dormice sleep through unending winter,
but they spare food: for you the cause of sleep
is that you drink much and stretch with too great

a mass the stuffing.
Thence no sound enters the bent ears
and deep slumber presses the place of the mind
nor do the lightnings of flashing light
assail the eyes.
Once they invent in tales a yearly rest for a youth,
abiding through the turns of night and day,
for whom the Moon prolonged
his sleeps.

Rise, trifler, who ought to be torn with rods:
rise, lest a long sleep, from whence
you do not fear it, be given you: snatch your limbs from the
soft, Parmeno, bed.
Perchance this ditty too, measured in Sapphic verse,
persuades sleep for you?
Drive off the Lesbian mode of rest,
cer iamb.

Boy, ho, rise and give the shoes
and the linen cloth.
Give whatever there is for a wrap
which you have already prepared, that I may go forth.
Give, that I may wash with fountain dew
hands and mouth and eyes.
And see that the chapel be opened
with no outward preparation:
pious words, harmless vows,
are plenty for the divine matter.
I do not demand incense to be burned
nor cakes of honeyed crust,
and the little hearth of living turf
I leave to empty altars.
God is to be prayed to by me
and the Son of the highest God,
majesty of one manner,
joined with the Holy Spirit.
And behold, now I begin the vows:
and the thought of the divinity
senses the presence, fearing.
Does hope, does faith, fear anything?

Almighty, known to me by the mind's worship alone,
unknown to the wicked and unknown to none of the pious:
lacking beginning and end, older than the age
which was or shall come: whose form and measure
neither mind will be able to embrace nor tongue to utter:
whom to behold and to hear commanding face to face
it is lawful, and to sit at the father's right hand,
for the Maker of things himself, himself the cause for things to
be created,
himself the Word of God, the Word God, forerunner
of the world which he was about to make: generated in that
time when time was not yet: brought forth before
the beam and ruddy Dawn should light the sky:
without whom nothing was done, through whom all things were
made: whose
throne is in heaven, to whom, sitting, the earth is subject
and the sea and the unconquerable chaos of dark night:
unresting, himself moving all things, quickener of the inert:
God from an unbegotten Begetter, who, offended by the fraud
of a proud people, called the nations into the kingdoms,
to be worshipped in the better stock of an adopted race:
whom it was permitted the forefathers to behold, at whose
divinity seen
it was also given to have seen the Father: who bore our
contagions
and, having suffered the mockeries of dreadful death,
taught that the way of eternal life is one that can be retraced:
and that not the soul alone returns, but that with the whole
body
it enters the heavenly regions and leaves the empty secret of
the tomb, covered over, to the vacant earth.

Son of the highest Father and bringer of salvation to our age,
to whom the Begetter handed over all the Father's virtues,
keeping back nothing from envy and full of gifts,
open a way for prayers and carry these to the Father's ears.
Grant, Father, a mind unconquered against all crimes
and turn away the viperous wickedness of the venom that would
harm;
let it be enough, that which the serpent betrayed ancient Eve
and joined deceived Adam: we, late seeds
of descendants, once foretold by truth-speaking prophets,
let us avoid the snares which the death-bringing snake
entangles.
Open a way which after the bonds of a sick body
may bear me on high, where the milky path of pure heaven!
surmounts the wandering clouds of the windy moon,
where the pious chiefs have gone and where once unharmed
Elijah, snatched in a four-horse chariot, passes above the air
and Enoch going before with solid body.
Grant, Father, the hoped-for breath of eternal light,
if I do not swear stones are gods and, looking up to the one
altar of the sacred to be revered, I bring unstained
libations of life: if I know you as Father of the only-begotten
Lord and God, and mixed with the two
the Spirit who was hovering over the watery waves.

Grant, Begetter, pardon and purge the tortured breasts:
if I do not seek you with the fibers of cattle, nor with blood
poured out

nor guess the divinity under secret entrails:
if I abstain from crime, liable to error, and if
I wish more than I trust to be approved good and pure.
Deign to accept the confessing soul, if I curse
falling members and if silently it repents and a deep dread
of the sense tortures, and the wounded mind anticipates
the late torments of Gehenna and suffers its own shades.

Grant, Father, that these vows be made valid by our praying.
Let me fear nothing and desire nothing: let me think this
enough,
which is enough; let me wish nothing shameful nor be to myself
a cause of shame; let me not do to anyone things which at the
same time
I would not wish done to me. Let me not be injured by a true
crime
nor stained by a doubtful one: a little seems to separate
the suspected and the truly guilty. Let there be no faculty
of being able to do ill, and let there be present a tranquil
power of being able to do well.
Let me be of slender fare and dress, let me be dear to friends
and always a father without wound of this name,
let me not grieve in mind, not in body: let all the members
perform their accustomed offices: nor let use, wounded in any
parts, miss anything lost.
Let me enjoy peace, let me live secure, let me think no
marvels of the earth, when the last hour of the day has come,
let a life well conscious neither fear death nor wish it.
When by your indulgence I shall seem pure from hidden things,
I shall despise all things, when the only pleasure shall have
been
to hope for your judgment; which while it puts off

its times and the day delays, drive far the savage
snake that lies in wait with flattering errors.
These pious vows, but trembling with sad guilt,
Son, as one who can be appeased, assert before the eternal
Father,
Saviour, God and Lord, mind, glory, Word,
Son, true from the true, light from light,
remaining with the eternal Father, reigning forever,
whom the harmonious songs of tuneful David celebrate:

and Amen strikes the air with voices that will answer.

Enough of prayers has been given to God,
although enough never for the guilty
is made by praying of the divinity.
Give the outdoor dress, boy.
A greeting must be said to friends
and farewell, which is done mutually.
And since through four hours

the sun with hurried horses

bends his course toward midday,
Sosias must now be advised.

For the time for calling friends now draws near;
lest we delay lunch for them or they for us,
run quickly through the neighboring houses, boy.
You yourself know who they are: and already while I speak,
return.
I have invited five; for six is a just banquet
with the host: if more, it is a brawl..
He has gone; we are left with Sosias.

Sosias, we must lunch. Already the whole sun is hot toward the
fourth hour:
the shadow is bent to the fifth mark.
Whether the stored dainties are juicy with lively taste
(for they are wont to deceive), prove by trying.
Shake the boiling pots with rolling palms,
quickly dip your fingers in the hot broth,
which the moist tongue may lick with vibrating return

Boy, skilled servant of winged notes,
fly hither,
make ready the two-leaved tablet,
for which there is much abundance of speaking,
finished with single points,
as one voice is discharged.
I roll rich books
and like thick hail
I roar with a torrent tongue:
for you neither do the ears hesitate,
nor is the page occupied
and with the right hand moved sparingly
it flies over the waxen sea.
When most of all I now speak
in the circuit of a roundabout speaker,
you already hold on the waxes the senses of our breast
scarcely spoken,
I would that my mind had given me
to feel as swift

as with the flight of a winged right hand
you outstrip me speaking.
Who, I ask, who has betrayed me?
Who has already said to you those things
which I was thinking to say?
What thefts in the inmost heart
does the winged right hand practice?
What order of things so new,
that there should come into your ears
what the tongue has not yet discharged?
Learning has not furnished this
nor any hand so swift
of fleet-footed shorthand:
nature has handed this gift to you
and God the present,
that you should know beforehand what I would speak
and should wish the same that I wish.

Portents shake from us peaceful slumbers,

such as we marvel at, when rather often in the high ether

wandering clouds in assembly join various forms

of four-footed beasts and birds, or when earthly
monsters are mixed with sea-things; until, blown apart by
purging east winds,
the clouds are thinned into liquid air,
now forums, now lawsuits, now the pomp of the wide theatre
is visited: and troops of horsemen and slaughters of robbers
I endure: a wild beast tears our faces
or we rage with the sword in the bloody arena.

I walk on foot through shipwrecking sea and leap the straits
at a run and fly above the air on sudden wings,
unspeakable loves also and incestuous disgraces of night
and tragic assemblies we suffer through dreams,
yet there is a refuge, as often as the portents of sleeps
broken quiet dissolves with shame and from the foul image
the free mind wakes: a hand well conscious
safely feels over the whole bed: the shameful
fault of the couch recedes and the crimes of fleeing sleep
manascunt,
I see myself applaud among the triumphing: again
I am dragged unarmed among captive Alans.
I behold temples of the gods and holy doors and golden palaces
and I seem to recline on Tyrian purple
and soon as a guest I lie down at smoky cookshops.
They say the divine bard placed under the leaves of an elm
the empty images of idle sleeps
and twin gates in number: that which with ivory arch
always gathers deceiving forms above the air:
the other, the horny, which sends forth true sights,
but if of doubtful things the choice is granted us,
it is better that trust be wanting to joyful things than that
empty things be feared,
behold I now would rather be deceived; for, provided always
sad things vanish, it is better to have gone without things to
be enjoyed,
than to tremble at evils, it is well enough, if fear be absent,
there are also those who would conjecture weepings and joys as
contrary
and draw events with varied report.

Go through the oblique worlds of heaven, evil dreams,
where wandering storm-clouds blow restless clouds apart;
dwell in the lunar poles: why do you invade our
thresholds and the dark chambers of a narrow roof?
Leave me to pass quiet nights in idleness,
until golden Lucifer returns to me with rosy rising.
But if soft sleep shall soothe me, vexed by no figures
in the night, with tranquil air,
this grove, the elm which greens with leaf in our field,
I dedicate to be inhabited by your watches.
