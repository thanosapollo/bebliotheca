=55.575= On the remainder of the fiftieth psalm, and on repentance. 1. I wish
to give back to you today the leftovers of yesterday's table; but when you
hear leftovers and table, beloved, suspect nothing sensible. For we are not
chopping meats, but we are giving spiritual thoughts.
We do not pour unmixed wine, but we sound a word into your mind. For on
the sensible table, if the meats remain one or two days, they are corrupted
and perish; but in the case of thoughts, if they remain a whole year, they
give forth their own bloom and fragrance. The meats remained, and they were
corrupted by time; the thoughts remained, and they remain giving forth their
own fragrance. Someone ate meats, and went toward corruption; someone ate
thoughts, and was led by the hand toward incorruption. Someone ate meats, and
belched much stench; someone ate thoughts of spiritual fragrance, and belched
much virtue, and gladdened the neighbor.
The prophet then, since he ate such things, said: My heart has belched
forth a good word. The waves are tossed again, but let the mind stand; the
bodies are shaken, but let the hearing be unmoved. See then, and attend with
accuracy to what is said. Yesterday you saw the king and prophet
warring, falling,
rising, conquering, being crowned, being proclaimed. You saw sin coming
on and being slain through repentance. Sin came on as a bear and
threw him down, but repentance entered and raised him. The medicine of the
wounds, the hope of salvation. You saw the prophet going in and rebuking the
prophet. You saw the king bearing the rebuke with philosophy, receiving the
cut, being delivered from the rot. You saw the Master's loving-kindness
shining, and making the dead live. You saw him saying, I have sinned against
the Lord; and the prophet saying: And the Lord has put away your sin. Short
is the confession, shorter still the healing =55.576= and the correction. He
sinned through the deeds, he confessed through the words.
God nodded, and the sore vanished, the wound was set right, and health
returned. But the prophet was not content with this, but after the rebuke,
after the correction, after the deliverance from the wounds, he also wrote a
psalm on the subject, so that his sufferings might become a healing-place for
the generation after these things as well, and his shipwreck might become a
harbor, and that from what he suffered and was set right, another who fell
into the same things might enjoy the same healing and correction. What then
is the psalm, and what is the inscription of the psalm? Let us hear with
accuracy. Unto the end, a psalm of David. [The inscription of the psalm has
clearly taught us the subject; and the very words of the psalm are sufficient
to teach the depth of the thought. One ought to know, however, that the psalm
also has a proclamation of things to come; therefore it has also been
inscribed Unto the end, the word making clear that the prophecy will have an
end. But let no one doubt whether, at the time of repentance, the great David
was counted worthy of prophetic grace. For this can also be learned through
the other psalms, through which he made the confession.] For also in other
psalms, and indeed also in the psalm set before us, he has taught us
expressly that he was not deprived of the grace of the Spirit. For your good
Spirit, he says, do not take away from me. [For he did not ask that it be
granted to him; but he begged that this not be taken. Being enlightened then
by this, and foreseeing that also the


people ruled by him would fall in lawlessnesses, and would undergo certain
penalties for these things, and that they would be enslaved, and become a
sojourner in Babylon, he composed the psalm, both healing his own wounds, and
preparing a fitting medicine for the people. Have mercy on me, O God,
according to your great mercy; and according to the multitude of your
compassions blot out my transgression. [And the words set before us fit both
the most divine David, and the people taken captive by the spear, and those
among us who are in a bad state. For the great wounds need medicines equal in
measure. For this reason the great David also begs that the whole of mercy be
poured out upon him, that the whole spring of compassions empty out the
traces of sin. And he reasonably calls the offense a transgression; for it
has a double transgression of the law] Blot out my transgression. You heard
the prophet saying: And the Lord has taken away your sin. What then do you
seek further? I remain giving thanks, I remain glorifying. My sin has been
taken away; I want the scar also to vanish; the sore has been taken away; I
want to recover my beauty as well.
2. He comes then and says: Have mercy on me, O God.
He does not mention the achievements of his life, but the Master's
loving-kindness, fleeing to mercy, the common harbor of all who have sinned.
Have mercy on me, O God. For your mercy is unspeakable, and surpassing
speech, unable to be interpreted; a consolation to the sinner, but also much
safety to the righteous. For not only are sinners saved by mercy, but the
righteous also are walled by mercy.
And that you may learn that the righteous are walled by mercy, hear what
he says to Peter, the pillar, the foundation, the one called Peter for this
reason, because he was made rock by faith: Simon, Simon, Satan has often
desired to sift you as wheat; but I have prayed for you, that your faith may
not fail. You see that even the righteous need mercy, both that they may
become righteous from sinners, and that having become righteous they may
remain righteous.
For the Apostle also became such from mercy: I give thanks to Christ
Jesus who strengthened me, because he counted me faithful, putting me into
ministry, who was formerly a blasphemer and persecutor and insolent. How then
were you saved? But for this I was shown mercy.
You see this one also being saved by mercy? He persecuted, he laid waste
the Church, he tore up the vines, he became a wolf. He read prophets, and he
transgressed the things prophesied. The zealot of the ancestral law read
prophets, and did not know the one prophesied. How then was he shown mercy?
Saul, Saul, why do you per =55.577= secute me? O the Master's loving-kindness,
where did he come down? God says to a man, Why do you persecute me? He
imitates his own Father. For that one also says to the Hebrew people: My
people, what have I done to you, or how have I wronged you, or how have I
wearied you? answer me. And his Son says to Saul: Saul, Saul, why do you
persecute me? What then did that one? Who are you, Lord? At once he confessed
the mastery. Did you see a well-disposed soul? Who are you, Lord? I am Jesus
the Nazarene, whom you persecute. O the Master's wisdom! Why did he not say,
I am the Son of God, I am the Word who was in the beginning, I am the one
seated at the right of the Father, I am the one existing in the form of God,
I am the one who stretched out heaven, I am the one who founded the earth, I
am the one who spread the sea, I am the one who made the angels, I am the one
present everywhere and filling all things, I am the one begotten from the
Father before the ages? Why did he not say those august and high and great
things, but, I am Jesus the Nazarene, the one from the city below? Because
the one persecuting did not know him.
For if he had known him, he would not have persecuted. He did not know
that he had been begotten from the Father; that


he was from Nazareth, he knew. If then he had said to him that I am the Son
of God, the Word who was in the beginning, the one who made heaven, he would
have been able to say that That one is another, and another is the one I
persecute. Did you understand what I said? If he had said to him those great
and high things, he would have been able to say: He is another; this is not
the one who was crucified. But that he might learn that he is persecuting
that one who was made flesh, who took the form of a slave, who lived among
us, who was crucified, who died, who was buried, who rose, the one from the
place below, he says: I am Jesus the Nazarene, whom you persecute, whom you
saw and know, the one who lived with you. For this reason he at once
confessed the mastery. The Master came down as far as the slave was able to
go up; and for this he was shown mercy.
Have mercy on me, O God, according to your great mercy. And the Canaanite
woman also flees to this harbor: Have mercy on me; my daughter is badly
demon-possessed. For where there is mercy, there is no examination; where
there is mercy, a court does not sit; where there is mercy, penalties are not
demanded; where there is mercy, salvation is without examination; where there
is mercy, the defense is forgiven. I seek only to be shown mercy. For what
reason do you demand penalties of me? Once I seek mercy, a salvation without
inquiry. Have mercy on me, O God, according to your great mercy. You said
great; say how much? I do not know; that it is great, I know; I cannot
measure it, I have not strength to grasp it. I do not know the quantity of
the greatness; the mind does not grasp it, the tongue does not interpret it.
I see the fruit of the mercy as great. For if it were not very great, few
would have been saved. This mercy his Son also displays. For having come he
saved all by mercy. Have mercy on me, O God, the harbor without waves, the
one full of calm. Great is the tumult of my sins, but much is the safety of
my Master's loving-kindness. He became man: this is of mercy. He came into a
virgin womb: this is of loving-kindness. He became what you were, that you
might become what you were not: this is of mercy. And having come, whom did
he at once call? Magi. After those, whom? A tax-collector. After him? A
harlot. After her? A robber. After him, whom? A blasphemer.
3. O new and unexpected things! The first-fruits of the calling, the
tyranny of sin. Because the affairs of the world were in a bad state; for
some were tyrannized by impiety, and others in Judaism and despair were held
by sin; therefore having come
=55.578= from the start he called the first-fruits of those who had
previously sinned, so that no one afterward might despair of his own
salvation. Are you impious? Think of the magi. Are you a plunderer? Think of
the tax-collector. Are you unclean? Think of the harlot. Are you a
man-slayer? Think of the robber. Are you a lawbreaker? Think of the
blasphemer Paul, and afterward an apostle; formerly a persecutor, and
afterward an evangelist; formerly an adulterer, and afterward a bringer of
the bride; formerly a weed, and afterward wheat; formerly a wolf, and
afterward a shepherd; formerly lead, and afterward gold; formerly a pirate
and a drowner, and afterward a helmsman; formerly laying waste the Church,
and afterward being entrusted with the Church; formerly cutting down the
vines, and afterward becoming a planter; formerly tearing down the temple,
and afterward becoming a builder. Did you see surpassing wickedness? did you
see virtue in its prime? did you see a slave's madness? did you see a
Master's loving-kindness? Do not tell me, I am a blasphemer; do not tell me,
I am a persecutor, I am unclean. You have the examples of all.
Flee to whichever harbor you wish. Do you wish in the New? do you wish in
the Old? In the New, Paul; in the Old, David. Do not tell me excuses, do not
tell me delay.


Have you sinned? Repent. Have you sinned ten thousand times? Repent ten
thousand times. I keep smearing these medicines, and I settle the
inflammation. For I know how great is the shame of conscience of those who
have sinned; I know how great is the despair. The devil stands sharpening a
sword, and saying these words. You have shamed your whole youth, you have
destroyed your whole life. Formerly you were a fornicator, afterward you
plundered, you were greedy, you perjured, you blasphemed. What hope of
salvation is there for you? You have perished. For the rest enjoy even the
present life. These are the devil's words, but mine are the opposite. Have
you perished? But you can be saved. Have you fornicated? But you can be
chaste. Have you committed adultery? But you can obtain salvation. Have you
fallen? But you can rise. Is your repentance small? But the Master's
loving-kindness is great. Have you murdered? But repent. Have you been
greedy? But change your mind. Have you committed adultery? But stand off.
Have you been wounded? But be healed. As long as you breathe, on the last
day, on the very bed, if you are lying gasping out your soul, about to go out
of this theater and of life, then repent. The straitness of the time does not
hinder God's loving-kindness. Do not tell me, How, and in what manner? I am
full of sores, and I have been filled with wounds; on the last day can I
confess? You can, and you very much can. Pray, work alms, leave in a will to
the poor.
You are going away there, and your work remains. And do not bring in to
me the opinion of the heretics; do not tell me: How am I saved? When God
works, do not demand penalties; whenever that one heals, do not despair. A
physician stands cutting, and applying ten thousand medicines, and you do not
say, How do you heal? but you yield to the art. You yield to the
fellow-slave's art, and you do not pry; and you do not yield to God's
loving-kindness? Do not say, How? but wonder, but be struck, but glorify.
Since I too ask you, how was the robber saved? He spent his whole life in
murders, in slaughters, in bloods, he dug through walls, he plundered
properties; he did not change his mind, he did not repent while he was on the
earth. Among men he was condemned, and by God he was crowned. He was not
master of life, and he became master of a kingdom. He lost the things here,
and he received the things there. How did he receive? tell me. By one word.
For what did he say? Remember me, =55.579= Lord, in your kingdom. And did the
word have strength? Not the word alone had strength, but also God's
loving-kindness mixed with repentance made the robber a citizen of paradise.
How? I do not know how. Do not demand of me the manner; I seek only to be
saved, I do not pry into the manner of the health. Deliver me, you beg the
physician, from the sickness, and you do not dare to say, How? It is God who
saves you, and you dare to say, How? How many adulterers, soft men, men who
lie with males, how many caught in tyranny dwell in prison? Ten thousand
accusations, bitter enemies, great power. The king only nods, and they are
released. A king, my fellow-slave (for what if he has put on purple? He has
nothing more of my substance, he is the same race as I), nods, and they are
released; and small documents, and the condemned man is set free. No ruler
contradicts, no jailer resists, no authorities of laws stand against; but the
king's dignity becomes a deliverance of the one held. And God, the Master of
angels and archangels, if he only nods, cannot save? And a king, if he
remits, remits punishment, not sin; but my Master remits sins. He says, I do
not want the adulterer to be an adulterer, and his word became a deed. He
says, Let the fornicator not be a fornicator, and his word changed that one's
manner, and
blunted the sin.


4. And that you may learn this accurately, hear: He said, I want heaven
to come to be, and heaven came to be; I want earth to come to be, and earth
came to be, and that substance which was not came into being; I want angels
to come to be, and the people of the angels came to be. His word works
intelligent substances and bodies that had not existed, and he cannot loose
sins? For what is sin toward the Master's loving-kindness? A spider's web.
Wind blows, and a spider's web is torn; God wills, and sin is not undone? I
say these things, not that I may make you more careless, but that I may lead
you into faith of a greater expectation and a better hope. Never despair, O
man; despair of that one who despairs of himself. Think then of this great
prophet, I mean David, after the grace of the Spirit, after the freedom of
speech toward God, after a multitude of achievements, after so many trophies,
proclaiming and saying: Have mercy on me, O God, according to your great
mercy. I have stretched myself upon the sea of the Master's loving-kindness.
And what do you want? tell me. You heard the prophet saying: And the Lord has
taken away your sin. What do you seek further? I do not seek this only; I
seek my beauty, I seek my freedom of speech. And according to the multitude
of your compassions, blot out my transgression. Do you see what he seeks?
What then? has it not been blotted out? what else do you want? Wash me still
more from my sin, and cleanse me from my lawlessness. For what? I seek more,
a greater purity of radiance. Wash me still more from my sin. For what did
you do, that it might come to be?
Attend with accuracy to the word; attend, and stretch yourself. The mercy
of God is unspeakable, uninterpretable, unable to be grasped, surpassing
mind, conquering reasoning. But even if the mercy of God is such, that men
may not become useless when they are shown mercy, it has need also of the
things from us. Attend to what I say: God shows mercy, but he does not show
mercy simply, but he says: Give you also. What then? Something of this sort
he says: You have ten thousand burdens of sins, you have many offenses; I
want to lighten them. Give you also a hand; not because I have need of you,
but because I want you also to contribute something toward your own
salvation; and how, I say. For example the robber was saved, and loosed so
many sins; but not by mercy alone, but also by his own faith. For what does
he say? Remember me, Lord, in your kingdom. What did God contribute toward
that one's salvation? Loosing of sins. What did the robber contribute toward
his own salvation? Faith. Of what sort? Great, high, touching heaven. How, I
say: Jews saw dead being raised, lepers being cleansed, demons being driven,
sea being bridled, earth gushing, a fish bringing a stater, a star leading
Magi, the creation above, the creation below attending as a guard, and after
all these things
=55.580= they crucified him, their own Master and of all. The robber saw
none of these, but him being spat upon, being insulted, being reviled; and he
was not scandalized, but he confessed a king. Do you see that he was not
shown mercy simply, but he also contributed? For the one being shown mercy
must himself also bring something in toward his own salvation. Again the
tax-collector, being a plunderer, being greedy, how was he saved? Christ was
passing by, and says to him: Come, follow me; and rising he followed him. As
then the robber contributed the word, so also the tax-collector furnished him
the following. For just as in the case of those who are sick and held by
incurable diseases, and needing costly medicines, if the physician comes in,
and says to the sick man: There is need of many kinds for the preparation of
the medicine; the sick man says: I do not have so much to give, I am held by
poverty; the physician says to him: Give me this and that kind, and all the
rest I furnish from myself; so


God also does. Give me confession and tears and repentance, and the rest I
grant you from my loving-kindness.
5. This David also begs, that these things be washed off. What then did
David do? what did David furnish? Have mercy on me; and, Wash me still more
from my lawlessness, and cleanse me from my sin. [For already, he says,
through Nathan the prophet you have given the forgiveness, and you have also
brought upon me misfortunes of every kind, as certain cauteries and cuttings;
but I still need washings, having received much of the stench of sin.
Wash me off then again, Master, that you may scrape off all the filth of
sin.] What then did David do? and what did he furnish? He did murder, then,
and adultery; but he furnished tears and confession, and he says: For I know
my lawlessness. It is an exchange. God gives, the one being saved gives. God
gave mercy, the one being saved gave confession. What do you give, O David,
that you may be saved, that I may mix the medicine? For I know my
lawlessness. Do you give this? And is there a man who does not know his sin?
Yes. How many are there who sin, and speak with freedom? how many plunder,
and do not even know that they plunder? how many are greedy, and do not even
perceive it? how many strip widows, and pry into others? how many oppress
orphans, and neither mourn, nor weep, nor confess? How then can they be
saved, not even knowing that they sin? Did you see the prophet crying out and
saying, For I know my lawlessness? [For not even after the forgiveness that
came from you did I receive forgetfulness of the sin, but I continually see
the images of my evils and the lawlessnesses that were dared, and by night I
dream, and by day I imagine,] For I know my lawlessness, and my sin is before
me continually. O nobility of soul! He did not hand over the memory of the
sin to forgetfulness, but even when it had been forgiven he wrote it. As in
an image, in his conscience each day he saw the woman who had been
adulterated, each day he saw the rot. And my sin is before me continually. I
see it, do you not see it; I write, do you blot it out. For see what happens.
If you remember it, God will not remember it; if you forget it, God will
remember it. [For I am, he himself says through the prophet, the one who
blots out your sins, and I will remember them no more; but you, remember.]
Why should I remember? That you may become well-disposed, that you may know
what I have forgiven you. For if you are always remembering the bulk of the
sin, you will always also be remembering the greatness of the benefaction. I
have forgiven you, he says, but do not forget the grace, and I will not
remember the sin, but you remember.
Such were the saints; they forgot the achievements, and remembered the
sins; not as men now forget the sins, and if they do something useful, they
bring it into the midst. Have you done a good thing? Hide, that your Master
may say it. Have you =55.581= done an evil thing? Remember, that your Master
may forget it. Yield to your Master. For do you not say the achievements as
your Master says them? If you say them, you lessen them; if your Master says
them, he increases them. How, I say: You give to a poor man; and if you say,
being asked there, how do you say? I saw a poor man hungry, and I fed him.
How does he himself say? He does not say them thus; but how? You saw me
hungry and you fed me. Leave it to that one who can say it greatly; leave it
to the one who says them and swells them to say them; but say your own sins,
that you may blot them out. But are you ashamed


to say, because you have sinned? Say them each day in your prayer. And what?
do I not say? Say them to the fellow-slave who reproaches you, say them to
God who heals them. For if you do not say them, God is not ignorant of them.
For does he want to learn them from you? When you were doing them, he was
present; when you were working them, he stood by, he knew.
You were working, and you were not ashamed. The deeds that put you to
shame you were not ashamed of, and the words that justify you you are ashamed
of? Say them here, that you may not say them there. Say, and weep. Your sins
have been written in a book; your tears are a sponge. Weep, and they are
blotted out; weep, and the book is found clean there.
Great is the sponge of the tears of the sins; great is the power of the
tears. Shall I tell you how much the tears have strength? What do you think
is a great achievement? But attend with accuracy to what I say. What do you
think the great achievement is? You will say martyrdom: For no one has
greater love than this, that one lay down his soul for his friends. Greatest
is the achievement of martyrdom, because the martyrs pour out blood. For the
deeds of the martyrs are manifest. The martyrs pour out blood, the sinners
pour out tears. And that you may learn how much power the tears have, did the
harlot not pour out blood? did she not pour out a spring of tears, and blot
out the sins?
And why do I speak of a harlot, an unclean woman? The pillar of the
Church, the foundation of the faith, the head of the choir of the apostles,
Peter, did he not deny Christ both once and twice and three times? did he
pour out blood? did he not pour out springs of tears, and weep, and wipe off
his sin? And I know my lawlessness. Do you not have a book in the house, and
write the daily account? Have a book in the conscience, and write the daily
sins. Something of this sort I mean: When you lie down on your bed, and no
one troubles you, before sleep comes on, bring the book into the midst, and
reckon up your sins, saying in yourself: Have I sinned today? whether in
word, or in deed?
6. This the prophet also exhorts: Be angry, and do not sin; the things
you say in your hearts, be pricked upon your beds. By day you have no
leisure; but both fear of rulers, and conversation of companions, and care of
affairs, and provision for raising children, and attention to a wife, and
care of a table, and ten thousand things drag you about. But when you come to
the bed, to the harbor without waves, and no one troubles you, say to your
heart and to your soul: We have spent the day today, O soul; what good have
we worked, or what evil have we done? and remember Gehenna, that by fear you
may add to the good, and take away the evil. If you are on the bed, and
remember your sins =55.582=, weep, and you can blot them out on the bed.
Entreat your God, and so send your soul along to sleep, having testified the
sins. Make an account to him, and your God will make an account to you. What
is heavy, what is burdensome, lying on the bed to reckon up the daily sins?
And when you reckon, hang your reasoning, scrape it, open it with the fear of
Gehenna, bring upon it a cutting that has no pain, make a fearful court for
yourself, make the reasoning lighter, that also on the next day you may
become more hesitant about doing the sins. For I know my lawlessness; and my
sin is before me continually. Against you alone have I sinned, and I have
done the evil before you. What is it that you say? I was a king, I feared you
only. I was a king, I did not fear the one who had been wronged. He was my
soldier, he could not punish me; but I am anxious because of you, lest you
demand penalties of me. Against you alone have I sinned, and


I have done the evil before you. [Having enjoyed, he says, many and great
gifts from you, I repaid the gifts with the opposite things, having dared to
do the things forbidden by the law. For he does not say this, that I did not
wrong Uriah; for he did wrong both that one and that one's wife; but the
greatest lawlessness has been dared against God himself, the one who chose
him and made him king. And he has well added, I have done evil before you.
For the history also remembers this: For, it says, the thing that had
happened appeared evil before the Lord. From the people taken captive by the
spear this happening is to be understood thus, Against you alone have I
sinned: I became, he says, ungrateful concerning your gifts, and I have
transgressed the laws set by you, and having enjoyed many good things, I have
become thankless concerning these; and having wronged the Babylonians in
nothing, I have been greatly wronged by them.
That you may be justified in your words, and conquer when you are judged.
I became, he says, a bringer of evils to myself; but your justice shines even
so. For when judgment comes to be, and the things done by you toward me are
brought into the midst, and the things dared by me are compared with those,
you will be shown righteous and loving-kind; but I will appear a lawbreaker
and ungrateful. The "that" then is not here indicative of a cause. For
neither David himself nor the people sinned for this, that God might be
justified; but on the contrary, even when the sin has been done by these,
God's justice is displayed.
For he has made all providence both of this one and of those.] That you
may be justified in your words, and conquer when you are judged. What is it
that he says? I have sinned against you; if a court comes to be, you conquer;
and if someone judges, you conquer. How? Since he saw God always wanting to
be judged with men: Hear, you valleys, foundations of the earth; for there is
a judgment for God toward his people; and again, My people, what have I done
to you? or how have I wearied you? or how have I grieved you? answer me; and
elsewhere: Hear, O heaven, and give ear, O earth, for the Lord has spoken; I
have begotten sons and raised them up; but they have set me aside; and
everywhere he saw God being judged with men; he says to him, The
victory-prizes are with you; That you may be justified in your words, and
conquer when you are judged.
Me, being a man, a farmer, you made a king; having become a king, you
appointed me a prophet. I threw down Goliath not =55.583= by nature of body,
but by power of faith. I put on purple. You cast out another, and you brought
me in; you gave me so much wealth.
7. That you may be justified in your words, and conquer when you are
judged. If you say my sins, and I do not say them, I am condemned. For this I
have need of mercy. For behold I was conceived in lawlessnesses, and in sins
my mother conceived me. From above, he says, and from the beginning of nature
sin has held sway. For the transgression of the commandment anticipated Eve's
conception. For after the transgression and the deprivation of paradise Adam
knew Eve his wife, and conceiving she bore Cain.
This then he wants to say, that sin, having held sway over our
forefathers, worked a certain road and path through the race. And we are
taught through all these things, not that the operation of sin is natural;
for then we would be free of punishment; but that nature inclines toward
stumbling, being troubled by the passions; yet the will conquers nonetheless,
using labors as fellow-workers. He does not then, as some have foolishly
supposed, accuse marriage, having so understood, I was conceived in
lawlessnesses; but he brings into the midst the lawlessness dared from above
by the forefathers, and he says that that


has become the spring of these streams. For if those, he says, had not
sinned, they would not have received the penalty of death; and not being
mortal, they would have been better than corruption; and with incorruption
passionlessness would altogether have been present; and with passionlessness
in force, sin would not have had room. But since they sinned, they were
handed over to corruption; having become corruptible, they have also begotten
such children; and with such, desires and fears and pleasures go along.
Against these reasoning also contends, and having conquered
it is proclaimed, but having been defeated it owes shames. And Symmachus,
i.e. for Conceived, has set Bore. [For behold you have loved truth. Thus, he
says, you judge, and you punish some, and you crown others, because you love
the truth. And loving the truth, and knowing the weakness of nature, share
pardon with those who ask for the medicines.
And the unclear and hidden things of your wisdom you have made clear to
me.] Leaving aside to mention the kingship, he mentions the prophecy. Thus
you have honored me, you have revealed your Son to me and made him known.
I learned that you have a Son, that you have one sharing the throne, I
learned that he is about to become man. We proclaimed the Gospels to the
world, the cross, the tomb, the resurrection, the ascension; I spoke his
descent, I spoke his ascent, I spoke his sitting at the right, I spoke the
calling of the Church, I spoke the salvation of the nations, I spoke the
casting out of the Jews, I spoke the election of the apostles. For behold,
The unclear and hidden things of your wisdom you have made clear to me. Thus
you have honored me, thus you have loved me, that even the greatest mystery,
your Son, you have revealed to me. And where did he make these things clear?
Hear the prophet himself, how he speaks his descent from heaven. He will come
down as rain upon a fleece. What is this? Since when the Son of God arrived,
he did not come shaking the world, nor sending forth lightnings, nor sending
thunderbolts, nor with the Godhead naked; for all would have perished, if he
had come with the Godhead naked; the mountains would not have borne it;
since, He looks upon the earth, and makes it tremble, because he touches the
mountains, and they smoke. If he had shown the substance naked, the sun would
have been quenched, the moon would have vanished, the sea would have been
dried, the earth would have perished, our nature would have been paralyzed.
For this he put on flesh, coming quietly and without noise, no one
=55.584= being aware, not even the devil himself; but he came quietly, and
dwelt in a virgin womb. O new and unexpected things! A stone is melted, and a
womb is not melted; the mountains are destroyed, and the womb bears God. How
shall I interpret? how shall I present it? O the heretic's madness! How did
the slave-woman bear the Master? You who examine the things above, speak the
things below; he who is present everywhere, how was he in the womb, and was
not shut in together? For he was both in the womb, and he sat on the throne.
But the how I do not know; I receive it by faith, I do not pry with
reasoning. But that he is about to arrive without noise, not troubling, nor
making a tumult, He will come down as rain upon a fleece. He did not say,
Upon the earth, but, Upon a fleece. For rain coming down upon the earth makes
a noise; but coming down upon wool and a fleece it makes no noise; for the
softness of the wool undoes the violence of the downpour of the rain.
8. And that you may learn that he came without noise, not troubling, not
making a tumult, not destroying, not laying waste, He will come down as rain
upon a fleece. You have spoken his descent that came to be without noise;
speak also his betrayal: He who eats my bread has magnified a heel against
me. Speak the envy of the Jews: They hated me without a cause. Speak also his
court: Why did nations rage, and peoples meditate


empty things? The kings of the earth stood by, and the rulers were gathered
together against the Lord and against his Christ. Speak also his cross: They
dug my hands and my feet; they numbered all my bones. Speak also his food and
his drink: They gave gall into my food, and for my thirst they gave me
vinegar to drink. Speak also the casting out of the Jews: I will not receive
calves from your house, nor he-goats from your flocks. Speak also the calling
of the nations: All nations, clap your hands, shout to God with a voice of
exultation; for the Lord most high. terrible, a great king over all the
earth. Speak also the revilings from the Jews: He hoped upon the Lord, let
him deliver him, let him save him, because he wants him. Speak also his tomb:
They set me in the lowest pit. Speak also his resurrection: For you will not
abandon my soul to Hades, nor will you give your holy one to see corruption.
Speak also his ascent: God has gone up in a shout, the Lord in a voice of a
trumpet. Speak also his sitting: The Lord said to my Lord: Sit at my right,
until I set your enemies a footstool of your feet. Speak also the glory of
the Church: The queen stood at your right in gold-shot clothing, clothed,
embroidered. Speak also the choir of virgins: Virgins shall be brought after
her. Speak also the election of the apostles: Instead of your fathers your
sons have been born. Speak also the baptism: Upon water of rest he nourished
me. Speak also the mysteries: You have prepared a table before me in the
presence of those who afflict me; speak also the chrism: You have fattened my
head with oil, and your cup that makes me drunk as strongest.
Speak also the asking of the nations: Ask of me, and I will give you
nations as your inheritance, and as your possession the ends of the earth.
For behold, The unclear and hidden things of your wisdom you have made clear
to me. [I for my part consider myself worthy of no pardon, having become
ungrateful after so many gifts. For you not only seated me on the royal
thrones, but you have also made clear beforehand the things that will be
after a long time, and the things unclear to others you have made clear to
me, so as even to teach the others the incarnation of your only-begotten Son
and the saving passion and the resurrection.
=55.585= Having been taught all these things beforehand by your holy
Spirit, I beg to have a share in that grace which I prophesy beforehand to
the others. Therefore I cry: You will sprinkle me with hyssop, and I shall be
cleansed; you will wash me, and I shall be made white above snow. For the
gift of baptism alone can work this cleansing.] Therefore he says that I have
need of the grace that will be given to all men. For that alone can wash me
off accurately, and give me the whiteness of snow. That the hyssop worked no
forgiveness of sins, it is easy to learn from the Mosaic writings. The hyssop
then is a riddle of other things. For in Egypt, sprinkling the doorposts with
hyssop with the blood of the sheep, they escaped the hands of the destroyer.
And those things were a type of the saving sufferings. For here too there is
blood, and a saving wood, and salvation furnished to those who approach with
faith. You will make me hear exultation and gladness; humbled bones will
exult. Fill, he says, the ears with this gladness, promising the perfect
cleansing, that the cheer may run through all the parts of the body, and the
bones now humbled by ill-treatment may bloom again, and recover their own
strength. Do not even look, he says, at the lawlessnesses dared by me, but at
me who lament these, and blot out my lawlessness. What do you want, O
prophet? Your sin has been loosed, your punishment has vanished.


I am not content with these; I have a loving-kind Master, who gives more
than I ask. And what do you want? I was a prophet; I want to be a prophet
again. I seek that freedom of speech, the gift of the Spirit, the great
grace. The grace has stood off from me, as a dove seeing mire; as a bee I
want it to return. Turn your face away from my sins and blot out all my
lawlessnesses. Say clearly what you want. Create a clean heart in me, O God.
[For since I have received the old age of sin, renew me by your
loving-kindness. These things he also promised through Ezekiel the prophet to
those taken captive by the spear in Babylon. For I will give them, he says, a
new heart and a new spirit. And he does not mean the all-holy Spirit, but the
impulse of the rational; that is, having trained you with this long training,
and having taught you what sort of fruits sin grows, I will prepare you to
choose virtue.
This the blessed David also asked here, both that his injured heart be
made new, and that the rational be strengthened, so as to run the straight
road. And renew a right spirit in my inward parts.]
9. O nobility of soul! O love concerning God! That you may speak to me
again, and I speak to you, I seek that friendship, the companionship. For
what, that I have been delivered from punishment? For me punishment is not to
converse with you. I love you, and I am mad. I cannot see your substance; for
it is invisible; but from the love I see your works, and I leap. I have not
seen you, but I saw the heaven, and I leaped, and I said: The heavens tell
the glory of God. I saw the earth, and I said: The earth is the Lord's and
the fullness of it. I saw the sea, and I rejoiced, and I said: This is the
sea, great and wide. I saw the day and the night, and I cried to you: Yours
is the day and yours is the night; you have prepared illumination and the
sun. I saw the mountains, and having wondered I said: The high mountains for
the deer. And just as the one who loves, even if he does not see the one
loved, but sees his cloak or shoes, rejoices and leaps, and as it were takes
a certain consolation of the love; so also I go about upon the body of the
creation, wandering and going mad, always saying to you: As the deer longs
for the springs of the waters, so my soul longs toward God the strong, the
living; when shall I come and appear to God. You are a king, you have a
diadem; what do you want? Let these go; I seek that beauty, I want the
prophecy. I despise this authority, I look down on the mire; these are
leaves, and night, and a dream, and grass. I seek my beauty. When will it
come? I cannot bear the =55.586= delay, since I have lost this freedom of
speech, which sin, coming on, walled off. Create a clean heart in me, O God.
And I do not seek a clean heart only, but also its inhabitant. And renew a
right spirit in my inward parts. He well said, Renew; for the house was
constructed from above. Sin has made it old, your grace will renew it. Do not
cast me away from your face; and do not take your holy Spirit away from me.
[Clearly then we have learned through these words that he was not stripped of
the grace of the holy Spirit. For he does not, as one stripped, beg to
receive it back, but he entreats not to be deprived of this, nor to become
far from the divine care.
For here he called the care a face. Restore to me the exultation of your
salvation; and establish me with a governing spirit. What he has not lost, he
has entreated to have, that is, the grace of the Spirit; and what he has
thrown off, he begs to receive back; and this was the gladness in God. I
enjoyed, he says, all cheer, when I had much


freedom of speech toward you, Master; but now stripped of this, I have also
been deprived of good cheer. And the slavery of pleasure stripped me of the
freedom of speech. Therefore I beg that my mind receive back the former
governance, and receive back the authority against the passions. For again
here he called a governing spirit the self-ruling reasoning.] And what do you
give me, O David, if you receive the Spirit? I will teach lawless men your
ways, and the impious will turn back upon you. If I receive the Spirit, I
converse with the world: All nations, clap your hands, shout to God with a
voice of exultation; for the Lord most high, terrible, a great king over all
the earth. If I receive the Spirit, I say: Rule in the midst of your enemies.
If I receive the Spirit, I say: He will not give to God an atonement for
himself, and the price of the redemption of his soul; and again I say: Do not
harden your hearts, as in the provocation; and again: All lawlessness will
stop its mouth. If I receive the Spirit, I say: Turn aside from evil, and do
good; seek peace, and pursue it. I will narrate your benefactions toward me,
and I will say: Come, hear, and I will narrate to you, all who fear God, how
many things he has done for my soul. I will teach lawless men your ways, and
the impious will turn back upon you. And where are the Jewish things? Do you
not sacrifice sheep or calves to me? You do not seek these. For sacrifice and
offering you have not wanted; whole burnt offerings and those concerning sin
you have not sought. You said: I will not receive calves from your house, nor
he-goats from your flocks. Sacrifice to God a sacrifice of praise. You do not
want these, but you seek the things of your Son; you want the turning back of
the error, and the choosing of the truth; you want that sacrifice. Deliver me
from bloods, O God, the God of my salvation. [He continually turns the murder
of Uriah upon the memory. For this he has also made clear in the preface,
that My sin is before me continually. My tongue will exult your
righteousness. This Symmachus interpreted thus: My tongue will speak
your almsgiving. For I will not be silent having enjoyed the forgiveness, but
I will continue hymning you, and narrating your graces. Lord, you will open
my lips, and my mouth will announce your praise. For sin is accustomed to
bind the tongue, to stop the mouth. The prophet then entreats, through the
forgiveness, to have a share in the former freedom of speech, and to move the
tongue to hymnody. For if you had wanted a sacrifice, I would have given it;
you will not be well-pleased with whole burnt offerings.
These also agree with the things said in the psalm before this. For we
have heard God saying there: I will not receive calves from your house.
Therefore having heard from the divine voice, he reasonably said, For if you
had wanted a sacrifice, I would have given it, and the rest. For you, he
says, have said that the sacrifices of the irrational are not well-pleasing
to you, for which reason I will also offer the sacrifice that pleases you.]
Since then I do not want these, what do you give me? I have also another
sacrifice, which you have been well-pleased with; A sacrifice =55.587= to God
is a crushed spirit; a crushed and humbled heart God will not despise. [A
victim, he says, pleasing, and well-pleasing to you our God, is moderation of
mind. Therefore having humbled my heart exceedingly, and as it were having
thinned it very much, I will bring the sacrifice pleasing to you.] I will
sacrifice to you the sacrifice of humility; only loose my sin, give me the
holy Spirit.
10. For that this sacrifice is much better than the Jewish, not only now,
but was also then, I mean humility; for those three youths, who in the
Babylonian furnace worked the flame into clay, the young and the aged, the
few and


more powerful than the many, the captives and stronger than the king, who
lost a fatherland, and did not lose faith, the naked and clothed, the poor
and well-supplied, the slaves and better than free men, who worked the
furnace into dew, and changed the elements, who quenched the operation of the
fire, whom the tyrant bound, and the fire loosed, they were entreating, and
they said: We have sinned, we have been lawless, we have done injustice,
having stood off from you. There is no ruler, nor prophet, nor leader, nor a
place to offer fruit before you and find mercy; but in a crushed soul and a
spirit of humiliation may we be received. A sacrifice to God is a crushed
spirit; a crushed and humbled heart God will not despise.
Do good, Lord, in your good pleasure to Zion, and let the walls of
Jerusalem be built. Then you will be well-pleased with a sacrifice of
righteousness, an offering and whole burnt offerings; then they will offer
calves upon your altar. [More clearly we have been taught from these words
that the psalm is full of prophecy. For these words fit those compelled to
dwell in Babylon, and longing for deliverance from the slavery.
For they beg that the city be counted worthy of a certain pity, the
circuits being set up again, of the worship accomplished according to law.
For now, he says, it is not possible for those dwelling in the foreign
land to offer the appointed sacrifices, the law declaring to sacrifice to
you in that city alone. But if we obtain the return, and set up the temple
again, then we will offer you the lawful sacrifices.] And the end of this
psalm also has another prophecy. For since he has set the gifts of the
all-holy Spirit above, then coming down he has shown God not being pleased
with the sacrifices according to law, he begs that the new Zion appear, and
that the heavenly Jerusalem be founded as a city upon earth, and that the new
constitution be furnished as quickly as possible, which does not offer
irrational victims, but the offering and sacrifice of righteousness.
Therefore attend here, I beg; for I want to make you all teachers, all monks;
not by not having wives, but by displaying piety, pious, breathing fire. What
then is the thing sought? This is it, he prayed, he confessed, he entreated,
he begged, he supplicated, he poured out tears, he asked for a clean heart,
he gave faith. The thing sought, whether he obtained it. Great is the
pleasure of the tossing. A helmsman is not so delighted when the waves are
raised behind him, and push the ship forward, the wind standing at the stern,
as I, seeing this sea, exult and leap. For I do not see waves only being
tossed, but also the multitude increasing and being moved; being tossed and
being choked, and not withdrawing; being forced, and not leaping away; being
afflicted, and fixing the mind. For the desire of the word wakes the
eagerness.
The thing sought, then, is this: He prayed, he begged, he entreated, he
supplicated, he put off the punishment, he was set up again from the penalty.
The thing sought, whether he returned to the former =55.588= freedom of
speech; whether he received the grace of the holy Spirit; whether he received
what he asked, a clean heart and a right spirit; whether the repentance had
so much strength, as to restore him to the former nobility. Attend with
accuracy: This psalm is the fiftieth.
Which? The one about the sin, the one about the confession, the one about
the asking, the fiftieth. After this he wrote a sixtieth, a seventieth, an
eightieth, a ninetieth, a hundredth. So that no one may say: What then, that
he wrote psalms? but he did not write them by the holy Spirit; for he did not
receive a holy Spirit; but he simply wrote the ones after these things by
human understanding; for he was empty of the Spirit


holy. For the sin was forgiven him, but the Spirit was not given him. For he
himself asked; but nowhere do we find an answer saying, that I have given it
to you. Attend, I beg: He wrote a fiftieth, a sixtieth, a seventieth, an
eightieth, a ninetieth, and a hundredth. He writes also another psalm after
the hundredth, after also many other psalms saying, The Lord said to my Lord;
Sit at my right, until I set your enemies a footstool of your feet. This
psalm then, spoken after the sin, we find spoken in the holy Spirit.
From where is it clear? Christ made it clear. For when he had worked all
things, he bridled the sea, he cleansed lepers, he put demons to flight, he
drove out wickedness, he tightened a paralytic, a voice came down from above:
This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well-pleased, hear him; the Lord
testified to him, the deeds cried out, the Spirit came upon him in the form
of a dove, John testified to him: Behold the lamb of God who takes away the
sin of the world; for the rest as he was about to go up upon the cross,
having called them, he says to them: What does it seem to you concerning the
Christ? I did not ask you at the beginning; I did not testify to you at the
beginning, because I had not yet furnished you a demonstration of divinity.
But when all the elements also showed my divinity, when the Father, when the
concord toward him, when my teaching led all there by the hand, when you do
not even have a shadow of contradiction, What now does it seem to you
concerning the Christ? whose son is he? Those ungrateful and shameless ones
say, Of David, denying the divinity, and not wanting to say that he is of
God. What then does he himself? How then does David in the holy Spirit call
me Lord, saying: The Lord said to my Lord; Sit at my right, until I set your
enemies a footstool of your feet? And if David had the Spirit, the sin was
loosed; and if the sin was loosed, he also received the asking. For David
says: Create a clean heart in me, O God, and renew a right spirit in my
inward parts.
Do you see that he returned again to his own dignity? Again he became a
prophet, again he converses about Christ, again he received back his own
freedom of speech, and the sin did him no harm. Did you see how great is the
power of repentance? What do I say? Both living and dying he had strength.
Barbarians came once, and were besieging the city. Hezekiah being righteous
was entreating God and asking, saying: Have mercy on me, Lord, and spare this
city. The prophet Isaiah comes to him, and says, that that one may not think
that his righteousness was heard; and yet David had died and had a thousand
years in Hades; what then does he say? I will shield you and this city for my
sake and for David my servant. David died, and his freedom of speech lives,
and the one who had died was standing before the one living. O a righteous
man fighting at the wall even after death! And here I will rest my word,
having sufficiently, as I think, given back the loosing of the things sought,
in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory and the power unto the ages of
the ages.
Amen.
