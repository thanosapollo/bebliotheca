* On the Ascension of Our Lord Jesus Christ
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

On the Ascension of our Lord Jesus Christ; it was delivered in the
martyr-shrine of Romanesia, where the bodies of martyrs, lying under the floor
near relics of heretics, were brought up, and were buried above separately.

=50.441=

1. And when we celebrated the memorial of the Cross, we kept the feast outside
the city; and now when we keep this bright and flashing day of the Ascension
of the Crucified, again we keep the feast outside the city. We do this not
wishing to dishonor the city, but hastening to honor the martyrs. For lest
these saints accuse us and say: we were not =50.442= worthy to see one day of
our Master celebrated in our tents; lest these saints accuse us and say: that
we poured out our blood for him, and were counted worthy to have our heads cut
off; but we were not counted worthy to see the day of his feast celebrated in
our lodgings; for these reasons, leaving the city, we have run to the feet of
these saints, by the present day, also making apology to them for the time
that has passed.
For if even before this it was necessary to run to these noble athletes of
piety, when they lay under the =50.443= floor; much more now must we do this,
when the pearls are by themselves, when the sheep have been separated from the
wolves, when the living have withdrawn from the dead. For they had no harm
even before this from the fellowship of burial; for those whose
spirits are in the heavens, their bodies were not harmed at all by the
neighborhood; those whose soul is in the hand of God, their relics suffered
nothing from the placing. So they had no harm, even before this; but our
people suffered no ordinary loss from the places, running to the relics of the
martyrs, but making the prayers with doubt and distinction because the tombs
of the saints were unknown, and where the true treasures lie. And the same
thing happened as if flocks of clean sheep, eager to enjoy streams, should
come to the clean springs, but some stench and foul smell falling from nearby
would make them rear back again; so it happened also with this flock. The
people walked toward the clean springs of the martyrs; but perceiving
heretical stench rising from nearby, they reared back again. Therefore this
wise shepherd and common teacher, who manages all things toward the building
of the Church, knowing this, did not endure to overlook this loss for long,
this warm lover and zealot of the martyrs. But what does he do? See the
wisdom: he covered over and blocked below the turbid and foul streams, and set
the clean springs of the martyrs in a clean place. And consider how much
philanthropy he showed toward those who had departed, and honor toward the
martyrs, and care toward the people.
Toward those who had departed he showed philanthropy, not moving their bones,
but leaving them to remain in the place; toward the martyrs honor, freeing
them from the wicked neighborhood; toward the people care, not leaving them to
make the prayers with distinction. For these reasons we have brought you here,
that the gathering may become brighter, that the spectacle may be more
splendid, not of men only, but also of martyrs assembled; not of martyrs only,
but also


angels. For angels too are present; today the assembly of angels and martyrs
has come to be. And if you wish to see both martyrs and angels, open the eyes
of faith, and you will see that spectacle; for if somehow the air is filled
with angels, much more the church; and if the church, much more the present
day, when their Master is taken up. That the whole air is filled with angels,
hear what the Apostle says, putting women to shame so as to have a covering on
the head: Women ought to have authority on the head because of the angels. And
again Jacob: The angel, he says, who delivers me from my youth. And those in
the house who were with the apostles said to Rhoda: It is his angel; and again
Jacob: I saw, he says, a camp of angels. And why did he see a camp of angels
on the earth =50.444= and an army? Just as a king commands the camps to sit in
each city, lest a barbarian war rise up and overrun the cities; so also God,
since there are also demons in this air, barbarian and wild, always raising
wars and enemies of peace, set against them the camps of the angels, that by
appearing only they may check them, that they may always procure peace for us.
And that you may learn that they are angels of peace, hear the deacons always
saying in the prayers: Ask for the angel of peace. Do you see that both angels
are present and martyrs? What then is more wretched than those left behind
today? What more blessed than us who have come and enjoy this festival? But
let the words about the angels wait for us at other times; and let us bring
the word to the subject of the present feast.
2. What then is the present feast? Solemn and great, beloved, and surpassing
the mind of men, and worthy of the munificence of the God who made it. For
today reconciliations have come to be for God toward the race of men; today
the long enmity has been dissolved, and the long war has been taken away;
today a certain wondrous peace has returned, never expected before. For who
would have hoped that God was going to be reconciled to man? not because the
Master is inhuman, but because the servant is slothful; not because the Lord
is cruel, but because the slave is ungrateful. Do you wish to learn how we
provoked this philanthropic, this gentle Master of ours? For it is just to
learn the subject of our former enmity, so that, when you see us honored, the
enemies and adversaries, you may marvel at the philanthropy of him who
honored, so that you may not think the change came from our own achievements,
so that learning the excess of his grace, you may not cease thanking him
continually for the greatness of the gifts. Do you wish then to learn how we
provoked our Master, the philanthropic, the gentle, the good, who manages all
things for our salvation? He once planned the complete destruction of our
race, and was so angry with us as to destroy us with women and children and
beasts and cattle and the whole earth. And if you wish, I will make you hear
the sentence itself: For I will blot out, he says, the man whom I made, from
the face of all the earth, and the beasts, and the cattle, because I
considered that I made man.
And that you may learn that he did not hate our nature, but turned away from
wickedness, he who said, I will blot out the man whom I made, from the face of
the earth, says to the man: The time of every man has come to me. And if he
hated man, he would not have spoken with a man. But now you see him neither
wishing to do what he threatened to do, but even the Lord making apology to
the slave, and conversing as with a friend of equal honor and telling the
causes of the coming destruction, not that


the man may learn the causes, but that by telling them to the others, he may
make them more temperate. But as I was saying, our race fared so badly before,
as to be in danger of falling even from the earth itself. But we =50.445= who
appeared unworthy of the earth, today have been taken up into the heavens; we
who were not even worthy of the rule below, have gone up to the kingdom above,
we have surpassed the heavens, we have taken hold of the royal throne; and the
nature for which the Cherubim guarded paradise, this sits today above the
Cherubim. But how did this wondrous and great thing come to be? how were we
who had offended, who appeared unworthy of the earth, and had fallen from the
rule below, taken up to such a height? how was the war dissolved, how was the
wrath taken away? How? for this is the wondrous thing, that peace came not by
those who were unjustly angry at God, but by him who was justly indignant
entreating us. For we are ambassadors on behalf of Christ, as though God were
entreating through us. What is this? he himself has been insulted, and he
himself entreats? Yes; for he is God, and therefore as a philanthropic father
he entreats. And see what happens: The mediator is the Son of him who
entreats, not a man, nor an angel, nor an archangel, nor any of the servants.
And what does the mediator do? The work of a mediator. For just as when two
turn away from each other and do not wish to be reconciled, some other coming,
and throwing himself in the middle of both, dissolves the enmity; so also
Christ did. God was angry with us, we turned away from God, the philanthropic
Master; throwing himself in the middle, Christ reconciled each nature. And how
did he throw himself in the middle? He himself received the punishment owed by
us from the Father, and he endured the punishment from there and the
reproaches here. Do you wish to learn how he received both? Christ, he says,
redeemed us from the curse of the law, becoming a curse for us. You saw how he
received the punishment coming from above; see how he also endured the
reproaches coming from below: The reproaches of those who reproach you, he
says, fell upon me. You saw how he dissolved the enmity, how he did not first
stand away, doing and suffering and transacting all things, until he brought
the adversary and enemy up to God himself, and made him a friend? And the
subject of these goods is the present day; for just as taking a first-fruits
of our nature, so he brought it up to the Master. And what happens in the
fields that bear ears of grain, someone taking a few ears, and making a small
sheaf, and offering it to God, through the small blesses the whole field; this
also Christ did, through that one flesh and the first-fruits he made our race
to be blessed. But why did he not bring up the whole nature? Because this is
not of a first-fruits, if someone offers the whole, but if someone offering a
little, through the little prepares the whole to be blessed. And yet if it was
a first-fruits, someone says, it was necessary that the first man who came to
be be brought up; for a first-fruits =50.446= is what is first brought forth,
the first that sprouts. This is not a first-fruits, beloved, if we offer the
first fruit that is fading and weak, but if we offer the approved. Since
therefore that fruit was liable to sin, for this reason it was not brought up,
even if it came first; but this one was freed from sin; for this reason it was
also brought up, even if it came after these things; for this is a
first-fruits.
3. And that you may learn that it is not the first fruit that sprouts that is
a first-fruits, but the approved and noble and come to the fitting prime, I
will furnish you the testimony from the Scriptures. If you enter, says Moses
to the people, into the land of promise which the Lord your God gives you, and
plant in it every tree that makes edible fruit, for three years you shall not
cleanse its fruit; but in the


fourth year its fruit shall be holy to the Lord. And yet if a first-fruits
were what is first brought forth, the fruit in the first year, this itself
ought to have been given to the Lord; but now he says: For three years you
shall not cleanse its fruit, but you shall leave it, because the tree is
fading, because it is weak, because the produce is untimely; but the fourth,
he says, shall be holy to the Lord. And consider the wisdom of the lawgiver:
he neither allowed him to eat it, lest before God he himself take the fruit;
nor did he permit it to be offered, lest it be offered untimely to the Lord.
But, Leave it, he says, since it is first; but do not offer it, since it is
unworthy of the honor of the one who receives. Do you see that it is not what
is first brought forth, but the approved, that is a first-fruits? And these
things have been said to us because of the flesh which he offered.
He brought up then the first-fruits of our nature to the Father; and the
Father so marveled at the gift, both because of the worth of the one offering
and because of the blamelessness of what was offered, as to receive it with
his own hands, and to set the gift near, and to say: Sit at my right hand. To
what nature did God say, Sit at my right hand? To that which heard, You are
earth, and to earth you shall go. For was it not enough to surpass the
heavens? was it not enough to stand with angels? was not this honor also
unspeakable? But it surpassed angels, passed archangels, surpassed the
Cherubim, went up higher than the Seraphim, ran past the principalities, did
not first stand until it took hold of the Master's throne. Do you not see this
interval of heaven toward the earth? or rather let us begin from below: do you
not see, from Hades toward the earth how great the interval is? from the earth
again toward heaven? from heaven again toward the heaven above? from that
toward the angels, toward the archangels, toward the powers above, toward the
royal throne itself? This whole interval and height he brought our nature up.
See where it lay below, and where it went up; it was not =50.447= possible to
go down lower than where man went down, nor to go up higher than where he
brought him up again. And Paul, making these things clear, said: He who
descended is himself also he who ascended. And where did he descend? Into the
lower parts of the earth, and he ascended above all the heavens. Learn who
went up, or what nature, or how it was disposed before this; for I gladly
dwell on the cheapness of our race, that I may learn with excess the honor
from the philanthropy of the Master. We were earth and ashes; but this is not
yet a charge; for it was the weakness of nature. We were disposed more
mindlessly than the irrational: For man was compared to the mindless cattle,
and was made like them. And this is, to become worse than the irrational, to
become like the irrational. For to be irrational by nature, and to remain in
irrationality, is of nature; but for those honored with reason to fall down to
that mindlessness is a charge of choice. So that when you hear that he was
made like the irrational, do not think that he wished to show men equal to the
irrational, but that wishing to declare them worse even than those, he said
this. For we became worse and more insensible than the irrational, not because
being men we fell down there, but because we also drove on to greater
ingratitude. And Isaiah, making this clear, said: The ox knew its owner, and
the ass the manger of its lord; but Israel did not know me. But let us not be
ashamed at the former things; For where sin abounded, grace superabounded.
You saw how we were less than the cattle; do you wish to see us also more
irrational than the birds? The turtledove and the swallow, sparrows of the
field knew the times of their coming in; but my people did not know my
judgments. Behold, we are more irrational than both asses and oxen, behold
also than the birds, the turtledove and the swallow. Do you wish to learn
another insensibility of ours? He makes us disciples of the ants; so we lost
the wits according to nature; For go, he says, to the


ant, and emulate its ways. We became disciples of ants, we who came to be
according to the image of God. But the one who made is not the cause, but we
who did not remain upon the image. And why do I speak of ants? we became more
insensible than stones. Do you wish me to bring the testimony of these also?
Hear, you ravines and the foundations of the earth, for there is a judgment of
the Lord toward his people. You judge toward men, and you call the foundations
of the earth? Yes, he says; for men are more insensible than the foundations
of the earth. What excess of wickedness then do you still seek, when we appear
more insensible than asses, and more irrational than oxen, and more ungrateful
than swallow and turtledove, and more unintelligent than ants, and more
insensible than stones, and equal to serpents? For their wrath, he says, is
according to the likeness of the serpent; the poison of asps is under their
lips. And what need to speak of the insensibility of the irrational, when we
even appear called children of the devil himself? For you, he says, are sons
of the devil. =50.448=
4. But nevertheless we the insensible and ungrateful, the mindless, those more
insensible than stones, those lower than all, the dishonored, the cheapest;
how shall I say? what shall I speak? how shall I bring forth this word? the
cheap nature, more unintelligent than all, has become higher than all today.
Today angels received what they long desired; today archangels saw what they
long longed for, our nature flashing from the royal throne, gleaming with
immortal glory and beauty. For this angels long desired, for this archangels
long longed. For even if the honor surpassed them, nevertheless they rejoiced
also at our goods; for even when we were punished, they grieved; for even if
the Cherubim guarded paradise, nevertheless they grieved. And just as a
servant taking a fellow-servant into custody, because of the command of the
master guards the fellow-servant, but grieves at what is happening because of
the sympathy toward the fellow-slave; so also the Cherubim received to guard
paradise, but nevertheless grieved at the guarding. And that you may learn
that it grieved, I make this clear to you from men. For when you see men
sympathizing with fellow-servants, no longer then doubt concerning the
Cherubim; for those powers are much more affectionate than men. What righteous
man then did not grieve, when men were justly punished, and after countless
sins? For this is the wondrous thing, that having learned their sins, and
having seen that they offended the Master, they grieved; Moses after the
idolatry; therefore he also said: If you forgive them the sin, forgive; but if
not, blot me also out of the book which you wrote. What is this? you see the
impiety, and you grieve for those being punished? For this reason, he says, I
grieve, because they both are punished and furnish the causes of just
punishment. And Ezekiel seeing the angel cutting down the people, cried out
greatly and wailed, and says: Alas, Lord, because you blot out the remnant of
Israel. And Jeremiah: Chasten us, Lord, he says, only in judgment, and not in
wrath, lest you make us few. Then Moses and Ezekiel and Jeremiah grieve, and
those powers suffered nothing at our evils? And how would this have reason?
That they make our things their own, learn how much joy they showed, when they
saw the Master reconciled to us. And if they did not grieve before this, they
would not even have been glad after these things. And that they rejoiced is
clear from what Christ says, That there will be joy in heaven and on earth
over one sinner turning. And if seeing one sinner turning angels rejoice,
seeing today through the first-fruits the whole nature brought into heaven,
how would they not have received the greatest pleasure? Hear then also from
another place the gladness of the peoples above at our reconciliation
=50.449=; for when our Lord was born according to the flesh, seeing that he
had now been reconciled to men; for he would not, if he had not been
reconciled, have come down so far; seeing this then, setting up a chorus on
the earth they cried out and said: Glory to God in the highest, and on earth
peace, among men


goodwill. And that you may learn that they glorify God for this reason,
because the earth received the goods, they also added the cause, On earth
peace, saying, among men goodwill, to those who had been made enemies, to the
ungrateful. You saw how they glorify God at others' goods, or rather at their
own; for they consider our goods to be their own; do you wish to learn that
even when they were about to see him going up, they rejoiced and leaped? Hear
Christ saying that they went up and came down continually. And this is of
those who desire to see the paradoxical spectacle. And whence is it clear that
they went up and came down? Hear him saying: From now you will see the heavens
opened, and the angels of God going up and coming down upon the Son of man;
for such is the custom of those who love; they do not even wait for the time,
but anticipate the appointed day with pleasure. For this reason they come
down, hastening to see that new and paradoxical spectacle, a man appearing in
heaven; for this reason angels everywhere, both when he was born, and when he
rose, and today when he went up. For behold two, he says, in shining clothing,
showing the pleasure through the form; and they said to the disciples: Men of
Galilee, why do you stand? this Jesus who was taken up from you into heaven,
will so come, in the manner you saw him going into heaven.
5. Here attend to me with exactness. Why then do they say these things? for
did the disciples not have eyes? for did they not see what was happening? did
the evangelist not say that while they were looking he was taken up? Why then
do the angels stand by teaching them that he went up into heaven? For these
two reasons, one, because they always grieved at the withdrawal of Christ;
that they grieved, hear what he says to them: None of you asks me, Where are
you going? but because I said these things, sorrow has filled your heart. For
if having friends and kinsmen we cannot bear being separated from them, how
the disciples seeing the Savior, the teacher, the guardian, the philanthropic,
the gentle, the good being separated from them, how would they not have
grieved? how would they not have been pained? For this reason the angel stood,
comforting the sorrow that comes from the going up through the return again.
For this, he says, Jesus who was taken up from you into heaven, will so come.
You grieved, he says, because he was taken up? But grieve no longer; for he
will come again. Lest they do what Elisha did seeing the teacher being taken
up, and tearing the little tunic; for he had no one standing by and saying
that Elijah will come again; lest then these do this =50.450=, for this reason
the angels stood by comforting the despondency. And this is one cause of the
presence of the angels; and a second not less than this, for which cause he
also added, Who was taken up. What then is this? He was being taken up into
heaven. And the interval was great, and the power of our sight was not enough
to see a body being taken up as far as the heavens; but just as a bird flying
into height, the more it goes up into height, so much the more it is hidden
from our sight; so also that body, the more it went up into height, so much
the more it was hidden, the weakness of the eyes not being enough to follow
the length of the interval. For this reason the angels stood by, teaching the
going up into heaven, lest they think that he went up as into heaven as
Elijah, but that he went up into heaven; for this reason he says, Who was
taken up from you into heaven. For he would not have added this simply. Elijah
then was taken up as into heaven, for he was a servant; but Jesus into heaven,
for he was Master; that one in a fiery chariot, this one in a cloud. For when
the servant had to be called, a chariot was sent; but when the Son, a royal
throne; and not simply a royal throne, but the paternal itself. For concerning
the Father Isaiah says, Behold the Lord


sits upon a light cloud. Since then the Father sits upon a cloud, for this
reason he also sent the cloud to the Son. And Elijah going up left a mantle
upon Elisha; but Jesus going up left gifts upon the disciples, not making one
prophet, but countless Elishas; or rather much greater and more splendid than
that one. Let us stand up then, beloved, and look toward that return. For Paul
also says, The Lord himself will descend from heaven with a command, with the
voice of an archangel; and we who are alive, who remain, will be caught up in
clouds to meet the Lord in the air, but not all. That we will not all be
caught up, but some will remain, and some will be caught up, hear what Christ
says: When two will be found grinding at the mill; one is taken, and one is
left; and two will be on one bed; one is taken, and one is left. What does
this riddle mean? what does this unspeakable mystery mean? Through the mill he
made clear to us all those living in poverty and hardship; and through the bed
and the rest he hinted at all those in wealth and honor. And wishing to show
that both from the poor they are saved and they perish, he said that of those
at the mill one is taken, and one is left; and of those on the bed one is
taken, and one is left; showing that the sinners are left here and remain for
the punishment; but the righteous are caught up into the clouds. For just as
when a king drives into a city, those in offices and authorities, and having
much boldness toward him, =50.451= going out before the city meet him, but the
condemned and those sentenced are kept inside awaiting the vote from the king;
so also when the Lord is present, those having boldness meet him in the middle
of the air, but the condemned and those conscious of many sins in themselves
wait here for the judge. Then we too will be caught up; I did not say we,
numbering myself into the vote of those caught up; I am not so insensible and
ungrateful as to be ignorant of my own sins. For if I did not fear to confound
the pleasure of the present feast, I would have wept bitterly remembering this
voice, since I also remembered my own sins. But since I do not wish to confuse
the gladness of the present feast, I will dissolve the word here, leaving you
the memory of that day in its prime, so that neither the rich man may rejoice
at wealth, nor the poor man count himself wretched at poverty; but each, from
what he is conscious of in himself, may do either this or that. For neither is
the rich man blessed, nor the poor man pitiable; but whoever is counted worthy
of that catching up in the clouds, blessed and thrice-blessed =50.452=, even
if he be poorest of all; just as he who has fallen away is pitiable and
thrice-wretched, even if he be most well-off of all. For this reason I say,
that we who are in sins may mourn ourselves; but those living in achievements,
let all take heart; or rather, let them not only take heart, but also make
themselves secure; let those not only mourn, but also change.
For it is possible also for the one in vice, having stood away from
wickedness, to return to virtue, and to be able to become equal to those who
from the beginning have lived well; which let us also be eager for; and those
conscious of virtue in themselves, let them remain in piety, always increasing
this good possession, and adding to the former boldness; but we who are
without boldness, and conscious of many sins in ourselves, let us change, so
that having also arrived at their boldness, we all in common and with one mind
with the due glory may receive the king of the angels, and enjoy that blessed
gladness in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory and the power,
together with the Father and the Holy Spirit, now and always, and unto the
ages of ages. Amen.
