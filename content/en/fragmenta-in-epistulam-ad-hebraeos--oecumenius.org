Or do not take "having become" of the flesh, lest you be thought to divide,
but of Christ, who is worshipped in one nature together with his flesh; for
once he has taken the economy in hand he speaks the lowly things without fear.

Hebr 1:5 Receive this too as the Father making the Son's generation according
to the flesh his own; for "I will be" is clearly said of what is according to
the flesh.

Hebr 2:9 One must know that the Nestorians stumble against the scripture and
read it thus: that apart from God he might taste death for everyone,
constructing this, that Christ had, they say, an indwelling of the God Word
and not a union, because when he was crucified the divinity was not with him;
for it has been said, they say, that apart from God he might taste death. But
see how an orthodox man answered: first, "by the grace of God" stands in the
text. Yet even if, as you would have it, he said "apart from God," it is to be
taken thus, that apart from God Christ died for all the others; for he died
not only for men but also for the powers above, that he might dissolve the
middle wall of partition and unite the things below with the things above.
Like this is what is said elsewhere: but when he says that all things have
been subjected, it is clear that this is except the one who subjected all
things to him.

Hebr 2:14 We who have been numbered among the children of God have shared in
blood and flesh, that is, we are in blood and in flesh and in corruptible and
463 earthly bodies. For this cause the only-begotten Word of God, being life
by nature, shared in the same things, and not in some other way but similarly
to us. How then does the devil hold power over death? Because he is ruler of
the sin from which death comes, and so he rules death; that is, the power of
death is sin. Having sin, then, and being the inventor of death, the devil
holds the power of death (for we said that sin is the power of death). Thus it
is: through his own death Christ abolished it, holding the devil as his
subject, which is the strength and power of death. For if this had not
overpowered man, death would not have entered the world.

Hebr 4:9-10 A sabbatism is a rest; for sabbath is interpreted rest. There
remains then a third rest, that of the kingdom of heaven. He called it
sabbatism from the archetypal rest of the sabbath, in which God rested from
his works.

Hebr 4:12 The cutting in two threatened in the Gospels the fathers have given
as the complete abandonment of the Holy Spirit. I think now this is said, that
it works a separation of the Holy Spirit and takes it away from the soul; once
that has happened, all the hard things will follow.

Hebr 4:15 Or "without sin": because, he says, he was not paying a penalty for
sins in suffering these things.

Hebr 5:1 But if the one taken from among men deals gently with those who are
ignorant and go astray, how much more will the one who besides being man is
also God deal gently and stretch out a hand to those who are ignorant and go
astray? But if that is not understood, "taken" will
more readily be received i.e. "set apart," being one of men and from them
taken as priest, he says.

464 Hebr 5:6 The word makes clear that even if Christ himself did not offer a
bloodless sacrifice (for he offered his own blood), yet the priests from him,
of whom God and high priest deigns to be, will offer a bloodless one; for this
is what "forever" makes clear. For he would not have said "forever" of the
sacrifice and offering made once by God, but looking to the ministers now,
through whom in the midst Christ ministers and is ministered, who also handed
over to them at the mystical supper the manner of such ministry.

Hebr 5:7 He did not for this reason call "days of flesh" the time when the
Lord was on earth in a perceptible way, as if he has now put off the flesh.
Far from it; for he has it, even if incorruptible. But "days of flesh" he
means the days of his fleshly life.

Hebr 5:9 See, here too is something befitting God.

Hebr 6:2 Rather these, thinking more in a Jewish way because there are various
and many baptisms in the law, wanted the baptism of the new as well, the one
that grants regeneration, to be undergone often, because they wished often to
be counted worthy of remission of sins.

Hebr 7:25 Or take "he intercedes" as in "we have him as advocate with the
Father." From the incarnation itself, he says, he appeals to the Father and
urges him to have mercy on us.

Hebr 8:10 That this is so is clear from here. Who in the old covenant easily
persuaded anyone to stand away from his own superstition? On the contrary,
Israel, being disobedient, was transferred from the knowledge of God into
error.

465 Hebr 9:1-4 The first as compared with the Holy of Holies, since it was not
first but middle; for first was where the bronze altar of the sacrifices and
whole burnt offerings was, and second this of which he speaks: in which were
the lampstand and the setting forth of the loaves on the table, third in which
were the golden censer and the ark of the covenant. I have noted three tents
in the Octateuch: one, of which he says "the worldly holy place," where the
bronze altar of the whole burnt offerings was, and it was permitted for all
the people to enter and perform the sacrifices; a second where the priests
were always permitted to perform the services, where the lampstand and the
table and the setting forth of the loaves were; a third where the censer and
the ark were, into which the high priest alone entered once a year.

Hebr 9:5-7 For above the ark there was as it were a square golden table, which
was called the mercy-seat, typing Christ who is called for us propitiation and
redemption. See the note in Exodus at the places concerning these things. In
Exodus at the places concerning these things he says that twice a day the high
priest entered the Holy of Holies to burn incense. For it is written thus: and
he shall burn incense upon it, that is upon the golden censer which was in the
Holy of Holies, Aaron incense of a fine composition early in the morning; when
he dresses the lamps he shall burn incense upon it, and when Aaron lights the
lamps toward evening he shall burn incense of perpetual offering. How then
does saint Paul say here: once a year the high priest alone? And we say that
with blood he entered once a year, as in that very place of Exodus he says,
but to burn incense twice a day. For here too he says: not without blood, that
is with blood, so that it is thus: once a year the high priest alone with
blood, not with incense.

466 Hebr 9:12 Or since he deigned to be head of humanity, the apostle says
that what was accomplished for us has been accomplished for him.

Hebr 9:27 Or that, since he was also man together with being God, he too, he
says, endured what is common to men; for just as men die once, so also Christ.

Hebr 9:28 Or "to bear the sins of many" he says of the cross, that he might
also quench them, giving the penalty for them. Now the Father, having made him
sin, sent him; for Christ was exceedingly a sinner, as having taken up and
made his own the sins of the whole world. But having given through the cross
the penalty that belongs to sinners, indeed the one owed, he will thereafter
come with the Father's glory, no longer as a sinner, no longer numbered with
the lawless. For that Christ is a sinner, hear: him who knew no sin, he says,
he made sin, as it were sin itself.

Hebr 10:1 You can also understand it thus: it has been said by Gregory among
the saints in the Apologetic and in the To those in public office that the
divine mysteries now are antitypes of greater mysteries. And again in the On
the Resurrection: "Let us partake," he says, "of the pascha, now typically,
but later more perfectly; for the legal pascha, I dare even to say, was a type
of a type." These things having been said thus. In these the apostle knows a
shadow and an image and coming good things, which he also calls "things," that
is, true; for as compared with shadow and image the "things" make clear what
is true (for there is also truth). Shadow then he knows the things in the law,
image our things as Christians, and coming good things and true things those
in the age to come. For as much as an image differs from truth, so much a
shadow from an image; for the image, even if it does not have the truth
itself, is yet a clear imitation of the 467 truth, preserving also form
through the colors and proportion of members and the color of the subject; but
the shadow is a faint appearance of the image, showing none of these things
which the image shows. And I think Gregory among the saints was led from these
apostolic sayings to say what is set forth. And similar things to Gregory many
other fathers have also said.

Hebr 10:4 For not only, he says, were the sacrifices offered for the sins that
came after, but rather for all, as not having been remitted by the sacrifices
already made.

Hebr 10:21 House of God he calls the faithful, according to "I will dwell in
them and walk among them."

Hebr 10:26 Note that he did not say "having sinned," but used the imperfect,
wishing to show remaining in the sin until the end; for from this it is
especially clear that he does not take away repentance.

Hebr 11:4 Or through the faith. And he speaks by the report, by the glory, by
the memory.

Hebr 11:27 Or that he did not fear lest the wrath become so great as to search
him out in flight; whence he fled not far but from the neighbors. And this was
of faith.

Hebr 11:32 The time, he says, suitable to the epistle, and as it were the due
measure.

Hebr 12:2 Or take "instead of the joy set before him" thus, as Gregory also
says: though it was possible for him, he says, to remain in his own glory 468
and divinity, he not only emptied himself as far as the form of a slave, but
also endured a cross, despising shame. He is sufficient then, he says, also to
recompense you for the afflictions undergone for him; for he was not only
crucified, but also sits at the right hand of God, the right hand and the
sitting showing equal honor.

Hebr 12:9 For they are not able to discipline us throughout, so as to make us
perfect, but God, always disciplining, makes us perfect; for either the
father's death or the child's growth stops the discipline.

Hebr 12:17 Some interpret thus: although he sought the blessing with tears,
that is, he did not find a place of repentance in between.

Hebr 12:23 And of firstborn he means the saints, those counted worthy of the
exceptional adoption compared with the rest of the faithful, from the
firstborn among us who from the birthright have some precedence.

Hebr 12:27 "Yet once" makes clear the adding of another "once" after the
second. For first the world was shaken at the legislation at Sinai; for the
earth was shaken, David says. Again at the sojourn in flesh the world was
shaken; for all Jerusalem was shaken, and the handmade things of Egypt will be
shaken. And earthquake he means the wide hearing of the
preaching, through which men were shaken from their old standing in error and
were transferred. Thus Cyril. And "yet once," he says, at his second coming
with glory, when he remakes and changes the creation; for then especially will
the 469 creation be shaken with the true earthquake and crash, or the transfer
from evil into good of the men then arriving. Attend also to the note of the
blessed Cyril on Haggai.

Hebr 12:29 Or he wants them to be frightened not to be ungrateful nor to
murmur at the afflictions, lest, he says, you meet with God angry and
punishing; or to console, as if he said: we will give thanks in the
afflictions, for we have a master able to consume the adversaries.
