To the imprisoned bishops, presbyters and deacons in prison for piety.
=52.541=
Blessed are you of the prison, and of the chain, and of the occasion of
the bonds; blessed and thrice-blessed, and this many times over, you who
have drawn the whole inhabited world by the love that is about you, and
have made even those far off lovers of you. Everywhere on land and sea
your achievements are sung, the courage, the unturned mind, the
unenslaved spirit. Nothing of the things that seem to be terrible struck
you with fear, not a court, not an executioner, not snowstorms of
tortures, not threats full of ten thousand deaths, not a judge sending
forth fire from his mouth, not enemies sharpening their teeth and
setting in motion ten thousand forms of plot, not so many slanders, not
shameless accusations, not death appearing before the eyes each day; but
the occasion of the things that were happening was sufficient for you as
consolation of the things that were happening.
On account of these things all crown you and proclaim you openly, not
friends only, but even the enemies themselves, even while they are
constructing these things; even if not openly, if someone should unfold
the conscience of any of them, he will find even among those much wonder
at you. For such is virtue, that it is admired even among those who war
against it; such is vice, that it is condemned even among those who
pursue it. And the things here are of this sort; but the things in the
heavens, what word will be able to set them forth? Your names have been
written in the book of life; you have been numbered with the holy
martyrs. I know these things clearly, not having gone up into heaven,
but having learned them from divine oracles. For if he who stood by a
marriage being violated, and was not able to set the lawlessness right,
but only to convict it, the fruit of the barren woman, the citizen of
the desert, since for this he dwelt in the prison and was beheaded, is
both a martyr and the first of martyrs; you who have stood by the laws
and ordinances of the fathers being transgressed, and by the priesthood
being insulted and violated, and have suffered so many things for the
truth, and for the dissolving of shameless slanders thus, consider how
great a recompense you will receive.
It is not lawful for you to have the wife of Philip your brother, that
noble and lofty man said this, and it was sufficient for him toward
unspeakable boldness of speech. You also say, Our bodies lie ready for
punishments, penalties, tortures; consume, spend these with whatever
penalties you wish; we do not choose to slander, but to die ten thousand
times. But you were not beheaded, but far more grievous things =52.542=
you have suffered. For it is not equal to lay down a head in a brief
turn of time, and to wrestle for so long a time with so many pains,
fears, threats, prisons, leadings away, courts, hands of executioners,
shameless tongues of slanderers, reproaches, revilings, jeers. For this
too is a greatest kind of contest, and the blessed Paul sets it in the
rank of great contests, saying thus: Remember the former days, in which,
having been enlightened, you endured a great contest of sufferings.
Then, speaking the contest, he has set this, saying thus: Partly being
made a spectacle with reproaches and afflictions, and partly having
become partners of those so suffering. And if those who shared have
contended, much more those who endured.
For you have endured not one, and two, and three, but many deaths in
your mind, even if not in experience. Rejoice therefore and leap; for
this the Master of the heavens commanded, not only not to grieve, nor to
fall down, but even to leap, and to be glad, whenever they cast out your
name as evil. And if one must rejoice at slander, whenever so many
things are added besides, slanders, blows, tortures, sharpened swords,
prisons, chains, leadings away, leadings about, so many companies of
enemies, consider how great the reward, where the things of the
recompense are crowned. Rejoice therefore, leap, play the man, be
strong, consider how many you have anointed through the things you
suffered, how many minds you have raised up, how many being shaken you
have established, not those present, but also those absent, not through
the sight of the things you have endured, but even through hearing alone
having benefited even those far off the greatest things.
Carry about continually that apostolic word: That the sufferings of the
present time are not worthy toward the glory that is going to be
revealed unto us. And expect also the swiftest loosing of the trials,
and a complete deliverance, and pray continually for us. For even if we
have been far removed from you by the length of the road, and have been
separated from you for a long time, yet as being present and being with
you, thus we embrace you, thus we kiss the dear head of each of you,
receiving the crowned ones with open hands, and expecting from this also
that there will be for us a greatest gain from the much love concerning
us. For if those who love you must await a great reward, consider how
great the things of the requital are for you who have shone forth in so
many contests.
