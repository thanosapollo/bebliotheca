Discourse on the text, "God saw everything that he had made; and behold, it
was very good;" and that the divine words are more precious than all sweet
things.
αʹ. Fair are the meadows of piety, not flourishing with colors of a season,
but teeming with heavenly flowers. And the flowers of piety are the fruits of
a godly way of life. For the virtues lead us up to that wonderful and original
beauty which has come into being according to the image of God and after his
likeness. This likeness according to virtue, then, does not allow a horse to
be called in place of a man. When therefore we pursue this, we are no longer
named wolf and dragon and serpent and scorpion, being carried down toward the
outrage of the irrational creatures; but we both are, and are called, and are
believed to be, a wonderful and precious image of God, preserving the original
beauty of the stamp. You became master of the irrational creatures; do not
imitate the manners of the household slaves; not because the irrational
creatures are worthless; for nothing evil was created by the Good. Let no one
suppose that the irrational creatures are ranked in the region of vice,
because they have been brought forward as an image of vice for those who do
not live according to virtue. For if this were so, how did the Scripture say,
And God saw everything that he had made, and behold it was good, and not
simply good, but also very good? When then the things that have come into
being were brought forward as an image of vice (for the lion is an image of
the tyrant, and the serpent of the one crooked and most wicked in manner, and
the scorpion of the striker, and the wolf of the rapacious), where, he says,
would the things that have come into being be good, being so plainly taken as
an image of vice? But those who love learning must know that these things
neither are, nor are called, evil in their own nature, but a condition fitting
to the irrational; yet when it comes into the rational nature, it outrages the
appearance and blunts the rational freedom. You received, O man, the dignity
of a master; do not pursue the dishonorable and slavish rank of the irrational
creatures; for you outrage yourself when you imitate the savagery of the
irrational, not as though the irrational were evil or so called, but as though
the dishonorable and slavish image were not fitting for the free. For just as
a man of note and of good repute, if he puts on or wraps himself in a slave's
garment, is outraged and put to shame, not as though the clothing were
worthless and filthy, but as though it were fitting for a slave and not
matching a free man; so if you take on the manner of a scorpion and a wolf, it
is not the nature of the irrational that is slandered, but your own condition
is outraged, having obtained mastery and pursuing the things of a slave. These
things have been brought in by me of necessity, so that no one may take what
has been said as an outrage against the creation, as though God had made
something worthless; for all things are truly good, and very good, if they
keep their own rank and pursue their own law. And that you may learn, beloved,
that nothing worthless has come into being from God according to nature, God
taught us to take an image even of these irrational creatures. For he proposed
that we imitate certain advantages of the irrational, so that we may not
slander the things that have come into being as vice, not the nature as a
whole, but such things as fit a rational condition. Or did you not hear today
the writer of Proverbs saying, Go to the ant, you sluggard, and emulate his
ways? Did you not understand how, having proposed the ant's love of labor, he
trained us toward unsluggish eagerness? Did we not teach you expressly the
unsluggishness from the ant, and the love of craft from the bee? For there is,
there is, as I said before, a culling of certain advantages even of the
irrational that fit a rational condition; yet these things are said toward our
shame and reproach. For since you did not imitate God, imitate, he says, the
ant. Have you never seen a true father rebuking his children, and after much
training leading them to imitation of household slaves, and saying these
things plainly: Imitate at least the household slave, inferior in dignity,
but excelling in skill? Hence the =56.520= God who loves mankind, as a
demonstration of our ingratitude, brings forward the ant convicting our
sluggishness, and the bee convicting our negligence. And it is clear from
these things that nothing evil came into being from God, but he gave you
perception and a discerning mind, to flee the manners of the irrational and to
seize their advantages. For even the lion can be an image of virtue, when you
take the image not toward tyranny but toward courage; for tyranny is one
thing, and courage another. For this reason also the Scripture, announcing the
Savior who was about to arise from Judah, says: Judah is a lion's whelp; from
a shoot, my son, you have gone up; having lain down you slept as a lion, and
as a whelp. See how great a likeness the divine word constructs from the
advantages that belong to the irrational. It is possible, then, to imitate a
lion with understanding; it is possible also to imitate a serpent, not in
injecting venom, but in taking on understanding; as also the Savior says to
the apostles: Be wise as the serpent. Hence, if the creature were worthless,
he would not have called his own image to imitation of the worthless, saying,
Be wise as a serpent; imitate of the serpent not the venom, not the wrath, but
the understanding. For if you imitate the venom, David cries out against you,
accusing and saying: Their wrath is according to the likeness of the serpent;
but if you imitate its understanding, you become a disciple of Christ who
said: Be wise as the serpent. For since the serpent is wiser than all the
beasts, as was just now read, he set wisdom against wisdom, not that you may
learn to work deceit, but that you may learn to turn aside from deceit.
βʹ. For the God who loves mankind wishes us not to be evildoers, but to be
shrewd; and shrewdness is one thing, and evildoing another. Shrewd, then, is
the one who is so not toward practicing vice, but toward fleeing vice; and he
uses shrewdness not toward plotting against others, but toward escaping the
things woven by others; but an evildoer is the murderer, the deceitful, the
one who crafts evils for others. Such a one is Jacob, prudent as the serpent,
not venomous as the serpent. For when he was about to meet his brother Esau,
full of vice and of bloodshed, he was calling upon God, saying, Lord, deliver
me from my brother Esau, for I fear him greatly; and he was beseeching God, as
setting the prayer against a wicked man. But when he met his brother, he tamed
the wrath with courtesy and flattery, and quenched the wickedness. For what
does he say to Esau? I have seen your face as if one should see the face of
God. And if Jacob was saying these things in order to conquer his brother
through deceit, he was speaking badly; but if he said it in order to escape
the fear, he used shrewdness well. Become then wise as the serpent: not
wrathful, not venomous, but wise, and simple as the doves. And the dove can be
taken both toward vice and toward virtue; and how, hear: If you are
unreasonable and without discernment, and experienced in vice only, the
Scripture slanders you as without understanding. For Ephraim, it says, is as a
dove without mind, having no heart. If then, fleeing the mindlessness, you
accomplish the simplicity, you have the understanding perfect, not mixing with
it l. it with vice; and you hear from the Lord, Be wise as the serpent, and
simple as the doves. Do not then, brother, simply read the Scriptures, and
take the images from it toward vices, dividing l. of it dividing this to this
one, and another to that one. Do you wish then to learn? Attend carefully to
the things =56.521= being said: The Scripture calls a lion; For a lion's
whelp, it says, is Judah; from a shoot, my son, you have gone up; having lain
down you slept as a lion. Here the phrase, As a lion, signifies the
courageous; for it also sets the same image concerning the righteous; For the
righteous, it says, is confident as a lion. Courageous then is the righteous,
not in doing evil, but in spitting out the things brought upon him by the
wicked. It also calls the Savior a lion, because he is from a royal tribe. For
just as the lion is king of the four-footed, so also of the Jews there reigned
the tribe of Judah, from which the Savior arose. And do not suppose, beloved,
that it is unworthy of Christ to be likened to the image of a lion; for the
Scripture shapes this image even of the Godhead itself. Hear then what the
prophet says: A lion shall roar, and who will not fear? The Lord God has
spoken, and who will not prophesy? According to the powerful, then, and the
fearful, and the royal, Christ is likened to a lion. And the devil also is
called a lion, not as powerful, nor as fearful, but as a striker, as a tyrant,
as one who makes away with. And concerning this the blessed Peter the apostle
says: Behold your enemy the devil goes about, as a lion, seeking to swallow a
soul. Divide then the contemplations of the readings, lest things that seem
contrary in the wording confuse your mind. The Scripture said a lion and a
lion; but the one according to the royal and the powerful, the other according
to the destructive and the poisonous. So also the Scripture shapes the
righteous and the ungodly through one image: The righteous, it says, shall
flourish as a palm tree, he shall be multiplied as the cedar in Lebanon. It
first made the image of the righteous, and then likewise of the ungodly. For I
saw, it says, the ungodly being exalted and lifted up, as the cedars of
Lebanon. See then how it brought forward the same image, but divided the
thoughts by the words. For of the ungodly it says: I saw the ungodly being
exalted and lifted up, as the cedars; but of the righteous it did not say, He
shall blossom as a cedar in Lebanon, but, He shall be multiplied. So then
imitate also the bee, O man, which the Scripture too teaches to imitate toward
love of labor. Go to the bee, and learn that she is a worker. Do not learn
that she has a sting, but that she works honey. For if you imitate the sting
of the bee, David accuses you, saying: All the nations surrounded me, as bees
a honeycomb, and in the name of the Lord I warded them off. Do you see how he
likens those who strike to a striking bee, and the righteous to the one that
drips honey? For the lips, it says, of righteous men drip wisdom; and, Fair
words are a honeycomb of honey; and their sweetness is healing of soul. This
sweetness of souls, then, beloved, I say, let us long for with all our soul,
and let us work the intelligible honeycomb in the hidden workshop of the soul,
and let us take the divine words above honey and honeycomb. For honey eaten in
much quantity often turns the sweetness into bitterness, and distorts the
appetite of the stomach, and overthrows the whole condition of the man; but
the word of God taken in much quantity does not trouble the soul, but heals;
For its sweetness is healing of soul; Therefore that wise man, wishing to set
in contrast the difference of honey and of word, says: To eat much honey is
not good, but one must honor glorious words. Let us have then, beloved, the
manifold honeycomb, the divine and sacred words, continually; and as that
honeycomb is completed from many and various shapes, yet has one beauty; so
also let the divine Scripture be a honeycomb for us, composed of many and
various prophets, yet dripping one honey of teaching. Such also is the
honeycomb of the Fathers for us, fitted together from various ages, yet
dripping one grace and one spiritual teaching. But let these things be enough.
And may it come to pass that we, by the prayers and intercessions of our
common Father, increasing the word by love of labor, work the divine law
always in the mind, and do that which was said by David, to have the law of
God always in the midst of our belly, that is, the divine thoughts, so as to
think we recognize the bounds of nature, and to honor the =56.522= truth, to
despise the world, and to send up glory to God, now and always, and unto the
ages of ages, Amen.
