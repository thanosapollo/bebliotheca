Of our father among the saints Athanasius, archbishop of Alexandria
Teachings to Antiochus the ruler.
1.1 A duke named Antiochus, coming to the most holy bishop Athanasius, being
held in a terrible sickness, was entreating him to pray for him to the Lord,
repenting of his sins. And when the bishop had called upon the lover of men,
God, he became healthy. And indeed he comes bearing with him his possessions
and sets them beside the feet of the bishop, entreating him that they be given
to the poor, and that he himself, having received the holy habit from a holy
man, might live the virtuous and solitary life; which the bishop did by the
grace of God. Then duke Antiochus says to the bishop, "I ought from when I was
baptized to have kept the faith and not to have defiled the baptism; but since
folly has worked great lawless deeds in me, I entreat your holiness to teach
me and to catechize from what things and works and thoughts I shall from now
on keep myself." Then the bishop, having made a prayer and sat down, was
teaching him in the Holy Spirit to fulfill each one of the commandments of the
teachings of Christ. He begins then to say to him, "It is necessary that the
one coming upon the unstained virtue and living a solemn life and repenting of
the former sins believe before all things in one God Father almighty, maker of
heaven and earth and of all things visible and invisible, and in one Lord
Jesus Christ the Son of God the only-begotten, through whom all things came to
be, and the rest. One God alone containing all things, but himself being
uncontained, and who can do all things, and nothing is impossible with him. If
then you believe from your whole heart and from your whole soul and, having
feared him, keep yourself from the things I say to you, your petition also
will be heard, and having believed you will receive from him the help and
victory of salvation unto the ages."
1.2 And having stood up they prayed, and after the prayer he says to him,
"Have simplicity and become harmless, and you will be as the infants not
knowing wickedness that destroys the race of men. And first speak evil of no
one, nor listen gladly to those speaking evil; but if not, you too listening
will be liable of the sin of the one speaking evil. For evil-speaking is a
wicked spirit and an unstable demon never making peace, but always dwelling in
divisions. Keep away then from it, and you will always have peace with all,
and put on simplicity and solemnity, in which there is no wicked stumbling,
but all things smooth and cheerful, and you will live unto the ages."
1.3 And standing up he prayed, and after the prayer he says to him, "Hear also
this third commandment: love truth and let all truth go out from your mouth,
in order that the Spirit of God may dwell in you. For those who lie set aside
the Lord. For they received a spirit of truth and became strangers to the true
Spirit. And flee an oath justly unjustly, and the truth will lead you into the
life eternal."
1.4 And having stood up they prayed, and when they had prayed the bishop
sitting says to him, "Hear also the fourth commandment: keep chastity and let
there not go up into your heart concerning another man's woman or concerning
fornication. And if anyone works this wicked work, he works death for himself.
See then, keep away from this thought. For where solemnity and chastity dwell,
there


lawlessness ought not to go up; for this is accomplished through labor of
prayer and humiliation. And doing this you will live unto the ages."
1.5 And having stood up they prayed, and the bishop sitting says to him, "Hear
also the fifth commandment: become longsuffering and understanding, and you
will lord it over all wicked works. For if you also will be longsuffering, the
all-holy Spirit will dwell in you. But in quick temper, the devil. Woe to that
man in whom both dwell. And hear also the working of quick temper, how wicked
it is and leads astray the servants of God. But it does not lead astray those
who are full in faith, nor does it work in them, because the power of God is
with them, but it leads astray the double-minded. When it sees such ones
standing firm, it throws itself into the heart of the man, and thus the man is
judged on account of the things of this life. For all these things are foolish
and empty. But longsuffering is acceptable and strong. Rejoicing and being
without care it glorifies the Lord at every time. The delicate spirit, not
having a custom of dwelling with a wicked spirit and hardness, withdraws from
such a one and seeks to dwell with meekness and quiet. And when it has
departed from the man in whom it dwells, that man becomes empty of the Holy
Spirit and is wholly blinded from the good mind. Thus then it happens to all
the quick-tempered. Keep away then from it, the most wicked demon, and put on
longsuffering and endurance, and you will live unto the ages."
1.6 And having stood up they prayed, and the bishop sitting says to him, "Hear
also the sixth commandment: I commanded you in the first commandment that you
keep the faith and the fear and the self-control." Antiochus answered, "Yes,
lord, so it is." The bishop answered, "But now I wish to make clear to you
their powers, in order that you may understand the power which they have and
the working. For they are double. They lie then upon just and unjust. You then
believe the just, and believe nothing to the unjust. There are two angels with
the man, the one of wickedness and the one of righteousness. The angel of
righteousness is delicate and shamefast and meek and strong. When this one
goes up upon your heart, immediately he speaks with you concerning
righteousness, concerning chastity, concerning solemnity and concerning every
good work. When all these things go up into your heart, know that the angel of
righteousness is with you. See then also of the angel of wickedness his wicked
works. Before all things he is quick-tempered and bitter and foolish, and his
works are wicked, overturning the servants of God. When this one goes up upon
your heart, know that they are from the wicked one and the hater of the good."
Antiochus says to him, "How I may understand him I do not know, unless I am
taught according to reason." The bishop says to him, "Hear in detail and
become understanding and longsuffering. Then moreover desire of women and of
many revels and desire of deeds of greed and of pride and the things like
these. When these go up upon your heart, know that the angel of wickedness is
with you. You have the workings of both. Understand them, and believe the
angel of righteousness, but stand away from the angel of wickedness, and
working the good you will be blessed unto the ages."
1.7 And when they had prayed the bishop says to him, "Remember that into the
first commandment I commanded you concerning faith and prayer and fear and
self-control." Antiochus answered him, "Yes, lord." And the bishop says to
him, "Hear also the seventh commandment: fear God and keep his commandments,
and you will be powerful in every deed; for fearing the Lord you will work all
good things; do not fear the devil, because there is no power in him, but
rather fear his wicked works, in order that you may not be lorded over by
them, because they are wicked. If then


you do not wish to work the wicked thing, fear the Lord always, and do not
forget his fear; for he who has the fear of God in his heart never thinks a
wicked thing in his mind, and working the good he has life in himself unto the
ages."
1.8 And when they had prayed the bishop says to him, "Hear also the eighth
commandment. Concerning self-control understand that it is double. Upon which
things it is necessary to keep oneself and not to do them. Keep yourself from
all wickedness, working the good. Hear in detail. Keep yourself from
drunkenness, lawlessness, from wicked luxury, from many foods and extravagance
of wealth and remembering of wrongs and blasphemy. These works are wicked for
the life of men. From these the servant of God must keep himself. For he who
does not keep himself from these cannot live. Hear also the names of these,
that is, the things that follow." Antiochus says, "For which, lord, are wicked
works?" The bishop says to him, "And there are many wicked things, from which
the servant of God must keep himself, falsehood, deprivation, false witness,
greed, boastfulness, and as many things as are like these. Or do these not
seem to you to be wicked?" And he answered, "Yes, servant of God." The bishop
says to him, "From all these the servant of God must keep himself, in order
that he may live to God and be written with those who keep themselves. Before
all things faith, fear of the Lord, concord, love, words of righteousness,
truth, endurance. Nothing is better than these in the life of men. If anyone
keeps these, he is blessed in his life. Then hear the things that follow
these: to serve widows and orphans, those of the servants of God who are in
want. To be a lover of strangers, quiet, to provide for one more in need, to
reverence every man, to bear insult, to be longsuffering, not remembering
wrongs, to admonish those who sin, not to press debtors, not to grieve those
in need, and as many things as are like these. Do these not seem to you to be
good?" Antiochus answered, "For what can be better than these?" The bishop
says to him, "Exercise yourself then in these and do not stand away from them,
and you will live unto the ages, and all who keep his commandments will live
unto the ages."
1.9 And when they had prayed the bishop says to him, "Hear also the teaching
of the ninth commandment; for it is very profitable: take away from yourself
double-mindedness and do not be double-minded at all to ask of God, saying in
yourself that how shall I be able to ask to receive from the Lord, having
sinned so many things against him? Do not reason anything such, but from your
whole heart turn to the Lord and ask without doubting, and you will know his
much compassion, that he will not abandon you, but will quickly fulfill the
request of your soul. For God is not as men remembering wrongs, but he himself
does not remember wrongs and has compassion upon his creature. You then
cleanse your reasoning and your soul from every filth of the enemy and ask
from the Lord, and you will receive whatever you ask, because he does not
abandon those who hope in him. For those who doubt toward God, these are
double-minded, and they receive nothing at all of their requests; but those
who are complete in faith receive all things which they ask with whole soul.
For every double-minded man, unless he cleanse the mind of his soul, is saved
with difficulty. And put on the faith, because it is strong, and believe God
that you will receive all your requests which you ask. If having asked some
time a request from the Lord you also receive it more slowly, do not be
double-minded, because you did not receive more quickly the request of your
heart, that is, of your soul. For altogether through some trial or a fall,
which you do not know, you did not receive the request of your soul. You then
do not cease praying until you receive it. But if you grow weary and are
double-minded praying and asking, blame yourself and not the one who gives to
you. See


then that double-mindedness is from the devil. You then serve from your soul,
and you will live to God, and all who think these things will live unto the
ages."
=1.10= And when they had prayed the bishop says, "Hear also the tenth
commandment: take away from yourself every wicked desire and put on the good
and solemn. But from the wicked desire, and especially if the servant of God
falls into grief and is not understanding, he is spent by it terribly. And
especially those not having a garment of good desire, but mixed up with this
age, these then it hands over to death." And Antiochus says, "What are the
things that hand men over to death? And make them known to me, in order that I
may flee from them." The bishop says to him, "Hear in what works the wicked
desire puts the servants of God to death. Before all things desire of a woman,
and extravagance of wealth, which is love of money, and of many and vain
foods, and of much drunkenness, of varied taste, and of vain human glory, or
rather every luxury and pleasure of this life is vain. These wickednesses then
and the desires put the servants of God to death. Keep away then from them,
and you will live to God. If you serve God in righteousness, you can lord it
over the wicked desire." Antiochus says, "I too wished to know in what ways I
must serve the good desire." The bishop says, "Work righteousness and virtue
of truth, fear of the Lord and faith and love and humility and endurance, and
the things like these, and be glued to the good works. And working these you
will be well-pleasing and a servant of God unto the ages." Again the bishop
says to him, "When the devil sees that through his own reasoning he cannot
harm you, then he brings toward you weaker monks, and through them he gives
you some pretext through words, or through works of rest, or a fleshly zeal,
in order to scandalize you and to throw you down. But you, understanding that
these are constructions of the enemy, do not begin to say into your mind. I am
from such a great family, and this one is a private man and cheap and weak,
and I have to fall at his feet, but rather for this fall before him,
remembering the commandment of our savior Jesus Christ, which exhorts you, 'if
anyone slaps you on the right cheek, turn to him also the other,' and do not
stand away from his feet before you receive forgiveness with prayer from him,
in order that the crown of humility may be rendered to you perfect unto the
ages. Amen."
=1.11= And when they had prayed the bishop says to him, "Hear also the
eleventh commandment: take away from yourself grief. For it also is sister of
double-mindedness and of quick temper." Antiochus says, "How, lord, is it
sister of these? For quick temper seems to me one thing and grief another."
The bishop says to him, "Why do you not understand the things said to you? For
those do not understand who throw themselves into the worldly and fleshly
things. Do you not understand that it is most wicked and most terrible to the
servants of God. But those who have the fear of God accomplish a work of
righteousness and of truth, and having the heart and the mind toward the Lord
they attain and understand, because they are clean and without care from every
thought of life and worldly worry and they have nothing except only the fear
of God in themselves. For where the Lord dwells, there is much understanding.
Be glued then to the Lord, and you will understand all things. Hear how grief
wears out the spirit and again saves. When the double-minded sets himself to
do something and fails through his double-mindedness, grief goes into the man
and grieves the Holy Spirit and constrains it. Then again quick temper, when
it is glued to the man concerning some matter and he is embittered exceedingly
and does some evil, again grief goes into the heart of the man, and he is
grieved at his deed which he did, and he regrets that


he worked a wicked thing. This grief then seems to have salvation, because
having done a wicked thing he repented. Both deeds then grieve the man; the
double-mindedness because he did not attain his deed, and the quick temper
because he did the wicked thing. Both then are grievous to the Holy Spirit.
Take away the grief from yourself and do not afflict the Holy Spirit that
dwells in you, lest it petition against you to God and depart from you. For
the Spirit of God given into this flesh does not bear fleshly grief nor
constraint. For grief upon the former sins alone has benefit, if you do not
sin again. For every cheerful man works good things and thinks good things and
despises the vain grief; but the grieving man always does wickedness. First he
does wickedness because he grieves the Holy Spirit given to the man cheerful;
and second, grieving the Spirit, he works lawlessness, not petitioning nor
confessing God. For always the petition of a grieving man has no power to go
up upon the altar of God, because the grief sits in his heart. The worldly
grief then mixed with the petition does not let the petition go up clean
toward the altar of God. For just as vinegar mixed with wine does not have the
same pleasure, so also grief mixed with the spirit does not have the same
petition. Cleanse yourself then from this wicked grief, and you will live to
God, and all who throw the grief away from themselves and put on love and
cheerfulness will live unto the ages. Amen. 2.t Of our father among the saints
Athanasius, patriarch of Alexandria, Teaching to Antiochus the duke.
2.1 A duke named Antiochus, coming to the most holy bishop Athanasius, being
held in a terrible sickness, was entreating him to pray for him in order that,
if he become healthy, he may turn thereafter to the Lord, repenting of his
sins. And when the bishop had called upon the lover of men, God, he became
healthy on the next day. And indeed he was coming bearing with him his
possessions and sets them beside the feet of the bishop, entreating that they
be given to the poor and to those who have need, and that he himself, having
received the solemn habit from the holy man, might live the virtuous and
solitary life; which the bishop did by the grace of God. Then duke Antiochus
says to the bishop, "I ought from when I was baptized to have kept the faith
and not to have defiled the baptism still; but since my folly has worked a
great negligence in me, I entreat your holiness to teach me and to catechize
and to admonish from what things and works and thoughts I shall from now on
keep myself, and of what other virtues I shall take care." Then the bishop,
having made a prayer and sat down, was teaching him in the Holy Spirit to
fulfill each one of the commandments of the teachings of Christ. He begins
then to say to him, "It is necessary that the one coming upon the unstained
virtue and living a solemn life and repenting of the former sins believe
before all things in one God almighty, maker of heaven and earth and of all
things visible and invisible, and in one Lord Jesus Christ the Son of God the
only-begotten, through whom all things came to be that are in heaven and on
the earth, being God our savior consubstantial with the Father, and in the
Holy Spirit the Lord and life-giver, who proceeds from the Father, who with
Father and Son is co-worshipped and co-glorified. One God alone who contains
all things, but himself being uncontained, and that he can do all things, and
nothing is impossible to him. If you believe thus from your whole heart and
from whole soul and from your whole strength and fear him and keep yourself
from the things I say to you, your petition will be heard and having believed
you will receive from him the help and victory and salvation unto the ages.
Amen."


2.2 And standing up he prayed, and after the prayer he says to him, "Have
simplicity and become harmless, and be as the infants that do not know
wickedness that destroys the salvation of men. First speak evil of no one nor
listen gladly to the one speaking evil; but if not, you too listening will be
liable of the sin of the one speaking evil, if you believe the evil-speaking
which you hear; for you believed and you yourself also will have it against
your brother. Thus then you will be liable of the one speaking evil. For
evil-speaking is a wicked spirit and an unstable demon, never making peace,
but always dwelling in divisions. Keep away then from it, and you will have
peace with all, and put on simplicity and solemnity, in which there is no
wicked stumbling, but all things smooth and cheerful, and you will live unto
the ages. Amen."
2.3 And standing up he prayed, and after the prayer sitting he says to him,
"Hear also this commandment: love truth and let all truth go out from your
mouth, in order that the Spirit of God may dwell in you. For those who lie set
aside God. For they received a spirit of truth and became dwellings of the
true Spirit. If then you keep the truth, you will be able to procure life for
yourself. And flee an oath justly or unjustly, and the truth will lead you
into the immortal life unto the ages. Amen."
2.4 And when they had prayed the bishop sitting says to him, "Hear also the
fourth commandment: keep chastity and let there not go up upon your heart
concerning another man's woman or concerning some fornication, or concerning
such names. For doing this you work a great sin, not quickly standing your
mind away from such a shameful appearance. For this thought is a great sin to
a servant of God. And if anyone works this wicked work, he works death for
himself. See then, keep away from this thought. For where solemnity and
chastity dwell, there lawlessness ought not to go up. And this is accomplished
through more frequent and laborious prayer. Do this then, and you will live
unto the ages. Amen."
2.5 And having stood up they prayed, and the bishop sitting says to him, "Hear
also the fifth commandment: keep chastity and longsuffering and become
understanding, and you will lord it over all the wicked works and you will
work all righteousness. For if you will be longsuffering, the Holy Spirit
dwelling in you will be clean, not being darkened by a wicked spirit, nor
being defiled by quick temper. For in longsuffering the Lord dwells, but in
quick temper the devil. Both spirits then it is unprofitable to dwell in the
same. For if taking a little wormwood you pour it into a jar of honey, is not
the whole honey made to vanish, and so much honey is destroyed by the least
wormwood. But if the wormwood is not thrown into the honey, the honey is found
sweet and becomes altogether useful to the master. See then that longsuffering
is sweetest above the honey and is useful to the Lord and he dwells in it, but
quick temper defiles the longsuffering. And hear also the working of quick
temper how wicked it is and leads astray the servants of God and overturns by
its own working. But it does not lead astray those who are full in faith, nor
can it work in them, because the power of the Lord is with them, but it leads
astray the double-minded. For when it sees such men standing firm, it inserts
itself into the heart of the man, and thus a man is embittered on account of
the things of this life, or of some friend, or concerning giving and
receiving, or concerning such defiled things. All these things are foolish and
empty and unprofitable to the servants of God, but longsuffering is great and
fortified, having the power strong and sitting in a great spaciousness.
Cheerful, rejoicing and being without care it glorifies the Lord at every
time, having nothing bitter in itself, remaining through all


in meekness and quiet. This longsuffering then dwells complete with those who
have faith, but quick temper first is folly and lightness of wits; then from
the folly comes bitterness, and from the bitterness wrath, and from the wrath
anger, and from the anger rage. Then the rage, being composed from so many
evils, becomes a great and incurable sin. For when all these human things
dwell in one vessel, where also the Holy Spirit is, that vessel does not
contain it, but the delicate spirit overflows, not having a custom of dwelling
with a wicked spirit and hardness. It withdraws then from this man and seeks
to dwell with meekness and quiet. Then when it has departed from the man in
whom it dwells, that man becomes empty of the Holy Spirit and thereafter
filled with the wicked spirits is unstable in every deed of his, being pulled
here and there by the wicked men, and is wholly blinded from the good mind.
Thus then it happens to all the quick-tempered. Keep away then from quick
temper, the wicked demon, and put on longsuffering and stand against the quick
temper, and you will be praised with the solemnity loved by the Lord. See then
that you do not reason away this commandment. For if you keep this, you will
be able to keep the rest also. Be strong then in them and be empowered, and
you will conquer and you will live unto the ages. Amen."
2.6 And having stood up they prayed, and the bishop sitting says to him, "Hear
also the sixth commandment: I commanded you in the first commandment that you
keep the faith and the fear and keep yourself." Antiochus answered, "Yes,
lord, so it is." The bishop says to him, "But now I wish to make clear to you
also their powers, in order that you may understand the power which they have
and working. For they are double. For they lie upon just and unjust. You then
believe the just, and believe nothing to the unjust. For the just has a
straight road, but the unjust a crooked. There are two angels with the man,
one of righteousness and one of wickedness. And the angel of righteousness is
delicate and shamefast and meek and strong. When then this one goes up upon
your heart, immediately he speaks with you concerning righteousness,
concerning chastity, concerning solemnity and concerning sufficiency and
concerning every just work and concerning every glorious virtue. When all
these things go up into your heart, know that the angel of righteousness is
with you. Believe this one then and his works and become holding to him. See
then also the works of the angel of wickedness. First of all he is
quick-tempered and bitter and foolish, and his works wicked, overturning the
servants of God. When this one goes up upon your heart, know him upon his
works." Duke Antiochus says to him, "How, lord, I may understand him I do not
know, unless I am taught according to reason." The bishop says to him, "Hear
in detail and ... ... become. When some quick temper or bitterness comes to
you, know; that he himself is in you. Then moreover desires of many deeds and
extravagance of foods and of many revels and of varied and not due
nourishments, and desires of women and greeds, pride also and boastfulness and
as many things as are near to these. When then these go up upon your heart,
know that the angel of wickedness is in you. You then, having recognized his
works, stand away from him and believe nothing to him. You have then the
workings of both angels. Understand them. Concerning [the works] of the faith
this is the commandment, that you believe the works of the angel of
righteousness and having worked them you may live to God, because to him is
the glory unto the ages. Amen."
2.7 And when they had prayed the bishop says to him 2.7 "Remember that into
the first commandment I commanded you concerning faith and fear and
self-control." He answered


"Yes, lord." He says to him, "Now hear the seventh commandment concerning the
fear: fear the Lord and keep his commandments, and you will be powerful in
deed, and your deed will be incomparable. Fearing the Lord you will work all
good things; this is the fear which you must fear. Do not fear the devil,
because there is no power in him, nor fear. But in whom the power is glorious,
there is also fear in him. For everyone who has power also has fear. But he
who does not have power is despised by all. The servant of the Lord then is
strong and ........... fear then the Lord, and you will live to him keeping
his commandments unto the ages."
2.8 And when they had prayed the bishop says to him, "Hear also the eighth
commandment concerning self-control: understand that the self-control is
double. For upon some things you must keep yourself, and upon some you must
not. To keep yourself from the wicked thing and not to do it, but do not keep
yourself from the good, but do it. And hear in detail." Antiochus says to him,
"Yes, I ask you, make all things clear to me in detail, because I have never
heard such a word of salvation, arming the man against every device of the
devil. For this reason I also made my notary write all this glorious and
virtuous teaching." The bishop says, "Keep yourself from all wickedness, from
drunkenness, lawlessness, from wicked luxury, from many and costly foods,
wealth, boasting, high-mindedness and pride and falsehood and evil-speaking
and hypocrisy and remembering of wrongs and all blasphemy. These works are
wicked for the life of men. From these works then the servant of God must keep
himself. For he who does not keep himself from these cannot live to God. And
hear also the things that follow these." Duke Antiochus says, "Are there
still, lord, wicked works?" The bishop says to him, "And there are many from
which the servant of God must keep himself, theft, falsehood, deprivation,
false witness, greed, boastfulness, and as many things as are like these. Or
do these not seem to you to be wicked?" He answered, "And very wicked to the
servants of God." The bishop says to him, "Keep yourself then from all these,
in order that you may live to God and be written with those who keep
themselves. The things then from which one must keep oneself are these; and
the things from which you must not keep yourself, but do, hear. First of all
faith, fear of the Lord, concord, love, words of righteousness, truth,
endurance. Nothing is better than these in the life of men. If anyone keeps
these and does not keep himself from them, he is blessed in his life. Then
hear the things that follow these: to serve widows and to visit orphans who
are in want, from necessity to ransom the servants of God, to be a lover of
strangers; for in hospitality doing of good is found; to be quiet, to become
more in need, to reverence every elderly man, to practice righteousness, to
keep brotherhood, to bear insult, to be longsuffering, not remembering wrongs,
to comfort those weary in soul, not to throw away those scandalized from the
faith, but to turn them and make them of good cheer, to admonish those who
sin, not to press debtors, not to grieve those in need, and as many things as
are like these. Do these seem to you to be good?" Antiochus answered, "For
what, lord, can be better than these?" The bishop says to him, "Exercise
yourself in these and do not keep yourself from all, and you will live to God
unto the ages. Amen."
2.9 And when they had prayed the bishop says to him, "Hear also the teaching
of the ninth commandment; for it is very profitable: take away from yourself
double-mindedness and do not be double-minded at all to ask from God, saying
in yourself that how shall I be able to ask to receive from the Lord, having
sinned so many things against him? Do not reason these things, but from your
whole heart turn to the Lord and ask from him without doubting, and you will
know his much compassion, that he will not


abandon you, but will fulfill the request of your soul. For God is not as men
remembering wrongs, but he himself does not remember wrongs and has compassion
upon his making. You then cleanse your heart from all these vain things and
