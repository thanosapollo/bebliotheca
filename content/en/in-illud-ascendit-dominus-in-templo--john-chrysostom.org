On the saying, When the Lord went up into the temple, in the midst of the
feast, and on Melchizedek. For Mid-Pentecost.
=61.739=
Pomegranate and apple and palm and the remaining fruits of the orchard sweeten
the throat of the body; but divine words of holy teachers mixed with love,
being sent on through the hearing of teaching, sweeten the larynx of the soul.
And just as he who has gathered the fruits of the trees, in the partaking of
the fruits, renders praises to the trees; so also he who has gathered the
divine sayings of the teachers in the basket of hearing, then sent them on to
the mind, and having tasted the sweetness of the word, sending forth praises
to the teacher from his lips, says: How sweet to my throat are your oracles,
above honey and honeycomb to my mouth! And the most wise Solomon, having
likened good words to honeycombs of honey, says: Honeycombs of honey are good
words. For just as the throat, tasting foods, knows how to distinguish the
good from the worthless; so also the mind of the soul knows how to distinguish
the useful words from the worthless, as it says in Job: For the throat tastes
meats, but the mind distinguishes words.
And let no one suppose that the master of the house has various trees in his
own garden, I mean palms and apples and olives, but that the Lord in his own
garden of the Church does not have various stems of the fathers, each
according to his own goodness bringing forth from the depth of the heart the
fruits of words for the enjoyment of those who enter. For one of them is like
a flourishing olive, thick with the leaves of faith, and fruitful with the
works of piety; another is like a palm, crowned with the palm-branches of
victory over the devil, and offering the sweetest fruit of well-doing to the
poor; of whom Scripture says, The righteous shall flourish as a palm tree. And
another is like an apple, furnishing two delights to men, I mean both the
fragrance and the sweetness to the throat; and praising such a one in the Song
of Songs, the bride Church, what does she say? As an apple among the trees of
the wood, so is my beloved among the sons of Israel. And she says, Under his
shadow I desired and sat, and his fruit was sweet in my throat. But what shall
I do, beloved, I who possess a very small leafage of oracles and fruits, being
able neither to cool those who are burned, nor to feed those who hunger with
the fruits of words? For it was good for me to learn with you the teachings of
the holy fathers. But since the fear of God and your longing stir us to speak,
taking two or three words of the sweetest Gospel as if they were palms, and
having tasted them, let us stir you up; for thus you will eagerly, boiling in
spirit, hasten toward the fair-fruited trees of the holy fathers.
Just now we heard the evangelist saying, In the midst of the feast Jesus went
up into the temple, and taught. The word of the Gospel has not passed by,
since the season of the feast is present; fitting to those present is the
reading of the Gospel, suited to the day is the narrative; for sister to the
present feast is the power of the word. In the midst of the feast Jesus went
up into the temple; but when the feast was finished, he no longer goes up into
the temple, but runs up into heaven. And in the midst of the feast Jesus went
up into the temple. And he says feast, not that which takes place among Greeks
and Jews, where there are drunkenness and revels and songs of harlotry, and
charms of fine singing, and dances that turn many ways, but a holy feast
befitting Christians, where there is not drunkenness and revels, but prayers
and hymnody and supplications; where there are not songs of harlotry that
dissolve bodies, but divine songs that strengthen souls; where there are not
dances that turn many ways, but the leapings of holy souls in gladness. But if
you wish to see what sort they are who among us sing and dance and leap, see
Isaiah singing and saying, I will sing now to the beloved a song of my
beloved, to my vineyard. And see also a choir of holy women, Miriam the sister
of Moses, with other women of Israel, singing victory hymns to God and saying:
Let us sing to the Lord; for he has been gloriously glorified. Whom David also
remembers in hymns saying, In the midst of young women =61.740= playing the
tambourine, in the churches bless God. And the same blessed David is not
ashamed to dance before the God-bearing ark, as one may hear him saying: And I
will play, and I will laugh, and I will dance before the Lord. For the infant
of Elizabeth, John, having seen the God-bearing cluster still hidden in the
unwedded womb, dancing in the linen of the belly leaped. And in the midst of
the feast Jesus went up into the temple. And a temple is a dwelling-place for
God alone. For the Greeks also say, we have a temple, not knowing that that is
not a temple, but a defiled thing. For where there is pouring out of blood and
of drink-offerings, and idolatrous steam of fat, and rites of demons, that
would not be a temple, but a defiled haunt of the demons. And in the midst of
the feast Jesus went up into the temple. And a temple is a dwelling-place
dedicated to God alone, where love and peace, where faith and chastity make
their abode. For this reason also the priest of God who performs the sacred
service to God must be peaceful and calm, not thieving, not deceitful, not a
lover of money, so that first God may set things right in his own soul, and
then so reshape the people toward his own image. The purified soul of each
faithful man can also be called a temple; for I will dwell in you, and walk
among you; but a temple truly holy, and a sacrificial victim, and a priest, is
Christ himself, in whom, according to the divine Apostle, All the fullness of
the Godhead dwells bodily; to whom the Father has said, You are a priest
forever according to the order of Melchizedek.
Now, having fallen unexpectedly into a very great question, I am at a loss
where to incline the eye of my thought; where I shall transfer the rudder of
teaching I do not know. For if the apostle, having come to the chapter
concerning Melchizedek, shrank from narrating it in detail, having said,
Concerning whom we have much to say, and hard to interpret, what would I say,
who fall as far short of the apostle as a man's discernment? And just as a
traveler, so long as he treads some level and open road, gladly completes the
journey; but when he comes to some place most rough or steep and hard to pass,
where there is a mountain reaching to heaven, standing still and calculating
the toil of the ascent, he begins to walk gently, lest he fail too quickly; so
also we, so long as we had the level and open ground of the Gospel, gladly
went forward with the step of the word; but when we have come to a thought
reaching to heaven, we begin gently to set foot on the heavenly ladder of
interpretation. What then is the question investigated among all Christians?
For what reason, he says, did the Apostle, having mentioned Melchizedek, call
him without father and without genealogy, having neither beginning of days nor
end of life? This one would not be a man, but God. And many, not having
understood the things said concerning him, have said that he is greater than
Christ, and have established a heresy for themselves, and those men are called
Melchizedekians; and contending with us and wishing to show that Melchizedek
is greater than Christ, they bring forward as a witness the Scripture that
says: You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek. How
then, he says, would Melchizedek not be greater, according to whose image and
order Christ serves as priest?
And others, having gone astray, say that he is the Holy Spirit. But we say
that he is a man of like passions with us, and that he is not greater than
Christ; why do I say than Christ? nor even than John the Baptist. For true is
the word of the Lord, Amen I say to you, there has not arisen among those born
of women one greater than John the Baptist. Nor indeed is he the Holy Spirit.
And that Melchizedek is not greater than Christ, let those who say this tell
us of what precinct, or of what region, they say he is. Is it heavenly? is it
earthly? is it under the earth? If then they say that Melchizedek is of the
heavenly things or of some other region, let them hear that his knee bends to
Christ; for the Apostle says that To him every knee shall bow, and every
tongue shall confess, of things in heaven, on earth, and under the earth.
If then Melchizedek bends the knee to Christ, Melchizedek who worships would
be less than Christ who is worshipped by him =61.741=. And why do the wretched
not hear the next verse spoken by the Apostle, that they may learn that he is
less than Christ? For he adds saying, Made like to the Son of God, just as we
too have been made according to the image and likeness of Christ. But perhaps
one of the hearers asks and says: What then is it that Melchizedek is called
without father and without mother and without genealogy? To whom we shall say,
that the Jews have said he was born of fornication, and for this reason is not
given a genealogy by Scripture. To whom we shall say, You have spoken badly;
for we find also others born of fornication and given genealogies. Straightway
Solomon, born of the wife of Uriah, was given a genealogy, and Abimelech son
of Gideon, who being born of fornication was given a genealogy. For what
reason then is Melchizedek not given a genealogy? But because Melchizedek was
a type of Christ, and bore the image of Christ. For this reason Scripture
omitted the father and the mother, and related him as without genealogy, so
that in him, as in an image, we might behold as in a mirror Christ who is
truly without father and without mother and without genealogy. For Christ is
without father on earth according to the flesh, without mother in the heavens
according to the Spirit, according to the Godhead. Such also is he who has
Neither beginning of days nor end of life. For Scripture did not give
Melchizedek a genealogy, but omitted both his birth and his end, so that, as
we said before, in that one we might behold this true Melchizedek. For
Melchizedek is interpreted king of righteousness, who is Christ, =61.742=
having neither beginning of days nor end of life. For God the Word neither had
a beginning of days, nor ever ceased or died; for Christ remains forever. For
the Melchizedekians contradict us saying, What then is it that the Father says
to him, You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek?
To whom we say, that this Melchizedek became a righteous man, and truly
bearing in himself the image of God. Moved then by a prophetic Spirit, having
now understood the offering that was going to be brought to God by Christ, he
honored God with bread and wine, imitating the Christ who was to come. Since
therefore in the synagogue of the Jews the order of Aaron offered sacrifice,
not bread and wine, but calves and lambs, and glorified God with bloody
sacrifices, God cries out to Jesus who was going to be born of the virgin Mary
and says: You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek, not
according to the order of Aaron, who served with calves and lambs and bloody
sacrifices, but, You are a priest forever according to the order of
Melchizedek, bringing the offering of the nations in bread and wine unto
perpetuity. But lest we seem, beloved, to chatter on by stretching out the
word, having set the word here, we will send you on to the fountain of the
fathers' teaching, who both will still the thirst of your soul, and will
gladden your hearts, filling them with the Holy Spirit, in Christ Jesus our
Lord, to whom be the glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
