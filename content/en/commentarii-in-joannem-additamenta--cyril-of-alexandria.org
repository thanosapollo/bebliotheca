1 Jo 10, 17 Since as God, knowing the thoughts of the Jews, he saw them
mocking at the things he said about dying for the sheep, he shows them from
what is said to be so impious as even to mock those things which God the
Father greatly loves, since he also thus saw remaining unclouded in his
incarnate Son, and shining forth, the image of his own nature, because of the
unchangeableness. Thus far the sixth book of Saint Cyril.
2 Jo 10, 20-21 Others said: These words are not those of one possessed by a
demon, and what follows. From the quality of the words they give a right
verdict; for it was madness to say of this one who says that the good shepherd
will die without hesitation and will readily run the risk for his own sheep,
while the hireling, who is not a shepherd, will flee, even growing numb at the
mere sight of the wolf, that he has a demon and is mad. Why do you listen to
him?
3 Jo 10, 26 The beginning of obedience is kinship; for faith makes us kin to
God, and the kinship makes us wise; but these are not wise, not receiving the
lessons that profit toward salvation. And by calling them strangers he
signifies that his sheep were also those from the nations who were about to
believe, and he threatened them with the migration which is unto the nations,
so that even through the threat he might frighten them into thinking what they
ought. 4 Jo 10, 29 Giving what he had as God by nature, receiving as man, and
bringing it back into his own glory, himself by himself, and going toward
this.
5 Jo 10, 31 3 For if the works did not bear witness to my words, in which I
said, calling myself equal to the Father, you would not have been about to be
indignant at my having said I am God by nature, unless all my works showed me
to be God by nature; for if I blasphemed, he says, by saying I am God, it is
likely for you to consider that through the things I have done I appear God
even more, and for this reason you seek to kill me.
6 Jo 10, 36 The Only-begotten is also the sanctifier of all the others, as
many as by participation in being sanctified ascend to sanctification. And
something is said in the divine Scripture to be sanctified in many ways. He
was sanctified, then, that is, he was appointed for a mission in the world in
order to save those in it; or you will understand being sanctified as being
set apart for sacrifice; for one died for all by the good pleasure of God the
Father. Or it is in order to make his own temple holy; for the flesh was not
holy according to its own nature, but, having received sanctification from the
Word united to it, it was sanctified; and according to this, as I think, he
says this.
7 Jo 10, 37-38 And he speaks also against the Arians, and that this is not
like what was said to the disciples; for the Lord, having said to them, If you
keep my commandments, you will remain in my love, just as I have kept the
Father's commandments and remain in his love, interpreting that he remains in
the love of Christ who does his commandments, which does not raise the
disciples up to consubstantiality with the Son, since they can also fall away
through no longer wishing to do his commandments, even if the Son unchangeably
does what seems good to the Father as God by nature, even if as man he says
these things for our benefit, that we may imitate him as far as possible.
Therefore we understand willing and not willing not in essences and in the
account of nature, but in choice, which is the case with the disciples, but
not with the Son; for from the natural goods that belong to the Son we
understand that he is consubstantial with the Father, and not from those
things which come to be and pass away in us.
8 Jo 10, 39 The evangelist also explains the hardness of the Jews, who were
not softened by so many good words, but sought to seize him and kill him, and
the power of the Savior. Being God by nature he escaped from those who were
raging, being in the midst of their hands; for the evangelist said this as in
wonder, through which he signifies that this one, easily fleeing unharmed from
the midst of those who would murder, would never have been caught, unless he
were willing.
9 Jo 11, 1 Therefore because of the love of the women, their brother also was
worthy to be loved by Christ, seeing that he loved him exceedingly as well.
10 Jo 11, 11-12 How is it not unreasonable for them to believe that he comes
there in order to wake him? Therefore they also say: If he has fallen asleep,
he will be saved. They do not think he is speaking about sleep, nor about
death, but they think he is saying some riddle, since somehow, with him
traveling fifteen stades, he would not have arrived in time to wake him.
11 Jo 11, 25 Since therefore Christ saw that she did not understand clearly
the things from himself, making it plain he says: I am the life, i.e. you need
not ask me from another, nor hope that your brother's resurrection will be
after long times; for I am present, the one who calls to this. And the Son is
life by nature, who calls all things into being and raises all by his own
power, raising them not simply, but unto life; for this reason he also yoked
life to the name of resurrection. And it is to be noted that the Lord calls
himself life, not a living creature, as some have impiously dared to say this
of him; for there is another difference between a living creature and life:
the living creature is receptive of life, but life is productive of living,
just as wisdom and the wise man. The one receives, the other gives.
12 Jo 11, 25 3 For just as the saints of old did not yet arrive at the
resurrection and the honors, but waited for us, so also the faithful must wait
for the common resurrection, so that all may rise even <with> the saints of
old. As those waited on our account, not receiving the honors owed them, so
that all might enjoy them in common, so we too do not enjoy the resurrection
alone, so that rising with the saints of old we may obtain the good together.
13 Jo 11, 33 For he seems to have had in mind what is said in the Psalms:
Command, O God, your power; strengthen, O God, this which you have wrought in
us, understanding "command" in a God-befitting way, even if it is spoken in
human voices, leading us from the things according to us into a thought that
is above us.
14 Jo 11, 33 The evangelist called the bitter threat against death an
indignation.
15 Jo 11, 38 Just as, then, when Christ goes forward to the tomb of Lazarus,
it is written that Jesus, being indignant in himself, comes to the tomb, so
then we understand in his case the indignation as the will according to
authority, as it were with movement.
16 Jo 11, 43-44 The command is God-befitting and kingly. And for this reason
the Savior cries out, contrary to his custom, so that through the type in
Lazarus he may prefigure the general resurrection, in what manner it is going
to take place, the trumpet sounding something great and the dead being raised
by the power of God. And he does not pray here, nor does he say, Thus says the
Lord, lest he be thought of in the rank of a servant, but with authority, as
God, he calls the dead from the tomb. And he came out immediately, since
nothing resists God when he commands, in order to show that in a moment and in
the twinkling of an eye the resurrection of all is also going to take place,
just as it happened in the case of Lazarus. And he comes out bound, for the
full assurance of those who see, preserving the whole form of the dead, lest
some should say that Lazarus had not died at all.
17 Jo 12, 31 Having become incorruptible, men thus, being sinless, seek glory
for God. How must one understand what has been said, and they have thus come
to be in mind, and alien to passions, attending only to the good? But since
the whole world was captured and slipped under the yoke of slavery,
worshipping the creation, and was mastered, tyrannized by Satan through the
excesses of the passions, God introduced the law through holy men, shining
upon us a little of the light of the knowledge of God. But since the many,
being hard of hearing, were not persuaded because of the error that held them,
we needed not only the counsel from the saints, but also the goodwill and
grace from above, which easily transfers us to the better. The saints pleaded
for the nations and idolaters, those who had been wronged by the devil and led
astray from the straight path as if by force. For he was then powerful and
hard to overcome for those who resisted, to be set free when God judges; for
as just he was able to have mercy even on these as weak and
crushed by the unbearable greed, and to punish the one who had come cruelly
upon the weaker. Therefore Christ, appearing as the righteousness of God the
Father, led us into the light of the knowledge of God, justifying us through
faith. Therefore the time of Christ's presence already showed the judgment and
justice upon the nations as present; for they were about to be delivered from
the devil's greed, Christ the just judge justly having mercy on them. And this
is what the saying signifies; for the world is not now condemned, when the
time for being justified was present; for understanding it thus we also do not
fight against that saying which says: For God did not send the Son into the
world to judge the world, but that the world might be saved through him; nor,
as some say, does he here call world the Jews who crucified the Lord, those
who were required to pay the penalty for their daring deeds against Christ;
for the death of Christ justifies all humanity. Therefore it is more
consistent to understand it thus. Accordingly, then, with this understanding,
the judgment will be against the one who has wronged the world, not against
the one who has suffered the injustice of the world. And the one who came to
save us more fittingly accomplished this, punishing the one who did the wrong,
and having mercy on the one who was wronged; the definition of this present
judgment is that the ruler of this world is cast out. And he calls him ruler
not as being this in the proper sense; for God alone is king of all, but
because through lack of understanding men enrolled this one as their ruler. He
therefore received the title from what happened; for thus also sin is said to
have reigned over the ungodly, although it is something without substance,
from the things which sinners do they seem to subject themselves to it.
Either, then, he is cast out of the rule, his strength having been shattered,
or "out" signifies the punishment and the going into Hades; for there the
devil was justly cast out, since he warred not only against men, but also
against Jesus, the God of all. Therefore also, being of old without experience
of bonds and punishment, he was now required to pay the penalties of his
daring deeds, as having come to the extreme of all impiety.
18 Jo 12, 37-38 He speaks many charges against the Jews; for many signs did
not put them to shame, but they were in a way unchangeable in hardness of
heart, nothing having been lacking of the things toward correction. And even
if the Jews, having been put to shame, had believed, still it was impossible
for the Holy Spirit to have lied, who spoke through the prophet Isaiah
concerning their unbelief; for they neither believed the prophets, nor did
Christ become known to the Jews, the power and the arm of the Father. And he
said "no one" hyperbolically, because few out of many were saved. And wishing
to convict their measureless perversity, the evangelist added also "before
them," showing that they did not even believe the things they saw.
19 Jo 12, 38 The Son is the arm of God the Father. Jo 12, 41 From the things
he here makes clear, that the Son has so great a glory, which Isaiah saw, it
is evident that there is no difference of Father and Son; for there is nothing
more that a man can behold, nor does Scripture speak of anything else greater
than this; for the Seraphim, which Isaiah saw beneath the throne of God, are
above and more honorable than all the angels. And that of the throne signifies
the nature of the Master, or sitting in the kingly rank, with those of
creation standing by in the form of servitude.
21 Jo 12, 44 What he says is of this sort. He who is able to recognize who it
is that sojourns through the flesh, and who lays hold of the intelligible and
better vision, this one truly finds me. And he finds, in the faith toward me,
that toward the Father. It is similar, then, as if he were saying: he who
believes in me does not believe in a man, but in the power of God, in which
<the> Father is known and believed.
