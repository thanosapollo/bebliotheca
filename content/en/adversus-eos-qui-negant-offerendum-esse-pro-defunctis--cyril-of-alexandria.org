* Against those who deny that offering is to be made for the dead
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

And Cyril the most wise, bishop of the city of the Alexandrians, also teaches
things similar to these in his discourse entitled Against those who dare to
say that one must not offer for those who have fallen asleep in faith, whose
beginning is 541 Those who direct the eye of their own mind; for he says thus:
We say then to them with forbearance, Be minded concerning the Lord; cease
opposing to the dogmas of the truth a tongue so 542 bold and false-speaking;
cease unadvisedly rebuking the customs of the Church. For they say that we
must not, going to the tombs themselves, make the prayers on behalf of those
who have fallen asleep, and also offer, even great things, wisely, as if to
Christ present, through the holy and mystical sacrifice. And a little later.
What then is the absurdity, even that Christ present bodily should receive the
prayers on behalf of those who have fallen asleep in faith? For if it is
unprofitable and truly vain, or otherwise even unseemly, that they should be
deemed worthy of an offering from us, let this not even take place in simple
prayers; then indeed then let someone, if he should wish, purge away the
superfluity of the sacrifice performed for them; but if that which [sets]
their words through nothing, and allowing the very making [of it] to be
altogether choked, insolently overbears the law of love, they show themselves
without contradiction hard and unpitying stones; what is there that prevents
the sacrifice from being brought up together with the prayers on their behalf,
and those who have chosen to do this from hastening to benefit those souls by
the aids that are offered; And again: When therefore we offer to God the
sacrifices and prayers on behalf of those who have departed, let no one find
fault; we are eager to do for them what is most excellent in the manner of a
prayer, through which 543 the Father would become more ready to have mercy,
and death itself is also abolished.
And again: For even if the souls of the dead have come to be outside the
bodies from earth, yet they are still reckoned among the living with God; who
is the one who refuses to say. And a little later. One would then see those
who wish to deprive those who have fallen asleep both of the mystical
sacrifice and of the gentleness from above as having sinned not moderately
against thoughts both wise and good. For they live, as I said, at least in the
image of God, they who are about to live in no time at all, and they are also
together with Christ himself. And a little later, having set forth the
sacrifice of Job on behalf of his children, for he says that "Job sent and
purified them, rising up in the morning and offering sacrifices for them
according to their number," and he adds saying: Therefore to sacrifice on
behalf of others is not rejected before God, nor does he permit [one] to do
[it] unblamed; he praises it then, as not being unprofitable. Then again,
having expounded the history and wonder-working in the Gospel according to
Luke concerning the paralytic and those who let him down through the 544
tiles, he adds and says: Christ did not leave unadmired the faith of those who
brought him. And he was enriched with the release of his toils, and the
genuine [faith] of another toward love of God made the Christ who sets free
from offenses and infirmities together well-disposed and a friend to him.
And again, having mentioned the centurion in the Gospel according to Matthew,
who says, "Lord, my servant is laid in the house a paralytic, grievously
tormented; and he says to him, I will come and heal him, and the centurion
answered and said, Lord, I am not sufficient that you should enter under my
roof; but speak by a word, and my servant will be healed. And Jesus having
heard, he says, wondered and said to those who followed, Amen I say to you, I
have found so great faith with no one in Israel." Then to him, "Go, and as you
have believed let it be to you. And the servant was healed in that hour." And
he adds and says: See then for me even in this (σιξ) the one not present being
saved through the faith of another. Why then do those who know nothing of the
[good things] impiously ascribe love of gain to us, if the gentleness from
above for those who have fallen asleep in faith [ut invitemus we are eager],
performing the holy and mystical sacrifice upon them [through which all of us]
have been shown mercy, and the very power of death has fallen, and the hope of
unending life has shone forth in Christ Jesus our Lord?
