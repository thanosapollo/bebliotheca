But Cerinthus also, who through revelations written as if by a great apostle,

came to us, says Eusebius book vi. Hist. cap. 20, also a dialogue of Caius, a
most learned man, held at Rome under Zephyrinus against Proclus, who was
championing the Phrygian heresy; in which, checking the rashness and daring of
those on the opposite side in composing new scriptures, he mentions only
thirteen epistles of the holy apostle, not numbering that to the Hebrews with
the rest.
129 THE THINGS WHICH FOLLOW ARE TAKEN FROM THE LITTLE LABYRINTH, WHICH IS
EITHER OF CAIUS, OR OF AN ANONYMOUS WRITER OF THE SAME AGE. For they say, the
heretics, that all the former ones and the apostles themselves both received and
taught these things which these men now say; and that the truth of the preaching
was preserved until the times of Victor, who was the thirteenth bishop in Rome
from Peter; but from his successor Zephyrinus the truth was counterfeited. And
what is said would perhaps be plausible, if first the divine Scriptures did not
oppose them; and there are also writings of certain brothers older than the
times of Victor, which those men wrote to the nations on behalf of the truth,
and against the heresies of that time; I mean of Justin and Miltiades and Tatian
and Clement and many others, in all of which Christ is spoken of as God. For who
does not know the books of Irenaeus and of Melito and of the rest, proclaiming
Christ God and man? And how many psalms and odes of brothers from the beginning
130 written by the faithful hymn the Word of God, Christ, speaking of him as
God. How then, when the ecclesiastical mind has been proclaimed from so many
years, is it possible that those until Victor preached as these men say? And how
are they not ashamed to lie against Victor in these things, knowing accurately
that Victor proclaimed Theodotus the cobbler, the leader and father of this
God-denying apostasy, out of communion, who first said that Christ was a mere
man. For if Victor according to them thought as their blasphemy teaches, how
would he have cast out Theodotus, the inventor of this heresy?

Eusebius brought these in book v. Hist. cap. 28. From the same work. I will
remind many of the brothers, then, of a matter that happened in our time; which
I think that if it had happened in Sodom, it would perhaps have admonished even
those. There was a certain Natalius, a confessor 131 not long ago, but in our
own times. This man was once deceived by Asclepiodotus. Asclepiades. see the
note and another Theodotus, a banker; these too were both disciples of Theodotus
the cobbler, the first who for this thinking, or rather folly, was cut off from
communion by Victor as I said, who was then bishop. And Natalius was persuaded
by them to be appointed bishop of this heresy on a salary, so as to receive from
them one hundred and fifty denarii a month. Being with them then, he was often
admonished by the Lord through visions. For our compassionate God and Lord Jesus
Christ did not wish a martyr of his own sufferings to perish, having gone
outside the church. But since he gave more careless heed to the visions, being
baited by the first seat among them, and by the shameful gain that destroys
most, at last he was scourged by holy angels, being not a little tormented
through the whole night; so that at dawn he rose, and putting on sackcloth, and
sprinkling himself with ashes 132, with much haste and tears he fell before
Zephyrinus the bishop, rolling under the feet not only of those in the clergy,
but also of the laity; and he flooded with tears the compassionate church of the
merciful Christ; and using much entreaty, and showing the weals of the blows he
had received, he was barely admitted to communion.
Eusebius brought these ibid. From the same work. 1 They have fearlessly
tampered with the divine Scriptures; they have set aside the canon of the
ancient faith; they have not known Christ; not seeking what the divine
Scriptures say, but laboriously practicing what form of syllogism may be found
for the establishment of their godlessness; and if someone puts forward a saying
of divine Scripture to them, they examine whether it can make a conjunctive or
disjunctive form of syllogism. And leaving the holy Scriptures of God, they
pursue geometry; as being from the earth and speaking from the earth 133, and
not knowing him who comes from above. Euclid, at any rate, is laboriously
studied in geometry among some of them; and Aristotle and Theophrastus are
admired; for Galen perhaps is even worshipped by some. And those who use the
arts of the unbelievers for the opinion of their heresy, and adulterate the
simple faith of the divine Scriptures with the cunning of the godless: that they
are not even near faith, what need is there even to say? For this reason they
have fearlessly laid hands on the divine Scriptures, saying they have corrected
them. And that I do not say this lying against them, whoever wishes can learn.
For if someone, collecting the copies of each of them, wishes to examine them
against one another, he would find them differing greatly. The things of
Asclepiades, at any rate, will be out of accord with those of Theodotus. And it
is possible to come by many, because their disciples have written with ambition
the things corrected, as they themselves call them, by each of them, that is,
obliterated. And again the things of Hermophilus do not agree with these; 134
for the things of Apollonides are not even consistent with themselves. For it is
possible to compare the things formerly prepared by them with those later
distorted again, and to find them greatly at variance. And of how great daring
this sin is, it is likely they themselves are not ignorant. For either they do
not believe that the divine Scriptures have been spoken by the Holy Spirit, and
they are unbelievers; or they think themselves wiser than the Holy Spirit; and
what else is it but that they are possessed? For they cannot even deny that the
daring is their own, when it has been written even by their own hand; and they
did not receive such Scriptures from those by whom they were catechized; and
they do not have copies to show from which they transcribed them. And some of
them did not even think fit to counterfeit them, but simply denying both the law
and the prophets, by a pretext of grace of a lawless and godless teaching, they
have slipped down to the last destruction of perdition. Eusebius brought this
