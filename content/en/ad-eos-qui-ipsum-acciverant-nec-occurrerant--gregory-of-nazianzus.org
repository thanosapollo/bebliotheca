ORATION III.
To those who had invited him and did not come to meet him.
=35.517=

A. How slow you are toward our word, friends and brothers, though you were
swift to play the tyrant and to tear me from my citadel of solitude, which I
had welcomed above all else, and which, as fellow worker and mother of the
divine ascent, and as that which deifies, I especially admired and set at the
head of my whole life. How is it that what you longed to take you now despise
once you have taken it, and you have shown yourselves better at longing for us
when we were away than at enjoying us when we are here, as if you wanted to
master our philosophy more than to profit by it? Or I may well say this too:
we became a satiety to you before you had tasted us or put us to the proof,
which is the strangest thing of all. =35.520=
B. And you did not even gather us in as strangers, or gather yourselves with
us, to speak more gently, out of reverence for this commandment if for nothing
else; nor did you take us by the hand as beginners, nor encourage us as timid,
nor comfort us as men who had been forced; but, I hesitate to say it, yet let
it be said, you have made the feast no feast for us, and you received us with
no good beginning; and you mixed gloom into the festival, which lacked what
most makes for joy, you my conquerors, for it would not be true to call you
lovers. So easily despised is everything that is easily conquered; and what is
lofty is courted, but what is humbled before God is dishonored.
C. What do you want? Shall I be judged against you, or shall I become the
judge? Shall I give a verdict, or receive one? For I hope both to win if I am
judged, and to vote against you justly if I give the sentence. The charge is
that you do not repay our love with equal measure, nor give honor in return
for our obedience, nor pledge the future by your present eagerness, which even
with that eagerness could barely be trusted, since everyone is hotter at the
start. Each of you sets something else above both the old shepherd and the
new, neither reverencing gray hair nor calling forth youth.
D. There is a splendid dinner in the Gospels, and a gracious host, and
friends, and the banquet is most sweet, for it is a son's wedding. He invites
them, and they do not come; he is angry; and I pass over what lies in between
because it is ill-omened; but, to speak moderately, he fills the banquet
through others. This, then, we pray may not be; but you have become to me (how
shall I say it mildly?) so much haughtier or bolder than they, inasmuch as
they, being invited, rise up against the dinner and insult the one who invited
them; whereas you, not being of those outside, nor of those invited to the
wedding, but yourselves having invited us, and bound us to this holy table,
and shown us the brightness of the bridal chamber, then =35.521= left us, for
this is the most splendid thing about you: one to his own field, another to
the newly bought yoke of oxen, another to the newly married wife, another to
some other small thing; you scattered and leapt away, caring little for the
bridal chamber and the bridegroom.
E. For this reason I was filled with despondency and perplexity, for I will
not keep silent about what I have suffered; and I nearly held back the word
which I meant to bring as a gift to the wedding, the fairest and most precious
thing I had; and I nearly let it loose upon you, whom I longed for, once I had
been forced; because I had taken hold of so splendid a theme, and affection
was sharpening my tongue, which is hottest for accusation and most fluent when
jealousy is born, having taken on unexpected grief from contempt. If any of
you, struck by the sting of love, has been slighted, he knows the feeling, and
will pardon those who suffer this, and who have come near this frenzy.
F. But it is not lawful for me now to lay hold of you with any reproach, nor
may it ever be. And even this, perhaps, I have laid hold of beyond measure:
the sacred flock, the praiseworthy nurslings of Christ, the divine
inheritance, through which you, O God, are rich, even if you are poor. And I
think those words also fit you: The lines have fallen to you in the fairest
places; for your inheritance is fairest for you; and I will not grant that the
numbered cities, nor the broadest of flocks, have anything more than we, the
few of the least tribe among the sons of Israel, the fewest among the
thousands of Judah, little Bethlehem among cities, in which Christ is born,
now and from the beginning well known and worshipped; among whom the Father is
exalted, and the Son is equalled, and the Holy Spirit is glorified together;
of one soul, minding the one thing, injuring nothing of the Trinity either by
preferring or by cutting off, as the bad umpires and measurers of the Godhead
do, who by magnifying one thing more than is right diminish and insult the
whole. =35.524=
G. But you, if you grant me anything, my husbandry, or my vineyard, my own
bowels, or rather those of our common Father, whom he begot in Christ through
the Gospel, show reverence also to us; for it is just, we who preferred you
above all; you are witnesses, and those who put into our hands this, whether
rule or service; and if more is owed to the one who has loved more, how shall
I measure the love for which I have made you my debtors through my own? But
show reverence more to yourselves, and to the image entrusted to you, and to
him who entrusted it, and to the sufferings of Christ, and to the hopes from
there, holding the faith which you have received, and in which you were
brought up, with which you also are being saved, and are trusted to save
others; for not many, be well assured, have your boast. Reckoning piety to
consist not in speaking often about God, but in being silent for the most
part; for the tongue is a slip for men when it is not steered by reason; and
always judging hearing less dangerous than speech, so as to take more pleasure
in learning than in teaching about God; leaving the more exact inquiry of
these things to the stewards of the word; yourselves being pious in word a
little, but more in deed, and showing the love toward him more by keeping the
laws than by admiring the lawgiver, fleeing vice, pursuing virtue, living by
the Spirit, walking by the Spirit, drawing knowledge from him, building upon
the foundation of the faith not wood, nor hay, nor straw, weak matter easily
spent when our works are judged by fire or purified; but gold, silver,
precious stones, the things that remain and stand.
H. May you do these things, and by them glorify us whether present or absent,
whether taking part in our words or preferring something else; and may you
become children of God, pure and blameless, in the midst of a crooked and
perverse generation; and may the cords of the ungodly who walk around =35.525=
not entangle you, nor may you be bound with the chains of your own sins, nor
may the word in you be choked by the cares of this life, and you become
unfruitful; but may you walk in the royal road, turning neither to the right
nor to the left, and led by the Spirit through the narrow as if it were broad;
and our affairs will be well both now and at the examination there, in Christ
Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory unto the ages. Amen.
