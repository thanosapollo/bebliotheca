ORIGEN'S SELECTIONS ON THE GENESIS.
=17.12= CH. 7.
Verse 4. I bring rain upon the earth forty days and forty nights. And I
will wipe out every rising thing which I made from the face of the
earth, from man even to cattle. Why then did he destroy the multitudes
of men by the flood? He wished to wipe out the race of Cain; but the
tribe of the pious was also mixed with it; wherefore it also shared in
the punishment. But wishing to make a certain beginning of a new life,
he preserved Noah and his children with the wives, being pious and
just, and having sprung from the piety of his kinsmen, and having hated
the mixing of lawlessness. Not indeed, as some say, did he do these
things from some wrath and repentance. For these are human passions;
but the divine nature is free of passions. And otherwise repentance is
fitting to those who after trial learn the nature of things; then,
learning by trial that they did not counsel rightly, they repent. But
God so sees the things that will be after many generations as already
having come to be. As therefore foreseeing and foreknowing, so he
administers all things. Why then does he repent, arranging all things
according to his own =17.13= foreknowledge? Therefore in the case of
God repentance is a change of economy. For I repent, he says, that I
have judged Saul as king; instead of: I have approved to stop this one,
and to appoint another. So also here: I considered that I made man; I
approved to destroy the race of men. But being a lover of man, he
preserved Noah as seed for the nature. And since he also created the
races of the irrational animals for the need of men, these too were
destroyed together with the men, except those preserved in the ark with
Noah. For the Master God commanded that two from each race of those
seeming unclean be saved, and seven of the clean. For since he was
about to grant to men to partake of meats, and the nurslings of piety
were also about to offer sacrifices to him, he ordered the clean to be
kept more numerous: the three pairs for the increase of the race, and
the one extra for sacrifice. For immediately Noah, after the pause of
the punishment, offered a thanksgiving sacrifice to God, from all, he
says, the clean cattle, and from all the clean birds. So that it is
clear that for this reason he ordered seven from each race of the clean
to be brought into the ark, in order that Noah, offering the one animal
from each race, might not destroy the pairs. Verse 10. And the Lord God
shut the ark from without of him. The security of the door being shut
had need of some more divine power. CH. 19. Verse 25. And he overthrew
these cities, and all the surrounding country, and all those dwelling
in the cities. Verse 20. And his wife looked back, and she became a
pillar of salt. The Lord destroys not only the impious, but also their
foods, being Sodomitic and harmful; among which is also the vine,
concerning which it is written: For of the vine of Sodom is their vine,
and their tendril is of Gomorrah. And at the same time he was saved
into the city, and the fire was being brought upon the cities, a sign
of the coming consummation. She did not become a pillar simply, but
especially a pillar of salt, because of: You are the salt of the earth.
And to the pillar of salt would fit: But if the salt be made foolish?
He does not go up to the mountain all at once according to the things
said to him, but first he comes to Segor, although not having dared
concerning himself to go up to the mountain. CH. 23. Verse 15. Land of
four hundred didrachms. It is to be noted that the four hundred is the
price of a tomb, because the tetrad and the hundred are of an
afflicting kind. And seeking the like from the Scriptures you will
find. =17.16= CH. 37. Verse 10. Shall we coming come, I and your
mother, and your brothers, to worship you upon the earth? Clearly the
mother of Joseph died before the dreams. Might we then perhaps say that
Mary and Joseph are prophesied to worship Christ? CH. 43. Verse 22. And
he said to them: Be gracious to you, do not fear. This man seems to
have been benefited toward piety with Joseph. CH. 40. Verse 34. We are
men, cattle-feeders. Every shepherd of sheep is an abomination to
Egyptians, but not to Hebrews. If therefore he stands over us as a
shepherd, he is an abomination to Egyptians; but if as a hireling,
perhaps even a friend to Egyptians.
