* Encomium on Saint Barbara

ΙΒʹ. Of our father among the saints, John the presbyter of Damascus, encomium
on the holy and glorious martyr of Christ, Barbara.
=96.781=
αʹ. The glory and grace and good fame of the saints, and of the
fair-victorious martyrs of piety, admittedly lies above every composition of
enco =96.784= mia, and is seated above the fashioning gathered from rhetorical
speeches and from the throat-noises of sophistic art. And I think even those
very skilled in such sciences, and who have spent and consumed their whole
life upon such practices and studies, would be struck with amazement and at a
loss before the magnitude of the subject; not to speak of us who are unlearned
and inexperienced, and have not, as the saying is, even with the tip of a
finger tasted this experience and skill and logographic consideration. But not
for this shall we, as if dizzying altogether at the undertaking of the
discourse, and seized with fear, and shrinking back, leave ourselves and the
present hearing-loving audience wholly without share of the benefit that
reasonably accrues from the issue of such contests. Come then, having invoked
from above the inclination of Christ, the overseer and prize-giver of such
contests, as gracious and helper, let us describe in speech, and as far as
possible set before the eyes, her who is now reasonably praised by us; and of
her whose all-people festival we keep today, of the fair-victorious martyr
Barbara, let us set forth as in brief the subject of the whole contest, so
that, having perceived the divine power that invisibly co-strengthens and
sinews the martyrs, we may procure for the soul no ordinary profit and
improvement; not trying to exalt the contests with sounds of eloquence and
with words. For these do not need the help that comes from speeches; but going
through the deeds themselves, I suppose, with bare phrasing.
βʹ. But rise up then for me, O Christ-loving and martyr-loving assembly, and
folding and contracting the mind from wandering upon things outside, hear the
things said attentively. For it is absurd, toward worldly theaters, I mean
horse-races or wrestling-grounds, or some such gymnastic contests, to run
together eagerly, and to wish to see, being led on, the rivalry of the
struggle and the intertwining of the opponents, and not to depart sooner, even
if they are pulled away by a thousand things, before they have watched the
end, and him who carried off the victory, taking up the prize and binding on
the crown, although they bring themselves no benefit from there. But here,
where a soul-profiting and spiritual theater is set before us, having as
president of the games not one of the kings upon earth, but the all-king who
rules all things visible and invisible, Christ our God, and as spectators the
thousands and myriads of the heavenly armies and powers, and as the one about
to contend, Barbara the celebrated and all-beloved crown-bearer and martyr of
Christ, and as opponents not one somewhere, or a second, but first, as the
Apostle says: For we have not the wrestling against blood and flesh, but
against the principalities, against the authorities, against the world-rulers
=96.785= of the darkness, against the spiritual things of wickedness in the
heavenlies, that is, for the sake of the heavenly things. And besides these,
also her own father according to the flesh, and the worldly governors and
rulers, whom the all-pure lamb of Christ, Barbara, had as wrestlers, composed
of

visible and invisible enemies.
γʹ. When such a spectacle of contests is therefore set before us, shall we not
hasten toward the watching of them, and survey the clashes and twists and
holds of both, and look upon the victory of our sacred victor and
crown-bearer? and the hateful demons being defeated and put to shame and
falling, and the angelic companies uttering shouts, and with victory-claps
praising and honoring the fair-victorious one, and Christ himself as president
of the games, as in the case of Stephen the first martyr, rising from the
royal throne, and holding forth the crowns, and receiving the victory-bearer
flowing with sweat, escorted by angels, and embracing her, and putting her to
rest in the heavenly chambers and bride-rooms? What is more delightful, or
sweeter, or more profitable to lovers of spectacle than such a sight? "Bless
those," says Basil the sacred teacher of the Church, "who have genuinely borne
witness, that you may become a martyr by choice, and may come out without
blood and scourges and torments." Is there any other watching or business more
profitable than this, and bringing greater wealth? I think not. But what has
happened to me, O friends? Before we have attacked the beginning of the
contests, the discourse has urged us toward the end. Come then, having called
it back, let us now set foot upon the forecourts of the martyrdom, and begin
the account in parts.
δʹ. The all-good and pity-loving Word of God, having seen his own fashioned
work, which he made from the beginning according to his own image, and to
which he entrusted the rule of earthly things, carried out from the delight of
paradise by the deceit of the intelligible serpent through the sensible one,
and thereafter enslaved, and with all its race not only subjected to
corruption, but also led into many-branched errors and passions of dishonor,
and so darkened in the soul's eyes by the gloom of impiety as even to have
left the God who truly is, and to have offered worship to woods and stones and
insensible matter, and driven out and thrown down by the wicked and apostate
demon into the extremity of Hades and of impiety, did not bear the outrage of
the destructive tyrant, and the desperate destruction of the deceived man. But
rather, taking up his own work in pity, the good Shepherd put on the fleece
from an unwedded mai =96.788= den, through which, having baited the adversary
and contended against him by the cross, he justly cast him out of the tyranny,
as one who had unjustly armed death against him who was inexperienced in sin,
according to which death had been appointed. For he who was altogether without
taste of sin was not subject to the accounts and penalties of death.
εʹ. He then who killed this one unjustly will justly be killed in return, and
will be deprived of the wealth he possessed. For that one's destruction is the
casting-out and fall of the authority; for the demonic tribe has been created
immortal by the Creator. And the Lord also said at the time of the passion:
The ruler of this world comes, and in me he will find nothing; that is, no
tillage of his seeds, or trace of sin, or smell, or shadow. For he did no sin,
to speak prophetically, nor was deceit found in his mouth. Wherefore also he
said again, Now is the judgment of this world; now shall the ruler of this
world be cast out, that is, he shall fall from the tyranny and be removed.
Having therefore through his own resurrection restored and renewed our lump,
he sends the sacred apostles thereafter to all the ends of the inhabited
world, announcing the saving preaching to mortals,


and leading them back to their own Maker, and dissolving the darkness of
atheism with the gleamings of the Spirit, and making the error vanish, and
teaching the knowledge of God. From this, as fire taking hold of much-wooded
and most ample matter is raised most quickly into a lifted flame, and is
transmitted by passage into the things nearby; and it cannot keep the
brightness and energy in a secret place, but makes it manifest to all, even to
those settled far away. So then also the divine fire, which the Lord who came
down from above upon the earth cast upon the earth, the consuming of all sin
and of a miserable and material habit and disposition, passing through the
apostles and their successors into human souls. And those that were smooth and
soft and fit for re-fashioning, and longing for and awaiting their own Maker
and redeemer from the captivity that held them, it at once wiped and cleansed
of the stain of sins, and turned them God-like and light-bearing and bright;
but those who had voluntarily received the quality of impiety in depth, and
who, like bats, were not willing to receive into their own eyes the light even
of the sun when it had shone, but chose to live in darkness and to roll
continually in the mire, it sent off to the last fire of Gehenna, as having
desired of their own will to be sick incurably.
ϛʹ. But not such was the all-sacred and most pure soul of the all-praised
martyr Barbara; but rather, having received the radiance of faith, and having
compared the light with the gloom, and having seen how incomparably it is
superior, possessing the divine knowledge over against the error and darkening
of abominations, =96.789= at once, as good and fat earth, seizing the heavenly
seed, and farming it carefully by the working of the commandments, she bore
fruit to thirty, and to sixty, and to a hundred, setting ascents in her heart,
and advancing toward perfection of virtues. In the end then she received the
prophetic saying, And all the earth shall be filled, it says, with knowing the
Lord, as much water covering seas; the companies of the nations, that is, the
grace that prophetically names them seas tropically. But the good-hating and
evil-loving demon did not bear to see the multitude of those being saved
advancing day by day. For he who at the beginning begrudged Adam with his wife
their sojourn in paradise would hardly endure the unnumbered myriads, not into
a sensible and earthly paradise, but into heaven, from which he himself has
been thrown as an iron lump, seeing them going up through faith in Christ. The
tempter therefore asks for the trial; for he has no authority against anyone
unless he receives it; but as of old, having heard God boasting of his own
servant, and saying, Have you set your mind upon my servant Job, that there is
no man upon the earth like him, blameless, just, true, God-fearing, abstaining
from every wicked deed? At once that one, struck with envy, answered, Does Job
worship the Lord for nothing, etc. But send me, that I may touch his flesh, if
he will not bless you to your face. Then God, wishing to show his own servant
to all, even in the last dangers, as firm and unconquerable, grants him the
request, that the boast may not seem to have been spoken at random. So also
then, the all-wicked one, having heard the Lord declaring encomium-wise
concerning the Church, And the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it,
naming gates of Hades the manifold forms of punishments that push and lead
down to Hades and death, asks again shamelessly for the trial. And having
received the authority, thereafter instruments full of impiety, and equal to
himself in cruelty of manner,


having chosen, he sets these as governors and rulers; and through them he sets
forth a dogma universally, that the worship of Christ should cease, and that
sacrifices should be offered to idols, and that those who contradict should be
destroyed unsparingly by all kinds of punishments. From this every age and
rank, of males and of females, was reaped without pity like grass, of those
who chose to keep the faith of Christ unharmed and unassailable even unto
death.
ζʹ. At that time then, when Maximian the impious was reigning, and Marcian was
governor, there was a certain toparch, it says, named Dioscorus, very rich,
but mad about the worship of lifeless idols. This man had an only-begotten
little daughter, named Bar =96.792= bara, distinguished by beauty of form, and
shining with dignity of manner. Having therefore built a high tower, he shut
the virgin up there, so that she might not be seen by men, because of the
blossoming and flashing brightness and splendor of her beauty. Dwelling there
then, having grown quiet apart from the tumults outside, she stirred up
God-fearing reasonings, and farmed them, and fed them; for solitude knows how
to become a mother of subtle and most high-natured thoughts. And the error and
worship of the idols she abhorred as leading down to the trap-door of Hades,
but the true faith of the Christians, and the august confession of the holy
and consubstantial Trinity, as leading up to the heavens, she cherished and
accepted; and the words concerning immortality of soul, concerning the kingdom
of heavens, and the enjoyment of the undefiled things, concerning Gehenna, and
the unending punishment there, and besides these also the unstable and
uncertain revolution of the temporary affairs of this world, she painted and
unrolled according to mind; and the lamp-bearing virgins, who are joined to
the incorruptible bridegroom Christ in the heavenly bridal chamber, and who
take their fill of the unspeakable blessedness within, she always imagined.
And she longed and desired and prayed to be counted worthy of that all-blessed
portion and unutterable joy and unspeakable glory.
ηʹ. While then the soul of the temperate and chaste virgin was crowding such
contemplations, the father stands over her. And since many magnates were
instructing him, and making much of her pairing and conjunction toward marital
intercourse, he was urging her to nod toward this. But she, spitting out such
words as nonsense and troublesome, looking with anger heavily upon the one who
begot her, utterly shakes off this soul-harming counsel, charging him to bring
her no longer the memory of such a thing. For the virgin thought it ridiculous
and full of all absurdity, having once dedicated and joined herself to the
heavenly and immortal bridegroom, if she should yield to those who, like
worms, wriggle in muddy pools in Hellenic mire. Her father then, building a
bathhouse at that time with much haste, having ordered the craftsmen to
construct two light-bringing windows toward the south, departed into a certain
country. But Barbara, the servant and bride of the Trinity, having inspected
the work of the building, forced the workmen to fit three windows to the
building. And returning and running back to her own tower, the fair virgin
sees the lifeless and insensible idols, to which the father was offering
reverence, and being filled with Holy Spirit, and taking up a God-inspired and
burning zeal, she spat into their dishonored faces, saying seasonably the
melody of the prophet David, May those who make you become like you, and all
who trust in you. O truly blessed and God- =96.793= loving soul! O maiden
boiling with faith,


adorned with faith! O girl who with divine understanding mocked the father's
folly and madness! O daughter who called back the simplicity and deceit of the
first-mother Eve by the firmness of her mind. That one, receiving a mere
assault of the enemy, was at once overturned; but this one not only harshly
beat back his charms, but also spat into his face through the idols. For she
stretched out and sent and shot the outrage of the spitting against the
first-evil one who works and deceives through them.
θʹ. When her begetter had returned from the sojourn, and had grown harsh and
indignant at the builders over the alteration of his own arrangement because
of the addition of the triple window, they transferred the cause to the
command of his daughter. And when he inquired of her the occasion of the
triple window, the all-praised one, seizing a seasonable pretext, theologized
expressly the glory of the Trinity, urging the unbeliever toward saving faith.
"Yes," she says, "for the Trinity also lights every man coming into the world.
For this sensible light happens to be a creature and workmanship of the light
conceived and worshipped in three suns holding one another, and a most dim and
faint image and shadow." But he who had been reared in impiety, and the
advocate of darkness, as if struck in the sight of the soul by the lightning
of the thrice-lighted Godhead, through the clear and unveiled confession of
the all-glorious girl, and rather darkened by the firmness of the gleaming,
becoming full of madness, and boiling over with anger, drawing the sword, the
darkened one rushed to slaughter the only-begotten little daughter, having
gone out beyond the bounds of piety and of nature together, because she had
shown him the path of life. Truly not only from a flowering plant does a fair
and most fragrant rose grow, but also from an impious root fruit of piety is
often given up. And Abraham the patriarch is witness, and for this called
father of the nations who have been zealous for his faith, first having
departed from ancestral polytheism and godlessness. Truly in this too the
divine prediction of our Savior Jesus Christ has been fulfilled, raising
daughter and bride against father and mother and mother-in-law, the new and
recent things against the old and nonsensical; and cutting in two the worse
from the better.
ιʹ. What then, the all-glorious Barbara? Seeing the all-defiled rush of the
father, or rather pitying him for the murderousness, having opened by prayer a
very great rock lying beside, she passed through the midst of it toward the
other part of the mountain. O the wonder! a rock split, receives the lamb into
its bosom, and her father and pursuer, more insensible than stones, was rather
turned to stone by hardness, and was brought over into rocky resistance. And
not even the paradox of the wonder was able to soften the hardness and
inhumanity of his soul, but having crossed the mountain, he sought her by
inquiry. And =96.796= having found and scourged her, dragging her by the hair
and hauling her from the mountain, he comes up again, and shut the blessed one
in a small house. And having reported the things concerning her to the
governor, he hands her over to be tortured, having sworn him by the gods to
consume her with bitter punishments. The governor then, having sat forward
upon the tribunal, ordered the martyr to stand by. And having seen the
blooming comeliness of her face, he was astonished, and wondered at the
brightness of the sight; and he began to try her with flatteries, forcing to
persuade, and to draw her by blandishments. For this is the first bait of the
devil. For either he tests to charm the man with pleasures and praises, and to
be softened, or to frighten and terrify with threats and


punishments. When then he had emptied all his former quiver, and saw the
contest-bearer spitting out the worldly charms, and reckoning all glory and
earthly luxury as a flower of grass, easily corrupted and easily withered, and
abhorring his urgings and enchantments as serpent's venom, and as poisons to
be fled, he at once turned to the other form of trials. And having seen her
likewise mocking the threats, filled with anger, as having missed the mark, he
uttered toward her. What then do you want, girl? to sacrifice to the gods, or
to be consumed with bitter punishments? Toward whom the martyr, with a steady
manner, and with a bold bearing, in few words both confessed the pious faith
piercing-clear, and pilloried the mindlessness and impiety of the godless
tyrants. Saying what? I, she says, am ready to sacrifice to my Lord Jesus
Christ, who made the heaven and the earth and the sea and all things in them.
But your gods the prophet well mocked: They have a mouth, and they will not
speak; they have eyes, and they will not see; they have ears, and they will
not hear; they have noses, and they will not smell; they have hands, and they
will not feel; they have feet, and they will not walk; they will not cry out
in their throat. May those who make them become like them, and all who trust
in them. For just as the worshipful name of God has been set upon them falsely
by you, they both lack and are bereft of the life-giving energy.
ιαʹ. Struck at these things the all-defiled and most reckless governor, with
boiling anger, orders the martyr, having been stripped, to have her flesh
unsparingly scraped with sinews of oxen, and the blows laid upon her to be
wiped with hair-cloths and weavings, so that her whole body might be dyed with
the bloods. Ah of your august and sacred confession, O fair-victorious martyr
of Christ Barbara, which through Holy Spirit you confessed before all
fearlessly and without shrinking, and theologized to the hearing of men, and
of angels who watch your contest, and of demons who invisibly campaign against
you together with the visible opponents; which the Lord has written down in
the heavens, and has confessed before his own first-lighted and God-principled
Father, and =96.797= again with haste will both confess and proclaim! Ah of
your most noble endurance and patience; through which you struck the devil and
his apostate demons, and moved the holy angels toward wonder and praise;
through which you dissolved the error of the idols as a spider's web, and both
strengthened and confirmed the true faith!
ιβʹ. The judge then, having seen the unchangeable and enduring quality of the
saint, orders her for the time to be thrown into prison, until he may consider
with what more bitter tortures he may lay hold of her. And in the middle of
that night a light exceeding-bright flashed around her, in which the Lord of
glory appeared, comforting her pain and grief by the appearing, and with
urging words making her bold toward the contests to come; "Take courage,"
saying, "Barbara, for very great joy will come to be in heaven and upon earth
over your contest. And do not fear the threats of the tyrant, nor be afraid.
For I am with you, and I will deliver you from all the blows brought upon
you." And at once the blows became invisible from her body. And having said
these things, the Lord of the heavens went up again into the heavens. And she
rejoiced greatly, and became full of cheerfulness and joy and gladness at the
urging of Christ, the genuine martyr and servant of Christ. And reasonably
indeed, O thrice-blessed girl, you rejoiced! for the subject of the joy is the
only true good-cheer, and gladness


unchangeable and inalienable. O your eyes truly blessed, counted worthy to see
the Lord of glory, upon whom the Cherubim in heaven, and the many-eyed ranks,
do not dare to gaze! O all-blessed ears, that have received the sound of the
words of God! O soul worthy of love and thrice-happy, whom he who rules
heavenly and earthly and under-earthly things, and God of all consolation,
comforted with urgings of his own mouth! You even while being in flesh have
received the earnest of the kingdom of the heavens, O bride of Christ, as a
manifestation of your whole dowry. For this reason you have thought and
reckoned the experience of the painful things as children's darts, or bites of
fleas; and being charmed by love of the one who appeared, and having been made
firm in soul, Who, you said, shall separate me from the love of Christ?
tribulation? or distress? or famine? or persecution? or scourges? or dangers?
For I am persuaded that neither life, nor death, nor angel, nor things
present, nor things to come, nor any other creation, shall be able to separate
me from the love of Christ.
ιγʹ. When morning had come, the martyr was led to the tribunal, the ruler
having commanded this. And having seen her weals invisible, and the body whole
and unharmed, not having even a chance tearing, the brain-harmed, he says, and
brain-seized, "You have seen how the gods claim you and love you, because they
have even healed your blows." But the martyr, having laughed at his madness
and thunder-struckness and derangement, answered back: "Your gods are like
you, deaf and blind and insensible, =96.800= and unmoved, unable to help
themselves, and how were they able to heal me, who am making their
people-deceiving vanity vanish? But he who healed me is Jesus the Christ, the
son of the living God, whom you with profane eyes and hardened soul have not
been born worthy to see." And as a beast kindled in anger by these words the
all-defiled one orders her sides to be scraped vigorously, and torches of fire
to be brought to the wounds, and to be struck upon the head with a hammer as
upon an anvil. But she, having gazed into heaven, said: "You know, Lord who
knows hearts, that longing for you I have come to you; do not abandon me unto
the end." And this torture too the noble martyr of Christ bore nobly, having
come to be outside not only of female weakness and unmanliness, but even of
human nature. For how would she have endured so many outrages and bitter and
painful punishments, had she not gone beyond the body of humiliation, by the
power of the Spirit that invisibly sinews and strengthens her.
ιδʹ. But not even with these things happening did the servant and advocate of
the devil take a satiety of madness; but he turned the fountainous mind of
wickedness toward another form of torture-instruments, having seated within
the dragon who is beginner and begetter and inventor of all evil, the
man-killer from the beginning and man-hater. The governor therefore, it says,
ordered the breasts of the martyr to be cut off with a sword. And as these
were being cut off, the lamb of Christ, looking up into heaven, said: Do not
cast me away from your face, Lord, and do not take your Holy Spirit from me.
And this blow too the martyr received enduringly and with thanks. Has another
device of misery harder than this been stored up for you, all-wicked demon, in
the depth of the soul? Has another form of torments more painful to the female
nature been taken up by you, or been left remaining? Has your mind not grown
weary inventing torture-instruments against the martyr? Add, if it seems good
to you, even others; for you will find adamant, or rather a soul unbreakable,
and firmer than adamant itself, a rebound-anvil to bear the strokes of your
hammers; It is hard for you toward


goads to kick. For by as much as you multiply the punishments, by so much
would you, even unwilling, increase the crowns for the martyr and all-praised
maiden, and would you, even not wishing, procure the brighter victory-prizes.
But for yourself you multiply the shames, being defeated at each wrestling and
assault, and falling, and being overturned on your back, and you heap more
also the unending punishments, and the flaming unquenchable coals, upon your
all-evil head.
ιεʹ. But it comes upon me also to wonder at the inhuman and beast-made manner
of the blessed one's begetter according to flesh, alien to the pious mind
according to spirit, and far from the paternal disposition. Truly even the
irrational beasts themselves, O compassionless father, you have surpassed in
cruelty and =96.801= inhumanity. For those, whenever their offspring are seen
being harmed by anyone, fight for them even unto their death; but you,
watching your own and only-begotten little daughter having the female
milk-bearing members cut off unsparingly with swords as in a butcher's stall,
were not even ashamed at the outrage of nature, nor were you bent toward so
many blows, being stony and insensible as to the heart. But why do we wonder,
brothers, at those who are drunk with the error of impiety, and have been
bacchic-mad with the madness of Satan? For just as the fair-victorious
martyrs, becoming possessed by the divine Spirit through the love toward
Christ, having conquered nature and gone above it, have come to be in the
things above nature, so also the persecutors of Christ and of these, being
full of the wicked spirit, and having enslaved themselves to the apostate
power, have themselves also slipped and fallen outside of nature, not toward
the better, but toward the worse. And as those were lifted above the human
condition, so much also these were flooded down below it, and have become like
the man-killing demons; and very reasonably. For those who come to Christ
become imitators of Christ through the same virtue; but those who have yoked
themselves under the yoke of the devil become, I suppose, similar and matching
to him. But let us see what again the adversary and opponent through his own
shield-bearers invents and crafts against the august and temperate Virgin.
ιϛʹ. For having seen that she bears and stands the outrages of the body
manfully, he devises for the soul an unendurable torture, and worthy of shame,
as he thinks. And it is truly such, and harder than the former. But to suffer
for Christ, even if it seems to have something reproachful and unseemly, yet
is more august and more seemly and more orderly than every adornment. For the
governor, it says, orders her to be paraded naked through all that surrounding
country, and to be scourged with unendurable blows. And you know entirely,
beloved, how much shame and modesty the thing brings to virgins, and
especially to those according to this holy girl, of whom not even the sun, so
to speak, was able formerly to take its fill. And he was forcing her to be led
around not only one marketplace and street, or a second, but also changing
fields and towns and peoples. For all, it says, that surrounding country.
Truly this trial is harder and more grievous than all burning and pain of
scourges for shamefast and august girls. But nevertheless she endured this
shame too through the love and desire toward Christ, who for us endured a
cross, having despised shame. For the virgin, having stripped off beforehand
the old man with the passions, of which also such shame of the clothing
exists, for this reason did not even reckon it unseemly; but as the
first-formed in paradise before the passions of sin were naked, and were not


ashamed, but when through the disobedience the passions were moved in them,
they supposed it unseemly =96.804= unless they wrapped the nakedness with fig
leaves; so also this virgin, having returned to that state before the
transgression of sin, stripped off the shame together with the remaining
passions. But nevertheless, that the defiled ones seeing her all-sacred
uncovered body going about might not seem to laugh, having gazed into heaven
she said: "Lord God, who wraps the heaven in clouds, cover my body that has
been stripped for you, that it may not be seen by these impious ones." And
when she had said these things, the Lord, having sent an angel, covered her
with a white robe; and she went about as a bride adorned and beautified, from
the paternal house toward the prepared bridal chamber, being led around and
escorted and paraded, crowned with the martyr's diadem, and with the athletic
marks for Christ, and being made august with the purple of the blood, and
distinguished above all brightness of pearls and emeralds and jacinths and
precious stones, and of golds interwoven. O the wonder! the passionate and
pleasure-loving, both shameless and very unashamed, stood the martyrs who
contend for Christ all-naked around, that they might, as they thought, triumph
over them, and at the same time feast their unbridled sight passionately. But
Christ, having wrapped his own contestant entirely with a covering of grace,
walled off the view from the shameful and wanton and abominable, and showed

ιζʹ. And having led her around all that surrounding country, they brought her
to a certain village, where they also overtook the governor. The governor
then, being at a loss as to what he might do again; for all his device was

brought forth a sentence against the much-contending martyr, to endure the
completion of a capital punishment. Then her father, filled with much anger
and satanic zeal, and having conceived the devil himself who is a man-killer
from the beginning, that he might seem to offer perfect worship to the idols
and abominations served by him, or rather to the spirits and demons of
wickedness who work through them, having drawn a sword, took her up onto the
mountain, desiring and aiming to become himself the own-hand and own-work of
the murder. And the fair and perfect dove, receiving with much joy the
slaughter for Christ, turned to prayer and supplication. For she was hastening
toward the prize of the upward calling, and was pressing toward the lifted
ascent into heaven. And nothing prevents us from hearing the words themselves
of the martyr's prayer, that through them we may sanctify the hearing.
"Unbegun, not handmade crown of martyrs, Lord Jesus Christ, who
stretched out the heaven and founded the earth; who shut up the abysses and
walled the sea; who commanded the rain-bearing clouds to rain upon wicked and
good; who =96.805= walked upon the sea as upon ground, and did not wet the
feet; who rebuked the wind and the sea, and changed them into calm; for all
things obey, my Lord Jesus Christ, your command, because they are your works;
Grant me, Lord, this request, and give to your servant grace, that whoever
remembers me in your holy name, and makes the memory of the days of my
martyrdom, Lord, you may not remember their sins in the day of judgment, but
become gracious to them. For you know, Lord, that we are flesh and blood, a
work of your undefiled hands." And when she had sent up the Amen, she prayed
again, saying: "Lord God of powers, creator of every breath and flesh, who
healed every


disease, and every infirmity, grant to your servant grace, that as many as may
come to the place where my relic lies, and the sanctified waters are, you may

she had sent up the Amen, there came a voice from heaven, saying: "Come, my
sanctified contest-bearer, rest in the treasuries of my Father who is in the
heavens. And all things whatsoever you have asked have been gifted to you by
me." And the blessed martyr of Christ, having heard these things, came to the
place, and had her head cut off by the sword of her father, together with the
holy Juliana in the same place. And as Dioscorus her father was coming down
from the mountain, fire fell from heaven, and consumed him, so that not even
the dust appeared, or was found. ιηʹ. The martyr has truly set a fair end upon
the contest. For having offered a prayer of the contests in the tower, she
also set a prayer as the crown upon them. And what is the power of the prayer?
She asked her own bridegroom Christ to become gracious to those who accomplish
her memory, the forgiveness of offenses, and the healing of diseases. And he,
having received the supplication, promises to bring the desire to fulfillment,
and through his own voice certifies this, and furnishes the confirmation. And
she stretched forth her neck to the sword with her whole soul; but the
child-killing father brought on the blow without compassion. And she was
escorted toward heavenly houses and a prepared rest, but he was led down into
Tartarus-recesses of Hades, and gloomy treasuries, and into a punishment made
ready. And she had bright angels guiding toward the ascent, but he, unsmiling
and fearful demons, dragging unwillingly toward the descent. O how might one
worthily lament, all-wretched and miserable! how did you not shudder to cut
off the holy head of the martyr! how did your hand not grow numb, to bring a
killing cut to your own daughter? But truly you received most swift wages of
pitiless inhumanity, and you reaped harvests more wicked than the wicked
seeds, and the worthy vengeance of the traitor Judas came upon you. For a
thunderbolt brought from heaven reduced your small body to ash, and the
=96.808= Sodomitic fire thunderbolted you here, but the unquenchable flame of
Gehenna will receive you there. For you were no longer worthy to live on, that
you might not, being pointed at with the finger, be called father of the
martyr. Your house no longer endures to receive you, from which you drove out
the virgin and all-praised one. ιθʹ. But you, lamb of Christ, and dove, and
bride, and every name whatsoever that is fair and graceful and honorable,
hail, having run through the double-course of the contests nobly. Hail, having
received prizes worthy of the contests. Hail, to whom many prizes are owed for
many of the struggles. Hail, who with a temperate reasoning mastered the
passionate leapings of the flesh. Hail, who trained the senses of the body in
youth of age in an elder-like and prudent way. Hail, who promised to keep
virginity, even before the martyrdom, undefiled and unstained for Christ.
Hail, who did not soil the bodily beauty with the passions, but dedicated and
entrusted it untouched to the Maker. Hail, who in the tower as in a safe
fortress, and an oratory of virgins, sent up frequent and unmixed prayers to
God, and by the unerring contemplation of the things that are was led up to
the Maker, and took your fill insatiably of the only truly fair and beloved.
Hail, who in the bath, the cleanser of bodily filth, symbolically imaged the
Trinity through the triple light-bringing of the windows, and typically drew
the mystical and saving baptism of souls by the triple gift of light. Hail,
who at once most clearly confessed the faith of the consubstantial Trinity
with a theological


mouth. Hail, who proclaimed in boldness the one of the holy Trinity who became
man for our salvation. Hail, who spat out wealth and luxury, and weavings of
silk threads, and of precious stones, and of gold-set pearls, and all bodily
good-order and fairness, and enjoyment of earthly delights, and exchanged
these for eternal and undefiled goods, which rise above sight of eyes, and
hearing of ears, and every sense and thought. Hail, who strengthened the
tenderness of age, and the weakness of the female, nobly and manfully with
intense mind. Hail, who did not play the coward toward the torments of the
punishments, but rather gave yourself courageously to all kinds of forms of
torture-instruments and rackings. Hail, who while spending time in flesh were
counted worthy to see the glory of Christ, as on the mountain the foremost of
the disciples, having seen it with Elijah and Moses, were fittingly blessed.
Hail, who endured the pains of scourges, and scrapings of irons, and rubbings
of very rough hair weavings, and streams of bloods, and flamings of fire, and
cuttings-out of members, and naked parading, and taking-away of head and life,
for Christ, that you might thereafter receive the body immortal, flashing
radiances of incorruption, and clothed with a robe of glory unutterable and
unspeakable.
κʹ. You men justly wondered at for endurance, and angels reasonably both
clapped =96.809= and hymned the contest, but the demons shuddered at your
hymnody and stance, and clothed themselves with shame and eternal reproach.
How the female and womanly tribe is adorned, and made august, and made bold
toward good-manliness by your contests, and is anointed! In you the
first-mother fittingly boasts, and is glad, as having acquired a little
daughter, who wrestled again the opponent dragon that had thrown her, and
overturned him, and called back the victory, and raised bright trophies
against him. You the holy of holies Theotokos and first-mother, virgin and
Mother of God, who first and alone glorified the female race, and who rules
all males and females, heavenly and earthly, as having become mother of him
who rules all, seeing you, in a womanly and girlish little flesh, having
thrown down him who boasts into the measureless, and who snorts that he will
wipe out the sea, and grasp the inhabited world as a nest, and having trampled
him down by force. And delighted by the virtues, having gone forward with
arms, she sat you near and established you, and imparted the highest honor and
boldness, and presented and set you by the royal throne.
καʹ. Every encomium-speech then, however well-tongued and superfluous, grows
dizzy over you, and fearing to approach, contracts. For it would be weak,
all-hymned and praiseworthy one, to hymn fittingly the supernatural advantages
of your virtues worthy of love. For the art of rhetorical eloquence, through
well-turned composition of words, and embellished and running persuasiveness,
praises bodily beauty, and fleshly fairness, which has been composed from
symmetry of members with good color. And besides often also strength,
well-being and dignity of mortal good birth, and a conspicuous rank, if it
happen, and the distinction of the fatherland, and an abundance of wealth
flowing and slipping and easily changing. Which things, when they have run
together at once to underlie the speech toward praise, are both hard to
procure and most laborious; yet all things whatsoever the speech has
distinguished by kind and listed, as a flower of grass withering and being
corrupted, and as a deceitful and phantom-like sight of good dreams, appearing
at once and running past, more quickly and in a short time sometimes even
being dissolved by time and


altered, correspondingly and consequently dissolve together also the
elaborated harmony and intertwining of the encomia. But for you, O august and

worthy of showing; yet not things perishing and being loosed, and preparing

the fair and worldly things that belong, and has made them unforgettable to
memory, and to run the long course together with the whole age; =96.812= or

scriptural saying has declared. For the things present, and the things to
come, and all things of which your bridegroom is master, have been granted and
gifted to you as dowry.
κβʹ. Since then you possess the victory-prizes from both sides against human
