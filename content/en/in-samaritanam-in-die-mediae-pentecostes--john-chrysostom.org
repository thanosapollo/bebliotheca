On the Samaritan woman. On the day of Mid-Pentecost.
=61.743=
Come then today, beloved, as the divine grace grants, let us set our hands to
the evangelical treasures. For love of learning about these things adorns the
souls, and stretches the earthly habit toward heaven. For love of learning
also made that wondrous Samaritan woman, from a water-bearer, into one filled
with God; for having found a treasure of teachings, she ran past; or rather
she did not find, but was found. For Christ did not go up to be sought by the
eagerness of those in need, but he himself was in labor of help at every
moment; he was constrained, caring for the paralyzed, sharing the pain of
those with fever, making the things of the blind his own, carrying about the
care for the lepers, running to the help of those possessed by demons, washing
the soiled souls of all, a teacher to women bearing water.
For then, having grown weary from the journey, he sat down by the well. For
Jesus, it says, being wearied from the journey, sat thus upon the spring. It
was about the sixth hour. A successive greed of loving-kindness: first, that
he grew weary, hastening to prepare beforehand the salvation of the Samaritan
woman; second, that he sat, waiting for her who appeared to be saved; third,
that he spent the hour of the meal in sitting-watch for her sake. It was, it
says, about the sixth hour. For though he was God, yet what appeared was still
clothed with the passions of nature, feeling toils, subject to sleep, troubled
by hunger, having every kinship with us except sin. Yet in him the longing for
those being saved conquered the toil of the body. She who had need delayed
more than he who had grown weary, and he who had no need remained. He was
burned by the drought of noon, and he watched the season for the presence of
her who was to be saved. For he sat, it says, upon the spring, not even lying
down as he awaited the hunt, but being in labor to grant salvation, he
prepared himself in readiness for the Samaritan woman. And she, I suppose, was
then coming out of the house, advancing little by little, using a woman's
gait, at ease, bringing the water-jar, often standing still in the midst of
her walking toward fruit, as if for a spectacle in a field. The scout is
unwearied and unseen, a skilled hunter, and having hidden the benefactor he
appears as one in want. For Jesus says, it says: Woman, give me to drink. He
sows the voice, as a grain, to a wandering dove: Give me to drink. He feigns
thirst, he who came to mix for her a cup of salvation; or rather, if one mines
the voice more finely, the Master's thirst is true; for he thirsted for her
salvation, and justly he cried to her: Give me to drink. You have, he says, in
yourself a hidden spring of good will; from that, filling me a stream, give me
to drink.
But the woman did not perceive the riddles, attending to the bare voice: How
is it that you, being a Jew, ask to drink from me, who am a Samaritan woman?
After the toil of the journey a trial of doctrines met Christ; but for one who
once chose to take up the things on our behalf, what happens is bearable.
Therefore he bears even the rebuke from the Samaritan woman. For he delighted
in descending from his dignity, that he might raise up the soul from the
sinner's cheapness. A sinful cheapness held the Samaritan woman; she was
defiled with a harlot's life, she took lover after lover, she had as marriage
the time of desire, she was a married bride to lovers, a widow always and a
newly-wed; or rather neither purely a widow, nor a bride; for she had neither
been separated from the first by death, nor joined to the second by lawful
marriages. Witness of this to her is the Master: You have well said, he says,
that I have no husband; for you have had five husbands, and he whom you now
have is not your husband.
This =61.744=.10 you have spoken truly. But she was a pearl mixed in mire, and
she did not escape the notice of such a merchant; for she showed the rays of
the soul by her words. How is it that you, she says, being a Jew, ask to drink
from me, who am a Samaritan woman? How you? And the "You" is of one angry. How
do you seek to drink from me? If as one who thinks the same things as I, I
will be ready for your request; but if you desire the things of the Jews, do
not seek welcome from foreigners; do not demand a gift which you flee. The
Samaritan woman thought that through the necessity of thirst she was netting
the Lord into Samaritan worship; but she did not know that she was being bound
by the words; for she had been bound by the answer to the Lord, or rather by
the voices from the Lord to her: If you knew the gift of God, and who it is
that says to you, Give me to drink, you would have asked him, and he would
have given you living water.
Since, he says, you mock my thirst, and call me a Jew who has no communion
with you, learn how far you miss in your testing of me. You do not know my
true generation, you do not know that the generosity from me is great, you do
not know what a flow of the spring is with me; for if you knew, you would have
asked; but now I do not blame you for not knowing, and when you have asked I
give.
He moves her who does not thirst toward an appetite of thirst; he turns her
who gives drink toward need of drink; so then the woman was wounded by the
words, and was caught by the desire of the water. For falling before the Lord
she said, Lord, give me this water, that I may not thirst, nor come here to
draw. She who formerly was thought worthy to give drink, now asks to be given
drink. For noble judgments are well-tilled ground; such was the judgment of
this blessed Samaritan woman, such the manner, cleansed of the thorns of
unbelief, not having Jewish envy. For she, expecting the Messiah, and having
obtained his divine teaching, believed sincerely; and she did not keep the
gift for herself alone, but she also persuaded her own citizens to run to him,
preaching apostolically: Come, see a man who told me all things that I did. Is
not this the Christ? O wise woman! She knew that a woman is not acceptable for
testimony; therefore she draws the men of her city as witnesses, that she
might become a sponsor of salvation for many. The evangelist therefore says:
And from that city many of the Samaritans believed in him, because of the word
of the woman testifying, that: He told me all things that I did. When
therefore the Samaritans came to him, they asked him to remain with them. And
he remained there two days.
And many more believed because of his word; and they said to the woman, We no
longer believe because of your speaking; for we ourselves have heard and know
that this is truly the Savior of the world, the Christ. Did you see, beloved,
the thirst of Christ, and how he had as food and drink the salvation of those
who believe in him? For my food, he says, is that I may do the will of the
Father who sent me, and finish his work. For this reason, thirsting, he gives
the Samaritan woman water to drink, and she, having drawn, gave over
abundantly to her citizens, without stint, the gift of the Lord. Through her
the people of Abraham is slower to learn than the Samaritan mind; for not even
the teachings beside the temple helped them; but even the conversation beside
the well helped her. Let us therefore put around ourselves the good things of
the Samaritan woman, let us make divine zeal our own, let us ourselves long
for well-disposed love of learning; and whenever we converse of the divine
things in church, let us think that what was said to her by the Master Christ
is said also =61.745= to us, Those who worship God must worship in =61.746=
spirit and in truth. To him be the glory and the power unto the ages of ages.
Amen.
