OF THEOPHILUS TO AUTOLYCUS

A glib mouth therefore and well-sounding speech furnish delight and praise
toward empty glory for wretched men who have the mind corrupted; but the lover
of truth does not attend to defiled words, but examines the work of the word,
what and of what sort it is. Since therefore, O companion, you struck me with
empty words, having boasted in your gods, the stone ones and wooden, both
hammered and cast and molded and painted, who neither see nor hear (for they
are idols and works of hands of men), and still you say that I also am a
Christian as bearing the name as a bad thing, I therefore confess that I am a
Christian, and I bear this God-beloved name hoping to be well-useful to God.
For it is not as you suppose, that the name of God is a hard thing, that it is
so; but perhaps you yourself, being still useless to God, think thus
concerning God.

But even if you say, “Show me your God,” I also would say to you, “Show me
your man and I will show you my God.” Since show the eyes of your soul seeing,
and the ears of your heart hearing. For just as those who see with the eyes of
the body consider the business of life and of earth, at once distinguishing
the things that differ, whether light or darkness, or white or black, or
misshapen or well-formed, or well-rhythmed and well-measured or unrhythmed and
unmeasured or over-measured or truncated, and likewise also the things that
fall under hearing, whether sharp-voiced or heavy-voiced or sweet-voiced, so
it would be also concerning the ears of the heart and the eyes of the soul to
be able to behold God. For God is seen by those who are able to see him,
whenever they have the eyes of the soul opened. For all have the eyes, but
some have them filmed over and not seeing the light of the sun. And it is not
because the blind do not see that already also the light of the sun is not
shining, but let the blind blame themselves and their own eyes. So also you, O
man, have the eyes of your soul filmed over by your sins and your wicked
deeds.

As a polished mirror, so must the man have a pure soul. Whenever therefore
there is rust in the mirror, the face of the man cannot be seen in the mirror;
so also when there is sin in the man, such a man cannot behold God. Show
therefore yourself also, if you are not an adulterer, if you are not a
fornicator, if you are not a thief, if you are not a robber, if you are not a
defrauder, if you are not an arsenokoites, if you are not an insolent man, if
you are not a reviler, if you are not wrathful, if you are not envious, if you
are not a boaster, if you are not arrogant, if you are not a striker, if you
are not a lover of money, if you are not disobedient to parents, if you do not
sell your children. For to those who do these things God is not manifested,
unless they first cleanse themselves from every defilement.

And for you therefore all things overshadow, just as a pile of matter whenever
it comes upon the eyes toward not being able to gaze at the light of the sun;
so also for you, O man, the impieties overshadow toward your not being able to
see God.

You will say then to me, “You who see, describe to me the form of God.” Hear,
O man: the form of God is unspeakable and inexpressible, not able to be seen
by fleshly eyes. For in glory he is uncontained, in greatness
incomprehensible, in height unthinkable, in strength incomparable, in wisdom
incommensurable, in goodness inimitable, in well-doing indescribable. For if I
say him light, I speak a work of his; if I say word, I speak his beginning; if
I say mind, I speak his prudence; if I say spirit, I speak his breath; if I
say wisdom, I speak his offspring; if I say strength, I speak his might; if I
say power, I speak his energy; if I say providence, I speak his goodness; if I
say kingdom, I speak his glory; if I say Lord, I speak him as judge; if I say
judge, I speak him as just; if I say Father, I speak him as all things; if I
say fire, I speak his wrath.

You will say then to me, “Does God become wrathful?” Especially; he is
wrathful at those who do base things, but he is good and kind and merciful
upon those who love and fear him; for he is a trainer of the godly and a
father of the just, but a judge and punisher of the impious.

And he is without beginning, because he is ungenerated; and unchangeable,
inasmuch as he is immortal. And he is called God because of having placed all
things upon his own security, and because of running; and the running is to
run and to move and to work and to nourish and to provide and to govern and to
make alive all things. And he is Lord because he is lord of the wholes, and
Father because he is before the wholes, and Craftsman and Maker because he
himself is creator and maker of the wholes, and Highest because he is above
all things, and Almighty because he himself holds and contains all things.

For the heights of the heavens and the depths of the abysses and the ends of
the inhabited world are in his hand, and there is not a place of his rest. For
heavens are his work, earth is his making, sea is his creation, man is his
molded thing and image, sun and moon and stars are his elements, having come
to be for signs and for seasons and for days and for years, toward service and
slavery of men; and God made all things from things not being into being, in
order that through the works his greatness might be known and thought.

For just as a soul in a man is not seen, being invisible to men, but through
the movement of the body the soul is thought, so it would be also that God
cannot be seen by human eyes, but through his providence and his works he is
seen and thought. For in the way that someone, having beheld a ship in the sea
equipped and running and coming down into a harbor, will clearly consider that
there is in it a steersman who steers it, so one must think that God is
steersman of the wholes, even if he is not beheld with fleshly eyes because he
is uncontained. For if at the sun, being a least element, a man cannot gaze
because of the exceeding heat and power, how not rather at the glory of God
being inexpressible a mortal man cannot look opposite?

For in the way that a ·oa, having a rind that contains it, within has
dwellings and many cases separated through membranes and has many seeds that
dwell in it, so the whole creation is contained by spirit of God, and the
spirit that contains together with the creation is contained by the hand of
God; as therefore the seed of the ·oa dwelling within cannot see the things
outside the shell, itself being within, so neither can man, being contained
together with all the creation by the hand of God, behold God.

Then an earthly king is believed to be, even though not seen by all, but
through his laws and ordinances and authorities and powers and images he is
thought. And God you do not wish to be thought through works and powers?

Consider, O man, his works, the change of seasons according to times and
turnings of airs, the well-ordered course of elements, the well-ordered
journey of days and nights and months and years, the varied beauty of seeds
and plants and fruits, and the many-colored generation of four-footed cattle
and birds and creeping things and swimming things, of freshwater and of sea,
or the intelligence given in the animals themselves toward begetting and
rearing, not for their own use, but for man to have, and the providence which
God makes preparing food for all flesh, or the subjection which he marked out
that all things be subjected to humanity, the ·ysis of sweet springs and
ever-flowing rivers, and the supply of dews and showers and rains coming
according to seasons, the all-varied motion of heavenly things, Dawn-bringer
rising and signaling beforehand that the perfect luminary comes, and the bond
of Pleiades and Orion, and Arcturus and the dance of the remaining stars
coming to be in the circle of heaven, to all of which the many-colored wisdom
of God has called their own names.

This is God alone who made light from darkness, who brought out light from his
treasures, and storehouses of the south and treasures of abyss and bounds of
seas and treasures of snows and hails, gathering waters in treasures of abyss
and gathering the darkness in his treasures and bringing out the sweet and
longed-for and delightful light from his treasures, bringing up clouds from
the end of the earth and multiplying lightnings into rain, who sends the
thunder for fear and announces beforehand the crash of the thunder through the
lightning, lest a soul suddenly troubled expire, but also measuring the
sufficiency of the lightning coming down from the heavens toward not burning
the earth; for if the lightning should take the full authority, it will burn
the earth, and if also the thunder, it will overturn the things in it.

This is my God the Lord of the wholes, who stretched out the heaven alone and
set the breadth of the under-heaven, who stirs up the hollow of the sea and
makes its waves sound, who is master of its might and calms the surge of the
waves, who founded the earth upon the waters and gave spirit that nourishes
it, whose breath makes alive the all, who if he should hold the spirit to
himself the all will fail.

This one you speak, O man, this one's spirit you breathe, this one you do not
know. And this has happened to you because of the blindness of the soul and
the maiming of your heart. But if you wish, you can be healed; give yourself
over to the physician and he will couch your eyes of the soul and of the
heart. Who is the physician? God, who heals and makes alive through the Word
and the Wisdom. God through his Word and the Wisdom made all things; for by
his word the heavens were made firm and by his spirit all their power. Most
strong is his wisdom; God by wisdom founded the earth, and prepared heavens by
prudence, by perception abysses were broken, and clouds flowed dews.

If you think these things, O man, living purely and holily and justly, you can
see God. And before all let faith and the fear of God lead the way in your
heart, and then you will understand these things. When you put off the mortal
and put on the incorruption, then you will see God according to worth. For God
will raise your flesh immortal together with the soul; and then having become
immortal you will see the Immortal, if now you believe him, and then you will
know that you spoke against him unjustly.

But you disbelieve that dead are raised. When it shall be, then you will
believe willing and not willing; and your faith will be reckoned into
unbelief, if you do not now believe. And toward what do you also disbelieve?
Or do you not know that of all affairs faith leads the way? For what farmer is
able to harvest, if he does not first trust the seed to the earth? Or who is
able to cross the sea, if he does not first trust himself to the ship and to
the steersman? And what one who is sick is able to be healed, if he does not
first trust himself to the physician? And what art or science is someone able
to learn, if he does not first give himself over and trust the teacher? If
therefore a farmer believes the earth and the one sailing the ship, and the
one who is sick the physician, you do not wish to trust yourself to God,
having so many pledges from him? First that he made you from not being into
being. For if your father was not nor your mother, much more neither were you
ever. And he molded you from a small and least moist substance of a ·anis,
which itself also was not ever; and God led you forth into this life. Then you
believe the statues made by men to be gods and to do virtues. And the God who
made you you disbelieve to be able also to make you again?

And the names of those whom you say you worship as gods are names of dead men.
And of which of these and of what sort? Is not Kronos found a child-eater and
consuming his own children? And if you should also say Zeus his child, learn
also that one's deeds and his conduct. First he was reared on Ida by a goat,
and having slaughtered this according to the myths and flayed it he made for
himself a garment. And his remaining deeds, concerning both sister-intercourse
and adulteries and corruption of boys, Homer and the remaining poets better
narrate concerning him. Why should I recount the rest concerning his sons,
Heracles having burned himself, and Dionysus drunken and raving, and Apollo
fearing Achilles and fleeing and

loving Daphne and being ignorant of the fate of Hyacinthus, or Aphrodite being
wounded, and Ares the bane of men, and still also ·eon ichor of these
so-called gods?

And these things are moderate to say, where indeed a god is found cut into
pieces, the one called Osiris, of whom also each year rites come to be as of
one perishing and being found and being sought according to limb; for neither
if he perishes is it thought, nor if he is found is it shown. And what should
I say of Attis being cut off or Adonis ·embomenon in a wood and hunting and
being wounded by a boar, or Asclepius being thunderbolted, and Sarapis the
fugitive from Sinope having come to Alexandria, or the Scythian Artemis
herself also having become a fugitive and a man-slayer and a huntress and
having loved Endymion?

For we do not say these things, but your writers and poets proclaim them.

Why should I recount the rest, the multitude of animals whom Egyptians
worship, both creeping things and cattle and beasts and birds and swimming
things in water, and still also foot-basins and sounds of shame? And if you
should also say Greeks and the remaining nations, they worship stones and
woods and the remaining matter, as we already said, likenesses of dead men.
For Pheidias is found in Pisa making for the Eleans the Olympian Zeus, and for
Athenians on the acropolis Athena.

And I also will ask you, O man, how many Zeuses are found; for Zeus in the
first is addressed as Olympian and Zeus Latiaris and Zeus Cassius and Zeus
Ceraunius and Zeus Propatōr and Zeus Pannychius and Zeus Poliouchos and Zeus
Capitolinus. And Zeus the child of Kronos, having become king of Cretans, has
a tomb in Crete; and the remaining perhaps were not even deemed worthy of
burial. And if you should also say the mother of the so-called gods, may it
not be for me to speak out her deeds through the mouth (for it is unlawful for
us even to name such things), or the deeds of her attendants by whom she is
served, how many taxes and tributes she herself and her sons furnish to the
king.

For they are not gods, but idols, as we have said before, works of hands of
men and unclean demons. And may such become those who make them and those who
hope in them.

Therefore rather I will honor the king, not worshiping him, but praying for
him. And God the really God and true I worship, knowing that the king has come
to be by him. You will say then to me, “Why do you not worship the king?”
Because he has not come to be for being worshiped, but for being honored with
the lawful honor. For he is not God, but a man, appointed by God, not for
being worshiped, but for judging justly. For in a certain manner he has been
entrusted with an economy from God; for even he himself does not wish those
who have been appointed under him to be called kings; for king is his name,
and it is not permitted for another to be called this; so neither to be
worshiped but by God alone.

So that in all things you wander, O man. And honor the king being well-minded
toward him, being subjected to him, praying for him. This

doing you do the will of God. For the law of God says: “Honor, son, God and
king, and be disobedient to none of them; for they will suddenly punish their
enemies.”

And concerning your laughing at me, calling me a Christian, you do not know
what you say. First that the christon is sweet and well-useful and not to be
laughed at. For what ship is able to be well-useful and to be saved, if it is
not first anointed? Or what tower or house is well-formed and well-useful,
whenever it has not been anointed? And what man entering into this life or
competing is not anointed with oil? And what work or ornament is able to have
good form, if it is not anointed and polished? Then air and all the
under-heaven in a certain manner is anointed with light and spirit. And you do
not wish to be anointed with oil of God? Therefore we for this reason are
called Christians because we are anointed with oil of God.

But also your denying that dead are raised; for you say, “Show me even one
raised from the dead, in order that seeing I may believe”; first what great
thing, if having beheld the thing that has come to be you believe? Then you
believe that Heracles having burned himself lives and Asclepius having been
thunderbolted has been raised. And the things said to you by God you
disbelieve? Perhaps I will also exhibit to you a dead man raised and living,
and this you will disbelieve.

God therefore exhibits to you many proofs toward believing him. For if you
wish, consider the end of seasons and days and nights, how they also end and
rise. And what also is not the resurrection of seeds and fruits that comes to
be, and this for the use of men? For if it should happen to say, a grain of
wheat or of the remaining seeds, whenever it is thrown into the earth, first
dies and is dissolved, then is raised and becomes an ear. And the nature of
trees and of tree-fruits, how do they not according to command of God from the
unseen and invisible according to seasons bring forth the fruits? Still
sometimes also a sparrow or of the remaining birds, having swallowed a seed of
an apple-tree or fig-tree or of some other, came upon some rocky hill or tomb
and voided, and that having taken hold grew up a tree, that which was once
swallowed and went through so much heat. And all these things the wisdom of
God works, toward exhibiting also through these that God is able to make the
universal resurrection of all men.

And if you also wish to behold a more marvelous spectacle coming to be toward
proof of resurrection, not only of earthly affairs but also of those in
heaven, consider the resurrection of the moon coming to be according to month,
how it wanes dies rises again. Still hear also in yourself a work of
resurrection coming to be, even if you do not know, O man. For perhaps
sometime having fallen into disease you lost your fleshes and the strength and
the form, and having obtained mercy from God and healing you again received
your body and the form and the strength; and just as you did not know where
your fleshes went having become unseen, so you do not know either from where
they came to be or from where they came. But you will say, “From foods and
juices being made into blood.” Well but this also is a work of God having thus
crafted, and of no other.

Do not therefore disbelieve, but believe. For I also disbelieved

this would be, but now having considered them I believe, at once also having
met with holy scriptures of the holy prophets, who also foretold through
spirit of God the things that have come to be before in what manner they have
come to be and the things present in what manner they come to be and the
things coming in what order they will be completed. Having therefore taken
proof of the things coming to be and having been proclaimed beforehand I do
not disbelieve, but I believe obeying God; to whom, if you wish, you also be
subjected believing him, lest now having disbelieved you be persuaded being
grieved, then in eternal punishments.

Of which punishments having been said beforehand by the prophets the poets and
philosophers having become later stole from the holy scriptures, toward their
dogmas becoming trustworthy. Nevertheless they themselves also foretold
concerning the chastisements that are going to be upon the impious and
unbelieving, in order that there may be witnesses for all, toward some not
saying that we did not hear nor know.

And if you wish, you also read ambitiously the prophetic scriptures; and they
will lead you more clearly toward fleeing the eternal chastisements and
obtaining the eternal good things of God. For he who gave a mouth for speaking
and molded an ear for hearing and made eyes for seeing will examine all things
and will judge the just, rendering to each according to worth of the rewards.
To those who by endurance through good works seek the incorruption he will
gift eternal life, joy, peace, rest and multitudes of good things, which
neither eye saw nor ear heard nor upon heart of man went up; and to the
unbelieving and despisers and those disobeying the truth, but being persuaded
by the injustice, whenever they are mixed in adulteries and fornications and
arsenokoitiai and greedinesses and the unlawful idolatries, there will be
wrath and fury, affliction and distress; and the end will hold such as these
eternal fire.

Since you added, O companion, “Show me your God,” this is my God, and I
counsel you to fear him and to believe him.

Since before these days there came to be a word for us, O most good Autolycus,
when you asked who is my God and you furnished the ears for a little to our
discourse, I set forth to you concerning my piety; and still also having taken
leave of one another with very much friendship we went each to his own house,
although you had the first things harshly toward us; for you know and remember
that you supposed our word to be folly. You therefore after these things
having urged me on, even if I am a private man in the word, nevertheless I
wish even now through this writing to exhibit more accurately to you the
vain-labor and vain religion in which you are held, at once also through a few
of the histories according to you which you read, and perhaps you do not yet
know, to make the true thing manifest to you.

For it also seems to me laughable that stone-cutters and molders or painters
or casters both mold and paint and carve and cast and construct gods, who,
whenever they have come to be by the craftsmen,

they consider them nothing; but when they have been bought by some and set up
into a so-called temple or some house, to these not only do those who bought
sacrifice, but also those who made and sold come with haste and array of
sacrifices and libations toward worshiping them and they consider them gods,
not knowing that they are such as they were also when they came to be by them,
whether stone or bronze, or wood or color, or even some other matter.

This then has also happened to you who read the histories and genealogies of
the so-called gods. For whenever you meet with their generations, you think
them as men; but later you address them as gods and serve them, not attending
nor understanding that such as you read them to have come to be, such also
they were begotten.

And of the gods then, if indeed they were begotten, a much generation was
found. But now where is generation of gods shown? For if then they were
begetting and being begotten, it is clear that it was necessary that generated
gods come to be even until now; but if not, such a thing will be thought weak;
for either they grew old, wherefore they no longer beget, or they died and no
longer are. For if gods were being begotten, it was necessary that they be
begotten even until now, just as also men are begotten; rather also more gods
ought to be than men, as Sibyl says:

If gods beget and remain immortal,
more gods than men would have been begotten,
nor would a place ever have existed for mortals to stand.

For if of men being mortal and short-lived the children being begotten are
shown until now, and it has not ceased that men not be begotten, wherefore
cities and villages multiply, still also lands are inhabited, how not rather
was it necessary that the gods who do not die according to the poets beget and
be begotten, just as you say a generation of gods has come to be? And toward
what then was the mountain called Olympus inhabited by gods, but now it
happens to be desert? Or for the sake of what then did Zeus dwell in Ida (he
was known dwelling there according to Homer and the remaining poets) but now
he is unknown? And why also was he not everywhere, but was found in a part of
earth? For either he neglected the remaining things, or he was unable to be
everywhere and to provide for all things. For if he was, if it should happen
to say, in an eastern place, he was not in a western place; and if again he
was in the western, he was not in the eastern. But of God the Highest and
Almighty and the really God this is not only to be everywhere, but also to
oversee all things and to hear all, still also not even to be contained in a
place; but if not, the place containing him will be found greater than he; for
the containing is greater than the contained; for God is not contained, but he
himself is place of the wholes.

And toward what has Zeus also left Ida? Whether having died, or that mountain
no longer pleased him? And where did he also go? Into heavens? No. But you
will say into Crete? Yes; where also a tomb is shown for him until now. Again
you will say into Pisa, the one who steals

until now the hands of Pheidias.

Let us come then upon the writings of the philosophers and poets.

Some of the Stoa deny even that God is at all, or, even if he is, they say
that God cares for nothing except himself. And these things altogether the
mindlessness of Epicurus and Chrysippus declared. And others say that there is
automatism of all things, and the world ungenerated and nature everlasting,
and they dared to speak out that altogether there is not providence of God,
but they say that God is only the conscience of each. And others again
dogmatize the spirit that has gone through the whole as God.

And Plato and those of his heresy confess that God is ungenerated and father
and maker of the wholes; then they hypothesize God and matter ungenerated and
they say that this has flourished together with God. But if God is ungenerated
and matter ungenerated, no longer is God maker of the wholes according to the
Platonics, nor indeed is monarchy of God shown, as far as it is according to
them. And still also just as God, being ungenerated, is also unchangeable, so,
if also the matter was ungenerated, it also was unchangeable and equal-to-God;
for the generated is turnable and changeable, but the ungenerated unturnable
and unchangeable.

And what great thing, if God from underlying matter made the world? For also a
craftsman man, whenever he takes matter from someone, from it makes as many
things as he wishes. But the power of God is manifested in this, that from
things not being he makes as many as he wishes, just as also to give soul and
movement is of no other but of God alone. For also a man makes an image, but
word and breath or perception he cannot give to that which has come to be by
him. But God possesses this more than this, the making rational, breathing,
perceptive. As therefore in all these things God is more powerful than man, so
also the making from things not being and having made the things that are, and
as many as he wishes and as he wishes.

So that the opinion according to the philosophers and writers is dissonant.
For these having declared these things, the poet Homer is found introducing by
another hypothesis a generation not only of world but also of gods. For he
says somewhere:

Ocean both, generation of gods, and mother Tethys,
from whom indeed all rivers and all sea.

Which things saying he no longer establishes God. For who does not know that
Ocean is water? And if water, then not God. But God, if he is maker of the
wholes, as also he is, then he is also creator of the water and of the seas.

And Hesiod himself not only spoke out a generation of gods, but also of the
world itself. And having said the world generated he was too weak to say by
whom it has come to be. Still also he said gods Kronos and Zeus from him, and
Poseidon and Plouton, and these we find later than the world. And still also
he histories that Kronos is warred upon by Zeus his own child. For thus he
says:

Having conquered by might father Kronos; and well each thing
he arranged for the immortals equally and indicated honors.

Then he adds saying the daughters of Zeus, whom he also addresses as Muses, of
whom he is found a suppliant wishing to learn from them in what manner all
things have been generated. For he says:

Hail, children of Zeus, and give a longed-for song.
Celebrate the race of the immortal blessed always being,
who were generated from earth and starry heaven,
and of dark night, whom the salty pontos nourished.
And say how first gods and earth came to be,
and rivers and pontos unlimited, raging with swell,
and shining stars and broad heaven above,
and how they divided wealth and how they portioned honors,
and also how first they held many-folded Olympus.
These things tell me, Muses having Olympian houses,
from the beginning, and say what first came to be of them.

And how did the Muses know these things, being later than the world? Or how
were they able to narrate to Hesiod, where indeed their father has not yet
been generated?

And matter in a certain manner he hypothesizes, and a making of world, saying:

Truly first chaos came to be, but then
broad-breasted earth, always a secure seat of all
immortals, who hold the peak of snowy Olympus,
and misty Tartara, recess of earth of broad ways,
and Eros, who is most beautiful among immortal gods,
limb-loosening, and of all gods and of all men
he subdues in breasts mind and prudent counsel.
And from Chaos Erebos and black Night came to be.
And Earth first generated equal to herself
starry Heaven, that he might cover her around all things,
that there might be for blessed gods always a secure seat;
and she generated long mountains, charming haunts of goddesses
of Nymphs, who dwell along wooded mountains;
and also she bore the barren pelagos raging with swell,
pontos, without desirable love; but then
having lain with Heaven she bore Ocean deep-eddying.

And having said these things not even thus did he make clear by whom they came
to be. For if in the first things there was chaos, and some matter lay
beforehand being ungenerated, who then was the one refashioning this and
rearranging and transforming? Whether did the matter itself reshape itself and
order itself? For Zeus after much time has been generated, not only of the
matter but also of the world and of a multitude of men; still also his father
Kronos. Or rather was there some lord that made it, and I say God, who also
ordered it down?

Still also in every manner he is found talking nonsense and saying things
contrary to himself. For having said earth and heaven and sea he wishes the
