Of Evagrius the Monk, The Causes of the Things concerning Monks, and the
Setting-forth of These according to Stillness.

Αʹ. In Jeremiah it has been said: "And you, do not take a wife for yourself in
this place; for thus says the Lord concerning the sons and the daughters
=40.1253= that are born in this place: They shall die of a pestilent death."
This the word showing, that according to the Apostle, "the married man is
anxious about the things of the world, how he may please his wife, and he is
divided; and the married woman is anxious about the things of the world, how
she may please her husband." And it is clear that not only concerning the sons
and daughters that will come to be according to the married life has it been
said in the prophet, "They shall die of a pestilent death"; but also
concerning the sons and daughters born in their heart, that is, fleshly
thoughts and desires, that these too shall die in the pestilent and weak and
slack mind of this world, and shall not obtain the heavenly and eternal life.
"But the unmarried man," he says, "is anxious about the things of the Lord,
how he may please the Lord," and he will produce the ever-fresh and immortal
fruits of the heavenly life.

Βʹ. Such is the monk, and thus the monk ought to be, abstaining from a wife,
not making a son or a daughter in the place mentioned before; and not only so,
but also being an immaterial soldier of Christ, free from care, outside every
business-like thought and deed, as also the Apostle says: "No one serving as a
soldier entangles himself in the affairs of life, that he may please the one
who enlisted him." In these things let the monk go, especially the one who has
left behind all the matter of this world, and is running off to the fair and
good trophies of stillness. For how fair and good is ascesis for stillness,
how fair indeed and good! For her yoke is kind, and the burden light; sweet is
the life, the practice dear.

Γʹ. Do you wish, then, beloved, to take up the solitary life as it is, and to
run off to the trophies of stillness? Leave there the cares of the world, and
the authorities and powers over them; that is, be immaterial, passionless,
outside every desire; so that, having become alien to the circumstance that
comes from these, you may be able to keep stillness well. For unless someone
lifts himself out from these, he would not be able to accomplish this very way
of life. Hold to brief and easily-despised foods, not many and
easily-distracting. And if a thought should also arise concerning costly
things as for the sake of hospitality, leave it there, do not add to it at
all; for through this the adversary lies in wait for you, he lies in wait to
make you stand away from stillness. You have the Lord Jesus somehow blaming
Martha, the soul that is eager about such things, and saying: Why "are you
about many things," and "are you troubled? One thing is needful," namely, he
says, to hear the divine word, and after this without toil of whatever is
found. Therefore he also immediately adds, saying: for "Mary has chosen the
good portion, which shall not be taken away from her." And you have also the
example of the widow in Sarepta, in what things she entertained the prophet.
Even if you have only bread, even if =40.1256= water, you are able in these to
make the wage of hospitality; and even if you do not have these, but only
receive the stranger with a good purpose, and give him a useful word, likewise
you are able to furnish the wage of hospitality; for a word has been said to
be better than a gift.

Δʹ. Such things you must think concerning almsgiving. Therefore do not desire
to have wealth for distribution to the poor. For this too is a deceit of the
evil one,


often approaching toward vainglory, and casting the intellect into occasions
of busyness. You have in the Gospel a widow testified to by the Lord Jesus,
who through two mites alone surpassed the purpose and power of the rich. "For
those," he says, "cast in from their abundance into the treasury, but this
woman her entire substance." And concerning garments, do not desire to have
superfluous garments; provide for the things sufficient for the necessity of
the body. Rather cast your care upon the Lord, and he himself will provide for
you. For to him, it says, there is care concerning us. If there is need of
foods or garments, do not be ashamed to receive the things offered to you by
others. For this is a form of pride. And if you yourself also abound in these,
give to the one who is lacking. Thus God wishes his own children to be
provided for. For this reason also the Apostle, writing to the
Corinthians, said concerning those who are lacking: "Your abundance for
their lack, that their abundance also may become for your lack, so that
there may be equality, as it is written: He who had much did not have too
much, and he who had little did not have too little." Having then the
necessities of the present time, do not be anxious for the coming time;
for example, of one day, or of a week, or of a year, or of months. For
when the time of tomorrow has come, the time itself will supply the
necessities, especially if you seek the kingdom of the heavens and the
righteousness of God. For "Seek," says the Lord, "the kingdom of God
and his righteousness, and all these things shall be added to you."

Εʹ. Do not acquire a servant; lest the adversary ever through this move some
scandal for you, and throw your mind into turmoil to be anxious about the most
costly foods. For you will no longer be able to care for yourself alone; and
even if the thought should come as toward bodily rest, yet think of the
better; I mean spiritual rest; for spiritual rest is truly better than bodily.
And even if it should strike the judgment for the sake of the servant's
benefit, do not be persuaded by it; for this is not our work; it belongs to
others, the holy fathers in the coenobium. Care for the benefit of yourself
alone, and acquire the manner of stillness. Do not love to dwell with material
men and men involved in circumstances; either dwell alone, or with brothers
who are immaterial and of one mind with you. For =40.1257= he who dwells with
material men and men involved in circumstances will altogether himself also
share in their circumstance, and will serve human commands, and vain talkings,
and all the other terrible things, anger, grief, madness of material things,
fear, scandal. Do not be carried away together with parental cares or kindred
friendships; but even refuse continuous meetings with these very people, lest
they ever strip you of the stillness in the cell, and lead you about their own
circumstances. "Leave," says the Lord, "the dead to bury their own dead; but
you, come, follow me." And if the cell in which you sit is also easily
involved in circumstances, flee, and do not spare it, lest you be dissolved
into love of it; do all things, practice all things, how you may be able to
keep stillness and to have leisure, and to be eager to be in the wills of God,
and in the struggle of the invisible ones.

ϛʹ. If you cannot easily keep stillness in your own parts, give your purpose
to sojourning abroad, and hasten your thought upon this very thing. Become as
some excellent merchant, testing all things toward stillness, and holding
through all things the things that are still and useful toward it. Only I say
to you, love sojourning abroad; for it delivers you from the circumstances
according to your own country, and makes you enjoy only the benefit according
to stillness. Flee the sojourns in the city, and endure those in the desert.
For "Behold," says the holy one, "I have gone far fleeing, and I lodged in the
wilderness." If possible, do not go into a city at all; for you will see there
nothing


useful, nothing serviceable, nothing beneficial to your way of life. "I saw,"
says the holy one again, "lawlessness and contradiction in the city."
Therefore pursue your own and undistracted places; do not be afraid of them.
And if you see there fantasies of demons, do not be terrified, nor flee the
stadium of our benefit; endure without fear, and you will see the mighty works
of God, the succor, the care, all the other full assurance unto salvation. For
"I waited," says the blessed man, "for the one who saves me from
faint-heartedness and from storm." Let not a desire of wanderings overcome
your purpose; for "Wandering with desire mines an innocent mind." Many
temptations are through this. Fear the fall, and be steadfast in your cell.

Ζʹ. If you have friends, flee continuous meetings with them; for meeting them
at long intervals you will be useful. And if you also perceive harm coming to
you through these very ones, do not approach at all. For you ought to have
friends who are beneficial and who contribute to your way of life. Flee also
the meetings of wicked and quarrelsome men; dwell with none of these; but even
refuse their base purposes; for they are neither made intimate with God
=40.1260=, nor indeed do they remain. Let peaceful men be friends to you,
spiritual brothers, holy Fathers. For these the Lord also thus named, saying:
"My mother and my brothers and fathers are these, who do the will of my Father
who is in the heavens." Do not lodge with distracted people, nor go along to a
banquet with them; lest they ever draw you about their own deceits, and lead
you away from the knowledge according to stillness; for this passion is
in them; do not turn your ear aside to their words, and do not receive
thoughts of their heart. For they are truly harmful. Let your longing be
upon the faithful of the earth, and the labor of your heart toward zeal
of their mourning. For
"My eyes are upon the faithful of the earth, that they may sit with me." And
if someone of those who walk according to the love of God, having come to you,
should invite you to eat, and you wish to go, go, but return more quickly to
your cell. If possible, never sleep outside this; so that the grace of
stillness may remain with you always, and you will have the worship of the
purpose in it unhindered.

Ηʹ. Do not become a desirer of good foods, and of the deceits according to
luxury. "For she who lives in luxury, while living has died," as also the
Apostle says. Do not fill your belly with foods of others, lest a longing for
these come to be for you, and it produce in you the longing concerning the
tables outside. For it has been said: "Do not be deceived by filling of the
belly." And if you also continually see yourself invited outside your cell,
refuse. For continual sojourn outside your cell is harmful; it takes away the
grace, darkens the mind, withers the longing. See, I pray you, a jar of wine
that has remained a long time in the place, and lies unmoved, how it makes the
wine bright, settled, fragrant; but if it is carried here and there, it is
troubled, gloomy, displaying the unpleasantness of all the evil from the lees
together. Therefore, having likened yourself to this, try the benefit, cut the
attachments of the many, lest your intellect become distractible, and trouble
the manner of stillness. Take thought for work of hands, and this very thing,
if possible, night and day, toward not being a burden to anyone, but rather
also toward sharing, just as also the holy apostle Paul exhorts saying; so
that indeed in this too you may wrestle down the demon of acedia, and drive
off all the other desires of the enemy. For the demon of acedia lies upon
idleness, and is in desires, as he says. You will not escape sin of selling
and receiving. Whether therefore you sell,


or buy, take a little loss from the equal; lest, having been carried by the
ways according to exactness or those of love of gain concerning the price, you
fall down upon the harmful causes of the soul, contentiousness =40.1261=,
perjury, by the exchanges of your words, and in such things dishonor and put
to shame the honorable dignity of our purpose. Thus also yourself,
considering, guard yourself in your selling and your receiving. And if you
choose the better, and this is possible for you, cast your care upon some
other faithful man, so that having thus become of good cheer, you will have
good and joyful hopes.

Θʹ. These things the manner of stillness thus knows to counsel you as the
useful things. Come then, I will also set before you the mind of the things
that are next brought forward in it; but you, hear me, and do the things I
command you. Sitting in your cell, gather your intellect; remember the day of
death; see then the deadness of the body; think of the calamity; take the
pain; condemn the vanity in this world; take care of both gentleness and zeal,
so that you may be able to remain always in the same purpose of stillness, and
do not grow weak. And remember also the present state in Hades; consider how
then the souls are there, in what most bitter silence, or in what most
terrible groaning, in how great fear or agony, or in what expectation; the
unceasing pain, the soul's endless tear. But also remember the day of
resurrection and of standing before God; imagine that fearful and dreadful
judgment; bring into the midst the things stored up for those who sin, shame
in the presence of God and his Christ, of angels, archangels, authorities, and
all men; and all the punishments, the eternal fire, the unending worm,
Tartarus, the darkness, the gnashing of teeth upon all these, the fears and
the torments. Bring then also the good things stored up for the righteous,
freedom of speech with God the Father and his Christ, angels, archangels,
authorities, and all the people, a kingdom and its gifts, the joy and the
enjoyment; bring the memory of both of these to yourself; and at the judgment
of the sinners groan, weep, put on the appearance of mourning, fearing lest
you yourself also come to be in these; and at the good things stored up for
the righteous rejoice and exult and be glad; and be eager to enjoy these, and
to be made alien from those; see that you never forget these, whether you
happen to be inside in your cell, or somewhere outside, do not cast away your
mind from the memory of these; so that even through these you may flee the
filthy and harmful thoughts.

Ιʹ. Let fasting be yours as much as you are able before the Lord. This will
thoroughly cleanse your lawlessnesses and your sins, it dignifies the soul, it
sanctifies the mind, it drives away demons, it prepares God to be near. And
having eaten once in the day, do not long for a second time, lest you become
expensive, and throw your mind into turmoil. From this you will be able to
have means for the works of well-doing, and also to mortify the passions of
the body itself. And if a meeting of brothers should come to be, and there is
need, so that you also eat a second and a third time, do not be gloomy nor
downcast; rather rejoice, becoming obedient to the need; and having eaten the
second or third time, give thanks to God, that you have fulfilled a law of
love =40.1264=, and that you will have God himself as steward of our life. And
there are times when also weakness of body happens, and you must eat a second
and a third time and often; thus let the thought be loosed for you. For the
bodily labors of the way of life ought not to prevail also in weaknesses; but
to yield in some things, so that having returned more quickly to soundness,
one may again be trained in the same labors of the way of life. And concerning
abstinence from foods, the divine word did not forbid something not to eat,
but said: "Behold I have given you all things as herbs of grass.


Eat, examining nothing"; and, "The things that enter into the mouth do not
defile the man." Therefore to abstain from foods, this would be of our purpose
and a labor of soul.

ΙΑʹ. Bear gladly sleeping on the ground and vigil and all the other hardships,
looking toward the glory that is going to be revealed to you with all the
saints. For "The sufferings of the present time are not worthy," he says,
"toward the glory that is going to be revealed to us." If you are
disheartened, pray, as it is written; and pray fearfully, tremblingly,
laboriously, both soberly and watchfully. Thus one must pray, especially
because of the ill-mannered and ill-occupied, our invisible enemies who wish
to abuse us in this. Whenever these see us standing in prayer, then they
themselves also eagerly set upon us, suggesting to our intellect those things
which one must not consider or think in the time of praying, so that they may
lead away our intellect captive, and make idle and vain and unprofitable the
petition and the supplication from the prayer. For truly vain and unprofitable
the prayer and the petition and the supplication happen to be, whenever they
are not accomplished, as has been said before, in fear and in trembling, both
soberly and watchfully. Then someone approaching a human king thus
accomplishes the petition with fear and trembling and sobriety; must one not
much more stand likewise before God the Master of all, and Christ King of
those who reign and Ruler of rulers, and likewise make the petition and the
supplication? Because to God is the glory unto the ages. Amen.
