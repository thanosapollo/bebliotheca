* Letter to Carpianus Prefixed to the Gospel Canons
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Eusebius to Carpianus, beloved brother in the Lord, greetings.
Ammonius the Alexandrian, having brought in much industry and zeal as was
fitting, has left us the gospel through four, setting beside the one
according to Matthew the concordant pericopes of the remaining evangelists,
so that of necessity the sequential thread of the three was destroyed so far
as concerns the texture of the reading. But so that, with the body and
sequence of the rest also preserved throughout, you might be able to know
the proper places of each evangelist in which they were led by love of truth
to speak concerning the same things, having taken starting-points from the
work of the aforementioned man I have drawn up for you according to another
method ten canons in number, those set out below. Of these the first
contains numbers in which the four have said similar things, Matthew Mark
Luke John; the second, in which the three, Matthew Mark Luke; the third, in
which the three, Matthew Luke John; the fourth, in which the three, Matthew
Mark John; the fifth, in which the two, Matthew Luke; the sixth, in which
the two, Matthew Mark; the seventh, in which the two, Matthew John; the
eighth, in which the two, Luke Mark; the ninth, in which the two, Luke John;
the tenth, in which each of them has recorded some things peculiarly. This
then is the subject of the canons set out below, and the clear account of
them is as follows. Upon each of the four Gospels a certain number is
prefixed, beginning in parts from the first, then the second and third, and
proceeding in order throughout until the end of the books. And at each
number a notation is prefixed in vermilion, indicating in which of the ten
canons the number happens to be placed. For example if it is αʹ, it is clear
that it is in the first; if βʹ, in the second; and so on as far as the ten.
If then, having opened any one of the four Gospels you wish, you should want
to attend to some chapter you please, and to know who have said similar
things, and to find the proper places in each in which they were led
concerning the same things, taking the prefixed number of the pericope you
hold, and seeking it within in the canon which the vermilion notation has
set underneath, you will know at once from the headings at the front of the
canon how many and who have spoken about what you seek; and attending also
to the numbers of the remaining Gospels that lie beside the number you hold
in the canon, and seeking them within in the proper places of each Gospel,
you will find them saying similar things.
