* On Pentecost

Of Leontius, presbyter of Constantinople, a discourse on holy Pentecost. As
many of you as hasten toward the present festive ever-flowing grace, let us
run to Christ, the fountain of good things. A fountain concerning which the
prophet cries out to the Father of lights: For with you is the fountain of
life, in your light we shall see light; that is, in your only-begotten Son the
Holy Spirit. Well then did the prophet name the Master Christ a fountain.
There is, there is, O friends, even a worldly fountain in various places to
behold, silver-colored, and for one bending over to look from above, by the
purity of the water, at the pebbles lying in the depth on the bottom. For when
this fiery gold-bowed sun on a summer day has more broadly rested his rays
unwetted upon the water's back, at once the traveler who has drawn near to
such a fountain does not pass by without drawing from it; for having hollowed
his hands in place of a mixing-bowl and having gladly bent his whole self, he
draws of the water, heals the thirst, soothes the burning heat, refreshes the
face that has been heated through, gathers the senses that have been
dissolved, widens the soul that is straitened, smooths the heart that is being
turned to stone, forces the knees that do not wish to walk to run back again.
But this silver-colored fountain often heals thirst, yet harms the spleen, and
often soothes burning heat, yet prepares the liver to be sick, and often holds
back the soul that wishes to leap out of the body, yet, having deposited a
chill in the chest, begets a long-lasting passion. But our Master Jesus
Christ, who has been well named a fountain, not as gushing silver-colored
streams, but as pooling God-mixed teachings, does not heal some part of the
faithful and wound some part; but granting soundness in its entirety, he
bestows on those who run to him those things which he himself possesses,
having borrowed from no one. For the Master Christ does not send the faithful
far off to draw the living water, but urges them to have it in the belly of
knowledge; for you just now heard him saying: If anyone thirsts, let him come
to me and drink; he who believes in me, as the scripture said, rivers of
living water shall flow from his belly. There is, O friend, also a fountain,
mother of four-flowing rivers, in the paradise of Eden, but that one was
hollowed upon earth, and has its stream from below, and is dissolved together
with the present life; for when those being baptized are not present, the use
of the waters is superfluous. But our Master Jesus Christ, the ever-flowing
fountain, does not well up from the earth, but is distributed from the
heavens; he is not circumscribed in a part, but is glorified everywhere; he is
not corrupted together with the corruptible things, but grants incorruption to
those who are being corrupted. And four rivers only suckle the fountain of the
paradise in Eden: Geon, Phison, Tigris, Euphrates. But these are partial and
of little flow, and do not water all the earth, and in winter they flood, but
in summer they dwindle; but of the fountain of this ecclesiastical paradise
the apostolic rivers are many and boundless, even if Judas grew cold. Consider
with all of them also the four great evangelical rivers, Matthew,


Luke, Mark, John, who water every village and fatherland and city with the
divine teachings. For if star differs from star in glory, according to Paul's
teaching, how should the spiritual things not have precedence over the
fleshly? What such thing is the paradise in Eden found to have, as this
ecclesiastical meadow is known to bear? There Adam is hidden as a runaway,
here the Master Christ is worshipped as God; there Eve is tripped, here Mary
is glorified; there a tree dropping death as unripe figs, here a tree
sprouting life; there a serpent tonguing false speech, here the Holy Spirit
making theology wise; there a lawgiving that was disobeyed, here knowledge of
God confirmed; there a rose-bed, here virginity; there a white-leaved lily,
here a newly illuminated bright-souled one; there climbing vines with great
clusters, here men who love the word; there wild leafage, here holy women;
there a voiceless fountain, here loud-voiced rivers. For you just now heard
the river Euphrates, I mean the evangelist John, crying out and saying: On the
last day, the great day of the feast, Jesus stood and cried out, saying: If
anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink. What do you say, O friend, or
rather why do you delay? Christ calls, let no one hesitate; a fountain cries
out, let no one excuse himself. We all thirst, let us all draw near; as much
as we draw, so much it overflows. Let us cry out, we friends of David, the
things of David toward the ever-flowing fountain, saying: In the manner that
the deer longs for the fountains of the waters, so my soul longs toward you, O
God. Well then did the evangelist say: On the last day, the great day of the
feast. Well did he name this feast both last and great; for it is last and
great and first. Last toward the Jews; for the last evils came upon them
because of their lawlessness against Christ, and the shame of their face was
uncovered. Whence the Lord, reproaching their metropolis Jerusalem, said: A
harlot's face has come to be for you; you have become shameless toward all.
Last then toward the Jews, first toward Christians; theirs the lees, ours the
clean drinking; theirs the grape-stones, ours the under-vats. Well then did
the evangelist name this feast last and great. For what is more magnificent
than this present feast? Every Master's feast gladdens the living only, but
this feast of Pentecost not only gladdens the living, but also addresses the
dead. This feast of Pentecost both enlightens the churches and roses the
tombs; this feast of Pentecost both makes the houses fragrant and myrrhs the
sepulchres. For what is more magnificent than this feast, in which the Holy
Spirit, having made his appearing, granted the illumination of the tongues to
the apostles? Today Christianity was rooted, today Judaism withered, today
Hellenism was filled with smoke, today heresy was blocked, today the divine
dogmatizing was widened. But some will surely say: And how was heresy blocked
today, and the divine dogmatizing widened? How? From the Lord's teaching. For
today the Lord taught us more clearly, through a vivid example, one essence of
Father and Son and Holy Spirit in three different hypostases. For if no one
can separate fountain and water and river, how do the children of the heretics
dare to divide the Father from the Son and the Son from the Holy Spirit? The
things upon the earth are undivided, and the spiritual things divisible? But
some will surely say: And who is the fountain? And who the water? And who also
the river? Who is the fountain? The Father of lights. And who the water? The
only-begotten Son. And who the river? The Holy Spirit. And from where is this
clear? Take the proof not from my words, but from the divine scriptures. And
that the Father is a fountain, hear the Father himself crying out through the
prophet toward the Jews who have


the blasphemous and miry pits, thus: They have forsaken me, a fountain of
living water, and have dug for themselves broken pits, which will not be able
to hold water. And that the Son is water, hear him saying to the Samaritan
woman: If you knew, woman, the gift of God and who it is that says to you,
Give me to drink, you would have asked him and he would have given you living
water. And that the Holy Spirit is a river, you just now heard the Lord saying
concerning this: If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink; he who
believes in me, as the scripture said, rivers of living water shall flow from
his belly. Then the evangelist, adding, said, lest they should think the Lord
was making a word about a worldly river: But this he said concerning the
Spirit, which those who believed in him were about to receive. Did you see the
ever-flowing fountain of orthodoxy? Did you see the untroubled water of piety?
Did you see the unmuddied river of the knowledge of God? Well then did the
evangelist name the present feast of Pentecost great; for this one is great.
For today the apostles had the confirmation, today the fishermen are sophists,
today the unlettered are teachers, today the single-tongued are many-tongued,
today the stammerers speak Roman, today the gloomy are bright, today the clay
ones are fiery, today the hook-fishers are dogmatists, today those fishing the
lake are netting the inhabited world, today those handling fishes catechize
men, today those fleeing wild beasts trample demons, today the unstored are
well-stored, today the idle are in action, today the poor are lenders, today
the persecuted are comforted, today those going naked are kings above, today
those hiding in a corner are discoursing in the synagogue. And why do I still
speak at length? Today those mourning orphanhood cry out a father's name. Hear
with understanding. For when the Master Christ was being taken up into the
heavens before the eyes of the apostles and had promised the apostles the
river of the Holy Spirit (for it was fitting that the fountain being taken up
should promise a river), the apostles were glad and made their souls ready as
cisterns, but they did not know the time when the fire-bearing river would
come to be. For the Lord, being taken up, signified the promise of the Holy
Spirit, but he did not make known at all the time when it would come to be.
Why? Because God does not work in a determined manner, but when he wills, then
he also appears. The apostles then, after the Lord's ascension, were waiting,
bearing the hope of the Holy Spirit and binding themselves together and
gathering the confidence toward one another. But when they saw one and a
second and a third day, that the gift of the promise was not being brought to
fulfillment, the apostles, moved by human reasonings, began, comparing with
one another, to say: What shall we do, men, brothers? Already we complete this
tenth day and we see nothing of the things said by the teacher come to
fulfillment. What is this strange and paradoxical thing? Shall we take hold of
the former fishing, or shall we still wait a little for the promise? We keep
this tenth day idle. What is the strange thing? What is the paradoxical?
Elisha the disciple of Elijah, having received the sheepskin at once, worked
wonders, the disciple of the disciple, and we are idle? What is the
paradoxical? Was Elijah perhaps more effective than our teacher? But we do not
dare to say it; for we saw face to face on the mountain of the transfiguration
how not only Elijah but also Moses worshipped our teacher as God. What is the
strange thing? What is the paradoxical? Elisha the plowman at once worked
wonders, having received a goad-pierced hide of an irrational animal (for what
was it fitting for a legal workman but to inherit the sheepskin?), and we who
are not disciples of a disciple but


apostles of God complete a tenth day idle? While the apostles were comparing
these things among themselves and still looking out from afar for the promise
of the hope, behold, suddenly the gates of the heavens are opened, and the
armies of archangelic powers were crying out, and the sea of the air was being
troubled with fear, and the cymbals of the clouds were sending forth a sound
of royal crashes, and all the earthly place was being shaken by the
earthquakes as a reed under wind, and the boundless hollow of the sea was
being covered with a fiery linen. And Luke, the writer of the apostolic Acts,
bears witness to my word, saying, as you just now heard: When the day of
Pentecost was being fulfilled, they were all with one accord in one place, the
apostles. And there came suddenly from heaven a sound as of a violent wind
being borne along, and it filled the whole house where they were sitting, and
there appeared to them tongues distributing themselves as of fire, and it sat
upon each one of them, and they began to speak with other tongues as the
Spirit was giving them to utter. As he was giving, not as he was being
commanded. Did you see the greatness of the feast? Did you see the gift of the
Holy Spirit? Did you see the shame of the Spirit-fighting heretics? They feast
Pentecost and they shoot the Spirit. Who forges tongues, if not the
clay-molder of the tongues? Who turns a great mouth on the lathe, if not the
one who prepared the she-ass to speak with joints of speech? Who makes the
unlettered into sophists, if not the Holy Spirit alone? But those of the
opposite side will say at once: And from where is this clear, that the Holy
Spirit gives the wisdom and the knowledge of the tongues? From where? Hear the
Lord, O heretic, saying to his own disciples from many years past: Do not be
anxious when they bring you before rulers and governors how or what you shall
speak, for it is not you who are speaking, but the Spirit of my Father who
speaks in you. O the paradoxical things. The Holy Spirit came down in fire,
sat as fire upon each one of the apostles, and being fire he did not burn the
apostles, but enlightened them. Why? Because it was not burning, but
enlightening, or rather both burning and enlightening, burning the
blasphemers, enlightening the pious. For just as the worldly and material fire
burns thorns, melts wax, dissolves lead, dries clay, brightens silver,
purifies gold, lights a house, so also the immaterial fire, the Holy Spirit,
wipes out sins, withers a grasping heart, wipes out a deceitful man, purifies
a soul that turns, brightens a faithful man, lights an ecclesiastical house.
Otherwise, the Holy Spirit came down in fire, that we may know clearly that
the Holy Spirit is of the divine essence. For the Lord our God himself is
fire, according to the saying that declares: Our God is a consuming fire. And
wherever you seek God showing a divine sign and furnishing a mystery, you will
find him doing it in fire. For thus also, having led the sons of Israel out of
the land of Egypt, he set beside them a pillar of fire, not burning but
guiding. Thus also God, making a first appearing to Moses, sent forth his own
voice through a flame of fire of a bush, not that he might hear but that he
might teach. Thus also God, appearing to Daniel, showed himself sitting on a
throne of fire, not that he might harm the prophet but that he might show
everywhere in what things God is. But some will surely say that these are the
marks of the old covenant. Show us, he says, that also in the new covenant God
works wonders in fire; for those are indications of the old. Hear, friend: if
you are ignorant, learn; but if you know, be reminded.


You surely bear in memory, O friend, that God, guiding the magi before all,
guided them through a star; it is surely clear to everyone that the star is
fire. Likewise the Lord named his own body, that of the holy table, a coal of
fire. And when was this? Hear Isaiah crying out from many years past: One of
the seraphim was sent to me, and in his hand he had a coal which he took with
the tongs from the altar and it touched my lips and he said to me: Behold,
this has touched your lips and shall take away your lawlessness and shall
thoroughly cleanse your sins. And what coal forgives sin, if not the Master's
communion? In the same way the Lord also named the baptism of our resurrection
fire. And from where is this clear? Hear John the Baptist crying out: He who
comes after me is mightier than I, whose sandals I am not worthy to bear; he
shall baptize you in Holy Spirit and fire. And the most capital of all, that
the Holy Spirit came down in fire. The more intelligent of the Jews, having
perceived this paradoxical energy of the Holy Spirit, and that the apostles,
who before this were single-speaking, without an interpreter, were furnishing
the teachings of piety to each one of those coming to them from varied
fatherlands in the local tongue, as you just now heard, being struck with
amazement, the more intelligent of the Jews began to say to themselves:
Behold, are not all these who are speaking Galileans? And how do we hear, each
in our own dialect in which we were born, Parthians and Medes and Elamites and
those dwelling in Mesopotamia, both Judea and Cappadocia, Pontus and Asia,
both Phrygia and Pamphylia, Egypt and the parts of Libya toward Cyrene, and
the Romans sojourning, both Jews and proselytes, Cretans and Arabs, we hear
them speaking in our tongues the great things of God? The Jews were struck
with amazement, seeing the apostles speaking in varied tongues; whence, being
struck, they said to themselves: Behold, are not all these who are speaking
Galileans, poor men and private men? Are they not unlettered and net-weavers?
Did we not often buy fishes from these? From where is this knowledge to them?
Did these perhaps net tongues instead of fishes? But we see them before this
single-tongued. From where do they put forth the various speeches of the
inhabited store as from a purse of the larynx? From where is this knowledge to
them? Now we have learned that their teacher is truly God; now we have learned
that the ancient things have passed away, behold, all things have become new.
Peter speaks Roman, which Moses was not able to do. Did we not hear him
saying: I am thin-voiced and slow of tongue? And he declined the divine
command of the sending because of the thorniness of the voice, and he did not
receive a correction, but remained having what nature granted. Let us leave
Moses and bless these, or rather let us glorify the God who works paradoxes in
them, who for the common benefit of all reshapes the single-formed
tongue-organ of the fishermen toward the need of the one who draws near. What
such thing was granted to Abraham? These have overshot all; they spit on
money, they despise glory, they heal without pay, they fast throughout, they
are made bright as those who feast, they command the dead, they put diseases
to flight and wither demons, being impressed they rejoice, being struck they
turn also the other cheek, they go without staff and wallet and double tunic,
they do not even wish to receive gold and silver and bronze by hearing. Let us
run to these who speak the great things of God, let us turn away from the rest
who babble and profit nothing. But these things the more intelligent of the
Jews were saying, but the lawless of the Jews again from


the contrary, moved by envy, these too began to contradict, as you just now
heard, that they are filled with new wine. What is: they are filled with new
wine? Instead of: they are drunk exceedingly and for this reason utter strange
things. The blessed Peter, having heard these things and being no longer able
to bear it (for the torch of the Holy Spirit was already burning him within),
standing in the midst, as you just now heard, began to say: Men Jews and all
who dwell in Jerusalem, let this be known to you and give ear to my words. For
these are not drunk, as you suppose; for it is the third hour of the day. Well
did the blessed Peter call the third hour as witness of sobriety. And what is
the third hour? The Holy Spirit. First hour the Father, as having awarded the
law. Second hour the Son, as having granted the grace. Third hour the Holy
Spirit, as having worked the varied tongues of the apostles. Peter then says:
Men Jews and all who dwell in Jerusalem, let this be known to you, and give
ear to my words. For these are not drunk, as you suppose. Let no one consider
the gift of God a drunkenness; either smell, or hear. Draw nearer, and learn
accurately. Do they smell of wine, or breathe grace? Do they stammer, or
orate? Are they moved as leaves by drunkenness, or have they been fixed as
pillars in sobriety? Do they nod with wine-soaking, or do they teach with the
knowledge of God? Do you consider the matter drunkenness, yes, Jews?
Drunkenness binds the tongue, twists the mouth, makes the eyes livid, sides
along the walls, makes one walk crooked and double. One is stripped by
drunkenness and does not perceive it; he is laughed at by those by whom he
ought to be honored. Are the apostles drunk, yes, Jews? Did Noah, who
carpentered the ark, get drunk, or we who preach the cross? Are the apostles
drunk, yes, Jews? Your ancestor Lot got drunk, who slept the sleep of
daughter-marriage; not we, the heralds of chastity? You have said well, O
Jews, that we are drunk. Not from wine as you understand, but from the divine
grace; for concerning this drunkenness the prophet was crying out from many
years past: They shall be drunk from the fatness of your house, and you shall
give them to drink of the torrent of your delight, because with you is the
fountain of life. Attend with understanding. For it is time now to seal the
word and to dismiss you more briefly today; for I think you are to address the
tombs and to honor the memory of those who have fallen asleep before by the
visitation. But nevertheless, O friend, hasten to honor and not to insult; do
not spit on him as one dead; for his Master lives and makes known to the one
who died before what you are doing. You go away today to rose the one who died
before; you do well, you are worthy of praises. But nevertheless rose him with
good-doing, not with wine-soaking; crown the one who died before with piety,
not with meat-eating; urge him on with the offering, not with the misfortune.
If in the present you drink wine upon the tomb, or eat meat, what have you
benefited the one who died before? Give an obol to the poor on his account,
and that one is gladdened; incense the belly of the hungry with bread, and
that one is sweetened. O the human indifference. We love the things that harm,
we hate the things that save. What reasonable thing is it today to spread
couches upon the grave, and to gather a public drinking-party and to make a
broader laughter as at a wedding? A wedding is one thing and a tomb another.
Why do they leap today upon the monument as at a worldly birthday? A worldly
birthday is one thing and a mournful funeral another. A woman often goes away
today to the tomb to rose her own husband; and she does not rose so much as
she distresses. For having taken along the smuggled-in friend, a lamp was
broken when a hand was raised, over certain dignitaries. And toward this the
preacher says: Let no one be grieved by the


thing done, nor let the stain of the garment spot the soul. For this too is an
operation of the Holy Spirit; for it is water and oil and fire. But such a
woman, leaping upon the husband's grave and provoking the husband even after
death, how shall she weep for the husband while smiling at the friend? Let us
take thought for good order, O friends, let us honor the visitation of the
Holy Spirit, let us hear the scripture, and let us obey the Lord. You surely
heard yesterday, O friend, what the Lord, having come to the monument of
Lazarus, did. Do the like yourself also as a Christian. And what did the Lord,
having come, do? Did he weep in measure, or drink wine without measure? Did he
groan in the spirit, having prayed, or did he leap and work a fight? It is
surely clear to everyone that he both prayed and wept and groaned in the
spirit and troubled himself, furnishing himself to us as a pattern of
sympathy. Let us then follow behind the Lord and hear the scripture saying:
Weep over a dead man, because light has failed. It said Weep, not Feast. Feast
in church, Weep at the tomb, and this in measure, as the Lord did Lazarus.
Hear the Ecclesiast saying: A time to weep and a time to laugh. Why do they
stamp their feet today upon the grave, and move satanic-voiced cymbals, and
sing theatrical songs? Did they conquer an enemy, or overthrow a tyrant, that,
having set up satanic choruses, they thus parade through the marketplace? O
the unfitting things. What unfitting things do the tomb-drunkards not do
today? Today they architect the tombs, today they promise to build the
monuments, today babbling they lavish: If I must sell my hairs, I will not
leave the tomb unhaired. I do not want the neighbor to travel through here,
nor widowers to pass by. And as soon as they stand away from the monument, at
once they also strip off the memory. For drunkenness is a destroyer of best
memory, an inventor of unseemly reasonings, a murderer of divine teachings.
Let us then turn aside from drunkenness, let us show the Jews to be
slanderers, who just now have said that the apostles are filled with new wine,
let us carry through the Lord's teaching that says, as we just now heard: If
anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink; he who believes in me, as the
scripture said, rivers of living water shall flow from his belly. Hear with
understanding. He taught concerning living water, not concerning wine that
makes drunk. But some will surely say (for contradiction is not lacking to a
drunkard): What then? Is the wine bad, since you slander it? And if the wine
is bad, why did our Master Christ change the water into wine? Thus, he says,
the wine is most excellent. Hear with understanding. We do not insult the
wine, for it is a gift of God, but we abhor drunkenness, for it is a work of
the devil. Nevertheless besides the things said hear this also; for already
the word looks about for silence. The Lord did change the water into wine. But
nevertheless he did this in Cana of Galilee at the wedding, not in Bethany of
Judea over Lazarus who had died. The Lord changed the water into wine, having
done this over those marrying, not over those mourning Lazarus. Where there is
a wedding, he transferred water into wine; where there is a dead man, he
furnished a tear. To God who fills all things let us send up glory, now and
always and unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
