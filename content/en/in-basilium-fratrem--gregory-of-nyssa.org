3 Just as then among those who went before, time being second lessened the
saints in nothing toward the glory according to God, so also now in the
account of virtue let the privileges of time be silent. For such a thing, as
we said, has the proof of God's providence for men. For he who knows all
things before their generation, as the prophet says, and perceiving the
devil's evil that goes through together with human generations, prepares a
physician suitable and corresponding to the sickness in each generation, so
that he might not look on the disease of men untreated, prevailing over the
race through a dearth of those who set it right.
4 For this reason, when once the Chaldean philosophy prevailed, who defined
the cause of beings in a certain motion of the stars, and did not think some
productive power of beings to lie above the things that appear: then he shows
forth Abraham, who using such education as a step, sought out the one thought
through the things seen. And he became a way of faith for those after him,
faith toward the God who truly is, himself having led the way toward this by
leaving the ancestral deceit and the kinship of the senses toward the
appearing creation. Then when the Egyptians had invented a certain demonic and
magical wisdom from the suggestion, I think, of the one who variously sinks
souls through deceit, he showed forth Moses, who would abolish the Egyptian
deceit by the excess of wisdom. And he who knows the Scripture altogether
recognizes the account, how, when the sorcerers displayed their own deceits
against the divine signs, Moses prevailed by force, having abolished all
Egyptian strength through the alliance from above. And you understand this
both through the other things and by the riddle of the rods.

And after these things, many generations later, when that Ahab, the woman's

in contempt of the care of the body, with a weathered face and shadowed by the

a goatskin as it is more seemly to have covered, and with the rest enduring
toward the air and turning to nothing toward the irregularity from heat and

concerning the idols with the divine fire that came to be about the sacrifice.
6 Then after that one another that one appears later by more years, who in the
spirit and power of Elijah came to dwell in life through Zechariah and
Elizabeth, and assembling the whole nation in the wilderness through
proclamation, and the gore upon the blood of the prophets, and the stains of
every kind, and the various


bonds of sin, with which, after the manner of sandals, the whole people at
that time was bound; all such things he both loosed by the proclamation of
repentance, and washed out with the sprinkling water at the Jordan, and in
nothing showed himself less than those who had gone before in virtue in the
things according to God.
7 And what? After this one was Paul hindered from running up to the highest
point of progress according to God, being second in time? Did he not at once
become a lover of the divine beauty that flashed upon his eyes, together with
casting off the scales from his eyes which were a symbol of the covering about
the heart, which, lying about the seeing faculty of the Jews' souls, prepares
them to be blind toward the truth? Did he not, when he had laid aside in the
mystical washing the filth of ignorance and deceit, at once get changed in
nature toward the more divine, and as if having stripped off this thick and
fleshy wrapping, sojourn in the heavenly sanctuaries themselves, weighed down
in nothing by the trailing of the body, and come to be within the divine
planting of paradise, there being perfected in the unspeakable mystagogy by
the truth, and from there taking the power of the word unto the obedience of
faith among all the nations? Whence he becomes father of nearly the whole
inhabited world, bringing to light through spiritual birth-pangs those being
formed through him according to Christ unto piety.
8 If then upon the other saints the order according to time lessened nothing
of the progress according to God, the grace cooperating equally with each
toward perfection, we reasonably dare to say that the man of God in our
generation also, the great vessel of the truth, Basil, is to be numbered with
those famous saints, the sequence of time hindering nothing, neither that high
desire of his toward God, nor the divine grace toward the perfecting of the
soul; so that the aim of the divine economy might not be harmed in anything,
or anything of the alliance contributed by him toward the mystery be lessened
because of the cause of time. And no one at all is ignorant of the aim of the
showing-forth of our teacher at this time.
9 For since the madness of men concerning the idols was quenched by the 9
proclamation of Christ, and all the vain objects of worship were already in
ruins and disappearance, the proclamation of piety having gone through nearly
the whole inhabited world, so that the one prevailing over human deceit was
shut out from every side, being driven from the inhabited world by the name of
Christ; and the inventor of evil, being wise toward evil, was not at a loss
for a wicked device, so as again to make the human race subject to himself
through deceit; but under the pretext of Christianity he brought back idolatry
in secret, having persuaded by his own sophisms those who look toward him not
to depart from the creation, but to worship this and to reverence this, and to
think the thing made to be God, named with the title of the Son. And if the
creation is from things that are not and has been alienated from the divine
essence according to its own nature, to make no account of this, but having
put the name of Christ upon the creation, to worship this, to serve this, to
have in this the hopes of salvation, to await the judgment from this.
10 And the apostate, having fallen wholly upon men sufficient to contain all
his evil, I mean Arius and Aetius, Eunomius, Eudoxius, and besides these many
others also, through whom he brought back again, as has been said, the
idolatry already failing, by the name of Christianity, and the disease of
those who serve the creation besides the one who created prevailed over men;
so that the deceit was also strengthened by the alliance of the kings then,
and all the ruling powers fought for such a disease. And when nearly all men
toward the prevailing

him, as a lamp that had failed. And like some beacon appearing above the
Church to those wandering by night upon the sea, he turned all toward the
straight road, grappling with prefects, mixing with generals, speaking freely
to kings, crying out in the churches, winning those standing far off

11 What then does his coming to dwell in life after other saints diminish of
his good repute according to God, so that because of this the feast upon this
man seems to be less than the feasts upon the saints? For consider, if it
seems good, comparing his life toward some one of the saints who went before.
Paul loved God; for this indeed is the head of good things, I mean that of
love; from which is all faith and all hope, and what is expected from
endurance, and the unchanging in every good, and the having more with every
spiritual gift. But let us examine how great was the measure of the love
toward God in Paul; you will say altogether that it was from the whole heart,
and from the whole soul, and from the whole mind. For the law gave this as the
highest bound of the love toward God. Therefore he who has dedicated all his
heart and soul and mind to God, and is disposed with inclination toward
nothing else of the things pursued according to this life, comes to be in the
highest bound of love. If then someone is able to show the life of the teacher
as having its inclination about some of the things pursued according to this
world, such as about wealth or power or desire of empty glory (for it is
likely not even worth saying the more slavish pleasures upon him), whatever of
these is found to be pursued, one must say that he is lessened according to
that in the measure of the love toward God, his desiring disposition having
flowed from God toward the material things. But if he was an enemy and foe of
all these and such things, first driving out from his own life all the
passionate disposition about these, then also cleansing the common life both
by the teaching word and by the example according to himself: it would be
clear that he had in himself that measure of the love toward God of which
nature did not have room for more. For he who loves God from the whole heart
and soul and mind, how would he be stretched beyond toward the more of the
measure of love, which has no place? Therefore if we have learned one bound of
perfect love, the one dedicated to God from the whole heart; and Paul and
Basil loved, being dedicated to God in the whole heart, someone having dared
to say that there is one measure of love in both would not miss the truth. But
indeed both the Apostle says that love is greater than all good things, and
the high Gospel votes with him. The Apostle, saying it more to be honored than
prophecy and


knowledge, more firmly founded than faith, and more lasting than hope, and
always remaining in the same, without which every pursuit toward good is
useless. And the Lord, hanging every law and all the prophetic mysteries upon
such a gift, himself also declares that love has first place of all. If then
in the surpassing and comprehensive of the achievements he is not left behind
the great Paul, then also in all the others, of which love leads the way, and
as many as spring from this, he will altogether be shown as having nothing
less. For just as he who shares human nature is in all the properties of the
nature; so also he who has accomplished the perfect of love in himself has all
the kinds of good things that are contemplated together with this, with the
prototype of the achievements. For whether it is faith that saves, or we are
saved through hope, or we await the grace through endurance, love believes all
things and hopes all things and endures all things, as the Apostle says. And
all the other things, that we may not spend time saying each, become a shoot
of the root according to love, as many as are pursued by the account of
virtue, so that he who has this is in want of none of the others. But indeed
the great Basil had this; he was therefore left behind of none of the good
things through it. And if he had all things, he was lessened in nothing at
all.
12 But someone will altogether say that that one saw a third heaven and was
caught up into paradise and heard unspeakable words which it is not lawful for
a man to speak. But neither did that one receive such a grace openly through
this flesh. For he does not hide the doubt, saying: Whether in the body I do
not know, or outside the body I do not know; God knows. And concerning this
someone having dared might say that in the body he saw no such thing, but by
the bodiless and intelligible contemplation there is nothing of these that he
left unseen. And a witness of these is the word from him, both that which he
went through upon himself and that which he left in writings. That one, having
gone round in a circle from Jerusalem to Illyricum, proclaimed the evangelical
word to all those in between; and the word of this one and the proclamation
took hold of nearly the whole inhabited world, itself being pursued equally
with the words of Paul by all. And let the rest be set aside, through which
the life of this one is brought together toward that of that one, such as:
that one was crucified to the world, and the world to this one. That one put
the body to death, and this one also perfected power in weakness. Christ was
the living for both, and death a like gain for each, and the departure toward
the Master more to be honored than the wandering life.
13 Or does it seem good to set John also over against the teacher? But as for
that one having first place among those born of women and having more than a
prophet, the divine voice bearing witness, it would be madness and impiety
together to bring another over against such a life through comparison; but
even to come behind so great and such a one has the proof of the highest
blessedness. And let us consider the argument thus. John was neither in soft
garments, nor was he a reed shaken in the winds. And he embraced the
wilderness before the inhabited places and again sojourned among the
inhabited. Let no one then find fault with the argument, if the truth will
also bear this witness to our teacher, that in these things he is not left
behind the great John. Who does not know how he counted a soft and broken diet
of life as an enemy, hunting in everything the enduring and manly before the
pleasant, warmed by the sun, exposed to frost, training the body with fasts
and self-control, dwelling in the cities as in deserts, harmed in nothing
toward virtue from the encounter, and making the deserts cities? For neither
did the living with the many turn him aside from the precise and sure way of
life. Nor, if he went toward himself to the remote place, was he able to be
stripped of those present for benefit; so that upon this one also according to
the


likeness of the Baptist the wilderness became a city, crowded by those
streaming in. And that he was not a reed, easily bent toward opposite
opinions, the unchanging in all the things judged by him through life shows.
Poverty was pleasing from the beginning; the judgment became some unshaken
rock; he desired to draw near to God through purity: this desire was a
mountain, not a reed; for he never bent under the contrary winds of the
trials. And the firmness of the love toward God it was for the Apostle alone
to show through his own words, in that neither life, nor death, neither things
present, nor things to come, nor any other creation was able to thrust his
heart away from the love toward God.
14 Thus also in all the things judged by him according to virtue he was in no
way reed-like and unstable in mind, but through all things the life had the
unchanging in the good things. John speaks freely toward Herod, and this one
toward Valens. And let us compare toward one another the dignities of these
men. The one had been allotted the power of some part of Palestine by the vote
of the Romans; and the bound of this one's rule was nearly the whole course of
the sun, stretching from the borders of Persia to the Britons and the ends of
the Ocean. And the aim of the freedom of speech toward Herod was, not to act
lawlessly against some woman but to chasten the desire upon himself as
forbidden by the law. And what was the teacher's freedom of speech toward
Valens? To leave the faith inviolable and unstained, whose lawlessness became
a pollution of the whole inhabited world. Let then the just examiner of things
set the power against the power, and the aim of this freedom of speech toward
that freedom of speech. For there the pollution was bounded in the body of
Herod; but here the lawlessness of the faith was an injustice of all human
nature. That one remains in the freedom of speech until death; and for this
one exile becomes the end of the freedom of speech, the king having condemned
this in place of the death-bringing vote. But also John, having died, was
believed to live; and for Basil the vote of exile is dissolved by the enemies
themselves, he having been softened in nothing through the threat concerning
the stand.
15 Would we dare also to go up in the argument upon the high Elijah, and to
show our teacher made like to that one's grace by the life according to
himself? But let no one demand of human nature the driving upon the flaming
chariot and the guiding of the fiery horses and the removal toward what lies
above of the upper lot. For neither did that one, remaining in the bounds of
nature, get saved impassible in the fire, changing by divine power the heavy
and earthy toward the upward-tending and light, nor that he made his own word
as some key of the supply from heaven, both letting go when he wished and
shutting again at will when he thought it better to do this. But also that he
endured for a long time without food, preserving his power in the same with
one food of that hidden barley-cake until forty days, let this too be set
aside as greater than according to a man. For imitation of the things above
nature is without resource for human nature. And let there be silent with
these both that small jar of meal and the cruse of oil; each sufficing toward
the need in the supply of food, and stretching the grace together with the
whole time of the famine unto three years and six months. For the wonders of
the energy from above have a power peculiar upon the works. And no one would
reasonably reckon such wonder-workings to human nature.
16 What then are the things of our teacher that have communion toward the
things contemplated in the prophet? Zeal of faith, enmity against those who
set it aside, love toward God, desire of the one who truly is toward none of
the material things


flowing off, a life examined through all things, a withered life, a gaze
stretched with the tension of the soul, unstudied gravity; a silence more
active than speech, care about the things hoped for, contempt of the things
seen, equal honor toward every appearing thing, whether someone of the
over-swollen in dignity should happen, or one of the humble and cast-off
should come forward. These and such things are those in which the life of the
teacher imitates the wonders of Elijah. And if someone puts forward the
fasting of the forty days, we also will put over against it the teacher's
scant eating through the whole of life. For the scant eating somehow neighbors
the without food, and especially when the one comes to be in a little time,
and the other lasts toward the whole life. Besides, there that barley
hidden-cake also held the prophet's tension, having altogether something such
in itself, by which the power of him who brought this food was preserved. And
a proof that it was not some one of the same race who, having made bread, set
the food beside him, but he was filled with an angelic preparation. Whence the
power coming to be in the body through that food was both full and unspent.
But here, the custom having been innovated in nothing, the measure of food was
reason, furnishing to the body not so much as nature wished, but so much as
the law of self-control commanded.
17 And the priesthood of the teacher imitates the riddles of the priesthood of
the prophet, drawing the heavenly fire toward the sacred rites through
trebling in the word of the faith. And we have been taught in many places by
the Scripture that the power of the Holy Spirit is called fire. And the
teacher neither loosed nor brought on a drought of earth. And there the great
prophet, having struck the earth with the scourge of rainlessness, himself
also becomes physician of the wound, granting them a relief from the healing
rival to the pain of the scourge. And if one must say some wonder of our
Elijah also toward this: when such a misfortune was once threatened by divine
will and the whole winter season had run by dry and no hope of fruits was
appearing, then the teacher, having fallen before God, stopped the fear at the
threat, propitiating the Divine with litanies and loosing through prayers the
gloom of the rainlessness. But also of the consolation of the famine which the
great Elijah showed upon one widow, our life also has some likeness upon the
teacher. For when a hard famine was once oppressing both the city itself in
which he happened to be dwelling and all the country subject to the city,
having sold his own possessions and exchanged the money into foods, when it
was scarce even for those strongly prepared to set a table for themselves,
that one lasted through the whole time of the famine, feeding both those
streaming together from every side and the youth of the whole people of the
city, so as to set forth the participation of this philanthropy equally even
to the children of the Jews.
18 And it differs altogether nothing whether one fulfill the divine
commandment through a cruse, or through some other means. For the consolation
of those in need does not seek the whence, but looks toward what comes to be.
And if Elijah became upward-tending through fire, this too is wonderful and
the wonder is beyond speech. Yet the other kind of the upward bearing is not
to be cast away either, when someone through the high way of life is housed
from earth into heaven, having made the virtues a chariot through the Spirit.
Which indeed everyone examining the things concerning him with good judgment
will agree to have been accomplished by the teacher.
19 Must one then also dare upon Samuel in the argument? But having yielded the
first places to the prophet in all the other things, we will show toward two
of the things said concerning him also the things concerning our teacher as
having them fittingly; the birth of both was God-given. For as the mother that
one, so also the father this one from a request


divine begot. And when he was once held by a death-bringing sickness still in
the new of age, the father saw the Lord appearing to him according to the
vision in sleep, the one in the Gospel who granted the child to the royal
official, and saying those things toward this one which the Lord said toward
that one: that Go, your son lives. Whose faith he also himself having imitated
carried off the same fruit upon the faith, having received the salvation of
the son from the philanthropy of the Lord. This one thing then we set down to
the wonders of Samuel; and another, that the same form of sacred rite was
pursued by the two. For both brought peaceful sacrifices to God, performing
the rite for the destruction of the enemies, the one upon the dissolution of
the heresies, and the other doing this against the foreigners.
20 And Moses the great is set forth as a common example to all who look toward
virtue; and no one would err who makes the virtue of the lawgiver the aim of
his own life. Therefore it would altogether be without envy to show our
teacher, in the things in which he was able, imitating the lawgiver upon the
life. In what things then was the imitation? Some ruling woman of the
Egyptians, having adopted Moses, educates him in the native education, he not
having departed from the maternal breast until it was necessary to nurse the
first age with such food. The truth bears this witness to the teacher also.
For being brought up by the outside wisdom he always held to the breast of the
Church, growing and being made sturdy in soul by the teachings from there.
After these things Moses denied the sophistical kinship of the falsely-named
mother. Nor did this one remain long being thought that which he was ashamed
of. For having shaken off all the glory from the outside words, as that one
the kingship, he deserted toward the humble life, just as Moses also made the
Hebrews more to be honored than the treasures of the Egyptians. And nature
working its own upon each (for the flesh of each desires against the spirit),
neither was this one outside the fight of the Egyptian reasoning, which it
brought against the one being pure; but allying with the better, he made dead
the one badly rising against the Hebrew. And the Hebrew was the reasoning
cleansed and unstained. For he who by putting to death the members upon the
earth allies with the soul imitates the valor of Moses which he worked against
the Egyptian. And it is fitting to run past the many things of the history, so
that he may not bring a great crowd upon the hearing, the wishing to set out
with precision all the things which the account holds upon Moses and in which
the likeness of the teacher toward the lawgiver came to be. 21 Moses left
Egypt after the death of the Egyptian and spent much time in the middle, being
solitary upon himself. This one also left the tumults in the city and these
material dinning-abouts, and was upon the remote place philosophizing toward
God. That one was shone upon with the light through the bush. We have
something akin of this vision to say upon this one also: that, it being night,
there comes to be for him an illumination of light as he was praying according
to the house; and that light was something immaterial, lighting the dwelling
by divine power, kindled by no material thing. Moses saves the people, having
delivered them from the tyrant. This people of our lawgiver bears witness to
the equal things, whom through the priesthood he led toward the promise of
God. And what need to say each, how many this one also led through the water,
for how many he torch-led the pillar of fire through the word, how many he
saved with the cloud of the Spirit, how many he fed with the heavenly food,
how he imitated the rock to which once the water was opened by the wood, that
is, to which once the type of the cross touched the mouth, how he gave drink
to those thirsting of that water, imitating the abysses by the multitude of
the inflow, what sort of tent of witness he constructed even bodily in the
suburb, having made the poor in body to be poor in spirit through the good
teaching, so that poverty became for them blessed, of the


true kingdom procuring the grace? And he created by the word a true tent
toward the dwelling of God, the soul of each, and constructing some pillars in
it; I mean the reasonings, the pillars that underprop the toilsome of virtue;
and washbasins likewise toward washing out the stains of the soul, washing off
the defilement with the water from the eyes. How many lampstands he set in the
soul of each, lighting the hidden things by the word. What sort of censers and
altars of the prayers he constructed of pure and unalloyed gold, that is, of a
true and pure disposition, of which the heavy lead of vainglory dimmed the
brightness of the things coming to be in nothing. What need to say the
mystical ark, what sort he crafted for each, putting in the soul the tablets
of the covenant written with the divine finger? And I say these things looking
toward that, that he made the heart of each an ark containing of the spiritual
mysteries, having the law written, being written down through the works by the
energy of the Spirit, for thus the finger of God is interpreted; in which also
the rod of the priesthood always flowered its own fruit, sprouting up through
the participation of the holy things; and the jar was not emptied of the
manna. For then the vessel of the soul becomes empty of the heavenly food,
whenever sin, having fallen in, prevents the inflow of the manna. And manna is
heavenly bread.
22 What need to say how with precision he both put on the priestly robe
himself and adorned the others by the example according to himself, always
bearing the ornament upon the breast, whose name is oracle and manifestation
and truth? All which I pass by for the more laborious to fit to the teacher
according to the tropic meaning; through which he both was such himself and
shared the ornament with the others. Often we have known him also to have come
to be within the gloom, where God was. For what was unseeable to the others,
the mystagogy of the Spirit made graspable to that one, so as to seem to be
within the compass of the gloom in which the account concerning God is hidden
away. Often he set himself against the Amalekites, having used prayer as a
weapon. When he raised his hands the true Jesus overcame the enemy. He
dissolved the sorceries of many sorcerers according to that Balaam; who, not
hearing the true word, but being persuaded by the ass-like teaching of the
demons, had the mouth inactive toward evil, the prayer of the teacher
transferring the curse into blessing. And these things are said by us in a
running-over and in brevity; and he who is not ignorant of the life of the
Saint will fit each to the truth of the things that came to be, how many who
had prepared plots against some through pharmacy and sorcery had the
wickedness without effect, not being permitted by the faith of the teacher to
bring the evil forward into perfection. But having left all the things through
the middle I will mention the end of both;
23 Each left life; and neither of them left to life a memorial of his own
relation concerning the flesh. For neither is a tomb of Moses found, nor was
this one heaped over with some material abundance; but together he passed by
life and all things through which the life of men has its constitution passed
by with him, so that no material memorial of the man was found from the
abundance of those left behind, burying his reputation toward the better; as
upon Moses the history saying this, that his tomb was not found until this
day. If then the Great Basil has been shown forth to us from the argument as
such, so as not to be far from the saints, being set beside each of the great
ones through the life, well does the sequence of the feasts now lead us upon
this one, the present feast.


24 But indeed it would be fitting to bring on the memory through the things
that have been pleasing to that one. Therefore it must be considered how,
having arranged the feast, we may keep festival pleasingly to the saint. Will
someone seek upon him the display-loving and boastful manner of encomia?
Fatherland and race, and the rearing from parents, and the pursuits of life
each, with which he grew and came to his prime, and through which he had the
illustrious and much-looked-at, having become among men? But the greatness of
the good things contemplated concerning him thrusts aside all this boastful
high-speaking, because the thing pursued turns out toward the contrary, there
not being such a power in words as will lay hold of the greatness of the
wonders according to worth. So that the wonder may not be brought down
together with the small-nature of the speech, and some lessening of the
opinion now concerning him in each come to be through those attempting to
praise, it would be better rather to increase the wonder in ourselves by
silence, than to diminish the praise through the speech. For what such thing
could someone say and make him more august? Would that one accept the
so-called nobility through flesh and blood to be taken up in the part of
praises? And who does not know the great one's disposition concerning the
flesh, how he was disposed contrary toward it, always wrapping it as some
runaway slave with the bonds from the reasonings, and through the highest
hardship and self-control, scourging and twisting that scourge-worthy slave
the body, as some inexorable master giving no relaxation to the prisoner? To
make august then the one so disposed concerning the flesh through bodily
nobility would be of the most unreasonable. For how will he now be honored by
this of which he was ashamed through the life? And likewise the memory of the
fatherland is cast out together with the argument concerning race. For he who
has set himself above the whole world and as if being crowded in all the
perceptible nature of the elements, so as not even to bear the heaven lying
above himself; but to rush with the soul into what is beyond and, peering over
the perceptible compass of the world, always to enter upon the intelligible
things and to travel aloft together with the divine powers, hindered in
nothing by the bodily trailing toward the journey of the mind; when would he
accept to name himself from some part of the earth and to gather praises for
himself through the good nature of the place? For it would be an outrage and a
lessening of the true praises, if, virtue being set forward for contemplation,
water, and leaves, and a clod, and such things should be wondered at. Besides,
everything which does not become a possession of choice, even if it be as good
as possible, brings no fair speech to that one with whom it was brought
together according to the same. Therefore let fatherland and race and every
such thing be silent, which is brought together with those who happen upon it
by automatic chance. And one would reasonably remember upon him that
fatherland and nobility which comes to those who have been eager through
choice.
25 What then is the nobility of Basil? And what is the fatherland? His race is
the kinship toward the Divine; and the fatherland, virtue. For he who has
received God, as the Gospel says, has authority to become a child of God. And
what more noble than the kinship toward God would someone seek? And he who is
in virtue and farms this and makes his way from this altogether makes this his
own fatherland, in which he dwells. Self-control was his hearth, wisdom the
possession, and justice and truth and purity the brilliant and much-looked-at
beauties of the buildings; dwelling in which he exulted more than those who
pride themselves in the marble and gold-spangled houses. If someone should
praise him from such a fatherland and make him august from such a race, he
will both speak true things and make the praises from the things that have
been pleasing to him; but let those who wish bear witness of earth and blood
and flesh and riches and powers and the illustriousness in these to the
friends of the world.
26 If then the encomium fitting for that one is uncontainable for our speech,
such pursuits would be to be declined and the


arts of the encomia to be struck out. How then shall we bring on the memory
upon him, someone will perhaps say, if we do not do this with encomia? How
will the law of the Scripture be fulfilled, which says that the memory of the
righteous must come to be with encomia, if to bring on the speech through the
true praises is impossible, and through the more common is insolent? But it is
perhaps not to be at a loss for some device, so that he may not be left
altogether unrewarded of our praises. What then is the device? Who does not
know that every speech divorced from the works is vain in itself and without
subsistence? And the nature of the works shows what is said in subsistence and
truth. Therefore the praise fulfilled through the works would be more to be
honored than the speech. And what is this? That through the memory of that one
our life come to be better than the custom. For just as upon a ring's bezel,
some most beautiful form appearing in the engraving, the wax stamped with the
seal has transferred into itself the beauty lying in the engraving, having
taken up the whole character of the seal with its own impressions; and no one
would so bring the well-disposed beauty of the carving into a description
through speech, as showing the beauty formed upon the wax; in the same way, if
one should make the virtue of the teacher august with bare speech, and another
should beautify his own life through the imitation toward that one, the praise
fulfilled through the life would be more active than the high speech.
27 Thus then, brothers, let us also, having imitated the self-controlled by
self-control in the things we do, praise the virtue according to worth, and
all the other things likewise: let the wonder of the wise be fulfilled in the
participation of wisdom; the praise of poverty in our also becoming without
possession of the material abundance; let the contempt of this world not be
said only, that there is some praiseworthy and glorious thing; but let the
life be a witness of the contempt of the things pursued according to the
world. Do not say only that that one was dedicated to God, but dedicate
yourself also to God; nor only that the hoped-for rest was his only
possession, but treasure up this wealth for yourself also as that one did; for
it is possible. That one transferred the citizenship from earth into heaven,
transfer it also. He deposited his own wealth in the inviolable treasuries of
heaven, imitate the teacher through these things also. For a disciple will be
perfected as his teacher. For even in the remaining pursuits, he who has been
a disciple to a physician, or a geometer, or a rhetor, will become an
untrustworthy praiser of the teacher's art, if he should wonder at the science
of the guide in speech, but show upon himself nothing worthy of the wonder.
For someone will say toward him: how do you call your teacher a physician,
yourself being unhealed? Or how do you call yourself a disciple of the
geometer, being inexperienced of geometry? But if someone shows in himself the
art in which he was a disciple, he makes the one who guided the art august by
the science according to himself. Thus let us also who boast Basil as teacher
show the discipleship by the life, becoming that which made him both famous
and great to God and men, in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory and
the power, unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
