* Narrative of the Image of Edessa
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Narrative of Constantine, emperor of the Romans in Christ the eternal King,
gathered from various histories, concerning the not-made-by-hands divine image
of Jesus Christ our God that was sent to Abgar, and how it was transferred
from Edessa to this all-blessed and queen of cities, Constantinople. 1 Not he
alone, then, was incomprehensible, the God Word co-eternal with the Father,
but also the greater part nearly, or even all, of his works is wrapped in the
same gloom of incomprehensibility; not only all that he established when he
created this whole, but also all that, having converse with us in the
assumption of our dough for the sake of economy, he worked according to that
first and single power of his Godhead. And it is wholly necessary that the one
who does not fail to know himself and who knows that he does not know the
things above him should not boast into the measureless, nor step emptily in
ignorance, and either contend to know all things or that those things which he
himself has not grasped do not even exist. 2 Therefore also concerning the
impress of this theandric form, which was stamped without writing by the
supernatural will of the one acting upon the fabric that received it, and was
then sent to Abgar for healing, but now has been transferred from Edessa to
this ruling city of cities by the economy of God wholly for her salvation and
guard, so that she may not seem lacking in any of the good things, being bound
in all things to hold first place over all, I think the pious and just hearer
and spectator ought to demand to learn accurately the history of the
particulars and to wish to receive unfalsified knowledge of the ancient
account, but the cause of how from a moist vapor, apart from colors and the
painter's art, the appearance of the face was formed in the linen fabric, and
how that which is of matter so easily corruptible did not receive corruption
by time, and all else that the one who applies himself to things as if by
nature is fond of investigating busily, knowing to yield to the unattainable
of the wisdom of God; since if anyone contends to grasp all things accurately
with the mind, being thrust into total ignorance and falling away into an
abyss of incomprehensibility he will be in danger of being damaged in the
vital things, the great ones, lest he seem to concede the small. 3 As many of
you, then, as are orthodox concerning the faith and more fervent concerning
zeal have gathered here, come, hear, and I will narrate to you the things
which, having busied myself with each under the due test and having gone
through the search for the truth not without labor, I was able to establish
accurately, both from those who have written history and from those who came
from there to us, who said that they were preserved among them in memory as
through secret things. 4 When our Lord and God and Saviour Jesus Christ had
sojourned to us for the restoration of our race, there was according to the
prophet's voice a multitude of peace upon the earth, and the many-ruled was
scattered, as if the whole inhabited world had been girt by one belt of the
Romans' empire and was arrayed under one commander, and for this reason all
mixings of all with all took place without fear, and men seemed not to inhabit
the earth in divided portions, but as being the possession of one master, as
also the whole is of one creator, having bent the neck as slaves to the first
they were at peace with one another. 5 For this reason also Abgar, then
toparch of Edessa, was friend and acquaintance of the one ruling Egypt, and
the servants of each went back and forth to one another. Whence also at that
time, in which our Lord and God, fulfilling the Father's will, was setting
forth the saving teaching to men and through the supernatural and strange
miracles was turning men to the faith concerning him, it happened that one of
Abgar's servants, named Ananias, going toward Egypt through Palestine, fell in
with and saw from afar Christ drawing the crowds out from the words of error
and


performing the strange things of the miracles. 6 When therefore he had
finished the journey to Egypt and, having treated of the things entrusted to
him, was returning, since he knew that his lord was also tyrannized by a
chronic arthritis and consumed by a black leprosy and making the disease a
double misfortune, or rather a manifold one; who both was held by the pains
from the joints and was distressed by the evils of the leprosy, and there was
also the shame of the disfigurement, because of which he was almost not even
to be seen by men, and he not only spent most of his time bedridden, but even
hid himself in shame from the friends who came to visit: for this reason again
in returning he was eager to learn more accurately about the same things, so
that he might have them firmly to report to his lord, that perhaps that one
too might be counted worthy of the healing through him. He found therefore the
Lord again at the same things, raising the dead, granting sight to the blind,
showing the lame whole, and strengthening all who were sick in any way. 7 When
therefore he was confirmed and knew these things to be accomplished openly by
the Lord, returning he made them known to Abgar and taught him at greater
length both what he had seen and what he had heard. Whence as "having
trafficked in the by-work as greater than the work" and as a messenger to him
of favorable gospels he was counted worthy of the fitting reception and was
recognized as one of the most well-disposed. And since the one who is ill
always makes the promise of healing as a thing to be seized, and hope fawning
about the man persuades him to set out eagerly after the hunt of what has been
reported, Abgar too rose up to summon by writing the one said to be able to
heal such things, and at once wrote to the Lord this letter carried about
everywhere, containing thus: 8 Abgar, toparch of Edessa, to Jesus the good
Saviour who has appeared, a physician in the city of Jerusalem, greeting. The
things concerning you and your healings have been heard by me, as being done
by you without drugs and plants. As the report is, you make the blind to look
up, the lame to walk, you cleanse lepers, and you drive out unclean spirits
and demons, and you heal those tortured in long disease, and you raise the
dead. And having heard all these things concerning you, I set down in mind the
one or the other of the two, either that you are God and having come down from
heaven you do these things, or that you are son of God doing these things. For
this reason therefore having written I asked you to take the trouble and come
to me and to heal the suffering which I have. For I have also heard that the
Jews mutter against you and wish to do you harm. And I have a city very small
and honorable, which will suffice for both of us to dwell in peace in it. 9
Since therefore Ananias furnished clear proofs of goodwill toward his lord and
happened to be experienced in the road and knew the painter's art, through him
he sent out such a letter to Jesus, charging him that if he should not be able
to persuade Christ by the writing to come to him, at least having copied
accurately the likeness of his form he should bring it to him, so that as in a
shadow at least he might be taught, not by hearing only but also by sight, of
what sort is the creator of these great wonders. 10 And having reached Judea
the one sent found Christ in the open air discoursing to the people that had
streamed together and working the extraordinary things of the miracles. And
because of the multitude of those who had come, one for one need and another
for another, Ananias not being able to approach Jesus, going away to a certain
rock a little raised from the earth not far from the Lord's sojourn sat down
and, as the Saviour was visible to him, set apart from the multitude and
overtopping the many, at once he fixed his eyes on him and his hand to the
paper and copied the likeness of what appeared. 11 Christ therefore knew these
things in the spirit and having summoned Thomas: "Go," he says, "to this place
and bring to me the man sitting upon the rock and copying my form, bringing
also the letter which he came having from home, in order that he may fulfill
the command of the one who sent


him." Thomas therefore having gone and having recognized Ananias from the
things he heard as finding him doing them, brought him to Jesus. And before
receiving the letter from him, Christ told him both the cause of his presence
toward him and the force of the letter, then having received this and gone
through it he set against it another letter to Abgar, having thus upon the
wording: 12 Blessed are you, Abgar, having believed in me not having seen me.
For it is written concerning me that those who have seen me not believe in me,
and that those who have not seen me, they themselves should believe and live.
But concerning what you wrote to me, to come to you, it is necessary that I
fulfill all things for which I was sent here, and after the fulfilling to be
taken up to the Father who sent me. And when I have been taken up, I will send
you one of my disciples, who will heal your suffering and grant eternal life
and peace to you and those with you, and will do for your city what is
sufficient toward none of the enemies prevailing against it. 13 Christ
therefore having given such a letter to Ananias, since he knew him also to be
anxious and taking thought about bringing the other command of his lord to an
end, that is, to carry the likeness of his appearance to that one, the Saviour
having washed his face with water, then having wiped the vapor from this in
the towel given to him, economically caused his own character to be stamped in
it divinely and beyond reason, and having given this to Ananias he commanded
him to give it to Abgar, so that he might have it as a consolation of the
longing and of the disease. 14 When therefore Ananias returning with these
things reached the fortress of Hierapolis, which in the speech of the Saracens
is called Membich, and in that of the Syrians Mabouk, having lodged outside
such a town, a heap of newly made tiles lying there, here Ananias hid that
sacred rag. And about midnight a great fire appeared circling such a place so
that all the things round about within the town seemed to be burned up with
fire, and already fearing for themselves they went out and searched concerning
the fire that was seen. And Ananias being found there they held him as the
doer of the daring deed and searched concerning the matter, and they inquired
who he was and where he was going and from where. And as Ananias was at a loss
at the strangeness of the accusation, he for the time made clear from where he
was and from where he comes and what he carries, and he showed that he had
laid down among the tiles the thing carried, from which the flame also seemed
to be kindled, and at once those men, wishing to know the truth of the things
said and having searched the place, found not only what had been set there by
Ananias, but also in one of the neighboring tiles another impress of the
divine image, the unwritten form having been transferred strangely and beyond
mind from the fabric onto the potsherd, which having seen and having become
full of amazement and of astonishment together, both because of this and
because no fire was found burning anywhere, but the flame seemed to be sent
out from the brightness in the form, they kept among themselves the tile that
had wiped off into itself the divine impress as some sacred heirloom and a
treasure of great price, having guessed the divine energy concerning it from
what had been seen, but fearing to detain the original and its servant they
sent them to Abgar. And now the form in the tile is preserved and honored
among the inhabitants of such a town, the unwritten of the unwritten form and
the not-made-by-hand of the not-made-by-hand. 15 And Ananias, having finished
the journey set before him, made clear to his lord the things in between,
giving also the saving tokens which he carried. 16 And this is the account
said by the many concerning this unwritten form of our Saviour in the fabric.
But a certain other account also is said concerning this, neither fleeing the
persuasive nor lacking good witnesses. Therefore I will set this out too, so
that no one may suppose that in being ignorant of this I strengthen the other.
And


wholly it is no wonder that in so great a time the history often wanders. For
concerning the vital point of the subject all agree alike, and they confess
that the form in the fabric was stamped strangely from the Lord's face. But
they differ about some one of the things of the affair, that is the time,
which does no harm to the truth, whether it happened earlier or later. And the
other of the accounts has thus. 17 When Christ was about, they say, to come to
the voluntary passion, when showing the human weakness he was seen agonizing
and praying, when also the word of the gospel indicates that his sweats
dropped as clots of blood, then, it says, taking from one of the disciples
this piece of fabric now seen he wiped the drops of the sweats in it and at
once there was stamped this visible impress of that God-like appearance; which
having committed to Thomas, after his ascent into the heavens he commanded to
send it through Thaddaeus to Abgar, fulfilling the promise through the
letters. 18 After therefore our Lord Jesus Christ was taken up into the
heavens, Thomas having given to Thaddaeus the not-written-by-hand formation of
the Lord's face sent it out to Abgar. Thaddaeus therefore having reached
Edessa remained first with one of the Jews there; this man was named Tobias.
And wishing before words to make himself known to Abgar from the works, the
disciple of Christ healed the sick of the city by the invocation of Christ
alone. Whence quickly, the report having been spread, which is wont to happen
in such things, for the strange things of affairs have many who report
concerning them, the hearing concerning the sojourn of the apostle of Christ
reached also Abgar through one of his magnates called Abdu. Having reasoned
therefore at once from the hope lurking in him that this was that one whom
Jesus had promised through the writing to send to him, and having learned more
completely the things concerning Thaddaeus from him, he ordered him to be
brought to him. 19 Tobias therefore having come made these things known to the
apostle, and that one, having said that he had been sent to him in power, next
came to Abgar. And when he was about to appear face to face to him, having as
it were set up such a likeness upon his own forehead, he thus went in to
Abgar. And he, having seen him approaching from afar, seemed to see a light
better than sight raying out leaping from his face, which the likeness lying
upon him sent forth. Whence, struck by the excess of the flashing brightness
and having as it were come into forgetfulness of the symptoms about him and of
the long-time paresis of the limbs, he sprang up all at once from the bed and
was forced to run to the meeting with the palsied limbs, having suffered the
same passion in another manner as those who saw the flashing form on the mount
Tabor. 20 Having therefore taken such a likeness from the apostle and having
reverently put it about his head and his eyes and his lips, and not having
deprived the other parts of the body either of such a touching, he knew at
once all the limbs being wonderfully strengthened and receiving the change
toward the better, and the leprosy being cleansed out and fleeing away, even
if still a small remnant of it was left on the forehead. 21 Having therefore
been taught then more clearly by the apostle the word of the truth and
concerning the strange miracles of Christ, both the divine passions and burial
and the resurrection from the dead and the taking up into the heavens, and
having confessed Christ as true God, he inquired concerning the impress of the
form in the linen, since setting himself more accurately to it he recognized
it as having its constitution through material colors, and he was astonished
at the power in it, by which he had strangely risen from the bed and was
numbered with the healthy. To these things Thaddaeus made clear the time of
the agony and the uncolored formation from the sweats and the


account of the coming of the Lord toward him and the other things which the
foregoing of the history made clear. 22 When therefore both from these things
and from the laying on of the hands of Thaddaeus in the name of Jesus Christ
the things that pained withdrew and the palsied were as it were tightened
together, and the disfigurement was scattered, and all things ran toward
health, Abgar, held by amazement from every side, said: "In truth you are a
genuine disciple of Jesus the son of God who heals without drugs and plants.
And I am bound by so much affection and faith concerning him, that if I did
not fear the superiority of the power of the Romans, who do not endure those
under them to take arms against one another, I would perhaps have moved arms
against the Jews who crucified the Lord and would have set them beside me and
enslaved them. But now since I have been taught that his passion also was
voluntary and I am persuaded that the senseless would not have prevailed
against him if he had not willed it, I busy myself about nothing further. But
I ask also to be counted worthy of the divine baptism and with all my house to
be made near and dedicated to the master Christ." 23 The apostle of the Lord
therefore, having first accomplished many miracles and having healed all from
their diseases, among whom was also the first who brought the report
concerning him to Abgar, whom he freed from a suffering of the feet, brought
Abgar to the divine font and having accomplished the things appointed over
him, baptized him and his wife and the children and all those in his house;
and he came out from this divine water of cleansing wholly clean and healthy,
the small remnant of the leprosy left on his forehead having also vanished all
at once. 24 From here, honoring and reverencing in every way such a likeness
of the Lord's form, the toparch added this also to the other things: from the
old notable founders and settlers of Edessa a statue of one of the notable
Hellenic gods had been set up before the public gate of the city, to which it
was necessary that everyone wishing to come within the town should do
reverence and render certain appointed prayers and thus take to the roads and
streets in the city. 25 This therefore Abgar then took down and gave over to
disappearance, and in the place of that one's standing he set up this
not-made-by-hands image of our Lord Jesus Christ, having glued it upon a board
and adorned it through the gold now appearing, having inscribed in the gold
these words: "Christ the God, the one who hopes in you does not fail." And he
decreed that everyone about to pass through that gate, instead of that old
stele, useless and unprofitable, should render the fitting reverence and the
due worship and honor to the much-wondered wonder-working image of Christ and
thus enter into the city of Edessa. 26 And such a thing, as a kind of
first-mark and dedication of the man's piety, was preserved until the sojourn
in life both of Abgar himself and of his son, who was established successor of
the kingship and of the paternal piety. 27 But the son of these and grandson
became successor of the ancestral and grandfatherly rule, yet he did not also
become heir of the piety. But he kicked away the piety, so to speak, and
deserted to the demons and the idols. Therefore also, as if rendering the
recompense to the demons, he wished, since his grandfather had given that
idolatrous stele over to disappearance, to bring the same condemnation also
upon the image of the Lord. But the crafty one did not succeed in the hunt. 28
For the bishop of the place, having known this beforehand, set the care that
was possible. And since the place in which the image lay preserved the shape
of a cylindrical hemisphere, having kindled a wick before the image and having
set a tile upon it, then having blocked the space from outside with plaster
and baked bricks, he evened the wall to a level surface; and in the not being
seen of the envied form that impious one stood off from the undertaking. And
the tile for this reason I think was appointed by the priest to be set before
the image, so that no rotting


from the mildew from the building and from the moisture from the plaster might
come to be in the fabric, the receptacle of the image, and it receive the harm
from time. 29 Time therefore flowed much in between, and both the setting up
of this sacred image and the hiding flowed away from the memory of men. When
therefore in his own times Chosroes the king of the Persians, sacking the
cities of Asia, reached also Edessa and having planted a palisade before it
moved every device and fitted every engine suitable toward the taking of a
city, and he carpentered all things toward discharges of missiles, shakings
down of walls, smashings of gates; the Edessenes having come into so great a
danger both themselves devised the things possible toward counter-array, and
they also sent embassies concerning help to the generals of the Romans. But
Ilion, then leading the Roman armies, and himself being hard pressed by the
enemies, was not able to send alliance to those in Edessa, but through letters
he encouraged them, reminding them of the Lord's letter and of the unlying
decision in it, through which the city is both said and believed to be kept
impregnable. 30 And the Persians, together with the open attack, also devised
the things that plot unseen, and having begun to dig from far off they
contrived to come inside the city through tunnels. And when they were as it
were underwater swimmers inside the wall under the earth, from such a cause
the plot became manifest to those within: it happened that in that part inside
the wall a smith dwelt, whose bronze vessels hanging through the house
produced a sound, the Persians cutting under the earth and carrying out the
soil. The people of the city therefore having come into helplessness and the
last despair flee to God and with pain of heart and tears they sought him out.
31 There appears therefore by night to the bishop, and this was Eulalios, a
certain woman well-ordered and seemly, better than according to a human,
suggesting to him to take the not-made-by-hands image of Christ and to make a
litany with it; and the Lord would wholly show his wonders. But the bishop
said he was wholly ignorant whether such an image exists at all either among
them or among any others. Then the one appearing in female form says to him
that above the gate of the city in this place in this manner such an image has
been hidden. 32 At dawn therefore the bishop, trusting the vividness of the
vision, having come with a litany to the place and having searched, found this
divine image unharmed and the wick in so many years not quenched, and upon the
tile set for guard before the lamp another likeness of the likeness stamped,
which also happens to be preserved in Edessa until now. 33 Having therefore
taken in his hands this divine image of the God-man Christ and having come
upon a better hope, he comes to that place at which the Persians digging had
been detected from the sound of the bronzes. And when those of the city had
begun to dig from within, when they came near to one another, having dropped
oil from that lamp upon the fire prepared by them against the enemies and
having sent it against the Persians in the tunnel they destroyed all, and
having been delivered from the plot from there they brought the like trial
against the engines outside the wall and burned these all at once and consumed
many of the enemies in them. And already having taken heart they had also made
frequent discharges of stones from the walls, by which it happened that the
camp-leader of the enemy army fell and many others with him; 34 and not only
this, but also the pyre kindled from outside by the Persians against those
within, which a boundless matter of olives and of many other trees cut down
was feeding, the power of the allied divine image made to come against
themselves; for as Eulalios from above, having spread this out with his own
palms upon the wall, was going through the city, suddenly when he came to this
place a violent wind having been raised against


those kindling such a pyre turned the flame and pursued these and burned them
as the Chaldeans of old. 35 These things are not an unwitnessed account
compacted by us for pleasure of hearing or for deceit, but three patriarchs
together, Job of Alexandria, Christopher of Antioch, and Basil of Jerusalem,
wrote them down and made known that these things are so, writing to Theophilus
the emperor who had insulted the sacred images, when through many things
proving the sacred and venerable of the divine images they treated also of
this, and it is possible for the one who wishes to read through that
many-lined letter and to learn concerning this. 36 But also if anyone has
labor-lovingly read the ecclesiastical history of Evagrius, he has wholly
known of what sort he says concerning this sacred image in the fourth book.
For he records that among the other things Chosroes did this also, wishing to
convict openly as false the account noisily spoken among Christians concerning
such a city, that it would be impregnable. For having gathered in a short time
by command a great store of woods and a boundless multitude by the
many-handedness of the army and having fixed these in two walls in a circle of
the city, then having thrown soil into the middle and building another wall
face to face he became higher than the walls of Edessa and was about as from a
commanding place to discharge the missiles against those who ran the risk on
behalf of the city, as good as already. 37 Which the Edessenes, seeing as a
mountain being raised opposite the walls, through which they expected the
enemies to mount the town as from the level, being in helplessness, yet
nevertheless counter-devising the things possible, themselves also attempted
to dig a trench before that newly-constructed wall, so that if they should be
able to set fire to the cross-timbers before the mound they might draw the
soil down toward the trench and thus that great wall, as if molded from a
dream, might the sooner be brought down and flow apart. 38 And when the
digging had been finished, but they missed the mark when putting fire to the
woods, the fire not being able, because the soil was packed together within
and the woods still green, to lay hold of the matter without some void, having
brought this sacred image into that newly-made trench and having sanctified
water from it and having sprinkled this upon the pyre and the woods they
accomplished that the fire became active; and the divine power joining in with
the faith of those who had done it, the water became as oil to the fire and
kindled the flame and consumed all that it met. Then therefore the king of the
Persians, having despaired of the taking of the city and having learned from
where they had the help, came to terms and having made peaceful treaties
returned home to his own. 39 But this man too was about, not long after, to
obtain a benefaction from this sacred image, the benefactress of his enemies
and destroyer of his own. For his little daughter, having been held by a
demonic spirit and having stood out of the natural state, cried continually,
being worked through it: unless the not-made-by-hands image should come from
Edessa, the inhabitant of this one would not go out from here. Which the king
having heard and having reckoned up the things at the siege, for not even from
him did the strange sudden strength and the daring of the Edessenes escape,
writes at once both to the one presiding over the city and to the metropolitan
Eulalios and to the common body of the city that the divine and all-mighty
image be sent to him the sooner, adding also the cause, the misfortune of the
daughter, and in every way both asking and forcing that he not fail of the
request. 40 But they, both suspecting the faithless of the Persian character
and suspecting that the Persian wished by craft to take away their strength,
and taking thought not to give up the protectress and benefactress, but also
not wishing to dissolve the peace for the sake of such a pretext, counsel a
counsel prudent and profitable to themselves. For having copied a painted
image equal in all things and like as far as possible of the unwritten


and having likened it toward the resemblance as far as they could, they send
it to the one who asked. 41 And when those conveying the image came within the
borders of Persia, at once through the king's daughter the demon cried out to
go out the sooner and to change the dwelling because of the power of the one
coming, only if the likeness that had been summoned should turn back and not
approach the palaces nor the city of the Persians, and toward this he asked
many things of the king and supplicated. And the king having promised, the
demon having gone out from the girl and the royal child having come into a
healthy state, Chosroes, either fulfilling the request of the demon who had
asked and making the promise firm, or fearing the power of the one coming
because of the cheap and abominable of his works, having sent commanded such
an image to return toward the city from which it had gone out, and adding
gifts from himself toward those who had sent it out. 42 This treasure of great
price, then, was among the Edessenes, the unemptying treasury, the image both
original and unwritten, honored always toward them and guarding them in
return. 43 And since toward this ruling city of cities the most good and most
beautiful things from everywhere have flowed together; and it was a divine
will that this sacred image too be stored up here with the other good things,
Romanos, the one holding the rule of the Romans, makes it a zeal through
himself to be well-off in this and to enrich the ruling city. 44 And having
sent at various times to Edessa he asked that this be sent out to him together
with the self-written letter of the Lord, and he promised to give them in
exchange Saracens up to a double hundred in number and two thousands of
stamped silver toward one myriad. But those of Edessa said it was not
profitable to them to exchange the guard and sentinel of their own city for
silver and mortal men. 45 And as he pressed and asked again, and they many
times put off the request, at last in the six thousand four hundred
fifty-second year of the generation of the world (ξφ. 21 24) the amir of
Edessa sent, asking that the emperor pledge himself by a written security
through a gold seal having the confirmation, that the armies of the Romans not
come against these four cities in a hostile way; I mean Rohan, which the
barbarian speech names Edessa, Haran, Sarotzi and Samosata, nor to plunder
their fields or to enslave those inhabiting them, and that the two hundred
Saracens of the same race be released to him together with the quantity of
silver previously promised, and that he give back in return the image sought
and the letter of Christ. 46 And the emperor, having yielded to all the things
put forward by the desire of such a good, and having given the things asked,
sent out Abramios the God-beloved bishop of Samosata, sojourning here at that
time, for the taking up of the divine image and of the letter of Christ our
God. 47 And both the one who sent and the one serving, watching lest he be
outwitted concerning the giving back and that instead of the unwritten and
true the one copied then because of the Persian circumstance be given to him,
having sought both of these together with another honored in the church of the
Nestorians, itself also long ago, as it seems, taken over from the original,
received them for confirmation; which were given back again, only the proper
and true being held. But this later. 48 Then a standing was raised among the
faithful in Edessa and a frequent tumult held the city, they not letting the
most precious of the things among them and the phylacteries of the city that
bore them be taken away, until the leader of the Saracens, having persuaded
some, having forced others, and having frightened others also with threats of

reached the Euphrates. And again a tumult not less than any of the former is
raised, that unless some sign should be shown from God, not even if anything
should happen would they give forth the things holding their security
together. A sign therefore is given to them, being unbelieving and making
trial. For the ship, with which it was set before them to cross the Euphrates,
still moored toward the parts of Syria, when only the bishops carrying the
divine image and the letter entered into it, the surge still holding the
factious, suddenly without rowers, without the one steering or pulling, put in
to the land opposite, steered by the divine will alone, which filled all who
happened upon it and saw with amazement and astonishment and persuaded them
willingly to yield the sending out. 51 From here the servants of the things
carried reach Samosata; and they were the high priest of Samosata and the high
priest of Edessa and his first-presbyter and certain others of the more devout
Christians, with whom also the servant of the amir was, who was named from
Rome. Here having spent time for certain days, many miracles having happened
there, they took to the road. 52 And again boundless miracles were
accomplished on the road by the sacred image and the letter of Christ. For the
blind looked up unexpectedly and the lame were shown whole, and the long-time
bedridden leaped, and those having dry hands were made healthy, and to speak
in short every disease and infirmity fled, and those being sanctified
glorified God and hymned his wonders. 53 Having therefore already accomplished
the greater part of the road they reach also the monastery of the all-holy
Theotokos, which is named the things of Eusebios, happening to be in the
so-called theme of the Optimatoi. And in the nave of such a house of care the
chest hiding the wonder-working image is laid up in a manner befitting saints,
and many having come forward from a sincere disposition were healed from their
own diseases. Where also a certain man came forward troubled by a demon, whom
the evil spirit, using as an instrument and crying out through him many of the
things that come to praise of the image and of the letter, since also of old:
"we know you who you are, the holy one of Israel," those of the like portion
with him said toward the Lord; at last he also as it were prophesied these
things: "Receive," saying, "Constantinople, glory and joy, and you,
Constantine Porphyrogenitus, your kingship." And these things having been said
the man was healed and was released at once from the attack of the demon. 54
And many have stood as witnesses of these words. For the emperor having sent
nearly the first of the council in office for honor and meeting of the
longed-for, and many of those from the bodyguard order having gone out
together with these, it happened that magistri and patricians together with
those from the lower order were both the hearers and the witnesses. 55 And
since the things said received the outcome the sooner, it is worth being at a
loss from where the foreknowledge accrued to the demon. For they are not
believed to have this from home, having slipped from the divine glory and
having become darkness instead of light. Or is it plain that just as the
divine power used Balaam as a servant of the then foretelling, and at other
times often others not worthy of the affair, by some wise and well-devised
economy wholly, so also now the power in the divine


image used the demon and through him made known beforehand the things about to
come out after a little. But of this having happened thus it is perhaps not
untimely to have made mention in between; but we will walk upon the things
next of the narration. 56 On the fifteenth of the month August, the emperors
customarily keeping the feast of the translation of the ever-virgin and Mother
of God in her all-august nave at Blachernae, about late afternoon the servants
of these precious things reached there, and the ark having within it the image
and the letter was set in the upper oratory of such a divine nave. And the
emperors having come forward reverently greeted this from outside, having done
reverence, then having conveyed this with honor and a bodyguard and frequent
lamps to the imperial trireme they reached the palace with it and in the
divine nave there, which has been named Pharos, perhaps because it has been
adorned as a kind of bright garment, they laid this up in it especially. 57
And on the coming of the days, which was the sixteenth of the month, having
again made the greeting and the reverence with shame and piety, and the
priests and the young emperors having taken it from there; for the old one was
left as house-keeper through weakness, with psalms and hymns and abundant
light having set it again through the descent toward the sea into the imperial
trireme, making the rowing almost in contact with the city, in order that it
might in a certain manner gird the town through its journey in the sea, they
put in outside the wall of the city toward the west. 58 Where, having gone out
from the ship, going on foot both the emperors and all those of the senate
council and the one ruling the sacred things with the whole fullness of the
church, with the fitting bodyguard, as another ark, or rather even above this,
they escorted the guarding vessel of the most holy and precious things, and
having gone through the things outside the wall as far as the Golden Gate. 59
Then having come from there within the town, with raised psalmodies and hymns
and spiritual songs and a boundless light of lamps, joining the all-people
escort they accomplished the journey through the middle of the city, believing
that the city would partake of sanctification and of better strength through
this and would be kept unharmed and impregnable unto the age. 60 And of the
idle crowd running together toward the spectacle and of the people being moved
and streaming from many places as long waves, a certain man palsied in the
feet and weak from a long time, leaning on his own servants, rose up toward
seeing the divine image going through, and at the same time as the sight
having been strangely healed and having known the ankles of his feet
strengthened he ran forward walking with his own feet and greeted the coffin
of the image and magnified God, narrating the miracle upon him. Whom all those
present having seen and having heard his words sent up glory to the God over
all who always does wonders and extraordinary things. 61 And so great an
outpouring of tears from pleasure and embassy and petition toward God and
thanksgiving came to be from the whole city as the divine image and the
venerable letter went through the middle of the town, as it is not possible to
show by word, the word being worsted by the sight. For the things of affairs
that have excess it is good rather to see than to hear, since the word is
accustomed to come behind the affairs. 62 The leaders of the festival
therefore, having reached the marketplace before the Augusteion and having
turned a little from the straight road toward the left, reach the sacred
precinct named for the divine Wisdom of God and set down the precious image
and the letter within the inner parts of the mercy-seat. 63 And here, the
whole fullness of the church having done reverence and having honored the
things that were fitting, those accomplishing the procession went out again
from there with the sacred freight and having reached the palaces of the
emperors, in the hall named Golden, upon the imperial throne, on which they
are accustomed to transact concerning the greatest things, they establish the
divine image for a time, having believed not unreasonably that the


imperial seat would be wholly sanctified and that it would reasonably impart
justice together with goodness to those sitting upon it. 64 And an extended
petition having customarily come to be, after the completion of this the
divine image was again taken up from here and was consecrated and dedicated in
the aforementioned nave of the Pharos in the right part toward the east, for
glory of the faithful, for guard of emperors, and for security of the whole
city and of the constitution of the Christians. 65 But O divine likeness of
the likeness of the unchanging Father, O character of the character of the
paternal hypostasis, O august and all-honored seal of the archetypal beauty of
Christ our God, for as to one living I faithfully converse with you, save and
guard always the one who piously and gently is emperor of us and who
splendidly feasts the remembrance of your sojourn, whom by your presence you
raised upon the grandfatherly and paternal throne; keep his offspring
indestructible for succession of the race and of the scepters. Award to the
polity a peaceful state. Keep this queen of cities unbesieged, and grant that
we, being well-pleasing to your archetype Christ our God, be received in his
heavenly kingdom, glorifying and hymning him, because to him belongs glory and
the worship unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
