Against the Heretics.
=60.745=
Knowing you to be dancers of piety and soldiers, I now set before you,
as before dancers, the sweet-voiced music of the moral teachings, and
now we sound in your hearing the austerity of dogma, like the voice of a
trumpet, to men under arms. For the form of piety is composed of both:
the moral teachings filling the order of the body, and the yoking of the
dogmas in the ordered dignity of the soul. For just as, when the body is
severed from the soul, the composition of the members is useless even if
it is praised, so also the beauty of moral virtue is dead if it is not
given life by the power of the dogmas. For what profit is there in
manners trained to self-control, when the self-controlled man does not
know the Judge of self-control? What gain is there in almsgiving, when
the Judge of almsgiving, being set aside, is angry? What benefit will
there be for soldiers who display much working of good things, yet are
enemies to the king? You have made every advantage of virtue unprofitable
for those who have not employed it, if the rightness of the dogmas is
not rooted in as well. For the fruits must come forth from a root, and
the sinews of the virtues must take their start from the head. Hanging
from this, and weaving the members of our deeds together with it, we
complete the whole body of piety, binding fast Christ as the head of the
body. For he himself, it says, is the head of the body, the Church; whom
heretics have disdained to have as head. It would indeed be worthy of
prayer that those called Christians should not rage in such ways against
Christ. But now we must even give them thanks for their madness, because
by their raving they make us keep watch, because they goad us toward a
longed-for battle. For I love a battle better than peace, I love a war
more blessed than love, I love a mind that parades itself, toward the
Greeks taking pride from Christ, yet toward us also stirring strife,
because we exalt Christ. For such was Arius: whenever he speaks to
Greeks, he speaks solemnly of Christ; whenever he converses with us, he
slanders Christ. Such is Eunomius: whenever he blames Hellenism, he
denounces the making of gods; but whenever he speaks toward us, he sets
up the making of gods. For it is a form of making gods to say that God
the Word is a fashioned God and a created thing, made to be worshipped
as a creature. What are you doing, you blinded heretic? You bring in
what Christ, as he built, tore down, and what you intend, he pulled up:
the deifying of creatures, creature-worship, planting things still
greater. Do not harm the nobility of Christianity; do not make it kin to
Hellenism. For worship of a creature, whether the thing worshipped is a
greater creature or a lesser, is brother to Greek worship. Paul, at any
rate, does not accuse the Greeks because they worshipped in a small way,
but because they worshipped the creation at all rather than the Creator.
For to worship a creature is equal in force both in small and in great;
rather, the heretic is even harsher toward the divine nature than the
Greeks. For the one who addresses the creation as God is not so insolent
toward the Divine as the one who names God a creature. For the one who
deifies creation seems to clothe a slave in the royal purple; but the
one who introduces God as a creature forces the Divine to live as a
private citizen. The Greek, indeed, is one who suspects the Divine, yet
serves it in every way; but Arius does not revere, he cannot bear the
praise of God. He does not refuse to revere and worship Christ, yet he
is pained when Christ is honored with the highest honors. If someone
says uncreated, they are at once angry; if under no master, they are
displeased; if sharer in the paternal dignity, they are provoked as at a
greater blow; if independent giver of gifts, they gnash their teeth at
this too: names of Christians, yet a mind that hates Christ.

Consider, Arius, what authority you set aside; hear him commanding
others in different ways as a master: I will give you, he says, the keys
of the kingdom of heaven; and, Today you shall be with me in paradise.
You, leaving the tax-booth, follow me. You, do not fear, but speak,
because I am with you. I say to you, leper, be cleansed. You, run, wash
in Siloam, and look =60.746= up. You, paralytic, your sins are forgiven
you. You, sea, be silent, be muzzled. You, come after me, and I will
make you fishers of men. You, destroy this temple, and in three days I
will raise it. You, make disciples of all the nations. And behold, I am
with you all the days until the end of the age. He said none of these
things as: The Father will give you the keys of the kingdom of heaven;
the Father will lead you into paradise; you, leper, be cleansed by the
will of the Father; you, from the Father, command the sea: Be silent, be
muzzled; you come after me, and the Father will make you fishers of men.
But the sayings are from a sovereign authority. And if anywhere the
voices of the authority are more moderate, as I thank you, Father, that
you have heard me, he adds the cause that belongs to the nature: Because
of the crowd standing around, he says, I said it, that they may believe
that you sent me; while those who gaped toward him crowned every lofty
utterance toward him.
A centurion approached concerning a servant, lifting him with as much
power as he had, in praises: Lord, he says, I am not worthy that you
should enter under my roof, but say the word only, and my servant will
be healed; and being applauded he received the gift. Amen, he says, I
tell you, not even in Israel have I found such faith. But go, and as you
have believed, let it be done for you. Again a woman with a flow of
blood, bringing the honor of doxology to the Master's tunic, let alone
to the Godhead, received the gift together with praises. And the robber
too was honored in paradise, because he called the one being crucified a
king. Greater faith than these is owed by us to the Master Christ,
inasmuch as we have also shared in greater knowledge. Be it so, however,
Arius: hold at least something near those things of that time. You do
not want me to think anything loftier concerning Christ than the things
said before? The mind is wicked, yet let it stop at this. Why do you
also dictate more lowly utterances to those who call upon him? I have
the words of those then as patterns for prayer. I do not say to Christ,
Lord, remember me in your service, but I speak the utterance by which
the robber was heard: Lord, remember me in your kingdom. I do not say to
God the Word, Lord, servant of the Father's gifts; for the centurion
does not permit me, crying that The Lord, say the word only, and my
servant will be healed. I do not say to him, Lord, if you ask of the
Father, I shall be saved, for I do not offer to the Godhead a smaller
token of a woman's faith, Arius. Thus I call the Son, as he delights to
be called; I approach with such utterances, with which those who came to
him returned having obtained. And if the robber and a woman and a
faithful soldier are unacceptable for the teaching of faith, revere at
least the choir of the apostles, where they had to call upon Christ for
gifts beyond nature. What does Peter utter when he raises a paralytic?
Aeneas, he says, Jesus Christ heals you; and again, working another
healing, There is, he says, salvation in no other name. What did Ananias
say when he healed Paul? Saul, brother, the Lord Jesus has sent me, who
appeared to you on the way, that you may look up and be filled with the
Holy Spirit. Whose power did Paul pray upon himself? Most gladly, he
says, will I boast in my weaknesses, that the power of Christ may
tabernacle upon me. Whom did James and John beseech concerning the
punishment of the Samaritans? Lord, they say, do you want us to say that
fire should come down from heaven and consume them? And what did the
choir of the angels hand down to men concerning him? He, it says, will
save his people from their sins.

And these too fall short of the dignity. For there is no hymn that lifts
the Son, none that equals him, but however high you may climb in
praises, whatever glory of words you may contrive, you will cry with
David, overcome: What shall I render to the Lord for all that he has
rendered to me? And if, Arius, uncreated is a voice of honor that lifts
the Son too high, it will =60.747= be yours to cry David's words toward
him with the parts exchanged: Lord, if I call you uncreated, what will
you render to me who have honored you with an honor greater than you?
But John will speak against them in reply: Fools and blind, we are not
worthy to loose the strap of his sandal. From every side, then, let us
be stirred toward the high speech of the divine nature; let us practice
great thoughts in the hymns of God; let us lift with all strength toward
the height the one who is worshipped; let us keep a poor mind in the
season of theology; let us hon =60.748= or the Father with high praises
toward the Son; let us glorify the glory of the Begetter, the likeness
of nature of the Begotten toward him, with equal honor; let us confess
the truth of the begetting; let us gain the Father's love by the service
of the one from him. For if anyone loves me, he says, my Father will
love him; to whom be the glory and the power unto the ages of the ages.
Amen.
