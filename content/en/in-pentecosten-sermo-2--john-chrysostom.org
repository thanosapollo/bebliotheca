* On Pentecost, Sermon 2
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

The grace of the Spirit has come down. For he who promised the gift of
the Spirit is true. The Paraclete proclaimed by the Master Christ has
taken up his dwelling. For he who foretold the coming does not lie: it
is not a slave announcing a slave's arrival, but a Master become the
messenger of a Master of equal honor, and a good teacher yielding place
to a good teacher; or rather, present with him who is present, going up
with him who goes up, and coming down with him who comes. For the grace
of the Trinity is undivided: neither did he who ascended withdraw, nor
was he who was coming absent. But God the Word went up as man, and is
present; the Paraclete came down, and did not leave the heavens. He
reigns with the Father, and lights the earth, and fulfilled the
economy, and sent kinds of tongues to the apostles. And a great sound
was heard all at once, and the roar of a violent wind rang out, and
the coming of the Spirit imitated the pattern of thunder; for it came upon
the sons of thunder.
And there appeared to them tongues as of fire, distributing themselves.
What was seen was fearful, but what was done was to be longed for: a
flame settling and not burning, fire sitting upon them and not
consuming; fire granting heavenly wisdom, fire dividing into countless
tongues, fire cut into ten thousand forms of speech; a flame teaching
the fishermen to argue as sophists, and making the leather-worker a
finished orator.
Or rather, the sophists were nothing so great. For they were
teachers of some single nation, but these have become instructors of
the inhabited world. A Galilean man spoke the speech of the Medes, and
knew the speech of the Parthians, and was not ignorant of that of the
Elamites; he carried on his lips the form of every speech. And none of
his hearers thought him a barbarian, nor fled from the words as if they
were counterfeit to the ear; he was heard as a citizen of every nation,
carrying about with him the tongue of the whole inhabited world, and
this we have received as the first fruit of piety.
For at the building of the tower speech was divided, and men no longer
knew one another's tongues. For it was necessary =52.808= that the
concord of the God-fighting counsel be hemmed in by ignorance of words,
that those who had worked badly together be parted by the change of
tongue, that they should learn by speech the trial of their mindless
undertakings, and that neither the speaker should speak, nor the hearer
understand, nor that they should waste their time besides in vain. For
what is the profit of your madness? What is the end of your grand work?
Why do you force yourself to grasp what cannot be grasped? Why do you
contend to seize heaven, you who are not even worthy to enjoy the
earth? Why do you talk of things on high, you who do not even hear your
neighbor's voice? I divide your speech, and I dissolve the work, not
fearing your ascent, nor being afraid for my kingdom, but caring for
you who toil and are worn out in vain. For neither a building of
stones, nor a tower put together by many hands, is of a nature to lead
up to heaven, but chariots of the knowledge of God, and a multitude of
righteousness, and a life cleaner than light, and wings of virtue lead
up toward that road. A divided tongue stopped those men from their
daring, God governing this both wisely and lovingly at once.
What he took from those as from evil men, this he granted to the
apostles as to good men; and he who punished the impious through the
tongue glorified the saints through the tongue. The ministers of the
Word receive the forms of speech, not enjoying the grace for display,
but accepting the gift for need. For the instructors of the inhabited
world had to know the things of the inhabited world. A teacher who is
unclear to those who learn is weak. But as men entrusted with such
great mysteries, they necessarily first received the knowledge of
tongues. For no one can initiate into the mysteries one who has not
first believed; and no one would believe, being inexperienced in what
is said. From this the grace of the Spirit began, and for this reason
it first worked wonders concerning sounds. For their sound went out
into all the earth. It was about =52.809= to overtake the inhabited
world as well, and to be measured by the bounds of the earth, and that
no one should be left untaught, nor that it should run past anyone
uninstructed.
And tongues dividing themselves appeared in the form of fire. For the
tongues of the apostles were about to work the things of fire, and a
double use was to be shown: to burn up the devil, and to give light to
those sitting in darkness. See a fire gentler to the faithful than any
oil; see a tongue more fearful to the devil than any fire; see a tongue
burning up the thorns of impiety, and dewing the crops of piety; see
twelve disciples being taught from one teacher; see twelve generals
arrayed under one king; see twelve bright luminaries rising from one
summit; see twelve pure rays going forth from one sun; see twelve lamps
kindled from one spark; see twelve branches that sprouted from one
vine; see twelve baskets filled from one table; see twelve rivers
brought forth from one spring; see twelve tongues speaking from one
grace. And do not think that they are drunk, nor that they are full of
new wine, nor insult the gift with unbelief, nor imitate the accursed,
nor call the working of the Spirit drunkenness. Drunkenness does not
know how to grant kinds of tongues; rather it even binds the tongue
each already has, and makes a man out of his mind, and cuts the course
of speech, and walking in the dark as among rocks it stumbles against
the teeth; it takes hold of the mind, and causes confusion, and
produces fantasy, and cuts the syllables short.
And drunkenness is a dream of one who is awake, and a vision of one who
is not asleep, and a thirsting madness, and a hungry demon, and a devil
sitting in wine, and a robber lying in wait at banquets, and burning up
the reasoning with unmixed wine, and turning the drug of the despondent
soul into poison. These things drunkenness is of a nature to produce.
The impious slanderously charged these things against the apostles; and
it is no wonder. Those who were sober seemed to the Jews to be drunk;
for they all =52.810= turned to insults against the saints, and mocked
the prophets as drunkards. And if they deny it, let us say to them: Are
these things not so, sons of Israel? says the Lord. And you gave my
sanctified ones wine to drink, and you commanded the prophets, saying:
Do not prophesy. But I myself also confess that they are drunk, yet not
with the drunkenness you yourself speak of, but with that more divine
and noetic drunkenness, of which the one who has drunk is filled with
wisdom, of which the one who has drunk is strong in mind, of which the
one who has drunk is awake and sober toward virtue, of which the one
who has drunk learns to love God, of which the one who has drunk shall
never thirst forever, of which the one who has drunk is mad with Paul,
and dares every fire and iron, and leaps upon dangers, and greets every
misfortune as gladness, and rejoices in insults as in praises, and
takes pride in outrages as in commendations, and is splendid in the
bonds for the Savior as in royal crowns. This mixing-bowl made the
apostles drunk; this cup the disciples of the Savior drank, and they
were drunk with the fairest drunkenness, and they were filled from the
holy table, and they say with the blessed David: You prepared a table
before me, over against those who afflict me; you anointed my head with
oil, and your cup that makes me drunk is as the strongest, and your
mercy shall pursue me all the days of my life.
For the one who drinks from this cup is sober in Christ, yet seems to
the foolish to be drunk, as he thinks the things of the Savior. This
mixing-bowl the Master Christ mixed for us also from blood, and he
called together many banquet guests for us, and he called together
countless fishes of the Gospel, and he filled Peter's net, and the
vision came to him by a wonder, and the storm made the fishing more
splendid for him; and those whom he did not hunt by persuading, these
he took alive when they were afraid, a lightning-flash helping him
toward the catch.
But let no one call this an outrage to the new flock, if they were
hunted by a lightning-flash; for Christ is accustomed to bridle kicking
colts by a lightning-flash. And whoever knows the calling of the
blessed Paul rejoices at being called with Paul, and having seen the
Master Christ by a lightning-flash, becomes a disciple of Christ. To
him be the glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
