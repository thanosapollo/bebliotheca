On Martha and Mary and Lazarus, and Elijah the prophet.
=61.701=
As many of you as bear love for the Master's resurrection, receive genuinely
the Christ-loving ointment of the gospel passage just now read. For now Mary
anoints, and the Church grows fragrant; now the alabaster jar is emptied, and
the altar is filled with sweetness. Mary anoints, Martha serves, and the one
risen from the dead reclines at table with the Lord. For honorable is the race
of men, because man came to be according to the image of God, but woman from
the image of the man, glorifying indeed the man, but not stamping the image of
Christ. And who is the witness of these things, that we may not reap blame?
Hear Paul crying out: A man ought not to cover his head, being the image and
glory of God; but the woman is the glory of the man. The woman is the glory of
her own man; for not of others', on account of the ancient vain counsel, but on
account of the later genuine childbearing. For this, the genuine childbearing,
freed the woman from the curse. And who is the witness of these things? The
same Apostle cries out: Adam was not deceived, but the woman, being deceived,
was in transgression; yet she shall be saved again through childbearing. Do you
see that through childbearing the woman is saved? not that Eve brought forth
Cain, but that the glorious Virgin bore Jesus. Mary therefore anoints, Martha
serves, and Lazarus reclines at table with the Lord, as you heard just now.
Mary paints the Church in an icon; for the ointment of the Church is unfailing.
Martha points out the synagogue; for she was of little faith and a lover of
earthly things. Lazarus bears the scepter of the resurrection, as you heard the
evangelist crying out just now: Jesus came to Bethany, where Lazarus was, whom
he raised from the dead.

Therefore they made him a supper there, and Martha served; but Lazarus was one
of those reclining at table with him. Mary then, having taken a pound of
ointment of nard, pistik and very precious, anointed the feet of Jesus, and
wiped his feet with her hair; and the whole house was filled with the fragrance
of the ointment. =61.702=.40 Do you see the work of a woman, and how she
painted the Church beforehand? Mary turned away the many women from their
wicked practices, and taught them to bear a pound of ointment, and not to cut
off the head of the Baptist; to anoint the Lord, and not to steal with Judas;
to loosen their hair, and not to bind up their sins; to be called the sister of
Lazarus, and not to be famed as the daughter of the devil. These things have
been said by me on account of the synagogue of the Jews, the one that did not
anoint the Lord, but gave him vinegar and gall to drink, the lover of blood,
the one ever working things contrary to the Master Christ, the one striving to
put Lazarus to death, whom he raised from the dead. And who then is more
faithful? the Lord raising, or those killing? As you heard the evangelist
saying just now: A great multitude of the Jews knew that he is there; and they
came not for the sake of Jesus only, but that they might see Lazarus also, whom
he raised from the dead; for many of the Jews went away on his account, and
believed in Jesus.

Do you see the synagogue of the Jews, how man-killing, prophet-killing, so as
to make haste to Lord-killing? To which the Lord himself in person, as to a
murder-loving and brigand-like one, said: Jerusalem, Jerusalem, the one killing
the prophets, and stoning those sent to her, how long will you slay, and not
turn back? You are drunk, drunk without drinking wine, you lover of blood. And
that you may learn that these words are no boast, hear the Lord saying through
the prophet: Hear these things, all you rulers of the house of Jacob, and the
remnant of Israel, that abhor judgment, and pervert all that is right, that
build Zion with bloods, and Jerusalem with iniquities; and again: When you
stretch out your hands to me, I will turn away my face from you; and if you
multiply prayer, I will not hear; for your hands are full of blood. What then
of the evangelist? Six days before the =61.703= Passover Jesus came to Bethany.
The Lord who came to Bethany, the same is present here also; for here Bethany
is lordly named the house that is present, since Bethany is said to be a house
of peace, and a house of peace is the present house of the Lord, into which we,
dwellers in it each day, cry to the householder: Glory to God in the highest,
and on earth peace, good will among men. He who came to Bethany, the same also
comes here; and he comes not as absent, but as being, and present, and coming.

This coming of the Lord the prophet also, having foreseen, said: Behold, the
Lord comes with strength, and his arm with dominion. Behold, the Lord comes
with strength. True is the word: The Lord comes with strength. How with
strength? As having spoiled hell, as having given Lazarus a taste of the
resurrection, as having filled the world with ten thousand benefits. Behold,
the Lord comes with strength. For who is stronger than the one who said,
Lazarus, come forth, come out here? Behold, the Lord comes with strength. He
comes who is not absent; for you heard, Let us go forth to meet the one present
and coming, not walking on the earth with the shifting of our feet, but
cleansing our souls with the improvement of our manners. Let us go forth to
meet the one present, giving thanks; let us do what the sister of Moses did;
let us utter cries more vigorous than hers, since the devil, who was sunk in
the pool of water, is harsher than Pharaoh. Let us then greet the symbols of
victory, not hand-held drums, but palm branches and shoots; let us cry out with
our fellow-believing brethren what we heard just now: Hosanna, blessed is he
who comes in the name of the Lord, the king of Israel. This royal coming of the
Lord Isaiah the prophet, having foreseen, said, as I foretold: Behold, the Lord
comes with strength, as raising the dead; and not simply the dead, but the
decayed, the dissolved, the ichor-flowing, the hair-shed, having raised Lazarus
on the fourth day from the tombs, as you heard the evangelist saying: Six days
before the Passover Jesus came to Bethany, where Lazarus was, whom he raised
from the dead. What such a thing did Moses do, such as our Master Christ?
Moses killed, for he did not make alive. Let the children of the Jews then be
ashamed, those praising Moses and reviling the Master Christ. But straightway a
Jew leaps aside, saying: And has no dead man risen among us? Did not Elijah the
prophet raise the son of the widow of Sarepta? but Elijah is a man.

How then do you boast over your teacher, testifying to him divine power,
because he raised Lazarus from the dead? What do you say, O Jew? do you compare
Elijah and the Master Christ? One thing is servile discipline, and another
divine authority. For unless you first knew the manner of the raising of the
dead, you would not perceive the word of divine authority. Hear with
understanding: the dead man at Elijah, the son of the widow of Sarepta, even if
he was delivered over to death, yet not through natural failure, but through
discipline and affliction of Elijah, that Elijah might be bent from
hard-heartedness to compassion. For Elijah once, as you know clearly, being
angry with the house of Ahab and the people of Israel on account of the
lawlessness of Jezebel, pronounced with an oath, saying: As the Lord the God
lives, if there shall be these years dew or rain upon the earth, except by the
mouth of my word. With this waterless decree of Elijah the God of all was not
pleased, but he fills the decree of the servant. For he wished the one who
bound the clouds with the deprivation of the rich rains to release the calamity
of the drought more quickly. But Elijah did not add himself to the
reconciliation. For otherwise God is moved to compassion, and otherwise man is
hardened. The Lord then, on account of the hardness of the servant, wished to
entreat the servant, that he might lend rain to the earth through his word. For
it was unbecoming to God to entreat a man; wherefore, as God, he pursued
another working. What one? Hear with understanding: as the drought was pressed
on, for three years and six months no cloud grew heavy toward the interest of
rains. Elijah then began to disregard thirst, but still he endured, bearing the
want, doing this out of contentiousness; for he wished himself also to be worn
away, or to be reconciled with the house of Ahab. What then of the Lord?
=61.704= He outwits his own servant, not entreating, but saying: Arise from
here, and go to the torrent Chorrath that is before Jordan, and drink water
from the torrent, and I have commanded the ravens to feed you there. The Lord
said these things, not that he might feed Elijah, but that he might shame him;
not that he might honor him, but that through the insolent food he might draw
him to compassion.

For it was unbecoming that the prophet be fed through ravens, and that a
flesh-bearing table be set before him who from the beginning cut through
ascetic labors. For the Lord was able either to feed the just man through
doves, or through the giving of the manna, in likeness of the sons of Israel,
or, in the manner of Daniel, to present to him Habakkuk, the man carrying
lentils; the Lord was able to feed Elijah solemnly, since in another descent he
fed him solemnly too. For when at another time Elijah, fleeing Jezebel, came
into the desert, and slept in the desert with great hunger and thirst, because
long is the road of flight, the Lord therefore sets bread and water by his
head, and again pricks his side, and not another part. Why? Hear with
understanding: he pricks his side, because he fled a threat of the side. And
the Lord says to him: Elijah, arise, and eat and drink, for great is the way
from you. And Elijah, arising and eating and drinking, went in the strength of
that food forty days and forty nights, not as hungering, but as filled. For
such is the divinely given food. He who fed him there genuinely was able also
in the present case to feed him from heaven; but he sets before him an
unbecoming table, that through the insolent food he might be bent to
compassion and pity. But he remained unbending. For it was needful that
Elijah, hearing the Lord say, Drink from the torrent, and I have commanded the
ravens to feed you there, should fall down, entreat, and say, By no means,
Lord; never has any common and unclean thing entered my mouth; do you set
before me ravens, the plotters of Noah? do you prepare a flesh-bearing table as
for a Gentile and foreigner, one from the beginning turned to ascetic customs?
it was needful that Elijah bear these words entreatingly. But Elijah did none
of these things; for he knew that if he arraigned the unbecoming table, the
Lord would say to him: What do you say, O Elijah? do you refuse to drink filthy
water, not having allowed the rain to come down? for you burned the land of
Israel; there is not found a drop of water in a well, nor moisture in a
spring. Accept then what you did. Do you not receive the flesh-bearing food of
ravens? There is not found bread, nor grain, nor grass-bearing vegetable, lest
rain or dew fall upon the earth by the shutting of your mouth; there is nothing
to be found except flesh alone, and that of dead things, of the beasts that
died from the failure of foods. Why then do you not receive the table of your
labors? That then Elijah might hear none of these things, he accepted the
unbecoming table.

For he chose rather to bear a long drought than to lend the rain-bearing word.
What then did the all-wise Lord? As he saw him persisting in hardness, he
pursues another working, stronger than the first. What one? He dries up the
river, he gathers in the ravens, he sends a voice to him: Go into Sarepta of
Sidonia; behold, I have commanded a widow woman to feed you there. Do you see
how the Lord hastened to bend him to compassion? For he did not send him to the
house of a ruler, or to a royal house, or to some wealthy woman, but to a
destitute widow, that thus also he might be bent to compassion. But even so he
remained unbending. For it was needful that he, hearing the Lord say, Go into
Sarepta of Sidonia; behold, I have commanded a widow woman to feed you there,
should fall down, cry out, entreat, and say, Do not bring me down into
humiliation, O Master; do you send me off to Sidonians, and command me to be
troubled by a widow woman, whose bread is filled with tears, whose table is
without rest of groanings, whose bed has a curse for a pillow? but Elijah did
none of these things. For he knew that if he excused himself from the widow's
table, the Lord would say to him: What do you say, O Elijah? =61.705= do you
excuse yourself from the widow's table, you who prepared to make all the land
of Israel barren, having worked her into a widow, barren and childless, not
bearing fruit, and do you excuse yourself from the widow's table? That then
Elijah might hear none of these things, he straightway hastened the journey to
the Sidonians, gaining for himself either gladness or pain. But hear what
follows on these things. Elijah, having been made a guest by the widow, found a
table without cost, genuine, suitable, ascetic, water and oil and flour, and
two little sticks. What then? did the widow have only two little sticks? Yes.
How? Hear, and I will tell you.

This widow, having been mentioned, bore an icon of the Church; wherefore the
prophet is sent to her. The prophet then, having entered into the widow's
house, found truly the treasury of our Church; he found water and oil and
flour, and two little sticks: water, baptism; oil, the chrism; flour, the
bread; two little sticks, the achievement of the cross. And Elijah cried out to
the widow: The jar of oil shall not fail, and the vessel of flour shall not
fail. For the grace of the orthodox faith is unfailing. Elijah therefore,
having found the widow's table without cost, rejoiced, exulted, saying to
himself: Here I will live from now on, here I will spend all my time; for I
found the table suitable; no one accuses me before the house of Ahab, and the
people of Israel, and the madness of Jezebel; let them have for inheritance the
drought; for I am not at all separated from these things here. Elijah saying
these things to himself, reckoning gladly, not perceiving, as a man, that in
his gladness he was about to find pain. You all know how the widow, having two
genuine children, her own, being raised from bed at dawn, sees one of her sons
delivered over to death. And if it is natural for a widow to grieve over a
child, a male, perfect, dying, no one of you is ignorant; for she counts the
life of her child dearer than all the wealth of the world. The woman therefore
was not added to the dead man, as is customary, nor did she bury him, nor was
she occupied with the funeral; but leaving her own dead, she laid hold of
Elijah, seizing, turning him about, making a crowd, crying out over all: What
have I to do with you, man of God? have you come to me to remind me of my sins,
and to kill my son? That is, You yourself killed him. Do you see how Elijah's
gladness turned to pain? He was held as a murderer; for so the widow said, What
have I to do with you, man of God? You entered to remind me of my sins, to kill
my son. You yourself killed him; your entrance prepared this. Did you not find
my child sick? did he not grow strong for one day? your entrance gave my son's
death. Why did you give me flour, the eater not being present? give me my son;
you killed him, wishing to inherit my house. Take you the flour, and give me my
son. You feed me, and kill my child? let me hunger with my own, and not be rich
with strangers. What have I to do with you, O man of God? you entered to remind
me of my sins, and to kill my son. You killed my son; you seem to me some harsh
one; is not your entrance accursed? What shall I do? tell me. Did you not flee
your God? did you not work the things of Jonah? You seem to me some rough one.
You breathe fire, you reach for murder, you bear drought, you burn captains of
fifty; for the things concerning you have become known to me. Is it not enough
for you that you strangled your countrymen, but you have also come here to work
the like? You struck many, but me you shall not strike; give me my son. What
then did Elijah? He was bent at once; for he knew the Lord's blow. Wherefore,
groaning greatly as defeated, he cried out saying: Woe is me, Lord, the witness
of the widow, with whom I dwell, you have afflicted =61.706= her, to kill her
son; the work is yours; for he knew, O Master. Behold, I am being pressed by a
widow; for you always send me off to most harsh women. I fled Jezebel, and fell
in with this one. I endured many trials, but this woman's tongue I am not able
to bear. You are the one who said to me, Master, Go into Sarepta of Sidonia;
behold, I have commanded a widow woman to feed you there. And behold, she turns
me astray; for she does not feed me. I am held as a murderer; and I am held
well as a murderer; for the boy was in the flower of youth at my entrance; how
then he was delivered over all at once to death, I do not know. Deliver me,
Lord, from this trouble; a theatre has stood around me, shame has been put upon
me, much affliction has been brought upon me. Give the soul, and I give the
rain; persuade the widow, and I water the land; give the child's return to
life, and I move the tongue to the giving of rain. These things however were
said on account of the raising of Lazarus spoken of before, and the
contradiction of the Jews, because the Master rules in one way, and a servant
raises in another. Elijah raised a dead man, but still he did not rule; for he
suffered many things and was afflicted and fell into straits. Wherefore he
cried out in prayer saying: Woe is me! And where there is Woe, there is pain of
soul. For thus also elsewhere you will find the prophet mourning and saying:
Woe is me! for I am left as a gathering of straw in harvest, and as a remnant
of grape-gleanings in the vintage; and again another prophet, Woe, woe for the
day! for near is the day of the Lord, and it is as affliction; and again
another, Woe! for it has perished from the earth. Everywhere the Woe is pain.
So also Elijah here: Woe is me, Lord, the witness of the widow, you have
afflicted her, to kill her son. And he breathed into the child three times. See
then here, how in the law of grace the mystery is painted in an icon. For the
words, He breathed three times, set forth the entrance of the undivided
Trinity. And he called upon God, and said: Let the soul of the child, Lord,
return, I say, into him. And it was so.

What do you say, O Jew? is the Lord such as Elijah? is Elijah such as the
Lord? did the Lord bend his knee? did he widen out his prayer? For every prayer
ascends to him; he let out only a voice, Lazarus, come forth, come out here, as
a master calling a servant. What then? did he come out as a servant obeying a
master? He came out; Hades did not delay, did not wait; death did not resist;
the powers below did not hold back, but rather they were amazed. For Hades,
having Lazarus already four days in his own places, dissolved on every side,
like an unpegged ship, was thereafter without care; the powers below had no
thought that Lazarus would thereafter be drawn up from the things below. But
when the Master's voice, having come down into the tomb all at once with much
illumination, began more quickly to plant again the hairs of Lazarus to the
head, to lay the marrow in the hollowness of the reeds, to direct the
distribution of the veins to life-bearing blood, the powers below were amazed,
crying out to one another: Who is this who calls? who is this who rules? who is
he who refashions dissolved dust? who is he who wakes a dead man as if from
sleep? who is he who breaks the adamantine gates? who is he who cries, Lazarus,
come forth, come out here? the voice is human-sounding, and the power of divine
power. Who is he who calls? he is not a man; the form is of a man, but the
voice of God. Let us send Lazarus forward, let us bear him up swiftly, lest the
one calling him come down here, lest the caller come down here while this one
delays. The dead began to be shaken and to move. Let us lose one, he says, and
not lose all.

Thus Lazarus leaped out from the bosom of Hades, praising and blessing and
glorifying our Lord Jesus Christ. Here let us seal the discourse; gathering the
palm branches let us go forth to meet the Lord, crying out and saying: Hosanna,
blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord. To him be the glory and the
might unto the ages.
