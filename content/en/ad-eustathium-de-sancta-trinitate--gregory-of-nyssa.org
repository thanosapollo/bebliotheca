GREGORY BISHOP OF NYSSA TO EUSTATHIUS CONCERNING THE HOLY TRINITY
The profession is humane even for all of you who practice medicine, and it
seems to me that of all things pursued in life, he who prefers your science
would hit upon the fitting judgment and not miss what is proper, if indeed the
most precious of all things, life, is something to be fled and full of pain,
if it is not possible to have it with health; and the giver of health is your
art. But for you the science is especially dexterous, and you make the bounds
of humanity greater for yourself, not limiting the grace from the art as far
as bodies only, but devising also the correction of the soul's illnesses. And
I say these things not only following the reports of the many, but also having
been taught by my own experience, in many other things and especially now in
the indescribable wickedness of our enemies, which, having flooded against our
life like a stream of evil, you skillfully dispersed, dissipating this heavy
inflammation of our heart 3,1.4 by the pouring on of soothing words. For I,
looking at the successive and varied attack of the enemies against us, thought
I ought to be silent and to receive what was brought upon us in quiet, and not
to rise against those armed with falsehood, this wicked weapon which often
thrusts its point even through the truth; but you did well in charging me not
to betray the truth, but to refute the slanderers, so that many might not be
harmed when falsehood prevails over the truth.

They seemed to me to do something like Aesop's fable, those who took up
causeless hatred against us. For as he made the wolf bring certain charges
against the lamb, being ashamed, as it were, of seeming to destroy without a
just pretext one who had not previously harmed him, and when the lamb easily
dissolved every accusation brought from slander the wolf in no way slackened
his attack, but was defeated by what was just and conquered with his teeth:
so those for whom hatred against us has been pursued as if it were one of the
good things, blushing perhaps at seeming to hate without cause, invent causes
and charges against us, and they do not persist to the end in any of the
things said, but now they say this, and after a little another, and again
something else is the cause of their hostility against us. And their
wickedness has stood on nothing, but when they are shaken off this 3,1.5
charge, they cling to another, and from that again they seize another; and
even if all the charges are dissolved, they do not stand away from hating.
They accuse us of preaching three gods, and they din the ears of the many, and
they do not cease constructing this slander persuasively. But the truth fights
for us, when we show both in public to all and privately to those who meet
with us that everyone who says three gods is anathematized by us and is judged
not even to be a Christian. But when they hear this, Sabellius is ready for
them against us, and the disease from him is buzzed against our teaching. And
again we too put forward against this the accustomed weapon, the truth,
showing that equally with Judaism we shudder at such a heresy. What then? Did
they grow weary after so many attempts and keep quiet? It is not so; but they
bring against us innovation, composing the charge against us in this way: they
accuse us, who confess three hypostases, of saying one goodness, one power,
and one Godhead. And they say this is not outside the truth; for we do say it.
But in accusing they bring this, that their custom does not have this and
Scripture does not agree. What then do we say to this too? We do not think it
just to make the custom prevailing among them a law and canon of right
teaching. For if custom is strong for a proof of correctness, it will be
possible for us too 3,1.6 surely to put forward in turn the custom prevailing
among us; and if they set that aside, we too surely must not follow them.
Therefore let the God-inspired Scripture arbitrate for us, and with those
whose doctrines are found in accord with the divine words, to these will
surely come the vote of truth. What then is the charge? For two things have
been brought forward together in the accusation against us: one, the dividing
of the hypostases, and the other, not numbering any of the God-befitting names
in the plural, but (as has been said) announcing the goodness and the power
and the Godhead as one, and all these things in the singular. Now toward the
division of the hypostases those who dogmatize the difference of substances
in the divine nature would not stand alien; for it is not likely that those
who say three substances would not also say three hypostases. Therefore this
alone is in the charge, that we announce the names ascribed to the divine
nature in the singular.

But our argument toward this is ready and clear. For he who condemns those who
say one Godhead will of necessity agree either with him who says many or with
him who says none; for it is not possible to think of something else besides
what has been said. But neither does the teaching of Scripture allow us to say
many, if it ever even mentions it, making mention of the Godhead in the
singular; 3,1.7 "For in him dwells all the fullness of the Godhead," and
elsewhere "For his invisible things from the creation of the world are clearly
seen, being understood by the things that are made, both his eternal power and
divinity." If then to stretch the number of Godheads to a multitude belongs
only to those who have fallen sick with the polytheistic error, and to deny
the Godhead altogether would belong to the godless, what argument is it that
slanders us for confessing the Godhead as one? But they uncover more clearly
the aim of the argument, agreeing to accept that the Father is God and that
the Son likewise is honored with the name of Godhead, but that the Spirit,
numbered with Father and Son, is no longer also included in the conception of
Godhead, but that the power of Godhead, standing from the Father as far as the
Son, separates the nature of the Spirit from the divine glory. Therefore we
too must make a defense, as we are able, briefly also toward this thought.
What then is our teaching? The Lord, handing over the saving faith to those
who become disciples of the word, joins also the Holy Spirit to the Father and
the Son; and we say that what has once been joined has the conjunction through
all things, for it is not ranked in one thing and fenced off in others. But in
the life-giving power, according to which our nature is refashioned from the
corruptible life into immortality, the power of the Spirit having been taken
together with Father and Son, and in many other things, as in the conception
according to the good and the holy and the eternal, wise, upright, ruling,
powerful, and everywhere clearly it has the inseparable in all the names
conceived toward the 3,1.8 better. Therefore we think it is well to consider
that what is joined to Father and Son in so many high and God-befitting
conceptions is distinguished in none. For we do not know in any of the names
conceived concerning the divine nature the difference according to better and
worse, so as to think it pious, while granting to the Spirit community in the
inferior of the names, to judge him unworthy of those that surpass. For all
the God-befitting names and thoughts have equal honor toward one another, in
that they do not disagree at all concerning the signification of the subject;
for the appellation of the good does not lead the mind to one subject, and
that of the wise and of the powerful and of the just to another; but however
many names you say, the thing signified through all is one, and if you say
God, you have indicated the same one whom you conceived through the remaining
names. If then all the names ascribed to the divine nature are equal in force
with one another according to the indication of the subject, leading the mind
to the same thing by different emphasis, what reason is there, while granting
to the Spirit community with Father and Son in the other names, to alienate it
from the Godhead alone? For there is every necessity either to give the
community in this also or not even to grant that in the rest 3,1.9. For if it
is worthy in those, it is surely not unworthy in this; and if according to
their argument it is too small to admit community of the name of Godhead with
Father and Son, neither is it worthy to share in any other of the
God-befitting names.

For the names, contemplated and compared with one another through the emphasis
contemplated in each, will be found to have nothing less than the appellation
of God; and a proof is that by this name many even of the inferior things are
named, or rather the divine Scripture does not spare this homonymy even upon
incongruous things, when it names the idols with the appellation of God. For
"Gods," it says, "who did not make the heaven and the earth, let them be taken
away and thrown under the earth"; and "All," it says, "the gods of the nations
are demons." And the ventriloquist in her divinations, bringing up for Saul
the souls that were sought, says she has seen gods. And Balaam too, being some
soothsayer and seer and carrying the oracles in his hand (as Scripture says)
and having established for himself the teaching from demons through the
busywork of augury 3,=1.10=, is related by Scripture to be counseled by God.
And it is possible, collecting many such things from the divine Scriptures, to
set them forth, that this name in no way ranks first beside the remaining
God-befitting appellations, because (as has been said) it is said by Scripture
homonymously even of incongruous things; but the name of the holy and of the
incorruptible and of the upright and of the good we have been taught by
Scripture is nowhere made common toward things that should not. Therefore if
they do not deny that the Holy Spirit shares with the Son and the Father in
the names piously said in a special sense of the divine nature alone, what
reason is there to construct that he is without community in this alone, of
which even the demons and the idols were shown to partake according to some
homonymous use?

But they say that this appellation is indicative of nature, and that the
nature of the Spirit is without community toward Father and Son, and for this
reason it does not even share in the community according to this name.
Therefore let them show through what they recognized the alteration of nature.
For if it were possible for the divine nature itself to be contemplated in
itself and to find through the things that appear both what is proper and what
is foreign, we would surely not have needed words or other proofs toward the
grasp of what is sought; but since it is higher than the understanding of
those who seek, and from certain proofs 3,=1.11= we analogically reckon by
conjecture concerning the things that escape our knowledge, there is every
necessity that we be led by the operations toward the investigation of the
divine nature. Therefore if we see the operations worked by the Father and the
Son and the Holy Spirit differing from one another, we conjecture from the
otherness of the operations that the natures that operate are also different.
For it is not possible that things standing apart according to the account of
nature should be brought together with one another toward the form of the
operations (neither does fire cool nor does ice heat), but with the difference
of the natures the operations from them also are separated from one another.
But if we conceive the operation of Father and Son and Holy Spirit as one,
differing or varying in nothing, it is necessary from the identity of the
operation to infer the unity of the nature. The Father and the Son and the
Holy Spirit alike sanctify and give life and comfort and all such things, and
let no one assign the sanctifying authority by exception to the operation of
the Spirit, having heard the Savior in the Gospel saying to the Father
concerning the disciples: "Father, sanctify them in your name." 3,=1.12= And
likewise also all the other things are worked equally for the worthy by the
Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, [every grace and power,] the guidance,
the life, the comfort, the change toward the immortal, the passage into
freedom, and if there is any other good that comes down as far as us. And the
economy above us, both in the intelligible creation and in the sensible, if
one must conjecture through the things known to us also concerning the things
that lie above, this too has not been set outside the operation and power of
the Holy Spirit, each receiving the benefit according to his own worth and
need. For even if the arrangement and administration concerning the things
above our nature is unclear to our sense, nevertheless from consequence one
would more reasonably agree, through the things known to us, that the power of
the Spirit is operative also upon those, than that it is alienated from the
economy in the things that lie above. For he who says that puts forward the
blasphemy bare and unconstructed, constructing the absurdity by no reasoning;
but he who agrees that the things above us also are ordered with Father and
Son by the power of the Spirit, leaning on the clear proof according to his
own life, affirms these things strongly. For human nature being compounded of
soul and body, and the angelic having been allotted a bodiless life, if the
Holy Spirit were operative only upon 3,=1.13= bodies, and the soul were
incapable of the grace from him, perhaps someone would have inferred from this
(the intellectual and bodiless nature in us being above the power of the
Spirit) that neither is the angelic life in need of his grace. But if
according to what is primary the gift of the Holy Spirit is a grace of the
soul, and by the intellectual and formless the constitution of our soul is
somehow joined toward the angelic and bodiless life, who of those who know how
to see the consequence would not agree that every intellectual nature is
ordered by the arrangement of the Holy Spirit? For since it has been said
"That the angels always see the face of my Father who is in the heavens," and
it is not possible otherwise to see the hypostasis of the Father except by
looking intently into it through the impress, and the impress of the Father's
hypostasis is the Only-begotten, and to this again no one would draw near
unless his mind has been illumined by the Holy Spirit, what is shown from
these things? That the Holy Spirit has not been separated from even one
operation that is worked from the Father. Therefore the identity of operation
in Father and Son and Holy Spirit shows clearly the unvarying character of the
nature; so that, even if the name of Godhead signifies nature, the community
of the substance will agree that this appellation is properly applied also to
the Holy Spirit.

But I do not know how those who construct all things bring the 3,=1.14=
appellation of Godhead to the indication of nature, as if they had not heard
from the divine Scripture that nature does not come to be by appointment; but
Moses was appointed god of the Egyptians, the one giving oracles saying thus
to him that "I have given you as a god to Pharaoh." Therefore the appellation
bears an indication of some authority, whether of oversight or of operation,
but the divine nature, in all the names conceived, as it is, remains without
signification, as our teaching is. For having learned benefactor and judge,
good and just, and as many other such things, we have been taught differences
of operations, but we are no more able through the understanding of the
operations to know the nature of the one who operates. For when someone gives
an account both of each of these names and of the nature itself concerning
which the names are, he does not give the same account of both; and of things
whose account is other, the nature also is different. Therefore the substance
is one thing, of which an account that discloses it has not yet been found,
and the signification of the names concerning it is another, they being named
from some operation or dignity. So we find from the community of the names
that there is no difference in the operations, and the community of the nature
has been clearly demonstrated, constituted through the identity of the
operations 3,=1.15=. Whether then Godhead is a name of operation, as we say
the operation of Father and Son and Holy Spirit is one, so we say the Godhead
is one; or whether according to the opinions of the many the name of Godhead
is indicative of nature, because we find no variation in the nature from the
identity of the operations we not unreasonably define the Holy Trinity as of
one Godhead. And if someone should define this appellation to be indicative of
dignity, I do not know by what argument he drags the name toward such a
signification; except that since it is possible to hear many saying such a
thing, so that the zeal of those who oppose may find no place against the
truth from anywhere, we too, being carried along toward such a supposition
with those who think thus, say that, even if the name signifies dignity,
according to this too the appellation will properly be applied to the Holy
Spirit. For the name of kingship indicates every dignity, "And our God," it
says, "is King before the ages." But also the Son of the King, who has all the
things of the Father, is himself proclaimed King by the holy Scripture, and
the divine Scripture says that the Holy Spirit is the unction of the
Only-begotten, interpreting the dignity of the Spirit from a metaphor of the
things customarily done here. For as among the ancients, for those being
brought into kingship, a symbol of this dignity was the unction applied to
them, which having come about there was from that time a change from private
lowliness to the superiority of rule, and he who was deemed worthy of this
grace received another 3,=1.16= name after the anointing, being called instead
of a common man the anointed of the Lord; for this reason, so that the dignity
of the Holy Spirit might especially be shown to men, he was named by Scripture
a symbol of kingship and unction, we being taught that the Holy Spirit shares
in the glory and kingship of the only-begotten Son of God. For as among men it
was not possible to pass into kingship unless unction had led the way, so the
word indicates the equal power from a metaphor of the names among us, that not
even the kingship of the Son is grasped without the dignity of the Holy
Spirit; wherefore he is properly called Christ, this name furnishing the
indication of the inseparable and undivided conjunction toward the Holy
Spirit. If then the Only-begotten God is Christ, and the Holy Spirit is
unction, and the appellation of Christ indicates the kingly authority, and the
anointing is a symbol of the kingship, then the Holy Spirit shares also in the
dignity. If then some say that the name of Godhead is indicative of the
dignity, and it has been shown that the Holy Spirit shares in this, then that
which shares in the dignity will surely also share in the name that represents
it.
