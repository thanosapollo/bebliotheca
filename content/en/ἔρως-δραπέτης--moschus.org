#+BEGIN_QUOTE
Runaway Eros edited by J. M. Edmonds
#+END_QUOTE

Cypris was crying her son Eros far:
"Whoever has seen Eros wandering at the crossroads,
he is my runaway; the one who informs will have a gift;
the wage for you is the kiss of Cypris; and if you bring him,
the kiss is not naked, and you, stranger, will have even more.
The child is very marked; among twenty children you would know him.
His skin is not white, but like fire; and his eyes
are piercing and flaming; bad mind, sweet talk;
for he does not think and speak the same; as honey the voice,
and as gall the mind is; untamed, a deceiver,
truthing nothing, a deceitful babe, playing wild.
His head is well-locked, and he has a bold forehead.
Small indeed are his little hands, but he shoots far,
he shoots even to Acheron and into the palaces of Hades.
Naked is the whole body, but his mind is well packed.
And winged as a bird he flies upon one after another,
men and women, and sits upon the inward parts.
He has a very small bow, and over the bow a dart,

small indeed the dart, but it is carried even to the aether.
And a golden quiver about his back, and inside are
the bitter reeds, with which he often wounds even me.
All these are wild; much more is his torch;
being a small lamp it kindles the sun itself.
If you catch him, bring him bound and do not pity.
And if you see him weeping, guard lest he deceive you.
And if he laughs, drag him. And if he wishes to kiss you,
flee; the kiss is evil, the lips are poison.
And if he says /take these, I grant whatever weapons I have,/
do not touch the deceptive gifts; for all have been dipped in fire."
