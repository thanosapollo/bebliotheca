PSALM 101.

A prayer for the poor, when he is faint, and pours out his supplication before
the Lord.

1. The psalm has a prediction of the misery of the people of the Jews in
Babylon and of the return from there; at the same time it also foretells the
calling and salvation of the nations. It also fits everyone wrestling with
some calamity =55.636= and entreating to obtain the divine inclination. For he
calls poor the one in need of divine providence, comparing him with the one
blessed according to, Blessed are the poor in spirit. Everyone therefore
dedicated to God, being poor in the affairs of life, but rich with God, is
faint with grief, or, He is disheartened, according to Symmachus, at the
wickedness and destruction of the many: such as the one who said, Woe is me,
soul! for the godly has perished from the earth, and there is none that does
right among =55.637= men; and the one who said, There is none that does
kindness. Lord, hear my prayer, and let my cry come to you. Prayer signifies
the supplication toward God and the entreaty; but petition the request
concerning the things he wants granted him as he asks. And he says: Receive my
supplication, O Master, and stretch out your help to me as I cry. For the cry,
Symmachus has called Wailing. Turn not your face away from me. God turns his
face away from some who pray in several ways: either of those who ask, if they
do not plead to receive what it befits God to grant, and what it profits them
to receive; or because, though they ask what it befits God to give, they do
not yet have a season to receive these things usefully. And there are also
those who think it right to obtain certain things for sinning. The holy man
therefore, pleading, says: May I not ask such things, at which you refuse and
turn your face from me. And I pray, not that I may not be afflicted, for that
is impossible, but that being afflicted I may not be defeated, and that I may
rejoice in hope, enduring in afflictions, glorying in them. In whatever day I
am afflicted, incline your ear to me; in whatever day I call upon you, hear me
speedily. These words of the psalm also show that it is a prediction of things
about to be, and not a narration of things already done. For since the people
happened to be in much prosperity and distinction in the kingdom of the divine
David, the prophet reasonably, using their person, offers the supplication,
and begs to obtain the divine care whenever the season for this should come.
For my days have vanished like smoke, and my bones have been scorched like a
firebrand. From here, according to his own idiom, he narrates the coming
calamities as already done, and says that the time of life has been consumed
like smoke, and from despondency the body has become a dry brand, as if burned
by some assault of fire. For so also Aquila translated: And my bones were
crumbled as a burning. I was struck like grass, and my heart was dried up. For
I too was quenched like grass, and I lost the former bloom. Because I forgot
to eat my bread; from the voice of my groaning my bone cleaved to my flesh.
For I threw off appetite for all food, and was stripped utterly of the former
well-being; for by the wasting from despondency the body was consumed, and my
skin lies upon bare bones. I became like a pelican of the wilderness; I became
like a night-raven in a building-plot. I watched, and



I became as a sparrow dwelling alone on a housetop. He uses many comparisons,
wishing worthily to set forth the calamities; and through each of the birds
named he shows the cowardice and the desolation of the guardian. For the
sparrow by agony drives out sleep, and the night-raven, fleeing inhabited
houses, runs to the deserted and demolished. For building-plots, Symmachus has
called Ruins. So also the other bird dwells in deserts. All the day my enemies
reproached me, and they that praised me swore against me. I became a
laughing-stock to the hostile, and he who was once envied and conspicuous has
now become an oath to those who formerly admired him; for they swear by my
calamities: may I not suffer what so-and-so have suffered. For I ate ashes
like bread, and mixed my drink with weeping, from the face of your wrath and
your anger.

2. These things he also said in the seventy-ninth psalm: You will feed us with
the bread of tears, and give us tears to drink in measure; and through these
he shows the excess of pain. For if the season of food was full of pain,
hardly would any other season be free of it. With these evils, he says, I am
flooded, because, Master, you have moved wrath against me: For having lifted
me up you dashed me down. For both the people and the temple were glorified
while God was dealing with them; and as much as it was lifted, it fell, the
people being led away, and the temple being made desolate together with
Jerusalem, in which the law and the worship according to it were performed,
having a shadow of the good things to come; and these too were taken out of
the midst. And he also speaks of what concerns himself. =55.638= For having
shown me high and made me conspicuous, you suddenly cast me off. For You
dashed me down he set from the metaphor of those who lift something to a
height and dash it down against the ground. My days declined like a shadow;
and I was dried up like grass. I am, he says, at the very setting of life, and
I am like a shadow that has already declined and is ending; and I imitate the
dryness of grass, calling on the hands of the reapers. But you, Lord, remain
forever, and your memorial to generation and generation. Human things are of
this sort, being humbled through wickedness after exaltation, and never
remaining what they are; but you, having nothing perishable, are unchangeable
and unalterable, and the promises from you are firm; and not to the present
generation, he says, but to another, concerning which, A generation that is
coming shall be declared to the Lord; and, Let this be written for another
generation. For the memorial of your promises shall be fulfilled, and you
could easily grant some change of the present evils. Therefore I say with
confidence that you will raise up Sion; for this he adds: You, arising, will
pity Sion, for it is time to pity her, for the time has come. Give, he says, a
better change to the calamity; for the time demands mercy. And through these
he hints at the end of the punishment bounded by the seventy years. So also
the admirable Daniel, having numbered the time of the captivity, offers the
supplication according to Zechariah who said: Lord almighty, how long will you
not have mercy on Jerusalem? With these the words of the spiritual melody also
agree: For it is time to pity her, for the time has come. And I think he
prophesies in these things the appearing among men of God the Word himself,
our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ, which would be the cause of the rebuilding
of Sion.

Where you have come, do not suppose for me the city on earth, nor think the
earthly Jerusalem is signified, but the renewal of the polity of piety. For
this is the time concerning which he himself says through Isaiah: In an
acceptable time I heard you. And Paul also writes that this time has been
fulfilled, when the Saviour sojourned,



saying: Behold, now is an acceptable time. For your servants took pleasure in
her stones, and they will pity her dust. Holy stones are rolled upon the
earth, it is said somewhere in the prophets. But Peter also says, You all as
living stones are built a spiritual house into a holy priesthood. And in this
way he calls those who are taken into the building of the Church of God. For
through the growing-together and harmony toward one another, and through
thinking the same things and saying the same things, and walking with the same
mind and the same judgment, they work one house for God. These stones then
your servants took pleasure in, that is, the prophets. For these, seeing
beforehand by the Holy Spirit the stones from all the nations that would be
gathered into the building of the new Sion about to be built throughout the
whole world, took pleasure, being well pleased in them. And you might say that
dust of the Sion understood by us is either those who live more earthily, yet
are numbered with the stones of the building; whence he also thinks it right
that those from the nation of the Jews who still even now pretend to worship
God more earthily, and are for this called dust, should obtain pity.

He says they are worthy of pity and mercy. Therefore the servants of God are
prophesied to pity these, but the stones of the new building to be well
approved. Or he calls stones those who believed in Christ after the
resurrection, and servants the holy apostles, through whom those who believed
in our Lord Jesus Christ were saved. And the nations shall fear the name of
the Lord, and all the kings of the earth your glory. The cause of these things
is the preaching that came from Sion into all the world. And he said well,
They shall fear; for then the nations did not possess his fear; but from the
time he commanded the apostles, Going, make disciples of all the nations. And
indeed The kings of the earth, not some, but All; therefore even the righteous
are said to be supports of the kings; for these are self-appointed; or rather
=55.639= they happen to be kings appointed by God, through ruling over
pleasures and keeping the royal image. For the Lord will build Sion, and he
will be seen in his glory. For since those who are ignorant of the divine
economies supposed the slavery of the Jews and the desolation of Jerusalem to
be weakness of God, he reasonably called the renewal of the city the glory of
God; i.e., when Sion has been built again, all will see the God of all in the
former glory. When he stood altogether in the midst of the choir of the
apostles, then he appeared in his glory, that is, by the signs of the
sufferings; for he says to Thomas: Bring your finger here and see my hands,
and the rest. And he calls the sufferings glory, since through them he
subjected the world. And you will understand otherwise also, The Lord will
build Sion. For God the Word, having become flesh and tabernacled among us,
built the Church, spiritually named Sion. He will be seen in his glory, when,
he having tabernacled among us, we behold His glory, glory as of the
Only-begotten from the Father, full of grace and truth. He looked upon the
prayer of the humble, and did not despise their petition.

3. He did not disdain them, he says, as captives and slaves, but received the
supplication and gave the freedom. And he calls humble the humble-minded,
concerning whom the Lord said: Upon whom shall I look, but [or] upon the meek
and quiet and who trembles at my words? And he hints at the apostles. Or he
also calls it a prayer of the prophets who humble themselves, concerning whom
it was said, A prayer for the poor; and concerning the petition he said: And
he pours out his petition before the Lord, through which he will rebuild



Sion, and will do the things spoken. Let this be written for another
generation; and a people that is being created shall praise the Lord. The
prayer through the psalm, and the promise concerning the nations being
prophesied, as if stamped on a pillar, has been given to the Jews to be kept
for the new people and the generation according to Christ, in which the
nations were created through faith; concerning whom the blessed Paul also
says: If anyone is in Christ, a new creation. And the divine David himself
said in the twenty-first psalm: A generation that is coming shall be declared
to the Lord, and they shall declare his righteousness to a people that shall
be born, whom the Lord made. And in Christ himself the two are created, the
people from the nations and the people from the circumcision, into one new
man. For creation now signifies alteration, but not coming-into-being; such as
also, Create a clean heart in me, O God; and, The Lord created me the
beginning of his ways for his works. For the speaking person was not created
once for all, but unto a beginning of ways of God that are for his works. For
to be created a beginning of certain things signifies a relation, but not an
alteration. The people therefore being created thus shall praise the Lord,
coming into knowledge of the only God. But if it signified coming-into-being,
he would not have said, He who is being created, but, He who was created. For
it is possible to remain not in being given being, but in being recast. Thus
the people, being taught both the old and the new, sends up the fitting hymn
to God the Saviour. For he stooped from the height of his holy place, the Lord
looked from heaven upon the earth. The Saviour is said to stoop from the
height at the time of the incarnation, as remaining above and appearing below.
For this is proper to those who stoop from a height. And he stooped to look
upon the earth, that is, to visit it. And the Lord, being an incorporeal and
intellectual essence, or rather above these, being bounded nowhere, is not
circumscribed by place; but he goes through by power through the whole
creation. For he was in the world, and the world came to be through him. And
he is said to dwell in the better parts of the world, according to, He who
dwells in the heavens; and, Our Father who is in the heavens, not by
enclosure, but being present to his own works by unspeakable power. Being said
then to be in heaven thus, by providing also for the earth, he looked upon it.
To hear the groaning of the bound, to loose the sons of those who have been
put to death; to declare in Sion the name of the Lord, and his praise in
Jerusalem. He sets before us the cause of the calling of the nations =55.640=.
And this is the appearing of the Saviour, which he made by bowing the heavens.
And who are the bound, but those whom the demon who hates good bound with
cords of sins? And sons of those put to death would be those from the nations,
inasmuch as their fathers died by their own sins, still worshipping idols,
each also being bound with cords of their own faults; whose turning Isaiah,
making known, said to God: And they shall follow you bound with manacles, and
in you they shall pray and worship you. For the Gospel, having begun from
Sion, was spread into all the earth. For from Sion shall go forth a law, and a
word of the Lord from Jerusalem, and he shall judge among many nations.

In the gathering of peoples together, and kings to serve the Lord. He answered
him in the way of his strength: Tell me the fewness of my days. Bring me not
up in the midst of my days. He calls the way of his strength the presence on
earth. For he came to bind the strong one. For he prayed to see whether his
life would reach those days in which the Only-begotten, having become man, was
about to appear on earth. For thus far, at the present, when I prophesy these
things and foresee things about to be, I seem to be in a part of a day of God;
but there is lacking to me the seeing with my own eyes of the outcome of the
things prophesied, which if I should obtain, I shall receive my life complete;
but if I miss the prayer,



I am like those who in the midst of days lay down life incomplete and
untimely. And it seems to me that because of such voices of the prophets our
Saviour said: Many prophets and righteous men desired to see what you see, and
did not see, and to hear what you hear, and did not hear. Your years are in a
generation of generations. He turned the word again toward the Lord. For his
years, which he made in flesh, are in a generation of generations, as having
the polity completed and entire; whence Paul also, wishing to set forth those
in perfection, said: That we all may arrive at a perfect man, at the measure
of the stature of the fullness of Christ. In the beginning you, Lord, founded
the earth, and the heavens are works of your hands. They shall perish, but you
remain.

4. The phrase In the beginning, as it stands, can take away the opinion of
those who say that the earth is an element of all things, as if all things had
come from it, nothing being prior to it. For it appears that it is not a
beginning, but that its foundations exist according to certain beginnings
conceived beforehand. It was said also to Job by the Lord himself, as having
founded the earth: Where were you when I founded the earth? And while the
Lord, through whom all things have come to be, founds the earth, it is said
also concerning the Father: God in wisdom founded the earth. And what was said
concerning God, Hanging the earth upon nothing, shows that it has obtained the
place in the very middle of the universe. And all shall grow old as a garment.
And as a covering you will roll them, and they shall be changed. For all
things that are seen will grow aged, and will imitate the oldness of a
garment; but you will change them and make them new, and will make them
incorruptible instead of corruptible. This the divine apostle also said: That
the creation itself also shall be freed from the bondage of corruption into
the freedom of the glory of the children of God. But you are the same, and
your years shall not fail. The creation then you rearrange as you will, O
Master; but you have a nature unchangeable and better than alteration. These
things indeed the divine Apostle assigned to the property of the Son in the
Epistle to the Hebrews. But nevertheless in the Son we behold the Father. For
whatever that one does, these things the Son also does likewise, and the
sameness of nature is recognized in each. For we know one energy of the
Trinity. The sons of your servants shall dwell, and their seed shall be
directed forever. And instead of Shall be directed, Symmachus set, Shall
remain. And it signifies that those released from slavery shall inhabit
Jerusalem; and the seed from them shall remain forever. And sons of servants
of God must not be taken altogether according to flesh, but according to
training and imitation of a true polity; for in this way some were also called
sons of the prophets, perhaps having come to be from them according to a
prophetic =55.641= habit; and seed of these themselves were those benefited by
them. And concerning this seed the divine Apostle said: Until the seed should
come, to whom it was promised; and, To Abraham the promises were spoken and to
his seed. And he does not say, To the seeds, as of many =55.642=, but as of
one, And to your seed, who is Christ. This seed shall remain forever. For
having been united to God the Word it has an undying being, and grants life to
those who believe, according to the voice of the Lord himself; for he says: He
who eats my flesh and drinks my blood shall live forever, in him Christ our
Lord, to whom be glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.

PSALM 102.



This psalm seems to be consecutive to the one set before it even according to
the subject itself. For in that one those lamenting the calamities in Babylon
entreated God to obtain the return; but here, having obtained what they asked,
they hymn the benefactor. But I think this hymnody fits all men who have been
released from a more bitter slavery and have been thought worthy of a greater
freedom. And the sense of the sayings will also show that this subject is more
true than that one.

1. Bless the Lord, O my soul, and all that is within me his holy name. The
soul, being in the middle, drags the body down toward pleasures, but the
spirit draws it up toward God, commanding it to bless him, that is, to speak
of God and glorify him continually. And Paul also mentioned the three, saying:
And may God himself make you completely ready, and may the whole body and the
spirit and the soul be kept. And this is said of the spirit of the man that is
in him, concerning which again he says: I will pray with the spirit, and I
will pray also with the mind. And Moses also mentioned body in, God took dust
from the earth and formed the man; and soul in, Let us make man according to
our image; and of the spirit, saying: And God breathed into his face a breath
of life. And the spirit exhorts all the powers of the soul to bless God, such
as the intellectual, the rational, the irascible, the appetitive, the
opinative, the imaginative, the sensitive, all of which he called Within;
which Paul interpreting says: Whether you eat or drink, do all things to the
glory of God. And the things within the body also furnish a hymn to God; but
also through the instrumental parts of the body he moves the active powers of
the soul toward hymn. And those who say the soul belongs to the spirit do not
say it as giving it life, but as being under its care. And he says the
rational power is within, according to which the Saviour also said the kingdom
of the heavens is within us. And the powers of the soul, when they are at rest
from the good things they can do, are said to be within; but when they have
been moved by energies into what is unseemly, they go out from the things
within. Which now he stirs up toward hymn of God. It therefore fits all to
say, Bless the Lord, O my soul. For those who are well disposed concerning the
divine graces stir themselves up to hymnody, repaying the benefactor with what
they can. And it is possible to hymn always, and to carry about the memory of
the benefactions ringing in the ear. These also consecrate all that is within,
and stir every thought into the divine hymn. For he called within the
reasonings and the ponderings and all the movements of the soul. Bless the
Lord, O my soul, and forget not all his recompenses. Again the soul is taught
to stir itself up, and to drive out the cloud of forgetfulness, and to renew
the memory of the benefactions. For he who remembers the divine benefactions
does not cease hymning him. The Recompenses, however, Symmachus has called
Benefactions. Then he goes through these in part. Who is gracious to all your
iniquities, who heals all your diseases. For this one has granted you
forgiveness of sins, this one has bestowed on you healing of the passions. The
paralytic enjoyed these. For he heard, Your sins are forgiven you; and, Take
up your bed and go to your house. Thus the sinful woman obtained both healing
and forgiveness; thus the robber, thus the tax-collectors, thus all who
believed. Who redeems your life from corruption. =55.642= Those led away as
captives in Babylon he freed from slavery, but he did not release them from
the corruptible life; but to us he has granted the hopes of the resurrection,
and gave the earnest of the Spirit, and clothed us with the tunic of
incorruption. Who crowns you with



mercy and compassions. By grace you have been saved, the divine Apostle cries;
and this not of yourselves; it is the gift of God, that no one may boast; and
again, Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners, of whom I am first;
but I was shown mercy, that in me first Christ Jesus might show all his
long-suffering, for a pattern of those about to believe on him unto eternal
life. The crown then is of grace and loving-kindness. Thus also the divine
Isaiah cries from the person of the bride: Let my soul rejoice in the Lord;
for he clothed me with a garment of salvation, and wrapped me with a tunic of
gladness, as a bridegroom he set a mitre on me, and as a bride he adorned me
with ornament; and after a little, And you shall be a crown of beauty in the
hand of the Lord, and a diadem of kingship in the hand of your God. And all
these are bridal gifts of the bridegroom and presents. For the bride brought
faith alone. Who fills your desire with good things. Since there is a
blameworthy desire, which even the divine law forbids, he well added With good
things; for the Master fills our good desires. For ask, he says, and it shall
be given you; seek, and you shall find; knock, and it shall be opened to you.
For everyone who asks receives, and he who seeks finds, and to him who knocks
it shall be opened. But seek the kingdom of God and his righteousness, and all
these things shall be added to you besides. Your youth shall be renewed as an
eagle's. And this making new again the Master granted us through all-holy
baptism, and scraping off the old age of sin, he made us young instead of
aged. In accord with these God says through Isaiah the prophet: But they who
wait shall change strength, they shall grow wings as eagles. For since, having
been created, we received the divine and royal stamps, then we dimmed and
corrupted these by the manifold practices of sin, the prophetic word promises
us the recovery of the kingship. For this bird is royal, and has been
entrusted with the kingship of the winged. And it also teaches us the manner
of the divine munificence, so that it is renewed by putting off the old man,
and putting on the new who is being renewed unto knowledge of him who created.
Thus, he says, your youth shall be renewed, so as always to remain in being
young in the incorruptible life. Since the eagle is of all birds the most
royal and highest-flying, and the only living thing able to look against the
flashes of the sun's light, and to gaze intently at them, he reasonably
compared the renewal of the soul, and the flight from earth to heaven, and its
life that will come to be in the extreme light, to the nature of an eagle. The
Lord doing acts of mercy, judgment for all who are wronged.

2. For not by reason of righteous deeds which we did, but according to his
great mercy he saved us through a washing of regeneration and renewal of the
Holy Spirit. And having seen us wronged by the devil of injustice, the enemy
of the truth, he opened for us the springs of mercy, but against that one he
brought forth the just vote. For as a compassionate father and being alone
good, redeeming the souls of those tyrannized by Satan from injustice and
usury, having taken up the handwriting against us and nailing it to the cross,
he showed us free. For holding this the devil had us as debtors, through what
we practiced, we having done this =55.643=. This judgment God made for us who
were wronged, having cleansed us through the blood of his Son from both what
we did wrong, and what we were wronged by the devil. Therefore Paul also
taught us rather to be wronged than to do wrong. For to those who are wronged
God makes judgment through benefaction; but to those who do wrong, through
punishment, so that they fall from the promise. For the unjust, he says, shall
not inherit the kingdom of God. He made known his



ways to Moses, his willings to the sons of Israel. And he did not work these
things, your salvation, from a change of mind, but from above he foretold
these things through the prophets, and through the divine Moses he sketched
our salvation. And the sea received a type of all-holy baptism, and the rock
was shown a shadow of the immortal springs, and the manna an image of the
heavenly luxury, and the rest also, that I may not go through each, can be
found sketched among them thus. Ways then and willings both signify the
virtues, according to, Lord, make known to me your ways; and that by Paul to
Elymas: Will you not cease perverting the straight ways of the Lord? Such also
is, I found David the son of Jesse, a man according to my heart, who will do
all my willings. He says then that, giving him the law, he taught Moses the
passages from the typical things of the law and the shadow to the good things
to come, which are spiritual and original, such as what circumcision is, what
the sabbaths, what the sacrifices, having taught these things also to whoever
is a son of Israel, concerning whom it was said: Israel my chosen. For God
makes these known to the clear-sighted, the Jew in secret, circumcision of
heart, a sacrifice of praise and of righteousness. The Lord is compassionate
and merciful, long-suffering and of much mercy. For these things both of old
became and now are causes of so great benefaction, Moses also having said: The
Lord is long-suffering and of much mercy, and true, doing mercy unto
thousands. These powers of his then he made known to Moses as being called
ways, and the virtues according to all things the abyss of the divine goodness
administers, the unmeasured mercy, the sea of loving-kindness. He will not be
angry to the end, nor will he be wroth forever. Therefore he will neither use
wrath throughout (for this is To the end), nor does he grant mercy continually
to the unworthy. Since how, because God is a just judge and strong and
long-suffering, how will he render to each according to his works? He mixes
then wrath with mercy, when it is a time of mercy putting it forward, but when
of wrath, bringing it on in a day of wrath and just judgment, in which, Paul
says, He will render to each according to his works. Therefore he says, He
will not be wroth forever, meaning the present age; for to the coming one he
reserved the judgment and the wrath. He did not do to us according to our
iniquities, nor did he recompense us according to our sins. For he did not
endure to measure the punishment by our sins, nor to stretch indignation
against us for the most part. For God, being true, does not have these virtues
according to habit, but being their spring and essence; whence he does not
punish in order to finish, remaining in wrath as far as destruction. For he
himself who says: When the wrath of my anger comes to be, I will heal again;
and he also lessens the punishments, not punishing according to desert. For
according to the height of heaven from the earth the Lord strengthened his
mercy upon those who fear him. As far as easts are distant from wests, he
removed our iniquities from us. If he had found distances longer than these,
he would altogether have brought these also into the midst, wishing to show
the infinite of the divine goodness; but human reasoning found neither a
measure higher than heaven, nor longer than the middle of evening and dawn.
And men who are in sins are in darkness and wests; but having been released
from faults, they clearly come to be transferred in light. So that reasonably
he called the release from sins a passage from wests to easts, wishing at once
also to teach us what is between sin and righteousness. As =55.644= a father
pities sons, the Lord pitied those who fear him.

3. The Creator and Maker shows a father's goodness, or rather those



who fear him, pitying, he set into the rank of sons. For he did not construct
them hating, but knowing the hearts of men, which he formed one by one, he
knows what profits each; and of what we have power and occasion, these he
gives, in order that having them in energy we may become his sons. And one
might also say that he took trial of our formation by becoming flesh, and had
a natural disposition, counting us sons. For he himself knew our formation. He
remembered that we are dust. For the Creator knows the weakness of our nature;
he knows also from where we were formed, and where we shall go again. And as
in a supplication, they put forward the worse of the things in us. For we are
not all dust, but also according to the image of God and likeness. Dust then
pitifully shows the weakness. Man, his day is as grass; as a flower of the
field, so he shall bloom. For a spirit passed in him, and he shall not exist,
and he shall not know his place any more. We have differed nothing from grass
and a flower, which in spring flourishes, but after a little withers and flows
away; for the word is again about the outer man. For according to the material
life, according to which we are dust, stretching the present time for some
days, we are compared to grass being dried. And according to Isaiah also, All
flesh is grass, and all glory of man as a flower of grass. The sun rose, and
dried the grass, and its flower fell; through which he sets forth the seeming
prosperities of men, wealth, glory, ranks, which wither like grass. And
Symmachus rendered, For a spirit passes through him, then he does not exist.
And he calls spirit the soul, at whose presence the body both lives and acts;
but when it has flown away, it is quenched and corrupted, so that not even the
former stamps are recognized, nor is it known that this is the body of
so-and-so, and that of so-and-so. And one might see this more accurately,
peering into a tomb. For all alike become a waste of corruption, and do not
preserve the former stamps. But the mercy of the Lord is from age even unto
age upon those who fear him; and his righteousness upon sons of sons for those
who keep his covenant, and remember his commandments to do them. Nature then
is thus perishable; but the divine loving-kindness grants the long-aged life,
and as far as grandchildren keeps the requital of righteousness owed to the
ancestors. Therefore through the patriarchs he thought the people born from
them afterward worthy of the calling and of his own visitation. Which he
showed through Moses, saying: Do not say: Because of my righteousnesses the
Lord chose me; for you are a stiff-necked people; but because of loving your
fathers. And you will understand otherwise also sons of the righteous men, the
zealots of their works. And he stretches his mercy through all; for always,
being good by nature of mercy, having as yokefellow to this mercy
righteousness upon those who keep his covenant, by which they were also
enrolled through virtue as heirs of the coming promises. For they do not
simply remember the commandments, but they do, for which they have also been
given; the Saviour also having said, He who has my commandments and does them,
he is the one who loves me. And he who inherits the promises is not he who
remembers, but he who has done the commandments. Thus for Jehu he kept the
kingship as far as a fourth generation, although he had not acquired perfect
piety, but had shown the zeal at the beginning of the kingship. Thus for the
divine David he kept unquenched the spark of the race, and this with many
impious having sprung up in the midst. This mercy, he says, he will stretch To
those who keep his covenant, and not simply those who remember his
commandments, but who set works upon the words, and who order their own life
according to these. The Lord in heaven prepared his throne, and his kingdom is
master of all.



It was said to be master of all, but not to reign, since not all are worthy of
his kingdom. Therefore to those who accomplish the evangelical life the
Saviour promised the kingdom of the heavens; but he also rules =55.645= the
unworthy, as Lord. And this is like what was said in another psalm: He who
dwells in the heights, and looks upon the lowly. For here too the prophetic
word teaches us that the Lord of all has the throne in heaven, but looks upon
and administers the creation, as being creator and king and Master of all.
Bless the Lord, all his angels, mighty in strength, doing his word, to hear
the voice of his words. 4. Reasonably he called the invisible powers into
partnership of the hymnody. For human nature is not sufficient to hymn the
benefactor worthily; but the incorporeal and holy natures, having a life free
of passions, are able to fulfill the divine commands. And the prophet also
says some other thought. The things according to you, he says, O soul, are
weak toward hymning God according to worth; yet let it do this in part. But
know that his angels, being mighty in strength, and doing his word without
falling, and being worthy to hear his voice and his words, bless him fittingly
and according to worth. And if among men there should be some pursuing an
equal-to-angel life, according to him who said, I can do all things in Christ
who strengthens me; let these also bless him; but let none of those who
transgress his word, and who disobey his voice, dare more rashly to do this.
Bless the Lord, all his powers, his ministers, doing his will; having said
angels, that he may not list in part the ranks in heaven, of which Paul
mentioned in part, saying, Above every principality and authority and power
and lordship and every name that is named; and again, Whether invisible or
visible, whether thrones or lordships or principalities or authorities; having
included all in one word, he called them powers. And having called those who
do his will ministers, he distinguished them from the homonymous contrary
powers. For he does not call these also to the hymn, the principalities of
this and the authorities and the world-rulers of this darkness and the
spiritual things of wickedness.

And he called all the heavenly ranks angels and powers; the one as serving the
divine commands and ferrying the divine words; and powers, as able to do what
is ordered and having received the powerful in their nature from him who made
them. Bless the Lord, all his works, in every place of his mastery, bless the
Lord, O my soul. Through these he persuades the soul not to shrink from the
hymn as falling far short of the heavenly. For not they alone, but all the
works of the Lord have been commanded in common to bless the Lord, each
according to power. For every place is fit for the hymn, and not heaven alone;
for through good deeds is the worthiness of blessing. And every soulless and
irrational thing, even if it seems harmful, as through unspeakable wisdom it
profits the whole, tends toward doxology of God, so that each according to the
worth of the place in which it is blesses God. Which then do, he says, O soul,
in whatever place you happen to spend time, whether on the earth or in a
better region. For there too bless with choirs of angels for heavenly and
perfect goods. But this does not even fit the Jews. For those, having been
commanded by those who enslaved them to sing from the odes of Sion, said,
doing what was lawful, How shall we sing the song of the Lord on a foreign
earth? But to us the divine Apostle charges that praying in every place we
lift holy hands. And this Master Christ said to the Samaritan woman: Amen,
amen, I say to you, woman, that an hour is coming and now is, when neither in
the place,



nor in Jerusalem, shall they worship the Father. For God is spirit, and those
who worship him must worship in spirit and truth. And Bless the Lord, O my
soul, the end is in accord with the prologue. For having called the
intelligible essences into partnership of the hymnody, he teaches us also, as
far as possible, to honor the Maker, and to offer a thanksgiving hymn measured
to the power, because to him is glory unto the ages. Amen.

PSALM 103.

1. The grace of the all-holy Spirit not only offers ethical and dogmatic
teaching to men, but also trains them accurately how one must hymn the Maker.
For this reason then it composed this psalm, using the blessed David as a
servant, so that all might learn with what hymns one must repay the
benefactor. Here however it goes through the common benefactions, and shows
the God of all both maker of all things together and administrator. Bless the
Lord, O my soul. God blesses a man, setting him in security and making him
more illustrious, as Abraham; but man not so, but then making himself more
illustrious. For whether God blesses, or a man blesses God, from both sides
the gain passes to the man; for the Divine is without need. And it teaches
each nursling of piety to hymn God the benefactor. Lord my God, how you have
been magnified exceedingly. Here too How is of intensity; and You have been
magnified exceedingly is not indicative of increase, but significative of the
unmeasured greatness; for so also Symmachus translated: Lord my God, you are
great exceedingly. For you were not small and then magnified; but being great
by nature, you were shown this to the pious. And how does he become great who
is always great, and needing no addition, but always being perfect? how is he
magnified? When those dedicated to him show an excellent life, when they hymn
him both through words and through works, then he is magnified, not himself
taking on something toward greatness, but being shown great to those who do
not know him through those who serve him, through great-working of creatures
and variety and providence. And the soul, rejoicing at so great a one, says:
Lord my God; for the voice is of one taking pride. And in what way he was
magnified he adds: You put on confession and magnificence. And Symmachus thus,
You clothed yourself with hymn and good fame. For no longer do those who enjoy
your goods fail to know you, nor do they offer your reverence to the idols,
but they hymn you, and pay you the debt of doxology. For even if the creation
of the angels, the stretching of the heavens, the founding of the earth, and
as many other things as the psalmist added to these, are sufficient for
showing the greatness of God, yet more sufficient than these is that the
Saviour became man and remained God, and suffered our things, and kept his own
impassibility. Therefore these things, being second in order, he nevertheless
ranked prior to the others, as being prior in power. He calls confession the
flesh from us, since through it he offered to God the confession on our
behalf, so as to cry to the Father: I confess to you, Lord of heaven and
earth, that you hid these things from wise and understanding, and as many such
things; and magnificence, since it was greatly and exceedingly fitting to save
our lump. But he also says that he put on this confession and magnificence,
not as about to put it off again, but so that it may be shown that, having
been incarnate, he was not changed into a man, but both remains God and
becomes man. Who wraps himself with



light as a garment. He wraps himself with light as a garment, as filled with
knowledge from every side, as having an incomprehensible essence. And light
also signifies the most unmixed knowledge of God; For he dwells in light,
according to Paul, unapproachable. For to dwell signifies being wholly
knowledge, being hard to think through for the intelligibles; for he called
the light unapproachable. For in accord with these the Apostle also says: Who
alone has immortality, who dwells in light unapproachable. For that light is
so great that no one dares to draw near; for the excess of the brightness
turns away the eyes. For if the sensible sun forces this upon those who try to
look insatiably, who is able to come into a thinking-through of the
unapproachable light? He himself then is also true light, and he wraps himself
with light as a garment, and he dwells in light unapproachable, and Cloud and
gloom are round about him, and he set darkness his hiding-place. And these are
not contrary to one another; for the unapproachable light, to those not able
to see, is the same as the gloom and the darkness; for neither the things in
this nor the things in that is it possible to behold; and both this and that
show the invisibility of the divine nature. Nevertheless John the theologian
also, narrating concerning the same economy, called it light, saying: And the
light shines in the darkness =55.647=, and the darkness did not overtake it.
This he puts on as a garment when he wholly covers and hides the power of the
godhead with the deficiencies of the flesh, such as hungering, thirsting,
growing weary, and enduring things like these. And he wraps himself with light
when he manifests his own godhead, rebuking the sea, shaking the earth,
darkening the sun; for he no longer allows the godhead to be hidden by the
humanity. Stretching out the heaven as a skin.

2. Through these he taught the ease of the creation. For as it is easy for a
man to stretch a skin and make a tent, so the God of all spread out the great
hollows of the heavens, using word alone. Who roofs his upper chambers in
waters. This the blessed Moses also taught; for he said God had said: Let
there be a firmament in the midst of the water, and let it be dividing between
water that is above the firmament, and between water that is below the
firmament. Who sets clouds his going-up, who walks upon wings of winds.
Through these he showed the providence that reaches everywhere. For he stands
over both winds and clouds, and he himself steers these and governs them, and
gives the need that grows from them in season. And he teaches at the same time
that the divine nature is present everywhere and stands over all. For since of
all sensibles the nature of the winds is the swiftest (for in an instant it
runs from the western parts to the dawn, and from the dawn toward evening),
not finding another more accurate image among sensibles, he said God is borne
upon wings of winds, hinting through these that he is present everywhere. And
we also find the God of all appearing to the sons of Israel in a cloud, and in
the tent making his own appearing through a cloud. And indeed when Solomon was
dedicating the house, a cloud covered the house; and Master Christ on the
mountain showed to the three apostles a bright cloud about himself; and when
he was being taken up, a bright cloud took him from their eyes. Who makes his
angels spirits, and his ministers a flaming fire. He showed him creator not of
the visible things only, but also of the invisible. And he named the same both
spirits and fire, teaching through each the powerful and the swift. For the
nature of spirit is sharp; but the energy of fire is strong. And the God of
all, using angels as servants, both benefits the worthy and punishes the
contrary. For this reason he also mentioned fire, signifying the



punitive energy. Who founds the earth upon its security. And Aquila and
Symmachus, Upon its seat. What security, O prophet, where so much water lies
underneath and a vast sea that bears it upon its back? For elsewhere also
showing this he said: Who made the earth firm upon the waters. For since the
foundation was not secure, but very rotten, he who made it set the security in
it. And what is this security? The power of him who worked it. For in his hand
are the ends of the earth. And he calls earth the man. This one then he founds
upon the security of the faith, who shall not be inclined unto the age of the
age, that is, in the coming. It shall not be inclined unto the age of the age.
For having seated it upon itself he gave it the unmoved; and it remains in
this way, as long as he himself should will. So also elsewhere he says:
Hanging the earth upon nothing. Abyss, as a garment, his covering. He set His
instead of hers; for so also Aquila and Theodotion translated: You wrapped an
abyss about her as a garment. And the divine Scripture calls abyss the moist
essence. So also at the beginning of the creation the blessed Moses said: And
the earth was invisible and unfurnished, and darkness upon the abyss. Since
then the earth is held round by water from every side, and the great and
unnavigable seas are its limits, and other navigable ones cut it through the
middle, he reasonably said the abyss lies upon the earth as a garment. And
otherwise you will find the abyss set for incomprehensibility, in, Your
judgments, Lord, are a great abyss. For his judgments being unsearchable,
there is no finding a comprehension of them; therefore they are compared not
=55.648= simply to an abyss, but to a great one. And God then, whose judgments
are a great abyss, incomprehensibility covers after the manner of a covering,
being named abyss. Waters shall stand upon the mountains. From your rebuke
they shall flee, from the voice of your thunder they shall be afraid. They go
up mountains, and go down plains into a place which you founded for them.
Through all he teaches the providence of God standing over the creation. For
this reason the sea, imitating the peaks of the mountains, will not flood the
mainland with the waves, but as we cower at the thunder, so she the lying de
dien bound. And Waters shall stand upon the mountains i.e., it has stood as a
mountain; and They go up mountains i.e., as mountains. I think he signifies by
these the clouds above the peak of the mountains. For the clouds especially
love to haunt these places, so that the inhabited parts may not be darkened
continually; Which, whenever you will, he says, all receive their own place,
and are dissolved, and become a linen cloth. And otherwise he calls mountains
the prophets because of the height of contemplation. In them waters stand,
with which those who thirst are given to drink the water of teaching. And of
what rebuke? That against the Jews. For another prophet shows this; for he
says, And I will give them not a famine of bread, nor a thirst of water, but a
famine of hearing a word of the Lord. For when God is angry the waters of the
prophets grow scarce, since they do not possess the supply from themselves.
And call voice of thunder that of the Gospel, which the waters of the prophets
do not flee, but they are afraid because of the superiority of the knowledge
in them. You set a bound which it shall not pass, nor shall they turn to cover
the earth.

3. The sea is ashamed before the sand, and raging as far as that, being
hindered as by a bridle by the divine bound, it is checked again and goes
backward. Or this: for neither do the prophets announce all their revelations;

cover the earth, that is, the man not able to contain; and Paul witnesses
saying: I heard unspeakable



words, which it is not lawful for a man to speak. Who sends forth springs in

proof of the divine providence, that it fills not only the need of men, but
also of the irrational. For this he cut mountains, and crafted a passage for
the waters, so that not only men may have the streams of the springs

Therefore they are also given to drink with the prophetic and apostolic

ass free? and who loosed his bonds? Watering mountains from his upper

to bubble from below, but also from above through clouds he offers the
irrigation. And he calls upper chambers of God the air, as of one house of the

herb for the service of men, to bring bread out of the earth. For the =55.649=
fodder grows, and nourishes the cattle that have come to be for the need of
men. And the fruits also are fed through the rains, and become ripe, and are

assigning to the others that which sprouts of itself. Except that both the
green herb and the grass can have been given to both. And he mentioned neither
beasts nor reptiles in these, because these use another food. For some eat
earth, others flesh. And wine gladdens a heart of man, to make a face cheerful
with oil; and bread strengthens a heart of man.

4. And Symmachus said more clearly: Making grass sprout for the cattle, and
green herb for the service of men, for food to grow from earth, and wine to

to strengthen



and nourish the nature of men, and with wine to gladden and make life more

from the wine assigned. For the word of God, as it is a vine, so also is an
olive. For I, he says, as a fruitful olive in the house of God. For his
teaching according to a different relation is both wine and oil and bread. The
trees of the plain shall be filled. But the Hebrew and the rest have set, Of
the Lord. Having gone through the fruitful, he necessarily mentions also the
unfruitful, as these too offering a necessary need to men. For this he also
named them of the Lord, as self-grown and not handmade, but having sprouted by
the divine word. And with what shall they be filled? With knowledge of God,
clearly. And how are they filled? By meditation of the law. Therefore he also
called them trees of the Lord, since those who meditate the law day and night
are said to be a tree planted by the outlets of the waters. The cedars of
Lebanon, which you planted, there sparrows shall nest. The dwelling of the
heron leads them. And Aquila thus: There birds shall nest. As of necessary
things after the fruitful he mentions also the unfruitful. And through Lebanon
a notable mountain and its cedars he has shown the remaining mountains and
trees of the earth. And these trees compose houses for men, and receive the

fit for dwelling, and he arrives everywhere as into a house prepared
beforehand. For this is, It leads them. And he calls sparrows the souls

delivered as a sparrow from the snare of hunters. And you would not err

said not to wish to be separated from those who possess him. This one leads
the sparrows; for he shows them how one must leap over the snares of the
hunters, he himself also having been snared by the denial, through repentance
fled the snare. The high mountains for the deer; a rock a refuge for the
hares. Providence, he says, does not overlook even the small of the living

the weak of the living things would easily be caught by the stronger, and they

sleep. And through the rocks he signifies both caves and dens, into which

the intelligible serpents, according to what was said by Christ: Behold I have

ranked among the unclean living things and the weak. Moses then, The camel and

intelligible rock, the Church, through which they are strengthened and
cleansed. And deer, since they happen to be clean living things according to

He made a moon for seasons. Having gone through all things according to earth,
and having shown the creation of the invisible natures, and

5. For the night works rest for men, but furnishes license to the beasts, so

belongs to the rational; but to seek, even of the irrational. But nevertheless
to these too God furnishes the necessary food. And beasts of the thicket and
cubs he calls the demons, who at the time of the Master's cross were roaring,

Peter denying, the remaining apostles scattered. They roared then to seize and
to seek from God food for themselves; first to seize; and not having succeeded
in the seizure, to seek. For they do not even hesitate from shamelessness to
demand the righteous from God into trials. Or did not the devil demand Job?
And he demanded the apostles also from Christ, so that the Lord said: Simon,
Simon, how often did Satan demand to sift you as the wheat? But I begged
concerning you, that your faith may not fail. The sun rose, and they were
gathered, and they shall lie down in their folds. When the sun rises some go
to their own dens; but men, having put off the toil of the day before
yesterday, again spend the day eagerly at the works. For when Christ had risen
=55.651= they withdrew. And Symmachus instead of They were gathered wrote They
shall withdraw. And where did they withdraw? Into their own folds, where they
lie down denning like reptiles. Man shall go out upon his work, and upon his
working until evening. Reasonably; since the resurrection of Christ gave those
who are truly men to have confidence, and urged them until evening, that is,
the consummation of life, to work their own work, that is, virtue. For this
God set us for working. How your works were magnified, Lord; you made all
things in wisdom. The prophet, having reckoned each of the things said, and
having learned the much care of God, sent up the hymn in the midst of the
narration, having said that all the products of God are wonderful and
exceedingly admirable and full of wisdom. For he found even the night,
slandered by some impious, exceedingly most useful, and the unfruitful trees
bringing in another need,



and the kinds of the beasts suited in many things to men. And wonderful is the
How, and wisdom, in which he made all things, is the Lord; for Christ is power
of God and wisdom of God, Which, as exceeding and exceedingly hidden, none of

crucified the Lord of glory, as Paul says. The earth was filled with your
creation; i.e., You filled this with goods of every kind, that is, of the

altogether consumed by those. With the sea then according to us he compares
the human life because of the unstable and ever-moving of the affairs in it;
and the innumerable kind of reptiles, the manifold and as it were many-faced
multitude of men, the variation being understood unto the varied of the habit.
For nature is the same for all, and the manner of coming-to-be is not other;
but the mind is many-cleft, and the heart in us of many judgments. And knowing
this someone said: As faces are not like faces, so neither the thoughts of
men. And we say that ships going through in this sea are the holy Churches of

shipbuilding art and steering knowledge we convey from one another the things

conversed toward Job: There is not upon the earth a like



to him made to be played with by the angels, that is, the righteous. For
these, having gone up through virtue even into the rank of angels, play with
him who through wickedness fell from that rank. And the Lord gave authority to
his own disciples to tread upon serpents and scorpions, and upon all the power
of the Enemy. And elsewhere he says: You crushed the heads of the dragons upon
the water. And reasonably in the word about the sea he mentioned this,
inasmuch as all our life also is somewhat briny, and is blown about from every
side and is moved together with wild waves. All things look toward you to give
their food in good season. From you, he says, all things convey the need in
season. For the irrational seek the food, but do not know the supplier; yet
they receive this from the Maker. When you give, they shall gather. When you
furnish the abundance, each of these enjoys the things suitable. And when you
open the hand, all things shall be filled with goodness. Through these he
showed the ease of the supply of the goods. For as it is easy for those who
have bent inward to stretch fingers, so it is easy for God to grant the

stretching on the cross, from which all things were filled with goodness, that

when you turn your face, they shall be troubled. And as you fill those who

away, all things are filled with trouble and fear. You will take away their

will, the separation of soul and body also comes to be, after which the body

spirit here he named the soul. You will send forth your spirit, and they shall
be created; and you will renew the face of the earth. Here he clearly

the widow, having breathed three times upon the child, bringing him back to
life through the indwelling spiritual grace. Thus Elisha also made alive the
child of the Shunammite, using the spiritual inbreathing, and working the life

the ages. Always, he says, it is just that he be hymned by all. The Lord shall
be glad upon his works. Through these he foretold the coming knowledge of God
of men. For when men have been released from the former deceit, and have
received the recognition of God, God is glad, not being worshipped, but seeing
them being saved. Who looks upon the earth, and makes it tremble; who touches
the mountains, and they smoke. Reasonably he added these things, teaching the
without-need of the divine nature. For he is not delighted as needing to be
worshipped, but as wishing to save, he shows the truth, and being able to
punish, he does not bring on the punishment, although by the appearing alone

also did on the mountain Sinai; =55.653= for having made his own appearing, he
showed the whole mountain smoking. I will sing to the Lord in my life, I will

Symmachus called the meditation Discourse; and Aquila, Conversation. And I

PSALM 104.

Alleluia.

This psalm also exhorts to hymn the God of all. And the inscription also shows
it. For Alleluia is translated, Praise the Lord. And it reminds of the
promises that have come to be from God toward the patriarchs, and of the goods
furnished through those by him to their descendants; for the memory of both
these and those is sufficient to spur and sharpen those who have enjoyed so
many goods into zeal of the virtue of the ancestors; and it teaches at the
same time also the new people that he did not unjustly strip that ungrateful
people of his own care. For since they were styled a people of God, and openly
enjoyed the divine providence, but later became utterly deserted of this,
through the psalms set forth he teaches all men how many things he benefited
them, and how ungrateful those became concerning the benefactions; so that at
once all might learn the justice of the vote brought out against those, and
seeing the wages of the ingratitude, might not walk the same road as those,
but might travel the straight. And they say the translation of Alleluia in
these things is, Praise to God Iao; and Iao the Hebrews left as some naming
untranslated to God. Necessarily then this leads the present psalm. For the
things in it tend toward doxology of God, as they have come to be, so also
being said. First then the present psalm happens to be of those inscribed
Alleluia. And I think it has this inscription, because it happens to be of a
character foreign beside the psalms and the things about the ode. For
throughout these seem to me to contain praises and hymns unto God; and
reasonably; for Alleluia is translated, Praise the Lord; neither struck
through a psaltery, nor said through an ode, but being this very thing,
praises and hymns, and containing the benefactions of God unto men.



1. Confess to the Lord, and call upon his name. In this psalm the Holy Spirit
commands the holy apostles to make manifest to the people from the nations
=55.654= all the wonder-workings which the Lord has done at his presence. And
Call upon, Symmachus translated Preach. And the word exhorts to offer the
thanksgiving hymnody, and to narrate the divine benefactions. Declare among
the nations his works. It is clear and plain that he works benefit not for
Jews only, but also for all the nations. Sing to him, and psalm to him;
narrate all his wonders. Sing, furnishing the tongues unto utterance of the
hymns; And psalm, understanding the things being melodized, repay him with
hymns and melodies, and teach those who do not know the wonders that have come
to be from him. Be praised in his holy name. And Aquila set Boast, instead of
Be praised. And the word exhorts not to think great upon wealth, not upon
strength, not upon power, but in the knowledge and providence of God. This the
divine Apostle also charged; For he who boasts, he says, let him boast in the
Lord. These things the most wise Anna also goes through: Let not the wise
boast in his wisdom. Let the heart of those who seek the Lord be glad. Having
begun from confession he ended in gladness. For a beginning of progressing is
to declare one's own sins, repenting through good fruits, according to, Make
fruits worthy of the repentance. For then we shall have confidence to call
upon his name, and we shall obtain the gifts from him, from which we ourselves
have known, benefiting others also; then, having been thought worthy of the
cheerfulness according to God, we shall be able to sing to him and psalm, and
thereafter to interpret the unspeakable things of the divine Scripture. For
these are his wonders. For then, as having accomplished so many things, we
shall be praised in his name. For gladness is a fruit of the hope unto God.
Seek the Lord, and be strengthened. And teaching how one must do this, he
added: Seek his face continually. For not once or twice, but through all the
life it is fitting to seek the help from above, and to reap the benefit; for
those who seek shall be unconquerable and undefeated. Ask, and it shall be
given you; seek, and you shall find; knock, and it shall be opened to you. For
everyone who asks receives, and he who seeks finds, and to him who knocks it
shall be opened. And what is seeking? To think that one continually. For he
who seeks has on the mind the thing sought. And he stirs the heralds of the
nations unto seeking the Lord; for from there they are strengthened, as being
in need of might toward doing the things commanded, and having preached to all
Greeks and barbarians, according to, The Lord will give a word to those who
evangelize with much power. And to seek continually is opposed to that
according to Moses, to appear three times of the year before him. And as
according to the history Samuel, ministering in the tent of the witness, saw
the face of God, so those progressing always in examination of dogmas and in
practice of the virtues seek his face continually, wishing to see this one

invisible God; therefore also he who sees him immediately sees the Father.
Remember his wonders which he did; his portents, and the judgments of his
mouth.

2. Do not give over to forgetfulness the unspeakable wonder-workings which the

things judged with the justice of a



trainer. And it seems to me here to mean the laws, those in heaven, those on
earth, those in the sea, those under earth, those under heaven. For not man
alone received a law, but all things that are seen; the sea, not to overstep
the shores; the mountains, to have been fixed; the earth, to stand; and all
things alike are administered by their own laws. And all these things came to
be by a command Seed of Abraham his servants, sons of Jacob his chosen. He
calls the same both this and that, as some having been styled descendants, and
others sons. And he named them chosen, as having been called a people of God
beside all the nations. And this he also showed through the things that
follow. Since not all those from =55.655= Israel, these are Israel; nor as

administers the whole world by divine judgments. He remembered his covenant

Hebrews toward his own knowledge. And the causes of the signs he revealed to
those about Moses alone, having shown a rational comprehension as they alone
are rational, so as to know through what cause he sometimes changed the waters
of the Egyptians, and sometimes each thing, and why it came to be according to
this order, and of what things these are images or symbols. For since not all
are able from themselves and through themselves to withdraw from the harmful,
to those thus weak God sends those who are able, guides and leaders of the
road that bears unto the goods. And these are: the law of God, Moses, and the
high-priestly word, whose symbol is Aaron. And those moved the tongue, and
lent the mouth to God and threw out the sayings; but all the power was sent
from him. He sent forth darkness and darkened, because they embittered his
words. And the admirable servants did not contradict the divine commands, but
they did the things ordered, and they brought on them the longest and
three-day night, or rather a darkness gloomier than night. For night, even if
it is moonless, is mixed with the brightness of the stars; but that darkness
the divine Scripture calls palpable. For God the guardian of the people did
not punish the Egyptians through some great things; for the all-powerful hand
was not at a loss to send on them a sudden wrath, lions, bears, famine, or
plague, or death; but through what he said he punished, also gently calling
them to repentance; and the greatest maltreatment of the people was not
through clay



and brickmaking simply, but through turning aside unto worship of idols.
Therefore little by little he punished those who led into the error, bringing
these too into recognition of the Master of all, through which the creation
served the scourges. Air into darkness, and the water into blood, and the

obeyed eagerly, having been spread under Egypt alone. The Scripture at any

what was it that distinguished days and nights, there being one stretched

perceive this plague; which also happened upon the other plagues. He changed
their waters into blood, and killed their fishes. For not the color of the

creeping like the destroyed infants, and entering the houses, and going as far

treasuries, so as to show the greatness of the wonder, that not even a
multitude of household servants or soldiers, which it is likely spend time in
the palaces, was able to drive off the frogs. He said, and dog-fly came, and
gnats in all their borders. For the works followed the things said =55.659=,
and immediately God commanding, and Moses saying, both dog-fly and gnats
filled the earth, not passing the borders of Egyptians, nor bringing the ruin
upon other men, but bringing the discipline upon the god-opposing. For this
the In all their borders signifies, i.e., outside the borders of the Egyptians
nothing such coming to be was to be seen. He set their rains hail, a fire

For this reason then both their vines and fig-trees, and the other kinds of
plants, were utterly corrupted by them. He said, and locust came and

Gilead is a name of a place, and Manasseh an appellation of a tribe. But also
to Ephraim I shall give back the former power. Judah however I shall prepare
to reign of all the tribes. For after the return the tribes have remained
undivided; and Zerubbabel led both these and those; and properly and truly
Master Christ, who sprouted from Judah according to flesh, reigns of all the
creation. Moab is a cauldron of my hope; upon Idumaea I shall cast my sandal,
to me foreigners were subjected. And I shall furnish such strength to my
people, as even to master the foreigners, and to take Moabites and Idumaeans
as subjects. And these things have been said more accurately in the
fifty-ninth psalm. More whitely and more clearly Aquila and the fifth edition
translated, among whom it has been said: Moab is a cauldron of my washing,
that is, a vessel of my washing. And I know having heard a word handed down in
secret by some Hebrew; for he said it is shown mystically through the things
set forth the birth according to flesh of Christ from the race of Moab, taken
through the Moabitess Ruth, from whom comes David the king, from whose seed
according to flesh Christ has been born. And it seems to me that God through



these things also promises to Moab the grace of the calling through the
regeneration of the washing. Since then he promises such things to Moab, he
leaves us to understand also concerning the remaining nations the like. And
the proclamation beforehand of what follows shows it. For Idumaeans were from
the hated Esau. I shall therefore cast my sandal upon that country itself of
the hated; I shall therefore step upon the former enemies, ranking them in a
country of friends. Who shall lead me away into a city of enclosure? or who
shall guide me as far as Idumaea; Is it not you, who thrust us away? The
prophet, having learned the calling of the nations, also through the 59th
psalm blesses those thought worthy to see the things about the Jews, praying
to be one of them. And Of enclosure, Symmachus rendered Fenced around, which
is, the Church. And he prays also to come into Idumaea, upon which he will
stretch the sandal and the grace that will be given to the nations. And Is it
not you, Symmachus rendered If not you, O God, who no longer allies with us,
but made us slaves. For we are not defeated, you being our general. Except
even then by your power, others having been cast off, we shall be of the
blessed portion being strengthened through you. Therefore he also added: And
will you not go out, O God, in our powers? And Symmachus: And not having gone
forward God in our campaignings? For we were never defeated, you being general
and leading the phalanx. Give us help from affliction, Lord; and vain is
salvation of a man. In God we shall do power, and he himself shall set at
nought our enemies. Pause then the painful things, and stretch your
inclination; for from nowhere else is it possible to obtain this. For all the
alliance from men, you not cooperating, is vain and useless. For this reason
then we call upon your providence. For through this alone we shall be able to
prevail over the hostile, in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be glory unto the
ages. Amen.
