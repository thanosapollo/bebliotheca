* On the Nativity of Christ

Of John, Archbishop of Constantinople, on the Nativity of our Lord Jesus
Christ.
Many and numberless are the God-loving festivals of the Christians. Yet
what is more festal than the present, in that the Master Christ was born
and the world was born again? Christ was born and Adam was recalled;
Christ was born and Eve was redeemed from sorrow; Christ was born and
the dragon was made to vanish; Christ was born and paradise was renewed;
Christ was born and the devil was condemned; Christ was born and Hades
was changed; Christ was born and the earth was created anew; Christ was
born and the air was cleansed; Christ was born and heaven was gladdened;
Christ was born and Judaism was diminished; Christ was born and
Christianity was made dense; Christ was born and the synagogue was
filled with smoke; Christ was born and the Church was made fragrant;
Christ was born and the light was added.
For today there is both an increase of bodily light and an illumination
of the spiritual.
For when Christ, the Sun of righteousness, rose from the virgin's
undefiled bridal chamber, the darkness of impiety was driven away and
the light of piety was spread abroad; the frost of blasphemy was melted,
the fruit of the knowledge of God grew fat, and the star of truth rose
upon all. Therefore the prophets, beholding this light from many ages
past, cried out to him, saying: In your light we shall see light. What
is "In your light we shall see light"? In you, he says, we shall
contemplate the Father; for you are light that makes light known.
How then do we keep festival today, when the immaterial light, Christ,
has immaterially peered forth from the unbreached virgin fountain? No
longer do we grope after idols, but we contemplate the altars; no longer
do we honor ventriloquists, but we call upon the prophets; no longer do
we serve enchanters, but we praise the apostles; no longer do we
practice idolatry under a tree, but under the Cross we confess the Lord;
no longer do we sacrifice our children to the demons, but we read our
hearts, and we offer the Gospels as incense to God; no longer do we
acknowledge mythologies; no longer do we exalt Plato and Aristotle, but
we glorify Peter and Paul; no longer do we circumcise the flesh, but we
seal the soul; no longer do we eat Jewish bitter herbs, but we swallow
the honey of grace; no longer, as captives in Babylon, do we say: How
shall we sing the Lord's song in a strange land? but, rejoicing
everywhere upon the earth, we cry: The earth is the Lord's, and the
fullness thereof; no longer, keeping festival in part, do we chirp
poorly, saying: God is known only in Judah, but, celebrating the
birthday universally, we sing richly in response: All nations, clap your
hands, shout to God with a voice of exultation.
No longer call upon Baal, but entreat God. That one babbles; this one
works wonders. That one sleeps; this one watches. All nations, clap your
hands, shout to God with a voice of exultation. What is "shout"? It
stands for "give thanks to Christ the maker of victory"; for a shout is
the acknowledgment of victory.
Let us therefore shout to Christ the maker of victory, and let us not
worship the falsely named idols, because all the gods of the nations are
demons, but the Lord made the heavens.
You heard just now the evangelist Matthew saying: When Jesus was born in
Bethlehem of Judea in the days of Herod, behold, magi came from the East
to Jerusalem, saying: Where is he who has been born king of the Jews?
For we have seen his star in the East and have come to worship him.
O the strange wonder, O the fearful mercy-seat, O the incomprehensible
mystery. In Bethlehem of Judea the Master Christ is born, and before all
he is seen by magi; in the days of Herod, and the beginningless one
takes a beginning of days; he is born in a cave, and with his hand he
bears all things; he is laid in a manger, and he becomes the food of
all; he is wrapped in swaddling clothes, and he grants the release of
sins; he comes forth from a womb, and he keeps the mother a virgin; he
flees into Egypt, and he leads up into the heavens; he is carried as a
child, and he is known as God; he bends Archelaus, and he strikes the
devil; he summons magi, and he thrusts away Jews; he feeds dogs, and he
strikes the children.
But when you hear these things, do not condemn the Master Christ of a
lack of compassion, but the Jews of sloth. For the order was reversed
not by nature but by purpose, and the dogs have become children, and the
children dogs. For the name was changed, since the manner of life was
also altered. Such a dog was the Canaanite woman, licking the idolatrous
meat-markets, and such a daughter she became when she loved Christ and
the martyric patterns of the apostles. For when she attended to
Hellenism, then the Lord said to her in reproach: It is not good to take
the children's bread and throw it to the little dogs; but when she was
added to Christianity, then she heard in praise: Take courage, daughter;
let it be to you as you will. And that the children of the Jews also
were named dogs instead of children, you need not learn from me, but
from the blessed Paul, who had himself come from being a dog: Beware of
the dogs, beware of the evil workers. And that the Hellenic dogs both
recognized and ran to the Master Christ more than the Jewish children,
take the proof from what has just been read.
The magi were Gentiles and the first-fruits of evils, yet as soon as
they saw the star in the East they ran down at once to Jerusalem and
endured toil, that they might behold him who said: Come to me, all who
labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. And they not only
endured toils, but they also brought gifts to the Master Christ, though
they had not yet read him who said: You shall not appear before your God
empty. See the faith of the magi. The manger did not scandalize them;
the cave did not lead them into unbelief; they did not say to
themselves, shaken by the word of doubt: Is this the king of the Jews?
How so? Instead of purple he is wrapped in rags; instead of a
gold-spread couch he is thrown upon the ground; instead of royal halls
he has taken a hyena's cave. Where is the throng of spearmen? Where is
the attendance of shield-bearers? Where is the fragrance of gladness? No
one sits beside him; one woman alone attends, and she herself appears
afraid lest the child born be known. He has none of the needful things;
we see nothing kingly in him; this is the offspring of some poor man.
Let us go out, let us turn back, let us look at the star with care, lest
we worship one in place of another and lose the gain. But the magi
neither said nor thought any of these things; they only saw the Master,
and at once they recognized him and worshiped and brought gifts. For it
was not the poverty of the cave that persuaded them, but the richness of
the star; the paltry sight of the Lord's appearance did not lead them
into unbelief, but the flashing brightness of the Godhead that shone
around him persuaded them.
Some will surely say: What then? Could this prophecy not have been made
by Jews, but by Gentile magi? Why then, they say, did the God of all
make this prophecy through magi? Why? Listen with understanding. For the
condemnation of the Jews. For since, as soon as they heard a prophet
concerning the incarnate coming of the Lord, they at once committed
murder, for this reason the Lord was well pleased to make the prophecy
through Gentile magi, that the Jews might know from this that the
blessing of Abraham has been transferred to the nations.
Hence the Lord also, signifying this from many ages past, blessing
Abraham, said: Multiplying I will multiply your seed as the stars of
heaven and as the sand beside the lip of the sea. He names the sons of
Israel sand, because of the barrenness of the soul and the saltness of
their knowledge, but the nations stars, because of the brightness of
knowledge. Hence also the blessed Paul, writing to the nations, said:
among whom you shine as lights in the world. So the magi, coming to
Jerusalem, asked the Jews, saying: Where is he who has been born king of
the Jews? For we have seen his star in the East and have come to worship
him. Where is he who has been born king of the Jews? Either announce, or
hear. The one born is a king; the gift is heavenly; the star is
rational; the grace is from the East; the message is catholic; the star
is analogous to the one born. In vain is the herald hidden, who cannot
be covered. And just as this sun cannot be hidden by anyone, for it
shines everywhere each day, so also the Master Christ, the Sun of
righteousness, being God, will be believed everywhere. To him be the
glory unto the ages. Amen.
