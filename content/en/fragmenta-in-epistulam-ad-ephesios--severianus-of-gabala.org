* Fragments on the Epistle to the Ephesians

:CUSTOM_ID: h1

Just as he wrote to the Romans when he had not yet seen them, so also to the
Ephesians, John having gone before and laid down the preaching among them,
opportunely exhorting them not to be held by the law. Since the apostles,
living in Jerusalem, were keeping the law because of those who had believed
from the circumcision, then since John had theologized sufficiently and left
nothing lacking of the proclamations unto knowledge of God, as far as was
possible for men, he comes to present the economy of the incarnation, the
greatness proper to it, clothing it also through words worthily of the
realities 305 and taking up the more difficult; for the dignity of the Godhead
was proper and confessed, but to narrate worthily the incarnation, lowly
according to what appears, was of spiritual power. Eph 1:1 Here too the
"through" belongs also to God according to Paul, and the Father has not been
diminished. And he reasonably says "through the will of God," that he may show
that as God willed John to be an apostle, so also me. And he writes this, that
the things said by him may be trustworthy. Eph 1:2 If each has been said by
catachresis, both "Father" and "Lord," the Son will be confessed greater than
the Father, if the one has the name of Father, and the other of Lord. But the
cause of these expressions has been confessed; for since he said "of our
Father," and he is Father of Christ, lest the same name be laid both upon the
Only-begotten and upon the faithful, and either drag the Son down contrary to
nature, or raise those up above nature, having said "of our Father" and
showing that the appellation is by grace, he added: and of the Lord Jesus
Christ; for it was not possible, the appellation being of a common nature,
that the dignity also be common. Why then is the Father Lord of these, but not
also himself Lord of the Son, if indeed the appellation was catachrestic upon
both? Eph 1:4 If then the saints were appointed unto adoption in Christ, the
Son is not adoptive, but Son by nature. Eph 1:5-6 If according to the rule the
will is an energy, what is the good pleasure of this will? For he would not
have said "will of will." Unto the praise of the glory of his grace. I have
already said beforehand that he adorns his own word with the dignity of the
economy. 306 Eph 1:10 Since the one formed fell through the disobedience, and
when men had acted impiously the heavenly things were alienated from the
earthly, Christ having come recapitulated all things. And after these things
he says this otherwise, saying: having made peace of the things in the heavens
and the things upon the earth. For he himself is our peace, who made both one,
the Jews and the Greeks, through faith in Christ. Eph 1:11 Clearly here he
says energy is one thing and will another. Having said "of him who works all
things according to the counsel," he said "of the will," that he may show the
will as decided. Whatever one confirmed from good counsel, these things have
been appointed by judgment; speaking, he used the expressions. Eph 1:13 You
too, he says, are of those foreknown, being appropriated to Christ through
obedience and the faith upon the hearing and the sealing upon the faith, which
is likeness toward Christ through participation of the Spirit. But from whom
did they hear the word of truth? Or from John the evangelist? For he was
preaching there. Wherefore Paul also discourses with them more loftily, they
having been furnished from that one's teaching. Eph 1:14,17-21 The redemption
is of the acquisition; for that we may be acquired and appropriated to God, we
are redeemed, being mindful of the blood by which we were redeemed and of the
whole economy. It is clear that he means God of Christ according to the flesh;
for nowhere here did he mention the dignity of the Godhead, but of the economy
according to us, which is confessed to belong to the flesh. And often
Scripture calls the very essence of God glory; for since it is ungraspable and
unattainable, it named it glory, that you may not demand an account but
worship. First Ezekiel calls the essence of God glory, having seen God sitting
in visions, and adding: this is the likeness of the glory of the Lord. Which
God interpreting elsewhere says: I multiplied visions 307 and was likened in
the hands of prophets. The glory then which Ezekiel spoke, this God says as
the "I." And Paul says: who being the radiance of the glory (of what glory?)
and the impress of his hypostasis. Here then also he means "God of our Lord
Jesus Christ" according to the flesh, but "Father of glory" according to
nature. And every name that is named, not only in this age but also in the one
to come. For in this age we name thrones, principalities, lordships,
authorities, powers, angels, archangels; but since there are myriad ranks of
invisible powers unknown to us and for this reason unnameable, and after the
resurrection they will be known and will receive with the knowledge also the
appellation from us, for this reason he said: and in the one to come. Eph
1:22-23 And he gave him as head over all things to the church, which is his
body, the fullness of him who fills all in all. Through this he made clear
that he calls the body of Christ the fullness of God, that is, the church. But
why fullness? Because it has been filled with God; for just as, he says, he
fills all things in all, so also in the church he is filled according to its
being the body of Christ. The church then is body of Christ, but fullness of
God. Of him who fills all in all. For he is in heaven, that is, working not as
on earth, and he is on earth, not working as in heaven, and in angels, in the
sun, but in each according to its nature; for Scripture speaks of God from the
things of which we partake, not from the things which he himself is; for this
is incomprehensible. Eph 2:2-3 World is said in two ways, both the creative
constitution and the wicked system of men. And now he divided the unlike
things, having called the wickedness and the sin of men "age," and "world" the
constitution of the whole, as also in the epistle to the Galatians he says:
that he might deliver us from the present evil age. He means this present age.
The common 308 apostasy of men then and error he named "age," of which those
now believing in Christ were once also a part. According to the ruler of the
authority of the air of the spirit. Clearly through this the apostle shows
that the devil was not an angel from heaven, but some aerial wicked spirit.
And even if the Lord says: I saw Satan as lightning falling from heaven, he
does not show the place from which he fell, but since lightning falls swiftly
from above, he compared the swiftness of the diabolic fall to lightning,
saying: as lightning is borne swiftly from heaven, so I saw his fall as swift.
Of him who now works. He made clear that the devil does not always have the
strength, but the weakness of men has become strength for him. Doing the wills
of the flesh and of the thoughts. From this it is shown that the flesh is not
opposed to the soul; for it was in accord unto sin. And we were by nature
children of wrath. Just as sons of gehenna, so also children of wrath. Eph
2:10 Since he said "created," he says: the creation is appropriation. And he
added "upon good works," that you may thereafter build yourself. Eph 2:13 He
did not say "nearer" than those who believed from circumcision, but "near."
Eph 2:14-15α Having broken down the middle wall of the fence, not the fence,
but the middle wall. And what is the fence? I will take away his fence and it
shall be for plunder, that is the 309 security of the Spirit. And what is the
middle wall? The unmingling toward the Greeks; for Jews were forbidden to mix
with Greeks, lest the Jews fall into idolatry, they who even without this were
practicing idolatry. And this is what David says: God settles the solitary in
a house. For the leaven had to become strong, that thus the whole of the world
might be leavened. The law of the commandments he means the customary one, as
from the Mosaic commandments. In dogmas, his own, having abolished, of which
is: not the things that enter make the man common (and this was undoing "eat
this, do not eat that"), and not giving a bill of divorce (this was contrary
to putting away a wife for every cause), and "you observe days and months and
seasons and years" (it was contrary to the things Moses gave). And altogether
there are many such dogmatic things by which he abolished the law of the
commandments, not the commandments as absurd, but the custom as no longer
useful. For the commandments are honored even if they are not in operation,
but the law thereafter, that is, our being under the law of the commandments,
was harming after the grace. Eph 2:15β-17 For Greeks withdrawing from idolatry
as from impiety, and Jews from the law as no longer useful, are built up into
some other newness of piety. Making peace. For the law does not want there to
be peace. Why? Lest the bodily peace raise war against piety. He will
reconcile both to God. Since the Greeks were wandering, and the Jews had
transgressed. Having slain the enmity in himself. The thought has breadth in
brevity of expression; for since it was the law that was separating Jews from
Greeks according to what was said before, and the law was about to cease, and
it did not cease otherwise than because the Lord rose from the dead, to whom
the church had to be joined as to another husband after the law, he says that
in his flesh he slew that enmity, having made the law cease. 310 And having
come he preached peace to you who were far off and to those near. This shows
that he does not call the law of Moses impious as idolatry is; for he says the
idolaters are far off, and the Jews near. And the "near" shows both a part of
piety and not the perfect; for if they had the perfect, they were not near,
but in it. And if again they were contrary, they were not near, but far off
with the Greeks. Eph 3:2-3 Here he wishes to show that he did not throw
himself upon them as upon strangers and those taught by others, but that
having been chosen as herald of the nations he owed the proof to these also,
and that as the Lord taught those around John, so also me according to
revelation. And he hinted that he was able to say something deeper, but he
measured the teaching toward what they could contain. Eph 3:10 That it might
now be made known to the principalities. Reasonably; for if it had been hidden
in God, all the authorities were ignorant. For he does not mean by "now" the
time of grace, but from when prophets began to reveal the things that would
be. Eph 3:15 In the heavens, he says, is the way of life of the just, but upon
earth that of all men; for the just are not ranked upon the earth with the
living, but the generation of those dedicated to God is as it were a
generation set apart. And this generation is called heavenly as pure, but that
of the rest earthly as it were soiled. And it must be noted that from the
things by which the apostle shows the name of the Father, it did not go up
from us above, but came from above to us, clearly as being by nature and not
by name only; for the catachrestic must altogether be after the natural, and
of the natural first that from above, then that from below. And it must be
noted that he said "patria" as of kinship, not "phratria"; for everywhere the
divine Scripture calls kinships "patriae." 311 Eph 3:18-19 He shaped it as it
were more typically into a type of the cross; for depth and height and breadth
and length, what else would it be than the nature of the cross? And he seems
somewhere to speak of the cross as double, not simply. But since the Lord's
economy is Godhead from above and humanity from below, and the apostolic
preaching stretched from north to south and from east to west, gathering both
the Lord's economy and the apostles' service, showing the double of the
economy as in a double cross, he spoke thus; for it was not possible for him
simply to say the places, the above and the below, and the line, the breadth
and the length. That you may be filled unto all the fullness of God, that is
the church; for he calls the church fullness. Eph 5:14 "Awake, you who sleep,"
and what follows we find written nowhere in the old or the new. What then is
it? There was then a gift both of prayer and of psalms, the Spirit suggesting,
as he says in the epistle to the Corinthians: each of you has a psalm, has a
prayer. What then is it? I will pray with the spirit, I will pray also with
the mind; I will sing with the spirit, I will sing also with the mind? It is
clear then that in one of these spiritual psalms or prayers lay this which he
mentioned. Eph 5:16 He who buys out another's slave buys him out and acquires
him. Since then the present time is enslaved to the wicked, buy it out, so as
to use it for piety. Eph 5:29 Since the lamb has been brought, we also eat his
body. And food is accustomed to be distributed into the body of the one eating
(clearly we eat his body spiritually 312). It is distributed into our body and
thus flesh from his flesh, and bone from his bones. Eph 5:32-33 Not the
mystery, but God. What then is it that he says? That this forming of Adam and
of Eve from him and the union became a type of Christ and of the church;
wherefore he says: we are bone from his bones, and flesh from his flesh, as
Eve was from Adam. The "but I speak unto Christ and unto the church," then, he
means the mystery of marriage, i.e. the things according to the marital law
and the creation from the beginning are a type of Christ and of the church.
For this reason he added: nevertheless you also, each one, that each may love
his own wife as himself. Why did he say "each one"? Because the things
according to Adam were a type of the church and of Christ. For this reason he
said: and you, each one, love. For the mystery of which the marriage was a
type has come to be and is true, but the type itself also must be fulfilled,
and each love his own wife. Eph 6:17 If the sword is the word of God, and the
sword is of the Spirit, then the Spirit is God, whose is the sword. And if he
called the Holy Spirit word of God, clearly as the Son is Word of God, so also
the Spirit is word of God. And he called the sword of the Spirit the punitive
energy of the Spirit. Eph 6:18 Praying in spirit he did not say simply, but
since we do not know how we ought to pray, he says elsewhere: the Spirit
himself intercedes for us, that these petitions of ours may have nothing
fleshly. And we have spiritual petitions helped by the power of the Spirit.
313 Eph 6:24 One who is being delivered, he says, from the corruption from the
devil, receives in incorruption the grace of the Spirit.
