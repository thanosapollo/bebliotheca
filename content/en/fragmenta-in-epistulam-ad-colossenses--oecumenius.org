453 Col 1:13 Of the Son of his love, periphrastically of the beloved Son. Col
1:15 Some, led astray from the images among us, which are from boards and wax,
thought thus also concerning the Son of God. For just as 454 these, they say,
preserve form and likeness of a man, yet not nature or substance, so also the
Son of the Father. But the impiety is Arian. But against those who say this
one must set what is written in Genesis 8, that it says: Adam begot according
to his image; for it is much more fitting to receive concerning image those
things which Scripture says concerning a son and father of men, since the
theorem before us also belongs to Father and Son of God, than the things upon
the image from wood and wax, which is not properly an image, but is called an
image by catachresis. Col 1:19 Not relatively but essentially as soul in body,
not becoming a soul to the body, but united in an ensouled body in the manner
in which soul is united to body. Col 1:27 Which wealth of the mystery is, he
says, Christ. Through him therefore you also believe you are brought near as
being in you through the faith, through whom, he says, we hope to obtain the
eternal glory. Col 2:5 And this he says at once praising, and at once hinting
that that is properly faith which has the firm and the stable. Col 2:9 Let us
understand that some such thing has come to be as also upon the light. And
every image and every example falls short of the truth; for how would the
sensible things signify the divine? Nevertheless it must be said: just as the
first-born light in the beginning, being bodiless and immaterial, was mixed
into the solar body and became in a certain manner embodied, yet even existing
in it is neither confined nor hindered from lighting all things, so also the
God Word, in the beginning fleshless, having become flesh is not hindered both
from being united to flesh according to hypostasis and from not being shut up
in it, but fills all things. And for this reason, I think, both the Lord
himself names himself light and Malachi has called him sun of righteousness,
and still also the 455 psalmist says: fire fell upon them, and they did not
see the sun. Or understand bodily, as having become flesh; for not having
dwelt, he says, does he migrate again, but he was incarnate having henceforth
one nature with his own flesh, even if he was composed from two of the
opposites. Col 2:11 And see: sometimes of the Spirit, sometimes of Christ he
calls the circumcision; for he knows one work and one energy of the Holy
Trinity. Col 2:13 Circumcision divides off the superfluous, as uncircumcision
has the superfluous. Superfluous therefore, he says, were you also, doing the
things not fitting; for everything outside the becoming is superfluous. Being
therefore dead in the sins through inactivity toward the good and
uncircumcised, he both circumcised you intelligibly and made you alive. Christ
from the sensible death, and you both from the sensible and from the
intelligible, that of the sins. And do not wonder if he says the Father has
made Christ alive; for also elsewhere it has been said that he raised himself;
for the things that come to be are common to Father and Son and Spirit. Col
3:17 And both through him and with him are well said; the one because of the
equal and consubstantial with the Father, the other, since he deigned to
become our mediator and high priest, when the Word became flesh according to
the Scripture. Col 3, Apart from those that lead to impiety. epilog zy col Not
having found well in the copy the scholia of the blessed John on the Epistle
to the Colossians, I wrote them as I was able. If therefore something is found
in them either light or blameworthy, let the reader know that such a fault is
mine.
