FROM THE SMALL TOME ON THE SONG
Which Origen wrote in his youth.
=13.36= For one who has not grasped the peculiarity of the persons of the
divine Scripture, both of those speaking and of those toward whom the word is,
the things said furnish much confusion, when he seeks the speaking person,
whatever it is, and of what sort is that toward which the word is, and when
the speaking person stopped, the one toward whom it is often being kept, and
another speaking toward the same, or those toward whom the word no longer
hearing, and another having succeeded to the things said, the speaker
remaining. And there is a time when both change, both the speaker and that
toward which the word is; or both remaining longer, it is not clearly shown
that they remain. And why must one seek examples of each of these, the
prophetic [books] being altogether filled with their difference? Which is also
no chance cause of the unclarity of the things said not being distinguished.
And this too is a custom of Scripture, to leap quickly from the word about
some things to that about others, and to do this unclearly and confusedly,
especially the prophets.
