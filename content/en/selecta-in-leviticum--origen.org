ORIGEN'S SELECTIONS ON LEVITICUS.
=12.397=
And he called Moses. It has been named Leviticus, because the matter of
the priesthood and the things of the sacrifices and all such things as
God commanded are contained in this Leviticus. Exodus also has
legislations of God; but how the priests were consecrated after being
anointed, and how the tabernacle was constructed and received a
harmonious joining together, and many other things, Leviticus relates.
And if it also contains certain other things, it is not surprising, for
the title is Leviticus, indicative of what is more essential in it. Of
the same. Those who do not look rightly upon the discourse concerning
sacrifices either despise it as a cheap legislation, having nothing
worthy of the Creator, or they impiously condemn the Creator himself as
legislating cheaply. But he who has understood what belongs to David and
to the Apostle, the one saying: "Open my eyes and I shall perceive the
wonders from your law;" and the other crying out: "The law is
spiritual," is able to see through that there are lessons worthy of God
hidden in these things. A man of you, if he bring gifts to the Lord. We
have observed in many places of the Scriptures that gifts are spoken of
in the case of living things, but sacrifices in the case of lifeless
things, according to what is said in Genesis: "And God looked upon Abel,
and upon his gifts, but upon Cain and upon his sacrifices he did not
attend." And this too must be said: in the case of living things, "A
man," it says, "of you, if he bring gifts;" but in the case of lifeless
things, "if a soul." The soul [offers] the lifeless thing; in order that
of the irrational only the rational may be left, and of the lifeless,
overcome by the fire, only the living may remain. They shall pour out
the blood upon the altar. An altar of offering is what the altar is
called, upon which are sacrifices of whole burnt offering; but an altar
of incense, the inner altar. Of the same. Since the Jews rejoiced in the
sacrifices, having become accustomed to these in Egypt, as even the
making of the calf in the wilderness testifies, God permitted them to
bring these to him, thereby bridling the disorderly impulses of
polytheism and [preventing] their sacrificing to demons. =12.400= But if
one soul sin involuntarily. Involuntary is whatever evil a person may
do. It is to be noted that Scripture calls the involuntary error a sin.
And the blessed Paul also mentions certain other things, saying: For the
law had a shadow of the things to come. Of the same. "If a soul sin
involuntarily," it says, "and do not do any of all the commandments of
the Lord," it is according to nature. For there are perhaps also certain
ordinances of the Lord which one must not do, according to: "I gave them
ordinances that were not good." A soul therefore sins involuntarily
when, on the pretext of piety, it keeps what ought not to be kept. And
it needs a sacrifice for forgiveness; whereas he who acts deliberately
does not need a sacrifice: as he who became a Jew in order to gain Jews,
and circumcised Timothy. That soul, whoever it be that touches any
unclean thing, or a carcass. And a carcass, he says, is the man who has
died to God, with whom one must not even eat; for he who touches him is
unclean. And this too is unclean: that which has been hunted by our
adversary the devil. Unclean cattle would be those who have approached
the word as brutish, but have sinned unto death, and have not become
worthy to be saved even with cattle, according to the saying: "Men and
cattle you will save, O Lord." Everyone who touches them shall be
sanctified. Some say that "shall be sanctified" means shall be defiled,
not because of the sacrifice, I think, but because of the boldness of
him who touches. For Haggai says: "Whoever touches shall be polluted."
Of the same. We say that the sacrifices were rather an occasion of
nourishment for the priests and a shadow of piety. And the gain from
sacrificing is twofold: both that honor is brought to God, and that what
one sacrifices one would not worship, nor would one think it to be God.
And you shall not die. The word seems to threaten death to priests who
drink wine and strong drink whenever they go into the tabernacle. For
those who pray on behalf of the people, the bishops, must turn away from
drunkenness, not only that from wine, but also that of affairs and of
human reasonings. And he does not forbid this as being improper in
itself, but because he knew the incontinence of the Israelites, that
whenever they took hold they went into excess. In order that when they
go into the holy things they may have the precision of the soul sober,
and a fine remembrance of God. Why did you not eat the [offering] for
sin in a holy place? And he shows that if the priests do not eat, the
sacrifice is incomplete, and the sin remains; for it was necessary both
that [some] of the blood be brought in, and that they themselves eat. If
today they have brought the things for their sin. Since it happened, he
says, that those around Nadab and Abihu, having offended against God,
died, when =12.401= it was time for them to eat the [offering] for the
sin of the people that what it had sinned might be forgiven, how shall
we, who today have been convicted in sins, procure the forgiveness
through eating? For the priest must first make atonement for himself.
These are the cattle which you shall eat. Wild beasts, then, are in no
way clean; for all are unclean according to the law that says:
"Everything that goes upon its hands among all the beasts shall be
unclean to you." And wild beasts in a generic sense would be all the
most savage of men, of whom the Prophet says: "When evildoers drew near
upon me to eat my flesh;" and what follows. Every beast that parts the
hoof. The cloven hoof [signifies] him who lives piously in this age and,
because of the age to come, hastens toward that one; and, in addition to
the parting of the hoof, also to divide with nails, stripping away the
superfluous and dead things from the hands, that is, from deeds; and
from the feet, that is, from the way of walking. In addition to these,
also chewing the cud: not only laying up the spiritual food, but also
recalling it and continually meditating upon it. This is the law
concerning the cattle and the birds and every soul. He makes much
discourse concerning clean and unclean; first he trains [us] that the
soul is to be holy. For if the natural thing, such as has come to be by
God's command, is unclean, how much more that which has come to be from
a wicked choice, and not from God's command? If someone should think
that that of which God is maker is unclean, how much more that which is
contrary to the will of God, and does what he forbade? If there come to
be in a man, in the skin of his flesh, a scar. These things are referred
to certain blemishes of the soul. And just as in the wounds of the body,
after the healing, sometimes a trace of having suffered is left behind
in the places in what is called a scar; in the same way the soul that
has received a wound of sin, even if it happen to obtain healing,
sometimes has as it were a scar left behind. And one must also
thoroughly know the manner of the cleansing from it, or of the
not-cleansing. To a man or a woman in whom there comes to be a touch of
leprosy. One must observe in the case of which persons it is said: "If
there come to be in anyone in the skin of his flesh;" or: "To a man or a
woman, in whom there come to be in them a touch of leprosy;" but in the
case of which: "Flesh, if there come to be in its skin an ulcer;" or:
"Flesh if there come to be in its skin a burn of fire." And these things
are a bruise. His garments shall be loosened. Through these things it is
hinted that one must not conceal the sins and cover them, the one who
has been leprous in soul. Just as of him whose garments are loosened his
unseemliness is [not] covered; so he wishes him to be laid open to all,
not becoming a whitened tomb. =12.404= And to have the mouth wrapped is
not to have freedom of speech to open the mouth; and to go out of the
camp is no longer to dwell together with the choirs of the saints. And
they shall slay the one bird into an earthen vessel. The bird is
sacrificed into an earthen vessel, living water having first been put
into it, in order that the cleansing may come about by water and blood,
which came forth from the side of the Savior. And a cedar wood, he says,
is the wood of our salvation. And the spun scarlet was a type of the
precious blood, through which the whole world is cleansed. And I think
the hyssop [signifies] the heat through the Spirit; and the scarlet cord
indicates the coming-together of the Word toward flesh. His head and his
beard shall be shaved. He is shaved as to the head, according to the
more capital and more principal of the doctrines; and as to the beard,
which is a symbol of the man, putting away the sins of the man; and as
to the eyebrows, putting away all conceit, and, if I may so name it,
superciliousness. And these things, he says, grown upon and blossomed
out on the soul, being called hairs of deadness, are a symbol. He shall
take two unblemished yearling lambs. The word divides the things offered
into three: one, the loosing of things previously sinned; one, the being
offered wholly to God; and another, that which is offered upon the one
being cleansed of the things done by him. And perhaps also the three
tenths of fine flour are taken, in order that one of the tenths may be
joined with the lamb that is slain, and the second with the remaining
one, and the third with the sheep. Already then, at the fifth cleansing,
he has not meal but fine flour, which is a symbol of the matter of pure
bread, and this mixed with oil; which, as nourishing light, is taken as
fulfilling the need of light and not of fire. And he has, by itself,
apart from the oil taken for the mixing of the fine flour, oil which is
offered through the priest, being of the measure of a cotyle. And it
seems to me that the priest is very much needed at: "The priest who
cleanses shall set the man who is being cleansed, and these things,
before the Lord." And if the house of someone come, he shall report it
to the priest. And we never say that the lawgiver said that someone must
go to the priest in order to accuse his own house; but rather, as from a
physician, to ask a remedy for what has happened. We say, then, that the
synagogue of the Jews has become leprous. And the person of the one
announcing is the choir of the holy prophets; who also report to the
great high priest, that is, before the indignation, the passions of the
madness of the Jews, not from hatred, nor from enmity, but from
reverence toward the benefactor, confessing their transgressions, yet
nevertheless entreating that they be pitied.
