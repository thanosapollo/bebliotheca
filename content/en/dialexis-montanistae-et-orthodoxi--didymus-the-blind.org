Dialogue of a Montanist and an Orthodox.
The Montanist said: We, persuaded by holy Paul, received Montanus, as
having that which is perfect of the Holy Spirit, that is, the Paraclete. For
Paul himself said: When that which is perfect comes, that which is in
part shall be done away; and: we know in part and we prophesy in part.
450 {O.} Did holy Paul speak truly, or falsely? {M.} Truly. {O.} Have
the things of the apostle then been done away? {M.} God forbid. {O.} How
then did he say, When that which is perfect comes, that which is in part
shall be done away, as speaking truly or as speaking falsely? {M.} He
himself said: we know in part and we prophesy in part. {O.} You speak
truly, but you have not understood that every word in this age is a part
of the mystery that will appear in the age to come. For now we believe
in Father and Son and Holy Spirit and we have it in the words; but then
is the knowledge, when we shall see face to face. Now we believe that
the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, with the voice
of an archangel and with the trumpet of God; but then, face to face.
When the things themselves have been seen face to face, that which is in
part shall be done away. For no longer do we have the faith from words,
but from the very sight, and this is "that which is in part shall be
done away." {M.} Will faith then also be done away? {O.} It will be done
away. That is, it ceases. Come, let us recall the whole passage itself
with the understanding of the Spirit. And it says thus: Love never
fails; but whether there be prophecy, it shall be done away. For we know
in part and we prophesy in part; but when that which is perfect comes,
that which is in part shall be done away. When I was a child, I spoke as
a child, I thought as a child; but when I became a man, I put away the
things of the child. For now we see through a mirror in a riddle, but
then face to face; now we know in part, but then I shall know even as I
have been known. You see that it shall be done away unto what is better;
which is what? When the things believed are present, faith will 451
cease. For we no longer believe, but we see. And faith that is seen is
not faith. For what a man sees, why does he also believe? And this is:
for now we see through a mirror in a riddle, but then face to face.
Then, when? Clearly, when the end. {M.} See then, Montanus the Paraclete
has come and has given us that which is perfect. {O.} When that which is
perfect comes, is that which is in part done away, or not? {M.} So it is
written. {O.} But we see that Montanus is being done away even to this
day; while the things of holy Paul increase. {M.} And yet you do away
with the things of Paul, who says that after Christ there are no
prophets. {O.} You err, not knowing the scriptures nor what the Church
teaches. For we know that there are prophets even after Christ. For
Jesus himself said: Behold, I send to you prophets and wise men and
scribes, and some of them you will kill and crucify, and some of them
you will scourge in your synagogues. And Paul says: Desire the greater
gifts, but rather that you may prophesy. And again he says: If therefore
the whole church come together in one place and all speak with tongues,
and there come in unlearned or unbelievers, will they not say that you
are mad? But if all prophesy, and there come in an unbeliever or an
unlearned man, he is convinced by all, he is judged by all, the secrets
of his heart also become manifest by all, and so falling on his face he
will worship God, declaring that God is truly among us. {M.} How then do
you say that after Christ no prophet has arisen? {O.} We confess that
prophets have arisen even after Christ, and

that the apostles themselves also have the gift of prophecy. 452 For how
did Peter convict Ananias and Sapphira of keeping back from the price of
the field, if he did not have the gift of prophecy? And how did holy
Paul say the things that would be at the last of the days, if he himself
also did not have the gift of prophecy? {M.} How then do you not receive
holy Montanus? {O.} Because he was a false prophet, saying nothing true.
{M.} Do not blaspheme, man, the Paraclete. {O.} I praise and glorify the
Paraclete, the Spirit of truth, but I abhor Montanus as the abomination
of desolation. {M.} How? {O.} First, because he says: I am the Father
and I am the Son and I the Paraclete. {M.} But you say that the Father
is another, and the Son another, and the Holy Spirit another. {O.} If we
say it, it is nothing trustworthy; but if the Son teaches that the
Father is another and the Paraclete, the Holy Spirit, another, we must
be persuaded. {M.} Show where he teaches this. {O.} When he says: I will
ask the Father, and he will give you another Paraclete, the Spirit of
truth, that he may be with you forever, whom the world cannot receive,
because it does not behold him; but you know him, because he remains
with you and shall be in you. How, hearing "another Paraclete," do you
not plainly understand another besides the one who is speaking? {M.} If
another and another and another, three gods. {O.} God forbid. {M.} How,
"God forbid"? {O.} Because the divine scripture unites those that have
the same nature and will. {M.} God forbid. {O.} You hear Paul the
apostle saying: In Christ Jesus there is neither slave nor free; there
is neither male and female; there is neither Greek and Jew, 453 but you
are all one in Christ Jesus. And we hear Christ himself saying of the
man and the woman that they are no longer two, but one flesh. And if
things that are in distinction are no longer two because of the sameness
of will and of nature, how is it possible for the incorporeal to come
under number and position? {M.} There is not then one God. {O.} One God
by the account of nature, but in hypostasis the Father is another and
the Son another and the Holy Spirit another. {M.} How then does the Son
say: In that day they shall know that I am in the Father and the Father
in me? And again he says: If anyone loves me, he will keep my word, and
my Father will keep him, and we will come to him and make our abode with
him. He who does not love me does not keep my words, and my word is not
mine, but his who sent me. These things I have spoken to you while
remaining with you; but the Paraclete, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father
will send in my name, he will teach you all things and remind you of all
that I said. {O.} Unwillingly you agree with the word of truth. For if
you are persuaded by what has been said, you will walk straight toward
the truth. You heard that he says: In that day you shall know that I am
in the Father and the Father in me, and he did not say: In that day you
shall all know that I am Father and Son. And you hear that he says: and
we will come to him, I and the Father; he did not say to him: I will
come, I and the Father and the Son, and I will make an abode; but also
"My word is not mine, but his who sent me" shows nothing other than the
peculiarity of the hypostases; and when you hear, These things I have
spoken to you while remaining with you, but the Paraclete, the Holy
Spirit, whom the Father will send in my name, he will teach you all
things that I said to you, how will you not, blushing at yourself,
confess the distinctness of the three hypostases? 454 {M.} In the day
... of judgment the three hypostases sit to judge the living and the
dead. {O.} Hearing "they sit" in a bodily sense, understand it
spiritually, so that you may not suppose places and separations of
bodies, but the firmness of the nature. For the Father is everywhere,
the Son everywhere, the Holy Spirit everywhere, and as there is nothing
in between mind and word and breath, so there is nothing in between
Father and Son and Holy Spirit. And one must understand the Father
perfect, in a perfect hypostasis, and the Son perfect in a perfect
hypostasis,

and the Holy Spirit, perfect in a perfect hypostasis, proceeding from
the Father. {M.} How is that possible? He himself says: I and the Father
are one. {O.} He did not say, I am one, but we are one, so that we may
know the subsistence of the hypostases. But I marvel at this, that you
remember the Gospels and do not walk straight toward their truth. {M.} I
am persuaded by the Gospels. {O.} Show then where it is written in the
Gospels: I am both the Father and the Son and the Spirit. {M.} He who
has seen me has seen the Father. {O.} But "the Father": not having said
that he himself is Father, but such as the Father is. {M.} Do you want
to know that those who do not receive the Paraclete are orphans? Hear
the Lord saying: It is expedient for you that I go away; if I do not go
away, the Paraclete will not come to you; but if I go, I will send him
to you; and when he comes, he will convict the world concerning sin and
concerning righteousness and concerning judgment. And a little later he
says: I will not leave you orphans; I come to you. Those therefore who
have not received the Paraclete are orphans. {O.} Truly they are not
only orphans, but also godless, who have not received the Paraclete.
{M.} Why then do you not receive him? {O.} We both have received and do
receive him, but you neither 455 knew nor received him, but were
deceived by Montanus, and with such a deceit as men would not have been
deceived by unless they had become vessels of the devil. For first he
persuades you to call the apostles orphans and the Lord Jesus a liar,
who said to them: I will not leave you orphans; I come to you. {M.} For
we say that Christ was not in the apostles. {O.} He who says that the
Paraclete was not in the apostles, but in Montanus, will certainly not
grant that Christ was in them either. For no one can say Lord Jesus
except in the Holy Spirit. And according to you they were found orphans
and the Lord Jesus Christ a liar, and Montanus the priest of Apollo
true. And what is more wretched than this deceit? {M.} We too say that
the apostles had from the Spirit, but they did not have the fullness of
the Paraclete. {O.} And he who says, But we all, with unveiled face,
beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, did not have the Spirit,
but Montanus the priest of the idol did? {M.} Was not Paul a persecutor?
{O.} But he became a chosen vessel. {M.} And Montanus. {O.} But from
this the true prophet is known, from his saying the things of God, and
the false prophet in his saying things contrary to God. {M.} And when
did Montanus say things contrary to God? {O.} When God and our Savior
says that the Paraclete is another besides himself and promises to send
him to the apostles, but Montanus says: I am the Father and the Son and
the Holy Spirit, it is clear that he says things contrary to the Son and
is a false prophet. {M.} And we say that the apostles had the earnest of
the Spirit, but not the fullness. {O.} That the apostles were temples of
the Spirit, and all the saints, hear Paul saying: Do you not know that
your bodies are a temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you, and in other
places: But you are not in the flesh, 456 but in the Spirit, if indeed
the Spirit of God dwells in you. Since then there are so many
testimonies saying that the Spirit was and is upon those who believe,
how do you say that he was not upon the apostles? {M.} I said that there
was an earnest of the Spirit in them. {O.} The Spirit himself is an
earnest of the promised good things, as for example we have a promise of
incorruption. Whence is the proof that we shall be incorruptible? From
the Spirit which he gave us, and thus we hope that every promise is
fulfilled from the presence of the Holy Spirit, so that he is an earnest
of the promised good things. And Paul says thus: He who confirms us with
you unto Christ and anointed us is God, who also sealed us and gave the
earnest of the Spirit in our hearts, that is, that he was confirmed with
you unto Christ. Whence is this? From the Spirit

which he has given us? For the proof of the things Christ promised is
from the presence of the Spirit: he promised the kingdom of heaven, he
promised incorruption, immortality, wisdom, sanctification, redemption.
What is the proof of this gift, and whence have we the certainty? From
the Spirit which he gave us. For he who has given the cause of the gifts
will also give what follows, and for this reason the Spirit of the glory
that is to be revealed has been called an earnest. {M.} And why do you
also turn away from the holy Maximilla and Priscilla and say that it is
not permitted for women to prophesy? Did not Philip also have four
daughters who prophesied, and was not Deborah a prophetess? And does not
the apostle say: Every woman praying or prophesying with her head
uncovered, if it is not for women to prophesy nor to pray? And if they
pray, let them also prophesy. {O.} We do not turn away from the
prophecies of women, and holy Mary prophesied saying: From now on all
generations shall call me blessed. And as you yourself said, the holy
Philip had daughters who prophesied, 457 and Mary the sister of Aaron
prophesied. But we do not permit them to speak in churches nor to have
authority over men, so as even for books to be written in their name.
For this is what it is for them to pray and prophesy uncovered; and she
did not shame the head, that is, the man. For could not the holy
Theotokos Mary have written books in her own name? But she did not, lest
she shame the head by having authority over men. {M.} Is praying or
prophesying with the head uncovered the not writing of books? {O.}
Entirely so. {M.} If then holy Mary says, From now on all generations
shall call me blessed, does she speak with boldness and unveiled, or
not? {O.} She has the evangelist as a covering. For the gospel has not
been written in her name. {M.} Do not take allegories as doctrines for
me. {O.} On the contrary, holy Paul also took allegories for the
confirmation of doctrines, saying: Abraham had two wives, which things
are allegorized. For these are the two covenants. But let us grant that
the covering of the head is not through allegory. Then set it before me
in all cases: if there is a poor woman and she has nothing with which
she is covered, must she not pray nor prophesy? {M.} And can she be so
poor as not to have something with which she will cover herself? {O.} We
have often seen women so poor as not to have something with which they
will cover themselves. But since you yourself do not want there to be
women so poor as not to have something with which they will cover
themselves, what do you do in the case of those being baptized? Must
they not pray when they are being baptized? And what do you say also of
the men who often have the head covered because of affliction? Do you
also forbid these to pray or prophesy? {M.} In that hour in which he
prays or prophesies, he is uncovered. {O.} Then he must not pray without
ceasing, but disobey 458 the apostle who teaches him, who says: Pray
without ceasing. And you also advise the woman being baptized not to
pray. {M.} Is this then why you do not receive Priscilla and Maximilla,
because they composed books? {O.} Not for this only, but because they
also became false prophetesses together with their leader Montanus.
{M.} Whence is it that they became false prophetesses? {O.} They said
the same things as Montanus? {M.} Yes. {O.} But Montanus was convicted
of having spoken contrary to the divine scriptures, and they too
therefore will be cast out together with him.
