LETTER OF CYRIL, BISHOP OF JERUSALEM, TO CONSTANTIUS THE EMPEROR, CONCERNING
THE SIGN THAT APPEARED IN HEAVEN
To the most God-beloved and most pious emperor Constantius Augustus, Cyril
the bishop in Jerusalem, greeting in the Lord.

1 These first-fruits of letters I send from Jerusalem to your God-beloved
majesty, fitting for you to receive and for me to offer: not filled with words
of flattery, but bearing news of heavenly visions of God; nor containing
well-composed rhetorical persuasions of words, but witnessing to the truth of
the predictions of the holy Gospels by the outcome of the events.

2 For others, from what they possess, often crown your honored head, bringing
gold-inlaid crowns adorned with most translucent stones. But we do not crown
you with things from the earth (for gifts from the earth have the earth as
their end); rather we bring with eagerness to the knowledge of your piety a
divine working of heavenly things accomplished in Jerusalem in the times of
your God-beloved reign; not so that you may now come from ignorance into
knowledge of God (for you already go before, teaching others also by the
things in which you are pious); but so that you may also be confirmed in what
you already knew, and, having received the ancestral inheritance of the
empire, and having learned that you have been honored from God with greater
heavenly crowns, you may even now render fitting thanksgiving to God the King
of all, and may be filled with greater courage against enemies, grasping in
very deed that your empire is loved by God, by the wonders he works in your
time.

3 For in the time of the most God-beloved Constantine of blessed memory, your
father, the saving wood of the Cross was found in Jerusalem, divine grace
granting the discovery of the hidden holy places to him who sought piety well.
But in your time, master most pious emperor, who surpass ancestral piety with
a greater reverence toward the divine, the wonders are no longer from earth
but from the heavens; and the trophy of the victory over death of our Lord and
Savior Jesus Christ, the only-begotten Son of God, I mean the blessed Cross,
flashing with gleamings of light, was seen in Jerusalem.

4 For in these holy days of holy Pentecost, on the Nones of May, about the
third hour, a Cross of immense size, formed of light, appeared in heaven,
stretched out above holy Golgotha as far as the holy Mount of Olives;
appearing not to one or two only, but shown most clearly to the whole
multitude of the city; nor, as one might suppose, swiftly passing by as a mere
appearance, but for many hours seen above the earth with the eyes, and by its
flashing gleamings overcoming the rays of the sun (for it would have been
overcome by them and hidden, if it had not given those who saw it radiances
more powerful than the sun); so that at once the whole multitude of the city
ran together to the holy church, seized with fear of the vision of God
together with gladness: young and old, men and women, and every age, even to
the girls already kept in the inner rooms of the houses, locals and strangers,
Christians together with pagans sojourning from elsewhere; and all with one
accord, as from one mouth, hymning Christ Jesus our Lord, the only-begotten
Son of God, the worker of wonders, and receiving in deed and by experience
that the all-pious teaching of Christians is not in persuasive words of
wisdom, but in demonstration of Spirit and of power, proclaimed not by men
only, but witnessed from the heavens by God.

5 Since then we, the inhabitants of Jerusalem, having received with our own
eyes the paradox of the wonder, have rendered and will render fitting worship
with thanksgiving to God the King of all and to the only-begotten Son of God,
and have made and will make fervent prayers also for your God-beloved power in
the holy places, and it was necessary not to give these heavenly visions of
God over to silence, I have been eager at once through these letters to
announce the good news to your God-inspired piety; so that, having built upon
the good foundation of the faith already present in you the knowledge from the
things recently shown by God, you may take up more firmly the hope in our Lord
Jesus Christ, and, taking courage and filled as usual with all manliness, as
having God himself as ally, you may most readily put forward the trophy of the
Cross, the boast of boasts, bringing with you the sign shown in heaven; whose
very form heaven, having shown it to men, boasted of more greatly.

6 This miracle, most God-beloved emperor, in accordance with the
testimonies of the prophets and the words of Christ laid down in the holy
Gospels, has been accomplished now and will again be accomplished more
greatly; for in the Gospel according to Matthew the Savior, granting knowledge
of things to come to his blessed apostles, and through them speaking to those
after them, most clearly announcing in advance, said: "And then shall appear
the sign of the Son of Man in heaven." Taking that divine book of the Gospel
into your hands as is your custom, you will find in writing the predictive
testimonies laid down concerning this; to which I especially exhort you,
master, to give heed with more frequent study, because of the rest of the
sequence of the things written there, which were announced in advance by our
Savior and need much observation with reverence, so that you may suffer no
harm from an opposing working.

7 These first-fruits of our words I offer you, most God-beloved emperor; these
first utterances I speak from Jerusalem, to you the most genuine and all-pious
worshipper of Christ with us, the only-begotten Son of God and our Savior, who
according to the divine Scriptures accomplished in Jerusalem the economy of
salvation, who here trampled death, and by his own precious blood wiped out
the sins of men, and granted life and incorruption and spiritual heavenly
grace to all who believe. May God the King of all himself, the giver of every
goodness, keep you, guarded by his power and grace, distinguished in brighter
and greater achievements of piety, and taking pride in the royal offspring of
genuine sons, for many peaceful cycles of years, the boast of Christians, with
all your household, for the whole world.

8 May the God of all grant you to us in health, adorned with every virtue,
showing the accustomed beneficence both toward the holy churches and toward
the empire of the Romans, and taking pride in greater prizes of piety, for
many peaceful cycles of years, with all your household, Augustus, most
God-beloved emperor.
