as the divine Paul says: "As in Adam all die, so in Christ all shall be made
alive." And this tomb is a witness, the joy of the living, the consolation of
the dead. This stone is a witness, the one that was rolled and that rolled all
the idols of the demons, and not only rolled them but also crushed them and
ground them fine and winnowed them with the fan of the cross and <from> the
earth "blew them away like dust from a threshing-floor and like steam from a
chimney" and by the blast and the rushing of the Spirit. These women
themselves are witnesses, in place of the women of old, bringing sensible and
intelligible myrrhs to the Bridegroom each day and night. The white-robed
priest is a witness, the angel of the church, crying to them: "Do not fear;
for I know that you seek Jesus the Nazarene who was crucified." But do you
seek the hour? For he says: "We shall find him ready as the dawn." Luke will
be able to report this also to you: "On the first of the sabbaths, at deep
dawn, they came to the tomb, bringing the spices they had prepared. And they
found the stone rolled away from the door of the tomb; and when they entered
they did not find the body of the Lord Jesus. And it came to pass, as they
were perplexed about this, behold, two men stood by them in garments that
flashed. And as they became afraid and bowed their faces to the earth, they
said to them: Why do you seek the living among the dead? Why do you think
about life what is foreign to life? He who is sought here by you sits in the
heavens; he who is felt for here rests in the bosom of the Father; he who
became dead that he might be Lord of dead and living did not leave life
behind; for he himself lends life to all, from him all nature draws breath, in
him what is moved is moved, what coheres coheres, what is at work is at work."
From there the women went out and met Jesus, and they saw the dawn as dawn,
and they said to him what David sang in the prophecies: "For with you is the
fountain of life; in your light we shall see light." But if you say, "The
women found him, but the apostles did not," and "How is the gain common? How
does what was found become the salvation of the whole nature?" you inquire
superfluously, you accuse in vain. For the women found what they had formerly
lost through Eve. She who furnished the occasion of the loss found the gain.
Only read the remainder of the prophecy: "We shall find him ready as the dawn,
and he will come to us as early and latter rain upon the earth." These things
have been fulfilled clearly according to the letter. The word does not need
contemplation; we have no need to allegorize the prophet's meaning: "He will
come to us as early and latter rain upon the earth." See him then appearing as
the early rain to the women, and as the latter rain to the apostles; for early
here he addressed the women with "Rejoice," and late in Zion he addressed the
disciples with "Peace." "Rejoice" to the women, that he might loose the
sentence of sorrow; "Peace" to the disciples, that he might dissolve the
enmity which the dragon planted. Therefore he said to them with this
distinction: "Peace to you; for the war is against the devil, the enmity
toward him remains; for his horn must be crushed, his venom must be poured
out, his sting must be trampled. Now I will forge weapons with which war shall
be waged against him."

6 Not in vain, then, did Andrew report to Peter: "We have found the Messiah,
who is called Christ," since God also uttered such things through the prophecy
of Isaiah: "If you seek, seek, and dwell with me." Hence Andrew dared to
question Christ: "For I know that you have no house upon the earth, you do not
build in corruption, you do not make a tent of brick. Where do you remain?
that I may become doorkeeper of the Master's court. For I have chosen to be
cast aside in the house of my God rather than to dwell in the tents of
sinners. Where do you remain? If I learn, I will not leave the door." But this
is the church's table now set forth from the Gospels; and I, as a servant,
have only set out its dishes; therefore, if it seems good to you, I will pass
to another table. The calling crowns Andrew; it shows the soldier the
election. The hiring set forth the dignity of the vinedresser, which Matthew
set before us thus: "From that time Jesus began to preach and to say: Repent;
for the kingdom of heaven has drawn near." "From that time." When? From when
he sanctified the Jordan and planted the gift of baptism and crushed the head
of the dragons in the waters and brought down the gift of the Spirit from
heaven, opened a way of life, flung open the gate of salvation: "From that
time he began to preach and to say: Repent; for the kingdom of heaven has
drawn near." For the creditor has come who blots out the charges for all; the
ungrudging king has sojourned among us; the judge is present, freeing those
liable from the prison, loosing the fetters, blotting out charges for all,
"who shuts his eyes that he may not see injustice, who makes his ears heavy
that he may not hear a judgment of blood." "Repent": wash your deeds with the
word, wipe the mouth with the tongue, cleanse your life with prayer, offer a
sacrifice which rules over many. "Repent": therefore the one who repents is
not only delivered from the terrible things but also receives an inheritance
of the good things, not only free from vice but also becomes a farmer of
righteousness. "Repent; for the kingdom of heaven has drawn near." You who are
strong, force your way; you who are weak, be strengthened; you who are lame,
run, and you who run, hasten, and you who hasten, do not trust in yourselves,
but in God who directs the course: "For it is not of him who wills nor of him
who runs, but of God who has mercy." "Repent": for there is no vice that
conquers repentance, there is no venom that can quench the power of this
honey; the devil wrestles with all and resists all and fights with all;
against repentance alone he does not dare to wrestle; for it casts the sheep
out of his throat, it tears the captive from the very hand of the tyrant.

7 "And as Jesus walked by the sea of Galilee he saw two brothers, Simon called
Peter and Andrew his brother." Seeing many he overlooked them, since they were
unworthy of the Master's eyes. "He saw two brothers," not in body only but
also the soul, nature and grace, way of life and kinship. "He saw two
brothers, Simon called Peter," not the one who would be called; for he had
already been counted worthy concerning the calling. For he knew that Christ
had said to him: "You are Simon, you shall be called Cephas, which is
interpreted Peter." See, before the confession you have the wage, and before
you work the vineyard you received the denarius, before you kindle the altar
you have received the sacrifice, before you preach you were crowned, before
you evangelize the prize of theology met you. And Andrew became the cause of
this very thing, and for this reason Matthew added: "And Andrew his brother,"
although he had already said once: "He saw two brothers"; yet see, now he
takes it up a second time, wishing to proclaim Andrew as Peter's benefactor.
Therefore he says: "Simon called Peter and Andrew his brother," the one who
fulfilled the commandment which Solomon wrote beforehand in Proverbs: "Let
brothers be useful in necessities," in things exceedingly profitable, in
things greatly urgent, in things that press very hard; for wisdom called these
necessary and inescapable causes: "And let brothers be useful in necessities."
Therefore Andrew did not overlook Peter when the calling was pressing; for you
have heard how he said to him: "We have found the Messiah, who is called
Christ." Run, Peter, he says, before Peters anticipate you, before others
seize the first places; for this one recruits from wherever he wills, this is
the one who gathers from "the highways and hedges," who seals harlots and
tax-collectors. Wherever he casts the seed, there the earth bears fruit; where
he plants the shoot the vine rises at once; wherever he casts the net, it is
found full of fish. "He saw two brothers, Simon called Peter and Andrew his
brother, casting a net into the sea; for they were fishermen." Marvel at
Christ's munificence, that he has chosen the poor and uneducated and
unlettered and fishermen. For wisdom does not need advocates but servants; for
no one supplies her, but from her we all receive all things; for no one lends
to the rich, for we all borrow from him. What need then will there be of the
wise in the preaching? For the one who is about to partake of the true wisdom
must become a fool.

8 Marvel at the wisdom of Christ and bless the election of the apostles. What
things did they exchange for what? For what follows makes it plain, when he
says to them: "Come after me and I will make you fishers of men." Forget all
the ancient things, deny the parents of the flesh that you may receive the
true Father. Let the whole world be small to you, when it is judged against
its Maker. "Come after me": for I lead you up to life, I guide you by the hand
to the light. "Come after me," that you may anticipate the patriarchs, that
you may receive the lot before the prophets; and being last you shall become
first, that you who are about the eleventh hour may become higher than those
hired together at dawn. "Come after me": for following me you will have a life
without toil; for without silver you will be rich, without drugs you will be
physicians of the inhabited world, without weapons you will be found stronger
than the enemies. "Come after me": "For I am the light of the world," "I am
the way and the life and the truth," I guide you by the hand to the Father, I
give to those who go after me "to tread upon asp and basilisk and to trample
lion and dragon." "And immediately leaving the nets they followed him." It was
fitting then to bless the good sense of the apostles; for they left what they
had, they disregarded the resource of life that they had. Their whole treasure
and their whole wealth was the nets; for they had their living in seines; the
catch of fish fed the hopes of their life. Yet they left even these, they did
not say to themselves: this man talks nonsense to us and superfluous myths, he
promises to make us fishers of men. And where is such a net? Where is a seine
able to hunt such things? What lake, what sea brings men to our nets? And what
will the profit of the hunt be? Where shall we use the catch after the catch?
But attending to the one who called and confirmed by faith and having
understood that the word had something hidden, they followed him; and at once
they became above the earth and appeared more glorious than kings, like
angels, ministers of God, heralds of salvation, servants of immortality, more
brilliant than sun and moon and stars; for their glory is not as under the
heavens but above the heavens, they have unquenchable light, an unceasing
course, ever-visible rays, with which we pray that our minds may be illumined.
And let us flee the darkness itself and turn aside from the brine of life, and
as we travel the unstable sea let us flee the surge of soul-destroying waves,
and let us become fishes of the upper seine of Christ's unlettered apostles,
dishes of the Master's table which the King of those who reign dines upon,
counting our salvation as food; to him be the glory and the might unto the
ages of ages. Amen.
