* On the Widow's Son

On the Son of the Widow. =61.789= "And it came to pass afterwards, that Jesus
went into a city called Nain, and there went with him his disciples in good
number, and a great crowd. And when he drew near to the gate of the city,
behold, there was being carried out a dead man, the only-begotten son of his
mother, and she was a widow." All sufferings are pitiable, and full of much
compassion. =61.790= When an only-begotten son has been released from the
present life, the sweetest mother cried out that she had become a mother in
name only, whose pangs at childbirth death had harvested. The vine had one
cluster, and the storm of death crushed it; she possessed one staff, and
common death =61.791= took this also. The poor woman was unstaked, and she had
been stripped of her staff; she counted life unlivable. She no longer had even
an ordinary consolation, having lost her only-begotten son. She was held by a
double pain; she had a grief that could not be consoled; no one was able to
lighten the ache; the tears ran like a river down her cheeks, and the woman
possessed a mourning that could not be borne. She considered the beauty of the
child; by affection the inward parts of her mind were cut. And she had become
wholly a widow, the heaviest thing of all, the most pitiable of all; she had
been stripped both of her husband and of her only-begotten. Truly pitiable is
the name of widow, filled with pity, laden with tears, bound up with
compassion.
No one of those who looked on remained without mourning; the inward parts of
all groaned; they could not find first whom to mourn, the dead child, or the
living widow. But I was wrong in calling the widow living; for what widow
lives a life? For even if she seems to live (I speak toward the present
things, not toward the things to come), she has died. For a widow in the
present things, if she was well provided, has her pain somehow a little stolen
from her, she salves her hope, the wound is treated, and the ache is
lightened, she is strengthened by her fruit and often sleeps a little. For
even if the things that grieve are many, and those who plunder her are ready,
still, when poverty is absent, the heavy thing is lighter. But a poor widow is
a harbor of tears, and a well of trials, an unwalled city, a waterless spring,
a mother of groaning, a hanger-on of gladness, a house-mistress of poverty, a
decreer of barrenness, a sojourner of famine, a weaver of pain, a staff broken
in pieces, a hard encounter, a bitter household, an unadorned statue, a
desolated pillar, a rootless tree, an unlighted mist, a porch of the church,
an inventor of varied prayers, speaking boldly against enemies, an accuser
against them; and crying out to God as to a husband, and having the vengeance
of God's wrath as a most ready avenger, splitting the heavens by her
successful petitions to God, with an embittered heart; beating her breast,
like an executioner, if she does not quickly find the help of God, for
vengeance on those who trample upon her unto injustice. And the childless
woman, poor upon the earth, unprotected, moves God together with all his
heavenly Powers, to wrath and indignation against those who do not wish to be
persuaded that God is judge of widows and orphans. But if it happens that a
child is present to her, she is somewhat delighted by his age, and is stolen
away by hope, and is strengthened by her fruit. She sees the branch, and
greets the root; she holds the cluster, and loves the one who planted it; she
has the part, and counts it the whole; she is entwined with the living, and
does not loathe the dead; she considers the features of the child, and mirrors
the image of the father. And so long as the little child is still about, the
widow is tossed more calmly in the heavy surge of life; but if it happens that
the child also dies while the mother lives, and takes leave of the mother, and
flows to the father, which is what the present widow has suffered, she
receives the whole pain at once.
For as soon as the only-begotten son of the widow just named had died, she,
clothed in the robe of mourning, began to utter rash words, and she was going
to law as against Him who in truth is not to be judged, bringing forward
blasphemous speeches; she was talking as one suffering violence, saying: Did
my son alone weigh down the world? Others are cramped by childbearing, and
from me, the only one, has the only one been torn away? I, the weak one, live,
and the husband has died together with the son. Where then will anyone counsel
me? And who will regard me? I thought I possessed at least a little boldness
through him; for it was enough for me only to be called mother. The father
left him to me at the breast, without money; and I, the unprotected widow,
setting my hands to work, reared him with care; I made him double and triple
garments, I supplied wages without stint to those who taught him; and now,
when I expected the harvest of good things, then I have received the hail of
trials. O the pain! But I have found what I shall do; I will be buried with my
son; I will satisfy those who envied me; kinsmen were devouring me as one who
possessed a manly son. Pardon me, all you who begrudge me my blessedness in
children; for already I go before to the tomb of my own true child; for living
is henceforth a mockery to me.
When the widow had cried these things and was in pain, at once there arrived
=61.792= our Maker, Christ God, and seeing her, God who foresees all things,
who also before making all things knows them clearly: For before I formed you
in the womb, I know you, and before you came forth from the womb, I sanctified
you; the father of orphans, and judge of widows, who turns tears into
gladness, who made the gate of the city of Nain a church, and showed the bier
of the dead man to be an altar. For where there is a gift of resurrection,
there is the adornment of an altar; and where the hands of God the Word are
stretched out, there the measure of the resurrection has been accomplished.
For the funeral procession became a place of sacred rite, and the bearing-out
became a bearing-in. For what does the Evangelist say? "And Jesus seeing her";
that is, the Savior, and God the Word, the Christ, was moved with compassion,
seeing her. Seeing what? A childless widow, husbandless, aged, bent, wailing,
having pulled her hair, sprinkling her head with dust, blinding her heavy
eyes, having torn an unseemly garment, beating her much-groaning breast, and
no longer able to cry out, having at last become beside herself. Seeing her in
this state, Jesus was moved with compassion over her, and coming near he said
to her: Do not weep. And she, occupied with the mourning, not having perceived
clearly the one who had arrived, having shaken off the mourning, answered the
Lord and said: You tell me not to weep? And what consolation will you bring me
now? For even if you move the whole world to my comfort, you will show me no
comfort. For of a mother's consolation, what is sweeter than a son? and
especially when they are widows, and have only-begotten sons as their boast.
How then do you tell me not to weep? Strangers mourn my son, and shall I not
weep? The stones groan, and shall I be silent?
My hope has been taken, my life has failed, my boldness has been plundered, I
see my own life as hopeless, and I am stripped of all hope. Death has reaped
my expectation; he has made me resourceless from every side; he left me, and
hunted him down; he left the root, and cut off the branch; he spared the
trunk, and withered the fair shoot; he left the old woman, and cut down the
young. I have no one to look upon. For if I had another son, I would have been
able to weigh the mourning in the balance, and by the sight of the living to
lighten the pain of the dead; but now nothing good is left to me, I have been
stripped of all gladness. I used to look at the image of the child, and I
beheld his original, the father; having this one, I pictured that one; I never
thought myself a widow. For the one from him lightened my misfortunes, and the
one born from me healed me more than the husband, and when I was grieving I
had this one as consolation. If ever despondency seized my reasoning, he would
take me in his arms; and embracing, kissing, delighting me with childish
words, he would make the cloud of despondency vanish; and you tell me, Do not
weep for your son? Who are you, who counsel me such things? And some of the
crowd coming near said to her: Why do you dare, O woman, to contradict God?
The Lord is present; he himself is speaking with you; come near, ask, entreat;
for his presence is not without effect. And she, wiping her eyes, and drawing
her garment about her, and covering her breast, fell at the Master's feet, and
answering the Lord said: Give me the child, and I take your counsel; for I am
found in the oldness of nature; for I look at Sarah, and I harvest hopes. And
coming near to the feet of Jesus, she said: Forgive the rashness of your
servant, Master, and the words that were baptized in my mourning. For you are
not ignorant of a mother's pain in the grief of children; for this is your
ordinance. I was rearing this one upon hopes. I have lost the husband; at
least grant me the child; refresh my old age; for I am already failing. I do
not say as Martha, If you had been here, my brother would not have died; for
even now, being present, you bear his soul, and seeming to go away you leave
no one. A little, Master, pardon your servant. You wept for Lazarus, whom by
your power you raised from the dead; and shall I not weep for my son? And
Jesus coming near touched the bier, he who touches the mountains, and they
smoke. And those who were carrying it stood still. And he said: Young man,
arise; take up a young man's =61.793= spirit, and trample death. I say to you,
be raised; do not delay toward the resurrection; for I am the life and the
resurrection. I called Lazarus from the tomb, and he at once leapt out of the
grave, having already on the fourth day rotted in the tomb, and much of his
inward parts plundered by the worms. But the voice of life, scenting the dead
man into a sweet savor, reforged the dissolution of the flesh, and the tomb
became a womb for the one lying there. But death has only lately hunted you; I
say to you, do not delay toward the resurrection; for I am the resurrection.
The one they saw was a corpse, and the Lord was speaking to the dead man as to
one who hears. When does a dead man hear? And what does the Master say? I say
to you, be raised; and immediately the dead man sat up; so that the watching
crowds became as if dead, first because of unbelief, second because of the
truth.
For why did he not leap down, but sit up? So that all might see the
resurrection unobscured, and the thing that was happening might not be taken
as a fantasy. All but saying to them: I give you who have heard full assurance
that he himself is the one who died a short while ago. And stretching out his
hand, Jesus took hold of the dead man, and gave him alive to his mother. And
she, having received the son, was held by joy and unbelief; rejoicing at the
resurrection of the child; but unbelieving as to the sense of the facts. With
haste the poor woman examined the son closely, she felt with her hands,
searching the placing of the eyes, of the nostrils, of the lips, of the
forehead, and the formation of the whole face; then the hands and the feet,
and, to speak simply, the whole body; and she reasoned with herself, saying:
Am I perhaps being =61.794= deceived? she says; am I holding another in place
of another? has he perhaps contrived this for my consolation, and the drawing
back of mourning, and against the excess of pain at my son's death? and has
Jesus handed over some stranger as my own, wishing to lighten my pain? I have
no witness of what has happened; I have never seen this, no other of those
with me has seen it; never was a dead man seen raised upon a bier. And taking
the son himself, she was talking, saying: Tell me, she says, and do not be
afraid, if you yourself are my son; say, do you know me? do you recognize who
I am? do you know if I am the one who bore you? I recognize the features, but
I do not believe the fact; the eye cries out, the pupil sketches, the eyelids
proclaim, the hearing agrees, the molded partition of the nostrils bears
witness, the lips give full assurance, the very age itself urges me. Move your
tongue, dearest, tell me the tokens of life. Joy has stirred a storm in me; I
see you, and I am held fast; I carried you in my arms, I nursed you at my
breasts when you were small. Tell me, my child, are you my son? and do not
frighten me.
But when she received full assurance upon all these things, she who had
mourned returned rejoicing, and she who had been lamenting was glorifying God,
holding the child with her right hand, and going through the city toward all,
crying with a loud voice: Magnify the Lord with me, and let us exalt his name
together; because he raised my dead one, because he staked the old vine, and
made her who was no mother a mother again, and cut off the springs of tears;
and glorify his name unto the ages; because to him belongs glory unto the ages
of ages. Amen.
