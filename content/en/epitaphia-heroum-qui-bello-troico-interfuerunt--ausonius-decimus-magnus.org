I have thought it to belong to the matter, that I should finish even a vain
little work of fitting material and should subjoin to the little book, which
has the commemoration of those who either as foreigners at Bordeaux or 1 as
men of Bordeaux taught abroad, Epitaphs, that is sepulchral titles 2 of the
heroes who took part in the Trojan War. Which ancient things, when I had
found them at a certain philologist, I converted in Latin speech, not that I
might serve a zeal of pursuing the order 3, but that I might restrain them
freely and not wander.

King of kings, I the son of Atreus, avenger of a brother's wife,
myself have fallen by the hands of my own wife.
What does it profit that I, grieving, punished the rape of Helen,
when Clytemestra slays the avenger of adultery?

O fortunate Menelaus, to whom is owed a seat of the gods
and Elysium decreed for pious shades,
beloved son-in-law of Tyndareus, beloved of the Thunderer,
champion of marriage, avenger of adultery,
mighty in eternal age and eternal youth,
having suffered neither the seasons of death nor of old age.

On the tomb of Ajax I, Virtue, am equally covered, overwhelmed,
myself weeping on the pyres of my own funeral,
having torn my unkempt hair, because the wicked son of Atreus
compelled me to yield to laid snares,
now I will give a purple flower from bright blood,
witnessing with a groan the crimes of the judgment.

Not one land holds the son of Aeacus: his bones are covered
on the Sigean shore, Larisa burned his hair.
A part lies hidden in tombs, a part was brought back by the fleet; 1

but in the whole world Homer will show him living again 2.

In this tomb is laid Ulysses born of Laertes:
read through the Odyssey, you who wish to know all.

Here is laid Diomedes, better than a good father,
driven from dowered Argos because of his wife's crime,
who founded Argyripa and Arpi famous for men,
more famous by a new city than by the ancient seat of his fatherland.

Good in counsels and in war, which pairing is rare,
dear both to the sons of Atreus and to the sons of Aeacus:
having obtained the prizes of virtue and of piety at once,
I, Antilochus, have died, my father Nestor having been saved.
This was not the order: but more justly he is the survivor,
without whom faithless Troy could not be taken.

In this tomb I am covered, already prodigal of a fourth age,
Nestor, famous in counsel and in eloquence,
whose son, slain, threw himself in place of my death
and by the wound of the son I the father live,
alas why does it please the fates so to dispose life,
so long for us, so short for Antilochus?

In the middle of the world I am covered, greater than paternal valor,
I, Pyrrhus, who as a boy even of a king bore the rich spoils.
Whom impious Orestes slew before the altars by fraud,
what wonder, already raging with his mother slain.

Nor does Pleuronia think me not worthy of a title,
which 1 was a common house with Diomedes,
with Euryalus and Sthenelus: for I third possessed this kingdom,
of which now a tomb is enough.

The sea holds Guneus, the tomb without a body has the name.
Fame among men, the spirit seeks again the sky,
all the elements are a common sepulchre for so great a leader.
Which? Heaven and earth and sea and the mouths of men.

A fated name was assigned to me, Protesilaus;
for I first of the Danaans died in the Phrygian war,
having entered the Sigean shores with a bold leap,
caught by the snares of the wheedling son of Laertes.

who, lest he press with his foot the shores of the Trojan land,
himself leaped down upon his own shield.
Why do I complain? This death already then my fates were singing,
when my father laid such a name on me.

Betrayed to punishment by the fraud of the wicked Laconian woman
and with a mutilated body, I, Deiphobus
have no tomb, except that which by the voice of one calling
both pious Aeneas and Maro composed for me.

This is the tomb of Hector, with whom his Troy is buried:
they are laid equally, who perished together.

Flower of Asia and the one survivor from so great a race,
a little child, but already to be feared by the Argives from his father,
here I, Astyanax, lie, thrown from the high Scaean gates.
For grief! The Neptunian walls of the Ilian rampart
have seen something more cruel than Hector dragged.

I, Sarpedon the Lydian, begotten of Jove, by my father's divinity
I hoped for heaven, but I am covered by this tomb,
wept with bloody tears: O iron fates,
even he suffers grief, who is able to prevent it.

Nastes and Amphimachus, renowned offspring of Nomion,
once leaders, we are dust and shadow.

Hector having been laid low, with neither gods nor strength equal,
I, Troilus, having met the savage son of Aeacus,
dragged by the two-horse chariot I am joined to my brother's honor,
because of whose example the punishment is not heavy for me either.

Yield far off and flee that myrtle, unknowing guest:
it is a crop of weapons grown with my blood.
Pierced with javelins and buried from the slaughter itself
I, Polydorus, am twice laid in this tomb.
Does pious Aeneas know, and you, impious king, that me
a Thracian punishment presses, a Trojan care covers?

The Trojan land laid Euphemus, leader of the Cicones,
at the likeness of spear-bearing Mars,
nor is it enough to have cut a title on the stone of the tomb;
moreover they load even the brow with the mass of a statue,
more quickly those things fall, which are so heaped and placed:
where the cult is greater, a great ruin lies beneath.

The lowest earth holds Hippothous and Pyleus in her lap:
the upper backs grow green with cabbage-stalks and mallows.
Nor does the culture of a garden vex the quiet ashes,
while with a sparing hand soft vegetables are tended.

Here Ennomus and Chromius lie: to whom Mysia was the kingdom
to whom the father was Alcinous and Oceanus a forefather.
What does so great nobility help? By how much more famous
is the race for these, by so much heavier is the condition of death.

This is not the tomb of Priam nor am I laid in this
seat: the Danaans tore off my head.
But I, when I had a torn funeral without a name,
I, the father, fled to the ashes of Hector,
there I found both my sons and Troy and Asia buried
and whatever of ours lies anywhere.

Whoever seeks the tomb of Priam, let him read Hector's first.
That is mine, which I myself first gave to my son.

XXIV A.-TO HECTOR

Of Hector and of the fatherland at once is a common sepulchre,
since the ruin of both was joined.

I who was a queen, who was born of famous Dymas,
who was the wife of Priam, who bore Hector,
here I, Hecuba, perished, overwhelmed by stones thrown on me,
but nevertheless I avenged myself first by the rage of my tongue.
Trust not in kingdoms and offspring and the stock of parents,
whoever you read this our σῆμα κυνὸς.

I, Trojan Polyxena, joined to the tomb of Achilles
had preferred to be covered, having died, with no turf.
Not well do you mix discordant tombs, Achaeans:
this was more to violate than to bury.

I was queen of Thebes, I who now am a Sipylean crag:
I injured the divinities of the children of Leto.
Mother glad and proud in twice seven children,
I as mother led as many funerals as I bore,
nor was this enough for the gods: surrounded with hard stone
I lost the likeness of a human body;
but grief, though the vitals are blocked, still clings
and with a pious spring waters perpetual tears,
for a crime! Are there such angers in heavenly minds?
The grief still endures, the image of the mother perishes .

Say, dog, whose tomb is this? -- A dog's. -- But this dog, who? --
Diogenes. -- Has he died? -- He has not died, but gone away. --
Diogenes, for whom a wallet was food-store, for whom jars were a seat,
has he gone to the shades? -- Cerberus forbids him from there. --
And whither then? -- Where the star of bright Leo burns,
he has now been added as a dog to just Erigone.

A wallet, barley-meal, a cloak, a staff, a cup, that scant furniture
was the Cynic's: but he thinks this too much.
For seeing a herdsman drink with hollowed hands:
why, cup, he said, do I carry you superfluous.

Your likeness, king Croesus, richest of kings,
Diogenes the Cynic saw among the shades.
Nothing of yours, he said, is yours, Croesus; all things remain to me.
I was naked: so I am. I had nothing: this I have.
The king said: I did not lack, when you, beggar, were without
all things; and do I lack, if only I do not need?

Sprinkle with wine the ashes and with the ointment of well-smelling nard,
guest, and add balsams to purple roses.
A tearless urn keeps perpetual spring for me
and I have changed ages, I have not died.
None of the joys of my old life have perished for me,
whether you think I remember all things, or nothing.

One letter indeed, but it shines parted with twin points,
the praenomen thus L: the mark alone makes it,
after .M. it is mocked: I think thus M: it is not seen whole:
the injured peak has split off with a fragment of the stone.
nor would anyone know by sure signs whether MARIUS OR MARCIUS OR METELLUS
lies here,
the letters lie wrenched with truncated figures,
all things you may interweave with confused marks,
should we wonder that men have perished? Monuments gape open
death comes even to stones and to names.

Phosphorus, through the spacious levels of the noisy circus
wont as victor to go over the sevenfold courses,
driving the first runs from the barrier without haste,
brave that you might master the horses that had gone ahead,
it was ready even to anticipate swift four-horse teams:
to conquer even victors is a better praise.

take this title as consolations of a vain tomb
and go swift to the Elysian wing-footed ones.
Let Pegasus run for you on the right from here, let Arion go left
as the trace-horse, let Castor give you a fourth horse.

Carus has established me as a common sepulchre for himself and his wife
and his children toward late obsequies,
and already long the monuments are empty and may this complaint
be longer and let each come in his own order,
he who was given by the law of being born, and through a peaceful lifetime
let him be laid, who is prior by birth, he prior.

All things which are desired in a long age of life,
Anicia consumed before a four times full lustrum.
As an infant she tossed, as a youth and a maiden she grew.
She married, she conceived, she bore, already a mother she died.
Who would accuse death? Who would not accuse it in this woman?
In the merits of age she is an old woman, in age a girl.
