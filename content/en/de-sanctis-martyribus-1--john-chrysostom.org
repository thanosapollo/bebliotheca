* On the Holy Martyrs
:PROPERTIES:

Encomium on all the saints who have been martyred throughout the whole world.

=50.705=

1. Since we celebrated the sacred festival of Pentecost, a number of seven
days has not yet passed, and again a choir of martyrs has come upon us, or
rather an encampment and battle-array of martyrs, in no way worse than the
encampment of the angels which the patriarch Jacob saw, =50.706= but a rival
of it and equal. For martyrs and angels differ in names only, but in deeds
they are joined; angels dwell in heaven, but so also do the martyrs; those are
unaging =50.707= and immortal, and this the martyrs too will have. But those
also were allotted a bodiless nature? And what of that? For the martyrs, even
if they are clothed with a body, yet it is immortal; or rather even before
immortality the death of Christ adorns the bodies more than immortality.

Heaven is not so brilliant, being adorned with the choir of the stars, as the
bodies of the martyrs, being adorned with the brilliant choir of wounds. So
that because they died, for this they especially have the advantage, and
before immortality they received the prizes, being crowned from death. You
made him a little lower than the angels, you crowned him with glory and honor,
concerning the common nature of men, says David; but even this little, Christ,
having come, restored, condemning death by death. But I do not insist from
this, but that even this defect of death has become an advantage; for if they
were not mortal, they would not have become martyrs; so that if there were not
death, there would not even be a crown; if there were not an end, there would
not be martyrdom; if there were not death, Paul could not say, I die daily, I
protest by your boasting which I have in Christ Jesus; if there were not death
and corruption, the same could not say: I rejoice in my sufferings for you,
and I fill up the deficiencies of the afflictions of Christ in my flesh. So
let us not be pained that we became mortal, but let us give thanks, that from
death the stadium of martyrdom was opened to us; from corruption we received
the matter of the prizes; from this we have the starting-point of the
wrestling-bouts. Do you see the wisdom of God, how the greatest of evils, the
chief of our disaster, which the devil introduced, I mean death, this he
changed into honor and glory for us, leading the athletes by it to the prizes
of martyrdom? What then? Shall we give thanks to the devil because of death?
God forbid! for the achievement is not of his purpose, but the gift of the
wisdom of God; he introduced it in order to destroy, and by bringing all back
to the earth to cut off all hope of salvation; but Christ, taking it, turned
it about, and through it led us up again into heaven.

Let none of us then find fault, if I called the full company of the martyrs a
choir and a battle-array, putting two contrary names on one thing; for choir
and battle-array are contrary, but here both have come together; for as if
dancing they went to the tortures with pleasure, and as if warring they
displayed all courage and endurance, and overcame the adversaries. If you look
at the nature of the things taking place, battle and war and array are the
things taking place; but if you examine the mind of those doing them, choirs
and festivities and festivals and greatest pleasure are the things being
accomplished.

Do you want to learn that these are more dreadful than war? I mean the things
of the martyrs. What is the dreadful thing in war? Camps stand on either side
=50.708= fenced about, flashing with weapons, and lighting up the earth,
clouds of darts are sent from every side hiding the air by their multitude,
streams of blood are borne upon the earth, and the fallen are many everywhere,
just as in a harvest of ears of grain, so the soldiers being brought down one
upon another. Come then, let me lead you from those to this battle. And here
too two arrays, one of the martyrs, the other of the tyrants; but the tyrants
are armed, and the martyrs fight with a naked body, and the victory belongs to
the naked, not to the armed. Who would not be struck with amazement, that the
one being whipped prevails over the one whipping, the bound over the loosed,
the one being burned over the one burning, the one dying over the one killing?
Did you see how these are more dreadful than those? Those, even if they are
fearful, yet happen according to nature; but these surpass all nature, and all
sequence of things, that you may learn that the things achieved are of the
grace of God. And yet what is more unjust than this battle? what more lawless
than the wrestling-bouts? For in wars both of those fighting are fenced; but
here it is not so, but one is naked, and the other is armed; in the contests
again it is permitted to both to raise their hands against each other, but
here one is bound, and the other with authority inflicts the blows, and as if
from some tyranny the judges have allotted to themselves the doing of evil,
and have assigned to the just martyrs the suffering of evil, thus they grapple
with the saints, and not even thus do they prevail over them, but after this
unequal battle they withdraw having been defeated; and the same thing happens
as if someone, leading a warrior into war, and having cut off the point of the
spear, and having stripped off the breastplate, should thus command him to
fight with a naked body, and he, being struck and beaten and receiving
countless wounds from every side, should set up a trophy.

For leading the martyrs naked, and binding their hands behind, and beating and
scraping them from every side, they were thus defeated; and those receiving
the wounds set up the trophy against the devil. And just as adamant being
struck itself does not yield, nor is softened, but dissolves the iron that
strikes; so indeed also the souls of the saints, so many tortures being
brought upon them, themselves suffered nothing terrible, but dissolving the
power of those beating them, they sent them out of the contests shamefully and
ridiculously defeated after many and unbearable blows. For they bound them to
the wood, and they dug through their sides, deepening furrows, as if plowing
earth, but not splitting bodies; and one could see flanks spread open, ribs
opened, breasts torn apart, and not even here did those blood-drinking beasts
stop their frenzy, but taking them down from the wood they stretched them over
the coals upon an iron ladder; and one could see again sights more bitter than
the former, double drops coming down from the bodies, some of the blood
flowing, others of the flesh melting; but the saints, lying upon the coals as
if upon roses, thus with pleasure beheld the things taking place.

2. But you, having heard of an iron ladder, remember the intelligible ladder
which the patriarch Jacob saw stretched from earth to heaven; through that
angels were coming down, but through this martyrs go up; and upon each the
Lord was set. These saints would not have borne the pains, if they were not
staying themselves upon this. But through that angels go up and down, and that
through this martyrs also go up is clear to everyone, I suppose. Why? Because
those are sent for the ministry of those who are about to inherit salvation,
but these, like athletes and crown-wearers, having been released from the
contests, have gone thereafter to the president of the games.

But let us not simply hear the things said, hearing that coals were lying
under bodies that had been scraped, but let us consider what we are when a
fever has fallen upon us: we think life unlivable, we are beside ourselves, we
are impatient, we are vexed like little children, thinking that fire nothing
less than Gehenna; but these, not a fever lying upon them, but a flame
besieging them from every side, and the sparks leaping upon the sores, and
biting the wounds more sharply than any beast, like some adamantine men, and
as if seeing these things happening in others' bodies, thus nobly and with the
courage proper to them they stood upon the words of the confession, remaining
unmoved through all the terrible things, and brilliantly displaying their own
courage and the grace of God. Have you often seen the sun rising at dawn, and
sending out saffron-colored rays? Such were the bodies of the saints, as of
certain saffron-colored rays, the streams of blood flowing around them from
every side, and lighting up their body much more than the sun does heaven.
This blood angels seeing were delighted, demons shuddered, and the devil
himself trembled. For it was not simply blood that was seen, but saving blood,
holy blood, blood worthy of the heavens, blood continually watering the good
plants of the Church. The devil saw the blood, and shuddered; for he was
reminded of another blood, the Master's; through that blood this flowed; for
since the side of the Master was pierced, you see thereafter countless sides
being pierced. For who would not with much pleasure strip for these contests,
being about to share in the Master's sufferings, and to be conformed to the
death of Christ? For this exchange is sufficient, and the honor far more than
the toils, and the recompense surpassing the contests, even before the kingdom
of the heavens. Let us not then shudder hearing that so-and-so was martyred,
but let us shudder hearing that so-and-so grew soft, and fell, when such
prizes were set forth.

But if you wish to hear also of the things after these, no word is able to
present them; for eye has not seen, he says, nor ear heard, nor has it arisen
upon the heart of man, the things which God has prepared for those who love
him; and no one of men has loved him thus as the martyrs. Nevertheless, since
the magnitude of the goods stored up surpasses both word and thought, we will
not for this be silent, but as far as it is possible both for us to speak and
for you to hear, we will try to show you dimly the blessedness =50.710= that
will receive them there; for clearly they alone will know, who through this
experience enjoy it. For these terrible and unbearable things the martyrs
suffer in a brief moment of time; but after the release from here they go up
into the heavens, angels going before them, and archangels escorting; for they
are not ashamed of their fellow-servants, but they would have chosen to do all
things for them, since those too chose to suffer all things for their own
Master Christ. And when they go up into heaven, all those holy powers run
together. For if when foreign athletes sojourn in the city, the whole people
streams from every side, and having encircled them they study the good
condition of their limbs, much more when the athletes of piety have gone up
into the heavens, the angels run together, and all the powers above stream
from every side studying their wounds, and as certain champions returning from
war and battle with many trophies and victories, thus with pleasure they greet
them all, and they embrace them; then they lead them with a great escort to
the king of the heavens, to that throne full of much glory, where are the
Cherubim and the Seraphim. And having come there and having worshipped the one
sitting upon the throne, they enjoy more kindness from the Master than from
the fellow-servants. For he does not receive them as slaves, although even
this is a very great honor, and one of which an equal cannot be found, but as
his friends; for you, he says, are my friends; and very reasonably; for he
himself said again: Greater love than this no one has, that one should lay
down his life for his friends.

Since then they displayed the greatest love, he greets them, and they enjoy
that glory, they share the choirs, and they partake of the mystical songs. For
if while being in the body, according to the communion of the mysteries, they
belonged to that choir, singing with the Cherubim the thrice-holy hymn, as you
who have been initiated know, much more now, having received their
fellow-choristers, they share that praise with much boldness. Were you not
shuddering at martyrdom before this? do you not now desire martyrdom? do you
not now grieve, that a time of martyrdom is not present? But let us also train
ourselves toward a time of martyrdom. Those despised life; you despise luxury;
those threw their bodies to the fire; throw you money now into the hands of
the poor; those trampled the coals; quench you the flame of desire. These
things are burdensome, but also profitable. Do not look at the present
burdensome things, but at the future good things, not at the terrible things
in hand, but at the good things in hopes, not at the sufferings, but at the
prizes, not at the toils, but at the crowns, not at the sweats, but at the
recompenses, not at the pains, but at the exchanges, not at the burning
=50.711= fire, but at the kingdom set forth, not at the executioners standing
around, but at Christ who crowns.

3. This is the greatest method and the easiest road toward virtue, not to look
at the toils only, but also at the prizes together with the toils, and not at
them by themselves only. Whenever then you are about to give alms, do not
attend to the spending of the money, but to the gathering of righteousness; He
has scattered, he has given to the poor; his righteousness remains unto the
age of the age; do not look at the wealth being emptied, but see the treasure
being increased. If you should fast, do not reckon the affliction from the
fast, but the ease from the affliction; if you should keep vigil praying, do
not think of the hardship from the vigil, but the boldness from the prayer.
Thus also soldiers do; they look not at the wounds, but at the recompenses;
not at the slaughters, but at the victories; not at those falling dead, but at
the champions being crowned. Thus also pilots look at the harbors before the
waves, at the merchandises before the shipwrecks, at the goods after the sea
before the terrible things in the sea. Thus do you also; consider how great a
thing it is, in deep night, all men sleeping and beasts and cattle, deepest
quiet being, you alone having risen to converse with boldness with the common
Master of all. But is sleep sweet? But nothing is sweeter than prayer.

If you converse with him privately, you will be able to accomplish many
things, no one troubling you, nor knocking you away from the petition; you
have also the time as an ally toward obtaining what you wish. But do you turn
about lying upon a soft bed, and hesitate to rise? Consider the martyrs lying
today upon the iron ladder, not a bed lying under, but coals spread
underneath. Here I wish to close the discourse, so that you may go away having
the memory of the ladder flourishing and fresh, and remember this in night and
in day; for even if countless bonds hold, we shall be able to break all
=50.712= easily, and to rise toward prayer, considering this ladder
continually. And not that ladder only, but also the other punishments of the
martyrs let us draw upon the breadth of our heart. And just as those making
their own houses brilliant adorn them from every side with flowery painting;
so also let us paint upon the walls of our mind the punishments of the
martyrs. For that painting is unprofitable, but this has gain; for this
painting needs no money, nor expense, nor any art, but in place of all it is
enough to use eagerness and a noble and sober reasoning, and through this, as
through a certain best hand, to sketch their punishments.

Let us paint then in the soul some lying upon frying-pans, others stretched
under coals, others tumbling into cauldrons, others being drowned in the sea,
others being scraped, others being bent upon a wheel, others being thrown down
a cliff; and some boxing with wild beasts, others being led to the pit,
others, as each happened to have ended his life; that by the variety of this
painting, having made our house brilliant, we may make it a suitable lodging
for the king of the heavens. For if he should see such paintings in the mind,
he will come with the Father, and will make a dwelling with us together with
the Holy Spirit; and our mind will thereafter be a royal house, and no
unseemly thought will be able to set foot, the memory of the martyrs like a
certain flowery painting being stored in us continually, and sending forth
much flashing, and the king of all, God, dwelling among us continually.

And thus having received Christ here, we shall be able after the departure
from here to be received into the eternal tents, which may it be that we all
obtain by the grace and loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom
and with whom to the Father be glory, together with the holy and life-giving
Spirit, unto the ages of ages. Amen.
