* Homily I on the Meeting
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of Hesychius, presbyter of Jerusalem, a discourse on the Meeting of our Lord
Jesus Christ.
1 The feast is called one of purifications, which one would not err in calling
feast of feasts, sabbath of sabbaths, having named it holy of holies; for it
recapitulates the whole mystery of the incarnation of Christ, it delineates
the whole presentation of the only-begotten Son. In it Christ was carried as
an infant and confessed as God, and was offered in arms as sitting upon a
throne, the maker of our nature, and he offered a pair of rational turtledoves
to Simeon and Anna. But if it seems good, let us insert the very words of the
Gospel; for there we shall see God's encampment. Thus then Luke set forth for
us the history of the feast: "And when the days of their purification were
fulfilled according to the law of Moses, they brought him up to Jerusalem to
present him to the Lord, as it is written in the law of the Lord that every
male who opens the womb shall be called holy to the Lord, and to give a
sacrifice according to what is said in the law of the Lord, a pair of
turtledoves or two young pigeons." It was necessary that he who became a
servant for our sake should offer purifications on our behalf, that he who did
not spare his own blood should not spare a pair of turtledoves. See then how
according to the law he works above the law as lawgiver, and, himself granting
the freedom of the Spirit, he nevertheless does not refuse the servitude of
the letter; he does not set Moses aside, so that in due time he may become a
trustworthy witness.
2 "And when the days of their purification were fulfilled, according to the
law of Moses." "Their," of whom? For Joseph was neither father, nor liable to
purifications, nor did Mary, nor the infant, need the purifications; for it is
said thus: "A woman, whoever is sown with seed and bears a male, shall be
unclean seven days." But Mary was not sown with seed, therefore she did not
need purifications. "A woman, whoever is sown with seed and bears a male,
shall be unclean seven days." Why? Because she brought Adam a second time into
life. "But if she bears a female, she shall be unclean twice seven days." For
what reason did he appoint the days double here? Because Eve is born again
from Eve, a woman is born, the unsound vessel, the weak container, the
shattered pot, the staff of deceit, the guide of disobedience, the counselor
of the dragon. But just as you heard the charges against woman, come and learn
also the achievement from her. A woman introduced virginity into the world,
she contained in her womb the God whom creation cannot contain, she bore in
flesh the one who refashioned our race; she first received Jesus from the
dead, she first began the preaching of the resurrection, she first announced
the joy to the disciples. "A woman whoever," it says, "is sown with seed." But
the Theotokos was a woman, yet she did not suffer the things of women; for her
womb did not receive a man's seed, Mary's field did not see a plow, the
virginal vineyard did not endure a mattock, so that the offering was not for
her, but was made for the whole race. For our sake Christ is circumcised, for
us he is also baptized, for us the purifications of the law are accomplished.
If he weeps, he washes nature clean; if he is struck, he frees our captivity;
if he bears the cross, he lightens us of the yoke of sin; if he is spat upon,
he releases Adam from the spitting of the curse.
3 "When the days of their purification were fulfilled," it says, "they brought
him up to Jerusalem, as it is written in the law of the Lord, that every male
who opens the womb shall be called holy to the Lord." These things the law
holds. But Christ, as we said, as lawgiver fulfilled his own law above the
law; for he did not open, but left the gate of the virgin closed, he did not
plunder the seal of nature, he did not harm the one who bore him; for he left
her the form of virginity intact. But if you disbelieve, learn from Ezekiel:
"And he turned me back by the way of the gate of the sanctuary, the outer one,
that looks toward the east, and it was shut. And the Lord said to me: This
gate shall be shut, it shall not be opened, and no one shall go through it,
because the Lord the God of Israel shall both enter and go out, and it shall
be shut." "Every male who opens the womb shall be called holy to the Lord."
But this one is not only holy. Therefore as much as he exceeds in
sanctification, so much also he has the advantage in the birth and has gone
above the commandment of the law. Nevertheless, however, "to give a sacrifice
according to what is said in the law of the Lord, a pair of turtledoves or two
young pigeons." Luke set forth the law incomplete, not seized by ignorance,
but led by wisdom. The law said: "a lamb and a young bird"; but if the one
offering was not well provided, it commanded "a pair of turtledoves or two
young pigeons and fine flour." But Luke omitted the offering of the rich; for
he mentioned the poor one who for our sake voluntarily became poor. There was
no lodging for him, it says; from where did he possess a lamb? Swaddled in a
manger, from where did he offer a sheep? Rather he had no need of a lamb; for
he himself was "the lamb of God," the true sheep. He was sacrificing himself
for the world; therefore Luke declared what the law commanded, and indicated
what Christ dispenses.
4 Then next: "And behold, there was a man," it says, "in Jerusalem, whose name
was Simeon, and this man was righteous and devout, waiting for the consolation
of Israel, and the Holy Spirit was upon him; and it had been revealed to him
by the Holy Spirit that he should not see death before he had seen the Christ
of the Lord." Great is Simeon's wealth, varied are the general's weapons, the
plant is full of fruits, the vine is full of clusters. Not only "there was a
man," but he was also "named Simeon." Besides this "righteous": not only
"righteous," but also "devout," and he was seeking consolation for Israel, and
also "the Holy Spirit was upon him," and also "he had been given an oracle
that he should not see death before he had seen the Christ of the Lord."
"Behold, a man": where? "In Jerusalem." The expression has astonishment, the
word is full of wonder: a man in Jerusalem, concerning which Zephaniah the
prophet introduced God himself saying: "I will search Jerusalem with a lamp
and I will not find." And does the light need a lamp? He does not say so, but
he calls the law a lamp. "And behold, there was a man in Jerusalem whose name
was Simeon." And what is the interpretation of the name that has been shown?
"Hearing of God," so that he acquired from the economy of the Spirit a name
fitting to his manner of life; for where there is "hearing of God," there
altogether is obedience to the law, keeping of commandments, a course of good
life, a goal of salvation coming through to the end; in which things the true
man is characterized, concerning whom he reasonably added these things.
5 "And this man was righteous and devout, waiting for the consolation of
Israel": this is the aim of a righteous man, not to seek his own, but that of
the other and of the many, to belong to the common salvation, not to his own
enjoyment. "And the Holy Spirit," it says, "was upon him": Simeon was a
vehicle of the Master, a throne of God, and therefore he received into his
hands the King of the ages. "And it had been revealed to him that he should
not see death before he had seen the Christ of the Lord": therefore the oracle
was to pass from life to life, to journey from light toward light, to leap
from day to day, not to grow old, but to be renewed, to receive renewal at the
time of the consummation. This man "came in the Spirit into the temple": the
fountain led the thirsty one to the fountain. "And when the parents brought in
the child Jesus, to do for him according to the custom concerning him, he also
received him into his arms." He was carrying in his hands the one who has in
his hand the inhabited world and the uninhabited, "who measured the water with
his hand and the heaven with a span and all the earth in a handful." And how
did Simeon offer a sacrifice that did not belong to him? For he was not a
priest; for this is not said, nor a Levite, nor a temple-warden, since the
liturgical office has been allotted to the tribe of Levi alone. He was not a
priest, but he was "righteous"; and righteousness has the priesthood under its
feet. He was not a Levite, but "devout"; and the devout man has the anointing
from himself.
6 And carrying the infant he said: "Now you dismiss your servant, Master,
according to your word, in peace." "Now you dismiss": for the time of release
has come, the ransom of the captives has arrived, the forgiveness of debts; a
king has been born freeing those in prisons, releasing those in bonds, calling
back those in death into life, lightening the burden of the earth. "Now you
dismiss your servant, Master." How do you know? I see the Master, he says,
wearing the form of a servant. Being Master he would not have worn a servant's
form, unless he had planned to clothe the servant with the Master's form. He
would not have exchanged the rank, nor being high have been humbled, unless he
had willed to lead the lowly up to height. But how do you "dismiss him
according to your word in peace"? For if by a word you brought forth creation,
by a word you altogether free the ruler of creation. "In peace": of what kind?
That of the cross; for that sword cut off the tyrant's head and gave peace to
those who campaign with Christ. "For my eyes have seen your salvation, which
you prepared." I have seen what many desired to see and did not see: God in
flesh, a sun shining in a cloud. "My eyes have seen your salvation," that is,
I have seen your incarnation, through which you arranged the salvation of men;
for I did not see naked godhead, since no one of men will see God as he is in
essence. "For my eyes have seen your salvation, which you prepared before the
face of all the peoples, a light for revelation to the Gentiles and glory of
your people Israel." The good things are common to Jews and Gentiles and
undivided. Therefore let not the Jew envy, nor the Pharisee be distressed, nor
the scribe tempt, let not Herod be anxious about his kingdom, let not Annas
and Caiaphas be afraid about the priesthood; the one who has come gives to
all, takes from none; he bestows freely, he grants without measure.
7 But Joseph and his mother, hearing the things spoken by Simeon, marveled how
Christ endures to become son of man. Mary marveled how a woman's womb
contained God, how the handmaid bore the one who frees creation. And he says:
"This child is set for the fall and rising of many in Israel and for a sign
spoken against," "for a fall" of those who remain in unbelief, "for a rising"
of those who are transferred from unbelief to faith. Christ was a stone laid
for building, but the blind were stumbling, their eyes wandering. Therefore
one must not reckon to him the "fall" of those who have fallen, but the
"rising." For if they only fell and did not also rise, you justly suspect him
as cause of the "fall." But if of those who fall from sloth those who will it
rise, the "fall" belongs to the slothful, the "rising" to Christ. Did not
Judas and Peter both fall, the one as having said: "What will you give me, and
I will deliver him to you?" the other as having said: "I will lay down my life
for you"? For he ought to have known that a mortal does not give his soul for
immortality. And again: "If I must die with you, I will not deny you." It was
possible to say: "If you strengthen me, I will not deny you. If you make me
secure, I the weak do not fear the shipwreck. If you share in the war, I do
not fear death." But the one belonged to the tongue, the other was a sin of
the heart. Therefore Peter, having fallen, rose and became a guide of others
who had fallen; but the other, led from a depth to a pit, threw the silver and
taking the rope hanged himself.
8 "A sign spoken against" is the cross; for while the Jews were crucifying,
the sun withdrew; the synagogue was slandering and the earth was shaken. The
people cried: "Crucify him," but the rocks, not bearing the bulk of the
blasphemy, were split. The elders and the scribes said: "We remember that that
deceiver said while he was still alive, After three days I am raised." But the
centurion cried: "Truly this was the Son of God." But even until now the word
of the cross is "to Jews a stumbling-block, to Gentiles foolishness, but to us
who are called Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God." "And a sword
will pass through your own soul also, that the thoughts of many hearts may be
revealed." Discord is called a sword because, just as the sword splits and
divides bodies, so also discord [divides] souls toward doubt; for even if she
was a virgin, yet she was also a woman; even if Theotokos, yet from our lump.
A sword therefore was passing through her soul, "that the thoughts of many
hearts may be revealed." What thoughts? The various ones concerning Christ at
the time of the passion, at one time depicting him as a prophet, at another
not as redeemer of Israel, enduring much doubt also in these things because of
the tossing that came from the passion, so that the disciples around Cleopas,
going along, said to him: "Are you the only one sojourning in Jerusalem and
you do not know the things that have happened in it in these days." Then when
asked "what things?" they added: "The things concerning Jesus of Nazareth, who
became a prophet, and what follows." Therefore they had him as a prophet and
as redeeming Israel; but because of the passion they were in danger of
overthrowing even this opinion concerning him, that he redeems Israel. And
this was not happening in vain, but so as to show that "the foolishness of God
is wiser than men." For all were sifted and shaken by the passion of the
cross, not only the ordinary disciples, but also the elect and his mother.
This established the whole inhabited world, in Christ Jesus our Lord, through
whom and with whom be glory to the Father, with the Holy Spirit, now and
always and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
