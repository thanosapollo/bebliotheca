ON PRAYER, Homily 2. That prayer is the head of every good, and procurer of
salvation and of eternal life, no one of all men is ignorant; yet it is
necessary nevertheless to speak the things according to our power concerning
the matter, so that the discourse may make more earnest those who are
accustomed to live in prayers, and to attend carefully to the worship of God;
and that those who have lived more slothfully, and have left the soul deserted
of prayer, may both learn the loss of the time that has passed, and may not
further deprive themselves of salvation for the remaining part of life.
Straightway then we have that greatest thing to say concerning prayer, that
everyone who prays converses with God; and how great a thing it is that one
being a man should speak with God, no one is ignorant; but to set forth the
honor in a word no one would be able. For this honor even surpasses the
magnificence of the angels; which they, knowing well, all appear somewhere
among the prophets offering the hymns and the services to the Master with much
fear, covering both their faces and their feet from much reverence, and by the
flying and by the not being able to remain in quiet showing the great fear;
teaching, I think, us also to forget human nature at the time of prayer, and,
being constrained by eagerness and by fear, to see none of the things present,
but to think that we stand in the midst of the angels, and to accomplish the
same worship as they.

For all the other things are widely separated, both those of theirs I mean and
those of ours, both nature, and way of life, and wisdom, and understanding,
and everything that one might name; but of prayer there is a common work of
angels together and of men; and there is nothing in the middle of either
nature, at least as regards prayer. This separates you from the irrational
creatures, this joins you to the angels; and quickly will one be transferred
into their =50.780= polity, and life, and way of living, and honor, and
nobility, and wisdom, and understanding, being eager to have spent the whole
life in prayers and in the worship of God. For what could be more holy than
those who converse with God? And what more just? And what more orderly? And
what more wise? For if those who speak with wise men are quickly brought by
the continual converse into a likeness of their understanding, what is fitting
to say concerning those who speak with God and pray? With how much wisdom, and
with how much virtue, and understanding, and nobility, and temperance, and
gentleness of manners, does prayer and the supplication fill them! So that one
would not err in declaring prayer to be a starting-point of every virtue and
righteousness, and that nothing of the things that contribute to piety can
enter into a soul deserted of prayer and supplication; but just as an unwalled
city would easily come under the enemies, by a great desert of anyone who
would hinder; so also a soul not walled with prayers the devil easily makes
his own, and readily fills with every sin. For first, whenever he sees a soul
fenced with prayers, he does not dare to come near, fearing the strength and
the power which the prayers furnish, feeding the soul more than grain does the
bodies; then those who pray earnestly do not endure to suffer anything
unworthy of the prayer, but, being ashamed before God, with whom they have
just now conversed, they quickly thrust away every device of the Evil One,
considering

with themselves, how great an evil it is, that one who has just now spoken
with God, and has asked concerning temperance and holiness, should immediately
turn aside to the devil, and receive into the soul the shameful pleasures, and
give the devil an entrance into the mind which God has just now visited, and
permit the demons to enter =50.781= into souls, in which the grace of the
Spirit has displayed much loving-kindness and providence. And how, hear: It is
impossible for a man to be able to bear a divine converse without the energy
of the Spirit, but it is necessary that this being present and taking hold
together of the holy contests, one should thus enter, and bend the knees, and
supplicate, and pray. For since it is greater than according to man to speak
to God, the grace of the Spirit must come into us to strengthen, and to
embolden, and to teach the greatness of the honor. Whenever therefore you
learn that you both speak to God, and have received an energy of the Spirit,
being about to converse, you will give no entrance to the devil into the soul
that has been sanctified by the Spirit. For just as those who have conversed
with a king, and have enjoyed gentle words and honor, do not think it worthy
to come into converse with beggars and those cast out; so he who has spoken
with God and has prayed would not think it worthy to receive the converse of
the evil and impious one. For truly he who is enslaved to pleasures converses
with demons, and has emulated their madness; just as he who is temperate, and
practices righteousness, dwells with angels, and emulates their magnificence.
And it seems to me that one would speak the truth if he said that prayers are
the sinews of the soul. For just as, I think, the body is held together
through the sinews, and runs, and stands, and lives, and is made firm; but if
one should cut these out, he dissolves the whole harmony of the body; so the
souls are fitted together through the holy prayers, and stand composed, and
run the course of piety readily.

But if you deprive yourself of prayer, you will do the same as if you had
drawn the fish out of the water; for as water is life to that one, so prayer
is to you. Through this it is possible, as through water, to fly up, and to
pass beyond the heavens, and to become near to God. Sufficient then even the
things that have been said to show the strength of the holy prayer; but it is
perhaps better, having come to the divine Scriptures, to learn from the
sentences of Christ the wealth which the prayers treasure up for those who
wish to spend their whole life in them. And he spoke, it says, a parable also
to them, toward the need to pray always. There was a certain judge in a
certain city, who did not fear God, and did not regard man. And there was a
widow in that city, and she came to him saying: Avenge me of my adversary; and
he was not willing for a time. But after these things he said in himself: Even
if I do not fear God, and do not regard man, yet because this widow causes me
trouble, I will avenge her, lest coming to the end she wear me out. And the
Lord said: Hear what the judge of injustice says. And will God not make the
avenging of his elect, who cry to him day and night, and is he long-suffering
over them? I say to you, that he will make their avenging speedily. Let us
learn, brethren, the hidden wisdom in the words of the Spirit, searching
according to our power, not how great it is, but as much as is reachable for
us. Those who make their life in the sea, going down to the very deep, bring
the precious stones to those on the land; but let us, having explored the
ocean of the divine Scriptures, run down =50.782= into the very deep of the
spiritual wisdom, as far as it is possible, so that we may bring you a good
treasure that adorns the souls better than the jewelled crowns adorn the heads
of kings. For the beauty of those is finished together with this life; but he
who crowns the soul with the words of the Spirit both now passes through the
time with all safety, and after the end of life comes with confidence to the
judgment-seat of Christ, full of virtue, and clean of every vice. What
treasure then do we bring you from the deep of the Scriptures, not touching
the very depth of the wisdom, but going down so far as is permitted? Christ,
leading men to prayer, and wishing to set before our souls the benefit from
it, introduces a certain wicked and perverse judge, who has thrown away all
shame from his eyes, and has driven the fear of God out of his soul. And yet
it would have been enough to suppose the person of a just and merciful man,
and, having compared that man's justice with the loving-kindness of God, to
show the strength of the supplication. For if a kind and gentle man receives
mildly those who bring a petition, how much more God, whose greatness of
loving-kindness not only exceeds our mind, but even the angels themselves? It
would have been enough then, as I said, to suppose the person of a just judge;
but now he introduces a savage and impious and man-hating judge, unmerciful
toward the others, but kind and gentle toward those who ask, so that you may
learn that every supplication easily draws even a wicked nature into pity and
mercy. For what reason then has Christ done this?

So that no one of all men may be ignorant of the strength of prayer. For this
reason, having set the widow before the most wicked of all, and having shown
him loving toward man contrary to nature, he transferred the word from the
wicked one to his own Father, the good, the gentle, the mild, the loving
toward man, who passes over lawlessness, who forgives many sins, who is
blasphemed every day, and bears it, who endures to see honor unto demons,
insult unto himself, blasphemies unto the Son; countless evils spoken and
unspeakable; then, being blasphemed, he bears it so mildly, but if he sees us
falling down with the fitting fear, will he not have mercy quickly? Hear, he
says, what the judge of injustice says: Even if I do not fear God, and do not
regard man, yet because she causes me troubles, I will avenge her. What do you
say? What fear did not have the strength to do, this supplication had the
strength to do? And a threat, and hope of punishment, did not bring the man to
what is just; but a widow appearing with a petition tamed the wild one. What
then is it fitting to infer concerning the God who loves man, if the widow
falling down has made the savage one so gentle? How much kindness, how much
loving-kindness will God display toward us, he who always wishes only to have
mercy, but never to punish, he who even threatened the punishments to us
through much loving-kindness, and prepared great honors, so that both fear and
hope of honor, the one may raise up toward virtue, and the other may hinder
from wicked deeds! I cannot withdraw my mind from the judge of injustice,
seeing in that man's gentleness contrary to nature the unspeakable
loving-kindness of God. For if he who never wished to display anything
=50.783= good, having suddenly changed, had mercy on her who asked, how much
care from heaven will the prayers bring us? And one would know the strength
and the power of the holy prayers, observing and seeing how many goods those
who always fall down before God enjoy each day and hour. For who does not know
that both the light of the sun, and of the stars, and of the moon, and a good
temper of airs, and foods of every kind, and wealth, and life, and countless
goods God grants alike to all men, both to the just and to the impious,
because of his much loving-kindness, which he uses toward us? And if he so has
mercy each day on those who do not ask, nor request, and gives them rest, of
how many goods is it likely that those will enjoy who have spent their whole
life in prayers and supplications? Come then, let us also say to you as many
of the just as it has been given through prayers to save both nations, and
cities, and the whole inhabited world. And in a memory of prayer Paul is first
worthy of memory, Paul the insatiable of the worship of God, the common
father, and ancestor of the servants of Christ. That guardian of the inhabited
world then, through prayer, and through continual supplication, saved all the
nations, saying always to us: For this cause I bend my knees to the Father of
our Lord Jesus Christ, from whom every family in heaven and on earth is named;
that he would give you according to the wealth of his grace, to be
strengthened with power through his Spirit, unto the inner man, that Christ
may dwell through faith in your hearts. Do you see how much prayer and
supplication are able? They make men temples of Christ; and just as gold, and
precious stones, and marbles make the houses of the kings; so prayer makes
temples of Christ. That Christ may dwell, he says, in your hearts. What
greater encomium of prayer could there be, than that it makes temples of God?
He whom the heavens do not contain, this one enters into a soul living in
prayers. Heaven is my throne, he says, and the earth a footstool of my feet.
What house will you build for me? says the Lord; or what is the place of my
rest? But nevertheless Paul builds a house through the holy prayers. I bend,
he says, my knees to the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, that Christ may
dwell through the faith in your hearts. And indeed from that also one would
see the power of the holy prayers, that Paul, who ran as if winged through the
whole inhabited world, and dwelt in a prison, and endured scourges, and wore a
chain, and lived in blood and dangers, and drove out demons, and raised the
dead, and stopped sicknesses, trusted none of these for the salvation of men,
but walled the earth with the prayers, and after the signs and the
resurrection of the dead, he ran to the prayers, as some athlete to the
wrestling-school from a crown.

For prayer is also the supplier of the resurrection of the dead and of all the
other things; and reasonably; for the strength which waters have upon the
trees, this prayers have upon the life of the saints. In this night watering
the soul, Paul =50.784= endured all the terrible things readily, offering his
back to the blows like a statue. Thus in Macedonia he shook the prison; thus
he burst the bonds like a lion with the prayers; thus he snatched the keeper
of the prison from the error; thus he dissolved the tyranny of the demons. And
indeed we know also those things which he writes to all men: Continue in the
prayer, watching in it, in thanksgiving, praying at the same time also
concerning me, that speech may be given me in the opening of my mouth in
boldness, to speak the mystery of the Gospel. What do you say? Do the prayers
give us such boldness, as to dare to beseech God on behalf of Paul? What
soldier dares to entreat the king on behalf of the great general? And yet no
general is so a friend to the king, as Paul is to God; but nevertheless the
prayers lead us into such honor, as to dare to beseech God on behalf of Paul.
Thus also Peter the great, more splendid than the heaven, escaped the prison,
especially through his own virtue, and the common salvation of men, yet then
also the prayer of the Church quickly opened the closed doors of the prison.
For Luke did not write in vain, that Prayer was being made earnestly of the
whole Church on behalf of Peter, but so that we may learn how much strength
the prayers possess in the heavens, so as to deliver Paul and Peter from
dangers, the pillars of the Church, the chiefs of the apostles, the renowned
in heaven, the wall of the inhabited world, the common safeguard of all earth
and sea. Tell me, how did Moses save Israel in wars? Did he not deliver the
arms and the army to the disciple, while he himself set the prayers against
the multitude of the adversaries? teaching us that the prayers of the just
have a power much greater than even arms, and horses, and money, and camps.
For this reason also the whole army, and many myriads, had the hope of
salvation in the prayer of the prophet; for when Moses was praying, the Jews
prevailed, but when he had ceased from the prayer, they had the worse of the
adversaries. Thus then we also, when we pray, will easily prevail over the
devil, but when we are remiss,

we train the Evil One against ourselves. And indeed also the people that had
acted impiously, Moses saved through prayer alone, and of the divine visions
and of countless goods he obtained through this alone. For this also made his
life near to those who dwell in heaven. Prayer convicted the power of fire,
and tamed lions; the one in the case of Daniel, the other in the case of the
three children. Through which, I think, it has also made it clear to all, that
as many as it may take who are in danger, it easily snatches from the terrible
things that hang over them. Prayer is a starting-point of salvation, a
procurer of immortality of soul, an unbreakable wall of the Church, an
unshaken safeguard, fearful to the demons, but saving for us the pious. This
gave birth to the holy prophet Samuel; for when nature was opposing the mother
toward offspring, prayer appearing quickly corrected the defect of nature.
Such is the fruit of prayer, such a prophet did prayer plant. For =50.785=
which reason Samuel became renowned in the heavens, a zealot of the angels
greater than according to man; for it was necessary, I think, that such an ear
of grain of prayer should appear, conquering the others in nobility, and in
the virtues of manners, and conquering the saints before him by so much, as in
the cornfields those of the ears that do well excel. With this David thrust
away so many and such wars, not moving arms, nor shaking a spear, nor drawing
a sword, but being walled with prayers; with this Hezekiah swiftly turned the
multitude of the Persians to flight. And they were bringing engines against
the wall, but he walled the wall with the prayers; and a war was dissolved
without arms from the prayer, a trumpet not sounding, and an army keeping
quiet, and arms not being moved, and earth not being reddened, but prayer
sufficing unto fear of the adversaries. Prayer also saved the Ninevites, and
quickly beat back the wrath being brought from heaven, and swiftly corrected a
corrupted =50.786= life. For it has such strength and power, that the
Ninevites lived the whole of life shamefully and wickedly, but prayer, having
entered into the city, quickly changed all things, and brought in with itself
temperance, and righteousness, and friendship, and concord, and care of the
poor, and all the goods.

For just as when some queen enters into a city, all the wealth must follow
along, so also when prayer enters into a soul, every virtue enters in with it.
For what a foundation is in a house, this prayer is in a soul. And it is
necessary that all, having first fixed this, as a base and a certain root in
the soul, should earnestly build upon it both temperance, and gentleness, and
righteousness, and care of the poor, and all the laws of Christ, [so that
having lived also according to them henceforth we may obtain the goods in the
heavens, by the grace and loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus Christ,] through
whom and with whom to the Father together with the Holy Spirit be glory and
power unto the ages of ages. Amen.
