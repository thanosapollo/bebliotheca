"Hear me, friends: a divine dream came to me in sleep," said the dream itself.
For this, I thought, was how I was beginning the speech, examining the dream
as a waking vision in my own mind. So let the dream too stand as waking
vision, and the deed as the prophecy had it. The meaning, then, was to compose
an encomium of Podalirius: first of Podalirius, and after that I was drawn on
to Machaon. And as I was at a loss which of the two I ought to praise, at last
it seemed right to praise both. For it was no longer lawful to leave the other
out, once I had conceived of both; and whichever of them it was who came from
the dream, it would come to one and the same thing if both were praised; and
it would stand well for me both toward that one and toward both. So then. When
a god has set the theme, should one fear more, or take heart? Fear one must,
lest we show ourselves worse before so great a judge; yet the hopes are
fairer, for it is likely he will take thought for it. He would not have set
the theme if what came of the speech were not going to turn out as he
intended. And besides, to call him as helper of the speech is wonderfully well
suited. For if the poets, to say what they themselves think fit, call on
Apollo and the Muses, surely our invocation is fairer, when we ask the very
one who set the theme to become leader of the Muses together with his father.
But you who have been called so often and in so many ways, and I can say that
you have also shown the way in other things and in the speeches themselves:
lead forth now too, as you please, the

speech. And the beginning of the encomium is not far off, but the same father
belongs both to the speech and to the young men. For such a fourfold lineage
no Greek has yet heard or related. For as the thesmothetae are of noble birth
through four generations, or rather as no thesmothetae are, nor any other race
of men at all. For they are fourth from Zeus through all who stand at the
peak. Apollo is from Zeus himself, Asclepius from Apollo, and they from
Asclepius, having taken up their nobility through the whole of the strongest
nature. Achilles is fourth from Zeus through Peleus and Aeacus, Minos and
Rhadamanthys sons of Zeus and Theseus of Poseidon, neither of them unique to
either parent, but together with many both gods and heroes, to some of whom
they are inferior, and with others they are set on equal terms. These alone
are unmatched in the number and excellence of their ancestors, surpassing
those from Zeus and from Apollo by the addition of Asclepius, at least those
of them who are heroes. And when they were born, their father reared them in
the gardens of Health. And when their age allowed it, he did not have them
taught the art of medicine, but taught it himself. For there was no longer any
need to go to Chiron's, when they had the master at home, next to whom Chiron,
even by his name, was already far second.

So born and reared and educated, while the affairs of the Achaeans were still
at rest, they were in Thessaly an ornament far more conspicuous than lakes and
plains and rivers, and they set the Greeks of that country on their feet, both
managing public affairs as became those people and setting right each man's
private misfortunes; nor was it possible to be ill wherever Machaon or
Podalirius appeared. But when the Greeks were stirred by the injustice of the
Trojans, they neither thought it right to buy off staying at home nor hid
themselves as some others did, but knowing that the time was theirs and
foreseeing the fortunes of the war, they willingly took the lead for the
salvation of all. And arriving at Troy they rendered a double service

to the Achaeans, and they were not present as physicians only, but they helped
with their arms as well, and they are said to have often put the enemy to
flight; and the taking of Troy came altogether to them, both in other ways and
through the disease of Philoctetes, whom Odysseus and the Atreidae, having
first judged the sickness incurable, left unjustly on Lemnos, but these men
healed it after it had grown for ten years. And Philoctetes becomes of use to
the Achaeans, and for Philoctetes the arrows of Heracles become of use by
their art. And when Troy was taken, foreseeing the later Greek colonies into
Asia and wishing at the same time that as many as possible should enjoy their
benefaction, they both civilize Teuthrania for the reception of their father,
and, as the Coan story has it, sailing to Cos the Meropid, which Heracles,
coming back from Troy still earlier, had sacked after charging its Meropian
inhabitants with injustice, they settle it and bring it into habits fitting
the nature of the land, imitating, as it seems, what is told of their
ancestor. For the poets say that Apollo set Delos, which had formerly been
drifting, by bracing it upon the sea, when first he was born there; and these
men, landing then on Meropis, judging it the fairest of all islands of like
size, both healed it and made it open to landing for all Greeks and
barbarians, when it had formerly been treacherous and suspect, and they
established prosperity as the island's rightful possession. And I think that
for the Rhodians too, among many long-standing glories, their rule is among
the first, the rule which they themselves preferred, choosing Asclepiads to
govern them, making them successors of the Heraclids. They also held the
Carian country and Cnidus, sacred to Aphrodite; for whatever either one or
both of them accomplished, let it be held in common for now. Cyrnus too had
some benefit of them.

But these things belong to many speeches. And having made

children as fellow-workers and successors of their own science, they set this
as it were as the crown of their benefactions to the Greeks, and I will add
now to all men as well, so that their aid and grace should never fail the race
of men, but that they should be, from ancestors and descendants, immortal
saviors of our nature, living among men for all time and present with all as
they were with those of their own day. For they put down those who were cried
up in Egypt, and made benefactions the badge of their race. They are not
settled in Thessaly nor in the suburbs of the Coans, but they filled all
things with medicine, as Triptolemus filled all things with grain through the
seeds; for everything went forth from these men and from their side. And just
as they say Medea, fleeing through the plain of the Thessalians, when her
drugs were spilled, made all Thessaly rich in drugs, so their science and love
of men, poured out to the greatest extent, both adorned and still adorns all
the cities of the Greeks and many barbarian places as well, and further saved
and still saves, and let the third tense be added to both. And if one man from
these, Hippocrates, had been born heir of the art, through all the ordinary
private persons in between, the earth would have had a sufficient crop, and
these men sufficient thanks from mankind for the sowing. But as it is, the
race of the Asclepiads was established as a people, preserving the art through
blood, so divine destiny led the way for Machaon and Podalirius in their
birth. And one might learn this by setting them against the Heraclids, for the
sake of the common need and of their private fortune. For the Heraclids, first
of all, were scattered, and there was not one body of them, nor even, so to
speak, one tribe. For they were not of equal honor, so that they stood to one
another as foreigners; and next, the strongest part of them is said neither to
have been free of misfortunes nor to have preserved their ancestral art
altogether. For not by benefactions to the common good, but by their own

power they acquired their splendor. But the Asclepiads, beginning from Machaon
and Podalirius, have become a common safety and salvation for all mankind,
having preserved the art of their ancestor as another badge of the race. And
besides, their fortune is worthy of their choice. For they were neither driven
out nor did they come as suppliants to any city, but they went through clean
of misfortunes, using one clan and one mind and one fortune throughout.

I return to the point from which I turned aside, to the founders and those who
first took the name of the Asclepiads. And while they were among men, they
benefited the cities by campaigns and associations and the begetting of
children worthy of themselves, and in a word by all civic power, not only
taking out the diseases of the body, but also healing the diseases of cities,
or rather not even allowing them to arise in the first place, saving their
subjects from both, and making their rule follow upon their art. And when they
were too great to remain among us, they, stripping off their bodies under
their father and their ancestors, pass into another ordinance, not as Menelaus
and fair-haired Rhadamanthys, to the Elysian plain and the islands beyond, but
becoming immortal they go through the earth, having left their ancient nature
only so far as they keep their age. And many have already seen them at
Epidaurus and recognized them moving visibly, and many elsewhere in many
places; and let this be the greatest thing said of them. For Amphiaraus and
Trophonius in Boeotia and Amphilochus in Aetolia both give oracles and appear,
but these dart everywhere on earth, like stars, common patrols and forerunners
of their father. And wherever there are entries for Asclepius, side-chambers
too are opened for them throughout the earth, and through all things their
communion with their father is preserved: of temples,

of sacrifices, of paeans, of processions, of the works they do. O blessed are
you in your ancestors above, both ways, and fortunate in those born from you,
and still in yourselves and your sisters, with whom Iaso and Panacea and Aegle
keep company, and Hygieia, the counterweight of all, children of Epione, named
for what they are; nor have you seats apart from one another, nor did you
pitch camp separately. O fairest chorus yourselves for your father, and
leading many up from among men, far the best of all chorus-leaders, and
besides officers of the rites and stewards of mixing-bowls and of all the
Graces: the other sacrifices and festivals are established by custom, so to
speak, but those from you and from the workshop about you are the most in a
day and against all the others, and they come purely from the heart, bringing
the cheer of what we know at first hand, your traces most many and most plain.
And always, as a shadow follows a man, light follows wherever you move. O
equal in portion with the Dioscuri and of like age in another time of birth,
you who have already stilled many triple waves, and have kindled many bright
lamps both in islands and on the mainland: this is the speech from me,
composed at once from sleep and from a dream. And you, by your gentleness and
love of men, disposing it toward fairer things, both stop the disease and may
you grant as much health as those need for the body to obey what the soul
wishes, and, to say it all, ease of life.
