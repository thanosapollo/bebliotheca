for the essence is not a body. Anom. If the unbegotten God is generative as
a whole, the thing begotten was not begotten essentially, the whole of his
essence having begetting, but not being begotten. Orth. According to your
wisdom, if the light is generative as a whole, it does not beget the
radiance essentially, but takes it on from outside, the whole of its essence
having begetting, but not being begotten. But if the light does not take on
the radiance from outside, but begets it from the essence, neither did God
acquire a Son from outside, but having begotten from the essence he remains
undivided, for he is bodiless. Anom. And if the essence of God, having been
reshaped, is called an offspring, his essence is not unchangeable, the
change having worked the formation of the Son. Orth. If the light μ28.1181
having been reshaped becomes a radiance, the light is not unchangeable, the
change having worked the formation of the radiance. But if the light has not
become a radiance by being changed, but the light remains light and is cause
of a radiance, God also remains God and is Father of his own radiance, not
having taken it on from outside, but having begotten from the essence.
Therefore he is also truly Father. Anom. And if the essence of God should be
better even than generation, the things concerning the Son will be confessed
as far as a mere appellation. Orth. If you knew that generation is not an
essence, you would have known that both the Son and every other essence is
better than generation. For if generation is an essence, all begotten things
are homoousios. But if not all begotten things are homoousios, generation is
not then an essence. And if generation is not an essence, it is not even
better than essence. For if generation is better than essence, it will be

If the offspring was in the unbegotten in a seminal way, after generation,
having taken on from outside, it was, as one might say, made man. The Son is
perfect not from the things from which he was begotten, but from the things
he took on; for things that take on by kinship are naturally disposed to
admit the name of the perfect differently, as standing from those things.
Orth. If the radiance is in the light in a seminal way, say also that the
Son is in the Father in a seminal way. But if it is foolish to say that the
radiance is in the light in a seminal way, it is also impious to say that
the Son is in the Father in a seminal way. And the Son is perfect, not being
perfected by advance, but having been begotten perfect from the perfect. For
this reason he has been called not only radiance, but also impress of the
hypostasis, and not of the energy. And the impress of the hypostasis is not
perfected from advance, but in the very being it has the perfect, as a man
is the impress of a man's hypostasis not from advance, but from the identity
itself. Anom. If there was a perfect offspring in the unbegotten, he is an
offspring from the things that he was in the unbegotten, and not from the
things that the unbegotten himself begot; for it is not possible that a
begotten nature existing in an unbegotten should be, for the same thing both
is and is not. For an unbegotten offspring is not, and being unbegotten he
was not an offspring, the dissimilar-parted blasphemy μ28.1184 holding

Aetius. And he was not first and then was begotten, nor has he been begotten

And a begotten nature and an unbegotten nature do not have the difference in

Gabriel, being of begotten nature, does not know himself homoousios with the
Son, the begotten is not a nature. And if the begotten is not a nature,
neither is the unbegotten a nature. [Do not call the Son generated, the
maker of all things, but say begotten as it is written. For begotten is that
which has a father, and generated is that which has a maker.] God then is
our maker through the Son, and of the Son he is Father and no longer maker,
for no one is maker of his own son, since he would no longer be a father,
but a maker. And a maker is one who brings something from outside into
generation, but a father is one who begets from himself. And hearing God
called maker do not suspect toil and labor, and hearing Father do not seek a
flowing, but think in a manner befitting God, that you may become God and
not die as a man. Anom. If the unbegotten does not present the hypostasis of
God, but the incomparable name is a human conception, God knows thanks to
those who conceived it, because through the unbegotten he does not bear the
superiority of the name in essence. Orth. If the unbegotten presents the
hypostasis of God and this is the incomparable name of God, how did the
divine Scriptures not teach us this? But Paul, the vessel of election, went
wrong, having taught that the incorruptible and the invisible belong to him
alone, and not the unbegotten. But since you μ28.1185 want to be wiser than
the divine Scriptures, say whether the incorruptible and the invisible and
the unlying do not present the hypostasis of God. For if they do not present
it, he went wrong in saying "to the incorruptible, invisible, only wise
God." For if he does not have the incomparable in these, he is not alone
incorruptible and invisible; but if he alone has the incorruptible and the
invisible, the incomparable is in these and not in your conception. Anom. If
the unbegotten is contemplated upon God from outside, those who contemplated
it are better than the one contemplated, having furnished him a name better
than the nature. Orth. Do not exalt yourself as having found the name of
God, lest you ever hear: "And you, Capernaum, if you are exalted as far as
heaven, you will be brought down as far as Hades." And that you are
high-minded above this is clear from this, from the God-inspired Scripture
saying that God has said: "I appeared," he says, "to Abraham and Isaac and
Jacob, being their God, and my name I did not declare to them," but you say
you have found it. And that the unbegotten is not a name of God is clear
from the Lord's saying "Go, make disciples of all the nations, baptizing
them into the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit," for
whatever you say is a name of the Father, this you will be forced to give
also to the Son and to the Holy Spirit; for if it is one of the Father and
another of the Son and another of the Holy Spirit, and you have found that
of the Father as you supposed, tell also that of the Son, and if you tell
that of the Son, tell also that of the Holy Spirit, that then you may hear,
professing to be wise you became a fool; for what the Scripture did not say
you will not find. And if you think the unbegotten belongs to the Father and
the begotten to the Son, which of these then will you give to the Spirit?
Anom. Even if the offspring is unchangeable in nature, yet it is so because
of the one who begot; but the unbegotten is an unchangeable essence not

because of the will, but because of the dignity in essence. Orth. And if the
Son of God is not unchangeable by dignity of essence, in what does he differ
from the other rational powers, they too having the unchangeable in the will
of the one who created? And how do you also hear him saying: "all things
whatsoever the Father has are mine," if he does not have the unchangeable in
the dignity of the essence? Anom. If the unbegotten is indicative of
essence, it is reasonably distinguished against the essence of the
offspring; but if the unbegotten signifies nothing, much more then the
offspring also indicates nothing, and how can one distinguish nothing
against nothing? And if the unbegotten utterance is distinguished against
the begotten utterance, silence μ28.1188 succeeding the utterance, it
happens that the hope of Christians both comes to be and passes away, lying
in a different utterance, but not in natures that are as the meaning of the
names wishes. Orth. When it is granted you to say the unbegotten of God,
then we say that the unbegotten signifies that the essence has not been
begotten, as the unlying that the essence does not lie, and neither the
unlying is the essence nor the unbegotten; for if the unbegotten is the

therefore unbegotten; but he is not unbegotten according to you, yet he is

begotten things are homoousios. And that the unbegotten and the begotten are
indicative of essence has been found from your utterance, which must be
rebuked as a sea that is surging. For the Spirit of truth proclaimed to us
Father and Son, the names signifying to us the relations, and you will not
contradict that God is Father by nature and in truth; and how is he Father,

who conceived that the unbegotten and the begotten are indicative of
essence, silence succeeding the utterance, is not found on the day of
judgment, those alone being found of which the Holy Spirit
spoke. Anom. If the unbegotten signifies nothing more toward superiority of
essence in relation to the offspring, the Son, being superior only by
utterance, will know those who addressed him as better than himself, not the
God who was addressed as his God and Father. Orth. You are the one who
addressed the Father as unbegotten and legislated that this is his essence,
for the Jews; for nowhere did the divine Spirit teach this. And that you
will not be found better than the Son, but have been confessed worse than
the devil who taught you. Anom. If the unbegotten essence is better than
generation, having the better from itself, it is unbegotten self-essence;
for it is not by willing, because it wills, that it is better than
generation, but because the unbegotten God, existing as self-essence, by no
account allows generation to be conceived against itself, thrusting every
examination and every reasoning to be carried against the begotten things.
Orth. Every essence is better than generation. For essence and generation
are not the same. And that generation is not essence is clear from this,
from the begotten things not being homoousios. And if the generated things
are not homoousios, generation is not then essence. And if generation is not
essence, neither is unbegottenness essence. And if unbegottenness is
essence, and generation is not essence, how do you say unlike? For that
which is, to that which is not, is neither like nor unlike. μ28.1189 Anom.
If the unbegotten upon God is indicative of privation, and the unbegotten
should be nothing, what account would take nothing away from nothing? And if
it signifies a being, who would separate being from being, which is itself
of itself? Orth. If the incorruptible and the immortal are indicative of
privation, let the unbegotten also be so. Let us then use the same
arguments; if the incorruptible upon God is indicative of privation, and the
incorruptible is nothing, what account would take nothing away from nothing?
And if it signifies a being, who would separate being from being, which is
indicative of privation, the incorruptible and the immortal and the
unbegotten? And if the unbegotten and the incorruptible, or again also the
immortal, signify the same, and

the Son also is incorruptible and immortal, he too then is unbegotten. And
if he is not unbegotten, being incorruptible and immortal, the unbegotten
and the incorruptible, or again the immortal, do not signify the same. And
if they do not signify the same, and you yourself rather judge the
unbegotten to be the essence; there will then according to you and according
to the arguments of your wisdom be an essence that is unbegotten, but not
immortal, nor indeed incorruptible. Anom. If privation is a taking away of
habits, the unbegotten upon God will be either a privation of habit or a
habit of privation. But if it is a privation of habit, how would what is not
present to God be numbered with him? And if the unbegotten is a habit, it is
necessary to presuppose a begotten essence, so that thus, having taken on a
habit, it may be named unbegotten. And if the begotten has shared in a habit
of unbegotten essence, having undergone a casting off it has been deprived
of generation, the begotten would be essence, and the unbegotten a habit.
And if the offspring is indicative of nothing, it is clearly significative
of habit, whether it is remolded by someone's authority, or this is what it
is called, someone's offspring. Orth. Again we will use the same arguments;
for one would say the same also of the unlying and of the immortal and of
the incorruptible; for the unbegotten is of the same use, whenever for the
time being we grant you to say what the Spirit of truth did not say. We too
then will say: if privation is a taking away of habits, the unlying upon God
is either a privation of habit or a habit of privation. But if it is a
privation of habit, how would what is not present to God be numbered with
him? And if the unlying is a habit, it is necessary to presuppose a lie and
a lying essence, so that thus, having taken on a habit, it may be named
unlying. And if the lying has shared in an unlying essence, having undergone
a casting off of habit it has been deprived of falsehood, there would then
be a lying essence, and the unlying a habit, and your high-minded wisdom,
having found so great an impiety of matter. μ28.1192 Anom. If the begotten
is a habit and the unbegotten is a habit, the essences are first of the
habits, and the habits, even if second of the essences, are nevertheless
more honorable. And if the unbegotten is cause of the begotten's being, and
the offspring as signified brings in together the cause of its own essence,
the offspring is indicative of essence, and not of a habit of the unbegotten
nature which brings nothing in together for itself; how would the unbegotten
nature not be essence, but a habit? Orth. Nothing is more honorable than
essence, O most wise one, and we say that the unbegotten upon God is neither
a habit nor an essence. And that he has not been begotten we have learned
through him, and this not from the divine Scripture, but from human nature
and thought. The unbegotten then is not cause of the begotten; for it is not
that God has not been begotten that is cause of the Son, but the essence of
the essence as the light of the radiance. Nor indeed does the offspring
bring in together its own essence, for otherwise all begotten things would
bring in together the same essence, both angel and horse and dog and man,
for all alike are begotten, but not of the same essence. And if this is so,
the begotten is not then essence. Even if then what the unbegotten is is not
introduced, it is not for this reason essence, for the unlying also
introduces nothing and is not essence; for if the unlying is essence and the
unbegotten is essence, the unlying is the same as the unbegotten. And the
Son is unlying, being truth, therefore unbegotten; but he is not unbegotten
according to you, yet he is unlying. Therefore the unlying does not have the
same signification as the unbegotten. And if it is not the same
signification, the unbegotten is not then essence, since neither is the
unlying. Anom. If every essence is unbegotten, such as that of God the
Almighty, how will you call the one passible and the other impassible? And
if by an allotment of unbegotten nature the one remains better than quantity
and quality and, to speak simply, every change, and the other has been
conceded as liable to passion, to have the indistinguishability in essence
ought to be left to chance according to the things already said; or at least
the consistent thing is to call the one that makes unbegotten, and the one
that is changed begotten. Orth. We ought then not even to answer toward the
inconsistency of the premises and of the inductions, but that you may not
seem to yourself to be prudent,

learn not to blaspheme. Saying the essence of the Son is passible, do not
expect to inherit impassibility from him, for if we have become partakers of
it, we remain impassible, therefore the one that furnishes impassibility to
others is impassible. And the one who says every essence is unbegotten is
not a Christian together with you, for we the things made do not have the
same account of being as the one who made. For this reason we also say that
the Son is a radiance, that we may not think him to have μ28.1193 the same
account of being that we have, we who exist from things that are not,
according to a command. Anom. If the unbegotten nature is cause of that
which comes to be, and the unbegotten should be nothing, how would the
nothing be cause of that which has come to be? Orth. The nature of God is
cause both of the Son and of the Holy Spirit and of the whole creation, but
of the Son as of Word, for "in the beginning was the Word," and of the
Spirit as of breath, for "the breath of the Almighty that taught me," and of
the creation as of a thing made, for "from the greatness and beauty of
created things the maker of them is analogously contemplated." And it is not
an uttered word sent out through the vocal organs, nor some breath that is
poured out and sent out through the respiratory organs, but both a living
and active Word, for "all things came to be through him," and a living and
active Spirit, for "all these things the one and the same Spirit works,
distributing to each privately as he wills." And the unbegotten is not cause
of the begotten, for it is not that God has not come to be that is cause of
the essences, but the essence is cause of the essences as has been said. For
if the unbegotten is cause, and the Son also is cause of the creatures, he
is then unbegotten; but he is not unbegotten according to you, yet he is
cause; therefore the unbegotten is not cause. Anom. If the unbegotten is a
privation, and privation is a casting off of habit, and the casting off is
utterly destroyed or is transferred to another, how is it possible for the
essence of God to have been named with the appellation of unbegotten nature
by a habit that is being transferred or destroyed? Orth. If being unlying he
is unlying according to a privation of falsehood, and being incorruptible he
is incorruptible according to a privation of corruption, say the unbegotten
also according to privation. And you are the one who named the essence of
God, you who dare such things fighting against God, for he himself saying as
has been said before: "I appeared to Abraham and Isaac and Jacob, your
fathers, being their God, and my name I did not reveal to them," you assign
great things to yourself; for either as having gone above those you have the
knowledge of the name from a revelation, or even when God was unwilling you
have grasped the name of his essence? Anom. If the unbegotten indicates a
privation not present to God, how do we say that he is unbegotten, and not
that he is begotten? Orth. If the incorruptible indicates a privation not
present to God, how do we say incorruptible, and do not say corruptible? And
if the incorruptible is not indicative of privation, neither is the
unbegotten, nor indeed of essence. For if the unbegotten is indicative of
essence, the incorruptible also will be indicative of essence, and if the
unbegotten and the incorruptible will be indicative of essence, the unlying
and the incorruptible would be the same as the unbegotten; and the Son is
unlying and incorruptible and therefore unbegotten. But he is not unbegotten
according to you, therefore the unlying and incorruptible will not be the
same signification, but we say that the unbegotten signifies that God has
not been begotten, and the incorruptible that he is not corrupted, and the
unlying that he does not lie. μ28.1196 Anom. If the unbegotten upon God is a
mere name, μ28.1196 and the mere utterance raises the hypostasis above all
begotten things, the utterance of men is more honorable than the hypostasis
of the Almighty, having adorned God the Almighty with an incomparable
superiority. Orth. We say that the unbegotten is neither a mere utterance
nor an essence. For if the unbegotten is essence, the begotten also is
essence, and if the begotten is essence, all begotten things are
homoousios; therefore the begotten is not essence. And if the begotten is
not essence, neither is the unbegotten essence, and the unbegotten is not a
mere utterance, for it signifies that

God has not been begotten, as the unlying that he does not lie and the
incorruptible that he is not corrupted. Anom. If a cause has been allotted
to every begotten thing, and the unbegotten nature is without cause, the
unbegotten does not indicate a cause, but signifies essence. Orth. If the
unbegotten signifies essence and the begotten essence, and essence differs
in nothing from essence insofar as it is essence; the unbegotten then
differs in nothing from the begotten. Anom. If with the essence of the
offspring the hypostasis of the unbegotten is co-indicated as cause, having
the indistinguishability against every cause, it is incomparable
self-essence, not showing the unapproachable from outside, but itself
existing incomparable and unapproachable because unbegotten. Orth. If
because of the appellation of the unbegotten you call the essence of God
incomparable and unapproachable, the Son according to you, not being
unbegotten, is neither incomparable nor unapproachable, therefore it is not
because of the unbegotten that he is incomparable. And how is the Father
also incomparable to the one who says: "as the Father raises the dead and
makes them live, so also the Son makes live whom he wills," or how
incomparable to the one who says: "for whatever the Father does, these
things the Son also does likewise"? or how incomparable to the one who says:
"all things whatsoever the Father has are mine," and in other places: "mine
are yours and yours are mine"? and how incomparable to the one who says:
"that they may honor the Son as they honor the Father"? Anom. If the
Almighty surpasses every nature, he surpasses because of the unbegotten,
which is cause of continuance for the begotten things. And if the unbegotten
is not indicative of causality, from where would the nature of the begotten
things have being preserved? Orth. If for this reason the Almighty surpasses
every nature, because of the unbegotten, the Son, according to you not being
unbegotten, does not surpass every nature, therefore it is not because of
the unbegotten that the Father surpasses every nature, but by the account by
which the Father surpasses every nature, by this account the Son also
