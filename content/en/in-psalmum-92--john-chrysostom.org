* On Psalm 92
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

On the saying of the ninety-second psalm, "The rivers have lifted up their
voices"; and concerning the Savior walking on the sea, on the five loaves and
the two fishes, and on the first-formed concerning the transgression.

=55.611=

1. Taking our starting-points from the things just now read to us from the
Gospel according to Matthew, in the storm of the sea, and in the
wonder-working of the Lord, and in the faint-heartedness of the apostles, it
is timely for us to speak the things of the prophet: The rivers have lifted
up, O Lord, the rivers have lifted up their voices, from the voices of many
waters; wonderful are the swellings of the sea. Who are the swellings of the
sea? The wonder-working of the Lord, that by a nod alone he caused the raging
sea to grow calm. And who are the rivers that lifted up voices in the sea, if
not altogether the disciples of the Lord? For the apostles are rivers, as
having come forth from the ever-flowing =55.612= fountain, Christ, according
to the saying: He who believes in me, rivers of living water shall flow from
his belly. And the Lord did not cease working wonders; and the apostles did
not stand away from righteousness, being tossed in the sea.

For when they perceived the Lord making his way across the back of the sea at
the fourth watch of the night, they thought it was a phantom. What sort of
phantom? The maidservant recognized the Master, and the disciples did not
recognize the Master? Having such proofs of his wonders, they doubted, when
they perceived him walking the back of the sea as on land. Was it not he who
through the Mosaic staff laid bare the deep gulf =55.613= and made the sons of
Israel pass through with a dusty foot? They thought it was a phantom, and they
were afraid. Thus they feared with a fear where there was no fear. For they
were afraid when they perceived the harbor of life drawing near. And when all
were afraid, Peter drawing near said to the Lord: Lord, if it is you, command
me to come to you upon the waters. And the Lord to him, Come. Then to the
disciples, Take courage, it is I, do not be afraid. I do not appear as a
phantom, but I speak truth. For I, the Truth, became man, that I might abolish
the one who makes phantoms. It is I, do not be afraid. Why do you imagine
things from the blowing of the winds? Was it not concerning me that the saying
spoke: He who brings forth winds from his treasuries? And now I have sent out
the winds, not that I might trouble you, but that I might establish you. When
the Lord was saying these things, Peter again leaping forward, as being hot in
faith, answered saying: Lord, if you are the Christ who is known by words, and
doubted by deeds; for nowhere have we perceived you walking the sea; if you
are the Christ, command me to come to you upon the waters. And the Lord to
him: Come, if you have a faith that bears you. I grant nothing to irony. If
you do not believe, you do not walk. And when Peter had leaped down toward the
Lord's promise, he began to walk the sea as on land, having his faith unshaken
by the surge. But when he had gone forward a little, since he was of human
condition, subject to change, being shaken as a man, he doubted, he grew
slack, and began to say to himself: The thing that appears is unbelievable; I
am walking the sea, the wet casts forth dust.

While Peter was doubting in this, the fellow-servant sea, as against an
unbelieving servant, began to swell with waves, and to chasten the
fellow-servant, and to sink the unbeliever. Whence Peter, perceiving that
faith was furnishing the footing, but unbelief was bringing a surge, cried out
saying: Master, save, I perish. What are you saying, O Peter? When you are in
danger, then you recognize the Master? What then does Christ do? I do not
leave you to the deep, I will stretch out the hand; for I bear all things in
the hand. And stretching out the hand, it says, the Lord took hold of him, and
says to him: O you of little faith, why did you doubt? Great is the divided
mind of the apostles. Before the resurrection they were shaken; after the
resurrection, having reaped firmness, they became unshaken in the faith. And
that before the resurrection they were shaken with many controversies, you
were just now hearing, how when evening had come the apostles came to the Lord
saying: Send the crowd away, for the place is desert, that they may go away
into the surrounding villages and buy themselves food. See here also how
unbelieving the thought is. They came to the Savior saying: Send the crowd
away, for the place is desert; and the Lord to them: Shall I send the crowd
away? Why do you bring dishonor upon me? Why do you pass sentence against the
teacher? Shall I send the crowds away, that they may buy food? And what will
those who sell them the loaves say? That while I am present these will buy
loaves?

If I were not bread, you would do well in urging the sending away; but now,
being bread, how shall I send away hungry those who wait upon me? The crowds
wait, and shall I drive away those who wait? What shepherd of sheep does this?
I sent Habakkuk the lentil-bearer to Daniel in the lions' pit; I was supplying
the manna from heaven to the sons of Israel for forty years, to the
ungrateful, and shall I starve the grateful? No, by no means. You give them to
eat. And the apostles say: Shall we give them to eat? From where shall we
give, who have been commanded to take nothing for the way? Except five loaves
and two fishes we have nothing more; and what are these for five thousand men
besides women and children? And the Lord says to them: From where shall you
give? You have the treasury of faith. Did I not say to you, that If you have
faith as a grain of mustard seed, you will say to this mountain, Be taken up
and be cast into the sea, and it will happen? Mountains are moved, and loaves
are not multiplied? You give them to eat. And when they were declining, and
growing dizzy at the giving, the Lord to them: Make the crowds recline. I am
sent to the mind [read: upon the ministry] =55.614=; I do not refuse serving
the servants. For this is my ancient practice. I served Adam in paradise; what
I undertook there, I do not refuse here also.

2. And it is altogether possible to say what service also, and when, God
undertook toward Adam. Hear with understanding. For it is good to discharge
the sixth day, the last of God's creation, in which we made the promise. For
this reason also the Creator served Adam alone, taking him at once as both a
fashioner and a partner of his own creatures. You all know clearly how in
paradise the Creator, having seated Adam in a high place, brought to him each
of the living things. The Master from the beginning did not refuse serving a
servant, his own creature; from which also in the Gospel just now read he did
not refuse to serve the servants, he made them recline upon grass, and taking
the loaves he broke them, and was giving them to the disciples to offer,
feeding, multiplying, working wonders, supplying what was needed. From which
also in the paradise of the ancient creation, bringing his goodness, having
seated Adam in a high place, the Creator was bringing each one of the living
things, that whatever Adam wished, this very thing he might name, taking him
as partner of his own creatures; which the Scripture set forth saying: And God
formed still from the earth all the beasts of the field, and all the cattle;
and he brought them to Adam, to see what he would call them. Let them learn
that they were brought forth for you. Receive my economy.

Was anything so great done upon an angel, or upon another creature, as upon
man? Since he formed him alone with his own hands, the Creator served him
alone, this on the one hand by the bringing forth of the living things, and
this on the other in the beauty of the garments. From which also in the
incarnation, making it known to his own disciples, he began to wash their
feet, presenting the impression of the ancient creation. And when Peter from
reverence was declining the washing of the feet, the Lord to him: If I do not
wash your feet, you have no part with me. I am the one who served Adam, this
on the one hand by bringing forth the living things, and this on the other by
building his side into a woman, bringing to him from himself what is his, and
before him what is mine through myself. From which he shows, friend, that man
alone among creatures is an incomparable and exceptional work of God. From
which also concerning him alone it is written: Let us make man according to
our image and according to likeness. Great is God's relation toward man; and
the Creator counted Adam worthy of so great a glory as no other creature
whatsoever shared; but Adam, having grown careless, slipped down into the pit
of Hades, drawing poverty upon himself instead of a kingdom, so that the
master became a slave, the all-ruler a beggar, he who was clothed with
comeliness not even well supplied with a staff, he who spent time with God
each day in friendly converse, then hiding under a tree as a thief, he who
received paradise as a dwelling becoming an exile from paradise, and being
settled opposite paradise, that seeing the loss of paradise he might not cease
blaming the vain counsel. For what else was Adam doing, having his dwelling
outside paradise, digging, toiling, delving, turning clods, flooded with sweat
on every side, than reproaching his wife with a great mouth, saying thus: See
the profits of your counsel; that you might play with the serpent, I have been
alienated from the gift of God?

Did you not promise me, saying, woman, if you taste of the forbidden fruit,
you will become blessed? Did you not promise to make me a god after the eating
of the forbidden fruit? How then have I, the god, been shown a digger? I, the
blessed, been found thrice-wretched? You stumbled, and I am scourged; I have
lost the side, and I have not found a helper. Woe is me, woman Eve, mother of
death, begetter of disobedience, expeller from paradise, lover of the dragon's
hissings, a fitting instrument of the devil's devices. Depart, woman; why have
you reaped my immortality? Why have you torn my royal robe? What you did not
weave, you have rent. Why did you teach me to sew fig leaves? Why did you
bring me a snaring fruit, woman of a sorcerer's mouth, saying: Receive, eat of
the fruit that was begrudged you? For on whatever day you eat, your eyes will
be opened, and =55.615= you will be as God knowing good and evil. What have
you done, woman? I have been blinded, I do not see. I have done the evil, the
good has leaped away. I toil like a ray; I do not hold authority as God.
Depart, woman, I do not know who you are. Before being joined I seek the
dissolution; I cannot bear the toil. You, being without care, do not feel the
pain. When you draw near to childbirth, then you will be reminded of the
sentence. Depart, you have become procurer of unbearable evils for me, woman;
I have been commanded to work the earth, to eat bread in the sweat of the
face; the earth has been commanded to sprout thorns and thistles for me. This
sentence I received from God. What shall I do? When shall I forge a mattock?
When shall I prepare a plow? When shall I teach a bull to obey the yoke? When
shall I raise the clods? When shall I cast seed? When shall I wait for the
ear? When shall I sharpen a sickle? When shall I reap a sheaf? When shall I
fill a wagon? When shall I clean a threshing-floor? When shall I score a
threshing-sledge? When shall I join a winnowing-fan? When shall I hide leaven?
When shall I shape bread? When shall I kindle ovens?

I cannot bear the pain. With how many toils and sweats do I eat the bread? See
of how many evils you have become procurer for me, woman, named Life, and
found death. Truly the woman is procurer of death, from then even until now.
And let not the company of women rise in faction against me when I say this
concerning woman; let me render the charge, if they are willing to receive it.
Let them not be angry with me, but with Christ-bearing Paul. If they dare to
insult, let them insult him. We as his disciples hand on his words; we do not
invent our own. Let them hear Paul crying through us: For I betrothed you to
one husband, to present a chaste virgin to Christ. And I fear lest somehow as
the serpent deceived Eve, he may deceive you also. Do you see that the man was
not tripped up, but the woman was deceived? Who was the one deceived? Who was
the one who played with the serpent? The serpent-mouthed and venom-casting
woman.

Who played with the serpent? She who practices mockeries. There was no one in
paradise except her and her husband; and fearing neither the solitude nor
anything else she was going about wandering, roaming, where she might find a
paramour, practicing adulterous sowing before the joining of marriage. If she
had been present with the husband, the =55.616= serpent would not have drawn
near to the serpent. He found her outside the husband, and baited her. For a
woman always being with a lawful husband does not throughout endure
corruption, does not receive defeat, does not incur adultery; but when the
husband is separated, even if she is spending time with angels, she becomes
easy to assail. Where else do women separated from lawful husbands practice
the abominable deeds, if not in the martyr-shrines, on the pretext of
supplication, ranging themselves with the marriage-breakers, and despising
even the martyrs themselves? For often they even swear by the martyrs not to
set aside the unlawful deeds. O of how many evils is Eve a teacher, an
obliteratress of the good things. And all women who love self-control are
called valiant. For there is a great difference even among women, as also
among men, as also among angels; and as many women as love self-control follow
the virgin, long for Susanna, greet Sarah, embrace Rebecca; but as many as are
outside self-control wander as Eve, slander as the Egyptian, shave as Delilah,
murder the godly as Jezebel. The word is catholic, the riddle is not partial.
Let not again some of the godless, having received the words freshly, begin to
have their ears bitten, preparing us for thoughts (read: dissensions).

We have no account of these. The word is apostolic. The riddle is because of
Eve. From which also in the yearly cycle we recapitulate the same things,
bringing forward as witness of what is said the divine Paul who has said, For
I betrothed you to one husband, to present a chaste virgin to Christ. Lest
somehow as the serpent deceived Eve, the wife of Adam, the cause of the fall.
And again the same Apostle, Adam, he says, was not deceived, but his wife Eve,
having been deceived, has come to be in transgression; but she shall be saved
through childbearing, if they remain in faith and love and sanctification with
self-control, not with licentiousness. He said, She shall be saved through
childbearing, not as Eve bore Cain, but as the virgin carried Christ in the
womb; to whom be the glory and the power together with the Father and the Holy
Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
