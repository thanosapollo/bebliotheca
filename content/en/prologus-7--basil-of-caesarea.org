PROEM OF THE SAME.
Concerning the judgment of God.

By the kindness and love of mankind of the good God, in the grace of our Lord
Jesus Christ according to the working of the Holy Spirit, having been
delivered from the error according to the tradition of those outside, and
having been reared from above and from the beginning by Christian parents, I
learned from them from infancy also the sacred writings, which led me toward
knowledge of the truth. But when I became a man, and using journeys often, and
encountering, as is likely, more affairs, in the other arts and sciences I
learned so great an agreement with one another of those who accurately pursue
each; but in the Church of God alone, for which Christ died, and upon which
the Holy Spirit poured himself out richly, I beheld a certain great and
exceeding disagreement of the many both toward one another and toward the
divine Scriptures; and the most dreadful thing, that those who preside over it
themselves stand in so great a difference of mind and of opinion toward one
another, and use so great an opposition toward the commandments of our Lord
Jesus Christ, and tear apart mercilessly the Church of God, and throw into
confusion unsparingly his flock, so that even in them is fulfilled now, if
ever, after the Anomoeans have sprung up, the saying, From among yourselves
men will arise speaking perverse things, to draw away the disciples after
them.

Seeing these and such things, and being at a loss besides as to what and from
where is the cause of so great an evil, at first I lived as in deep darkness,
and as upon a balance I inclined now this way, now that; one drawing me toward
himself in one way, through the long-standing custom of men, or again
thrusting me away in another way through the truth recognized in the divine
Scriptures. And suffering this for a long time, and busying myself about the
cause which I named =31.656=, I came to the memory of the book of Judges,
which both relates that each did what was right in his own eyes, and also
makes clear the cause in saying that, In those days there was no king in
Israel. Having then remembered these things, I reasoned also concerning the
present things that which is perhaps fearful and paradoxical to say, but most
true to understand: that perhaps even now, by the setting aside of the one and
great true and only King and God of all, so great a disagreement and battle of
those in the Church comes about, each departing from the teaching of our Lord
Jesus Christ, and vindicating certain reasonings and private definitions from
his own authority, and wishing rather to rule contrary to the Lord than to be
ruled by the Lord. Having reasoned this, and being struck with terror at the
excess of the impiety, and searching still more, I was persuaded no less also
from the affairs in life that the aforementioned cause is true. For I saw that
every good order and agreement of a multitude is accomplished up to that
point, as long as the common obedience of the others toward one certain ruler
is preserved; but every disagreement and division, and further many-rule, is
paved from anarchy. And I once saw even a multitude of bees marshaled by a law
of nature, and following in good order their own king. And many such things I
saw, and many I heard; and those who have been occupied with these things know
still more, so that from these also what is said is shown to be true.

For if of those who look toward one nod, and use one king, good order with
agreement is proper; then every disagreement and division is a sign of
anarchy. According to the same reasoning, then, such disagreement both toward
the commandments of the Lord and toward one another as is found even among us
would be an accusation either of withdrawal of the true king, according to:
Only he who now restrains, until he be taken out of the way; or of denial of
him, according to: The fool said in his heart: There is no God. Upon which,
as a certain sign or proof, it brings: They were corrupted, and became
abominable in their practices. Here then the discourse showed the appearing
wickedness as a character of such impiety lurking in the soul in secret. But
the blessed apostle Paul, turning more vehemently into fear of the judgments
of God those who have lost the heart, defines this instead of punishment
=31.657=, that those who have neglected the true knowledge of God are
condemned. For what does he say? And as they did not approve to have God in
knowledge, God gave them over to a reprobate mind, to do the things that are
not fitting, being filled with all unrighteousness, wickedness, greed,
malice, full of envy, and the things that follow. And I think the Apostle
understood this judgment, not from himself (for he had Christ speaking in
himself), but having been led from that voice of his, in which he says that
he speaks in parables to the crowds for this reason, that they may not
understand the divine mysteries of the Gospel, because they themselves first
closed their eyes, and heard heavily with their ears, and their unintelligent
heart was made fat; that they may have to endure as punishment the blindness
concerning the greater things, who, having first willingly blinded the eye of
the soul, were darkened; which David, fearing to suffer, said: Enlighten my
eyes, lest I sleep unto death.

From these and such things then I reasoned it to be clear, that in general the
wickedness of the passions, through ignorance concerning God, implants a
reprobate knowledge; but particularly the disagreement of the many toward one
another comes upon us because we have made ourselves unworthy of the Lord's
oversight. But if ever I also planned to come to the inspection of such a
life, I was not able to measure the magnitude of such insensibility, or
unreason, or madness, or through the excess of the wickedness I do not have
what to say. For if even among irrational things we find agreement among one
another thus accomplished through obedience toward the leader; what would we
say, we who are found in so great a division toward one another and in so
great an opposition toward the commandments of the Lord? Or do we not consider
that all these things have now been set forth by the good God for our teaching
and shame, but on the great and fearful day of judgment will be brought
forward unto shame and condemnation of those who are not trained, by him who
both already said, and always says: The ox knew his owner, and the ass the
manger of his lord; but Israel did not know me, and the people did not
understand me, and many other such things? And that which was said by the
Apostle: If one member suffers, all the members suffer with it; if one member
is glorified, all the members rejoice with it; and: That there may not be
schisms in the body, but that the members may have the same care for one
another =31.660=, being moved clearly by the one indwelling soul. For what
reason was it so arranged? I think, that such a sequence and good order may be
preserved much more in the Church of God, to which it was said: But you are
the body of Christ, and members in part; the one and only truly head, which is
Christ, clearly ruling and joining each to the other toward concord. But among
those in whom concord is not accomplished, the bond of peace is not kept, the
meekness in spirit is not guarded, but rather division and strife and jealousy
are found.

It would be of much boldness to name such as members of Christ, or to say
that they are ruled by him; but of a simple mind, to say with frankness, that
there the mind of the flesh rules and reigns, according to the voice of the
Apostle, who says definitively, that To whom you present yourselves as slaves
unto obedience, you are slaves of the one whom you obey; and who goes through
clearly the properties of such a mind, as when he says: For where there is
jealousy and strife and divisions among you, are you not fleshly? and who
teaches at the same time by declaration both the hardness of their outcome
and their incompatibility with piety, through the things he says: The mind of
the flesh is enmity against God; for it is not subject to the law of God; for
it cannot even be; because No one is able, the Lord says, to serve two lords.
Then, when the only-begotten Son of God himself, our Lord and God Jesus
Christ, through whom all things came to be, cries, I have come down from
heaven, not that I may do my will, but the will of the Father who sent me;
and that I do nothing of myself; and, I received a commandment what I should
say, and what I should speak; and when the Holy Spirit, who distributes the
great and wonderful gifts, and works all things in all, and speaks nothing
from himself, but whatever he hears from the Lord, these things he speaks;
how is it not much more necessary that the whole Church of God, being eager
to keep the unity of the spirit in the bond of peace, fulfill that which was
said in the Acts, that The heart and the soul of the multitude of those who
believed was one; no one clearly setting up his own will, but all in common
seeking in the one Holy Spirit the will of the one Lord Jesus Christ, who
said: I have come down from heaven, not that I may do my will, but the will
of the Father who sent me; to whom he says: And I do not ask concerning these
only =31.661=, but also concerning those who believe through their word in
me, that they all may be one. Upon these things, and more that have been
passed over in silence, having been thus clearly and unanswerably fully
assured that the agreement of the whole Church of God together, according to
the will of Christ, in the Holy Spirit, is necessary, but that the
disobedience toward God in the division toward one another is dangerous and
destructive (For he who disobeys, he says, the Son shall not see life, but
the wrath of God remains upon him), I thought it consequent thereafter to
examine this: which things then are able to have forgiveness of sins with
God, and how many and how great sins one having committed becomes liable to
the judgment of disobedience.

I find then, taking up the divine Scriptures, in the Old and New Testament,
that the disobedience toward God is clearly judged neither in the multitude of
the things sinned, nor in the magnitude of the sins, but in the transgression
of any commandment whatsoever, and that the judgment is common against every
disobedience of God; in the Old reading that fearful end of Achar, or the
history concerning the one who gathered wood on the Sabbath, of whom each is
found never otherwise to have sinned against God, nor to have wronged a man,
neither a great thing nor a small thing at all. But the one, upon the sole and
first gathering of wood, gives the inescapable penalty, not even finding a
place of repentance; for by the commandment of God he is immediately stoned by
the whole people; and the other, because he only stole something of the
devoted things not yet brought into the synagogue, nor yet received by those
appointed over such things, became a cause of destruction not only to himself,
but also to wife and children, and further even to the tent itself with all
his own. And already the evil of the sin was about to spread also over the
whole people in the manner of fire, even though they neither knew what had
happened, nor had consented with the one who sinned, if the people, having
quickly been crushed from the fall of the men who were slain, had not
perceived the wrath of God; and Joshua the son of Nun fell

together with the elders, sprinkling dust, and thus the guilty one, having
been detected through the lot, gave the said penalty. But perhaps someone
will say that these are reasonably suspected also of other sins, from which
they were also caught in these; and that the holy Scripture mentioned only
these, as more grievous and worthy of death. But even if such a one should be
very bold to add something and to take away, will he not also accuse Mary the
sister of Moses of a multitude of sins, whose virtue I think escapes none of
the faithful? This woman, when she said against Moses =31.664= only something
in the part of censure, and this true (For he took, it says, an Ethiopian
woman for himself), experienced so great an indignation of God, that not even
when Moses himself was entreating was the penalty of her sin forgiven her.
When then I see Moses himself, the servant of God, that great one, who was
deemed worthy of so great and such honor from God, and so often witnessed to
by him, as to hear: I know you above all, and you have found grace before me;
when I see this man, at the water of contradiction, for no other reason than
only that he might say to the people murmuring through lack of water, Shall
we bring you water out of this rock? for this alone immediately receiving a
threat, that he would not enter into the land of the promise, which was then
the chief of the promises toward the Jews; when I see this man entreating,
and not being granted; when I see him deemed worthy of no forgiveness through
so many accomplishments for that brief word, I truly see the severity of God,
according to the Apostle, and I am truly persuaded that that is true: If the
righteous is scarcely saved, the ungodly and sinner, where will he appear?
And why do I say these things, When I hear that fearful sentence of God,
which he pronounces even against the one who has transgressed one commandment
in ignorance, I do not have how I may worthily fear the magnitude of the
wrath. For it is written: And a soul if it sin, and do one of all the
commandments of the Lord, which one ought not to do, and did not know, and
shall trespass, and take the sin, he shall bring a ram without blemish from
the sheep, of silver value, for a trespass to the priest. And the priest
shall make atonement for him, concerning his ignorance, which he did not
know, and he himself did not know, and it shall be forgiven him. For he
trespassed a trespass before the Lord.

But if upon those in ignorance the judgment is thus inescapable, and the
sacrifice for the purification is necessary, which even the righteous Job is
witnessed to offer for his sons, what would one say concerning those who sin
in knowledge, or those who are silent toward these? And that we may not seem
to reckon the indignation against these by likely conjectures only, it is
necessary again to remember the God-inspired Scripture itself, which suffices
for the present even through one history to present the judgment of such. And
the sons, it says, of Eli the priest, were pestilent sons. And because the
father did not proceed more vehemently against these being such, he stirred
such wrath in the long-suffering of God, that, when the foreigners rose up,
his sons were slain by them in the war in one day, and the whole people was
defeated =31.665=, and a sufficient number of these fell, and there also came
to pass concerning the ark of the holy covenant of God things which were never
heard before, so that that which it was not lawful either for Israelites, or
even for all the priests themselves, nor at any time to touch, nor did a
chance place receive it, this was carried from one place to another by impious
hands, and instead of the holy things was deposited in temples of idols. Upon
which how great a laughter and mockery happened to be, even against the name
of God itself, among the foreigners, it is possible to conjecture. Upon these
things Eli himself also is recorded as having used a most pitiable end; having
received a threat that his seed also

would be removed from the priestly dignity; which also came to pass. So many
things happened to the people. Such things the father suffered through the
transgression of the sons, who though for his own life he was never accused
of anything; and he did not endure to be silent even toward those themselves,
but he exhorted them much concerning no longer remaining in like things;
saying, Do not, children, do not; the reports which I hear concerning you are
not good; and constructing more vehemently the magnitude of the sin, he set
before them the danger more fearfully; For if a man sinning sins, he says,
against a man, they will pray concerning him to the Lord. But if he sin
against God, who will pray concerning him? Nevertheless, since, as I said, he
did not show the fitting zeal against them, the things said came to pass.
Such then I find as many as possible the judgments according to every
disobedience in the Old Testament; but again when I come to the New, our Lord
Jesus Christ not releasing from punishment even the things according to
ignorance, but stretching the threat more vehemently against those in
knowledge, through the things he says: And that servant, who knew the will of
his lord, and did not prepare himself, nor do according to his will, shall be
beaten with many; but he who did not know, and did things worthy of blows,
shall be beaten with few; when I find such sentences of the only-begotten Son
of God himself, and the indignation of the holy apostles against those who
sin; such and so great sufferings of those who have sinned even any one thing
whatsoever, not less than the things said from the Old, but rather even more,
I learn thoroughly the vehemence of the judgment.

Since also To whom they committed much, they will ask of him the more. See
then also blessed Paul, showing together both the dignity of the calling and
the =31.668= indignation against every sin, of what sort he says: For the
weapons of our warfare are not fleshly, but powerful to God toward the pulling
down of strongholds, pulling down reasonings, and every height that is raised
against the knowledge of God, and taking captive every thought into the
obedience of Christ; and not only this, but also Having in readiness to avenge
every disobedience. Where also, having examined each of the things said more
carefully, it is possible to know the will of the divine Scripture more
accurately, that it did not leave us to wander with certain deceived opinions,
each soul toward a slip of sins, thinking that some of the sins are avenged,
but some are left unavenged. But what does he say? Pulling down reasonings,
and every height that is raised against the knowledge of God, so that every
sin, through contempt of the divine commandment, is called a height raised
against the knowledge of God, which is also shown more clearly in the book of
Numbers. For when God, having enumerated the involuntary sins, and having
arranged the sacrifices upon them, was already about to legislate also
concerning the voluntary the things fitting for the people, he begins thus:
And a soul which shall do with a hand of pride (calling a hand of pride the
daring of those who sin willingly, which the Apostle names a height raised
against the knowledge of God), a soul then, he says, which shall do with a
hand of pride, whether of the natives, or of the sojourners, this one will
provoke God, and that soul shall be utterly destroyed from the midst of its
people. Because it despised the word of the Lord, and scattered his
commandments; that soul shall be utterly wiped out; its sin is in it. One must
observe here that, if that soul is not utterly wiped out, its sin is not in it
alone, but also upon those who did not show the good zeal, as has been written
in many places, and has often come to pass. And that from the lesser things we
may be trained in the fear upon the greater, let us learn thoroughly of what
sort is the indignation he makes in Deuteronomy against those who disobey a
priest or a judge. He says then: And a man, who if he do in

pride so as not to obey the priest who stands by to minister in the name of
the Lord your God, or the judge, whoever he may be in those days, that man
also shall die, and you shall take away the evil from Israel. And all the
people having heard shall fear, and they shall not be ungodly =31.669= any
longer. Toward which things, how one being worthily moved would be struck
more dreadfully, it is consequent to consider. Then he says: And taking
captive every thought into the obedience of Christ; Every thought, not this
or that.

And having in readiness to avenge; and here again, not this or some, but Every
disobedience. Has then the worst custom deceived us; has the distorted
tradition of men become a cause of great evils for us, declining some of the
sins, as it were, and choosing others indifferently; and pretending to be
vehemently indignant against some, such as murder, and adultery, and such
things; but judging others not even worthy of a bare rebuke, such as anger, or
reviling, or drunkenness, or greed, and as many such things, against all of
which even elsewhere Paul who speaks in Christ gave the same sentence, saying
that: Those who practice such things are worthy of death. But where every
height raised against the knowledge of God is pulled down, and every thought
is taken captive into the obedience of Christ, and every disobedience is
equally avenged, there nothing is left undestroyed, nothing unavenged, nothing
remaining outside the obedience of Christ. For the apostle Paul showed a
common and greatest impiety against every disobedience, saying that: You who
boast in the law, through the transgression of the law you dishonor God. But
are these words only, and not deeds? See then: in Corinth the one who had his
father's wife, accused of nothing else except this alone, is not only himself
handed over to Satan for destruction of the flesh, until he correct the
trespass with fruits worthy of repentance, but he also clothes the whole
Church together, since it did not proceed against the sin, with those charges:
What do you wish? Shall I come to you with a rod? and after a little: And you
are puffed up, and did not rather mourn, that the one who did this work might
be taken out from your midst. And what then Ananias, the one in the Acts?

What other evil is he found to have done except that very thing? Where then
does it appear worthy of so great wrath? Having sold his own possession, he
brought the money, and laid it at the feet of the apostles; having kept back
from the price, upon this at that very hour together with his wife he is
condemned to death, not even having been deemed worthy to learn the words
concerning repentance upon the sin, not finding even a time to be pricked at
this, not receiving a term to repent. And the servant of such and so great a
judgment, the minister of so great =31.672= wrath of God upon the one who
sinned, blessed Peter, who was preferred above all the disciples, and who
alone was witnessed to and blessed more than the others, who was entrusted
with the keys of the kingdom of the heavens, when he hears from the Lord, If I
do not wash you, you have no part with me, what heart even somehow stony will
he not put to shame toward fear and trembling of the judgments of God? and
these things though he gave no impression of any sin, nor of any contempt, but
rather used an exceeding honor concerning the Master, and showed the reverence
fitting to a slave and disciple. For seeing his own and all men's God and
Lord, and King, and Master, and Teacher, and Savior, and all things together,
girded in the form of a servant with a towel, and wishing to wash his feet,
immediately, as coming to a sense of his own unworthiness, and being struck at
the dignity of the one approaching,

he cried out Lord, do you wash my feet? and again: You shall never wash my
feet unto the age.

Upon this he receives so great a threat, that if he had not again, knowing the
truth of the words of the Lord, anticipated by correcting the contradiction
with obedience, nothing of the things that went before, neither of his own
accomplishments, nor of the blessings and gifts and promises from the Lord,
nor even the revelation itself of the good pleasure of God and Father of such
and so great a kind toward the only-begotten Son, would have sufficed to
console the present disobedience. But if I wish to recount these things, as
many as I find from both Old and New Testament, time would perhaps fail me as
I narrate. And already also when I come upon the very voices of our Lord Jesus
Christ in the Gospel, the words of him who is about to judge the living and
the dead, which among the faithful are more trustworthy than every history,
and every other proof, I learn thoroughly in them a great, that I may so say,
necessity of obedience toward God in all things; and no forgiveness at all,
upon any commandment, left to those who do not repent of the disobedience,
unless it is something else to dare, even to take into thought, against so
naked, clear, and absolute sentences; For heaven, he says, and earth will pass
away, but my =31.673= words will not pass away. There is no difference here,
there is no division, nothing at all has been left anywhere. He did not say:
These or those, but, My words, all together clearly, will not pass away. For
it is written: The Lord is faithful in all his words; whether forbidding
anything, or commanding, or promising, or threatening, both upon the doing of
the things forbidden, and upon the leaving undone of the things commanded. For
that equally with the working of evils the leaving undone of good works is
also avenged, even the aforementioned judgment upon Peter would suffice toward
proof and full assurance for a soul not sick with complete unbelief; who,
having done nothing forbidden, nor indeed having left undone some such
commandment, which accuses the one who leaves it of sloth or contempt, but
having only been cautious to receive a service and honor from the Master,
received such a threat which he would not have escaped, if he had not, as was
said before, anticipated the wrath by the speed and vehemence of the
correction.

But since the good and compassionate God, being long-suffering toward us, was
well pleased also often to show us the same thing and through many things,
that by the multitude and the continuity the soul, having been with difficulty
at some time pried and washed, may be able to rub off the long-standing custom
of transgression, it is necessary for the present to remember only those
standing on the left of our Lord Jesus Christ on the great and fearful day of
judgment; to whom he said who received all the authority of the judgment from
the Father, who comes to bring to light the hidden things of the darkness, and
to make manifest the counsels of the hearts: Depart from me, you cursed, into
the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels. And he
added the cause, not saying: Because you murdered, or fornicated, or lied, or
wronged someone, or did some other of the things forbidden, even the smallest;
but what? Because you neglected the good works. For I hungered, he says, and
you did not give me to eat; I thirsted, and you did not give me to drink; I
was a stranger, and you did not gather me; naked, and you did not clothe me;
sick and in prison, and you did not visit me. These and such things by the
grace of the good God, who wishes all men to be saved, and to come to
knowledge of truth, and who teaches man knowledge, having learned thoroughly
in the God-inspired Scriptures, and having recognized the most dreadful cause
of such disagreement of the many both toward one another and toward the
commandments of our Lord Jesus Christ, and having been trained in that fearful

judgment of so great a transgression, and having been taught also that every
disobedience of every judgment =31.676= of God is equally avenged; and
further having learned thoroughly that fearful judgment, that upon those who
did not sin, but nevertheless partook of the wrath, because they did not show
the good zeal against those who had sinned, although often they had not even
consented to the sin; I reasoned it necessary, even if late in time, because
of always waiting for those who have taken up the same contest of piety, and
not being confident in myself alone, but perhaps not untimely, now at least
toward a reminder for those who contend the contest of piety, having
collected from the God-inspired Scriptures both the things that displease
God, and the things with which he is well pleased, to set them beside, as I
am able, according to common prayers; that we may be deemed worthy by the
grace of our Lord Jesus Christ and the teaching of the Holy Spirit, having
leapt away both from the custom of our own wills, and from the observation of
human traditions; and having aligned ourselves with the Gospel of the blessed
God Jesus Christ, our Lord, and having lived well-pleasingly to him in the
present time both through a more vehement withdrawal from the things
forbidden and a more eager care of the things approved, in the age to come of
immortality we may be able to flee the wrath that comes upon the sons of
disobedience, and be found worthy of the eternal life, and of the heavenly
kingdom promised by the Lord Jesus Christ to all Those who keep his covenant,
and remember his commandments, to do them. And remembering the Apostle who
said: In Christ Jesus neither circumcision avails anything, nor
uncircumcision, but faith working through love; I reasoned it consequent
together and necessary, to set beside first the healthy faith and pious
opinion concerning Father and Son and Holy Spirit, and thus to join on the
ethical things.
