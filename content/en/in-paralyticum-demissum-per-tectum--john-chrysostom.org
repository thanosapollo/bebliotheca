* On the Paralytic Let Down Through the Roof
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

HOMILY On the paralytic let down through the roof, that he is not the one
lying in John; and on the equality of the Son toward the Father.

=51.47=

1. Having lately come upon the paralytic lying on the bed by the pool, we
found a great and large treasure, not by digging through earth, but =51.48=.40
by digging through his mind: we found a treasure having not silver and gold
and precious stones, but endurance and philosophy and patience =51.49= and
much hope toward God, which is more precious than all gold and all abundance.
For the sensible wealth is laid open to plots of robbers, and to mouths of
slanderers, and to hands of housebreakers, and to knaveries of servants, and
when it has escaped all these, then it often brings the greatest destruction
upon those who possess it, stirring up envious eyes, and from this begetting
countless storms. But the spiritual wealth has escaped all these grips, and is
above all such despite, mocking robbers and housebreakers and the envious and
slanderers and death itself. For it is not parted from the possessor even by
death, but then especially the possession of it becomes more secure for those
who have it, and it journeys with them, and is transferred with them to the
life to come, and becomes a wonderful advocate for those with whom it goes
away there, and makes the Judge gracious to them. This wealth we too have
found with much abundance buried in the soul of the paralytic. And you are
witnesses, who drew it out with much eagerness, yet did not empty it. For such
is the nature of spiritual wealth: it imitates the inflows of waters, or
rather it even surpasses their abundance, increasing more then, when it has
many who draw from it. For entering into the soul of each, it is not divided,
nor diminished, but arriving entire to each it remains inexhaustible
continually, never able to fail; which is just what happened then. For though
so many fell upon the treasure, and all were drawing from there according to
their power; and why do I speak of you, when from that time until the present
day, having made countless people rich, it remains in its own perfection? Let
us not then grow weary toward this spiritual abundance; but as much as
possible let us now also draw it out, and let us see a Master who loves
mankind, let us see an enduring servant. For thirty-eight years wrestling with
an incurable illness and being scourged continually, he did not grow
impatient, he did not let fall a blasphemous word, he did not accuse the One
who made him, but nobly and with much gentleness he bore that misfortune. And
how is this clear? someone says; for Scripture has taught us nothing clearly
about his earlier life, but that he had thirty-eight years in his infirmity it
made clear; that he did not grow impatient, nor was angry, nor despaired, this
it did not add. And yet this too it made clear, if one attends carefully, and
not incidentally, nor simply. For when you hear that when Christ stood over
him and was not known, but was thought a mere man, he conversed with him with
such gentleness, you will be able to see his former philosophy as well. For
when He said to him, Do you want to become healthy? he said nothing of the
sort one might expect, that You see me lying paralyzed so long a time, and you
ask if I want to become healthy? Have you come to trample on my evils, to
reproach, and to mock, and to make a comedy of the misfortune? Nothing of this
sort


did he even say, nor even =51.50= thought, but with gentleness: Yes, Lord, he
says. And if after thirty-eight years he was so meek, so gentle, when all his
vigor had been thrown down and the strength of his reasonings, consider what
he is likely to have been in the beginnings of the terrors. For you all surely
know that the sick are not equally hard to please at the beginning of the
illness and when much time has gone on; for then especially the sick become
difficult, when the disease has advanced to length, then they are unbearable
to all. But he who after so many years philosophizes thus, answering so
patiently, it is quite clear that also in the former time he bore that
misfortune with much thanksgiving. Reckoning these things then, let us too
imitate the patience of our fellow-servant; for that paralysis is sufficient
to brace our souls; for no one is so sluggish and slack as, having considered
the magnitude of that misfortune, not to bear nobly all the terrors that fall
upon him, even if they be more unbearable than all. For not his health alone,
but also his disease has become a cause of the greatest benefit to us; for the
healing raised the souls of the hearers to the glorifying of the Master; and
the disease and the infirmity anointed you toward patience, and called you to
equal zeal; or rather it also displayed the very love of God toward mankind.
For even the delivering him to such a disease, and stretching the infirmity
over so long a time, is of the greatest care. For just as a goldsmith, having
thrown gold into a furnace, allows it to be tested by the fire until he sees
it become purer; so also God allows the souls of men to be examined in terrors
until they become pure and translucent, and have reaped much benefit from this
testing; so that this too is a greatest kind of benefaction.

2. Let us not then be thrown into tumult, nor be restless, when temptations
fall upon us. For if the goldsmith saw how much time the gold must be left in
the furnace, and when to draw it up, and does not allow it to remain in the
fire until it is destroyed and burned up; much more does God know this, and
when He sees them become purer, He releases them from the temptations, so that
they may not be tripped and fall by the excess of evils. Let us not then grow
impatient, nor be faint-hearted, whenever something unexpected falls; but let
us yield to Him who knows these things with accuracy, as long as He wishes, to
fire our mind; for He does this profitably and for the gain of those who are
tried. Therefore a wise man exhorts saying: My child, if you come to serve
God, prepare your soul for temptation, make straight your heart, and endure,
and do not hurry in a time of calamity. Yield all things to Him, he says; for
He knows accurately when we must be taken up from the furnace of evils. We
must then yield to Him everywhere, and give thanks through all, and bear all
things with a good mind, whether He benefits, or whether He chastises, =51.51=
since this too is a kind of benefaction. For a physician is a physician not
only when he bathes, and feeds, and leads the sick man out into gardens, but
also when he burns and cuts, equally he is a physician; and a father is a
father not only when he treats his son kindly, but also when he casts him out
of the house, and when he rebukes, and scourges, equally he is a father, and
not less than when he praises. Knowing then that God is more affectionate than
all physicians, do not be overcurious, nor demand of Him the accounts of the
healing, but whether He wishes to let go, or whether He chastises, let us
offer ourselves equally to both; for through both He leads us back to health,
and to His own intimacy, and He knows of what each of us has need, and what is
profitable for each, and how and in what way we must be saved, and He leads us
this road. Let us then follow wherever He commands, and let us not be
over-precise, whether He commands us to walk by an easy and smooth, or by a
hard and rougher road; as also with this paralytic. One then


kind of benefaction was this, to purify his soul over so long a time, having
delivered it, as to some furnace, to the firing of temptations; and another
not less than this, even in the temptations themselves to be present, and to
give him much consolation. He was the one holding him together and containing
him, and stretching out a hand, and not letting him fall. And when you hear
that it was He, do not deprive the paralytic of the praises, neither him, nor
any other man who is tried and endures. For even if we philosophize ten
thousand times, even if we are stronger and more powerful than all, if the
impulse from Him is absent, we will not be able to stand even against a chance
temptation. And why do I speak of us, the cheap and discarded? For even if it
be a Paul, and a Peter, even a James, even a John, deprived of the help from
above he is easily convicted, and tripped, and falls. And for these things I
will read you the voice of Christ himself; for He says to Peter: Behold, Satan
has asked to sift you as wheat; and I have prayed concerning you, that your
faith may not fail. What is it, to sift? To lead, to lead around, to shake, to
move, to toss, to test, which happens with those being sieved; but I, He says,
held back, knowing that you are not able to bear the temptation; for to say,
That your faith may not fail, is of one showing that if He had permitted, his
faith would have failed. And if Peter the fervent lover of Christ, who had
given his soul for Him ten thousand times, and always leaping out before the
choir of the apostles, and being blessed by the Teacher, and for this called
Peter, because he had an unshaken and unoverturned faith, would have been
carried away and fallen from the confession, if Christ had permitted the devil
to tempt as much as he wished, who else will be able to stand without His
help? Therefore Paul also says: But God is faithful, who will not allow you to
be tempted beyond what you are able, but will make with the temptation also
the way out, so that you may be able to bear it. For not only, he says, does
He not allow a temptation beyond power to be brought on, but also in the very
one brought on according to power He is present supporting =51.52= us and
holding us together, when we first contribute what is from ourselves, such as
eagerness, hope toward Him, thanksgiving, endurance, patience. For not only in
the dangers beyond power, but also in those according to power we need the
help from above, if we are going to stand nobly; for elsewhere too he says: As
the sufferings of Christ abound toward us, so through Christ our consolation
also abounds, so that we may be able to console those in every affliction
through the consolation with which we ourselves are consoled by God. So that
the one who consoled this man is Himself the one who permitted the temptation
to be brought on. See also after the healing how much care He displays. For He
did not leave him and go away, but finding him in the temple He says: See, you
have become healthy; sin no longer, lest something worse happen to you. For if
He had permitted the punishment in hatred, He would not have released him, He
would not have secured him toward the future; but to say, Lest something worse
happen to you, is of one checking in advance the coming terrors. He loosed the
disease, and He did not loose the agony; He drove out the infirmity, and He
did not drive out the fear, so that the benefaction that had come might be
unmoved. This belongs to a caring physician, not only to loose the present
things, but also to secure toward the future, which Christ also did, holding
that man's soul together by the memory of the past. For since when the things
that pain us have gone, often the memory also goes with them, wishing it to
remain continual, He says: Sin no longer, lest something worse happen to you.

3. And not from this alone is it possible to see His providence and His
gentleness, but also from the very thing that seems to be a rebuke. For He did
not parade his sins, but that through sins he suffered what he


suffered, He said; but what the sins were He did not make clear, nor did He
say, You sinned this and that, nor, You transgressed this and that, but having
indicated this with one bare word, Sin no longer, and having said so much as
only to remind, He made him more earnest toward the future, and He made his
patience and courage and philosophy all clear to us by putting him under
necessity to recount the whole misfortune as a tragedy, and displaying His own
eagerness; For while I am coming, he says, another goes down before me; but
the sins He did not parade. For just as we wish our own things to be shadowed
over, so also God much more than we; therefore He performed the healing before
all, but the exhortation or the counsel He makes privately. For He never
parades our sins, unless perhaps He sees us insensible toward them. For when
He says: You saw me hungry, and you did not feed; and thirsty, and you did not
give drink, He says this at the present time, so that we may not hear these
words at the future. He threatens, He parades here, so that He may not parade
there; just as He also threatened to overthrow the city of the Ninevites for
this reason, so that He might not overthrow it. For if He wished to publish
our sins, He would not have =51.53= said beforehand that He will publish; but
now He foretells for this reason, so that by the fear of that publishing, even
if not by the fear of the punishment having been chastened, we may wash all
away. This also happens at baptism; for He leads the man to the pool of the
waters, having made his sins clear to no one, but the gift He makes apparent
to all, and makes it clear, while of the sins no one else knows except Himself
and the one receiving the remission. This also happened in this case, and He
makes the reproof without witness, or rather what was said is not only a
reproof, but also a defense, as if apologizing for so great a maltreatment and
saying to him, and showing, that not in vain, nor idly did He allow him to be
maltreated so long a time, He reminded him of the sins, and stated the cause
of the infirmity. For finding him, it says, in the temple, He said to him: Sin
no longer, lest something worse happen to you. Since then we have reaped so
much from the former paralytic, come let us come also upon another, even if he
lies in Matthew. For also in the mines wherever one finds gold, there he digs
through more again; and I know that many of those who read simply think that
one and the same paralytic is lying in the four evangelists; but it is not so.
Therefore one must rise up and attend with accuracy. For the inquiry is not
about chance matters, but this discourse, having received the fitting
solution, will be useful both toward Greeks, and toward Jews, and toward many
of the heretics. For thus all accuse the evangelists as fighting and
disagreeing; it is not so, may it not be, but even if the persons are
different, one is the grace of the Spirit, which moves the soul of each; and
where there is the grace of the Spirit, love, joy, peace; there is no war and
dispute, and fighting and some contention. How then shall we make it apparent
that this paralytic is not that one, but another besides that one? From many
signs, both place, and time, and seasons, and day, and from the manner of the
healing, and from the presence of the physician, and from the isolation of the
one healed. And what of this? someone says; for have not many of the
evangelists reported other signs not differently? But it is one thing to speak
differently, and another contrary-wise; for the former does not make any
disagreement, nor a fight; but this now set before us has much opposition, if
the paralytic at the pool is not shown to be another besides that one recorded
in the three. And so that you may learn what it is to speak differently, and
what it is to speak contrary-wise, of the evangelists one said that Christ was
bearing the cross, and another that Simon of Cyrene; this


does not make any opposition, nor a fight. And how is it not contrary, someone
says, to bear and not to bear? Because both happened. And when they had gone
out from the praetorium, Christ was bearing; and as they went on, Simon took
it from Him and carried it. Again in the case of the robbers, one says that
the two blasphemed Him =51.54=; and the other that the one stopped the mouth
of the one accusing. But this again is not contrary either. Why? Because here
too both happened, and at the beginning both were acting wickedly; but after
these things, when signs had occurred and the earth was shaken, and the rocks
were split, and the sun was hidden, one of them was changed, and became more
sober, and recognized the crucified, and confessed His kingdom. For lest you
think that this happens by some necessity and force of someone pushing him
from within, and lest you be at a loss, He shows you even on the cross still
keeping his former wickedness, so that you may know that having changed from
within and from himself, and having enjoyed the grace of God, he thus became
better.

4. And many other such things can be gathered from the Gospels, which seem to
have a suspicion of opposition, but it is not opposition, but both what was
said by this one and by that one has happened; even if not at the same hour,
and the one said the former, and the other the second; but here there is
nothing of this sort; but the multitude of the signs mentioned shows even to
those attending in any way that this one was another, and that one another.
And this too would be no small proof toward showing the evangelists agreeing
with one another, and not fighting. For if it is the same man, the fight is
great; but if that one is another, all dispute is loosed. Come then let us
state the very reasons why we say this one is not that one. What then are
these? The one is healed in Jerusalem, this one in Capernaum, and the one by
the pool of the waters, this one in some little house; behold from the place;
and that one at a feast; behold from the season; and that one had thirty-eight
years in the infirmity; but concerning this one the evangelist says nothing of
this sort; behold also from the time; and that one was healed on a sabbath;
behold also from the day; for if this one too was healed on a sabbath, Matthew
would not have passed this over in silence, nor would the Jews present have
kept quiet; for those who even when he was not healed on a sabbath were
impatient for another reason, much more if they also had the grip from the
season, would not have spared the charges against Christ. And this one is
brought to Christ; but to that one Christ Himself arrives, and for that one
there was no man to help; For Lord, he says, I have no man; but this one had
many of his own, who even let him down through the roof. And of that one He
corrected the body before the soul; for having first braced his paralysis,
then He says: See, you have become healthy; sin no longer; but here not so,
but having first healed his soul (for He says to him: Take courage, child,
your sins are forgiven you), then He corrected the paralysis. That then this
one is not that one has been clearly demonstrated to us from these things, and
we must next, having come to the beginning of the narrative, see how Christ
healed this one, and how that one, and why differently; why the one on a
sabbath, and that one not on a sabbath, and to that one He Himself went, but
this one =51.55= He waited to be brought to Him, and why of that one He heals
the body first, but of this one the soul first. For He does not do these
things simply nor idly, being wise and provident. Let us attend then, and let
us see Him healing. For if in the case of physicians, whenever they cut, or
burn, or also in another way operate on one maimed and weakened, and cut a
member, many stand round both the sick man and the physician doing these
things, much more must we do this here, by as much as the physician also is
greater, and the disease more severe, accomplished not by human art, but by
divine


grace. And there it is possible to see even skin being cut, and ichor flowing,
and rottenness being stirred, and to endure much disgust arising from the
spectacle, and much pain and grief, not from the sight of the wounds only, but
also from the anguish of those being burned and cut; for no one is so stony
as, standing by those suffering these things, and hearing them wailing, not to
be broken, and confused, and receive much despondency in the soul; but
nevertheless because of the desire of the spectacle we endure all these
things. But here nothing of this sort is to be seen, no fire being applied, no
iron being dipped, no blood flowing, not the sick man in pain, not wailing;
and the cause is the wisdom of the one healing, needing none of these external
things, but being sufficient to itself. For it is enough only to command, and
all the terrors are loosed. And this is not what is wondered at, that He
performs the healing with such ease, but that also without pain, bringing no
toil upon those being healed. Since then the wonder is also greater, and the
healing more, and the pleasure of the spectators pure of all despondency, come
let us watch with accuracy Christ healing. And having embarked into a boat He
crossed over, and came into His own city, and behold they brought to Him a
paralytic thrown on a bed, and Jesus seeing their faith said to the paralytic:
Take courage, child, your sins are forgiven you. They are less than the
centurion according to faith, but greater than the one at the pool. For that
one neither dragged the physician, nor brought the sick man to the physician;
but he approached as to God, and says: Say only a word, and my servant will be
healed. But these did not drag the physician into the house, and in this they
are equal to the centurion; but they brought the sick man to the physician,
and in this they are less, because they did not say, Say only a word. Yet they
are much better than the one lying. For that one says: Lord, I have no man, so
that when the water is troubled, he may throw me into the pool; but these knew
that Christ needs nothing, not waters, not a pool, not anything else of this
sort; but nevertheless Christ released not only the centurion's, but also
this one and that one, from the diseases, and He did not say: Since
you brought lesser faith, you will receive the healing likewise; but the one
who displayed the greater He sent away with praises and crowns saying: Not
even in Israel have I found so great faith. But the one who brought less than
that one He praised in nothing, yet He did not deprive him of health, nor even
that one who displayed no faith at all. But just as physicians treating the
same disease received =51.56= from some a hundred gold pieces, from some
halves, from some less, from some nothing at all; so also Christ received from
the centurion much and unspeakable faith, from this one less, from that one
not even the chance amount, and nevertheless He healed all. For what reason
then did He also deem the one who contributed nothing worthy of the
benefaction? Because not from sloth, nor from insensibility of soul, but from
not knowing Christ and never having heard any wonder concerning Him, either
small or great, he did not display the faith. Therefore he also enjoyed
pardon; which also the evangelist hinting said: For he did not even know who
He ever was, but recognized Him from sight alone, when he met Him a second
time.

5. Some then say that when those who brought him believed only, this man was
healed; but this is not so. For seeing their faith, not of those who brought
only, but also of the one brought. What then? if another has believed, is
another not healed? someone says. I do not think so, unless perhaps either
through untimely age, or through exceeding weakness he is unable to believe.
How then in the case of the Canaanite woman, someone says, the mother
believed, but the daughter was healed? and when the centurion disbelieved, how
did the child rise and was saved? Because the sick were not


able to believe. Hear then what the Canaanite woman says: My daughter is badly
demon-possessed, and sometimes she falls into the water, sometimes into the
fire; and she who is darkened and demon-possessed, and never able to be in
herself, nor healthy, how would she have been able to believe? As then in the
case of the Canaanite woman, so also in the case of the centurion; the child
was thrown in the house, himself not knowing Christ, nor who He ever was, how
then was he going to believe in the unknown, of whom he had not yet taken any
experience? But in this case one cannot say this; but the paralytic believed.
How is this clear? From the very manner of the bringing. For do not hear
simply that they let him down through the roof; but consider how great it is
for one who is ill to endure to suffer this. For you surely know this, that
the sick are so faint-hearted and hard to please as often even to refuse
treatments on the bed, and to choose to bear the pains from the diseases
rather than to endure the burden from the helps. But this man endured both to
go forth from the house, and to be thrown into the marketplace being carried,
and to display himself with so many present. And this too is a custom of the
sick, to die within rather than to uncover their own misfortunes. But this
sick man was not so, but seeing the theater filled, the entrances walled off,
the harbor shut, he endured to be let down through the roof. So inventive is
longing, and resourceful is love. For the one who seeks finds, and to the one
who knocks it will be opened. He did not say to those belonging to him: What
=51.57= is this? why are you in tumult? and why do you hurry? Let us wait for
the room to be emptied, for the theater to be dissolved; those gathered will
withdraw, we will be able to approach Him privately, and to confer about these
things. Why must my misfortunes be set forth in the midst with all seeing, and
be let down from above, and be unseemly? None of these things did that man say
either to himself or to those carrying him, but he thought it an ornament to
have made so many witnesses of his own healing. And not from this alone was it
possible to see his faith, but also from the very words of Christ. For when he
was let down and brought, Christ says to him: Take courage, child, your sins
are forgiven you. And having heard these things he was not angry, he did not
grow impatient, he did not say to the physician: What is this? I came to be
healed of another passion, and you yourself heal another? These are a pretext
and an excuse, and coverings of weakness. Do you forgive sins that are not
seen? None of these things did he say, he did not think; but he waited,
permitting the physician to use the way of healing which He wished. Therefore
Christ also did not go away to him, but waited for him to come, so that He
might display his faith to all. For was He not able to make the entrance easy?
But He did none of these things, so that He might display to all his eagerness
and fervent faith. For just as He went to that one who had thirty-eight years
because no one was present with him; so this one, since he had many of his
own, He waited to come to Him, so that He might both make this man's faith
clear through being brought, and teach us that man's isolation through going
away to him, and uncover to all this man's eagerness and that man's patience,
and especially to those then present. For since Jews, certain envious and
hating mankind, are accustomed to envy the benefactions of neighbors, and now
to attack the wonders from the season saying that He heals on a sabbath, and
now from the life of those benefited saying: If this man were a prophet, He
would have known who the woman was who was touching Him; not knowing that this
especially belongs to a physician, to mix with the sick, and always to appear
beside those who are ill, and not to flee them nor to leap away. Which also
addressing those He said: Those who are healthy have no need of a physician,
but those who are in a bad way. So that they may not again make the same
charges, He first shows that those who


approach are worthy of the healing through the faith which they display.
Therefore He showed both that man's isolation, and this man's fervent faith
and eagerness; therefore He healed that one on a sabbath, but this one not on
a sabbath; so that when you see them even on another day charging and rebuking
Christ, you may learn that then too they were not charging because of the
observation of the law, but because they could not bear their own envy. And
why did He not come first to setting the paralysis upright, but says: Take
courage, child, your sins are forgiven you? This too very wisely. For it is
also a custom of physicians not first to loose the diseases, but to destroy
their sources. As often when the eyes are troubled by a wicked humor and a
corrupted flux, the physician, leaving off to heal the sick pupil, took care
of the head, where the root and the source of the infirmity was; so also
Christ did, He first holds =51.58= back the source of the evils. For the
source of evils and root and mother of all is the nature of sin. This
paralyzes our bodies; this brings on the diseases; therefore here too He says:
Take courage, child, your sins are forgiven you; and there He says: Behold you
have become healthy; sin no longer, lest something worse happen to you,
showing in both that from sins these diseases were born. And in the beginning
and in the preludes of creation the disease from sin struck down upon the body
of Cain. For that man after the fratricide, after that lawlessness, then had
his body paralyzed. For to tremble is nothing other than paralysis. For when
the power that manages the living being becomes weaker, no longer able to
support all the members, it leaves them of its own providence, then those
having been slackened tremble and are carried about.

6. This Paul also made clear; for charging the Corinthians with a certain sin
he says: For this reason many among you are weak and sick; therefore Christ
also first destroys the cause of the evils, and having said, Take courage,
child, your sins are forgiven you, He raises his mind, He wakes the soul that
has been thrown down; for the word became a deed, and having entered into the
conscience it touched the soul itself, and threw out all agony. For nothing so
makes pleasure, and provides courage, as accusing nothing of oneself. Take
courage, child, your sins are forgiven you. For where there is remission of
sins, there is adoption. Thus then we too cannot first call Father, until in
the pool of the holy waters we wash away the sins. When then we come up from
there having put off that wicked burden, then we say, Our Father, who is in
the heavens. But for what reason in the case of the one having thirty-eight He
did not do so, but corrected his body first? Because for that one by the
length of the time the sins had been spent; for the magnitude of temptation
can make the burden of the sins light; just as also in the case of Lazarus He
says that he received his evils, and here he is consoled; and again elsewhere
He says: Console my people, speak to the heart of Jerusalem, that she has
received from the hand of the Lord double her sins. And again the prophet,
Lord, give us peace, for you have rendered all things to us, showing that the
punishments and the chastisements make a remission of sins, and this can be
made apparent from many places. It seems to me then that He discussed nothing
with that one about remission, but secured him toward the future, as of the
things already transgressed by the length of the infirmity; or if not this,
because he had not yet been persuaded of anything great concerning Christ,
therefore He came first to the lesser, and the apparent and clear, the health
of the body; but in this case not so, but since he believed more, and had a
higher soul, therefore He discussed with him about the more severe =51.59=
first disease; and besides all these things, so that He might display the
equal honor toward the Father. For just as there He healed on a sabbath,
wishing to lead them away from the Jewish observation, and from the


same charges to take a starting-point of showing Himself equal to the One who
begot Him; so also here, having foreseen what they were going to say, He said
these words, so that taking a beginning and a pretext from there He might show
Himself being of equal honor with the One who begot Him. For it is not equal,
when no one is charging, nor accusing, to let down from oneself into the
discourse about these things, and when others furnish the causes, to construct
this very thing in the rank and form of a defense. For the manner of that
demonstration struck against the hearers; but this was less burdensome, and
more acceptable, and everywhere we see Him doing this, and displaying the
equality not so much through words as through deeds. This then also the
evangelist hinting said, that the Jews were persecuting Him, not only because
He was loosing the sabbath, but also because He was saying God was His own
Father, making Himself equal to God, which is much greater; for through the
demonstration of the deeds He was constructing this very thing. What then did
the envious and wicked, wasting away at others' goods, and seeking from
everywhere to find grips? Why does this man, he says, blaspheme? For no one
can forgive sins except God alone. Just as there they were persecuting Him
because He was loosing the sabbath, and taking a starting-point from their own
charges He made clear the equality toward the One who begot Him in the rank of
a defense saying: My Father is working, and I am working; so also here, from
the things they charge, from these He shows the indistinguishability toward
the Father. For what does he say? No one can forgive sins, except God alone.
Since then they themselves laid down this definition, they themselves brought
in the canon, they themselves wrote the law, from their own words He then
trips them. You, He says, have confessed that it belongs to God alone to
forgive sins; the equality is undisputed. And not these only say this, but
also the prophet thus saying: Who is a God like you? then showing what is
proper, he added, Taking away lawlessnesses, and passing over injustices. If
then some other appears doing this same thing thus, he also is God, and God
like that one. But let us see how Christ syllogizes with them, how mildly, and
gently, and with all care. And behold some of the scribes said in themselves:
This man blasphemes. They did not bring the word out, they did not bring it
forth through the tongue, but they were reasoning in the secrets of the mind.
What then did Christ? He brought into the midst those secret counsels of
theirs before the demonstration according to the healing of the body of the
paralytic, wishing to show them the strength of His divinity. For that it
belongs to God alone to show the secrets of the mind of His divinity, You know
hearts most alone, it says. Do you see that the "alone" again is not said
toward the distinction of the Son? For if the Father knows hearts most alone,
how does the Son know the secrets of the mind? For He Himself knew, it says,
what was in the man; and Paul showing that this is proper to God, to know the
secrets, says: But He who searches the hearts, showing that this has the same
=51.60= strength as the appellation God. For just as if I say the one who
rains, I indicate no one else but God through the deed, since this belongs to
Him alone; and if I say, the one who makes the sun rise, and I do not add God,
nevertheless I indicate Him through the deed; so also Paul, having said, He
who searches the hearts, showed that it belongs to Him alone to search the
hearts. For if this did not have the same strength as the name God toward
showing us the one indicated, he would not have set it by itself. For if this
were common to Him also toward the creation, we would not have known the one
indicated, the sharing having produced confusion in the mind of the hearers.
When then this appears proper to the Father, and appears also to the Son,
toward whom the equality is undisputed from here too, therefore, Why, He says,
do you reason wicked things in your hearts? for what is easier to say, Your
sins are forgiven you, or to say, Rise and walk?


7. Behold He also makes a second demonstration of the remission of the sins.
For to forgive sins is much greater than to heal a body, and so much greater
as soul than body. For just as paralysis is a disease of the body, so also sin
is a disease of the soul; but that, even if it was greater, was unclear; this,
even if it was lesser, was apparent. Since then He is going to use the lesser
toward the demonstration of the greater, showing that He did thus because of
their weakness, and condescending to their lowliness He says: What is easier
to say, Your sins are forgiven you, or to say, Rise and walk? For what reason
then do you come to the lesser because of them? Because the apparent furnishes
a clearer demonstration of the unapparent. Therefore He did not first raise
him, until He said to them: But so that you may know that the Son of man has
authority to forgive sins on the earth, then He says to the paralytic, Rise
and walk, as if He were saying: Greater is the sign of the remission of the
sins; but because of you I also add the lesser, since this seems to you to be
a demonstration of that. For just as there, having praised the centurion who
said, Say only a word, and my servant will be healed; for I also say to this
one, Go, and he goes; and to the other, Come, and he comes, He confirmed his
judgment through the praises; and again having charged the Jews blaming Him
over the sabbath, that He again looses the law, He showed that He is lord to
change laws; so also here, this man having said, He makes Himself equal to
God, promising things that belong to the Father alone, having blamed them and
accused them, and having displayed through the works that He does not
blaspheme, He furnished us an irrefutable demonstration that He can do these
things, which also the One who begot Him can. See then how He wishes to
construct this, that the things that belong to the Father alone, these also
belong to Him; for He did not simply raise the paralytic, but having said:
=51.61= But so that you may know that the Son of man has authority to forgive
sins on the earth; thus it was a work for Him and eagerness especially to show
this, that He has the same authority as the Father.

8. Let us then hold all these things, both those spoken lately and those
spoken before that day, with accuracy, and let us ask God that they remain
unmoved in our mind, and let us introduce the eagerness from ourselves, and
let us continually meet here. For thus we will both keep the things said
before, and we will acquire others again; and if something flows away with
time, we will be able easily to recover them by the continual teaching. And
not only will the doctrines remain sound and uncorrupted, but also the life
will enjoy much care, and with pleasure and with good cheer we will be able to
complete the present life. For whatever passion troubles the soul when we
arrive here, it will be able easily to be loosed; since now too Christ is
present, and the one who approaches Him with faith will receive the healing
easily. Someone is boxing with continual poverty, and lacks the necessary
food, and has often gone to sleep hungry, but having entered here, and having
heard Paul saying that he spent his time in hunger and in thirst and
nakedness, and that not in one and two and three days, but he endured this
continually (showing this then he was saying: Until the present hour we both
hunger, and thirst, and are naked), he will receive sufficient consolation,
having learned through the things said, that not hating him nor abandoning him
did God permit him to be in poverty; for if this were of one hating, He would
not have permitted this in the case of Paul, who is His friend most of all
men; but caring and providing, and leading to more philosophy. Does another
have the body besieged by disease and countless evils? Sufficient consolation
for this man would be the bodies of these paralytics, and with these the
blessed and noble disciple of Paul, who was continually in infirmities, and
never drew breath from the long weakness, which also Paul was saying: Use a
little wine because of your stomach


and your frequent weaknesses, and not simply weaknesses. Another, having been
slandered among the many, acquired a wicked reputation, and this very thing
pains the soul and eats it continually; having entered and heard, Blessed are
you, when they reproach you, and say every wicked thing against you lying;
rejoice, and leap, because your reward is much in the heavens, he will put
away all despondency and receive all pleasure; For leap and rejoice, He says,
when they cast out a wicked name for you. And those who hear badly He consoles
in this way, but those who speak badly He frightens otherwise saying that
Every idle word which men may speak, they will give an account concerning it,
whether good, or evil. Another has lost a little daughter or a son, or one of
those belonging to him, and this man arriving here and hearing Paul groaning
over the present life and desiring to see the future, and being weighed down
by the sojourn here, he too taking a sufficient drug will go away having
heard him saying: But concerning those who have fallen asleep I do not want
you to be ignorant, brothers, so that you may not grieve as also the rest who
do not have hope. He did not say, Concerning those who are dying, =51.62= but,
Concerning those who have fallen asleep, showing that death is sleep. Just as
then if we see someone sleeping, we are not thrown into tumult, nor restless
expecting that he will certainly rise; so when we see someone having died, let
us not be thrown into tumult, nor fall; for this too is a sleep, longer
indeed, but sleep nonetheless. By the name of falling asleep then he consoled
those mourning, and he overturned the accusation of the unbelievers. If you
mourn the one who has gone away unbearably, he says, you will be according to
that unbeliever who does not have hope of resurrection. That one laments well,
inasmuch as he can philosophize nothing about the things to come; but you who
have received so many demonstrations concerning the life after these things,
for what reason do you fall into the same weakness as that one? Therefore he
says, But concerning those who have fallen asleep we do not want you to be
ignorant, so that you may not grieve as also the rest who do not have hope.
And not from the New only, but also from the Old it is possible to take
sufficient consolation. For when you hear of Job after the loss of the money,
after the destruction of the herds, having lost not one and two and three, but
a whole choir of children in the very flower of age, after so great virtue of
soul, even if you are weaker than all, you will be able easily to recover
yourself and to be lifted up. For you, O man, both attended the child who was
ill, and saw him lying down on the bed, and heard last words being spoken, and
stood by as he breathed the last breaths, and you closed eyes, and shut a
mouth; but that man did not even stand by those gasping out the soul, he did
not see them breathing out, but the house became one tomb for all, and on the
same table brain likewise and blood was poured, and wood, and tile, and dust,
and flesh cut through, and all was mixed alike. But nevertheless after such
and so many things he did not lament, he did not despair, but what does he
say? The Lord gave, the Lord took away, as it seemed good to the Lord, so also
it happened; may the name of the Lord be blessed unto the ages. Let us too
utter this voice at each of the things that happen to us, whether a loss of
money, and infirmity of bodies, and despite, and slander, even if any of human
evils happens, let us say these things: The Lord gave, the Lord took away; as
it seemed good to the Lord, so also it happened, may the name of the Lord be
blessed unto the ages. If we philosophize thus, we will never suffer any evil,
even if we suffer ten thousand things; but the gain will be greater than the
loss, the goods more than the evils, making God gracious to yourself through
these words, beating back the tyranny. For as soon as the tongue uttered these
words, immediately the devil leaped away; and when he has leaped away, the
cloud of despondency also is driven out, and the reasonings that afflict us


run away, leaping out together with him, and besides all these you will also
attain all the goods both here, and in heaven =51.63=. And the example is
secure from Job, from the apostles, who having despised the terrors here
because of God, attained the eternal goods. Let us be persuaded then, and in
all that happens let us rejoice, and let us give thanks to the philan =51.64=
thropic God, so that we may both pass the present life with ease, and attain
the goods to come, by the grace and philanthropy of our Lord Jesus Christ, to
whom be the glory, honor and power always, now and ever, and unto the ages of
the ages. Amen.
