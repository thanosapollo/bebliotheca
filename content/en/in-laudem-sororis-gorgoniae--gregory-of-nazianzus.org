We would do injustice to a holy and celebrated end, if we did not also
make mention of her good things; and this when she is so longed for and
sought after. I will make mention, as briefly as may be. She longed for
the departure (for she had much confidence toward him who calls), and
she preferred being with Christ to all things upon the earth; and no
one of those who are exceedingly amorous and hard to restrain so loves
the body, as she, having cast off these fetters, and having passed
beyond the mire with which we live, to become purely with the
Beautiful, and to receive her beloved wholly, and I will add also her
lover, of whom we are now illumined with small rays, and only so far as
to know from whom we have been separated. And she does not miss even
this desire, being so godly and lofty, and what is greater than this,
she enjoys the Beautiful in advance through foreknowledge and much
vigil. This one sleep of the sweetest succeeds, and one vision that
encompassed the departure with an appointed term, and made this day
known, so that she might be prepared and not disturbed, God governing.
20. For her, then, the good of purification and perfection was close at
hand, which, as a common gift and foundation of a second life, we have
all received from God. Or rather, her whole life was purification and
perfection; and she had that of regeneration from the Spirit, but the
security of it from what she had lived before. And for her almost
alone, if I may dare to say it, the mystery was a seal, but not a gift.
=35.813= And seeking that one thing be added to all, the perfection of
her husband (do you wish me to describe the husband briefly? a husband
of that woman, and I do not know what more one must say), so that she
might be consecrated to God with her whole body, and not depart
perfected by half, nor leave any of her own incomplete, she does not
miss even this petition from him who does the will of those who fear
him, and brings the requests to fulfillment.
21. And when all things were according to her mind, and nothing of the
things longed for was lacking, and the appointed day was near, thus
already she prepares herself for death and for the departure, and she
fulfills the law concerning these things through lying down. And having
charged husband and children and friends with such things as were
fitting for one who loved her husband, and loved her children, and
loved her brothers, and having philosophized splendidly concerning the
things there, and having made the last day a day of festival, she falls
asleep, full not of the days according to man, inasmuch as she did not
even pray for these, knowing them to be evil for herself, and for the
most part with the dust and the error, but exceedingly full of those
according to God, as I do not know if anyone easily of those who have
ended in rich gray hair, and have numbered many circuits of years. Thus
she is released, or rather, better to say, she is taken up, or she
flies away, or she is transplanted, or she goes a little ahead of the
body.
22. But what a thing of hers has almost escaped me! And perhaps you
would not have allowed it, O you her spiritual father, who both kept
the wonder accurately, and made it known to us; a great thing for her
honor, and a great thing for us as a reminder of virtue, and a longing
for the same departure. And a certain shudder runs under me, and a tear
together, as I remember the wonder. She was already being released and
breathing her last, and a choir of her own and of strangers around her,
granting the farewell honors; and the assent of an aged mother, and a
rending of a soul jealous of the departure, and the affection of all
mixed with agony, some longing for what they might hear, a spark of
memory, others for what they might utter, but no one daring. And the
tears were mute, and the travail of grief untreated (for it did not
even seem holy to honor with dirges one departing thus), but a deep
silence, and the death was a kind of rite. =35.816= And she was
breathless and motionless and voiceless, as it appeared, and the
silence of the body seemed a paralysis, as if already the vocal organs
had died, through the withdrawal of that which moves them. But the
shepherd who carefully watches all her things, through the wonder in
all things, perceiving the lips moving gently, and having put his ears
to her lips (for he had confidence both from his manner and from
sympathy), do you yourself relate the mystery of the quiet, what it was
and of what sort; no one will disbelieve you speaking. What was being
murmured was psalmody, and the departing words of psalmody; and if one
must speak the truth, a testimony of the confidence,

with which the going-out. And blessed is whoever rests with those
words: In peace in the same place I will lie down and sleep. These
things were both sung for you, fairest of women, and they came to pass;
and the psalmody was what was happening, and with the departure the
funeral oration; O you who have well made peace from the passions, and
have received the sleep owed to the beloved, in addition to the common
falling-asleep, as was fitting for her who both lived and departed in
the words of piety.
23. I know well that the things now present to you are better and far
more honorable than according to the things seen, a sound of those
keeping festival, a choir of angels, a heavenly order, a vision of
glory, both of the other and of the illumination of the highest
Trinity, purer and more perfect, no longer fleeing the bound mind that
is scattered by the senses, but the whole being contemplated and
grasped by the whole mind, and flashing upon our souls with the whole
light of the Godhead. May you enjoy all the things of which, while
still upon earth, you had the emanations through the genuineness of
your inclination toward them. And if there is also any account of our
things to you, and this is a gift from God to holy souls, to perceive
such things, may you receive also our word in place of many and before
many funeral offerings, which we have rendered to Caesarius before you,
and to you after him, since indeed we were entrusted with funeral
orations of siblings. And if someone should honor us also after you
with the like, I cannot say; only may we be honored with the honor in
God, both sojourning and dwelling =35.817= in Christ Jesus our Lord, to
whom be the glory and to the Father with the Holy Spirit unto the ages.
Amen.
