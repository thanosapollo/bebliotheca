* On the Passion of our Savior Jesus Christ
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

A discourse of Chrysostom on the passion of our Savior Jesus Christ, for
the holy and great Saturday.
When I behold the greatness of the Master and reckon the insults of his
forbearing loving-kindness, I am beside myself in mind, how he who
received all judgment from the Father stood before Pilate to be judged,
how the potter was judged by the clay. For what of the most outrageous
things did he not endure for us? He who purchased all things was sold,
he who made the tree of life was brought to the tree of death, and being
struck with a hand he bore it, he who restored a withered hand to
health, and he endured spittings, he who with spittle and clay created
eyes. He endured the pains from the nails, he who made those who came
against him cower with a breath, and he gave his back to blows, he who
tortured Egypt with ten plagues.
But O the Jewish ingratitude! Do you repay these things to the Lord?
For of which of the good things, O Jew, did you not enjoy? Having eaten
manna, you offered gall; having received water from the rock, you gave
vinegar as the drink; having become a man from virgin earth, you do not
receive him who is born of a virgin, but you nail the Judge to a cross
as a condemned man and you plot to put the resurrection to death. But
see all things, even if you are impious toward the power of the
Crucified. He was being nailed to the wood and he was shifting
creation; he was hanging and that which bore him was shaken. I pity
your blindness, O Jew. The ground trembled and you, striking, laughed;
the elements were beside themselves and you paraded the insult;
"Prophesy to us, Jesus, who is it that struck you." Even the word
itself trembles as it narrates the passion, yet even trembling it does
not keep silent about what took place, so that those who looked and
were not afraid, and those who hear, may not be put to shame. What then
happened upon these things? The sun was wasting away, seeing the
Creator being put to death. The Master was dying of his own will, and
the light did not wish to live, but the work died together with the
Maker.
But you, even when these things had happened, did not run to
repentance. A strange and wondrous thing: above he banished the sun
and below he was shut in a tomb, and a stone was laid upon the rock,
and death held life, and night hid the true morning star, and soldiers
guarded the King of the heavens as a runaway, and seals of Jews kept
the seal of the faith. But then one could see an astonishing and
wondrous sight. For the tomb, having received Jesus with a seal, kept
the seals, but did not save the deposit, that what is written might be
fulfilled: "You will not abandon my soul to Hades"; and it is no wonder
if for three days the tomb did not hold Jesus, whose voice raised
Lazarus of four days. To him be the glory unto the ages of ages.
Amen.»
