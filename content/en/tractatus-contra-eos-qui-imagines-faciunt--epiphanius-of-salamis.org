* Treatise against those who make images

Discourse of Saint Epiphanius against those who practice making images
according to idolatrous custom in likeness of Christ and of the Theotokos
and of the martyrs, and still also of angels and prophets.
1 having plastered the wall they stamped the images with varied colors.
2 because, they say, we make the images of the saints for their memorial
and honor. 3
falsely named 4 but from where did bones and nerves fitted together become
evident to him upon the painting of the archangel, of that one's
intelligence which he says. 5 those who think that in this they honor the
apostles, let them learn that instead of honoring, they dishonor them
more.
For Paul, having insulted the falsely named priest, declared him a
whitened wall. 6 "for we know," says John, "that when he is manifested, we
shall be like him," and Paul proclaimed the saints "conformed to the son
of God"; how then do you want to see the saints who are about to shine in
glory in an inglorious and dead and speechless, the Lord saying concerning
them: "for they shall be," he says, "as angels of God." 7 and how do you,
painting angels who exist as spirits and always living in the dead,
worship, the prophet saying "he who makes his angels spirits and his
ministers a flaming fire"? 8 and I say that they themselves do not even
want to be worshipped.
9 "See that you do not; for I am a fellow-servant of yours and of your
brothers who have the testimony of Jesus. Worship God," he says. 10 but
neither did the apostles want to be worshipped. for even when they were
sent to evangelize, they did not want themselves to be worshipped, but
Christ who sent them. for he who received authority from him to bind and
loose on earth and in heaven said to Cornelius that "I am a man of like
passions according to you," and he taught not himself to be worshipped
but the savior Christ.
11 and concerning the angels the fathers who assembled at Laodicea, for
they were altogether gathered because of such a matter, say: "if anyone
abandons the church of God and names angels, let him be anathema; because
he has abandoned our Lord Jesus Christ and has come to idolatry." 12 how
does anyone say to paint the incomprehensible and ineffable and
unthinkable and also uncircumscribable, whom Moses was not able to look
at fixedly? 13 some say that because he became a perfect man from Mary
the ever-virgin, for this reason we make him a man. 14 and for this did
he become incarnate, so that you might be able to paint with your hands
the incomprehensible through whom all things came to be? 15 is he then
not like the Father nor does he make the dead live? 16 where did he,
having come upon earth, ordain these things for you, to make a likeness
to him and to worship and to look upon? 17 this is the ordinance of the
evil one, so that you may despise God. 18 for God in all the old and the
new abolishes these things, saying exactly: "you shall worship the Lord
God and him only shall you serve."
