SPURIOUS. ON THE NEW SUNDAY And on the apostle Thomas.
=63.927=
I have come, pressing to pay the remaining debt. For though I am poor, I press
upon your kindness. I promised to set forth the unbelief of Thomas; and now I
am here to render it to you. For I am eager to pay the first debts first, lest
I be held by the interest that gathers upon them. Join me then in the payment
of the debt, and beseech Thomas, that placing upon my lips his holy right hand
which touched the Master's side, he may nerve my tongue for the exposition of
what you long for. And I, taking courage from the intercessions of the apostle
and martyr Thomas, proclaim his former doubt and his second confession, which
is the base and foundation of our Church. When the Savior entered to his own
disciples, the doors being shut, and went out again as he had entered, Thomas
was left alone.
This too was a work of divine economy, so that the disciple's absence might
become the occasion of greater security and certainty. For if Thomas had been
present, he would not have doubted at all; and if he had not doubted, he would
not have inquired with such care; and if he had not inquired, he would not
have handled; and if he had not handled, he would not have proclaimed him Lord
and God; and if he had not called Christ Lord and God, we would not have been
taught to hymn him so. So Thomas, even when not present, guided us toward the
truth, and when he came later he made us more firm in the faith.
The disciples, then, said to Thomas when he came in last, We have seen the
Lord, we have seen him who said, I am the light of the world; we have seen him
who said, I am the resurrection and the life and the truth; and we found the
truth of the words shining in the deeds; we have seen him who said, After
three days I rise; and seeing the resurrection, we worshiped the one who rose;
we heard him say to us, Peace be with you, and we turned the storm of grief
toward the gladness of calm; we beheld his hands that received the points of
the nails, we beheld the hands that cry out against the frenzy of the
God-fighting dogs, we beheld the hands that wove incorruption for us, we
beheld also the side that cries more brightly than any herald the compassion
of the one who was struck; we beheld the side itself, which angels hymn, and
those who believe revere, and demons shudder at. We received also a divine
breath from his divine mouth, a spiritual breath, a breath that supplies every
grace. We were ordained by the Master as masters of the forgiveness of
offenses;
we became also lords of the judgment of sinners, receiving from him such a
word as a watchword: If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven them;
if you retain the sins of any, they are retained. Of such words of the Savior
we took our delight, of such gifts we partook; for it was not possible that we
should not be enriched, having met with a rich Master; but you alone =63.928=
remained poor, not being present. What then did Thomas say to them? You have
seen the Lord? It is well. Therefore him whom you have beheld, reverence more
greatly; him whom you have looked upon, continue proclaiming. But I: Unless I
see in his hands the print of the nails, and put my finger into the print of
the nails, and put my hand into his side, I will not believe. But you would
not have believed, if you had not first beheld; so I too, unless I see, will
not believe.
Remain, O Thomas, in such longing, remain earnestly, that as you see I may be
made firm in soul; remain, seeking him who said, Seek and you shall find; do
not pass on with mere searching, unless you find the treasure you seek; remain
knocking at the door of irrefutable knowledge, until he who said, Knock, and
it shall be opened to you, opens it a little for you. I love the division of
your thoughts, as cutting through every division; I love your love of
learning, as cutting out all contention; I hear with pleasure your saying
again and again: Unless I see in his hands the print of the nails, I will not
believe.
For as you disbelieve, I am taught to believe; as you dig with the mattock of
your tongue the fields of the divine body, I harvest the fruit without toil
and heap it up for myself. Unless I see with these eyes of mine the furrows in
his holy hands which the impious plowed, I will by no means agree with your
words; unless I put this finger of mine into the hollows of the nails, I would
not receive your Gospel; unless I grasp with this hand of mine that side, the
unimpeachable witness of the resurrection, I would not believe your teaching.
For every word that has the advocacy of all the facts is strong and sure; but
every voice deprived of the witness of the works is fading, poured from the
lips into the air.
I am about to proclaim to men the wonders of the teacher; how then shall I
speak in words what I have not received with my eyes? How shall I persuade the
unbelieving to believe things to which I myself have not yet attended? Shall I
say to the Jews and to the Greeks that I have seen my Master crucified, but
have not seen him risen, but have heard? And who will not laugh at my voice?
Who will not spit upon the proclamation? For hearing is one thing, and sight
another; and the report of words is one thing, and the seeing and proving of
deeds another. Thomas having knowledge so doubtful, after eight days the
Master came again to his own disciples gathered together. He allowed Thomas in
the intervening days to be instructed first by his companions, he permitted
him to be burned with thirst for the sight of him; and when his soul had been
fiercely kindled with longing for the vision, then at last, in due season, the
one longed for overtook the one who longed.
And in the same way as before, the doors being shut, he did this, and again,
as also earlier, he said to them, Peace =63.929= be with you, that there might
be identity both of the deed and of the miracle, and that he might confirm the
apostles' report, and show the exactness of his second appearing. Then he says
to Thomas: Reach your finger here, and see my hands. O height of boundless
love of mankind! O sea of immeasurable condescension! He did not wait for the
disciple's approach, he did not wait for the one in need to come forward and
ask and obtain what he wished, he did not deprive him even for a little of the
request, but the beloved himself compelled the lover toward the one longed
for, he himself by his voice drew the finger of the one who longed toward
himself, he himself with the Master's tongue drew the servant's right hand,
saying to him: Reach your finger here, and see my hands. I heard, Thomas, not
being present as man, but being present as God, what you spoke to your
brothers; I was with you in the divinity, separated from you in the humanity.
Do you wish me to remind you of the words spoken before? Did you not say,
Unless I see in his hands the print of the nails, and put my finger into the
place of the nails, and put my hand into his side, I will not believe? Did not
these words flow through your lips? Are not these words those of your
thoughts? For these things I have come again, for the things about which you
doubt; this second time I have drawn near, for the things you desire, I have
arrived. And now for you, the one, I have come to you, I who came down from
the heavens for the wandering sheep, and did not leave the heavens. Do not
then be ashamed to learn what you long for, do not be abashed to examine
closely what you seek; do not refuse to set your finger upon these hands of
mine. I endure even a curious finger, as I endured the nails; I bear the
inquisitiveness of the one who loves, as I bore the spite of those who hate;
crucified by enemies I was not angry, and being searched by you I will not
refuse. Reach your finger here, and see my hands that were wounded for you,
that the bruises of your souls may be healed; see my hands, and consider with
yourself whether I am that one who was crucified willingly, or someone else
besides him; see my hands, which I allowed to keep the marks of the Jewish
madness, so that when the Jews are shameless as usual on the day of judgment,
and say to me, We did not crucify you, Master, I may show the enemies my hands
in this form, and by the sight put the faces of the Jews to shame.
See my hands and the truth of my resurrection; do not think it some fantasy.
Hold these hands as hostages of your regeneration; hold these hands as pledges
of the rising again from the tombs; hold these hands, an anchor of the deep in
Hades. Fear no storm of this life, shudder at no worldly tempest, fear not the
blasts of contrary winds, take no thought of the squalls and the reefs of the
enemies' sea. Sail the deep of life with courage, sail holding the anchor of
the spirit, sail looking to heaven as to a harbor, sail fearing only the
shipwreck of denying me; laugh at death as a corpse, mock corruption as
powerless, embrace the end for my sake as the beginning of a more inward life,
and reach your hand, and put it into my side; draw with your hand from my
life-bearing spring the drink you long for, and relieve your thirst. Reach
your hand, and put it into my side; bring your right hand into the infirmary
of nature, and take out the medicine of your choice; I bear the laying-on of a
loving hand, I who received a wound from a spear. Reach your hand, and put it
into my side, that you may have it to contend for, that you may have to say to
those who speak against the truth that after the resurrection you saw me and
learned me and handled me exactly. Reach =63.930= your hand, and put it into
my side; for it was for your sake that I left it so, I who healed the bodies
and souls of others, foreknowing as God that you would wish to see it so, that
you, seeing the marks of the passion of my flesh, might heal the passion of
your soul. Reach your hand, and put it into my side, which I have kept so, as
you see, that when I come again from the heavens, and sit as judge of the
living and the dead, the Jews may see the works of their evil doing appearing
face to face, and become self-condemned. And do not become unbelieving, but
believing. Unbelief is an evil: it sinks the mind. Faith lifts it up to
heaven; unbelief blinds the soul; faith lights the thoughts; unbelief
overlooks even the visible; faith sees even the invisible; the unbeliever is
altogether ignorant.
Do not become unbelieving, but believing; drive away the cloud of unbelief,
and behold the pure rays of faith. Become also through all things an apostle
worthy of my divinity; become such as he must be who has met with me and
obtained the things which you have obtained. You were called like the other
apostles, you were honored like them, arm yourself like them; like them you
have beheld what they saw, like them you were entrusted, as a friend, with my
whole mystery; like them proclaim my power. Do not say again after seeing
once: Unless I see again in his hands the print of the nails, I will not
believe. While I am with you, inquire as you will; while you have the heavenly
vine standing before you, search all its branches and its clusters. For I will
go up into the heavens, from where I came to earth, I will go up where I am, I
will go up in the humanity from where I came down with you in the divinity, I
will go up with this body, I who departed from there without this, and
remained there; I will go up with your nature to the Father's bosom, He who is
in the bosom of the Father; for I have accomplished the work for which I made
this journey.
Thomas then, having touched the Master's hands and the divine side, and having
become full of fear and overflowing joy together from the sight of what he
desired, at once moves his tongue to hymnody, crying to the Lord: My Lord and
my God; you are Lord and God, you are both man and lover of mankind, you are a
strange and wondrous physician of nature; you do not cut the passions with
iron, you do not burn the wounds with fire, you do not gather from herbs the
strength of medicines, you do not bind the suffering wounds with visible
bandages; you have invisible bandages of mercies, invisibly binding together
what has come apart; you have a word sharper than iron, you have a saying
stronger than fire, you have a nod gentler than a drug. As Creator you
sanctify the creature without toil, as fashioner you refashion the formed
things without labor. You scraped away leprosy as you willed, you made the
lame runners, you made the paralyzed carry their own beds, you commanded those
born blind to wash off the darkness, you drove the demons out from your
creatures, you were seized by enemies because you willed it, you willingly
suffered all things according to the flesh at the hands of the Jews for my
sake, My Lord and my God.
I have recognized my Master, I have recognized my fisher and guardian, I have
recognized my king and lord, My Lord and my God. I believe, Master, in your
economy, I believe in your condescension, I believe in your assumption of my
first nature, I believe your cross that is worshiped, I believe in your
sufferings of the flesh, I believe in your three-day death, I believe in your
resurrection;
henceforth I no longer pry; I believe, I no longer call to account; I believe,
I no longer weigh in the balance; I believe, I no longer search with
curiosity; I believe my eyes, I believe my right hand. I have learned from
what I saw not to call to account; I have learned from what I handled to
worship, not to wrangle; I know one Lord and God, the Master Christ, to whom
be the glory and the power unto the ages of ages. Amen.
