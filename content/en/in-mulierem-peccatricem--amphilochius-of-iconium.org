* On the Sinful Woman
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Discourse of Amphilochius, bishop of Iconium, on the sinful woman who anointed
the Lord with myrrh, and on the Pharisee. Christ sufficiently refreshed our
souls of late, being entertained at Zacchaeus's house; for where Christ is
entertained and reclines with men and partakes of our drink and table, all
things are turned toward the account of gladness. For who of publicans or
harlots and of those who have done unspeakable evils, seeing the Maker of
heaven and earth entering under a publican's roof, and the Giver of the ears
of grain taking human bread in his hands, and the Supplier of the clusters
blessing the winepresses by sharing in the drink, would not justly reckon the
matter a feast and a festival? This is truly a feast, this is truly the
gladness of an angelic banquet, to see the Master with servants, God with men,
the Judge with those under judgment, partaking of a common table. For this he
came upon earth without leaving heaven desolate, and became man without
putting off being God, that both sailing the sea he might draw up from the
depth of sin those storm-tossed on the sea of this life, and going about
villages and cities, running through lanes and paths and roads, he might lead
back to his own flock those wandering at the crossroads as unshepherded sheep.
For he himself is the one who seeks the lost sheep, who left the ninety-nine
and came for the search of the one. For he was seeking the one, neither
despising the many nor preferring the one to the multitude. But he was leaving
the ninety-nine, for they had to lodge securely in the fold; and going about
he was seeking the one, lest it become a banquet for the devil. For an
unshepherded sheep is a ready dinner for wild beasts, and an unsealed soul is
easy for demons to plot against. Hence of late he snatched Zacchaeus as a
sheep from a wolf's mouth and mixed him with the fold and deemed him worthy of
a seal. For as a shepherd wishing to hunt a sheep that has wandered off lets a
tame animal go, that grazing freely it may draw the quarry that has leapt
away, so also the Word of God left the flesh which he took from the Virgin, as
a sheep in pasture, at Zacchaeus's table, that by the common law of the
banquet, having drawn him into company, he might secretly join him to his own
flock. But the Pharisees, not understanding this, were murmuring, seeing him
eating with publicans. But let those burst as an old wineskin, for they cannot
receive the new wine of the teaching; but let us walk behind the loving
shepherd. For he who joined Zacchaeus the publican to the rational flock of
the apostles, himself also, having drawn the harlot, the sinful woman, the
worker of countless evils, from the throat of the devil as a ewe-lamb,
restored her to the unassailable fold. And that you may know both the
lovingkindness of Christ and the irrationality of the Pharisees and the return
of the sinful woman, I will set before you the very evangelical sayings; for
if you listen to the texture of the reading, you will easily take the imprint
of the sense of the interpretation. A certain one of the Pharisees, it says,
asked Jesus that he might eat with him. And entering into the house of the
Pharisee he reclined. O unspeakable grace! O ineffable lovingkindness! He both
feasts with Pharisees and does not shake off publicans and receives harlots
and converses with a Samaritan woman and deems a Canaanite woman worthy of a
word and yields the

hem to the woman with the issue of blood. Yes, for he is a physician laying
hold of all the passions, that he may benefit all, wicked and good together,
ungrateful and well-disposed. Hence even now, called by the Pharisee, he
enters into a house still full of evils. For where there is a Pharisee, there
is a starting-point of wickedness, a lodging of sin, a reception of arrogance.
But even with his house in such a state the Lord does not think it unworthy to
come. Reasonably; for just as the sun is not harmed by casting its own rays
upon mire, but on the contrary even wipes away the inherent foulness, itself
in no way insulted, so also Christ as sun of righteousness takes hold of every
unholy and profane place and consumes the stinking sin with the rays of his
goodness, suffering neither insult nor diminution nor defilement according to
the account of the Godhead. Hence he readily nodded to the Pharisee who was
inviting, remaining quiet, silent, leaving his life unrefuted. First, that he
might sanctify those invited, the one who called, the establishment of the
house, the foods of the luxury; then showing that the incarnation was not a
phantom, from the reclining, the eating, the drinking, the consuming of the
foods. And otherwise, since the harlot was about to approach and display that
hot and fiery manner of repentance, for this reason he quickly nods to the
Pharisee who is inviting, that having tragically displayed her own evils
before the eyes of scribes and Pharisees she might teach them how sinners,
being downcast over their sins, ought to propitiate God. For behold, it says,
a woman in the city, who was a sinner. A woman, the easily-slipping nature,
the first net of the devil, the introduction of the deceit, the teacher of the
transgression, who became a helper but was shown an enemy, who became by
nature beautiful but by choice was shown evil, the procurer of death, who
displayed the beauty of the tree and lost the whole paradise. And behold a
woman, it says, in the city, who was a sinner, bearing the burdens of Eve,
weighed down with many evils. And I will tell the abundance of her former
evils, that you may know the lavishness of her repentance. God, taking a bone
from Adam's side and fleshing it, made Eve, and calling her woman gave her as
a helper to Adam. But after they sinned and transgressed the law and were cast
out of paradise and received death as the penalty, that the race might not be
utterly destroyed, being consumed by death, he arrays marriage against death,
that the one may sow and the other reap, the one cut and the other sprout. And
that after being subjected to death the grace of marriage was given, is clear
from Adam being joined to Eve after the exit from paradise. For it is written
that after going out from paradise, then Adam knew his wife. Before the sin,
then, virginity was keeping the tunic of nature undefiled, but after the
transgression, after the sentence of death, marriage was introduced in its
place, that overflowing it might conquer death as it draws, and by bringing
forth it might defeat it as it harvests. Since therefore the law of marriage
was given for the succession of the race and for the increase of nature, it
sowed pleasure in the man, and made the female coaxing; not that they might be
stirred to unions in a harlot's way, but that they might be joined to marriage
lawfully. Hence the lawful mixing of marriage is honorable before God, but
that which is accomplished for the sake of pleasures has been subjected to
death. For marriage is honorable and the bed undefiled; but God will judge
fornicators and adulterers. Those then who mix lawfully with their husbands
for the sake of childbearing are blameless, as Sarah and Rebecca and Rachel,
and whoever else after her; but those who by a principle of luxury arouse the
young toward license, as corrupting the temple of God, are given over to
corruption. For if anyone, it says, corrupts the temple of God, God will
corrupt him. Of whom this one also was one, the sinful woman now at hand. For
trafficking in nature and with outward

dyes reddening her cheeks and by art forcing herself to appear beautiful, she
was dragging the young toward license, turning them unawares into the pit of
fornication. And I say these things not mocking her for what she did before,
but praising her for what she has suddenly become from what she was; for I say
what she was, that I may show what she has become now; I tell the faults of
her sin, that I may show the achievements of repentance. But this woman who
formerly used her body not blamelessly, snaring some by locks of hair,
poisoning some by tears, bewitching some by ointment, and calling all from
every side to the mire of license, changes her shameful and luxurious love
into a divine and heavenly charm. For when she saw Jesus now conversing
without fear with the Samaritan woman, now receiving the Canaanite woman, at
another time proclaiming the theft of the woman with the issue of blood, and
here eating with publicans, there taking the houses of the Pharisees, she
reasoned: If he receives harlots and sinners and publicans, how long, goaded
without restraint, do I draw from the sea of sin? I do not remain young, I do
not remain beautiful; for all things pass, all things wither, both flowers and
lilies and the beauties of faces. What then shall I suffer for what I have
done? For already I have in mind the fire of Gehenna, already repentance takes
hold of my soul, because, forcing myself to appear beautiful for the
destruction of the young, I was running in the streets of the city, in the
marketplaces, in the lanes, having my feet as a net and my tongue as a
dragnet. O how many young men I was charming, carrying about a look full of
shamelessness! For being adorned for the ruin of the spectators, here I was
piling my head with many-plaited cords, there I was letting wandering locks
from the crown stray over the forehead; at another time I was reddening the
cheeks and painting the eyes, and at another I was sending forth streams of
tears, throwing the soul by flattery. What then shall I become after these
things? What physician shall I find of these boundless passions? If I tell my
affairs to men, the manner of the confession becomes useless to me. But shall
I cover the evils? But I cannot hide; for whom shall I escape, being unable to
escape God? Where then shall I flee, finding the Judge everywhere, who does
not appear, yet everywhere convicts my evils? One hope of salvation is left
me, one provision of life lies before me, to recognize Jesus and run to him.
For he who receives publicans does not refuse a harlot; he who eats with
Pharisees does not decline the tears of the sinful woman. Since then I have
learned that he is lodging with Simon the Pharisee, a leprous and sinful man,
I will run to him. But what shall I ask when I approach? Health of eyes? But
the grace is temporary. Deliverance from disease? But the achievement is
small, for eternal death is heavier than the present one. Passing over all
things of the body I will seek the healing of the soul. For I find one release
from the evils that hang over me, if I see the Judge, if I anticipate the time
of punishment. I will imitate Rahab the harlot; I will pursue the virtuous
manner of a woman; for God wants nothing from us but a change of mind. Having
considered these things piously and anointing her understanding toward faith,
she comes in upon Jesus where he was reclining, taking her former
shamelessness as a ground of boldness. And she says nothing to him; for she
did not dare, for she knew that the overseer of thoughts has no need of words.
For what did she have to say to him who knows all things? That she had sinned?
That she had become a worker of many evils? That loving and being loved she
had served the common pleasure? These things were clear to God, not only as
they were being done, but also as they were being laid bare in the unseen
council-chamber of the soul. Knowing then that he knows all things and that
nothing can hide from him, she shuts the door of her tongue, but speaks with
her tears. For standing, it says, beside his feet weeping, she began to wet
his feet with

tears. But even if she was not speaking with the tongue, yet with unutterable
groanings she was sounding, uncovering the crushing of the heart, making a
spectacle of the multitude of sins, parading the unseemly reasonings, the
unholy thoughts, the profane deeds, the lawless associations; for there was
none of the evils done by her which she was not tragically displaying with
tears. For she knew that of the things she was confessing she was receiving
amnesty. For I said, it says, I will confess my lawlessness to the Lord, and
you have forgiven the impiety of my heart. And not only was she sounding
unutterably with the groanings of the heart, importuning the Lord, but also by
her posture she was sketching the beauty of repentance. She wept, as having
laughed much, washing away the evil laughter with good tears, and with the
drops of the eyes washing away the filth of the cheeks, that in the things in
which she had sinned she might also make her defense, in the things in which
she had broken the law she might propitiate the Lawgiver. For just as David
washed with tears the bed which he had unlawfully defiled by intercourse: For
I will wash, he says, my bed every night, I will wet my couch with my tears,
so also this woman, through the very eyes by which she had dragged many of the
young into license, sending forth springs of tears, washed away the
hard-to-wash filth of sin, herself putting forward the tears as a bath of
repentance for herself. For she herself was supplying the tears as water, but
was receiving the forgiveness invisibly from Christ. But indeed, not only
imitating Abraham but perhaps even conquering him, she washed the feet of
Christ. For he, setting a basin, washed with waters and wiped with a towel,
but she, not drawing water, but sending forth fountains of tears, washed the
feet of Jesus. And taking care not to insult holy feet with sinful tears,
putting under the beauty of the hair as a towel she was wiping the feet. And
one could see the whole woman through and through bent toward the service of
Jesus; for the eyes from above were sending channels as fountains of tears,
and the soul as a basin lying under was receiving the drops dripping from the
feet, and the locks were wiping, fulfilling the form of a towel, and the
hands, pouring the alabaster of myrrh, were anointing the divine feet with
myrrh, honoring the myrrh with myrrh; For myrrh, it says, poured out is your
name. Did you see how a sinful woman, with no share in the divine laws,
conquered the ungrateful mind of the Jews? For they were casting stones at
him, but she was gladdening him with the myrrhs of fragrance. But the Jews, as
ungrateful, as without understanding and without sense, were repaying the
benefactor with evils, greeting the cornerstone with stones; but she was
anointing with myrrh the feet that were about to stand for her sake all day
upon the timber of the cross. And why do I say that she conquered the
ungrateful people of the Jews, when she even overshot the whole choir of the
saints? For she received a grace which kings did not receive, which potentates
did not obtain; For kings of Tarshish and islands shall bring gifts, and all
the kings of the earth shall worship him. And indeed according to the prophet
they both gave the gifts and worshipped from afar, but none of them kissed the
feet of Jesus. How could they? Magi came, taking a star as ally of their
guidance, but they were bringing gifts from afar, knowing the measure of their
own rank. For heaven, he says, is my throne, and the earth the footstool of my
feet. Let the woman then be praised as having received the honor of the whole
earth, as having touched undefiled feet whose dust nations and tribes shall
lick, according to what was said, that they shall lick the dust of his feet.
She touched undefiled feet; she shared the body of Christ with John. For he
leaned upon the breast, for from there he was about to wipe off a divine
teaching; but she was anointing the feet that walk on our behalf. But Christ,
who does not judge the sin but praises the repentance, who does not punish the
things that have passed

but tests the things to come, passing over her former evils with amnesty
honors the woman and praises the repentance, justifies the tears and crowns
the purpose. But the Pharisee, seeing the wonder, is struck in his mind and,
being hit by envy, does not accept the woman's repentance, but even slaps with
abuse the one who has so honored the Lord, and lessens the dignity of the one
honored, voting ignorance against him. For seeing, it says, the Pharisee who
had invited him said in himself: This man, if he were a prophet, would have
known who and what sort the woman is who is touching him, that she is a
sinner. O foolish and without understanding and altogether a Pharisee! In
saying these things you are not convicting the woman's manner, but you are
accusing your own choice, saying that he does not know who the woman ever was.
Therefore you did not honor him by the invitation as God who knows all things;
are you not then ashamed, O splendid accuser and slanderer, that you call him
as God and able to bless, but cry out against him as a man and knowing nothing
beyond us? This man, he says, if he were a prophet. And how much better than
you, O Pharisee, is the woman in Shechem, who did not know a prophet and from
the first sight confessed the Savior with good sense. For Lord, she says, I
perceive that you are a prophet. And how much more marvelous than you is this
sinful woman also, whose sin you see, but whose repentance you do not look at.
But you even condemn her whom the Judge justifies, and you blame and censure
her whom God, having accepted, crowns, because seeing God in the form of a man
reclining at your house she recognized and honored him; and uncovering the
wounds of the soul she asked for mercy and amnesty of the things she had
lived. But you, having honored by the invitation, dishonor by the abuse,
saying: If he were a prophet he would have known who and what sort the woman
is who is touching him. Wretched man! Because he did not refute your evils, do
you vote ignorance against him? Because he entered under your roof, weighed
down with many evils, do you take away his knowledge? But since you deemed him
worthy to enter under your roof, to recline with you and to lay a hand upon
your dishes, he nodded and did not think it unworthy: do you reckon him one of
the many? And were you worthy to lodge God as a guest, or to set a table for
the one who prepares a table in the wilderness? But being loving toward men he
did not refuse to receive even drink and food from your household servants.
Why then do you accuse a loving Master, O Pharisee, who likewise balances the
yoke of kindness toward all? And why, straining out the gnat of the woman, do
you swallow the camel of your own evils? And for yourself you wish God to be
longsuffering, but for her severe? Why do you provoke the Judge over others'
charges, asking pardon for your own? Why have you and Judas agreed to test the
Lord? For you, as clean from filth, dragging the woman over her sins, vote
ignorance against God, but he as a lover of the poor is indignant, saying: Why
this waste of the myrrh? For it could have been sold for much and given to the
poor. O the ungrateful mind! O the thankless manners! Do you set down as
waste, Judas, the service of Christ, and call vain expenditure what has gone
forth to the honor of God? How much have we given of what we have received?
Let us reckon from when the world was displayed, how many rivers of mercy flow
from his nature, and God does not reckon the abundance a loss. How many
fragrances does the earth sprout? Herbs of roses and lilies, and storax, nard
and stacte and the other things from which the good myrrh is prepared. And God
does not reckon it a waste. And that a small alabaster of myrrh might be
poured upon the feet of Christ, do you murmur? Did he then receive it for
nothing, that you should murmur? He received myrrh and displayed a manner of
repentance; he received tears and stopped a fountain of sins. What waste is it
then, if the woman was saved, for whose sake paradise was shut, for whose sake
Adam was cast out together? But does this grieve you, Judas? Reasonably; for
it also

grieved the devil, her salvation. For he saw through her the race henceforth
being turned toward repentance, and he is bitten and distressed, no longer
having a net through which he will hunt man; hence he also urged you to
murmur. To what purpose, he says, is this waste? For it could have been sold
for much. Already a sale, Judas? Already a rehearsal of betrayal, a beginning
of evil riddles? But Jesus does not refute his disease, does not lay bare the
love of money, lest he check the coming betrayal. But Christ, rebuking, says:
Why do you cause trouble for the woman? Why, in addition to the former evils,
do you revile the human being? The race of women has toiled sufficiently; let
no one hinder their salvation, let no one trouble her who has washed with
myrrh the feet that walked upon the earth on her behalf. For the poor you have
with you, but take me also with the poor; for for your sake I became poor,
being rich, that you might be enriched by my poverty. You put me to death and
I do not accuse; she is burying me and you murmur? For she, having cast the
myrrh upon my body, has done it toward burying me. Are you not then ashamed,
Judas, that she, being a sinner, honors me with myrrhs, but you, being an
apostle, insult me with a sale? And the woman prepares the things for the
tomb, but the disciple betrays to death. The woman is a sinner, I know; but
she had nothing to grant me but a fountain of tears, propitiating the fountain
with a fountain, bringing an immaterial mercy-seat to a possessionless
teacher. But you even assay the myrrh, wretched man, saying it is worth three
hundred denarii; and you assay it not that you may praise her magnanimity,
because she spent the whole wealth gathered from evils upon the honor of
myrrh, but that by murmuring you may show that you have fallen into an
abominable evil, having been so much damaged. But it is no great thing if you
are displeased at the deprivation of three hundred denarii, when having taken
thirty you sold me the Lord. For what, he says, do you wish to give me, and I
will deliver him to you. Wretched man, does a slave sell a master? The order
has been reversed; and I buy you from sin with my own blood, but you sell me
for thirty obols? And then does a man sell God? And granted that he sells, who
is the buyer? And for how much will someone buy God? And why do you make the
matter cheap? Does someone sell God in the form of a man for thirty obols, as
a household servant, as a barbarian? At least distinguish, for how much the
indwelling God, and for how much the appearing man? What, he says, do you wish
to give me? What do you wish to receive? For they have nothing to give worthy
of God. And they set for him, it says, thirty pieces of silver. Does someone
sell for thirty obols an unpaid physician, a physician giving eyes to the
blind, raising the lame to a course? I say these things, that I may catechize
your mind, that you cause trouble for the woman, that she honored as a dead
man him who is free among the dead even before the death, that through the
myrrh she prefigured the grace of the burial and of the resurrection. But you
will have the rope as fruit of the betrayal, but her memory, wherever the
gospel is preached, will be indelible. He spoke and the work has come to be
thus; that the myrrh of Aaron, of Eleazar, has ceased and the horn has been
idle, but her alabaster is stretched out with every age, having the fragrance
of the memory unemptied. But toward Judas thus, and toward the Pharisee
murmuring Christ speaks thus: Simon, I have something to say to you. O
unspeakable grace! O ineffable lovingkindness! God and man confer, and he sets
forth a problem and a rule of lovingkindness, dissolving his wickedness. For
Simon, he says, I have something to say to you; I have to say what I have said
to none of the ancients, not to a patriarch, not to a prophet, not to a
lawgiver. For then they were seeking an eye for an eye, a tooth for a tooth,
as I was requiring the just; but since you cannot bear the just, I introduce
grace in place of the law, I will tell you an unspoken mystery. And he says:
Teacher, say it. There were two debtors to one man. See the wisdom of God;

he is silent about the woman, lest he make the answer knavish. The one, he
says, owed five hundred denarii and the other fifty. Fearful is the manner of
the narrative; our life is a ledger, writing invisibly both reasonings and
deeds and wanderings of eyes and movements of soul. But the loving lender
looses the fear, who tears the handwriting of sin and not only tears but also
smooths it with the waters of baptism, lest a trace of a letter or a syllable
remaining become a reminder of the evils that have passed. Not having then, he
says, of them to repay, he granted to both. Did you see a loving lender, how
he lends, but does not take back? And how, being treated ungratefully, he does
not grow numb, but his hand is stretched out to those who ask? Not having, he
says, of them to repay, he granted to both. He forgave when they did not have,
not when they did not wish; for it is one thing not to have and another not to
wish. As I mean something of this sort: God seeks nothing from us but
repentance, hence he wishes us always to rejoice and to run to repentance. If
then when we wish to repent the multitude of sins shows our repentance without
strength, we do not pay the debt not from not wishing, but from not having.
For this reason then he says: Not having of them, that he may show that seeing
them wishing to repay the debt through repentance, but not being able because
of the multitude of sins, as loving he forgave them, freeing the debtor of the
debt not from deed but from choice. Not having then of them to repay, neither
scourging, nor racking, nor handing over to insult, he granted to both. Who
then ought to love him more? And answering, Simon said: I suppose that he to
whom he granted the more. See the Pharisee's cleverness; as he saw himself
already being shut out from the truth by the word, he has put "I suppose,"
fearing to give a complete answer. But the Lord was not recording his mind,
but snatching the answer says to him: You have answered rightly. And turning
toward the woman he said to Simon: Do you see this woman, the sinful one, the
one forbidden to you, but being saved for me? I entered into your house (for
the house is yours and not mine, full of abuse); you did not give water upon
my feet, feet that were dusted for your sake, that endured toil that I might
free from toils those who toil and are heavy-laden. You offered the honor by
half, having marveled at the things above, but not serving the things below.
But you did not give water for my feet, but she, sending forth springs of
tears from the eyelids, wiped away the filth of her sin. You did not give me a
kiss, as would that Judas had not either, who worked the betrayal by a kiss;
but she from the time she entered has not ceased kissing my feet. You did not
anoint my head with oil, for let not the oil of a sinner grease my head. For
how were you going to honor the head, you who neglected the feet? But she even
anointed my feet prophetically with myrrh; For myrrh, it says, poured out is
your name; poured out, not spilled. For since the vessel of the Jewish mind
was unsound, for this reason from your alabaster-case the myrrh was poured out
upon my feet, that through me the grace of the fragrance might be channeled to
the nations. For which reason I say to you: Her many sins have been forgiven;
because you, having received me under the same roof, neither honored me with a
kiss nor served me with an anointing, but she, having received amnesty of many
evils, honored me from tears and myrrhs, mixing as it were a twofold
mixing-bowl. Let us then bless the human being who covered the evils of Eve,
the sinful woman, the harlot, the procurer of good things, who displayed a
manner of repentance and laid bare a law of lovingkindness, who had the Judge
himself as advocate, who by tears conquered the lament of the judgment. As
many then as are present, be zealous for what you have heard, and imitate not
the

pleasure of the harlot, but the lament. For the pleasure generated the
lament, but the lament procured the release of the evils. Wash then your body
not with waters, but with tears; anoint the members not with myrrhs, but with
chastity. Put on not the weavings of the Seres, but the incorruptible garment
of temperance, that you may obtain the same glory, sending up thanks to the
Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world, to whom be the glory and the
honor, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages
of ages. Amen.
