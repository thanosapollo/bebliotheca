* Homily I on Holy Mary the Theotokos
:PROPERTIES:

:END:

Of Hesychius, presbyter of Jerusalem, on holy Mary the Theotokos
1 Every well-disposed tongue fittingly greets the Virgin also as Theotokos
and imitates Gabriel, the prince of the angels, according to its power. And
the one says "Hail" to her, while the other addresses her with "the Lord from
you," because of the Lord who was born of her and who appeared in flesh to the
race of men. This one names her Mother of light, that one Star of life,
another addresses her as Throne of God, another as a Temple greater than
heaven, another as a Seat not less than the seat of the cherubim, another
again as an unsown Garden, fertile, uncultivated, a Vine rich in clusters,
flourishing, untouched, a pure Turtle-dove, an undefiled Dove, a Cloud that
conceives the rain without corruption, a Purse whose pearl is brighter than
the sun, a Mine from which the stone that covers the whole earth comes forth
with none quarrying it, a Merchant-ship full of cargo and needing no
helmsman, a wealth-making Treasure. And others likewise call her a mouthless
Lampstand lighting itself from itself, an Ark broader than Noah's, longer,
more glorious; that one was an ark of living creatures, this one of life;
that one of perishable animals, this one of immortal life; that one bore
Noah, this one bore Noah's Maker; that one possessed second and third
stories, this one the whole fullness of the Trinity, since the Spirit came to
dwell, and the Father overshadowed, and the Son tabernacled within, being
carried in the womb. "For the Holy Spirit," he says, "shall come upon you,
and the power of the Most High shall overshadow you. Therefore also the holy
one who is born shall be called Son of God." You see how great and of what
stature is the dignity of the Virgin Theotokos. For the only-begotten Son of
God, the Maker of the world, was carried by her as an infant, and he remade
Adam and sanctified Eve and was abolishing the dragon and opening paradise
and securing the seal of the womb. Both things most fittingly and according
to reason: for he was opening paradise, because he was about to bring in the
thief and to lead by the hand into it all the heirs of the kingdom. He was
securing the seal of the womb, because the one being incarnate, being God and
Word, had no need at all of a door for entrance or for exit.
2 To you then, O Virgin, the prophets divide the praises, and each of the
God-bearing ones speaks as many mysteries of the wonders as he was entrusted
with. And one calls you the Rod of Jesse, that he may hint at the unharmed
and unbending quality of virginity. And another likens you to the bush that
was burning and was not consumed, that he may hint at the flesh of the
Only-begotten and the Virgin Theotokos; for she was burning and was not
consumed, inasmuch as she gave birth and did not open the womb, she conceived
and did not corrupt the matrix, she brought forth the infant and left the
belly sealed, she supplied milk and kept the breasts untouched, she carried a
child and did not know the child's father, she became a mother and did not
become a bride. A son was nursed and a father was not found; the field bore
fruit and the fruit possessed no farmer, it yielded a harvest and received no
seed; a river ran and the spring was closed on every side, as a sign that you
both became a mother and did not suffer the things of mothers; you gave birth
as a woman and were not corrupted as a woman; you carried according to the
law of nature, because you awaited the appointed time of the pangs; yet you
also conceived contrary to the law of nature. Another addressed you as a Gate
shut, yet lying in the east, and bringing in the king while the doors were
shut. So also bringing him out. He has called you a Gate, because you became
a door of the present life for the Only-begotten; a Gate lying in the east,
because "the true light that enlightens every man coming into the world"
comes forth from your womb as from some royal bridal-chamber. You brought the
king in while the doors were shut, but you also brought him out again; for
neither when he was conceived, nor at all when he was being born, did the
King of glory open the doors of your womb, nor did he loosen the bonds of
virginity. He who is the bridegroom from you named you "a garden enclosed"
and "a fountain sealed"; he foretold in the Song "a garden enclosed," because
the sickle of corruption or of vintage did not touch you, yet the flower from
the rod of Jesse he presents purely to the race of men and purely, cultivated
for you by the pure and undefiled Spirit alone; "a fountain sealed," because
a river of life going forth from you filled the inhabited world, yet a
marital branch did not draw from your fountain.
3 Concerning you David does not cease harping, he who is from you: "Arise, O
Lord, into your rest, you and the ark of your holiness." "Arise": From where?
From the bosom of the Father, not that you may be separated from the Father
(for this is not lawful either to think or to say), but that you may
accomplish the economy which was predetermined for you from above before the
ages and before the generations. "Arise," that you may raise those who have
fallen, that you may set upright those who have been tripped, that you may
take up your own possession, which until now is tyrannized by the enemy.
"Arise, O Lord, into your rest," which you appointed upon the earth and
assigned in Bethlehem, the cave and the manger and the swaddling-clothes; for
in the heavens you have no need of rest, but you yourself are the rest of all
creation; yet on earth you suffer the things of the flesh for our sake. For
did you not endure hunger and thirst and toil? Yet even while hungering you
are the bread of life, and while thirsting you yourself are the consolation
of those who thirst (for you have been established as a river of
incorruption), and while toiling in the journeys of the dry land you walk the
waves of the sea without toil. "Arise, O Lord, into your rest, you and the
ark of your holiness": the Virgin, the Theotokos, it is quite clear. For if
you are a pearl, fittingly she is an ark; since you are the sun, the Virgin
will necessarily be called heaven; since you are an unfading flower, then the
Virgin is a plant of incorruption, a paradise of immortality.
4 Unto whom Isaiah fittingly proclaimed these things beforehand from God,
saying: "Behold, the virgin shall conceive in the womb and shall bear a son,
and they shall call his name Emmanuel." "Behold, the virgin": Which one? The
chosen among women, the exceptional among virgins, the solemn ornament of our
nature, the boast of our clay, she who freed Eve from shame and Adam from the
threat, she who cut off the boldness of the dragon; whose desire's smoke did
not touch her, nor did the worm of sensual pleasure harm her. "Behold, the
virgin shall conceive in the womb": From where, O prophet? "I do not say," he
says; "for this has been reserved for Gabriel. Many divide the wonders of the
Theotokos, yet many of them too have been left aside; but I have been
entrusted with the birth of the ever-virgin, Micah with the place in which
the wonder was done, David with the time; for he himself placed 'From the
womb before the morning star I begot you' in the prophecy of the psalms.
Gabriel then shall say the from-where; for he comes down from heaven, he is
sent to the Virgin for this very thing, he is present at the time of the
birth and is entrusted of necessity with interpreting the substance of the
birth. But if you wish to learn even from me, go on to what follows in the
prophecy: And she shall bear a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel."
What is Emmanuel? "God with us." Is God then conceived from marriage and
corruption, and can he be born or incarnate from a man and a marriage-bed? By
no means, but from the energy of God, from the visitation of the Most High,
from the presence of the Spirit.
5 Therefore when the Virgin was saying to Gabriel: "How shall this be to me,
since I know not a man?" that is: "The wonder astounds me, the word strikes
me with amazement," and the angel's oracle puts the Virgin beside herself.
"You speak wonders, but to me, because of their strangeness, they are
suspect. I fear the novelty of the good news; I am troubled at the extreme
unusualness of what is said. 'How shall this be to me, since I know not a
man?' How shall I be a mother, not having become a bride? I have not
corrupted the flower of the season, I have not loosed the girdle of
virginity, I have not slackened the bond of the womb, I have not undone the
seal of the belly." And the angel immediately added: "The Holy Spirit shall
come upon you, and the power of the Most High shall overshadow you," that is:
"If you knew a man, you would not have borne God, who was well pleased to
take the form of a servant from you, for your sake, without corruption; you
would not have become the instrument of the incarnation of him who brought
all things into being. But since you are pure of the marriage-bed, since you
have kept the temple incorrupt and the tent free of every stain, both the
Father comes to dwell with you, and the Spirit overshadows, and the
Only-begotten is born of you with flesh. Do you then in no way hesitate at
the mysteries that follow; do not hesitate, giving birth in a cave to the
Craftsman of all creation; do not hesitate, seating in a manger him who sits
upon the backs of the cherubim, having no place in the inn and yet being
mother of the King of things above and of things below."
6 For he willingly becomes poor from you, that he may both bridle the rich,
and become for the poor a ground of endurance and a teacher of thanksgiving.
He is willingly laid in the manger, that those who are beast-like may run to
it and find the Word sitting upon the manger, that they may take up from him
the dignity of reason, that they may find wheat when they expected chaff,
that they may run as cattle, thinking to find handfuls of barley on the
manger, and may eat the true bread, the food of life, the banquet of light,
the meat of joy, the sweetness of incorruption, from which comes the
knowledge of the kingdom, the earnest of adoption, the inheritance of the
heavens, the communion of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit:
to him be the glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
