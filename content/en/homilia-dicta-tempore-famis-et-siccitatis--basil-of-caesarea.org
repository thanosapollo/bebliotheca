HOMILY DELIVERED IN TIME OF FAMINE AND DROUGHT.

"The lion shall roar, and who will not be afraid? The Lord God has spoken, and
who will not prophesy?" Let us make the prelude of the discourse prophetic,
and let us take as a fellow-worker for the need of the matters at hand the
God-bearing Amos, who healed passions like the evils that now distress us,
=31.305= setting forth the counsel and judgment concerning the things that are
profitable. For this prophet himself, in the course of former times, when the
people had abandoned the ancestral piety, and had trampled the exactness of
the laws, and had slipped into the service of idols, became a herald of
repentance, exhorting them to conversion and bringing forward the threat of
the punishments. And let it be granted me in prayer to make use, up to a
point, of the zeal of the ancient history. Yet may I no longer see also the
outcome of the things that then happened as following upon us. For the people,
having disobeyed, and like a hard and unmanageable colt that has taken the bit
in its teeth, was not drawn toward what is profitable; but turning aside from
the straight way, it ran so far in disorder, and raged against the charioteer,
until, falling into the pits and the precipices, it suffered a destruction
worthy of its disobedience.

Which may it not happen now upon us, my children, whom I begot through the
Gospel, whom I swaddled through the blessing of the hands. But let there be a
well-disposed hearing, an obedient soul, receiving the exhortations softly,
yielding to the speaker as wax to the one who seals, so that through this one
diligence I too may receive a fruit of my labors that gladdens, and you, in
the time of deliverance from the terrors, may praise the exhortation that was
given. What then is it that the discourse indicates, and still holds the souls
in suspense with the hope of hearing, delaying to make public what is
expected? We see the heaven, brethren, closed, bare, and cloudless, making
this clear weather gloomy, and grieving us by the very purity which we greatly
desired at first, when, having been covered for a long time by the clouds, it
made us dark and sunless. And the earth, dried out to the utmost, is
unpleasant to look upon, barren for farming and unfruitful, split into
fissures, and receiving the shining ray into the depth. And springs have
failed us, both rich and ever-flowing, and the streams of great rivers have
been spent, and the smallest children ford them, and women cross with burdens;
and for many of us even the drink has failed, and we have come to be in want
of the means of living. New Israelites, seeking a new Moses, and a rod that
works wonders, so that again rocks, being struck, =31.308= may heal the need
of the thirsty people, and strange clouds may sprinkle down upon men the
unaccustomed food, the manna. Let us take care that we not become for those
born later a novel tale of famine and punishment. I saw the fields, and I wept
much at their barrenness; and I poured out a lament, because rain was not
poured out upon us. Some of the seeds dried up before the sprouting, remaining
in the clods such as the plow had hidden them; and others, having peeked out a
little and sprouted, were pitiably withered by the heat; so that now someone
may fittingly invert the Gospel saying, saying: "The laborers indeed are many,
but the harvest is not even little." And the farmers, sitting upon the fields,
and folding their hands upon their knees (this being the posture of those who
mourn), weep over their vain labors, looking toward infant children and
wailing; gazing toward their wives and lamenting, touching and feeling the dry
stalks of the crops, and groaning loudly, just as fathers who have lost sons
in the flower of age.

Let it be said then also to us by the same prophet, of whom we made mention a
little before in the prelude: "And I," he says, "withheld the rain from you
three months before the harvest; and I will rain upon one city, and upon one
city I will not rain; and one portion shall be rained upon, and the portion
upon which I will not rain shall be dried up. And two or three cities shall be
gathered into one, to drink water, and they shall not be filled; because you
have not turned back to me, says the Lord." Let us learn then that through
turning away and neglect God inflicts these blows upon us, not seeking to wipe
us out, but hastening to set us right, after the manner of the kind among
fathers and the careless among children, who are angry at the young and rise
up, not hastening to give some evil, but leading them from childish neglect
and the sins of youth into care. See then how the multitude of our sins has
even driven the seasons out of their own natures, and has changed the forms of
the times into unnatural mixtures.

Winter did not have the customary wet together with the dry, but bound and
dried out every moisture with ice, and continued without share of snows and
rains. Spring again showed the one part of its properties, I mean the heat;
but it did not have the participation of the wet. And heat and frost, newly
overstepping the bounds of creation, and wickedly agreeing together for the
harm against us, drive men from livelihood =31.309= and from life. What then
is the cause of the disorder and the confusion? What is this innovation of the
seasons? Let us search, as those who have mind; as rational, let us reason. Is
it that he who steers the whole is not? Has the master-craftsman God forgotten
his economy? Has he been deprived of authority and power? Or does he have the
same strength, and has not been loosed from his might, but has been carried
away into hardness, and has changed the exceeding goodness and the care for us
into hatred of men? No one of sound mind would say so; but the causes are
clear and evident, on account of which we are not administered in the
customary way. We receive, and we do not give to others; we praise the
benefaction, and we deprive those in need of it. Being slaves, we are set
free, and we do not pity our fellow-slaves; being hungry we are fed, and we
pass by the needy. Having God as an unfailing provider and steward, we have
become stingy and without communion toward the poor. Our sheep are
many-bearing, and the naked are more than the sheep. The storehouses are
cramped by the multitude of the things stored, and we do not pity the one who
is cramped. For this the just tribunal threatens us. For this God also does
not open his hand, because we have shut out brotherly love. For this the
fields are dry, because love has grown cold. The voice of those who make
litany cries in vain, and is scattered toward the air. For we too have not
heard those who ask. And what is our prayer and our petition? You men, except
a few, are busy with tradings; you women, you serve them in the work of
mammon. Few then remain with me also for the prayer, and these dizzy, yawning,
turning about continually, and watching when the psalmist will complete the
verses, when they will be taken away, as from a prison, from the church and
from the necessity of the prayer.

And these smallest children, who have laid aside the tablets in the schools
and are crying out with us, pursue the matter rather as a relaxation and a
delight, making a feast of our grief, because they are freed for a little from
the burden of the teacher and the care of the lessons. But the multitude of
grown men, and the people held in sins, runs through the city unrestrained,
and taking liberty, and bright-faced; the one who carries about in his soul
the cause of the evils; the one who set the disaster in motion and worked it.
And unperceiving and blameless infants hurry and are gathered to the
confession, having neither the occasion of the things that grieve, nor
knowledge or power of praying in the accustomed way. =31.312= Come you into
the midst for me, you who are mixed up with sins; you fall down and weep and
groan; let the infant do the things that belong to its age. Why do you hide
when you are accused, and put forward the unaccountable as a defense? Is the
judge being played with, that you should bring in a substituted person? That
one too ought to be present with you altogether, not only. Do you see how the
Ninevites also, entreating God by repentance, and mourning over the sins which
Jonah cried out after the sea and the whale, did not set forward the infants
alone into the repentance, while they themselves lived their life in luxury,
they themselves in feasting; but first the fasting tamed the fathers who had
sinned, and the punishment afflicted the fathers, and as a matter of addition
the infants were forced to lament, so that the gloom might prevail through
every age, both perceiving and unperceiving; the one by choice, the other by
necessity.

And so God, seeing them humbled, as having condemned themselves to a misery
exceeding themselves, a whole race together, both pitied the suffering, and
relaxed the punishment, and granted joy to those who had mourned with good
perception. O the well-tuned repentance! O the wise and dense affliction! They
did not even leave the irrational creatures unprofitable to the punishment;
but they contrived that even these should cry out of necessity. For the calf
was separated from the cow, the lamb was driven from the mother's teat, a
nursing child was not in the arms of her who bore it; the mothers in their own
folds, the children in their own; and pitiable voices from all crying against
one another and echoing back. The children, starving, sought the fountains of
milk; the mothers, cut through by the natural passion, called back the
offspring with sympathetic voices. The infants, starving in the like manner,
were broken with most violent weeping and gasped; the mothers were pierced in
the inward parts by the natural pains. And for this the God-inspired word has
preserved their repentance in writing as a common school of life. The old man
among them lamented, and plucked and tore his gray hair. The young man, and
one in his prime, =31.313= wailed more violently; the poor man groaned; the
rich man, forgetting luxury, practiced hardship as a thing of self-control.
Their king changed the brightness and the glory into shame. He laid aside the
crown, and poured dust upon his head; having thrown off the purple, he changed
into sackcloth; having left the high and lifted-up throne, he was dragged
pitiably upon the earth; having left the peculiar and kingly ease, he lamented
together with the people; he became one of the many, when he saw the common
Master of all angry. This is the mind of well-perceiving slaves. Such is the
repentance of those held in sins. But we accomplish the sin intensely, and
take up the repentance carelessly and sluggishly. Who, having prayed, lets a
tear fall, that he may receive rain and timely drops? Who, wiping out sins,
after the imitation of the blessed David, wet his bed with lamentations? Who
washed the feet of strangers, and rinsed off the dust of the journey, that in
time of need he might entreat God, seeking a release from drought? Who fed the
child bereft of a father, so that now God may feed the grain for us, which is
being cuffed as an orphan by the intemperance of the winds? Who tended a widow
ill-treated by the difficulties of life, so that now the necessary food may be
measured out? Tear up an unjust bond, that thus the sin may be loosed. Wipe
out the acknowledgment of heaviest usuries, that the earth may bear the
customary things. For while bronze and gold and the unfruitful things are
generating contrary to nature, she who bears according to nature becomes
barren, and has been condemned to unfruitfulness for the punishment of those
who dwell upon her. Let those then who honor greed, who gather wealth with
excess, show what is the power of the things stored up, or what the profit, if
God being angry should drag out the punishment still further.

Those who heap this up will perhaps become paler than the gold, if they do not
have the bread, which until yesterday and the day before was despised because
of the ready abundance of plenty. Grant that there be no one selling, nor that
grain exist in storehouses; and what is the use of the heaviest purses? Tell
me. Will you not be buried with those? Is not the gold earth? Will not useless
mud lie beside the mud of the body? You have acquired all things, and you do
not have one necessary thing, the power of feeding yourself. With all your
wealth produce one cloud; invent a way for a few drops; hasten the earth into
fruit-bearing; loose the disaster with the proud and swaggering wealth.
Perhaps you will call upon some of the devout, that by prayers, like Elijah
the Tishbite, he may grant you a relaxation of the terrors, a man without
possessions, pale, unshod, without a house, without a hearth, without
resource, covered with one tunic, as Elijah =31.316= with the sheepskin,
having prayer as a companion, self-control as a housemate.

And if, being in need, you obtain the help from such a one, will you not laugh
long at the much-cared-for possessions? Will you not spit upon the gold? Will
you not scatter as dung the silver, which formerly, naming it all-powerful and
most dear, you recognized as a powerless helper in the time of need? For you
he also condemned this disaster, because having you did not give; because you
passed by those who hungered; because you did not turn toward those who
wailed; because being worshipped you did not pity. Evils come even through a
few upon a people, and a people has had a share of some wickedness. Achan
committed sacrilege, and the whole array was scourged. Zimri again fornicated
with the Midianite women, and Israel fell into judgment. Let us all then both
privately and in common examine our own lives; let us attend to the drought as
to a pedagogue, reminding each of his own sin. Let us also say, with good
perception, the voice of the noble Job: "The hand of the Lord is it that has
touched me." And first of all let us reckon our disaster as belonging
originally to our sins. But if we must add also another thing, there are times
when such misfortunes of life are also brought upon men as a trial for souls,
so that in the difficulty the proven may be tested, whether poor or rich; for
both are accurately tried through endurance. And in this time especially they
are shown, the one, whether he is communicative and brother-loving, the other,
whether he is thankful and not, on the contrary, blasphemous, swiftly changing
his mind with the turns of life. I know many (not having learned by hearing,
but having come to know the men by experience), so long as life is measured
out for them toward plenty, and goes, as the saying is, with a fair wind,
acknowledging the grace to the benefactor moderately at least, even if not
perfectly; but if somewhere through the contrary condition the affairs should
be overturned, and the rich man should become poor, and the strength of the
body disease, and the glory and distinction shame and dishonor, being
ungrateful, speaking blasphemous things, shrinking from the prayer;
complaining against God as against a debtor who delays, not being disposed as
toward a Master who is angry. But away with such a thought from the
reasonings! And when you see God not granting the customary things, reason
thus with yourself: Is God unable to provide the food? And how? He who is Lord
of heaven and of all the ordering, wise steward of seasons =31.317= and times,
governor of all things, having appointed the seasons and the tropes to yield
to one another as a well-ordered chorus, so that by their own variety they may
suffice for our different needs; and now at the fitting time the wet may be
added, and again the hot may take its turn, and the cold may be mixed into the
year, and we may not lack the need of the dry. God then is able; and the able
being present and confessed, has the good perhaps failed? This argument too
does not stand. For what necessity persuaded the one who is not good to create
man in the beginning? And who pressed the Creator, even when he did not wish,
to take dust, and to form such beauty from clay? Who persuaded him by
necessity to grant to man the reason after his own image, so that starting
from there he might receive the learning of arts, and might learn to
philosophize concerning the highest things, which he does not touch by sense?
And so, reasoning thus, you find the goodness accompanying God, and even until
now not being left behind. Since what hindered, tell me, that what is seen
should not be drought, but a complete conflagration? and, having shifted the
sun a little from its customary course, and having drawn near to the bodies
about the earth, in an instant to burn up everything that is seen? or to rain
fire from heaven, after the likeness of the punishments of sinners already
given? Come to yourself and to your own reasonings, O man; do not do the
things of foolish children, who, having been rebuked by a teacher, tear up his
tablets; and when a father, for their benefit, has put off the food, they rend
the garment, or they scratch the mother's face with their nails. For the storm
tests the pilot, and the stadium the athlete, the battle-line the general, the
disaster the great-souled, and temptation tests and tries the Christian. And
griefs prove the soul, as fire the gold. Are you poor? Do not be downcast. For
excessive dejection becomes a cause of sin; the grief baptizing the mind, and
producing a dizziness of helplessness, and the want of reasonings generating
the unthankfulness. But have the hope toward God.

Does he not look upon the straitness? He has the food in his hands, and he
delays the giving, that he may test your steadfastness, that he may learn the
mind, whether it is not like that of the unrestrained and the ungrateful. For
those too, so long as the food is in the mouth, speak well, flatter,
super-admire; but when the table has been put off a little, they strike with
blasphemies as with stones those whom a little before they worshipped as equal
to God because of the pleasure. Go through the Old Testament, the New, and you
will find in each many who were fed in different ways. Carmel had Elijah, a
high and uninhabited mountain, a desert a desert man; and the soul was for the
=31.320= righteous man all things, and the provision of life was the hope
toward God. And living thus, he did not exchange life for famine; but the most
rapacious and especially gluttonous of the birds, these brought the food, and
became ministers of nourishment to the righteous man, they who by custom
snatch the foods of others; and by the master's command they changed their
nature, becoming faithful guardians of the loaves and the meats. And we have
learned from the spiritual history that the ravens brought these things to the
man. And the Babylonian pit also had the Israelite youth; a captive as to the
disaster, but free as to the soul and the mind. And what were the things from
those? The lions, contrary to nature, fasted; and his feeder Habakkuk was
carried through the air, the angel conveying the man together with the meats;
and so that a righteous man might not be pressed by famine, the prophet was
carried over so much earth and sea in a small portion of time, as much as is
spread from Judea as far as Babylon. And what again of the people of the
desert, of whom Moses was guardian? How was their life administered in forty
years? There was no man sowing there, no ox drawing a plow, no
threshing-floor, no winepress, no storehouse; and they had the food unsown and
unplowed, and a rock supplied the springs, not existing before, but having
burst forth at the need. I leave off enumerating one by one the things of the
providence of God, which he has often shown paternally upon men. But you
endure a little upon the disaster, as the noble Job, and do not be overturned
by the surge, nor throw away any of the cargoes of virtue that you carry.
Preserve thanksgiving upon the soul as a most precious deposit, and you too
will receive of the thanksgiving the double of the luxury.

Remember the apostolic saying: "In everything give thanks." Are you poor? You
have altogether another poorer. You have food for ten days, he for one. As a
good and well-disposed man, equalize your surplus toward the needy. Do not
hesitate to give from the little; do not prefer your own advantage out of the
common danger. And if the food should be reduced to one loaf, and the one who
asks should stand at the doors, bring forth the one from the store, and having
set it upon the hands, having stretched them toward heaven, speak a word both
pitiable and well-disposed: "One loaf, which you see, Lord, and the danger is
plain, but I set your commandment before myself, and from the little I give to
the starving brother; do you then also give to the servant who is in danger. I
know your goodness, I am confident also in the power; you do not put off the
graces to a time, but you scatter the gifts when you will." And if you speak
and act thus, the loaf which you give from the straitness becomes a seed of
farming, it generates the fruit many-fold, an earnest of the food, a procurer
of mercy. Speak also =31.321= you the word of the Sidonian widow in like
things, remember the history fittingly: "As the Lord lives, I have this only
in my house for nourishment for me and for the children."

And if you give from what is lacking, you too will have the cruse of oil
overflowing by grace; the jar of meal unemptied. For upon the faithful the
generous grace of God imitates the wells, which are always being emptied and
not being drawn dry, bringing in the double in return. Lend, O destitute one,
to the rich God. Trust him who always receives into his own person on behalf
of the afflicted, and from his own house repays the grace. He is a trustworthy
surety, having the treasures spread everywhere of earth and sea. And even if
sailing you should demand the loan, you will receive upon the midst of the sea
the principal with interest. For he is generous in the additions. The disease
of the starving man, famine, is a pitiable passion. Famine is the chief of
human disasters, this a more wretched end than all deaths. For in the other
dangers, either the edge of a sword brings on the end swiftly, or the rush of
fire quickly quenches the living, or wild beasts, having torn with the teeth
whatever of the members is vital, do not allow one to be punished by the
dragging-out of the pain. But famine has the evil idle, the pain dragging on,
the disease sitting within and lurking, a death always present, and always
delaying. For it spends the moisture according to nature; it cools the heat;
it contracts the bulk; it withers the power little by little. The flesh lies
about the bones as a spider's web. The complexion has no bloom. For the red
flees, the blood being melted away; the white is not, the surface being
blackened by the leanness; and the body is livid, pitiably mixed from the
passion with paleness and the black; knees not supporting, but being dragged
by force. A voice thin and powerless; eyes weakening in the hollows, lying in
the sockets in vain, as the things frozen in the husks of the tree-fruits. A
belly empty and collapsed, and shapeless, having neither bulk nor the natural
tension of the inward parts, but plastered to the bones of the back. He then
who passes by such a body, of how many punishments is he worthy? And what
excess of cruelty will he leave remaining? And how is he not worthy to be
numbered with the untamed of the wild beasts, and to be seen as accursed and a
manslayer?

For he who has in his power the healing of the evil, and willingly puts it off
also toward greed, would reasonably be condemned in the same rank as those who
kill with their own hand. The passion of famine has often compelled many even
to move the =31.324= bounds of nature; for a man to touch the bodies of those
of the same race, and for a mother to receive again badly into the belly the
child whom she brought forth from the belly. And this drama a Jewish history
has tragically told, which the diligent Josephus wrote for us, when the
terrible sufferings overtook the people of Jerusalem, paying just punishments
of the impiety toward the Lord. You see even our God himself often passing by
the other passions, but being compassionately moved in the inward parts over
those who hunger: "For I have compassion," he says, "upon the crowd."
Therefore also in the last judgment, where the Lord calls the righteous, the
one who shares holds the first rank; the feeder is the first-stander of those
honored; the one who supplied the bread is called before all; the kind and
ready to share is sent on to life before the other righteous. The
uncommunicative and stingy is given over to the fire before all sinners. The
time calls you to the mother of the commandments; and take thought greatly,
that the time of the festival and of the dealings may not pass you by. For
time flows, and does not wait for the one who delays; the days press on, they
pass by the sluggish. And as it is not possible to stop the stream of a river,
unless someone, taking it up at the first encounter and onset, should use the
water for what is needed; so neither to hold back the time that is driven by
necessary circuits, nor, when it has passed, to undo it backward, unless
someone should take hold of it as it comes on. And for this seize the
commandment as it flees and accomplish it, and having taken hold, grasp it
from all sides in the arms. Give a few things, and acquire many; loose the
original sin by the sharing of food. For as Adam, having eaten badly, passed
on the sin, so we wipe out the treacherous eating, if we heal a brother's need
and famine. Peoples, hear; Christians, give ear; that thus says the Lord, not
haranguing with his own voice, =31.325= but sounding through the mouths of the
servants as through instruments. Let us who are rational not appear more
savage than the irrational. For those use the things that grow from the earth
from nature as in common. And flocks of sheep graze down one and the same
mountain; and horses in full multitude feed on one and the same plain; and all
things one by one thus yield to one another the necessary enjoyment of the
needs; but we, we take the common things into our bosom, we alone have the
things of the many. Let us be ashamed at the humane tales of the Greeks.

Among some of those there is a humane law, one table, and the foods in common,
making the many-personed people almost one hearth. Let us leave those outside,
and let us go over the example of the three thousand; let us emulate the first
array of the Christians; how all things were common to them, the life, the
soul, the agreement, the table common, an undivided brotherhood, love
unhypocritical, making the many bodies one; fitting the different souls into
one concord. You have many examples of brotherly love from the Old Testament
and from the New. If you see an old man starving, send for him and feed him,
as Joseph did Jacob. If you find an enemy in straits, do not add to the anger
that holds you the vengeance; but feed him as that one did the brothers who
sold him. If you meet a younger man worn down, weep thus, as that one did for
Benjamin the son of old age. Greed perhaps tries you also, as the mistress did
Joseph; it pulls you by the garments, that you may despise the commandment,
and may love more that gold-loving and ornament-loving thing rather than the
Master's command. When then a reasoning comes, warring against that
commandment, and drawing the self-controlled mind into love of money,
compelling you to neglect brotherly love, and holding you toward it, throw off
the garments you also, being angry withdraw; keep the faith for the Lord, as
that one did for Potiphar; administer the want in one year, as that one did in
the seven. Do not give all things to pleasure, give something also to the
soul. And consider that you have two daughters: the ease here, and the life in
the heavens. If you do not wish to give all things to the better, divide at
least equally between the unrestrained child and the self-controlled. Do not
show the sojourn here over-rich, and the other naked and clothed in rags, when
you must stand before Christ, and come into the sight of the judge, having the
form of a bride, and as a calling the life according to virtue. Do not then
present to the bridegroom the bride shapeless and unadorned, =31.328= lest
having looked he turn away the face, and having seen he hate, and deny the
union.

But having arrayed her with the fitting adornment, keep her beautiful for the
appointed time of the marriage; so that she too with the wise virgins may
light the lamp, having the fire of knowledge unquenched, and not lacking the
oil of the good deeds; so that the God-bearing prophecy may be confirmed by
works, and what was said may also fit your soul: "The queen stood at your
right hand, clothed in a garment of gold, embroidered." "Hear, daughter, and
see, and incline your ear, and the king shall desire your beauty." For these
things the Psalmist proclaimed beforehand in a general way, announcing the
beauty of the general body; but it will fit properly also to the soul of each,
if indeed the Church has its constitution from the things of each. Take
thought for me rationally of the present and the future, which you must not
betray for shameful gain. A body will leave you, your mark in this life. At
the appearing of the expected judge, who will come without doubt, you will
shut off from yourself the giving of the honors and the heavenly glory, and
you will open unquenchable fire, and gehenna, and places of punishment, and
bitter ages in pains, instead of a long and blessed life.

Do not think that I, like some mother or nurse, am shaking false bugbears at
you, as those are accustomed to do concerning the infant children; when they
wail in disorder and without end, they silence them through fabricated tales.
But these things are not a myth, but a word proclaimed beforehand by an
unlying voice. And know accurately according to the Gospel prediction, "one
iota or one tittle shall not pass away." But also the body that has vanished
in tombs shall rise, and the same soul that was shut out by death shall again
dwell in the body. And an accurate examination of the things that have been
lived shall be present, not with others witnessing, but with the conscience
itself witnessing. And to each the things according to worth shall be measured
back by the just judge. To him belongs glory, might, and worship, unto the
ages of ages. Amen.
