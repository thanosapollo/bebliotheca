* On the Narrow Gate and on the Lord's Prayer
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Concerning living according to God, and on the saying, Narrow is the gate, and
the rest; and an interpretation of the prayer, Our Father.
=51.41=
αʹ. The reading of every God-inspired Scripture becomes for those who attend a
knowledge of piety; but the august writing of the Gospels is an excellence of
higher teachings; for the sayings contained in them are ordinances of the most
high King. Wherefore also a certain fearful punishment has been threatened
against those who do not keep exactly the things said by him. For if he who
transgresses the laws of the rulers upon earth undergoes an inexorable
punishment, how much more shall he who sets aside the commandments of the
heavenly Master be given over to unbearable torments? Since therefore the
danger of inattention is great, let us attend with much exactness to the
hearing of the words just now read to us from the Gospel. And what are these?
Narrow, he says, is the gate, and straitened the way that leads unto life; and
few are they who find it. And again: Wide is the gate, and broad the way that
leads unto destruction, and many are they who go through it. Hearing these
words continually, and seeing the zeal of men toward vain things, I greatly
marvel at the truth of the things said. For all walk through the wide way, all
gape after the present affairs, and never take thought of the things to come;
but they press uninterruptedly toward bodily enjoyments, and they let the
souls waste away in famine; and receiving ten thousand wounds every day, they
never have perception of the evils in which they are; and for the sufferings
of the body they go to those who treat these things; and they even send for
these men themselves to their homes, and they provide wages as many as
possible, and they display much endurance, and they bear the laborious
treatment, that they may purchase its health; but when the soul is in a bad
state they neglect it altogether, and they do not hasten to receive its most
love-worthy health, although they know clearly that the body is mortal and
perishable, and resembles the flowers of spring; for like those it withers and
is quenched, and is given over to corruption; but they know the soul to have
been honored with immortality, and to have been made according to the divine
image, and to have been entrusted with the tiller of the living creature. For
what a charioteer is to a chariot, and a helmsman to a ship, and a musician to
an instrument, this the Fashioner legislated the soul to be to this earthly
vessel. For this holds the reins, and moves the rudders, and strikes the
strings, and doing this well, it strikes up the all-harmonious melody of
virtue; but when it either slackens the tones, or stretches them beyond what
is due, it injures both the art and the harmony. This then most men neglect,
and they do not even deem it worthy of a brief care, but they spend all the
time of their life =51.42= upon bodily concerns; And some embrace the life of
sailors, and fight with waves and winds, carrying life and death about with
themselves, and holding the hopes of safety in a few planks; and others take
up the sweat of husbandry, yoking ploughing oxen, and ploughing the earth, and
now sowing and reaping, now planting and gathering vintage, and all their time
journeys with such hardship; and others go after tradings, and for these they
make journeys on land and on sea, and they prefer the foreign land to their
own, and leaving fatherland, and race, and friends, and spouses together with
children, for the sake of a few gains they embrace the strange land. And why
must one enumerate all the crafts which men have invented for the needs of the
body, in which spending their days and nights they procure for themselves its
service, but they overlook the soul hungering and thirsting and parched and
filthy, and troubled by ten thousand evils? And after many sweats and toils
they do not even make the mortal body stronger than death, and they subject
the immortal together with the mortal to the immortal punishments.

βʹ. For this reason, greatly lamenting the ignorance poured around the souls
of men, and the thickness of the mist lying upon them, I wished to find some
high lookout showing me all the races of men; and I wished also to obtain a
voice sounding round all the ends, and sufficing for all upon the earth, and
to stand and cry out, and to shout that Davidic voice: Sons of men, how long
will you be heavy-hearted? why do you love vanity and seek falsehood,
preferring the things that perish to the heavenly? How long do you shut your
eyes, and stop your ears, and do not hear the divine voice, which cries every
day: Ask, and it shall be given you, seek, and you shall find, knock, and it
shall be opened to you; for everyone who asks receives, and he who seeks
finds, and to him who knocks it shall be opened? But since some, being more
imperfectly disposed and more inclined toward the things of this life, and
taking pleasure in flesh-loving reasonings, do not make their requests as is
fitting, for this cause the common Master showed us a teaching of prayer,
saying: When you pray, do not use vain repetitions, as the Gentiles do; for
they think that they will be heard in their much speaking; naming vain
repetition the babbling which is offered through many words, but is stripped
of all benefit. The Lord then, forbidding vain repetition, hints that those
who pray ought not to ask for the things that flow and perish; not beauty of
body, which is withered by time, and consumed by disease, and covered by
death; for such is bodily beauty. It is a short-lived flower, =51.43=
appearing for a little in the spring of youth, but after a little being
destroyed by the old age of time. And if one should wish to examine even its
substance, then he will be able to spit upon it still more. For it is nothing
else than phlegm and blood and flux and the juice of food that has been
chewed. For from this both eyes, and cheeks, and noses, and brows, and lips,
and the whole body is watered; and if ever the watering of these should fail,
the comeliness of the face will altogether fail together with it.

Not wealth of money which, after the likeness of river waters, both flows in
and flows away, and now leaps to this man, now to that, and flees those who
hold it, and does not endure to remain with those who love it, and has ten
thousand plotters, and moths, and robbers, and sycophants, and burnings, and
shipwrecks, and onsets of wars, and uprisings of peoples, and knaveries of
household slaves, and subtractions of documents, and additions, and
diminutions, and the other terrible things which grow upon those who love
money from the love of wealth. Not power of dignities; for many painful things
grow beside this also, consumptions of cares, continual sleeplessnesses, plots
from those who envy, devices from those who hate, the fluency of orators
stealing the truth with well-tongued words and procuring much danger for those
who judge. For there are, there are certain vain repeaters and empty-speakers,
asking these and such things from the God of all, and making no account of the
things that truly are good. And those who are sick do not teach the physician
the use of the drugs, but they only endure the things offered by him, even if
the manner of the treatment be laborious; and those who sail do not command
the helmsman to hold the tiller in such and such a way, and to steer the
vessel, but sitting upon the decks they endure his skill, not only being
carried with fair winds, but also enduring the last danger; but to God alone,
who knows exactly to give what is expedient for us, those whose minds are in a
bad state do not endure to yield, but they ask as beneficial the things that
are destructive, doing a thing like a sick man entreating the physician to
give him not what dissolves the disease, but what nourishes the matter that is
mother of the disease. But the physician will not endure the supplication of
the one who is ill, but even if he see him weeping and lamenting, he follows
the law of the art rather than being bent by this man's tears, and we name the
disobedience not inhumanity but loving-kindness; for yielding to the sick man,
and granting the things toward pleasure, he works the things of enemies
against him; but resisting him, and fighting the desire, he uses pity and
loving-kindness; so also the physician of our souls does not endure to give to
those who ask the things that will be to their harm. For neither do
affectionate fathers endure to stretch out to infants still quite young the
knives they seek or coals of fire; for they know clearly that such a gift is
harmful to them; but some of those who have fallen into the last unreason not
only ask beauty of body, and wealth, and power, and such things from the God
of all, but they also curse their enemies, and they entreat that some
punishment be brought upon them, and him whom they pray to be gentle and
loving toward them sel =51.44= ves, this one they wish to become ungentle and
inhuman toward their enemies. These things then the Master, checking
beforehand, both charges not to use vain repetitions; and he teaches what one
must say in the prayer, and in few words he trains all virtue; for those words
are not only a teaching of prayer, but also a training of a perfect life.

γʹ. And what these are, and what is their meaning, let us examine with much
exactness, and let us keep them securely as divine laws. Our Father, who art
in the heavens. O how great the excess of loving-kindness! O how great the
excellence of munificence! What word will suffice for thanksgiving to him who
pours forth such goods for us?

Consider, beloved, the cheapness of your nature and of mine, search out the
kinship, the earth, the dust, the clay, the brick, the ashes; for having been
formed from the earth, we dissolve again into the earth after the end. Having
thought these things then, be struck at the untraceable wealth of the much
goodness of God concerning us, that you were commanded to call him Father, the
earthly the heavenly, the mortal the immortal, the corruptible the
incorruptible, the temporary the eternal, he who yesterday and the day before
was clay, the God who is before the ages. But you were not taught in vain to
send forth this voice, but that, reverencing the naming of Father brought
forth by your tongue, you may imitate his goodness, as he also says elsewhere:
Become like your Father who is in the heavens, because he makes his sun to
rise upon evil and good, and rains upon just and unjust. For he cannot call
the loving God Father who has a savage and inhuman disposition; for he does
not preserve the characters of goodness in the heavenly Father, but he has
transformed himself into the savage form, and has fallen from the divine
nobility, according to what was said by David: Man being in honor did not
understand, he was compared to the senseless cattle, and was made like them.
For when one leaps like a bull, and kicks like an ass, and remembers injuries
like a camel, and is gluttonous like a bear, and snatches like a wolf, and
strikes like a scorpion, and is underhanded like a fox, and neighs after women
like a mare-mad horse, how can such a one send up the voice that befits a son,
and call God his Father? What then must such a one be named? A beast? But the
beasts are held by one of these defects; but this man, having gathered all in
himself, has become even more irrational than their irrationality. And why do
I say a beast? Such a one is more grievous than every beast. For those,
although they are wild by nature, having enjoyed human art, often become tame;
but this man, being a man, and having changed their wildness which is
according to nature into the tameness which is against nature, what defense
will he have, leading out his own meekness which is according to nature into
wildness which is against nature, and making the thing wild by nature tame,
but making himself who is tame by nature wild? and taming a lion, and making
it manageable, but constructing his own anger more savage than a lion? And yet
there, two hindrances exist, both that the beast has been deprived of reason,
and that it is more wrathful than all; but nevertheless through the wisdom
given by God he prevails even over the savage nature. And he who upon the
beasts conquers nature, upon himself loses together with nature also the good
of choice; and a lion =51.45= he makes a man, but he overlooks himself
becoming from a man a lion; and to that one he grants the things above nature,
but for himself he does not even procure the things according to nature.

How then will such a one be able to call God Father? But he who is gentle and
loving toward his neighbors, and does not avenge those who sin against him,
but repays the injustices with benefactions, is not condemned when he calls
God Father. And attend to the exactness of the word, how it legislates for us
mutual love, and joins all into a loving disposition. For he did not command
to say, My Father, who art in the heavens, but, Our Father, who art in the
heavens, that having been taught to have a common Father, we may show a
brotherly goodwill toward one another. Then teaching us to leave the earth and
the things about earth, and not to gape below, but to take the wings of faith
and to fly up through the air, and to pass through the ether and to seek the
one called Father, he commanded us to say: Our Father, who art in the heavens;
not because God is only in the heavens, but that he may prepare us who are
rolling below about the earth to look up into the heavens, and having shone
around us with the beauty of the heavenly goods, may transfer all our desire
there.

δʹ. Then he added a second saying, saying: Hallowed be thy name. And let no
one foolishly suppose that he grants an addition of sanctification to God in
saying: Hallowed be thy name; for he is holy, and all-holy, and most holy of
holy ones. And the Seraphim offer this hymnody to him, crying with unsilenced
cries, Holy, holy, holy Lord of Sabaoth, heaven and earth are full of his
glory. But just as those who offer acclamations to kings, and call them kings
and autocrats, do not grant what they do not have, but they acclaim what they
have; so also we do not offer to God the holiness that is not, saying,
Hallowed be thy name, but we glorify that which is; for Hallowed be has been
said instead of Glorified be. We are taught then through this voice to pursue
the life according to virtue, that men, seeing this, may glorify our heavenly
Father; which he also says elsewhere: Let your light shine before men, that
they may see your good works, and glorify your Father who is in the heavens.
After this we were taught to say: Thy kingdom come; for being tyrannized by
the passions of the body, and receiving ten thousand assaults of temptations,
we have need of the kingdom of God, that sin may not reign in our mortal body
so as to obey it in its desires, nor may we present our members as weapons of
unrighteousness to sin, but that we may present weapons of righteousness to
God, and may serve as soldiers to the king of the ages. And we are taught
besides these things not to be very much glued to the present life, but to
despise the present things, and to desire the things to come as remaining, and
to seek that heavenly and eternal kingdom, and not to be held by the pleasant
things here, not by comeliness of bodies, not by abundance of money, not by
plenty of possessions, not by costliness of stones, not by magnificence of
houses, not by gover =51.46= norships and generalships, not by purple and
diadem, not by cookeries and seasonings and all kinds of luxuries, not by any
other of the things that bait our senses, but having said farewell to all
these, to reach uninterruptedly after the kingdom of God. Thus having taught
us this virtue also, he commanded us to say: Thy will be done, as in heaven,
also upon the earth; for having put into us the love of the things to come,
and the desire of the heavenly kingdom, and having wounded us with that
longing, he prepares us to say: Thy will be done, as in heaven, also upon the
earth. Grant us, he says, Master, to imitate the polity in heaven, that what
you yourself will, we also may will. Therefore succor a choice that is weary,
and that desires to do your things, but is hindered by the weakness of the
body; stretch out a hand to those who press to run, but have been forced to
limp. The soul is winged, but the flesh weighs this down; that one is swift
toward the heavenly things, but this one is slow toward the earthly; but when
your help is present, even the things greatly impossible will be possible. Let
thy will then be done, as in heaven, also upon the earth.

εʹ. And since he mentioned the earth, and there is need for those who have
been born from it and dwell in it, and who wear an earth-born body, also of
the corresponding food, he necessarily added: Give us this day our daily
bread. He commanded to ask for daily bread, not luxury, but nourishment, that
which fills up what is lacking of the body, and prevents the death from
famine; not tables swelling, nor varieties of meats, and tricks of cooks, and
devices of bakers, and flower-scented wines, and the other things which
sweeten the throat, but load the belly, and darken the mind, and prepare the
body to leap against the soul, and make the colt hard to rein for the
charioteer.

The word did not teach us to ask these things, but Daily bread, that is, that
which passes over to the substance of the body, and is able to hold this
together. And this too we were not commanded to ask for a great number of
years, but only that which suffices for us today; For do not be anxious, he
says, for the morrow. For why does anyone take thought concerning the morrow,
who will not altogether see the morrow, but receives the toil, and does not
reap the fruit? Take courage in God who gives food to all flesh. He who has
given you the body, and breathed in the soul, and constructed you a rational
living creature, and prepared all the goods for you before your forming, how
will he overlook you after you have been formed, He who makes his sun to rise
upon evil and good, and rains upon just and unjust? Taking courage then in
this one, ask only the daily food, and leave to him the care of the morrow, as
also the blessed David said: Cast upon the Lord your care, and he himself will
nourish you. Thus having trained the highest philosophy through the things
said, knowing that it is of the impossible things, being men and wearing a
mortal body, not to stumble, he taught us to say: And forgive us our debts, as
we also forgive our debtors. Three goods according to him he works through
this word; those who are =51.47= extreme in virtue he teaches moderation of
mind, and he charges them not to take courage in their achievements, but to
fear and tremble and to remember the former sins; as also the divine Paul
does, after ten thousand achievements saying: That Jesus Christ came into the
world to save sinners, of whom I am first; he did not say, I was, but, I am,
showing that he had unceasing memory of the things he had done. For those then
who are extreme in virtue he contrived through these words a safety from
humility; but those who have stumbled after the grace of holy baptism he does
not let despair of their own salvation, but he teaches them to ask from the
physician of souls the drugs of forgiveness. And besides these things, the
word also proposes a teaching of loving-kindness. For he wishes us to be
gentle toward those who are liable, unremembering of injuries toward those who
sin against us, and by the pardon concerning these to grant pardon to
ourselves, and ourselves to bring in beforehand the measures of
loving-kindness. For so much we ask to receive, as we provide to our
neighbors, and of so great a pardon we deem ourselves worthy to obtain, as we
grant to those who owe. Besides these we were commanded to say, And lead us
not into temptation, but deliver us from the evil one. For many grievous
things come to us from diabolic working, and many also from men, either openly
=51.48= outraging, or plotting unseen. And the body sometimes rising up
against the soul works a grievous harm; and sometimes falling into all kinds
of sicknesses it brings upon us pains and distresses. Since then there are
many and different grievous things falling upon us from many places, we were
taught to ask from the God of all the release from these. For when he defends,
every storm is quenched, and the surge is changed into a calm, and the evil
one withdraws having been put to shame, just as once leaving the men he went
against the swine, not having dared to do even that without the one who
commanded. And he who does not have authority against swine, how will he be
able to prevail over men who are awake and sober, and guarded by God, and who
hold him as their own king? For this reason also at the end of the prayer he
showed the kingdom and the power and the glory of God, saying: For thine is
the kingdom and the power and the glory unto the ages. Amen. For these things,
he says, I ask from you, because I know you to be king of all, possessing
eternal might, and able to do all things whatsoever you will, and possessing
glory that cannot be taken away. And for all these things let us give thanks
to him who has deemed us worthy of so many goods, because to him belongs all
glory, honor and might, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit,
now and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
