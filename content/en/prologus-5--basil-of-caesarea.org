ASCETIC DISCOURSE OF THE SAME.
The ascetic life has one aim, the salvation of the soul, and it is necessary
to guard with fear every purpose that is able to cooperate toward it, as a
divine commandment. For not even the commandments of God themselves look
toward anything else, except toward the one who obeys being saved. It is
necessary therefore, just as those who enter the bath strip themselves of
every covering, so also those approaching the ascetic life, having been
stripped of all worldly matter, to come within the life according to
philosophy. The first thing, then, and that of which it especially belongs
to the Christian to take care, is to be stripped of the passions according
to vice, the various and diverse ones, through which the soul is defiled;
and it is necessary in the second place also that the renunciation of
possessions be accomplished by the one looking toward the lofty life,
because the care and attention of material things furnishes much
distraction to the soul. When therefore several, looking toward the same
aim of salvation, accept the life with one another, this must before all
things be established among them, that there be one heart in all, and one
will, and one desire, and, as the Apostle legislates, that the whole
fullness of the company become one body fitted together from different
members.
And this cannot otherwise be accomplished, unless that prevail, that neither
a garment, nor a vessel, nor anything else of the things useful for the
common life, be named as assigned to anyone, so that each of these may
belong to the need, and not to the one who possesses it. And just as the
small garment is unfitting =31.884= for the greater body, or the more
complete one for the smaller, but the commensurate is both beneficial and
well-fitted for each; so also all the other things, the bed, the coverings,
the warm wrap, the sandal, ought to belong to the one who greatly needs it,
not to the one who possesses it. For just as the wounded man uses the
medicine, not the healthy one, so also of the things devised toward the
easing of the body, not the one who lives in luxury, but the one who needs
the easing, enjoys them. And since the characters of men are various, and
not all counsel similarly concerning the useful things, in order that there
may not be some disorder, each living according to his own will, it is
fitting that the one who has been testified among all to excel in
understanding and steadiness and exactness according to the way of life,
this one they should set over to lead the others, so that the good in this
one may become common to all who imitate him. For just as, if several
painters should paint the character of one face, all the images will have
similarly toward one another, because of being made like to the one; so the
many characters, if they look toward the imitation of the one, the good
character of the life will shine upon all alike. One having therefore been
preferred, all the particular wills will be idle; and toward the one who
excels all those following will be formed, having been persuaded by the
apostolic command that orders every soul to be subject to the authorities
that excel; and that those who have set themselves against will receive
judgment for themselves. And the true and perfect obedience of those under
authority toward the leader is shown in this, in not only abstaining from
the unfitting things according to the counsel of the one who presides, but
not even doing the praiseworthy things themselves without his judgment.
For self-control and all bodily hardship has usefulness toward something;
but if someone, following his own impulses, does what is pleasing to
himself, and does not obey the one who presides when he counsels, the fault
will be greater than the accomplishment. For he who arrays himself against
the authority has set himself against the ordinance of God; and the reward
of obedience is greater than the accomplishment according to self-control.
Thus it is fitting that love be common and equal for all toward one
another, as a man naturally has toward each of his own members, equally
wishing health for the whole body, because the pain of each member also
furnishes equal distress to the body. But just as among ourselves the pain
of each of the ailing members equally touches the whole body, yet there are
nevertheless some members more honorable than others (for we do not have
similarly concerning the eye and concerning the toe of the foot, even if
the pain has equality); so the sympathetic disposition and the loving
relation ought to be equal from each toward =31.885= all who live in the
company; but the honor, according to what is reasonable, will have the more
upon the more useful. And since they are altogether debtors of loving one
another with the equal measure of disposition, it is an injustice for some
particular factions and tent-fellowships to be found in a company. For the
one who loves the one above the others accuses himself, as not having the
perfect love in the others. Therefore both unseemly fighting and particular
affection are likewise to be cast out from a company. For from fighting
enmity comes to be; and from particular friendship and companionship
suspicions and envies come to be. For everywhere the deprivation of
equality becomes a beginning and occasion of envy and ill-will for those
who are made less.
On this account we also received a commandment from the Lord, to imitate
the goodness of the one who likewise makes the sun rise upon just and
unjust. As therefore God gives to all a common participation of the light,
so also the imitators of God should bring upon all both a common and
equally-honored ray of love. For where love is lacking, there hatred
altogether enters in its place. And if, as John says, God is love, it is
altogether necessary that hatred be the devil. As therefore the one who has
love has God, so the one who has hatred nourishes the devil in himself. On
this account it is fitting that love be equal and similar from all to all,
but that honor come to be for each according to what is fitting. And among
those thus fitted together with one another, bodily kinship will have
nothing more according to love; nor if someone should be a brother of
someone according to flesh, or a son, or a daughter, will the fellowship of
blood come to be in a greater disposition toward the one belonging by race
than toward the others. For the one who in these things follows nature
accuses himself, that he has not yet perfectly departed from nature, but is
still administered by flesh. And unprofitable speech, and untimely elation
through conversation with one another, let it be forbidden. But if anything
is useful toward the building of souls, this alone it is fitting to be
spoken, and even the useful things themselves to be spoken in good order
according to the fitting time by such persons as have been entrusted with
speaking. And if someone should be of the lesser, let him wait for the
exhortation of the one who excels.
And whisperings, and conversations toward the ear, and significations
through nods, let all these be cast out, because whispering has the
suspicion of slander, and signification through a nod furnishes to the
brother an indication of some hidden intrigue. And such things become a
beginning of hatred and of suspicion. And when there is some conversation
coming to be toward one another according to necessity, let the need make
the measure of the voice, so as to converse with the one nearby =31.888=
with a milder voice, and to call the one far off with a greater voice. And
for the one counseling someone, or commanding concerning something, to use
a greater and more striking voice, as insolent, let it not be in a company.
And there is no going forth from an ascetic house, except for the appointed
and necessary exits. And since the companies are not of men only, but also
of virgins, all the other things, as many as have been said, will be common
both to these and to those; but one thing it is fitting to know, that the
life among women demands greater and more abundant seemliness, and the
accomplishment of non-possession, and that of quiet, and that of obedience,
and that of brotherly love, and the exactness concerning going forth, and
the guarding of meetings, and the disposition toward one another, and not
having particular companionships. For in all these things the life of the
virgins ought to be accomplished with greater zeal. Let the one entrusted
with good order not seek the pleasant for the sisters, nor be eager to be
agreeable to them through the things according to their desire, but let the
solemn and the fearful and the venerable be always about her. For she must
know that of the things done amiss beyond what is due in the common life
she will render the account before God.
And let each of those in the fullness of the company not seek the pleasant
from the one who leads, but the beneficial and useful; nor let her dispute
concerning the things commanded (for such a custom becomes a practice and a
following of anarchy); but just as we receive the commandments of the Lord
without examination, knowing that all Scripture is God-inspired and
profitable, so also let the sisterhood receive the precepts of the one who
presides without distinction, accomplishing every thing that is counseled
with eagerness, not from grief or from necessity, so that the obedience may
become for her with a reward. And let her bear not only being taught the
things toward exactness, but even if somewhere the teacher should forbid a
fast, or counsel a taking of restorative food, or should also instruct some
other of the things that bring toward easing when need demands, let her
accomplish all things likewise, having been persuaded that what is said by
that one is law. And when according to some necessity there must come to be
speech concerning some of the things sought by need, whether toward some
man, or toward the one who has the care, or also toward another who is able
to be of some use toward the thing sought, it is fitting that the speech
come to be from the one who presides, one or a second of the sisters being
present with her, for whom already through life and time it is without
danger to come into the sight and conversation of someone. And if someone
thinks something useful by herself, let her submit it to the one who
presides, and through that one what must be spoken will be spoken.
