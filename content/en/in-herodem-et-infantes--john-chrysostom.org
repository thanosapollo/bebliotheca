On Herod and the Infants.
=61.699=
I have always and at all times wanted to set forth the spiritual word, being
persuaded by Paul who says: Comparing spiritual things with spiritual; and
especially because I was shown to be a debtor from the previous Sunday named
for the Lord. I do not know how this happens with me, that my poverty is never
released from the ever-present debt, while you, insatiable lenders in
spiritual things, keep collecting the obligation with interest. But
nevertheless I do not lament, O friends, but rather I rejoice more; for I am a
hopeful slave of a rich master. Therefore I do not ask pardon, but I beg for a
delay. For I have God who supplies the word, who says: Open your mouth, and I
will fill it, not because of the worth of the speaker, but because of the
pious love of hearing of those who listen. You heard, then, the evangelist
Matthew saying: When King Herod saw that he had been mocked by the Magi, he
was exceedingly angry, and he sent and killed all the children who were in
Bethlehem, and in all its borders, from two years old and under, according to
the time which he had carefully learned from the Magi. These things were at
the birth of Christ according to the flesh. But hear how it was foretold from
many ages.
That it might be fulfilled, he says, which was spoken by Jeremiah the prophet,
saying: A voice was heard in Rama, Rachel weeping for her children. Do you see
how many generations beforehand the madness of Herod in the last days was
proclaimed? For he sent, he says, and killed all the children in Bethlehem,
and in all its borders. O the madness of Herod, or rather the wickedness of
the devil! For this too was his work; he armed Herod against the infants. But
nevertheless he raised the sword of infant-slaughter against himself; for
though Herod devised many things he did not kill Jesus, whom he sought; and
Herod's failure is the devil's misery.
Seeing, he says, that he had been mocked by the Magi, he was exceedingly
angry, and he sent and killed all the children in Bethlehem, and in all its
borders. O Herod, why are you rashly enraged? Why do you arm yourself against
the infants? You were mocked by the Magi, and you murder the babes? Did the
babes guide the Magi? A star announced to them the Master who had been born.
Unable, then, to shoot the star with an arrow, you mur
=61.700= der the babes? Or was it for this that you took hold of the kingdom,
that you might display your strength against those at the breast? Do you make
war on infants, Herod? Do you make mothers childless? Do you dry up breasts?
Do you empty arms? Do you wound wombs? Do you multiply lamentations for women
in labor? Will you kill the babes from two years old and under? For you have
somewhere to plunge the sword. This is not to murder, but to cut down those
untimely. Show the sword to the children; if, being frightened, they cower,
then kill, slaughter; but if they run toward the sword with gladness, why do
you bring the sword against those who smile at the sword? Do you think, Herod,
to hunt down the Savior and murder him? But since you have wholly chosen this,
send out a decree, recall the Magi, send the star along, imprison Gabriel,
interrogate the prophets, seek the Virgin mother; if you get hold of these,
then you will be able to find Christ as well.
You do not glorify, but you seek him who was born without flux? Do you fight
against God, O king? Do you pry into him who is King? Do you wish to seize him
who holds you? Do you wish to destroy him who wishes to save you? Do you
craftily plot to hold him who stands beside you, and who, because of your
wickedness, does not wish to be seen by you? This is he, Herod, who from many


ages has cried: The wicked shall seek me, and they shall not find me. Do you
despise him as an infant? He is far older than Abraham according to the
Godhead; and not only older than Abraham, but also the Creator of Adam. He has
not put on cowardice, but he is pursuing a dispensation. Why then, Herod, are
you rashly enraged? The gain of untimely wrath is a fall. Why do you do the
same things as Pharaoh? He in Egypt ordered the male children of the Hebrews
to be thrown into the river, and you in Bethlehem order the male babes of the
Jews to be murdered. O like-minded wrath! O equally weighted pitilessness! O
companion in wickedness! Rather it is necessary now to speak more truly, that
Herod is far more savage than Pharaoh. And likewise the spearmen of Herod are
far harsher than the shield-bearers of Pharaoh. Pharaoh, even if in Egypt he
ordered the male babes of the Jews to be murdered, nevertheless attempted this
as a foreigner, fearing that this race of the Israelites might grow strong and
sack the Egyptians. For sparing the Egyptians he slaughters the children of
the Israelites; he makes war through fear, =61.701= he does not kill through
envy. For so Pharaoh was more humane than Herod, because he even dissolved the
law he had decreed, and became the undoer of his own ordinance. For as soon as
he found Moses being carried in a chest on the river, he took him up through
his daughter, and not only did he not kill him, but he even raised him as a
son, dissolving the law out of sparing the babe.
What do you say, Herod? Pharaoh saves Moses, and you have chosen to hunt down
the Master Christ? That man preserves the one who is going to scourge Egypt,
and you want to kill the one who saves the inhabited world? So again also the
shield-bearers of Pharaoh are more honorable than the spearmen of Herod. For
the midwives, commanded at the birth of the babes to drown the males of the
Hebrews, did not pursue this, being lovers of God, and that though they were
Egyptian by race; for their inward parts were wrung within them, knowing what
mothers suffer who are robbed of male babes by a violent death. Therefore they
chose rather to offend the temporary king than to set themselves against the
greater King and God. But Herod did not display his wickedness toward another
nation, but he crafted his madness against his own people. And Pharaoh bared
such wrath upon Egypt alone, and no further; but Herod ordered the babes to be
cut down while green, not only in Bethlehem, but also in all its borders.
O that unjust decree, which then suddenly swooped down upon city and villages
and fields and streets! There was nowhere for the mothers to hide the
children; for they could not bear to remain outside their mothers' arms. And
if ever a mother wished to hide her own child, that child gave itself over to
weeping, drawing death upon itself by its love for the breast. For just as a
fish cannot bear to live outside the water, so also a babe deprived of its
mother's breasts receives destruction not long after.
O that universal dirge! I cannot find how to describe the forms of the
mothers' lamentations. If there had been an invasion of barbarians, the
mothers would have borne the pain more lightly; for a shared calamity knows
how to sting the sufferer more moderately. It was not an invasion of
barbarians, =61.702= but civil war; it was violence that had no petition, it
was lawlessness that had the law as advocate. The mothers cried out, and there
was none who heard; the babes wailed in answer, and the one who pities was not
present; the breasts poured milk upon the earth, and Herod rather was frozen
like a stone. The spearmen were more savage than the king; they pursued the
babes in one way and another, some of them dashing against the walls, some
smashing upon the rocks, and some leaving them to a long strangling. For the
taskmaster of such a drama, the devil, was throwing them into confusion. Upon
these things then and the like the mothers grew faint, and drunk with the
passion they took no thought of seemliness. They tore their tunics,


they flung their locks to the air, they made public the breasts that ought to
be hidden, they struck their chest with stones, they scraped their cheeks as
executioners do, they called the heaven to witness as a silent judge, they
made petition to the Judge and Master of all, saying: What is this so great
cruelty of the king, Master?
He is acting upon your own fashioned ones; you created, he does not cease
slaughtering; you bestowed the gift, he inflicts the loss. And why did we even
bear males, if the death of males is so bitter? If the command is yours, order
us ourselves also to die with them; but if it is the undertaking and command
of the lawless king, why do you not the sooner hunt him down? But it is likely
that the mothers, stung by the passion, cry these things, not knowing what is
profitable for their nestlings. But what is more blessed than those who are
plotted against for the sake of the Master Christ? What is more blessed than
these children, that they were not slaughtered for themselves alone, but also
as if Christ himself were being murdered?
Truly it is timely to say to the mothers the saying of the Lord: Do not weep,
mothers, do not weep; for a little while they have lost your bosoms, but they
have inherited the bosom of Abraham. Do not weep, mothers, do not weep; and do
not hinder the infants from coming to me; For of such is the kingdom of the
heavens; in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory and the power, unto
the ages of ages.
