9 Jo 1, 32 For even if it was possible to see with sensible eyes the Spirit
coming down as in the form of a dove, yet not for this is there every
necessity that the matter be manifest to all. 12 Jo 2, 1–9 According to
contemplation the Word of God has come down from heaven, in order that, having
appropriated the human nature after the manner of a bridegroom, he might
persuade it to bear the spiritual seeds of wisdom. And the marriage is
assembled on the third day, that is in the last times of the age; for the
third number introduces for us beginning and middle and end. To this what was
said through one of the prophets appears to be akin: he will strike and he
will bind us up; and he will heal us after two days, and on the third day we
shall rise and we shall live before him; and we shall know, we shall pursue to
know the Lord also, as dawn ready we shall find him; for he struck because of
the transgression in Adam, but again he himself bound us up according to the
third day, that is in the last times, when having become man for us he showed
the nature sound, having raised it all from the dead in himself. Therefore
through this he said the third day on which the marriage was accomplished. And
in Cana of Galilee, he says, not in Jerusalem, in order to say outside Judaea
in the country of the nations; for Galilee of the nations, the people sitting
in darkness saw a great light; for it is clear that the Jews thrust away the
bridegroom from heaven, but the church from the nations received him gladly,
and the savior does not come of his own motion; for he was called by many
voices of saints; but wine had failed the guests; for the law perfected no
one, it did not suffice to gladden perfectly, nor did even the measure of the
innate astringency extend so as to be able to preserve. Therefore it would be
true also in our case that they have no wine. But God does not overlook nature
worn down by lack of goods, but he showed forth wine better than the first;
for the letter kills, but the spirit makes alive; for the divine lessons of
the evangelical teaching bring in the blessing most full. And the master of
the feast wonders at the wine, that is the new; for each of those who belong
to the divine priesthood is struck by his word that is above the law. And
Christ commands that it be given to him first; for the laboring farmer must
first partake of the fruits, and let the hearer understand what I say. 22 Jo
5, 45 For it was not fitting that he, leaving the dignity of the judge, should
make the accusations against them as in a court. There is one who accuses you,
Moses, in whom you have hoped; for in the time of the judgment he will be set
as their accuser, because they did not attend to his words nor believe his
predictions, through which he indicated the coming of the Lord. 23 Jo 6, 1–2
The passing over and the change of places sufficiently tested the purpose of
the multitude; for while the more slothful through hesitation remained in the
country, the more eager followed him even into the desert, being confirmed in
their souls day by day from the signs that were done. 24 Jo 6, 5 He displays
the unavariciousness of those with him and the voluntary poverty, who did not
even have so much as to buy the necessary foods. 25 Jo 6, 5–7 He ought, having
heard whence shall we buy bread, that these may eat, to have referred the
whole to the power of the Lord. But now, having thought nothing worthy of the
power of the Lord, he was considering low and human things, saying:


two hundred denarii of bread are not sufficient for them, that each may take a
little; being then thicker in understanding and somewhat slower than he ought,
as one going toward the grasp of the more divine things, he puts forward the
question, exercising the disciple toward faith; for this is what testing means
in these things, even if, just as the blessed evangelist insisted; for he
himself knew what he was about to do. 27 Jo 6, 9 Likewise with Philip he is
shown to have imagined nothing great nor to have thought anything worthy of
the teacher through what he added: but what are these among so many? 31 Jo 6,
33–35 Since we were all deadened by sin, or since after the resurrection he
gives the eternal and incorruptible life to those who believe, when there will
be no sensible food nor drink and for this he shall not hunger. The manna of
those who partook in the desert nourished the body for a little, but
contributed nothing to the soul toward virtue and well-living; for all those
except a few were detected having been impious, but the living bread, having
recovered the souls of believers by words of life, procured the true life for
the world. 32 Jo 6, 35 32 The body, being sensible and nourished by sensible
bread, is in no way satisfied with the once-for-all partaking; for having been
filled, at once again by nature, a little interval having passed, it begins
again to hunger. But the intellectual and bodiless soul, having partaken of
the living words of the Lord and having been spiritually fed with the heavenly
bread, is by nature to remain without need for all the time thereafter. Whence
he said with truth: I am the bread of life and the rest. 33 Jo 6, 35 What the
bread from the earth is accustomed to do, giving life to the weak nature of
the flesh, this he also does through the activity of the Spirit, making the
spirit alive and holding together the body itself unto incorruption, giving
incorruptible after the resurrection to those who believe in him. Or he will
call his own flesh bread of life here as being eaten in the mysteries. 35 Jo
6, 46 And he seems to hint at Moses himself again; for they thought foolishly
that through entering into the gloom he had beheld the unspeakable nature of
God with the eyes of the body, which is according to nature. But lest by
saying this clearly he should seem somehow to urge them on to their customary
boldness, he has set it indefinitely against all. And if the Son alone sees
the Father, he is outside of all and above all and over all. 37 Jo 6, 53 It is
possible, I think, also in another way and a deeper contemplation to take what
is said; for those who have sincerely received his incarnate economy and with
the reasoning of the soul through assent have as it were tasted the doctrine
eat the flesh rationally and partake through faith of the blood, inheriting
eternal life from what has been said before. 38 Jo 6, 54–55 Therefore he says:
he who eats my flesh and drinks my blood has eternal life. And it is possible
also otherwise to show the partaking of these as spiritual; for the teaching
of the evangelical preaching and the reading of the wording is easy for all
who encounter it to take in, being grasped like flesh seen by all, but the
meaning of the things shown, being incomprehensible to the many, is hidden
like blood in flesh in the word of the disciples. Which wanting us to partake
spiritually he used such words; for just as in the body the flesh is evident,
being seen by all, but the blood, being for the time scattered in the passion,
is invisible, unless it should be poured forth when the body is wounded, in
the same way the Son of God also names flesh the wording of his teaching heard
similarly by all, but the contemplations and the mind lying within, being hard
to behold and incomprehensible to the many, he called blood enigmatically.
Whence he added saying: for my flesh is true food. And the addition of "true"
presents the altered and exceptional character of the food and of its manner,
through which those who eat and drink rationally are mixed


with the Lord, being brought to one blending and conjunction with him through
love. 39 Jo 6, 60 Hard indeed was the word that was thought to make the
hearers some flesh-eaters and blood-drinkers, but to those who take the
spiritual things spiritually nothing appeared hard, but being words of piety
it procures eternal life for them. 40 Jo 6, 62 Even if my descent from heaven
seems most of all to escape all human beings and for this is thought doubtful
or even unbelievable to those who hear, yet my ascent into the heavens after
my resurrection, coming under the sight of the disciples, will clearly
dissolve the doubt about the descent; for I would not be able, while they were
looking, to be taken up into heaven, unless I had first made the descent
toward you from there. And he uttered this also for the refutation of the
impiety of the Marcellians, in order to show the one who was formerly in
heaven being named Son of Man, so that not the body, but the one who assumed
the body having become Son of Man may be believed. 41 Jo 6, 63 And this
belonged to his godhead, to bring the unspeakable things into the midst. 44 Jo
7, 15 If they had known that he himself is the wisdom, the God Word in the
beginning, in whom all the treasures of wisdom and of knowledge exist, they
would not have wondered how he knew letters having learned them in no way, but
they would have known clearly that he himself also granted to the others the
discovery and the knowledge of things. 46 Jo 7, 19 For Moses, legislating,
commands to abstain from bloodshed, but you already through extremity of
wickedness are counseling about my destruction. 47 Jo 7, 23 Moses permitted to
circumcise on the sabbath, but he in no way allowed to kill. How then, when
that one shows the greater and the less, do you deal exactly about the less,
but overlooking the greater you do not perceive that you are working murder on
account of a sabbath, although you do not keep it through the whole. 48 Jo 7,
23 For who does not know that those, working with the hand and circumcising
with iron and mutilating flesh, were truly profaning the sabbath through the
act, but he, having healed with a bare word and authority, showed through the
wonder-working that he was also lord of the sabbath. 49 Jo 7, 28–29 He granted
that they know him and whence he has come, as they say without judgment, yet
he refutes them as being ignorant of the one who sent him through unbelief
toward him. Not only so, but he also showed through the works that he knows
the Father and has been sent by that one. 52 Jo 7, 33–34 He was about, not
long after the economy of the passion, to depart from earth to heaven and no
longer to be seen here by the impious, although they seek him. Therefore he
said: you will seek me and you will not find me, and where I am you cannot
come. For the ascent into heaven and the arrival to him was impossible for
them through the impiety of the works; for the kingdom of the heavens has been
promised only to the saints who have done the commands and just ordinances of
the Lord. 53 Jo 7, 38 He sends them to the scriptures, in order that from
those' prediction about him they may be led into faith, rivers of living water
will flow from his belly. 54 Jo 7, 40 The opinion was various as in a
disturbed multitude; for they neither attended exactly to what was said nor
for the sake of learning. For this reason he answers them nothing. 56 Jo 8,
17–18 From what has been said he refutes not only the Jews' unreasonableness,
but much more also the impiety of the Sabellians, introducing clearly two
persons, of himself and of the Father. 57 Jo 8, 21–22 When the Father sent him
to become a propitiation of our sins, those who believed and accepted the
grace received release of the former evils, but those


who disbelieve through insensibility, having set aside the grace, remained
similarly subject to their sins. So he was foretelling what would come to be
after his taking up, which not having understood they said foolishly: will he
kill himself and the rest. 58 Jo 8, 24 Being worldly and unworthy of the
kingdom above through the present lawlessness they were in no way able to come
where he himself was going. For those who die in sins, Gehenna and a furnace
of fire and the outer darkness have been prepared. Whence testifying
beforehand he said: I said to you that you will die in your sins. 59 Jo 8, 26
Even if you do not take into mind the time of the judgment, yet the one who
sent me, he says, is true, who appointed the time of recompense, who also sent
me to proclaim this and to make it manifest to the world. 60 Jo 8, 28 I know,
he says, that now you do not understand what is said nor whom I call Father;
for those things surpass you to understand. But on the cross you will know my
power and that I accomplish nothing contrary to the Father, when you see my
whole creation being moved, and the Father from this clearly showing
indignation at what is happening, who is with me, and now taking pleasure
together in the things said and done by me. 61 Jo 8, 37 just as they were also
reproached through Ezekiel saying: your mother is a Hittite and your father an
Amorite and through Isaiah: hear the word of the Lord, rulers of Sodom, give
heed to the law of our God, rulers of Gomorrah. For of those whose works they
imitated, of these they were also said to be sons. 63 Jo 8, 44 That one,
having deceived the first man, made the race mortal through that one's
transgression, that one armed Cain toward brother-killing, that one prepared
through the generations that the blood of the just be poured out through the
unjust, whose wickedness they themselves also imitating were plotting against
Christ. 64 Jo 8, 44 For having become an apostate from the truth from the
beginning he confused all things through a lie, this way slandering God toward
human beings, and that way human beings to God, as God toward Eve, and human
beings to God, saying of Job, does Job worship God for nothing? and again: for
since you are enemies of the truth, for this you persecute me, since if not
for this tell the accusation. 65 Jo 8, 45 Because being akin to the lie you do
not admit the truth spoken by me; for the inability of faith does not lie in
the nature of the matter, but in their own purpose. 66 Jo 8, 47 He who is of
God hears the words of God. He says those who believe and obey his commands
are of God, clearly, because having received from him the type of piety and
having come into a habit of virtue they were named sons of God thereafter; for
he does not say them as having come to be of God according to essence; for
nothing of all things has subsisted as a part from the undivided and
indivisible essence, but having brought them into being only by good pleasure
and his own will. And from what is added he interprets what is set forth; for
what does he say toward the disobedient? for this you do not hear, because you
are not of God. Just as he said these are alien from God not through the
essence, for they were from him, but through the purpose, in the same way he
also says those who obey are of God through the voluntary character of the
faith and of the purpose, the virtue. And he says hearing instead of not with
the sensible ears, but those who are persuaded by what is said. 67 Jo 8, 55–56
The Word named in the beginning here keeps a word, that is the command of the
Father, in order that the Word who keeps may be understood as Son and not the
one kept. Having wished then quietly to make them attend and, though they are
ignorant, to teach about both his dignity and his antiquity he says: Abraham
your father rejoiced that he might see my day, and he saw and was glad. He
rejoiced being deemed worthy of his divine visions through which he spoke
oracles to him; for he prayed to see clearly the day of the Lord which he saw
and was glad; for with eyes at the oak of Mamre he saw him appearing to him
when he also received the promise of Isaac,


when he also confessed him judge of the whole inhabited world. 68 Jo 8, 59 how
was he hidden? not having wrapped himself into a corner of the temple nor
having fled into a little house, as one might suppose, nor indeed having
turned behind a wall or a pillar, but having made himself invisible to those
plotting by divine authority he went out through the midst of them not being
seen and passed by thus. 69 col1 Jo 9, 1–3 Thinking that they fall into
diseases through their own or ancestral sin they make such a question. But
Jesus, making the divisions of things with divine knowledge, forbade both now;
and yet there are some punished for their own faults as the paralytic, and
there are also through the parents as where he says: I struck your children
and you did not receive instruction. Others through none of these, others
through testing as Job. But this blind man was formed blind for this, in order
that in the time of the sojourn Christ, healing him, might make it known to
all that he himself is the fashioner of every living thing. Some were also
struck through ancestral sin just as it is also said through the prophet: in
vain I struck your children, you did not receive instruction. 70 Jo 9, 4 Light
having come into the world he calls day the time of the sojourn, in which he
had to, working the works of the Father, show his own kindred
consubstantiality toward that one. And he calls night his own withdrawal. 71
Jo 9, 5 He calls himself light both because of lighting the souls of those who
believe in him and because he was about to open the eye of the one blind from
birth. 72 Jo 9, 6–7 Through this it is shown that he himself created all
things and that he made the human being from clay; for through the things by
which he fills up what was lacking through clay, he makes clear that he also
made the whole human being from earth and that he himself again fashions those
constituted after these things from seeds and bloods; for behold in the case
of the blind man he shows both, that he constituted his other body from bloods
and seeds, but having left the eyes unformed now he fills them up, which is
the fairest part of the body, through the clay, having displayed in the one
living thing the two works of the creation. And when he had gone and washed,
his eyes were opened, the clay not ceasing from the washing of the water, but
paradoxically withdrawing at his application and the clay unspeakably filled
up the eyes. 73 Jo 9, 8–9 Of some saying he is the same from the character of
the face but not believing because of the eyes, and of others doubting about
this, the one who had received sight, dissolving the doubt for them, said that
I am he. 75 Jo 9, 10 When they ought from the work to know who was the one who
did the wonder, they seek from the appearance to recognize who he was. 76 Jo
9, 10–11 Having put away the doubt they inquired thereafter how his eyes were
opened. He answered and said the truth, but for the time he names Jesus a
human being because of the human body. 77 Jo 9, 15 When the Pharisees asked he
used the same narrative also toward them. 78 Jo 9, 16 And they reached such an
extremity of impiety as to prefer the sabbath unreasonably to his divine
power, when they ought from what had come to be paradoxically to reckon that
the one who had laid hold of such power was master of sabbath together and of
all legislation. 79 Jo 9, 16 79 Through extreme impiety they preferred the
sabbath to his divine power although even in the case of Moses the command of
the sabbath was not otherwise believed except through the sign that came to be
upon the manna. They ought then, having seen the wonder that came to be for
the benefaction of human beings, to have yielded toward the rest of the
sabbath as then in the beginning and now toward learning that the ground of
the command is changed into benefaction of human beings. But those, being
blind through unbelief, do not see that God also until now does his own works
on the sabbath nor that the priests in the temple


profaned the sabbath. Therefore also as blaspheming against him they said that
this one is not from God. But others, even if having come in part to
perception, fought against those, saying these things come to be by divine
power, not indeed through activity of sinners, and they had their own opinion
divided. 80 Jo 9, 17 Being split from one another they inquire again of the
blind man. And he no longer names him simply a human being, but a prophet,
speaking freely. 81 Jo 9, 18–19 The Pharisees came to such shamelessness as
not even to believe the one who had received sight himself, that he was
formerly blind, although the neighbors and those who knew him said this. Until
they sent for his parents and asked them if he is their son, whom they say to
have been born blind. 82 Jo 9, 22 For thus had wickedness prevailed over the
rulers so as not only to harm themselves through unbelief, but already also to
shut the way of salvation to the rest through threat. 83 Jo 9, 24–25 Through
excess of wickedness the Jews called the just one a sinner. But the one who
had received sight not only clearly confesses the grace of the benefaction of
the Lord, but also toward refutation of their impiety said: one thing I know,
that being blind he made me receive sight. 85 Jo 9, 26 They asked continually
not in order that having wondered they might stand off from the impiety, but
in order that they might fight still more against what was said. 86 Jo 9, 27
Knowing that they wished to fight, he answered them thus. 87 Jo 9, 28 As
reviling him they named him exceedingly a disciple of the Lord, and themselves
of Moses, not as honoring that one, but as opposing the teaching of the Lord.
88 Jo 9, 29 Speaking without judgment and not knowing that he was the one who
spoke to Moses, they speak things contrary to themselves, now saying we do not
know whence this one is, and at another time having said we know this one
whence he is. But the Christ when he comes, he says, no one knows whence he
is. 89 Jo 9, 30 As refuting them as unreasonable he says that one ought not
through your ignorance to set aside that one's power, but it is necessary to
bring forward that one's friendship toward refutation of your unbelief. 90 Jo
9, 31 Lest he seem through the things in which he is silent to agree with
those who call him a sinner he shows that through the things in which he
benefited him he is godly; for the good pleasure of God that came to be about
my healing is a mark of the piety of the one who was heard. 91 Jo 9, 32 For of
these holy ones and prophets who were distinguished and of Moses himself no
one opened eyes of a blind man. And he says from birth, lest someone allege
either the art or nature, which it was likely to be said in the case of
someone of those seeing from a time and blinded later and again receiving
sight. 92 Jo 9, 33 Through this he refutes the daring of those who said this
human being is not from God, because he does not keep the sabbath. 93 Jo 9, 34
Not being able to look against what was said they turned to insults, saying
things that are not allowed, not even being taught by the blind man that it is
impossible for the one who has not come into existence to sin, but after each
one's entrance into life using the self-determination of the soul as he
wishes, nor does a child suffer for a father. Those then speaking and doing
all things without judgment cast him out and separated him from their own
council. 94 Jo 9, 35–36 Besides healing him he also wished to grant the
illumination of true knowledge. Therefore he asked him if he believes, in
order that when he had agreed he might also hand over the exactness of the
doctrine; for he was grateful toward the benefactor. Therefore having heard do
you believe in the Son of God he at once having agreed hastened to learn
exactly who he is before believing in him, saying and who is he, Lord, that I
may believe in him. 95 Jo 9, 38 He did not agree with words only, but also
through the worship he showed the devotion of the soul, no longer naming him a
human being or a prophet, but confessing him Lord and God through the worship.
96 Jo 9, 39 Not only those blind in the eyes were healed, but also those
formerly being blind in unbelief were illuminated through the teaching of the
Lord, but those


who seemed to see have become blind through unbelief. He said this looking
toward the outcome of each one's way of life, both of the Jews who disbelieved
and of the nations who believed; for the aim of his presence came to be for
the salvation of all, but the purpose of human beings made some seeing, and
others blind. 97 Jo 9, 40–41 The blindness being different, the one according
to body, the other according to purpose, the Lord brings no blame upon the one
who unwillingly underwent the blindness, but the one who underwent this from
psychic purpose according to wickedness he calls accursed and lawless as
having been darkened willingly. Therefore as being such he says the sin of the
Pharisees will be indelible. 98 Jo 9, 41 If you were blind, having lost the
sights by necessity, you would not have sin; for no one willingly undergoes a
passion. But since thinking you see bodily you have made yourselves blind
through unbelief, for this your sin remains indelible. 99 col1 Jo 10, 1–5 When
all the prophets had announced his presence toward human beings he entered as
through a door through them toward the lost sheep, he alone having done all
the things announced beforehand, no other having been able to enter through
the door nor to be born of a virgin or to open eyes of blind men and as many
other things as have been said about him. And as refuting the error of the
deceivers he calls them thieves as wanting to snatch away the name of Christ
through a lie. Therefore the one who alone entered through the door said
before is named shepherd by all the saints. And he calls doorkeeper the one
opening to this one the Holy Spirit who proclaimed his entrance through the
prophets, having shut the door to all the others through the signs shown
beforehand, <which> he opens to the Lord alone who confirmed the things about
himself through the wonder-workings. Whose voice the rational sheep hear, he
says the faithful, and they follow him, which he also calls his own, the
unbelievers being alien. These he also separates from the impious, leading
them out as his own and governing and guiding them upon the life according to
virtue. Whose voice of the divine commands they also recognize, and they will
not obey an alien one who proclaims the unlawful things, whom they must also
flee. He calls doorkeeper the Paraclete, the Holy Spirit who proclaimed his
entrance through the prophets, but having shut the door to all the rest
through the signs shown beforehand and having opened this to the Lord alone
who also confirmed the things about himself through wonders. 100 Jo 10, 1–5
When the prophets had announced beforehand his presence toward human beings
and through the prophetic contemplation had prepared beforehand for him the
entrance into life he alone traveled through all the things written about him
and as having entered through a door into human life he came to the lost sheep
of the house of Israel. To this one, he says, the doorkeeper opens and the
sheep hear his voice, naming rational sheep those who recognized his voice and
followed the words of piety; for since of the sheep some became obedient and
compliant to the shepherd, but others having skipped away far off through
disobedience went into self-chosen destruction, he necessarily distinguished
his own from the alien, the ones being saved from the ones being destroyed,
the ones obeying from the ones disobeying. 103 Jo 10, 5 It is necessary that
those dedicated to the Lord flee all the lawlessness of the alien, and turn
aside from his impiety altogether and neither hear a voice nor be persuaded by
a word because of the harm of the deceit. 104 Jo 10, 6 As he mentioned sheep
and a difference of shepherds in a word of proverb they did not understand the
force of what was said. 105 Jo 10, 7 Toward the subjects of the matters he
also changes the uses of the names. Here then he calls himself a door as


shutting in the sheep, and both keeping off and refuting the thieves. 106 Jo
10, 8 For this he also used the wording exactly, having said as many as came,
but not as many as were sent; for the prophets of God came having been sent,
but the false prophets, no one having sent, came upon the distortion of those
being deceived. 107 Jo 10, 8 He calls thieves and robbers not the holy men and
prophets, but also those who came before his presence upon the ruin of human
souls, such as was Theudas and Judas the Galilean, who came,, were not sent;
for the name of having been sent is more fitting in the case of prophets. 108
Jo 10, 9 For those who believe in him and come under his rational flock enter
and go out with fearlessness, feeding on the care of the shepherd now, but in
the age to come being sent through him as through a door into the eternal
life. 109 Jo 10, 10 He calls thief the adversary, of whom leading the sheep
astray and casting them into destruction it was necessary that the common
savior come, who not only secured the flock from the plot of the robbers, but
also having given eternal life prepares them to be in glory and honor. 110 Jo
10, 12–13 He names hireling neglecting the sheep each of the rulers of the
people, having the name of shepherd, but doing nothing toward the security of
the peoples; <but> they used them as alien; for they were not their own.
Therefore seeing the wolf they fled, leaving the flock given over to the
beasts, not at all being grieved over those they saw the peoples being
destroyed by the devil the intelligible wolf. 111 Jo 10, 14–15 He calls his
own sheep those who for the time believed from the Jews and recognized through
the benefactions toward them their own shepherd. Just as, he says, it is clear
to know and to be known by the sheep, so also the knowledge of the Father
toward the Son and that of the Son toward the Father is constituted through
seeing for oneself. And he constitutes the two persons, in order to show that
he was not himself the Father. And what he said, not long after he did,
offering himself a ransom for all. 112 Jo 10, 16 As God who knows beforehand
he says this about the people from the nations as about to be joined to Israel
and then he said it in word, but later it came to be in work and he has made
from both his one holy church. 113 Jo 10, 16–18 He himself was the one
formerly shepherding Israel invisibly, but since Israel in time fell through
impiety into destruction, it was necessary that he also come in his own sight
upon the lost sheep of the house of Israel, in order that in addition to the
other benefactions, having given his own soul also for the salvation of the
sheep, he might display the disposition of a good shepherd. And here he
discusses about the passion, teaching that this comes to be for the salvation
of the world and he does not come upon this unwillingly. Then he says the
marks of the shepherd and of the hireling and shows him thus holding as the
Father, where indeed he is shepherd and the sheep are his own. Speaking then
loftily in the parables he shades the word, in order that he may not give a
clear handle to the hearers. 115 Jo 10, 17 Not now, he says, does the Father
love me, but always before eternal times, yet since by good pleasure of the
Father I give myself over to death, this pleases the Father. 116 Jo 10, 18 For
not by necessity, but willingly he came upon the passion and he had authority
to raise himself from the dead. 117 Jo 10, 18 Senseless is this one who says
the body of Christ is without soul and that godhead is instead of soul; for it
is senseless to think a passible godhead, but the soul is passible. And they
say the godhead of the Son is passible for this, in order that they may show
him less than the Father. Yet also in the case of the Father we find, he says,
