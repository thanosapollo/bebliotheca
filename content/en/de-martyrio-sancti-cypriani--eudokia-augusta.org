1 And she would harshly send away all the young men, having set Christ the
Lord alone as her betrothed. But he, gathering a host, at the royal seats,
wanted to defile the noble maiden by force. And as many as followed with her
cried out shrilly, and all rushed from the chambers with weapons, and they at
once made Aglaidas's newcomers unseen. But he himself, bearing all love within
his heart, struck as by a gadfly unawares, was clasping the girl. And she at
once accomplished the mighty seal of Christ, and immediately threw the
shameless one on his back, and with her hands she tore the face of Aglaidas
and his thick-haired cheeks, and she rent his fair garments; and she showed
him as a laugh to all, completing the same course as godlike Thecla. And
having done these things thus, she went back again to the houses of God. But
he, angry, entreated the evil-working man, counselor of impious magic,
Cyprian, and promising him two talents of gold and of bright silver, that he
might by necessity persuade the unwilling virgin to consent to love, not
knowing the mighty force of unwearied Christ. And the magician, pitying the
wretched mortal, from an incantation quickly called a harsh evil-working
daemon. And coming swiftly, he said, "Why do you call me? Speak." And he said:
"Terribly has love of one Galilean girl overpowered my heart; but tell me
whether you yourself have power to give her to the bed; for I desire her
terribly." And the sober adversary nodded that he would give the unhoped-for.
And Cyprian in turn addressed the murderous one: "Tell me your deeds, that I
may have courage in my heart." And he said: "Formerly I was the best of the
angelic ranks, but persuaded by my begetter I left the Lord, the highest of
the sevenfold sphere. You shall know all that I have done; and I will tell it.
The foundations of the pure heaven I myself by my wickedness set apart in two,
throwing them into confusion, and I hurled the company of heavenly dwellers to
earth. And again I deceived Eve, mother of mortals, by necessity, and I made
Adam withdraw from much-gladdening paradise. And I myself wrought the
kinsman-slaying hand of Cain. I soaked the earth with blood; and an unseemly
thorny fruit springs up for the race of mortals because of me. I gathered
rites hateful to God; and I accomplished stolen beds. I deceived the mortal
mind to worship feeble idols, and I instructed men to offer sacrifices to the
grim bull. I myself also stirred the Hebrews fiercely to stretch the ruling
Word of God, the Son without beginning, upon the stake. I shook cities
together and cast down high walls, and from strife I set many chambers
dancing, having led the dance. But having wrought all these evils, even
boundless ones, how shall I not even succeed with this high-minded maiden?"
And Cyprian said to the miserably evil-delighting daemon: "Taking this herb,
soak the chamber of the honorable girl in a circle; but I myself will come
last and put an ancestral mind in her heart. And she will greatly obey you who
desire her, even in a dream." But the virgin maiden then, having her form upon
the earth, at the third night hour was singing the good God. But when the girl
was shaken in the midst of her midriff, and in her own mind perceived the
wickedness of the evil-worker, her kidneys half-cut, inflamed with fire, she
quickly recognized the Lord, whom she desired. And she immediately signed her
whole body with the cross by her hand, and crying out she said: "God glorious,
who rule all things, begetter of the unstained child Jesus Christ, who bound
the Tartarean dread-monster serpent in the gloomy halls, and those whom he
took alive in fetters, all of them, most glorious one, you saved; who
stretched out the starry heaven with your hand, and set the earth in the midst
of chaos upon watery backs; who gave flaming torches to the colts of Titan,
and made the white moon a yokefellow of night; who fashioned mortal man
all-like to your own form, whom you commanded to be filled with the feast of
paradise, and him who by the counsels of the most dread beast, the serpent,
was drawn away from the well-wooded ground, you yourself seeking

saved, O Lord, with merciful heart, and you healed his wound by your stake,
cleansing all this one's pains by the name of Christ: for because of him all
the man-slaying earth appears, heaven is stretched out, and the ground is
fixed, and waters pour forth, and the whole threshold knows you alone as Lord,
all-ruler. Come, save your handmaid by your mighty counsel, and let not a
dread blame overcome me; for for your sake, undying-worker, I wish continually
to remain a holy virgin. For in my heart, O best one, I loved you exceedingly,
Jesus, my much-praised master; for you kindled a blazing torch of your desire,
and set it in my spirit. Therefore now never put your handmaid under the hand
of an adversary, a hateful, lawless, god-opposing one, nor permit me, blessed
one, to go out from your oracles, but ward off the sinning confederate, the
dread sophist." Thus she, making each of these things known, immediately
arming her own body with the seal of God, at once drove the abominable-faced
daemon by the name of Christ; and she sent him forth wholly dishonored. And he
went with much shame before the face of the magician. And Cyprian asked the
daemon: "Where is she, whom I commanded you to bring here as quickly as
possible?" And the adversary said: "Do not inquire into all things clearly
with your cunning; for I was terribly afraid when I perceived a fearful sign."
And the magician, smiling, trusting in harsh works, called again another
baneful Beliar. And he spoke to Cyprian: "I have learned both your command and
this one's wickedness; therefore my begetter sent me as a helper of your
pains." And the magician, immediately rejoicing, said: "Here, daemon, take
this; soak the whole house of the holy virgin with the drug. And I follow
behind you; and I think I shall persuade her at once." And he went. And the
most holy, modest virgin in the middle of the night sent up a prayer to the
Lord, sending these things from her mouth: "Rising from my bed in the midmost
night I come to speak, most glorious one, the sins which I have wrought,
before the face of your righteousness and of true judgment. O you who rule the
begetters, ungrudging giver of mercy, lawgiver of the aetherial and helper of
the heavenly, whom earth trembles at; and who most shamefully destroyed the
force of the destructive god-opposer, and received the sacrifice of Abraham
the begetter as if it were a great hecatomb; who cast down Bel and slew the
dragon also, and through your pious servant Daniel taught all the nations of
the Persians your godhead; who through well-beloved Christ, your child,
ordered all things well and kindled light upon the earth; who brought the dead
after their fate back again into the light: I entreat you, Lord, do not permit
me to go upon evils, but guard my body always unharmed, O Lord, and grant me a
burning torch of virginity, that I may behold the bridal chamber with our
bridegroom, Christ, and pay the covenants which I undertook; for his is the
power and the honor together with glory. Amen." Thus she was praying each of
these things, but the daemon, because of his pride, fled with shame, downcast.
And coming he stood before the magician, and Cyprian asked: "Tell me, daemon,
where is she whom I commanded you to bring, the maiden?" And he answered:
"Truly a sign overpowered me mightily, which I have seen, a thing dreadful to
Hades for all, overpowering, not to be yielded to." But Cyprian called another
stronger one, who ruled over all and was begetter of the black-faced. And he
said to him: "Being a nobody, did you yield, weak one?" And he answered
boldly: "I am now bringing that maiden to you, and be most ready." And Cyprian
answered: "Tell me a sign, which when you have accomplished you will come to
be inside of victory." And he said: "First I shake her limbs with fevers; but
when after the sixth day I have again astonished her, in the night I bring her
to you most ready." And coming, the reckless one, standing by the holy virgin
in the form and like garments of another maiden, sat on the bed, and with
deceit he lifted up a word: "I have come, from this very dawn taking delight
with you in lovely virginity, since Christ the Lord sent me forth, that he
might accomplish me here. But, friend, tell me this: how great is the prize of
lovely virginity, and how great a wage is stretched out: for I see you here
like

unto one dried of blood, of a parched life and a most juiceless table?" And
the modest virgin answered: "The prize-gift is a nothing, but a greater wage
attends." And the wicked-counseling one himself said: "Was not Eve a virgin on
the floor of paradise with Adam? But when in later times she was mingled in
the bed of first-born Adam, she was shown as mother of children. From there
she sowed the race of all mortals, and she learned all good things." Then
indeed Justa, persuaded by the daemon, was about to go outside the doors; and
the accursed one, rejoicing, went before the child outside. But when she
perceived the deceit of the destructive-minded enemy, she turned her mind back
to prayer, and immediately signing her body with the cross, and sending a
blast from her mouth, she thrust the most shameful heavy-daemon from the
house. And breathing a little from the tumult, she spoke thus: "I know thanks
to the Immortal, and the fiery disease has been quenched." And praying she
said: "Christ, you who rule your honors, nail through my body: I tremble
before you, O best one: and again have mercy on me by your ordinance; and give
glory to bear your name." And the god-opposer came very downcast before the
face of the magician, terribly distressed. And Cyprian then struck him with
words of reproach: "Did you too then fear the eye of the glancing-eyed young
woman? But tell, having seen, what strength is in the girl." And the adversary
said: "Do not ask me nor inquire; I cannot tell the sign such as I saw; and I,
a wretch, trembling, drew back and fled at once. But if you wish to learn,
yourself swear a great oath." And Cyprian answered and asked: "What oath shall
I swear?" And he answered again: "By all my mights, which I have and rule."
And when he heard, he immediately swore never to leave the proud one. And then
the bold one said: "When I beheld the seal of Christ stretched upon the stake,
trembling I fled." And he addressed him: "Come, tell me: is he himself much
stronger than you?" And the adversary said: "Hear me, and I will speak true
things: for as many things as we here under baneful sinfulness have done,
giving mortal men over to deceits, it is yieldable for all. But toward that
life there is a bent bronze-struck instrument, itself fired, lying in the
midst; and if anyone nearby should sin, angel or mortal, with the same the
messengers quickly carry them to the judgment-seat of cross-fixed Christ ..."
And Cyprian said: "Come, leap out; for I go to friendship swiftly, and speed
was pleasing to me. And I was longing for the cross-rejoicing one, lest
somehow I should dare the like things." And the hideous one answered: "Having
sworn a great oath, do you care for transgression?" And he asked: "Tell me,
accursed one, what oaths did I myself now swear to you?" And he said: "To my
mighty powers." And the magician answered: "I do not fear you nor your works,
most hateful one; for in this night I learned all truth from you because of
the maiden's prayers and holy entreaties and the vehement cross; for you are
very powerless. Therefore now in my limbs I will set the mighty seal, which
you said is greatly ruling; and I make light of your friendship, having
refused your counsels." And when he had said these things, he immediately gave
honor to Christ, and drove the dread daemon, saying "Go far away; for I seek
Christ." And the most shameful one went at once. And Cyprian, taking the
magical books, set them on the stout shoulders of young men to bring to the
house of the unstained God, and he himself followed them. And falling before
the feet of the divine priest, Anthimus, meeting him he said these things:
"Servant of the immortal heavenly one, I have preferred to enroll my heart in
the army of Christ and in the book." And he, angered, said to him: "Keep your
wickedness back; is it not enough for you, being outside from us, to do the
things you have planned? And avoid the royal things; for the strength of the
all-ruler is unconquered." And Cyprian answered: "I too in my own mind have
recognized that the power of Christ is mighty and greatest; for in this night
I sent against the holy girl god-opposing daemons, that they might hunt the
mind of the strong-hearted maiden in the bonds of deceit. And she, perceiving
in mind, by prayer and the seal of Christ overpowered them mightily. And now
bear with me and have mercy on me; and respecting your suppliant,

receive, O best of men, the books, from which I, a sinner, used to work
countless evils, and grind these to dust with fire, pitying my soul." And the
priest, persuaded, took the books and burned them all, and blessing that man
with gentle words he sent him away, often advising him to enter into the
precincts of God. And Cyprian, going back again to his own house, would make
as it were dust the forms of feeble images, and all the gloomy night he was
striking his body and said: "How should I appear to the eyelids of Christ,
having done so many evils? And how should I bless God with our mouths, through
which I made light of others, calling destructive daemons in my mouths?" And
scattering the ash, he was asking to bring down the pity of God silently upon
the earth, for he trembled greatly to cry out. But when rose-armed bright Dawn
came on of the great Sabbath, then there was a feast for all. And so he
himself also went, a newcomer of great God, into the holy assembly, and going
about he prayed such things: "Master, if I think I am worthy to be your
servant, grant me as I go into your halls to hear a word from the scriptural
books for a very good omen." And when he went upon the threshold of the
temple, David spoke, the divine son of Jesse: "See, glorious one, and do not
let go, O you who rule, nor again, escaping my notice, make me far from you."
And again the great prophet Hosea said these things, inspired: "Come then, he
shall not be a child." And David again said: "My eyes have gone before at
dawn, the light-appearing driver of gloomy night, that I may always follow
your more divine words." And Isaiah on the other side: "Let not your fear ever
take away the spirit, my child and Jacob whom I love, whom I have chosen as
champion of all neighbors above others." And Paul the god-speaker said thus:
"Christ himself, ruling, bought us from the harsh curse of the former law."
And again the interpreter David of the excellent lyre said thus: "Who would
tell the power of the immortal God and speak in all ears the hymns of the
all-ruler?" And then a prayer of the Lord of more divine words; and afterward
the priest's address; and then again a catechetical word of men: "Go out from
the temple of God, you half-finished mortals." And gentle Cyprian still sat in
the seats in silence. And there was a certain Asterius, a servant, who said to
him: "Go out from the royal halls." And he answered him back: "I am a servant
of cross-fixed Christ, and do you drive me outside?" And the servant rebuked
him: "But you are not yet a complete slave of the mighty God." And he himself
said again: "God is living always, who alone showed the most shameful
evil-daemons, and saved the maiden, and pitied my heart. It is not lawful for
me to come outside these houses, unless I should become inside the faith of
Christ." And the attendant of God, learning these things, went quickly to the
priest announcing. And he then called him; and as was fitting, he spoke many
and baneful words to Cyprian, asking what he had done. But he, having prayed
so much, set in motion the works of God, as many as he had wrought about the
world. And then with divine baths he made him holy. And on the eighth dawn he
became a loud-voiced reader of the much-honored Christ-spoken books. And on
the twenty-fifth he was accomplished as a lesser and smaller servant and held
the doors of the holy mystery-service together. And when fifty days came, he
immediately became worthy of the diaconate. And he mightily overpowered the
ranks of shameless, helpless god-opposers, and he drove hateful diseases from
the limbs of men; and already he was leading many into the flock of Christ,
who were denying the evil-believing blindness of idols. But when the year took
its end, he obtained an equal seat of a priest. And sixteen years in
succession he remained then administering the chair of the elder of the
people. And then the blessed good bishop Anthimus called all the neighboring
priests together; and having said in the assembly all things that were fitting
of Christ, still looking upon the world he gave the seat to Cyprian. And after
a little, Anthimus having gone to heaven, he entrusted the flock, of which he
was champion, to the noble man. And Cyprian, administering the bright house of
God, received the maiden, honoring her with the diaconate; and he no longer
called her Justa, but named her blameless Justina; and he set her as mother of
all the tender girls, servants of great Christ.

And having saved many disobedient, of wicked religion, advising them to desire
Christ, he was bringing them with toil into the flock of the ruler, who always
has the honor. Amen. 2 "As many as care for the mystery-serving faith of
much-hymned Christ, look upon my blooming tears, that you may learn from where
so great a pain is mine: and I know clearly that you know; for I speak true
things: and as many as delight in unseemly idols, learn; for I tell the
deceitful things of these. For not even any other among men was more
evil-religious than I, and consorted with daemons, nor was a follower of vain
idols, neither to learn what they are nor again what force is in them. I was
that Cyprian, whom once as a boy our parents gave as a gift to Apollo. And
while still an infant I learned the rites of the beast, the belly-going
serpent. And when the seventh year came I was thereafter initiated to Mithraic
Phaethon; and I dwelt in the high city of noble Athenians. And I became a
resident, since this pleased my fathers who begot me. And setting foot on the
tenth year I lit torches for Deo, and I undertook the white mourning of Kore.
And of Athena, who is toward the height of the city, I performed the serpent
rites. And having been made an attendant I went to the glen of Olympus, which
the ignorant call to be a seat of the feeble blessed ones. There I heard the
sound of certain words and a din. I saw grasses and trunks, enough to astonish
the sight, which the dread evil-daemons were watching in vain. There I saw the
seasons and the changing winds, and likewise many days, which certain wretched
ones inside, harsh, helpless, fashion by fantasy. And I saw a greatly
shameless chorus of those singing, and others again accomplishing Ares' works
in the throng. And of others again I saw ambushes and mocking ways, and those
in panic. There I saw as many companies of goddesses and of the blessed; for I
myself remained there forty days and another eight besides these. From there
are sent, as from mighty palaces, air-roaming spirits to the earth, for all
nations to do as many unseemly things as they wish, the worst things. And I
had from blooming trees a leaf-tipped feast of setting Phaethon. And coming to
the fifteenth year, I myself was being taught of all breaths and gods by seven
men, hierophants, and the works, as many as belong to lawless daemons. For my
parents longed that I should learn as many things as are in earth and air and
the brine, not only as many as belong to destruction for men, but as many as
belong to blooming grass and well-trunked planting, and as many as coil about
the weaker body of a man, and as many as the evil-minded helpless one found,
the ruler of this earth, the glancing-witted serpent, dishonoring the counsel
of the immortal ruler, unto strife. From there I came to be in horse-grazing
blooming Argos; and it was the feast of white-robed Tithonian Dawn. And I
became an initiate again, and there I saw the knot of air and of the
many-folded heaven and a blast, and the kinship of waters and of well-pastured
ploughland and again of dewy streams into the divine air. And I went as far as
Elis; and I saw in Sparta the mighty image of Tauropolos Keladeine, that I
might learn the wood-raging, very many-formed nature, and change pebbles and
styluses and characters of the world and old-wives' myths. And going the land
of Phrygia I became a very wise diviner, and from the midriff I learned as
many things as are in the midst of the liver, and from the Scythians birds and
sounding signs and also the bent courses of animals besides these, omens of
vigorous men, who then see the things that follow, and the hummings of wooden
boards, and likewise of stones, and voices from tombs of those long dead, and
the din of doors, and the throbbings of mortal cares, and bloody swellings, as
many as stain the limbs; and when the food of ants goes through the joints;
and the converse of words and the numbers of verses; and the visible toils of
flesh, and the pillars of nature; and as many oaths as are true and also not;
and as many contrary counsels of men as there are. Nor did any practice of
things earthly or of things aerial and of things in the deep escape me, nor a
many-turned phantom, nor a hidden mind, nor a deceitful nor again a
much-caring nor a crafty one, until of feeble deceit and

of evil-workers and of all such things as are upon this earth. But when after
these things I went within the twentieth year, then I came to the land of
gloomy men of Egypt and I reached the ground of Memphis. I was tried there too
in as much as is not yieldable for a mortal: how they are akin to the earthly,
and how the averted spirits are called; and of which stars they have desire,
and then in ordinances and in works, and how they flee; and how they
themselves guard the unsmiling gloom; and what sort of spirits are opposite to
them; and as many rulers as there are of most dread Erebus together with the
god-opposers, and how they are like in souls and bodies to beasts and to
swimming things; and as many things as they care for and as many as they do,
swift course and knowledge, memory, fear, deceitful art, traces of the foot,
secret forgetfulness of many and sports of the people, and as many things as
are like these. And there I knew the trembling of the earth, and likewise the
birth of rainy showers and the din, and the swell of earth and of sea,
imitations as if true, always-being images of immortal wisdom. There I
perceived the souls of great-strengthed and tall mortals, of the dread-monster
Giants, terribly pressed within misty gloom, and by a phantom carrying the
earth upon their backs only, as a man might carry heavy timbers, a burden,
upon his shoulders. I saw daemonic converse and of crooked serpents, and
bitter blasts, which they send to the earthly unto doom; from there the
roaming ones send upon men countless dread daemons, hastening about into the
wood. And I saw the earth toiling under a daemon and not sitting upon the
unstable water because of up-bearing bases, which they allotted. I also
reached a country where the helpless ones change: which the serpent in strife
of the divine force wrought into a crooked, evil-rejoicing whole life of
mortals; from which many spirits labor, bringing impiety to men of equal
foundation who go upon the ground. And there I perceived one liable of evils
coming according to strife, coming suddenly against a pious mortal; and a
madman against a wise and straight-judging one, a destructive man, and there
nothing is holy, nothing is a judged deed. There I saw the unpleasing
many-wrought form of falsehood. And it had a triple form of lewdness of
evil-stitching, bloody, burnt about, like foam and liver. And of wrath I saw a
winged type, wretched, rough, beastlike. And deceit again I beheld,
continuous, hidden, plumed with deceitful words. And of hate I saw a hateful
form, blind; and toward the back of the head it would have four pupils fleeing
the bright eye of surrounding-shining light; and from the head countless feet
alone were hanging, terrible, and there was no belly in that one; for it is
pitiless and wars without inward parts. Zeal and dread envy are like to one
another, but destructive envy bears a sickle-like tongue. And I saw a very
thin perversity, like a dried corpse: and it had many eyes, and the pupils
arrows, always rushing toward defense of the mind. And I saw the form of an
insatiable daemon; and it had above a head, narrow and long: and it had two
mouths, one upon the breast, and the other again upon the back, devouring the
solid earth and heavy rocks, hungering also flesh to satiety, wasting with
wickedness. And of wealth I saw the love; it had a grasping and sharp body;
you would say a sickle was being looked upon; and the pupils of the eyelids
were always failing. And commerce likewise I saw, low, quick-wandering; and it
bore all hope of wealth as a load upon the shoulders. And I saw the form of a
vain one; and it had a good spirit and fat flesh; but it had no white bones.
And I perceived the high-flying worship of idols; and on the crown above it
had two dense wings, with which it seemed to cover all things, but it was not
even able to make a covering of its own limbs. There I saw the heavy terror of
double-faced hypocrisy, thin as a whole, but it had mouldy breasts, secretly
wasting, wind-destroyed, as if rending. And I saw the form of madness: and it
had two natures together, of a young man and of a woman, naked, within
shamelessness and far from strength. And I saw a reckless evil-daemon, who had
a long tongue much

greater than all the limbs entirely. And I saw a mad one; and he had a head
equal to a nut, a most empty soul, accomplishing all things that are found
out. And of all the harsh things there I perceived the matter, the form which
the unseemly ones bearing throughout the world go down, destructive,
evil-daemons, dread-monsters, five and sixty and another three hundred daemons
of grievous passions, who also rule vainglory. And I saw a great reproach of
virtue, and likewise of wisdom and of vain justice, from where they lead
astray the men of the wisdom of the Hellenes. For one sees an image, and truth
has fled entirely; as a shadow all things are, and useless dust always. For in
these all wickednesses are worked toward the wandering of many. But it is not
lawful for me myself to make unspeakable books; and having spoken a little
from many I would have told you my impiety among all. But I will say this too:
when already I had become a man of thirty years, I left the ground of dark
mortals, and I came to the city of ancient-born men of the Chaldaeans, and
very hastening, that I might learn the course of aether, which these
themselves say is upon flaming fire, and the chosen men of these think upon
light. From there I learned the changing nature of stars, as when one gathers
boundless herb from calyxes, and the starry ranks appearing like wars. They
themselves showed the kinships and houses of each and their friendship, halls
and food and drink and the intellectual friendship being accomplished upon
light. They themselves showed me the portions of the silvery heaven, five and
sixty and another three hundred. And there was a fellow-worker of visible
nature with the stars, and they were winding a pretext; and they themselves
also obeyed the leader. And they were showing forth cunning from them and such
a way, always hiding the much-caring command of hidden things, and having from
sacrifices only soothings. And others they do not hear and they do not care
for drink-offerings, but only mouldy light is a care to them; but even so I
saw them going, persuaded for the sake of a gloomy counsel, that they might
give a share of light to stars that do not shine, being mixed little by
little. And astonishment held me entirely, when I saw mediators, that even to
the aerial gloomy ones wealth is a care. And I greatly wondered perceiving
their ordinances, which they set to be midmost among one another, guarding
most faithful oaths in their heart. There also piety and friendship together
and swiftnesses and vehement cunning, that they might be mixed with one
another, which the leader appointed, counselor of evil-stitching; for he made
them wise, having drawn breath from the air and a tongue of fair speech from
the plant-tending earth, and from the nether things having taught all
deceit-contriving things. From there, having persuaded, he held back the whole
threshold of the world not to forget nature, but a pious God; for chattering
he set all things into commerce, and having scattered all things the sinner
rules upon the earth. Believe me in this, that I saw the daemon himself,
having soothed him with many drink-offerings and sacrifices. Believe that
seeing him I also addressed him with words and heard words of friendship from
him. And in all things he addressed me as a youth fair of nature, as Iambres,
and a mighty initiate, a match for that man's works. And he nodded to me to
accomplish the leader of the world, laboring together with him, since he saw
the works of my life. From there, honoring me, he put into my hands a wretched
company of evil daemons, and as I was leaping out he said: "Cyprian, you have
been born a mighty mortal." And rising from the seat he sent me forth, and he
provided astonishment to those looking; from there likewise all the honored
ones of that one honored me greatly. And his form was like flowering gold,

Syria, accomplishing many wonders under the dread art of magic, to some
providing a cure of love, and to others myself of envy and hateful jealousy,

powerless. And while Aglaidas had many sleepless nights, and magic-workings
from me and strifes of the adversary seven weeks and another three after

against the maiden; for not only did love overpower young Aglaidas, but it
also touched my heart, raging. And it was an astonishment to see the ranks of
so many helpless ones, being overpowered by the girl's prayers, together with
the serpent; for not even our Beliar was able to engrave the desire, although
he had shaken very many dread things against us. For I spoke to him thus: "If
you have so great power, let love abate from our midriff, that we may not
somehow suffer so many pains vainly." And as I was hearing he said to
sacrifice to the eagle-ruling one of lewdness the things I spoke. And he
accomplished very many things, but he accomplished none of these things more
than before. And he showed of rulers,

begetters, it is not yet fated to follow the end of death. But a pain of the
heart is with me, not of the limbs; as when some fiery vapor coming from the

unstained one who blesses will be." For the girl's dread wrath did not escape
her fellow-furrowers, and they were ordering a vigorous man as quickly as
possible to put the maiden into true joints; but the young woman by the
Christ-receiving cross sent strength into the mind of her parents. And at
another time the daemon sent a raging plague upon the people and sent an
oracle to the neighbors, that the ungovernable wrath would not sooner abate,
until Aglaidas should quickly bring Justa to the bed, which is lawful. But the
servant of Christ stopped the cry of the people with holy prayers, and drove
the plague from the city. From there as many as dwell about the city, having
been tried, were glorifying Christ, and they poured outrage upon me; as being
the destructive man of that city all were terribly hating me. But I was going,
respecting kinsmen, citizens, both together. And last, seeing as many things
as the force of the cross had done, I perceived this in my mind, and I was

God thus mightily overpowers you

me with such vain baneful words. Then to you, dear men, as many as know my
groaning, I tell my wretched life, that looking you may make almsgiving; and

he will help me, that I may flee the dread paths of which I learned before."
