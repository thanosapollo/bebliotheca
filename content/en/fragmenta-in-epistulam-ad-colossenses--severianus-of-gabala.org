Most of this epistle is difficult of understanding, because many do not know
the hy315pothesis of the letter and the aim of the apostle. And the hypothesis
is this. Those who had believed from among the Greeks had the Greeks still
dragging them away into the Greek observances, and those from among the Jews
the Jews urging them to keep the law, as though grace were more incomplete
without the law. And the word to the Greeks concerning them was that it is
right to worship angels also, saying their gods to be angels; for God, being
great and inaccessible to men, supplies through these angels as through
mediators the energies to those in need. And, they say, the Scriptures also
know the benefit of the angels; for God set the boundaries of the nations
according to the number of the angels of God, and Michael is prince of the
Jews, and the prince of Persia is another angel. And again: behold the prince
of the Greeks comes, and Jacob: the angel who delivers me; and in the Acts
concerning the apostle Peter: his angel, they say, is; and the Lord concerning
the children: hinder them not to come to me, for their angels always be316hold
the face of my Father in the heavens. For from all these things, of which some
believed and some disbelieved, both Jews and Greeks gathered together,
asserting that the grace through the Lord Jesus is superfluous, the angels
from the age being sufficient to bring those who obey them to God; and they
said it is incomplete to pass over all the observances among the Jews and the
Greeks. Toward these things is the aim of the apostles in the epistle in hand.
314,col2 It is worth while to state the hypothesis which we found from the
epistle; for otherwise many things in that to the Colossians are understood
otherwise by most, 315,col2 because the hypothesis is not much known nor the
aim of the apostle. Those who had believed from among the Greeks had also the
still-Greeks wishing to draw them away into the Greek observances, and of the
Jews those who had believed were persuading them to keep the law, as though
grace were more incomplete without the law. Toward these things both Greeks
and Jews were persuading the Colossians to give heed to angels: the Greeks
saying their gods to be angels, since God is uncontained and great, not
attainable by men, and through these angels as through mediators supplies the
benefits to those receiving them; and the Jews, because God set the boundaries
of the nations according to the number of the angels of God; so also Michael
is prince of the Jews, so also the prince of Persia withstood me. And again:
behold the prince of the Greeks was coming, as also Jacob: the angel who
delivers me from my youth; and in the common Acts concerning Peter: his angel
is; and the Lord: see that you despise not one of these little ones, for their
angels see the face 316,col2 of my Father in the heavens. For from all these
things, to which some believed and some disbelieved, Jews and Greeks were
gathering together that the grace through the Lord Jesus Christ is
superfluous, angels from the age being those bringing those who obey. And also
to pass over the observances both among the Greeks and among the Jews is
incomplete. Toward these things is the aim for Paul, and these things are
corrected. Therefore beginning he says: Col 1:2 It must be noted that in few
verses Christ is often remembered; for of the one being set aside he
continually makes the memorial, impressing upon the souls the longing for him.
Col 1:5-6 Now he shows them that the hope laid up of the resurrection and of
the kingdom was not accomplished by the administration of angels, but by the
presence of our savior Jesus Christ. By saying the word of the truth he shows
that it is not without danger for the gospel to be passed over, being true and
undeceiving. By adding as also in all the world he confirms their faith; for
not only, he says, did the gospel reach as far as you, 317 but already also
into all the world, which is a sign of the power of Christ energizing in the
preaching. For it was not neglected, but bears fruit and grows. 316,col2 And
the hope laid up of the resurrection and of the kingdom was not accomplished
by the administration of angels, but by the presence of Jesus Christ. And the
gospel of the one present to you, he says, is not falsehood, that it be passed
over; nor did it come to you alone, but to all men. And it did not come and
was disregarded, but it is bearing fruit and growing. 317 Col 1:7 He commends
Epaphras as one of the saints and as the one catechizing them, that they may
give the more heed to his teaching. Col 1:9 And what was the will of God? That
you know and perceive that it is not possible for you to be saved through
angels but through Jesus Christ. How then shall we come to know? In spiritual
wisdom, not worldly. Col 1:11 He especially added longsuffering to endurance;
for endurance without longsuffering does not stand, and endurance without joy
has no reward. And there is need especially in these things also of joy; for
not the one grieving at the afflictions for Christ's sake, but the one
rejoicing in them receives the reward from God. And what is the reward? The
adoption, the inheritance, the manner of life with the angels. Col 1:13-14
Both before the flood and after the flood, and before the law and in the law,
the angels served God for your salvation. But God did not translate us into
his kingdom; but now through our Lord, his Only-begotten, his kingdom 318 has
been given to us. And the Son of his love, not that Christ is Son of the love
of God, but as some are called sons of disobedience and others sons of
Gehenna, and the devil son of perdition, and another son of this one, to whom
he ran and attached himself, being not by nature a son but by the kinship of
the manner; so also the savior was called Son of the love of God, because he
is not only by nature Son of God but also by the genuineness of the love; for
the Father, he says, loves the Son, and the Father has given all things into
his hands; or because, loving us, he suffered for us. Thus then must it be
read: into the kingdom of the love of his Son. 317,col2 And before the flood
and after the flood, before the law and in the law there were angels serving
God for our salvation, but they accomplished nothing. But now God through the
economy of the Lord translated us into his kingdom. Whom he loved; 318,col2
for he loves the angels as ministers, but Christ as a Son. He did not
therefore bring you into that of the angels, but into the kingdom of the Son
of his love. We are under the heir, not under the servants. He says then Son
of love, since he is loved as a Son, not because he is of the love of God; for
neither are the sons of disobedience sons by nature, nor are the sons of
Gehenna children, nor is the devil by nature a son of perdition, nor the
adversary a son of sin; so neither is the Lord a son of love. But since there
are beloved servants, and the Son is loved as a Son, he said: of the Son of
his love. Or thus: he translated us into the kingdom of the love of his Son.
Since, loving us, the Son suffered for us, that he might make us worthy of his
kingdom, therefore he says: he translated us into the kingdom of his Son; for
having the unalterable relation toward the one who begot him in loving and
benefiting sinners, he is the image of the Father. 319 Col 1:15-17 He wants to
say that he preceded; for if he did not precede all, how are all things
created in him? And he said all things to be created in him, that, turning
away from hoping in angels, we may have hope in Christ; and the in him instead
of through him. And these things were said to the Jews, that they not suppose
him to be from then, from when he came to be in body with them. All things
through him and unto him have been created. Instead of unto him the cause of
the creation runs back; or he said unto him, that nothing of the things
created is outside him, but all things are contained by him. And all things
cohere in him; either through him, or instead of in his counsel and power to
hold together all things, according to the mystery hidden in God, the one who
created all things. That he may show you that he is also before all, he said
that firstborn of all creation begotten. And by the induction he shows this
more, saying that in him were created all things. For if he were not before
all, how were all things being created in him? So that firstborn is not taken
strictly, since it is contrary to the Only-begotten; for by nature he is
Only-begotten who is alone to the Father, but firstborn the one having other
brothers, of whom he is first. And it is not possible for both to be taken
strictly. And he said all things to be created in him, that, turning away from
hoping in angels, we may have our hopes in Christ. And Paul has this cause of
the theology, and to show him creator of all creation. And the in him is said
instead of through him. Col 1:18 Just as he called him firstborn of all
creation as being before the creation, so firstborn from the dead as risen
before the other dead unto incorruption. For no one would say that the dead
having risen will all be brothers of Christ, and for this reason it is said
with reference to the end; for the majority are sinners and impious. So also
in the passage before us the time is dragged in, as also here. That he may
become in all things preeminent. In all things, in what things? Those
according to the economy; for first in obedience, first in baptism, first in
manner of life, first 3in suffering, first from the dead; so that reasonably
he is preeminent in all things, having become the beginning of those who
follow. 319,col2 And he himself is the head of the body, of the church.
Creator indeed of the 320,col2 creation, and creator also of our return. That
he may become in all things himself preeminent. For if he is head of the
church and firstborn from the dead, and first in manner of life, and first in
sufferings, and first in obedience, and first in baptism; in all things he is
preeminent according to the economy. Col 1:19- This has the sequence toward
the he himself is head of the church. And he said fullness meaning the church,
well also already said of him because she has been filled with the Holy
Spirit. And he says that Christ was well pleased that all the fullness dwell
in him, and so through himself reconcile and unite all things into himself.
In the [epistle] to the Ephesians he showed that the church is said to be the
fullness of God. God was well pleased therefore that his fullness, that is,
the church filled with him, dwell in Christ as in its own head, that is, to
follow after him and to be drawn out from him, but not of angels; for he was
well pleased through him to reconcile all things, not through angels; for also
the achievements for the world are through Christ. Col 1:21-22 And with such
things he teaches them that not angels will reconcile them to God but the Son;
for he says: he reconciled us in the body of his flesh. 321 Col 1:23 Not only
as far as you did the gospel come but also into all the inhabited world; but
no one anywhere has been moved away, but all remain steadfast. Therefore also
you are now made secure. Col 1:24 If, he says, anything is lacking unto the
suffering, but I fill up the things remaining of the afflictions for Christ's
sake, I rejoice suffering for your sake. And why for your sake? Because he
suffered preaching to them. For even if Christ is the head of the body, of the
church, the afflictions stirred up through those rising up against the word of
truth are named afflictions of Christ, and very reasonably; the one delighting
in these afflictions and contending would say that he fills up the
deficiencies of the afflictions of Christ not outside the aim. Col 1:26-27 The
economy and the incarnation of Christ is a mystery, and the glory of the
mystery is the resurrection from the dead, and the riches is the great mercy
from the remission of baptism, and the regeneration is gifted to those being
baptized. What is the riches of the glory of this mystery? Lest, he says, you
become poor running for support to the protections of angels. Riches, he says,
Christ was given to us. Let us not become poor, having turned to the
protections of angels. Col 2:3 If in Christ are hidden the treasures of the
knowledge, it is clear that not through angels are we to partake of them, but
through Christ himself. Col 2:6-7 And if also to walk being rooted seems
contrary, in the passage before us it is not contrary; for he shows us all
that the 322 being rooted is the unshaken firmness of the faith, and the
walking in Christ is to walk manifestly according to the same manner of life
in the footsteps of Christ. Col 2:9-10 He says the church as being filled full
of the divinity of the Father. And the meaning is such: the church is full,
and in Christ dwells bodily, that is, as a body united to a head, and
everywhere he calls Christ head of the church. And you are in him filled full,
instead of through him filled full. And filled full with what? Manifestly with
the Holy Spirit. Who is the head of all rule and authority. Not only, he says,
of the church is he head, for this is peculiar to the economy, but also of
every invisible power, since he is also creator of it. He says the church
filled full by his divinity, as he says elsewhere: of the one filling all
things in all. And the bodily here is as body in head. Why then did he not
add: which is the church? Col 2:11 That is, circumcising us from the sin of
the flesh through baptism he made alive to himself. So that the baptism in
Christ is spiritual circumcision; for it cuts off the sins of those believing.
Col 2:12-13 For through baptism is the taking away and the circumcision of
sins. Having been buried together with him in baptism, in which also you were
raised together. Those being baptized confess through baptism to commune with
him of his death in the blood of Christ; and having communed with him of
death, accordingly they will also enjoy the resurrection. And the word of the
resurrection is double; for the one is spiritual, the other bodily. For all
flesh of men will rise because of the resurrection of Christ from the dead,
but as many as were not baptized in Christ but died in their unbelief, these
will share indeed in the common resurrection, but will not enjoy the redemp
tion and the promise. 323 But as many as were baptized into Christ, these
gained even before the common resurrection the gift of another resurrection;
for already from the death of the sins they rose again. Therefore he also
said: in which you were raised together, not "in which you will be raised."
They then who have been raised from the death of the sins, how will they still
live as about to die again? Not then in vain did they rise? But if as about to
live forever, not then do they live according to the flesh, serving the
desires of the flesh, but according to spirit they will live. He calls the
body of sin the unseemly deeds; for these are accomplished through the body.
The circumcision of Christ therefore is the baptism; for it takes away the
foreskin, the body of sin. And as the foreskin is superfluous to a Jew, so sin
to soul and body. And altogether by the change it remained. And he calls sin
the foreskin of the flesh. Col 2:14-15 When the law was given cursing those
transgressing, all the people of Israel standing cried out, and agreed with
the things said, receiving them, and the agreement became as a handwriting.
This handwriting then, the agreement toward the law, Christ erased with his
own decrees; for he decreed against the observances of the law, as concerning
the foods, that not the things entering but the things going out defile the
man, and concerning the keeping of the sabbath, that on the sabbath he healed;
and many other such things one observing will find. Therefore he beforehand
abolished with the decrees against sin the punishments of the law against
sinners, giving remission of sins and repentance unto salvation. Since in the
curses the people said the amen, as a subscribed handwriting it was laid
down. Having stripped off the rulers and the authorities he made a show of
them openly, 324 triumphing in the cross. Since through the stripping off and
the laying aside of the flesh he brought down the opposing powers, having
stripped off the rulers and the authorities he made a show of them openly,
triumphing over them in himself. For until the cross and his very death our
Lord was concealed that he was God and Son of God, and by the miracles he was
striking, but by the body he was concealed; therefore also Satan tempted him,
wishing to learn whether he is the one preached by the prophets, in order
that, if he should learn that he is, he might hinder the economy. But the evil
one accomplished nothing, for he was not even able to learn; therefore Christ
was concealed until then. But since he was nailed and died and was buried and
rose and fulfilled all the things of the economy, he stripped off the
concealing, and with head bare he showed the divinity to be with the body.
First then was the work of God the resurrection from the dead, then the ascent
into the heavens, then the energy through the ministers and the other saints
in his name. Having stripped off therefore the concealing, he made a show
openly of the opposing powers. And what is the making a show of these? That
they have become weaker than men, that they are trampled, that they are mocked
by them in the name of the Lord. But others took the having-stripped-off
instead of having stripped. 323,col2 And how did he erase the handwriting
against us? When the law was given cursing those transgressing, all the people
of Israel cried out aloud, receiving and agreeing; as therefore a handwriting
the agreement came to be. This handwriting then he erased with the decrees.
Which? Those freeing from the manner of life according to the law and
providing the manner of life in grace. Since he sojourned for our sake,
loosing the disobedience and taking away 324,col2 the sin, he was offered and
bore our sins. Destroying these he brought down the diabolic power; instead of
saying having stripped, having stripped off the rulers and the authorities he
made a show of them, and through this stripping triumphing and making an
example of the opposing powers. Therefore he stripped them off in himself, and
he triumphed over them through what he suffered and accomplished. Col 2:16-18a
Therefore let no one judge you. He teaches that the law is abolished, Christ
having erased the handwriting against us. And he teaches that also the evil
one has fallen, the Lord having stripped off and made an example of the rulers
and the authorities. He commands therefore no longer to obey these things,
abolished through the energy of Christ; so that, neither, he says, to the Jews
persuading you to keep the things in the law submit yourselves. For those
things were a shadow of the body of Christ, which is the church. And the
shadow runs on ahead of the body, but has no hyposta325sis of itself.
Therefore he says: obey not the law. But neither yet to the Greeks leading you
away to worship angels or the elements of this world; for they enter upon what
they have not seen, from their own mind and from their own reasoning being
puffed up. The let no one judge you therefore was said concerning the Jews,
and the let no one defraud you of the prize concerning the Greeks. And he
says: let no one lie in wait for you, they now conquering and wrestling down
the evil one. 324,col2 Which things are a shadow of the things to come, but
the body is Christ's. 325,col2 When these things came to be, he says, they
were a shadow of the things to come; and the shadow runs on ahead of the body.
Since therefore the shadow is the things of the law, what is the body? The
things of Christ are not a shadow. The body having come therefore, the shadow
is superfluous. Col 2:18b What humility? In saying that we are lowly, but God
is great and transcends to be served by us. Since therefore we do not reach up
to him, through his angels we appease him and are brought to him. And above
also he said: who is the head of all rule and authority; and now he says: why
do you come upon the elements and angels, having left behind their head, which
is Christ? Since they were saying that God is great, he is ungraspable, he is
invisible, and we do not reach there; therefore, since we do not contain the
great one, through the angels we are brought near. And this, he says, is
humility. Col 2:19 From here he showed the aim which we say, that because some
urge to be brought through angels, therefore he was stirred. It is necessary,
he says, to hold the head; for the head of all is God the Word. Why then,
leaving the head, do you hold the body? For even if the power of the angels
should have a reason of harmony joining 326 two members or more. But certainly
from the head the benefit comes to all, to angels as angels, to archangels as
archangels, to the visible things as visible. Set for me the heaven to be as a
head to the world, instead of eyes the sun and moon, and so composing each
part of the world in the order of members, you will find one body of the
whole. And as soul in body and body of soul, so the invisible powers in the
visible things. But more sovereign than all these is the head, from which the
benefit comes to the whole body, supplied of course from the head. Col 2:20-22
If for this, he says, you die with Christ, so as not to worship the elements
of this world nor to live as in them, why do you endure those decreeing to you
according to the manner of life of this world as living according to it? Why
as living in the world are you decreed: taste not, touch not, handle not,
which things are all unto corruption with the abuse? These things are set
forth as Greek dogmas; for these men in their own error command not even to
touch nor to taste any ensouled thing. For all these things were given to men
unto destruction, as for destroying them and consuming them in the use and the
manner of life. Touch not therefore the things given by God according to their
commandments and their teachings. Col 2:23 Instead of fearing these things
(I mean the touch not, taste not, handle not) as seeming to have a certain
reason, since they belong to those humble-minded and sparing the body to be
temperate from foods and not honoring their own flesh in surfeit. But even if
the temperance is good, the second is impious; for they abstain not only as
being better than the belly, but also as not owing to wrong the ensouled
things, which were given unto destruction in the use of life, and they wish to
be more merciful than God toward the irrational. 327 Col 3:3 Our life is
hidden, instead of that the blessedness of the eternal life which will be
manifested to all, when also the glory of Christ is manifested in his second
presence. Col 3:5 Just as he often calls sin flesh, so also now numbering the
members of the flesh he says fornication and uncleanness and the things
following; for these are members of sin. Col 3:9-10 He says the old man to be
the mind of sin, and the members the evil deeds; and the new man the spiritual
mind, and its members the good deeds. He calls flesh the sin, of which also he
numbers the members. And having numbered the man composed of these members, he
urges to put off the old man with his deeds; so that the old man is the mind
of sin, and its members the deeds of the sins. And as there is an old man, so
there is also a new, that is, the spiritual mind; and its members the good
deeds, as he also numbers, saying kindness, longsuffering and the things
following. Col 3:11 This is like unto that all things may be in all. And what
he says is of this sort: that in all those minding the things of God, God
works all things. For when you mind the things of Christ and do them, he is
the one doing through you, and he is in you. It is like: that God may be all
things in all; not heaven in heaven, nor earth in earth. But what he says is
of this sort: when you mind his things and do them, God is the one doing
through you, and he himself is in you in your doing the things that seem good
to him. 328 Col 3:14 Without love going before, nothing of the things needed
is perfected; for then also we refrain from wronging one another and take
thought to do well, when we love one another. Col 3:15 Let not
those misleading you, he says, defraud you of the prize, but the peace of
Christ arbitrate in you unto being united to Christ; and become
thankful. To whom? Manifestly to
Christ who called you, and not run for support to angels. Let not the ones
misleading defraud you of the prize, but the peace of Christ arbitrate in your
hearts, that is, to be united to Christ and to mind his things. Then again
exhortation. Col 4:1 Of the slaves serving you with goodwill, he says, you
also, the masters, reward them according to the just and the equal in the
fitting care. The equality therefore according to the goodwill.
