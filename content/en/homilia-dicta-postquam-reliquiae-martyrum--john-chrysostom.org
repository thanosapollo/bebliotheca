HOMILY II.

=63.467=

When the empress had come at midnight to the Great Church, and from there had
taken up the relics of the martyrs, and had escorted them through the whole
marketplace as far as Drypia, the martyrium being nine miles from the city:
the address was spoken in the martyrium, she herself being present, and the
whole city, and the magistrates.

αʹ. What shall I say, and what shall I speak? I leap and I am mad with a
madness better than sobriety; I fly and I dance and I am borne aloft, and I am
already drunk with this spiritual pleasure. What shall I say, and what shall I
speak? The strength of the martyrs? The eagerness of the city? The zeal of the
empress? The running together of the magistrates? The shame of the devil? The
defeat of the demons? The nobility of the Church? The power of the cross? The
=63.468= wonders of him who was crucified? The glory of the Father? The grace
of the Spirit? The pleasure of the whole people? The leaping of the city? The
gatherings of the monks? The choirs of the virgins? The ranks of the priests?
The vigor of men in the world, of slaves, of free men, of rulers, of the
ruled, of the poor, of the rich, of strangers, of citizens? It is timely to
say over all: Who shall speak of your mighty acts, O Lord? Who shall make all
your praises to be heard? Women kept in inner chambers and softer than wax,
leaving their roofed dwellings, rivaled the strongest of men in eagerness,
covering so great a length of road on foot; not the young only, but those
already grown old; and neither the weakness of =63.469= nature, nor the luxury
of their way of life, nor the pride of high rank became a hindrance to their
eagerness. Magistrates in their turn, themselves leaving their carriages and
the rod-bearers and the spear-bearers, mixed themselves among the private
citizens.

And what need is there to speak of women, or of magistrates, when even she
herself who wears the diadem and is clothed in the purple, along the whole
road, did not endure to be even a little apart from the relics, but like a
handmaid followed the saints, touching the casket and the linen laid upon it,
and trampling down all human pride, and appearing in the midst of so great a
theater, before so great a people, she whom it is not lawful even for all the
eunuchs who move about in the imperial courts to see? But the longing for the
martyrs, and that tyranny, and the flame of love persuaded her to cast off all
these masks, and with bare eagerness to display the zeal that is for the holy
martyrs.

For she remembered also blessed David, who, though clothed in purple and
wearing a diadem, and holding the scepters of the Hebrew people, when he was
bringing up the ark, leaving all that tent, leaped and danced and sprang,
playing and dancing greatly, and through his leaps displaying the pleasure he
had in what was being done. And if in the shadow and the type so great a
warmth had to be shown, much more in grace and in truth; since she too was
bringing up an ark far better than that of David. For it has not stone
tablets, but spiritual tablets, and grace blossoming upon them, and a gift
flashing, and bones shining back against the very rays; or rather they send
forth a brighter lightning. For when they see a ray of the sun, demons suffer
nothing; but the brightness leaping out from here they cannot bear: they are
blinded and they run away and they flee from a great distance;

so great also is the power of the ashes of the saints, not sitting only within
the relics, but also going further and driving out the unclean powers, and
sanctifying with great abundance those who approach with faith. For this
reason also this Christ-loving woman followed the relics, continually touching
them, and drawing the blessing to herself, and becoming a teacher to all the
others of this good and spiritual commerce, and teaching all to draw from this
spring which is always being drawn from, yet never emptied. For just as the
streams of springs as they gush are not held within their own bosoms, but
overflow and pour over; so also the grace of the Spirit that sits beside the
bones and dwells with the saints goes forth also to others who follow it with
faith, and runs from souls to bodies, and from bodies to garments, and from
garments to sandals, and from sandals to shadows. For this reason not only the
bodies of the holy apostles were at work, but also the handkerchiefs and the
aprons; and not only the handkerchiefs and the aprons, but even the shadows of
Peter were doing things more powerful than the living.

Already somewhere even a mantle brought down upon the body of Elisha brought
down a double gift upon him; for not only the body of Elisha, but that garment
too was filled with grace. For this reason also in the case of the three
children: for not only their bodies =63.470= did the nature of the flame
reverence, but even their very sandals; and in the case of Elisha, even after
he had died, he still undid death in this way, when another dead man was
thrown into the prophet's tomb. So also it has happened today: for as the
relics were being carried, burnings of demons, wailings and lamentations were
raised from every side, as the ray of the bones leapt out and burned up the
phalanx of the opposing powers.

βʹ. For these reasons I leap and fly with pleasure, because you have made the
city a desert, emptying the city; because you have shown us today the wealth
of the Church. See how many sheep, and nowhere a wolf; how many vines, and
nowhere a thorn; how many ears of grain, and nowhere tares. A sea was
stretched from the city as far as the present place, a sea freed from waves,
having no shipwreck, set free from reefs; a sea sweeter than all honey, more
drinkable than drinkable waters. One would not err in calling this sea also a
river of fire; so the torches through the night, dense and continuous,
stretched all the way to this martyrium, offered to those who saw them the
appearance of a river of fire. And these things were in the night; but when
day appeared other torches again were seen; for those the rising sun hid and
made dimmer, but those in each person's mind it showed brighter; for the fire
of your eagerness was hotter than that visible fire; and each one carried a
double torch, that of fire in the night, that of eagerness both in the night
and in the day; or rather I no longer even call that night; for it rivaled the
day, carrying you about, the sons of light, and showing you brighter than ten
thousand stars and the morning star.

For just as those who are drunk make the day night, so those who keep vigil
through the night and stay awake make the night day. For this reason through
the whole night they sang that prophetic word: Night is illumination in my
delight; and that darkness shall not be darkened from you; and night shall be
light as day; as its darkness, so also its light. For of what day has this
night not become brighter, when all were leaping in so great an excess of joy,
having spiritual joy, so many peoples having poured out and flooded the road
and the marketplace?

For there was not even bare ground to be seen, but covering the whole road
with human bodies, you showed one golden and continuous chain, and one river
borne with much rush through the whole journey; and looking up to heaven we
saw moon and stars in the midst, and below the multitude of the faithful, and
in the midst the empress advancing, brighter than the moon. For just as the
stars below are better than those above, so also she is far brighter than that
one.

What then? Is the moon so great as a soul in so great a rank, adorned with so
great a faith? What would one marvel at in her first, the zeal hotter than
fire, the faith more solid than adamant, the contrition of mind and the
humility, through which she eclipsed all, having cast off kingdom and diadems
and all the pride that comes from them with great abundance, and having put on
the robe of humility instead of the purple, and through this shining more
greatly? For there have often been many empresses, who shared only her robe
and her diadems, and the imperial glory; but of this one alone =63.471= this
adornment has been distinctive, and this trophy belongs to her alone. For of
empresses she alone escorted martyrs with so great honor, with so great
eagerness and reverence, mixing with the multitude, and cutting off all the
escort, and driving out almost the whole inequality of life with great excess.

For this reason she has benefited the people no less than the martyrs. For
just as all looked toward the relics, so they gaped also at her attendance,
both rich and poor, seeing her clinging fast to the bones through so great a
journey, neither growing weary nor giving up, but hanging upon the coffin. For
these reasons we do not cease calling you blessed, and not we only, but also
all the generations after these.

γʹ. For what has happened here the ends of the inhabited world will hear, and
as much land as the sun looks upon; and those after us will hear, and those
after them, and no time will give over what has happened to forgetfulness, God
displaying it everywhere in the inhabited world and everywhere among the
generations after these with great distinction. For if he caused the deed of a
harlot woman to reach the ends of the inhabited world, and established it as
immortal in memory; much more will he not allow the deed of a modest and grave
and chaste woman, who has shown such reverence even in a kingdom, to be
hidden, but all will call you blessed, the hostess of the saints, the
patroness of the Churches, the one of like zeal with the apostles. For if you
have obtained a woman's nature, yet it is possible for you also to rival
apostolic achievements. Since that Phoebe too then, who received the teacher
of the inhabited world and became his patroness, was a woman and shared the
same nature as you; yet she shone so greatly that that holy man, worthy of the
heavens and greater than all the apostles, proclaimed her and said, She became
a patroness of many, and of myself also. And Priscilla also had obtained a
woman's nature; but this was no hindrance to her being proclaimed and her
memory becoming immortal. And there was then also another great choir of women
who took in hand an apostolic life. Therefore we too, numbering you now with
them, will not err, because you are a harbor of all the Churches, and you have
used the present kingdom toward the acquisition of the coming kingdom, setting
Churches upright, honoring priests, dissolving the error of heretics,
receiving martyrs, not with a table, but with a mind, not with a tent, but
with a purpose, or rather both with a tent and =63.472= with a purpose.

Miriam also once went before the people, carrying the bones of Joseph, and she
sang a song; but she after the Egyptians had been drowned, you after demons
have been choked; she after Pharaoh had been drowned, you after the devil has
been brought down; she having cymbals, you having a mind and a soul sounding
above a trumpet; she after Jews had been set free, you after the Church has
been crowned; she leading out one people of one tongue, you ten thousand
peoples of different tongues. For you have led out for us ten thousand choirs,
some in the speech of the Romans, some in that of the Syrians, some in that of
the barbarians, some in the Greek voice striking up the songs of David; and
one could see different nations and different choirs all having one lyre, that
of David, and crowning you with prayers. The gladness of this feast also
sought the most God-beloved emperor, who with you draws the plow of piety; but
this too was of your wisdom, to hold him at home today, and to promise his
presence for tomorrow. For lest the multitude of horsemen or the tumult of
armed soldiers harm virgins, aged women, old men, and throw the feast into
confusion, doing things worthy of her own wisdom she divided the festival. For
if both had been present today, the things of the feast would have received
their end today; but in order both to work quiet for the present day, and by
the addition of tomorrow to lavish the gladness, she divided the service with
him, and herself being present today, she promised us his presence tomorrow.
For just as she shares the kingdom with him, so also piety, and she does not
leave him without a share in the achievements, but everywhere she takes him as
a partner.

Since then our spiritual festival must be stretched also into the coming day,
let us show the same eagerness again, so that just as today we saw this
Christ-loving woman with the city, so tomorrow we may behold the God-loving
emperor arriving with the army, and offering to God the same sacrifice, that
from reverence, that from zeal, that from faith; and taking the holy martyrs
as partners in the prayers, let us pray for the emperor and the empress, for
length of life, a rich old age, children and children's children, and before
all these things, that this zeal be stretched further, that the reverence
increase, and that they so complete the present life as also to reign together
through the undefiled ages with the only-begotten Son of God. For if we
endure, he says, we shall also reign with him; and that we may attain the
eternal goods; of which may we all be counted worthy, by the grace and love of
mankind of our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom to the Father be glory together
with the Holy Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
