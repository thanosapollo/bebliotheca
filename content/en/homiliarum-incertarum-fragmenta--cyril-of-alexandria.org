* Fragments from Uncertain Homilies

Senseless also are those who say that the Son is of another kind and alien
from the essence of God and Father; for it is impossible that he who is from
God by nature should be created and made; for if everything that has the power
to beget begets what is consubstantial with itself, and the Father has in
truth begotten the Son, then assuredly he has begotten him consubstantial with
himself. For is it not absurd that from man a creature of no alien kind is
begotten, and indeed from ox and from horse, since each begets that which is
by nature like itself; 462 and yet to think that the divine and most high
nature is not even among those things in which creation is? If then that which
has been begotten is a creature, created also is he who begot; but the Father
is unbegotten, for he has not come to be through any; uncreated therefore also
is the Son; and if he is the impress of the hypostasis of him who begot, and
God in truth is he who begot, God therefore also is his impress; for the
impress marks out: God, God; and creation, creation. For if one wished to
point out to any the created and made creation, he cannot say, Behold for me
the nature of the godhead, and from it you will understand that one; for he
who says this will appear ignorant; for the nature of the godhead will not of
itself show forth the created essence; in like manner, if one wished to see
the uncreated nature, he would not see it as in an image in creation;
wherefore the Lord said to Philip, He who has seen me has seen the Father; and
the likeness of the Son toward the Father is assuredly natural, and the
impress is of the essence.

VI. Do you see that God is anointed by God? For when he has become man,
remaining what he was, then also, in a manner after us, he is anointed humanly
for apostleship; for the manhood is anointed with the divine Spirit, not acted
upon as upon mere men, such as prophets and patriarchs; and the anointing is,
as it were, the whole presence of him who anoints. The Son is anointed when he
was brought into the inhabited world, that is, when he became flesh; for then
he had communion with creation, having united to himself the created thing,
and having anointed the manhood with the godhead, so as to make the both one.

VII. He who has led many sons to glory would not have slipped away from being
in truth a Son; for he was not careless of being what he is, as though he had
not become it, but he remains Lord in the form of a slave.

VIII. Then one might understand with us him who has partaken of blood and
flesh in a manner like us, as being other than we are according to nature; for
no one would say that it beseems a man to partake of manhood; for how would
one understand that which is a thing by nature as having taken it, as being
other than what it is? But what is, In like manner he took part of the same?
It is assuredly this, that he has become after us in blood and flesh, having
taken a body from the holy virgin, not without soul, as it seemed to some of
the heretics, but endued with a rational soul; and thus he came forth man from
woman, not having made light of being God, but even in the assumption of flesh
remaining what he was always. But since the word is deep, attend with an
awakened mind how we have had communion of blood and flesh; for the soul is of
another kind than blood and flesh, yet we have been compounded into one man of
both, by the skillful workings of him who fashioned us, things opposite to one
another by nature having run together into an unspeakable unity with each
other. Will you, as Isaiah says, measure the water with the hand, and the
heaven with a span, and all the earth with a measure? Will you that we see the
things above us from the things after us? Let us then, setting forth for an
example in brief the man of soul and body, leap up to the thoughts concerning
Christ; we say that there has come to be a true union, 464 the Word having
taken to himself the human; for he made the body from the holy virgin his own,
his own, as it were, being said also to be that of each of us; and I think
this is what it signifies that he became flesh, not according to a change or a
translation; for the divine nature is without change; but that it is ever
somewhat customary with the divine scripture to call the whole man, I mean him
of soul and body, and also to declare him from the flesh alone, as is that, I
will pour from my Spirit upon all flesh. One therefore we confess Son,
begotten divinely of the Father, according as he is understood and is the
Word, and brought forth, the same, of woman according to the flesh; for thus
he will be understood to have had communion of blood and flesh in a manner
like us, that through his own death he might bring to nought the devil,
through whose envy death entered into the world; for the death of Christ has
become, as it were, a root of life, and an abolishing of corruption, and a
turning away of sin and a limit of wrath; we had become accursed, and under
the sentence of death in Adam; but the Word, who knew no sin, has been named a
son of Adam, and through him the charges of the first transgression are
undone; for the nature of man appeared in Christ soundly having the blameless
thing, and this has saved those upon earth.

IX. It is not, however, in the very nature of the Word that he has been called
High Priest and such names, but in the economy with flesh and in the measures
after us. Come then, let us first seek this: who was he that was able and had
authority to lay hold of angels, if that had been profitable, but did not
rather lay hold of them, but of the seed of Abraham? If then some suppose that
these things are said concerning a common man, how was it possible for 465
him, not yet begotten nor existing, to lay hold of the nature of the holy
angels, if he had wished to do this, but instead not having done that, and
having laid hold rather of the seed of Abraham? And otherwise too, for I think
that one must also perceive this, that to say a man laid hold of manhood is
altogether senseless. Therefore he who is outside the nature of man, being
God, lays hold of the seed of Abraham; for having taken a body endued with
soul and mind from the holy and Mother-of-God Mary, he was made like to the
brethren in all things, that is, to us; then he became for us a merciful high
priest, and with this also faithful. How then did he become? It follows to
see: was he not merciful even before he became after us? Was he not good as
from a good Father, but only when he became man, then also merciful? Did the
Word then advance, which was from God the Father, and become better than
himself, when he laid hold of the seed of Abraham? Did he then become what he
was not, and was he rather profited by becoming man? How then is he still
unchanged according to nature, if he has become what he was not? How has the
same remained? Where has he emptied himself and brought himself down to
lowliness, if he has become better than himself? But it is not so with these
things, may it never be; for he was merciful and is always; and because this
is so, he laid hold of the seed of Abraham, that he might become for us a
merciful priest; and in what manner, I will say: it was not possible for man
to be justified in the law; it was not possible to transgress the commandment
given and not undergo penalties immediately and even unto death; and Paul
writing will confirm it: If anyone has set aside the law of Moses, he dies
without mercies upon two or three witnesses. That he might therefore deliver
us from the penalty in the law and the curse and the cutting off, he has been
named our priest, not seeking penalties for the things in which we sinned, not
making those who have transgressed 466 through human weakness liable to
judgment, but rather justifying by faith, and releasing offences, and
declaring them holy and sharers of his own nature, and thus joining them
through himself to God and Father; for Christ has become a merciful high
priest toward us, no longer allowing the legal cutting-offs to prevail, but
rather releasing those who have grown weak from the ancient charges; and
faithful, because lasting and perpetual, and worthy of trust in faith for the
things promised, and having an untransgressable priesthood. Merciful therefore
and faithful, that is, ever remaining, he became priest when he laid hold of
the seed of Abraham, the Word being God, and in the form and equality of the
Father; why then do they slander the truth? Why do they beg off the Son from
being what he is, for the sake of the love toward us?

X. But the Christ-fighters, gathering to themselves from every side the
inventions of craftiness for aid, slander the very divine writings; and indeed
they say that the blessed Paul called Jesus apostle and high priest; and
apostleship, they say, and priesthood are manners of ministry; and at any rate
the disciples were sent, but Aaron and his sons exercised priesthood; and if,
they say, the measure of the Son is in these, how can he be consubstantial
with God and Father? O great perversity! They play-act a knowing of the truth,
and their aim is to think or say nothing sound; for though they confess with
us that, although the Son exists in the form of God and Father, he emptied
himself willingly, they do not praise the mystery of the economy with flesh,
but hearing the apostle say, Apostle and High Priest Jesus, they do not
receive in mind that, 467 the Only-begotten having become after us, was then
also named Jesus through the voice of the angel; then he was also named
apostle and high priest. Then either let them deny plainly that the Word
became flesh, that is, man, having taken a body endued with soul and mind, and
then let all the things written concerning him be turned to the fitting aim;
or if they fear to deny the mystery, let them wonder with us at him who has
become for us in the things after us, remaining God even so; for then, prying
into the measure of the emptying, they will find the same one apostle in a
human manner, and preached divinely by the sent ones; for whom did the blessed
disciples preach to the nations, and this as true God? Is it not our Lord
Jesus the Christ? Not apostle because of the human? Not himself being God,
because he was the Word of the Father? How shall we behold him having become
high priest? According to Aaron? According to the law of Moses, sacrificing to
God the lambs of the flock? Offering turtledoves and pigeons and fine flour
bathed with oil? And yet none of these things has Emmanuel done; for he
exercises priesthood above the law; he himself was the sacrifice, the true
lamb, the same one the spotless and guileless high priest, not ministering for
his own transgressions, for he was superior to sin as God, but that he might
undo the sin of the world; therefore he himself has become the minister of his
own offering; for a common man ought not to offer the sacrifice for the life
of the world. Let us test more closely, otherwise, what the blessed Paul says:
Consider the apostle and high priest of our confession, that is, the word of
faith which we preach. What then does he minister, to himself and to the
Father? 468 For we believe in one God, Father almighty, maker of all things
visible and invisible, and in one Lord Jesus Christ, his own Son. Therefore
let those who say that he has become priest, and stumble because of this,
teach us: what priest makes for himself the ministry, offers to himself the
sacrifice? What priest appears in equal rank and in glory with him who
receives from him the ministry? But indeed, the Son having become high priest
because of the human, he has brought to himself, and through himself to the
Father, our confession, and he is seen upon the thrones of the godhead; and
Paul writing will confirm it: Now the sum of what has been said is this: we
have such a high priest, who sat down on the right hand of the throne of the
Majesty in the heavens, a minister of the holies, and of the true tabernacle,
which the Lord pitched, and not man. Therefore he exercises priesthood in a
human manner because he has become man, and sits in council divinely because
he has remained Word.

XI. That Christ is a living God even some who have leapt away from the
doctrines of the truth do not altogether and in every way refuse to say; but
what does his own disciple say? As therefore you have received Christ Jesus
the Lord, walk in him. And we have received Christ as a living God and Lord in
truth.

XIII. The law prefigured these things beforehand, and the prophets also
preached them beforehand. For it is written in the book of Moses: You shall
take fine flour and make of it twelve loaves, and you shall set them two rows
upon the clean table before the Lord, and you shall put upon the row
frankincense and salt, and they shall be for loaves for a memorial set forth
before the Lord. For the bread, the one come down from heaven and giving life
to the world, who would it be other than Christ the Savior of all? In
imitation of him, the blessed disciples too are called loaves; for having
become partakers of him who feeds us to eternal life, they also feed through
their own writings those who hunger and thirst after righteousness. And as the
Savior is the true light, so he named the disciples light too: You are the
light of the world; so also he himself being the bread of life has granted to
his own disciples to be understood in order of the loaves. And see with me the
simple skill of the law; for he says, you shall put upon the loaves
frankincense and salt; and frankincense is a symbol of fragrancy, and the
salts of prudence and sense. Both were fully present in the holy apostles. 471
For their life was fragrant, and at least they declared, We are a fragrance of
Christ to God; and beside this they were also all-wise; wherefore they also
heard, You are the salt of the earth. And I hear the prophet David also
singing and saying concerning them: There is Benjamin a youth in ecstasy, the
princes of Judah their rulers, the princes of Zabulon, the princes of
Nephthali. For out of well-nigh every tribe of Israel the blessed disciples
had been chosen, and they were universal luminaries, holding forth the word of
life; and the paradoxical thing: for the wise of the Greeks have brilliant
eloquence and speak very smoothly; but the disciples of our Savior have become
artisans and sea-farers and fishers, having no boast of words, no abundance of
tested phrases; but unskilled in speech, rich in knowledge. And the writings
of the Greeks and the finery of phrases have been silenced, but the power of
the gospel preachings holds sway over what is under heaven. God has made
mention of these things also through the voice of Jeremiah, speaking
concerning the enemy of all, I mean of course the satan: Woe to him who
multiplies for himself the things that are not his, and binds himself heavily
with the pledge; for suddenly they will rise up that bite him, and they that
lie in wait for you will wake, and you will be for a spoil to them. The satan
gathered all men upon earth, who were not his, and showed them worshippers of
himself, weighing down his pledge; but they woke who plundered his vessels;
for he had hunted with the net of the apostolic initiation all the misled, and
they brought to God those under heaven.

XIV. The Savior distributes talents now too to his own household servants, at
least according to the parable in the gospels. Spread out then your hearts and
receive rejoicing: Become approved moneychangers; trade the thing given with
knowledge; multiply manyfold by fairnesses; great and holy-befitting is the
boast, and it procures the praises of genuineness; and, I say this letting go
what is still greater: it works a citizen of heaven, brings up to the kingdom,
makes one to be glorified together with Christ the Savior of us all. Let us
see this also from the very evangelical writings. He who took five talents
from the householder, and worked also other five besides, with what does he
come forward saying? Lord, you gave me five talents; behold, I have made other
five. Do you see how great is the boast of diligence? And what did Christ say
to him? Well, good slave and faithful; over few things you were faithful, over
many I will set you; enter into the joy of your lord. What is greater than
this for glory? What more desirable for prosperity? Praises from Christ and a
masterly vote crowning the one who labored; he names faithful the one who bore
fruit spiritually; for did he not bring the gain as to the Master? for does he
need anything in the things of himself? He himself is the fullness of all
things, and as the all-wise John says: Of his fullness all we have received.
But when we ourselves gain ourselves, and profit also others, then Christ
rejoices; he reckons our well-pleasing as wealth; and the still greater thing
I will say: the thing is made, as it were, food for him. That what I say is
true, you will learn again from the evangelical writings. For he instructed
the woman of Samaria; then, when she said, When the Christ comes, he will tell
us 473 all things, he made himself manifest saying, I am the one speaking to
you. And the woman is strained on a run, and he has initiated into the
mysteries the city of the Samaritans; and Christ knowing that many will
believe on him said to the holy disciples: I have food to eat which you know
not; for salvation feeds, as it were, the most gentle nature of the lost. The
one then is the genuine household servant; for I will go up again to the aim
of the word: he has lifted up the crown of fairness, for he has been carried
back, as it were, into the joy of his own master; but he who took the talent,
then having become slothful, and having worked altogether nothing, and having
rather hidden it in the earth, has fallen into the last penalties. Let us flee
then his imitation; let us send away from our own mind the unprofitable and
cursed sloth, that, of course, toward good efforts; let us not bury in
idleness the grace given to us by Christ. The God of all says somewhere
through the voice of Isaiah: Speak to the heart of Jerusalem; but what if the
teacher weaves out words for those accustomed to profit, while they do not lay
up the initiation in mind and heart, but only clap, and repay the initiator
with bare praises, and remain without taste of the holy teachings? Does sloth
beseem then the initiator? Then what words will he find at the divine tribunal
to help him, when he is charged with the slackness of the hearers, and says
for this reason the sloth? For he will be condemned rather, as may be seen
from the gospel parable; for he who took the talent from the householder
buried it in the earth, and brought it back having it unmultiplied, saying,
Lord, behold, you have your own. Was he then praised and honored with a
reward, or rather 474 condemned as slothful? For, he says, you ought to have
put my silver to the moneychangers, and I coming should have received my own
with usury. Therefore I spend the masterly silver upon you as skillful
moneychangers; but you, receiving it, lay up the thing given in mind and hide
it in the depth of your own hearts, knowing well that of the seeds sent into
the earth, those poured into the clods bear fruit, while the unplanted ones
are plundered.

XV. Yet the Lord was eager neither to make his godhead plain through the whole
nor to hide it altogether, the one for the sake of those then, the other for
the sake of those after these; so as to show manifest the works both of his
godhead and of his manhood, that neither should the divine power be dulled nor
the human nature disbelieved. For since a heresy was about to spring up saying
that the body was a phantom and came down from heaven, for the incorruptible
nature would not have taken flesh of a corruptible body, nor would it have
accepted a stain or a pollution or a spot; that God might assure the race of
men that he became what we are, remaining what he was, for this reason he
permitted the flesh to suffer its own things, and showed the working double,
suffering as man and working as God the same one; for he is not one and
another, even if in one way and in another.

Of the holy Cyril on, Father, into your hands I commit my spirit. Great again
and exceptional is the achievement for our souls through Christ the Savior of
us all. For since we have been justified through the faith in him, and in him
475 the blameless nature of man was found, having been enriched, and he has
made new for us the thing, that souls parting from their bodies no longer run
into Hades as aforetime, but rather are sent into the hands of the living God.
And knowing this the holy Stephen said, Lord Jesus, receive my spirit; and the
blessed Peter writes: Wherefore also those who suffer according to the will of
God, let them commit their own souls to a faithful Creator.

469 XII. Cyril, in a homily titled on, Immediately after the affliction of
those days the sun will be darkened and the moon will not give her light and
the stars will fall from heaven; but concerning that day and hour no one
knows. But when he says even this, he does not altogether signify himself; for
he did not add exactly Nor the Son of God, but in a loosed way he says, Nor
the son; that you may think something of this sort: the disciples were eager
to learn all things with exactness, and they attempted, that I may so speak,
even to busy themselves with the depths of the Lord. But the thing was far
above them. That therefore he might not, having refused the explanation as
they wished to learn, seem somehow to grieve those who were genuine, he says
in a shadowed way, But concerning that day or the hour, no one knows, nor the
angels, nor the son, except the Father; that you may think thus: even if man
has been called to sonship and has been shown a new son, yet let him not seek
the things beyond nature, nor think it right to learn the things which the
Father has not revealed even to the angels; and if an angel knows not, neither
will a man know, even if he has been called to sonship.
