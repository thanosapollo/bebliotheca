Of the same, a discourse on prayer, divided into 153 chapters.

PROLOGUE.

When I was fevered with the burning of the unclean passions, you restored me
as usual by the touch of your God-loving letters, comforting my mind that was
failing in the last extremities, and imitating the great guide and teacher
blessedly; and it is no wonder; for the distinguished things have always been
your portion, as also of blessed Jacob; for having served well for the sake of
Rachel, and having received Leah, you seek also the desired one, as having
filled out her sevens as well, I suppose. And I would not deny that, having
toiled the whole night, I caught nothing; yet, having let down the net at your
word, I caught a multitude of fish, not great ones I think, yet a hundred and
fifty-three, and these I have sent in the basket of love through the equal
number of chapters, having fulfilled your command; and I marvel at you, and
greatly envy the excellent purpose of one who loves the chapters on prayer.
For you do not simply love those that have their being under hands and on
paper through ink, but those established in the intellect through love and
freedom from resentment. But since all things are twofold, one opposite the
one, according to the wise Jesus, receive besides the letter also the spirit;
and understand that the mind of the letter altogether precedes; for if this is
not, there will not even be a letter. Therefore the manner of prayer also is
twofold, the one practical, the other contemplative; so also of number, the
one is the ready quantity, the other the signified quality; having therefore
divided the discourse on prayer into a hundred and fifty-three, we have sent
you an evangelical provision, so that you may find the delight of a symbolic
number and a triangular and hexagonal figure, showing at once a pious
knowledge of the Trinity, and at the same time the outline of this ordered
world. But the number one hundred by itself is square, and fifty-three is
triangular and spherical; for the twenty-eighth is triangular. And the
twenty-fifth is spherical; for five times five chapters. Therefore you have
the square figure, not only through the tetrad of the virtues, but also the
wise knowledge of this age, resembling the twenty-fifth, through the =79.1168=
spherical character of the times. For week moves upon week, and month upon
month, and from year to year time is rolled, and season upon season, as we see
in the movement of sun and moon, of spring, and of the rest. And the triangle
would signify to you the knowledge of the Holy Trinity. Otherwise: but if
through the multitude of the numbers you take the hundred-and-third, which is
triangular, it is fitting to understand practical, natural, theological, or
also of faith, hope, and love, gold, silver, precious stones. But such is the
number; and you would not treat with insolence the lowliness of the chapters,
as one who knows both how to be filled and how to be in want, and indeed as
remembering him who did not reject the two mites of the widow, but even
accepted them above the wealth of many others; therefore, knowing it as a
fruit of goodwill and love, you will keep it, bidding your genuine brothers to
pray for the one who is sick, that he may be well, and taking up his own bed
may walk thereafter, by the grace of Christ our true God, to whom be glory
unto the ages of ages. Amen.



CHAPTER 1. If anyone should wish to prepare fragrant incense, he will compound
the translucent frankincense, and the cassia, and the onycha, and the stacte
equally, according to the law. And these are the tetrad of the virtues; for if
they are most complete and equal, the intellect will not be betrayed.

CHAPTER 2. A soul purified through the fullness of the virtues prepares the
order of the intellect unshaken, making it receptive of the sought-for state.

CHAPTER 3. Prayer is a conversation of the intellect with God. Of what sort of
state, then, does the intellect have need, in order that it may be still, go
out unturned toward its own Master, and converse with him with nothing
mediating?

CHAPTER 4. If Moses, having tried to approach the bush burning on the earth,
is prevented until he looses the sandal of his feet, how do you yourself,
wishing to see him who is above all thought and sense, and to become a
conversation-partner with him, not loose from yourself every passionate
thought?

CHAPTER 5. First pray concerning the receiving of tears, that through mourning
you may soften the wildness that exists in your soul, and having confessed
your lawlessness against yourself to the Lord, you may obtain forgiveness from
him. =79.1169=

CHAPTER 6. Use the tears toward the accomplishment of every request; for your
Master greatly rejoices receiving prayer in tears.

CHAPTER 7. If you pour forth fountains of tears in your prayer, by no means
exalt yourself in yourself as being above the many; for your prayer has
received help, that you may be able eagerly to confess your sins, and to
propitiate the Master through the tears.

CHAPTER 8. Do not then turn the remedy of the passions into a passion, lest
you more greatly provoke the one who has given the grace; many, weeping for
sins, having forgotten the aim of the tears, have gone mad and been struck
with amazement.

CHAPTER 9. Stand laboriously, and pray intensely, and turn away the encounters
of cares and reasonings; for they trouble and throw you into uproar, that they
may dissolve you from the tension.

CHAPTER 10. When the demons see you being eager to pray truly, then they
suggest thoughts of certain affairs that are supposedly necessary, and after a
little they raise up the memory concerning them, moving the intellect toward a
search for them; and as not finding, it is greatly grieved and despondent; but
when it stands to prayer, they remind it of the things sought and remembered,
so that the intellect, being made slack toward knowledge of them, may lose the
fruitful prayer.

CHAPTER 11. Struggle to set your intellect, at the time of prayer, deaf and
speechless, and you will be able to pray.

CHAPTER 12. When a temptation meets you, or a contradiction, or it irritates,
or moves anger toward defense against the opposing one, or to break out some
voice, remember prayer and the judgment according to it; and immediately the
disorderly movement in you will be still.

CHAPTER 13. Whatever you do toward defense against a brother who has wronged
you, all of it will become a stumbling-block to you at the time of prayer.

CHAPTER 14. Prayer is an offspring of meekness and freedom from anger.

CHAPTER 15. Prayer is a putting-forth of joy and thanksgiving. =79.1172=

CHAPTER 16. Prayer is a remedy of grief and despondency.



CHAPTER 17. Having gone, sell your possessions, and give to the poor, and
having taken the cross, deny yourself, that you may be able to pray
undistractedly.

CHAPTER 18. If you wish to pray praiseworthily, deny yourself according to the
hour, and philosophize very many hard things, very many, for the sake of
prayer.

CHAPTER 19. Whatever hard thing you philosophize while enduring, of this you
will find the fruit at the time of prayer.

CHAPTER 20. Desiring to pray as one ought, do not grieve a soul; otherwise you
run in vain.

CHAPTER 21. Leave your gift, he says, before the altar, and having gone first
be reconciled with your brother, and then you will pray undisturbed; for
resentment darkens the governing faculty of the one praying, and darkens his
prayers.

CHAPTER 22. Those who heap up griefs and resentments for themselves, and seem
to pray, are like those who draw water and throw it into a pierced jar.

CHAPTER 23. If you are patient, you will always pray with joy.

CHAPTER 24. When you pray as you ought, such things will meet you, that you
may seem it to be altogether just to use anger; but there is no just anger
against the neighbor at all; for if you seek, you will find that it is
possible even without anger for the matter to be well disposed; therefore use
every device toward not breaking out in anger.

CHAPTER 25. See that, seeming to heal another, you yourself are not
unhealable, and give a cutting-off to your prayer.

CHAPTER 26. Sparing anger, you will find sparing, and you will show yourself
prudent toward conceit, and you will be among those who pray.

CHAPTER 27. Arming yourself against anger, you will never endure desire; for
this gives materials to anger, and this =79.1173= troubles the noetic eye,
damaging the state of prayer.

CHAPTER 28. Do not pray in the outer figures only, but turn your intellect to
a perception of spiritual prayer with much fear.

CHAPTER 29. Sometimes having stood suddenly to prayer you will not pray, and
sometimes having toiled greatly you will not attain the aim, so that, having
sought still more and received, you may have the achievement as an inviolable
possession.

CHAPTER 30. When an angel has stood by suddenly, all those who trouble us
stand away, and the intellect is found in much ease, praying healthily; but
sometimes, when the customary war is laid upon us, the intellect boxes, and is
not allowed to look up, for it has been previously affected by the various
passions; yet seeking further it will find, and to him who knocks intensely it
will be opened.

CHAPTER 31. Do not pray that your own wills come to be; for they do not
altogether agree with the will of God; but rather, as you were taught, pray
saying: Let your will be done in me; and in every matter thus ask him for what
is good and beneficial to the soul; but you do not altogether seek this.

CHAPTER 32. Often praying I asked that what seemed to me to be good for me
come to be, and I persisted in the request, unreasonably forcing the will of
God, and not giving it over to him, that he himself rather might manage what
he knows to be beneficial; and indeed having received, afterward I was greatly
distressed, because I rather asked that my own wish come to be; for the matter
did not meet me such as I supposed.



CHAPTER 33. What else is good but God? Therefore let us give over to him all
the things that concern us, and it will be well for us; for the good one is
altogether also a provider of good gifts.

CHAPTER 34. Do not immediately seek the request as one fetching it in power;
for he wishes to benefit you still more as you persevere with him in the
prayer; for what is higher than conversing with God, and being occupied with
the company toward him?

CHAPTER 35. Prayer is an ascent of the intellect toward God. =79.1176=

CHAPTER 36. If you long to pray, renounce all things, that you may inherit the
all.

CHAPTER 37. Pray first concerning being purified of the passions, and second
concerning being delivered from ignorance, and third from every temptation and
abandonment.

CHAPTER 38. Seek only in your prayer the righteousness and the kingdom, that
is, virtue and knowledge, and all the remaining things will be added to you.

CHAPTER 39. It is right to pray not only concerning one's own purification,
but also concerning every one of the same race, that you may imitate an
angelic manner.

CHAPTER 40. See whether you have truly stood before God in your prayer, or are
defeated by praise of men, and hasten to hunt these, using the standing of
prayer as a covering.

CHAPTER 41. Whether you pray with brothers or alone, struggle to pray not by
habit but by perception.

CHAPTER 42. The manner of prayer is collected thought with reverence and
compunction, and pain of soul in confession of faults with voiceless
groanings.

CHAPTER 43. If your intellect still looks around at the time of prayer, it
does not yet pray as a monk, but is still worldly, beautifying the outer tent.

CHAPTER 44. When praying, guard your memory with power, that it may not set
its own things before you, but may move you toward knowledge of the standing;
for the intellect is greatly accustomed to be plundered by the memory at the
time of prayer.

CHAPTER 45. Either the memory brings you fantasies of old affairs when you
pray, or new cares, or the face of one who has caused grief.

CHAPTER 46. The demon greatly envies a man praying, and uses every device to
damage his aim; therefore he does not cease moving the thoughts of affairs
through the memory, and heaving up all the passions through the flesh, that he
may be able to hinder his excellent course and the going-out toward God.

CHAPTER 47. When the most wicked demon, having done many things, is not able
to hinder the prayer of the zealous one, he slacks a little =79.1177= and
afterward takes vengeance on him after he has prayed; for either having
kindled him to anger he destroys the excellent state gathered from the prayer
in himself, or having irritated toward some irrational pleasure he insults the
intellect.

CHAPTER 48. Having prayed as one ought, expect the things that one ought not,
and stand bravely guarding your fruit; for to this you were appointed from the
beginning, to work and to keep; therefore having worked do not leave unguarded
what was toiled; otherwise you have been of no benefit praying.



CHAPTER 49. Every war that is gathered between us and the unclean spirits
comes to be concerning nothing else than spiritual prayer; for it is greatly
hostile to them and most burdensome, but to us saving and most gentle.

CHAPTER 50. What do the demons wish to work in us, gluttony, fornication, love
of money, and anger and resentment, and the remaining passions? That the
intellect, thickened from them, may not be able to pray as it ought; for the
passions of the irrational part, having begun to rule, do not allow it to be
moved rationally and to seek the Word of God.

CHAPTER 51. We pursue the virtues because of the reasons of the things that
have come to be, and these because of the Lord who gave them being, and he is
accustomed to appear in the state of prayer.

CHAPTER 52. The state of prayer is a passionless habit, snatching the
philosophic and spiritual intellect by highest eros into noetic height.

CHAPTER 53. Not only anger and desire must he who is eager to pray truly rule,
but also come to be outside of passionate thought.

CHAPTER 54. He who loves God always converses with him as with a Father,
turning away every passionate thought.

CHAPTER 55. It is not the one who has obtained impassibility who already also
prays truly; for he can be in the bare thoughts and be occupied in their
histories, and be far from God.

CHAPTER 56. Not even when the intellect does not linger in the bare thoughts
of affairs has it already also taken hold of the place of prayer; for it can
be in the contemplation of the affairs, and chatter in their reasons,
=79.1180= which, even if they are bare words, yet being contemplations of
affairs, stamp the intellect and lead it far from God.

CHAPTER 57. Even if the intellect come to be above the contemplation of bodily
nature, it has not yet perfectly seen the place of God; for it can be in the
knowledge of the intelligibles, and be varied toward it.

CHAPTER 58. If you wish to pray, there is need of God who gives prayer to the
one praying; therefore call upon him saying, "Hallowed be your name, let your
kingdom come," that is, the Holy Spirit and your only-begotten Son; for thus
he taught, saying that God is to be worshipped in Spirit and truth, that is,
the Father, since also the three are God.

CHAPTER 59. He who prays in Spirit and truth no longer honors the Creator from
the creatures, but hymns him from himself.

CHAPTER 60. If you are a theologian, you will pray truly, and if you pray
truly, you are a theologian.

CHAPTER 61. When your intellect, by the great longing toward God, little by
little as it were withdraws from the flesh, and turns away all the thoughts
from reflection or mixture, becoming full of reverence and of joy together,
then consider that you have drawn near the boundaries of prayer.

CHAPTER 62. The Holy Spirit, suffering with our weakness, visits us even when
we are unclean, and if it find our intellect praying to it in love of truth,
it mounts upon it, and destroys all the phalanx of reasonings or thoughts that
encircle it, urging it on to works of spiritual prayer.

CHAPTER 63. The rest, through alteration of the body, produce in the intellect
reasonings or thoughts and contemplations. But God does the contrary: upon the
intellect itself



he mounts, and putting knowledge into it as he wishes, and through the
intellect calming the incontinence of the body.

CHAPTER 64. No one who loves true prayer and is angry or resentful is outside
of madness; for he is like one who wishes to see sharply and is troubling his
own eyes. =79.1181=

CHAPTER 65. If you long to pray, do nothing of the things opposing prayer,
that God drawing near may journey with you.

CHAPTER 66. Do not form the Divine in yourself when praying, nor allow your
intellect to be stamped toward some form; but approach immaterial to the
immaterial, and you will understand.

CHAPTER 67. Guard against the snares of the opposing ones. For it happens that
when you are praying purely and undisturbed, a strange and foreign form
suddenly stands upon you, to lead you away into conceit, guessing the Divine
to be there, so as to persuade you that what was suddenly uncovered to you is
the Divine as quantitative; but the Divine is without quantity and without
figure.

CHAPTER 68. When the envious demon is not able to move the memory in prayer,
then he forces the temperament of the body toward making some strange fantasy
for the intellect, and forming it; and he who has a habit in thoughts is
easily bent, and being eager toward immaterial and formless knowledge is
deceived, holding smoke instead of light.

CHAPTER 69. Stand upon your watch, guarding your intellect from thoughts at
the time of prayer, to stand upon its own stillness, that he who suffers with
those who do not know may visit you also, and then you will receive a most
glorious gift of prayer.

CHAPTER 70. You will not be able to pray purely, being entangled with material
affairs and shaken by continual cares; for prayer is a putting-away of
thoughts.

CHAPTER 71. One bound cannot run, nor can an intellect enslaved to passions
see the place of spiritual prayer; for it is dragged and carried about from
the passionate thought, and does not stand unshaken.

CHAPTER 72. When the intellect now prays purely, unerringly and truly, at that
time the demons no longer come in from the left, but from the right; for they
suggest to it a glory of God, and some figuration of the things dear to sense,
so that it seems to have attained perfectly the aim concerning prayer. And
this, a wondrous and gnostic man declared, comes to be from the passion of
vainglory, and from the demon that touches the place according to the brain,
and pulses the veins. =79.1184=

CHAPTER 73. I think the demon, touching the said place, turns the light around
the intellect as he wishes, and thus the passion of vainglory is moved, into a
reasoning forming the intellect, light as it thinks, toward the refashioning
of the divine and essential knowledge; and such a one, not being troubled by
fleshly and unclean passions, but standing by purely as it seems, thinks that
no opposing activity any longer comes to be in himself, whence he suspects the
manifestation that has come to him from the demon, who also forms him, as we
said before, to be divine.

CHAPTER 74. The angel of God, standing by, by word alone stops all the
opposing activity from us, and moves the light of the intellect to work
unerringly.

CHAPTER 75. The saying in the Apocalypse that the angel brings incense, that
he may give it to the prayers of the saints, I think is this grace through the
angel



being worked; for he produces knowledge of true prayer, so that the intellect
thereafter stands outside of every shaking, both acedia and faint-heartedness.

CHAPTER 76. The bowls of incense are said to be the prayers of the saints,
which the twenty-four elders carry.

CHAPTER 77. And a bowl is to be understood as the friendship toward God, or
the perfect and spiritual love, in which prayer is worked in Spirit and truth.

CHAPTER 78. When you think you have no need of tears in your prayer for sin,
consider how far you stand from God, owing to be in him through all, and you
will weep more warmly.

CHAPTER 79. And indeed recognizing your measures, you will mourn gladly,
calling yourself wretched according to Isaiah, as being unclean, and having
unclean lips, and existing in the midst of such a people, you dare on the
contrary to stand by the Lord Sabaoth.

CHAPTER 80. If you pray truly, you will find much assurance, and angels will
come together with you and will illuminate the reasons of the things that come
to be. =79.1185=

CHAPTER 81. Know that the holy angels urge us on to prayer, and stand by
together with us, rejoicing at once, and praying for us; if therefore we are
negligent, and receive opposing reasonings, we greatly provoke them, in that
they struggle so much for us, but we do not even wish to entreat God for
ourselves; but despising their liturgy, and leaving their God and Master, we
converse with unclean demons.

CHAPTER 82. Pray mildly and undisturbed, and chant understandingly and
rhythmically, and you will be as a young of an eagle lifted on high.

CHAPTER 83. Psalmody calms the passions, and works the incontinence of the
body to be still. But prayer prepares the intellect to work its own activity.

CHAPTER 84. Prayer is a fitting activity, to the dignity of the intellect, or
rather a better and sincere judgment of it.

CHAPTER 85. Psalmody is a type of the varied wisdom, but prayer is a prelude
of the immaterial and varied knowledge.

CHAPTER 86. Knowledge is most excellent, for it is a fellow-worker of prayer,
awakening the noetic power of the intellect toward contemplation of the divine
knowledge.

CHAPTER 87. If you have not yet received a gift of prayer or of psalmody, sit
in wait, and you will receive.

CHAPTER 88. And he spoke to them also a parable toward their needing always to
pray and not to faint; therefore do not faint for now, nor be despondent as
not having received; for you will receive later; and he added to the parable:
"For if I do not fear God, and I am not ashamed before man, yet because the
woman causes me toils, I will do her vengeance"; thus then God also will do
the vengeance of those who cry to him night and day, speedily. Be of good
cheer, therefore, persevering laboriously in the holy prayer.

CHAPTER 89. Do not wish the things concerning you to come to be as it seems to
you, but as it is pleasing to God, and you will be undisturbed and thankful in
your prayer. =79.1188=

CHAPTER 90. Even if you seem to be with God, guard against the demon of
fornication. For he is greatly a deceiver and most envious, and wishes to be
quicker than the movement and sobriety of your intellect, and to drag it from
God as it stands by him with reverence and fear.



CHAPTER 91. If you take care of prayer, prepare yourself toward onsets of
demons, and endure steadfastly the scourges from them; for they will come upon
you as wild beasts, and will maltreat all your body.

CHAPTER 92. Prepare yourself as an experienced contestant, even if you
suddenly see a fantasy, not to be shaken, even if a sword drawn against you,
or a torch holding your face, not to be troubled, even if some unpleasant and
bloody form, that your soul by no means fall, but stand confessing your good
confession, and you will more easily look upon your enemies.

CHAPTER 93. He who bears the grievous things will also obtain the very joyful,
and he who endures in the unpleasant things will not lack the pleasant.

CHAPTER 94. See that the wicked demons not deceive you through some vision;
but become collected, turning to prayer, and call upon God, that if the
thought is from him, he himself may illuminate you, but if not, that he may
quickly drive the deceiver from you; and take courage, that the dogs will not
stand, when you use the approach to God in a fiery way; for immediately, being
scourged invisibly and unseen by the power of God, they will be driven far.

CHAPTER 95. It is right that you not be ignorant of this deceit either, that
at a time the demons divide themselves, and if you seem to seek help, the rest
enter in angelic figures, driving out the first, toward your being deceived by
them, in judgment, as being supposedly holy angels.

CHAPTER 96. Take care of much humility, and an insolence of demons will by no
means fasten upon your soul, and a scourge will not draw near your tent,
because he will command his angels concerning you to guard you, and they will
invisibly drive all the opposing activity from you.

CHAPTER 97. He who takes care of pure prayer will hear noises and knocks and
voices and outrages from demons, but he will not fall together, nor will he
betray the =79.1189= reasoning, saying to God: I will not fear evils, because
you are with me, and the like.

CHAPTER 98. At the time of such temptations, use a short and intense prayer.

CHAPTER 99. If the demons threaten you to appear suddenly from the air, and to
strike you with amazement, and to snatch your intellect, or as beasts to
injure your flesh, do not be terrified at them, nor altogether care for their
threat; for they try to frighten you, testing whether you altogether attend to
them, whether you have perfectly despised them, whether you stand by God
almighty, and creator, and provider of the all, in the prayer.

CHAPTER 100. Why have you so irrationally stood by him, as one who has passed
by his unsurpassable fear, and is frightened at gnats and beetles? Or have you
not heard the one who says: "You shall fear the Lord your God"? and again:
"Whom all things shudder at and tremble from the face of his power"? and the
rest.

CHAPTER 101. As bread is food for the body, and virtue for the soul, so also
spiritual prayer is food of the intellect.

CHAPTER 102. Do not pray in a Pharisaic way, but in a publican's way, in the
holy place of prayer, that you too may be justified by the Lord.

CHAPTER 103. Struggle not to curse anyone in your prayer, lest you dissolve
the things you build, making your prayer abominable.



CHAPTER 104. Let the debtor of the ten thousand talents instruct you, that if
you do not forgive the debtor, you yourself will not obtain the forgiveness;
for he handed him over, he says, to the torturers.

CHAPTER 105. Send away the necessities of the body, in the standing of prayer,
lest being pricked by a flea, or a louse, or a gnat, or a fly, you be deprived
of the greatest gain of your prayer.

CHAPTER 106. It has come to us, that as one of the saints was praying, the
wicked one so resisted, that at the same time as he stretched out the hands,
that one was transformed into a lion, and raised the forefeet upright, and
struck his own claws on both sides into the two loins of the contestant, and
would not stand away before he lowered the hands. But he never slackened these
until he completed the customary prayers. =79.1192=

CHAPTER 107. We have known such a thing to have happened also to John the
Little, who was still in a pit, or rather a super-great monk, who remained
unmoved from the company of God, the demon having been wound around in the
form of a dragon, and chewing his flesh, and belching upon his face.

CHAPTER 108. You have altogether also read the lives of the Tabennesiote
monks, as it says, that while the abba was speaking a word to the brothers,
two vipers came under his feet; and he, having undisturbed made them as an
arch, received them within, until he ceased speaking the word, and then he
showed them to the brothers, explaining the matter.

CHAPTER 109. Concerning another spiritual man again we have read, that as he
was praying, a viper coming fastened upon his foot; but he did not let down
the hands before he completed the customary prayer, and he who loved God above
himself was not harmed at all.

CHAPTER 110. Have an unwandering eye in your prayer, and having denied your
flesh and the soul, live according to intellect.

CHAPTER 111. To another holy man praying intensely, being still in the desert,
demons standing by for two weeks tossed this one as a ball, and shook him into
the air, receiving him on the mat, and they were not at all able to bring down
his intellect from the fiery prayer.

CHAPTER 112. To another again, God-loving and taking forethought of prayer,
walking in the desert, two angels stood by and were in the middle of him,
journeying with him; but he did not attend to them at all, lest he be deprived
of the better. For he remembered the apostolic saying that says: "Neither
angels, nor principalities, nor powers will be able to separate us from the
love of Christ."

CHAPTER 113. A monk becomes equal to angels through true prayer.

CHAPTER 114. Longing to see the face of the Father who is in the heavens, do
not seek at all to receive a form or figure at the time of prayer.

CHAPTER 115. Do not long to see angels, or powers, or Christ sensibly, lest
you become completely delirious, receiving a wolf instead of =79.1193= a
shepherd and worshipping the enemy demons.

CHAPTER 116. The beginning of deception is vainglory of intellect, from which
the intellect being moved tries to circumscribe the Divine in figure and
forms.

CHAPTER 117. I will say this of my own, which I have also said to younger
ones: Blessed is the intellect that at the time of prayer has acquired perfect
formlessness.

CHAPTER 118. Blessed is the intellect that, praying undistractedly, always
takes on more longing toward God.



CHAPTER 119. Blessed is the intellect that at the time of prayer becomes
immaterial and possessionless.

CHAPTER 120. Blessed is the intellect that at the time of prayer has acquired
perfect insensibility.

CHAPTER 121. Blessed is a monk who reckons himself the offscouring of all.

CHAPTER 122. Blessed is a monk who sees the salvation and progress of all as
his own, with all joy.

CHAPTER 123. Blessed is a monk who reckons all men as God after God.

CHAPTER 124. A monk is one who has been separated from all and fitted together
with all.

CHAPTER 125. A monk is one who considers himself with all, because he seems to
see himself in each without omission.

CHAPTER 126. This one accomplishes prayer, who always brings as fruit all his
first-thinking to God.

CHAPTER 127. Stand away from every lie and every oath as a monk, and one
longing to pray; otherwise you fashion in vain what is unfitting.

CHAPTER 128. If you wish to pray in spirit, do not draw up anything from
flesh, and you will not have a cloud opposing you at the time of prayer.

CHAPTER 129. Entrust to God the need of the body, and you will be manifest as
also trusting to him that of the spirit. =79.1196=

CHAPTER 130. If you attain the promises, you will reign; therefore looking
toward these, you will gladly bear the present poverty.

CHAPTER 131. Do not refuse poverty and affliction, the materials of
unburdensome prayer.

CHAPTER 132. Let the bodily virtues be hostages for you toward the psychic,
and the psychic toward the spiritual. And these toward the immaterial
knowledge.

CHAPTER 133. Watch, when praying against reasonings, whether they would easily
abate, whence this has come about, lest you suffer an ambush and being
deceived give yourself over.

CHAPTER 134. There is a time when the demons suggest reasonings to you, and
again irritate you supposedly to pray against them, or to contradict them, and
they withdraw willingly, that being deceived you may think concerning yourself
that you have begun to conquer the reasonings and to frighten the demons.

CHAPTER 135. A prayer against a passion, or a demon troubling you: remember
the one who says: "I will pursue my enemies and I will overtake them, and I
will not turn away until they fail, I will crush them, and they will not be
able to stand, they will fall under my feet," and the rest. And these you will
say seasonably, arming yourself with humility against the opponents.

CHAPTER 136. Do not think you have acquired a virtue, not having first arrayed
yourself even to blood for it; for one must stand against sin even to death,
contestingly and blamelessly according to the divine Apostle.

CHAPTER 137. If you benefit someone, you will be harmed by another, that being
wronged you may yield, or do something unfitting, and scatter badly the things
you gathered well. For this is the aim of the wicked demons; therefore one
must attend prudently.

CHAPTER 138. Receive the burdensome onsets of demons, taking thought how you
may stand away from their slavery.



CHAPTER 139. By night the demons seek to trouble the spiritual teacher through
themselves. And after day they surround him through men with circumstances and
slanders and dangers. =79.1197=

CHAPTER 140. Do not refuse the fullers; for even if they strike, treading, and
stretching they card, yet through these your perception becomes bright.

CHAPTER 141. As long as you have not renounced the passions, but your
intellect opposes virtue and truth, you will not find fragrant incense in your
bosom.

CHAPTER 142. Do you long to pray? Having migrated from the things here, have
your citizenship in the heavens through all, not by a simply bare word, but by
angelic practice and more divine knowledge.

CHAPTER 143. If only when faring ill you remember the Judge, as he is fearful
and impartial, you have not yet learned to serve the Lord in fear, and to
rejoice in him in trembling. For know that even in the spiritual easings and
feastings one must rather worship him with reverence and shame.

CHAPTER 144. A discerning man is the one who has not, before perfect
repentance, allowed himself to drop the painful memory of his own sins, and of
the judgment in eternal fire, the exacting of these.

CHAPTER 145. He who is held in sins and provocations, and dares shamelessly to
stretch himself toward knowledge of more divine things, or even treading upon
immaterial prayer, let this one receive the apostolic rebuke, as it is not
without danger for him to pray with uncovered and unveiled head. "For she
ought," he says, "to have authority upon the head, because of the angels
standing by," clothing herself with fitting shame and humility.

CHAPTER 146. As the ungraspable and intense vision of the sun at still midday
and most violent blazing will not benefit one with ophthalmia, so neither
would the impression of the fearful and supernatural prayer in spirit and
truth benefit at all the passionate and unclean intellect, but even on the
contrary it raises the Divine to indignation against himself.

CHAPTER 147. If he who is without need and impartial did not receive the one
who approached the altar with a gift until he was reconciled with the neighbor
who was grieved toward him, consider of how much watch and discernment there
is need, that we may give acceptable incense to God on the noetic altar.

CHAPTER 148. Do not be word-loving, nor glory-loving, otherwise the sinners
will no longer work upon your back, but upon your face, and you will be a
delight to them at the time of prayer, being drawn out and baited by them in
outlandish reasonings.

CHAPTER 149. Attention seeking prayer will find prayer; for prayer also
follows attention, if anything else does, upon which one must be zealous.

CHAPTER 150. As sight is better than all the senses, so also prayer is more
divine than all the virtues.

CHAPTER 151. The praise of prayer is not simply the quantity, but the quality,
and this the ones who went up into the temple make clear, and the, "When you
pray, do not babble"; and the rest.

CHAPTER 152. As long as you attend to the proportion of the body, and your
intellect tends the delights of the tent, you have not yet seen the place of
prayer; but far from you is the blessed way of this.



CHAPTER 153. When standing to prayer you come to be above every other joy,
then you have truly found prayer.
