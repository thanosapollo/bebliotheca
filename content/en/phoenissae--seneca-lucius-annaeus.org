** Oedipus Antigona
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1-h1
:END:

Guide of a blind father, only comfort of this spent
side, daughter, whom it was worth so much to me
to have begotten even like this: leave your unlucky
father. Why turn my wandering step back onto the
straight path? Let me fall. Alone I will find a better
road, the one I seek, that can draw me out of this
life and lift sky and earth from the sight of this
unholy head. How little this hand has done! I do
not see the day that knows our crime, yet I am seen.
Loose the clinging hand from me now, and let the
blind foot go wherever it will. I will go, I will go
where my Cithaeron throws out its broken ridges;
where Actaeon, having ranged the mountain swift
among the rocks, lay as strange prey to his own
hounds; where through the dark wood and the forest
of a shadowed glen a mother drove her sisters, struck
by the god, and, glad in the evil, held out a head
fixed on a shaking ivy-wand; or where Zethus' bull
ran, dragging a mocked body, where through bristling
brambles the blood of a fierce bull still shows the
flight; or where Ino's cliff presses the deep sea with
a huge peak, where a mother, fleeing one crime and
making a new one, leapt into the strait to drown her
child and herself. Happy those to whom a kinder
fortune gave such good mothers.

There is another place of mine in these woods that
claims me. I will seek it at a hard run. My step will
not hesitate. I will go there stripped of every guide.
Why do I delay my own seat? Cithaeron, give death
back, restore that lodging of mine, so I may die an
old man where I should have died an infant. Take
the old punishment. Always bloody, savage, cruel,
fierce, whether you kill or spare: this corpse has
long been yours. Finish the father's charge, and now
the mother's too. My spirit is eager to carry out the
ancient penalties. Why hold me, daughter, bound by
a pestilent love? Why hold me? My father calls.
I follow, I follow. Spare me now. Laius, wearing the
bloody badge of the stolen kingdom, rages. Look:
with hostile hands he seeks and gouges these empty
faces. Daughter, do you see your father? I see him.
At last spit out the hated breath, deserter soul, brave
only in a part of yourself. Drop the slack delays of
a long punishment and let death in whole. Why do
I sluggishly drag on this life? Can I do no crime
now? I can, wretch that I am. I warn you: I leave
my father. I leave, girl. After my mother I fear
everything.

** Ant.
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1-h2

No force, father, will loose my hand from your body.
No one will ever snatch me as your companion. Let
my brothers hunt with iron the famous house of
Labdacus, those rich kingdoms. The chief share of
my father's great realm is mine: the father himself.
The brother who holds Theban scepters, having seized
the kingdom, will not take this man from me, nor
the other driving Argive troops. Not if Jupiter should
thunder with the world torn up, and a bolt fall in
the middle of our clasp, will I let this hand go. You
may forbid me, father: I will steer you though you
refuse, I will guide the unwilling step. Do you make
for level ground? I go. Do you seek the cliffs? I do
not block you; I go first. Use me as guide wherever
you will. For two of us every road is chosen: you
cannot die without me; with me, you can. Here a
high crag rises in a steep ridge and looks far over
the spaces of the sea below: shall we seek this?
Here a bare rock hangs; here the split earth gapes
with broken jaws: shall we seek this? Here a greedy
torrent falls and rolls the worn fragments of a fallen
mountain: shall we rush into this? While I am first,
I go where you will. I do not beg, I do not urge.
Do you long to be put out, and is death your greatest
prayer, father? If you die, I go before; if you live,
I follow. But turn your mind, call back the old heart,
and master defeated troubles with great strength.
Stand. In evils so great, to be beaten is to die.

** Oedip.
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h2-h1
:END:

Whence in an unholy house this rare pattern?
Whence this girl unlike her own stock? Fortune,
do you believe it? Is anyone pious from me? It
would never be, I know my fates well, except to
do harm. Nature herself turns to new laws: a river
rolled back would drive its swift waters to the
spring, and the sun's lamp would bring night,
the evening star would make day. So that something
may be added to my miseries, we too shall be pious.
The only safety for Oedipus is not to be safe. Let
me avenge a father still unavenged. Right hand,
why do you hang idle in exacting the penalty?
Whatever has been exacted so far, you gave to the
mother. Let go of your father's hand, high-hearted
girl: you prolong my funeral and lead the long
rites of a father still alive. At last cover this hated
body with earth. You sin with an honest mind: you
call it piety to drag an unburied father. He who
forces a man unwilling to die stands on a level
with him who checks a man in haste; yet they are
not on a level: I judge the second heavier. To
forbid a man who longs to die is to kill him. I
would rather be ordered than have death torn from
me. Leave off what you have begun, girl: the right
of my life and death is mine. I left kingdoms
gladly; I keep the kingship of myself. If you are
a faithful companion, hand your father the sword,
the one known by a father's killing. Do you hand
it over? Or do the sons hold that too, with the
kingdom? Wherever there is need of a crime, let
it be there. I leave it. Let my son have it, but
both of them. Better pile flames and a vast mound:
I will throw myself on the high pyre. I will cling
to the fires, climb the funeral heap, unlock this
hard breast, and give to ash whatever still lives
in me. Where is the savage sea? Lead me where
a ridge has been thrown down in high rocks, where
the swift Ismenus draws its grim shallows. Lead
me where the wild beasts are, where the strait is,
where the sheer place is, if you are a guide. A man
about to die is pleased to go there, where the Sphinx
sat on a high crag, weaving traps with her half-wild
mouth. Steer my steps here. Halt your father here.
Let that dread seat not stand empty: set a greater
monster back in place. Sitting on this rock I will
speak dark words of our fortune that no one may
untie. Whoever you are who plough the lands once
held by the Assyrian king, and Cadmus' grove
marked by the snake, where holy Dirce hides, and
worship as a suppliant; whoever drinks the Eurotas
and honors Sparta, famous for the twin brothers;
and you, farmer, who shear Elis and Parnassus and
the Boeotian fields of rich soil: turn your mind.
The savage plague of Thebes, joining grief-bringing
words in blind measures: what did she set down
like this? What so inextricable? Son-in-law of his
grandfather and rival of his own father, brother of
his children and parent of his brothers; a grandmother
bore children in one birth to her husband, and
grandsons to herself. Who unfolds monsters so
great? I myself, who took the spoils of the beaten
Sphinx, will stick, a slow interpreter of my own
fate.

Why waste further words? Why try to soften a
wild breast with prayers? This sits in my mind:
to pour out this soul that has long wrestled with
death, and to seek the dark. For this night is not
deep enough for my crime. I want to be sunk in
Tartarus, and if there is anything beyond Tartarus.
At last I want what has long been due. I cannot
be kept from death. Will you refuse me iron? Will
you dodge the paths that harm by a fall, and stop
a neck from being set in tight nooses? Will you
take away the herbs that bring death? What, in
the end, will that care of yours gain? Death is
everywhere. God saw to this best: anyone can
snatch life from a man, but no one can snatch
death; a thousand ways to it lie open. I ask for
nothing. My spirit is used to using a bare right
hand well. Right hand, come now with all your
force, all your pain, all your strength. I do not
mark one place for our wound. I am guilty entire:
exact death where you will. Break open the breast
and tear out the heart that can hold so many
crimes; lay bare all the folds of the guts. Let the
broken throat sound under driven blows, and torn
veins flow with nails driven in. Or turn your wrath
where you are used to: cut these wounds open
again and soak them with much blood and rot.
By this way draw out the hard, unconquerable
soul. And you, father, wherever you stand as judge
of my punishments: I never believed this crime
could be paid enough by any penalty, nor was I
content with that death, nor did I buy myself back
in part. I wanted to die for you limb by limb.
Exact the debt at last. Now I pay the penalty;
then I gave you funeral offerings. Be here, and
press the idle right hand inward, and sink it more.
Then it was timid: it tasted the head with a small
draught, and barely drew out the eyes that longed
to follow. That spirit still clings in me, it clings,
when the face pressed on a refusing hand. You
shall hear the truth, Oedipus: you dug out your
eyes less boldly than you offered them. Now put
the hand into the brain. Finish death on the side
where I began to die.

** Antig.
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4-h1

A few words, great-hearted father, I beg you to
hear with a quieted mind from a daughter to be
pitied. I do not ask to lead you back to the show
of the old house and the habit of a kingdom strong
in famous flower, or that you should bear, with a
slack and peaceful breast, angers not even broken
by the mere delay of time. And this was fitting
for a man of such strength: not to lie under pain,
nor, beaten by evils, to turn the back. It is not
virtue, father, as you think, to fear life, but to
stand against huge evils and not to turn and give
ground. The man who has trampled the fates,
thrown away and cut off the goods of life, and
loaded his own misfortunes himself, who has need
of no god: why should he long for death, or why
seek it? Both belong to the fearful. No one has
scorned dying who has coveted it. The man whose
evils can go no further is set in a safe place. Which
of the gods, even grant the will, can add anything
to your evils? You yourself can no longer, except
this: to think yourself worthy of killing. You are
not. No fault has touched this breast. And call
yourself more innocent, father, because you are
innocent even against the gods' will. What is it
that maddens you, that has fastened new goads
on your pain? What drives you to the seats below?
What drives you from these? That you may lack
the day? You lack it. That you may flee a house
noble with high walls, and your country? Country
has already died for you while you live. Do you
flee sons and mother? Fortune has removed you
from the sight of all, and whatever death can take
from anyone, this life has taken from you. The
tumult of a kingdom? The crowd of your former
fortune has been ordered away from you. Whom,
father, do you flee?

** Oedip.
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4-h2
:END:

I flee myself. I flee this breast that knows all
the crimes, I flee this hand, and this sky and the
gods, and I flee the dread crimes I did while
innocent. Do I press this soil where fruit-bearing
Ceres rises? Do I draw these airs with a pestilent
mouth? Do I fill myself with a draught of water,
or enjoy any gift of the kindly mother, the earth?
Do I, unholy, incestuous, accursed, touch a chaste
hand? Do I take in with the ear any sounds through
which I might hear the name of father or of son?
Would that I could cut these paths and, driving
in my hands, tear out all the way by which voices
travel, where the approach for words lies open
in a narrow track. Daughter, then your unhappy
father would have fled the sense of you, who are
part of my crimes. The unholy thing clings and
breaks open again and again, and the ears pour
in whatever you, eyes, gave me. Why do I not
send this head, heavy with darkness, to the eternal
shades of Dis? Why do I hold my ghosts here?
Why do I weigh on the earth and wander mixed
with those above? What evil is left? Kingdom,
parents, children, virtue too, and the rare glory
of a clever mind have perished. A hostile lot has
taken all from me. Tears remained: I tore those
from myself as well.

Stand off. My mind admits no prayers, and seeks
a new penalty equal to the crimes. And what
penalty can be equal? Death was decreed even
for the infant. Who ever drew lots so sad? I had
not yet seen the day, I had not yet loosed the delays
of the closed womb, and already I was feared.
At once night seized some at birth and stole them
from the new light. Death went before me. Someone
inside a mother's flesh has borne the doom of a
hasty fate: but did he also sin? Hidden, buried,
doubtful even whether I was, a god drove me as
the accused of an unspeakable crime. With that
witness a father condemned me, and leapt across
the tender feet with hot iron, and sent me into
the deep woods as feed for beasts and savage birds
that guilty Cithaeron often rears, stained with
royal blood. But the man a god condemned, a
father cast off, death also fled. I kept faith with
Delphi: I attacked my father and laid him low
with an unholy killing. Another piety will pay
for this: I killed my father, but I loved my mother.
I am ashamed to speak the wedding-song and our
torches. Force yourself to suffer these penalties
too, though unwilling: tell a crime unknown,
savage, unused, that peoples would shudder at,
that no age would not deny was done, that would
shame a parricide. Onto a father's bed I carried
hands sprinkled with a father's blood, and for the
crime I received a greater crime as pay. The
father's deed is light: a mother was led into my
chamber, and, lest there be too little crime, she
was fertile. Nature can bear no charge greater
than this. If there is still one more, we have given
those who could do it. I threw away the scepter,
pay of a father's killing, and this again has armed
other hands. I know the fate of my kingdom best:
no one will bear it without holy blood. A father's
mind forebodes great evils. The seeds of the coming
ruin are already thrown. The faith of the pact is
scorned. This one refuses to yield the seized power;
that one calls on right and the gods who witnessed
the struck treaty, and as an exile stirs Argos and
the Greek cities to arms. No light ruin comes to
weary Thebes: spears, flames, wounds press, and
if there is any evil greater than these, so that no
one may not know they were born from me.

Have they any love of a parent or of what is
fair, those greedy for blood, power, arms, deceit,
dreadful, criminal, to put it shortly, mine? They
vie in every crime and count nothing, when anger
drives them headlong, and sons born through crime
think no crime is crime. No shame of an afflicted
father touches them, no country: the breast, stunned
for a kingdom, rages. I know where they are
carried, what huge things they prepare to set in
motion, and therefore I seek a road of timely death
and hurry to die while no one in my house is more
guilty than I. Daughter, why do you weep, fallen
at my knees? Why tame the untamed with a prayer?
Fortune has this one hold by which I can be taken,
unconquered by others: you alone can soften hard
feelings, you alone can teach piety in our house.
Nothing is heavy or wretched to me that I know
you have willed. Only command. This Oedipus,
if you bid it, will swim the Aegean straits, and
will take in his mouth the flames the earth vomits
from the Sicilian mountain, rolling balls of fire,
and will offer himself to the serpent that rages
savage at Hercules' theft of the grove. If you bid
it, he will give his liver to the birds. If you bid
it, he will even live.

Messenger, Oedipus, Antigone

** Iocasta Satelles

Happy Agave: she carried in the hand that did
it a dreadful deed, and, a maenad bloody from
a son given in pieces, she took the spoil. She did
the crime, but the wretch did not run to meet her
own crime. This is light, that I am guilty: I made
the guilty. This too is still light: I bore the guilty.
It was wanting to my troubles that I should love
an enemy as well. Winter has three times laid
down its snows, and Ceres has three times already
lain down under the sickle, while a son wanders
as an exile and lacks his country, and as a fugitive
asks the help of Greek kings. He is son-in-law
to Adrastus, whose power rules the sea the Isthmus
splits. This man draws his own peoples and seven
kingdoms with him to help his son-in-law. What
I should wish, or what I should decide, I scarcely
know. He claims the kingdom back: the cause of
the one claiming is good, the cause of the one
claiming it this way is bad. What vows shall I
make as a parent? I see a son on both sides. I
can do nothing piously with piety safe: whatever
I wish for one son will be the other's harm. But
though I love both with equal feeling, the mind
leans where the better cause and the worse lot
draw it, always favoring the weak. Fortune binds
the wretched more to their own.

Queen, while you raise tearful complaints and
wear out time, a savage battle-line is here in bared
arms. Already the bronze stirs war, and the

give love back to the brothers, peace to all, and
check unholy arms by a mother's standing in the

If you seek kingdoms and the hand cannot be
empty of a savage scepter, any land in the whole

From here Tmolus, known to Bacchus, lifts its
ridges, where broad spaces lie for fruit-bearing

are heavier than exile for all. Now set before you
the evils of war, the doubtful turns of uncertain
Mars. Though you drag all the strength of Greece
with you, though the soldier unfold arms far and
wide, the fortune of war is always in a doubtful
place. Whatever Mars decides, the sword makes
two men equal, though they be unequal; and blind
Chance turns hope and fear. You seek an uncertain
prize, a certain crime. Grant that all the gods
have favored your vows: the citizens have yielded
and, turned away, have sought flight; the soldier,
lying in a deadly disaster, has covered the fields.
You may exult, and as victor wear the spoils of
a thrown-down brother: the palm must be broken.
What kind of war do you think this, in which the
victor admits an accursed crime if he rejoices?
This man whom you, unhappy, long to beat, you
will mourn when you have beaten him. Come,
dismiss the unlucky fights, free the country from
fear, the parents from grief.
