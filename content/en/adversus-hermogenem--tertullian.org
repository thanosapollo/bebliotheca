* Chapter 1
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

We are accustomed, for the sake of brevity, to prescribe against heretics from
their posteriority. For as far as the rule of truth is prior, which even
foretold future heresies, so far later doctrines of every kind will be
prejudged heresies, because they are those that were being foretold by the
older rule of coming truth. But Hermogenes' doctrine is so new. In short he is
to this day a man in the world, and by nature also a heretic, even turbulent,
who thinks talkativeness eloquence, and reckons impudence constancy, and
judges it the office of a good conscience to curse each one. Besides he paints
unlawfully, marries constantly, defends the law of God toward lust, despises
it toward art, twice a forger, both by branding-iron and by stylus, wholly an
adulterer, both of preaching and of flesh, since he also stinks with the
contagion of those who marry, and this apostolic Hermogenes himself has not
persevered in the rule. But let the person see to it; my question is with the
doctrine. He does not seem to acknowledge another Christ the Lord in another
way; yet he makes another him whom he acknowledges otherwise; rather he takes
away all that God is, unwilling that He made the universe from nothing. For
turned from Christians to philosophers, from the church into the academy and
the porch, he took from there, from the Stoics, to set matter with the Lord,
which itself also always was, neither born nor made, having no beginning at
all nor end, from which the Lord afterward made all things.

* Chapter 2
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h2
:END:

This first shadow, plainly without light, the worst painter colored with those
argumentations, laying down in advance that the Lord made all things either
from Himself or from nothing or from something, so that, when he has shown
that He could have made them neither from Himself nor from nothing, he may
then confirm what remains, that He made them from something, and so that that
something was matter. He denies that He could make from Himself, because
whatever the Lord had made from Himself would have been parts of Himself;
further, that He does not come into parts, as indivisible and unchangeable and
always the same, inasmuch as He is Lord. Otherwise if He had made something
from Himself, something would have been of Himself. But everything, both what
was made and what made, must be held imperfect, because it would be made from
a part and would make from a part. Or if the whole had made the whole, He
would have had to be at once both whole and not whole, because He would have
had to be whole, in order to make Himself, and not whole, in order to be made
from Himself. Further, most difficult. For if He were, He would not be made,
for He would be; but if He were not, He would not make, because He would be
nothing. But him who always is, not to be made, but to be unto the age of
ages. Therefore that He did not make from Himself, who would not be of such a
condition that He could have made from Himself. Likewise he contends that He
could not make from nothing, defining the Lord as good and best, who as much
wills to make good and best things as He is; rather that He both wills and
makes nothing that is not good and best. Therefore all things ought to have
been made by Him good and best according to His own condition. But evils also
are found made by Him, certainly not from His choice nor from His will;
because if from choice and will, He would make nothing incongruous and
unworthy of Himself. What therefore He did not make by His own choice must be
understood as made from the fault of some thing, from matter without doubt.

* Chapter 3
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3
:END:

He adds also another thing: that God, always God, was also Lord, never not
God. Further, that He could in no way have been held always Lord, just as also
always God, if there had not always been something before whose Lord He
would always be held. Therefore matter always was for God the Lord. This
conjecture of his I will hasten from this point to destroy, which I have
thought it right to add thus far on account of those who do not understand, so
that they may know that the rest of the arguments also are as much understood
as refuted. We say the name of God always was with Himself and in Himself, but
Lord not always. For the condition of each is different. God is the name of
His substance, that is of divinity; but Lord not of substance, but of power.
Substance always was with its name, which is God; afterward Lord, namely the
mention of a thing acceding. For from the time when those things began to be
toward which the power of the Lord would act, from that time by the accession
of power He both was made and was called Lord, because God is also Father and
God is also Judge, yet not therefore always Father and Judge, because always
God. For He could not be Father before the Son, nor Judge before the offense.
But there was a time when there was not for Him the offense and the Son, which
would make Him Judge and which would make Him Father. So also He was not Lord
before those things of which He would exist as Lord, but only going to be Lord
at some time, as Father through the Son, as Judge through the offense, so also
Lord through those things which He had made to serve Him. Do I seem to you to
be arguing, Hermogenes? Scripture stoutly patronizes us, which distinguished
both names for Him, and showed each in its own time. For God indeed, which He
always was, it names at once: In the beginning God made heaven and earth; and
thereafter, as long as He was making those things of which He was going to be
Lord, it sets God only. And God said, and God made, and God saw, and nowhere
yet Lord. But when He had completed the universe, and most of all man himself,
who was properly going to understand the Lord, He is also surnamed Lord. Then
He also joined the name Lord: And the Lord God took the man whom He formed;
And the Lord God commanded Adam. Thence Lord who before was God only, from the
time He had one whose He might be. For He was God to Himself, but God to
things then when also Lord. Therefore as far as he will think matter always
was because the Lord always was, so far it will stand that nothing was,
because it stands that the Lord was not always. I too will add, on account of
those who do not understand, of whom Hermogenes is the last line, and indeed I
will twist his own experiments against him. For when he denies that matter was
born or made, in this way also I find that the name of Lord did not belong to
God toward matter, because that must have been free which, by not having an
origin, did not have an author; what it was it owes to no one, and therefore
serving no one. And so from the time God exercised His power upon it, by
making from matter, from that time matter, having suffered God as Lord,
demonstrates that He was not this for so long as it was this, certainly.

* Chapter 4
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4
:END:

From this point at last I will begin to treat of matter, that God compares it
to Himself as likewise not born, likewise not made, likewise eternal, set
forth without beginning, without end. For what other rank of God than
eternity? What other status of eternity than to have always been and to be
going to be, from the prerogative of no beginning and no end? If this is
proper to God, it will be of God alone, whose it is proper, namely because if
it is also ascribed to another, it will no longer be proper to God, but common
with him to whom it is also ascribed. For even if there are those who are
called gods whether in heaven or on earth by name, yet one God the Father,
from whom all things; how much more among us that which is proper to God ought
to belong to God alone, and, as I said, it would no longer be proper, because
it would be of another. And if He is God, it must be unique, so that it may be
of one. Or what will be unique and singular, unless that to which nothing will
be equaled? What principal, unless that which is above all, unless that which
is before all, and from which all things? These God has by having them alone,
and by having them alone He is one. If another also has had them, there will
already be as many gods as have had the things that are God's. Thus Hermogenes
brings in two gods. He brings in matter equal to God. But God ought to be one,
because what is highest is God; but it will not be highest unless it has been
unique; but it will not be able to be unique to whom something will be
equaled; but matter will be equaled to God when it is assessed as eternal.

But God is God, and matter is matter. As if diversity of names resists
comparison, if the same status is claimed. Let nature too be diverse, let form
too be not the same, provided the reason of the status itself be one. God
unborn; is not matter also unborn? God always; is not matter also always? Both
without beginning, both without end, both also authors of the universe, as
much he who made as that from which He made. For matter also cannot not be
counted authoress of all things, from which the universe consists. How will he
answer? That matter is not at once compared to God if it has something of
God's, because by not having the whole it does not meet the fullness of
comparison? What has he left God more, so that he may not seem to have given
the whole of God to matter? Or whereby, he says, even with matter thus having,
authority and substance remain saved for God, whereby He is the only and first
author, and is assessed Lord of all. But truth so demands one God, by
defending that whatever is His is of Him alone. For so it will be His, if it
has been of Him alone, and from this another God cannot be admitted, while no
one is allowed to have anything from God. Therefore, you say, we too do not
have anything of God. On the contrary we have and will have, but from Him, not
from ourselves. For we shall also be gods, if we have deserved to be those of
whom He preached, I said, you are gods, and, God stood in the assembly of
gods; but from His grace, not from our property, because He alone is who makes
gods. But he makes proper to matter what it has with God: or if it received
from God what is God's, I mean the rank of eternity, it can both be believed
and have something with God and not be God. But what sort of thing is it when
he confesses it has something with God, and wants that to be of God alone
which he does not deny matter has?

He says it remains saved for God, that He both is alone and first and author
of all and Lord of all and comparable to no one, which things he soon ascribes
to matter also. He indeed is God. God will bear witness; and He has sometimes
sworn by Himself that there is no other such as Himself. But Hermogenes will
make Him a liar. For matter also will be such as God, unmade, unborn, having
no beginning nor end. God will say, I am the first. And how first, to whom
matter is coeval? But among coevals and contemporaries there is no order. Or
is matter also first? I alone, He says, stretched out heaven. Yet not alone,
when He also stretched it out from that from which He also stretched it. When
he proposes that matter was with the status of God saved, see that he is not
laughed at by us that God likewise was with the status of matter saved, yet
with a status common to both. Therefore it will also remain saved for matter
that it too was, but with God, because God also is alone, but with it. And it
itself first with God, because God also is first with it, but it also
incomparable with God, because God also is incomparable with it, and authoress
with God and mistress with God. Thus to have something and not the whole of
matter. So Hermogenes left it nothing which he had not also conferred on
matter, so that not matter is compared to God, but God rather to matter. And
so, since those things which we claim as proper to God, to have always been,
without beginning, without end, and to have been first, and alone, and author
of all, belong also to matter, I ask what diverse and alien from God and
thereby private did matter possess through which it would not be compared to
God? In which all things proper to God are reviewed, they sufficiently
prejudge concerning the remaining comparison.

* Chapter 7
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5
:END:

If he contends that matter is lesser and inferior to God, and therefore
diverse from Him, and therefore inco(??)parable to Him, as to the greater, as
to the superior, I prescribe that what is eternal and unborn admits no
diminution and humiliation, because this also makes God as great as He is,
lesser than none and subject to none, rather greater and more sublime than
all. For as the rest of things which are born or end and therefore are not
eternal, once opposed to an end, whereby also to a beginning, admit those
things which God does not admit, I mean for the present diminution and
subjection, because they have been born and made, so also God therefore does
not admit them because He has been neither born at all nor made. And the
status of matter also is such. Therefore from two eternals, as unborn, as
unmade, God and matter, by the same reason of a common status, having on equal
terms that which admits neither to be diminished nor to be subjected, that is
eternity, we say neither is lesser or greater than the other, neither humbler
or superior than the other, but both stand equally great, equally sublime,
equally of solid and perfect felicity, which is assessed eternity. For we
shall not be next to the opinions of the nations, which if ever they are
forced to confess God, yet want others also below Him. But divinity has no
grade, inasmuch as it is unique, which if it will also be in matter, as
likewise unborn and unmade and eternal, will be present on both sides, because
it will nowhere be able to be less than itself. How then will Hermogenes dare
to discern, and so to subject matter to God, eternal to eternal, unborn to
unborn, authoress to author? daring to say, And I first, and I before all
things, and I from whom all things; we were equals, we were together, both
without beginning, without end, both without author, without God. What God
subjects me to a contemporary, a coeval? If because He is called God, I too
have my name. Either I am God or He is matter, because we both are what the
other of us is. Do you think therefore he has not compared matter to God,
which of course he subjects to Him?

* Chapter 8
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h6
:END:

Yet he even prefers it to God and rather subjects God to matter, when he wills
that He made all things from matter. For if He used it for the works of the
world, already matter also is found superior, which supplied Him the means of
working, and God seems subject to matter, whose substance He needed. For there
is no one who does not need that from which he uses; there is no one who is
not subject to him whom he needs in order to be able to use: so also no one,
by using from another's, is not lesser than him from whose he uses, and no one
who grants use from his own is not in this superior to him to whom he grants
use. And so matter itself indeed did not need God, but granted itself to God
who needed it, rich and wealthy and liberal, to one lesser, I suppose, and
weak and less fit to make from nothing what He wills. A great benefit truly it
conferred on God, that He might have today one through whom God would be known
and called almighty, except that already not almighty, if not also powerful
for this, to bring forth all things from nothing. To be sure matter also
granted something to itself, that it too might be able to be known with God,
coequal to God, rather even a helper; except that Hermogenes alone has known
it, and the patriarchs of the heretics, the philosophers. For to the prophets
and apostles it has lain hidden until now, I think even to Christ.

* Chapter 9
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h7
:END:

He cannot say that God used matter as Lord for the works of the world, for He
could not have been Lord of a coequal substance. But perhaps He used it by
request, and therefore by request, not by dominion, so that, when it was evil,
He nevertheless endured to use from the evil, namely from the necessity of His
mediocrity, by which He was not able to use from nothing, not from power,
which if He had had at all, as God, over matter, which He knew to be evil, He
would have converted it beforehand into good, as Lord and good, so as thus to
use from good, not from evil. But because He is good indeed, yet not Lord,
therefore by using such as He had He showed His necessity yielding to the
condition of matter, which, if He had been Lord, He would have amended. For so
must Hermogenes be answered, when he defends from dominion that God used
matter, and from a thing not His own, namely not made by Himself. Already
therefore evil is from Himself, who, if not author of evil, because not its
maker, is certainly its permitter, because its dominator. But if matter is not
also His, as being evil, it will not be of God; therefore He used from
another's either by request, as needing it, or also by injury, as prevailing
over it. For by these three modes another's things are taken, by right, by
benefit, by onset, that is by dominion, by request, by force. Dominion not
being at hand, let Hermogenes choose what befits God, to have made all things
from matter by request or by force. Would God therefore not have judged it
better that nothing at all should be made than that it should be made by
request or by force, and that from evil?

* Chapter 10
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h8
:END:

Would He not, even if matter had been best, equally have thought it unseemly
for Himself from another's, though good? Foolishly enough therefore He labored
at the world for the sake of His glory, that He might show Himself a debtor of
another's substance, and that not good. Therefore, he says, would He make from
nothing, so that evils also would be imputed to His choice? Great, in good
faith, is the blindness of heretics for an argumentation of this kind, when
for this reason they either want another God good and best to be believed
because they think the Creator author of evil, or they set matter with the
Creator so that they may derive evil from matter, not from the Creator, when
no God at all is freed from that question so as not to be able likewise to
seem author of evil, whoever he is who, even if he did not himself make evil,
yet suffered it to be made by anyone and from anywhere. Let Hermogenes
therefore also hear, while elsewhere we distinguish concerning the reason of
evil, that for the present he too has accomplished nothing by this injection
of his. For behold, even if not author, yet God is found an assenter of evil,
who endured the evil of matter so much from the good before the constitution
of the world, as He, being good and a rival of evil, ought to have amended it.
For either He could amend and would not, or He willed indeed, but could not, a
weak God. If He could and would not, He too is evil, because He favored evil,
and so already He is held guilty, because, although He did not institute it,
yet, because if He had not willed it to be, it would not be, He himself
already made to be what He did not will not to be. Than which what is fouler?
If He willed that to be which He himself was unwilling to have made, He acted
against Himself, when He both willed to be what He was unwilling to have made,
and was unwilling to have made what He willed to be. As good He willed it to
be, and as evil He was unwilling to have made it. What by not making He judged
evil, by enduring He announced as good. By enduring evil for good, and not
rather rooting it out, He is found its assertor, evilly, if through will,
foully, if through necessity. Either God will be a servant of evil or a
friend, having consorted with the evil of matter, not to say even having
worked from its evil.

* Chapter 11
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h9
:END:

And yet from where does Hermogenes persuade us that matter is evil? For he
will not be able not to call evil that to which he ascribes evil. For we
define that what is eternal cannot admit diminution and subjection, so as to
be counted inferior to another coeternal. So also now we say that evil does
not belong to it, because it cannot even be subjected from this, which can in
no way be subjected, because it is eternal. But since elsewhere it is agreed
that the highest good is that which is eternal, as God, through which He alone
is God, while He is eternal, and so good, while God, how will evil be in
matter, which as eternal must necessarily be believed the highest good? Or if
what is eternal will be able also to be capable of evil, this will be able to
be believed of God also, and without cause he has been eager to transfer evil
from God, if it belongs even to the eternal, by belonging to matter. Now
indeed if what is eternal can be believed evil, evil will be invincible and
insuperable as eternal, and then we labor in vain concerning taking evil away
from ourselves, then God also commands and precepts this in vain, rather God
has even constituted judgment in vain, going to punish with injustice of
course. But if on the contrary there will be an end of evil when its president
the devil has gone into the fire which God prepared for him and his angels,
first relegated into the pit of the abyss, when the revelation of the sons of
God has redeemed the condition from evil, subjected of course to vanity, when
with innocence and integrity of the condition restored the cattle have agreed
with the beasts, and little children have played with serpents, when the
Father has put enemies under the Son's feet, of course the workers of evil, -
therefore if an end belongs to evil, a beginning must necessarily have
belonged, and matter will have a beginning, by having also an end of evil. For
the things that are counted to evil belong according to the status of evil.

* Chapter 12
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h10
:END:

Come now, let us believe matter to be evil and worst, of course by nature, as
we believe God good and best, likewise by nature. Further, nature ought to be
held certain and fixed, as persevering in evil with matter as also
inconvertible and unchangeable in good with God. Namely because, if nature
will be changed in matter from evil into good, it will be able to be changed
also in God from good into evil. At this place someone will say: Therefore
sons of Abraham will not be raised from stones, and offspring of vipers will
not make fruit of repentance, and sons of wrath will not become sons of peace,
if nature will not be mutable? You will look rashly to those examples, O man.
For those things that have been born, stones and vipers and men, do not belong
to the cause of matter, which is (??)nnala; for the nature of these, by having
an institution, will be able also to have a cessation. But hold matter once
determined eternal, as unmade, as unborn, and therefore to be believed of
unchangeable and incorruptible nature, even from that opinion of Hermogenes
himself which he opposes, when he denies that God could make from Himself,
because what is eternal is not changed, being about to lose of course what it
had been, while by change it becomes what it was not, if it were not eternal;
but the Lord eternal cannot be other than what He always is. By this
definition I too will deservedly strike him back. I equally reprehend matter,
when from that evil, even worst, good and best things are made by God: And God
saw that they were good, and God blessed them, of course because best, not
certainly as if evil and worst. Matter therefore admitted change, and if it is
so, it lost the status of eternity; it died at last from its own form. But
eternity cannot be lost, because unless it cannot be lost, it is not eternity.
Therefore it could not have admitted change either, because if it is eternity,
it can in no way be changed.

* Chapter 13
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h11
:END:

And it will be asked how from it good things have been made, which have in no
way been made from change? Whence in the evil and worst the seed of the good
and best? Certainly neither does a good tree put forth evil fruits, because
neither is God except good, nor a bad tree good ones, because neither is
matter except worst. Or if we shall give it something also of good germ, it
will no longer be of uniform nature, that is evil in the whole, but already
then double, that is of good and evil nature, and it will be asked again
whether in good and evil it could have agreed with light and darkness, sweet
and bitter. Or if the diversity of both, of good and of evil, could have run
together, and matter have been of a double nature, fertile of both fruits,
already neither will the good things themselves be counted to God, so that
neither may the evils be imputed to Him, but both kinds, taken from the
property of matter, will belong to matter. By which pact we shall neither owe
thanks of goods to God, nor envy of evils, because He worked nothing from His
own genius. Through which it will be manifestly proved that He served matter.

* Chapter 14
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h12
:END:

For even if it should be said that, though from the occasion of matter, He
nevertheless by His own choice brought forth goods, as if having found the
good of matter, although this too is foul, certainly when from the same He
also brings forth evils, at least these, by bringing them forth not from His
own choice, He serves matter, having nothing else to do than to bring forth
from evil, unwilling of course, as good, from necessity, as unwilling, and
from servitude, as from necessity. What then is worthier, that He founded
evils from necessity, or from will? Since He founded from necessity, if from
matter, from will, if from nothing. For already you labor without cause, lest
God be constituted author of evils, because even if He made from matter, they
will be counted to Him who made, likewise inasmuch as He made. Plainly it so
matters whence He made as if He had made from nothing, and it does not matter
whence He made, so that He made from that whence it more became Him. But it
more became Him to have made from will than from necessity, that is from
nothing rather than from matter. It is worthier to believe God even author of
evils free than a slave. Whatever power belongs to Him rather than pettiness.
If even so we grant that matter indeed had nothing of good, but that the Lord,
if He put forth any good, put it forth by His own virtue, other questions
equally will arise. First, if good was not in matter at all, good was not made
from matter; which of course matter did not have. Next, if not from matter,
already therefore it was made from God. If neither from God, already therefore
it was made from nothing. For this remains according to Hermogenes'
arrangement.

* Chapter 15
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h13
:END:

Further if good was made neither from matter, because it was not in it, as in
the evil, nor from God, because nothing could be made from God, as Hermogenes
defines, good is found already made from nothing, as made from none, as
neither from matter nor from God. And if good from nothing, why not also evil?
rather why not all things from nothing, if something from nothing? Unless the
divine virtue was insufficient for producing all things, which brought forth
something from nothing. Or if good proceeded from evil matter, because neither
from nothing nor from God, it will follow that it proceeded from a conversion
of matter against the denied conversion of the eternal. So whence good
consisted, Hermogenes will now deny that it could have consisted. But it is
necessary that it proceeded from some of those from which he denied it could
proceed. For the rest if therefore evil is not from nothing, lest it become
God's, from whose choice it will seem made, but from matter, so that it may be
of that from whose substance it will have been made, here too, as I said, God
will be held author of evil, who, when by the same virtue and will He ought to
have brought forth all goods from matter, or only goods, yet did not bring
forth all goods, but also evils, of course either willing evils to be, if He
could effect that they should not be, or not availing to effect all goods, if
He willed and did not make, while it makes no difference whether through
infirmity the Lord existed as author of evil or through will. Or what reason
was there that when He had made goods as if good, He also brought forth evils
as if not good, when He put forth only things not congruent with Himself? What
need was there, with His own work brought forth, also to care for the business
of matter likewise even by bringing forth evil, so that He alone might be
known good from good, but matter might not be known evil from evil? Good would
have flourished more without the breath of evil. For Hermogenes also storms
the argumentations of certain ones, who say that evils were necessary for the
illumination of goods to be understood from contraries. Therefore either not
even for that reason was there a place for bringing forth evil, or if some
other reason demanded that it be introduced, why could it not also have been
introduced from nothing, the reason itself being about to excuse the Lord,
lest He be thought author of evil, which now, when He works from matter,
excuses evils? If it excuses, so far everywhere and from every side the
question is driven thither where they do not want, who, by not examining the
reason of evil itself, nor discerning how they either attribute it to God or
separate it from God, expose God to more and unworthier destructions.

* Chapter 16
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h14
:END:

Therefore in the preconstruction of this article, perhaps to be treated also
elsewhere, I for my part define that either both good and evil must be
ascribed to God, which He made from matter, or to matter itself, from which He
made, or both to both, because both are bound to each other, he who made and
that from which He made, or the one to the one; for there is not a third
besides matter and God. Further if both will be God's, God will seem also
author of evil; but God, as good, will not be author of evil: if both
matter's, matter will seem also matrix of good; but matter evil in the whole
will not be matrix of good: if both will be of both, in this too matter will
be compared to God, and both will be equals, equally akin to evil and good;
but matter ought not to be equaled to God, lest it make two gods: if the one
of the other, of course the good of God and the evil of matter, neither will
evil be ascribed to God nor good to matter; and yet God, by making both goods
and evils from matter, makes with it. If these things are so, I do not know by
