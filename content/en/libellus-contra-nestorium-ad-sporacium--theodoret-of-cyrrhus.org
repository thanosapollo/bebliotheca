Theodoret, Bishop of Cyrrhus, Against Nestorius, to Sporacius.

And since the whole world is little by little wasting away, and is hastening
toward the dissolution of this universe, and everyone thought himself a
sufficient teacher of piety, because through the conceit that comes from
ignorance no one would endure to learn, the man-slaying demon, the father of
the lie, the sower of tares, no longer armed himself against the Church with
the outward and open enemies; but having found an instrument that received, so
to speak, his whole craft at once, he introduces, under the pretext of
orthodoxy, the denial of both the Godhead and the love for mankind of the
Only-begotten, confounding the simple character of our faith, which has not
been turned into a craft, with Hellenic sophisms. You have surely heard of
Nestorius somewhere; for the man is well known. This man, setting out from the
little town of the Germaniceans, reared from the beginning in I know not what
sort of pursuits, changing from one land to another, like an Egyptian plague
struck the great city of the Antiochenes. In this city, having a moderate
share of liberal learning, and having trained a voice as beautiful and as
great as could be, he approached the Church of God and crept in, and having
been enrolled in the list of presbyters, and entrusted with teaching the
people of God, he showed at once in the opening what he would be throughout
his whole life. For he did not emulate the noble and fruitful character of
speech, able to till the souls of men; but leaning toward what pleases the
crowd, and becoming, if anyone ever did, a hunter of applause that dissolves
into air, =83.1156= he drew the rabble-like and unstable multitude to
affection for himself, wearing a grey cloak, walking with a gloomy look,
shunning the uproar of the marketplace, hunting by paleness of body the
reputation of being continent, at home for the most part attending to books,
and keeping company with himself in quiet. With such postures and fictions
baiting the many, he advanced far in age, pursuing the appearance of being a
Christian before being one, and preferring his own glory to the glory of
Christ. But when, according to what was said by the Master, "Nothing is hidden
that shall not be known, nor secret that shall not come into the open"; and,
"Good works are evident beforehand, and those that are otherwise cannot be
hid"; according to the unsearchable abyss of the judgments of God, Nestorius
also, by the vote of those around the palace and the thrones, and of him who
then held the scepters of the inhabited world, is entrusted with the
presidency of the orthodox catholic Church at Constantinople, and no less of
the whole inhabited world. Here then I come also to the dramas of which almost
all earth and sea are full. For when he had seized that greatest throne,
snatching the presidency of the high priesthood as a kind of tyranny, he was
no longer able to contain within himself the birth-pang of impiety, but taking
from the authority an ignorant boldness of speech, he brings into the open and
sets before all the blasphemy he had long since conceived against the
Only-begotten; and he chooses open war against the apostolic doctrines, and
tries to cast out the words of the holy fathers who, from the time the Gospel
was proclaimed, have been the guides of the orthodox faith; troubling the
Church of the imperial city, and troubling also the whole inhabited world,
paying no heed to the divine Paul crying out: "He who troubles you shall bear
the judgment, whoever he may be." But on the lampstand of the divine pulpit of
the high priests he sets not the lamp of truth, but the gloom of impiety. And
the first attempt of his innovation is this: that one must not confess the
holy Virgin, the mother of God the Word according to the flesh, as Theotokos,
but only as Christotokos, although the heralds of the orthodox faith from of
old and from long ago, according to the apostolic tradition, taught both to
name and to believe the Lord's mother as Theotokos. Come then, let us now
bring into the open the blasphemous craft of terms, and the distinction known
to no one before. The appellation Christ, he says, signifies the two natures,
both the Godhead of the Only-begotten and the manhood; but the word God said
absolutely presents the simple and bodiless essence of God the Word; and the
word man indicates only the human nature; therefore, he says, it is necessary
to confess the Virgin as Christotokos, but not as Theotokos, =83.1157= lest we
unwittingly say that God the Word received the beginning of being from the
holy Virgin, and so be forced as a consequence to confess the mother as older
than the one born from her. And so that I may not seem to accuse the man of
these things with empty insults, I will make him himself, by what he said, a
witness of his own impiety. For having banished from his mind the memory of
the apostolic teachings and of every saint, in the Church of the orthodox he
cried out many such utterances: "Mary did not bear God, my good man, but she
bore a man, an instrument of the Godhead." And again in other nonsense: "A
Greek is without blame when he introduces a mother for gods."

Let these few of his many words suffice for me for now, as the discourse
hastens on to what follows. Wherefore, since lawless men shall perish by an
extraordinary death, he was also cast out of the high priesthood by the divine
vote through the saints assembled at Ephesus, and by imperial nod condemned to
dwell in the Oasis, and from this point he received the prelude of the coming
punishment of the impious, becoming a waste of his own madness, and fulfilling
upon himself that apostolic saying, that "The sins of some men are evident
beforehand, going before unto judgment." But these things, as having been
examined also by many others, it is necessary to pass over. Let us go on to
what follows, and pass over to the confession of the holy Fathers. We, obeying
everywhere the evangelical and apostolic doctrines, according to the tradition
of the holy Fathers, believe that God the Word, the Creator and Lord of this
universe, the only-begotten Son of the Father, the one before the ages,
unmixed with body and with all that belongs to body, who is all that the
Father is except only being Father, in the last days, moved by his own love
for mankind, by the good pleasure of God and Father, taking from the holy
Virgin flesh consubstantial with her who bore it and becoming man, was born as
God together with what he assumed, not having cast off what he was (for the
divine is free from all change), but having taken on also becoming man. This
one, according to the apostolic word, was tried also by human passions in
every way, according to human likeness and law, apart from sin; for hunger,
and toil, and tears, and such things, present the truth of the human nature;
and that he was crucified under Pontius Pilate, and died, and was buried, and
rose on the third day, and was taken up into the heavens, worshipped together
with his own Father and glorified together by every intelligible and sensible
nature; and that he will come again to judge the living and the dead, in no
way diminished as to the divine nature by the form of a servant; but one
only-begotten God and Lord of all, confessed and worshipped forever together
with his own =83.1160= flesh; therefore, my good man, the Virgin is proclaimed
Theotokos by those who worship Christ as God; not as though the divine nature
of the Only-begotten received the beginning of being from her; for God is and
will be and was in the beginning, never having received the beginning of being
from anyone; but already being, the Word of God made the dwelling in the
virgin and undefiled womb the beginning of the incarnation and of the economy
concerning us. And of this the apostle Paul is a witness; and the divine
evangelist John is a witness.

Saint Paul crying thus: "God sent forth his Son": the one who is, clearly; for
what is not is not sent, but comes to be; "born of a woman, born under the
law," and so on. And John, called by the Master son of thunder because of the
loud and heavenly character of his doctrines, having first said that the Word
was God, and in the beginning, and was with God, and always was, and that all
things came to be through him, and that he himself is Lord of all, and having
shown that his being is without beginning and consubstantial with the Father,
as befits the Only-begotten; then, descending also to the unspeakable words of
the economy, he says: "And the Word became flesh, and dwelt among us." How
then is she not Theotokos, O wisest of all men, as you think, who both
conceived God the Word become man, and bore him, and is of necessity believed
to be mother of the one born? And all the prophets proclaim these things. Or
have you not heard Isaiah crying: "Behold, the virgin shall conceive in the
womb, and shall bear a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel"? not,
according to you and your twaddle, an instrument of the Godhead, but God with
us, become man like us. And of Jeremiah, himself also declaring the Word who
became man to be God, in the words: "This is our God; no other shall be
reckoned beside him. He found out every way of knowledge." And a little later:
"After these things he appeared on earth, and consorted with men." And of the
Forerunner's mother also, moved prophetically by the adorable Spirit and
crying: "Whence is this to me, that the mother of my Lord should come to me?"
These things, said by many before and no less said now, it is necessary to
pass over. That the holy Virgin is Theotokos, then, what has been said is
enough for the well-disposed. But I will try in a few words to overturn his
wise craft of terms. To take it up a little from earlier: the appellation
Christ, he says, signifies the two natures; but God, of the simple and
uncompounded; and man presents the human nature. What are you saying? When
therefore the Lord in the Gospels, teaching the more divine things, and as he
himself said the heavenly things, says to Nicodemus (for I will converse with
you as with one who knows the divine =83.1161= Scriptures): "No one has
ascended into heaven except he who descended from heaven, the Son of man who
is in heaven." Since he clearly said Son of man, shall we then say that the
human nature came down from the heavens, according to your impious and unwise
wisdom? For I will use your own sophisms against you. Away with the blasphemy.
For the Master's flesh did not come down from the heavens, nor does it fill
all things.

But here the discourse of the Master Christ is about the Godhead; for he who
came down from the heavens, and fills all things, and does not leave those
places from which he came down, is God the Word, crying through the prophet:
"Do I not fill heaven and earth? says the Lord." And again: "Who measured the
water with his hand, and heaven with a span, and all the earth with a
handful?" and: "Heaven is my throne, and the earth the footstool of my feet."
Inasmuch as he is God, being in heaven, and coming down to us, and filling all
things; but inasmuch as he became man, after the condescension toward us, both
going up into the heavens and being glorified together with his own Father.
For this is also shown by what was said to the sacred psalmist David: "The
Lord said to my Lord: Sit at my right hand." See, the Lord's voice is an exact
refutation of your superfluous wisdom of words. For having set first the Son
of man coming down from the heavens, and being in those places from which he
came down (for this he showed by saying: "who is in heaven"), he showed the
divine essence that contains all things, coming down to us by the word of the
economy, and leading our nature in himself to the Father's throne, according
to what was said by the divine Paul: "And he raised him, Christ, from the
dead, and seated him at his right hand, far above every Principality, and
Authority, and Power, and Lordship, and every name that is named, not only in
this age, but also in the one to come." Not, of course, that the divine
essence of the Only-begotten was after the resurrection from the dead counted
worthy of the Father's glory; but that then, after the ascent into the
heavens, God the Word is honored and worshipped with the Father's glory
together with his own flesh. And elsewhere the same apostle Paul also casts
out this empty sound of yours. For writing to the Corinthians he says: "The
first man is of the earth, earthy; the second man is the Lord from heaven."
See, having set first only the appellation man, he added: "The Lord from
heaven," interpreting the divine essence by saying "from heaven." And often
when the God-bearing prophets and apostles say God, they indicate the human
nature. Jeremiah crying: "This is our God; no other shall be reckoned beside
him." Then, that I may not speak of each particular: "After these things he
appeared on earth, and consorted with men." Not, of course, =83.1164=
prophesying that the Godhead of the Only-begotten would be graspable by the
eyes of men; but by "He appeared" he clearly indicates the flesh of God the
Word. And of the same sense is also what was said in the forty-ninth psalm by
the divine David: "God will come manifestly." And the prophet Zephaniah also
prophesies this same thing, saying: "The Lord shall appear upon them, and
shall destroy all the gods of the nations of the earth." And no less does the
evangelist John proclaim this unspeakable mystery: "That which was from the
beginning," he says, "which we have heard, which we have seen and our hands
have handled, concerning the Word of life." Not, of course, knowing the
bodiless and invisible nature of God the Word as handled by hands, but
honoring the body of God the Word with the appellation. For so it is, and this
faith was handed down to us by those who from the beginning were eyewitnesses
and ministers of the word, and by the sacred catalogue of all the holy Fathers
after them. That after the Only-begotten Word of God became man like us, while
remaining God, and through the form of a servant accepted our passions into
himself, he is no longer divided from his own flesh either by appellations or
by nature; but if one says man, he means God the Word; and if one names him
God, he no less names him together with his own flesh. And let these things
suffice so far. For what lies before us is not a display of a multitude of
testimonies, but a refutation of the uneducated craft of terms and verbal
distinction.
