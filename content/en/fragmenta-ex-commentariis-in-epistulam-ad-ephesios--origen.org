* Fragments from the Commentaries on the Epistle to the Ephesians

Eph. i 1 Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ through the will of God. Let us hear
what Origen also says about this: if the preposition "through" indicates the
ministerial (as we examined at greater length when interpreting "all things
came to be through him," also setting down the objections that appeared to us
against this), it must be asked how Paul is an apostle of Christ Jesus not
from the will of God but through the will of God. One person then will say
that Paul is not altogether precise everywhere about the peculiarities of the
prepositions, which is what he also does in the case of other meanings.
Another, knowing the difference between "through God" and through the will of
God, will say that it would have been absurd if it had been written Paul an
apostle of Christ Jesus through God; but if the will of God is ministerial,
while he who uses it is the one who leads, this would not be unreasonable. You
will also attend concerning the will of God, whether it can be ranged upon
Christ: so that just as he is power of God and wisdom of God, so also he may
be his will, having him as the hypostasis of God. But if it should appear to
someone discordant to say that the will of God is substantialized, let him
attend whether the seeming discordance is not similar also in the case of
power of God and wisdom of God and word of God, and truth and resurrection and
way; for it seems to me similar concerning all these, as being substantialized
in the only-begotten Word. 2 Eph. i 1b [to the saints who are and faithful in
Christ Jesus.] [And Origen says] we found "to the saints who are" lying only
in the case of the Ephesians; and we inquire, if <the> "who are" added to "to
the saints" is not superfluous, what it can signify. See then whether, just as
in Exodus the one who speaks to Moses says his own name is <the> one who is,
so those who share in the one who is become ones who are, being called as it
were from not-being into being; for God chose the things that are not, says
the same Paul, in order to bring to nothing the things that are. And someone
will ask how he brings the things that are to nothing; but let us hear him
adding "so that no flesh may boast before God." For if someone who has shared
in being, forgetting the share, credits to himself the cause of being, and
does not bring all thanksgiving to the one who from not-being granted him
being like him and according to the image, then the being is brought to
nothing. [i 3] Blessed is the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who
blessed us in every spiritual blessing in the heavenlies in Christ. One person
will say that, because of the blessings in Leviticus and Deuteronomy and if
anywhere else in scripture such things have been recorded, the spiritual
blessing is spoken by way of contrast with those; as if the God and Father of
Jesus Christ were blessing us not in a bodily blessing like those, but in a
spiritual one. And the heterodox will take it in this way, thinking they would
also get from such an interpretation an occasion for cutting the godhead
apart. But we, having shown that not even in the case of those under the law
have the blessings been given, if they were understood bodily, to those
entrusted according to the law and who have lived soundly, will convict those
who have so supposed either of making the one who gave the blessings a liar,
or of the thing not understood from the ready interpretation having been
promised to those who lived as he commanded. For it follows that, since the
law is spiritual, the blessings in it also are spiritual. And the prophets,
for example, have not obtained the blessings bodily; for they did not lend to
many nations, men going about in sheepskins, in goatskins, being in want,
afflicted, ill-treated, wandering in deserts and mountains and caves and the
holes of the earth, nor have they been blessed in city or in field; nor have
their storehouses been blessed, which they did not even possess in the first
place. And see whether from these we shall not compel those who understand
otherwise to change (if they keep the dignity of the God who gave the
blessings) toward his having given them spiritually to his blessed ones. And
not only is every blessing spiritual with which they have been blessed


Paul and those like him, and none of them bodily, but also in the heavenlies.
For it was fitting for these whose citizenship is in heaven, who store up
treasure in heaven and have their heart where their treasure is, also to
obtain the spiritual blessings in the heavenlies. And how God and Father of
our Lord Jesus Christ has already blessed us in every spiritual blessing and
in the heavenlies is worth examining; for it was not said "who will bless" us.
And see whether "in the heavenlies" can be, i.e., "in the intelligible things
and outside the senses"; for so also someone stores up treasure in heaven, and
no longer has the heart on earth, that is, in the material and bodily things,
but in heaven, always conversing with the intelligible nature itself. And for
us to consider more accurately, both things have been said about the blessing,
both that it is spiritual and that it is in the heavenlies; and besides these
that it is in Christ, as it were in the Word and the Wisdom and the Truth and
the Power. 3 Eph. i 4b [that we should be holy and blameless before him.]
[Origen says] And someone might ask, toward some being holy and blameless
before God, whether the prophetic word does not oppose this, saying "no living
thing will be justified before you"; and if before God no living thing will be
justified, how will they be holy before him? For if they will be holy and
blameless, they will also be justified. Someone taking refuge in the ambiguity
of the prophetic word will say that "every living thing is not justified
before God" signifies something of this sort, as it were: a whole living thing
and out of the whole of its own time will not be justified before God, because
it has in any case sinned at some time, which, if it is so, does not prevent
some from being holy and blameless before him, having become such from
correction; or also that, <if> not every living thing, still some are
justified. [i 5] in love having predestined us for adoption through Jesus
Christ unto him. Either "in love" is to be joined with "having predestined,"
or "he chose us in him in love before the foundation of the world, having
predestined us in adoption <through> Jesus Christ unto him," so that we should
be holy and blameless before him. And the name "adoption" makes clear that
those predestined by him are not sons of God by nature; at any rate the name
of adoption would not be ranged upon the Savior, but upon those who, having
received the <spirit> of slavery unto fear, and having paid back the fear,
become worthy of freedom and of hearing "I no longer call you slaves," who
also for this reason receive the spirit of adoption. And when someone receives
the Son, not having had him before, then he has also made room for the spirit
of adoption; for thus through Christ the adoption comes upon us. Yet with a
view to arriving unto him he chose us in him before the foundation of the
world, that we should be holy and blameless before him, having predestined us
in adoption through Christ; for to these is added "unto him." [i 5b] according
to the good pleasure of his will. The name "good pleasure" I think is not very
worn in the custom of the Greeks, but was coined by those who translated; for
they used the word in the prophetic writings, as in the 84th psalm, "You were
well pleased, Lord, with your land," and in the 105th, "Remember us, Lord, in
the good pleasure of your people"; and in Habakkuk, "and if he should draw
back, my soul is not well pleased in him." Still, good pleasure indicates the
"well" and the "to seem"; so that it would not be said of things that seem to
us not well, but only of things that seem praiseworthily. It must therefore be
asked how, having predestined in love for adoption those whom God predestined,
he predestined according to the good pleasure of his will; and a demonstration
must be found that there is good pleasure of his will upon these; for also,
with a view to the glory of his grace toward those who are saved being
praised, his will was well pleased. 4 Eph. i 6 [in the Beloved.] [Origen says]
The Savior is called "beloved" simply; since even if human beings go astray
about the grasp of righteousness and of truth and of wisdom, still all would
confess that they "love" wisdom and truth and righteousness. For this reason


"beloved" is said of the Only-begotten of God, all loving the things conceived
of him, whether missing the mark or hitting it; just as all are in awe of God,
but those who fall away from the sound notion about God miss the mark. [i 7]
in whom we have the redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of
trespasses. Redemption <or> ransom takes place for those who are captives and
have come under the enemies; and we have come under the enemies, the ruler of
this age and the wicked powers under him, and for this reason we needed
redemption and the one who buys us back, so that he might recover us who had
been alienated from him; the Savior therefore gave the ransom for us, his own
blood; wherefore we have the redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of
trespasses. Forgiveness of trespasses follows upon the redemption, forgiveness
of trespasses not being possible for someone before he is redeemed. First then
we must be redeemed and no longer be under the one who took us captive and
held us, so that, having been set free and having come outside his hands (so
to speak), we may be able for our benefit to receive the forgiveness of
trespasses and, healed from the wounds of sin, to work according to piety and
the remaining virtues. [i 8] according to the riches of his grace which he
made to abound toward us. The one who has understood what it is to "be saved
by grace and not from works," and who compares trespasses with trespasses,
would see, according to the parable that speaks of the five hundred and the
fifty denarii, who ought to confess more thanks to the householder who
remitted the debt. And further, observing how the sufferings of the present
time are not worthy toward the glory about to be revealed to us, he would
perceive the riches of the grace of God, and how it abounded thus upon the
called and elect, especially those alien from the covenants of God and
strangers to his promises, who by the trespass of Israel were drawn from the
paternal kindness toward salvation; and still more someone would contemplate
the riches of his gr<ace> which he made to abound toward the blessed as
greater, observing "do you not know that we shall judge angels?" and "into
which things angels desire to look," and what is said about the one who will
be over all, "that as I and you are one, that they also may be one in us." And
this whole riches of the grace of God, which he made to abound toward the
saints, being supplied from some beginning and increasing and multiplying,
either has the one who obtains the things said before competing in return and
stretching himself alongside as far as possible, or, being left behind, as far
as it is possible to do all that falls to him. If then someone should be left
behind, the grace would as it were become empty toward such a one; but if he
should do what is from himself without shortfall, he would say the apostolic
word, "his grace which is toward me did not become empty." 5 Eph. i. 9 [in all
wisdom and understanding having made known to us the mystery of his will
according to his good pleasure which he purposed in him for the administration
of the fullness of the times.] [Origen says] What is "all wisdom," and after
this what is "understanding"? Jesus also used the word in Wisdom, beginning
from "all wisdom is from the Lord and with him it is unto the age." If then
someone has observed the things that are seen and what things in them, or also
outside them, are not seen, and, dividing by reason the invisible things
separately and the visible things separately, should examine the wisdom by
which God has made all things (for "he made all things in wisdom," says
David), this one would look upon all wisdom from the Lord. But it is likely
that someone, despairing that the one who still wears this body can already
come to be in all wisdom and understanding, and also hearing "we know in part
and we prophesy in part," will stumble at "in all wisdom and understanding
having made known to us the mystery of his will," as said more as a compliment
than truly; for even if the mystery of God is made known in wisdom to Paul and
those like him, still it is not in all wisdom that all things are made known
to him who knows in part and in part


prophesying. See then whether toward this inquiry another punctuation of the
reading of the things in the passage does not fit, as follows: "unto the
praise of the glory of his grace which he bestowed on us in the Beloved, in
whom we have the redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of trespasses,
according to the riches of his grace which he made to abound toward us in all
wisdom and understanding"; having paused the punctuation of this passage here,
from another beginning he will read thus, "having made known to us the mystery
of his will according to his good pleasure," so that "in all wisdom and
understanding" does not attach to "having made known to us the mystery of his
will" but to the things above. For God bestowed grace on us in the Beloved in
all his own wisdom and his understanding; but also, having in the Beloved the
redemption through the blood of Christ, we received these things in all wisdom
and understanding of God; and in all his wisdom and understanding we have
obtained the forgiveness of trespasses, as he made the riches of his grace
abound toward us in all wisdom and understanding; all which things he has
done, having made known to us the mystery of his will according to his good
pleasure. But if the former reading also holds, such an account will be given,
not opposing the activity of knowing in part and of the one who pursues laying
hold; in all his own wisdom and understanding God administered us, having made
known the mystery of his will. For in order that we may be able to know the
mystery of his will and may have strength to make room for this, wearing a
body of humiliation, we need all wisdom of God administering us. And if the
name of mysteries is taken for the divine and unspeakable things and those not
fit to reach many, it is clear that the will of God concerning those who will
be saved also has to do with mystery; for the great multitude of the kindness
of God is hidden for those who fear him, as David says, "how great is the
multitude of your kindness, Lord, which you hid for those who fear you." And
attend whether "predestination" and "purpose" differ, so that after the
predestination the purpose comes to be; so that as it were according to the
thoughts of God the predestination comes to be, but according to the same
things already the purpose concerning those whom he predestined follows along
as they are in some way substantialized and as the predestination comes into
work; whence also first is written "in love having predestined us for
adoption," and second "according to his good pleasure which he purposed in
him." And it must be examined what the fullness of the times is; and beside
this lies "the fullness of the time" said by the same Paul, "but when the
fullness of the time came, God sent forth his son." And just as the time for
the heir who has come to a man's age to receive the paternal property might
somehow be named fullness of his time or of his season, so in the fullness of
the times would come to be the one who, through the ways and advances that
bear toward perfection, has arrived at being able to receive the things
predestined and purposed by God for the saints. 6 Eph. i 10 to sum up all
things in Christ, the things in the heavens and the things on the earth, in
him. [Origen says] The name "summing up" has been said of banking and similar
accounts that are calculated together and of givings and spendings or
receivings gathered into one heading, whence I think the apostle also has used
the word here. For there being many accounts of the administration of the
things in the heavens and of the governance of the things on earth, of all,
inasmuch as they are parts of one whole world, breathing together and nodding
together toward one result, there is a "summing up" in the Christ. For not
only are the divisions of the things administered and the accounts one by one
of the things governed in the Word of God and his Wisdom, but also the summing
up and (as one might say) the gathering-up of all. "Heading" is often also
said of the comprehensive thought; "a heading, he says, upon the things said,"
and "summarize many things in few."


And it is called a heading, as I think, from our head also having all things
in itself and all our sense-organs of life as in a heading. 7 Eph. i 11 [of
him who works all things according to the counsel of his will.] [Origen says]
As many things as God works and does and accomplishes, all these he works
according to the counsel of his own will; for there is nothing that can make
him do anything contrary to the counsel of his will; for he holds and rules
all things. And in order that Paul may present the considered character of the
workings of God that come to be according to his will, he set the counsel
before the will. For God more than all does all things with counsel. Wherefore
he works all things according to the counsel of his will. 8 Eph. i 13 in whom
you also, having heard [the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation, in
whom also having believed you were sealed with the Holy Spirit of the
promise.] [Origen says] in whom (clearly Christ), toward others who have
heard, you also having heard the word of truth, which word of truth is the
gospel of your salvation, <in whom> also having believed you were sealed with
the Holy Spirit of the promise. And as to the phrasing, the second "in whom"
will seem to be superfluous, which has also become the cause of it seeming
that, with the thought hanging, the subordinate phrasing has not been brought
in. But it is not so; one of those before us used to say that from much love
concerning Jesus Paul mentions him most continually and as it were
superfluously, as also in the case of "in whom also having believed" one can
see according to the "in whom." And it is very much according to the
Ephesians, who have shared in unspeakable words as Paul set them forth, to
hear "in whom you also, having heard the word of truth." For I do not think
that those who have heard the preaching alone, and have not received the
accounts of the matters in interpretation and clear unfolding, hear the word
of truth. For the word and the preaching differ, as also elsewhere Paul
teaches saying, "and my word and my preaching were not in persuasive words of
wisdom but in demonstration of spirit and power"; for there too it must be
observed that he says his word is other than his preaching. And it must also
be examined <whether> in another epistle it lies that some have heard the word
of truth, so that the observation may appear more accurately according to the
intention of Paul. But where "gospel" is named must be observed, <it must be
observed> also the words added to it, for example according to "my gospel," or
"beginning of the gospel of Jesus Christ," or "eternal gospel," or gospel of
the salvation of the Ephesians; or simply "gospel," as when Paul says about
Luke, "whose praise in the


gospel is through all the churches." And everyone who has heard the word of
truth, which is a gospel of the salvation of the one who hears and believes,
is sealed with the Holy Spirit of the promise; so that if he should hear, but
not believe, he would not be sealed. But another will say that even if he
should believe, but should not advance so far as to hear the word of truth,
not even so would he make room for the seal of the Holy Spirit of the promise,
<which> is truly an impression and a making-clear of those things that it is
fitting should be impressed and made clear by the Holy Spirit of the promise.
And it must also be asked whether anywhere "Holy Spirit of promise" has been
said, and what must be taken for the Holy Spirit of the promise. I think then
that just as the Holy Spirit, coming upon someone, makes him holy, and the
spirit of wisdom makes him wise, and the spirit of understanding makes him
understanding, so also the spirit of the promise is somehow already in the
promise. [i 14] which is an earnest of our inheritance, unto redemption of the
acquisition, unto praise of his glory. You will ask whether everyone who
shares in Holy Spirit shares in the Spirit of the promise, or only the one who
has heard a word of truth, which is a gospel of salvation, and has believed,
this one receives the Holy Spirit of the promise; and further let us also
attend to these, whether everyone who shares in any way at all in Holy Spirit
has the earnest of the


inheritance. And it is better to understand that, just as in the case of those
who buy something together and give an earnest, an earnest is given according
to the proportion of the quantity of the whole money, so according to the
proportion of the future goods of each that have been foreknown of a saint an
earnest of the inheritance is given, so that from the earnest the one able to
observe differences of the power of earnest and earnest would already declare
concerning a greater and a lesser inheritance laid up for the fellow-heirs of
Christ. And just as the earnest of the inheritance is not something outside us
(for in each is the Holy Spirit of the promise, the earnest of the promise),
so the inheritance is not from outside the one who inherits, but in the mind
of the one who inherits and in his soul; for nothing of the things outside can
be compared with the perfection of a mind that contemplates beauties of Wisdom
and Word of God and Truth. This Holy Spirit of the promise, which is an
earnest of our inheritance, is given for the present to the saints, so that
they may be redeemed and acquired for God, being fitted unto praise of the
glory of God. And "unto praise of his glory," besides what has been given, can
also make clear something of this sort, "with a view to each one praising the
glory of God, now receiving the Spirit of the promise as an earnest of the
inheritance, and later the inheritance itself"; not however (as someone might
suspect to be signified from the wording) as if God needed those who praise
his glory, but as the greatest goods accruing to those who have contemplated
what is praiseworthy of the glory of God and are not able to be silent about
it, but are always coming to be beside being gladdened in hymning it. 9 Eph. i
15–17 [For this reason I also, having heard of the faith among you in the Lord
Jesus and that toward all the saints, do not cease giving thanks for you,
making mention in my prayers, that the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the
Father of glory, may give you a spirit of wisdom and of revelation, in
knowledge of him, having the eyes of the heart enlightened.] [Origen says] "in
knowledge of him, having the eyes of the heart enlightened" will seem to be a
solecism unless we render it as a hyperbaton; wherefore we think the sequence
of the phrasing is as follows: for this reason I also, having heard of the
faith among you in the Lord Jesus, having the eyes of the heart enlightened in
knowledge of him, and that toward all the saints, do not cease giving thanks
for you, making mention of you in my prayers, that the God of our Lord Jesus
Christ, the Father of glory, may give you a spirit of wisdom and of
revelation, so that you may know what is the hope of your calling, and what
follows. If the Father is father of something underlying and substantial, and
is also called Father of glory, clearly the glory is something underlying and
substantialized; and perhaps our Savior, just as he is Word and Wisdom and
Truth and Peace and Righteousness, so also is Glory; and if ever Glory of God
has been recorded as having been seen, nothing else must be understood to have
been seen than the Son of God. The spirit of wisdom then makes wise, and the
spirit of revelation takes away the veil from the heart, so that with unveiled
face, mirroring the glory of the Lord, one may be transformed into the same
image from glory to glory. No one then who is not wise has a spirit of wisdom,
and none of those who, when Moses is read, still have a veil upon the heart
have already received the spirit of revelation; unless perhaps the spirit of
wisdom makes wise in different ways by quality of the contemplations of
wisdom, and by quantity of the taking-away of the veils upon the letters it
does not similarly minister the revelation of those from whom the veil is
taken away. Besides these one must hear "having the eyes of the heart
enlightened in knowledge of him"; for if knowledge of God and recognition of
God are not the same, but the one who recognizes as it were re-cognizes what
he once knew and had forgotten, as many as come to be in recognition of God
knew him of old; wherefore all the ends of the earth will remember and


will turn to the Lord. And "having the eyes of the heart enlightened"
indicates the bodiless and intelligible nature of our governing part and of
the true light. [i 18–20] so that you may know what is the hope of his
calling, and what the riches of the glory of his inheritance in the saints,
and what the surpassing greatness of his power toward us who believe according
to the working of the might of his strength which he worked in the Christ when
he raised him from the dead. [Of Origen] The calling of God has a hope, which
one must know from much attention of life and quickness of mind; and his
inheritance has riches of much glory in the saints in the places above. The
one to whom, according to the prayer of Paul, the spirit of wisdom and of
revelation is given will himself know the things laid up for those who are
called and will contemplate the things hoped for, which are riches of glory
according to his inheritance, which he gives to those called to this holy
inheritance. And you will ask whether God himself is also the inheritance of
the saints, so that the heirs of God are understood also in this way, in order
that, just as we say heirs of lands and heirs of houses, ranging the
inheritance upon the possessions, so we may also understand the heirs of God.
And the law that says "but to the sons of Levi he will not give a lot in the
midst of their brothers, because I am their portion, says the Lord," and
elsewhere "the Lord is their inheritance," also has such a sense. The riches
of the glory of the inheritance of God therefore come to be in the mind of the
saints who will make room for so many things of the contemplation of God and
of his Word by a surpassing greatness of power of God, that toward the saints
and those who believe. For it is not of an ordinary power from God that we
have need in order to know what is the hope of his calling and what the riches
of the glory of his inheritance in the saints, but of a greatness of his power
coming to be in us who believe from him, and coming to be according to the
working of his strength that holds all things, which might of his strength has
now been named by Paul; and using this working of the might of his strength he
worked what he worked in the Christ when he raised him from the dead. [i
20–23] and seated him at his right hand in the heavenlies above every rule and
authority and power and lordship and every name that is named not only in this
age but also in the one to come; and he subjected all things under his feet,
and gave him as head over all things to the church, which is his body, the
fullness of him who is filled all in all. These things must be held to have
been said with a bodily character, not as if certain seats or perceptible
thrones were lying in the heavens upon which the Father sits and the Son at
the right of the Father; for understanding it thus we would not say these
things worthily of the divine nature. For neither is the earth subject bodily
to God himself as he sits in heaven, as a footstool of his feet. For how can
the heaven be a throne, measured by his span? and the earth a footstool of his
feet, itself also enclosed by his handful? For Isaiah says, "who measured the
water with the hand and the heaven with a span and all the earth with a
handful?" I think then that being near God and being far from him are
intelligible, all the saints who live according to the will of the Father of
all things also drawing near to God, and the impious being far from him, of
whom the prophet says "behold those who put themselves far from you will
perish." As therefore it has been understood who is near God and who is far
from him, so it must be held that the saints are said to be at his right and
the impious as it were at the left, just as the Savior also says "he sets the
sheep on the right and the goats on the left." But also "having seated him at
the right" is something of this sort, "having established and granted him a
kingdom and a rule," according to which he oversees and rules the saints,
benefiting them and imparting himself, Jesus who has become above all through
the philanthropic toward


all administration. Many then are the things that rule and lead those who need
oversight from him, and many the things that exercise authority and many that
lord it, and many also that impart their own power, some in this age, and some
that will work also in the age to come; but none of these rules a rule as
great as the Son. For all the things that rule need him as their ruler; but
also authorities will exercise authority better being under the authority of
Christ, the power of God excelling beside every remaining power; but even if
some are led into a beneficial slavery by those who lord it well, it is
necessary that these that lord it, receiving the science of lording from the
Lord of those who lord it, lord it over those they lord over. Whence the one
who raised Christ from the dead seated him at his right hand in the
heavenlies, above every rule and authority and power and lordship, and every
name that is named not only in this age but also in the one to come; for it is
likely that there are also some unspeakable and indescribable names named
according to the arrangement of the whole, not graspable by us, some in this
age, but also <some> in the one to come. And from where did the apostle have
these names, if not from the Holy Spirit that revealed them to him? And how
"and he subjected all things under his feet" has been said must be examined,
it being clear that there are countless numbers who still are not subject to
the Word of God. For we do not yet see all things subjected; and "he must
reign until he puts all enemies under his feet" makes clear that not yet are
all the enemies under his feet. One person then will say that this too has
been said according to foreknowledge. Another, presenting the ranking of God
by which he ranks each into some order, will say this: he subjected under his
feet all things that are of the order of those being subjected. And he gave
him as head over all things to the church, which is his body, <the fullness>
of him who is filled all in all. Finding the church called body of Christ, we
ask whether at some time, as the remaining body beside the head, one must
understand it as an instrument of the head, or as the body of the human being,
of which body the head is a part, so the whole church of Christ is a body of
