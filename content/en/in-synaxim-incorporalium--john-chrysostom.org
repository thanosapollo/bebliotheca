Homily on the synaxis of the incorporeal ones.
1 We do not keep festival for the angels in vain, paying them a prize and a
wage for the benefactions toward us; for they do not cease, with hymns and
unspeakable voices, propitiating the Master in the heavens for our sins. That
these things are so in fact, and not an empty word lacking witness, the divine
Gospel teaches; for having seen on earth the heavenly one, in
swaddling-clothes the uncircumscribed one, they did not hide the mystery from
men, but at once they announced it to the shepherds, they supposed they should
share in the benefaction, and in a single word they praised the glory in the
heavens and the peace on earth: Glory to God in the highest, and on earth
peace, among men good will. A man-loving army of a man-loving God does not, as
being bodiless, disdain those entangled in flesh and sinews and bones, nor
does it endure the passion which we endure toward one another; for among men
the healthy loathe the sick, the unhurt the wounded, the sober those who
revel, the temperate the unchaste, the continent the incontinent, whereas they
ought to suffer with them and through sympathy offer medicine to the one who
suffers; for it is not that those who sympathize partake of the other's
disease, but they themselves impart their own health.
2 But the mind of the angels, not looking to the bodily tent, but greeting the
kindred wing of the soul, rejoices seeing the grace in it even dancing among
men. And somewhere one of the angels said, astonished at the virginal
birth-pangs: The only-begotten Son who is in the bosom of the Father, who
pours forth to us the Holy Spirit, the creator of the angelic choir, who
arched the heavenly vaults and commanded the sun to run the day, and decreed
that the moon travel the night, who established the earth without foundation
and bridles the sea, lies in a manger, wrapped in swaddling-clothes. Born from
an unwedded womb he seems to be meditating something great; and the meditation
of God is saving. I know in part what is being meditated: he crafts against
the devil's crafts, he wishes to call back the exile, to loose the bound, to
renew the one who is being corrupted. I rejoice with human prosperities; I
find again the companion of God in paradise. This was what was biting the
devil; for always boasting and puffing great things, now he utters things low
and sullen and not boastful; for he does not know what is being done, but
conscience goads him, and the remembrance of the ancient deceit which he
plotted against the first-formed humans, bringing in death in exchange for
life and exchanging idolatry for piety.
3 Such things the choir of the angels spoke, looking upon our well-being. And
reasonably we too honor those who love us and those who intercede for us
toward the Master. Or have you not heard how the prophet Daniel brings in the
archangel Michael sparring with the overseer of the Persians for the freedom
of the Israelites? For the one was forcing the people to be slaves to the
Persians, and the other was eager to loose them from the captivity. And
Michael conquers the adversary; and the Jews, having crossed the Euphrates,
took again their ancestral hearth and renewed the burned temple of Solomon,
the work stretching to forty-six years; for sometimes hindered by the
neighbors, and sometimes beating back those who hindered, at last they
attained their desire, Nehemiah accomplishing the end of the building for
them. Setting out from this they said to the Lord: Forty and six years was
this temple in building; and will you in three days


raise it up? These then are the things under the old; but those of the new
covenant are greater. For throughout the whole time of the economy angels
served; angels announced Christ conceived and born, angels ministered when he
was baptized and when he worked wonders, angels bore witness when he was
buried and when he rose, they bore the Redeemer up to the heavens and again
they will escort the Judge from the heavens. If then we too are eager to dwell
with angels and to revel in noetic contemplations, let us serve the King of
the angels with much fear and trembling. Let us not unawares spend our life on
ourselves in revels and drunkenness and clappings and dances; and as if
seeking the door of repentance in the night we shall not find it; or rather we
shall find it, the Master calling us and saying: I am the door of the sheep.
4 And indeed it is necessary to praise angels; they themselves, hymning the
Creator, render him gracious and well-disposed toward men; and I mean angels,
our allies, those who campaign against the opposing powers; of whom is the
archangel Michael, who arrays himself against the devil over the body of
Moses, and stands against the ruler of the Persians for the freedom of the
people; of whom is that one who turns the ass aside and restrains Balaam from
his unseemly divination, and since the seer did not understand what was being
done, by the voice of an irrational creature he brought the rational one to
his senses; of whom is the one who has drawn the sword and urges Joshua the
son of Nun to join battle with the enemies; of whom is the one who smote the
eighty-five thousand of the Assyrians in a single night and joined death to
the sleep of the barbarians; of whom is the one who transported the prophet
Habakkuk with an aerial leap, that he might give the prophet Daniel, in the
midst of the unfed lions, a taste of food. These then and such as these are
against the enemies; and there are those who also excel as champions against
the passions; of which sort was the divine Raphael, who by the dissection of a
single fish healed a demon-possessed girl, and for a blind old man crafted it
that he should see the sun. But neither are those who served the new covenant
worthy of silence; of whom a great part is the archangel Gabriel, who spoke an
oracle to Zachariah and exacted the unbelief with silence, because he did not
believe that the barren woman would conceive on account of the economy that
was to come, who announced as good news to the reverent Mary the unwedded
birth-pangs; this one, leading shepherds to the cave, announced the
uncontainable one in a manger, the uncircumscribed one in swaddling-clothes.
Those sent up a concordant song of doxology: Glory to God in the highest, and
on earth peace; among men good will. Another admonishes the betrothed of the
Virgin not to put away as corrupted her who was incorrupt; another suggests to
him, on account of Herod's infanticide, flight and return; another hinders the
magi from returning to the tyrant; and they were ministering on earth to the
Creator even though they beheld the uncreated one in flesh; for they marveled
at his strength, by which the tempter fell; and according to the voice of
David, taking him up on their hands, they carried him out of the range of the
stones; for the Jews, it says, took up stones, that they might cast them at
him. Others ran together to the Passion; others after the resurrection,
sitting at the empty tomb, said to the faithful women: Why do you seek the
living among the dead? And after the ascension they escorted the disciples as
bodyguards, loosing Peter's bonds, lightening Paul's dangers on land and sea,
mediating for the faith of Cornelius, striking Herod the slayer of the just
when he was speaking greatly.
5 Are they not then worthy of the greatest honor, those who campaign for our
life? For not only were angels appointed as guardians of nations: When the
Most High, it says, divided the nations, the sons of Adam whom he scattered,
he set the bounds of the nations according to the number of the angels of God.
But in fact an angel was allotted to each of men; the word is not mine, but of
the divine Scripture; for when the maidservant Rhoda was announcing to the
apostles that Peter stood before


the doors, having escaped Herod's prison, they, not believing, said: It is his
angel. And the Savior also bears witness to these things, speaking about the
children: See that you despise not one of these little ones; for I say to you
that their angels in heaven always behold the face of my Father who is in
heaven. And over each Church Christ set guardian angels, as he says revealing
to John: Say to the angel of the Church of Smyrna: I know your poverty and
your affliction, but you are rich. What do you say, Master? Am I rich, being
poor? Why do you enrich me with an empty name? For if I am rich, I am not
poor; but if I am poor, I am not rich; for great is the distance of wealth and
poverty, as you yourself testify, having set a great gulf between rich and
poor. The faces are festering; the rich man turns away from the beggar, the
beggar looks askance at the rich; the things are contrary, the life is uneven;
for the one groans under sufferings, the other cackles at pleasures; this one
prays to obtain bread, that one overflows with gluttonies; this one has not
even a cotyle of wine, that one, reveling, belches out his drunkenness; this
one is wrapped in a rag, that one delights himself in varied garments; this
one is cast on the ground, keeping vigil, that one on a bed snores, dreaming
of luxury; this one asks for a small coin, that one is heaped with gold; for
he who treasures up but does not distribute resembles a beggar awaiting a rich
burial. But they do not even equally enjoy the seasons; for winter is harsh to
the beggar who has no warmth, and summer is wretched to the rich man who from
satiety cannot bear the heat. How then is the same man poor and rich? True is
the word; for Truth is the one who utters it. We are poor through the want of
money, but rich through the treasures of faith. And it is better to be poor in
the things of this life, but rich in the spiritual; for of the former the end
is corruption, of the latter the gain is inalienable. For this reason the
Savior says: Do not treasure up for yourselves treasures on the earth, where
moth and eating make them vanish, where thieves dig through, and the rest. It
is good then for us to treasure up the heavenly things, feeding the poor,
lodging the homeless, clothing the unclad, releasing debtors, and transferring
the loan to God: He who has mercy on a beggar, it says, lends to God. For
these things angels, beholding, report to the Judge of all; and he commands
them to say to the one who works the good: Your prayers and your alms have
gone up for a memorial before God.
6 And the most wise Daniel, young in years but old in mind, who was a captive
through the evils of others, but through his own virtues even carried off the
first prizes against the most shameful of the elders, and advocate of the
blameless Susanna, the one who bridled the lions and made a cake of
destruction for the dragon of Bel, the spectator of others' dreams and lover
of mystical contemplations; for Daniel, it says, was a man of desires, desires
not of flesh nor of passions of the soul, but of inspired wisdom and
understanding; whence God announced to him beforehand the things to come, the
empires of the earth, those of the Medes and Persians and Macedonians and of
the Romans themselves, and upon all these our Lord Jesus as a stone being cut
from an uncut mountain, that is, being born in the flesh from an unwedded
virgin, the revival of the dead, the change of the living, the judgment
through fire, the crown of the pious and the torment of the impious. Such a
man says that the angels are a great matter; for by command of God who created
all creation they were allotted their portions and hold sway over the
strongest of the nations, one to oversee the Persians, and the archangel
Michael to guard the people of the circumcision; and that a contention arose
for both overseers, not according to a passion of anger, but according to some
divine and ineffable reason, the one forcing the Jews to be slaves to the
Persians, the other pulling them toward freedom. One might suppose the
overseer of the Persians saying such things: Let be, Michael; why


do you shake ancient rights, wishing to lead the Jews out from the yoke of the
Persians? It is not a new thing; I received them from Chaldeans and Medes. The
slavery is the penalty of impiety. For they themselves were slaves to foreign
gods, setting aside the benefactor God, who afflicted the Egyptians with
varied plagues, who led them on foot through the sea, who lit the night with a
pillar of fire, who cooled the day with the shadow of a cloud, who feasted
them with aerial foods, who gave them drink from spontaneous springs. And what
of the most unseemly things did they not do, driving out strangers, marrying
unlawfully, being greedy, seizing orphans and widows and sojourners, making a
calf, killing children? Did they not saw Isaiah? Did they not throw Jeremiah
into the pit? Did they not slaughter Zechariah beside the holy altars? Did
they not shoot the prophet Uriah with javelins? Did they not stone Naboth for
gain? Having dared all the terrible things against men, they keep their hands
thereafter against God. Let them be tamed by slavery, lest living in luxury
they do something worse. Instead of God they worshipped demons; instead of
demons they shall be slaves to barbarians, and leaving the land flowing with
milk and honey they shall work the foreign lands, plowing for others, reaping
fruits for others, fattening the bellies of the nations with unresting sweats,
seeing children not of the circumcision being emasculated; For they shall
make eunuchs, it says, in the house of the kings of the Babylonians, seeing
their women defiled by incontinent men, and dominated by harsh women, ordered
to carry water and gather firewood, their little daughters worn out at the
distaffs and the looms. Delivered from the Egyptians they were ungrateful; let
them learn to be sober by being slaves to the Persians; they crossed the Red
Sea, they shall not again cross the Euphrates, returning to their ancestral
hearth; for even if they ever run away from the Persians, a heavier master
will not be lacking to their minds; freed from brickmaking, they did not
forget knavery.
7 To these things Michael: Will you not cease attempting the impossible? Do
you expect Israel to remain in slavery? Seventy years the grim things have
been bounded. As a father God disciplined them; he struck them when they
sinned, and when they turn he does not turn away. For even if they themselves
are worthy of calamities, still God has not forgotten the promises toward
Abraham, he does not set aside the covenants toward David; he keeps the live
coal of the race, he warms the spark of the seed; he does not refute the
mouths of those who bear the Spirit. The expected one must come; all nature
gapes through the voice of Isaiah, awaiting the strange mystery, it waits to
see a virgin in birth-pangs; For behold, it says, the virgin shall have in the
womb and shall bear a son. Persians too shall worship that one's infant; the
noetic powers shall bend the knee, as David said: And let all the angels of
God worship him. What need is there to say more? The commander-in-chief
conquers, he puts the adversary to flight. The Jews according to the appointed
time were delivered from the captivity, Zerubbabel brought them back, being a
descendant of David; they recovered Palestine, they rebuilt the temple, they
renewed the sacrifices. When five hundred and forty-eight years had run by, an
unwedded virgin gives birth; a king without successor is born; magi worshipped
him, being not alien to the Persians. Angels glorified the birth: Glory to God
in the highest, and on earth peace, among men good will. Christ is baptized
<into> our redemption; and angels coming ministered. He undergoes a passion
that dissolves death; and again angels bear witness to the raising of the
dead, turning men from seeking the living among the dead. He is taken up to
the heavens; and angels again announce his second coming: For behold, it says,
two men stood by them


in white garments and the rest. We too, awaiting him, let us rekindle the
lamps of our souls, dripping on the spiritual oil, passing the day of life
with unsleeping hearts, glorifying with unceasing mouths the power of the
Trinity; for the power of God is unto the ages of ages, amen.
