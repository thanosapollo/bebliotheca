And he endured all these things. Verse 11. They came each from his own country
to him, Eliphaz king of the Temanites, Baldad tyrant of the Sauchaeans, Sophar
king of the Minaeans. Friendly indeed is your presence, and friendly the
exhortation. Eliphaz:


God scattered me, or, contempt of my God. Teman, their eclipse. Sophar, a
watcher, or contempt. Baldad, growing old, and glorious. Of the Sauchaeans,
their idle talk. Verse 11. And the three friends of his, having heard all the
evils that had come upon him, came each from his own country to him. If anyone
seeks why after so long a time the friends have arrived, let him consider
first that they dwelt in different countries, and after a very long time many
perhaps heard, but the paradox of the disaster also seemed unbelievable, even
if Job sits upon a dunghill =17.65= and without help. And they waited to
confirm the report through more, or even to send out and see whether the
things said were also true. In these things, then, the time was spent. In
addition to these, when they knew the truth, being kings and about to go
abroad, they had necessity first to set the things of the house well in order,
and to set over those ruled the ones in their place. All these things occupied
them, and they delayed. And if they had not been exceedingly beloved some and
just, they would not, leaving their own countries and kingdoms, have come for
the visitation, but would have fulfilled the need through others. And it is
possible to consider that there was also some divine economy of the delay of
the arrival. For since together with the discourse of the friends the whole of
the contest was also loosed, God having spoken, and having recovered the just
man and crowned him, some divine economy came to be, that the friends delay,
in order that the things of the disease be prolonged, and the just man, having
contended more greatly, be proclaimed more justly by God. Verse 11. And they
came to him with one mind, to comfort and to visit him. Symmachus rendered
thus: For they arranged together, having come, to sympathize with him, and to
console him. See also from this, that another time also passed, until, having
sent to one another, they made the gathering to the same and the arrival.
Verse 12. And having sprinkled earth upon their heads. Verse 13. They sat with
him seven days, and seven nights. And Scripture did not say that they were
sitting seven days from morning until evening, nor seven nights from evening
until morning. It is clear then that, sitting the greater part of the day,
they withdrew to fulfill the necessary needs; and coming back again, they sat
until far into the night, and going away again, they came early to him again.
Which indeed is proper to the most genuine and most sympathetic friends. And
Scripture, using the common custom, said thus simply: They sat with him seven
days and seven nights. Since even when it says that Jacob served fourteen
years, and was burned with the burning of the day and the frost of the night,
did he never go under a roof? Did he meet with no rest in the whole time?
These things, then, even to discuss in detail is idle talk. For as I said,
Scripture uses common custom. But since some, mocking Scripture, from this too
tear it down as false: For how, they say, would they continue without food and
without sleep for so many days? we have been forced to be carried along with
their untimely narrowness. CH. 3. But in these things the second chapter has
been completed; and making a preliminary consideration of the third, we say,
=17.68= that the just man is in pain, and no one would deny it; for virtue is
not of the insensible, but of those who philosophize in the passions. And he
cannot, according to what appears, being most sharply stung by the blow in the
flesh; and according to what is understood, as a just and god-loving man
seeing some scandalized at their sufferings, as the glory of God being torn
down upon him, if the just man endures these things. For even when he says:
Why have you set me as an accuser of you? he himself was not happening against
God, God forbid! for to whom would he have to petition? But this is what he
says: Why through me, Master, is your name blasphemed? And he is disturbed no
less and is dizzy, lest perhaps he has undergone a greater abandonment from
God. For a god-loving soul is not so punished as by suspecting it has been
left behind by God; and ten thousand times it chooses death, rather than after


pains and such a suspicion to be numbered among the living. Now at the loss of
the possessions and the sudden death of the children we heard what sort of
thanksgiving voices he sent forth, and how he trained the wife who had thought
something unseemly about the Master, and what things he uttered after trial
and contempt and the sharp passion, and how none of the human accidents
disturbed him; but when the friends arrived, just and sympathetic men, he
thought it timely, seeing their endurance and the love upon them, to speak and
to make public the pains according to the soul, and he utters according to the
history what is without danger. For one was the devil's eagerness, to prepare
the just man to blaspheme God. And he is nowhere seen to have accomplished
this, but rather even speaking well, and sending up thanksgiving voices. And
he curses the day of birth, and longs for death, because of the causes spoken
above. And toward the meaning he speaks mysteries; and he philosophizes about
the things already having happened, and gives full assurance of the future.
For such always are the sayings of the holy prophets. And coming upon the very
words, let us make the meaning clear, he himself guiding toward the
interpretation, who also strengthened holy Job toward the contests. Verse 1.
After this Job opened his mouth. That a just man opens the mouth, one ought
not simply thus to attend to the things said, but to seek the benefit from the
words. For such utterances of the saints are always full of benefit; and a
just man opening the mouth makes public hidden treasures. Wherefore also the
vessel of election, Paul, writing says: My mouth has opened toward you,
Corinthians. And concerning the Lord it has been written that Opening his
mouth he taught. And simply that one is witnessed to open a mouth, who has
first been taught the measures of silence. Therefore Job also opens the mouth
=17.69= after this; that is, after the great trophy, after those sufferings,
he does not utter unexamined, being in pain in the body, the tongue
anticipating the lawful order of prudence, but he is shown even in the voices
as being temperate. And the witness of the opening of his mouth makes clear
both the calm of the soul and the becomingness of the words. CH. 19. Verse 1.
How long will you make my soul toilsome? Just as heavy bodies laid upon bodies
of animals make the bearers grow weary; so there are certain heavy words,
which when someone has heard he grows weary. As pressed by a weight, of the
power of the words spoken not badly, and harshly, or upon reviling by the
other two who came with Baldad, as a perception of a heavy burden, Job says,
having grown weary in the soul at the things they have said: How long will you
make my soul toilsome? And a little later: You see how the tyrant of the
Sauchaeans has been written beforehand. Tyrannical are his words, which Job
having heard, says: How long will you make my soul toilsome? Nothing else so
reaches a man's soul as a hard word. Often one who has endured the tortures
brought upon the body has not endured words. The perfect athlete, however,
even if growing weary, yet endures the harsh words. For to endure insult is of
a great man. Wherefore among his own brave deeds the Apostle says: Being
reviled we bless, being persecuted we endure, being defamed we exhort. As
refuse of the world we have become, the offscouring of all until now. And a
little later he says then, because of the, How long will you make my soul
toilsome? You see? It is Job, whose book also contains nothing else
exceptionally but his manliness and his endurance. But nevertheless he
perceives the toils with which he had grown weary from the hard word of those
who had come to visit him. Verse 3. Know only, that the Lord has made me thus.
The things you see about me, do not think have come to me from elsewhere than
from God. Fear, then. For the one bringing these things upon me is able to
bring them upon you also. Verse 3. You speak against me; not being ashamed of
me, you press upon me. Often also someone speaks against, doing badly. But
when he does not


blush, he speaks to that one's face, not convicting, nor saying the true
things, but reproaching and insulting. For one must not insult those being
punished by God, but lament and fear. Verse 4. Yes indeed in truth I have gone
astray, and error lodges with me; to speak a word which one ought not. Then he
says by hypothesis: I grant you, as it is =17.72= supposed, that through the
sins I suffer these things; was it then necessary for you to reproach me thus?
And error lodges with me. Even if by hypothesis I had done such things as one
ought not; even if I were such, was it not necessary for you, seeing scourges,
the ichors, the worms, the loss of the belongings, to be ashamed? But you
trample upon me further, not pitying me, nor sympathizing with my
circumstance. Verse 5. But let be, that you magnify yourselves against me, and
you leap upon me with reproach. You do not reproach me moderately, but leaping
as it were mounting and trampling me you reproach. Verse . And his fortress he
has raised upon me. As if I am under a wall, a fortress of the circumstance,
which I cannot flee, until God judges to take it down. Verse 7. Behold I laugh
at reproach, and I will not speak. This is the: Overcome the evil in the good.
Of the same. Behold at your reproach I am thought to laugh, not to speak. CH.
20. Verse . If his gifts go up into heaven; and his sacrifice touch the
clouds. Of the impious, if the gifts go up into heaven, if they be raised very
high, if his sacrifice touch the clouds. Whenever the heretic prays, whenever
his sacrifice goes up in high-talk, whenever it seems to have been fixed; then
he will be destroyed to the end. For his prayer is reckoned to him for sin,
and this prayer of his is sin. Verse 7. For when he seems already to have been
fixed, then he will be destroyed to the end. Like, But the enemies of the
Lord, together with being glorified and raised, failing as smoke they failed.
Verse 7. And those who have seen him will say: Where is he? Who are those who
have seen him? We the churchly, who have seen their falls, will say: Where is
he? Verse 8. As a dream that has flown away it shall not be found. Such men
dreaming, and fashioning phantasms, do not rest the mind upon the truth, going
astray, and they become as a dream that has flown away, which shall not be
found. Verse 10. Let inferiors destroy his sons. The sons and disciples of the
one who taught these things. Verse 10. And let his hands kindle pains. His
hands, he says, that is the deeds, will kindle punishment for him. And his
hands. Of this impious and fallen one, whose prayer is reckoned for sin, whose
sacrifice if it go up into heaven, and =17.73= touch the clouds, then he is
destroyed to the end; this one's hands will kindle pains; that is, they will
also spend in last pains. And he speaks concerning the sons of the lawless
one. Verse 13. Gall of an asp in his belly. For he has teachings which are not
sweet, but gall of an asp, as it were wicked, that concerning the wine of
their teaching. For from the vine of Sodom is their vine, and the rest. And
after a few: And gall of an asp in the belly of the heterodox, and of the one
being impious in the doctrines contrary to the truth. Verse 15. Wealth
gathered unjustly shall be vomited. For what other wealth is gathered unjustly
than that one in their words? For it is gathered unjustly. But the good one
will not allow them, having fallen, to have wealth gathered unjustly always in
themselves; for he will make them vomit it. Verse 15. From his house an angel
will drag him out. For an angel according to energy and benefaction of God
will drag him out from that very one built badly, which is a church of those
doing wickedness. For this is his house. But an angel will drag him out from
his house. Verse 17. Let him not see milking of nomads. A heterodox does not
see milking of nomads. And nomads are certain souls pastured under the good
Shepherd, who said: I am the door. Through me if anyone enter, he shall be
saved. And he shall go in, and go out, and find pasture. Verse 18. As a
striphnos unchewed, unswallowable. The words and the doctrines tough and
dense, those coming to be against God have nothing sweet. Striphnos is a
plant, which


is made rough on the tongue of the one chewing. As therefore, he says, that
plant, even if someone should very much contend, having softened it with the
teeth, he cannot swallow; so also the one wishing to enjoy the alien things
will carry off no profit of the labors. Verse 20. Therefore his good things
will not bloom. The things he seems to do as good will not bloom; whenever he
seems already to have been filled, he will be afflicted. Verse 20. There is
not of him salvation for the belongings. His belongings cannot procure
salvation for him. Otherwise. Verse 22. And every necessity will come upon
him, and he will send upon him a wrath of anger. Verse 22. And every necessity
will come upon him. These things are hortatory for us, that we stand away from
the alien things. =17.76= Verse 22. Wash pains upon him. Instead of a
snowstorm, pains come upon such a one. Verse 24. And he shall not be saved
from a hand of iron. Iron he does not mean this perceptible one, but a most
cutting word. Verse 25. And let a dart go through his body. These darts, the
inflamed ones, wound him. Verse 25. Let unburnable fire devour him. We learn a
certain mystery concerning the punishment laid up, that we may guard ourselves
from suffering it. He said unburnable fire to devour the impious; this fire,
the material one with us, is not unburnable fire; for it is seen. But that
fire is invisible, the one coming upon the sinners, which burns them within.
It is intelligible and punishing. If you wish to take an image of that fire,
think for me the one fevering to excess, and being inflamed by fire. You will
not find by sight the fire in him, but it underlies, and burns his inward
parts. Thus someone fevering from the sins is burned. Verse 25. And let a
sojourner maltreat his house. The one who warred against that people was a
sojourner, and not a proselyte; who maltreated his house, destroying the
supposed sanctuary, and the city which they said to be holy. Verse 27. And let
heaven uncover his lawless deeds. I.e., And from God and from heaven he will
undergo the dreadful things. And he said these things, since Job, having said
he had been struck by God, remembered also as having been wronged by men. He
constructs therefore that all things were brought upon him through sin. Verse
27. And let earth rise up against him. This very earth rises up against that
people. Verse 29. And a possession of belongings to him from the overseer.
Does one learn what overseer was named here? that God has been called
overseer, and the overseers of that one are ambitious in every way, the good
and noble, to bear the image. And just as the one who has imitated a royal
image is honored for having imitated well, and is honored also for having
adorned it; so blessed is an overseer, the one who has understood the true
overseer, and emulating him, and becoming as among men God; or having as
overseer the one who makes him truly an overseer in Christ Jesus; to whom the
glory and the might unto the ages of the ages. Amen. CH. 21. Verse 5. Looking
in upon me, take wonder. You say you are wise, and know why I suffer these
things, as of God not sleeping upon the sins of men. And you have dared to say
I am a sinner, and have justly arrived at these things. Verse 7. And they have
grown old also in wealth. They are not newly rich, but they have made many
years =17.77= being wealthy, and always increasing the wealth. Verse 8. Their
seed according to soul. Of the just man the seed is according to spirit; For
he sows into the spirit, and from the spirit he reaps eternal life; but of
such, since a soulish man does not receive the things of the spirit; for it is
folly to him; the seed is not according to spirit, but according to soul, the
inferior. Verse 8. And their children in eyes. All things whatever they do,
they do toward being seen by men. For children are the works of each. There
are not children of the heterodox in heart, in good reasonings, but in eyes,
toward being seen by men. Verse 9. Their houses prosper. Whenever you enter a
synagogue of heretics, and see there a multitude being gathered, see, that
their houses prosper. Verse 9. And fear nowhere.


They are fearless. They do not fear God, they despise the Godhead; saying such
things about God, they do not fear, that they speak about God. Verse 9. And a
scourge from the Lord is not upon them. Of the same. If you see a man speaking
such things against God, being healthy in the body, seeing, keeping the
sense-organs, and seeming sometimes even in the worldly things to prosper;
see, that it has been fulfilled upon him, being impious, the: And a scourge
from the Lord is not upon them. Verse 10. Their cow has not calved untimely.
Of the same. The ox is an agricultural animal, and breathing together toward
agriculture. And they too farm. And they farm Sodom and Gomorrah, they farm
Egypt. Their cow has not calved untimely; but she too brought forth, and
generated a child, in order that their wicked earth be farmed. Verse 10. And
she that has in the belly of them was saved, and did not slip. Whatever they
conceive, these they also carry, not conceiving from the fear of God, but from
wickedness. Behold he travailed injustice, he conceived toil, and bore
lawlessness. Verse 11. And they remain as eternal sheep. What mind is there of
the: they will remain as eternal sheep? Concerning whom Christ says: My sheep
hear my voice, and I give them eternal life, and the rest; these are the
eternal sheep. Since therefore the heterodox are mimes, and they wish to
imitate the flock of Christ, ordaining for themselves overseers, presbyters,
deacons, teachers, a people, catechumens, imitating the eternal sheep, they
fulfill the: And they remain as eternal sheep. And they are not eternal sheep,
but they remain as eternal sheep. =17.80= Verse 11. And their children play.
Verse 12. Having taken up psaltery and cithara. Ours take up psaltery and
cithara, hymning God, and glorifying him. But their children, the mindless
among them, play, taking up psaltery and cithara. Whenever they take up the
words of the Old Covenant, they play, and laugh at the oracles of the law and
of the prophets. Taking up the harmonic things, psaltery that of the law,
cithara the prophetic, they play at them. Verse 12. And they are gladdened
with a voice of a psalm. And they themselves say psalms of Valentinus, and
they say odes of Basilides, and they utter some such things, being gladdened
with a voice of a psalm. Verse 13. And in a rest of Hades they were put to
sleep. And in a rest not of heaven, but of Hades they were put to sleep. And
we pray not to be put to sleep in a rest of Hades, but in a rest of God in the
holy heavens with Christ reigning over us; to whom the glory and the might
unto the ages of the ages. Amen. CH. 22. Verse 2. Is it not the Lord who
teaches understanding and knowledge? The things being administered by the Holy
Spirit must be counted worthy of being written down. Therefore it is written
down for the one encountering to be benefited from the things being read. If
then the words of the three, of those who came to Job, were not useful, so
that the one encountering take something from the things said by them toward
Job; providence would not have made even the words of the three be written
into the book of Job. It is possible then to be benefited from his words,
looking into the doctrines; but to see the charge of the words concerning one
thing only, when they accuse Job, thinking him to suffer through the things he
has sinned; not seeing that there are also many other causes of things
happening to men. Such things are considered to be good or harsh, or however
one wishes to name them. And after a few: Is it not the Lord who teaches
understanding and knowledge? The doctrine is sound; that a true teacher of
virtue cannot be a man. For the one teaching knowledge to man, even in the
Psalms it has been written, that it is no other than God. And, Teach me, he
says, your ordinances, the Prophet, knowing the true and perfect teacher to be
God. And God teaches by shining upon the soul of the one learning from him,
and lighting the mind with the light, with his true word. Therefore just men
also


teach us, having received a teaching grace. Verse 4. And he will enter with
you into judgment. God making an account of the one provided for by him,
convicts him. And he will enter with you into judgment. We have often said,
whenever this doctrine has fallen in other words, =17.81= that God is so
philanthropic, that, even judging us kindly, he permits us to speak toward
him, as being judged with us, concerning the things we wish. I used then those
from Isaiah: Come, and let us converse, says the Lord. For the, let us
converse, is such: If you have something to convict me of, and my providence,
as having neglected you, as not having done the things due, speak; And I,
whatever I have I will say, I will persuade you, condemning you as being
worthy of being condemned. Verse 5. Is not your wickedness much? Reasonably I
do not simply say he has become unjust; since he suffers such things, of such
a size as none of them has fallen into, such as you. It is likely through the
innumerable number of your faults, these things have come to you. Of the same.
He did not first name sin, then the wickedness; but seeing the wickedness the
base habit, then after this the things being sinned from the base habit, which
he said to be innumerable sins. Verse 5. And your sins are innumerable.
Eliphaz supposes this concerning Job. He knew him rich, and he says: You were
taking pledges from your brothers, as from those owing not owing. For the, You
were taking pledges in vain, is such. Verse . You were taking pledges of your
brothers in vain. Of the same. And he stretches on again another sin of his.
Is it ever possible to take garments? Not of a naked one, but of a rich one
having many garments laid away, to take. Three ... indeed he of the many there
took a garment; but of a naked one, but of a rich one. If however someone have
one tunic, and this tunic, on a pretext of demanding what is mine, I strip
off, not having pitied that naked one, I have taken away the garment of the
naked. And many such things happen among us, whenever we lend, whenever we
come toward debtors. Often a debtor of ours is poor to excess, not being able
to pay back; and we, having shut our inward parts, become pitiless and raw,
not seeing that he feeds a wife and children, and is scarcely fed from the few
obols being procured for him. We demand bitterly, sometimes even having
superfluities with ourselves. These things then he says accusing Job, that he
took away a garment of naked ones. Verse 7. Nor did you give water to the
thirsty to drink; but of the hungry you deprived a morsel. On the same.
Therefore not only the doing injustice, but also the not doing good is liable.
All these things are accusations, and truly blameworthy. But they are not true
concerning Job, nor were they said well concerning him. Of the same. Such
things happen in the bitter =17.84= demands. Sometimes someone has that very
one morsel; and even if he does not have a whole loaf, we take it from him
saying: But you owe. Verse 9. And you sent widows away empty. Let us take care
if a widow enter our house, lest we send her away empty. According to power
let each according to what he has fill the bosom of the widow. Verse 9. And
you maltreated orphans. Of the same. One must maltreat no one, and especially
one must spare the orphans. For concerning the helpless souls God commanded to
fight. And rather than the other men, poor, widows, orphans need help. Verse
10. Therefore snares have surrounded you. He calls snares the things that have
happened to him, in which he has fallen, into which also his soul is snared
from the things that have happened to him. Verse 11. The light has turned out
darkness for you. You were in light, when your belongings were, when your
children were alive, when your body was healthy. But now as it were you have
gone down into darkness, into these circumstances. On the same. That is, in
desolation, and under the open sky, a wanderer, and a fugitive, and without a
hearth. Verse 11. And water has covered you having been put to sleep. The,
water has covered you having been put to sleep, is such: The opposing energy
has covered you. Since you were not awake, since you did not sleep. And
Scripture knows a certain sleep of sin. Wherefore it has been commanded to us
saying: Do not give sleep to your eyes, nor slumber to the eyelids, that you
may live


as a gazelle from snares, and as a bird from a trap. Verse 12. Does not the
one dwelling the high things oversee? But you think that God dwelling in the
high things does not see the things being done. As then overseeing all, he
brings down those lifted in mind by the insolent habit. Verse 12. And those
being carried by insolence he has humbled. If someone is carried by insolence,
and as it were is swept down by an insolent habit and deed, this one is
humbled by God. It is likely then, since you too have insolented widows and
orphans, and have taken pledges of your brothers in vain; therefore you have
suffered these things, and have been humbled. Verse 13. And you said, What has
the Strong One known? It is likely that you were saying by yourself, What has
the Strong One known? As it were, Can God have known all things? Verse 13. Or
will he judge according to the gloom? Of the same. And God is able to judge
even having come to be in a storm. Verse 14. And he travels through the circle
of heaven. The providence of God goes around. =17.85= Verse 15. Will you keep
an eternal path? Are you able to watch the eternal path, according to which
the providence of God travels? Verse 15. Which just men have trodden. Of the
same. Just men have trodden the eternal path. For they walk according to an
eternal path. Since all things lie before them to do, so as to obtain the age
to come. Even if therefore they seem to walk equally, yet the soul itself
steps upon eternal paths. Verse 1 0. Who were taken untimely. Often just men
have died before the season. For having been perfected in a little the just
man has filled long times; for his soul is virtue in the Lord. Verse 1 0. A
river flowing upon, their foundations. Of the same. The foundations of the
just are such. They always advance, they always increase in the good things.
Verse 18. And who filled their houses with good things. Of the same. He filled
the houses of the just with good things. I do not hear these houses; but their
houses, those according to the governing part, he filled with good things. Of
what sort of good things? Temperance, justice, courage, prudence, those from
the works. That is the being filled of houses with good things. Verse 19. And
a blameless one sneered at them. Whom? or the counsel of the impious? As it
were: they wish to teach certain things contrary to the truth, in saying;
which things they say having wretched doctrines. These a blameless one seeing
sneers. Verse 20. And fire will devour their remainder. Of the same. This
which has been left according to the impious, the former having died, the fire
will devour. Verse 22. And he took from his mouth an exposition. I was seeking
by myself the exposition, what it signifies. And I found in another edition
instead of it a confession; He says then rather: Confess concerning your sins;
take from the mouth of God an introduction with confession; and take up his
words from your heart. And Eliphaz was saying these things, admonishing, as he
thought, Job. Verse 23. And if you turn, and humble yourself before the Lord.
He admonishes him toward turning, thinking him to have suffered these things
