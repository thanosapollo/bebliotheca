ENCOMIUM ON THE HOLY MARTYR JULIAN.

=50.665=

αʹ. If on earth such honors belong to the martyrs, after their departure from
here what sort of crowns are woven in the heavens for their holy heads? If
before =50.666= the resurrection the glory is so great, how great is the
brightness after the resurrection? If from their fellow servants they enjoy so
great a service, of how much goodwill will they obtain =50.667= from the
Master? If we who are evil know how to honor and to be struck with awe at
those of our fellow servants who succeed, because they contended for Christ,
how much more will our heavenly Father give countless good things to those who
have toiled for him? For he is munificent and a lover of mankind; yet it is
not for this reason that great honors await them, but because he is also their
debtor.

The martyrs were not slain for us, and yet we run together to their honor. And
if we, for whom they were not slain, run together, what will Christ not do,
for whom they laid down their heads? If to those to whom he owed nothing he
gave so many good things, these, to whom he is a debtor, with how many gifts
will he not repay them? He owed nothing before this to the inhabited world;
For all have sinned, says Paul, and fall short of the glory of God; rather he
owed punishment and chastisement; yet though he owed us punishment and
chastisement, he granted eternal life. If then to those to whom he owed
punishment he gave a kingdom, to those to whom he owes eternal life, what will
he not give? and with how many honors will he not honor them? If he was
crucified for those who hated him, and poured out his blood, for those who
poured out their blood through the confession of him, what will he not
accomplish? If he so loved those who turned away and leaped off as even to die
for them, those who loved him with the greatest measure (For no one has
greater love than this, that one should lay down his life for his friends),
with how much goodwill and service will he receive them? The athletes of the
contests outside both wrestle, and conquer, and are proclaimed, and are
crowned in the same arena; but these athletes of piety are not so; they
wrestled in the present age, but they are crowned in that age to come; they
boxed here with the devil and prevailed, but they are proclaimed there. And
that you may learn that this is true, and that the crowns are not given to
them here, but that all the gifts await them there, hear Paul saying, I have
fought the good fight, I have finished the course, I have kept the faith;
henceforth there is laid up for me the crown of righteousness; where and when?
Which the Lord, the righteous judge, will give me on that day. Here he ran,
there he is crowned; here he conquered, and there he is proclaimed.

You heard him also today crying out and saying, These all died according to
faith, not having received the promises, but having seen them from afar, and
having greeted them. Why then for the athletes outside are both the victories
and the crowns together, but for the athletes of piety the victories and the
crowns are not together, but after so great an interval of time? They sweated,
they toiled here, they endured countless wounds, and he does not crown them at
once?

No, he says; for the nature of the present life does not admit the greatness
of that honor; the present age is perishable and short, that one is infinite,
and immortal, and without end. For this reason he allotted the toils to the
short and temporary age, but stored up the crowns for the unaging and
immortal, so that both the burden of the toils =50.668= might be cut short,
being dissolved together with the brevity of the time, and


the enjoyment of the crowns might remain lasting and without end, extending
together with the immortality of those infinite ages. Wishing then to honor
them more greatly he deferred the gift; and not for this reason only, but so
that they might thereafter have the pleasure pure. For just as he who first
lives in luxury and enjoys ease, but after these things is afflicted, does not
perceive the present luxury by reason of the expectation of the coming evils;
so he who first boxes, and contends, and endures countless evils, but after
these things is crowned, does not perceive the present evils, being renewed by
the hope of the coming good things. And he not only lightened them of the
present toil by the coming hope, but also by the order, having arranged that
the affliction come before the ease, so that they might not be stretched too
greatly by the present evils while looking toward those things. So also those
who box receive the wounds eagerly, looking not toward the pains, but toward
the crown; so also sailors, enduring countless dangers, storms, and a certain
harsh war, and arraying themselves against wild beasts, and against the
evildoers on the sea, reckon none of these things, but look toward the
harbors, and the wealth from the trade; so also the martyrs, suffering
countless evils, and having the body cut apart by various tortures, looked
toward none of these things, but gaped toward heaven and the good things from
there. And that you may learn that the things that are by nature burdensome
and unbearable become light and most easy by the hope of the things to come,
hear the chief of such good things saying: For the momentary lightness of our
affliction works for us an eternal weight of glory from excess unto excess.
How, tell me? While we look not at the things that are seen, but at the things
that are not seen.

βʹ. And these things have not been said by me simply, but for your sake, so
that when you see someone living in luxury and enjoying ease in this life, but
about to be punished there, you may not call him blessed because of the
present luxury, but may count him wretched because of the coming punishment.
And again when you see someone who is about to enjoy much honor there being in
affliction and distress, and in countless evils in this temporary life, do not
weep because of the present evils, but call him blessed, and think him
enviable because of the crowns laid up for him in those infinite ages. This
holy man, then, the nation of the Cilicians brought forth, which also brought
forth Paul; for he was that man's fellow citizen, and both were put forward
for us from there as ministers of the Church. And when the stadium of piety
opened, and the season called him to the contests, he falls in with a harsh
beast, the one then judging. And consider the device. For seeing that his mind
was firm, and that it was not possible by the violence of the punishment to
unstring the tension of his eagerness, he casts him into delays and
postponements, bringing him in and leading him out continually.

For he did not, having heard him on one day, cut off his head, lest the
swiftness of the punishment make the course easier for him, but each =50.669=
day he brought him in, led him out, applied interrogations, threatened
countless tortures, baited him with flattering words, moved every device,
attempting to shake the unshaken foundation; and he led him about for a whole
year everywhere in Cilicia, putting him to shame, or rather, as he did not
suppose, making him more illustrious; and the martyr himself also cried out in
harmony with Paul, Thanks be to God who leads us in triumph in Christ Jesus,
and makes manifest through us the odor of his knowledge in every place. For
just as myrrh, so long as it lies in one place, anoints with fragrance that
air only, but when it is carried forth into many districts, fills all with its
own virtue; so indeed it happened then also in the case of the martyr. For he
was being led about as one about


to be put to shame, but the opposite came out; the athlete became more
illustrious through that procession, and he made all who dwelt in the land of
the Cilicians zealots of his own virtue. He was led about everywhere, so that
the spectators might not only learn the wrestlings by hearing, but also see
the crowned one himself; and the longer he made the double-courses for him, by
so much the more illustrious all the races became; the greater he set the
arenas, by so much the more wondrous he showed the wrestlings; the more he
stretched the affliction into length of time, by so much the more proven he
made his endurance. For gold too, the longer time it consorts with the nature
of fire, becomes purer; just as then also the soul of the saint, being tried
by time, shone forth the more, and he was carrying about nothing other than a
trophy against himself and against the devil, the martyr, a refutation of the
cruelty of the Greeks, a demonstration of the piety of the Christians, the
greatest proof of the power of Christ, an exhortation and counsel to the
faithful, so as to persevere eagerly in the same contests, a herald of the
glory of God, a teacher of the science of such wrestlings.

For he was calling all to migrate toward his own zeal, counseling not by the
voice only, but also through the very deeds sending forth a voice more
brilliant than a trumpet. And just as the heavens tell the glory of God, not
sending forth a voice, but by the brilliance of the sight sending the
spectator on to the wonder of the craftsman; so indeed that martyr too was
then telling the glory of God, being himself also a heaven, and far more
radiant than this visible one. For the choirs of the stars do not make the
heaven so brilliant as the ichors of the wounds made the body of the martyr
more brilliant. And that you may learn that the wounds of the martyr are more
brilliant than the stars fixed in heaven, consider. Toward that heaven and the
stars both men and demons look, but toward this man's wounds the faithful men
look, while demons do not dare to look back, but even if they attempt to see,
they are immediately blinded in their eyes, not being able to bear the flash
leaping out from there. And this I will confirm not only from the things that
happened of old, but also from the things that are still happening even now.
For take some demoniac and madman and lead him =50.670= to that holy tomb,
where the relics of the martyr are, and you will surely see him leaping away
and fleeing. For just as one about to step on coals, so he springs out at once
from the very forecourts, not daring even to look back toward the coffin
itself. And if now after so great a time, when he has become dust and ash,
they do not dare to look up toward the memorial, nor toward the bare bones of
the saint, it is quite clear that then also, when they saw him being reddened
with blood from every side, gleaming with wounds more than the sun with its
rays, they were struck with awe, and having been struck in their eyes they
withdrew.

γʹ. Did you see how the wounds of the martyrs are more radiant and more
wondrous than the heavenly stars, and have greater strength? The saint, then,
was being led into the midst, and bitter punishments stood around from every
side, fear of the things to come, toil of the things present, pain of the
things that had come upon him, agony of the things expected. For like certain
wild beasts the executioners, having stood around his body, were digging
through the ribs, tearing the flesh, stripping the bones, walking toward the
very inward parts of the bowels. Yet even searching through everything they
were not able to plunder the treasure of the faith. Now in the case of the
royal treasuries, where gold is stored and other unspeakable wealth, if you
only divide the walls, if you open the doors, you immediately see the treasure
lying forth; but here in the case of this holy and Christ-bearing temple the
opposite was happening.

The executioners divided the walls,


and they tore open the breast, and the wealth stored within they neither saw,
nor were able to take; but what those in Sodom suffered, standing at the very
door of Lot's house, yet not finding the entrance; so indeed these too were
searching through the body of the martyr from every side, but they were not
able to lay hold of the treasure, and to empty the wealth of the faith. Such
are the achievements of the soul of the saints, inalienable and unconquerable,
stored as in some inviolable place in the courage of the soul, and neither do
the eyes of tyrants see them, nor can the hands of executioners snatch them,
but even if they divide the very heart, which most of all has been entrusted
with the courage of the soul, even if they cut it into small parts, not even
so do they empty the wealth, but they even make it greater. And the cause is
God who dwells in such souls; and it is impossible for the one warring against
God ever to conquer, but he must go away having been laughed at and having
been shamefully defeated. For this reason then also the opposite of the
customary things was happening. For everywhere deeds prevail over words; but
then words prevailed over deeds; how? They themselves applied fire and iron
and tortures; they applied punishments, chastisements, scourges; they dug
through the ribs from every side, and the one suffering remained unassailable;
that one only uttered, and sent forth a bare voice, and speech prevailed over
works.

For the holy voice leaped out from the martyr's mouth, and it drew with itself
a light more radiant than the sun's ray. So great is the light of this, as the
interval from heaven to earth; or rather not even this whole can it pass
through, whenever either a roof, or a wall, or a cloud, or some other body
falls in between, but =50.671= the onward course is blocked and walled off by
such coverings; but the voice of the martyr, having leaped out from that holy
tongue, sprang up into heaven. It passed the heaven of heaven; angels saw it,
and made way, archangels, and stood aside; the cherubim and the other powers
guided it upward, and did not withdraw before they had led it to the very
royal throne. And after this voice, when the one then judging saw that he had
devised everything in vain and to no purpose, and that he was kicking against
the goads, and striking adamant, what does he do? He goes at last toward the
confessed defeat, and he leads the martyr out of the present life; for the
death of martyrs is a clear defeat of those who kill, and a brilliant victory
of those who are slain. But you, consider for me how he invented a certain
harsh and bitter manner of his end, being sufficient to display both that
man's cruelty, and the martyr's courage. What then was the manner of the
punishment? Having brought a sack, and having filled it with sand, and having
thrown in scorpions, and snakes, and vipers, and dragons, he threw in with
those also the saint, and casts him into the sea. And the martyr was with
beasts, and again a righteous man was being shut up together with beasts; and
I said "again," so that you may be reminded of an old narrative, that
concerning Daniel. That man they shut into a pit, this man they threw into a
sack; those then placed a stone upon it, this man sewed the sack, making the
prison narrower for the righteous one. But everywhere the beasts reverence the
bodies of the saints, unto the shame and accusation of those honored with
reason, and deemed worthy to be men, yet who by the excess of their own
beastliness hide even that fierceness; such as this tyrant too was likely to
be. And one could see a strange wonder, not less than in the case of Daniel.
For just as the Babylonians then, seeing that man after many days coming up
from the pit of the lions, marveled, so also the angels, seeing the soul of
Julian going up from the sack and the waves into heaven, marveled. Daniel
overcame and conquered two lions, but perceptible ones; this man overcame and
conquered one lion, but an intelligible one.

For our enemy, he says, the devil goes about as a roaring lion,


seeking whom he may swallow; but he was defeated by the courage of the martyr;
for he had laid aside the poison of sin; wherefore he did not swallow this
man; for this reason he feared neither a lion, nor the rage of the beasts.

δʹ. Do you wish that I tell also some other old narrative, where there was a
righteous man and beasts? Be reminded of the flood in the time of Noah and of
the ark; for then too there was a righteous man together with beasts; but Noah
entered a man, and came out a man; but Julian entered a man, and came out an
angel; that one entered from the earth, and came out onto the earth again;
this one entered from the earth into a sack, and from the sack he went away
into heaven. The sea received him, not in order to kill him, but in order to
crown him, and after the crown it gave back to us this holy ark, the body of
the martyr; this we hold until the present day, being a treasure of countless
good things. For God divided the martyrs with us, having taken the souls
himself, he gave the bodies somehow to us =50.672=, so that we may have a
continual reminder of virtue, these holy bones. For if someone seeing the
weapons of a combatant stained with blood, a shield, and a spear, and a
breastplate, even if he be the most sluggish of all, immediately leaps up, and
becomes hotter, and springs out toward the war, having taken from the sight of
the weapons an exhortation to attempt the same things; we, not seeing weapons,
but the very body of the saint that was deemed worthy to be stained with blood
through the confession of Christ, even if we be the most cowardly of all, how
shall we not have much eagerness, as if some fire of this sight falling into
our mind, and calling us toward the same contest?

For this reason God deposited with us the bodies of the saints until the time
of the resurrection, so that we may have a ground of the greatest philosophy.
But let the praises of the martyr not be diminished by the weakness of our
tongue, but let them wait for God the president of the games. He who crowns
them, that one will also praise; for their praise is not from men, but from
God; for even these things which we have said, we said not in order to show
the martyr more illustrious, but in order to make you more eager. But let us,
leaving the encomia, turn the whole discourse toward you; or rather it is not
possible to leave the encomia of martyrs, whenever one discourses in church
concerning the things that are profitable. But give heed; for I wish today to
cut out a wicked and old custom, so that we may not only come to the martyrs,
but also imitate martyrs. For the honor of martyrs is not only to come to
them, but also before this to emulate their courage. Wherefore one must first
speak the wicked custom; for when the disease is unknown it is not easy to
apply healing; for this reason I first uncover the wound, and then I apply the
medicine. What then is the wicked custom? Some of those gathered here today
(for may it not be mine to condemn the whole Church with such a condemnation),
through a certain listlessness and simplicity, abandoning us tomorrow, leap
away to Daphne, pouring out tomorrow what we gathered today, and tearing down
what we built. So that the presence here may not become fruitless for them,
having discoursed a little concerning these things we will end the discourse.
Why do you hurry to the suburb of the city, tell me? Behold the suburb of the
Jerusalem above; behold a spiritual Daphne; there are springs of waters, here
springs of martyrs; there cypresses, unfruitful trees, here relics of saints
and roots planted below, and stretching the branches into heaven. Do you wish
also to see the fruit of these branches? Open for us the eyes of faith, and I
will immediately show you a nature of wondrous fruits. For the fruit of these
branches is not of orchard-fruits, and of tree-fruits, nor any other of these
things that perish and are destroyed,

but healing of bodies


set on fire, =50.673= and forgiveness of sins, abolition of wickedness,
healing of diseases of the soul, intense prayer, boldness toward God, all
things spiritual, and full of heavenly good things.

These fruits, always being harvested, always abound, and never abandon their
own husbandmen. And the trees that grow on earth give the produce once in the
year, and if you do not harvest, when the season of winter has come on, they
lose their own comeliness, the fruit perishing and flowing down; but these
know no winter, no summer, are not subject to the necessity of seasons, nor is
it possible to see them bare of their own fruits, but they stand throughout in
the same comeliness; corruption does not touch them, nor change of seasons.
How many, then, from the time this body was planted in the earth, have
harvested countless healings from this holy coffin, and the fruit has not
failed; they reaped the fields, and the ears were not spent; they drew from
the springs, and the streams were not emptied, but there is a certain
continual inflow, never failing, but always furnishing the bubbling wonder
more than what is emptied. And it not only works wonders, but also persuades
one to philosophize. For if you are rich, and high-minded, and have a soul
inflamed, having come here, and having seen the martyr, and having reckoned
the interval between your wealth, and this man's abundance, you will
immediately put down the swelling, and having laid aside the inflammation you
will go away having much health in the soul; and if you think yourself poor
and contemptible, having come and having seen the wealth of the martyr, and
having laughed at the money outside, you will thus withdraw having filled
yourself with much philosophy, even if insults, even if losses, even if
scourges are brought upon you; having seen that you have not yet suffered so
many things as this holy martyr, you will again receive sufficient consolation
from here. Did you see of what sort the fruits of these roots are? how
unassailable? how spiritual? how they touch the soul itself? I do not forbid
going away to the suburb, but I forbid tomorrow; for what reason? So that the
delight may not have condemnation, so that the pleasure may be pure, and that
condemnation may not steal in; for it is possible on another day both to grant
the delight, and to be delivered from the sin. And if you wish even now to
enjoy delight, what is more delightful than this gathering? what more graceful
than the spiritual theater? than your members? than the company of the
brothers? But do you wish also to share in a bodily table? here it is possible
after the gathering has been dismissed, near the martyrium, having halted
under a fig-tree and a vine, both to grant ease to the body, and to deliver
the conscience from condemnation.

For the martyr, being seen from nearby and being near and standing by the very
table, does not allow the pleasure to be poured out into sin, but like some
pedagogue, or a most excellent father, being seen with the eyes of faith, he
puts down the laughter, he cuts off the unseemly pleasures, he abolishes all
the skippings of the flesh, which there it is not possible to escape. For what
reason? Because tomorrow choruses of men occupy the suburb; and the sight of
such men often leads even the one wishing to be temperate unwillingly out
toward imitation of the same unseemliness, and especially when the devil also
is present in the midst of them; for he is present, being called by the
whorish songs, by the shameful words, by the demonic =50.674= procession. But
you have renounced all this procession, and have enlisted in the worship of
Christ on that day on which you were deemed worthy of the sacred mysteries. Be
reminded then of those words and of the covenant, and flee the transgression.

εʹ. And I wish also to discourse toward those present and not going up, and to
put into these men's hands the salvation of those.

For a physician, having come in


to the one who is sick, discourses a little with the one lying there; but the
things concerning medicines and foods and the rest of the therapy, having
called those belonging to him, he enjoins all upon those. Why then? Because
the one who is sick does not immediately receive the exhortation, but the one
who is healthy attends to the things said with much eagerness; for this reason
I wish to discourse with you together with these. Let us occupy the gates
beforehand tomorrow, let us lie in wait on the roads, men the men, women the
women, let us drag them down from the beasts of burden; let us lead them back
here, let us not be ashamed; where a brother's salvation is, there is no
shame. If those men are not ashamed going up toward the lawless procession,
much more we must not be ashamed, being about to lead them back toward this
sacred festival. When a brother's salvation is set forth, let us decline
nothing. For if Christ died for us, we must endure all things for them; and if
they stretch out blows, and if they revile, hold fast, and do not withdraw
before you have led them back to the holy martyr; and if there is need to sit
a court from those passing by, let those who wish hear; I wish, say, to save a
brother, I see a soul being destroyed, and I cannot bear to overlook the
kinship; let him who wishes accuse, let him who wishes bring charges; or
rather no one will accuse, but all will also praise, all will embrace. For I
am not contending and fighting over money, nor defending myself through a
private enmity, nor over any other of the things of life, but over a brother's
salvation; these things who will not accept? who will not marvel? The
spiritual kinship has made us more affectionate than fathers, though we are in
no way related to one another according to fleshly kinship. If you wish, let
us also take the martyr with us; for he is not ashamed to come and save the
brothers. Let us set him before their eyes, let them fear him present, let
them reverence him exhorting and entreating; for he is not ashamed even to
entreat. For if his Master entreats our nature; We are ambassadors on behalf
of Christ, says Paul, as God entreating through us, Be reconciled to God; much
more will the servant do this; one thing only grieves him, our destruction;
one thing gladdens, our salvation, and for this reason he will decline nothing
to do for it. Let us then not be ashamed either, nor think it superfluous.

For if men who are hunters go away to cliffs and mountains and pits and every
impassable place, wishing to hunt either a hare, or a gazelle, or some other
of such things, or even often these wild birds; you, being about to lead back
not a cheap irrational creature, but a spiritual brother, for whom Christ
died, from destruction, and not mountains and glens, but to go out the gate,
=50.675= only hang back and blush? and what pardon will you have, tell me? do
you not hear a certain wise man exhorting and saying, There is a shame that
brings on sin? But you fear lest someone accuse? Transfer the cause onto me
who have spoken; say that the teacher so commanded; I am ready to be judged by
those who accuse and to give accounts; or rather no one will accuse either you
or us, even if he be exceedingly shameless, but all will accept, and will
marvel at us for the care, not only those in our own fatherland, but also
those who dwell in the neighboring cities, that so great a tyranny of love is
among us, so great a zeal of affection. But why do I speak of men? the Master
of the angels himself will accept us. Knowing then the wage, let us not
despise the hunt, nor let us come back alone tomorrow, but let each so arrive
having the catch with him. If you arrive only at that hour, at which going out
of the house he takes to the road, and =50.676= you shift him toward the
arrival here, there will thereafter be no difficulty, but when the season has
passed that man too will know much gratitude to you, and all the others will
praise you, and will marvel;

and what is greater than all,


the Master of the heavens will furnish you many wages for these things, and
will increase this trade and the praise.

Thinking then of the gain that comes to us from there, having all poured out
before the city, and having seized our brothers, let us lead them back here,
so that tomorrow also the theater may become full for us, and the festival
thus completed; so that for the zeal here the holy martyr may receive us into
the eternal tents with much boldness; which may it be that we all obtain, by
the grace and loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom and with
whom is the glory to the Father, together with the Holy and life-giving
Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
