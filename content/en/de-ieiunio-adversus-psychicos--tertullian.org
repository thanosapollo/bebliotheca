* Chapter 1
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

I would wonder at the psychics if they were held by luxury alone, by which
they marry too often, if they were not also torn by gluttony, by which they
hate fasts. Lust without the gullet would of course be counted a monster,
since these two are so united and compacted that, if they could have been
wholly disjoined, the pudenda would not first cling to the belly itself. Look
at the body: it is one region. In short, the order of vices follows the
arrangement of the members. First the belly, and at once the rest of
wantonness is built under stuffing; through greed of eating, lustfulness
passes. I recognize, then, an animal faith by its zeal for the flesh, of which
it wholly consists, as prone to many-feeding as to many-marrying, so that it
deservedly accuses the spiritual discipline, rival by its substance, in this
kind of continence as well: putting reins on the gullet by no meals at times,
or late ones, or dry ones, just as on lust by a single marriage.

It already wearies me to engage with such people; it already shames me to
wrangle about those whose very defense is not modest. For how am I to protect
chastity and sobriety without taxing the adversaries? Who they are I will name
once: the outer and inner sausages of the psychics. These raise controversy
with the Paraclete; for this the new prophecies are refused: not that Montanus
and Priscilla and Maximilla preach another God, nor that they dissolve Jesus
Christ, nor that they overturn any rule of faith or hope, but that they
plainly teach fasting more often than marrying. On the measure of marrying we
have already published a defense of monogamy. Now the fight is over the
chastisement of diet, the second, or rather the first, continence. They charge
us with keeping our own fasts, with often stretching Stations into the
evening, with observing xerophagies as well, drying our food of all flesh and
all sauce and of all the juicier fruits, and with neither eating nor drinking
anything winey; abstinence from the bath too, matching a dry diet. So they
object novelty, from whose unlawfulness they prescribe that it must be judged
heresy if it is a human presumption, or pronounced false prophecy if it is a
spiritual decree, so long as on either side we who announce otherwise hear
anathema.

* Chapter 2
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h2
:END:

For as far as fasts are concerned, they oppose definite days appointed by God:
as when in Leviticus the Lord commands Moses that the tenth day of the seventh
month be a day of atonement, saying, It shall be a holy day for you, and you
shall afflict your souls, and every soul that has not been afflicted on that
day shall be cut off from its people.

At any rate, in the Gospel they think those days were marked off for fasts in
which the bridegroom was taken away, and that these are now the only lawful
fasts of Christians, the legal and prophetic antiquities having been
abolished. For where they wish, they recognize what is meant by, The Law and
the prophets until John. So for the rest, they say, one must fast
indifferently, by choice, not by command, according to the new discipline, for
the times and causes of each person; that the apostles too observed it so,
imposing no other yoke of fixed fasts to be undergone in common by all, and
likewise none of Stations, which do have their own days, the fourth and the
sixth of the week, yet run at large, neither under the law of a precept nor
beyond the last hour of the day, when prayers too generally close at the ninth
hour, from the example of Peter, which is reported in the Acts. Xerophagies,
though, they call a novel name of an affected duty, and next door to heathen
superstition, like the purifications of Apis, Isis, and the Great Mother by
excepting certain foods; whereas faith, free in Christ, does not even owe the
Jewish law an abstinence from certain meats, the apostle having once for all
admitted the whole meat-market, that detester of those who, as they forbid
marrying, so order abstinence from foods created by God. And therefore, they
say, we were already then marked out as in the last times departing from the
faith, giving heed to the seducing spirits of the world, to the doctrines of
liars, having a branded conscience. With what fires, I ask you? I suppose
those with which we so often lead out marriages and daily cook dinners! So too
they say we are struck with the Galatians, as observers of days and months and
years. Meanwhile they also hurl Isaiah, who declared, Not such a fast has the
Lord chosen, that is, not abstinence from food, but the works of justice which
he subjoins; and that the Lord himself in the Gospel answered all
scrupulousness about diet in brief, that a man is not defiled by what is put
into the mouth, but by what comes out of the mouth, since he himself ate and
drank even to the remark, Behold a glutton and a drinker! so too the apostle
teaches that food does not commend us to God: we do not abound if we eat, nor
fall short if we do not eat.

By these and suchlike senses they already tend so subtly that anyone more
inclined to the belly can think the duties of food taken away, or lessened, or
delayed, superfluous and not so very necessary, God of course preferring the
works of justice and innocence. And we know what the persuasions of carnal
comforts are, how easily it is said, I must believe with my whole heart, I
must love God and my neighbor as myself. For on these two precepts hang the
whole law and the prophets, not on the emptiness of my lungs and intestines.

* Chapter 3
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3
:END:

So we must first affirm what is in danger of being secretly undermined: how
much that emptiness is worth with God, and, before all, whence the very reason
arose of earning God in this way. For the necessity of the observance will be
acknowledged when the authority of the reason has shone out, to be reckoned
from the beginning. Adam had received from God the law of not tasting of the
tree of the knowledge of good and evil, to die if he tasted. But he himself,
then turned back into a psychic after the spiritual ecstasy in which he had
prophesied that great sacrament toward Christ and the church, and no longer
capable of the things of the Spirit, yielded more readily to the belly than to
God, consented to food rather than to the precept, sold salvation for his
gullet. He ate, in short, and perished; saved otherwise if he had preferred to
fast from one little tree: so that already from here animal faith may
recognize its own seed, drawing from then on the appetite for carnal things
and the refusal of spiritual things. I hold, then, that from the beginning the
murderous gullet was to be punished with the torments and penalties of hunger,
even if God had commanded no fasts. Yet by showing whence Adam was slain, he
who had demonstrated the offense had left me to understand the remedies of the
offense. Of my own accord I would have counted food, by whatever means and at
whatever times I could, as poison, and taken hunger as the antidote, through
which to purge the primordial cause of death, transmitted in me too with the
race itself, sure that God wills that of which he willed not the contrary, and
confident enough that the care of continence would please him by whom I had
learned that the fault of incontinence was condemned. Further, since he
himself both commands fasting and properly calls a soul shattered, of course
by straits of food, a sacrifice, who will still doubt that this was the reason
of all macerations in diet, that by food again forbidden and the precept
observed the primordial sin might now be expiated, so that man might make
satisfaction to God through the same material of the cause through which he
had offended, that is, through the interdiction of food, and so rekindle
salvation in a rival way by hunger, as he had put it out by stuffing,
despising many lawful things for the sake of one unlawful?

* Chapter 4
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4
:END:

This reason was kept in the providence of God, who modulates all things
according to the times, lest anyone from the other side, to throw down our
proposition, should say, Why then did God not at once appoint some
chastisement of diet, nay rather even enlarge the permission? For in the
beginning he had assigned man only herb and tree food: Behold, I have given
you every seed-bearing herb sowing seed which is upon the earth, and every
tree which has in itself the fruit of seed-bearing seed shall be to you for
food. Afterward, however, to Noah, after enumerating the subjection of all the
beasts of the earth and the birds of heaven and the things that move on the
earth and the fish of the sea and every serpent, They shall be to you for
food: as the green herbs I have given you all things, but flesh in the blood
of its soul you shall not eat. For by this very fact, that he excepts from
eating only that flesh whose soul is not poured out through blood, it is plain
that he conceded the use of all remaining flesh. To this we answer that it was
not fitting to burden man with any further law of abstinence, who just then
could not bear so light an interdiction, of one fruit namely; that he was
therefore let go, to be strengthened by liberty itself. Likewise after the
flood, in the reforming of the human race, one law for the time, of abstaining
from blood, was enough, the use of the rest being permitted. For the Lord had
already shown judgment through the flood, and had also threatened through the
requiring of blood from the hand of a brother and from the hand of every
beast. So, preministering the justice of judgment, he sent out the material of
liberty, preparing discipline through pardon, permitting all things in order
to take some away, meaning to exact more if he had committed more, about to
command abstinence when he had first sent indulgence, so that, as we have
said, the primordial sin might be more expiated by the working of a greater
abstinence in the opportunity of a greater license.

* Chapter 5
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5
:END:

At last, when a familiar people began to be chosen for God and the restoration
of man could be begun, then all laws and disciplines were imposed, even those
that would cut back diet, certain things being taken away as unclean, so that
man might more easily at some time bear fasts, having used a perpetual
abstinence in certain things. For the first people too had recarved the crime
of the first man, found more prone to the belly than to God, when, snatched
from the hardness of Egyptian slavery by the mighty hand of God and his high
arm, he was seen as its lord, destined for a land flowing with milk and honey,
but at once stumbled at the surrounding view of a barren wilderness, sighing
over the losses of Egyptian satiety, and muttered against Moses and Aaron:
Would that we had died, struck by the Lord in the land of Egypt, when we sat
over the flesh-pots and ate bread to the full. How have you led us out into
this desert to kill this synagogue with hunger? With the same preference of
the belly he was going to lament those same leaders of his and umpires of God,
whom he was exasperating by desire of flesh and by recollection of the
Egyptian plenty. Who will feed us with flesh? There have come into our mind
the fish which we ate in Egypt free, and the cucumbers and the melons and the
leeks and the onions and the garlic. But now our soul is dry; our eyes see
nothing but manna. So to them too the angelic breads of xerophagy were
displeasing; they preferred to smell of garlic and onion rather than of
heaven. And therefore from men so ungrateful the more pleasing and more edible
things were withdrawn, for the sake at once of punishing the gullet and of
training continence, that the one might be condemned, the other taught.

* Chapter 6
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h6
:END:

Now if we have rashly called back the reasons of diet chastened by God, and to
be chastened by us for God's sake, to the trials of the beginnings, let us
consult common conscience. Nature herself will declare of what sort she has
been accustomed to show us, before food and drink, with saliva still virgin,
at least in matters to be done by sense, by which divine things are handled:
whether of a much stronger mind, of a much livelier heart, than when that
whole dwelling of the inner man, packed with meats, flooded with wines,
already heating with excrements to be cooked, is made a rehearsal-room of
latrines, in which plainly nothing remains so near as to savor of wantonness.
The people ate and drank, and rose up to play. Understand the modesty of holy
Scripture: it would not have marked play unless it were shameless. Besides,
how few remember religion, when the places of memory are occupied, the members
of wisdom hindered? No one will remember God as is fitting, as is right, as is
useful, at that time when it is customary for a man to lose even himself. All
discipline is either killed or wounded by diet. I lie if the Lord himself,
reproaching Israel with forgetfulness of him, does not put the cause down to
fullness. The beloved has grown fat and been thickened and widened, and has
forsaken God who made him, and departed from the Lord his savior. In the same
Deuteronomy, bidding that same cause be guarded against, he says, Lest, when
you have eaten and drunk and built excellent houses, your sheep and oxen
multiplied and silver and gold, your heart be lifted up and you forget the
Lord your God. He set the enormity of gluttony before the corruption of
riches, which riches themselves procure. Through them, of course, the heart of
the people had been thickened, lest it should see with the eyes and hear with
the ears and understand with the heart, blocked by the fats which he expressly
took away from eating, unteaching man to study stuffing.

But he whose heart was found lifted rather than fattened, who through forty
days and as many nights prolonged a fast beyond the faculty of human nature,
spiritual faith supplying the strength, both saw with his eyes the glory of
God and heard with his ears the voice of God and understood with his heart the
law of God, already then teaching that man does not live by bread alone but by
every word of God; when indeed the fatter people could not even steadily look
on Moses himself, fed by God and his hunger fattened by his name. Deservedly,
then, the Lord showed himself to him even in the flesh, a colleague of his own
fasts, no less than to Elijah. For Elijah too, first by this, that he had
imprecated a famine, had already sufficiently vowed himself to fasts. The Lord
lives, he said, before whom I stand in his sight, if there shall be dew in
these years, and rain. Then, fleeing Jezebel who threatened him, after the one
food and drink which, wakened by an angel, he had found, he too in forty days
and nights, with empty belly and dry mouth, came to mount Horeb, where, when
he had turned aside into a cave, with how familiar a meeting of God was he
received! What are you doing here, Elijah? a voice much friendlier than, Adam,
where are you? For that threatened a fed man; this soothed a fasting one. So
great is the prerogative of a restricted diet that it makes God a tent-fellow
of man, equal in truth with equal. For if the eternal God will not hunger, as
he testifies through Isaiah, this will be the time when man is equaled to God,
when he lives without food.

* Chapter 7
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h7
:END:

We have therefore already come to examples, that we may unroll, by the
efficacy of usefulness, the powers of this duty which even reconciles an angry
God to man. Israel had sinned in the drawing of water, gathered at Mizpah by
Samuel, but so at once washed away the sin by fasting that it also poured out
the peril of battle. At the very moment Samuel was offering the holocaust (in
nothing do we hear the clemency of God more procured than by the abstinence of
the people), and the Philistines were moving to battle, then and there the
Lord thundered with a great voice over the Philistines, and they were thrown
into confusion and fell in the sight of Israel, and the men of Israel went out
from Mizpah and pursued the Philistines and struck them as far as Bethor, the
unfed the fed, the unarmed the armed. These will be the strengths of those who
fast to God. Heaven fights for such. You have a form of defense necessary even
for spiritual wars.

Likewise when Sennacherib, king of the Assyrians, with several cities already
taken, was aiming blasphemies and threats at Israel through Rabshakeh, nothing
else turned him from his purpose into the Ethiopias. Then what else consumed a
hundred and eighty-four thousand of his army by an angel than the humiliation
of king Hezekiah? Since, the hardness of the enemy being announced, he tore
his garment, put on sackcloth, and ordered the elders of the priests in the
same dress to go to God through Isaiah, fasting of course attending the
prayers. For there is no time for food in peril, nor any cult of satiety in
sackcloth. Hunger is always the follower of grief, as gladness is an accessory
of stuffing. Through this follower of grief and through hunger even that
sinful city Nineveh is freed from the predicted ruin. For repentance of crimes
had sufficiently commended the fast, performed for three days, even the cattle
being starved, with whom God was not angry. Sodom too and Gomorrah would have
escaped if they had fasted. Ahab also knows this remedy. When, after
transgression and idolatry and the killing of Naboth, slain by Jezebel on
account of the vineyard, Elijah had upbraided him, How have you killed and
taken possession of the inheritance: in the place where the dogs had licked
Naboth's blood they shall lick yours too, he cast himself down and put
sackcloth on his flesh and fasted and slept in sackcloth. And then the word of
the Lord to Elijah, Have you seen how Ahab has stood in awe from my face:
because he has stood in awe, I will not bring the hurt in his own days, but in
the days of his son I will bring it, who was not going to fast. So fasting is
a work of reverence toward God, by which Hannah too, the rival wife of
Elkanah, once barren, easily obtained from God to fill a belly empty of food
with a son, and a prophet at that. But not only a change of nature, or the
turning aside of perils, or the blotting out of sins, but also the recognition
of sacraments, will fasts earn from God. Look at the example of Daniel. About
the dream of the king of Babylon all the sophists are troubled; they even deny
that it can be known from human excellence. Daniel alone, trusting God, and
knowing what would earn the grace of God, asks a space of three days, fasts
with his brotherhood, and so, his prayers commended, is instructed throughout
in the order and meaning of the dream; the tyrant's sophists are spared, God
is glorified, Daniel is honored, destined to receive no less a grace of God
afterward as well, in the first year of king Darius, when from meditation on
the times predicted by Jeremiah he set his face to God in fasts and sackcloth
and ashes. For the angel sent to him at once professed this as the cause of
the divine condescension: I have come, he said, to show you, inasmuch as you
are pitiable, by fasting, of course. If pitiable to God, he had been terrible
to the lions in the den, where indeed an angel procured a meal for him fasting
six days.

We render the rest as well. For we now hasten to new proofs. On the threshold
of the Gospel, Anna the prophetess, daughter of Phanuel, who both recognized
the infant Lord and preached many things about him to those expecting the
redemption of Israel, after the outstanding title of old and once-married
widowhood is further increased by the testimony of fasts also, showing in what
duties one ought to sit by the church, and that Christ is understood by none
more than by the once married and the often fasting. Soon the Lord himself
dedicated his own baptism, and in his that of all, by fasts, having it in him
to make bread from stones, perhaps even to make the Jordan flow with wine, if
he had been such a glutton and drinker. Rather, he was initiating the new man,
to the shaming of the old, by the virtue of scorning food, that he might show
him to the devil, seeking again to tempt him through food, stronger than
hunger entire. He then prescribed a law for fasts, that they are to be gone
through without sadness. For why should what is saving be sad? He taught also
that one must battle against the more dire demons by fasts. For what wonder if
the wicked spirit is led out by the same operation by which the Holy is led
in? Finally, that the honor of the Holy Spirit, with the charism of prophecy
besides, had hastened upon the centurion Cornelius not yet baptized, we read
that his fasts were heard. And I think the apostle too, in the second to the
Corinthians, among his labors and dangers and hardships, after hunger and
thirst, enumerates very many fasts as well.

This principal kind in the chastisement of diet can already prejudge the more
inward operations of abstinence as well, as themselves useful or necessary
according to measure. For the exception of certain foods is a partial fast.
Let us therefore look also at the novelty or vanity of xerophagies, whether in
these too there is not an operation of religion as ancient as it is effective.
I return to Daniel and his brothers, preferring the food of vegetables and the
drink of water to the royal dishes and wine-jars, and from then on more
handsome, lest anyone fear also for the look of his little body, and besides
cultivated in spirit. For God gave the young men knowledge and understanding
in every literature, and to Daniel in every word and in dreams and in every
wisdom, by which he might know this too, by what means recognition of
sacraments is obtained from God. Finally, in the third year of Cyrus king of
the Persians, when he had fallen into meditation on a vision, he provided
another form of humiliation. In those days, he says, I Daniel was mourning
through three weeks; I did not eat pleasant bread, flesh and wine did not
enter my mouth, I was not anointed with oil, until the three weeks were
finished; which being completed, an angel was sent addressing him thus:
Daniel, you are a pitiable man; do not fear, since from the first day on which
you gave your soul to meditation and humiliation before God your word has been
heard, and I have entered at your word. So the pitiable state and humiliation
of xerophagies drive out fear and turn the ears of God and make men sharers of
hidden things.

I return also to Elijah. When ravens had been accustomed to fill him with
bread and flesh, why afterward at Beersheba of Judah did that certain angel,
when he had wakened him from sleep, without doubt offer him bread only and
water? Had the ravens failed who would feed him more freely, or had it been
hard for an angel to snatch some servant from somewhere at the king's banquet
with a most furnished dish and transfer him to Elijah, as a meal of reapers
was shown to Daniel hungry in the lions' den? But an example had to be set,
teaching that in a time of pressure and persecution and of any circumstance
one must live on xerophagies. With such diet David expressed his confession,
eating ashes indeed as bread, that is, bread dry and dirty as ashes, and
mixing his drink with weeping, of course in place of wine. For abstinence from
wine too has its own titles, which both vowed Samuel to God and consecrated
Aaron. For of Samuel the mother said, And wine and strong drink he shall not
drink; for such she herself also was when she prayed to God. And the Lord to
Aaron: Wine and sicera you shall not drink, you and your son t(??)us after
you, whenever you enter the tabernacle or go up to the altar, and you shall
not die. So true is it that they will die who have ministered in the church
not sober. So too he upbraids Israel next: And you gave wine as drink to my
sanctified ones. And this restriction of drink also is a portion of xerophagy.
Yet wherever abstinence from wine is either exacted by God or vowed by man,
there let a pressure of food also be understood, laying down a form beforehand
for drink. For as the eating is, so also the drink. It is not likely that
anyone should sacrifice half a gullet to God, sober in waters and drunk in
foods. But whether the apostle also knew xerophagies, who had celebrated
greater things, thirst and hunger and many fasts, who had refused
drunkennesses and revels, there is proof enough even from his disciple
Timothy, whom, warning him to use a little wine for the stomach and constant
weaknesses, from which he was abstaining not from rule but from devotion
(otherwise custom would have helped the stomach more), by this very fact
commended abstinence from wine as worthy of God, which from necessity he
dissuaded.

* Chapter 10
:PROPERTIES:

:END:

Likewise they accuse our Stations as enjoined, and some also as appointed into
the late hour, under the name of novelty, saying that this duty too is to be
undergone by choice and not to be held beyond the ninth hour, from their own
custom of course. But as to what belongs to the question of injunction, I will
answer once for all causes. Now to the proper point of this kind, I mean the
measure of time, we must first demand from themselves whence they prescribe
this form of closing Stations at the ninth hour. If because Peter and those
with him are read to have entered the temple at the ninth hour of prayer, who
will prove to me that they had performed a Station that day, so that he may
interpret the ninth hour as the close and discharge of the Station? On the
contrary, you would more easily find that Peter at the sixth hour had first
gone up to the upper parts to pray, for the sake of taking food, so that
rather the sixth hour of the day can end this duty, which seemed about to
finish it after prayer. Further, since in the same commentary of Luke both the
third hour of prayer is shown, under which those initiated with the Holy
Spirit were held for drunk, and the sixth, at which Peter went up to the upper
parts, and the ninth, at which they entered the temple, why should we not
understand that, with indifference plainly saved, one must always and
everywhere and at every time pray, yet that these three hours, as more marked
in human affairs, which divide the day, which distinguish business, which
sound in public, were so also more solemn in divine prayers? Which Daniel's
argument also recommends, praying three times a day, of course by the
exception of certain hours, and of no others than the more marked, thereafter
apostolic, the third, the sixth, the ninth. Hence then I will say that Peter
too, from older usage rather, observed the ninth, praying the third time by
the duty of the last prayer.

These things, however, for the sake of those who think they act from the form
of Peter, which they do not know; not as if we reject the ninth, which we very
much observe on the fourth of the sabbath and the sixth, but because of those
things which are observed from tradition we ought to bring a reason so much
the worthier as they lack the authority of Scripture, until they are either
confirmed or corrected by some heavenly charism. And if, he says, you are
ignorant of anything, the Lord will reveal it to you. So, setting aside the
confirmer of all these things, the Paraclete, the guide of all truth, inquire
whether a worthier reason is brought among us for observing the ninth, so that
that reason may be assigned even to Peter, if he then performed a Station. For
it comes from the death of the Lord, which, although it ought always to be
commemorated without difference of hours, we are yet more impressively bound
to it then according to the very name of Station. For soldiers too, never
unmindful of the oath, obey Stations more. So the pressure must be celebrated
up to that hour in which the world, darkened from the sixth, performed a
mournful duty for the dead Lord, that we too may then return to gladness when
the world also received back its brightness. If this savors more of Christian
religion, while it more celebrates the glory of Christ, I can equally fix the
standing of a late Station from the same order of the matter, that we fast
until late, awaiting the time of the Lord's burial, when Joseph took down the
requested body and laid it. Hence it is also irreligious to cool the flesh of
servants before that of the Lord. But thus far I would have joined these
things from the challenge of arguments, striking conjectures with conjectures,
and yet, I think, with more faithful ones. Let us see whether anything of the
kind also from the antiquities takes our part.

In Exodus, that posture of Moses fighting against Amalek with prayers,
persevering until sunset, was it not a late Station? Do we think Joshua son of
Nun breakfasted on that day on which, while defeating the Amorites, he
commanded a Station even of the elements themselves? The sun stood in Gibeon
and the moon in Aijalon; the sun and the moon stood in Station until the
people was avenged of its enemies, and the sun stood in mid heaven. But as it
drew toward sunset and the end of one day, there was not a day such before or
at the last, so long of course that, he says, God heard a man, the sun's peer
namely, so long intent on the duty, a Station even longer than late. At any
rate Saul himself, when set in battle, plainly enjoined this duty: Cursed is
the man who shall eat bread until evening, until I avenge myself of my enemy,
and his whole people did not taste, and the whole land was breakfasting. But
so great an authority did God grant to the edict of that Station that
Jonathan, son of Saul, although ignorant of the fast defined until late, had
admitted a taste of honey, and was soon convicted of the offense by lot, and
barely exempted from the peril by the prayer of the people. For he had been
guilty of the gullet, though a simple one. But Daniel too, in the first year
of king Darius, when fasting in sackcloth and ashes he was making confession
to God, And while I was still speaking in prayer, he says, behold a man whom I
had seen in dreams at the beginning, flying swiftly, drew near to me as it
were at the hour of the evening sacrifice. This will be the late Station
which, fasting until evening, offers a fatter prayer to God.

But I believe all these things unknown to those who are troubled at ours, or
perhaps found out by reading alone, not also by attention, according to the
greater force of the unskilled among the most glorious multitude, of course,
of the psychics. For this reason we have steered through the several kinds of
fasts, of xerophagies, of Stations, so that, while we recount according to the
provision of each Testament how much the duties of food refused or cut or
delayed profit, we may beat back those who weaken these things as empty;
likewise, while we equally show in what order of religion they have always
been, we may refute those who accuse these things as new; for neither is that
new which has always been, nor empty which is useful. But this too is in the
midst, that some of these duties, commanded by God to man, have constituted a
law, some, offered by man to God, have discharged a vow. Yet even a vow, when
it has been accepted by God, makes a law for afterward through the authority
of the acceptor; for from then on he has commanded to be done who has approved
what was done. And so from this too the wrangling of the other side is met in
another kind, when they say, Either it is false prophecy, if a spiritual voice
has appointed these solemnities, or heresy, if a human presumption has
invented them. For, reprehending that form in which the ancient things also
ran, and from it twisting back what the adversaries of the ancients too will
be able to retrace against those, they will be bound either to refuse those as
well or to take up these, once they are brought out; necessarily, especially
since these too, from whatever institutor they are, whether spiritual or only
faithful, run to the same God to whom the ancient things also run.

For without doubt both heresy and false prophecy will be judged by diversity
of divinity among us all, priests of the one God the Creator and of his
Christ; and so far I defend this part indifferently, offering them to fix
their step where they wish. It is the spirit of the devil, you say, O psychic.
And how does he enjoin the duties of our God, and none to be offered to other
than our God? Either contend that the devil works with our God, or let Satan
be held the Paraclete. But you affirm a man antichrist; for by this name
heretics are called in John. And how has whoever he is arranged these duties
in our Christ toward our Lord, when antichrists too have gone forth toward
God, against our Christ? On which of the sides, then, do you think the Spirit
confirmed among us, when he commands or when he approves what our God has
always both commanded and approved? But again you plant terminal stakes for
God, as about grace so about discipline, as about charisms so also about
solemnities, so that the duties have ceased just as his benefits have, and so
you deny that he still imposes offices, because here too The Law and the
prophets until John. It remains that you take away the whole, so far as in
you, as idle.

For already in this kind too you reign, enriched and filled, not running upon

times, enjoining every ταπεινοφρόνησιν, since the prison must be learned, and

diet taken up, so that a Christian may enter the prison such as he would have
come out of it, not about to suffer a penalty there but a discipline, not the
torments of the age but his own duties, and so go forth more confident to the
contest from custody, having nothing of the flesh, so that the torments may
not even have material, when he is cuirassed in a lone and dry skin, and horny
against the claws, the juice of blood already sent ahead as baggage of the
soul, and she herself already hastening, who by often fasting already knows
death from near.

It is plainly yours to furnish cookshops in the prisons for uncertain martyrs,
lest they miss their custom, lest they weary of life, lest they be stumbled at
a new discipline of abstinence, which not even that Pristinus of yours, no
Christian martyr, had touched, whom, stuffed for some time from the
convenience of a free custody, bound, I suppose, to all the baths as if better

church, and to all the lures of this life as if worthier than the eternal, not

tickled with a few claws (for that is what drunkenness felt), he could not

prophets, therefore they who observe it are heretics. Why then do you hesitate

You prescribe that solemnities have been appointed for this faith by the
Scriptures or the tradition of the elders, and that nothing further of
observance is to be added, because of the unlawfulness of innovation. Stand on
that step if you can. For look, I convene you as fasting besides the pascha
beyond those days in which the bridegroom was taken away, and interposing the
half-fasts of Stations, and you yourselves sometimes living on bread and
water, as it has seemed good to each.

In short you answer that these things are to be done by choice, not by
command. You have therefore moved the step by exceeding tradition, when you
undergo what has not been appointed. But what sort of thing is it, that you
permit to your own choice what you do not give to the command of God? Shall
more be allowed to human will than to divine power? I remember that I am free
from the age, not from God. So it is mine to do a duty to the Lord of my own
accord; so it is his to enjoin it. I ought not only to obey him but also to
court him; for the one I render to his command, the other to my choice. It is
well, though, that bishops too are accustomed to command fasts to the whole
people, I do not say for the industry of collecting contributions, as is the
catch of yours, but sometimes also from some cause of ecclesiastical anxiety.
So if even from a man's edict and all as one you practice ταπεινοφρόνησιν, how
do you brand in us the very unity also of fasts and xerophagies and Stations?
unless perhaps we offend against decrees of the senate and mandates of princes
opposed to meetings. The Holy Spirit, when he was preaching in whatever lands
he wished and through whom he wished, from foresight of imminent either
ecclesiastical temptations or worldly plagues, as Paraclete, that is advocate
for entreating the judge, used to command the remedies of such duties, suppose
now for training the discipline of sobriety and abstinence; we who receive him
necessarily observe also what he then appointed. Look at the Jewish fasti, and
you will find nothing new, if all posterity thereafter keeps by hereditary
religion what was commanded to the fathers. Besides, throughout the Greeces
those councils are held in certain places from all the churches, through which
both the deeper things are treated in common, and the very representation of
the whole Christian name is celebrated with great veneration. And how worthy
of faith is this, under whose auspices men gather from everywhere to Christ!
See how good and how pleasant it is for brothers to dwell in one! This you do
not easily know how to sing, except at the time when you dine with a crowd.
But those assemblies, first working by Stations and fasts, know how to grieve
with those who grieve, and so at last to rejoice with those who rejoice. If we
too, in diverse provinces, perform those solemnities which the present
discourse has then defended, mutually represented in the Spirit, it is the law
of the sacrament.

He also reproves those who order abstinence from foods, but from the foresight
of the Holy Spirit, already precondemning heretics who would command a
perpetual abstinence for destroying and despising the works of the Creator,
such as I find with Marcion, with Tatian, with Jupiter, today's heretic from
Pythagoras, not with the Paraclete. For how small is the interdiction of foods
among us? Two weeks of xerophagies in the year, and not entire, the sabbaths
and the Lord's days of course excepted, we offer to God, abstaining from those
things which we do not reject but defer. Yet writing to the Romans he now
pricks you, retractors of this duty: Do not for the sake of food, he says,
undo the work of God. What work? That of which he says, It is good not to eat
flesh and not to drink wine. For he who serves in these things is pleasing and
propitiable to our God. One believes that all things are to be eaten, but
another, being weak, eats vegetables; let him who eats not make nothing of him
who does not eat. Who are you, who judge another's servant? Both he who eats
and he who does not eat gives thanks to God. But when he forbids controversy
to be made by human choice, how much more by divine? So he knew to accuse
certain chastisers and interdicters of diet who would abstain from disdain,
not from duty, but to approve those who did so to the honor, not to the
insult, of the Creator. And if he has handed you the keys of the meat-market,
permitting all things for food in order to establish the exception of
idol-offerings, yet he has not shut the kingdom of God in the meat-market.
For, he says, the kingdom of God is not eating or drinking, and, Food does not
commend us to God, not that you should think it said of dry food, but rather
of rich and carefully dressed, since, subjoining, Neither if we have eaten
shall we abound, nor if we have not eaten shall we fall short, it sounds more
to you who think you abound if you eat and fall short if you do not eat, and
therefore pull these things down.

How unworthily too you interpret the Lord to your lust as eating and drinking
everywhere; but I think he also fasted, who pronounced blessed not the filled
but the hungry and thirsty, who professed a food not what the disciples
thought, but the finishing of the Father's work, teaching to work the food
that remains unto eternal life, in the ordinary prayer too commanding bread to
be asked, not also the riches of Attalus. So too Isaiah did not deny that God
has chosen a fast, but enumerated of what kind he has not chosen. For in the
days of your fasts, he says, your own wills are found, and you shake all who
are subject to you, or you fast for insults and strifes and strike with fists.
Not such a fast have I chosen, but such as he subjoined, and by subjoining he
has not taken it away but confirmed it.

For even if he prefers the works of justice, yet not without a sacrifice,
which is a soul distressed by fasts. He is certainly the God whom neither a
people incontinent of the gullet pleased, nor a priest, nor a prophet. There
still remain the monuments of concupiscence, where the people most greedy of
flesh was buried, cooking the quails even to cholera. Eli is dashed before the
doors of the temple, his sons fall in battle, his daughter-in-law dies in
childbirth. For this blow the shameless house had deserved from God, defrauder
of the fleshly sacrifices. Sameas, a man of God, when he had prophesied the
issue of the idolatry introduced by king Jeroboam, after that king's hand
dried and at once restored, after the altar poured out, invited by the king on
account of these signs by way of satisfaction, plainly excused himself (for he
had been forbidden by God) to touch food there at all; but soon rashly fed by
another old man who lied himself a prophet, according to the word of God done
there over the table, he was not buried in his fathers'. For thrown down by
the meeting of a lion on the way and laid among strangers, he paid the penalty
of a deserted fast. These will be examples both to the people and to bishops,
even spiritual ones, if they have admitted any incontinence of the gullet. But
neither among the dead has the warning ceased, where in the rich feaster
indeed banquets are tortured, but in the poor man fasts are refreshed, having
Moses and the prophets as teachers. For Joel too cried out, Sanctify a fast
and a service, already then looking ahead that other apostles and prophets
would also sanctify a fast and preach duties that serve God. Whence even those
who flatter in eating to idols and in adorning them in the shrine and in
saluting them at each hour are said to do a service.

But the heathen also recognize every ταπεινοφρόνησιν. When the heaven is
stunned and the year dry, barefoot processions are proclaimed, the magistrates
lay down the purple, turn the fasces back, utter prayer, renew the victim. In
certain colonies besides, by a yearly rite, covered with sacks and sprinkled
with ashes, they throw a suppliant spite on their idols; baths and booths are
shut until the ninth hour. One fire in public at the altars, water not even in
the dishes. A Ninevite halt of business, I believe. A Jewish fast at any rate
is celebrated everywhere, when, leaving the temples, along every shore,
wherever in the open, they at last send prayer to heaven. And although they
disgrace the duty by the dress and ornament of grief, they still affect a
faith of abstinence and sigh for the authority of the lingering star. But it
is well that you, heaping blasphemies on our xerophagies, equal them to the
chastity of Isis and of Cyheles. I admit the comparison as evidence. Hence it
will stand as divine, which the devil, emulator of things divine, imitates.
From truth a lie is built; from religion superstition is compacted. Hence you
are the more irreligious as the heathen is the more ready. He, in short,
sacrifices his gullet to an idol-god; you will not to God. For your belly is
god to you, and your lung a temple, and your paunch an altar, and the cook a

You are old, if we would say the truth, you who so indulge the gullet, and you
boast yourself prior with reason; I always recognize the savor of Esau the
hunter of beasts; so you study everywhere to track fieldfares, so you come
from the field of your most lax discipline, so you fail in spirit. If I offer
you a lentil reddened with boiled must, you will at once sell all your
primacies; with you the agape boils in the pots, faith is hot in the kitchens,
hope lies on the dishes. But agape is of more account, because through it your
young men sleep with their sisters. The appendages of the gullet, of course,
are wantonness and luxury. Which alliance the apostle also knowing, when he
had first said, Not in drunkennesses nor in revels, added, Nor in beds and
lusts. To the indictment of your gullet it belongs that a double honor is
assigned among you to those who preside, in double portions, though the
apostle gave a double honor as both to brothers and to overseers. Who is
holier among you, unless more frequent in feasting, unless more lavish in
catering, unless better furnished in cups? Deservedly you men of soul and
flesh alone refuse things spiritual. If the prophets pleased such, they were
not mine. Why then do you not constantly preach, Let us eat and drink, for
tomorrow we die? As we do not hesitate to command openly, Let us fast,
brothers and sisters, lest perhaps tomorrow we die. Let us openly vindicate
our disciplines. We are sure that those who are in the flesh cannot please
God, not of course by the substance of the flesh, but in care, but in
affection, but in working, but in will. Leanness does not displease us; for
God did not give flesh by weight, as he did not give the Spirit by measure.
More easily, perhaps, through the narrow door of salvation will the thinner
flesh enter; more quickly will the lighter flesh be raised; longer in the tomb
will the drier flesh last. Let the boxers and Olympic pugilists be stuffed.
Ambition of body suits those who also need strength, and yet they too grow
strong by xerophagies. But ours are other thews and other strengths, as also
other contests, for whom the wrestling is not against flesh and blood, but
against the powers of the world, against the spiritual things of malice.
Against these one must stand not with flesh and blood, but with faith and a
strong spirit. Besides, a fatter Christian will perhaps be more necessary to
bears and lions than to God, except that even against beasts he will have to
train leanness.
