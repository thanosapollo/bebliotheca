* On that saying, The grace of God has appeared to all men

Homily of Saint John Chrysostom on The grace of God has appeared and on the
Theophany.

1. Recently we discoursed to you concerning chastity, as you remember, and we
read a law of this sort, which runs thus: Everyone who looks at a woman to
lust after her has already committed adultery with her in his heart. Fearful
is the saying, for shameless is the passion; terrible is the sentence, for
grievous is the desire.

2. Just as, then, a man who has a wild dog that leaps upon all who pass by
does not endure that it be unbound and free, but binding it with an iron chain
he hands it over to the hands of the servants to hold it securely, so also God
does not allow our eyes, which leap more shamelessly than dogs upon the bright
forms of bodies, to be unbound, but binding them, as with an iron chain, by
the fear of the legislation, he has handed them over to reason to hold
securely, having foretold and threatened that if they should escape and seize
upon any of those who pass by, he will demand of him the punishment of
adulterers. For everyone who looks at a woman to lust after her has already
committed adultery with her in his heart.

3. And this he threatened, caring for those who see. For in the case of dogs
and men, it is not the biting dogs, but the bitten men, who receive the
wounds; but in the case of eyes that look wantonly and of women who are seen,
it is not the women who are seen, but the men who look, who receive the
wounds. There the one bitten was struck; here the one who bites has taken the
venom.

4. For this reason he secures them, for this reason he has set eyelids and
eyelashes around the eyes, that you may not have the windows always open. For
when doors are always open, the robber enters with great ease, and with great
license he plunders the treasure of chastity. For this reason the glances of
the eyes are called pupils, that is maidens, that you may learn that one must
feel shame and blush; for just as maidens unacquainted with marriage and kept
in the chamber would not endure even to look upon their own household with
shamelessness, so also the pupils of the eyes must, like maidens unacquainted
with marriage sitting in a chamber, that is in the eye, not shamelessly give
themselves over to all sights, since they would no longer be maidens, but
shameless dogs.

5. But the things concerning chastity have been said sufficiently both then
and now for those who attend; and what is sought is not that our teaching be
continual and your hearing continual, but that something more also come from
our teaching and from your hearing unto the account of your life, unto our
boasting, unto the glory and praise of God, that I too may be able on that day
to boast from your achievements and say: Behold I and the children whom God
has given me.

6. Let our words become deeds through you. For in the case of husbandmen what
is sought is not this, to yoke ploughing oxen and cut a deep furrow and cast
the seeds, but to show the fields waving and the threshing-floor filled with
sheaves, so that when he comes who thoroughly purges it, whose fan is in his
hand, he may find nowhere on the threshing-floor chaff, but everywhere wheat,
everywhere ripe fruit able to be carried to the royal storehouses. For he will
come, he will come altogether, that one who baptizes you in the Holy Spirit
and fire; and the wheat he will gather into the storehouses, but the chaff he
will burn with unquenchable fire. Great is the punishment, but if we will, we
shall not receive the punishment nor shall we be chaff.

7. For such is sin: just as chaff is food of irrational animals and
fuel of fire, so also sin is fuel of the coming fire. Do you wish to learn how
sin is fuel of fire? If anyone builds, he says, upon this foundation, gold,
silver, precious stones, wood, hay, stubble, each one's work will become
manifest; for the day will declare it, because it is revealed in fire. If
anyone's work shall be burned, he shall suffer loss. Behold, sin is fuel of
fire.

8. Hear how it is also folly and food of irrational passions; hear David
saying: My lawlessnesses have gone over my head; as a heavy burden they were
heavy upon me; my bruises have stunk and festered from the face of my folly.
Chaff again is light and easily tossed, and by a small blast of wind is
quickly lifted and carried about everywhere. Such are those among men who are
easily kindled by anger, those who are quickly puffed up by mindlessness. For
this reason someone exhorts saying: Winnow not with every wind (that you may
not remain chaff) but be established upon the rock.

9. For this reason Christ came, not only that he might abolish the old sins,
but that he might also set us right toward the things to come. This also Paul,
showing that he came not only because of the old sins, but also because of the
future achievements, cried out today: The grace of God has appeared, training
us. But rise up; for again we open the treasures, again we show the pearls;
let no one then run past the beauty of the things said: The grace has
appeared.

10. Why did he not say: the grace was given? That you may learn that, before
the grace appeared, the nature of men sat in darkness; for to those sitting in
darkness Christ shines, which also the prophet, proclaiming beforehand, said:
The people sitting in darkness have seen a great light. The saving grace of
God has appeared. Did you see the agreement of apostles and prophets? The
people sitting in darkness; for such is the nature of darkness: wherever it
may overtake men, it immediately seats them and does not allow them to go
further, the walking becomes slippery and dangerous. Therefore, guiding even
the inactivity of our nature toward virtue, he said: The people sitting in
darkness have seen a great light.

11. And this apostolic saying, has appeared, shows us not this only, but also
another thing besides these. What then is this? That we did not, seeking, find
the light, but this appeared to us; we did not go to him, but he came to us.
And making this clear Christ said: You did not choose me, but I chose you. And
the Apostle again, crying in harmony with these things, said: Then I shall
know even as I also was known; but now I did not know, but I was known. And
writing to the Philippians he said: I press on if I may also lay hold of that
for which I was also laid hold of, showing through all things that our
salvation has not become our own achievement, but by divine grace we were all
saved; which then he also hints at here saying: The grace of God has appeared.

12. What grace? For there is also a grace of the old, and John cried
saying: We received grace upon grace. For truly grace also was that of the
old, having delivered them from the slavery in Egypt and from countless other
evils; but this grace is greater. For then it delivered from the Egyptians,
now it has freed from the tyranny of the demons; then it delivered from the
madness of Pharaoh, now from the possession of the devil; then through Moses,
now through the Only-begotten; then through a rod, now through a cross; then
through a red sea, now through a bath of regeneration; then it led out from
clay and brickmaking, now from death and sin; then into a land flowing with
milk and honey, now it has led into the kingdom of the heavens.

13. Truly the saving grace of God has appeared. But for what reason, O blessed
Paul, did you pass over so many achievements in one word? Uncover the grace
for me, tell me the sea of gifts. It is enough, he says, to present all
things, the loving-kindness of the giver; for when it is the grace of God, the
grace has no measure. The grace of God has appeared, God became man, for you
he took the form of a slave, that he might make the slave free. And just as a
master, loving a household-servant exceedingly, puts on his garment, so also
Christ, loving our nature, put on its garment. The mercy of a man is upon his
neighbor, but the mercy of God is upon all flesh. Did you see how he showed
both the greatness of the grace and the universality of the gift, by naming
the giver?

14. But let us see what saving is. Graces take their names from those who
give, as when a ruler gives a grace, it is called a ruler's grace, when a king
gives a grace, it is called a royal grace; since then here also the Saviour
gave the grace, the grace is called saving. For this reason he says: And you
shall call his name Jesus, because he shall save his people from their sins.

15. And how, he says, if it is a universal grace and he poured out the gift
upon the inhabited world, did he promise to save his people only? For this
Jewish people alone was so named before, but this one did not remain alone so
named; but since after these things they were found unworthy of the honor, the
appellation passed over to the whole inhabited world.

16. For that we are his people, hear clearly Hosea saying and Paul
interpreting: I will call the not-my-people my people. And that it was said
concerning us is clear from there: we are the not-people, we became a people.
And hear another thing clearer than this: And it shall be in the place where
it was said to them, You are not my people, there they shall be called sons of
the living God. In what place? In Judea, he says; for there the prophets said,
You are not my people, and there Christ said to the disciples: Do not go into
the way of the Gentiles, but in that same Judea again he said: Go, make
disciples of all the nations. Did you see how in the place where it was said
to them, You are not my people, there they were called sons of the living God?
For this reason he says: He shall save the people from their sins.

17. Great salvation indeed; for it is not so great to be delivered from death
as it is great to be delivered from sin; for death is because of sin, not sin
because of death. And that you may learn that this deliverance is greater and
that, this being taken away, death is not fearful, consider this from the
Master's own body.

18. For that body did not commit sin, and falling into death it raised bitter
pangs for it and tore its belly; and not only was it not swallowed, but it
also destroyed it utterly. And just as Daniel, casting a cake into the mouth
of the dragon, destroyed the beast, so also Christ, throwing his own flesh
into the mouth of death, split its belly; for the sting of death is sin.
Having therefore taken away the sting, he left the beast thereafter inactive.

19. But that which is sought is this: why, having said, the saving grace of
God has appeared, did he add, training us? For grace does not train, but
remits sins; it gives pardon, it does not bring on training. But do not fear
the name of training; there is training that is chastisement and there is
training that is teaching; For whom the Lord loves he trains, he says, and he
scourges every son whom he receives. Here training is the chastisement. Hear
elsewhere how training is a teacher: Blessed is the man whom you shall train,
O Lord, and from your law you shall teach him. According to this then the
saving grace of God has appeared, training us.

20. And how does grace teach? For grace remits sins, but this remission of
sins is a teaching of achievements; for in the case of men who have sense,
lashes and blows do not train many so much as forgiveness of sins. When
therefore the one who has sinned sees the loving-kindness of the one who
forgave, he makes himself more exact toward the achievements to come. And thus
grace trains him, pricking more than lashes; for he blushes and is ashamed to
fall into the same things again, he is put to shame by the greatness of the
gift of the benefactor, and grace becomes training.

21. Do you wish to learn also from another place how it is both grace and
training, how Christ both granted grace and trained? He saw the paralytic, he
compacted his sinews, he corrected the weakness of his nature, he brought the
body back to its former health, then later seeing him he said: Behold, you
have become healthy (this is grace); sin no longer (this is of training and
teaching). And he entrusted both to the disciples; for having said, Going,
baptize all the nations in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the
Holy Spirit, he showed the grace, the remission of transgressions; and adding,
teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you, he manifested the
training. Which things also Paul making clear said: The saving grace of God
has appeared, training us.

22. For all these things then let us give thanks to God and let us have this
grace always in memory; whether you are puffed up, or whether you are in good
cheer, grace will train you to be moderate. For when you consider that the
Master of the angels, God who shares the throne of the Father, took a slave's
form, you will never be able to hold a passion of anger or arrogance in the
soul.

23. Thus then Paul also trains us in humility, showing that this very thing is
grace and teaching; wherefore he also reminds of the grace first. And where
does he do this? Writing to the Philippians and wishing to persuade them to
yield the first places to one another, he advised thus: In humility preferring
one another as servants of yourselves. Then he added the teaching from the
grace saying: Let this mind be in you which was also in Christ Jesus, who,
being in the form of God, did not count it robbery to be equal with God, but
emptied himself, taking the form of a slave; and being found in fashion as a
man he humbled himself.

24. Did you see how, bringing the grace into the midst, he called the matter
teaching? So also, when he exhorts toward love, he does, reminding of the
grace and saying: As Christ loved us and gave himself up for us, so also you
love one another. Let us then be trained from the grace and let us give thanks
to the God who loves mankind both for the gift that has come to us and for the
teaching from the grace, because we have both been delivered from the former
sins and toward the achievements to come we have the greatest security, the
remission of sins.

25. And as in a mirror, the teaching of the word, let us consider our life
with exactness and let us set in order the neglected of our deeds. And what
men sitting in the barbershops do after having the hair of the head cut,
holding the mirror in the right hand, looking round lest somewhere some error
in the cutting has happened to the head, according to these do you also;
taking the teaching of the word as a mirror, from it behold all your life, and
if you see some sin that has happened, correct it at once.

26. So also the women do; for rising immediately from the bed, they wipe the
face and arrange the hair of the locks, and looking toward the mirror they
make the testing of the beauty of the face, so that nothing neglected may pass
by. You too then, taking the mirror of the teaching, form and stamp the beauty
of the soul; for you also have a husband whom you are about to please; just as
those women do nothing before this work, so also you prefer nothing to this
zeal, but even if you should see the whole house neglected, first please the
husband, and then arrange the other things well. For that you also have a
husband, hear Paul saying: I betrothed you to one husband to present a virgin
to Christ. And by how much greater is the worth of this husband, by so much
greater must the zeal from us become; for he looks to beauty of soul and
wishes this face to be adorned. For all the glory of the king's daughter is
within.

27. Let us then adorn this glory, that with this, all of us presenting
ourselves to the King of glory, we may obtain the eternal and immortal honor;
by the grace and loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom to the
Father be the glory together with the Holy Spirit, now and always and unto the
ages of ages; amen.
