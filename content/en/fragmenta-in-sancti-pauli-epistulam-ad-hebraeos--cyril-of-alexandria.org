His manner of exposition has also become exceedingly wise for him, and very
finely and skillfully arranged. For it is diversified both from legal writings
and spiritual thoughts and from the manifold fore-announcement of the holy
prophets, and his aim is to teach everywhere and to persuade those who will
come upon it that the things through Moses were types travailing with the
power of the truth, but the truth is 363 Christ; declaring superfluous the
most needful exhibition as from type and shadow for those of old. And besides
this he also makes this clear to us, that even to those who have lived
splendidly in the law it has come about not otherwise that they come into
glories and are well-approved, except through the faith that is toward God,
and that faith is an ancient thing and well-worn among the holy fathers. But
he did not use the beginnings and prefaces customary to him, inasmuch as he
was under suspicions and slanders among those of the Jews who had believed as
one attached to those from the nations and teaching all everywhere to hold
the law as nothing and to despise the ancestral customs as vain, and as it
were to go upon another path, as one may learn this from the Acts of the
Apostles. Most economically therefore he folds away the customary things, and
has been silent about the grace of the apostleship; but he has rather gone on
to the need of mentioning fathers and holy prophets, having believed that
through this the word would be acceptable to them. He said then Having spoken
to the fathers in the prophets. God has spoken to those of older time in the
Spirit through the mouth of saints and through the voice of prophets, and
indeed he said through one of them, putting to shame the insanities of the
Jews "Your fathers, where are they, and the prophets, shall they live
forever? Only receive my statutes and my commandments, as many as I command
in my spirit to my servants the prophets." But even if the grace of prophecy
was in the Spirit for those of older time, it has not come about apart from
the God Word who is from the Father; for his Spirit speaks likewise to
saints. And no one would say, if he had sense, that the Holy Spirit,
completing a ministerial 364 service toward us, in equal measure with
prophets receives from the God Word the knowledge or revelation concerning
whatever he would will, and thus makes it toward us; for thus he will differ
in no way from the holy prophets if it should be so; but knowing all the
things in him, he is said to receive from him and to announce to saints.
Therefore the Only-begotten was speaking even of old through the Holy Spirit
to those of older time; and he was speaking in many manners, commanding the
august and great mystery of his own incarnation to be formed; one may then
hear him saying through prophets "I have multiplied visions and have been
likened in the hands of prophets," and again "I myself who speak am present,
as a season upon the mountains as feet of one evangelizing a hearing of
peace, evangelizing good things." For at the consummation of the ages the Son
himself has spoken to us through himself, no prophet any longer intervening
and voice of saints, but the Only-begotten having become according to us
through himself has made the words toward us. And we say that the Father has
spoken in the Son, not as through some man lying specially in the middle and
ferrying across to us not rather his own words but those from another, but
with his own voice, the Son speaking to us through the body; for the flesh
was of the Only-begotten himself and


not of some other; for being God by nature he has become man with remaining
God; for then he was making manifest to us the ineffable will of God and
Father; for this reason "his name is also called Angel of Great Counsel."
Rightly then Paul says that in the last of the days God and Father has spoken
toward us in the Son; for he knew Emmanuel to be God, and the Word from God
having also come to be in humanity with us, he recognized the free 365 in the
form of the servant, he confessed the fullness of him who emptied himself for
us, he beheld the height of the glory of him who let himself down for us into
humiliation, who also for this cause is said to have been appointed heir of
all things, although he has authority over all things inasmuch as he is also
understood and is God; for again he goes back up into his own wealth even
with flesh. For he has remained in no way in the poverty according to us; not
having become flesh for this, that he might remain poor with us, having cast
away his own and God-befitting wealth, but that rather we who are sick with
the lack of the divine gifts might be enriched by his poverty. And indeed
being Son by nature and co-eternal with the Father, when through the holy
virgin he also endured the generation according to flesh for us, as being
brought back into beginnings of sonship he said through David "The Lord said
to me You are my Son, today I have begotten you"; for the today makes the
indication of the present time according to which he has also become flesh;
and being by nature also Lord of all things. For John has testified
concerning him that "he came unto his own," calling the world his own, as
being called into an unaccustomed glory of the kingdom he said "But I have
been appointed king by him," it is clear that by God and Father; and he
endured such things, in order that as man having been made son, although
being God according to nature, he might make a way through himself for the
nature of man of the participation of adoption, and might call to the kingdom
of heavens those tyrannized by sin; for just as we have had as a kind of
inheritance from a father running through the whole race from him the
disasters from the transgression in Adam, having been loaded with curse and
death, so again the splendid things in Christ will run through the whole
human race; for the Only-begotten receives for us, not at all for himself,
for he is full because he is also God by nature, and he needs nothing at all,
rather he himself enriches the whole creation with the goods from above. 366
He has therefore been appointed as man heir of all things economically, in
order that thereafter as a heritage belonging and proper to himself he might
recover those upon the earth, unholily snatched away by the devil and the
wicked powers with him. And indeed God and Father said to him "Ask of me and
I will give you nations as your inheritance" and so on; which having been
brought to fulfillment, he says toward his own Father concerning us "Those
whom you have given me out of the world were yours and you have given them to
me." Observe then how he goes up into the wealth existing to him according to
nature even with flesh; for if those in the world were of the Father, in what
manner would they be understood as of the Word who reigns together with him?
Therefore he also said that "all mine are yours and yours mine, and I have
been glorified in them." How then is he commanded to ask, and receives
nations into inheritance, and makes a possession even of the ends of the
earth? For the Maker of all things and Father, having willed to save man who
had leapt away perishably from the slavery under God, has sent into this
world the Only-begotten God Word in order that having become flesh and having
lived together with men as man, he might proclaim release to captives and
recovery of sight to the blind, and might call also an acceptable year of the
Lord. Therefore even if he should be said to have received and to have been
appointed heir because of the human, we shall not be ignorant of the economy;
for where at all did he become poor, if he did not become according to us,
that is man with remaining God? Thus persuading us to think



the divine Paul necessarily added Through whom he also made the ages. How

holy virgin has borne Emmanuel to us, how does he say that the ages have been

no way at all do injustice to the God-befitting antiquity of him who is thus

the divine John the evangelist also said "He who comes after me has come to

beauty of the superiority of him who begot him and indeed also of glory

likewise life from life; and he has stood so far above the whole creation as
even the Father himself would be understood. For he is unbegotten from
unbegotten, and not numbered with those brought over from not-being into
being; for the Word was in the beginning and the Word was God; how then will
the He was not be true when said of him by those who war against the divine

the word of his power. For "all things came to be through him and without him
came to be not even one"; and again he himself holds up all things toward
unshaken continuance. We were saying that to those of older time God has
legislated through Moses and the prophets; but we have become according to
the voice of the prophet "taught of God." For we have had as teacher the Word
from God himself who became man for us, the maker of the ages, the radiance
of the paternal glory, the character of the hypostasis; having considered
these things in circuit we shall add the things next; these are ..... Being
Word of God and Father all-powerful "He spoke and they were generated, he
commanded and they were created, he established them unto the age and unto
the age of the age, he set a command and it will not pass away." For since
the nature of things brought over into being does not have incorruption nor
yet the being able to have itself in the same way throughout; for this is
proper to the substance above all; he himself bears all things by the word of
his power. For he himself is Word enhypostatic and as generated from God the
Father according to nature, and his word is all-powerful and energetic and
able to accomplish all things easily; and he adds to these Having made
purification of sins he sat at the right of the majesty on high, having
become so much better than the angels, as he has inherited a more excellent
name beyond them. Having shown him character of the hypostasis of the Father
and indeed also radiance of his glory, he passes necessarily to the economy
of the incarnation through which we have also been saved and have been
enriched with the remission of sins, and have been sanctified through his 369
blood. For as the most wise Paul himself says "In whom we have the redemption
through his blood, the remission



of trespasses." And John also writes thus concerning God and Father "If we
walk in the light we have fellowship with him and the blood of Jesus Christ
his Son cleanses us from all sin." We have therefore been cleansed by the
holy blood of Christ the Savior of us all, who has sat at the right of the
majesty on high, but when? When he made purification through his blood, then
he is said to have sat, and to have become also better than angels, and to
have inherited beyond them the more excellent name; for he has been called
Son. For the Word has become flesh although being Word and radiance of the
glory of the Father and character of his hypostasis, not making in his own
nature the labors, so to speak, of the economy with flesh; for he endured "a
cross, having despised shame," and dishonors and insults and spittings; but
in order that he might release us from every defilement, having shown us
clean by his own blood, and might join us through himself to God and Father;
"for through him we have had the access," and he himself is the door and the
way. Yet even if "the Word became flesh and tabernacled among us," having
made his own body that taken from the holy virgin, still he was in glory and
nature of godhead and did not depart from the highest thrones of God and
Father. For he was man upon earth, but again he filled heaven and all things
were full of him. And he was made a little lower than angels because of the
measure of the humanity; for the nature of man sits below the glory of the
angels; but even being thus he was "above every principality and authority
and lordship and every name that is named not only in this age but also in
the one to come." And knowing this the divine Paul writes "For you know the
370 grace of our Lord Jesus Christ that being rich he became poor for us,
that we by his poverty" might be enriched. Having become so much better than
the angels. For Angel is a name significant of ministry, and it intimates to
us a household-servant measure; but Son indicates the substantial and natural
existence from the Father; for this reason then he has not said comparatively
greater or more honorable, lest someone reckon concerning him and them as
concerning those of the same kind; but he has said Better, in order that he
might make known the differing of the nature of the Son toward the generated
things; and of these we have the demonstration from the divine scriptures,
David singing "For better is one day in your courts than thousands," and
Solomon crying out "Wisdom is better than precious stones"; for how are they
not of another essence and other in nature, wisdom and the stones from earth?
And what kinship is there to the courts in the heavens and the houses upon
earth? Thus then there is no kinship of the Son toward the angels; and there
being no kinship, Better was not said comparatively, but distinctively
because of the differing of his nature from those; and the apostle himself
then, interpreting Better, sets it not in some other thing, or in the
difference of the Son toward the generated things, saying that he is Son, and
they servants; and he as Son sits at the right with the Father, and they as
servants stand by and are sent and minister. And these things having been
written thus, the Son is not signified from these as generated, O Arians, but
rather other than the generated things, and own of the Father, being in his
bosom; for also the Having become written here does not signify the Son as
generated, as you suppose; for if he had 371 simply said having become and
been silent, there was a pretext for the Arians; but since he has said Son
beforehand, having shown through the whole passage that he is other than the
generated things, he did not even set having become absolutely, but joined
better to having become; for he considered the expression indifferent;
knowing that of a confessedly genuine Son he who says having become says the
equal of having been generated that he is better; for the gen[n]erate does
not differ, even if



someone says he has come to be or has been made; but the generated things,
being works of creation, it is impossible to be called generated, unless
perhaps after these having partaken of the generated Son they also are said
to have been generated; not at all through their own nature, but through the
participation of the Son in the Spirit. And this again the divine scripture
knows, saying of the generated things "All things came to be through him" and
"He made all things in wisdom"; but of the generated sons "There came to be
to Job seven sons and three daughters," and "And Abraham was a hundred years
when Isaac his son came to be to him." Therefore if he is other than the
generated things, and the Son is the only own offspring of the essence of the
Father, the pretext of the Arians concerning having become has been made
vain; for even if having been shamed in these they force again to say that
the sayings have been said comparatively, and that therefore the things
compared are of the same kind, so that the Son is of the nature of the
angels, they will be shamed first as speaking the things of Valentinus and
Carpocrates; of whom the one has said the angels of the same kind with
Christ, and the other says that angels are creators of the world; for having
perhaps learned from them these also compare the Word of God to the angels.
But they will be put to shame imagining such things, from the hymn-singer
saying "Who shall be likened to the Lord among sons of God," and who "is like
you among gods, Lord?" they will hear 372 nevertheless, if perhaps they
learn, that in those of the same kind admittedly comparison is accustomed to
take place, and not in those of another kind. No one then would compare God
toward man, nor again man toward the irrational, nor woods toward stones
because of the unlikeness of nature; but God is an incomparable thing, and
man is compared toward man, and wood toward wood, and stone toward stone; and
one would not say upon these Better, but the more and the more. Thus Joseph
was more beautiful beyond his brothers, and Rachel than Leah; and a star is
not better than a star, but rather "differs in glory"; but upon those of
another kind, when someone compares these toward one another, then better is
said toward the differing, just as it has been said upon wisdom and the
stones. If then the apostle had said By so much more the Son advances than
the angels, or by so much he is greater, there would have been a pretext for
you as of the Son being compared toward the angels; but now saying that he is
better and differs by so much, as Son has stood apart from servants, he shows
him to be other than the nature of the angels; and saying again that he is
the one who has founded all things, he shows him to be other than all the
generated things; and he being other and of another essence beyond the nature
of the generated things, what comparison or likeness of his essence toward
the generated things? For this reason even the Son himself has not said My
Father is better than me, lest someone suppose him foreign of that nature;
but he said greater, not by some magnitude, nor by time, but through the
generation from the Father himself; yet even in saying He is greater he has
shown again the property of the essence. And the apostle also, not wishing
primarily to distinguish the essence of the Word toward the generated things
said 373 Having become so much better than the angels; for it is
incomparable, or rather it is other; but looking toward the incarnate economy
of the Word and the economy that then came about from him, he wished to show
that this one is not like those before, in order that by as much as he
differs in nature from those previously sent from him, by so much also more
the grace that has come about from this one and through him may become better
than the ministry through angels; for it was of servants only to demand the
fruits, but of Son and master to grant the debts, and to transfer the
vineyard.

For since the first man has trampled the divine commandment, then the nature
of man has been sick with the very easy-to-be-carried-aside toward sins; for

beginning and before the anointing or the endless ages and before he became
379 according to us having had no share of sanctification? And if this is
true, he was not free of sin. How then does the most wise Paul say that the
Son is radiance of the glory of the Father, and likewise character of his
hypostasis? For the character entirely forms upon himself the archetype;
therefore neither is the Father himself holy according to nature. Then who is
the one who sanctifies him? Having then said farewell to their vomitings, we
shall confess that the Son exists holy according to nature just as indeed
also the Father himself, and we say that he has been sanctified then
according to flesh, when he is said to have been emptied having been called
man, in whom the sanctification is according to participation from God and
not according to his own nature. And it is no wonder if the Word being God
appropriates the things proper of the humanity both in love of good and
economically. For he who wholly willed to suffer the emptying, how would he
reasonably decline the things of it? Therefore if the Word from God the
Father should be understood according to himself, he is without need of every
good, and we say that the Holy Spirit is his own. But since as I said he
himself has become flesh he anoints his own temple with his own Spirit; for
the Father works whatever he would work through no other except through the
Son alone in Israel. Therefore he is said to receive the Spirit economically
as man; "For Jesus from Nazareth, he says, how God anointed him with Holy
Spirit and power"; but he gives it as God to those worthy to receive this.
And indeed he breathed upon the holy apostles saying "Receive Holy Spirit,"
and the divine Peter also addressed the crowds of the Jews concerning him
"Having then been exalted by the right hand of God and having received the
promise of the Spirit from the Father he has poured out this which you see
and hear." You hear in these things also very clearly that he has been
exalted by the right hand of God, and to receive and 380 to give the Spirit,
and he is exalted clearly when he has humbled himself; for just as he let
himself down into this willingly, so again he exalts himself even with flesh
himself being the hand of the Father, through which he works all things in a
God-befitting way. He is therefore exalted through himself according to the
will of the Father, when having broken through the bonds of death and having
said to the spirits below "Come out," he has sat at the right of the Father.
The Son according to nature has therefore been anointed with the oil of
gladness, when also the Father has been called his God, he having become
