The last catechesis of the same to those about to be enlightened. Discourse 3.

Today is the last day of the catechesis; wherefore I also, the last of all,
have arrived at the last. And I have arrived last, announcing to you that
after two days the Bridegroom comes. But rise and light your lamps, and with
bright light receive the King of heaven. Rise and watch; for not in the day,
but in the middle of the night, the Bridegroom =167= comes toward you; for
this is the custom of the bridal procession, that in deep evening the brides
be handed over to the bridegrooms. But do not run past the word, having heard
simply that the Bridegroom comes; for it is truly great and full of much love
for mankind. He did not bid the nature of men go to him, but he himself came
to us; for such is the law of marriages, that the bridegroom come to the
bride, even if he be most wealthy, and she lowly and rejected. But among men
nothing marvelous happens in this; for even if there is great difference in
worth, there is nothing of nature between; for even if the bridegroom be rich,
and the bride poor and lowly, yet both are of the same nature. But with Christ
and the Church the marvel is that, being God and of that blessed and
unadulterated substance (and you know how great the distance between God and
men), he deigned to come to our nature; and leaving his paternal house, not by
removal, but by the dispensation in the flesh, he ran to the bride. This at
least the blessed Paul himself also, knowing and marveling at the excess of
this guardianship and honor, cried out greatly and said: For this cause shall
a man leave his own father and his own mother, and shall be joined to his own
wife. This mystery is great; but I speak concerning Christ and concerning the
Church.

And what is marvelous if he came to the bride, seeing that he did not refuse
even to lay down his soul for her? And yet no bridegroom ever laid down his
soul for a bride; for no one, no lover, however mad he be, is so consumed with
longing for his beloved as God is eager for the salvation of our souls. Though
I must be spat upon, he says, though beaten, though ascend upon the cross
itself, I will not refuse even to be crucified, so as to win the bride. And he
suffered and endured these things, not having marveled at her beauty; for
before this nothing was more shameful nor more unpleasant than she. Listen at
least how Paul sketches her shapelessness and her shame: For we also were once
=168= foolish, disobedient, going astray, serving desires and various
pleasures, hateful, hating one another. We hated one another; so great was the
excess of wickedness; and God did not hate us who hated one another, but those
in such shame, those being in shapelessness of soul, these he saved. And
coming and finding her who was about to be led as bride to him naked and
unseemly, he clothed her with a clean garment, whose brightness and glory no
one, neither word nor mind, will be able to set forth. How shall I say it? As
a garment he clothed himself about us; for as many of you as were baptized
into Christ have put on Christ. Beholding this garment from above with
prophetic eyes, David cried out and said: The queen stood at your right hand.
The poor and rejected one has become queen all at once and stands near the
king, and as bridegroom and bride standing in a holy chamber, so the prophet
shows the Church and Christ: arrayed in a gold-woven robe, embroidered.
Behold, he told you also the garment; then, that hearing of gold you may not
fall upon the sensible things, he raises your mind and sends it toward the
contemplation of the intelligible, adding and saying: All the glory of the
daughter of the king is within. Do you wish to see also her sandals? Neither
are these sewn from sensible matter, nor composed from common skins, but from
gospel and peace; for he says, shod your feet in the readiness of the gospel
of peace. Do you wish me to show you also the sight of the bride herself,
flashing and having immeasurable beauty, a great multitude of angels and
archangels around her? But come, let us take hold of the hand of Paul the
bride-conductor, who, cutting through the multitude, will be able to bring us
in to her. What then does he say? Husbands, love your own wives, as also
Christ loved the Church and delivered himself up for her, that he might
sanctify her, having cleansed her with the washing of water in the word. Did
you see her body clean and bright? Did you see her flashing more beautiful
than the rays of the sun? =169= Then he adds, that she may be holy and
blameless, not having spot or wrinkle or any such thing. Did you see the very
flower of her youth, her very prime of age? Do you wish to learn also her
name? She is called faithful and holy. For he says, Paul, apostle of Christ
Jesus, to the saints who are in Ephesus and faithful in Christ Jesus.

But hearing the name of the bride I called to mind an old debt of mine; for I
was promising you on account of what we are called faithful. On account of
what then are we called? We the faithful were entrusted with things which the
eyes of our body cannot see; so great are they and dreadful, and they
transcend our nature. For neither will reasoning find them out, nor human
speech interpret them, but the teaching of faith alone knows them well.
Therefore God made for us eyes twofold, those of the flesh, and those of
faith. When you enter into the holy initiation, the eyes of the flesh see the
water, but the eyes of faith see the Spirit; those contemplate the body being
baptized, these see the old man being buried; those the flesh being washed,
these the soul being cleansed; those the body rising from the waters, these
see the new man rising from this holy purification and flashing forth. Those
see the priest from above laying on the right hand and touching the head, but
these behold the great high priest from heaven stretching out the invisible
right hand and touching the head; for it is not a man who then baptizes, but
the only-begotten Son of God himself. And what happened with the Lord's flesh,
this happens also with ours; for as there John, as it seemed, held him by the
head, but the Word of God led down into the streams of the Jordan and was
baptizing, and from above the paternal voice said, This is my beloved Son, so
also the Holy Spirit by his visitation. =170= This happens also with your
flesh; for baptism happens in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the
Holy Spirit. Therefore John also said, instructing us, that not a man baptizes
us, but God: After me comes one stronger than I, whose sandal strap I am not
worthy to loose. He himself will baptize you in the Holy Spirit and in fire.
Therefore also the priest baptizing does not say, I baptize so-and-so, but,
So-and-so is baptized in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy
Spirit, showing that it is not he who baptizes, but the Father and the Son and
the Holy Spirit, whose name is invoked. Therefore also our announcement today
is called faith, and we permit you to say nothing else before, until you say,
I believe. This word is an unmoved foundation, having from above an unshaken
building. Therefore Paul also says: For he who comes to God must believe that
he is. Therefore also you, coming to God, first believe, and then utter this
word; for if this is not so, you can neither say nor understand any of the
other things. And lest I pass over that unutterable and untestified
generation, this one that took place below, of which many became witnesses,
bringing it into the midst, through it I will make you certain of the things
by proof, that without faith it is not possible ever to receive even this. He
whom none can contain, who holds and sustains all things, came into a virginal
womb; how, tell me, and in what manner? It is not possible to demonstrate; but
if you come to faith, that will be able to make you certain; for the things
that transcend the weakness of our reasonings must be entrusted to the
teaching of faith. This manner of the generation not even Matthew himself, the
writer, knows. For that she was found with child of the Holy Spirit he said,
but the manner he did not teach. Gabriel does not know it either; for he too
says only this: The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most
High will overshadow you. But the how and in what manner not even he himself
knows.

=171= But the discourse concerning faith we shall concede to the teacher, and
it is possible for us also at another season to speak to you, when many of the
uninitiated are present; but the things which you alone must hear now, and
which it is not possible to say with those mixed in with you, these it is
necessary to say today. What then are these? After tomorrow, on Friday, at the
ninth hour, some of you must be required with words and set down covenants
with the Master. And I did not remind you of this day and hour simply; but
even from these one can learn something mystical. For on Friday, at the ninth
hour, the robber entered into paradise, and the darkness that was from the
sixth hour until the ninth was dissolved, and then the sensible and the
intelligible light was lifted up over the inhabited world a sacrifice; for
then Christ says: Father, into your hands I will commit my spirit. Then this
sensible sun, seeing the sun of righteousness shining from the cross, turned
away his rays. When therefore you also are about to be brought in at the ninth
hour, remember also the greatness of the achievements, and count these gifts
to yourself, and you will no longer be upon the earth, but you will rise up
and lay hold even of the heavens themselves with your soul. It is needful then
that you, being brought in then all together (for observe this too, that all
things are given in common to you all, so that neither should the rich despise
the poor man, nor should the poor think he has anything less than the rich;
for in Christ Jesus there is neither male nor female, neither Scythian nor
barbarian, neither Jew nor Greek; not only of age and nature, but every
inequality of worth also has been abolished; one dignity for all, one gift,
one bond of brotherhood for us, the same grace), it is needful then that you,
being brought in then all together, bend the knee, all of you, not standing
upright, and stretching out your hands to heaven give thanks to God for this
gift. For upon the knee the holy laws bid us lie, so that through the posture
also we confess the lordship. For that to bend the knee belongs to those who
confess servitude, hear what Paul says: that at him every knee shall bend, of
things in heaven and =172= things on earth and things under the earth. Having
bent the knees then they bid you say, initiating you, these words: I renounce
you, Satan. Tears came upon me now, and my mind was troubled, and I groaned
bitterly. On account of what was I reminded of that sacred evening, on which I
was counted worthy to utter this blessed voice, on which I was led to that
dreadful and holy initiation? I remembered the purity of that time, and the
sins, as many as I gathered after it up to the present day. As therefore those
women who have been brought down from wealth and affluence into uttermost
poverty, seeing other maidens being wed and given to wealthy bridegrooms and
enjoying much honor and being led forth with attendance and procession, grieve
and are in pain, not envying the fair things of others, but learning more
exactly their own misfortunes in the good fortunes of others, such a thing
indeed have I too now suffered. But that I may not, recounting my own ills,
now lead the discourse to more gloom, come, let us return again to you. I
renounce you, Satan. What has come to pass? What is this strange and
paradoxical thing? You who feared, who trembled, have you risen up against the
tyrant? Do you despise his fierceness? Who lifted you to such resolve? Whence
came confidence to you? I have a weapon, he says, strong. What weapon, what
alliance? Tell me. I enlist under you, O Christ, he says; therefore I am of
good courage and rise up; for I have a strong refuge. This made me superior to
the demon, who before this feared and trembled. Therefore I renounce not him
alone, but all his pomp. And the pomp of the devil is every form of sin:
theaters of lawlessness, horse races, assemblies of laughter and full of foul
speech; the pomp of the devil: bird auguries and oracles, omens and
observations of times, and symbols and amulets and incantations. The cross has
a marvelous amulet and the greatest incantation, and blessed is the soul that
says the name of Jesus Christ crucified; call upon this, =173= and every
disease will flee, and every satanic plot will give way to you. Remember
therefore these words; these are the covenants with the Bridegroom. For as at
marriages the documents of the wedding gifts and of the dowry must be drawn
up, so also now before the marriage this takes place; he found you naked and
poor and unseemly, and he did not run past; he needs only your purpose. Bring
in then these words instead of a dowry, and Christ will deem it great wealth,
if you keep these things at all times; for his wealth is the salvation of our
souls. Hear how Paul says: rich unto all and upon all who call upon him.

But after these words, after the renunciation of the devil, after the
enlistment toward Christ, since you have become his own now and have nothing
in common with that one, immediately he bids you be sealed, and upon your
forehead he gives you the cross. Since it is likely that that beast, having
heard these words, will be made fiercer still, for it is shameless and wishes
to leap even upon your very sight, imprinting upon your face through the
chrism the cross, he represses all its madness; for it will not dare
henceforth to look against such a sight, but as one seeing rays leaping out
from there, so, blinded in its eyes, it departs. But the cross is imprinted
through the chrism; and this chrism is at once oil and myrrh, myrrh as for a
bride, and oil as for an athlete. And that again it is not a man, but God
himself who anoints you through the hand of the priest, hear Paul saying: And
he who establishes us with you into Christ and anointed us is God. And when
this ointment has anointed all your members, you are able with authority to
seize the dragon, and you will suffer nothing dreadful. After this anointing
then there remains for you to come to the pool of the holy waters. Then the
priest, having stripped from you the garment, himself leads you down to the
streams. On account of what then naked? He reminds you of your former
nakedness, when you were in the paradise =174= and were not ashamed; for he
says, Adam and Eve were naked and were not ashamed, until they took the
garment of sin, full of much shame. Be not ashamed therefore here either; for
the pool is far better than paradise. There is no serpent here, but Christ is
here, initiating you into the regeneration from water and Spirit. There are
not here trees fair and beautiful to see, but there are here spiritual graces.
There is not here the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, neither law and
commandments, but grace and gifts. For sin shall not lord it over you; for you
are not under law, but under grace.

But since you heard the things said with such pleasure, I will ask of you one
reward of the pleasure, which I asked of you also at the beginning. When you
go down into the pool of those waters, remember my lowliness. This I asked of
you also yesterday, when I made mention also of Joseph, who said to the chief
cupbearer: Remember me when it goes well with you. And I said to you at the
beginning: Remember me when it goes well with you. But now I do not say,
remember me when it goes well with you, but remember me, since it has gone
well with you. And that one said: Remember that I did nothing evil. But I say:
remember me that I did many evil and wicked things. Great boldness toward the
King you all now have; we send you as common ambassadors for the nature of
mankind; you offer him not a golden crown, but a crown of faith; he will
receive you with much goodwill. Pray therefore for the common mother of all,
that she may be unmoved and unshaken; and for the high priest, through whose
hands and voice you obtain these good things. Speak much with him concerning
the priests who sit with us, concerning the race of men, that he may forgive
us not leftovers of money but sins. Let the achievements be common; for great
is the boldness you have toward the Master, and with a kiss he will receive
you.

=175= But since I have mentioned the kiss, I wish now to speak to you also
about this. Whenever we are about to touch the sacred table, we are bidden to
kiss one another and to greet with a holy kiss. On account of what? Because
our bodies being divided, at that moment through the kiss we weave together
our souls with one another, so that our assembly become such as was that
apostolic one, when the heart and the soul of all those who believed was one.
For so must we draw near to the holy mysteries, bound together with one
another. Hear what Christ says: If you offer your gift upon the altar and
there remember that your brother has anything against you, go away first, be
reconciled to your brother, and then offer your gift. He did not say, first
offer, but, first be reconciled and then offer. Therefore we also, the gift
being set forth, first being reconciled with one another, then approach the
sacrifice. But there is also another mystical reason of this kiss. The Holy
Spirit made us temples of Christ. Kisses therefore of one another, we kiss the
entrances of the temple. Let no one therefore do this with an evil conscience,
with a defiled mind; for the kiss is holy; for he says, greet one another in a
holy kiss. Having remembered all these things, let us keep them continually,
the enlistment, the renunciation, and the boldness which the Master now grants
us, and let us preserve it unspotted and unblemished, that with much glory we
may meet the King of heaven and be counted worthy to be caught up in the
clouds and to appear worthy of the kingdom of heaven; of which may we all be
granted to attain, by the grace and love for mankind of our Lord Jesus Christ,
to whom be the glory unto the ages. Amen.
