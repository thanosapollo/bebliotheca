PROVERBS.
BEGINNING OF A.
1.1 You think archaic things: that is, simple-minded. 1.2 An Abydene
after-course: the unpleasant, because the Abydenes after dinner bring
their own children to those feasting, shouting and making a din and
causing unpleasantness to the guests. 1.3 The life of Habron: the
luxurious. For a certain Habron became rich; whence also
habrodiaitos. 1.4 The landholder is always rich next year. 1.5 Fearless
fear: of those who fear in vain. 1.6 Agamemnonian wells: for Agamemnon
dug wells beside Aulis and all Greece. 1.7 The sacrifice of Agamemnon:
of the disobedient and hard; for when Agamemnon was sacrificing the ox
fled and was barely caught. 1.8 Barley-cake too is good with bread: of
those giving second things to some. 1.9 Agathonian pipe-playing: the
sweetest and most cheering.
=1.10= A hammer-wrought statue: of the insensible. =1.11= The unlaughing
rock: of things that cause grief. On which Demeter sat when she was
seeking her daughter. =1.12= A sea of goods; and: A treasure of goods;
and: Skeins of goods; and: An anthill of goods: it is said of an
abundance of goods. =1.13= Purer than a rudder: inasmuch as in the sea
the rudder atton is clean. =1.14= Do not despise a rustic orator: that
one must not despise even the cheap. =1.15= Garden of Adonis: of things
short-lived and untimely and not rooted. =1.16= This is another
Heracles: of the strong. =1.17= Enough of the oak: of those changing to
some better way of life. For the ancients, ceasing from acorns, partook
of grain and wine. =1.18= Salt and a bean: of those pretending to know,
but not knowing. For the seers set out salt and a bean. =1.19= Untimely
goodwill differs in nothing from enmity: Hippolytus said this to
Phaedra when she claimed to love him greatly. =1.20= The cap of Hades:
of those hiding themselves by certain devices. For such are the things
of Hades. =1.21= Eat the tortoises yourselves if you have them:
fishermen caught a great tortoise and, hauling it up, were dividing the
one at hand. And they called Hermes as he was passing by to the feast.
And he said the saying set forth. =1.22= If he asks for wine, give him
a blow: of those asking for good things but receiving terrible ones.
=1.23= Taking, we have been taken: of those who hoped to conquer, then
were captured by those same. =1.24= The laughter of Ajax: of those
laughing madly. =1.25= With unwashed hands: of those handling the
sacred with profane hands. =1.26= You feed a whetstone: of those using
much food, but adding nothing to the body. =1.27= A headless tale: of
those speaking incomplete things. =1.28= You sing in vain: of those
laboring in vain. =1.29= Word at once, deed at once: of things
accomplished swiftly. =1.30= The man who flees does not wait for the
sound of the lyre: of those who ought to do each thing quickly and
sharply. =1.31= To stork in return: to give favors in return. For it is
said that storks feed their parents when they have grown old. =1.32= An
insatiable jar: of the gluttonous. =1.33= You farm the winds: to those
who toil and partake of nothing. =1.34= A human is a human's divinity:
of those unexpectedly saved by a human. =1.35= Worth the whole: of
things exceedingly precious. =1.36= Always he casts a young girl toward
an old man: of older men wishing to lie with young women. =1.37= You
are worthy of the shield at Argos: of the well-born. =1.38= Child of
the wind: of the changeable and light. =1.39= To go up onto the roof
with unwashed feet: of those going ignorantly upon certain works.
=1.40= From the starting-line: that is, one must be good from the
beginning. =1.41= In place of a bad dog you demand a pig: of those
giving evils in return for evils. =1.42= Every hedgehog is rough: of
the difficult and ill-tempered. And especially of the injurious or of
eunuchs. =1.43= You speak things older than the hide: of those
narrating foolish things; for the hide, on which Zeus used to write
down the things that happened, is very ancient. =1.44= You mock me from
on high: of those speaking from a height; as if, you utterly despise
me. =1.45= A chorus of the idle: of those doing evils.

BEGINNING OF B.
1.46t Royal eye: thus they used to call the one sent upon some office.
=1.47= A Seriphian frog: of the voiceless. =1.48= Throw into the water:
instead of "sink it." Of those worthy of destruction. =1.49= In mire
you will never find bright water: of those mixing the fairest things
with the most shameful. =1.50= A cough instead of a fart: of those who
fart and pretend. =1.51= An ox in the city: of things marvelled at. For
Lysias set up an ox on the acropolis. =1.52= Water to a frog; and: fat
to a weasel: of those wishing to obtain what they desire. =1.53=
Bath-keeper: of the busybody. For these, having leisure, are
busybodies.

BEGINNING OF G.
1.54t Know yourself: of those boasting beyond their power. =1.55= Know
yourself: an apophthegm of Chilon. And the proverb is applied to those
boasting beyond what they are. =1.83= From any wood: say the full form,
that From any wood a pillory might come to be. And it is said of those
cheap in appearance, but necessary for uses. =1.87= You hide yourself
in honey: of those pursuing luxury. =1.88= The trap has caught the
mouse: of those justly caught. =1.93= To spit into the bosom: similar
to: not to speak boastfully. =2.19= To Cercopize: instead of to deal
craftily and deceive. And it has been transferred from the Cercopes.
=2.24= To Cretize: of lying. For the Cretans are deceitful. =2.70= An
ass hears the lyre: of the uneducated. =2.97= She bears wind-eggs: the
false and unstable.
