THEODORET, BISHOP OF THE CITY OF CYRRHUS, one of the 630 holy fathers at
Chalcedon, to the questions put to him by one of the bishops from Egypt.
Index of them.
1. If God is one God, it is clear that in every respect he possesses the one,
the simple or the composite. How then, the Father having begotten, and so on?
2. If God has a hypostasis that exists, or a will existing within, and a Son
existing within, how, being composed of so many, is he named simple? 3. If the
simple is what is by itself, as with the letters, for example iota, and the

three hypostases and three persons be named? 5. If God is indicative of the
essence, and Father of the person, how is saying Son of God the same as saying
Son of the Father? 6. If according to our nature whatever is a begotten thing
is consubstantial with the one who begot it, how is it not according to that
nature that whatever has been begotten exists after the begetter, and so on? 2
7. If the divine essence is one, how do some attach the incarnation and the
union and the indwelling in the economy to the God Word alone, and so on? 8.
Those who say that the one who is truly and properly Son has been united to
the form of a servant, how do they not worship a dyad of sons? 9. If what
contains is greater than what is contained in essence, but less in dignity,
heaven and earth a house of angels, and so on. 10. If what contains is for the
guarding and preservation of what is contained, how does God, containing the
all, not do so for guarding, and so on? 11. Since the Lord continually names
himself Son of man and not of the woman, and for this reason the unbelievers

the evangelists Matthew and Luke, giving the Lord's genealogy according to the

according to the Mosaic law the brother of the one who died childless, taking

17 THEODORET, BISHOP OF THE CITY OF CYRRHUS, one of the 630 holy fathers at
Chalcedon, to the questions put to him by one of the bishops from Egypt.

1. Question. If God is one God, it is clear that in every respect he possesses
the one, the simple or the composite, the mortal or the immortal, the begotten
or the unbegotten, the begetting or not begetting. How then, the Father having
begotten and the Son not having been begotten, and the Son having been
begotten, and the Father not having been begotten, and the Spirit neither
having begotten nor having been begotten, can the Father and the Son and the
Holy Spirit be called one God, having acquired so great an opposition of being
begotten or of not being begotten and of begetting or not begetting? Answer.
God is one by the coexistence of the three divine hypostases, which differ
from one another not in essence but in the modes of existence. And the
difference of the modes of existence does not divide the one of the essence.
And just as in the case of Adam and Eve and Seth the account of the essence is
one (for it is a rational 18 soul and a mortal body), but the modes of
existence are different (for Adam came to be from the earth, Eve from the
side, and Seth from the seed), and in the different modes of existence the
account of the essence remains one, both indivisible and unvarying, so also in
the case of God, by the identity of the essence of the persons, the Father and
the Son and the Holy Spirit are believed to be one God. For the mode of
existence contributes nothing to the account of the essence. Therefore the
things said in the same way unvaryingly of the three divine hypostases, in
these understand the one of the essence. And the things not in the same way,
these understand as belonging to the mode of the existence of the persons.

2. Question. If God has a hypostasis that exists, or also a will existing
within, and a Son existing within, how, being composed of so many, is he named
simple? Answer. Just as God is wholly everywhere and wholly in each and in
himself, and of this we have faith, but we do not have inquiry, so also his
simple is wholly Son and is wholly having a Son and is wholly will and is
wholly having will. For God is not according to the created nature, so that
his being and his having would be understood in composition, as nature is. But
as he is above nature, so also his being and his having are above composition.

3. Question. If the simple is what is by itself, as with the letters, for
example iota, and the composite is what is with another, for example pi, and
by being of the same essence both, both the iota and the pi composed of three
iotas, the composite is not taken away from what is composed 19 of both, how
is God said to be uncompounded, having the composition from two persons
understood as consubstantial (I mean Father and Son) and one person that from
what is at hand gives a suspicion of a peculiar essence (I mean the Holy
Spirit), and is called one and uncircumscribed, so great and of such a kind
being the distinction and division of his hypostases, or rather of the
persons? Answer. The iota and the pi differ from one another in nothing
according to essence. For each is a line, and a line differs from a line in
nothing. But the iota differs from the pi by such-and-such a placing,
according to which that is iota and this is pi. And the pi, if it is a line,
is one. But if it is pi, it is a triad and composite and generated. For it
came to exist as being pi part by part and by a temporal extension under the
one who composed it and made it. Therefore the one who wishes to contemplate
the one who is above nature according to some natural analogy must take from
the analogy only those things that fit the one contemplated through the
analogy. For example, the one and the three belong to the pi in a generated
and composite way, but to God in an ungenerated and uncompounded way. For the
one who does not have the one who made and composed him is neither generated
nor composite. Therefore in the case of God we speak of eternal coexistence,
but no longer also of composition, but in the case of the pi of composition
because of its coming to be part by part. God then is one, the Trinity by the
monad of the essence, without any division and
distinction according to this, just as the pi is one by the monad of the line,
without any division and distinction according to this. But there is a
difference in the Holy Trinity according to the modes of 20 the existence of
the hypostases, and in the pi according to the qualitative placing of the
line.

4. Question. If the union of the divine hypostases is indivisible, how can
three hypostases and three persons be named, and not rather one hypostasis,
three-named, three-personed? Answer. Of the divine hypostases the one of the
essence is indivisible, but the three of them is divisible. Therefore the
three hypostases are three by peculiar names. And the hypostasis which we call
Father, we no longer call the same hypostasis also Son, but another. For we
say that the triad of the names belongs to the triad of the hypostases, not to
their monad. Therefore God is one by the one and indivisible of the essence,
but the persons are three by the division of the hypostases.

5. Question. If God is indicative of the essence, and Father of the person,
how is saying Son of God the same as saying Son of the Father? Answer. Even if
we say the Son disjoinedly, sometimes Son of the Father, sometimes Son of God,
yet disjoinedly him: we never understand. The Son then is Son of the Father
who is God according to essence. And saying Son of the Father is the same as
saying Son of God, since whatever belongs to the person, this also belongs to
the essence of the person.

6. Question. If according to our nature, whatever is a begotten thing, this is
consubstantial with the one who begot it, how is it not according to that
nature that whatever has been begotten exists after the begetter? And if not
the second, then neither will the first be understood similarly to us in the
case of the divine generation. 21 Answer. We, having existence prior to
generation, beget after existence. But God begot together with existing.
Therefore there is not with God an after-existence of a begotten thing, since
there is not even a pre-existence of generation.

7. Question. If the divine essence is one, how do some attach the incarnation
and the union and the indwelling in the economy to the God Word alone, the
Word of God having the indivisible toward the Father and the Spirit? How then
will the things that belong to the consubstantial Trinity because of the
indivisible be able to fit consistently to the one person, that is, to the God
Word? Answer. The divine essence is one and all its things are common to those
who have it, but the divine hypostases are three, whose proper things belong
dividedly to each. Since therefore the incarnate economy is proper to the God
Word, for the taking of the flesh came about for his manifestation, for this
reason we consistently attach the things of the economy to the God Word
according to the mode of counter-showing. And for this reason the divine
Scripture also makes the words dividedly toward the Father and toward the
economy, as when it says "the Father loves me" and "the Father, who has given
me." And likewise it divides the words toward the Spirit and toward the
economy, as "the Spirit drove him out into the wilderness to be tempted by the
devil" and "if I by the Spirit of God cast out the demons." But toward the
Word of God and toward the economy it nowhere makes such a division, but
according to the account of counter-showing, concerning one and the same
person, it makes the narration undividedly of the things that fit dividedly to
each nature.

8. Question. Those who say that the one who is truly and properly and really
Son has been united to the form of a servant, by the contrast of the 22 one
who is not truly nor properly nor really Son, yet who is nevertheless also
called Son, how do they not worship a dyad of sons, even if the two persons
happen to be of different essence? Answer. Such a contrast has come about for
the clarification of who gave what to whom, that is, that the form of God,
having taken the form of the servant in its own rank, showed it to the
creation. Therefore the divine Scripture also, setting out from this
knowledge, proclaims the one Lord
Jesus Christ, sometimes according to his unplaced sonship, when it says
"through whom he also made the ages," sometimes according to the placed, when
it says "in these last days he has spoken to us in a Son, whom he appointed
heir of all things, through whom he also made the ages." [These] do not fit
the form of the servant. For this reason, by such a contrast, what fits has
been assigned to each form. For if Christ is not unplaced Son according to one
nature and placed according to another, as the account of the contrast wishes,
but according to the same nature the Son is unplaced and Christ is placed (for
it is impossible that the scriptural voices proclaiming Christ unplaced and
placed [the] Son be abolished), it is necessary to understand the God Word as
Son according to the contrast of the truly and properly and really and of the
neither truly nor properly nor really. And if this is plainly absurd, then the
contrast of the natures on their dyad is not open to accusation, as being
constructive of this. And that the form of the servant is seen in the rank of
the form of God and not in its own person of all the authority and honor
according to person, this is destructive of a dyad of sons. For what is in
rank is the same as the person that ranked it, and not by nature.

9. Question. If what contains is greater than what is contained in essence,
but less in dignity, for example heaven and earth of angels 23 and of human
beings, a house of those who dwell, and a body of a soul, and God contains the
all, how is he not less than the all by the account of dignity? Answer. This
account is not universal. Therefore it was not asked soundly. For those things
of which the contrary can be found through the same, these, as not having the
necessary, do not even have the credible. For there are some contained things
that are less than the containing both in essence and in dignity, as the
stones of the fruits. And further, the containing things mentioned contain the
contained by being, but God contains the all by will. And the other contained
things themselves also need the containing, but God does not need that which
contains him. And for the other contained things being and remaining are not
from the things that contain them, but for the all both belong from God. And
the containing things mentioned exist because of the contained, but God does
not exist because of the all. Therefore God is greater than the all, as in
essence, so also in dignity.

10. Question. If what contains is for the guarding and preservation of what is
contained, how does God, containing the all, not contain the all for the
guarding of the evil and for continuance? For in the part of the all the evil
is contemplated. Answer. The all is a work of God, and God, keeping the all,
keeps his own work. And just as God did not make anything evil by nature, so
neither does he keep anything evil by nature.

11. Question. Since the Lord continually names himself Son of man and not of
the woman, and for this reason the unbelievers try 24 to show that the
Master's birth came from marital union (for they say that "of the man" is a
name not of a female but of a male), by what arguments shall we refute such
abuse as vain? Answer. If the Master's birth came from marital union, the
Scripture would not have said "as was supposed, son of Joseph." For "as was
supposed" has no place in the case of those born from marital union, and to
overturn by one unclear and incomplete voice all those voices that plainly
proclaim Christ born of the Holy Spirit and Mary the virgin is among the most
absurd things. And toward the destruction of their vain attempt we use such
arguments: if because Christ did not call himself son of the woman he is for
this reason not of the woman, it is clear also because he did not call himself
son of some man. And if Christ is not son of some man, of necessity he is not
even son of man. And further, if just as Joseph was styled son of Heli
according to the law without marital union, God having wished in this way to
give Joseph as son to Heli, what absurdity is it thus also to give Christ as
son to Joseph without marital union?
For this reason the divine grace pre-arranged that the virgin be betrothed to
a man who had two fathers, one according to nature from marital union, one
according to the law without union, pre-sketching in that one's generation the
generation of Christ, who was born of the Holy Spirit as son to God, and born
of the wife of Joseph as son to Joseph. "The Holy Spirit," it says, "will come
upon you, and the power of the Most 25 High will overshadow you. Therefore
also the holy thing born of you will be called Son of God." But if what is
born of the wife of Heli is son of Heli according to the law of God, much more
is what is born of the wife of Joseph according to the divine good pleasure
son of Joseph without marital union.

12. Question. If the evangelists Matthew and Luke, giving the Lord's genealogy
according to the flesh, the one wrote his genealogy according to nature, the
other according to law, because the races of the Jews are constituted thus,
how is the blessed Luke not shown to be at variance with Matthew, having set
out through his own Gospel more begetters of Christ according to law than
according to nature, the opposite having been obliged to be written by him,
since it is necessary to count the fathers according to law as fewer than
those according to nature? For it is possible to become a father according to
law, once the father according to nature has begotten the child. How then, as
I said, has the order been reversed among the evangelists, and Luke, who ought
rather to name equal or even fewer begetters of Christ according to law than
according to nature, is found naming more? And if in the genealogy they say
things at variance with themselves and with one another, how can they possess
credibility in the remaining teachings about the Savior, from the start moving
to contradiction the one who wishes with reason to be added to our religion?
Answer. In the genealogy according to Luke, Heli alone 26 is father of Joseph
according to the law. And from Heli as far as Nathan all those set under are
sons according to nature of those set before, who because of the relation
which Heli has toward Joseph are counted as grandfathers and
great-grandfathers, Joseph alone according to the law, and all the rest
according to nature. And this being so understood, all the slander of the
evangelists on the contradiction is destroyed, which from the misunderstanding
of those voices was thought by those who misunderstood.

13. Question. If according to the Mosaic law the brother of the one who died
childless, taking the wife of the one who had departed, begot children from
her, according to nature for himself, but according to law for the brother,
then, if it happened that the remaining brother had a wife, did he take
together with this one also the wife of the dead brother as wife? And how is
it not absurd? And if such a woman also happened to be barren, how did the
marriage not bring, besides the absurd, also the useless, and the command of
the law was shown from every side to be without resource, the one who died not
receiving the memorial through childbearing? And what benefit also came to the
one who died? That through another's childbearing that one after the end
should be named father of a foreign offspring? Answer. The law not preventing
the Israelites from taking wife upon wife if they wished, not only a kinswoman
but also a captive and a concubine, nothing absurd then arises from the
brother of the one who died taking wife upon wife, the law not being
disobeyed. For every absurdity stands upon disobedience of the law. And even
if it happen that the 27 woman is barren, yet this is unclear to the man and
to the woman, and it is not just to overlook the law because of the unclear.
And this law was set, in order that what death took away from the one who
died, that is, both fatherhood and the heir, these it might provide him by the
providence of the law. For if human beings come to marriage for the sake of
memorial of an heir, it is clear that by whatever
mode God should wish to provide these to a human being, the matter does not
become useless. And there is also another benefit attached to this law, that
by this additional marriage the lot remain in the same tribe and not pass to
another tribe. And since the woman, once joined to the first husband, has
become one body, for this reason the one who died does not become father of a
foreign offspring, but of the one born from his own body. For just as the one
born from such a union is named son of the one who died, so also that woman is
named his wife.

14. Question. If the aforesaid law was enjoined on the Israelites before the
times of David, how did the evangelist Matthew mention the fathers according
to nature equally before David and after David, while Luke made the memorial
of the fathers of Christ according to nature before David, but after David of
those according to law? And if the names of the fathers according to nature
and according to law are not found without omission after the migration of
Babylon in the divine Scriptures, but have been written according to a
statement, how will the things written according to a statement be able to be
believed by the more accurate? Answer. Luke also mentioned the fathers
according to nature of 28 Heli, both those before David and those after David.
And Heli alone was father of Joseph according to law, who because of the
sonship according to law is led up as far as Nathan according to the relation.
And that the Master Christ was named son of Joseph who had two fathers, the
one according to nature, the other according to law, the divine grace
arranged, in order that, when we hear that Christ is son of Joseph, yet was
not begotten from him, we may not be startled. For just as Joseph himself was
called son of Heli, yet was not begotten from him, because it seemed good to
the divine law thus from the wife of Heli to give a son to Heli, so it seemed
good to God from the wife of Joseph to give a son to Joseph not begotten
according to nature from him. "Joseph," it says, "son of David, do not fear to
take Mary your wife." For what is born of someone's wife without fornication
is of necessity son of the husband and of the wife, in whatever mode God
wishes to give a son to the husband, either through union or without union.
And the evangelists did not make the exposition of those named in the
genealogies using a statement, but the written histories. For the holy
evangelists who wrote the genealogies were Hebrews from Hebrews, among whom
there was a great zeal to have in writing the genealogy of both the royal and
the priestly tribe. Indeed after the return from Babylon Ezra cast out from
the priestly ministry those whom he did not find genealogized in the catalogue
of the priests, and this is shown from the book of the Chronicles.

15. Question. Since Isaiah, prophesying into the Master Christ, says "who
shall declare his generation," does he speak of what is hard to 29 declare or
of what cannot be declared, and is this glory to be taken of the Godhead or of
the flesh? Answer. The generation of Christ according to the flesh is
genealogized and the mode of this has been proclaimed in a scriptural
arrangement, as we have already said before, from the Holy Spirit and from the
holy Theotokos and ever-virgin Mary. But his generation according to the
Godhead is without genealogy. The prophetic saying then indicates the
undeclarable, not the hard to declare, of the generation.

16. Question. If God abolished the ancient worship, as not being pleased with
it, and introduced instead that of the Christians as pleasing to him, and the
orthodox alone pleasing God are fewer in number than Greeks and Jews and all
heretics, what is the proof that it is not according to poverty of power of
the one who chose this worship instead of that that the error remains
unuprooted, and how is the dissolution of that not useless, another error
holding the world? Answer. The Master God having been preordained through the
old covenant to introduce a worship instead of a worship,
if he had not introduced this instead of that, it was possible then only to
slander God on poverty of power, as having preordained things beyond power.
But now, having introduced this instead of that, first, it being proclaimed by
words witnessed by divine powers, he abolished both Judaism and Hellenism, and
later by works likewise coming about by divine powers he makes the uprooting
of all the error among human beings 30 and demons, and with this also of all
the evils in the world. And as for what concerns the worship, a teaching of
faith and of manner of life, if not all who received the worship have had and
have the same opinion about the same things, but some rightly, some not
rightly, this is not an accusation of God on poverty of power, that those from
their own carelessness or other weakness do not understand rightly the things
believed by them, but of their carelessness. And it has been said in the
worship being introduced concerning the fewness of the orthodox. Sometimes
"many," it says, "are called, but few chosen," sometimes "that narrow and
afflicted is the way that leads to life and few are those who find it." And
likewise also concerning the heresies, which the Lord in his own parables
named tares and rotten fishes. If then there is nothing false in the worship
of the Christians, but by the witness of the things * having the future
believed from the things that have gone before, no pretext then is left either
of slandering God on poverty of power, or of saying the worship useless, a
worship being introduced instead, now being dissolutive of both Judaism and
Hellenism, and later also of the heresies.

17. Question. If the things that were going to happen, such as trophies of
wars, attacks of plagues, dissolutions of the precincts, prophets and apostles
have foretold, and among Greeks also words through oracles have foretold, from
where are the better of these recognized, once the prediction of the things
that will be has come about from each 31 side? Answer. All these things belong
to the same God, both the prediction of the words and the outcome of the
works, of the one who through both the prophets and the apostles pre-signified
what he was going to do. And likewise also through those outside piety he
himself pre-signified what he was going to do. For just as through the seer
Balaam he blessed Israel and cursed his enemies, and having done both through
the pre-signifying of the things that would be, and likewise also through
divination he pre-signified to Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon the capture of
Jerusalem, as the prophet Ezekiel says: "and the king of Babylon will stand
upon the ancient road, at the beginning of the two roads, to divine a
divination and to shake a rod and to inquire in the carved things and to
inspect the liver at his right. And there came a divination in Jerusalem" and
so on, so also through the seers among the Greeks, whatever received the
outcome through the works, he himself pre-signified. And the difference of the
prophets and of the seers is great, first that the God in whose name the
prophets and the apostles prophesied, of this one they have had the knowledge
and the faith and the worship. And all that the prophets foretold concerning
both the destruction of the Greek gods and oracles and the constitution of the
Christian things has received the outcome. And then, that nothing of what the
seers foretold, either against the God of the truth and those who worship him,
or on behalf of the constitution of the Greek things, has received the
outcome. And of these a witness is the destruction of the Assyrians, who
setting out from their own divination said "is it without the Lord that I have
gone up against this land to make it desolate? 32 The Lord said to me, Go up
and make it desolate," and having gone up contrary to the divination he was
made desolate.

18. Question. If all who have sinned are punished at the resurrection, and

gain of the
Christian and of the Greek, of the baptized and of the unbaptized, of the

is worse than the unbelievers. For he is so much worse than the unbelievers,
as it is worse to sin with knowledge than without knowledge, as the
inexcusable sin is than the sin that has a defense.

19. Question. If the heretics strove with all their power to know and to keep
the truth of the doctrines and were not able, how is it not unjust to subject
these as having fallen away from the truth to punishments? Answer. So far have
the heretics not sought the truth with all power, that even when this is
pointed out to them by those who have found it they cannot bear to receive it.
And if this defense were true, they ought to pardon one another when they err,
as having constituted the heresies by weakness of power and not by wickedness
of mind, heresies at war with themselves and with the truth. 33 But now,
inasmuch as they condemn one another on the alien of the mind, they are clear
that from the love of glory or the antipathy of the heresiarchs all the
heresies have had the starting-points of their constitution. For which reason
the leaders of the heresies were named by the apostle "grievous wolves." And
that it is impossible for the one seeking the truth with the whole heart and
power to fail of the finding, the Lord bears witness saying "everyone who asks
receives and the one who seeks finds and to the one who knocks it will be
opened."

20. Question. If in the churches of the heretics powers are worked, such as
healings of diseases and drivings of unclean spirits, bearing of fruits of the
earth and gushing of oil, how is it not necessary that from these they be
confirmed in the error? Answer. Just as "he made the sun rise on evil and good
and rains on righteous and unrighteous" is not confirmatory of evil and
unrighteous in the wickedness and injustice, but preparatory unto just
punishment, so it is not confirmatory of the heretics in the error that some
powers are worked among them. For if working powers were a proof and sign of
piety, the Lord would not have declared unapproved and unworthy of intimacy
with him those who have said: "Lord, did we not prophesy in your name and in
your name cast out demons and in your name do many powers?" saying to them "I
never knew you: depart from me, you who work lawlessness." For the one who
taught us to feed and give drink to an enemy hungry and thirsty, much more
does he himself do the like of what 34 he taught us, heaping fire upon the
heads of those who worthily 34 have not known the giver of the grace.

21. Question. If the passible is shown in those who suffer pain and those who
feel pleasure, and of this the sinners and of that the righteous share at the
judgment, how does the one who says that human beings obtain impassibility
after the resurrection speak truly? Answer. We are called impassible having
risen, because from what we are according to nature we do not receive a
change, neither into the opposed according to being and not being, nor into
the contrary according to more and less. For example, we are not turned from
joy into grief, nor do we rejoice over the same good, sometimes more,
sometimes less. And even if over greater goods we rejoice more, and over
lesser less, yet even so according to the proportion of the goods the quantity
of the joy remains unchangeable. And this in the case of those rising unto
life. And in the case of those rising unto judgment the same account, when
they are released, as from death, so also from the judgment. For according to
the Savior, of those who rise some rise unto life and some unto judgment.
22. Question. If a mark of change is taking the good and the base into

the righteous, or again those who happen to be in rest and who have in mind
the punishment of the sinners? For each then gives the other more sensation,

23. Question. If we received the knowledge of both good and evil from God
together with being, how is he not cause of both, who has implanted in the
nature both the knowledge of both and power toward the fulfillment of those?
Answer. God has given us not only the power of our being and of knowing and
doing the good and not doing the evil, but he also granted us the self-chosen
and established us masters of choosing according to preference what seems good
of the things known. And he set our being good or bad not in the knowledge of
the things known, but in the choice of those who choose. God then is not cause
of our being good or bad, but our choice. For just as the one who sees a
fornicating woman and knows her to be a fornicating woman is not a fornicator
from the knowledge, but when the knowledge awakens the passion of desire, not
even so is he a fornicator, but when the choice consents to the passion, then
he is a fornicator either according to the deed or according to the
disposition, so also of good and bad human beings the knowledge is not cause
of their being good or bad, but the choice that has chosen according to
preference what seems good.

24. Question. If God gave the things already mentioned, and crowns and
punishes each according to the deed of human beings, how does he justly do
both, the human being having both from him? 36 Answer. We and what is in our
power differ from one another. We are male and female, but in our power is
being temperate or fornicators. We are then crowned or punished because of
what is in our power. For unto what our choice leads us, through these we are
crowned or punished. For the powers which God gave us toward doing, these he
subjected to the authority of the choice, and God has the judgment toward the
choice, it being governing of the choosable powers in us, and not toward the
nature. And we received from God the authority of doing and of not doing, to
do the just things, and not to do the unjust. When then we so do and do not
do, we are justly crowned. And transferring the doing and the not doing into
the opposed, we are justly punished.

25. Question. If the more bodily things said both about gods by the poets and
about God by the prophets have according to allegory another meaning, how are
both not shown to be a myth? Answer. The things said allegorically by the
prophets about God were said according to a referring from the things by
nature unto the things not by nature, as for example "the Lord smelled a smell
of fragrance." In the case of human beings "he smelled" is according to
nature, but in the case of God by catachresis and not by nature. But the
things said mythically by the poets about the gods are not said according to a
referring from the things by nature unto the things not by nature. For a human
being does not swallow a human being according to nature, in order that we may
also understand according to a referring Cronus having swallowed Zeus. And the
myth is an inconsistent account, having nothing else except the lie. 37

26. Question. If the resurrection happens because of the recompense of what
has been lived for each, how do infants, or even those who died in the womb,
not rise superfluously, neither obtaining recompenses of works, nor being able
through the untimely of the age to receive
sensation of rest or of affliction? Answer. For the one who believes it true
that "it is sown in weakness, it is raised in power," for this one it is also
possible and fitting to believe the resurrection of the infants. For the one
who provides them the incorruptibility is able also to grant them the power of
sensing the goods that belong. Besides, neither "is there there small or
great," but all are perfect in the spiritual age. For "it is raised in power"
indicates nothing other than this. And further, if there is no resurrection of
the infants, God will be found to have formed them in vain. And if God does
nothing in vain, it is necessary then that they too come to being through the
resurrection. And how is it not untimely to condemn Herod toward the vengeance
of the killing of the infants, the infants not being those who are avenged?
And the leapings of gladness of the Baptist John in the belly of the mother
bear witness to these, and the hymn of infants and of those at the breast.

27. Question. If the baptism given by the heretics is false and vain, why do
the orthodox not baptize the heretic who takes refuge in orthodoxy, but leave
him in the bastard baptism as in a true one? And if he happen also to have
received ordination from those, and they themselves accept this too as firm,
how then do the one received and those who received have the blameless?
Answer. When the heretic comes to orthodoxy, 38 the error is corrected, of the
evil opinion by the transfer of the mind, of the baptism by the additional
anointing of the holy myrrh, of the ordination by the laying on of hands, and
nothing of the former remains unloosed.

28. Question. If Job did not sin when he cursed the day, it is clear that as a
righteous man he was heard. And if he was heard, God overturned his own bound
and it is necessary to seek in the divine Scripture the destruction of that
day. And if he was not heard, how is he not shown a sinner having been
unheard? "For he will do," it says, "the will of those who fear him." And
concerning the sinners, that "if you multiply the petition, I will not listen
to you." Answer. The aim of Job in the present curse was this, as if he said
"would that that day in which I was born had been accursed according to this
day." Why? "In order that I," he says, "might not have been born." For it is
not possible for anyone to be born in such a day so accursed. Or is this curse
then not so having come about as awaiting the outcome? For if it had so come
about, he would have recalled his own voice, when he was released from the
afflictions and came to be in the better things. For through the excess of his
affliction he uttered the words of the curse, of which having been released it
is not clear that he had the same mind about the same day.

29. Question. If among the things forbidden by the pious is the observation of
palpitations, why are the pious, wishing with all power to thrust this away,
not able, whether this announces something bright or gloomy? And if this is
base, how 39 does it come about contrary to our purpose? And if good, which I
do not think, for the sake of what has it been forbidden? Answer. The
foreknowledge of the future belongs to the soul illuminated by the divine
Spirit toward the knowledge of the things as yet unclear and not by the
unwilling movement of the body. But the palpitation is a bodily passion coming
about from the running through of the natural spirit in the body of all the
living things. Therefore the saints judged such a bodily movement to be
unworthy to be a criterion of the future. Just as sneezings of noses and
sounds of ears come about contrary to our purpose, so also the palpitation.
And further, for those who have received the hope of the coming state and have
one aim, to look toward that through all things preparedly, it is superfluous
to foreknow the bright or gloomy here through palpitations. And if the same
palpitation that pre-signifies wealth comes about for the worldly human being
and for the ascetic, how has the pre-signifying through palpitation
of wealth not been proved false, the choice of the ascetic not only not
choosing the additional acquisition of the things not present, but not even
the possession of the things present?

30. Question. If fishermen take the fishes from the waters unto death and
corruption, how did the Master Christ, intending through the apostles to bring
human beings "into eternal life," promise them to be "fishers of men"? Answer.
Since it was necessary that the human beings caught by the apostles by the net
of the kingdom die from their former life in sins, for this reason the Lord
likened the catch of human beings to the catch of the fishes. And he added 40
"unto life," having added to the thing shown what was lacking in the example.

31. Question. If the monks, being held by sober reasoning, turned away from
marital pleasures, why in the imaginings according to sleep do they suffer the
unwilling things, and not only unto the chance ones but there are times when
they think in the imagining to have intercourse with mothers and sisters? What
then must one use toward the release from such a war? And whether the one
warred upon must keep away from the mysteries, and whether one must approach
them, either having washed or having used something else, so as not to be
cleansed in a Jewish way, teach, since there is much inquiry about this even
among the more rational. Answer. Since those dedicated to God have an
unsleeping enemy opponent, who by all the modes possible to him plots to set a
blemish on the pious, for this reason they suffer the unwilling things
according to sleep. And yet the opponent, knowing that we are neither praised
for the solemn things not in our power, nor blamed for the shameful (for
praise and blame belong to the things done by us with consent of the
reasoning), and that dreams are of the things not in our power, nevertheless
even through the dreams he moves in us the deeds of both, both of the solemn
and of the shameful, having in himself these hopes, lest perhaps either by the
working of the solemn and of the shameful he awaken us unto vain boasting of
things not done by us, or by the working of the shameful he be able to find us
taking pleasure in the lawless deed. But to see such dreams is not in our
power. But to take pleasure in the so unwilling deed or to be grieved, this is
in our power. If then he find us taking pleasure in the unwilling deed, 41 the
opponent boasts as victor. But if he find us grieving, he is made vain,
defeated in the counsel. Sufficient then toward the release from the evil is
to be grieved at what has happened unwillingly and with tears to wash away the
loathsome of the imaginings so coming about unwillingly in us, and it is not
at all just that those so suffering the unwilling things keep away from the
communion of the divine mysteries. Otherwise we destroy according to this the
difference of the willing and of the unwilling evils.

32. Question. If the Lord and Master Christ declared the tares to grow
together with the wheat, how have the tares multiplied, and the wheat has
almost failed? And what is the proof that the wheat that has failed is wheat?
And how has the prediction of their growing together not been proved false?

59. Question. If all the things of life are good, why are those who abstain
from these praised in Scriptures and those who hold to them slandered? And if
they happen to be base, why have the base things been yoked together with our
life, and this when the creator happens to be good? Answer. It is not simply
praiseworthy to abstain from the goods of life, nor simply blameworthy to hold
to them, but each coming about with a fitting account, both the abstaining and
the holding to them is praiseworthy. And coming about oppositely, both are
blameworthy. And that nothing base according to essence has been yoked
together with our life, the divine Scripture makes clear praising the
generation of the created things, saying "and God saw all that he made, and
behold they were very good." And when we have voluntarily diverted the good
things, the base things come about. For there is not a base thing in life, the
good things in it 63 remaining unchangeable, so that there is nothing base
except the diversion of the good. Nothing base then has been yoked together
with our life. For the base things are base by irrational use and not by
nature, and both using and not using, either well or basely, is choosable.

60. Question. If there is no sensation in the elements, why does Moses bear
witness to heaven and earth to the people, and Isaiah accusing the people
commands these to hear? Answer. The things said to the heaven and the earth
are said to the rational ones in them, as the things said to the city are said
to the citizens, as "Jerusalem, Jerusalem, who kill the prophets and stone
those sent to her." For the city did not kill and stone, but the citizens.

the meaning of "do not follow me, so as through all things to be with me
according to my manner of life before the cross." For he wished little by
little to unaccustom the disciples from the sight and presence of his body.
Therefore neither was he seen through all things to the disciples in the forty
days, in which he spent time upon
the earth after the resurrection, nor did he establish himself altogether
unseen to them. But from an interval he made both, both being seen to them and
not being seen.

the "still" signifies the second generation of these. 65

seeing imagine to see the one who was not Samuel as Samuel. And the truth of
the words came about from God who had given the demon to appear in the shape

since Saul did not hear Samuel, who had said to him the taking away of the
kingdom, but even after the divine vote that had rejected him from the kingdom
still laid claim to the kingdom, for this reason
God judged him unworthy of pre-signifying the things that would be through

66. Question. If male and female were created for the sake of childbearing,

are not useful toward childbearing after the resurrection, yet toward a

through them into a thought of the wisdom of God which set these as a device

melodies also came about fittingly, leading the souls into the sensation
fitting to the things sung. For they were sung either wailingly or lamentingly
or piercingly or in other modes, banishing of slackness from the mind of those
who sing.

68. Question. If the medical art is useful and necessary for human beings, as

follows the 68 salvation of the body. For the one who cares for the salvation
as one must does not need the help from the material things, always having the

word of the Savior is unlying, saying "seek the kingdom of God and all these
things will be added to you."

69. Question. If infants who die have neither praise nor blame from works,

baptized, that the baptized obtain the goods through the baptism, and the not

baptism by the faith of those who bring them to the baptism.

the air 69 heavens, as "the birds of the heaven" and "rain from heaven" and
"the stars of the heaven." From the sequence then of the voices set we
understand heavens according to essence two and besides these
another, of whose sight the blessed Paul obtained, and according to intervals

71. Question. If attaching ignorance to Christ is impious, it is clear that he
has the perfect foreknowledge of all things. How then is he not cause of the
betrayal of Judas and of the sworn denial of Peter, who foreknew these things,
but chose these? And concerning the making of the devil and of the
first-formed by God the same account would fit. Answer. If it was necessary
that Christ be betrayed through one of his own disciples, "that the Scripture
might be fulfilled," it is clear that Christ is cause of the fulfillment of
the Scripture and not of the betrayal of Judas. For of this Judas himself was
cause, which God, having foreseen as going to be, foretold through the
Scripture. And the foreknowledge is not cause of what is going to be, but what
is going to be is cause of the foreknowledge. For the future does not follow
the foreknowledge, but the foreknowledge follows the future. In no way then is
the one who foreknows cause of what is going to be. So that Christ is not
cause of the betrayal, but the betrayal is cause of the foreknowledge of
Christ. And in the case of the devil and of the first-formed the same account.

72. Question. If in the night the sun is hidden, how is the 70 heaven not
shown to be a sphere? For the holy man from the philosophers said that, "as a
cupping-glass to a body, so the heaven lies upon the earth, and the one
through the light is upward-borne, and the other through the heavy is
downward-borne. Therefore by the counter-pull they are held together by one
another." And if this is true, the whole heavenly circle being planted into
the earth, it is necessary that the luminaries exist within and shine always.
How then are they hidden? Answer. If it is confessed that there are many other
things that are on the same and equal plane and through the length of the
interval are hidden from the sight, such as the ships in the sea not seen by
one another, the breadth by the surface of the waters bounding the sight so as
not to stretch further than the horizon, what wonder, if also in the case of
the luminaries the hiding comes about through the same cause? And that through
the counter-pull of heaven and earth the heaven and the earth stand, in the
case of the first heaven and the earth it is possible to guess this because
they came about together, but in the case of the firmament and the earth no
longer. For the earth stood before the firmament, bearing all the wet essence,
without a counter-pull of the firmament. For the firmament came about later
than the earth.

73. Question. If because according to the Greeks, being a sphere and being
moved, the heaven is in a place, how because it has been fixed as a vault or

to both is in a place and for both the quality and quantity of the place has

condemnation and ignorance concerning the things already mentioned holding
both equally? Answer. If the Christians said the heaven not to be in a place,
just as the Greeks say, the condemnation from one another would have been
going to be equal, saying the heaven not to be in a place. But having used
toward one another voices significant of the heaven being in a place, such a
condemnation has been altogether destroyed. And if of the heaven being a
sphere and being moved spherically being impossible, and of its being as a

upon the ignorance. And concerning the place we guess about it from the things
according to us, that just as among us when there is a circular body, both
even and hollow and from every side
equal-perimeter, if it be covered upon the waters, it is borne upon the
waters, in this manner the heaven is borne by the waters. "The one who
stretched," it says, "the heaven as a vault." By the name of the vault he made

74. Question. If the difference according to the forms is useful for us here

rising without want at the resurrection, if we rise with the same difference

proof of this? And how does the parable concerning Lazarus and the rich man
not show the different of the forms? For altogether from this the rich man
recognized Lazarus. And if some should say that knowledge was given him toward
the recognition of Lazarus and of Abraham, from where will they establish
this? And also in the Lord's case, when "the bodies of the saints who had
fallen asleep rose and appeared to many"? Altogether by his own form each
became easily recognizable to those known. Answer. There are many causes
through which those who rise must rise in their own form, first toward a
showing of the divine and great knowledge of God, who was able in so
numberable a multitude of those who rise to preserve for each his own forming.
And then in order that he not be thought creating new human beings and not
raising those who died, for this reason he raises each of those who rise in
his own form, yet not in an untimely age. And further, in order that the just
of the judgment not perish, each of those who rise must have his own form. For
if the forms of both the one who did injustice and the one who suffered
injustice are unknown, the just of the judgment perishes. And further, if when
the needs have been loosed through which the difference of the forms became
necessary, this is found superfluous and useless, it is necessary then that
with this the difference of the namings also be superfluous and useless. How
then does the rich man call Lazarus, the persons not being recognized from the
difference according to the naming? And the narration according to Lazarus and
the rich man is neither a parable, even if it has been said parabolically, 73
nor a history, if indeed a parable is an account containing a likeness of a
thing that has happened toward what will be, and a history an account
containing a narration of a thing already having happened. For neither before
the resurrection does the recompense of what each has lived come about, nor
after the resurrection is "they have Moses and the prophets: let them hear
them" true. And the narration concerning the rich man and Lazarus is a sketch
of an account having a teaching of human beings not being able after the exit
of the soul from the body according to some providence or zeal to obtain some
benefit.

75. Question. If God alone is immortal, according to the apostle, how is "we
shall not all sleep" true according to him? Answer. God is said to have the
immortality alone, because he does not have this from another's will, as all
the remaining immortals, but from his own essence.

and from them the number of the days is constituted, how do the three days
before the bringing forth of the luminaries not show the number after the

of the light the division of light and darkness came about, and from the
division of light and darkness the day and the night came about. Whence it is
clear that before the making of the luminaries the prevalence of light
according to the bound of God made the day and the prevalence of the darkness
the night. And when the luminaries came about they were appointed to have
authority, the one of the 74 light and of the day, the other of the darkness
and of the night. For the creator the light with its
bounded prevalence he allotted to the greater of the luminaries. And such days
as the light now makes with the luminary, it also made before the making of
the luminaries, according to the bound of God who bounded its prevalence as
twelve-hour. For that bound even now presses the sun toward the
from-the-beginning measured twelve-hour movement.

77. Question. Since some have interpreted variously and unclearly "in the sun

the ungenerated nature toward the ungenerated nature according to the nature
does not have a difference, and being such-and-such or such-and-such even for
the sake of a need is alien to the ungenerated nature.

78. Question. If "as Jonah in the belly of the sea-monster the three-day, so

sign of faith of having been sent by God as a herald of the kingdom of the

to Pilate. And on the day on which Pilate received him, on this he also

priests and condemned, on the
morning he was delivered to Pilate and at the sixth hour of the Preparation he

80. Question. If the soul is not the blood of the human being, why when that
is poured out does the living thing perish, and through what examples is it
shown that there is a soul in the body or something else invisible in the
creation? Answer. Having the voices of the creator of the creation that
distinguish the soul from the body, we ought not to demand more credible
examples than these unto presentation of the truth of what is sought. For by
giving the Devil authority against all the bodily constitution of Job to
strike it as he wishes, and preventing him from not touching his soul, he
shows the unstruck soul something other besides his struck 77 flesh. And
likewise also "do not fear those who kill the body but are not able to kill
the soul" makes this clear, that there is something of the human being who has
been destroyed that remains in immortality even after the death of the body,
which it is absurd to call blood that has been poured out and corrupted. It is
then plain that the soul is something invisible to human beings according to
its own nature.

81. Question. Why does the number seven receive a change? For on the Sabbath
the air for the most part is altered, and the progress of the human being
according to age in this number receives the increase, teeth growing in the
seventh month, and in the same year these being changed, and being doubled
again, he acquiring a seminal power. And to speak briefly, the number of seven
works the increase of human beings and makes a distinction of the diseases,
and in the law it has acquired the more reverend of the remaining numbers.
Answer. Toward the power of the nature of the works the fulfillment of the
nature comes about and not toward the temporal extension bounded by a week of
months or of years. Therefore the named works of nature in the question the
nature often leads unto their own completeness sooner or later than the
numbers of seven. Which would not happen, if the nature followed the week and
not the week the nature. The week then is not cause of the fulfillment of the
works of nature, but the power of nature is cause of the week, according to
which it happened to nature to complete its own works. And likewise also
concerning the other numbers of both months and years, through which the work
of nature is fulfilled in the 78 remaining living things and plants, the power
of nature is cause of the equal time, and of the more and less. And the number
seven is more reverend in the divine Scripture, because in it is contained all
the time both of the making of the world and of the rest of the one who made
it, a hexad of the making, and a monad of the rest. In order then that the
memory of the making of the world be kept in human beings, for this reason he
appointed the number seven more honorable than the other numbers in the divine
Scripture.

82. Question. If rejoicing and exulting in the gifts is forbidden as

beings," by the doubling of "rejoice" indicating the intensification of the
joy. Neither then does the joy destroy the
gentleness, nor does the gentleness destroy the sensation of the joy, but
through each the other is kept.

83. Question. Since some concerning the constitution of the world have said
guessing that it will be constituted six thousand years only, if 79 this is
shown true through clear examples either from the divine Scripture, and if it
is unclear, let us learn. Answer. It is possible through many scriptural

84. Question. If each is then shown ungrudging, when what he knows and is able
to do as good he grants to those near, how is God not envious, being able to
make all gods and not having made them? Since God is good * and if not
envious, how did he not do this not by inability? For it is necessary to
suspect one of two. Answer. The absurdity contained in this question is of
many modes. For in it God has been arranged with the creatures, who
ungrudgingly grant to those near what they are able to do or know as goods.
For the near of God does not need God unto provision. For he has toward him in
all things the equal. And further, if God made gods only, he would not have
made the world. For the world of necessity needs the parts that differ from
one another by an overstepping and understepping of essence. And further, if
nothing of the impossibles comes about, and it is impossible for a god to come
about (for God is uncreated and unmade), how is it not irrational then to say
God ungrudging, when he does the things not possible? And further God is good,
because he is uncreated and creator. And none of the created 80 can according
to essence become uncreated, that is God, and this question is like the
account that says "the eye is good and more honorable than the feet." But if
God is ungrudging, why did he not make the feet eyes? For this account also
gathers the same irrational absurdity, that either through envy or through
inability God has not made the feet eyes.

85. Question. If the good then appears good, when it is compared with the bad,
and the world is good, it is clear that the non-world is bad, that is the
before-world. And if both are from God, that is the before-world and the

automatic and not under him? And how does the one who says "that all things
are from him" speak truly? For in the all both the things formerly not being
and now being are contained. Answer. The bad is nothing other besides the
turning from the good. Therefore the bad is later than the good. For it is a
turning of the good. But if this, it is clear that the bad does not appear bad
from the comparison of the good, but from its own nature. And the not-being
before the world was altogether not-being. And the altogether not-being is
neither good nor bad. Therefore nothing is compared with the so not-being. And
further the altogether not-being is neither from God nor from another nor
automatic. Before the world then there was nothing else, except God.
Therefore, the comparison of the beings being toward the beings, the world is
not then compared with the non-world, nor is the non-world contained in the
world. For the non-world is nothing, and what is contained is a being. And
when we say from the not-being 81 God has made the beings, we do not give a
position of the being to the not-being, but the complete destruction of the
being.
