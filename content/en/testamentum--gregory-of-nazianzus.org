A copy of the testament of Saint Gregory the Theologian, transcribed from
the original instrument, in which autograph signatures are also preserved,
both of himself and of the witnesses who signed. In the consulship of
Flavius Eucherius and Flavius Evagrius, the most illustrious, on the day
before the Kalends of January.
I, Gregory, bishop of the catholic church in Constantinople, being alive
and of sound mind, with healthy understanding and with my reasonings in
vigor, have made this my testament; which I command and wish to be valid
and firm before every court and every authority. For I have already made
my mind plain, and I have dedicated all my substance to the catholic
church at Nazianzus, for the service of the poor who are under the
aforesaid church. Wherefore I also appointed three to be stewards of the
poor, according to this my purpose: Marcellus the deacon and monk, and
Gregory the deacon, who came from my household, and Eustathius the monk,
who likewise came from my household. And now also, keeping the same
conscience toward the holy church at Nazianzus, I hold to the same
purpose.
If therefore it should happen that I come to the end of life, let my heir
be the aforesaid Gregory the deacon and monk, who came from my household,
whom I long ago set free, of the whole of all my substance, both movable
and immovable, that belongs to me everywhere; and let all the rest be
disinherited. Yet in this manner: that he restore all my substance, 156
both movable and immovable, to the holy catholic church in Nazianzus,
withholding nothing whatsoever, except those things which in this my
testament I specifically leave to certain persons, by way of legacy or
fideicommissum; but that he faithfully preserve all things, as I said
before, for the church, having the fear of God before his eyes, and
knowing that I have ordered all my substance to accrue for the service of
the poor of the same church, and that for this I appointed him heir, so
that through him all things might be preserved for the church without
omission. As for the household servants whom I have set free, whether of
my own purpose or from the commands of my most blessed parents, I wish
all these now also to remain in freedom, and that all their peculia remain
to them firmly and undisturbed. I further wish my heir Gregory the
deacon, together with the monk Eustathius, who once came from my
household, to hold the estate in Arianzus that came down to us from the
property of Rheginus. And the brood-mares and the sheep, as many as I
already, while present, ordered to be given to them, of which I also
delivered to them both the use and the ownership, I wish to remain theirs
undisturbed by right of ownership. And further, as a special bequest, I
wish Gregory the deacon and my heir, who has served me faithfully, to
hold by his own right of ownership gold solidi to the number of fifty. To
the most reverend virgin Russiana, my kinswoman, I ordered stated amounts
to be given each year, so that she might live in a free manner; all of
which I wish and command to be given to her each year without delay,
according to the pattern that I appointed.
Concerning her dwelling I then made no arrangement, not knowing where it
was more pleasing to her to live; but now I wish this also, that, in
whatever estate she may choose, a house be built for her, befitting a
free person, for the seemly manner of life of a virgin; which house she
shall have, clearly, without disturbance for use 157 and fruit until the
end of her life, and after that she shall restore it to the church. I
wish also that two girls be added to her, whom she herself shall choose,
yet so that the girls remain with her until the time of her life. And if
she should be well pleased with them, it shall be permitted her to honor
them with freedom; but if not, they too shall pass to the same church.
Theophilus the servant who attends me I have already set free. I wish
therefore that there be given to him now also, by way of legacy, five
solidi. And Eupraxius his brother I wish to be free, and that there be
given to him, by way of legacy, five gold solidi. I further wish
Theodosius my notary to be free, and that there be given to him also, by
way of legacy, five gold solidi. My dearest daughter Alypiana (for of the
others, Eugenia and Nonna, there is little account, whose life also is
blameworthy) I wish her to pardon me, if I have not been at liberty to leave
anything to her, having already promised all things to the poor, or
rather having followed the promises of my most blessed parents, whose
mind I think it neither holy nor safe to set aside. As many things,
however, as have been left from the goods of my blessed brother
Caesarius, in silk or linen or woolen clothing or in burichalia, I wish
these to belong to her children, and that neither she nor her sisters
trouble in anything either my heir or the church. Meletius my
son-in-law, let him know that he holds wrongly the estate at Apenzinsos
that came from the property of Euphemius; and concerning this I also
formerly wrote many times to Euphemius, condemning his cowardice if he
should not recover his own.
And now I call to witness all, both rulers and ruled, that Euphemius is
being wronged; for the estate must be restored to Euphemius. The deed of
sale 158 of the village of Kanotala I wish to be restored to my most
reverend son, bishop Amphilochius. For it is among our papers, and all
know that the contract was also dissolved, and I received back the
price, and I long ago delivered both the use and the ownership of the
estate. To Evagrius the deacon, who has labored much with me and shared
my cares, and has shown his goodwill through many things, I confess
thanks both before God and before men, and God will repay him with
greater things; but that even the small tokens of friendship may not be
lacking from us, I wish there to be given to him one camision, one
sticharion, two pallia, thirty gold solidi. Likewise also to our dearest
fellow-deacon Theodulus, our brother, I wish there to be given one
camision, two sticharia of those in the homeland, twenty gold solidi,
from the account in the homeland. To Elaphius the notary, being
well-disposed and having given us good rest during the time he served, I
wish there to be given one camision, two sticharia, three pallia, a
sigillion, twenty gold solidi in the homeland. This my testament I wish
to be valid and firm before every court and every authority. But if it
should not have force as a testament, I wish it to have force as a will,
that is a codicil. And he who attempts to overturn it shall give account
on the day of judgment, and shall have the account.
In the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. I,
Gregory, bishop of the catholic church in Constantinople, having read
the testament and being pleased with all the things written above, have
signed with my own hand, and I command and wish it to have force. I,
Amphilochius, bishop of the catholic church in Iconium, being present at
the testament of the most reverend bishop Gregory, and being asked by
him, have signed with my own hand. 159 I, Optimus, bishop of the
catholic church at Antioch, being present as the most reverend bishop
Gregory was making his testament, according to the things written above,
and being asked by him, have signed with my own hand.
I, Theodosius, bishop of the catholic church in Ida, being present at the
testament of the most reverend bishop Gregory, and being asked by him,
have signed with my own hand. I, Theodulus, bishop of the holy catholic
church at Apamea, [was present at the testament of the most reverend
bishop Gregory, and being asked by him, have signed with my own hand].
I, Hilarius, bishop of the catholic church in Isauria, being present [as
the most reverend bishop Gregory was making his testament, and being
asked by him, have signed with my own hand]. I, Themistius, bishop of
[the holy church of God] at Hadrianopolis, [was present at the testament
of the most reverend bishop Gregory, and being asked by him, have signed
with my own hand.] I, Cledonius, presbyter of the catholic church in
Iconium, being present [at the testament of the most reverend bishop
Gregory, and being asked by him, have signed with my own hand.]
John, reader and notary .... of the most holy .... church of
Nazianzopolis .... having made the copy of the divine testament
deposited in [the church under my charge], the most holy church of the
holy and glorious theologian Gregory, I have issued it.
