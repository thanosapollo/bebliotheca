* On the Return

Of the same, John Chrysostom, when he came from Asia.

1. Moses the great, the servant of God, the head of the prophets, the
traveler of the sea, the charioteer of the air, the table-setter of the
manna, he who was cast out by her who bore him and saved by her who made
war (for his mother exposed him, but the Egyptian woman took him up and
nourished him), he who was nourished in Egypt and had his citizenship in
heaven, who set up so great and so mighty a trophy against the
Egyptians, who made the sea solid and split the rock and conversed with
God as a friend:

2. This man, so great and so mighty, if he left his own people for only
forty days, found them in revolt and transgression. But I, not for forty
days, but for fifty and a hundred and more, found you both assembled and
displaying philosophy and a greater piety; but this is because this
people is more temperate than that one.

3. For this very reason that man, coming down, wove accusations against
Aaron; but I, arriving, weave encomia and compose crowns. For where
there is transgression there are charges, but where there are
achievements, praises and crowns. For this reason, even if I spent a
long time away, nevertheless I was confident in your mind, your love,
your faith, your goodwill, because I knew that my wife, who had been
joined to me, was chaste.

4. So it is also in outward affairs. For a man, when he has an unchaste
wife, does not even permit her to look out of the house; and if ever he
is also forced to go abroad, he hastens to return quickly, pricked by
suspicion as by some goad. But he who has a chaste wife remains abroad,
having left the character of the wife as a sufficient guardian of
security.

5. This Moses and I suffered. For he, since he had left the synagogue as
an unchaste wife, God presses him: "Arise, go down, for the people has
broken the law." But I received no such command; rather, with confidence
I was healing the sick. "For they that are whole have no need of a
physician, but they that are sick."

6. But even if I was left away from you, it was not through contempt,
but because of your wealth. For the things accomplished through me, or
rather through grace, become your crown. And for this I rejoice and leap
and fly with pleasure. I rejoice, but I cannot interpret the greatness
of the joy. What then is to be done, how may you learn the greatness of
the joy? I call your conscience to witness. For from what you yourselves
suffer when you behold me, you know what ours is like, being persuaded
from the sight of the vision.

7. For if the presence of me, one man, filled so great a people with so
great pleasure, what pleasure will the sight of so many longed-for men
not work in my soul? And Jacob, seeing one son, Joseph, rejoiced, and
the old man was kindled again to life; but I see not one but myriads
after that pattern, and a whole people.

8. For these reasons I rejoice, because I have received back my
paradise, a paradise better than that paradise. For there a serpent was
plotting, but here Christ initiating into the mysteries; there Eve
deceiving, here the Church crowning; there Adam being deceived, here a
people being proclaimed; there various trees, here varied and spiritual
gifts. In paradise trees that wither, in the Church trees that never put
off their fruit. And the marvel: the paradise not only keeps the plants
but also transforms the plants. If I find a thorn, I make a vineyard; if
I find a wild olive, I produce an olive. For such is the nature of this
earth.

9. For these reasons I rejoice and leap; yet I do not, because I
rejoice, omit the defense. For since the length of so great a time has
taken us away, receive my defense, beloved. If you sent a slave
somewhere, would you not, when he returned, demand an account? Where
were you spending your time, where did you consume so much time? And I
am a slave of your love. You bought me not by laying down silver, but by
displaying love. I rejoice in this slavery, and may I never be loosed
from it. For this slavery is better than a diadem, this slavery procures
the kingdom of heaven, this slavery is better than freedom, this slavery
prepares a throne for me at that judgment seat, this slavery is not of
necessity but of purpose.

10. For who would not gladly be a slave to you, who are such mad lovers?
Even if I had a soul of stone, you made it softer than wax. What shall I
say of the longing you displayed, the madness of yesterday and the
frenzy, the shouts in the marketplace? You hallowed the air, you made
the city a church. I was being honored, and God was receiving it.
Heretics were put to shame, the Church was being crowned, you were being
proclaimed. For the prosperity of a Church is when the root is bound to
the branches, when the shepherd lodges with the sheep.

11. I came and received good tidings. What kind? That you trampled down
heretics, that you convicted those who had broken the law against
baptism. Did I say in vain that the chaste wife, while the husband was
absent, convicted the adulterers; that while the shepherd was not
present she drove away the wolves; that without the helmsman the sailors
saved the ship; that without the general the soldiers set up the trophy;
that without the teacher the disciples, without the father the children;
or rather, not without. For your achievements are my crown, my boast.

12. But we desired, he says, to keep the Pascha with you. I make my
defense, even if you have especially loosed the anger by the sight. For
if a father, receiving a prodigal son, was reconciled and did not demand
an account of him but immediately embraced him, much more the children
who have received a father.

13. But we wished, he says, to keep the Pascha with you. Yet I will make
a defense for this too. You wished to keep the Pascha with me? You are
not hindered. Keep the Pascha with me today. Shall we then keep two
Paschas? No, but one and the same many times. For just as the sun always
rises and we do not see many suns, but there is one rising every day, so
also the Pascha is always celebrated and is one, always being
accomplished.

14. Our things are not such as the Jewish ones. I am not a slave to
place, I am not subject to the necessity of times. "For as often as you
eat this bread and drink this cup, you proclaim the death of the Lord."
Let us proclaim today also the death of Christ. But then it was a feast,
and today it is a feast. For where there is love, there is a feast.
Where you have received back a father, children, there is a feast. For
that feast too is of love. "For God so loved the world that he gave his
only-begotten Son for us."

15. But many were also baptized while you were not present. And what of
this? Grace has nothing less, the gift does not limp. They were not
baptized with me present, but they were baptized with Christ present.
For is it a man who baptizes? A man lends the right hand, God moves the
hand. Do not doubt concerning the grace, beloved. Why? I say: baptism is
a gift.

16. Attend with accuracy to the word. Just as if you transact a petition
and receive it signed, you do not inquire into what reed the king wrote,
nor into what paper nor into what ink, but you seek one thing only,
whether the king signed; so also in baptism the paper is the conscience,
the reed is the tongue of the priest, the hand is the grace of the
Spirit. Whether then through me, or through that man, the same hand
writes.

17. We are servants, not masters; and Paul is a servant. "So," he says,
"let a man account us as servants of Christ and stewards of the
mysteries of God." "For what have you that you did not receive?" If I
have anything, I received it; and if I received it, it is not mine, but
the gift of him who gave it. Do not then doubt, beloved. The grace is
complete; the place did not hinder, whether in a ship. Philip baptized
on the road, Paul in a prison, Christ brought the robber into paradise.

18. I rejoice for these things and am glad, and I demand your prayers,
with which I went away, with which I also returned. I had a share in the
sea, but on the sea your love made a calm. I did not enter a ship
without you, I did not go down into an inn without you, I did not enter
a city or a church without you; but even if I was separated in body, I
was bound together in love.

19. I saw the Church even on the sea unshaken. For such is love: it does
not know how to be straitened. I went into a church, I stood beside an
altar, and throughout I remembered your Church, offering prayers, and I
said: Lord, keep in deposit the Church entrusted to me. I am not
present, but you are present, you who led me there; and by the grace of
God more than I asked came to pass. I asked that he might keep it, and
he even increased it. The present multitude bears witness: so many
flourishing vines and nowhere a thorn, and so many sheep and nowhere a
wolf; and if somewhere there is a wolf, he is transformed and becomes a
sheep. He himself kept you, he himself brought me back, your prayers
corrected my illness. This I say every day.

20. My absence crowned your city. That you loved even before this was
clear, but not quite clear to all; he who loved knew alone, but the
separation proved the love. Those who were always coming in Asia
reported to us: You have kindled the city. And yet love withers with
time, but your love grew, it did not suffer forgetfulness, but was
kindled more. But I ask that you love me likewise while I am present.
This is my treasure, this is my wealth; for this I seek your prayers.
Your prayers are a wall to me and security.

21. Do not say to me: I am a layman; how shall I pray for a priest? Hear
Scripture saying: "But prayer was made earnestly by the Church," and the
bonds of Peter were loosed. And prayer loosed the silence of Paul.
Prayer bridled lions. Prayer muzzled the sea-monster. Prayer opened
paradise. Prayer spread open the vaults of heaven. The prayer of
Cornelius spread the heavens. Prayer made the publican righteous.

22. This security I ask of you, for this I ask a favor, that God,
receiving your prayers, may grant you the abundance of both present and
future goods in Christ Jesus, to whom be the glory unto the ages. Amen.
