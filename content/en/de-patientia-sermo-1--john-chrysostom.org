On patience, and that one should not weep bitterly for those who have died.

=60.723=

Come, today more than on the former days let us enter more earnestly upon the
word of teaching; for the word often knows how to comfort the pain; and
measured teaching is able to dissolve despondency. For many of those who
yesterday were with you I now do not see being with you, but either called
away to the Master, or also lying on beds, and all these awaiting death.
Wherefore I also see the lament from you, much tumult in the city, much
disturbance in the Church, the tears of the mourners without measure, the
habits and the affairs of the many out of place. For often I have exhorted you
about this and have reminded you of the matter; I mean, of bearing with
patience and thanksgiving the falling-asleep of children and of one's own and
of kinsfolk, and not to behave so unseemly, nor to reckon those going away to
God as lost; and especially wherever some infants and uncorrupted have left
this life. Therefore let us not groan over those, but rather let us lament
ourselves who live in sins. For if today we do not become sober, when then
shall we turn back? if the death of your brother does not chasten you, who
then will turn you back? if seeing a dead man lying you do not set your life
right, when then will you move God to pity? Behold then we see, behold we
learn by the very experience. Therefore let us spare, I beseech, and let us
not be troubled in vain and to no purpose, nor imagine ourselves as immortal.
For one is the road of death, and there is no other road; common is the cup,
and given to all; the only hour, and an evil hour; the only bridge, and having
no passage =60.724=; the debt of all, the only firstfruits, and the end of
all; the divine sword, and not regarding appearance. For death does not
shudder at a king, does not honor a high priest, does not pity grey hair, does
not have mercy on beauty, does not spare youth, does not have compassion on an
only child, is not bent by tears, does not tremble at a ruler, does not fear
tyrants, is not bought off with money, does not accept anyone's person, nor
again a contrary person, but death comes upon all equally. Those today with
us, tomorrow there before us; those today greeting us in friendship, tomorrow
being sent forth by us pitiably; those today in life, tomorrow in the tomb;
those today being manifest, tomorrow breeding worms; those today being
anointed with myrrh, tomorrow stinking; those today living in luxury here,
tomorrow mourning there; and, to speak simply, those today living as
parasites, tomorrow being condemned there. Whom also often seeking, and not
knowing their falling-asleep, we say to one another: Where is so-and-so the
ruler? and we hear: He has passed on now; and, Where is that potentate? we ask
one another, and we learn: He has died; and, Where is the proud king? There,
he says, he has gone ahead; and, Where is so-and-so the companion? He has
crossed over there. There, where?

Where the Judge of judges; there, where the fearful one stronger than the
fearful; there, where the eternal King and inexorable Judge. There? Where
then, or how, or in what place, or in what manner do those who have taken them
happen to be? No one has been able to say; but this only we know, that there,
where the only eternal and only immortal, where the only good and only lover
of mankind, the maker of souls and of bodies. Into that great and fearful
tribun =60.725= al, where there is not laughter, but lament; where there is
not adornment, but darkening; where there is not

distraction, but examination; where there is not reviling, but a defense;
where there is not getting rich, but shuddering; where there is not respect of
persons. but righteous judgment; where we all stand naked, slaves and free,
noble and inglorious, sinners and righteous, rich and poor; some being put to
shame, others being crowned. Where there is the fantasy of kings? Where the
pride of rulers? Where the mercilessness of those who are rich? where the
respect of persons of those who judge? where the much-fancied beauty of youth?
youth, the insensible, that has long hopes of life; youth, the factious heart,
the much-anxious pleasure; youth, that often and in many ways falls together,
that badly flatters the flesh; youth, that is flattered toward deceit, the
clay, the wretched, the easily withered grass, the ear ready to perish; youth,
the easily forgotten dream, the insubstantial shadow; youth, the godless hand,
the painless heart, the stretched-out eye, the lamp ready to be quenched;
youth, the boiling of the bloods and the custom of stains; youth, the
hard-mouthed horse and fit for every destruction. Yet the one solemn here is
pitiable there; the one getting drunk here, there needing a drop of water; the
one ready for reviling here, there having his tongue burned; the one clothed
here in silk garments, there standing naked.

Do not say to me: Give me today, and take tomorrow; for I do not look at you
here, who you are, but I await you there, where you are. Do not say to me: God
is a lover of mankind, even if I sin, he suffers with me; but look also at
that, that here he has mercy, there he examines; here he overlooks, there he
searches; here he awaits the turning, there he delivers over to the fire.
Where is a wise man there, brethren? where a potentate; where the tumult?
where the costly luncheons? where the dinners? where those drinking the wine
with drums and dances? where the parasitism and the theaters, and these while
our brethren are hungry and thirsty, unfortunate and suffering hardship, going
naked, weeping, longing for death, and seeking this out of despondency? Alas!
the cup fearful to all becomes desirable to those in necessity, and the life
longed for by all has become hated among those, and this light longed for
among us has become abominable among those! Blessed is God who works all
things toward what is profitable. Death is not evil, O brethren, but even
beneficial. The word is not mine: Death is rest to a man, the divine Job
declared; that he who has died has been justified from sin, and has ceased
from the greed of life, and has stopped adding evil to evil, and has given
over increasing his burden, and has rested from provoking his God. Hear with
understanding; death is consolation to infants, death is relief to slaves,
refreshment of those who toil, fulfillment of those who owe; death is a
loosing of those who are anxious, a visitation of those worn down; death is a
hindrance of sin. If death were not set before us, we would eat ourselves up;
if we did not have a judge coming, we would not have hope of life; if we were
not awaiting punishment, we would in no way cease from evils, nor put away the
corruption from transgression. Did you see the Master's love of mankind? The
penalty from transgression has become saving.

For if the one who died is righteous, rejoice, because going with confidence
toward the Master he travels in ease; but if he is also a sinner, do not
grieve; for he has gained for himself the evils which he was about to add. If
therefore we were remaining here, we would justly weep over the dead; but if
we all altogether travel there, let us not so mourn those who have gone a
little ahead; let us not, the things which we well perform over the dead,
abolish these through the things which we practice badly. Do you not see what
we do over those who have gone before? With psalms and hymns we send them
forth, signifying through this the thanksgiving toward the Master; and we
clothe them in new garments, foretelling the new clothing of our incorruption.
We pour on myrrh and oil, believing also a chrism of

baptism going along with them as a provision for the way; with incense and
candles we accompany them, showing that having been loosed from the dark life,
they have gone toward the true light; we arrange the coffin lying toward the
east, signifying beforehand the resurrection to him through the arrangement.
Therefore let not those who believe the resurrection grieve so, even as also
the rest who do not =60.726= have hope. For he who does not hope the
resurrection justly reckons the dead man as lost. But let children of Greeks
play at these things, let the peoples of Samaritans do these things; but let
us hear Christ, and believe, That an hour is coming, and now is, when the dead
will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who have heard will live, and
those in the tombs will rise, and the earth will be renewed. Therefore if the
dead will rise, as they will altogether also rise, let us not behave so
unseemly over those who have fallen asleep, I beseech; let not the faithful
emulate the unfaithful; let us not tear our tunics, but rather let us prick
the soul, because the same cup awaits us also.

Let us not strike our arms, lest we emulate the Greeks; let us not call
together the wailing women, lest we move the wrath of God still more; let us
not strip the hair of the head, lest we put to shame our head, Christ; let us
not lament for long days, lest we disbelieve the resurrection; let us not
speak anything blasphemous with the tongue, lest we harm both the one who has
fallen asleep and ourselves. Have you given a child? Give thanks, hymn,
worship the one who took what he entrusted to you; glorify the one who chose
whom he fashioned, and who received the fruit of your womb uncorrupted;
glorify the one who saves the offspring of your inward parts; worship
according to the great-souled Job; give thanks, that you have at all offered
to the Master a blameless sacrifice, a pure victim, a clean offering, a young
Isaac, as Abraham once. Christ has taken, let us worship; the one who formed
has formed again, let us not murmur; the creator has sought the creature, let
us not fight; for we are not even able. Have you given a brother? Worship
Christ who took him, because for you he once became mortal. Have you furnished
an infant?

Give thanks to him, because once for you he became an infant in a manger. Has
he taken a slave from you? Hymn him, because for you the maker of all things
took the form of a slave. Have you buried an only-begotten son? Glorify, fall
down, be reverent, remember, that for you God delivered his only-begotten Son
in flesh unto death. Have you given a girl being chambered for marriage? Give
thanks, lest perhaps being vexed you give also another. Have you sent forth a
wife, a mother of children? Hymn, make yourself secure, lest you strike
against the Master, lest perhaps you also depart. What are you doing, O man?
for again we take up the same. You see a dead man lying, and you dare to
blaspheme? you stand by at such a mystery, and you fight with the one who
separated the soul? Rest, you say to God, and you insult God? Why do you
contend with the judge? why do you strike against the unconquered? why are you
troubled with wrath? why are you drunk with grief? do you not see of what sort
this great mystery is? do you not see of what sort a hard crossing awaits us
also? And even if hard, we pass through it. Do you not see that the road is
heavy? But we all walk through it. Become sober, O man. The one lying
trembles, and you play? that one is examined, and you fancy? that one
trembles, and you do not make ready? that one is struck with amazement, is
wholly troubled, seeing what he never saw, and hearing what he never heard.
Wherefore he also sweats, as those harvesting in the field, and takes leave of
us all, and greets all, while the tongue is able to speak. Farewell, he says,
farewell, brethren, and pray for me. For I go upon a road which I never
traveled, and into a world of souls, from which no one has returned, and into
fearful abodes, where there is no one journeying with me, and into a fearful
tribunal, from which I do not know what happens to me, and into strange
affairs, about which no one has told. Behold I seek a helper,

and there is no helper; behold I seek the one who ransoms, and there is no one
who delivers; behold I seek the one journeying with me, and there is no one
who suffers with me. Where is the father who begot? where the mother who was
in labor? where a brother? Where a brother does not ransom a brother, how will
a man ransom a man?

These things those leaving us utter toward us, and then they travel their
eternal road. For who is a man who will live, and will not see death? or Who
will deliver his soul from the hand of Hades? Blessed is God, that a man,
brethren, behold lies, and is no longer attached to any of the things of life;
behold he has become still, behold he has ceased from vain things. A little
fever, and all have become idle; one hour, and the one who appeared becomes as
not having appeared; one turn, and His spirit will go forth, and he will
return to his earth, from which he was taken. In that day all our reasonings
will perish =60.727=; in that hour a man will leave his father and his mother,
friends and kinsfolk, sons and daughters, and will travel alone. isolated, his
eternal road.

Let us come together with those journeying before us, before we also depart;
sit, and do not trouble the one lying; be still, and loose the tumult; quiet
yourselves, look in, and see the great mystery; shudder, and await the same;
kings, behold, and no longer think great things; rulers, look in, and no
longer fancy great things; see the one lying being separated from life, and
then do not recognize yourself as king or ruler. For what sort of king, where
he does not ransom himself? But behold he too trembles at the cup; behold as
one of all he is in agony; behold he has wholly become pitiable, the one a
little before fearful has become a corpse; behold he is led away as a
condemned man, whom yesterday the condemned trembled at, behold he has been
troubled, behold he has been wholly shaken, behold all the wisdom and the
power has been swallowed, behold he has become wholly amazed. For he saw
angelic authorities, and forgot his own authority; he saw fearful armies of
angels, and reckoned his own authority as a spider's web; he saw masterly
forms, and he altered his form; he heard a sentence of soul, and wholly
trembled in the flesh, and such things he thinks toward himself, being amazed
and considering where is the fantasy of kings, where the authority of rulers.
Alas! behold a true order, behold a mighty power, behold an unlying authority,
behold fearful forms, behold an immortal army.

Considering these things and things like them and more unspeakable, which only
those who have gone before and been tried know, some lying on the bed leap up,
wishing to flee, but not being able; others gnash the teeth, others strike the
jaws, others turn the eyes pitiably, as they see the powers of the body
failing little by little, the tongue speaking, and the deposit being firmly
accomplished, the opposing powers standing by calling to account, accusing,
attempting to snatch, then also the thief having entered and having announced
and having torn the soul from the body. Then we need many prayers, many good
deeds, then much protection of the angels, much guiding of the hand for us in
the ascent of the air. For if going here into a country and a foreign city we
need the one who guides by the hand, how many helpers and guides do we need to
bring us through safely and to pass the principalities and the authorities and
the world-rulers, the unseen ones of this air, whom the divine Scripture names
pursuers and tax-gatherers and tribute-collectors? A good fellow-traveler then
for us is almsgiving and compassion, able unharmed to lead us to the gate of
the heavens and to bring us through safely; good advocates for us there are
the poor, whom here before death we have shown mercy. Therefore let us go
ahead before we are overtaken, let us run before we are caught.

Life is short, but the art is long, the end is near, and the fear is much, and
there is no one who looses. Let us go out into the tomb, while we are in the
body; let us see where our vain mind has its dwelling; let us see what we
become after a little, and let us not be led astray; let us see how we are
dissolved, and let us set ourselves right; let us see into what we end, and
let us take thought of the end. As many as you who look closely at beauties,
busy yourselves with these in the grave; as many as you who are lifted up into
principalities and authorities, look into the coffins, and fear God; as many
as you who gather griefs and remembrance of wrongs toward one another, and are
not dissolved, come, see how you are dissolved. Bend carefully over the
coffin; see lying there those who were once kings, see those who were once
rulers among the remains, see the fearful spectacle of the remains, and say:
Which there is the king, and which the ruler? which the soldier, and which the
general? which the rich, and which the poor? which the younger, and which the
old man? which the well-formed, and which the Ethiopian? Can you not say, that
it is not all dust? not all ash? not all stench? Are not all the things once
lovable now seen as abominable? O the folly! What is this great mystery about
me? The one yesterday longed for by me now lies before me abominable; the
member yesterday mine, now I see as another's; him whom a little before I
embraced, now I do not even wish to touch; with tears I wet as my own, and I
flee the ichor as not my own. I am forced under =60.728= by the inward parts
to approach the one who stinks, but I am hindered by the corruption and the
worms of this one.

I consider the image a little before of the one lying, but nowhere do I behold
this in him. Where is the beauty of the face? Behold it has been blackened.
Where the nodding and well-formed eyes? Behold they have melted. Where the
comeliness of the hair? Behold it has fallen away. Where the stretched-out
neck? Behold it has been shattered. Where the swift tongue? Behold it has
become still. Where the adornment of the hands? Behold it has been loosed.
Where the greatness of stature? Behold it has flowed out. Where the costliness
of the garments? Behold it has rotted. Where the myrrhs and the spices? Behold
they have stunk out. Where the folly of youth? Behold it has passed. And
simply where is the much-fancied man?

Behold again the dust has become dust. Yet do not stop the reasoning at the
tomb, but from there pass over to the resurrection; and consider at once and
believe, that the one present lying. will again be rising, and the tongue now
silent will speak again then, when every knee of things in heaven and on earth
and under the earth is bent to God, and every tongue will confess. And as
Daniel says, I was considering until thrones were set, and the Ancient of days
sat. His throne a flame of fire, his wheels burning fire; a river of fire was
drawing before him; a thousand thousands stood by him, and ten thousand ten
thousands were ministering to him. A tribunal sat, and books were opened.
Truly much is the fear there, much the trembling, unspeakable the necessity,
unceasing tear, unsilenced groanings.

When he will come suddenly upon a high and lifted-up throne, then the heaven
is rolled up as a book, the stars are scattered, the powers of the angels run
before, the tombs are opened, the bodies rise, the springs dry up, the abysses
are shaken, all the earth is made to totter. Of what sort must we be then? of
what sort then, giving account for deeds, for thoughts, for an idle word, for
the things in night, for the things in day, for the voluntary and involuntary?
This it is necessary to be anxious about, this it is good to look at night and
day. What reasonable thing shall we say then? what shall we answer in defense,
tell me, O man? Behold you live in luxury fifty years or a hundred, and you
are rich, and you beget children, and you dower and marry sons and daughters,
and you rule and are king of nations and peoples; and after these things
death, and after death a judgment having no end nor repentance. For there is
not, he says, in

death the one who remembers you; but in Hades who will confess to you? Truly
no one. For even if we are troubled ten thousand times, and we beg many
things, yet there is no one who delivers. For this especially we bless those
dying as infants; for this we all say: Would that being infants we had died!
Do not therefore, what we ourselves desire, seeing it in our own children, be
despondent. For the cup of death is dangerous for us, but for the infants it
is saving; and the thing fearful among all is praiseworthy for those; and what
we have as a beginning of the punishment there, is among those a beginning of
salvation. For concerning what shall those be called to account who have not
even at all received experience of sin? concerning what shall those be
punished who do not arrive at knowledge of good or evil? O of blessed infants
blessed also the falling-asleep! O death of the uncorrupted, a beginning of
the truly eternal life that exists! O end a beginning of unending joy!

But what do the faint-hearted say? A child, he says, I had, only-begotten at
once and well-formed and well-born; this one was the succession of my race,
this one was the heir of the substance, this one the comfort of my
despondency, this one was the staff of my old age, this one the boast of the
fatherland, this one the branch of my root, wholly bright in appearance,
wholly gentle and dexterous, easy of address, desired by his own and by
strangers, by those who see and by those who hear; this one so great was
suddenly snatched up out of my hands, and the one seen a little before is not
present being beheld, but having left the one who begot him, he has gone away
to the one who formed him; having left me who reared him, he has traveled
toward the one who called; having been separated from a mother's inward parts,
he has gone down into a tomb of earth the mother of all.

Shall I not weep for this one, tell me? shall I not lament, nor seek him as
the pupil of an eye? I am not able, he says, I am not able; for even if you
bring in ten thousand doctrines to me, even if ten thousand times you try to
exhort that parents not lament their own children, you will not persua
=60.729= de, by the God who ordained it so, and who bound together the bond of
nature, and who put the fire toward the children about the inward parts of the
parents. I do not say this, O man, nor do I legislate this, not to weep at all
over the children; for even if I say it, I say an impossible thing. For I too
obey laws of nature, and I do not array myself against the season of
compassion; since I too am a mortal, since I too have been born of a mother,
and I am of the same nature. I know the wailing of fathers, I know the
groaning of mothers, I know their sea of despondency, I know the distress of
their trouble; I understand their inward imagining over children, how they
paint within themselves the child not present as present, considering their
image with tears, setting all their habits and words and affairs before the
eyes. But the wondrous Job was not so; for when that man's children and
servants and oxen and camels and all that belonged to him had come to
destruction, he was not troubled, he did not speak ill, as the many, but he
rose, and falling to the earth, from which he was taken, worshipping the
Master he said: For I myself came forth naked from my mother's womb, naked
also I will go away there. The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it
seemed good to the Lord, so also it has come to be. May the name of the Lord
be blessed unto the ages. I am a slave, and I am subject, and I do not array
myself against the one who made; for the matter is not strange; the children
have died. but they have not been lost. For what sort of shepherd does not
have authority in his flock? The good shepherd has chosen his sheep. A little,
and we also depart. Let us also imitate this man, and let us not mourn so
without measure. Let us weep as faithful, and let us not behave unseemly as
unfaithful; as Christ wept for Lazarus, so let us weep; for showing you
measures and bounds, he shed a tear. For how was it needful to weep for him
whom after a little he was about to raise? But that you may learn up to how
much to weep, that we may both show the compassionate of nature, and may drive
off the imitation of the unfaithful; and then let us also

pray that, that we will be of the same portion of those being separated from
life in virtue in the time of the age; and that we having gone away there may
not see our own children in ease, but ourselves be in unbearable punishment.

For as many infants as were separated before the filth and corruption of this
difficult and much-groaning flesh, they utter such voices toward those who
begot them, even if not in words, yet in deeds: Do not then weep over us,
fathers, helping nothing; for you were counseling to have us with you in the
vain life, where all things are joyless and painful, where all things are
unfaithful and unstable, where nothing is lasting nor firm; but our Master and
God having loved us, as from the mouth of a lion, so snatched us from the vain
life; as roses from thorns, so having separated us from the sins of =60.730=
this age; as dearest lambs, so the good shepherd set us apart from you, as
from darkness into light. And we dwell in the country of the living, where all
things are still and unfactioned, where all things are full of light and
pleasing to God, where there is no one doing injustice nor being tyrannized,
where there is not sin nor blackening, where the unapproachable light and
unspeakable joy, where there is not toil nor tears, where there is not thought
nor anxiety, not groaning nor wailing, but only God being praised and
glorified.

The holy angels separated us peacefully. from the body; we passed unharmed the
principalities and the authorities of the air; for we had good guides. For
they did not find in us what they were seeking, they saw nothing in us of the
things they were hoping. They saw the body unstained, and they were put to
shame; they saw the soul harmless and pure, and they were turned back; they
saw the tongue unstained and pure and blameless, and they were muzzled; we
passed, and we made them cheap; we went through them, and we sang against
them: The snare was broken, and we were delivered. Blessed is God, who did not
give us into a hunt for their teeth. Whence also the angels meeting us were
rejoicing, the righteous were greeting, the holy were delighting saying: Well
have the lambs of Christ come. The luxury of paradise was opened to us, the
bosom of Abraham was shown to us; for the Master received us kindly; he
uttered a cheerful voice toward us; he beheld us with a cheerful eye;
wherefore he also enrolled us in the book of life. For we said toward a Judge
who loves mankind: Righteous Judge, Lord, you have deprived us of the earthly
things, do not deprive us of the heavenly; you have separated us from parents,
do not separate us from the saints; we have your seal of baptism sound, we
have offered the body to you unstained through much infancy. Do not then
begrudge us as having our blameless salvation before the season, nor weep,
because we have been transferred from the worse to the better, if indeed
instead of corruptible things we have received the incorruptible. We hymn with
angels, we dance with the saints; but we also intercede for you who are in
sins; we have been numbered with the infants killed by Herod for Christ; we
rejoice with the sons of Job. Therefore do not be anxious for us, but rather
lament yourselves; for look to the defense of your own end; and as knowing and
firmly believing, that our Master has chosen for what is profitable to us, and
has ranked us in the portion of those being saved, whenever young children and
uncorrupted come here from you toward us, rejoice and be glad, and with the
righteous Job sing: The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it seemed good
to the Lord, so also it has come to be; may the name of the Lord be blessed
from now, and unto the age of ages. Amen.
