OF THE SAME FROM THE 39TH FESTAL LETTER
B Concerning the divine scriptures.
But since we have made mention of the heretics as of dead men, and of us as
having the divine scriptures unto salvation, and I fear lest, as Paul wrote
to the Corinthians, a few of the simple be led astray from the simplicity
and the purity by the craftiness of men, and thereafter begin to encounter
other =72= so-called apocrypha, being deceived by the homonymy of the true
books, I beseech you, bear with it, if concerning the things which you know,
I also, making mention of these, write both because of the necessity and the
usefulness of the church. And being about to make mention of these, I shall
use, for the establishment of my own daring, the pattern of the evangelist
Luke, saying also myself: Since some have attempted to set in order for
themselves the so-called apocrypha and to mix these with the God-inspired
scripture, concerning which we have been fully informed, even as they who
from the beginning became eyewitnesses and ministers of the word delivered
to the fathers, it seemed good to me also, having been urged by genuine
brethren and having learned from the beginning, to set forth in order the
books that are canonized and handed down and believed to be divine, in order
that each one, if he has been deceived, may condemn those who led him
astray, and he who has remained pure may rejoice again being reminded. There
are then of the old =73= testament books in number all twenty-two, for so
many, as I have heard, the letters among the Hebrews also are delivered to
be, and in order and in name each is thus: first Genesis; then Exodus; then
Leviticus; and after this Numbers; and then, Deuteronomy; and next after
these is Jesus the son of Nave; and Judges; and after this Ruth; and again
next, of Kingdoms books four; and of these the first and second are numbered
as one book, and the third and fourth likewise as one; and after these, of
Paralipomenon first and second, likewise numbered again as one book; then
Esdras first and second likewise as one; and after these, a book of Psalms;
and next Proverbs; then Ecclesiastes; and Song of Songs; in addition to
these there is also Job; and then, Prophets, the twelve being numbered as
one book, then Isaiah, Jeremiah, and with him Baruch, Lamentations and an
=74= epistle, and after him Ezekiel and Daniel. Up to these the things of
the old testament stand. And the things of the new again it is not to be
hesitated to say. For they are these: Gospels four, according to Matthew,
according to Mark, according to Luke and according to John; then after these
Acts of apostles, and epistles called catholic of the apostles seven, thus:
of James one, and of Peter two, then of John three, and after these of Jude
one; in addition to these of Paul the apostle there are epistles fourteen,
written in order thus: first, to the Romans, then to the Corinthians two,
and after these to the Galatians, and next to the Ephesians, then to the
Philippians, and to the Colossians, and after these to the Thessalonians
two, and that to the Hebrews, and immediately to Timothy two, and to Titus
one, and last that to Philemon one; and again the Apocalypse of John. =75=
These are springs of salvation, so that he who thirsts may be filled with
the oracles in these; in these alone the teaching-school of piety is
evangelized; let no one add to these, nor let anything of these be taken
away. And concerning these the Lord put the Sadducees to shame, saying:
"You err, not knowing the scriptures nor their power", and to the Jews he
exhorted: "Search the scriptures, for they are they that testify concerning
me".

But for the sake of greater accuracy I add also this, writing of necessity,
that there are also other books outside of these, not indeed canonized, but
appointed by the fathers to be read to those who have newly come forward and
wish to be catechized in the word of piety: Wisdom of Solomon and Wisdom of
Sirach and Esther and Judith and Tobias and the so-called Teaching of the
apostles and the Shepherd. And nevertheless, beloved, those being =76=
canonized, and these being read, nowhere is there mention of the apocrypha,
but it is an invention of heretics, who write them when they wish, and
assign and add times to them, in order that, bringing them forth as ancient,
they may have a pretext from this to deceive the simple.
