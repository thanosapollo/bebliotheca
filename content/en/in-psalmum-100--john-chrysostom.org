ON THE HUNDREDTH PSALM.
=55.629=
1. Again, beloved, the blessed David the prophet exhorts us to sing
spiritually; again he prepares us to sing the fair song with one mind;
again, putting forth the spiritual and heavenly strain, he makes us spiritual
through the ode. For making the melody by the Holy Spirit, and being struck as
a living and rational lyre by the Holy Spirit, he teaches us also to sing
spiritually in all things. And he sings, not proposing to delight and sweeten
the ears, but wishing to gladden and benefit the mind. For he does not stop
the things sung at hearing only, but also sends the things said as far as the
soul. For to hearing he delivers them toward instruction and knowledge of the
truth, but into the soul he plants them toward security and confirmation of
salvation. For he sings the song, not putting forth the words with bare
utterances, but correcting the life of those who sing with things profitable.
For this song is not of this life or worldly, but divine and spiritual, and
filled with all seemliness. For the songs of this life, and the world's
melodies that ring in the hearing and deceive the mind, lead far from benefit,
dragging down into desires of destruction.

For being full of shameful words, and being filled with lawless appetites,
toward the luxury of the things sung and toward the slackness of the melody,
they make womanish the courage of the soul, and together slacken the nobility
of the body. But the heavenly ones, having their supply from the Holy Spirit,
bringing the mind into order toward the good order of the words and toward the
seemliness of the thoughts, make the soul also seemly. For the soul, being
moved together with the things spoken, and nodding along with the things
understood, carries its habit there, where also the diction draws the moving
organ of the Spirit's melody. Since then such is the power in the song, and so
great is the energy in the melody, come then let us see what also today's
melody of the prophecy that was read to us contains. What then does he say?
Mercy and judgment will I sing to you, O Lord. Very profitable, beloved, and
saving is this song, making its beginning from mercy and judgment. For the one
shows the abundance of God's loving-kindness, =55.630= the other his great
benefaction that has come to us. For mercy signifies the good pleasure that
has come to men from grace: Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace,
good will among men; but judgment the salvation predetermined from above: For
whom he predestined, these he also called; and whom he called, these he also
justified. For the good God judged to save by mercy and loving-kindness those
being destroyed by sin. This then the prophet, having considered, proclaims,
teaching us through the song to make thanksgiving unceasing, saying: Mercy and
judgment will I sing to you, O Lord.

And it signifies also another thing, both prophesying according to the mind,
and by the Holy Spirit narrating the things to come as present. For beholding
the just judgment of God that has come upon the unbelieving people of the
Jews, who did not receive the one proclaimed by the law and the prophets,
justly punished for unbelief, and justly also deprived of the promise of the
law; and seeing also the people from the nations being saved in mercy, because
of the ready obedience and faith toward the Master, he sounds forth
beforehand, with a sweet and pleasant voice setting before all the goodness of
the


Master; that each, bearing the song on the tongue, and sweetly sounding forth
the melody in the song, may have the grace of the Master's benefaction
ever-remembered. Mercy, he says, and judgment will I sing to you, O Lord. It
is good then that we also sing this song unceasingly, that together we may
become thankful toward God the benefactor, and may correct our own life, doing
all things by mercy and judgment. For do, he says, mercy and judgment in the
midst of the earth; Because I desire mercy, and not sacrifice, says the Lord.
But also, Do mercy, he says, each toward his neighbor, and direct a just
judgment, because in these is my will, says the Lord.

For if we walk thus, and conduct ourselves in these things, we too shall say
with boldness as we sing, Mercy and judgment will I sing to you, O Lord. And
that you may learn, beloved, that these things have this manner, consider the
sequence of the things written, and you will find the truth of the things
said. For he adds saying: I will psalm, and I will understand in a blameless
way; when will you come to me? Do you see how, having corrected his own life,
and having walked =55.631= in every way of righteousness, and having set no
blemish or stain on his own ways, and understanding every uprightness and
judgment, and the lawful way of life witnessed in works, he sings in the song
with a cheerful face, narrating the deeds?

2. And he psalmed in understanding, numbering the works with an uncondemned
heart. And having prepared, according to what is written, the ways of the
Lord, and having made straight the paths of God, he was awaiting the Master's
presence, calling and saying, When will you come to me? all but answering such
things he was saying. I have prepared the vessel of my mind, I have leveled
the hard things of my heart, I have thrown away every stumbling-block, having
left no hindrance of your presence. I have made straight the way of my soul, I
have wiped clean the senses of my heart, I have made the thoughts in me pure.

I am ready for the reception of your presence. When will you come to me? Come
upon me without hindrance, my Master; you will not find in me a way of
wickedness. For I was walking in the innocence of my heart, in the midst of my
house. And that you may know that he was ready, working all righteousness,
hear him announcing the works with a sweet voice: I was walking, he says, in
the innocence of my heart, in the midst of my house. I did not set before my
eyes a lawless thing; I hated those who do transgressions. A crooked heart did
not cleave to me; when the wicked one turned aside from me I did not know. Him
who slandered his neighbor in secret, this one I drove out. With a proud eye
and an insatiable heart, with this one I did not eat. My eyes are upon the
faithful of the earth, that they may sit with me.

Do you see, beloved, of how many and how great things the blessed one was a
worker, and how with much boldness and confidence he went through the
achievements of the deeds? Shall I then interpret the things said, or shall I
leave it to you to know from the words the power of the things written? For
what is clearer than the things said? And what so self-interpreting of the
things now listed? But that we may also examine them part by part, setting
forth the words as they stand, come then taking them up again, let us
remember, and let us see how much benefit they furnish when continually
remembered. I was walking, he says, in the innocence of my heart, in the midst
of my house.

First, a great and fair achievement is innocence, not stopping only at the one
who possesses it, but also being sent on to the neighbors; as Wisdom says:
Child, if you are good, you shall be wise for yourself and for your neighbors;
but if you


turn out evil, you alone shall draw the evils. Fair then is innocence, a
Master's imitation, a divine likeness. For the Lord is innocent and holy. This
innocence is a safeguard of the soul, a cleansing of the mind, a composure of
manners, a quiet of thoughts, a gentleness of face, a meekness of eyes, a
leader of friendship, a mother of love, a companion of faith, a perfection of
righteousness; and, to put it simply, innocence is a root of every virtue, and
a foundation of all goodness. It does not know the substance of enmity, it
does not understand the savagery of wrath, it does not know the working of
wickedness; but it is as a queen escorted by all the virtues. Fair then is
innocence, being the cause of so many goods. From this he begins saying: I was
walking in the innocence of my heart. And he did not simply say, In innocence,
but, In the innocence of my heart, instead of, I did not have some things on
the mouth, and others in the heart; nor did I speak peaceful things with the
mouth, but consider evils in the heart; but such as I was with the mouth, such
also was I considering in the heart. I was walking then in the innocence of my
heart, in the midst of my house.

Here, beloved, house is not this handmade one, composed of stones and woods,
nor the one furnished from formless matter, but the rational temple of the
soul, the one that copies outwardly the inward invisible image, the one formed
by the noetic and rational nature, the one made alive by the divine
inbreathing, the one that bears the soul and is borne by the soul, the one
capable of gifts of the unmoved power. In =55.632= the midst of this I was
walking. And he calls middle the ruling part of the heart, since it lies in
the middle of the whole human body. Evil ways are the reasonings and the
thoughts, as also the Savior says: From the heart come forth evil reasonings.
For in this also is all the movement of human nature. And what he says is of
this sort: If a tumult arises, the heart is moved; if a disturbance happens,
the heart is thrown into tumult; if an uprising of an enemy occurs, the heart
is disturbed; if fear arises, the heart, shaken within by palpitations, also
shakes the neighboring member of the body, and leaving off, and again drawing
itself in, does not allow it to be still, but being moved more frequently
furnishes a greater struggle, and disturbs the whole body, alters also the
color, changes also the form, and remakes the habit, makes the face livid too,
stops the working of the eyes, makes the lips trembling, loosens the tension
of the knees, and having bound the tongue that moves all things, shows it
stammering, and, so to speak, renders the whole man savage and deathlike; and
when war has happened, it hastens, pressing toward the fight, but is in agony,
not remaining still, until it has won the wrestling.

And when this has ceased, and victory has come, being changed at the same
turn, it turns to gladness, it greets quiet and makes the one just now
disturbed to rejoice, and again if it calls back the first condition,
restoring to each member its use; again it gladdens the eyes, composes the
lips, makes healthy the organ of the tongue, gentles the look, and like some
fair charioteer, turning back, it brings the man into the ancient order.

3. But the righteous man is not such, nor receiving disturbance or fear in the
heart. For even if an uprising of enemies happens, he says with confidence: If
a camp should array itself against me, my heart shall not fear; if war should
rise up against me, in this I hope. For this reason he says I was walking in
the innocence of my heart, in the midst of my house; that is, I was looking
about, I was searching the things concerning myself, that no root of
bitterness springing up might trouble and defile the sense of my heart; but
that


innocence, being kept in me, might become for me a procurer of all the
virtues; whence I did not set before my eyes a lawless thing.

For I did not have a purpose of sinning, nor was it set before me to act
lawlessly, working things alien to salvation; but before my eyes was fear of
God, keeping of commandments, works of righteousness, every way of life
according to the law. I did not set before my eyes a lawless thing. For as
many as have a purpose of sinning, to these also lawlessness is set forth, and
they have sin as if leading the way, first being determined according to the
heart, then having stretched the whole eye of the mind toward it, thus they
run into the transgression. But I was alien to these. For there was not before
my eyes a lawless thing, but my whole purpose was to live according to the
law. But also I hated those who do transgressions. Not only I myself did not
wish to act even against the law of God, but I also hated those who transgress
any of the commandments; For I hated those who do transgressions.

And here "I hated those who do transgressions" is to be understood in two
ways. For some are those who transgress the commandments of God, and the
ordinances, and the judgments; and these are transgressors of the law. Others
again transgress the saving confession of the Master himself, and are
transgressors of God. And there is a great difference between the
transgression of both. For the former still have a hope of salvation,
possessing the root of the knowledge of God; but the latter have altogether
fallen from salvation, having denied the very cause of salvation. I therefore,
he says, hated all those who do transgressions. For the beginning of sin is to
stand away from God, as the most wise one says. But we not only do not hate
the transgressors, but even greet them as friends, when we ought not even to
say greeting to such; For there is no peace to the ungodly, says the Lord; and
we eat and drink with them, whose very meeting we ought to flee. =55.633= and
not even to consider as men those who have given themselves over to the
demons, and have committed their own life to lifeless stones and woods. Or
does not the same prophet teach us elsewhere saying: Did I not hate those who
hate you, O Lord, and was I not consumed over your enemies; With a perfect
hatred I hated them, they became enemies to me? Let us then also imitate the
prophet, and let us hate those who do the transgressions, that we may not
through our indifferent communion with them be found fellow-workers of their
transgression. For in every way the communion with them is to be hated,
because having turned away from the light they walk in the darkness (And what
communion has light with darkness?), and because having denied the name of
Christ, they put upon themselves the name of Belial (And what agreement has
Christ with Belial?), and because having fled the entrance here, they rave
around temples of idols, defiling their own life together with the pollutions;
And what agreement has a temple of God with idols?

I hated then those who do transgressions. A crooked heart did not cleave to
me; when the wicked one turned aside from me I did not know.

Again here, beloved, attend to the power of the things said. A crooked heart
did not cleave to me. There was nothing crooked in me, nor twisted, but even
if the things that press be countless, even if the things that happen be
wicked, even if the things that oppose be contrary, A crooked heart did not
cleave to me, no wicked reasoning, no alien thought. Not because I am pressed,
not because I am in straits, not because I see sinners prospering, and the
ungodly growing rich, and all the lawless being directed, am I twisted in
heart, reasoning wicked things, and saying: Why do the ungodly live, and are
strengthened in wealth? Their seed is according to soul, their houses prosper;
Their oxen are fat. I did not reason about these things anywhere, but even if
those prosper, and I am pressed, I know that Tribulation


works patience, and patience proof, and proof hope, and hope does not put to
shame. A crooked heart did not cleave to me. Reasoning thus rightly, and
walking straight, when the Wicked One was going up upon my heart, and I was
not giving him place, in his having no room against me, I did not know when he
arrived, I not receiving him; thus I also did not know when he turned aside.
For wickedness as a shadow running past withdrew, working no harm in me.

4. Let us also then be instructed, if ever a hard or circumstantial thing
happens to any of us, and a reasoning of the Wicked One returns, that he may
not lord it over our ruling part, but may pass by having kept our heart
unharmed, so that we too may say: When the wicked one turned aside from me I
did not know.

Him who slandered his neighbor in secret, this one I drove out. Let us learn a
fair lesson, beloved, not only not to slander, but not even to hear slander.
For slander is a terrible evil, an unstable demon, never making the man
peaceful. For what evil is there not from slander? From it enmities spring up,
through it strifes are constituted, divisions from it take their beginning,
wicked suspicions arising from there furnish the causes of countless evils;
hatred as from a spring gushes from it; and as many evils as one, having
considered, will find, all these he would find pouring from slander, drawing
all into wickedness. Slander knows, having filled the man with wrath, and him
taking murder from it, to make him murderous against the neighbor. It knows
also, when the soul has grown savage, to make it stand away from brotherly
communion, and to show the one who was a friend a little before an enemy
without cause. This, having touched also Mary =55.634= the sister of Moses,
showed her leprous from itself at once, and brought upon her wrath and anger
from the Master.

This also overturns whole houses and rouses cities at peace into war. This
also looses the bonds of fair peace, and tears the bond of great love. This
also, making men stand away from the commandments of God, teaches them to act
lawlessly, and, tearing them from the friendship of God, leads them far from
the truth. This also became, at the first, a cause of the destruction of the
first-formed Adam, and deprived him of the way of life in paradise, and of the
heavenly luxury. For having entered into the mouth of the serpent and worked a
lie against the unlying goodness of God, and having said, Why has God said,
You shall not eat from every tree that is in the garden? it brought a
transgression of the commandment, having twisted the obedient one from the
truth; and having drawn to itself the one who was a companion of the Master,
it at once set him as an enemy through the deceit, and showed him once for all
alien to the goods. Let us therefore refrain from slander, becoming far from
it, that we may not in vain acquire enmity toward our own Master.

Let us cease from slander, that we may not become unjust judges of the truth,
and be found as false witnesses against the law. For he who slanders a
brother, he says, or judges the brother, slanders the law, and judges the law.
But if you judge the law, you are no longer a doer of the law, but a judge.
And there is one judge and lawgiver, who is able to save and to destroy. Do
you see, beloved, how much weight slander has? Let us cease from this, that we
may not bring judgment upon ourselves. Let us drive away from us also those
who wish to slander, that we may not, consenting to others' evils, procure
destruction for ourselves; and let us not hear with pleasure those who
slander, that we may not be filled with diabolic thoughts. For this slander
also is named diabolic, having received the name by nature from the one who
begot it, when one, occupying himself with slander, is a slave to the devil,
performing the works of slander. And he who does not receive such a one frees
himself from this empty


sin, but overturns the one sinning so that he may not bring an unjust
accusation against the neighbor; and he also makes the one being slandered
free of the accusation; and he becomes an umpire of peace and a leader of
friendship, abhorring the purpose of the slanderer. Let us also learn,
abhorring the work, to say: Him who slandered his neighbor in secret, this one
I drove out.

And that you may learn, beloved, how slander is a worker of no truth, attend
also to the very appellation of the name, and making the distinction by the
following saying. For slander is a teacher of accusation; but the, In secret,
shows the falsehood of the matter and its lack of boldness. For if it is true,
it is good to reprove to the face, and with boldness to make peace; but if in
secret, you bring a false accusation, working a wicked suspicion against the
neighbor, not even being able to prove the things put forward by you. It is
good then to drive away the slanderer as a liar and a clothes-thief, that we
may not, disturbing the soul that is at peace, make it warlike toward the
neighbor through slander. Him then who slandered his neighbor in secret, this
one I drove out. With a proud eye and an insatiable heart, with this one I did
not eat. And that pride is hateful even with God himself, hear Scripture
saying: God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble. And again
elsewhere: Pride is hateful before the Lord and men.

And, Why is earth and ashes proud? And in many places you will find pride
abominable and very much hated. Thus then he also says here, With a proud eye
=55.635= and an insatiable heart, with this one I did not eat; as pride being
most harmful and damaging, not only to the one who has taken it up, but
also to the one who for a little while plays the part of it. And he also
joined the insatiable in heart to the proud, as the purpose of both being one,
both of the one who is lifted up against the neighbor and of the one who
likewise is insatiable against the neighbor.

5. It is good then, beloved, that we put off pride, and take up humility, that
we may not hear, He who exalts himself shall be humbled, but may hear, He who
humbles himself shall be exalted; and to be content also with measured
self-sufficiency, and not by the insatiability of the heart to turn aside from
righteousness, that we may not hear: Because of the multitude of your
injustice your hinder parts were uncovered, for your heels to be made a
spectacle. Let us then flee also the way of life of the proud and of those
insatiable in heart, that we may not by the custom of communion with them
become imitators of their wickedness. Let us then practice doing and saying:
With a proud eye and an insatiable heart, with this one I did not eat. And
these things are said as from the person of the righteous man; but again as
from the person of God the Holy Spirit, making the answer, says: My eyes are
upon the faithful of the earth, that they may sit with me. For truly the eyes
of God are upon the faithful, attending to them, that they may not become
outside the truth. And if you wish to learn that the thing said is true,
consider the blessed apostles, and see these being faithful of the earth, and
that through their faith the whole inhabited world has been filled with the
preaching of salvation. For into all the earth their sound went out, and into
the ends of the inhabited world their words. For indeed the whole inhabited
world, having partaken of their faith, has been filled, having the unemptied
wealth of piety. These faithful then sit with the Master, hearing: But you the
twelve shall sit upon twelve thrones judging the twelve tribes of


Israel. My eyes are upon the faithful of the earth, that they may sit with me.

O the wonder! Those of clay sit, and the bodiless stand by. But I do not set
at naught the angels of God; far be it; but I proclaim the loving-kindness of
God, through which he honored human nature. And if the holy apostles shall sit
becoming judges of the unbelievers, it is necessary also that those who have
had the faith of the apostles and have believed likewise with them, themselves
also sit with the apostles, being about to be judges of those who did not
receive the faith, nor confess the saving word. For perhaps, that I may so
speak, there will be twelve faithful nations, =55.636= those catechized by the
apostles, which will judge together with the apostles the twelve tribes of
Israel, who were persuaded neither by the apostolic preaching, nor believed
the Master's will, so that they also, having been gathered by the faith, may
hear: My eyes are upon the faithful of the earth, that they may sit with me.
Then let us see the things that follow. What does he say? Walking in a
blameless way, this one ministered to me. He who does pride did not dwell in
the midst of my house; he who speaks unjust things did not prosper before my
eyes. Toward the boasting of the righteous man God likewise answers in return,
and sets forth that such must those be who have gone through in the faith. He
says, Walking in a blameless way, this one ministered to me.

For I choose such ministers, those who walk in a blameless way, and neither
turning aside to the right, nor to the left, nor stumbling in the things
concerning the way of righteousness and of truth. For blessed are the
blameless in the way, who walk in the law of the Lord. Toward then the said
voice by the prophet, the, I will psalm, and I will understand in a blameless
way, he says here: Walking in a blameless way, this one ministered to me. And
again, as he says, I was walking in the innocence of my heart, in the midst of
my house; and, With a proud eye and an insatiable heart, with this one I did
not eat; he answers in return saying: He who does pride did not dwell in the
midst of my house, but also, He who speaks, he says, unjust things did not
prosper before my eyes. For as there he says, Him who slandered his neighbor
in secret, this one I drove out; so here, He who speaks unjust things did not
prosper before my eyes. Then see what he says: In the mornings I slew all the
sinners of the earth, to destroy from the city of the Lord all those who work
lawlessness.

Here then he hints at the things concerning the coming age. For since the
present age is night, but the coming one day, as the most wise Paul says, The
night has advanced; but the day has drawn near; he names morning the rising of
the everlasting light, of which the righteous are about to enjoy. Into this
morning then he will slay the sinners, sending them into the eternal fire. For
this is also the death of the sinners, as also the Savior himself says: But
fear rather him who is able to kill both soul and body in gehenna. In the
mornings then I slew all the sinners of the earth, to destroy from the city of
the Lord all, that is, from the Jerusalem above, those who work lawlessness.

For in that only the righteous rest, enjoying the eternal kingdom, of which
may it be that both we and you all enjoy, having been well-pleasing to him,
having lived holily in Christ Jesus our Lord; to whom be the glory and the
power, unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
