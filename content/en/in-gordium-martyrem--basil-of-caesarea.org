Homily 18.
On Gordius the Martyr.
It is a law of nature for the bees not to leave the hives before their king
leads the flight. Since therefore I have now seen the people of the Lord going
out for the first time upon the heavenly flowers, the martyrs, I seek the
leader. Who is the one who has moved this great swarm? Who is the one who has
transferred the winter gloom into spring brightness? For now indeed for the
first time the people, poured forth from the city as from certain hives, have
occupied all together this adornment before the city, this august and
all-beautiful stadium of the martyrs. Since therefore the wonder of the martyr
has also raised us and led us, making us forget the weakness, come then, let
us too with the voice according to our power, as with some flower of the man's
works, buzz around, doing things both holy and at the same time pleasing to
those present. For when a righteous man is praised, peoples will rejoice, the
wise Solomon said to us just now.

And yet I was at a loss with myself as to what in the world the riddle of the
proverb-writer means. Is it that, when some rhetor or clever speech-writer has
composed a speech toward the astonishment of the hearers, with some elegant
sound striking the ears, the peoples rejoice, accepting the invention of the
thoughts, and the arrangement, and the pompous quality of the diction =31.492=
and its being hammered toward harmony? Or this he would never have said, he
who nowhere used such a form of speeches; nor would he have urged us to parade
in panegyric in the encomia of the blessed, he who everywhere preferred the
pedestrian quality of the diction and the unadorned quality of the voice.

What then is it that he says? That peoples rejoice with a spiritual joy at the
mere remembrance of the things accomplished by the righteous, being led into
zeal and imitation of the good things from which they hear. For the history of
men who have lived well produces as it were some light for those being saved
toward the way of life. Because at the same time we have heard the Spirit
narrating the life of Moses, and immediately zeal of the man's virtue entered
us, and the gentleness of his manner appeared to each both enviable and
blessed. For with the other men encomia are composed from the increase of the
words; but for the righteous the truth of the things done by them is enough to
show the surpassing quality of their virtue. So that whenever we narrate the
lives of those who have distinguished themselves in piety, we first glorify
the Master through the servants; and we eulogize the righteous through the
testimony of the things we know, and we gladden the peoples through the
hearing of the good things. For Joseph's life is an exhortation toward
temperance, and the narratives of Samson a summons toward courage. The divine
school, then, does not know a law of encomia, but reckons the testimony of the
things done in place of encomia, as both sufficient for the saints toward
praise, and being enough for those who have set out toward virtue unto
benefit. For the law of encomia is to search out fatherland, and to inquire
into race, and to narrate upbringing; but our law, having silenced the
speeches of the neighbors, fills the testimony from each one's own things. For
what am I more august because of this, if the city once, having borne hard and
great contests, set up splendid trophies against the enemies? And what if it
is well situated, so as to be suitable both toward winter and summer? And if
the same is both productive of men and sufficient to feed cattle, what profit
to me from these things? But it even prevails over those under the sun with
herds of horses. What then can these things do for us


to make us better in human virtue? Shall we perhaps, narrating also the peaks
of the neighboring mountain, how they are above the clouds and have risen
through much of the air, deceive ourselves as if we were filling the praise
for the men through these things?

It is most ridiculous of all that, when the whole world has been despised by
the righteous, we fill their encomia from a few of the things they have
dishonored. The memory, then, is enough toward perpetual benefit. For it is
not they =31.493= who have need of an addition toward good repute, but for us
who are in the life the memory is necessary because of the imitation. For just
as lighting follows fire of itself, and being fragrant follows perfume; so
also the beneficial necessarily follows the good deeds. And yet even this is
no small thing, to hit accurately upon the truth of those times.

For some dim rumor has been handed down to us, preserving the man's deeds of
valor upon the contests. And somehow our case seems to resemble that of the
painters. For they too, whenever they copy images from images, fall short, as
is likely, of the originals by a great deal; and we, having been left behind
the very sight of the deeds, are in no small danger of diminishing the truth.
But since the day has come bearing remembrance of a martyr who contended
conspicuously in the testimonies for Christ, let us say as much as we know.
This man was born from this city, whence we love him the more, because the
adornment is our own. For just as the fruitful among plants set the fruits
they have reared beside their own earth; so also this man, having sprung up
from the loins of ours, and having risen to the furthest height of glory,
granted to the one who bore and reared him the enjoyment of the fruits of his
own piety. For the fruits from abroad are also fair, whenever they are both
sweet and nourishing; yet much sweeter than the imported are the native and
local, granting us besides the enjoyment still some embellishment through the
kinship. And he had been enrolled in a distinguished military service, so as
to be entrusted with the command of a hundred soldiers, and by strength of
body and courage of soul he was eminent in the military rolls. But when the
tyrant of that time stretched the bitterness and savagery of his soul as far
as the war of the Church, and raised a god-fighting hand against piety, and
there were proclamations everywhere, and edicts were spread through every
marketplace and every notable place, that Christ not be worshiped, or that
death be the penalty for those who worship; and there was a command that all
bow down to idols, and consider stones and wood shaped by the impress of art
as gods, or that those who did not obey suffer irreparable things; and there
was confusion and tumult through the whole city, and plunderings against those
who were pious; money was seized, the bodies of the Christ-lovers were torn
with blows, women were dragged through the middle of the city; there was no
pitiable youth, no venerable old age; =31.496= but those who did no wrong
underwent the things of criminals, the prisons were cramped, those who had
prospered were empty of houses, and the wildernesses were full of those who
had been banished; and the charge for those suffering these things was piety,
and a father handed over a child, and a son informed against a father,
brothers raged against one another, and slaves rose up against their own
masters; and some terrible night had seized the life, all having failed to
recognize one another from the devil's madness; houses of prayer were thrown
down by unholy hands, and altars were overturned, and there was no offering
nor incense, no place of sacrifice, but a terrible gloom, like some cloud,
held all things; the ministers of the divine had been driven out, and every
company of piety was terrified, and demons danced, defiling all things with
the smell of fat and with blood; then this noble man, anticipating the
compulsion from the courts, having thrown off the belt, was beyond the
borders, having despised power, having despised glory, wealth of every kind,
kinship, friends,


household servants, enjoyment of life, all things that are much sought after
by men, he ran away to the deepest wildernesses, inaccessible to men, having
judged the life with the beasts more gentle than communion with those who
worship idols, after the zealot Elijah; who, when he saw the idolatry of Sidon
prevailing, ran up to the mountain of Horeb, and lived in the cave, seeking
God, until he saw the one longed for, as it is possible for a man to see God.

Such then was Gordius also, having fled civic tumults, marketplace crowd, the
conceits of rulers, the courts, the sycophants, those who sell, those who buy,
those who swear, those who lie, shameful speech, jesting, the other things
which, like trailing boats, the populous cities drag after them. Keeping the
ears pure, keeping the eyes pure; and before all things having been purified
in heart, that he might be able to see God, and become blessed; he saw through
revelations, he was taught the mysteries, not from men, nor through men, but
having the great teacher, the Spirit of truth. Whence, having come to a
conception of the life, how unprofitable, how vain, how weaker than every
dream and shadow, he was stirred more vehemently toward the desire of the
upward calling. And like an athlete, perceiving himself sufficiently trained
and anointed for the contest, by fasts, by vigils, by prayers, by the
continual and unceasing meditation of the oracles of the Spirit, having
watched for this day, on which the whole city in a body, keeping festival for
a war-loving =31.497= demon, had occupied the theater, watching a horse
contest. When therefore the whole people had been gathered above, no Jew was
absent, no Greek; and a not small multitude of Christians was mixed in with
them, those living without guard, and sitting with the council of vanity, and
not turning aside from gatherings of those who work evil, who were then also
present as spectators of the speed of the horses and of the skill of the
charioteers; but even masters had let slaves go, and children ran from the
schools toward the spectacle, and there was present also as much of women as
is common and obscure, and the stadium was full, and all were already intent
upon the spectacle of the horses' rivalry; then indeed that noble man and
great in soul, great in resolve, having come down from above from the
mountains upon the theater, was not struck with fear at the people, did not
calculate to how many hostile hands he was handing himself over; but with an
untrembling heart and a proud resolve, having run past those sitting around
the stadium as if they were continuous rocks or many trees, he stood in the
midst, confirming the word, that "The righteous is confident as a lion."

And so unstartled was he in soul that, having come into the conspicuous part
of the theater, with unturned boldness he cried out that voice, of which until
now there are some who heard it: "I was found by those who did not seek me; I
became manifest to those who did not ask for me"; making clear through these
things that he was not brought to the dangers by compulsion, but of his own
will gave himself to the contest; having imitated the Master, who in the
darkness of the night, being unknown to the Jews, made himself known. At once
therefore the theater turned at the paradox of the sight, a man savage in
appearance, through the long dwelling in the mountains having a squalid head,
deep of beard, filthy in clothing, all his body wasted, carrying a staff, and
wearing a wallet; and upon all these there sat some grace, shining around him
from the unseen. When therefore it was known who he was, at once a mixed shout
was raised by all; those of the household of the faith clapping from exceeding
joy, and those warring against the truth urging the judge toward the murder
against him, and prejudging him to death. Everything therefore was filled with
shouting and tumult; horses were disregarded, and charioteers were
disregarded; and the display of the chariots was a vain noise. No one's


eye had leisure to look at anything else than Gordius, nor did any ear endure
to hear anything else than =31.500= his words. And some indistinct murmur,
like a breeze, going through the whole theater, sounded over the hippodrome.
But when through the heralds silence was signaled to the people, the flutes
were put to sleep, and the many-toned instruments were silenced; Gordius was
heard, Gordius was watched. And at once he was snatched up to the ruler
sitting there somewhere, and arranging the contest. For a while therefore with
a meek and gentle voice he inquired who and from where he was. And when he had
said the fatherland, the race, the form of the rank in which he had been, the
cause of the flight, the return, that I am here, to display in deed both the
contempt of your commands, and the faith toward God, in whom I have hoped; for
I have heard, he says, that you excel many in savagery; so I have chosen this
time as suitable for me toward the fulfillment of my desire. Having kindled
the ruler's wrath as fire with these words, he raised against himself the
whole madness of the man. And Call, he says, the executioners. And where are
the leaden weights? and where the scourges? Let him be stretched on the wheel,
let him be racked on the wood, let the instruments of punishment be brought;
let the beasts, the fire, the sword, the cross, the pit be made ready.

But what does the wretch gain, he says, dying only once? What then do I lose,
Gordius said, having anticipated him, not being able to die many times for
Christ! And he, in addition to the savagery from nature, was still harsher
looking toward the man's rank, thinking the youthful elevation of mind a
dishonor of his own. And the more he saw the heart unstartled, the more
greatly he grew savage, and was more contentious to conquer the man's
persistence by the invention of the pains. And he did these things. But the
other, looking toward God, was charming his own soul from the sacred Psalms,
saying: "The Lord is my helper, and I will not fear what man will do to me";
and this: "I will not fear evils, because you are with me"; and as many things
akin to these, summoning toward courage, he had been taught from the divine
oracles; he who was so far from yielding to the things threatened and
crouching, that he even called the terrible things upon himself. Why do you
delay? he says; why do you stand? Let the body be torn, let the limbs be
racked; let it be tortured as you wish. Do not begrudge me the blessed hope.
For by as much as you stretch the terrible things, by so much greater a
recompense you procure for me. These are exchanges for us with the Master.
=31.501= In place of the weals that rise on the body, a luminous garment will
bloom upon us in the resurrection; in place of dishonor, crowns; in place of
prison, paradise; in place of condemnation with the criminals, the life with
angels. Sow many things in me that I may reap many times more. And when they
were not able to take him with the fearful things, and the matter was
impossible, having changed they began to flatter. For such is the devil's
method: he strikes the coward with terror, he softens the courageous. Such
also were the devices of the villain then. For when he saw him not yielding to
the threats, he tried to get around him with deceits and baits. And he
promised gifts, and he gave some, and he pledged that others would come from
the emperor, distinction in the army, sources of money, everything whatever he
might wish. And when he failed of the attempt (for the blessed one, having
heard the promises, laughed at his folly, if he thought he would give anything
equal in worth to the kingdom of the heavens), then indeed he was unbearable
in wrath, and he bared a sword, and set the executioner beside him, and with
hand and tongue murdering he condemned the blessed one to death. And the whole
theater moved over to this place; and as much as was remaining of the
inhabitants, poured out before the wall, saw that great and contested
spectacle, marvelous both to angels and to all creation, painful to the devil,
and fearful to demons. The city was emptied of


its inhabitants, as of some stream, the multitude having flowed over all at
once to this place. No woman accepted being left behind from the spectacle, no
man obscure or notable.

The guards left the watches of the houses; the workshops of the merchants were
unlocked; and the wares were scattered through the marketplace; and one watch
and safety of all, that all alike went out, and not even a criminal remained
in the city. Slaves left the service of masters; and as much as was foreign of
the people, and as much as was local, was here present toward the man. Then
even a virgin daring the sight of males, and an old man and a sick man forcing
the weakness, were outside the wall. And already, as the blessed one was
rushing toward the life through death, those close to him, standing around,
embraced him, wailing and kissing him for the last time; and shedding a hot
tear over him, they begged him not to give himself to the fire, not to consume
his youth along with it, not to leave this sweet sun. And others tried to
deceive him with persuasive counsels: By a word only say the denial, but in
soul have the faith as you wish. For God does not attend at all to the tongue,
but to the mind of the one speaking. For thus it will be possible for you both
to soothe the judge and to propitiate God. But he was unbending, and untamed,
and unwounded by every assault of trials. You would liken the unshaken quality
of his mind to the house of the prudent man, which neither unbearable forces
of winds, nor violent water breaking down from clouds, nor torrents bursting
around, because of the safety from the rock, are able to shake. Such was the
man, preserving unshaken the firmness of the faith toward Christ. Seeing with
spiritual eyes the devil running around, and moving one toward a tear, and
cooperating with another toward persuasiveness; to those weeping he said the
word of the Lord: "Do not weep over me; but weep over the god-fighters, who
dare such things against those who are pious, who through this flame which
they kindle against us are treasuring up for themselves the fire of Gehenna.
Cease weeping, and crushing my heart. For I am ready not only to die once for
the name of the Lord Jesus, but even ten thousand times, if it were possible."
And to those counseling the denial through the tongue he answered only this
much, that the tongue created by Christ does not endure to utter anything
against the one who created it. For with the heart we believe unto
righteousness, and with the mouth we confess unto salvation. For is the
military rank despairing of salvation? For is there no pious centurion? I
remember the first centurion, who, standing by the cross of Christ, having
recognized the power from the wonders, while the daring of the Jews was still
hot, did not fear their wrath, nor indeed was he held back from announcing the
truth, but he confessed, and did not deny, that truly he was the Son of God. I
know also another centurion, who while still in the flesh recognized the Lord,
that he is God and king of the powers, and by a command alone through the
ministering spirits is able to send forth helps to those in need; whose faith
also the Lord declared to be greater than all Israel. And Cornelius, being a
centurion, was he not counted worthy of the vision of the angel, and at last
through Peter obtained salvation? For his alms and his prayers were heard
before God. I wish to be a disciple of those men.

How then shall I deny my God =31.505= whom I have worshiped from childhood?
Will not heaven shudder from above? Will not the stars grow dark over me, and
will the earth receive me at all? Do not be deceived; God is not mocked. From
our own mouth he judges us, from the words he justifies, and from the words he
condemns. Have you not


read the fearful threat of the Lord? that "Whoever denies me before men, I
also will deny him before my Father who is in the heavens"? And in place of
what do you counsel me to be clever with these things? That I may accomplish
something for myself from such a device? that I may gain a few days? But I
shall lose a whole age. That I may flee the toilsome things of the flesh? But
I shall not see the good things of the righteous. It is clear madness to
perish with a device, with villainy and deceit to procure for oneself the
eternal punishment. But I counsel you also. If you think badly, learn piety
anew; but if you are playing a part for the time, "Putting away the lie, speak
truth." Say: that the Lord Jesus Christ is to the glory of God the Father. For
every tongue will send forth this voice, when "At the name of Jesus every knee
shall bow, of those in heaven, and of those on earth, and of those under the
earth." All men are mortal, but martyrs are few. Let us not wait to become
dead, but let us pass from life into life. Why do you wait for the spontaneous
death? It is fruitless, profitless, common to beasts and men. For the one who
has come into life through generation, either time has consumed, or disease
has dissolved, or more violent necessities of accidents have destroyed. Since
therefore it is necessary by all means to die, let us procure life for
ourselves through death. Make the constrained thing voluntary. Do not spare a
life whose deprivation is necessary. And yet even if earthly things had like
duration, it would have been necessary to be eager to exchange them for the
heavenly; but if they are both short-lived, and in worth falling short by so
much, it is a terrible madness by eagerness about these things to fall from
the blessednesses stored up in the hopes. Having said these things, and having
traced the type of the cross around himself, he went toward the blow, having
turned nothing of his color, nor having altered anything of the brightness of
his face. For he was disposed not as one about to meet an executioner, but as
one about to commit himself to the hands of angels who, having received him at
once newly slain, would transfer him toward the blessed life as they did
Lazarus. Who will narrate the shout of that people? What thunder has sent
forth so great a sound from clouds to the earth, as was then given up from
below toward heaven? This is the stadium =31.508= of that crowned one. This
day saw that marvelous spectacle, which time has not dimmed, nor has custom
dissolved; the excess of things coming after has not conquered it. For just as
always seeing the sun we always marvel; so also we have the memory of that man
always fresh. For the righteous shall be in everlasting remembrance, both
among those on earth, as long as the earth is, and in the heavens, and before
the righteous judge; to whom be the glory and the power unto the ages of the
ages. Amen.
