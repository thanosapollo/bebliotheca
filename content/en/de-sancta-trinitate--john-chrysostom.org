On the Holy and Consubstantial Trinity.

=48.1087=

αʹ. Those who sharpen their tongues against the holy and consubstantial
Trinity, and rage, and attempt to pull down the dignity of the only-begotten
Son and of the Holy Spirit, are convicted by the Holy Spirit himself who spoke
through the holy prophets, and by the Word who proclaimed his own incarnate
presence, which came to pass for us from a holy Virgin, both ineffable and
immutable, through his own worthy ones; and they shall be convicted most
perfectly on the day of his righteous judgment, when he comes again from
heaven to be glorified in his saints, and to be marveled at among those who
have kept his faith unchangeable. They have dared, beloved, children of the
Arians, and held by the madness of the Macedonians, to speak of a great God
and a small, and of a created God, bringing Hellenism in among us. For it
belongs to those to worship a creature, and to magnify one of their gods, and
to diminish another.

For if anything is lacking either in the Son or in the Spirit, he is not God.
For Paul, blaming them in the letter to the Romans, says: "They worshipped and
served the creature rather than the Creator, who is God over all, blessed."
And if they worship a creature, saying that the Son and the Spirit are a
creature, they are accursed according to Moses who says: "Accursed is everyone
who worships a creature and a thing made." But we do not worship a creature,
but an uncreated Trinity, unchangeable and immutable and consubstantial,
having nothing imported, or slavish, or come into being by progress, as
formerly not existing, but later supervening. For look at the testimonies of
the holy and consubstantial Trinity, and worship this rightly, that you may
not perish.

And God said, he says: "Let us make man according to our image and according
to likeness." Tell me then, with whom does God take counsel? With the angels,
he says. But the angels do not have the image of God; but he speaks to the
co-creating Son and the Holy Spirit. And that you may be persuaded that this
is true, hear the psalmist saying: "By the word of the Lord the heavens were
established, and by the spirit of his mouth all their power." And you are not
ignorant that the Word is the Son. For John the theologian says: "And the Word
was God"; and concerning the Spirit, "God is Spirit." God the Father therefore
says to God the Son, and to God the Holy Spirit: "Let us make man according to
our image." And he did not say, Mine and yours, nor Mine and thine. And
Scripture, signifying that there is one image of the Holy Trinity, says: "And
God =48.1088= made man, according to the image of God he made him."

As also Paul cries concerning the Son: "Who being the radiance of the glory,
and the impress of the hypostasis." And again elsewhere Scripture: "Come ye,
and going down let us confuse their tongues." By saying, "Come ye," he calls
those of equal honor. For if he had commanded angels, he would not have said,
"Come ye," but imperatively, "Go." And look at the Father's voice, calling Son
and Spirit. For if this had been said to one only, he would have said, "Come,
and going down let us confuse their tongues"; but to say, "Come ye," is the
voice of one toward two of equal honor. And hear also blessed Job clearly
showing the inseparable Trinity; for he says: "The Lord lives, who has thus
judged me, and the Almighty, who has made my soul bitter, and the divine
spirit that remains in my nostrils." Having said Lord and Almighty and divine
Spirit, the consubstantial Trinity


he proclaimed. Learn also the hymn of the Seraphim, crying thrice: "Holy,
holy, holy." For they do not say only once, "Holy," nor twice, "Holy, holy,"
nor a fourth time, nor do they say, "Holies, holies, holies"; lest the many
names lead us out into polytheism, but one only, and thrice: "Holy, holy,
holy." For through these they show one and equal doxology of Father and Son
and Holy Spirit. And hear also the prophet saying again: "The Spirit of the
Lord spoke in me, and his word in my mouth." It has already been shown that
the Word was God; for having said Spirit of the Lord and Word and Lord he
proclaims an undivided Trinity. And see also the three children in the furnace
calling all creatures and things made to the doxology of God, sun, moon,
stars, heavens, angels, powers, men, spirits, every creation visible and
invisible and intelligible; and nowhere did they number Son or Holy Spirit
among the things made. But you will surely say: They did not know Spirit or
Son; for they were Jews; but you are immediately convicted from Scripture
itself; "For the fourth," it says, "was as the likeness of a Son of God."

And concerning the Holy Spirit it says: "God raised up by the Holy Spirit a
younger lad, whose name was Daniel." And in many other places the Holy Spirit
has been proclaimed beforehand in the Old. But they knew Father and Son and
Holy Spirit as being of the one Godhead and power; and they knew to call the
servants to the doxology of the Godhead. And that you may know that nothing
created or imported is added in the Trinity, Paul, having set the Father
first, in another =48.1089= place sets the Holy Spirit first; for he says
thus: "One God the Father, from whom are all things, and we unto him; and one
Lord Jesus, through whom are all things, and we through him; and one Holy
Spirit." And conversely: "There are distributions of gifts, but the same
Spirit; and there are distributions of ministries, but the same Lord; and
there are distributions of operations, but the same God, who works all things
in all." And all these things one and the same Spirit works, distributing to
each privately as he wills, and not as he is commanded. And when the Son is
baptized the Father bears witness from heaven, and the Holy Spirit in the form
of a dove comes down upon the one of equal honor. Through all these we are
taught to understand one kingdom of the undivided Trinity. And God the Word
himself, who for us was truly made flesh, being about to go up whence he was
not separated, says to his holy apostles: "Going, make disciples of all the
nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the
Holy Spirit." We have been sealed, but there is one seal of the Holy Trinity,
one baptism. How then do you dare to alienate the Son, or the Holy Spirit,
from the paternal essence? For if they were alien, they would not have been
numbered with the Father in the baptism. For on what account, according to
your word, O God-fighter, if the Son is created, or the Spirit, is he included
together with the Father? For if the name of the Father alone could save the
one being baptized, why are the creatures included together with him, as
though the Father alone were unable to save? God forbid! If then, fleeing the
consubstantial things, you proclaim weakness of the Father, you will be
compelled by the truth to confess with us an uncreated and consubstantial and
almighty Trinity.

But you will say to me concerning the Father, that it is written, "You the
only true God." But also "the Word was God"; and how then is he equal to the
Father? for to say concerning the Father, "You the only true God," showed the
Son not a natural, but an adopted God. What then, you who war against your own
salvation? since it is written, as John says concerning the Father, that "God
is light," and concerning the Son, that "He was the true light," do you on
this account dare to say the Father is lesser, because "the true light" is not
added, but concerning the Son only, "He was true light"? As then you do not
dare to diminish the Father because "true" is not added; so hearing concerning
the Father, "You the only true God," and concerning the Son, "God"


only, do not dare to diminish the Son, the names being set over against one
another, for the Father "true God," for the Son "God"; and conversely "He was
the true light" for the Son, and for the Father "light" only. As then it is
not to be dared to say concerning the Father that he is not true light, and
concerning the Son, true God; for he himself said, "I am the truth"; so also
concerning the Holy Spirit, that he is Spirit of the truth. Through these
testimonies learn to be pious toward an undivided Trinity: "All things
whatsoever the Father has are mine," said the only-begotten God, who is in the
bosom of the Father. Moses says: "He who is has sent me." He who is is the
Father, he who is is the Son, the one who is toward the one who is, begotten
without beginning and without time. The Father has all things, truly life, and
immortality, and light, and almighty, and God, and Lord; which the Son also
has. For "I am," he says, "the truth and the life"; and, "He was the true
light"; and, "The Word was God"; and Thomas the apostle says: "My Lord and my
God"; as also the psalmist says: "With my hands I sought God, and I was not
deceived." Thomas handled the Word in unbelief, and was not deceived, having
confessed him Lord and God in faith.

Yes, he says; I call the Son of God God, yet not without beginning, but
created; therefore also a servant. And how, "Who being in the form of God, did
not count it robbery to be equal with God, but emptied himself, taking the
form of a servant"? If then he is a servant, for every creature is a servant,
how, being a servant, did he take the form of a servant? and how did he
=48.1090= empty himself, if he was not perfect God? For what he did not have
he received, remaining what he was; he did not become what he was, but being
in the form of God, through love of men he unchangeably took the form of a
servant. And if you do not wish to confess him without beginning, was there
then a time when the Son was not? Yes, he says. See then, I speak according to
your word: there was a time when the Father was not; for he who has no son is
not called father. And the Father had a beginning of being father; for from
the son a father is known. Was there then also for the Father a time when he
himself too was son of another father? Alas for such blasphemy! for thinking
to worship the Father perfectly, you are impious toward him. If then the Son
himself made the times and seasons, for all things came to be through him, how
do you dare to say that there was a time when the Son was not? Therefore
according to your word, time is greater than God the Word; for what is first
is of necessity also greater.

βʹ. And do you wish to know that the Son is co-eternal with the Father? Isaiah
says: "I am God first, and I am unto the things after these; before me another
God did not come to be, and after me there shall not be." If then you say the
Son is later than the Father, how does the Father say that "After me there
shall not be another God"? and if you take this of the Son, how does he say:
"Before me another God did not come to be"? See then through these that the
Son is co-eternal with the Father. And hear the testimonies concerning the
Son, and flee from speaking ill of him: "The Lord rained fire from the Lord,
and fire upon Sodom." He calls the Father Lord, the Son Lord, the Holy Spirit
Lord; as he says, "And the Lord is the Spirit"; and he did not say: The lesser
Lord from the great Lord. And Moses says, "And let all the angels of God
worship him"; and an angel does not worship a creature. And that you may learn
that this has been said concerning the Son, hear Paul saying: "And when he
brings the firstborn into the world, he says: And let all the angels of God
worship him." And David also cried: "The Lord said to my Lord, Sit at my right
hand, until I make your enemies a footstool of your feet." And he did not say,
Lord of my Lord; but he said, Lord to my Lord; "Behold," says Isaiah, "the
virgin shall have in the womb, and shall bear a son, and they shall call his
name Emmanuel"; which is interpreted, "God with us." Yes, I call him Son, yet
subordinate and lesser than the Father.


Isaiah convicts you again, saying that "A child was born to us, a son also was
given to us; and his name is called Angel of great counsel, wonderful
counsellor, mighty God, potentate, prince of peace, father of the age to
come." The prophet proclaims him potentate, mighty God; and you say he is
subordinate? that one calls him angel of great counsel; and you say small? And
look also at Jeremiah crying concerning him: "This is our God; another shall
not be reckoned beside him." Does he say this concerning the Father, or
concerning the Son? Concerning the Father, he says. Was the Father made flesh,
or the Son? The Son. Therefore according to your word the Father was made
flesh. But now I see, according to the prophet's word, that it is the Son who
also consorted with men. Yes, the Lord was made flesh, but the Father remained
without flesh. Be convicted then, O foolish one, that he speaks concerning the
Son, through what follows: "This is our God; another shall not be reckoned
beside him; he found out every way of knowledge, and gave it to Jacob his
servant, and to Israel his beloved; afterward he was seen upon the earth, and
consorted with men." Do you see him also as lawgiver? For if he himself found
out every way of knowledge, gave it to Jacob his servant, and to Israel his
beloved, he himself is the one who also appeared to Moses and gave the law.
And whence is this clear? Hear the prophet saying: "I will make a new covenant
with you, not according to the covenant which I made with your fathers, when I
took hold of their hand, to lead them out of the land of E=48.1091=gypt;
giving my laws in their hearts, and upon their minds I will write them; and
they shall be to me a people, and I will be to them God." Do you see that he
himself is the lawgiver of Old and New? For that he himself legislates the
New, not even you will doubt. "And you, Bethlehem house of Ephratha, are you
not least among the thousands of Judah; for from you shall come forth to me a
ruler; and his goings forth are from the beginning, from days; and he shall
shepherd my people Israel." You see one people of Father and Son. The Son
says: "I will be to them God, and they to me a people." The Father says
concerning Christ: "And he shall shepherd my people Israel."

Learn one kingdom of the Father and Son. And as many humble things as you hear
him saying after these, after he was made flesh by his own good pleasure, and
humbled himself for the salvation of men, understand these in a manner
befitting God; for the letter kills, but the spirit gives life. For if to the
apostles who had seen his wonders, and had healed every disease in his name,
he said, "I had many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now"; what
toward the Jew who is much thicker? For if, that he might say, "Before Abraham
came to be, I am," they took up stones to stone him, how, if he had said
something of the high things to them, would they have endured even to hear at
all? So also in the Old one might find many things said concerning the Father
toward the thickness of those who heard; as, "Seven eyes of the Lord looking
upon the world"; and, "In the shelter of your wings you will shelter me"; and,
"Awake; why do you sleep, Lord?"; and, "If you do not turn, he will polish his
sword; he stretched his bow, and prepared it; his arrows he has worked for
those who are burning." And how? For the Divine is neither winged, nor held by
sleep, nor is there a sword about God, or a bow, or arrows; for the Divine is
bodiless. But the prophet threatens these things, wishing to lay hold of the
thickness of the hearers; so also God the Word, made flesh for us, imitating
his own Father, utters humble things, looking toward our salvation. For since
they thought him opposed to God, saying that "This man is not from God"; and,
"For a good work we do not stone you, but for blasphemy, because you, being a
man, make yourself God"; and for this they sought to stone him, because he not
only loosed the sabbath, but because he said God was his own Father, making
himself equal to God, and because they could not contain the high things and
those


worthy of his divinity, he begins to say humble things, showing through all
that he is not opposed to God. "If you loved me, you would have rejoiced, that
I go to the Father who sent me, because my Father is greater than I." For when
he made himself equal to the Father, they attempted to stone him. And if these
things are not so, you will be compelled to praise the Jews, that they justly
crucified him, as opposed to God; for he who makes himself equal to God and
Father, not being so, is opposed to God. Paul then lies thereafter, saying:
"He did not count it robbery to be equal with God." What then is the Savior,
according as he was not made flesh? So also, "I go to my Father and your
Father, and my God and your God"; otherwise his according to nature, and
otherwise ours according to grace and adoption; otherwise as through his own
good pleasure, and through the created assumption, and otherwise ours as of
created things. And if you doubt in these, he could have said: Our Father and
yours. For what reason did he distinguish himself, except teaching us this
thought befitting God?

You see, he says, how he needs the Father's help, saying: "Now my soul is
troubled. And what shall I say? Father, save me from this hour." You read
badly; he said, "Now my soul is troubled"; and having distinguished here, he
added, "What shall I say? Father, save me from this hour; but for this I have
come to this hour." For by saying, "For this I came to this hour," he showed
that he was not shrinking from the saving death. For "with desire I have
desired to eat this passover with you," he says. "If it is pos=48.1092=sible,
let this cup pass from me; yet not my will, but yours be done." Does he then
fear death, or come unwillingly to the passion? he who said, "The Son of man
must suffer many things, and be killed, and be buried, and on the third day
rise"; he who said, "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it";
he who said, "I have authority to lay down my soul, and I have authority to
take it again"; and, "No one takes it from me; I lay it down of myself, and I
take it again"; he who rebuked Peter, who a little before had been blessed at
the word, "You are the Christ, the Son of the living God," then thinking his
passion unworthy of him, having heard: "Get behind me, satan; you are a
stumbling-block to me, because you do not mind the things of God, but the
things of men"; he who set his face to go up to Jerusalem, that through his
passion he might furnish salvation to all; he who through the prophets
proclaimed beforehand even the form of his passion, through Jeremiah: "They
devised an evil device against me, saying: Come and let us put wood into his
bread, and let us wipe him out from the earth," signifying the cross; and
through David: "They gave gall into my food, and for my thirst they gave me
vinegar to drink"; and, "They dug my hands and my feet; they divided my
garments among themselves, and upon my clothing they cast a lot"; and through
Isaiah: "I have given my back to scourges, and my cheeks to blows, and I did
not turn my face away from the shame of spittings." You know then all these
things fulfilled on the cross. For by Pilate he was scourged, by a servant he
was struck, by lawless men he was spat upon, by soldiers he was fixed with
nails on a cross, they dividing his garments and casting a lot. He who through
many things signified the passion, how does he shrink now? Why then does he
say, "Father, if possible, let this cup pass from me"? The devil did not know
who he was; for seeing him thirsting, hungering, growing weary, and his other
economies, he thought him a mere man, but seeing his divine wonders, he
suspected God; he therefore doubted concerning him, not knowing him. For if he
had known accurately that he was God, he would not have dared to approach him;
as also Paul says: "For if they had known, they would not have crucified the
Lord of glory." The Master therefore puts forward cowardice, drawing that one
on, that coming upon him as upon a man, he may be routed by him, and all the
captives who were held by him may be redeemed. Just as if


some king being about to engage with some weak tyrant, and knowing that he
will flee him, feigns cowardice, saying to his forces, "Let us flee, as it
were, from his face, so that he may not run away from us, but may pursue, and
thus turning we may seize him"; so also the Master put forward cowardice, that
the devil might come upon him as upon a man, and find him who would tear him
apart, and deliver all those held by hades. For so it says: "An angel appeared
strengthening him." He who is worshipped by angels, and before whom every knee
bows of things in heaven and on earth and under the earth, how did he receive
strength from an angel? Not strength, but a doxology of an angel saying:
"Yours is the kingdom and the power, Master." "No one knows that day, or the
hour, neither the angels, nor the Son, except the Father." And how, he says,
is he equal to the Father, who does not know that day? he who made it, does he
not know it? And why then does he say, "Nor the Son"? And look at a
contemplation: For two knowledges and understandings one might find in
Scripture, one according to act, and another according to knowledge. For
"Adam," it says, "knew Eve his wife," not as formerly not knowing; for how, he
who said, "This now is bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh"? "And Abraham
knew Sarah his wife, and Jacob =48.1093= Leah and Rachel." Did he then not
know them, who had served fourteen years for them? He knew them according to
knowledge, but at the end also according to act. The Father therefore, not
judging anyone, but having given all the judgment to the Son, saw the day both
by knowledge and by act; but the Son, having come to save the world, and not
to judge, saw it according to knowledge; and since he has not yet judged, he
does not know the day, signifying the knowledge according to act.

γʹ. But Paul says: "Consider the apostle and high priest of our confession,
Christ Jesus, being faithful to him who made him"; and elsewhere: "God made
him Lord and Christ"; and Solomon: "The Lord created me the beginning of his
ways for his works." Do you see how Scripture signifies him a creature and a
thing made? But the sequence will convict you, O God-fighter; "For every
priest," it says, "taken from men, is appointed for men, to offer gifts and
sacrifices." For he took from us the flesh that was made, without sin, which
he offered to the Father as a sacrifice for us. According to the flesh then,
understand "to him who made him," and not according to the Godhead. So also,
"God made him Lord and Christ." For before the economy he is everywhere
proclaimed God, as has already been shown, and nowhere does anyone call him a
thing made; but because of the union of the created flesh he says, "God made
him Lord and Christ"; toward the thickness of the hearers; "The Lord created
me the beginning of his ways," it says. Do you see that he says creature? But
let us go on to the sequence of what follows: "Before the age he founded me,
and before all hills he begets me." First, O vainglorious one, learn that the
book is called Proverbs; and everything spoken as a proverb is not the same as
the force of the word. For the Lord also likened the kingdom of the heavens to
a net and leaven and a grain of mustard, and to so many other things; and we
do not at all say that the kingdom is any of these.

But I will not speak as one being carried about: "The Lord created me the
beginning of his ways, before the age he founded me, and before all hills he
begets me." How then is that which is begotten created, or founded? or how is
that which is created begotten? For if created, not begotten; we do not create
what we beget, nor beget what we create. And how is it possible that the
uncreated Father has begotten a created Son? For we being created beget
created things; but the Father being uncreated begot an uncreated Son. And
Aquila translating said, "He acquired me." Therefore "he created me" proclaims
beforehand what was to come as already having come to be; for this also is a
custom of Scripture; as, "They dug my hands and my feet"; me, he says, whom
you see having united created flesh to himself by his own good pleasure,


from the beginning I am, I do not have the beginning from Mary. For "in the
beginning was the Word"; and, "Before Abraham came to be I am." Confess then
an uncreated Son, and co-unoriginate with the Father, that you may not give
also to the Father a beginning of becoming father. If you say the Son is
co-unoriginate with the Father, how did the Father beget the Son? willing, or
not willing? If we say, Willing, they contradict: See, the will was before the
Word; for before he willed, he says, the Son was not; and if we say, Not
willing, they say: The Divine is tyrannized; and everything tyrannized is not
God. But we say that just as the sun has its rays and its brightness
co-eternal, so the Father has Son and Holy Spirit; that we may not say also
the Father is under a beginning. For if he is said to be under a beginning,
before the Son began, the Father was not father, according to your word, and
you will be compelled to put the Father under a beginning impiously; or if
not, I too will bring against you: Did the Father become father willing, or
not willing? If you say, Willing, see then, before he willed he was not
Father; there was then a time when the Father was not father; and he himself
therefore began to be father; if you say, Not willing, he has been tyrannized,
and God is not tyrannized; for what is tyrannized is not God. Do you see into
how great impiety you fall, if you do not confess the Son co-unoriginate and
co-eternal and co-creating with the Father? And how am I to say co-creating?
for it is written that "All things came to be through him" =48.1094=. Do you
not hear the prophet saying concerning the Son, "Out of the mouth of infants
and sucklings you have perfected praise," and what follows; that "I shall see
the heavens, works of your fingers, moon and stars, which you have founded"?
Do you not see the authority of Christ saying: "My Father works until now, and
I work"; and, "As the Father raises the dead and makes alive, so also the Son
makes alive whom he wills"; and, "Young man, I say to you, arise"; and to the
unclean spirit, "I command you, be silent, and come out from him"; and, "Take
heart, child, your sins are forgiven you"? For since those said, "No one can
forgive sins, except God only," convicting them from their own mouth, he says:
You said that it belongs to God only to forgive sins; see then I forgive sins,
and confess my Godhead. And if you do not believe, from the strengthening of
the body see and learn also the health of the soul. Then he says to him:
"Having arisen take up your bed, and go to your house"; and again to another:
"Stretch out your hand"; and to the sea: "Be silent, be muzzled"; "Girl,
arise"; "Lazarus, come out"; "I will, be cleansed"; and all the rest, through
which he shows the equal honor toward the Father; learning all which, believe
that he is consubstantial with the Father, and the Spirit also consubstantial.
For if he proceeds from the Father, and receives from the Son, the Spirit is
not alien from the essence of God. Spirit of God, Spirit of Christ, Spirit of
truth, Spirit the Paraclete, set over against. "God is Spirit"; "And the Lord
is the Spirit"; as also Peter to Ananias: "Why has it been agreed among you to
tempt the Holy Spirit?" And showing that he is God, he says: "You have not
lied to a man, but to God." Do you see that the Spirit to whom he lied is God?
And Paul says: "You are a temple of God, and the Spirit of God dwells in you."
Clearly the Spirit who dwells in us is God; "For in him we live, and move, and
are"; and, "The second man into a life-giving Spirit"; and elsewhere he says,
"The flesh profits nothing, it is the spirit that makes alive."

δʹ. Do not then dare to utter anything against the consubstantial and almighty
Trinity. And again, "No one knows the things of the man, except the spirit of
the man that is in him; so also no one knows the things of God, except the
Spirit of God"; no longer, "that is in him," lest anyone think the Spirit
composite. For "the Spirit searches all things, even the depths of God." Yes,
he searches, but he does not comprehend; for it is not written that he
comprehends. How then does he search? as a busybody? as an alien? as not
knowing? For if indeed


it is written, "Do not seek things deeper than you, and do not examine things
stronger than you; the things that have been commanded you, these consider;
for there is no need of the hidden things." If he is alien according to your
word, how did he dare to search the depths of God? Concerning the Father it is
written: "I am God proving kidneys, and searching storehouses of the belly."
Will you then, because "I comprehend" is not added, say that the Father does
not know? If he searches and proves not knowing, that you may dare to say this
also concerning the worshipful Spirit? How did he say, "Separate then for me
Paul and Barnabas for the work to which I have called them"? You see the
authority; understand the Godhead. And Agabus: "Thus says the Holy Spirit: The
man. whose this belt is, thus they shall bind in Jerusalem." And the apostles:
"It seemed good to the Holy Spirit to keep nothing more"; and again: "And

"Take heed to yourselves, and to all the flock in which the Holy Spirit has

authority and the power. The Spirit then is not alien from God. "I saw," says
Isaiah, "God upon a throne high and lifted up, and the Seraphim around him,"
and what follows; and I said: "Woe is me, for I am pricked, because having
unclean lips, I dwell in the midst of a people having unclean lips, and the
king the Lord of sabaoth I have seen with my eyes"; and he said, "Who will go
to this people?" And I said: "Behold I, send me." And he said to me, clearly
the one sitting on the throne, "Go to this people, and say: Hearing you shall
hear, and shall not understand, and seeing you shall see, and shall not
perceive." Who is the one sitting upon a throne? The Father, he says. And if
you learn from Paul that it is the Spirit, do you confess him God? Show, he
says, and I believe.

Hear him then saying: "Well did the Holy Spirit speak through Isaiah the
prophet: Go to this people, and say: Hearing you shall hear," and what
follows. Paul says, "The Holy Spirit said"; Isaiah says, "The Lord of sabaoth
said," because it is indifferent to Scripture to speak concerning Father and
Son and Holy Spirit. No longer then say a great and a small God, falling into
Hellenism. For if the Son is a small God, Paul lies saying: "Awaiting the
blessed hope of the glory of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ." Whom
therefore Paul calls great God, do not you call small; for in God all things
are perfect. And again: "From whom is Christ according to the flesh, who is
God over all, blessed unto the ages." Nor ever name a created God; for impious
is he who worships a creature. What then? do you not say the body of the Lord
is created? See then, you too worship a creature, O foolish one. No one says
to a king, Strip off your purple, or, rise from your throne, that I may
worship you bare, but he worships a king sitting upon a throne, and being in
purple. For God the Word was well pleased to unite to himself without stain
the created flesh from us; it is therefore worshipful together with God the
Word, as he also de=48.1096=ified it. And signifying this the divine David
cries: "Exalt the Lord our God, and worship the footstool of his feet, because
it is holy." *** In another saying he says: "Heaven is a throne for me, and
the earth a footstool of my feet." Do you command us to worship the earth, O
blessed David? and how does Moses legislate, to worship neither heaven, nor
the earth, nor the things in them? You see a mystery. Since he was about,
without sin, to unite our flesh to himself unto one worship, and


our flesh is from Adam, from the earth; according to this he says, "And
worship the footstool of his feet." We do not worship the earth, but God the
Word who united to himself without sin the flesh of Adam formed from the
earth. No longer then call the Son a created God; for say, where did the
Father say, I created a son for myself; or the Son, the Father created me? Nor
take the human voices of Christ into a diminishing of him. For if, these
things having been said, and the human things having been shown in him, some
dared to say that he was made flesh in seeming and in fantasy; if these things
had not come to be, into what impiety would they not have run aground? Let
everyone then who believes the holy Scriptures worship an uncreated Trinity,
unchangeable, immutable, almighty, co-unoriginate, co-eternal and
consubstantial, Father, and Son, formerly without flesh, but at the end by his
own will perfectly become man, without sin, unchangeably and immutably; for
having become man he remained being God; for he was not turned from his own
glory and dignity; and the Holy Spirit, the Spirit who spoke through the
prophets. And those who say the Son is changeable, or the Spirit, or mutable,
or of another essence, or come to be from things that are not, or that there
was a time when he was not, let the catholic Church anathematize them. And
these things suffice for him who wishes, even if they are few; for to go
through all is impossible; for time will fail me as I narrate. For I do not
think the world itself would contain the books being written. But we the
unworthy, who have been counted worthy to be benefited from the divine
Scriptures, let us worship Father and Son and Holy Spirit, to whom be the
glory and the power, unto the ages of ages. Amen.
