OF ORIGEN ON THE SONG OF SONGS. =17.253= CHAPTER 1. Let him kiss me from
kisses of his mouth. Let him not betroth me through prophets, she says, but
let him converse through himself, according to spiritual sense; through which
John says: And our hands handled concerning the Word of life; and she says
these things as having heard the prophets saying: The king has desired your
beauty; and elsewhere: As a bridegroom will rejoice over a bride, so the Lord
will rejoice over you. And as often as, seeking some divine doctrine, we grasp
it, let us consider ourselves to have been kissed from the mouth of the
bridegroom; but while we are at a loss, let us suppose that we, holding on,
say, Let him kiss me from kisses of his mouth. For your breasts are better
than wine, and the scent of your myrrhs than all spices. That is, your virtues
above the virtues among us, according to, His virtue covered the heavens; for
he is wisdom itself and righteousness; but we are wise and just by
participation, according to choice and power, coming to be in the fragrance of
these: Your name is myrrh poured out. Therefore maidens have loved you.
Perhaps prophesying the power of the name of Christ that filled the world,
according to his coming; so that it came to be according to Paul, to some a
scent from death unto death, to others of life unto life, the bride would have
said, Therefore all have loved you; but not only the maidens being renewed day
by day, and having no wrinkle or spot; and it was poured out, so as no longer
to be kept silent, shut up in secrets. The king brought me into his chamber.
That is, she means as impossible the soul worthy of love, or the Church, or
the ruling faculty of Christ; into which Paul having entered said: But we have
the mind of Christ, that we may see the things granted us by God; and what
=17.256= are these? Things which eye has not known and ear has not heard, and
which have not come up upon a human heart; for these God has prepared for
those who love him; for in the chamber of the bridegroom all the treasures of
wisdom and knowledge are hidden; for she, as having gone before the maidens,
was brought in, and stood as queen at the right of the king, in gold-wrought
clothing, according to David; but concerning the maidens, virgins shall be
brought to the king after her, and so on. We will love your breasts more than
wine; uprightness has loved you. That is, even if now through weakness we love
the wine above your breasts, O bridegroom, when we have been improved, we come
to love them above this. Wine then that gladdens maidens is the things of the
law; but
perfection is the breasts of the bridegroom; then in defense of not knowing
how to love, she says: Uprightness has loved you; but we have not yet made our
own paths straight. I am black and beautiful, daughters of Jerusalem; as tents
of Kedar, as curtains of Solomon. And the Church from the nations says these
things to the souls from Jerusalem, or to Jerusalem, confessing the black,
because not from illustrious nor from enlightened fathers; wherefore also to
be compared to darkness; but beautiful because of the word which she received;
and to be like the curtains of Solomon which he had, with which among other
things he possessed in his glory. Do not look at me because I am darkened,
because the sun has looked askance at me. Or that, me having been darkened by
sin, Christ overlooked because of unbelief; whom the Scriptures proclaim sun
of righteousness. Sons of my mother fought in me; they set me as a keeper in
vineyards; my own vineyard I did not keep. That is, the teaching disciples of
Christ, wanting to cleanse me of the wicked reasonings toward this, and of the
demons cooperating with these, fought these in me, being sons of the same
mother as I, of freedom I mean; some being taken up into the according to


election of grace remnant; but I having received the covenant according to the
Jerusalem above, and being born again from promise. And they did not first set
her as keeper, then fight; but having fought first they made her fit to be set
in vineyards for keeping ... And this Moses, and the prophets, of whom each
was a field full, which the Lord blessed; .. and a vineyard in which someone
having come to be, and not keeping his former habit as being a vineyard from
Sodom, uses the present word. If you do not know yourself, O beautiful among
women, go out you on the heels of your flocks, and shepherd your kids upon the
tents of the shepherds. And the much-talked-of saying among the Greeks has
been anticipated, having been handed to wise Solomon, the =17.257= Know
yourself; according to which the soul is now threatened by the lover and
bridegroom, if she will not keep the beauty given her according to the image
of God, the casting out from within, and being ranked among the last,
shepherding the left-hand kids. And you will shepherd them upon the tents of
the shepherds, now of this one, now of that one, as some wanderer; until,
trained by experience, you might turn back toward knowledge of yourself; and
the word will also fit the Church; and one knows oneself, knowing whether one
has a good or a worthless disposition; and is left far behind of the way
toward virtue; or stretches forward to the things in front, forgetting the
things behind; and whether one has not yet received or been perfected, or
draws near the bounds of beauty; and it is also a work to set apart for each
action how they have come to be, and for every reasoning and word; and he
calls women the souls that are not pure nor indifferent; and neglecting her
own knowledge, she would become tossed and carried about by every wind of
teaching in the dice-play of men; and everywhere rather shepherding her own
left-hand kids than she shepherds beside the beautiful word; and this word is
kindly, even if she is never smooth, she is already henceforth beautiful among
women, being numbered with the Church of God, and being more beautiful than
the little ones in it; for the so-called women of the Church are incomparably
better than the souls of the nations going after the lovers of the opposing
powers, being called women, but not virgins; for how is she not better, who
has looked up toward her own creator? And Go out is like being handed over to
Satan, for destruction of the flesh, having been set apart from the Church;
after, as she was saying to the maidens, having been brought by the king into
his chamber. How have your cheeks been made beautiful as a turtledove's? But
Paul also says the Church of the bride is members of Christ; of which, as it
were a face, those who differ from the rest in beauty, thinking nothing
shameful; and bearing for her the modesty of self-control as fruit, those who
perhaps formerly were not beautiful, changed so much that it was said to the
bride: How have your cheeks been made beautiful, having no spot or wrinkle;
and perhaps also by knowing the one God only; for the turtledove also pairs
with one only. We will make for you likenesses of gold with points of silver.
And this: The silver was not much nor poured out; a few things said in secret
by the prophets and the wise of old; but when he sojourned, the things in the
inaccessible parts of the temple have been seen by the one who sees the true
veil being split from top to bottom for the things within to be seen, whenever
he who has lain down having slept as a lion and as a cub is raised. And after
these things. For then those who have become conformed to his resurrection
will themselves receive gold of corresponding likenesses; for they will be
multiplied with silver and with gold. =17.260= Until the king is in his
reclining, nard gave its scent. For she says, says, my nard with which I
anointed him has given the scent of the bridegroom; for having anointed the
feet of Jesus with her own nard, and wiped them with her hair, she took this
up transformed from the scent of the bridegroom; perceiving which, when it
came upon the head, she says, My nard has given me the scent of the
bridegroom; wherefore as from the skins of Jesus the


whole house of the disciple was filled with the myrrh. A bundle of stacte is
my brother's son to me, he shall lodge between my breasts. Having said that
the nard of the bridegroom has given me the scent, now I say that my brother's
son breathes stacte not being dispersed, nor breathing outside; but bound up
and giving the fragrance more densely; and the Church from the nations, having
had the first people as brother, since from him Christ was according to the
flesh, has called him son of a brother and bridegroom; and since Aquila has
rendered father's brother, it must be said that the people of old is father of
the Church from the nations; since from the law among them and the prophets
came to be the birth in God and the leading from us into piety; this one then
of the bride having been so rendered as father, the Savior is brother,
likewise having come to be under law as they. Behold you are beautiful, my
neighbor, behold you are beautiful; your eyes are doves. Now the bride seems
first to have looked more clearly upon the beauty of the bridegroom; and
through her dove eyes to have perceived the excellence also in the beauty of
the word; and perhaps she also hints that the common bed of herself and of the
bridegroom is the body, in which while still being the soul is counted worthy
of communion of the word; Paul then says, Our bodies are members of Christ;
for through the "our" he says the body is of the bride; and through "members
of Christ," of the bridegroom; and she calls the bridegroom shady because of
the density of the contemplations in the word and in wisdom; and if the common
bride of both is flourishing, it is no wonder that also upon the body all good
action through the body is signified. CHAPTER 2. I am a flower of the field, a
lily of the valleys. He calls flower that which is on the way to becoming
fruit; so in the place around the earth, called field, the bridegroom Word is
a flower in relation to the things to come; for when the perfect comes, the
flower will change into becoming fruit; and since they do not have room for
more than the flower, he speaks upon earth; therefore the bridegroom has
become as a flower of the field; for he emptied himself, taking a slave's
form, so that we might be able to behold his glory after these things; and
perhaps to the more well-born and even places named fields he is a flower, but
to those lower and more hollow than these, a lily; the bridegroom having said
these things compares his =17.261= neighbor to other daughters, the remaining
souls, who are thorns when compared with her. And such are the souls not near
the bridegroom; but the one near is a lily shining in the midst of him. As an
apple among the trees of the wood, so is my brother's son among the sons;
under his shadow I desired and sat, and his fruit was in my throat. It was
fitting for the bride, having become wholly of the beauty of the bridegroom,
to say nothing about herself, but in addition to what the bridegroom said
about himself, to compare him to an apple; and the sons beside them to trees
of a wood; and by excess of longing, even to desire his shadow, and to send
his quality through to the depth; and she seems to say these things to the
maidens, as the bridegroom said the first things to the companions; and sons
he either means the companions whom she compared, running past the bridegroom,
to fruitless trees, or those alien to him. And under his shadow she both
desired and has sat; and Jeremiah also says in Lamentations: The spirit before
our face, Christ the Lord, was taken in our corruptions; of whom we said, In
his shadow we shall live among the nations; for how was his shadow not going
to become a cause of life for us, who have become free of the shadow of the
law? For the law had a shadow of the good things to come, we being no longer
under law but under grace, even if now we are under a shadow that differs
greatly; for after the present life we shall behold the truth no longer
through a mirror and in a riddle, but face to face; and the bride will also
say, Until the day dawns and the shadows are moved; for our life is as a
shadow, and in the shadow of death the one from the nations was sitting, and
those still unbelieving sit. Bring me into the house of wine. These things she
says to the friends of the bridegroom, the holy angels or


apostles and prophets; all but saying, Mix me with the body of Christ. Support
me with myrrhs, pile me with apples, for I am wounded with love. Symmachus has
rendered thus: Lean me back upon vine-flower, that is, upon fragrant trees
that produce good fruit, blooming; the worthless trees being called fruitless
or of bad fruit; and some of the copies have: Support me with unmyrrhed
things; which is to be understood either as faithful, yet not so as to be
styled members of her who has no spot or wrinkle, or as outsiders to the faith
who are good; and "pile me with apples," Symmachus making clear, said, roll
apples around me; for the bride wishes to rest among many apples being rolled
around her; which I think is fruit of the apple among the trees of the wood of
the bridegroom, that she may partake of their quality; For I have been wounded
with a love-charm, she says, according to Symmachus; from the chosen arrow,
according to Isaiah. =17.264= The voice of my brother's son, behold this one
has come leaping upon the mountains, bounding upon the hills. Some have
assigned this to the preceding, but the Hebrew sets it from its own section;
and it is clear that the bride, conversing with the daughters of Jerusalem,
suddenly perceives the voice of the bridegroom, conversing with some as is
likely; which she perceives speaking upon the mountains and the hills lying
beside the place of the bride, so as not to be out of tune with a fawn; then
by eagerness concerning the bride, he came near the house and appeared behind
it; then having leaped he reaches as far as the windows of the house, as if
amorously wanting to look in at her; and near the house where the bride is,
many nets of an ambush for the bride and those about her have been spread,
which the bridegroom having cut through as stronger, looked through them; and
he calls the bride to come near; encouraging her in deed to despise the nets,
as already split, and the hard time through them having run past, which he
calls winter, bringing unprofitable and violent rain; and he also urges
through the good things of the present time, speaking of flowers as in spring
and the tending of vines; and he describes the voice of a turtledove, and the
unripe figs of a fig tree, and vines blooming with cyprus; and the place where
he will rest together; where having come to be he wished her to appear to the
bridegroom with her whole face, and to grant her to hear the voice sweet to
her. My brother's son answers and says to me: Rise, come, my neighbor, my
beautiful one, my dove; for behold the winter has passed, the rain has gone
away and gone to itself; the flowers have appeared in the land; the time of
cutting has arrived; the voice of the turtledove has been heard in our land;
the fig tree has put forth its unripe figs; the vines bloom with cyprus, they
have given scent, and so on. Rise, he says, from the sensibles toward the
intelligibles, that we may be together; The rain has gone away; we would have
said that rain had come to be, the time before the Sojourn, God having
commanded the clouds to rain the legal and prophetic word; and that this has
ceased, since the law and the prophets are until John; and spring and summer
after the Sojourn; because there is no longer need of rains when the flowers
through Christ have appeared in the land; and from his sojourn, the fig tree
is not cut down as fruitless, having come to be in the former time; for now it
has put forth unripe figs, and the vines bloom with cyprus; wherefore he says,
one of the branches of Christ, we are a fragrance to God. Rise, come, my
neighbor, my beautiful one, my dove; and come you, my dove, in the covert of
the rock, next to the outwork; show me your face, and make me hear your voice,
for your voice is sweet, and your face is beautiful. He wishes the soul to
pass beyond the sensibles; and as of a city a wall and outwork, he calls the
sensible world; so the soul that communes with the word must come to be in the
covert of the rock, not only outside the wall of the city, but also of the
outwork; so that having come near she may with unveiled face behold as in a
mirror the =17.265= glory of the Lord; being persuaded by the one who says,
Show me your face; and he also desires, as she converses, to hear the voice,
marveling at it as sweet; and an outwork is what


Isaiah names a surrounding wall, saying: he will set a wall, or a surrounding
wall; he wishes then that she, having gone out of the bodily things, come to
be not only in the wall, which I think is the word concerning the world, but
also next to the outwork; which is a last word of the bodily things, and a
beginning of the incorporeal. Catch for us little foxes that destroy
vineyards, and our vines bloom with cyprus. These things the bridegroom says
to the
friends, either angels, or holy men, as many as are teachers of the Church;
and the urging is for the salvation of the vineyards; so that when the knavish
powers that corrupt the beginning of the putting forth of the fruit are
seized, the vines may be able to advance from blooming with cyprus as far as
the perfecting of the fruit, being tilled by God; and through free will being
able both to bear fruit and not; or perhaps also humans, certain foxes through
heterodox sharpness hindering those who have begun to run well; whom while
still little he wishes to be seized before they advance further in impiety;
for having become a great fox, he is uncatchable by those, and is hunted by
the bridegroom alone; for beginning to work the worse things, they are also
easily caught by the friends; and he would also call the vineyards little; for
a fox has no power against the great ones. My brother's son is mine, and I am
his; he who shepherds among the lilies, until the day breathes and the shadows
are moved. The bride says to the companions of the bridegroom that she has no
share with the foxes; for he has shared blood and flesh with us, she says,
and we with him of incorruption. Or that God comes to be in a soul, and the
soul is relocated into God; so also Paul: The body is not for fornication, but
for the Lord, and the Lord for the body; for the body is the bride, Church of
Christ, that which is in no way for fornication, but always for the Lord; and
the Savior himself: Remain in me and I in you; and in what follows it is
written conversely: I am my brother's son's, and my brother's son is mine. Or
perhaps because the beginning of the benefaction has come from the bridegroom;
for if he had not shown himself to her, she too was not dedicated to the
bridegroom; for having seen his divine beauty, she came to a saving
love-charm, after which, having been dedicated to his beauty, she says, and I
to him; then she took the order conversely before the perfect came, saying, I
am my brother's son's; but when he is perfectly revealed, leaving nothing
hidden, perceiving this she says; and my brother's son is mine; and it has
been said somewhere in the prophecies, the Lord himself will shepherd us unto
the ages, but not as now he shepherds us among lilies that are in the midst of
thorns; therefore having said: I am a flower of the field, a lily of the
valleys, he adds; as a lily in the midst of thorns, so is my neighbor among
the daughters; and keep how concerning the bride he did not say; wherefore
also he has arrived, so that those being shepherded may not be stuck on
thorns. =17.268= Until the day breathes and the shadows pass. Wherefore also
he boasts to say mystically in Jacob: I became of the day, being burned with
the heat; for in the heat of the present life we need the one who shepherds,
because of the noonday demons; for he says to the obedient: he will not fear
from a noonday demon; of which sort also is, The sun will not burn you; since
the one who bears our weaknesses and was pained concerning us became for us
burned with the heat; that he might buy us out from them; as also from the
curse of the law, having become a curse for us; and he shepherds also among
lilies, even until the shadows of life pass; according to, as shadows our days
pass by; and perhaps also before his sojourn, through law and prophets, he
shepherded among lilies while there was still burning heat; but after the
sojourn, he shepherds, but not among lilies; for as the day having breathed it
has been said, beloved, know that it is the last hour. Turn, liken yourself,
my brother's son, to the gazelle or to a fawn of deer upon the mountains of
the hollows. The former things the bride said to the maidens, but the present
things to the bridegroom; so as, to the present time


to take up a fitting likeness toward the gazelle, because of sharp sight; and
toward a fawn of deer, through disappearances of the race of serpents; and
doing these things in the mountains of the hollows, saying Turn, from being in
the form of God; and take these up because of the love toward me, sojourning
among humans; of whom the more through vice spend time in hollows; and
mountains of hollows are the righteous who rise above the evils; and the Word
having turned is likened, among those dedicated to contemplation, to a
gazelle; but among the more practical and those who pull down the opposing
powers, to a fawn of deer; and "or to a fawn" is a mild disjunction; and
Theodotion said, upon the mountains of incense; no less signifying the saints.
CHAPTER 3. Upon my bed in nights I sought him whom my soul loved; I sought
him, and I did not find him; I called him and he did not obey me; I will arise
then and I will go around in the city, in the markets, and in the streets; and
I will seek him whom my soul loved; I sought him, and I did not find him; I
called him, and he did not obey me; the watchers found me, those going around
in the city; Have you seen him whom my soul loved? as a little when I had
passed from them, until I found him whom my soul loved; I held him, and I did
not let him go; until I brought him into my mother's house and into the
chamber of her who conceived me. Having spoken about the mysteries before the
marriage, now she calls him through the night upon the bed; wanting to share
in more secret things, and to partake of the more perfect rest; for this is
the bed; and in night, in a time of quiet, and far from the sight of those
unable to contemplate such things; and the soul of the one seeking the deeper
things of the divine word upon the bed must have been wounded beforehand with
love; and the one longed for waits for the rising of the one who longs; so
that having stretched =17.269= the passion he might at the right time show
himself to her who has toiled very much in the search for him; for having said
that upon my bed in nights I sought him whom my soul loved, and that I sought
him and did not find, she adds: I will arise then and I will go around;
therefore having put off lying on the back and the shutting of the eyes, let
us rise up being persuaded by the one who says, Rise then, you who sleep; and
the whole world is the city which one must go around, coming to be in the
contemplation concerning him in the wise teachings; for these are the markets;
from which it is possible to take something useful, but also in the streets;
for wisdom is hymned in exits, and in streets she conducts frankness; and we
too have been commanded to write upon the breadth of our heart; and seeking
things greater than according to markets and streets she says she has not
found; yet she journeys toward the finding, being found by those who keep the
city and go around it; they would be the ministering spirits; these are those
whom the Lord sent to go about the earth; of whom then she inquires about the
one longed for; and an answer of these has not been recorded; and we are
taught if we are going to find the one longed for, to pass by a little, and to
become better than such angels; concerning whom Peter seems to speak in an
Epistle teaching the superiority of the things laid up for the perfect; into
which angels desire to look; and it is desirable to surpass such by a little,
that the longed-for may be found, and being found may be held, and being held
may not be understood; for the holding is useless, if continuous study does
not hold what has been understood; wherefore she says: I did not let him go,
until I brought him into my mother's house; so that also after the departure
from here, he might enter with her into the house of the Jerusalem above,
where she may bring in the more common contemplations of the word; but the
more secret into the chambers of her who conceived the bride; and the same one
is shown as mother and conceiver, because in the conceiving is the beginning
of the constitution; but she is called mother after the forming, and the
perfecting of what was conceived. Who is this who comes up from the wilderness
as stems of smoke, incense-offered? myrrh and frankincense from all powders of
a perfume-compounder? Very beautiful is the soul that comes up in life and
word, having fled the sins among the many, which


is said to come up from a wilderness; and that the righteous is a fragrance of
Christ in every place to those being saved, is understood as incense-offered,
similarly to stems sending up sweet smoke, gladdening the breaths of those who
count blessed. CHAPTER 4. Your teeth are as flocks of the shorn that have come
up from the washing; all of them bearing twins, and there is no barren one
among them. And bearing twins otherwise, because of the double of the
understanding, the delightful and the spiritual. As a scarlet cord are your
lips, and your speech is beautiful. That is, let the preparation of the word
be fiery so as to be able to gain the hearer, or it also shows the blooded
=17.272= and living character of the words going out through the lip, as if
being dipped in the true drink of the blood of Christ. As a rind of the
pomegranate is your apple besides your silence. He adds to the praise of
self-control that from silence; the bride knowing times of beautiful speech
and of praised quiet; and since a faithful one hides matters by breath and the
bride hides in the depth the contemplations, the as-it-were grains of the
pomegranate, each embodied in its own rank in the ruling faculty; wherefore he
added: Besides your silence; showing the doctrines kept silent as grains of a
pomegranate under its rind. Your neck is as the tower of David, built in
Thalpioth; a thousand shields hang upon it, all darts of the mighty. Aquila
has rendered Thalpioth as battlements; and Symmachus, heights; which it
follows that the tower above the battlements is wisdom, from which likewise
hang shields, the believed doctrines, which the Apostle spoke in the singular
as shield of faith; and darts of the mighty, the words that powerfully
overturn the persuasiveness of the things contrary to the truth; and instead
of battlements, the fifth edition has rendered commands, interpreting the
sense rather than the wording; for if commands have not been laid down
beforehand, it is impossible for the unlying knowledge to be built. Your two
breasts are as two twin fawns of a gazelle that graze among the lilies, until
the day breathes and the shadows are moved. The ruling faculty, as in the case
of a bride, is breasts; which is most sharp-sighted; wherefore he compared
them to two fawns of a gazelle grazing in beautiful and fragrant places;
signifying both the intelligibles and the sensibles; and fawns, since now we
know in part; but when the perfect comes, we put away childishness; when the
burning heat of the present life ceases, and a time of cooling comes on, the
shadow also being dissolved; for the present life is a shadow; for then as
perfect, but not as fawns, we shall graze. You have heartened us, my sister
bride; you have heartened us with one from your eyes, with one in a setting of
your neck. The setting is said from being set in and being fitted to the neck
of the bride; and if the breasts of the bride remained as two fawns of a
gazelle, why have they been made beautiful? it would not have been said
wonderingly concerning the, as I said, thinking faculty, but also the doubling
of the wonder showed the increase of beauty; of which sort is "you have
heartened" said a second time; and garments of the bride, those who have put
on Christ Jesus, bowels of mercies and kindness and humility; and lips the
teachers; and honeycombs of honey good words; and prophets and apostles have
worked honeycomb and honey. A garden enclosed, my sister bride; a garden
enclosed, a fountain sealed. Second, "a garden enclosed" was said, once of one
advancing, and once of the perfect, when his ruling =17.273= faculty having
been seen through, has been stamped with the seal of theology; or also now the
bride is a garden enclosed only, but in the age to come through the perfection
and the firmness, she is also a fountain sealed. For to everyone who has it
will be given and he will have abundance; now then Scripture is simply a
garden; but elsewhere as concerning another, it also adds the of
vegetable-growing. Awake, North wind, and come, South, blow through my garden,
and let my spices flow. The bride seems to rebuke the North wind, having
received the authority from the one who rebuked the wind


bridegroom; wherefore among the three evangelists it is said that the wind was
rebuked; and the winds simply obey him; Christ then, the devil being driven
out, is housed in the souls; concerning whom is, God will come from Teman,
that is from the South. CHAPTER 5. Open to me, my sister, my neighbor, my
dove, my perfect one, for my head has been filled with dew, and my locks with
drops of night. Necessarily the addition of "to me" of the "open"; for it is
possible to open also to opposing powers; and he seems to speak of the dew as
troublesome, as also of the drops of the night; for he seems to seek rest from
these. These things follow the first; for after the resurrection he entered,
the doors being shut; and he calls Thomas, and through him every unbelieving
