OF THE SAME SAINT CYRIL ARCHBISHOP OF ALEXANDRIA
SOME FEW FRAGMENTS ON THE FIRST EPISTLE TO THE CORINTHIANS
OF CYRIL ARCHBISHOP OF ALEXANDRIA SOME FEW FRAGMENTS
ON THE FIRST EPISTLE TO THE CORINTHIANS.
Paul a called apostle of Jesus Christ.
The divine Paul says that he has been sent by Christ, by the good pleasure of
God and Father; for this I think is the Through the will of God; for since
through the Son the willing and the working of the Father is, through him
again we say that the saints have been appointed to apostleship. And Sosthenes
the brother etc. That the power of the preachings will benefit not only
Corinthians, but also all who have been called in Christ into the
sanctification through faith, he makes clear saying To those sanctified in
Christ in every place of them and of us; for the law has not commanded to do
this in every place, but to come to the divine 250 temple itself in Jerusalem;
for it feared, as is likely, the easy-to-be-carried-away of the mind among
them, lest perhaps having been commanded to worship or sacrifice in every
place, they [would have] the law as a pretext of change toward the more
shameful things, and would worship the carved things, setting up temples and
altars for themselves; but since we from the nations have now been called
through faith toward Christ into kinship as toward God, and have been enriched
with the security through the Holy Spirit, we have been appointed to worship
not all in Jerusalem, but in every place; and this then was what was said
through the prophets "And they shall worship the Lord each from his own
place." He writes then together with Sosthenes to the church of God, which
Christ himself presented to himself; and who was it seen to be? all those
sanctified and called through Christ toward justification, calling upon his
name in every place of them and of us, that is whether in the land of the
Jews, or then in the cities and countries of the nations. And he proclaims
Christ God by nature, and with him and through him he works out the glory to
God and Father; for he names those sanctified a church of God, and again he
assigns them to Christ saying "But if anyone has not the Spirit of Christ,
this one is not his." Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord
Jesus Christ. He crowns those who have believed with grace from God and Father
and the Lord Jesus Christ; for through him and in him are all things from God
and Father, and he is co-giver and co-supplier with the one who begot him of
the goods from above, and distributor of the gifts from heaven, and he himself
is the peace of all, making splendid by the grace from himself 251 beginnings,
thrones and authorities and, to say it simply, every rational creation. You
were fitted together in the same mind and in the same judgment. That it was
necessary and wise to persuade that one must be dignified with the master's
name, saying himself through one of the holy prophets "But to those who serve
me a new name shall be called, which shall be blessed upon the earth;" for we
have been named Christians, and we have been enriched with the new name, of
which some have been justly deprived who depart from the right things of the
Church; and attaching themselves to wandering spirits and to the
marvel-mongerings of certain unholy false teachers, they have had their name,
and very reasonably; for they are not of Christ, having shared the blasphemy
against him, and having been split into various opinions and judgments,
although


Christ has called his own in peace. and that he himself is one, and has not
been divided; we all therefore are one in Christ, and we have been fitted
toward this in the same mind and in the same judgment, he having bound us into
sameness of soul according to the spiritual union of him who says concerning
us to God and Father I will that as I and you are one, so "also they may be
one in us." Has Christ been divided? was Paul crucified for you? If another
endured the cross for us, let us also be called of him; and if we have been
baptized perhaps into the calling of another, let that one have us, and let us
inscribe him savior and redeemer; but if "Christ died for our sins," and we
have been buried together with him and him alone "through baptism into death,"
let him, he says, be rich in those who have been bought; for we have been
saved through him and by him who laid down his own soul for us. 252 For Christ
did not send me to baptize, but to evangelize. He has been sent, it is
confessed, to proclaim Jesus to the nations; but we say that it is a work of
the apostleship to fulfill as in time and needs, if then he should choose,
also the baptizing of some. but in order that he might not appear to spend the
time of needing to preach on other pursuits, having well left to those
appointed throughout the countries and to all for oversight to do this as at
leisure, he himself rather evangelized, inasmuch as he had been entrusted with
need of the word and had been very greatly accurate in the mystery of Christ;
but just as the divine Stephen, although having been appointed a deacon of
tables, did not make the need to initiate into mysteries a thing to be sent
away, so also the divine Paul although having been set for the need to
evangelize the mystery of Christ, fulfilled according to time also the need of
baptism, the authority lying before him toward both, and nothing barring him
if he should choose to do this. Where is a wise man? where a scribe? where a
disputer of this age? To those who think great things upon worldly wisdom and
do not accept the faith, clearly that toward Christ, the gospel is
foolishness; but to us not so, far from it; for we have been disposed that it
is power of God, and a way of salvation carrying off toward sanctification,
and training toward the knowledge of the truth with wise abundant preachings.
and I at least think that he now calls wise the rhetors, who always weaving
the persuasive with the falsehood by the cleverness of words, were eager to
procure for it the seeming of the truth; and scribes I think the grammatists
or grammarians, whose custom it was to busy themselves with the writings of
the poets and to interpret them to the young; and disputers he says those
accustomed to fight with words and as it were examiners with accuracy of the
253 things, who by findings of thoughts, as if having the seeming to be
unassailable in each of the things set forth, pretend to seek the truth, but
not at all rightly, rather altogether carelessly; for they have been ignorant
of the God of all, who without toil established this universe by ineffable
energies, and brought all the things that were not into being, having only
nodded; therefore the wisdom of the world has truly been made foolish. For
since in the wisdom of God the world did not know God through wisdom, God was
well pleased through the foolishness of the preaching to save those who
believe.
Wisdom of God he names also the human understanding and the fluency, or the
very flowering of the diction; in order that he may teach that God is maker of
mind and supplier of prudence and of fluency, even if some used them toward
things that were not fitting; and foolishness of the preaching, he says the
simplicity of the words; for the divine disciples became unskilled in the
word, but rich in the knowledge. Since also Jews ask a sign and Greeks seek
wisdom. According to both the word of Paul is true; for when once Christ was
driving from the sacred precincts those selling the sheep and the oxen,


turtledoves and doves, and saying "Do not make the house of my Father a house
of merchandise," they were bearing against him saying "What sign do you show
us that you do these things?" and "Who gave you this authority?" and some of
the scribes also were coming after a very great exhibition of signs saying
with love of evil "Teacher we wish to see a sign from you." 254 therefore
counting the voices of the saints worthy of altogether little account, through
which it was likely they would be able to learn that he himself was the
Christ, they were seeking divine signs; whence the Savior said to those sent
by John "Having gone announce to John what you hear and see; blind look up,
lame walk" etc. and the Greeks seeking the splendor in diction and in words,
also consider the manner of the economy with flesh to be foolishness, and they
say For why at all was it needed that the Word from God being God by nature,
being able by will and nod to accomplish the things according to his mind,
become man and endure death? and how would it come about, they say, that a
virgin give birth? and how will that which has been destroyed by death also
rise? but it is not unfitting for them to say such things "For a soulish man
does not receive the things of the Spirit; for it is foolishness to him," but
to us it is power of God and wisdom; for God and Father has saved through him
the world under heaven, as through power that is in him naturally, and of the
ineffable wisdom, his own clearly; for the Son is wisdom and power of God and
Father, through which all things have been brought toward generation, and the
things made are saved. and if someone, he says, should choose to compare the
power in God with the powers of men, he will altogether behold that what seems
of the divine works to have been done perhaps as in weakness, and as it were
not with the whole power of the maker, is beyond and better than all human
strength; and according to this same manner you will also understand
concerning wisdom, that what seems to be foolish is wiser than men. as if
toward the magnitudes and beauties of the elements, I mean of heaven and sun
and of the remaining stars and of fire and water and of the others, someone
should wish to compare the small and least of the creatures, altogether little
power and wisdom will be found in these, and as from 255 being inferior ...
someone would seem to be; but there would not become anyone of those among us
nor of those thus ... a worker. truly therefore, that the weak of God is
stronger than men, and the foolish of God is wiser than men. therefore the
economy has the all-wise, for Christ has reigned understandingly over all
under heaven in wisdom. Announcing to you the testimony of God. Testimony of
God he says Christ; for God and Father has testified upon him saying "This is
my beloved son in whom I was well pleased." And I in weakness and in fear etc.
For although having used so moderate an instruction, I was fearing greatly, he
says, lest perhaps being found better than the mind that is in us, they might
benefit nothing, but rather do injustice; for to those infantile in mind a
tender and easily-grasped word would be fitting toward knowledge, "but solid
food is of the perfect, of those who through habit have the senses exercised
toward discernment of good and evil." therefore, he says, the word toward you
has come as in much weakness, and not in the whole power of the initiator; nor
indeed in persuasive words of human wisdom but in demonstration of spirit and
of power in order that your faith may not be in wisdom of men but in power of
God; for the apostles were adding also divine signs to the preaching, making
the matter a clear demonstration of the need to believe that God has been
testified in these through the Spirit and through the things themselves; as
using some ineffable and unspeakable power and energy, and accomplishing the
wonder-workings easily. for the little myths of the Greeks would reasonably
256 need the word that fights alongside


and as it were wrapping the uncomeliness in them; but a bare and
easily-grasped word, and as testified in power of God, is sufficient for the
divine preaching.
But a wisdom not of this age nor of the rulers of this age who are being
brought to nothing. The word of the holy initiators proceeds through a word
that creeps on the ground, but it injects into us the unadulterated knowledge
of the divine mysteries, and shows Christ being wisdom, not through the fine
speech of the wise in the world, but testified from his own nature, whom none
of the rulers of this age knew, who are the wicked and opposing powers, who
have spoken in the wise of the world. For if they had known, they would not
have crucified the Lord of glory. The word has much likelihood; for Christ has
triumphed through his own cross over the wicked and opposing powers, having
nailed to the wood "the handwriting against us," he drove away Satan from the
tyranny against us, he brought to nothing the sin of the world, having opened
to the spirits below the gates of Hades, he took down the might of death; then
how is the passion upon the cross not a thing hated to the unclean demons?
they would not therefore have crucified, if they had known accurately that he
was the redeemer and Lord of glory. for that it was a work of diabolic
savagery that Christ be crucified through the madness of the Jews, the divine
John showed clearly saying concerning Judas "For after the morsel, he says,
Satan entered into him," who did not know Jesus to be God; for if he both knew
and was persuaded, how was he tempting him in the desert saying "If you are
son of 257 God, say that these stones become breads?" and it has become
confessed that there was also zeal for the Savior himself to escape the notice
of the rulers of this age, in order that he might both suffer having escaped
notice and by his own blood acquire the world under heaven for himself and for
his own Father. And the depths of God. Depths of God the hidden and stored-up
knowledge of the sacred writings, which the Spirit knowing reveals to the
souls of the saints, showing the mind in them to be some divine mind. But a
soulish man. Soulish is the one living according to flesh and not yet having
the mind illuminated through the Spirit, but having only the innate and human
understanding, which the creator casts into the souls of all. But the
spiritual judges all things. The spiritual judges all things and as it were
thins them out with accuracies, but is himself judged by no one; and that
those said to be soulish need altogether spiritual teachers toward benefit and
training, he makes clear adding also and saying For who has known the mind of
the Lord who will instruct him? like as if he would say No one would instruct
the soulish man, unless he should have mind of Christ; and the 258 to instruct
he says instead of he will prepare to understand; and who would be those
having mind of the Lord, again he himself showed saying But we have mind of
Christ, that is the Spirit that speaks in saints, and uncovers mysteries to
them, and shows the mind in them to be some divine mind. therefore of the
things they have written, the better and higher, they have economically
measured the instruction to the habits of those being initiated, to those able
to understand and to speak. And I, brothers, was not able to speak to you as
to spiritual, but as to fleshly, as to infants in Christ. Not as being at a
loss of the word that is able to bring up toward perfect knowledge was I
prevented from speaking to you the more perfect things, but since you are
infants in habit and have the mind not yet firm, not having advanced toward
knowledge of the more perfect things, and not yet able to contain mysteries. I
planted. Paul planted, but Apollos watered, God was calling toward increase.
what then is the planting or the in the beginning of the faith, and that God
is Lord of all to know and to say? and what is the watering or as it were a
laying-down and pouring-on of words from teaching, nourishing toward
robustness the


mind of the one who has believed in order that he may appear also
fruit-bearing? and what is the increase from Christ or the confirmation
through Spirit and running-up "into a perfect man" supplied analogously to the
pursuits and eagernesses of each? 259 You are God's tilled field, God's
building. Since Christ has bought us with his precious blood having endured
the death of the flesh, we are altogether of the one who bought, not of
others, even if some have become toward us ministers and reconcilers and
mediators and initiators of the grace from him; wherefore we are named not of
others but of Christ alone who also is lord of us. As a wise master-builder I
have laid a foundation.
Since he named those who have believed a house of God, he reasonably names
Christ a foundation; and many teachers have come about among the Corinthians,
but Paul boasts to be first of the others, and as it were some firstfruits of
those who evangelized to them the mystery of Christ, who is an unshaken
foundation, holding up those being built upon him, and showing temples, being
fitted together with one another through the faith and of the Holy Spirit's
grace agreeing into sameness of soul; concerning this foundation God and
Father was saying through a prophet "Behold I cast into the foundations of
Zion a costly stone a precious cornerstone, into her foundations, and he who
believes upon him shall not be put to shame." Paul therefore says very well
that he himself has laid the foundation, and that others can work upon and
build, but not still to lay down also another besides the one already lying
which is Jesus Christ; "for there is not another name under heaven given among
men, in which we must be saved." "For there is one God and Father, from whom
are 260 all things and we unto him, and one Lord Jesus Christ, through whom
are all things and we through him, and he himself is the head of the body of
the church." if then anyone denies the foundation and declines the rock, he
does not build upon it but upon the sand; and thus he will also very easily be
shaken down; for what Christ does not hold up, this will altogether be moved,
not having a firm standing. As of my not coming to you some were puffed up.
Since some were in Corinth thinking themselves sufficient and not needing
teachers, and to lift themselves above Paul in wisdom of worldly fluency; and
through this they were being puffed toward disdain standing against the
others, yet not blameless according to the life. since then some have been
puffed up while I am absent and think that through this I stay away as fearing
to come to words with them, which is not so, I will come God willing and
permitting. But I will come quickly to you, if the Lord should will. He
promised to arrive except not altogether, but as was fitting moderately adding
the necessary If the Lord should will; for we must not otherwise either think
or speak or only thus. And I will know not the word of those who have been
puffed up, but the power. What is the benefit of a splendid diction having
nothing of the goods, of which sort that of the Greeks is? but that of the
God-inspired scripture is simple and customary, yet it benefits richly and
carries off splendidly into true knowledge of the 261 God who is by nature;
and it also completes toward this caretakers of every [admir]able deed; which
having come about, one would also be enriched with the noteworthy grace of the
gifts through Spirit; for not in word is the kingdom of the heavens, but in
power; and the In power you will understand, if it should be borne upon the
holy apostles themselves, as in strength and energy through the Spirit,
according to which they are also wonder-workers; but if upon us ourselves, you
will receive what has been said as in power of the holy and sincere life. What
do you will? shall I come to you in a rod or in love? Do you wish, he says,
that I bring a wise rebuke as with some rod upon those


who have sinned? or having been brought in a spirit of meekness and as in love
shall I run past having been silent? but the rebuke was better for them at
once cutting the terrible and the slack and the attempt of such lawlessness;
concerning which also the God-inspired scripture said concerning those from
Israel through the voice of Joel "And a son and his father were going in to
the same maidservant, in order that they might profane the name of their God."
this fornication he says is not even among the nations to be heard; for he had
heard somewhere as is likely of Theseus whetted against his own child, both
having cursed him, and also having killed him slandered from a stepmother; and
certain others mocked by the fine speech of the poets upon such charges. To
give over such a one to Satan for destruction of the flesh.
He showed through the things by which he has given the one who has fornicated
into destruction of the flesh in order that the spirit may be saved, what is
"Not in word but in power is the kingdom of the heavens;" for it is a clear
indication of the 262 authority that has been wondered at and given to him
from above. for our Lord Jesus Christ has honored the saints. To be judged
before the unjust and not before the saints? He calls saints those sanctified
in spirit, and unjust those who have not yet believed as going most readily
toward anything whatever of the evils; for nothing of the out-of-place things
is unpracticed to those who do not yet know the God who is by nature and
truly. Do you not know that the saints will judge the world? And they will
judge it not altogether and in every way sitting as judges; "For there is one
lawgiver and judge;" but by the dignity of the life and by the well-obedience
toward all all but defining the sin of the world, because they have not become
imitators of them. for we all are of one nature, even if having obtained the
earth as mother and being in these perishable bodies, but if some from us have
become both God-loving and good, what word then will henceforth help those who
have chosen the inglorious and accursed life upon the divine tribunal? for it
being possible to be dignified equally with the others, they have gone of
their own will toward anything whatever of the out-of-place things. Do you not
know that we will judge angels? not to speak of things of life? And we will
read the verse as in a question and a slight stop, as this is to be read
according to a question with a slight stop For who is the one daring to say,
that you are unworthy of the so most cheap tribunals, you who by the splendor
of the life judge the world? then he also brings a reasoning of what has been
said establishing the firmness of the word by comparison of the greater. and
angels here not holy, but rather the 263 apostates he says, those who have not
kept their own beginning, who being from above and not burdened with bodies
from earth, will be condemned by the saints according to the equal manner with
the world through the equity of the saints, being accused upon all the most
shameful things. if then the saints are better than angels, how would they be
unworthy of least tribunals upon worldly matters? If then you have tribunals
of things of life. To judge the things of life is altogether small and worth
nothing to the very equitable in church; and we have been taught this also
from the Lord; for someone was coming to him saying "Teacher tell my brother
to divide with me the inheritance; but he to him Man, he says, who set me a
judge or divider over you?" for it would rather be fitting also to the more
intelligent in church to judge the spiritual things, to examine skillfully the
word concerning faith; and this has been numbered among the goods from above
and through the Spirit; for to some is given a word of wisdom and a word of
knowledge, and to others also discernment of spirits. Do you not know that
your bodies are members of Christ? How would our things be members of Christ?
we have him in ourselves both perceptibly and intelligibly; for he dwells in
our hearts through the Spirit,


and we have also partaken of his holy flesh, and we have been sanctified
doubly; and he has dwelt 264 in us as life and life-making, in order that he
may bring to nothing through himself the death that has fallen upon our
members; and this releases us from corruption and from the snares of death and
makes us better than fleshly passions. and we receive in ourselves the
unleavened teaching of the divine oracles that nourishes the governing part,
and we live together with Christ, clearly spiritually, having prevailed over
the passions of the flesh. if then he himself is "the head of the body of the
church," we then are the members according to each; whenever therefore we fall
down into out-of-place pleasures, then indeed then we will sin against him, of
whom we have also become members; for we will as it were gratify fornicators
with his things; his members are defiled by the sin and are made members of a
harlot. for just as the one glued to a woman will be one body toward her, so
also the one bound together with God intelligibly will be one spirit toward
him; for we have become "partakers of divine nature," and we have been joined
and sanctified through the Spirit in Christ.

OF THE SAME CYRIL FROM THE 3RD VOLUME ON THE CORINTHIANS.
Every sin which a man may do is outside the body, but the one who fornicates
sins into his own body. Someone might say Tell me, the murderer, in what
manner will he work the murder? is it not through blood? and if someone should
choose to love-blame others, is he not such through a tongue? then what sort
of the faults will there be which would be done, not through the body? will
then the spirit-bearing one be shown somewhere a speaker of false words? may
it not be; for he speaks in Christ, and Christ is the truth; he will then be
true. if we examine the matter in this way, if someone should choose perhaps
to be crooked in manners, he will altogether be such toward others somewhere
and not toward himself. for the murderer will kill, not himself, but others,
and the reviler will insult not himself, but others, and the greedy will also
do injustice not to himself but upon others, and upon each of the out-of-place
things you will find such a path of some of the things done. therefore
according to this manner Every sin is outside the body, but the one who
fornicates sins into his own body. for he will be caught doing injustice to
his own body itself, 266 and in what manner, I will say. the members of our
body having remained members of Christ through chastity, will altogether
partake of the life and glory from him; "for he will transfigure the body of
our humiliation conformed to the body of his glory;" but if they should become
members of a harlot, how would they receive the transfiguration and the
intelligible splendor of the conformity toward him? for when the bodies from
earth have been raised at the time of the resurrection "The righteous will
shine as the sun," but the impious and profane and those who have lived
out-of-place, will be raised, yet they will not still also live through the
well-famed life, and they remain without share also of glory; for they will
rise not "in judgment nor indeed in counsel of righteous," but upon penalty
and justice, for they will go away into the outer darkness; for how is the
thing being punished in glory? and he adds also something else, not
insufficiently having to persuade to hate the accursed pleasure of the body.
Since both the one who murders and the one who dishonors and the one who
reviles, even if he does the sin with a body, yet nevertheless he practices it
toward others, of these manners it is said Every sin is outside the body, but
the one who fornicates does injustice to his own body itself; our bodies
through chastity remain members of Christ, and they partake of the life and


glory from him; but if it should be defiled, it is deprived of such glory. Do
you not know that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit in you. For the
Spirit "of adoption" has dwelt in us, that is the Holy, "in which we cry Abba
the father;" and we have been bought with a price, our Savior Christ of all
having laid down his own soul for us 267; we are then temples of a living God,
for Christ lodges in us through Holy Spirit, having in his own nature also the
Father and God from whom he has appeared essentially; for he himself said "If
anyone loves me, he will keep my word, and my Father will love him, and we
will come toward him and will make an abode with him." let then the
ill-adorned pleasure be away from our minds as from a temple of God, and let
rather the good smell of self-control come up as in an order of incenses, and
let us present "our bodies a living sacrifice, holy, well-pleasing to God, our
rational worship;" for we were bought "not with corruptible things, with
silver or gold, but with precious blood," according to what is written;
therefore let us serve the one who bought, let us present ourselves to him
into obedience, "and our members weapons of righteousness to God;" for it has
been written that "But the body is not for fornication but for the Lord, and
the Lord for the body." whenever then we keep the members of the body, not
having the defilement from love of flesh, then also the Lord will be for the
body; for being holy by nature as God he dwells in saints. But I say to the
unmarried and to the widows, it is good for them if they remain as I also.
Those from Israel were being caught very light and easily-accessible toward
anything whatever of the things according to pleasure for them; and thus they
were justly deprived by law of the need to give opinions; for upon them, he
says, it is not to add, and from them it is not to take away; but the
introducers of the evangelical decrees, having the mind standing toward the
good, and having had Christ himself speaking in themselves, were reasonably
268 emboldened to make the words of the instruction upon much authority, even
if something of the things to be practiced should not lie beside in the sacred
writings; being some such one also the divine Paul says But I say to the
unmarried and to the widows, it is good for them if they remain as I also, and
the things following; for where a divine law is not for us he has necessarily
set the I say; but where Christ was the one commanding, he says the I charge,
adding at once the Not I, but the Lord; and what then of the necessary things
has been overlooked by Christ legislating? and what do the initiators invent
as the better? and yet how is it not unlearned and altogether stricken not to
think the things from Christ to be in good order? what then shall we say? the
evangelical and divine preaching did not reject marriage, God I think
measuring the commandment somewhere to the nature of man; for the Lord said to
the Pharisees tempting him who asked him "If it is allowed to send away his
wife" not upon fornication, that the one sending away "his wife apart from a
word of fornication, makes her to be adulterated." toward these the disciples
say to him "If the case of the man with the woman is thus, it is not expedient
to marry; but he said to them There are eunuchs who were born thus from a
mother's belly, and there are eunuchs who were made eunuchs by men, and there
are eunuchs who made themselves eunuchs through the kingdom of the heavens;
let the one able to contain contain." for he set it forth for those willing to
accomplish it, yet he did not bring it under a law, because he knew that not
all are able to prevail over the movements of the flesh.
269 And this has altogether something such also the shadow according to law;
for it has commanded to make the manners of the sacrifices into glory of God,
this on the one hand for sin and ignorances, this on the other into cleansing
and throwing-away of filth, and indeed also those on sabbath those according
