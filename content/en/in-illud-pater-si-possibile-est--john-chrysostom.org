On the saying, Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass from me. On
Great Friday.
=61.751=
The teachers of the Church are in no way unlike a child-loving mother, who
endures every toil and labor for the rearing of the dearest children. For as
she, putting forth the breast, furnishes the unwearied nourishment of milk to
the dearest one, so also the teacher of the Church, putting forward the breast
of the mind to the children of the Church, with longing of soul exhorts them
to draw the milk of the word. And just as the mother, after the course of the
rearing from milk, also breaks up the hard-to-crumble bread, and having
seasoned the vegetable foods with condiments, and having thoroughly softened
them with fire, becomes a servant of the life of her own children: in the same
way the teacher also, after the catechesis of the divine oracles, and as it
were after the milk-feeding, having broken up the hard-to-interpret bread of
the word with the sword of the Spirit, and having gathered the varied and
different interpretations and Scriptural contemplations into one blend of
teaching, imparts the word as savory to the children nourished by him.
Therefore we too, beloved, having milk-fed you in the former days with
easily-dissolved words, now let us lead you toward the contemplation of
teachings, the Holy Spirit seasoning for you the word of the teaching as
savory. And such is the bread of the word set before us upon the table of the
Scriptures: Why did our Lord Jesus Christ, having come to take upon himself
the suffering and dying for the life of all, when he came to the celebrated
cross, say: Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass from me. For many,
not having understood the aim of the wisdom, and not having attended to the
treasure hidden in the saying, accuse him of cowardice. But come, we too,
beloved, let us for now converse with him as infants, that we may ferry
across, as to infants, the thoughts given us by him. Let us say then to him,
not as perfect, but as infants: For what reason, Lord, having come to suffer
for us, having come to be crucified, do you cower and pray, saying: Father, if
it is possible, let this cup pass from me? If you emptied yourself, taking the
form of a slave, no one compelling you, why now do you ask another and say:
Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass? And yet you were constrained and
you were hastening to drink this cup. For you said, I have a baptism to be
baptized with; and how am I constrained, until I drink it? and he who is
constrained and hastening to drink it, you decline and say: Father, if it is
possible, let this cup pass. For were you ignorant that you were about to
endure death for us? And yet you yourself said, that: The Son of man must be
delivered up and crucified and on the third day rise. Why then, having come
near the cross, do you decline and say: If it is possible, let this cup pass?
And if you wished this, why did you rebuke your disciple Peter when he was
hindering you? for you said, The Son of man must be crucified and buried and
on the third day rise. And Peter said: Be it far from you, Lord; this shall
not be to you; to whom, being angry, you said, =61.752= Get behind me, Satan;
you are a stumbling-block to me, because you do not mind the things of God,
but the things of men. If then you rebuked him then, as speaking human things,
why now do you seek what that one then was hindering, and say: Father, if it
is possible, let this cup pass?


We learn that you are creator of the things seen and unseen; for you healed
diseases by the touch of a hand, and you set fast by a word bodies of the dead
that had been loosed, and you dried up springs of blood with the hems of
garments, from five loaves you fed five thousand men to satiety, you rebuked
winds and sea, and all things obeyed you with trembling; and the worker of so
many wonders, and the looser of deaths, now you fear death, and say: Father,
if it is possible, let this cup pass? And if you fear death, in whom
thereafter shall we have the hopes of life? and if you fear death, how do you
say, I am the life and the resurrection? for life and resurrection never fear
death. And what is this also that you say: Father, if it is possible, let this
cup pass? Do you then not know, you who know all things before their
generation, do you not know whether it is possible, or impossible, for the cup
to pass? And how did you say: No one knows the Father, except the Son? and you
know the Father, than whom there is nothing greater, but whether it is
possible for the cup to pass, you do not know? And how also does Paul say,
that: There is no creature unmanifest before him, but all things are naked and
laid open to his eyes? and again, For the word of God is living, and active,
and sharper than any two-edged sword, and piercing as far as the division of
soul and spirit, of joints and marrows, and a discerner of the thoughts and
intentions of the heart. If then there is no creature unmanifest before him,
but all things are naked and laid open to his eyes, how do you now, as one not
knowing, say, Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass from me, unless
there be some hidden mystery in the word? This word does not become you, Lord;
for you are God, Son of God, Word, wisdom, power, light, sun of righteousness,
immortal fountain, eternal life, resurrection of us who have been put to death
by sin. Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass. O how hard to reach is
the word, and hard to contemplate, and hard to discern! O how narrow it is and
compressed among many men! Teach us, Lord, this mystery of your word; for we
are yours, and we have believed in you. Let not the giant Eunomius be lifted
up in his blasphemy, nor let him revile the camp of the Lord, Israel; for that
one reviles us, but the insult runs back upon you our Master. Awaken the mind
that lies in our heart, as you did the most meek David; give us one stone of
the word, that having slung it we may dissolve the head of their impiety, that
all the camp may know today, That you are Lord most high, alone over all the
earth. Great is the gathering of heretics that lies upon us, saying what? Do
you see how he fears and cowers? do you see how he prays to the =61.753=
Father, saying: Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass? Now Eunomius
rejoices, now Arius is glad, as having found words that hiss blasphemies
against the truth. But do not rejoice, all you foreigners; for the yoke of him
who smote us has been broken. God is with us. Know, nations, and be defeated.
For if you should again be strong, you shall again be defeated, and whatever
counsel you take, the Lord will scatter it. For the mouth of the Lord has
spoken these things: Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass. O cup
filled with life and immortality! which Eunomius and Arius, having taken,
having muddied with their own evil faith, give their neighbor a treacherous
overthrow to drink, they who think the sweet blend of thoughts in it to be
bitter, and who do not even fear the voice, Woe, as the Scripture says: Woe to
him who gives his neighbor a treacherous overthrow to drink! Woe those who say
the bitter is sweet, and the sweet bitter; who set the light as darkness, and
the darkness as light! Woe those who are wise in themselves, and knowing
before themselves!


But we, beloved, having taken the cup of our Lord, let us with longing of soul
draw the teaching that fills it. Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass.
Not as you think, Arius, does our Lord then cower, or say these things fearing
death; for how would he have feared who has authority to die and not to die?
for he says: I have authority to lay down my soul, and I have authority to
take it again. For if he were being compelled by someone, you would speak well
in saying he cowers; but now he himself says, I have authority to lay down my
soul, and I have authority to take it again; that is. I am not compelled by
anyone, since he also said: I have authority to lay down my soul, and I have
authority to take it; but just as I emptied myself, taking the form of a
slave, no one compelling me, and I built my own body. For wisdom built herself
a house; in the same way I am able also, my body having been loosed by death,
to raise it through the life. For I am he who says, Destroy this temple, and
in three days I will raise it. So then it is not cowering, nor fearing death,
that I say these things, Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass, but
putting forward the word of a hidden mystery. This saying is a bait for the
devil; I must hook him with these words. The devil saw me having done many
signs, that I healed diseases by the touch of a hand, that I put to flight
legions of demons by a word, that I scraped away the hail-spots of lepers with
the word as with a scraper, that I set fast paralyzed knees by a voice, that I
rebuked winds and sea, and all things obeyed me with trembling; and having
seen from the very facts that I am Son of God, he understood that, If I am
crucified, that one has perished; if I go down into the underworld, I will
crush his iron bars, and I will shatter his bronze gates. And having seen
these things, fearing, he flees from setting up the much-honored [the] trophy
of the cross for me. What then do I do? As a wise fisherman I pretend to
cower, I pretend to fear death, and I say: Father, if it is possible, let this
cup pass; that that one, from such humble words of mine, having supposed me to
be a cowardly man, fleeing death, may hasten to set in the midst of the earth
the wood-fixed mystery of the cross, as he thinks, against me. I must then as
a wise fisherman craft against him, I must endure all things for the life of
all. For if that one crafted from the beginning toward the destruction of
Adam, much more will I craft for the salvation of all. With treacherous words
he deceived Adam, with divine words the treacherous one shall be deceived.
Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass from me. If the fisherman, when
he lets down the hook into the sea, does not at one time give slack to the
hook, and at another time show the worm as fleeing, drawing it back with the
hand, the fishes do not leap on. I put around the hook of my divinity the worm
of the body; I let down the hook hidden in the worm of the body into the depth
of this life; if the worm is not moved as a worm, the one who ought to be
hooked does not come upon the hook. I must then fashion myself as a worm, and
speak: For I am a worm, and not a man; that that one =61.754= may run upon it
and touch the hook, and be drawn by me, and the thing written in Job be
fulfilled: And you shall lead the dragon with a hook. I become as a man
cowering, I become as one fleeing death; let me say, My soul is exceeding
sorrowful unto death; let me say, Father, if it is possible, let this cup
pass. Hearing these words, that one rejoices and is glad; for he hastens to do
the things contrary to me. When he hears, Father, if it is possible, let this
cup pass, he is glad; and what does he say in himself? This one too is a man.
I swallowed Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, the patriarchs and the prophets; let me
swallow this one too. For behold, as a man, he cowers; he is a man; let me
swallow him. Let him swallow me as a man, and


he will find me in his belly working as God. Father, if it is possible, let
this cup pass. Let him swallow the leaven of the body, and he will find the
unquenchable coal of the divinity, terribly burning him from within; For I
came to cast fire upon the earth. Father, if it is possible, let this cup
pass. I will speak as a lamb, I do not wish him to see the flashing sword of
the divinity, lest the wolf flee. Let him swallow me as a lamb, and he shall
be pierced through from within by the three-edged sword; For I did not come to
cast peace to him, but a sword. Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass.
I hide the shepherd God in the lamb of the body; I do not wish to make the
shepherd God manifest, lest the wolf flee; if he sees the shepherd, the wolf
withdraws. I will speak as a lamb. Father, if it is possible, let this cup
pass. I will speak as a lamb not having a shepherd; for I became as a man
without help. Let him swallow me as a lamb, and he shall be shattered by the
shepherd within with the staff of the cross. He shall chew the grain of
mustard, and he will find the pungency of the divinity terribly torturing him;
he shall chew me as a man, and he will find the firm rock violently breaking
his teeth; that he may learn no longer to chew men; and the thing written
shall be fulfilled, But God shall shatter his teeth in his mouth. Father, if
it is possible, let this cup pass. If he knew of what sort this cup is, which
I with irony pray away, that one would have prayed that it pass; for it is a
goad against him, but for my own a salvation. When he swallows me, then he
will perceive whom he swallowed; when he sees the sun vanishing because of me,
the day being darkened, then he too shall be made bitter; and the thing
written shall be fulfilled: Hades below was made bitter meeting you. Father,
if it is possible, let this cup pass. Let not such words, spoken in wisdom,
scandalize you then; wait and you shall see the issue of my words, and then
you shall marvel at the wisdom of my sayings. He thinks my craft to be
foolish; and he does not know that The foolishness of God is wiser than men;
he thinks from such things that I am most weak, and he does not know that The
weakness of God is stronger than men; he does not know, that I indeed drink
the cup, but that one shall be made drunk and darkened, but my own shall be
awakened and raised. Now he rejoices hearing such words of mine, Father, if it
is possible, let this cup pass; when however he swallows me, when he sees
rocks splitting, and the monuments and the tombs being burst open, and that
one himself being torn, and the dead leaping up and springing into heavens,
then he shall repent and mourn over the things at which he now rejoices. He
does not know that the snare which he sets, he sets for himself, and he
himself shall fall into it, according to what is written: He dug a pit, and
excavated it, and he shall fall into the hole which he made; he does not know
that the cross which he is about to set up is for me a bridal chamber, but for
him a cross; for me it is a bride-chamber, but for him death. O cup, goad of
the devil, putter-to-flight of demons, winnowing-place of sins, workshop of
life, mercy-seat of sinners, cup of immortality! Nevertheless, not as I will,
but as you. The wretched one knew that, as many things as I said, came to
pass; he heard me saying: I thank you, Father, that you heard me; and I knew
that you always hear me. But now since I prayed that the cup of death pass,
lest, having supposed through my prayer the cup to be pas
=61.755= sing, he become slack about the matter of the cross, I make the
issue of my prayer doubtful, and I say, Nevertheless not as I will, but as
you. I attach to my Father the authority of death; and yet I myself have
authority to lay down my soul, and I have authority to take it again. I hide
my divine dignity with rational leaves; I hide the authoritative power of my
divinity with


human words; I say, Not as I will, but as you. The blind one does not know
that my will and that of my Father is one. For all things whatsoever the
Father has are mine; and, I and the Father are one. Nevertheless not as I
will, but as you. He shall hang the grain of wheat, and when he swallows me,
you shall see him terribly hanged by the three-stranded cord. But perhaps
someone of those who hear will say: And did Christ then mock the devil? or was
it permitted to mock him? To whom we shall say, Read the Scriptures, and you
will find the Father himself saying to Moses: Do you see how many things I
have mocked the Egyptians? Therefore the Lord also mocked the devil and the
demons. For he who was speaking great things had to be jeered at as mindless,
saying: By my strength I will do it, and by the understanding of my hands I
will take away the bounds of nations, and the whole inhabited world, as a nest
I shall ta =61.756= ke, and as eggs left behind I will take up, and there
shall be no one who will speak against me. He then had to be mocked, and the
demons, that he might show those who boast such things to be even more
mindless than children. Therefore they did not even know what they were
doing; For if they had known, they would not have crucified the Lord of
glory. And the prophet also was proclaiming beforehand concerning this
dragon, that he shall be mocked, saying: This dragon, whom you formed to
mock him. But that we may not,
lengthening the word too much, seem to hinder those after us who wish to hand
on more brightly the sense of the Scripture, let us here furl our word with
silence. For we were led to speak upon this very heading, not as being able to
speak without omission, but to narrate according to our own power, and to give
a starting-point to those who love learning; For give a starting-point to a
wise man, and he will be wiser. And you too, beloved, having gathered from the
Scriptures toward the confirmation of the things said, still more whiten the
hard-to-contemplate sense. For it is not because of cowardice that our Lord,
as we said before, prays away the cup of death, but by a divine economy; that
from my least words and thoughts and from your labors glory may be sent up to
Christ, now and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
