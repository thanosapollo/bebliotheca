* MYRSON
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Will you now, Lycidas, sing some sweet Sicilian song for me,
a longing, sweet-hearted, erotic one, such as the Cyclops
Polyphemus sang to sea-shore Galatea?

* LYCIDAS
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h2
:END:

And to pipe is dear to me, Myrson; but what shall I sing?

* MYRSON
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3
:END:

The sweet love of Scyros, Lycidas, that is envied,
the secret kisses of the son of Peleus, the secret bed,
how the boy put on a robe, and how he faked the form
and how among the girls of Lycomedes Deidameia,
handling him, knew Achilles in the bridal chamber.

* LYCIDAS
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4
:END:

Once the cowherd seized Helen, and led her to Ida,
a bad grief to Oenone. And Lacedaemon was angered,

and gathered all the Achaean people, nor did any Greek
neither of Mycenaeans nor of Elis nor of Laconians
remain in his house, fleeing wretched Ares.
And Achilles alone was hidden among the girls of Lycomedes,
and he was taught wools instead of weapons, and with a white hand
he had a maiden's labor, and he appeared as a girl;
for he was made female equal to them, and so much a bloom
purpled on snowy cheeks, and the walk
he walked of a maiden, and he packed his hair with a veil.
But he had a man's heart, and he had a man's love;
and from dawn until night he sat beside Deidameia,
and sometimes he kissed her hand, and often of her
he lifted the fair warp, and praised the cunning threads;
and he ate not with another age-mate, and he did all things
hastening toward a common sleep. And he also spoke a word to her:
/other sisters sleep with one another,/
/but I alone, and you alone sleep, bride./
/the two maiden age-mates, the two fair;/
/but we sleep alone according to beds; and the wicked/
/Nysaean deceitful one badly parts me from you./
/for I of you .../
