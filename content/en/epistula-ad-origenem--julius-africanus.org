Of Africanus concerning the story of Susanna.
Greetings, my lord and son and in all things most honorable Origen, from
Africanus. When you were making the sacred dialogue toward the ungrateful
one, you recalled the prophecy of Daniel in youth; and as was fitting, I
welcomed it then. But I wonder how this part of the book escaped you, being
spurious; for this pericope is a graceful 79 writing otherwise, but it is
shown to be a recent and fabricated writing and is refuted in many ways.
For when Susanna had been ordered to die, the prophet, seized by a spirit,
cried out that the sentence was unjust. First, that Daniel prophesies in
another manner by visions and dreams through all time and obtains
appearances of an angel, but not by prophetic inspiration. Then after
having somehow thus uttered this wondrously, he also somehow convicts them
most paradoxically, as not even the mime of Philistion. For the rebuke
through the Spirit was not enough, but having set each apart privately he
asks where he saw her committing adultery. And when the one said under a
holm-oak (prinos), he answers that the angel will saw (prisein) him, and to
the one who said under a mastic (schinos) he likewise threatens that he
will be split (schisthenai). Now in Greek utterances such things happen to
sound alike, the sawing (prisai) from the holm-oak (prinos) and to split
(schisai) from the mastic (schinos), but in the Hebrew they differ
entirely. And from the Hebrews to the Greeks was translated all that is
current of the old covenant. But among the Jews, being captives in
Babylonia, being strangled and thrown unburied in the streets, as is
related in the former captivity of Israel, and their sons being taken away
for castration and their daughters for concubines, as had been prophesied,
they were not then also judging concerning death, 80 and these things
concerning the wife of Jehoiakim who had become their king, whom the king
of the Babylonians had made a colleague of the throne; and if not this
man, but some other Jehoiakim of the people, whence did such a residence
belong to a captive, and was there a wide-spreading garden? And before all
these things this pericope, together with two others at the end, is not
contained in the Daniel received from the Jews. And upon all, of so many
prophets who have gone before in succession, no one has used another's
saying or thought; for their word, being true, was not impoverished; but
this one, threatening the one of those two, reminds of the Lord saying:
You shall not kill the innocent and righteous. From all these things it
seems to me that the pericope is an addition. But also the character of
the diction differs. I have knocked; but do you also sound back to me and,
writing in reply, instruct. Greet all my lords; all who know you greet
you. I pray that you be in health together with the company [and]
according to mind toward God.
