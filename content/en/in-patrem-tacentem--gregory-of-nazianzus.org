ORATION XVI.
On his father silent because of the plague of hail.
A. Why do you undo a praised order? Why do you force a tongue =35.936= that is
servant to law? Why do you call forth a word that yields to the Spirit? Why,
leaving the head, have you hurried to the feet? Why, passing by Aaron, do you
put forward Eleazar? I do not accept that a spring be blocked, and a torrent
be carried along; that a sun be hidden, and a star be shown; that gray hair
withdraw, and youth legislate; that wisdom be silent, and inexperience play
the young man. Nor is the greater rain altogether more useful than the lesser;
for what then? If the one, being more violent, sweeps away the earth, and
damages the farmer besides in his capital; while the other, flowing gently,
and sinking into the depths, fattens the field, and benefits the one who
plowed, and nourishes an ear into ripe fruit. Nor among words is the more
abundant more profitable than the wiser. For the one perhaps, having gladdened
a little, has gone away, and together with the struck air has dissolved,
having been able to do nothing beyond this, and has bewitched the greedy
hearing with fluency. But the other has passed into the mind and, having
opened the mouth wide, has filled it with Spirit, and has appeared longer than
the generation, and has farmed many things in few syllables.
B. And I do not yet speak of the true and first wisdom, which this wonderful
farmer and shepherd bears away as first prize. First wisdom is a praiseworthy
life, both purified for God, or being purified, for the most pure and most
bright, who asks from us only one sacrifice, purification, which it is dear to
Scripture to call a contrite heart, and a sacrifice of praise, and a new
creation in Christ, and a new man, and such things. First wisdom is to look
down on the wisdom that lies in speech, and in turns of words, and in the
counterfeit and superfluous antitheses. But for me may it be to speak five
words in church with understanding, rather than ten thousand in a tongue, and
with an unmeaning trumpet-voice, that does not raise my soldier to the
spiritual war. This wisdom I praise, this I embrace, through which the ignoble
were glorified, and into which the despised were preferred, and with which
fishermen netted the whole inhabited world with the bonds of the Gospel,
having conquered the wisdom that is being abolished by the completed and
cut-short word. For the one wise in speech is not wise to me, nor whoever has
a well-turning tongue, but an unstable and uneducated soul, like =35.937=
those tombs which, being fair and comely without, rot with corpses within, and
cover much stench; but whoever speaks little about virtue, yet shows much by
what he works, and adds credibility to the word through his life.
C. Better to me is beauty that is seen than that painted in speech; and wealth
which the hands hold, than that which dreams fashion; and wisdom, not that
which is made splendid by speech, but that which is proven through works. For
a good understanding is to all who do it, he says, but not to those who
proclaim it; and of this wisdom the most exact test is time, and a crown is
truly old age of boasting. For if one must not call a man blessed before the
end, as it seems both to Solomon and to me, and it is unclear what will be
born from the coming day, our life below having many turns, and the body of
humiliation being moved up and down and falling over; how is he not also
safer, and for this more blessed, who has emptied the much of life
blamelessly, and is as already toward the harbors of the common sea of life,
than the one who still has a long voyage?
D. Do not then shut a tongue that has spoken many things well, whose fruits
are many, and many the offspring of righteousness; whose children, how many
they are, and who the treasures, lift your eyes round about, and see: all this
people, whom you begot in Christ through the Gospel. Do not then grudge us the
useful, more than a few words, nor already give the prelude of the coming
loss. Speak, briefly indeed, but things dear to me and most sweet; not
audible, but known by the spiritual cry, according to which God also hears
Moses when he is silent; and, Why do you cry to me? is said to the one who
converses intellectually. Fit the people for me, for your nursling, and after
this for a shepherd, and now also for a chief-shepherd. Teach me something
about the pastoral art, this people about ready obedience; philosophize
something about the present plague, about the just judgments of God, whether
we grasp them, or whether we are ignorant of the great abyss. How both mercy
is by measures, =35.940= according to the holy Isaiah (for not even the good
is without judgment, even if it seemed so to those who had already toiled in
the vineyard, not understanding the unequal in the equality), and wrath is
according to the account of the sins, being called a cup in the hand of the
Lord, and a bowl of falling drunk down, even if from all some of the desert is
taken away, and the unmixed of wrath he mixes with loving-kindness; bending
from the severe toward the yielding for those being trained by fear, and for
those who from the small affliction conceive and travail with conversion, and
bring to birth a perfect spirit of salvation; yet watching still for the lees,
the last of the wrath, that he may empty it all upon those not being healed
from the kindness, but even being hardened according to the heavy-hearted
Pharaoh, and bitter taskmaster, stored up as a proof of the power of God
against the ungodly.
E. Say, from where such plagues and scourges? and what is the account
concerning these things? Is it some unordered motion of the all, and uneven,
and ungoverned both drift and irrationality, as if no one were overseeing the
things that are, and chance brings these things, as it seems to those unwisely
wise, and to those carried at random by the unordered and dark spirit; or by
some reason and order, just as the all was set up from the beginning, and was
mixed, and was bound together, and was moved in a world-fitting way, as known
only to the one who moved it, so also it is moved about and transferred, being
led by the reins of Providence? From where barrennesses, and wind-blights, and
hails, our present plague and admonition? From where corruptions of airs, and
diseases, and shakings of earth, and uprisings of sea, and the terrors from
heaven? And how is the creation that was made for the enjoyment of men, the
common delight and of equal honor, changed into the punishment of the ungodly,
that by those things with which we were honored and did not give thanks, by
these we may also be trained, and may know the power from what we suffer,
since we did not know it from the good things we have experienced? How to some
are the sins given double from the hand of the Lord, and the measure of
wickedness is filled up by the doubling, according to which Israel also is
brought to sense; but to others, through the sevenfold being repaid into the
bosom, the sins are emptied? And what is the measure of the Amorites not yet
filled? And how is the sinner either let go, or punished =35.941= again; the
one perhaps being kept for there, the other being healed from here? And how
does the just man either suffer ill, being tried perhaps, or fare well, being
preserved, if indeed he is poor in mind, and not very much above the things
seen, and as conscience teaches each of these, the own and unlying tribunal?
What is the plague, and from where? Whether a proof of virtue, or a test of
wickedness, and that it is better to be bent as by punishment, even if it is
not so, and to be humbled under the mighty hand of God, than to be lifted as
by a trial. Teach these things and admonish, that we not bear the present
plague too impatiently, or, having fallen into a depth of evils, become
contemptuous (for there is also such a passion among the many), but that we be
of sound mind toward the admonition, and not call forth the greater, through
insensibility toward this one.
F. Terrible is barrenness of earth, and loss of fruits; and how not, when they
were already gladdening with hopes, and drawing near to the storehouses? And
terrible is an untimely harvest, and farmers looking sullen at their toils,
and as sitting beside the offspring as dead, which a tame rain nourished, and
a wild one harvested; of which the reaper did not fill his hand, nor the one
gathering the sheaves his bosom; nor did he obtain the blessing upon them,
which those who pass by grant to farmers. And a pitiable sight, earth
insulted, and shorn, and not having her own adornment. Which the blessed Joel
laments, destruction of earth, and trial of famine, if anyone else, having
made a tragedy of it; and another prophet laments, setting the last disorder
against the former good order, in the things he says speaking of the wrath of
the Lord, who breaks the earth: Before him a paradise of delight, and behind a
plain of disappearance. These things then are terrible, and beyond terrible,
so long as the present things alone grieve, and the sense of a harder plague
does not yet pain; since, just as in diseases, the passion that is always
grieving is more painful than the one not present; but still more terrible
than these the treasuries of the wrath of God hold among themselves, of which
may you not take experience; nor indeed will you take it, fleeing to the
compassions of God, and drawing the one who wills mercy by tears, and turning
away the sequel of the wrath through conversion. Still these things are
gentleness, and loving-kindness, and tame training, and elements =35.944= of a
plague that pedagogues infancy; still smoke of wrath, a prelude of torments;
but not yet a fire being kindled, the peak of the motion; nor coals being lit,
the last of the scourge; of which he threatened one part, and stretched out
another, and held back one by force, and brought another on; likewise both
training by the plague and by the threat, and making a path for his wrath,
through excess of goodness; beginning from the lesser, so as not to need the
more violent; but training also by the greater, if he should be forced toward
these.
G. I know a sword being polished, and a drunken knife in heaven, commanded to
slaughter, to set at naught, to make childless, not to spare as far as flesh,
and marrows, and bones. I know the Impassible as a bear at a loss, and as a
leopard meeting on the way of the Assyrians, not of those then only, but also
if anyone now is an Assyrian in wickedness; and it is not possible to flee the
might of his wrath and the speed, when he watches over our ungodly deeds, and
zeal pursues his enemies, knowing how to eat the adversaries. I know a shaking
out, and a shaking up, and a boiling, and a breaking of heart, and a loosening
of knees, and such being the penalties of the ungodly; for I leave aside
speaking of the tribunals from there, to which the sparing here hands over, as
it is better to be trained and purified now, than to be sent on to the torment
from there, when it is a time of punishment, not of purification. For just as
he who remembers God here is better than death, and this has been most finely
philosophized by the divine David; so there is not in Hades for those who have
gone away confession and correction. For God has shut up here both life and
action, and there the examination of the things done.
H. What shall we do in a day of visitation, with which one of the prophets
frightens me, whether of God's pleading toward us, or of that upon the
mountains and hills, which we heard, or of whatever kind and upon whomsoever
it comes, when he both confutes toward us, and sets himself against our face
=35.945= setting the sins, the bitter accusers, and setting against the things
by which we have been well treated the things we have broken the law, and
striking reasoning with reasoning, and correcting deed with deed, and
demanding the dignity of the image, which has been muddied and confused with
wickedness, at last he leads them away condemned by themselves, and sentenced,
and not even having it to say that we suffer unjust things, which here is
sometimes enough for those who suffer as a consolation of the condemnation?
I. But there who is an advocate? What pretext? What false defense? What artful
persuasiveness? What device against the truth will cheat the court, and steal
the right judgment, which sets all things for all in a balance, both deed, and
word, and thought, and weighs the better against the wicked, that the sinking
side may win, and the vote come with the greater, after which there is no
appeal, no higher judge, no defense through second works, no oil from the wise
virgins, or from those who sell, for the lamps that fail, no repentance of a
rich man melting in flame, and seeking correction for his own, no appointed
time of change; but both only, and last, and fearful is the tribunal, and just
more than it is fearful, or rather for this even more fearful, that it is also
just? When thrones are set, and the Ancient of days sits in front, and books
are opened, and a river of fire is drawn, and the light is before, and the
darkness prepared; and they who have done the good things will go into a
resurrection of life, that which is now hidden in Christ, and later is
manifested together with him; but they who have practiced the worthless things
into a resurrection of judgment, which those who have not believed have
already been condemned by the word that judges them; and the ones the
unspeakable light will receive, and the vision of the holy and royal Trinity
shining more clearly and more purely, and mixing whole with the whole mind,
which indeed I set as the kingdom of heaven especially and alone; but for the
others, with the other torments, or rather before the others, the having been
cast away from God, and the shame in the conscience that has no end. And these
things later.
J. But now what shall we do, brothers, broken, and humbled, and drunk, not
from sikera, nor from wine, which shakes and darkens for a little, but from
the plague which the Lord has brought on =35.948=, who says: And you, heart,
be shaken and be moved; and who gives the despisers to drink a spirit of grief
and of compunction; to whom it is also said: See, you despisers, and look, and
wonder, and vanish? How shall we bear his rebukes; or what answer shall we
give, when upon the multitude of the benefactions, in which we have remained
ungrateful, he still also reproaches us with the plagues, and counts out the
healing, from which we have not been healed? And he may say children, but
blameworthy of good things, and sons, but alien and limping from their own
paths through pathlessness and roughness; How ought you, and from where, to
have been trained, and I have not trained? Through milder drugs? I brought
them on. I passed by the Egyptian blood being drunk from springs and rivers
and every gathering of water, the first plague. I passed by the frogs, and the
gnat, and the dog-fly, the next scourges; but from the cattle, and the oxen,
and the sheep I began, the fifth plague, and I struck the irrational, still
sparing the rational. The breaking was nothing to you; but you have become to
me even more irrational than those struck, and more untrained. I held back the
rain from you; one portion was rained on; and a portion upon which I did not
rain was dried; and you said: We will play the man. I brought hail upon you,
training by the contrary plague, I harvested your vineyards and thickets and
offspring; and I did not crush your wickedness.
K. I know that you are hard, and an iron sinew is your neck; these things
perhaps he will say to me, who am not even admonished by the plagues; he who
sets aside sets aside; he who breaks the law breaks the law; the admonition
from above is nothing; the scourges are nothing. The bellows has failed, the
lead has failed, which things I also earlier reproached you through Jeremiah;
In vain the silversmith silversmiths; your wickednesses have not been melted.
Will you wait for me, says the Lord, being angered? Or has my hand not
strength to bring on other plagues also? There are still with me also blisters
boiling up from furnace soot, which Moses, or if anyone according to him is a
servant of a motion of God, sprinkling into heaven, trains Egypt with disease.
There is still also locust, and a darkness that may be felt, and the last
plague in order, but first in pain and in power, the destruction and loss of
the firstborn, which, in order to flee, and to turn away the destroyer, it is
better to anoint the doorposts of the mind, contemplation and action, with the
great and saving seal, with the blood of the New Covenant, being crucified
together with Christ =35.949= and dying together, so as also to rise together,
and to be glorified together, and to reign together, both now and at his last
appearing; but not to be broken, nor to be crushed, nor to lament, when the
Evil one strikes us untimely, and in this dark life, and destroys the
firstborn, and the offspring and motions of our life consecrated to God.
L. May it not be for me, with the other plagues, to be reproached with these
also by the Good, who comes toward me with a sideways wrath because of my
sidewaysness: I struck you with jaundice, and with burning, and with gangrene;
and nothing more. A sword from without made you childless, and not even so did
you turn toward me, says the Lord. May I not become a vineyard of the beloved,
after being planted and trenched, and secured with a hedge, and a tower, and
with all things, as much as was possible, running wild and bearing thorns, and
for this being despised, so that the tower is taken down, and the hedge taken
away, and I am not pruned, nor dug, but become for all a plunder, and a common
insult, and a trampling. These things are my fear and word; and thus I bear
the plague impatiently, and I pray such a prayer, which I will also add to the
things said: We have sinned, we have broken the law, we have been ungodly,
because we have forgotten your commandments, and have gone after our wicked
mind; because we have lived unworthily of the calling and of the Gospel of
your Christ, and of his holy sufferings, and of the emptying for us; because
we have become a reproach to your beloved. Priest and people have gone aside
together; all have turned away, together we have become useless. There is none
who does judgment and righteousness, there is not even one. We have shut your
compassions, and your loving-kindness, and bowels of mercy of our God, through
our wickedness, and the evil of our practices, with which we have walked. You
are kind, but we have broken the law; you are long-suffering, but we are
worthy of plagues. We know your goodness, even though we are without
understanding; we have been scourged with few of the things we have sinned.
You are fearful, and who will stand against you? Trembling will take mountains
from you, and to the greatness of your arm who will contend? If you shut the
heaven, who will open? and if you loose your cataracts, who will hold them
together? It is a light thing in your eyes to make poor =35.952= and to make
rich, to make live and to make die, to strike and to heal; and your having
willed is a completed action. You were angered, and we sinned, says one of
those of old, making confession. But for me it is also time to say the
contrary: We sinned, and you were angered; for this we have become a reproach
to our neighbors. You turned away your face, and we were filled with
dishonors. But cease, Lord; let go, Lord; be propitiated, Lord; do not give us
over to the end because of our lawlessnesses, nor train others through our
scourges, when it is possible for us to be brought to sense through the
torment of others; of whom these? Nations that do not know you, and kingdoms
that have not been subjected to your might. But we are your people, Lord, and
the rod of your inheritance. For this train us, only in kindness, and not in
your wrath; lest you make us few, and a nothing beside all who dwell the
earth.
M. With these words I draw the mercy; and if it were possible to propitiate
concerning the wrath with whole burnt offerings or sacrifices, I would not
have spared even these. But you yourselves also imitate a timid priest; yes,
beloved children, yes, sharers of both divine admonition and loving-kindness.
Gain your souls in tears; stay the wrath, having made your practices better.
Sanctify a fast, proclaim a service; these things the blessed Joel commands
you with us. Gather elders, infants sucking breasts, an age pitiable, and
especially worthy of the loving-kindness of God. And I know the things which
he commands both to me the minister of the Lord and to you, who have been
counted worthy of the same glory, to enter in sackcloth, and to beat night and
day between the pavement and the altar, pitiable in the figure, more pitiable
in the voices, crying earnestly both for themselves and for the people,
sparing nothing, neither toil, nor word, by which God is propitiated; Spare,
Lord, your people, saying, and do not give your inheritance to reproach, and
as many things as follow of the petition; having so much more of the
affliction, as also of the dignity, training the people through themselves
toward compunction, and correction of wickedness, and the long-suffering of
God that follows these, and a holding back of the scourge.
N. Come then all, brothers, let us worship, and fall down, and weep before
=35.953= the Lord who made us. Let us set a common mourning, divided both by
ages and by kinds. Let us lift the voice of the supplication; instead of the
cry hated by him, let us bring this up into the ears of the Lord of Sabaoth;
Let us anticipate his wrath in confession. Let us will to see him, as he has
been angered, so also being changed. Who knows, he says, if he will turn, and
repent, and leave a blessing? I know this clearly, I a surety of the
loving-kindness of God. And what is against nature for him, having left the
wrath, he will go toward what is according to nature, the mercy. For toward
that he is forced by us; but toward this he has the impulse. And if he strikes
being forced, how will he not let go, using his nature? Only let us have mercy
on ourselves, having opened a way to the just bowels of the Father. Let us sow
in tears, that we may reap in gladness. Let us become Ninevites, not
Sodomites; let us heal the wickedness, not be finished with the wickedness.
Let us hear Jonah preaching, lest we be flooded with fire and brimstone. And
if we go out of Sodom, let us go toward the mountain, let us flee to Zoar, let
us enter together with the rising sun. Let us not stand in all the surrounding
country; let us not look around, lest we be fixed into a pillar of salt, a
pillar truly immortal, and an accusation of a soul turning back toward
wickedness.
O. Let us know that to sin nothing is truly above man, and of God alone; for I
leave aside saying something about angels, lest we give occasion to the
passions, and a door to wicked contradictions; but the being unhealed belongs
to the wicked and opposing nature, and of those being worked by that. But the
turning when we sin belongs to men, but to reasonable ones, and of the portion
being saved. For even if the dust drags something of wickedness, and the
earthly tent weighs down the mind being carried up, or having been created to
be carried up; yet let the image cleanse away the mud, and set the yoked flesh
up, lightening it with the wings of the word. And it is better neither to need
such a purification, nor to be purified, the first dignity remaining to us,
=35.956= toward which we also hasten from the pedagogy here, nor to fall from
the tree of life by the bitter taste of sin; but it is better, than not to be
trained when we fail, to turn when we sin. For whom the Lord loves he trains,
and the rebuke is fatherly; but every soul unadmonished is unhealed. Therefore
it is not hard to be struck, but it is harder not to be brought to sense by
the plague. One of the prophets says, speaking about Israel, the hard and
uncircumcised in heart: Lord, you have scourged them, and they have not
grieved; you have trained them, and they have not willed to receive training;
and again, And the people did not turn, until they were struck; and, Why is it
that my people has turned a wicked turning, from which they will be wholly
crushed, and destroyed?
P. Fearful then, brothers, is it to fall into the hands of a living God; and
fearful is the face of the Lord upon those who do evil things, and wiping out
the wickedness with utter destruction; and fearful is a hearing of God, even
perceiving the voice of Abel through the silent blood; and fearful are feet
that overtake wickedness; and fearful is the filling of the all, so that there
is nowhere to flee a motion of God, neither having flown up into heaven, nor
having gone into Hades, nor having run away toward the east, nor having hidden
in depths or limits of sea. And before me Nahum the Elkoshite also fears, in
the things in which he publishes the burden against Nineveh, the jealous God,
and a Lord who avenges with wrath the adversaries, and using such a surplus of
severity, as not even to leave a second vengeance against the wicked. For when
I hear Isaiah threatening the people of Sodom and the rulers of Gomorrah, and
saying: Why should you still be struck, adding lawlessness? I am almost both
filled with shuddering, and confused with tears. It is not possible, he says,
to find an addition of plague for the addition of sin; so you have gone
through all things, and have emptied every kind of plague, always calling
forth some newer one through your own wickedness. There is no wound, nor
bruise, nor inflamed plague; the plague is of the whole body, he says, but
also unhealed. For there is no poultice to lay on, nor oil, nor bandages. And
I pass by the things that follow of the threat, lest I become heavier than
your present plague. =35.957=
Q. Only let us recognize the subject of the evil. From where have farmings
been dried, storehouses been put to shame, and pasture of flocks failed, the
fair things of the earth been lessened, the plains not been filled with
fatness, but with dejection; the valleys not multiplied wheat, but been wept
over; the mountains not dripped sweetness, as later for the just, but been
stripped of adornment and dishonored, and received the curse of Gilboa from
the contrary things? All the earth has become as from the beginning, before it
was adorned with its own beauties. You visited the earth, and you made it
drunk, but a wicked visitation and a destructive drunkenness. Alas for the
sight! the things of our fruitfulness are in stubble, and the sowing is known
by small remnants, and scarcely does our harvest bear firstfruits to the Lord,
being grasped by months rather than by sheaves. Such is the wealth of the
ungodly, such the farmings of those who sow badly; to look for more, as the
ancient curse, and to bring in few; to sow, and not to reap; to plant, and not
to press; where ten yokes of oxen work, to make one jar; and to hear of good
fruit among others, themselves being pressed by want. From where these things,
and what is the cause of the breaking? Let us not wait to be confuted by
others; let us become examiners of ourselves. A great drug of wickedness is
both confession, and flight from the failure. I first, just as I announced to
my people from above, and have fulfilled the work of the watchman (for I did
not hide the coming sword, that I might preserve both my own soul, and those
of the hearers); so I will also announce the disobedience of my people, making
his things my own; if somehow I might thus obtain some loving-kindness and
refreshing.
R. One of us has squeezed a poor man, and has torn away a portion of land, and
has overstepped a boundary badly, either having stolen, or having played the
tyrant, and has joined house to house, and field to field, that he may take
something from the neighbor; and he has contended that no one have a neighbor,
as if he alone would dwell upon the earth. And another has defiled the earth
with interests and excesses, and gathering from where he did not sow, and
reaping where he did not scatter; farming not the earth, but the need of those
who lack. And another has robbed God who has given all things of firstfruits
of threshing-floor and winepress, and has become both ungrateful together and
foolish; neither having given thanks for what he had, nor having worked the
future, if nothing else, through goodwill =35.960=. And another has not had
mercy on widow and orphan, nor shared bread and a little food with the one in
need, or rather with Christ who is fed through those who are even little fed;
the one who has the many things perhaps, and beyond hope, (for this is already
the most unjust,) and is straitened by the many storehouses, and filling some,
and taking others down, that he may build greater for the coming fruits, not
knowing that he is seized away before the hopes, and will give an account of
the plenty here and of the fancy, having become a bad steward of others'
goods. And another has turned aside a way of the humble, and has made a just
man go sideways in unjust things. And another has hated one who confutes in
the gates, and has abhorred a holy word. And another has sacrificed to his own
net that gathers many things, and having the plunder of the poor in the
houses, either has not remembered God, or has remembered badly, Blessed is the
Lord, having said, because we have become rich; and he has supposed
lawlessness, as having these things from him, from whom he will be punished.
For because of these things the wrath of God comes upon the sons of
disobedience; because of these, either heaven is shut, or is opened badly; and
much more, if not even having been struck we would turn, and would draw near
to the one who draws near by nature.
S. What shall we say to these things, we the grain-buyers, and grain-dealers,
and watchers of the difficulties of the times, that we may have plenty, and
may revel in the disasters of others, and may acquire, not as Joseph the
things of the Egyptians, for a greater stewardship (for that man knew both to
gather and to give grain well, just as also to know the famine beforehand, and

acquisition; who worship the gold and the silver, as those of old Baal, and
Astarte, and the abomination Chemosh; who honor the costly and transparent
things of stones, and the soft and flowing of clothing, a spending of moths,
and of robbers, =35.961= and treasures of tyrants, and of thieves; who exult
in a multitude of slaves and of four-footed things; who are broadened in the
plains and in the mountains, and having some things, and taking on others, and
about to take others, according to the Solomonic leech that cannot be filled,
likewise with Hades, and earth, and fire, and water, and seeking another
inhabited world for acquisition, and blaming the bounds of God as smaller than
according to their own desire and greed? And what of those who sit on the high
thrones, and raise the ruling tent, and the brow higher than the theaters, and
do not reckon the God over all, and the unapproachable height of the true
kingship, that they may rule the subjects as fellow-slaves, they who need the
equal loving-kindness? And consider for me also those who live softly on ivory
couches, whom the divine Amos well takes hold of, and who anoint themselves
with the first myrrhs, and who clap to the voice of the instruments, and who
hang upon those fleeing as if they were standing; but not both sharing the
pain and suffering with the crushing of Joseph; when they ought to be kind to
those who have fallen first, and to acquire mercy by mercy; to wail for pine,
because cedar has fallen, and to be admonished by the plague of the neighbors,
and through the evils of others to set their own things well, having so much
more than those who have gone before, as to be saved themselves through those,
and not for others to be brought to sense through them.
T. Philosophize these things with us, O divine and holy head, and one that has
gathered much experience by the long time, from which wisdom comes. Restore
your people by these. Teach to break bread for the hungry, and to bring in
unsheltered poor, and to cover nakedness, and not to overlook those of blood;
and now especially, that our good may be from the need, not from the surplus;
by which fruit-bearing God is gladdened more, than by a multitude of the
contribution and a greatness of the giving. Upon these, and before these,
become for me Moses, or Phinehas today. Stand about us, and propitiate; and
let the breaking cease, whether through a spiritual sacrifice, or through
prayer and rational intercession. Hold back the wrath of the Lord by the
mediation; stay the sequence of the scourge. He knows to respect a father's
gray hair that intercedes for children. Petition concerning the wickedness
that has passed; guarantee the future. Bring forward =35.964= a people
purified by plague and fear. Ask the bodily food; ask before this also the
angelic that comes down from heaven. If you do this, you will make God your
own, you will tame heaven, you will give back both early rain and late; The
Lord will give kindness, and our earth will give her fruit, the one below at
least, the daily, and our dust, the eternal, which we will store in the divine
winepresses through you, who bring both us and our things, in Christ Jesus our
Lord, to whom be the glory unto the ages. Amen.
