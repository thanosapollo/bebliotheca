To Flavian the bishop
1.1 Our affairs, O man of God, are not in a good state; for the evils,
advancing among those who have stirred up against us an unjust and unprovoked
hatred, are no longer guessed at through certain conjectures, but are pursued
with boldness, as if they were 1.2 some of the good achievements. And you who
until now are outside such evil are slack about stopping the flame that is
feeding in the neighborhood, as if you did not know that those who counsel
well about their own things put down with much zeal the burning of the
neighbors, by the help they give those nearby procuring that they not need 1.3
help in like cases. What then is it that I say? Holiness has left life, truth
has fled from us; of peace formerly we at least had the name carried about in
our mouths, but now not only is she not, but not even her name has been left
to us. And that you may know more clearly what I lament, I will set out the
tragedy for you in a few words. 1.4 There were some who reported that the most
reverend Helladius was ill-disposed toward us and that he went through to all
that I was the cause of the greatest evils to him. I was not persuaded by what
was said, looking to myself and to the truth in the facts; but when the same
things were reported to us with one voice from all, and the facts agreed with
the rumors, I thought it fitting not to overlook this rootless and unplanted
ill-will untreated. 1.5 Therefore I also wrote to your Godliness, and I urged
many others who were able to contribute something toward the matter at hand to
this zeal; and at last, having celebrated among the people of Sebasteia the
memorial of the most blessed Peter being held for the first time, and having
spent with them at the same time the memorials of the holy <40> martyrs
customarily performed by them, I was returning again 1.6 to my own church. And
when someone reported that he was spending time in the mountain parish
celebrating memorials of martyrs, at first I kept to the road, judging it more
becoming that the meeting take place at the metropolis; but when one of those
close to me, meeting me in haste, affirmed that he was ill, leaving the
carriage in the place in which I was overtaken by such a report, I crossed the
interval between on horseback, precipitous and almost impassable by the 1.7
roughest ascents. And it was fifteen milestones, as we heard from the locals,
by which the interval in the middle was measured. Having with difficulty
crossed these, some on foot and some by the horse, at dawn, using even a part
of the night, at the first hour of the day I arrive at the Andaimonians; for
so the place is named, in which he was 1.8 holding church with two other
bishops. And seeing from a distance from a ridge lying above the village the
gathering of the church in the open air, we crossed the place between at a
walk, going forward on foot, both I myself and the company with me, and
drawing the horses by hand; so that the two things happened to come about
together, that he returned from the church to the house and that we drew 1.9
near to the martyr-shrine. And with no delay there was sent by us the one who
would announce to him the presence; and after a small interval had passed the
deacon who served him met us, whom we asked to announce with speed, so as to
spend more time with him, in order to find opportunity that none of what was
in us =1.10= be left untreated.


After this I sat in the open air waiting for the one who would call me in, and
I was set forth as an untimely spectacle to those sojourning at the synod. And
no little time passed, and drowsiness upon these things and acedia and the
weariness from the road stretching the acedia and the heat strong and those
looking toward us and pointing us out to one another with their fingers and
all such things were so heavy to me that the word of the prophet was true of
me =1.11= that My spirit was in acedia upon me. And when the hour had already
advanced toward midday I was brought in, having much regretted even before the
meeting that I had given myself the cause of that dishonor; and more harshly
than the insolence coming to me from enemies my own reasoning pained me,
fighting in a way against itself and repenting in afterthought for what it had
proposed. =1.12= When then the palace was with difficulty opened to us and we
came within the inner rooms, the many were shut out from the entrance, but my
deacon entered with me, who with his hand supported the body worn from the
labor. And I, having greeted him and standing a little so that there might be
for me an invitation to a seat, when none of these things happened, I sat down
in distress on one of the benches far off, waiting if perhaps he would utter
something kindly toward us or would nod something humane to us at least with
his brows. But all things were contrary to the hope; for from this point there
was a nocturnal silence and a tragic gloom and astonishment and amazement and
complete voicelessness and time stretching in quiet through no small interval
as =1.13= in a black night. And I, frozen in mind at these things because he
was not even willing to share the common speech, discharging the meeting
through these well-worn things, such as the "you have come well" or the "from
where have you come?" or "for what?" of himself, or "what is the zeal of the
presence?", I made that quiet an image of the sojourn =1.14= in Hades. Rather
I condemn the example; for in Hades there is much equality, none of those who
work the tragedy of life above the earth troubling that sojourn also; for
glory does not go down, as the prophet says, with men, but each soul, having
left what is now pursued by the many, I mean insolence and arrogance and
conceit, sojourns below simple and unfurnished, so that none of the =1.15=
wretched things here are among those there also. Yet to me Hades or a
lightless prison or some other gloomy place of punishment the present things
seemed to be, as I reasoned of how many good things we have become successors
from our fathers and what sort of narratives =1.16= we shall leave to those
after us. But why do I speak of the fathers' loving relation toward one
another? For it is no wonder that being men they should wish to have nothing
more than one another in like-honored nature, but in humility should consider
one another to excel themselves; but that most of all laid hold of my mind,
that the master of all creation, the only-begotten Son who is in the bosom of
the Father, who is in the beginning, who exists in the form of God, who bears
all things by the word of his power, not in this only humbled himself, in
sojourning through flesh with human nature, but even receives his own betrayer
Judas drawing near with his own mouth through a kiss, and having entered into
the house of Simon the leper he reproaches not having been kissed by him as
inhumane; but I =1.17= was not even counted in place of a leper. Who to whom?
I cannot find the difference; the one who has come down from where, to the one
lying where? if indeed one looks at the things of this world. For if one
should examine the things of the flesh, it is perhaps so inoffensive to say
that the nobility and the free =1.18= is of like honor on both; but if one
should seek the true freedom and nobility of the soul, both alike are slaves
of sin, both alike need the one who takes away sins. Another is the one who by
his own blood redeemed us from death and from sins, who also bought us


and showed no arrogance against those bought, the one calling the dead back
into life, the one healing every sickness both of souls and of bodies. =1.19=
Since then the conceit against us and the bulk of arrogance was all but
cramped by the heavenly height, and I saw no matter or occasion toward the
disease, from which such a passion becomes pardonable in those who take up
this disease from some circumstance, whenever either family or education or
superiority of rank inflates the more empty characters, I did not know how to
admonish myself to be still, my heart swelling within toward the unreason of
what was happening and spitting out the reasonings =1.20= on behalf of
endurance. When indeed I most admired the divine apostle so clearly describing
to us the civil war, saying that there is in the members a law of sin that
wars against the law of the mind and often makes the mind captive and subject
to itself, seeing in myself this opposed array of the two reasonings, both of
the one being harsh toward the insolence from arrogance =1.21= and of the one
putting down what was swelling. And when by God's grace the better inclination
prevailed, then I said to him: "1Surely nothing of the things pursued
concerning the care of the body is harmed by my presence, and =1.22= is it
timely to withdraw?"2 And when he said that the body had no need of being
treated, I said some healing words toward him, as far as was possible; and
when he indicated through a few words that he had grief against us upon many
injustices, I answered these things to him, that "Among men falsehood has much
power toward deceit, but the divine tribunal does not receive the fallacy from
deceit. And my conscience has such confidence in the matters toward you, that
I pray that there be for me forgiveness of the other sins, but if anything has
been done by me against you, that this remain forever unforgiven"2. =1.23= And
being indignant at the word, he no longer accepted that the proofs be added to
what was said. =1.24= The hour was more than according to the sixth, and the
bath was ready and the feast in preparation and the day was a Sabbath and an
honor of martyrs; and again how does the disciple of the gospel imitate the
master of the gospel? The one eating and drinking with tax-collectors and
sinners excused himself to those who reproached him as doing this according to
humanity, but the other =1.25= judges communion at table a pollution and a
defilement. After that labor which we underwent from the journey, after so
great a heat in which sitting in the open air at his doors we were roasted,
after that gloomy dejection which we endured having come before his eyes, he
sends us away again over the same interval through the same road, distressed
in a body already failed and worn out; so that with difficulty around late
afternoon, having suffered many evils in the middle, we overtook the company;
for also a cloud from a whirlwind coming about suddenly in the air reached
even our marrows with a violent rain; for we were unprepared through the
exceeding =1.26= heat as toward a guard against rain. Yet by God's grace, as
those saved from a wave or from a shipwreck, we gladly overtook our company,
and having rested the night with one another, we were brought through alive as
far as our own places, having gained this from the meeting, that of all the
things that had happened before, the insolence that had just now come against
us renewed the memory. =1.27= And we are compelled henceforth to take counsel
even for ourselves, or rather for him himself; for that his purpose was not
checked in the things that had happened before led him to this swelling
excess; therefore that he too may become better than himself, perhaps
something ought to be done even by us, so that he may learn that he is a man
and has no authority of insolence and dishonor against those of like mind and
like honor =1.28=. For look, let it be granted to be true (I speak


by hypothesis) that something painful has been done by me against him; what
tribunal was constituted against us upon the things that happened or were
suspected? What proof convicted the injustice? What canons were read against
us? What lawful sentence of a bishop ratified the judgment =1.29= against us?
And if any of these had even happened lawfully, the danger would altogether
have been about the rank; but what canons legislated insolence against freedom
and dishonor against those of like honor =1.30=? Judge a just judgment, you
who look toward God, in what do you consider the dishonor against us
pardonable =1.31=? If the dignity should be judged according to the
priesthood, equal and one provision has come from the synod to both, or rather
the care of the correction of common things, =1.32= <so as> to have equality
in this. But if some should look at us in themselves stripped of the dignity
according to the priesthood, what does the one have more than the other?
Family? Education? Freedom toward the best and most well-reputed? Knowledge?
These things are either found equally among us also, or altogether =1.33= not
in a lesser degree. But he speaks of wealth? May it not come to necessity to
narrate these things about him. Or it is enough to say only this much, how
much he was at the beginning and how much he has now become, and to leave to
others to search out the occasions of the increase of the wealth that up to
the present on almost every day through the fine =1.34= practices is increased
and fed. What then is the authority of the insolence against us, if there is
neither superiority of family nor distinction of rank nor exceeding power
=1.35= of words, nor is there some prior benefaction? When even if these
things were, even so the matter according to insolence would be unpardonable
in the case of free men; and when none of these is, I do not think it well to
overlook so great a disease of conceit untreated. And the treatment is to bend
down the arrogance and to part the empty bulk, the blast according to conceit
having been a little blown through; but how this may come about, God will
care.
2.τ Concerning those going away to Jerusalem, to Censitor
2.1 Since you asked, O friend, through the letter, I thought it fitting to
answer you in order about all things. I say that those who have once dedicated
themselves to the high way of life do well to look throughout toward the
voices of the gospel, and just as those who straighten what lies under the
rule according to the straight line upon the rule change the crooked of what
is in their hands into straightness, so I think it is fitting, applying as
some straight and undistorted rule, I mean the evangelical way of life, 2.2
that they be directed toward God according to that. Since then there are some
of those who have taken up the solitary and private life, for whom it has been
held as a part of piety to see the places in Jerusalem, in which the signs of
the sojourn of the Lord through flesh are seen, it would be well to look
toward the rule, and if the guidance from the commandments wants these things,
to do the work as a command of the Lord; but if it is outside the commandments
of the Master, I do not know what he wants [what it is] to do what has been
appointed, becoming for himself 2.3 a law of the good. Where the Lord calls
the blessed toward the inheritance of the kingdom of the heavens, he did not
number going away to Jerusalem among the achievements; where he proclaims the
beatitude, he did not include such zeal. And what neither makes blessed nor is
well-fitted toward the kingdom, for the sake of what it is 2.4 pursued, let
the one who has mind examine. And if what is done were beneficial, not even so
would it be a good thing to be pursued by the perfect; but since we learn with
accuracy that what is done also rubs a harm of soul upon those who have set
themselves in the exact life, it is not worthy of the long zeal but of the


greatest guard, so that the one who has chosen to live according to God may
not be pierced by any of the things that do harm.
2.5 What then is the thing that harms in these? The reverend way of life lies
before all, both men and women; and a property of the philosophical life is
seemliness, and this is achieved in the unmixed and private [life] of living,
so that the nature be unmixed and unconfused, neither of the women among men
nor of the men among women rushing toward the things 2.6 observed of
unseemliness. But the necessity of the journey destroys the accuracy in these
things and leads toward indifference of the things observed; for it is
impossible for a woman to run through so great a road, unless she have the one
who saves her and, because of the natural weakness, is led up upon the beast
and led down from there and held in the difficulties. And whichever we
suppose, whether she has as known the one who fulfills the attendance or as
hired the one who provides the service, according to either part what is done
does not escape blame; for neither resting herself upon the stranger nor upon
her own does she keep the law 2.7 of temperance. And of the inns and lodgings
and cities according to the eastern places, having much license and
indifference toward the evil, how will it be possible that the one passing
through smoke not have the eyes stung, where hearing is polluted, and the eye
is polluted, and the heart is polluted receiving the unfitting things through
eyes and hearing? How will it be possible to pass 2.8 without passion through
the passionate places? And what more will the one who has come to be in those
places have, as if until now the Lord were sojourning bodily in those places
and departing from us, or as if the Holy Spirit were abounding among the
people of Jerusalem and were 2.9 unable to cross over toward us? And indeed if
it is possible to infer God's presence from the things that appear, one would
rather think God to dwell in the nation of the Cappadocians than in the places
outside; for as many altars as there are in these, through which the name of
the Lord is glorified, one would not number the [altars] of almost =2.10= the
whole inhabited world. Then even if the grace were more in the places
according to Jerusalem, sin would not sojourn among those living there; now
however there is no form of uncleanness that is not dared among them, both
fornications and adulteries and thefts and idolatries and potions and envies
and murders; and especially such an evil sojourns, so that nowhere is there
such readiness toward murder as in those places, running upon one another like
beasts with the blood of those of the same race for the sake of cold gain.
Where then these things happen, what proof is there that there is more grace
in those places? =2.11= But I know the contradiction of the many toward the
things said by me; for they say: "1Why did you not legislate these things also
upon yourself? For if there was no gain to the one who went abroad according
to God in place of coming to be there, for the sake of what in vain =2.12= did
you undergo so great a road?"2 Let them hear then the defense from me on
behalf of these things. For me, through this necessity in which I was
appointed to live by the one who manages our life, there came to be <a
command> of the holy synod, for the sake of the correction of the church
according to Arabia, to come as far as the places; and since Arabia is
bordering on the places according to Jerusalem, I promised that I would also
take counsel with those who preside over the holy churches in Jerusalem
because their affairs are in disturbance and they need the one =2.13= who
mediates. Then also, the most pious emperor having provided the ease of the
road through a public carriage, no necessity came to be for us to suffer these
things which we also observed upon the others; for the carriage was for us in
place of a church and a monastery, through the whole road all singing together
=2.14= and fasting together to the Lord. Let our case then scandalize no one,
but rather let our counsel become persuasive, that concerning the things to
which we submitted the two eyes, =2.15= concerning these we also counsel. For
we


confessed the Christ who appeared to be true God both before coming to be upon
the place and after these things, the faith neither being lessened nor after
these things increased, and we knew the becoming-man through the virgin even
before Bethlehem, and we believed the rising from the dead even before the
tomb, and we confessed the ascent into the heavens to be true even apart from
seeing the mount of Olives; and we were benefited from the journey only so
much, as to know in comparison that our things are much holier than those
outside. =2.16= Wherefore You who fear the Lord, in whatever places you are,
praise him; for a local change does not work an approach of God, but wherever
you are, God will come toward you, if indeed the lodging of your soul is found
such that the Lord dwell =2.17= in you and walk about. But if you have the
inner man full of evil reasonings, even if you are upon Golgotha even if upon
the mount of Olives even if under the tomb of the Resurrection, you are as far
from receiving Christ in =2.18= yourself as those who have not even confessed
at the beginning. Counsel then, beloved, the brothers to go abroad from the
body toward the Lord and not from Cappadocia into Palestine. And if someone
should put forward the voice of the Lord that charged the disciples not to
separate from Jerusalem, let him understand what was said. Since the grace and
distribution of the Holy Spirit had not yet come upon the apostles, the Lord
commanded them to remain upon the same, until they should put on power =2.19=
from on high. If then what was from the beginning were happening until now,
the Holy Spirit managing each of the gifts in the form of fire, all ought to
be in that place in which the distribution of the gift happened; but if the
Spirit blows where it wills, those who have believed here also become
partakers of the gift according to the analogy of the faith, not according to
the sojourn in Jerusalem.
3.τ To the truly most modest and most devout sisters Eustathia and Ambrosia
and to the most modest and most reverend daughter Basilissa, Gregory in the
Lord greeting.
3.1 The meeting of the good things and of those according to my heart, and the
tokens of the great humanity of the Master on our behalf that are shown in the
places, became for me a matter of the greatest joy and gladness; for through
both the feast according to God was made clear to me, both through seeing the
saving signs of the God who made us live and through meeting souls in which
such signs of the Lord's grace are spiritually contemplated, so as to believe
that truly in the heart of the one who has God is 3.2 Bethlehem, Golgotha, the
Olive, the Resurrection. For he in whom Christ is formed through the good
conscience, and who has nailed his own flesh with the divine fear and has been
crucified together with Christ and has rolled away from himself the heavy
stone of the deceit of life and has come out of the bodily tomb so as to walk
in newness of life, and who has left the material and earth-bound life of men,
but has gone up through the high desire into the heavenly way of life and
thinks the things above, where Christ is, and is not weighed down by the
burden of the body but has lightened it through the cleaner life, so that for
him the flesh travels together on high in the manner of a cloud, this one
according to my judgment is of those named places, in which the memorials of
the Master's humanity on our behalf are seen.
3.3 Since then I saw the holy places even perceptibly, and I saw also in you
the clear signs of such places, I was filled with so much joy that the
narration of the good 3.4 is beyond word. But since it seems, being a man,


to be difficult, that I may not say that it is impossible, to harvest the good
unmixed with the evil, for this reason there was mixed for me with the taste
of the sweet things also something of the things that make the sense bitter.
Upon which, after the good enjoyment of the things that gladden, I was going
again toward the fatherland gloomy, reasoning that the word of the Lord is
true that said the whole world lies in the evil one, so that no part of the
inhabited world is free of the lot of the worse; for if the place that
received the holy trace of the true life is not clean of the evil thorn, what
must one think about the remaining 3.5 places, in which the sharing of the
good was sown through bare hearing and preaching? And looking toward what I
say these things, there is altogether no need to set them out more nakedly
through words, the things themselves crying the painful things more loudly
than every known voice. 3.6 One enmity the lawgiver of our life legislated for
us, I mean that toward the serpent, having commanded the power of hating to be
worked toward nothing else except toward the turning away of vice; For I will
put enmity, he says, between you and between that one. And since vice is
something varied and of many forms, through the serpent the word hints at
this, characterizing the many-turned character of vice by the dense setting
3.7 of the scales. But we have become in treaty toward the serpent through
doing the wills of the adversary, and we have turned the hatred against one
another, or perhaps not even against ourselves but against the one who has
given the commandment; for he says You shall love your neighbor and hate your
enemy, having commanded to consider only the enemy of our nature an enemy, and
having said that everyone who shares the nature is neighbor of each; but our
heavy-hearted generation, having set us apart from the neighbor, made us
cherish the serpent and delight in the marks 3.8 of his scales. For I say that
to hate the enemies of God is lawful and that such hatred pleases the Master,
and I call enemies those who in every way deny the glory of the Master,
whether the Jews or those who openly worship idols or those who through the
doctrines of Arius make the creation into an idol and take up the Jewish
error. But if Father and Son and Holy Spirit are piously glorified and
worshipped by those who have believed, in the unconfused and distinguished
Holy Trinity one nature and glory and kingdom and power and the pious worship
and the authority over all, 3.9 what reasonable cause does the war have here?
When the doctrines of the heresy prevailed, even to run risk toward the
authorities, through which the doctrine of the adversaries seemed to be
strengthened, was well, so that the saving word not be dominated by human
powers; but now, throughout the whole inhabited world From the end of heaven
and as far as the end of the same, piety perhaps being preached in the open,
the one who wars with those who preach piety does not fight those but the one
piously preached by them; for what other aim ought there to be for the one who
has the divine zeal, except that the glory of God be announced in every way?
=3.10= As long then as from whole heart and soul and mind the only-begotten
God is worshipped, having been believed to be in all that which the Father is,
and likewise also the Holy Spirit is glorified in like-honored worship, what
specious occasion for warring do those who are wise in the superfluous have,
splitting the untearable tunic and dividing the name of the Lord toward Paul
and Cephas and declaring the approach of those who worship Christ abominable,
all but crying that openly in words, =3.11= Far from me; do not draw near to
me, because I am clean? And let it even be granted that something more
according to knowledge is present to them, beyond the things they think they
have grasped; do they have more than believing that the true Son of God is
=3.12= true God? And in the confession of the true God all the pious and
saving thoughts are included, that he is good, that he is just,


that he is powerful, that he is unturned and unchangeable and always the same,
able to be turned neither toward the worse nor toward the better; for the one
he is not by nature, the other he does not have; for what is higher than <the>
highest =3.13=? What is better than the good? So that the one contemplated in
every perfection of the good has the unchangeable according to every form of
turning, not showing such a thing in himself according to certain times, but
always having in the same way both before the economy according to man and in
that and after this, nothing of the unturned and unchangeable nature being
remade toward the unbecoming in the operations =3.14= on our behalf. For what
is by nature incorruptible and unchangeable is always such, not being changed
together with the low nature whenever it comes to be in that according to
economy, according to the example of the sun, which having submitted the ray
to the gloom did not dim the light of the ray, but changed the darkness
through the ray into light; so also the true light having shone in our
darkness was not itself overshadowed =3.15= by the darkness, but through
itself lighted the gloom. Since then the human was in darkness, as it is
written that They did not know nor understand, they go through in darkness,
the one who shone upon the darkened nature, having led the ray of the divinity
through all of our compound, I mean through soul and body, appropriated the
whole human to his own light, having worked that also, by the blending toward
himself, to be what he is; and as the divinity was not corrupted having come
to be in the corruptible body, so neither was it changed into turning when it
healed the turnable of our soul; for even in the medical art the one who
treats the bodies, whenever he touches the one who has suffered, does not
himself become passionate but healed =3.16= the sick; and let no one because
of this, taking the gospel not as one ought, think that our nature in Christ
is remade toward the more divine according to some progress and sequence
little by little; for the Progressing in stature and wisdom and grace has been
historied by the scripture toward proof that the Lord has truly come to be in
our lump, so that <the> of those who dogmatize that instead of the true
theophany some seeming has come to be, shaped in bodily form, may not have
some room =3.17=. For this reason as many things as are proper to our nature,
the scripture histories even about him without shame, the eating the drinking
the sleep the labor the nourishment the progress of the bodily stature the
increase, all things through which our nature is characterized, except the
impulse according to sin; for sin is a miscarriage of nature, not a property,
as also disease and maiming have not been grown together with us from the
beginning, but happen contrary to nature; so also the operation according to
vice is thought as some maiming of the good grown in us, not being grasped in
that it itself exists =3.18=, but being contemplated in the absence of the
good. The one then who recast our nature toward the divine power preserved it
in himself unmaimed and without disease, not having received the maiming that
comes to be from sin for the purpose; For he did not do sin, he says, nor was
deceit found =3.19= in his mouth. And this we do not contemplate about him
after some temporal interval, but immediately the man in Mary, in whom wisdom
built her own house, was by his own nature from the passionate lump, but
together with the coming-upon of the Holy Spirit and the tenting of the power
of the Highest, what the thing that tented was by its own nature, immediately
that became; For without all contradiction the =3.20= lesser is blessed by the
better. Since then the power of the divinity is something infinite and
unmeasured, and the human is short and of no account, as soon as the Spirit
came upon the virgin and the power of the Highest overshadowed, the tent being
fixed through such an occasion drew along with it nothing of the human
rottenness; but what was in the constitution, even if it was man, yet it was
