=130.28= If indeed from the conjunction of the simple elements, I mean earth,
and water, and air, and fire, or of the so-called atoms according to Epicurus,
the things in the world have come to be, the generated will be found
incomparably better and more honorable than the ungenerated, and the thing
made than the one who made it, and by as much as man is more excellent than
the simple elements and the aforesaid atoms. How then would things contrary by
nature have run together unharmed into the completion of one world? And how
would they have agreed, having neither will nor reason? Or how did things
altogether soulless and irrational and without sensation bestow reason upon
man? And it was necessary also that to that to which they allotted the greater
part of their own substance, I mean the elephant, or whatever is larger than
this, they should rather have allotted together also the ruling and kingly
power over all the inferior things. =130.29= But as it is, man, who falls far
short of these in bodily size and strength, rules over that one through the
reason which he has altogether been enriched with from the Creator who has
reason. And how would the ordered things be said to have come to be from
things borne about at random and as it happened? =130.257= The Law, and the
prophets, of whom Christ was the fullness, have written down all things
concerning this in detail, and indeed also concerning the Holy Trinity. But
they have been inserted scatteredly, and most things have been overshadowed
with obscurity according to divine providence, lest the Jews, still being
childish in mind and not yet able to understand higher things, should turn
aside into unworthy notions, and should drag the impassible down to passions.
For even when God began to speak to them at Horeb, they declined such a voice
through their own weakness, saying: We will not add to hear the voice of the
Lord our God, and we will not see the great fire any more, lest we die. And
the Lord said =130.260= to me, says Moses who wrote these things: Rightly all
that I have spoken. I will raise up a prophet for them from among their
brothers, like you, and I will put my word in his mouth, and he shall speak to
them whatever I command him. These things thus; but the sense of the words is
that Since they cannot now hear, I will raise up a prophet for them, and so
on, who will announce to them as much as they are not able for the present to
receive. From these things, then, are the things concerning Father and Son and
Holy Spirit, and the things concerning the divine incarnation, and simply as
many things as have been enacted through the Gospel. For the "like you"
indicates the likeness rather according to legislating. But Jesus the son of
Nun was neither deemed worthy of such a theophany as Moses, nor became a
lawgiver as that one, nor found grace as great as that one. But Christ, having
become like that one in legislating, in all other things as Master stands far
above. And that it was not the son of Nun who was the prophet to be raised up,
hear. Moses has said in Deuteronomy to the people, that The Lord your God will
raise up a prophet from among our brothers, and so on. But the son of Nun had
long before been appointed by God through the hands of Moses, as Leviticus
records. Therefore the one to be raised up is other than the one already
raised. But, as it seems, the Jews are ignorant of what is written at the
conclusion of Deuteronomy after the death of Moses, that And there has not yet
arisen a prophet in Israel like Moses, whom the Lord knew face to face. It was
necessary then, when God said to Moses that I will raise up a prophet for
them, that Moses also say this then to the people. But he, having learned this
at Horeb, said it later to the people according to Deuteronomy, waiting
economically for the appointment of Jesus the


son of Nun, lest they suppose this one to be the fitting prophet; after his
laying on of hands, however, then he announced this to the people. So much
then for these things. And though there are countless passages both announcing
beforehand the things concerning Christ, and declaring the things concerning
the Holy Trinity, we shall leave most, and rather as many as these men
wickedly attempt to misinterpret, lest we give an opening and occasion for the
wicked contradictions; For the whole house of Israel are contentious and
hard-hearted, says Ezekiel; But we shall take up a few that are clear and
indisputable, and as many as will refute these men, whether they are
altogether willfully deaf or mindless. {1Book of Genesis.} 1 And God said, Let
us make man according to our image, and according to likeness. The Father,
that is, to those of his own substance who share with him the creative and
lordly dignity. For there is no place here for the idiom of the Hebrew
dialect, =130.261= which sometimes uses the singulars plurally. For otherwise,
even abusing such an idiom where it ought not, we shall fill those who
contradict with much confusion. {1From the same.}1 And God said, Behold, Adam
has become as one of us, that is, as each of us of the persons in the one
Godhead. For even if it has been said ironically, still it cannot at all be
significant of one person. {1From the same.}1 And God said, Come, and going
down let us confuse their tongues. For the one who says "Come" says it to two
at least, and from this a Trinity is completed together. And "Come, let us do
this" you will say to those of equal power and consubstantial, and not to
angels, which is absurd. Or of whatever sort and however many you may say, we
shall set them aside as unwitnessed. How does God converse? In our manner? It
is more pious to say that the divine will, and the first impulse of the
intellectual motion, this is the Word of God. And Scripture figures him in
extended discourse, that it may show that he did not will the creation only to
come to be, but that it be brought into generation through a certain
fellow-worker. For he was able, as he said from the beginning, to go through
all things. In the beginning God made the heaven and the earth. Then he made
the light, then he made the firmament. But now, introducing God as it were
commanding and conversing, it hints at the one to whom he converses according
to what is left unspoken, not grudging us the knowledge, but kindling us
toward the desire, through the traces and intimations it suggests of the
ineffable. For what is acquired with toil is received with great joy, and is
carefully guarded. But of those things whose procurement is ready, the
possession is easily despised. For this reason by a certain way and order it
leads us into the notion concerning the Only-begotten, although there was not
even thus need of spoken word for the bodiless nature, since the very things
thought could be imparted to the one cooperating. So what need of word for
those able from the thought itself to communicate their counsels to one
another? For voice is for hearing, and hearing for the sake of voice. But
where there is not air, not tongue, nor ear, nor a winding passage bringing
the sounds up to the sensation in the head, there there is not even need of
words, but from the very thoughts in the heart, as one might say, is the
transmission of the will. Which then, as I said, so that our mind might be
stirred up to the search of the person toward whom the words are, this of the
dialect has been taken up wisely and artfully. {1From the same.}1 And God
appeared to Abraham by the oak of Mamre, as he sat at the door of his tent at
midday. And looking up with his eyes =130.264= he saw, and behold three men
stood over him. And seeing, he ran to meet them, and bowed down to the earth,
and said, Lord, if I have found favor before you, do not pass by your servant.
And he said to him, Where is Sarah


your wife? And he answering said, Behold, in the tent. And he said, Returning
I will come to you, and so on. Behold it is written, that God appeared to
Abraham, but that those who appeared were three, and though they were three
the conversation takes place toward one, altogether for an indication of the
three-hypostatic character of the one Godhead. And if you shall say that the
one is God, but the others angels, how did they endure to sit down together
with the Master? {1From the same.}1 The Lord rained upon Sodom and Gomorrah
brimstone and fire from the Lord out of heaven. This is indicative of two
persons, and declarative of self-will and equal power, and with the two the
third also is brought in, because of the inseparable and co-bearing.
{1Of Exodus.}1 Hear, O Israel, the Lord your God, the Lord is one. For the
"Lord," and "God," and "Lord" hint at the three hypostases, but the "is one"
at the one Godhead and nature in three. {1Of David.}1 Upon God I will praise a
word, upon the Lord I will praise a saying. Upon God I have hoped, I will not
fear what man shall do to me. And again: Sacrifice to God a sacrifice of
praise, and pay your vows to the Most High, and call upon me in the day of
your affliction. And again, The Lord lives, and blessed be God, and let the
God of my salvation be exalted. And further, In your light we shall see light,
that is, in your Son we shall know the Holy Spirit. For through him, just as
the things concerning the Father, so also the things concerning the Holy
Spirit we have been more clearly initiated. And many such things we find that
announce the Holy Trinity. And the angelic hymn too, Holy, holy, holy Lord,
through the trisagion declared the three-hypostatic, and through the Lord the
unity of the Godhead. And more manifestly concerning Father and Son David says
from the person of the Father to the Son, From the womb before the morning
star I have begotten you, womb being understood in a manner befitting God as
the paternal substance and nature. For whom else will you be able to say has
been begotten timelessly? And from the person of the Son: The Lord said to me,
You are my Son, today I have begotten you, he indicates his temporal
generation, that from the Virgin. For "I have begotten" is instead of "I was
well pleased that he be born," or "I created." And Solomon has said in
agreement with this in the person of the son, The Lord created me, and so on.
Then he added: And before all the hills he begets me. For the "created" is of
his lower and temporal generation, but the "before all the hills he begets me"
of the =130.265= upper, and timeless, and pre-eternal. And again David, By the
Word of the Lord the heavens were established, and by the Spirit of his mouth
all their power; calling Lord the Father, and Word his Son, because as word is
toward mind, so he is toward the Father, not only because of the impassibility
of the generation, but also the conjunction, and the announcing; and Spirit,
the Holy Spirit. And again concerning the Word and Son, He sent his Word, and
healed them, the Father that is the Son, those sick in error. And concerning
the Holy Spirit, You will send forth your Spirit and they shall be created,
those of the new creation that is, and you will renew the face of the earth,
which had grown old in sins. And neither is a prophetic word sent, poured out
through the vocal organs into the air and dissolved. For he says in other
places: Forever, O Lord, your Word remains in heaven. Nor is a Spirit likewise
thrust out through respiratory organs, and dissolved, and poured out. For
these things belong to bodies. It is clear then that Word, and Spirit, are
hypostatic, and eternal, and creative. And Job too says, The Lord lives, who
has thus judged me, and the Almighty, who has embittered my soul, and the
divine Spirit that remains in my nostrils. And Zechariah from the person of
God and Father, I am with you, and my Word, and my Spirit. And Isaiah, Now the
Lord has sent me, and his Spirit.


{1Book of Genesis.}1 Jacob says to his two wives: And the angel of God said to
me in sleep, I have seen all that Laban does to you, and so on. Then, I am the
God who appeared to you in the place of God. If then he was simply an angel,
one of the ministering powers, how did he call himself God? Or it was
altogether the Son, who is angel of God and Father, as word of mind; for the
word is announcer of the mind, and further also as after the incarnation
having announced to us the name of fatherhood. For I have manifested, he says,
your name to men, and as angel of the great counsel. For thus Isaiah named
him; further also naming him wonderful counselor, and mighty God, one having
authority. For who is counselor of God, if not the consubstantial, and
self-powerful, and equal-in-honor Son? And counsel of the Father, that through
men. And the counsel of the Father, and of the Son, hints at self-will. And
that this Son also, being God, became man, the same says: For a child has been
born to us, a Son also has been given to us. For for us the Son was born a
child, and consorted with us. For concerning the Son properly he prophesied
these things, and not concerning a son simply, and as a paradoxical work. And
what is paradoxical in a son simply being born a child? Wherefore also he
added the following most clearly for an indication of his =130.268= Godhead,
and a distinction from simple sonship. {1Of Jeremiah.}1 This is our God,
another shall not be reckoned beside him. Then he added, After these things he
appeared upon the earth, and consorted with men. And again in another passage:
Behold our God himself will come and will save us. And giving also a sign of
his incarnate presence he added, Then the eyes of the blind shall be opened,
and the ears of the deaf shall hear. Then the lame shall leap as a deer, and
the tongue of the stammerer shall be clear; all of which having come to pass
are an unlying witness of his incarnation. And the same has said again: Not an
ambassador, not an angel, but the Lord himself saved them. For an ambassador
is the mediator, such as Moses was, and whoever is according to that one; and
yet through a mediator, and through an angel many have often been saved, as
those led by Moses, and through Jesus the son of Nun, and such as these. And
again, those around Hezekiah, upon whom the angel slew the 185 thousands of
the Assyrians in one night. It is clear then that the words indicate a
manifest visitation of God. {1Of Isaiah.}1 The Lord himself will give us a
sign. Behold the Virgin shall conceive, and shall bear a son. For everyone who
conceives is not a virgin. But this one, conceiving, was a virgin. Wherefore
also as a paradox it has been given as a sign. {1Of the same.}1 Before the
child knows good or evil, he disobeys wickedness so as to choose the good. And
to what child will you fit these things? For Christ alone, he says, did no
sin, nor was guile found in his mouth, that is he sinned neither in word nor
in deed. But of all the others no one, he says, is clean from defilement.
{1Of Micah.}1 And you, Bethlehem, land of Judah, are by no means least among
the rulers of Judah. For from you shall come forth for me a ruler, who shall
shepherd my people Israel. And that he may teach that this is not a mere man,
but united to God, he added, His goings forth are from the beginning, from the
days of eternity; his activities according to the Godhead, he says, or his
generation from the beginning from intervals of eternity, that is eternal. But
Zerubbabel was born in Babylon. Do you see exactness even as far as the place
where Christ was born? {1Of Daniel.}1 I was considering in a vision of the
night, and behold with the clouds of heaven one as a son of man was coming.
And he came as far as the Ancient of days =130.269= and was brought before
him, and to him was given the rule, and the honor, and the kingdom, and all
the peoples, tribes, tongues serve him. His authority is an eternal authority,
which shall not pass away. And his kingdom shall not be


destroyed. Who then is the one who as a son of man received the inalienable
authority over all? No man at all, but the God-man Christ, concerning whom
David also has said to God and Father: O God, give your judgment to the king,
and your righteousness to the son of the king. For Christ both as God is king,
and son of a king. And as man he has become king, and son of king David,
wherefore also later he said, Authority has been given to me in heaven and on
earth. To whom the Father after the receiving of the kingdom, as to a man,
said, Sit at my right hand. For the Lord said, he says, to my Lord: Sit at my
right hand until I make your enemies a footstool of your feet. Or who are two
lords of David, of whom the word is, if you do not say these? And David,
having said, O God, give your judgment to the king, and so on, added that
Before the sun his name remains, that of the Godhead, or of the sonship that
is. For he always was both. Who then is such a king, if not this one? And to
whom have all the nations been enslaved, and been blessed, and called blessed,
if not Christ alone? And if you still wait for him, when Elijah shall run
before this one, you speak of the second descent of the Savior, when also is
the consummation of the world. For there being two descents, one according to
his incarnation, and the other according to the coming world-wide judgment,
the one has already come to pass, whose forerunner was John, the second
Elijah; and the other will come to pass having as forerunner Elijah the second
John. For both are of the same Spirit, and of the same grace, and power, as
far as running before Christ. The anointed and messiah whom you await, is he a
mere man, or God-man? For if a mere man, he is not the one whom the prophets
announced. For these add to him signs of Godhead, distinguishing him from mere
men. And from where else having learned do you expect this one? And if he is
God-man, behold you also confess another person of Godhead without noticing.
For you will not say it is the Father at all. For a child, he says, has been
born to us, a Son, but not the Father. And since to anoint and to chrismate
signify the same, the same one is the anointed, and Christ. Understand then,
you foolish among the people, and you stupid, be wise at last. And Isaiah too
says to you, You blind, look, and you deaf, hear. For either you are plainly
willfully blind, or you are altogether without sensation. When then will you
distinguish homonymies of names, or significations of words, or peculiarities
of expressions, through which the truth is grasped?
{1Concerning the passions of Christ.}1 David says from the person of Christ: A
congregation =130.272= of evildoers surrounded me. They pierced my hands, and
my feet. They numbered all my bones. They divided my garments among
themselves, and upon my clothing they cast a lot. To whom else will you fit
these things besides Christ, concerning all of which the Gospel records things
in accord? And through Isaiah also Christ has spoken beforehand concerning
such things: I have given my back to scourges, and my cheeks to slaps, and I
did not turn my face away from the shame of spittings. And again David: They
gave gall into my food, and for my thirst they gave me vinegar to drink. And
if you shall understand these things concerning the people in captivity, the
fact that nowhere has it been written that they suffered these very things in
kind, but many other and grievous things, will stop your mouth. And concerning
his slaying Isaiah has spoken beforehand: As a sheep he was led to slaughter.
And that you may know that this has become saving for us, the same says again,
By his bruise we all were healed. And that you may learn that because of his
slaughter the race of the Jews was delivered over to destruction, he added,
And I will give the wicked in return for his burial. For what is more wicked
than Jews who slew the one who had benefited them so greatly?


{1Concerning his resurrection.}1 David says, that You will not abandon my soul
to Hades, nor will you give your holy one to see corruption, of the body that
is. Then, You have made known to me ways of life; and Hosea more clearly says:
And he will heal us after two days, on the third day, and we shall rise, and
we shall live before him, saying these things in the person of the
fallen-asleep saints, whose bodies were raised together with Christ according
to his three-day resurrection, and appeared to many, as the Gospel teaches.
And the Hebrew Josephus also says in the eighteenth book of his Antiquities:
And there comes about at this time Jesus, a wise man, if indeed one must call
him a man. For he was a doer of paradoxical works, a teacher of men who
receive the true things with pleasure, and he won over many of the Jews, and
many also from the Greek. This was the Christ, and when the first men among us
had indicated him, the Jews nailed him to a cross, Pilate having sentenced
him. And those who at first had loved him did not cease to announce his
things. For he appeared to them on the third day living again, the divine
prophets having said these things and countless other wonders concerning him.
{1Concerning the church from the nations, from Isaiah.}1 Rejoice, thirsty
desert. Let the desert exult, and let it bloom as a lily, and it shall
blossom, and the deserts of the Jordan shall exult. And the glory of Lebanon
was given to it, and the honor of Carmel. Sensibly then these things have
neither come to pass, nor will they. For who would choose to dwell in the
desert, unless you mean the Christian monks? But anagogically, =130.273= and
very much so. For the desert is the church from the nations, formerly of the
unfruitful, and a dwelling-place of the intelligible beasts, which, burning
with the fire of the passions, thirsted for knowledge of God and salvation.
And having obtained the preaching it rejoices, and exults, and blooms with the
brightness and fragrance of the virtues. And there was given to it also the
glory of Lebanon, that is of the ancient people, whom he also named Carmel.
And the glory of this and honor is piety, and intimacy with God, and the
height of the glory and of the law, and the abundance of the divine helps, and
the luxuriance of the graces. And again, Rejoice, barren one who does not
bear, break forth and cry, you who do not travail, because many are the
children of the desolate rather than of her who has the husband. For the
church indicated was barren, and unfruitful of fruits of virtue formerly, but
later having received the seeds of piety she became many-childed, surpassing
in multitude the Jewish synagogue, whose husband God was formerly, loving and
providing for and guarding her, or also the law. And again in the person of
God and Father Isaiah says to the Son and God: Behold I have given you for a
covenant of nations, to establish the earth, and to inherit the inheritance of
a desert, and so on. And through David also he promised to the Son saying, And
I will give you nations, your inheritance. And the same David was entreating
the Father concerning these saying, Appoint, O Lord, a lawgiver over them. And
Isaiah also has said in the person of such a lawgiver: A law shall go forth
from me, and my judgment for a light of nations, and upon my arm nations shall
hope. And what is this law besides the evangelical, which enlightened the
nations darkened in error? For the Mosaic went forth many years before.
{1Of the same.}1 Concerning the new covenant of the gospel Isaiah again
prophesied saying, From Zion shall go forth a law and a word of the Lord from
Jerusalem. For our Lord Jesus Christ, now sitting on mount Zion and teaching,
now spending time in the city Jerusalem, legislated the evangelical law and
word. For the prophet does not now speak concerning the Mosaic. For how, that
which was legislated on Sinai, but not on Zion, nor in Jerusalem, and long ago
much before Isaiah, just as the evangelical much after


Isaiah? Wherefore also he said fittingly, that it shall go forth. For the old
went forth. And concerning this new one Jeremiah also has spoken beforehand
saying, Behold days are coming, says the Lord, and I will make with you a new
covenant, not according to the covenant which I made with your fathers in the
day when I took their hand, to lead them out of the land of Egypt, because
they did not remain in my covenant and I neglected them. And these and such
things have been overshadowed, lest the books be destroyed. For when seeing
him present, and working wonders, and furnishing a perfect demonstration of
his own Godhead =130.276= they were not ashamed, but crucified him, would they
have spared the prophecies concerning him, if they had received these
unshadowed and naked? And further also that the name of Christ will be a
calling for us, Isaiah has spoken beforehand, saying, You the Lord will
destroy, but to those who serve me a new name shall be called, and they shall
be blessed upon all the earth. And again, Nations shall see your glory, and
all the kings your righteousness, and it shall be called the new name, which
the Lord shall name it. And Hosea also spoke beforehand, And it shall be,
having said, at the last his name shall be manifest in all the earth, and by
his name many peoples shall be called, and they shall walk according to his
ways and shall live in them. {1Of the same.}1 And in you they shall pray,
because in you is God, and there is no God besides you. For you are God, and
we did not know. The "And in you they shall pray" then is a prophecy
concerning those who have believed in Christ. And the "Because in you is God"
announces the Godhead in the assumption. And the "For you are God, and we did
not know" is a certain apology of the Jews in the coming judgment, even if
vain and unacceptable. If Christ was a deceiver, how have as many things as he
spoke beforehand come to pass in the end? Such as the things concerning the
desolation of the temple of Jerusalem, and the capture, and the overthrow of
the city, and the utter destruction of the Jews, and as many things as
concerning his disciples, and the wonders in his name, and simply all things.
=130.312= And darkness over the abyss, says Moses. Whence these men, setting
out wickedly, say that the darkness was, of which the evil principle is the
productive cause. Then also God divided between the light and between the
darkness. If then the light is a being, and the darkness is a being, then that
which was divided from the light. These things the dark ones say concerning
the light. But what do =130.313= we? Darkness is not some substance, but an
accident, and nothing other than absence of light. For when the heaven was
rounded in the beginning, darkness came into being beside from obstruction of
the surrounding things, which even now is accustomed to happen in the case of
things blocked. And the division of these is the measurable interval of the
absence of light. And in a certain manner the absence and privation of light
becomes a habit and a by-existence for the darkness. And an evil principle,
that is an evil God, there is not. For if God, he is not evil, but a fountain
of goodness; and if evil, he is not God. For how is vice and sin God? And if
these are God, all things are gone, and overthrown. {1From the same.}1 How
shall there be two principles, good and evil? For good and evil, that is bad,
are contrary to one another, and destructive of one another, and incompatible.
And then each will be in a part of the all, and will be circumscribed by this
part; and either they will touch one another, and be destroyed by one another,
or there will be something in the middle walling them off, and behold three
principles. And again either they are at peace, which the evil cannot, or they
will fight, which the good neither wills, nor can. For the evil being at peace
is not evil; and the good fighting is not good. Therefore not two principles,
and these contrary, and most warlike. But one altogether good. And the evil is
not a principle, but privation and failure of the good. {1From the same.}1 If
both have been born gods, the good and the evil, they will have another
principle. And a dyad is not a principle, but on the contrary the monad is
principle


of all things, there is therefore also one God, the cause and creator of all
things. {1From the same.}1 If the good is a being, the evil is a non-being.
And if the evil is a being, the good is a non-being, according to the account
of immediate contraries, of health and disease, of life and death. Therefore
not an evil God. {1From the same.}1 The good God has goodness as essential and
natural, but the evil will altogether have the privation of goodness as a
habit. And how is he God from falling away and deficiency? {1From the same.}1
If there were two Gods, or three, or simply many, the world would long ago
have perished, being acted upon and moved now one way now another, and
differently. But the being always moved and conducted in the same way and
likewise, and the harmony through all things, and agreement, and order, and
continuance, and preservation proclaim one and the same as the cause, and
creator, and provider, and holder-together. =130.316= {1From the same.}1 If
this lower world is evil, then we too are evil, being a part of it. And to us
who are evil by nature truth does not belong. For the truth is clearly a good;
and the good is alien to the evil. And to those not possessing truth the
things opined are both false and without subsistence. But if the indicated
world is good, it is altogether also a work of a good one. For of a good
caused thing the cause is good, just as of an evil one evil.
{1From the same.}1 If indeed you confess the world good, but God and Father
evil, and not creator of this world, there will altogether also be another
evil world, a work of the evil God. But there is not. Therefore neither is
there an evil God. For God is a principle and productive cause; and if things
begun and made are not, neither will there be a principle at all. Therefore
then there is not an evil God. {1From the same.}1 The God of the Old, they
say, Covenant does not have the all-embracing and divine foreknowledge. For he
asks Adam, Where are you? and Cain, Where is Abel your brother? and Abraham,
Where is Sarah your wife? and many such things. To whom we say. The indicated
questions are not of ignorance, but rather of knowledge. For if he had not
known both Adam and Cain to have sinned, he would not have asked at once; and
it is clear from what follows. For he said to Adam, although he had not yet
confessed anything concerning the eating: Who told you that you are naked, if
not from the tree of which I commanded you alone not to eat, from it you ate?
And to Cain, although he too was trying to escape notice: The voice of the
blood of your brother cries to me. These things are of one who knows very
well, but who wishes through the pretended question to bring those who have
sinned to repentance, having recognized that they have been found out. And
again also the: Where is Sarah your wife? was of one who foreknew that he both
has a wife, and that she is called Sarah. And he who knew these things knew
also altogether where she was. For he would not, when she was standing behind
the door, and after the promise of childbearing had laughed secretly, have
said: Why did Sarah laugh? Then he also adds the cause of the laugh. For
saying, he says, in herself, Shall I then truly bear? For these things are
even of a knower of hearts. And he asked Where is Sarah your wife?
economically, that Abraham, having learned from this that he is a knower of
the future, and knowing all things, might attend to him as to a greater, and
for the rest believe all things, whatever he may say. And what was said
concerning Sodom and Gomorrah also, that Having gone down I will see if they
are completed according to their cry; and if not, that I may know, has been
said economically, teaching us not to punish from foreknowledge, but to wait
for the actual diagnosis. For if he did not know, how =130.317= did he say:
The cry of Sodom and Gomorrah comes to me? The earth, he says, cries even
before the deeds, or the deeds themselves cry out. And there are many such
questions not only in the Old, but also in the New of Christ. And all are
economic, and constructing something else. For it is proper to God to know all
things; and he who does not know all things is not God of all.

just, but that of the new good, and on account of these things you insist that
they are other and other? For if the just is good, the just is altogether
good. For the good and the just are not divided against one another; and the
good is altogether just. And though countless passages proclaim that the
lawgiver of the old is good, I shall set down one: Confess to the Lord that he
is good; let the house of Israel say that he is good; and the two following
verses. And the lawgiver of the new also: And my judgment is just; and to
John: Suffer it now; for thus it is fitting for us to fulfill all
righteousness. And that one is the lawgiver both of the old and of the new,
Jeremiah has spoken beforehand saying: Behold days are coming, says the Lord,
and I will make with you a new Covenant, not according to the Covenant which I
made with your fathers, when I took their hand to lead them out of the land of
Egypt, because they did not remain in my Covenant, and I neglected them. And
again he himself, the lawgiver both of this and of that, Christ, said: I came
not to destroy the law, but to fulfill; that is, being incomplete because of
the incompleteness of the Hebrews, to perfect it with the greater philosophy,
and that which befits men. For the one was a pedagogue training toward the
Gospel, and this is a mystagogue teaching the more perfect things. And the one
regulated the bodies, and this equips the souls. The old then is a
fellow-worker of the new, and a preparation, and a making of the way.
