[EPISTLE OF CLEMENT TO JAMES.]
Clement to James, the lord and bishop of bishops, who governs the holy church
of the Hebrews in Jerusalem and the churches everywhere well founded by the
providence of God, with the presbyters and deacons and all the rest of the
brethren. Peace be always.

Let it be known to you, my lord, that Simon, who was appointed to be the
foundation of the church on account of the true faith and the most secure
subsistence of his teaching, and for this reason was renamed Peter by Jesus
himself with an unlying mouth, the first-fruits of our Lord, the first of the
apostles, to whom first the Father revealed the Son, whom the Christ blessed
with good reason, the called and elect and table-companion and
fellow-traveler, the good and approved disciple, who was commanded to
enlighten the darkest part of the world, the West, as being able more than
all, and was enabled to succeed, and now, to how far do I lengthen the word,
not wishing to signify the painful thing, which of necessity I must say even
if slowly: this man, on account of his measureless affection toward men,
having publicly proclaimed against the present wicked one the good king that
is to come over the whole world, having come as far as Rome and saving men by
his God-counseled teaching, himself changed the living of this present life by
violence.

But in those very days in which he was about to die, the brethren having been
gathered together, suddenly seizing my hand and rising up in the church he
said: Hear me, brethren and fellow servants. Since (as I was taught by the
Lord and teacher Jesus Christ who sent me) the days of my death have drawn
near, I ordain this Clement bishop for you, to whom I entrust my chair of
discourses, who from the beginning accompanied me to the end and thus heard
all my homilies, who, to sum up, having shared in all my trials was found
persevering in the faith, whom I have proved above all to be godly,
philanthropic, pure, learned, temperate, good, just, long-suffering and
knowing nobly to bear the ingratitude of some of the catechumens. Wherefore I
impart to him the authority of binding and loosing, that whatsoever he shall
ordain on earth shall be decreed in heaven. For he will bind what ought to be
bound and loose what ought to be loosed, as knowing the canon of the church.
Hear him therefore, knowing that he who grieves the one who sits before in the
truth sins against Christ and provokes the Father of all; wherefore he shall
not live. And the one who sits before must hold the place of a physician, and
not have the rage of an irrational beast.

While he was saying these things I fell before him and besought him, declining
the honor and authority of the chair. But he answered: Concerning this do not
deem me worthy; for this has been decreed to me to come to be, and all the
more if you decline it, because such a chair has need not of a bold man who
loves the chair, but of one reverent in manner and learned in word. But grant
the better man, who has journeyed with me more than you and heard the
discourses and learned the administration of the church, and I will not compel
you to do well unwillingly. But you will not be able now to present one better
than you; for you are the better first-fruits of the nations being saved
through me. However reckon also the other thing: that if, fearing the danger
of sin, you do not undertake the administration of the church, know well that
you sin the more, you who are able to help the godly who are as it were
sailing and in danger but do not wish to,
considering only your own things and not the common advantage of all. But that
you must altogether undertake the danger, this I know rightly, since I do not
cease to demand it of you for the help of all.

Therefore the sooner you consent, so much the more will you lighten my
despondency. But I myself also know, O Clement, the griefs and despondencies
and dangers and reproaches from uninstructed crowds that I am giving you as
gifts, which you will be able to bear nobly, seeing the great reward of
patience that is being rendered to you from God. But consider justly with me
when Christ has need of your alliance: now, when the wicked one began war
against his bride, or in the coming time, when, having conquered, he reigns
having need of no further help? Is it not evident even to one who has but
little understanding that it is now? Therefore with every resolve hasten in
the time of the present necessity to ally yourself with the good king, who by
nature renders great rewards after victory. Rejoicing therefore take the
oversight (rather seasonably, as having learned from me the administration of
the church) for the salvation of the brethren who take refuge with us.

However I wish to remind you briefly of the things of the administration
concerning all and for all. You must live blamelessly, with the greatest zeal
shaking off all the occupations of life, neither becoming a surety nor an
advocate nor entangled in any other worldly matter. For Christ does not wish
to establish you judge and arbiter of the moneys or occupations of the present
worldly affairs, lest, being held by the present cares of men, you have no
leisure to separate by the word of truth the better of men from the worse. But
let those who learn these things attend to them among themselves, and let them
not keep you busy away from the words that are able to save. For as it is
impious for you to take up worldly cares, leaving to do what you were
commanded, so for each layman it is a sin if they do not stand by one another
even in the worldly needs. And as for you, let all, if they do not understand
concerning what they must be without care, learn from the deacons, that you
may have the care of the church alone, both to administer it well and to
furnish the words of the truth. For if you are busied with worldly cares, you
will ensnare both yourself and your hearers; for not being able through the
occupation to furnish the profitable things, you also will be punished as not
having taught the profitable thing, and those who have not learned will perish
on account of ignorance. Wherefore presiding over them with leisure you may
seasonably furnish the words able to save them, and let them listen to you,
knowing that whatsoever the ambassador of the truth shall bind on earth is
bound in heaven, and whatsoever he shall loose is loosed. But you shall bind
what ought to be bound and loose what ought to be loosed. And these are the
things concerning you the president, and the things like to these.

But the things concerning the presbyters let them be these. Above all things
let them join the young to marriage in haste, anticipating the snares of
youthful desire. But let them not neglect marriage even of those already old;
for in some even when grown old there is a vigorous desire. Therefore that
fornication, having taken a range, may not cast against you the same
pestilence, guard beforehand and search lest the fire of adultery be kindled
in you unawares. For adultery is a very terrible thing, so much that it holds
the second place of punishment, since the first places are rendered to those
who are in error, even if they are temperate. Wherefore as presbyters of the
church exercise the bride of Christ toward temperance (but I call the body of
the church the bride); for if she, being temperate, is apprehended by the
kingly bridegroom, she will obtain the greatest honor, and you will enjoy
great gladness as invited to the weddings. But if she be caught having sinned,
she indeed shall be cast out, but you shall pay the penalty, lest the sin has
come to be through your negligence. Wherefore above all things be careful
about temperance; for fornication has been appointed very bitter before God.
But the forms of fornication are many, as Clement himself also will narrate to
you; however the first adultery is for a man not to use his own wife alone and
a woman not to use her own husband alone. If anyone is temperate, he is able
also to become philanthropic, for which he will obtain eternal mercy. But as
adultery is a great evil, so philanthropy is the greatest good. Wherefore love
all your brethren with grave and merciful eyes, doing to the orphans the
things of parents, and to the widows the things of husbands, furnishing the
foods with all cheerfulness, the marriages to those in their prime, and for
those without craft of their own considering through their occupations the
occasions of the necessary food: to the craftsman work, to the inactive mercy.
But I know that you will do these things if you fix love in your mind. But for
its entrance there is one sufficient occasion, the common partaking of salt.
Wherefore strive to become table-companions of one another more frequently as
you are able, that you may not bargain it away; for it is the cause of
well-doing, and well-doing of salvation. Therefore let all render their lives
common to all the brethren according to God, knowing that, giving things for a
season, you shall receive eternal things. Much more feed the hungry and give
drink to the thirsty and clothing to the naked; visit the sick; appear to
those in prisons, help as you are able; receive the strangers with all
readiness into your own houses. However, that I say not each particular,
philanthropy will teach you to do every good thing, as misanthropy points out
the ill-dealing to those who do not wish to be saved. Let the brethren who
have causes not be judged before the authorities, but let them be reconciled
by the presbyters of the church in every way, readily obeying them. However
flee greed also in this way, as able to deprive you of the eternal goods under
pretext of a seasonal gain. Balances, measures, weights, the just things of
the places keep carefully; toward deposits be grateful.

However you will endure to do these things and the things like to them until
the end, if you have in your hearts an unsevered remembrance of the judgment
that comes to be from God. For who would sin, being fully persuaded that by
the righteous God, who alone now is long-suffering and good, a judgment has
been appointed to come to be at the consummation of life, that the good may
enjoy forever the rest of the secret goods, but the sinners, found as evil,
may obtain the punishment of the secret things for the age? And that these
things are so, were it not that the prophet of the truth had said on oath that
it shall be, it would perhaps be reasonable to doubt. Wherefore being
disciples of a true prophet, having laid aside the double-mindedness from
which the ill-dealing comes to be, readily undertake to do well. But if any
one of you doubts concerning the things said to be, let him confess it without
shame, if he cares for his own soul, and he will be fully assured by the
president. But if he has believed rightly, let him live with confidence, as
fleeing the great fire of condemnation and entering into the eternal good
kingdom of God.

Therefore let the deacons of the church, looking wisely to the bishop, be
eyes, examining the doings of each one of the church, who is about to sin,
that being seized by admonition by the president he perhaps may not accomplish
the sin. Let them look after the absent, that they do not fail to come
together to hear the discourses, that they may be able to cleanse by the word
of truth the despondencies that fall upon the heart each time from worldly
mishaps and evil intercourse; for if they grow rougher with much time, they
become fuel of fire. And let them learn those who are sick also in the flesh
and
bring them to the notice of the multitude that does not know, that they may
appear, and let them furnish the things needful by the judgment of the
president; but even if they do this secretly of him, they do not sin. These
things therefore and the things like to them let the deacons provide.

Let those who catechize be first catechized; for the work is concerning the
souls of men; for the guide of the discourses must be fitted to the many
judgments of the learners. The catechizer therefore must be learned and
blameless, experienced and fearless, as you yourselves will know Clement to
be, who is about to catechize after me; for it is much for me now to speak in
detail. However, if you be of one mind, you will be able to be carried into
the harbor of rest, where is the peaceful city of a great king.

For the whole business of the church is like to a great ship carrying through
a violent winter men who are from many places and wish to inhabit a certain
city of a good kingdom. Let then God be to you the master of this, and let the
pilot be likened to Christ, the prow-officer to the bishop, the sailors to the
presbyters, the bench-masters to the deacons, the pursers to those who
catechize, to the passengers the multitude of the brethren, the depth to the
world, the contrary winds to the temptations, and the persecutions and dangers
and all kinds of afflictions to the waves, and the gusts of the winter streams
and the blasts to the discourses of the errors and false prophets, and the
capes and the rugged places to the judges in high places threatening terrible
things, and the two-seas and beast-like places to the most unreasonable and
those who doubt concerning the promises of the truth. Let the hypocrites be
likened to the pirates and be understood.

However account only the sins to be a strong whirlpool and a Tartarean
Charybdis and murderous wrecks and deadly dissolutions. Therefore that sailing
with a fair wind you may be carried more safely into the harbor of the
hoped-for city, pray being obedient; but prayers become obedient through the
good deeds. Let the passengers therefore remain seated firm upon their own
places, that they occasion not by disorder a rolling or a listing. Let the
pursuers remind of the wages. Let the deacons neglect nothing of the things
entrusted to them. Let the presbyters as sailors diligently equip the things
needful for each. Let the bishop as prow-officer wakeful counter the words of
the pilot alone. Let Christ be loved as the saving pilot and alone be believed
concerning what he says. And let all pray to God for sailing with a fair wind.
Let those sailing expect every affliction as skirting the world, a great and
troublous depth: sometimes desponding, persecuted, scattered, hungry, thirsty,
naked, straitened, and again being united, congregated, keeping quiet; but
also being sea-sick, giddy, vomiting (that is, confessing the transgressions
as physicians bile, I mean the sins from bitterness and the evils heaped up
from disordered desires, which, having vomited as it were by confessing, you
are lightened of the sickness, receiving the saving health that comes of the
care). However know all of you that the bishop toils more than all of you,
because each of you suffers his own affliction, but he his own and that of
each. Wherefore, O Clement, preside as helper to each according to your power,
having taken on yourself the cares of all. Whence I know that I have received
grace in your undertaking the stewardship, not given it. But take courage and
bear nobly, as knowing that God will render to you, when you arrive at the
harbor of rest, the greatest of the goods, an incorruptible reward, since you
undertook the greater toil for the salvation of all. So that if many of the
brethren hate you for justice to the utmost, their hatred will in nothing hurt
you, but the friendship of the
righteous God will greatly profit you. So try to shake off the praise that
comes of injustice, and through righteous administration to catch the
profitable praise that is from Christ.

Having said these things and more than these, again looking to the multitude
he said: And you also, my beloved brethren and fellow servants, yield in all
things to the president of the truth, knowing this, that he who has grieved
him has not received Christ, with whose chair he has been entrusted; and he
who has not received Christ will be reckoned as having rejected the Father,
wherefore he shall be rejected from the good kingdom. Wherefore endeavor to
come to all the assemblies, lest as absentees you receive the accusation of
sins through the despondency of the commander. Wherefore above all let all
think the things that concern him, knowing this, that on account of each one
of you the wicked one, being most hostile to him alone, wars against him.
Therefore endeavor to continue in affection toward him and in goodwill toward
one another and to listen to him, that both he may be lightened and you may be
able to be saved. But some things you ought also to understand of yourselves
because he is not able to speak openly on account of the plots: as, if he is
hostile to any one, do not wait for him to say, Do not contend with this man,
but wisely follow his counsel, becoming enemies to those whom he is hostile to
and not associating with those with whom he does not associate; that each one,
striving to have all for friends, being reconciled to him may be saved,
listening to his association. But if any one remains a friend to those whom he
himself is hostile to and speaks to those with whom he himself does not
associate, he himself is one of those who wish to destroy the church; for
being with you in body, not being with you in judgment, he is against you,
much worse than the enemies appearing from without, scattering those within
with a seeming friendship.

And having said these things, in the midst before all, laying his hands upon
me, he besought me to be seated in his own chair. And when I had sat, he
immediately said to me: I require you before all the brethren present, that
whenever I am released from this life (as is owed to me) you not hesitate to
send to James the brother of the Lord, having written in epitome even the
reasonings from your boyhood, and how from the beginning until now you
journeyed with me, hearing the discourses and deeds preached by me in each
city, then at the end also to declare the occasion of my death as I foretold.
For it will not grieve him very much, knowing that I piously endured what I
must needs suffer, and he will obtain the greatest consolation, having learned
that after me not an unlearned man, not ignorant of life-giving words and not
knowing the canon of the church, was entrusted with the chair of the one
teaching; for the deceitful discourse of the crowds hearing destroys the
souls. Whence I, my lord James, he having said these things, promising did not
hesitate, as I was commanded, to make into books by chapters the much of the
discourses in the cities that have been already written to you and sent by
him, as it were for a token, and thus to send them to you, inscribing: The
Epitome of the Sojourning Preachings of Peter by Clement. However I shall
begin to set forth, as I was commanded.
