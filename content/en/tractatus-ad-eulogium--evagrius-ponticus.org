Of the same, a discourse to Eulogius the monk. =79.1093= CHAP. 1. Those who
inhabit the heavenly region by labors do not gaze79.1096 upon the belly, nor
upon the care of perishable things, as those who make the prayers for the sake
of gain, thinking piety to be a means of provision, but by noetic vision they
partake of her nourishing food of the heights, undefiled companies, being
shone about with the light from the glory. And you yourself (O initiate of the
virtues, Eulogius), being nourished in the sacred substance by the radiance of
the highest things, strip off the bulk of the flesh in the assemblies of the
thoughts, knowing that the matter of flesh is constituted as food of thoughts;
as one who has seized the wiles with the goad of the labors, you appoint me to
become a mouth of your works against them, and if it were not rash to break a
command of love, I would have declined such a seafaring of the voyage; but
since one must rather obey those of like soul, not set oneself against them:
be you to me the things toward God, that speech may be given me in the opening
of my mouth, and I will be to you the things of obedience, that it may be
given you to reap the fruits of the seed.

CHAP. 2. The first of the bright contests is exile, especially when toward
this you also go abroad alone, stripping off fatherland, race, and substance
as an athlete, standing before the bright contests; if you thus begin
according to God, walking the road of a beginning with fervent faith and a
divine Spirit, and at the end of endurance keeping this sound, the soul will
be gilded about with wings of virtue, withdrawing from its own places and
hastening to fly toward heaven itself. But the begetter of vice contrives to
cut off the wings of this way of life, mirroring the land from which the race
has come out, especially in the assaults that come upon us in sickness; and in
the sojourn of the most fervent going-abroad he draws back the reins for a
little, until he learns that the soul is seasick about the afflictions, and
then the night-raven, bringing on the night of thoughts, darkens the soul from
the ray of the better things. And if one should array himself alone in the
wrestling-ground of the desert, and the body happen to be wounded by sickness,
so much the more he shows the exile harsh to the soul, suggesting that the
things of the virtues are accomplished not in place but in manner, and that
having at home the consolation from one's race, one would there have preserved
the contests of renunciation without toil; where there is also a gentle
service of the weakness, and not as now ill-treatment and painful fainting;
and especially in that the zeal of hospitality has grown scarce from the
brotherhood; wherefore also especially, "Go," he says, "bringing yourself as
joy and glory to your race, to whom you have also left the grief unbearable,
without sympathy"; for many, even without fleeing the fatherland, have chosen
virtue from the midst of their race. But he who has clothed himself with the
purple of afflictions in the array of exile, and has been crowned about with
the hopes of the toils by faith, by endurance =79.1097= of thanksgiving shakes
off the snowfalls of these thoughts from the outside, and by as much as they
dig through to turn the heart back, by so much still more let us sing against
them: "Behold, I have gone far fleeing, and lodged in the wilderness"; for
they both afflict by reproaching and flatter to turn aside, that of the
purpose

emptying, they may also cut off the thanksgiving in endurance, and so
thereafter widen the snares with much license, even from the things that
belong.

CHAP. 3. For this reason let him who is beginning to lay hold of virtue also
reckon the war that comes against it, lest being seen untrained he be pulled
down as unready. For the courage of virtue is praiseworthy even when there is
peace, but its valor is above praise even when war has come on; for this is
virtue, not only through the things it works, but also through the terrible
things by which it is warred upon; dispassionate is he who through very many
wars has conquered suffering, but passionate is he who says he possesses
virtue unwaged-upon; for according to the array of the virtuous generalship of
the labors, the vice of the adversaries is also arrayed against it; a heart
that has no war has been stripped of being in virtue. For virtue has been
called a practical name from the deeds of valor. Virtue does not seek the
praises from men, for it is not delighted by honor, the mother of evils. The
beginning of honor, then, is people-pleasing, and the end of this is pride.
For he who demands honors exalts himself, and such a one does not know how to
bear contempt; desire of honor is a fantasy, for he who loves it also imagines
an inheritance. Let the labor of the virtues be honor to you, and praise
according to will be dishonor; you who dissolve the passions of the flesh, do
not seek glory from flesh, but seek the better, and it will be glory to you;
he who wishes to be honored envies the one who is better esteemed. And he who
has been defeated by excessive honors heaps hatred on this one with jealousy;
he wishes no one to be preferred before himself, and he snatches the first
prizes, lest he appear lesser; he does not bear to honor the one who is well
thought of when absent, but even in his labors he mocks the glory that creeps
on the ground; insult is a most sharp slaughter to the glory-lover, and he
does not altogether escape wrath from it.

CHAP. 4. Such a one is slave to a barbarian mistress and has been sold on to
many masters, to conceit and envy and jealousy and the aforesaid picked troops
of the spirits; but he who dashes the spirit of honor with humility will pull
down the whole legion of the demons; he who by humility offers himself a slave
to all will have been made like the one who humbled himself and took the form
=79.1100= of a slave. If you measure yourself by the smallest measure, you
will not measure yourself against a companion, and he who uncovers the
weakness of the soul with laments will not think great things upon the labors
done in himself. Demons bring cheapness and insult upon the humble-minded,
that not bearing the contempt they may flee humility, but he who through
humility bears the dishonors nobly is rather pushed up through them to a
height of philosophy. David when insulted did not answer back, but even
checked the vengeance of Abishai. And you, when insulted, do not insult in
return, but even rightly soothe the one who avenges you; bear insult, your own
progress, and with lips shut the door of anger; you will answer those who
threaten not at all, that by silence you may put to sleep the smoking lips;
then you will pain the threateners and insulters when you set bits on your
jaws. For you, being silent, will not be eaten by the insult, but he is bitten
much more by your silence, whenever you long-sufferingly bear an insulter's
boldness; shake off the praise of men from the inward things, that you may
shake out the show-loving thought before it. And watch also self-pleasing
especially in stillness, lest at some time, magnifying you above the one who
grew bold, you despise that one. One of the brothers was insulted by a pious
man, and having endured injustice he went away sharing both in joy and in
grief, the one because being wronged he was insulted and was not troubled in
return, the other because the pious man was deceived and, having deceived,
rejoiced. And understand for me that the deceiver also suffered the two
things: at that in which he troubled, having altogether rejoiced; at that in
which he was not troubled in return, having rather been grieved.

CHAP. 5. When the demons see us not kindled at the same scraping of insults,
then standing over us in stillness they lever up the governing part, that
toward those with whom we were at peace when present we may grow bold when
they have gone. Whenever then you have accomplished contradiction or insult
toward the brother, reckon yourself to have slipped, lest being still you find
a battle of thoughts in your heart, the one reproaching the manner of the
insults, the other reproaching in return as the terrible things even of you
who did not insult in return. Whenever bitterness from provocation embitters
the brothers in a cenobium, then the thoughts send in blessing the one who is
alone, that emptying them of long-suffering they may also cut them off from
love. But he who overturns anger by long-suffering and grief by love overturns
two wicked beasts with twin deeds of valor as they fight in anger; he who
exhorts and falls on his knees, that he may drive away the anger, the one who
provoked, drives both out from himself, because he who makes peace among those
who are angry wars down the spirit of anger; but it will be wroth at this one,
making bold against him the anger of those of the same race, because he
received into himself the battle of the neighbors; he =79.1101= who bears the
bold-hearted for the sake of peace, this one is forced to become a son of
peace; but the bond of peace is to be sought not upon men only, but also in
your body, and in your spirit, and in your soul.

CHAP. 6. For when you unite the bond of this triad of yours with peace, then
as united to the commandment of the divine Trinity you hear: "Blessed are the
peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of God." For if by the labors you
make peace with the flesh that desires against the spirit, you will have fame
of the beatitudes beyond an age, having conquered the war in your body that
campaigns against the law of your mind and takes you captive to the law of sin
that is in your members. Great is the bond of peace, in which joy also is
united, illuminating the eye of the understanding toward the contemplation of
the better things; if then we acquire the peaceful joy in the labors, we will
through it thankfully beat off the hard things that come on, and we will not
receive the demon of grief as it bellows, who, mutilating especially in the
afflictions, leaps upon the soul and prepares a place for the spirit of
acedia, that they may darken the soul and at once gather up the labors. Let
then the joy of peace be a written law of our heart, both drowning grief and
driving away hatred, and making wrath vanish, and dissolving acedia, and
exchanging grief. For lurking in peaceful long-suffering, and pooling in
thanksgiving and in endurance, it is a sea of virtues drowning the opposing
array of the devil with the cross. And understand for me that the contrary joy
stands over us in confusion, lest somehow, being overshadowed, you be deceived
into holding another instead of another. For the demons also conform
themselves to the spiritual gifts, that having led the mind astray by the
counterfeit shadow they may make it stand off from the wits; for spiritual joy
stands over a heart when nothing is set forth, because it finds the leader of
grief according to God lifted up, and then it also hands the soul over to the
Spirit of grief, because it has made it captive of the spiritual joy.

CHAP. 7. And I say this also, as the experience of the things sought dictated
to one of the brothers, that instead of rejoicing in the Lord the destroyer
introduces a joy of his own. And instead of the grief according to God he
tries the mind with the contrary, that by the reverse changes of the ray of
the better things he may with cares the soul

darken. The griefs of vice happen to be twofold, yoked on in each working, and
the one stands over a heart when a grievous cause is not seen, and the other
is born, pushed from strange causes; the grief according to God =79.1104=
recalls the soul with tears, not receiving the joy and grief set opposite, and
it cares for the death that comes on, and judgment; and this it has gaped
toward, awaiting it little by little. Desires that have failed plant griefs;
but prayers and thanksgivings wither these; between those who are angry a
middle grief is shaken. He therefore who first comes to himself, if he rise
from the passion and give a hand to the other, by apology drives away the
bitter grief. Grief happens to be a disease of soul and of flesh, and it takes
the one captive, and on the spot it withers the other. From contrary things
grief is born, and from grief wrath, and from these is born frenzy, railing;
if you wish to trample grief and wrath together, embrace the long-suffering of
love, and clothe yourself with the joy of guilelessness; let not your joy be
grief to another; he who rejoices at injustice will lament in good repute, and
he who bears griefs, suffering unjustly, will brightly exult; for the things
to come are contrary to the things present.

CHAP. 8. In the afflictions be thankful especially, because through them you
perceive more clearly the grace of the help. For thus, thankfully shaking off
the afflictions that come on, you will not darken the most bright beauty of
endurance; whenever a demon dashes a scourge against you according to the
flesh, he has been found a procurer of a very great wage for you, if you
accept the blow as a subject of thanksgiving. For thus you will also put him
to flight from you. And that your wage may rain more through endurance, let
your endurance be general through all the courageous labors, because through
every vice acedia also campaigns against you, and spying out all your labors
it tries; and whom it does not find nailed to endurance, this one it weighs
down under itself and bends down; but if the demon of acedia grow weak toward
the endurance of the labors, the beast again bites the accomplishment of
presumption; if then by understanding of safety you drive this, you will also
drive a vainglorious spirit in it at the time of the synaxis. Whenever the
spirit of acedia fall upon you, it votes psalmody as burdensome to the soul,
and it sets hesitation beside zeal as an adversary, that having touched the
flesh with speed it may lay it down in the memory, as having been wearied
supposedly from some cause. Whenever then we are wakeful at night, let us not
lay the synaxis down to acedia, lest the demons standing over gather the tares
of the thoughts and at once sow them in the heart; for when we lose the
assembly of the hymns, then we gather together the gathering of the thoughts;
having been awakened before the synaxis, let us pre-exercise thoughts of light
in the heart, that being made ready we may have, with a waking understanding,
to stand by the psalmody. =79.1105=

CHAP. 9. Now with a rush the psalm is to be spoken in sobriety, and now one
must try to converse in the psalmody; for according to the ambush of the
adversary it is necessary that we too be transformed, because at one time they
suggest driving the tongue, when acedia holds the soul round, at another they
provoke the words to be sung, when the noetic eye takes its food from the
light as strength. For he who stretches himself out toward the vision of the
high things will receive a keener eye, according to the example of Elisha.
When a thought of yours, an enemy, stands over the heart, do not seek other
things instead of other things through prayer, but whet against the enemy the
sword of tears; for thus if we dash most vehemently upon the battle, we will
make him stand off from us more quickly. Love beauty together with the work of
the hands, and very much the memory of prayer; for the one does not always,
but the other has an unceasing passage of the working. Do not delay to give
the debt of prayer, hearing a thought through a pretext of works, and do not
be in tumult

in the work, disturbing the body, lest you also disturb together the heart of
prayer. As our outer man works with hands toward not burdening anyone, so let
the one in the wits work, toward the mind not being burdened. For then the
thoughts offer their own working to the soul, when they find it idle from the
thoughts according to God. Plow, the works of the hands toward the love of
men, and the wit of the reasoning part toward the love of wisdom, that the one
may be hospitality of strangers and a burning of hesitation, and the other a
general of contemplation and a boiling-off of thoughts; let us with boldness
rebuke the thoughts at the pleasures, turning all the anger upon them, lest,
receiving the things from them with pleasure, we become conversely meek toward
them.

CHAP. 10. Prepare yourself to be meek, and a fighter, the one toward the one
of the same race, the other toward the enemy; for in this is the use of anger,
in fighting against the serpent according to the enmity; and in this also is
the reasonable mildness of the meek, in long-suffering toward the brother
according to love, and in warring against the thought. Let the meek then be a
fighter, the meekness being divided from the guile-minded thoughts, just as
also the fight from those of the same race of nature; do not reverse the use
of anger into what is against nature, so as to be angry at the brother,
according to the likeness of the serpent, and to be made friends with the
serpent according to the consent of the thoughts. The meek, even if he suffer
the terrible things, does not stand out from love; for for the sake of this he
is long-suffering and covers, is kind and endures. For if to be long-suffering
belongs to love, to fight in anger does not belong to love; for anger wakes
grief and wrath, but love lessens the three. If you have a firm base in love,
attend rather to this than to the one who makes you stumble; serve God with
fear and love, the one as Master and judge79.1108, the other as lover of men
and nourisher; he who has acquired the virtues of love takes captive the
passions of the base, and he who has from the holy Trinity these three, faith,
hope, love, will be a city with a triple wall, towered with the virtues. Not
in not receiving only from another will you be shown loving, but in giving
unsparingly you will be recognized as one who has renounced; in your giving
the matter, contend to cast the seeds clean, lest instead of wheat nettle come
out for you. Remember God in the things you provide, both of giver and of
receiver, that he may reckon to you with praises the wages of the
renunciation.

CHAP. 11. The possessionless has the pleasure of life without care, but the
possession-lover has as care the pain of wealth through all; then you will
drive a people of thoughts captive, when you do not give the heart to the
cares of the materials; then you will also bear the cross without distraction,
when you deny the desire of acquiring; but the thought of matter prophesies
old age to you, and diseases, that it may divide your hope toward God with the
money. Let him who has chosen to practice the renunciation be walled with
faith, and be strengthened with love, and be confirmed with hope. For faith is
not abandonment, but a substance of the better things in hope of endurance,
and in the love of life. When you renounce all the materials from outside,
take care that the gloomy thoughts reproach poverty to you, and bring you want
and cheapness and ill-repute, that the murderous ones may craftily work a
change of mind from such a bright virtue. If then you attend with the
understanding of the contest, you will rather find that through the things
with which they reproach you, through them the crown is plaited for you; for
as you renounce, you renounce through those through whose contests you are
reproached. Do not then give in to the wrestling of the inward thoughts,
because it is not at the beginning of the renunciation that the end is
praised, but at the end of endurance the beginnings are crowned, nor in the
bodily

exercise only are the contests assembled, but also in the wrestling of the
thoughts that which concerns a crown is sought.

CHAP. 12. Judge the thoughts at the tribunal of the heart, that when the
robbers are being killed the arch-robber may fear; for he who is an exact
examiner of the thoughts is also a true lover of the commandments. Whenever
then a thought hard to find stands over your heart, then kindle more intense
labors upon it; for either not bearing the heat as contrary it flees, or it
endures this as being of the straight road. And there are times when the
demons, suggesting a thought supposedly good to the heart, and immediately
being transformed, pretend to oppose this, that from the opposition you may
think that they also know the inward thoughts of your heart; and not only, but
that they may be judged =79.1109= to judge your conscience, as by the
opposition the evil has been defeated by the good; and there is again a time
when they hint to you their own intrigue, that you may seem to yourself to be
prudent. When thoughts transfer you to a place which they suggested we love,
then they make us regret again, that they may make us unstable from every
side, and unfruitful. Therefore let us not fly ourselves from places to
places, but let us rather be bent toward stillness and toils, because from our
own slackness the thoughts take the power against us. And he who knows a trial
of a thought, in whatever place he was called, let him remain in this with
God; but he who does not know still walks in a contest. Let the change of
places be toward the more spiritual, and not toward the more restful; for
endurance and long-suffering and love give thanks in the afflictions; but
acedia and lightness and self-love rejoice in the rests. The unquiet is warred
upon in the soul through the senses about the sights, but the lover of
stillness, guarding the senses, wars against the thoughts.

CHAP. 13. Let then your governing part gather the order of the senses to the
ordinance of the law, lest with look and hearing you stir up scourges of evils
for your soul; being in two substances, take care to distribute the order to
each, that the one may do deeds of valor, and the other may not speak against,
and do not give the commands to the tyrant, because when this has been given
to his fire you will pay even the last farthing. When fighting against the
causes of the passions you put them to flight, let not a wicked thought
magnify you, lest somehow having trusted a spirit of error you also be struck
out of your wits; but seek to reckon the excesses in the things for which you
labor, that the ends of the accomplishments may not be stolen through the
things within. Some, having been praised upon accomplishments, grew listless
at the labors with time, and the praise passed through, and the labors were
dissolved. Some, being hardened through a bulk of good things, were thought
great, and the conscience of the soul was ulcerated, and the disease of the
praise was widened, and the thoughts, leading the soul astray from the wounds,
carried off the labors in the praises. Whenever the most laborious grow rich
with the honors from men, at that time the demons, having prepared the
dishonors, also bring them on, that, being from the honors, they may not bear
the dishonors and may not carry the insults. When upon sins you give a great
repentance, then the thoughts, magnifying the contests of the labors, make
sins small, and often cover them with forgetfulness, or even signify that
these have been forgiven, that having given in a little to the labors you may
not reckon to howl more the laments of the stumblings against them.

CHAP. 14. But he who boxes to cut out the passions that have fallen on will
marshal =79.1112= more armed men of passions to the battle. Do not be
unmindful when you have stumbled, even if you repent,

but have as a memory of your sin the mourning toward your humility, that,
having been humbled, you may of necessity cut out pride. If one of those who
have done the unlawful things should wish to live over again most excellently,
let him stand opposite the deeds by an exchange of the better things; for he
who sets the opposite deed against each wickedness shoots the dragon with the
understanding quiver of the virtues, and the most sharp weapon of the beast is
vainglory, shooting down the labors, which he who has anticipated with the
hidden generalships of the works has drawn near to bring down the whole head
of the devil. Seal your spices of the labors with silence, lest being loosed
by the tongue they be stolen by glory; hide your tongue in the practical
manner of the ascesis; for being silent you would have as witnesses of the
life your trustworthy fellow-livers, the labors. He who does not have to be
witnessed from the present labors, to whatever people, let him not witness to
himself with the tongue; for some, having stripped themselves of the labors of
hardness, as a covering of slackness compare the deeds of those who have gone
away in time, unsteadily offering as clear witnesses works that are not
present. As you hide your sins from men, so also hide the labors from them;
for the counter-craft of the hidden things of the heart, the secret trips and
the counter-wrestlings, they set as props against them; but if we hide the
faults with safety, and unsafely uncover the labors against them, we also do
the contrary in both. But you are ashamed of the publication of the shameful
things, lest the misfortunes of your soul make reproach and contempt. Fear
also the display of the labors, lest somehow you bring a people-pleasing
charge, the ruin of the soul; and if you also reveal to God alone the
stumbling-steps of the shame, do not reveal to men the counter-contests
against them, lest they be thought to be crownings of victory.

CHAP. 15. Let those who receive the power of the labors from the grace not
think they have this as from their own strength; for the cause of all the good
things for us is the word of the commandments, just as also of the evils the
deceptive one of the rejections. The good things then that you accomplish,
offer the thanksgiving to the cause of the good things; and the evils that
trouble you, throw to the leader of these. Assign the end of every transaction
as thanksgiving to the better, that when your offering has been brought
legally the vice may be put to shame. For he who has joined the thanksgiving
to the deed will have the treasure of the heart unravaged, having towered a
double wall against vice. Praiseworthy is this man, who has yoked the gnostic
to the practical, that from both springs the plot of the soul may be watered
toward virtue; for the gnostic wings the noetic substance =79.1113= with the
contemplation of the better things, and the practical puts to death the
members that are upon the earth, fornication, uncleanness, passion, vice, evil
desire; those then who have been fenced about with the full armor through both
of these will thereafter easily trample upon the wickedness of the demons. The
demons war against the soul through the thoughts, and they themselves are more
harshly warred against through endurance, and thereafter they come to the
battle in fear, looking under at the general of the boxing as strong. If you
wish to be general against the phalanx of the demons, fence the gates of your
soul with stillness, and also touch your ear with the words of fatherhood,
that then rather, having learned, you may burn the thorns of thoughts,
whenever you hear a paternal admonition. Do not be a judge of the works, but
an examiner of the sayings.

CHAP. 16. For it is a custom for such thoughts to lead you away to the
practical of the one who admonishes you, that having set you as a terrible
judge of this they may of the

beneficial admonition make you stand off. Do not refuse when being admonished,
even if you are gnostic; for if the practical be unyoked from the gnostic,
there is need of the one who yokes the harmony of either of the virtues to the
fearful justice of the judgment. He who shakes the paternal commandment out of
the ear will also be disobedient to a legal commandment; do not only, being
delighted, speak accomplishments of Fathers, but also demand yourself as a
most laborious worker of these. He who more laboriously seeks of what sort a
thought is opposed to what sort of labor is found a craftsman in wrestling
against the error; and in all your noetic labors, in whichever he is lacking,
the one who has plundered this slips in instead of it. Remember with
understanding of the labors to keep the heart, lest forgetfulness of the care
of the better things, having plundered this, hand it over to the captivity of
the thoughts; for the forgetfulness of the captivity leaps toward vice, that
when the mind has been stolen by that, this may readily come in; a mind being
stolen from God, and being distanced from memory, also sins indifferently
through the outer senses; for such a one is unable to train hearing and
tongue, because he has kindled the love-charm of the labors from the outside.
He who loves to hear fully the one who blames so-and-so, the two work together
with two cooperating spirits; for the evil-hearing happens to be a coworker of
the accusation, and they love one another toward a ruin of heart.

CHAP. 17. Fence your ears from the slanders, lest you doubly sin together with
them, both accustoming yourself to a terrible passion and not checking those
from being tongue-sick. The mocker plunders a soul of the better things,
digging through a rotten hearing with calumnies; but he who runs through the
railing tongue of the neighbor also puts his own railing to flight; he who
provides pleasant hearings to the railer milks a beast's venom into the ears.
Let not your ear taste of this =79.1116= bitter antidote, lest somehow you
also mix such for another; do not bewitch your ear with slanders, lest having
been sold to a passion you be slave to a many-passioned state; for one passion
of the many, having found a place in you, brings others also into the same
fold; then the governing part is enslaved to a multitude of evils, when yoked
to a passion it unyokes the labors. He who tries to search the thoughts of
others does not search his own deeds in work; do not mock the one who has died
as having lived carelessly, lest as from a habit of the living you also become
a bitter judge of the dead; do not attend to those who stumble with a boastful
thought that lifts you as a judge, but attend to yourself with a sober
thought, and an assayer of your own deeds. Groan when stumbling, and do not
puff when accomplishing; do not boast to be not-contemptible, that you may not
put on the evil as an ornament. For some, not being able to be known from
piety, have hastened to be known even thus from vice; and others, having grown
with envy, skirmish pretexts, and they set at nought those who are stable in
the virtues. Some, being rumored into base things among those around, put on a
solemn life, not that they may lament the base stumblings with the labors, but
that they may overshadow the reports of the blames. But do not beautify
yourself for the one who deceives you, nor exult at being deceived; for if
through a bare name you lay hold of the things of the zealous, you work not

champion to boast, and to lift himself against the brotherhood as cheap; and
thus they make him narrate the contests as having been accomplished from his
own strength alone, that "I did these things, and I exercised thus, and

I was ill-treated," still stopping the mouth from saying this, "Yet not I, but
the help in me." For they do not allow him to confess God as a helper upon the
things they make him boast, that having supposedly completed the whole of the
contests from his own strength he may demand the praises of the prizes wholly,
that he may also be sea-sunk into the depth of blasphemy, unfeelingly hinting
himself as self-helped.

CHAP. 34. Thus then, the heart being sounded through with the glory of the
thoughts, and not being arrayed against, it will not be outside the frenzy in
the hidden of the wits, the governing part of wits being in danger of being
shaken out, either through dreams that are made trusted, or being transformed
in vigils, or appearing in a transformation =79.1140= of light. For Satan
himself is transformed into an angel of light toward our deceit, showing
perhaps that he will give gifts, so that having fallen you may worship, or
announcing good tidings to take up as another Elijah, or promising to hallow,
of which some, having received the faith, missed about the truth, and became
mind-harmed. You then, O suppliant of the holy Trinity, knowing these things
in which you love to labor, keep your heart with all guarding, lest attending
only to the labors from outside you be craftily noosed by those within. My
words then, O child, have been spoken toward you, and let your heart keep my
sayings; remember Christ who guards you, and do not forget the worshiped and
consubstantial Trinity, to whom glory unto the ages. Amen.
