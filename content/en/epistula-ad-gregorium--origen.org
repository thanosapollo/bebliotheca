2.1 1 Greetings in God, my lord, most earnest and most reverend son
Gregory, from Origen. Natural ability toward understanding, as you
know, is able to bring forth a work when it has taken on exercise,
leading, as far as is possible, that I may so name it, to the end of
that which one wishes to practice. Your natural ability then is able
to make you a perfect Roman jurist and a Greek philosopher of one of
the schools counted distinguished. But I wished that you would put
the whole power of your natural ability to use, finally, for
Christianity; and for this reason I would have prayed that you take
also from the philosophy of the Greeks those things that can become,
as it were, encyclical studies or preparatory disciplines for
Christianity, and those things from geometry and astronomy that will
be useful for the interpretation of the sacred scriptures; so that
what the children of the philosophers say about geometry and music
and grammar and rhetoric and astronomy, as fellow-workers with
philosophy, we too may say about philosophy itself in relation to
Christianity. 2 And perhaps something of this sort is hinted at by
what is written in Exodus from the person of God, that it be said to
the sons of Israel to ask from neighbors and fellow-dwellers vessels
of silver and of gold and clothing; so that, having spoiled the
Egyptians, they may find material for the construction of the things
taken up into the worship toward God. For from the things with which
the sons of Israel spoiled the Egyptians the things in the holy of
holies were constructed, the ark with the covering and the cherubim
and the mercy-seat and the golden jar, in which was stored the manna,
the bread of the angels. These things then likely came from the
finest gold of the Egyptians; and from some second gold after that,
the lampstand solid gold throughout, near the inner veil, and the
lamps upon it, and the golden table on which were the loaves of the
presentation, and between both the golden censer. And if there was a
third and a fourth gold, from that the holy vessels were constructed.
And from Egyptian silver other things were made; for while sojourning
in Egypt the sons of Israel gained this from the sojourn there, to
have come into possession of so much precious material for the things
useful to the worship of God. And from Egyptian clothing it is likely
that there came to be whatever needed the works, as the scripture
named them, of embroiderers, the embroiderers sewing together with
the wisdom of God such garments for such, so that there might come to
be the veils and the curtains outer and inner. 3 And why must I,
digressing unseasonably, establish for how many useful things the
things taken from Egypt are to the sons of Israel, things which the
Egyptians did not use as they ought, but the Hebrews, through the
wisdom of God, used for piety? Yet the divine scripture knows that
for some it has come about for harm to have gone down from the land
of the sons of Israel into Egypt; hinting that for some it comes
about for harm to sojourn with the Egyptians, that is, with the
learnings of the world, after having been reared in the law of God
and in the Israelite service toward him. Ader the Idumaean, then, as
long as he was in the land of Israel, not tasting the breads of the
Egyptians, did not construct idols; but when, having fled the wise
Solomon, he went down into Egypt, as having fled from the wisdom of
God he became kinsman to Pharaoh, having married the sister of his
wife and begetting the child who was reared among the children of
Pharaoh. Wherefore, even if he returned to the land of Israel, he
returned in order to split the people of God, and to make them say
over the golden heifer: These are your gods, Israel, who brought you
up from the land of Egypt. And I too, having learned by experience,
would say to you that rare is the one who has taken the useful things
of Egypt and gone out of it and constructed the things for the
worship of God; but many is the
brother of the Idumaean Ader. And these are those who from some Greek
cleverness have begotten heretical thoughts, and have constructed, as
it were, golden heifers in Bethel, which is interpreted house of God.
And it seems to me that through these things too the word hints that
they have set up their own fabrications upon the scriptures, in which
the word of God dwells, which are tropically called Bethel. And the
other fabrication the word says has been set up in Dan. And the
borders of Dan are last, and near the Gentile borders; as is clear
from the things written in that of Jesus the son of Nun. Some of the
fabrications then are near Gentile borders, which the brothers of Ader,
as we have explained, fabricated. 4 You then, lord son, attend first of
all to the reading of the divine scriptures; but attend. For we who read
the divine things need much attention; so that we may not too rashly say
or think some things about them. And attending to the reading of the
divine things with a faithful and God-pleasing preconception, knock
at the things of it that have been shut, and it will be opened to you
by the doorkeeper, of whom Jesus said: To this one the doorkeeper
opens. And attending to the divine reading, seek rightly and with
faith in God that does not waver the mind of the divine writings that
is hidden from the many. But do not be content with knocking and
seeking; for most necessary also is the prayer concerning the
understanding of the divine things; to which the Savior, exhorting,
not only said: Knock, and it will be opened to you; and: Seek, and
you will find; but also: Ask, and it will be given to you. These
things have been dared from my fatherly love toward you. And whether
the things dared are well or not, God would know, and his Christ, and
the one who partakes of the Spirit of God and of the Spirit of
Christ. And may you partake, and always increase the participation,
so that you may say not only: We have become partakers of Christ, but
also: We have become partakers of God.
