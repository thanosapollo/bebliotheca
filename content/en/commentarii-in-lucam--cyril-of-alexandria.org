{3Zu Lk. 9, 27–29 The disciples, not yet having received the power from on

80 fulfill in Jerusalem, that is, the mystery of the economy with the flesh
and the saving passion, I mean that upon the precious cross; for they
always call it glory. And it is true that the law through Moses and the
word of the holy prophets showed forth beforehand the mystery of Christ,
the one in types and shadows, all but drawing it as on a tablet, and the
others having proclaimed in many ways that he would also be seen in due
times in the form that is according to us, and that for the salvation and

ZU LK 9, 29 And we say that the transfiguration was accomplished not, I

light-like glory wrapping it about and recoloring the most dishonored
character of the flesh as it were into a more glorious appearance,
according to It is sown in dishonor, it is raised in glory, well and
rightly said by the blessed Paul. For now this very thing is naked flesh,
not dyed with any glory, not adorned with natural brightness, but having
only and merely the ingloriousness and weakness from nature; but at the
time of the resurrection there will be a God-befitting change and a change
of glory, not of form. For it will then flash around with a covering of
divine glory; 81 for the righteous will shine forth like the sun in the


kingdom of the Father according to the Savior's voice. ZU LK 9, 32φ The
blessed disciples doze off a little while Christ is occupied with prayer;
for he was fulfilling human things in the manner of the economy. Then,
having woken thoroughly, they became spectators of so august and
paradoxical a change. And the divine Peter, perhaps thinking that the time
of the kingdom of God had now arrived, accepts the sojourns on the
mountain, and says that three tents must be made: one for Christ, and the
others for Moses and Elijah. But he did not know, he says, what he was
saying; for it was not the time of the consummation of the age, nor yet of
the saints' needing to receive participation in the hope promised to them.
For as Paul says, he will transform the body of our humiliation to be
conformed to the body of his glory, Christ, that is. Since then the
economy was still in its beginnings and not yet finished, how was it
likely that Christ should cease from love toward the world, departing from
being willing to suffer for it? For he saved the world under heaven even
by enduring death according to the flesh itself and by abolishing it
through the resurrection from the dead. ZU LK 9, 35 Therefore, besides the
unspeakable and paradoxical vision of the glory of Christ, something else
also was done that is useful and necessary 82 for confirmation of faith in
him, and not only for the disciples but also for us ourselves. For from a
cloud from above, as from God and Father, a voice was given down of one
saying: This is my Son, the beloved, the chosen.
ZU LK 9, 36 And when the voice came to be, he says, Jesus was found alone.
While Moses and Elijah were standing, he rather commands them to hear him.
And in order that the truth may not be slandered by some who say that the
Father commanded them rather to hear Moses and not Christ the Savior of us
all, the evangelist necessarily marked it, saying that when the voice came
to be Jesus was found alone. Him, therefore, he commanded them to hear;
for he himself is the end of law and prophets. For this reason he also
addressed the crowds of the Jews, saying: If you believed Moses, you would
believe me; for he wrote about me.
ZU LK 9, 37–40 We have heard that one of those from the multitude ran up
to Christ, relating an intolerable sickness of a child; for he said that
he was torn savagely by an evil spirit, and that he endured a most

cried out against the choir of the holy apostles, saying that they had not
been able to rebuke the demon. But it would have been better, honoring
Jesus rather, to ask for help and to seek the grace from him. For he
consents when he is honored and believed by us, that 83 he can do all
things and is weak toward nothing at all; for he himself is the Lord of
powers, and whatever at all is capable of power, this altogether has from
him the being what it is; but when we disbelieve, we insult his glory, and

your disciples, and they were not able to cast it out. And yet it was

From every side, then, it is clear that the one who said concerning the
holy apostles that they were not able to cast it out accused his power
impiously. And in another way too Christ is indignant when holy men are
slandered, who were also entrusted with the word of the sacred
proclamations and have been set as mystagogues of the world under heaven,
being witnessed by the grace from him that they were his disciples and
that they flash forth the light of true knowledge of God to those
everywhere, the paradoxical mighty working usefully following their
teachings. ZU LK 9, 42 But I think it better, according to the first
interpretation, to understand as unbelieving the father of the demoniac.
Wherefore he also ought to have gone away grieving. But in order that no
one may suppose that Christ himself also was too weak 85 for the
accomplishment of the paradox, he rebuked the unclean spirit, and
immediately the young man was delivered from being ill. ZU LK 9, 42φ He
has said well that He gave him back to his father; for he was no longer
the father's, but of the spirit that held him; when he had shaken off the
usurpation, he became again his own father's; and the gift is Christ's,
who has also given to the holy apostles authority to be able to accomplish
the God-signs and to rebuke unclean spirits. And the blessed evangelist
says that the crowds were astonished at the majesty of God.
ZU LK 9, 43φ All things that Jesus did were worthy of wonder among all.
For something exceptional and divine shone forth from all his working, so
that the Scripture that says is true: You will set glory and magnificence
upon him. Even if all indeed wondered at all the things Jesus did, yet he
did not say the things that follow these to all, but to his own disciples,
that Even if the things I do are all such that all wonder at them, yet
consider to what a degree I am about to humble myself, being delivered up
for all, that I may take away the sin of the world. Therefore, in order
that the mystagogues of the inhabited world might know accurately the
mystery concerning him, he usefully and necessarily makes the prediction
and says: Put you into your hearts 86 these words; for the Son of man is
about to be delivered into the hands of men. And what sort of occasion it
is that prepared Christ to say these things, it is necessary, I think, to
say. Therefore he brought up onto the mountain Peter and James and John
and was transfigured before them; his face shone as the sun. He showed
them the glory with which he will shine upon the world in due times; then,
having come down from the mountain, he freed someone from an evil and
harsh spirit. But he had in every way to endure the saving passion for us
and to bear with the perversity of the Jews. Which having happened, it was
not unlikely to think that the disciples would be in turmoil


and perhaps think that among themselves and say: He who is so great in
glory, who raised the dead with God-befitting authority, who rebukes seas
and winds, who crushes Satan with a word, how has he now been caught and
fallen into the snares of murderers? Have we then been deceived in
thinking him to be God? Therefore, in order that they might know what
would in every way and altogether come to be, he commands them to make the
mystery as it were stored in the mind: For put, he says, you into your
hearts; and the You in these words is of one distinguishing them from the
others; for he wanted them to know what would happen, yet not also to
speak it to others. For it was not only necessary that the common people
learn that he would suffer, but it was better that, in addition to this,
being fully assured that he also came to life again in a God-befitting
way, having abolished death, they readily escape the harms from being
scandalized. When therefore, he says, the time of suffering is at hand, do
not say: How did he who is so great in glory and accomplisher of so many

Then someone might say: And how did the disciples not know the mystery of
Christ? For they were from the flock of the Jews, yet not lazy and not
unversed in the writings of Moses; and indeed they had become chosen for
this reason by Christ. Then how did they not know the mystery of Christ,

ZU LK 9, 46φ Let the one who thinks Jesus to be a mere man learn again that
he has been led astray and has gone far from the truth. For let him know
that even if the Word, being God, became flesh, yet he has inalienably the
being what he was and has remained God. For it belongs to the God over all
alone to be able to search hearts and kidneys and to know hidden things,
and to no other besides him at all. But see, Christ examines the reasonings
of the holy apostles and lets down the eye of the Godhead into the hidden
things. God then 88 is the one who is conspicuous in such great and most
God-befitting dignities. Yet let us search out that: did all the blessed
disciples have this disease in common? But I think it has much that is
improbable, to suppose that all at once received one and the same disease.
But one, as I think, having suffered it, in order that the most wise
evangelist may not be found weaving a slander against any of the
fellow-disciples, he declares indefinitely, saying that A reasoning entered
among them as to which of them would be greater. Yet that Satan is
exceedingly terrible toward villainies, it is possible again to see even
through these things; for he plots in many ways against those who have
loved the all-fair life. And if he should be able through the pleasures of
the flesh to storm the mind of someone, he leaps on savagely and sharpens
the goad of love of pleasure. But if someone should be youthful and be
borne beyond such snares, he readily proceeds by soul-sicknesses, preparing
plots against them; for he sows something that is not in accord with God
and raises the passion of love of glory, little by little shaking them down
into accursed arrogance. A passion then of empty glory ran in upon one of
the holy apostles; for to debate at all


as to which of them would be greater belongs to one who is contentious and
desires to stand before the others. Yet Christ, who knows how to save, did
not doze; he saw in the disciple's mind the reasoning upon this very thing,
like a root of bitterness springing up, according to what is written, and
before it was raised on high, before it grew stout, he digs the evil up
from its root; for passions beginning in us are easily conquered, but when
they have grown stout they are hard to cast off and are not moderately
shameless. How then does the physician of spirits cut off the passion of
love of glory? He took a child, he says, and set it beside himself. And he
made the matter an occasion of benefit both to the holy apostles themselves
and to us after them; for this disease for the most part grazes upon those
people who are in some superiority over others. Of what then did the child
that was brought forward make a type and image? Of a life without malice
and without love of glory; for it has an unadulterated mind, an
unadulterated 89 heart, and is in simplicity of reasonings; it does not
love glory, it does not know the manner of superiority over anyone, nor
even does it flee seeming to be at a disadvantage. It has much calm in mind
and heart as from simplicity and harmlessness. And indeed the Savior
himself once said to the holy apostles, or rather to all who love him:
Amen, I say to you, unless you turn and become as these children, you
cannot enter into the kingdom of God. And what turning and being likened to
children is, the blessed Paul makes clear in writing: Do not become
children in minds, but be infants in malice, and in minds become perfect.
And once again, when women were bringing infants to him, then the disciples
preventing them, he said: Let the children come to me; for of such is the
kingdom of the heavens. Those who are such he receives and loves and deigns
as it were to have near, as having chosen to think his things; for he said:
Learn from me, that I am meek and humble in heart.
ZU LK 9, 48 Then he says: Whoever receives this child in my name receives
me; and whoever receives me receives the one who sent me. When therefore
for those who honor holy ones the reward is one and the same, whether he
should happen to be small or in overlying honor and glory (for he receives
Christ and through him and in him the Father), how is it not utterly stale
to contend with one another and to seek to excel others? And he makes still
more clear the aim upon this saying and says: For the one who is lesser
among you all, this one will be great, meaning the moderate 90 minded and
one who thinks nothing high about himself through piety. For it is written
that Everyone who exalts himself will be humbled and he who humbles himself
will be exalted. ZU LK 9, 49φ Did a goad of envy strike the holy disciples?
Do they envy those who are well-pleasing? Do you hinder, tell me, the one
who in the name of Christ crushes evil demons? And yet how ought one not
rather to think that it was not that one himself who was the worker of the
paradoxes, but the grace in him accomplishing the paradox in the power of
Christ, that is? How then do you hinder the one who is conspicuous in
Christ? Yes, he says; for he does not follow with us. What then, if the one
crowned with the grace from Christ has not become numbered among the holy
apostles? Very many are the differences of the gifts from Christ. And this
the blessed Paul will teach, saying that To one is given a word of wisdom,
to another a word of knowledge, to another faith, to another gifts of
healings. What then is the He does not walk with us? The Savior gave to the
holy apostles authority over unclean spirits, so as to cast them out and to
heal every disease and every infirmity among the people; and indeed they
have also worked. They returned then rejoicing and saying: Lord, even the
demons are subject to us in


your name. They thought then that it was permitted to none of the others,
but to them alone, to bear the dignity given. For this reason they come,
asking to learn whether another also is permitted to do this. We have
something of this sort also in the more ancient writings. For God said
somewhere to Moses: Choose for yourself seventy men from the elders of
Israel, and I will take from 91 the spirit that is upon you and I will give
it to them. And the chosen were gathered into that ancient tent except two;
for they remained in the synagogue. And when the spirit of prophecy fell
upon them, not only did those who had run together into the holy tent
prophesy, but also those who remained in the camp. And Joshua, he says, who
stood by Moses, said: Eldad and Modad are prophesying in the camp, lord
Moses; hinder them. And Moses, he says, said to Joshua: Are you jealous for
me? Who would grant that all the Lord's people be prophets, when the Lord
gives his spirit upon them. But it was even then Christ who persuaded the
hierophant Moses to speak these things in the Holy Spirit. And indeed to
the holy apostles he himself through himself says: Do not hinder the one
who crushes Satan in his name; for he is not against you, he says. For he
who is not against you is for you; for they are for you who love Christ,
because they wish to fulfill the things unto his glory, being crowned with
the grace from him.
ZU LK 9, 51 That is, when the time had come at which he had thereafter to
endure the saving passion, to go up into heaven, and to sit with God and
Father, he judged to go up to Jerusalem. For this I think is what Set his
face means. ZU LK 9, 52–55 He sent messengers to prepare lodging for him
and those with him. And they, having come to a village of Samaritans, were
not received. At these things the blessed 92 disciples were indignant, and
not rather for their own sakes, but because they had not honored the Savior
and Lord of all. Then, not being ignorant of the greatness of the authority
present in him, they said: Lord, do you wish that we say fire come down
from heaven and consume them. And they said this, not yet having taken hold
of the new grace, but still holding to the former custom, looking to Elijah
who twice burned up with fire the fifties and those who led them. And
Christ rebukes them for saying this.
ZU LK 9, 52–54 The sense of the things read, then, has this compass. But
let us search each thing finely; for it is written: Press milk and there
will be butter. Did the Savior not know what would be? It is not true to
say otherwise: knowing all things, he knew in every way that those sent
would not be received by the Samaritans. Then how is this in dispute? How
then did he command them to go ahead? It was his custom through every
manner to press on to benefit the holy apostles and sometimes to make
testing them a by-work of the way. As I mean something of this sort: He was
once sailing across the lake of Tiberias together with those named, then he
receives sleep in the manner of the economy, and a storm of no ordinary
kind having been stirred, the boat was in danger; for he allowed in the
manner of the economy the surge to be raised against the boat, in order
both to exercise the faith of the disciples and to make evident the
superiority of his own power. Which also happened; for they, being of
little faith, said: Master, save us, we are perishing, and he, having been
raised, showed at once that he is also master of the elements; for he
rebuked the sea and the surge and there came to be a very great calm. So
then also in this matter; for he knew that those who announced beforehand
that he 93 would lodge with them would not be acceptable to the Samaritans,
but in order that this again might become an occasion of benefit to the
holy disciples, he allowed them to go. And what sort of economy of the
matter was it? He was going up to


Jerusalem; having the time of the passion already neighboring, he was about
to bear with the drunken outrage of the Jews. Therefore, in order that when
they see him having suffered they may not be scandalized, considering that
he wants them also to be forbearing, even if some insult, he made the
insult from the Samaritans, who did not receive the messengers, a kind of
pre-exercise of the matter. The disciples, following the master's tracks,
ought rather to have endured in a holy manner and to have said nothing of
the things from anger against them. But they were not such; held by a
hotter zeal they wanted to bring down fire from heaven upon them, so far as
it depended on them.
ZU LK 9, 55 Therefore the Master of all speaks truly, saying thus: For my
counsels are not as your counsels, nor as my ways your ways. But as heaven
is distant from the earth, so my ways are distant from your ways and your
thoughts from my thoughts. He therefore rebuked his own disciples usefully,
wrapping up the quickness to anger gracefully and persuading them to be
forbearing. And he benefited them also in another way. They were about to
be mystagogues of the world under heaven, to go about cities and countries
proclaiming everywhere the evangelical and saving proclamation; but it was
necessary that those wishing to do this also fall among certain impious men
who would not endure the divine proclamation nor even allow Jesus as it
were to lodge with them. Therefore, in order that they might learn that
those who priestly serve the divine proclamation ought rather to be full of
forbearance and gentleness, not vengeful and angry and leaping savagely
upon those who offend against them, they were taught through this 94
rebuke. And that the priests of the divine proclamations became forbearing,
Paul will teach, saying: For I think God has exhibited us the apostles
last, as appointed to death, because we have become a spectacle to the
world and to angels and to men; being reviled we bless, being blasphemed we
exhort, as we have become the refuse of the world, the offscouring of all
until now.
ZU LK 9, 57φ To love the gifts from above and from God is truly worth
acquiring and would become a bringer of every good. Yet the Master of all,
though he is rich in giving, does not give simply to all and
indiscriminately, but rather to those who would be worthy to receive. If
then someone should wish to be counted worthy of such prizes, let him rid
his own soul of the filths and accusations unto baseness; for thus he will
be acceptable. And if he is not of such a mind, let him withdraw far. And
this the force of the evangelical words set before us will teach us. For
someone came saying to Christ the Savior of us all: Teacher, I will follow
you wherever you go. And he did not receive the man, saying that the foxes
have dens, but he himself has nowhere to lay his head. How then does he
send away the one who had been stirred to this, if following him is a
bringer of every good? For he said that the one who follows him will not
walk in the darkness, but will have the light of life. What then do we say
to these things? This was not that one's aim. And this is to be seen by
those who attend accurately, that first the approach has much that is
uneducated, then that it is full of the last shamelessness; for he did not
seek simply to follow Christ as also many others of the 95 Jewish
multitude, but he rather leapt upon the apostolic dignities and sought to
have this following and was self-called to this, although the blessed Paul
writes that No one takes the honor to himself, but one called by God, as
also Aaron and the divine disciples. And in another way too he justly made
him dismissed; for he ought to have taken up his own cross, that is


to die to the world, or rather to bid farewell to the vain distractions of
the present life; for the friendship of the world is enmity of God. Being
such, he convicts the man obliquely, not rather reproaching, but
correcting, in order that he may become better than himself. For this
reason he says: The foxes have dens, and what follows.
ZU LK 9, 58 And the simpler and ready sense of the things said is something
of this sort, that Even the beasts and the birds have hiding-places and
lodgings, but I cannot yet escape the troubles from many; for I have
nowhere to lodge and rest and lay my head. Yet the declaration hidden
within comes through deeper thoughts. For he seems to call foxes and birds
of heaven the evil and knavish and opposing powers, or the herds of the
demons; for they have been so called in many places by the God-inspired
Scripture. For the blessed psalmist said concerning some: They will be
portions of foxes. And in the Song of Songs it is written: Catch for us

law of Moses, having first said: You shall love the Lord your God, set
second the reverence toward parents, saying: Honor your father and your
mother. Come, let us search out among ourselves what sort of occasion it is
through which we have made eager the honor and love toward parents. That we

at all, brought us into being, and father and mother have become ministers
of the passage into being for those from them. Will not then the first
benefactor be loved rather than the second ones after him? Therefore piety
toward those who begot us must yield to love toward God, and human things
must come behind the divine. And this the Savior himself also taught us,
saying: He who loves father or mother above me is not worthy of me.
Therefore he permits loving those who begot us, yet not above himself. For
this reason also, having said to the disciple that he must rather follow,
and having permitted others to bury the dead, he taught that the things
according to us would reasonably be thrown behind the love toward God,
because the things of God are also greater than reverence toward parents;
for he himself is generator and maker of all. We must therefore, if we are
going to follow Christ, make nothing more honorable than this, but whatever
concurs with this, pursue and do, and whatever opposes, decline and flee,

the greater and fairer. One can see, then, even the holy apostles leaving a
still greater need than this 98, the care concerning the poor, because of
the ministry of the word, yet not so leaving it as not also to make
provision of this. But the lesser was also managed, the ministry concerning
the tables, and the greater was not hindered, the announcement of the
proclamation.
