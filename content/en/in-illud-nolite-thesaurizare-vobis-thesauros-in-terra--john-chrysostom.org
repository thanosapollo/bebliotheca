* On the Saying, Do Not Lay Up for Yourselves Treasures on Earth
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

(Cod. 27 of the collection of St. Sabas, fol. 27a-29b.) "Of the same, on
'Do not lay up for yourselves treasures on earth,' and what follows"
The Master who loves mankind, the only-begotten Son of God the Word, who
is like in all things to the Father and to the all-holy Spirit, from those
things which he himself grants through love of mankind, from these he also
calls us to love of mankind; therefore he said, "Be merciful, even as your
heavenly Father is merciful"; "who makes his sun rise on the evil and on
the good, and sends rain on the just and on the unjust."
When he says "the =630= Father," it is clear that he proclaims the nature
of the Holy Trinity. For God is the Father, God is the Son; for neither is
the Son alien from the nature of the Father; it is clear that the Holy
Spirit also is God.
For how did Peter say to Ananias, "Why has Satan filled your heart to lie
to the Holy Spirit? ... you have not lied to men but to God"? Therefore
let us worship the Godhead of the Trinity, that we may not fall away from
the Godhead. Where then does he lead us by the hand, saying, "Do not lay
up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moth and rust consume, and
where thieves dig through and steal; but lay up for yourselves treasures
in heaven, where neither moth nor rust consumes, nor thieves dig through
and steal"? Since even pitiably he exhorts us, calling us to love of
mankind and saying: You asked and I gave; but when you too are asked by
the poor, grant. You sought little and I gave you much. And you, when you
are asked for much, grant even if it is little. The earth nearly forbade
the hopes, it forbade the produce, the heavenly milk. Fear and trembling
shook the hearts of men. I waited for the giving, that you might seek the
giving; I postponed the grace, that you might come forward with the grace.
I threatened for a little, that you might learn him who sends the rain and
worship him who feeds, that you might recognize him who grants you life.
I, as I was recognized, was entreated. You have given a richer gift. For
he is the one who does "exceedingly above all that we ask or think." All
the earth was filled to know the Lord through you who make supplication,
as abundant water covering seas, or rather to cover the produce. And the
deeds cry out before the words. Therefore you who have received great
things from me, grant even small things to the poor.
I grant the great things; you give the small from the great which you
received from me; give my things to me; I count it as grace, and I too
will make them with much addition. For this is why I called you, he says,
also to give to others, that you might hold them more securely; for as you
hold them you do not even so hold them, but when you give them to others
for my sake, then I will make them with every addition. Thus the
friendship of God surpasses all things. Let us then give to the =631= God
who has given. For he who gave to us, having received nothing from us,
much more after receiving will give more greatly. I poured out the blood
for you; you at least give silver for me, not after the manner of Judas
unto betrayal, but to the poor through love of mankind.
I, being God, became man for you; you grant the bread for yourself to the
poor, not only that you may feed a poor man; but that you may also feed me
through the poor man and fill him who said, "I hungered and you gave me to
eat" and "inasmuch as you did it to one of these my brethren, the least,
you did it to me." Thus says God the Master of all: "for the poor man has
been left to you." Just as if someone should give silver to a child, he
orders him to hold it with all security, and having received it he makes
it secure with all diligence, so God wills: give, he says, to the one in
need, hand it over into the more secure bosom of the poor man, that no one
may snatch it, give it into the inviolable treasury, that no one may steal
it away, scatter it into the more flourishing earth, that you may receive
a hundredfold. And where is the interest, tell, he says, O Master? The
interest, he says, you shall inherit eternal life. But again one of the
foolish says: Teacher, who will be able to plunder my things from my
treasures? The bolts are shut with all security.
Many and faithful guards stand by, those entrusted with my deposits. I
have secured wall and house from every side; iron bolts and much guarding.
O the madness! The wretched gold lies in a prison, having sinned nothing,
and is not even deemed worthy of light, the one more precious than all
matter, as we suppose. Yes, the honor is in dishonor, with trustworthy
guarding. And who can snatch my things from mine, he says? But you escaped
your own notice, O man, guarding unguardedly the gold, which never has
faithfulness. For it is set forth, quickly passing for the many from this
one to that one, it is set forth for the many ...... and for rulers and
sycophants and the plots of those in power and thieves and robbers and
housebreakers. I am eager to leave you and pass into others' hands; for
gold leaps much like a locust from place to place, from this one to that
one.
But when through =632= all these things it cannot leave you, then at last,
when you are dying, it brings you nothing, nor even goes with you as far
as the tombs, but is eager after a long time to see the light and quickly
to leap into the hands of others. Therefore the prophet also cries: "Do
not fear when a man grows rich, or when the glory of his house is
multiplied, for when he dies he shall not take all things." But the
senseless one also says: My children have become rich; what is that to
you, wretched one, who go away naked and poor? What is that to you who are
required to give account there for the things left behind by you here?
For you will be required of double accounts there, for the things you
stored up badly and for the occasions you gave your heirs to spend your
labors on prodigality and sorceries and fornication and unlawful
contracts. Therefore Scripture also says, "The sins of some men are
evident, going before unto judgment"; but for some they also follow after,
through the unlawful expenditures that take place by your heirs. Therefore
Christ also said: "Do not lay up for yourselves treasures on earth; but
lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven." Do you wish to learn its
weakness and its strength? Set the treasures before yourself in the midst,
both lying there and feverish, and you will be in a bad way, constrained
from every side and dragged toward that inexorable departure. And set the
gold into the midst and say: O gold, sharp for men unto sin; often I swore
falsely because of you; often I blasphemed because of you; often I passed
through dangers of the sea because of you; I insulted those who begot me
because of you; I hated my friends because of you; I turned away from
those of my family because of you; I became liable to countless evils;
because of you I have sins; you at least help me once in return for my
many evils; at least benefit me once, lighten my fever, ease my disease,
ransom my death; I bought a household slave, a man, for fifty gold pieces.
You go with ten thousand gold pieces, or even as many more, and buy me
relief. I do not say to you, Ransom my death, for you cannot. But at least
buy me a term of a few days of life. And that one answers you. Why, O man,
are you deceived? Did you not learn my strength, that only here for a
little time I have disturbance? Did you not often see the man rich today,
poor tomorrow? Did you not often see the judge today, a prisoner tomorrow?
Did you not often see =633= the master higher than all today, tomorrow a
household slave least of all? Did you not often see that we enjoyed the
same bonds, both I and the one who possessed me? For a little time I have
the disturbance and the tumult here. For did you ever see me go abroad?
Have I not been reckoned as this clay to the master, unable to help anyone
from the evils, except when I am scattered toward the hands of the poor?
But do you wish that I help you, O man? Loose me from the bonds here and I
will loose you from the bonds there; release me from the prison, hand me
over to the poor, scatter me to the beggars; "He scattered, he gave to the
poor; his righteousness remains forever." Permit me to feed the orphans,
if you wholly wish that I follow you.
Permit me to cover those who go naked and I cover you on that day. "For
blessed is he who understands concerning the poor and needy; the Lord will
deliver him in an evil day." Do you wish that I help you? Let me help the
poor; for by alms and faiths sins are cleansed. I have a double power and
strength. If I am guarded here, I become a judge there and I condemn those
who guard me. "For the judgment is without mercy to him who has not done
mercy." If I am scattered here, I become almsgiving and I have mercy on
those who have mercy on me. "For blessed are the merciful, for they shall
receive mercy." Therefore Christ said: "Make for yourselves friends from
the mammon of unrighteousness, that they may receive you into their
eternal dwellings." Therefore permit me to change nature and I become
instead of gold almsgiving, and I enter in to the King of those who reign,
and I become an advocate for you with much boldness and I advocate for you
as I advocated for Cornelius. "For your prayers and your alms have gone up
before God."
Let us guard ourselves, brethren; let us become merciful even as our
heavenly Father is merciful; because to him belongs honor and might
together with the Holy Spirit, now and always and unto the ages of ages.
Amen.
