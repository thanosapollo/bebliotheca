OF OUR FATHER AMONG THE SAINTS GREGORY THE THEOLOGIAN ARCHBISHOP OF
CONSTANTINOPLE, VERSES. BOOK I. THEOLOGICAL VERSES. SECTION I. DOGMATIC
VERSES. Αʹ. On the Father. I know that we are making a long voyage on rafts,
or with tiny wings toward the starry heaven 398 we hasten, we whose mind has
risen to reveal the Godhead, which not even heavenly ones have strength to
worship as is fitting, or the bounds of the great Godhead, and the tiller of
all. 399 Yet (for God often was not more pleased with a gift 399 from a more
abundant hand than from little love), therefore I will boldly break into
speech. But flee far off, whoever is a sinner; my word comes either to the
pure or to those being purified; but let the profane, as beasts, when Christ
was shining on the sheer mountain and Moses was writing the law on tablets, at
once be crushed under the splitting crags. Those then such things; and as the
Word thrust the worst from our choir, who have a god-fighting heart. But I
will set this voice as a preamble in the pages, which godly men of old put
forth, bringing terror to a stubborn people as two witnesses of the words, 400
Moses and Isaiah (and I will speak to those who know): the one giving the
newly framed law, the other when it was loosed: Heaven, hear; earth, receive
my words. Spirit of God, do you raise up for me mind and tongue, the
loud-roaring trumpet of truth, so that all may rejoice in heart, mingled with
the whole Godhead. There is one God without beginning, without cause, not
circumscribed by anyone existing before or to come after, having the age
around him, and boundless, the great Father of the good great Only-begotten
Son, having suffered nothing in the Son of as many things as belong to flesh,
since he is mind. One God another, not another in Godhead, the Word of God,
this the moving paternal seal of that one, Son of the unoriginate 401 alone,
and most only of the only, equal in rank, so that the one may remain whole as
begetter, but the Son world-setter and dispenser, the strength and thought of
the Father. One Spirit from the good God, God. Begone all whom the Spirit has
not stamped to reveal his Godhead, but who either have depth badly, or an
unclean tongue, half-lit, envious, self-taught knowers, a hidden spring, a
lamp in dark bosoms. Βʹ. On the Son. But first we will sing the Son, honoring
the blood that is the cleanser of our passions. For there is need 402 to
succor even a mortal with heavenly things because of a tongue fighting against
the Godhead, evil-minded, self-slaying. There was nothing before the great
Father. For he who holds all things within, and nothing is above the Father;
the Word of God sprung from the great Father; timeless Son, image of the
archetype, nature equal to the begetter. For the glory of the Father is the
great child, and from him he shone forth, as the Father alone knows and he who
shone forth from the Father. For nothing was near the Godhead; but this
nonetheless is clear to all mortals alike, as it is to me, that it is not
lawful to bring anything of my own birth to the Godhead, not a flux, nor a
shameful cutting. For if I 403 am not a passionless begetter; for I am also
bound; he is in no way passible, whoever is wholly uncompounded, bodiless. For
of those whose natures are far apart, what wonder even if the generations are
other? If time is before me, time is not before the Word, whose begetter is
timeless. When the Father was without beginning, or rather left nothing of the
Godhead above, then also the Son of the Father, having the Father as a
timeless beginning, as the light of the sun the great most beautiful circle;
even if all forms are below the great God,

so that we may not, setting anything between Father and Son who always are,
tear away the Lord Son from the Lord Father. For what is before God, whether
time or will, is for me a cutting of the Godhead. 404 As God, as begetter, a
great begetter. And if it is greatest for the Father to have no originating
cause of the excellent Godhead, it is no less for the holy offspring of the
great Father to have such a root. Therefore do not shut God off from God. For
you did not know the Child far from the Father. And the unbegotten voice, and
the generation from the Father, were not two forms of Godhead; who fashioned
them, and both around that one being outside, but the nature is uncut for me;
if then the Word has generation, neither will the Father, being fleshless,
receive anything of flesh (never is the mind of men so reckless as to think
so), and you have God the Son, a boast worthy of the begetter. But if, bearing
a vain favor to the Godhead of the great Father, and scratching empty fear in
your breast, 405 you should deny the one, and cast Christ among creatures, you
have insulted the Godhead of both, O empty-minded one, the one you robbed of
the Child, and the other is not God, if he was made. For everything that never
was, this belongs to them; even if something of God should remain by the great
words and be firm. And what word is this, that you, best one, starting from
here, should afterward become God by the passions of Christ, but that he
should be brought under bonds, and that you should call your fellow-slave one
honored with the prizes of slavery instead of God? If the great God afterward
made him a good instrument (as a smith working a hammer for a wagon), so that
God might acquire me by the hand of the First-born; thus also the creation of
heavenly Christ would be 406 far better, if the Word is for the sake of that,
not that for the sake of Christ. Who would say these things? But if because he
received a body, succoring your passions, therefore you will also set measures
on the greatly glorious Godhead: did he who pitied you sin? But to me he is
more admirable; for he scraped off nothing of the Godhead, and he saved me, a
physician stooping over foul passions. He was mortal, but God. Of David's
race, but Adam's Fashioner. Flesh-bearing, yet also outside the body. Of a
mother, but a virgin; circumscribed, but unmeasured. And a manger received
him, but a star led the Magi, and the gift-bearers went and bent the knee. As
a mortal he came to the contest, but as unconquered he overcame 407 the
tempter in three wrestlings; he submitted to food, but he fed thousands, and
changed water into wine. He was washed, but he cleansed sins, but he was
proclaimed by the Spirit under a thunderous voice Son of the Unoriginate; as a
mortal he received sleep, and as God he put the sea to bed. The knee grew
weary, but for the palsied he fixed strength and knees. He prayed; but who
heard the prayers of the feeble? He was a sacrifice, but high priest; a
sacrificer, but God. He offered blood to God, and cleansed the whole world.
And a cross lifted him, but sin was fixed with nails. But why should I tell
each thing? He was mixed with the dead, and he rose from the dead, and he
raised the dead before. Those things are of mortal poverty, this of the wealth
of the fleshless; 408 do not you dishonor the Godhead with the mortal things,
but make the earthly form greatly glorious by that, which, being well-disposed
to you, the immortal Son shaped. Γʹ. On the Holy Spirit. Soul, why do you
delay? Sing also the boast of the Spirit, and do not cut with words what
nature has not set outside. Let us tremble at the great Spirit, of like
Godhead, by whom I knew God, who is God opposite, and who makes God here;
all-powerful, of shifting gifts, hymn of the holy choir, life-bearing of
heavenly and earthly things, high-throned, coming from the Father, divine
might, self- commanded, neither Child (for one only is the good Child of one
excellent), 409 nor outside the unseen Godhead, but of like glory. Whoever in
the pages of the God-inspired law longs to grasp the Godhead of the heavenly
Spirit will see many dense paths coming together into one, if he is willing,
and if he has drawn anything of the holy Spirit into his heart, and if the
mind has looked sharply. But if he longs for a naked voice of the lovely
Godhead, let him know that he longs for a word not prudent. For it was not
fitting, while that of Christ had not yet been shown to more of mortals, to
bring upon the weakest hearts an unbelievable burden. Nor

for beginners is the season of a more perfect word. And who with still dim
eyes either showed the whole rays of fire, or satisfied them with more
insatiable light? It is better if you lead the fire-warmed rays little by
little, 410 lest somehow you even harm the springs of sweet light. For as the
word, showing beforehand the whole Godhead of the Lord Father, shone the great
glory of Christ, appearing to few prudent mortals, so also afterward, showing
the brighter of the Child, it flashed under the Godhead of the gleaming
Spirit. A little it shone under for these, but the more was left for us, among
whom it was afterward divided even in tongues of fire, bearing a sign of
Godhead, when the Savior leapt from earth. For I know fire as God to the evil,
as light to the good. Thus I gathered Godhead for you. And if you are amazed
hearing Son and not-Son of one Godhead, and you trust in well-turned opposing
words, God himself coming upon me will give me a word here too. 411 From one
first-born came a wife and Seth, a half-cut, and offspring of a pair by the
laws of marriage; unbegotten and begotten, yet they were mortals alike.
Remembering these, do not dishonor anything of the Godhead, bringing this
below before. There is one nature, unmeasured, uncreated, timeless, good,
free, and equally worshipped, one God in threefold gleamings rolling the
world. By these I am raised another new one, when by the bath, death being
buried, I come back to light. For a threefold Godhead raised me light-bearing.
I will not lie to you, dear purification. If by Godhead 412 having been washed
I should cut apart the gleaming Godhead, it were better ... but I tremble at
the end of the evil word, in hope of the divine gift and of the baths. If it
has wholly cleansed me, he is also wholly holy God for me. And the mortal who
is a sinner would have the equal, himself cutting apart his own Godhead, the
prize of God. And if we hear anything either concerning the Child or the good
Spirit in the divine words and in God-bearing men, that they have the second
things of God the Father, I bid you to think thus by the words of deep-bosomed
Wisdom, that it goes up to an unoriginate root, it does not cut Godhead, so
that you may have one power, not many-worshipped. 413 From a monad is a Triad,
and from a Triad a monad again, neither a ford, a spring, a great river, and
one stream, driven in threefold types upon the earth; nor again a torch of a
pyre coming into one, nor a word going forth from mind and remaining within,
nor some gleam from waters by solar motions, trembling around walls, unstable,
fleeing before it draws near, drawing near before it flees. For neither is the
nature of God unstable, or flowing, or coming together again; but what is of
God is firm. But thinking thus you would offer a pure sacrifice within. In
threefold lights one nature is established. 414 Neither is the monad
numberless, since it stands in three excellent ones, nor is the Triad
many-worshipped, since the nature is uncut. The monad is in Godhead, and the
things of which the Godhead is are three in number. Each is one God, whenever
you declare it alone. Again one God without beginning, from whom the wealth of
Godhead, when the word has some memory of three, so that on the one hand there
may be of the threefold lights a holy proclamation to mortals, and by the
other we may glorify the far-shining monarchy, and not take pleasure in an
assembly of gods that is many-ruled. For equal to me is the many-ruled and the
wholly unruled in fight. And strife is division; and this hastens toward
dissolution. Therefore far from Godhead is the many-ruled for me. And let them
call three gods, as many as time, or thought, 415 or power, or will has split
from one another, each the same of itself, never being uncontested. But of my
Triad there is one strength, one thought, one glory, one power. Therefore also
the monad is unflowing, having great honor by one harmony of Godhead. So much
has the Triad unveiled a gleam to my lights, from wings and a divine veil
within the temple, under which the queenly nature of God is hidden. And if
there is something more for angelic choirs, let the Triad know this more. Δʹ.
On the world. Come then, let us also hymn the creation of the great God,
fighting against lying opinions. 416 One God; but matter and forms are a myth

feeble, thought by the prudent of the Greeks as co-unoriginate. Of these as
many shapes as they fashion as holy gods were not, but came to be, the great
God willing. And who ever saw matter without form, or who an immaterial shape,
even having toiled much in the whirlings of mind? For neither did I find a
colorless body, or some bodiless color. Who separated things which nature,
bringing into one, did not? But nonetheless let us cut; and consider for me,
if they were wholly unmixed, how they have come into one, or what world stands
with all things split through and through? But if mixed, how were they mixed?
who mixed them without God? And if there is a mixer, God, receive this one
also as creator of all. 417 Form also a potter threw upon clay, turning a
wheel, a goldsmith on gold, a stone-carver on his stones. Give something more
of our mind to God, O lover of the unoriginate. Matter is the more, with forms
being moved. He thought, and they came to be in forms; divine thought, the
much-woven mother of all. For it is not fitting to raise form from form as
some painter does, looking beforehand at what mind alone has not drawn. And
you, evil darkness of Manichaeus, were not before, a rival throne of the
highest light. If it was God, it was not darkness. For it is not fitting for
evil to rival God. If darkness, you do not know God. To be of one mind is
unworlded; but of those fighting, the strength of the better. And if equal in
the struggle, 418 who is a third bringing into one by wisdom and loosing
strifes? And this is a very great wonder, since you raise a dread battle,
setting them of one mind again, you have forgotten the fight. I am soul and
body. The one a fragment of Godhead of boundless light; the other was
fashioned for you from a root of darkness. And things far apart you have
brought into one; if I am a common nature, I loose the battle. If the battle
is dread and firm, I am no longer a nature woven from both. For there is no
common offspring of those fighting, but of those loving. Such is the gloom of
your heart. But for me there is one God without beginning, uncontested, one
good light, the strength of simple and woven minds that are high-godded, 419
of heavenly and earthly; but the darkness came last, not a nature standing
circumscribed, but this is our wickedness. And wickedness is a loosing of
command, as night is a setting of the sun, feeble old age of youth. And winter
a dread upward course of the sun has brought. The first among heavenly lights,
having lost light and glory by his arrogances, always hates the race of
mortals. And from him the first mortal also tasted of man-slaying wickedness,
and of death, when he fanned the flame for me with his deceits. This is the
nature of late-born evil, of which that one is father. A ruin is the rust of
strong iron; 420 but a self-slaying ruin, wickedness, I planted, following the
wrestlings of the envious one through which device. World, if you were then a
Triad, near the unoriginate in glory. And how have Christ-bearing men, skilled
in divine things, set you so far off, that not a very great number of circling
years is measured, from when the great Word of God fixed you? But if you were
fixed afterward, let us consider what the divine thought was doing (for God is
not idle for me, nor unfinished), before this all stood and was adorned with
forms. Ruling in empty ages as highest, he was moving, gazing on the dear
gleam of his own beauty, 421 the equal common gleam of the triple-lighted
Godhead, as it is conspicuous to the Godhead alone and to those whose God he
is. He was moving also the types of the world which he set, seeing them in his
great thoughts, the world-begetting mind of what would be afterward, but for
God even of what is present. All things are before God, as many as will be, as
many as have come to be, and as many as now are present. For me time has split
thus, some before, some behind; but for God all things into one, and they are
held in the arms of the great Godhead. Therefore as much as my mind has cut,
hear. Mind was in labor with all things, and the labor broke afterward, in
season, when the great Word of God unveiled. 422 He wished a noetic nature to
stand, both heavenly and earthly, gleaming mirrors of the first light, the one
shining above, a minister of the Lord, full-lighted, great, and the one here
having glory, gushing Godhead, so that he may rule more heavenly ones, and
there may be of more a bliss-giving light. For this is the nature of my Lord,
bliss

to grant. So that creation drawing near to God may not, longing for
god-opposing glory, lose light and glory (for to bear measures is best, and
measurelessness worst), therefore the steep Word, thinking kindly for those to
come, cast far off as much light of the Triad as is around-throned, and the
mortal nature of angelic choirs, the angelic 423 not very far off as a
stander-by, but ours even very far off, since we were born of earth mixed with
Godhead; and a simple nature is better. Of worlds the one that was earlier is
another heaven, a space of the God-bearing, visible to minds alone,
all-shining, into which afterward a mortal of God journeys from here, when he
becomes God, having cleansed mind and flesh; but the other was fixed mortal
for mortals, when the grace of lights was about to stand and a herald of God,
in beauty and size, and a kingship of the image; both first and last by the
words of the great God. 424 Εʹ. On Providence. Thus the great Mind fixed a
boundless world of broad foundation, bearing all things within, and himself
being above all; and what device is there to grasp the uncontainable? But when
he made it, he leads it under a first impulse, as a top under a blow, driven
in whirlings, moved by great unmoved words. For neither is the nature of so
great and such a world automatic, to which there is nothing like to think; do
not entrust so much to automatic words. And who ever saw a house which hands
did not complete? 425 Who a ship, or a swift chariot? who a shield and a
helmet? Nor would it have remained so long a time, if it were without
beginning, and a choir without a leader would cease for me. Nor is it fitting
for anything else to have the commander of all, than him who made it. This one
granting the stars as leaders of our race and of all life together, tell me,
for the stars themselves what other heaven will you roll? And for that again
another, always leaders upon those leading? One king is fellow-starred with
more, of whom one is good, and one evil, one a speaker, and one a merchant,
and one a wanderer, and a steep throne bears another as over-minded. And in
sea and war there is a common fate for more of other birth. Stars which they
bound together, a common end did not bind. 426 And those whom they split, a
common end bound. If then each has some first necessity, this is a myth; but
if some common better one rules it, the constellations are contrary to
constellations. And who then mixed them? For he who bound also loosed if he
should wish. And if God, how first, which God has shaken out for me? Unless
you roll God himself too under your stars. And if there is no one ruling, how
will it stand? For I have not seen. Let those have God outside with such
words. For either God is, or the stars are leaders. But I know this, that God
steers all these things, the Word of God dispensing here and there as many
things as he has set above, and as many as below, by thoughts; to some he gave
427 harmony and a sufficient course always firm, and to others a turning life,
and bearing many forms; of which he has shown the one to us, and the other he
guards in the recesses of his wisdom, and he wishes to refute the empty boast
of a mortal. And some things he has set here, and some on the last days he
will meet. And a land-worker shears all things in season, as also Christ is
the best judge of my life. This is my word, starless, self-commanded. But you
tell me hour-setters and subtle Fates, and life-bearing circles and measures
of a journey; and loose for me the laws of life, and terror for sinners, and
hope meeting the good afterward. 428 For if a circle bears all things, by that
one's revolution I am whirled, and even the willing itself the circle will
bring on, and there is for me no turn leading toward the better, of will and
of mind, but a pole rolls me this way. Let the great glory of Christ be
silent, the angel star from the East leading the Magi to the city, where
Christ shone, offspring of a mortal, timeless Son. For he was not one of as
many as astrologers are tellers of, but a stranger and not shown before, seen
in Hebrew books, from which the children of the Chaldeans, having learned
beforehand, as many as have a life of watching stars, gazing at one only from
more

whom they were watching, 429 a newly appearing one running through the air
from the East to the land of the Hebrews, they inferred a lord; then when the
devices of the art fell together for the astrologers, of astrologers
worshipping the lord with the heavenly ones. But let them go their own road,
which Christ the Lord assigned, fiery, always-running, unshaken, both fixed
and wandering and backward-going, as they say, whether there is some
unnourished nature of fire, or some body, a fifth which they call, having a
circling path; but we go up our own road. For we hasten to a rational and
heavenly nature, and we are bound of earth. 430 ʹ. On the same. Begone those
who have denied the Divine, and do not even refer the unspeakable harmony of
the all to some cause both making and holding together the whole. Begone those
who know some swarm of gods, or a power of daemons both good and evil. Begone
those who have denied Providence, as if fearing to be saved from God; but
either assigning the all to an unstable bearing, or even giving it to motions
of stars, which are moved how, and from where, and from what; 431 if of God,
how first, which God turns? For it belongs to the weaver also to reweave. And
if not of God, how will it stand from anarchy, or even a fight of the
stronger? And how is it not a fight? For the taking away of providence is a
taking up against God; unless you make even God fated, bearing unwilling the
tyranny of the weaving. Thus these are caught by their own words. But for us
one God leads this all, wisely rolling and weaving, as he may wish, by his own
nods and words, even if he does not seem to turn all things dexterously. Many
indeed even of the wisest are distressed, by the unequals here, both of old
and still now. Why must I speak of all? But consider for me 432 what David
suffers; seeing the evil doing well (as they seem), he is greatly dizzy, lest
someone give the nature of things to a bearing, as if no one is overseeing us.
But the same one knows the solution; for consider for me the end of all, he
says, as reversed. You have sown; you will see also the ear of the sowing. And
if you do not know the words, not also the Word; for neither, if you are of
the drawing, also a painter; nor if you are of lines, also a geometer, you the
same. The one thing you have grasped, follow the other wisely. This too is a
word, to be persuaded by a word. If all things are clear, where is believing,
tell. For faith is an unmeddlesome assent. And I perhaps also think this
wisely; 433 I did not promise you these things when you entered, 433 to be
rich, to be without care, to be strong, or to have good children; but a good
life and the things expected; so as instead of the now to expect the greater.
How then is it just, as in a bargain, both to seek the thing sold and to have
the money? Unless this is a prize for you alone of all. How very insatiable,
and of a strange contribution! Either throw those away, dearest, or give these
also; if indeed your word must stand for you, and you do not go outside of
what has been well agreed. How then, you would say, did the ancients also have
these things? 434 Not yet then was the faith rooted, and faith was the things
here of the things thought, as with milk for infants being made men. And a
proof of the word. For then also the greatest of evils were somehow
pardonable; and the not greatest of evils were to be honored; the worth in
both being counterbalanced. And the vengeances of the wise were beyond
measure. Let David persuade you of these things, and Moses, and Elisha, of
whom I tremble remembering; the one punished insufficiently for his faults,
and the one instead of small things, even having received a vision of God; 435
and having saved a law, and so great an army; and the one from a brief
insolence even to infants giving back so great a blow beyond hopes. Thus
shadowy then was even the law. Thus a purification of sacrifices through
blood, and weak sprinklings of unclean fleshes; thus best was the flight of
the worse, even if something more was not added, piety being circumscribed by
small bounds for those then. Shall I say something greater than what I have
said? It will be spoken. They did not yet even have so great a contribution as
we have, by the sufferings of God, 436 and the lamb slain for my evils. So
that theirs was the more; and if it was more, give the more to be of the toils
and

the debt, and for us less, who have obtained more. And this instead of all and
before all must be said: do not greatly praise the well-running of life, you
who know to think a greater of the things seen; lest somehow the hard-to-walk
be a height for me, and for you the easy of the course be cliffed on both
sides. My grievous is better than your fair. Did you ever see oxen, the one
great in a manger, lifted, shining with good flesh, 437 tossing the neck, and
the other bent down, wrinkled, ugly with tokens of farming; then the one kept
for slaughter, and the other feeding both himself and the master. Of these who
is more fortunate, do you think? Not the narrow one, and worn by a yoke? Quite
clear. But the breadth is dear to you, even if it is evil and compounded with
evil. Or would you even praise the bulk of one dropsical? Or does the
mind-struck even seem to you to have strength? And is the sweet wholly a
saving drug? The bitter has often saved many; and the sweet as things mostly
lead toward the worse. And is a father's rod not venerable to a son who has
mind? And to whom goodness is an evil 438 he is neither a son, nor is he
struck with a rod. And what if you wrestle, as Job, for a crown? Sing these
things to yourself, and you will be easier, making a consolation of the
toiling, and by the thankful acquiring the hope. For it belongs to a bad slave
to worship the master when he smiles, but when he strikes to think him evil.
But one must receive every turn of the tiller, by which he leads me through
from wrestling and storm, until he brings me to rest at fair harbors; if
indeed he did not fix the ship in vain, but toward a fair and dexterous end.
Ζʹ. On noetic essences. As in rainy air when it is fair, 439 a ray of the sun,
meeting clouds with beaten revolutions, rolls a many-colored iris, and around
it everywhere the ether nearby is made to gleam, with circles dense and
dissolving outside; such also is the nature of lights, of the highest light
always gleaming off lesser minds with rays. The one is a spring of lights, a
light neither nameable nor graspable, and fleeing the speed of mind going
near, always running out ahead of the minds of all, so that by longings we may
be stretched toward a height always new. And the second lights from a Triad
that has a kingly boast, gleaming angels, unseen, who have gone around a great
throne, since they are light minds, 440 fire and divine spirits running
quickly through air, eagerly ministering to great commands, both simple and
noetic and gleaming, neither coming from fleshes (for fleshes, when they have
been fixed, are destroyed again), nor going upon fleshes, remaining what they
became. I wished to say wholly unwearied; but hold the horse even being very
hot, shutting him with the bits of mind. And some are standers-by of the great
God; and others rule the whole world with their aids, each having another
oversight from the Lord, seeing men and cities and all nations, and privy to
rational sacrifices for day-beings. Soul, what will you even do? the word
trembles, having stepped into heavenly 441 beauties; and a mist has met me,
and I cannot either set the word farther or withdraw. And as when some
traveler crossing a rough river has suddenly sprung back, and is held even
though eager, and his heart surges much around the stream; need has fixed
courage, and fear has bound the rush; often he lifted a sole upon the water,
and often again he withdrew, and of those fighting, necessity conquered fear.
So also for me going near the unseen Godhead, there is terror to set the
standers-by of the pure high-ruling one under a fault, a form sated with
light, lest somehow I also strew a road of wickedness for more. And terror to
grave an unturned good in my words, 442 until I also see some crooked one
