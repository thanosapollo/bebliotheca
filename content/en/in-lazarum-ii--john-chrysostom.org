On Lazarus of four days. ii.
=62.775=
Beloved, just as a mother who loves her children, having offered the
breast to the infant, is delighted, while the child draws the tender
nourishment of the milk; but when the clot of the milk, having curdled,
blocks the channels of the breast, then indeed the child whimpers, and
the mother is in pain, having the will to nourish spread open, yet
seeing the nourishment not being given; so likewise we also, having
yesterday set the breast of the understanding before you, were
delighted, while you drew the milk of the word; but when the cloud of
forgetfulness stole beneath the mind, it cut off the discourse, then
indeed you were indignant as being deprived of the thoughts of the
evangelical teachings, and we were distressed, having the eagerness to
speak stretched forth, yet seeing the mind hindered by forgetfulness.
But perhaps this has happened to us, that we may know that it is not of
him who wills, nor of him who runs, nor of him who pursues, but of God
who has mercy.
Come then, therefore, since God gives grace to those who speak, not
because of the worth of the speakers, but because of the need of the
hearers, having called upon him, let us draw according to our power the
drinkable stream of teaching from the evangelical readings just now read
out to you. "There was a certain man sick," it says, "Lazarus of
Bethany, from the village of Mary and Martha her sister. Now Mary was
she who anointed the Lord with ointment, whose brother Lazarus was sick.
And Jesus said: This sickness is not unto death, but for the glory of
God, that God may be glorified through it." Answering I say, this
sickness is not unto death, but for the glory of God; it is not a
sickness, but good tidings; it is not a sickness, but a safeguard. O
sickness, physician of those who are ailing, guide of faith, adversary
of death, opponent of the devil, author of salvation! O sickness,
unbreakable foundation of virtue, teacher of faith, steward of piety! O
=62.776= sickness driving away the fevers of souls, and sprinkling the
sweats of baptism, and weaving bright robes of the soul! O sickness,
giver of heavenly bread, excellent torch-bearer of faith, snare of the
devil and seal of man! "But when Jesus heard," it says, "then he
remained in the place where he was two days."
What then was the necessity for the Lord, having heard, to remain, and
not go at once to the one who was sick? But he was altogether awaiting
the death of Lazarus, so that in the death he might display to those who
saw a faith greater than the wonders. After this therefore Jesus says to
the disciples: "Lazarus our friend has fallen asleep; but I go that I
may awaken him." For truly the death of men is reckoned as sleep with
the Lord. "I go, that I may awaken him." What then? Being here, are you
unable to give life to the dead man? But the Jews do not receive the
grace thus while I am absent; for perhaps, when he has risen, they will
suppose that Lazarus came back to life by some chance. I myself
therefore come, so that, becoming eyewitnesses in my presence of the
wonders done by me, and receiving the grace from me, they may be brought
into a firm faith. "Lazarus our friend has fallen asleep; but I go to
awaken him." And the disciples, having heard, said: "Lord, if he has
fallen asleep, he will recover." They answered not so much piously as
medically. For sleep, when prolonged, harms those in lethargy, but
rather looses fevers by sweats. Since therefore the disciples supposed
that the Lord was speaking of the sleep of slumber, he said with
frankness: "Lazarus our friend has died; and I rejoice for your sake,
that you may believe, because I was not there; but let us go to him." He
who does not will the death of the sinner now rejoices at the death of a
friend; "I rejoice, not for myself, nor for the one who has died, but
for you; for I have used the death toward the foundation of your faith."
O death, Christ's gladness! =62.777= O death, filled with life! O death,
release from death, author of salvation, resurrection of the fallen,
rest of those who toil, strength of the sick, refreshment of the
righteous! O death, overthrow of the devil, demolition of demons,
renewal of the first-formed Adam! O death, fellow-worker of gladness,
messenger of incorruption, dancer of joy! But that I may cut the word
short and speak, Jesus comes to Bethany, the sisters of Lazarus met him,
they fall at the knees of the Lord weeping and saying: "Lord, if you had
been here, our brother would not have died." Was he then not there? But
in the body he was not there, yet in the Godhead not only there, but
also in the heavens. For how did he know when Lazarus died, if he was
not present according to the invisible power of the Godhead? And they
said: "Lord, if you had been here, our brother would not have died."
And the Lord said to them: "Believe, and you shall see the glory of
God"; teaching that faith is the leader of good works. And having seen
them weeping, and the Jews who were present, "He groaned in the spirit,
and troubled himself, and said: Where have you laid him?" What is this,
"He groaned in the spirit"? Wait, beloved, and in a little you will find
the solution of what is sought. "He troubled himself," not as we are
troubled by cowardice or grief, but he himself troubled himself, and
says: "Where have you laid him?" Did he then not know where he was
buried, he who had known when he died? But he mixes human words with the
divine wonders, in order to show that he was also a man, as the prophet
also says: "And he is a man, and who shall know him?" And he set this
first in order to signify that there is also something else, which is
God. "Where have you laid him?" And Mary says: "Come and see, Lord." And
Jesus wept. What need was there to weep for him whom he was about to
raise in a little while? But Jesus wept, that he might show us the
sympathy and the love of man toward those of the same race; Jesus wept,
that he might teach by deed rather than by word the weeping with those
who weep; Jesus wept; he wept, he did not mourn, declining a complete
tearlessness as harsh and inhuman, and thrusting away a love of mourning
as ignoble and unmanly; but setting order upon sympathy, he wept.
He comes to the cave, and sees a stone lying upon the tomb. He commands
the Jews standing by to roll away the stone from the mouth of the cave.
He who says: "If you have faith =62.778= as a grain of mustard seed, you
shall say to this mountain, Be taken up and be cast into the sea, and it
shall be done for you"; how now are you yourself unable to roll away the
stone? "But I do not do this as one unable," he says, "but so that they
may not think what is happening to be a phantasm, I command the Jews
themselves to roll away the stone with their own hands, reserving the
greatest sign for myself; so that they themselves may become witnesses
of the signs done by me." Then after the rolling away of the stone,
Jesus, having looked up to heaven, said: "I thank you, Father, that you
have heard me." When then did he pray? Now receive, beloved, the
solution of the saying sought by you a little before; for when he
groaned in the spirit, then he prayed. And having said these things, he
cried with a loud voice, saying, "Lazarus, come forth." And immediately
the dead man came out, bound hand and foot with grave-bands. O power of
a voice that tore Hades, that crushed gates of bronze, that shattered
bars of iron, that laid the devil low, that abolished death, that raised
a dead man! O power of a voice, gathering the sundered members into one
and setting them upright, and bringing what was from non-being into
being!
O power of a voice, raising the four-day dead man as from sleep, and
leading forth from the tomb the one bound with grave-bands as one easily
loosed and swift of foot! Set your mind, beloved, upon the voice, and
you will find it to be the same Word that says in the making of the
world, "Let there be light, and there was light; Let there be lights in
the firmament of heaven, and they came to be," the same now also saying:
"Lazarus, come forth." And the juices that had flowed away from the
flesh filled the sockets again, and the hairs that had fallen recognized
their own rank, and were planted in the members. "Lazarus, come forth";
and the dead man was rising, and the four-day man was equal to one who
had not even died at the first. "Lazarus, come forth"; and the soul was
being drawn up from the underworld, with no devil contradicting, no
death wrestling against, and rejoicing it recognized its own dwelling.
Having seen the divine work of these wonders, beloved, do not doubt the
resurrection; let Lazarus become a mirror for you, and having seen
yourself in him, believe the raising. For the same voice will raise us
all, as the Apostle also says somewhere: "For the trumpet shall sound,
and the dead shall be raised incorruptible"; by the grace of our Lord
Jesus Christ, to whom be the glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
