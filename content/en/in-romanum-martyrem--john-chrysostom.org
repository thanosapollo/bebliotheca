On the Holy Martyr Romanus. Homily 2.
=50.611=
1. Wrestling-schools grant courage to bodies, and knowledge of the athletic
art; but memorials of martyrs arm the souls against the arts of the demons,
and train them for the grips against those. For by making public the athletic
strain and the unyielding wrestling against the scourges, they raise piety
unto =50.612= boldness, as in the pits, by the narratives of the sufferings,
setting forth a double-course stretched taut by every martyr. Such is the
memorial of the athlete crowned today. For who would not dare the pits against
the devil, having trained his soul with the wrestlings of the martyr, whom a
multitude of so many dangers did not shake? For a great =50.613= tyranny of
impiety was then dancing in the world, and life imitated a sea shaken from the
depths, and the triple-waves of the deep passed over onto the land, and a
violent surge of impiety was flooding around the ship of piety, in which many
helmsmen were dead on every side, and no small number of sailors underwater;
and all things were full of bitter fears and shipwrecks.
Kings were blowing more violently than a hurricane, tyrants were bringing on
fearful triple-waves, the thrones of rulers were shaken together, judges were
proclaiming denial of Christ, lawgivers were threatening bitter punishments,
men were being snatched toward the sacrifices of the demons, women were being
dragged toward the abomination of the altars; virgins too were being hauled
toward this madness, priests were being given over to flights and slaughters,
the faithful were being driven from the sacred precincts. Against so great a
battle the martyr was arming himself, and to so many dangers he sets his mind
in opposition, laughing at the array as at some shadow-fight, and as in the
pits, sprinkling the judges with faith instead of dust, thus he was stirring
the mind of the one then judging, having stopped a course being practiced by
him against the Church. Therefore the noble one was quickly snatched toward
punishment, and many varieties of torments were being brought forward; but the
martyr was like a lyre, being moved to sound by the plectrum of the tortures;
the executioners standing around were crushing the body, but he, like some
bronze, when struck, was sounding beneath them the strain of piety; hanging
him on wood they were scraping him, but he was embracing the wood as a tree of
life; they were tearing the cheeks as the ribs of the just man, but he, as if
receiving more mouths, was orating, and by a novel fall he put the adversary
to shame. For when he saw the judge calling him out toward zeal for the
demons, he asks that an infant be brought from the marketplace, as if he would
make that child a judge of the things being sought by the judge; and to the
infant that had been brought he put the question concerning the matters at
hand. Child, he says, is it just to worship God, or the gods so-called by
these men?
Great is the abundance of the martyr's wisdom; he seats the little child as
judge of the judge; and it quickly gave its vote for Christ, that even
children might be shown wiser than impious judges, or rather that the martyr
might appear not only a martyr, but also a trainer of martyrs. Yet not even
this tripped up the judicial madness, but at once the martyr was snatched onto
wood together with the infant, and prison succeeded the punishment of the
wood, and a sentence succeeded that, distributing varied punishments to the
athletes: for it condemned the infant to death, and the martyr to the cutting
out of the tongue. Who has heard such a manner of judgment? Judges scourge
condemned men, forcing them to confess what they know in themselves, but the
judge of impiety cuts out the tongue, forcing silence about what he knows in
himself. O terrible invention of evil craft. The soul that thinks the things
of Christ, he says, I did not drive out, even if I cut through the tongue
that speaks for Christ. Cut out, O tyrant, a tongue, that you may
learn that nature even without a tongue orates for Christ; peck the tongue
from the mouth, that you may learn that he who promised the gifts of tongues
is true. For he cut out the organ of the tongue, but the word was rushing
forth more violently, =50.614= as if freed from some hindrance, the tongue; a
novel and paradoxical spectacle: a fleshly man speaking to fleshly men without
flesh. Fitting then for the martyr is the prophet's strain: Our mouth was
filled with joy, and our tongue with exultation; the mouth was filled with
joy, having offered the tongue as a novel sacrifice to Christ; the tongue had
great exultation, having appeared a forerunner martyr of the martyr. O tongue
that anticipated the soul toward the companies of the martyrs; O mouth that
bore a hidden martyr; O tongue that acquired the mouth as an altar; O mouth
that acquired the tongue as a victim; you had, O noble one, without knowing
it, the mouth as a temple, a temple in which you sacrificed the kindred tongue
as a paradoxical lamb.
2. What orator then would worthily crown your virtues? You received a tongue
from nature, but you reared it as a martyr; you received a mouth as a guard of
the tongue, but with the tongue you made the mouth an altar; you received a
plectrum for sounding, but you showed it, when cut, as an ear of grain; you
received a tongue as a servant of words, but you sacrificed it as an
unblemished sheep to Christ. With what fitting title shall I honor your
tongue? With what name shall I adorn your tongue? The executioners were
bringing the iron to it, but it, like bound Isaac, did not leap, but lying as
on an altar in the mouth it received the blow with pleasure, teaching the
tongues of men that they must not only speak for Christ, but also be
slaughtered. You seized, O noble one, the generous honor of the patriarch's
sacrifice, offering an only-begotten offspring of the tongue instead of an
only-begotten child. Well then did Christ plant a second tongue for you in
return; for he found you a good farmer of the former; well did he grant you
the fleshless tongue; for a fleshly one was not fitting for an angelic mind;
well did he pay you the recompense of the tongue. You lent your tongue to the
Master for sacrifice, and he lent back a voice to it for oratory; and there
came to be an exchange of the tongue and of Christ with one another, the
tongue being cut for Christ, and Christ speaking for the tongue. Where now for
us is Macedonius, who fights against the Paraclete who gave the gift of
tongues?
And that I do not lie in attaching the gifts of the charisms to the divinity
of the Paraclete, we have as witness the blessed Paul, who now cries out to
your love of hearing: All these things one and the same Spirit works,
distributing to each one individually, as he wills. As he wills, he says, not
as he is commanded. But lest by adding some additions to these we bury your
memory under the multitude, remembering this voice which is strong on behalf
of the Paraclete, let us thus go out from here, thinking great things against
those men, yet for now =50.615= pardoning them as they wander, and worshiping
the divinity of the Paraclete. The prophetic trumpet then, signaling
beforehand the universal symphony unto Christ, was saying, That they shall
know me, it says, from their small even unto their great, And every tongue
shall confess to the true God. And the prophet, as I said, encloses every
tongue in the net of the knowledge of God; but we today shall hear even a
tongueless orator advocating for piety; for as some lyre that has no plectrum
he praises the Creator. Let the blessed Romanus also say then: My tongue is
the pen of a swiftly writing scribe. What tongue? Not the one the iron took
away, but the one the grace of the Spirit forged; for when the tongue was
plundered, the grace of the Spirit was brought in instead. The apostles also
had tongues, but that the power of the one working might be shown, the clay
was idle, and the heavenly fire was speaking. The Mosaic Scripture also had
the image of the thing beyond speech; for in that there is a bush and fire.
And the apostolic fire prefigured the voices of the preaching upon the bush,
and it grants a voice to the lifeless, that when it has touched living organs
it may be believed. For if the touch of the fire made the lifeless vocal, how
was it not going to fill rational souls somewhere through touch to strike up
the all-harmonious strain?
The celebrated Romanus also partook of this grace, and having had his tongue
cut off he was convicting the tyrant with a clearer sound. And the tyrant
would not at all have run to the cutting out of the tongue, if he had not
feared the streams of the rebukes, if he had not been struck by the torrent of
the preaching, if he had not supposed he would lay the waves of the
evangelical oratory. But let us see from where the tyrant comes into the
necessity of such daring.
3. The impious man once having sacrificed to demons, and having become full of
smoke and the savor of fat, and defiled with the drops of impiety, was going
at a run toward the church, and carrying the bloodied axe, he was seeking the
bloodless altar for an unlawful liturgy. But the tyrant's frenzy did not
escape the martyr. At once then leaping out into the forecourts, he checks the
flood of impiety already being borne; and as some resourceful helmsman, seeing
the sea being borne against the prow, does not endure to be still, but runs
through the whole ship with a light foot, and having raised the stern by the
rudder, he sets the vessel face to face with the waves, and having lifted what
was in danger, he splits the triple-wave in the middle, and by a certain art
he furrows the swelling deep; this also the blessed Romanus did.
When the idolatrous sea was roaring blasphemies, and raging against the
church's ship, and vomiting a foam of bloods against the altars, he alone arms
himself against the raging sea, and seeing the ship failing at the extremity,
he awakens the Master in the boat, and he awakens him sleeping the sleep of
long-suffering. He sees the deep being storm-tossed by contrary winds, and he
utters the words of the disciples in danger: Master, save, we perish; pirates
surround the ship, wolves besiege the flock, robbers dig through your bridal
chamber, adulterous hissings ring around your bride, again the =50.616=
serpent is housebreaking paradise; the rock, the foundation of the Church, is
being shaken; but throw the evangelical anchor from heaven, and steady the
rock that is being shaken; Master, save, we perish. The common danger divides
the martyr, and he speaks freely toward the Master; he lets loose the tongue
flowing against the tyrant, saying, Stop this mad course, O tyrant; know the
measures of your weakness, reverence the bounds of the Crucified; and the
bounds of the Crucified are not the walls of the church, but the ends of the
inhabited world; shake off the mist of madness; look to the earth, and
consider the weakness of your nature; look up to heaven, and reckon the
magnitude of the war; spit upon the weak alliance of the demons; consider that
the demons, having been struck by the cross, put you forward as champion of
their own altars.
Why do you pursue the incomprehensible? Why do you shoot at the boundless? Is
God circumscribed by walls? The divine is uncircumscribed. Is our Master seen
with eyes? For he is unseen and formless in essence, but according to the
human he is written and seen. Does he dwell in stone and wood, selling
providence in exchange for ox and sheep? Does an altar mediate his
transactions? This is the greedy begging of your demons; my Master, or rather
the Master of all, Christ, dwells in heaven, and drives the world as a
charioteer, and a sacrifice to him is a soul looking up toward him, one food
for him, the salvation of those who believe. Cease moving weapons against the
Church; the flock is on earth, and the one who shepherds is in heaven; the
branches are on earth, and the vine is in heaven; but if you cut out the
branches, you multiply the vine. Your hands are full of gore, your sword is
from irrational victims; spare the guiltless beasts, and apply the iron to us
who rebuke; spare the silent irrational creatures, and slaughter us who
accuse. For I do not so fear the man-slaying iron as the axe upon the altar;
the man-slaying iron tears the body, the altar-axe kills the soul; the
man-slaying iron slaughters the one being sacrificed, but the altar-axe
destroys both the one being sacrificed and the one sacrificing together. Cut
my neck, and do not defile an altar; you have a self-bidden victim, why do you
hobble the bound bull that tosses its head? If you desire to sacrifice,
sacrifice a rational victim in the forecourts of the church. The tyrant does
not bear the immoderate boldness of the martyr, but at once he begins from the
tongue of the victim.
He cuts out the tongue then, not wishing to kill, but warring against the
preaching; not so much envying the herald as grudging the one being heralded.
But He who catches the wise in their craftiness gives back from heaven the
cut-out organ of the voice, and with an invisible tongue he props up the
limping sound, and grants the voice to the tongueless, showing by deed the
human creation to the tyrant. And as well-diggers, stirring the =50.617=
veins, work a wider outflow for the water, so also the tyrant, digging through
the root of the tongue with iron, was being flooded with more violent streams
of rebukes.
I wished to dance through to the end in the martyr's theme, but the time of
due measure has come, and it commands me =50.618= to be silent; for what has
been said is enough for you toward benefit, and the teachings of the father
are necessary toward the completion of what has been said; but let us also
embrace what has been said in the bosom of memory, and let us open the furrows
of the soul for what is about to be said, and above all let us worship the
wonder-working Christ, because to him is the glory with the Father and the
all-holy Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
