OF GREGORY, BISHOP OF NYSSA, ON HIS OWN ORDINATION, TO EVAGRIUS, CONCERNING
THE GODHEAD.
The ministry of the spiritual feast has come even upon us, who are more
fitted to share in it from others than to offer ourselves to others as being
fit. And yet I for my part thought it altogether right to be exempt from such
contributions because of the poverty of speech, according to a certain law of
the banquet. For I hear that those more luxurious in their manner of life,
when they prepare the feasts in turn for the common mess, if anyone from the
roll of the diners should be poorer, have this one, by common agreement, as a
guest exempt from contribution. So I too wished to be a boarder at the luxury
of the rich. But since this fair and rich host does not spare even us, but
commands us to serve, I will say to him: Friend, lend me of your loaves; and
by loaves I mean the alliance that comes through prayers. For how is it
possible to welcome with a poor and beggarly word your hearing, which has
been luxuriating in these spiritual honeycombs? And perhaps it would have
been well, as in the gymnastic contests (for in those the stadium belongs
only to the strong, and the rest become the audience of those who contend),
so also in this spiritual =9.332= stadium that only those who swell with
vigor in the power of words should strip against the contestants, but if
there should be anyone such as I am, whose hair is gray, whose power is
sluggish from time, and whose speech is trembling and limping, that this one
should be left to take delight in the sweat of the contestants. Therefore let
us not lengthen the preface for you, brothers, occupying ourselves with
wonder at those who went before. Already you have been filled, already you
have become rich, and the satiety is from sweet things; for with these the
word that went before nursed you. Perhaps then it is useless to lay satiety
upon satiety, loading the memory, upon the golden words, with something like
leaden coins; except that often even the worse material, mixed with the
better, contributes to a partnership in beauty. The proof is near: you see
this roof above our heads, how fair it is to look at, how the gold blooms
gracefully upon the carvings? This, being all golden on the surface, is
incised beneath with certain polygonal circles smeared with dark blue. What
then does the dark blue mean for the craftsman? So that, I think, by the
interchange of color the gold may shine forth more. If then the dark blue
mixed with the gold shows the gleam more beautiful, it is perhaps not
untimely that our black ink also be written in beside the brightness of the
words already spoken. Still we are chatting with you in prefaces, but hear.
=9.333=
I hear that Moses the great, when he was pitching the tabernacle for the
Israelites, and that Bezalel, who by the divine Spirit had the wisdom of
architecture self-taught, set forth the ambition in common to rich and poor,
the rich choosing gold and purple and the precious of the stones, and the
poor not rejecting wood and skins and the hair of goats. But perhaps the
recollection of the history is not untimely; for the thought that ran under
my heart I wish also to set before the common gathering. By the divine Spirit
Bezalel became wise from being an unskilled man; for so the history says. Let
the fighter against the Spirit hear, then: he who has called the Holy Spirit
divine, whose coming-upon, like some footprint, stamps the grace of wisdom
upon the soul, does he insult the dignity of the Spirit by the title of the
divine? Does he propose that we think something small and lowly concerning
him, or is something of the things in creation signified by this name? Does
he think the divinity acquired for the Spirit? Does he conceive some
doubleness and composition about the simple and uncompounded? Perhaps it is
not possible to be carried along with such suspicions, but he altogether
confesses that the Spirit, being divine by nature, both is such and is so
called. You see how of itself the truth is unveiled to you; for the preaching
of the Christians does not know many divine natures, since then of necessity
many gods also would be invented. For it is not possible for many gods to be
conceived unless a difference according to nature =9.334= announces the
multitude of gods. If then one divine nature is believed by all to be, and
the Holy Spirit is divine by nature, why do you cut with speech what is
joined by nature? But who will give me the power of that word whose end was
the salvation of those who heard? Peter sent forth one voice to the men of
Jerusalem, and so many thousands of men were netted by the fisherman in the
one cast of the word. And yet so many and such words are worn among us by the
teachers, and what addition of those being saved has there been?
That which is failing fails, says one of the prophets; that which is dying
dies. That which has gone astray does not return. The bond of love has been
dissolved; peace has been snatched from our treasures. O the evils; for I am
led to groan over the suffering. Love was once our possession, this
inheritance was ancestral to us, which the Lord stored up for us through the
disciples, saying: A new commandment I give you, that you love one another.
But this inheritance the successive heirs, receiving it child from father,
preserved until our fathers, but this prodigal generation has not kept it.
How has the wealth of love gone, slipping from our hands? We are poor of
love, and the enemies take pride in our goods.
I was jealous of the lawless; so says the psalmist, but I, having slightly
=9.335= parodied the saying, will read: I was jealous of those unlike us, I
was jealous seeing the peace of sinners; they stand together with one another
and we are cut apart from one another; they lock shields with themselves and
we dissolve our own locking of shields. Having stolen our possession, the
clothes-snatcher of souls, carrying it, threw it to the enemies of the truth,
not benefiting those, let no one think this (for the inventor of evils cannot
become a benefactor), but that he might make them worse in their conspiracy
concerning evil. But, What is it to me to judge those outside? says the one
who said it; but I: how shall I bear without tears the alienation of the
brothers? How has this young brother gone, leaving the ancestral substance?
That one shown in the Gospel is another, even if he hides the youth of the
soul by the gray hair of the body; how did he withdraw into a far country,
running away from the faith? How has he too gone, having halved the substance
of the father in dragging down the high things of the doctrines toward the
low and swine-like thoughts, having spent the wealth besides upon the
heretical harlots? For heresy is a harlot, enchanting with deceived
pleasures. For if he should ever come to himself as that one did, if he
should again desire the ancestral luxury, if he should run back to the rich
table, at which there is much of the daily bread that feeds the hired
servants of the Lord (and hired servants are all these who work the vineyard
of God in hope of the promise), what a running there would have been toward
=9.336= him, not of one father, but of so many fathers meeting him, embracing
him, greeting him with kisses. There is the first robe of the faith, which
the three hundred and eighteen souls, spinning well for the Church, wove;
there the ring on the hand, having in itself the seal of the faith stamped
upon it, the choruses, the calf, the music, all the other things that have
been enumerated in the Gospel except the jealousy of the brother. But why do
we vainly fashion dreams for ourselves? The heart of the brothers has been
hardened and is unyielding. They put forward the common fathers and do not
receive the inheritance from them; they claim the common nobility and are
alienated from kinship with us; they stand against our enemies, but toward us
they are hostile, having become as it were some borderland of us and of the
adversaries, and they are both and neither: they do not confess the right
word, and they think it unworthy to be named heretics.
O a novel thing: they have been equally at war both toward the truth and
toward the falsehood, like some rootless tree easily =9.337= leaning this way
and that toward contrary impulses. I heard the evangelist John in the
Apocalypse saying through an enigma to such as these that it is necessary
either to be exactly boiling or exactly cooled, to be boiling altogether in
the Spirit, and to be cooled toward sin. For would that you were, he says,
cold or hot; but that which is neither of these, yet touching both, is
nauseous and fit for vomiting. What then is the cause that of old, in the
time of the disciples, a great multitude was brought to the Church through
the Lord, but now the long and most beautiful words of the teachers run past
unused?
Someone will perhaps say that then the wonders from the works cooperated with
the apostles, and the word had credibility through the gifts. I too say that
the strength from the works is a great weight toward persuading. But now what
must one reckon the things that are happening? Or do you not see the like
wonders of the faith? For I claim as my own the achievements of our
fellow-servants, who walking in the same Spirit bear witness in the power of
healings to the truth of the word, men come from beyond the borders, citizens
of our father Abraham, setting out from Mesopotamia, they too having gone out
from their land and their kindred and the whole world, looking toward heaven,
being in a way away from human life, standing above the passions of nature,
touching this life only so much as is necessary, and with the greater part
they journey aloft together with the bodiless =9.338= powers, aged in
appearance, solemn to look upon, gleaming with gray hair, their mouths
secured with silence, not knowing how to fight with words, not having learned
to dispute. They have such authority against the spirits that by command
alone they accomplish what they will, and the demons withdraw not by arts of
syllogisms but by the authority of the faith, not being driven around into
the impasse of contradiction but being driven out into the outer darkness.
Thus the Christian knows how to syllogize; these are the achievements of our
faith. Why then do we not persuade, if the grace of healings abounds, if the
teaching of the word overflows, And all these things the one and the same
Spirit works, distributing to each individually as he wills? Why does the
addition of those being saved not come about?
And let no one suspect me of reckoning the present grace small; I see the
vine flourishing, thriving with the branches and abounding with fruit; I see
the field waving with the multitude of ears; deep is the standing crop, stout
the sheaf, much the seed. But what am I to suffer? Having in such things a
nature that cannot be filled, I am held by the passion of those who love
wealth. No limit of abundance stops the desire; that which is always being
added stirs the appetite toward more, becoming fuel of the desire of the
greater. The things seen gladden me =9.339= and the things not seen grieve
me; I exult in those present and I am pained at those absent. A novel
passion, mixed from opposites, surrounds the soul, pleasure blended with
grief. If I look up toward you, I rest the longing upon you; if I reckon what
is lacking, I have no way not to groan at the misfortune. For men, having
left off delighting in the Lord and rejoicing at the peace of the Church,
turn certain essences into a technical art and measure magnitudes, measuring
the Son against the Father and granting to the Father that which is more than
the measure. Who would say to them that the unquantified is not measured, the
formless is not assayed, the bodiless is not weighed, the unbounded is not
compared, that which is not compared does not admit the account of the more
and the less? For from the setting of limits beside one another we come to
know the more; but of that whose end is ungraspable, the exceeding is
inconceivable. I heard the refrain, which we all in common went through
hymning: Great is our Lord, and great is his strength, and of his
understanding there is no number. What then is this? Number the things said
and you understand the mystery. Great is the Lord: he did not say how great
(for it was not even possible to say the how much), but by the unboundedness
of the signification he leads the mind toward the infinite. Likewise, Great,
he says, is his strength; and having heard strength, understand the power.
And Christ is the power of God and the wisdom of God. But also of his
understanding there is no number. Isaiah interprets the understanding, saying
clearly: Spirit of wisdom and understanding. =9.340=
I heard in the beatitudes those who thirst for the Lord being called blessed.
Perhaps then the thought is not outside the season; and receive my word, even
if it should seem to hang apart from the things present. If someone traveling
through midday, the sun boiling over the head with hotter rays and roasting
with the flame every moisture in the body (and let there be set before the
example a land rough and hard to travel and thirsty), then such a one should
happen upon a spring, whose stream is fair and transparent and gently cooling
and overflowing without stint, will he sit down by the water and philosophize
about the nature, examining whence and how and through what and such things,
of the sort it is customary for those who talk vanity to say, that some
moisture sown in the depth of the earth and leaping through and being
squeezed together becomes water, or that veins poured out in the hollows
below, if they should be opened at the mouth, pour forth the water; or,
having let all such things go, does he bend toward the stream and, putting
his lips to it, heal the thirst and cool the tongue and still the longing and
give thanks to the one who has given the grace? Imitate then you also the one
who thirsts, since it has also been said that Blessed are those who thirst,
and having learned what kind and how many good things the Holy Spirit causes
to spring, do the thing of the prophet: Open your mouth and draw =9.341=
Spirit; Widen your mouth and he who has the authority of the gifts will fill
it. Do you wish to learn how many good things are poured forth from the
spring of the Spirit? Incorruption of soul, eternity of life, kingdom of the
heavens, unceasing gladness, joy that has no end. But looking toward the
things present I make the loss of what is lacking small; my house has been
filled with good things, the treasuries of the gold of Arabia are full, and
perhaps after a little even ambassadors from Egypt will come and will stretch
forth their hand to God, and the kingdoms of the earth with us will sing the
victory hymn to the one who calls all into his kingdom, to whom be the glory
and the power unto the ages of the ages, amen.
