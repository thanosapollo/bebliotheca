=49.223= FIRST CATECHESIS.
To those about to be illumined, and why it is called a bath of regeneration
and not of remission of sins, and that it is dangerous not only to swear
falsely, but even to swear, even if we swear truly.
αʹ. How longed-for and beloved is the choir of our new brethren; for I already
call you brethren even before the birth-pangs, and before the delivery I
embrace the kinship toward you. For I know, I know clearly to what honor you
are about to be led, and to what rule; and all are accustomed to honor those
about to receive a rule even before the rule, laying up goodwill for
themselves beforehand by their attendance toward what is coming. And this I
too do now; for you are about to be led not to a bare rule, but to the kingdom
itself; or rather not simply to a kingdom, but to the very kingdom of the
heavens. For this reason I beg and entreat you, that you remember me when you
come into that kingdom, and what Joseph said to the chief cupbearer: Remember
me, when it shall be well with you; this I too say to you now: Remember me
when it shall be well with you. I do not demand this return for dreams as he
did; for I did not come to interpret dreams for you, but about to narrate the
things in the heavens, and bringing good tidings of such goods, As eye has not
seen, nor ear heard, nor have they entered into the heart of man; such are the
things Which God prepared for those who love him. Joseph then said to that
cupbearer: Yet three days, and Pharaoh will restore you to your chief
cupbearing; but I do not say, Yet three days, and you will be brought to the
cupbearing of a tyrant; but yet thirty days, and not Pharaoh, but the king of
the heavens will restore you to the fatherland above, to the free Jerusalem,
to the city in the heavens. And he said that You will give the cup into the
hands of Pharaoh; but I do not say that you will give the cup into the hands
of the king, but that the king himself will give into your hand the cup that
is fearful and full of much power, and more precious than either creation. The
initiated know the strength of this cup; and you too will know it a little
later. Remember then, when you come into that kingdom, when you receive the
royal garment, when you put on the purple dyed with the Master's blood, when
you bind on a diadem having radiances leaping out from every side more
brilliant than the sun's rays. For such are the bridegroom's wedding-gifts,
greater than our worth, but worthy of his loving-kindness. For these things I
already from here and before those sacred bridal chambers call you blessed;
and I do not only call you blessed, but =49.224= I also praise the good sense,
that you did not, as the more careless of men, come to the illumination at
last breaths, but already from here, as well-disposed servants, prepared with
much goodwill to obey a Master, you brought the neck of the soul with much
gentleness and eagerness under the yoke of Christ, and you received the easy
yoke, and you took the light burden. For even if the things of grace are equal
for you and for those initiated at the end, yet the things of choice are not
equal, nor the things of the preparation of the matters. For those receive it
on the bed, but you in the bosom of the Church, the common mother of us all;
and those wailing and weeping, but you rejoicing and being glad; those
groaning, but you giving thanks; and those stupefied with much fever, but you
filled with much spiritual pleasure.


Hence here all things are in harmony with the gift, but there all things are
contrary to the gift; for there is lamentation and much wailing of those being
initiated, and children stand around weeping, and a wife tearing her cheeks,
and friends downcast, and servants full of tears, the whole appearance of the
house imitates some wintry and gloomy day; and if you unfold the heart itself
of the one lying there, you will find it more sullen than these. For as winds
striking against one another with much force cut the sea into many parts; so
also the thoughts of the terrible things then holding him, falling upon the
soul of the sick man, split his mind into many cares. When he looks at the
children, he thinks of their orphanhood; when he looks toward the wife, he
reckons widowhood; when he sees the servants, he considers the desolation of
the whole house; when he returns to himself, he reminds himself of the present
life, and being about to be torn away, he receives a great cloud of
despondency. Such is the soul of the one about to be initiated. Then in the
midst of the tumults and the disturbance itself, the priest enters, being more
fearful than the fever itself, and harsher than death to those belonging to
the sick man; for the entrance of the presbyter is thought to be a greater
despair than the voice of the physician despairing of his life, and the
occasion of eternal life seems to be a symbol of death. But I have not yet set
the coping-stone of the evils; for in the midst of the intimates making tumult
and prepar49.225ing, often the soul, having left the body empty, leaped away,
and even being present it benefited many not at all. For when he neither
recognizes those present, nor hears a voice, nor is able to answer those words
through which he will lay down the blessed covenant with the Master common to
us all, but like idle wood or a stone the one about to be illumined lies
there, differing in nothing from a dead man, what is the profit of the
initiation in such insensibility?
βʹ. For the one about to approach these sacred and fearful mysteries must be
awake and aroused, be clean of every worldly care, be full of much sobriety,
of much eagerness, banish from the mind every thought alien to the mysteries,
and prepare the house clean from every side, as being about to receive the
king himself. Such is the preparation of your mind, such are the thoughts
among you, such is the purpose of the soul. Await then the worthy return of
this excellent judgment from God, who conquers with recompenses those who show
the obedience toward him. But since it is necessary for fellow-servants also
to contribute the things from themselves, we too will bring in the things from
us, or rather not even these are ours, but these too are the Master's. For
what have you, he says, that you did not receive? and if you received it, why
do you boast as not having received? I wished first of all to say: Why ever
did our fathers, passing over the whole year, legislate that the children of
the Church be initiated at this season? and for what reason after the teaching
from us, having loosed you and stripped you, they send you naked and unshod
with the tunic only to the voices of those who exorcize. For they did not
simply nor at random type out for us both this figure and this season; but
both these things have some mystical and unspeakable reason. And this I wished
to tell you; but I see that the discourse now presses us toward something more
necessary. For it is necessary to say what baptism is, and for what reason it
entered into our life, and how many goods it brings us. But, if you wish, let
us first converse about the name of this mystical cleansing. For there is not
one name for it, but many and of all kinds; for this cleansing is called a
bath of regeneration; For he saved us, he says, through a bath of regeneration
and renewal of the Holy Spirit. It is also called


illumination, and this Paul again called it; For remember the former days, in
which having been illumined you endured a great contest of sufferings; and
again, For it is impossible for those once illumined and having tasted of the
heavenly gift, and having fallen away, to renew them again unto repentance. It
is also called baptism; For as many of you as were baptized into Christ, you
put on Christ. It is called burial; For you were buried with him, he says,
through baptism into death. It is called circumcision; In whom also you were
circumcised with a circumcision not made with hands, in the putting off of the
body of the sins of the flesh. It is called a cross; For our old man was
crucified with him, that the body of sin might be abolished. It is possible to
say even more names than these, but lest we con49.226sume the whole time on
the names of the gift, come, having come to the former name, and having said
the meaning of this, let us rest the discourse; but for now let us draw the
teaching a little higher. There is the common bath of all men, that through
the bath-houses, which is accustomed to wipe off the filth of the body; and
there is also a Jewish bath, more august than that, but much lower than that
of grace; for it too wipes off bodily filth, but not simply bodily, but also
touching a weak conscience. For there are many of the matters which are not
unclean by nature, but become unclean from the weakness of the conscience. And
as with children the masks and the other bugbears are not fearful by nature,
but seem to be fearful to the children because of the weakness of nature, so
also with these things of which I spoke; for example, to touch dead bodies is
not unclean by nature, but when it takes hold of a weak conscience, it makes
the one who touched unclean. For that the thing is not unclean by nature,
Moses himself who arranged these things showed, carrying with himself the
whole dead Joseph, and remaining clean. For this reason Paul also, conversing
with us about this uncleanness which comes not by nature but through weakness
of conscience, says somehow thus: Nothing is common of itself, except to him
who reckons something to be common. Do you see the uncleanness coming not from
the nature of the thing, but from the weakness of the reasoning? And again,
All things indeed are clean, but it is evil for the man who eats through a
stumbling-block. Do you see that not the eating, but the eating through a
stumbling-block, this is the cause of uncleanness?
γʹ. Such a stain the Jewish bath cleaned out; but that of grace not such, but
the real uncleanness which with the body has also put much stain into the
soul; for it makes clean not those who have touched dead bodies, but those who
have touched dead deeds. And if one is an effeminate, if a fornicator, if an
idolater, if having worked anything terrible whatever, if having all the
wickedness among men, having fallen into the pool of the waters, he comes up
from the divine streams cleaner than the sun's rays. And lest you think the
things said to be a boast, hear Paul speaking about the power of the bath: Be
not deceived; neither idolaters, nor fornicators, nor adulterers, nor
effeminate, nor abusers of themselves with men, nor covetous, nor drunkards,
nor revilers, nor extortioners shall inherit the kingdom of God. And what is
this to the things said, he says? for the thing sought is to show whether the
power of the bath cleanses out all these. Therefore hear what follows: And
such were some of you, but you were washed, but you were sanctified, but you
were justified in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ and in the Spirit of our
God. We promised to show you those approaching the bath becoming clean of all
fornication; but the discourse proved more, not only clean, but also becoming
holy and just; for he did not say, You were washed, only, but also, You were
sanctified and you were justified. What could be more paradoxical than this,


when without toils and sweats and achievements righteousness is being born?
For such is the loving-kindness of the divine gift, it makes righteous without
=49.227= sweats. For if a king's letter, a few letters set forth, lets those
liable to countless charges go free, and leads others even to greatest honor,
much more the Holy Spirit of God, able to do all things, will both release us
from all wickedness, and will grant us much righteousness, and will fill us
with much boldness. And as a spark falling into the midst of a vast sea would
immediately be quenched, or even become invisible, drowned by the multitude of
the waters; so also all human wickedness, when it falls into the pool of the
divine streams, is drowned and made to vanish more quickly and more easily
than that spark. And for what reason, he says, if the bath remits all our
sins, is it not called a bath of remission of sins, nor a bath of cleansing,
but a bath of regeneration? Because it does not simply remit our sins, nor
simply cleanse us of the faults, but so as if we had been born from above. For
it also creates us from above and constructs us, not molding again from earth,
but creating from another element, the nature of the waters. For it does not
simply wipe the vessel, but recasts it whole again. For things wiped, even if
they are cleaned out with accuracy, have traces of the quality and bear the
remnants of the stain; but things that have fallen into a furnace and been
renewed through the flame, having put off all filth, send forth the same
radiance as those newly-cast coming out from the kiln. As therefore someone
taking a golden statue dirtied by much time and smoke and dust and rust, and
having melted it, gives it back to us most clean and flashing, so also God,
taking our nature rusted with the rust of sin, and having received much smoke
from the faults, and having lost the beauty which he placed in it from the
beginning, melted it from above, and as having cast it into a furnace, the
waters, and having sent upon it the grace of the Spirit instead of flame, then
newly-cast from there and become new he leads them up henceforth to look
against the sun's rays with much brightness, having crushed the old man, and
having constructed a new one brighter than the former.
δʹ. And hinting at this crushing and this mystical cleansing for us the
prophet said from above: As a potter's vessel you will crush them. For that
the discourse is about the faithful, the things above show us clearly; For you
are my son, he says; today I have begotten you. Ask of me, and I will give you
the nations as your inheritance, and as your possession the ends of the earth.
Did you see how he remembered the Church from the nations, and said the
kingdom of Christ stretched everywhere? Then he says again: You will shepherd
them with a rod of iron; not burdensome, but strong; as a potter's vessel you
will crush them. Behold, the bath too was taken more mystically; for he did
not say simply earthenware vessels, but potter's vessels. But attend. For
earthenware vessels once crushed would not receive correction, because of the
hardness once engendered in them from the fire; but the vessels of the potter
are not earthenware, but of clay; whence even having been distorted they would
easily return to the second shape by the wisdom of the craftsman. When
therefore he speaks of an incurable disaster, =49.228= he does not say
potter's vessels, but an earthenware vessel. When at any rate he wished to
teach the prophet and the Jews that he had delivered the city to an incurable
disaster, he commanded him to take an earthenware jar, and crush it before all
the people, and say: Thus shall the city too perish, and be crushed. But
whenever he wishes to hold out good hopes to them, he leads the prophet down
to a pottery, and does not show him an earthenware vessel, but shows him a
vessel


of clay being made in the potter's hands, falling apart, and he adds saying:
If this potter, having taken up the vessel that fell apart, shaped it again,
shall I not much more be able to set you right when you have fallen apart? It
is possible then for God not only to set right those who are of clay through a
bath of regeneration, but even those who have received the energy of the
Spirit and slipped, through accurate repentance to lead them back to the
ancient state. But it is not the season for you to hear the discourses about
repentance; or rather may there never be a season for you to fall into need of
those drugs, but may you always remain keeping unimpaired the beauty and the
brightness which you are about to receive now. In order then that you may
always remain, come, let us also converse with you a little about conduct; for
in this wrestling-school the falls are without danger for the athletes; for
the wrestling is toward one's own, and they practice all things on the bodies
of the teachers. But when the season of the contests arrives, when the stadium
is opened, when the theater is seated above, when the president of the games
is present, then it is necessary either, having been careless, to fall and
withdraw with much shame, or, having been eager, to obtain the crowns and the
prizes. So also in your case, these thirty days resemble some wrestling-school
and gymnasia and practice. Let us learn already from here to prevail over that
wicked demon; for against him we are about to strip after baptism, against him
to box and fight. Let us therefore learn already from here his grips, whence
he is wicked, whence he can easily abuse us, so that when the contests arrive
we may not feel strange nor be disturbed, seeing the wrestling-holds as new,
but having already practiced among ourselves, and having learned all his
methods, we may with confidence take hold of the wrestling against him. From
every side then he is accustomed to abuse us, but especially through the
tongue and through the mouth. For nothing is so fitting an instrument for him
toward our deceit and destruction as an unbridled tongue and an unfenced
mouth. From here come our many falls, from here our hard charges. And someone
making clear the ease of the slips through the tongue said: Many have fallen
by the sword, but not so many as by the tongue. And showing again the hardness
of the fall the same one said: A slip from the ground rather than a slip from
a tongue. And what he says is such: It is better, he says, to fall and crush
the body, than to let forth such a word as destroys our soul. And he does not
only speak of the falls, but also exhorts to take much care, so as not to be
tripped, saying thus: Make for your mouth a door and bolts; not that we should
construct doors and bolts, but that with much safety we should shut the tongue
against the unfitting of words. And again elsewhere showing that with our
eagerness, and before ours, we need the impulse from above, so as to hold this
beast inside, having stretched out his hands to God the prophet said: The
lifting of my hands is an evening sacrifice. Set, Lord, a watch for my mouth,
and a door of enclosure upon my lips. And he who exhorted the former things,
again he too says: Who will give upon my mouth a watch, and upon my lips
=49.229= a cunning seal? Do you see each one fearing these falls, and
lamenting and advising, and praying that the tongue enjoy much guarding? And
for what reason, he says, if this organ brings us so great destruction, did
God put it in us from the beginning? Because it also has much benefit; and if
we are eager, it has benefit only, and no destruction. Hear at any rate what
he says who said those former things: In the hand of a tongue are life and
death. And Christ also makes the same things clear saying: From your words you
will be condemned, and from your words you will be justified. For the tongue
stands in the middle of the use toward either; you are lord. So also the sword
lies in the middle; and if against the enemies


you use it, the instrument has become saving for you; but if you thrust the
blow against yourself, not the nature of iron, but your lawlessness becomes
the cause of the slaughter. So also let us reckon about the tongue; it is a
sword lying in the middle; sharpen it then against the accusation of your own
sins; do not thrust the blow against a brother. For this reason God surrounded
it with a double wall, with the barrier of the teeth and the enclosure of the
lips, so that it may not easily and without consideration bring forth the
words that are not fitting. Bridle it inside. But it does not endure? then
also chasten it through the teeth, as having given over its body to these as
executioners, and having made it bite; for it is better for it to be bitten by
the teeth now when it sins, than then seeking a drop of water and being fried
not to obtain the consolation. Many other things too then it is accustomed to
sin, reviling, blaspheming, sending forth shameful words, slandering,
swearing, swearing falsely.
εʹ. But lest saying all things at once today we bury your mind, we set down
for you for now one law, that concerning the guarding of oaths, saying
beforehand and declaring this, that if you do not flee oaths, I do not say
false oaths only, but if you do not flee even those that come about upon the
just, we will no longer converse with you about another subject. For it is
also absurd that the teachers of letters not give a second lesson to the
children, until they see the former well fixed in their memory; but that we,
not being able to recite the former lessons with accuracy, should throw in
others again beforehand; for this is nothing other than drawing into a pierced
jar. In order then that you may not stop our mouth, make much eagerness about
the matter. For this sin is also hard, and exceedingly hard; and it is
exceedingly hard because it does not seem to be hard, and for this reason I
fear it, since no one fears it; for this reason the disease is incurable,
since it does not even seem to be a disease; but as simply to converse is not
a charge, so this too does not seem to be a charge, but this lawlessness is
dared with much boldness; and if someone accuses, immediately laughter follows
and much mockery, not of those accused about the oaths, but of those wishing
to correct the disease. For this reason I stretch out a long discourse about
these things; for I wish to pull up a deep root, and to wipe out a chronic
evil; I do not say the false oaths only, but even the true oaths themselves.
But so-and-so, he says, is a reasonable man, belonging to the priesthood,
living with much sobriety and piety. Do not tell me this reasonable man, the
sober, the pious, the one belonging to the priesthood; but, if you wish, set
=49.230= that this is Peter or Paul, or even an angel come down from heaven;
for not even so do I turn toward the worth of the persons. For I read a law
not slavish but royal, that concerning oaths; and when a king's letters are
read, let all the dignity of the slaves be silent. For if you have to say that
Christ commanded to swear, or that Christ does not punish this when it
happens, show it and I am persuaded; but if he fences it off with such
eagerness, and takes such care about the matter, as to set the one who swears
with the Evil One For what is more than these, the yes, and the no, he says,
is of the devil, why do you bring so-and-so and so-and-so into the midst for
me? For God will not bring you this vote from the carelessness of
fellow-servants, but from the command of his own laws. He commanded, he says,
it was necessary to obey, and not to put forward so-and-so, nor to busy
oneself with others' evils. Since the great David sinned a hard sin; is
sinning therefore without danger for us because of this, tell me? For this
reason then it is necessary to make this secure, and to emulate only the
achievements of the saints; but if somewhere there is carelessness and
transgression of law, it is necessary to flee with much eagerness. For our
account is not toward fellow-servants, but toward the Master, and to him we
will give the accounts of things lived


all. Let us therefore prepare ourselves for that court; for even if ten
thousand times the one transgressing this law is admirable and great, he will
altogether give the punishment laid down upon the transgression; for God is
not a respecter of persons. How then is it possible and in what manner to flee
the sin? for it is necessary not only to show the charge hard, but also to
advise how we might be released. You have a wife, you have a servant, you have
children, and a friend, and a kinsman, and a neighbor? Entrust to all these
the watch upon these things. Custom is hard, and terrible to trip, and hard to
guard against, and often throws us even unwilling and not knowing? Therefore
as much as you know the power of custom, so much be eager to be released from
the wicked custom, and transfer yourself toward the other, the most useful.
For as now that one has often had strength to trip you even when you are eager
and guarding and anxious and taking thought, so if you transfer yourself into
the good custom of not swearing, you will never be able, not even unwilling,
nor being careless, to fall toward the falls of oaths; for custom is truly
great, and has the power of nature. In order then that we may not toil
continually, let us transfer ourselves into another custom, and ask this favor
of each of those who associate with you and are with you, that he advise and
exhort to flee oaths, and when you are caught, convict you. For the watch that
comes about toward you from them is also for them advice and exhortation of
the achievement. For he who accuses another about oaths will not easily
himself fall into this pit; for much-swearing is no ordinary pit, not only
when it happens about the least things, but also when about greatest matters.
But we even buying vegetables and quarreling over two obols, and being angry
at servants and threatening, call God everywhere as witness; and a free man
who has partaken of some bare dignity you would not have dared to call as
witness about such things in the marketplace, but even if you attempt it, you
will pay the penalty of the outrage; but the king of the heavens, the Master
of the angels. even about wares, and about money, and conversing about
=49.231= chance things, you drag into witness; and how are these things
bearable? Whence then might we be released from this wicked custom? Having set
around the guards whom I said, having defined for ourselves a stated time
toward correction, and having imposed a condemnation, if when the time has
passed we have not achieved this. How much time then will suffice us for this?
I do not think those who are exceedingly sober and aroused and watching over
their own salvation will need more than ten days, so as to be completely
released from the wicked custom of oaths. And if after the ten days we should
be seen swearing, let us impose a penalty on ourselves, and let us define a
greatest punishment and condemnation of the transgression. What then is the
con49.232demnation? I no longer define this, but I leave you yourselves to be
lords of the vote. Thus let us manage the things concerning ourselves, not
only upon oaths, but also upon the other failings, and defining for ourselves
a time with most hard punishments, if ever we fall into these, we shall go
away clean toward our Master, and we shall be released from the Gehenna of
fire, and we shall stand with boldness before the judgment-seat of Christ;
which may it be that we all obtain, by the grace and loving-kindness of our
Lord Jesus Christ, with whom to the Father be the glory together with the Holy
Spirit, unto the ages of ages. Amen.
=49.231= SECOND CATECHESIS.
To those about to be illumined; and concerning women who adorn themselves
in plaits and gold; and concerning those


who have used omens, or amulets, or incantations, all of which are alien to
Christianity.
αʹ. I came first to demand of you the fruits of the things said the other day
toward your love; for we do not speak for this, that you may only hear, but
that you may also remember the things said and furnish us the demonstration
through the works; or rather not us, but God who knows the unspeakable things
of the mind. For this reason it is also called catechesis, that even when we
are absent the word may sound in your minds. And do not wonder, if when only
ten days have passed we came to demand the fruits of the seeds; for even in
one day it is possible at once both to cast down seeds and to make a harvest;
for not by our own power only, but also being strengthened by the impulse from
God, thus we are called to the contests. As many then as received the things
said, and fulfilled them through the works, let them remain stretching forward
toward the things in front; but as many as have not yet touched this good
working, let them touch it from here, that they may also beat back the
condemnation from carelessness by the eagerness after these things. For it is
possible, it is possible even for the one who has been exceedingly careless,
having used eagerness afterward, to recover all the loss of the time that has
passed. Therefore he says, Today if you hear his voice, do not harden your
hearts, as in the provocation. And he says this exhorting and advising, never
to despair, but as long as we are here, to have good hopes, and to lay hold of
the things in front, and to pursue toward the prize of the upward calling of
God. Let us then do this, and let us examine the names of this great gift. For
as the magnitude of a dignity being unknown makes those honored more careless,
so being recognized it renders them grateful and works them more eager. And
otherwise it would be shameful and ridiculous for those enjoying such glory
from God and honor, not even to know what the names of it wish to indicate.
And why do I speak about this gift? for if you think of the common name of our
race, you will receive a greatest teaching and exhortation toward virtue. For
this name man =49.232= we do not define according to those outside, but as the
divine Scripture commanded. For a man is not whoever simply has hands and feet
of a man, nor whoever is only rational, but whoever practices piety and virtue
with boldness. Hear at any rate what he says about Job; for having said that
There was a man in the land of Ausitis, he does not sketch him from these
definitions from which those outside do, nor does he say this, that he had two
feet and broad nails, but putting together the marks of that piety he said,
Just, true, God-fearing, abstaining from every wicked deed, showing that this
is a man. As therefore another also says: Fear God, and keep his commandments,
because this is every man. And if the name man furnishes such exhortation
toward virtue, much more the name faithful? For you are called faithful for
this, because you both believe God, and you are entrusted by him yourself with
righteousness. holiness, purity of soul, adoption, kingdom of heavens; he
entrusted and deposited these things too with you; you again entrusted and
deposited others with him, almsgiving, prayers, sobriety, all the rest of
virtue. And why do I say almsgiving? even if you give him a cup of cold, you
will not lose even this, but he also guards this with accuracy unto that day,
and will give it back with much abundance; for this indeed is the wonderful
thing, that he does not only guard the deposits, but also makes them abound
with the recompenses. This he also commanded you to do according to your
power, in the things with which you were entrusted, to increase the holiness
which you received, and to work the righteousness from


the bath more splendid, and to make the gift more bright; as then Paul also
did, having increased by the toils after these things and by the eagerness and

and the drug become a wound for us. For as much greater the grace has become,
so much more is the punishment for those who sin after these things.
γʹ. In order then that we may not return toward the former vomit, let us train

and having stood off from the former evils, thus to approach the grace, hear
what John says, and what the chief of the apostles says to those about to be

which he repented; for this reason we are also commanded to say: I renounce
you, Satan, that we may no longer return toward him. =49.235= As then it
happens with the painters, so let it also happen now; for they too, having set
forth the boards, and drawing white lines, and sketching the royal images,
before they bring on the truth of the colors, with all authority they both
wipe out some things and write others instead, both correcting the things that
have been missed and transposing the things that are badly; but whenever they
finally bring on the dye, they are no longer lords again to wipe out and write
instead, since they damage the beauty of the image, and the matter becomes a
charge; do this then also you; consider the soul to be an image for you.
Before then the true dye of the Spirit comes upon it, wipe out the customs
badly put into you; whether to swear, or to lie, or to insult, or to speak
shamefully, or to make laughter, or you have a custom of doing some other of
such things not approved, take out the custom, so that you may not return
toward it again after baptism. The sins the


bath makes vanish, correct the custom yourself, so that when the colors have
been brought on and the royal image has shone, you may no longer thereafter
wipe out, and make wounds and scars in the beauty given you by God. Check then
anger, quench wrath; and if someone wrongs you, even if he insults, weep for
that one; do not be indignant yourself, suffer with him, do not be provoked,
nor say that I have been wronged unto the soul; there is no one being wronged
unto the soul, unless we wrong ourselves unto the soul; and how, I say: Did
someone take away your substance? he did not wrong unto the soul, but unto
money; but if you bear malice, you wronged yourself unto the soul. For the
money taken away harmed nothing, but even benefited, but you, not having let
go the anger, will there give accounts of this remembering of evil. Did
someone revile and insult? he wronged nothing unto the soul, nor even unto the
body. Did you revile in return and insult? you wronged yourself unto the soul,
being about to undergo accounts there of the words which you said. And this I
wish you to know most of all things, that no one can wrong a Christian and
faithful unto the soul, not even the devil himself. And this is not only the
wonderful thing, that God made us unconquerable to all the plots, but that he
also constructed us as fit toward the working of virtue, and there is no
hindrance, if we wish, even if we are poor, even if weak in the body, even if
cast aside, even if nameless, even if servants. For neither poverty, nor
sickness, nor maiming of body, nor slavery, nor any other of such things would
ever become an obstacle toward virtue. And why do I say a poor man and a
servant and nameless? even if you are a prisoner, not even this becomes an
obstacle for you toward virtue; and how, I say: Did someone of those within
grieve you and provoke you? let go the anger for him; have the bonds and the
poverty and the being without honor become an obstacle for you toward this at
all? And why do I say obstacle? they even helped, then, and cooperated toward
checking the pride. Did you see another prospering? do not envy; for neither
here does poverty become a hindrance. Again, when it is necessary to pray, do
this with a sober and =49.236= watching mind, and nothing will be what hinders
even here. Show all gentleness, reasonableness, sobriety, gravity; for these
too do not need the helps from outside. And this especially is the greatest
thing of virtue, that it has need not of wealth, not of power, not of glory,
not of any other of such things, but of a soul only that has been sanctified,
and it seeks nothing more. And see this same thing happening also upon the
grace; for even if someone is lame, even if he has had his eyes cut out, even
if maimed in the body, even if having fallen into extreme sickness, the grace
is hindered by none of these from coming on; for it seeks a soul only
receiving it with eagerness, and it passes by all these outside things. For
with the soldiers outside, both a measure of body and a good condition of
flesh those about to enroll them into the camp seek; and the one about to
serve as a soldier must have not only these, but also be free; for if someone
is a slave, he is cast out; but the king of the heavens seeks nothing such,
but even receives slaves into his own camp, and those grown old and slack in
the members, and he is not ashamed. What is more loving-kind than this? what
could be more good? For this one seeks the things in our power only, but those
the things not in our power. For to be a slave or free is not ours; again to
be tall and short is not in our power, to be grown old and sound, and as many
such things; but to be reasonable or good and the things like these, of our
judgment. And these things alone God demands of us, of which we are lords; and
very reasonably; for not through his own need, but through benefaction he
calls us upon his own grace, but the kings through their own service; and
those drag to a perceptible war, but this one to a spiritual battle. And not
only upon the wars outside, but also upon the contests one would see the same
analogy; for those about to be dragged into that theater do not first go down
into


the contests, until the herald taking them leads them around under the eyes of

become a hindrance? For it is not by laying down silver that one can

than clay; for the honor of the material is not from the nature, but from our
mind. For if someone examined accurately, iron is much more necessary than
gold; for the one brings no use into the life, but this constructed the
greater part of our use serving countless crafts. And why do I speak of a
comparison of gold and iron? for these stones are more necessary than the
costly stones; for from those nothing useful would come,

and gravity. So that even if someone is well-formed, but the husband is
