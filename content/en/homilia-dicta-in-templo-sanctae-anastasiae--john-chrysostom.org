HOMILY VII.

=63.493=

Homily spoken in the temple of Saint Anastasia.

αʹ. Short indeed is the gathering of those present, but great is the longing;
therefore the gathering is not short either. For we do not seek multitudes of
bodies, but a prepared mind, a winged understanding, a hearer becoming higher
than all the things of this life; and if there be even one such, he is
sufficient to suffice the speaker; since even the Samaritan woman was both
poor and unclean and of another race, and yet she was judged worthy of those
long discourses by the Master of the inhabited world; and he often passed by
Jewish crowds, either not conversing, or overshadowing the conversation; but
to a barbarian woman, who had had five husbands, and was living unlawfully
with a sixth, he conversed alone, having sent the disciples on purpose to the
marketplace, so that he might not frighten the catch. So great is the
providence of the Master of the inhabited world even for one soul, if only he
find them displaying a mind and eagerness suited to the hearing of what is
said; which that woman then also furnished.

From a harlot, at any rate, she became an evangelist, after those rebukes
crying out and saying: Come, see a man who told me all things that I did; is
not this the Christ? He paraded her life, and brought the sins into the midst,
saying: For you have had five husbands, and he whom you now have is not your
husband. But the rebuke did not strike her, but rather drew her toward
intimacy. For such are noble souls: by the things at which others take
offense, by these they are corrected. For if she had been some other
[courtesan] of the ungrateful, she would have been struck by the rebukes,
would have sprung away, would have been stirred to anger and indignation; but
she, after the accusation, rather becomes intimate with the teacher, and asks
him about greater contemplations; for she said, I perceive that you are a
prophet; and having learned him from the knowledge of her own evils she
marvels the more and inquires. These things were of a soul wishing to
philosophize. For she asks nothing about a worldly matter, nor about money,
nor about health of body, nor about deliverance from poverty, though living
with extreme beggary, but about the sacred laws, and about the citizenship of
ancestors, and the worship of fathers, saying: Our fathers worshipped on this
mountain, and how do you say that in Jerusalem is the place where one must
worship? And in the opening too she does this same thing, saying: How do you
ask to drink from me, being a Samari =63.494= tan woman? so concerned a soul
she had, and suited to be saved. For this very reason also he who knows the
secrets of the mind, finding rich soil, cast the seeds with a lavish hand,
raising her up little by little. All these things, then, have been said by me,
so that you may learn that our gathering is not short. For if there one woman
became a sufficient theater, much more we, seeing so many men and so many
women with such eagerness, will not shrink back, but will use the accustomed
teaching.

For if the Master of the angels, at whom the Cherubim shudder, did not refuse
to converse with a harlot woman alone, what defense would we have, and what
pardon, passing by so great a gathering? Come then, let us again prepare for
you the accustomed table, and set the mixing-bowl, and pour out the unmixed
wine, making a spiritual banquet. For these meats do not burst a belly, but
wall a mind; this wine does not make the


hearer beside himself, but even makes the drunken one temperate. Such is the
nature of this table, which can also suffice us in place of full armor. For we
need weapons too, since there is a war for us every day, not against fellow
men, but against invisible powers, against the phalanxes of the demons, more
barbarous than countless enemies, against an implacable tyrant, fighting us
without herald, and neither announcing beforehand the time of the war, nor
being seen, but striking from the unseen. Depicting this war, then, the
blessed Paul, the general of the inhabited world, cried out saying: Finally,
brethren, be strong in the Lord, and in the might of his strength; and again
elsewhere, For our wrestling is not against blood and flesh, but against the
principalities, against the powers, against the world-rulers of the darkness
of this age.

βʹ. Did you see how he anoints the minds of the soldiers? how he raises the
understanding? how he arms the camp, both cutting out sloth and casting out
hesitation? For since these two things especially betray the soldiers in wars,
both to betray courage by cowardice, and, having collapsed, to increase sloth,
and to give the enemies leave to attack them unguarded; for neither would he
who fears beyond measure become useful in war, becoming easy to capture
through the passion, nor again could he who is wholly rid of fear get the
better of the enemies, by being too confident, from =63.495= the fearlessness
of the soul loosening the preparation; correcting both these things, Paul both
makes them ready for the contest, so that they may not be slothful, depicting
the phalanx of the enemies, and again prepares them to be confident, so that
they may not fall, bringing into the midst the power of Christ who generals in
such wars. Therefore, just as an excellent general he knits the phalanx,
banishing from the faithful the passions that ruin the courage of the mind,
and displaying his own love, and joining those who have stood far from him in
the exactness of their way of life by the name of kinship. For Finally, he
says, my brethren. For this man displays far greater affection toward all than
those who loosed the same birth-pangs for us, and he carried the inhabited
world about in his soul; so great was the breadth of his love; and not in
security only, but also in dangers.

For even dwelling in prisons, and expecting death, and being in danger
concerning the last things, he cared for the disciples, and often wrote the
letters from prisons with the hand that had the chain about it, with the right
hand that was bound; and entering a court, and about to give account, and to
be led to death, as far as it concerned the judges, having let go all those
things, the fear, the danger, the threats, the deaths, the punishments, the
chastisements, the executioners, the wrath of the rulers, the devices of the
plotters, the scourges, he remembered the faithful even at that time; so was
his soul released from the bonds of the body, so, even while living in the
flesh, it clung to the heavenly vault, and as having shifted mooring toward
the lot there, so he did all things even while spending time on earth. And
that you may learn that the words are not of exaggeration, nor the things said
of flattery, hear him saying this: As it is right for me to think this about
you, because I have you in my heart. But what has been said is not yet great
compared to what will be said; for this too is great, but what comes after is
far greater; for having said, Because I have you in my heart, he added, Both
in my bonds, and in the defense and confirmation of the Gospel. Do you see how
he never cast them out of his mind? For if bonds and courts and a chain did
not ruin the memory, much more the times of ease. Because I have you in my
heart, he says.




Do you see the word growing little by little? Great is it even to have them in
the heart, but far greater to have them even in the chains; and greater still
is it even in the defense and confirmation of the gospel. For here he seems to
me to hint at the time at which he was being led in before the judges,
undergoing the danger concerning the last things. And there, he says,
standing, I was not looking at this, how I might be delivered from the dangers
lying upon me, nor how I shall beat off the plots; but luxuriating [was] in
your love, and conversing with you though absent; and neither length of road,
nor crowd of affairs, nor magnitude of dangers, nor fear of rulers, nor
uprising of peoples, nor death in view, nor swords being bared, nor crowds of
executioners, nor any other of such =63.496= things took me away from your
memory. For nothing is more tyrannical than love, nothing higher than it; it
flies above all these darts, and is higher than the reeds of the devil,
surveying all things from the summit of the heavens; and just as a violent
rush of wind falling upon it drives off troublesome dust, so also the strength
of love is wont to drive off the assault of all the passions. Which indeed
also happened in Paul's case; for he had as sufficient consolation in all
things the salvation of those who were loved, the memory. And what is,
Confirmation of the gospel? For even if the expression is one, yet it has a
vast sea of thoughts; and I shall try to unfold this, and to examine it from
every side. For the word of God is a pearl, shining through all things, not in
the multitude of words, but in brevity displaying a great power of words
[of thoughts?]. But wake up, and you will see how great a treasure the power
of this expression uncovers for us.

γʹ. What then is confirmation of the gospel, and what sort of confirmation of
the gospel does he call it, and for what reason does he mention this
expression, having recalled courts and prisons and a chain? When the preaching
was sown (for it is necessary to bring the account further back, so as to make
the teaching clearer), everything was filled with many tumults, and all things
were full of confusion. For since they, being only eleven, were arrayed
against the inhabited world, prying up an ancient custom, dissolving a
long-standing error, overturning laws of fathers and grandfathers and
ancestors, shaking and pulling down the ancestral customs of the cities, and
speaking in opposition to all, philosophers, rhetors, rulers, judges, tyrants,
peoples, slaves, free men, tillers of the ground, sailors, a hard war was
kindled, and, as I said before, all things were full of tumults; everywhere
precipices, everywhere reefs, and not so does a raging sea, cut through by
contrary winds, become disturbed, as the whole inhabited world then was moved,
the old customs being torn up that had been fixed in so great a time, and not
in one and two and three cities, but everywhere in the inhabited world, and
new doctrines being introduced, of which no one had ever heard before.

From this there was an unheralded war of all against them; for they also
divided houses, and tore asunder kinships; for the nature of the preaching,
going its way, and making many its own, set wars for those who did not receive
the word of piety, and a father disowned a son, and a husband despised a wife,
and masters warred against household slaves, and rulers against the ruled, and
this was more bitter than any civil war; if indeed one must call it a war, and
not something else heavier than this. For in wars the things of the array are
equal, and each of the camps both strikes and is struck; but then it was not
so, but the one side warred with authority, and the others were only warred
upon, and they ought not [were not permitted] to strike, nor to defend
themselves against the plotters; for so the general of the array commanded,
saying: I send you as sheep in the midst of wolves; become therefore wise as
the serpents,


and harmless as the doves. And he commanded them not only not to go out
against the plotters, but even to furnish them to revel in the outrage,
saying: for =63.497= to turn the right cheek, and to send the sheep in the
midst of wolves, hints at nothing else than that he allotted to them to
suffer, so that the trophy might become more splendid. How? Because being
eleven they got the better of the inhabited world, because suffering, not
doing, being struck and not striking, being plotted against and not plotting,
being scourged and not scourging, being driven and not driving, being
persecuted and not persecuting, being killed and not killing, and because,
like sheep set forth for slaughter, they changed all the wolves to the
gentleness of sheep, those who were raging, those breathing murders, those
becoming wilder than beasts.

As the word then ran and piety was being sown, from every side fires and
hatreds and wars were kindled, not for the teachers only, but also for those
being taught. For as soon as someone received the word, he went about as a
common enemy to all, and was driven from his fatherland, and was transplanted
to exile, and fell from his property, and was in danger concerning freedom
itself, or rather concerning life itself; and the tyranny of nature had no
power then, but, as I said before, even children were despised, and brothers
and kinsmen were in the rank of enemies, and with the teachers the disciples
suffered the incurable things. And making these very things clear Paul said:
For remember the former days, in which, having been enlightened, you endured a
great contest of sufferings, this indeed being made a spectacle by reproaches
and afflictions, and this having become partners of those so treated. For you
also suffered with the prisoners, and you accepted with joy the plunder of
your goods, knowing that you have a better possession in the heavens and one
that remains. And again writing to the Thessalonians he said: For you became
imitators, brethren, of the Churches that are in Judaea, because you also
suffered the same things from your own countrymen, just as they also did from
the Jews, who both killed the Lord, and drove us out, and are contrary to all
men. And writing again to the Galatians he said: Did you suffer so many things
in vain, if indeed it was even in vain. And narrating his own sufferings, he
said: In much endurance, in distress, in prisons, in labors, in watchings, in
hunger, in thirst and nakedness; and again, Forty save one I received, three
times I was beaten with rods, once I was stoned, a night and a day I have
spent in the deep, in dangers of rivers, in dangers of robbers, in dangers
from my own race, in dangers in wildernesses; and again, My daily conspiracy,
the care of all the Churches; and again, The ethnarch of Aretas the king
guarded the city of the Damascenes wanting to seize me, and through a window I
was let down in a basket through the wall, and I escaped his hands; and again
elsewhere: We were accounted as sheep of slaughter; and in another place
again, May the Lord grant mercy to the house of Onesiphorus, because he was
not ashamed of my chain, but having come to be in Rome he sought me more
eagerly and found me. δʹ. What would one say of the things of the false
apostles, the things of the false brothers, the countless and many-formed
wars? for not only were the punishments and the chastisements being moved, but
also the clevernesses of rhetors, and the constructions of philosophers; and
not from those only, but also from men of the marketplace many devices were
raised against the Chur =63.498= ch; which hinting at he said: Alexander the
coppersmith showed me many evils; whom you also beware of; for he has greatly
withstood our words. As therefore the sea is not without waves; so also Paul's
soul was filled with countless trials, daily dangers, from without, from
within, through words, through deeds, through money, through deceits; and no
word will be able to set forth the clouds of those darts, the multitudes of
the waves.




Since then the affairs were in these things, not [only] those mentioned, but
also far more, and not teachers only, but also disciples were in danger, and
not disciples only, but also teachers (for if one part were in trial, and the
rest were in security, this was going to become a consolation to that; but now
both were in being warred upon and being struck); and seeing these things many
of the weaker were dissolved, they grew numb, they continued being at a loss,
seeing the good things of the preaching in hopes, a kingdom and resurrection
and incorruption, but the painful things here in experience and endurance,
frying-pans, furnaces, prisons, wars, enmities, hatreds, deaths, dangers, and
their teachers now in prisons, now in leadings-away [read: synagogues], being
insulted, being led away; and from every side there were clouds of evils, and
they had a deep night of trials, which held all and made them be greatly
tumultuous and disturbed; see how through one expression he raises up their
mind. For he does this also from another place, saying in another letter:
Therefore straighten the hanging-down hands and the paralyzed knees; not
speaking about knees and hands, but about reasonings that are lame, being
dissolved by the multitude of the trials. Then having exhorted, he also
introduces a greatest consolation saying: For yet a little, how very little,
he who is coming will come, and will not delay. But since this was still in
hopes, he again exhorts them in another way also, making them more cheerful
not from others' examples but from their own: For remember, he says, the
former days, in which, having been enlightened, you endured a great contest of
sufferings. Be ashamed of yourselves, reverence your own achievements, do not
display an end unworthy of the beginnings, receive the consolation from home.
Having then consoled both in this way and in that, he consoles also through
this expression. How and in what manner? Calling the bonds and the chain a
confirmation of the Gospel. For not only, he says, do the dead rising, nor
lepers being cleansed, nor demons being driven out, but we also being bound
confirm the Gospel. How, tell me, and in what manner? for the things said are
new, and much of it resembles a riddle. Hear then now how: if we preached
without fear, suffering nothing unpleasant or terrible, the preaching would
have seemed suspect even to many of those who wished to slander us; but now
being driven, being persecuted, being cut, being burned, being thrown down
precipices, suffering countless things and not giving in, but becoming more
eager by this, we furnish even to those who wish to be exceedingly shameless a
sufficient proof that we are heralds of truth, and that there is a certain
divine power in us, making all these things easy, =63.499= and not allowing
the multitude of the trials to get the better of those who preach, and this
though they are few, to prevail through so many hindrances. If then anyone
should wish to take a clear proof that the power sounding in us is divine, let
him take the trials, the dangers, the chains, the prisons. For it is not of
human power to prevail through so many precipices, to sail through so many
waves, to pass through so many darts, but these things are of a divine and
unconquerable strength. So that the chain is a confirmation of the gospel, not
for those only, but also for us who suffer; for it makes us more proven,
stronger, it makes us more despise the plotters. Therefore he also said,
Affliction works endurance, and endurance proof, and proof hope, and hope does
not put to shame.

εʹ. Do you see how affliction confirms the Gospel? For this reason also
elsewhere, when he asked for deliverance from the trials, and came forward
many times about these things, he heard: My grace is sufficient for you; for
my power is perfected in weakness. Weakness here he calls the persecutions,
the trials, the dangers, the plots, the insolences. And what he says is such:
I was able to hinder these things, he says, and to quench


the war, and to hold back the waves, but I did not do it, so that my power
might be shown more greatly. For his power was not so shown, if these things
did not happen, as when they happened, but had no strength. Since we also say
that that man is an excellent helmsman, not the one who can steer the ship on
a calm sea, but the one who, among reefs and waves and storms and winds, saves
those who sail; and a physician, the one who, when countless diseases lie upon
him, snatches away the one who is ill; and a general, the one who, when
enemies have attacked from every side and are striking, is able to set up the
trophy; and a shepherd, the one who, when countless wolves lie upon him and
other plotters, keeps the flock in safety. So then this especially made the
wonder then also, that suffering countless things they prevailed and got the
better of those who were doing these things to them. When at any rate they
threw Peter and John, the son of thunder, and the foundation of the faith,
into prison, consider how they are at a loss when they have been released.
Having brought them, at any rate, they say in the midst of the theater: What
shall we do to these men? The Jews who were reared on prophetic bloods, a
people raging and mad, who dug down the altars, who killed the prophets, who
were brought up together with murders, who became wilder than beasts, who
still dripped from the Master's blood, who crucified; this people, having
taken two unlettered fishermen, private men, obscure, more voiceless than
fishes, of whom the one did not even bear a mere threat of a doorkeeper; these
then having taken, having them in the midst bound, naked, not clothed with
abundance of money, not with strength of bodies, not with cleverness of words,
not =63.500= with power of rhetoric, not with distinction of race, not with
greatness of fatherland, fishermen from fishermen, standing in extreme
poverty, they are at a loss what they shall do with them, and they say: What
shall we do to these men? Do you see how great virtue is? how the trials are a
confirmation of the gospel? And they say to them: Did we not charge you with a
charge not to speak upon this name?

And you wish to bring upon us the blood of this man. If he is a mere man, why
do you fear? and if he is God, for what reason do you not worship? Did you not
cry out the other day saying, His blood be upon us and upon our children? from
where do you fear the blood? what has shaken your mind? did you not bind? did
you not scourge? did you not crucify? did you not see him dead being taken
down from the cross? did you not behold him being buried, and being hidden in
the earth? did you not set seals upon the tomb? did you not buy the soldiers?
did you not spread a rumor, that the disciples stole him? Why do you fear now?
why do you tremble at his blood? Do you see the truth shining through all
things? For when they saw after that plot the matter being raised, and the
beginnings bright and clearer than the sun and from the very starting-line
displaying that it will all at once take the whole inhabited world, and will
drive error utterly, and that it will not even be able to bear its unspeakable
rush and power; they have feared, they tremble at the prisoners, the
condemned, the scourged, those plotted against, the two, the private men. For
these reasons Paul calls the trials a confirmation of the gospel; for these
reasons also elsewhere he says: And I want you to know, brethren, that the
things according to me have come rather to the progress of the Gospel; so that
the more of the brethren, having become confident in the Lord by my bonds,
dare more abundantly to speak the word without fear. Who has seen, who has
heard, that bonds make courage, and a chain works boldness of speech, not for
the one bound only, but also for those being taught? But nevertheless, when
these things were happening, through the opposites the preaching was being
raised, and the disciples, while the teacher was being warred upon and plotted
against and bound, were more confident, leaped more, sprang more.

Have you praised the things said? Then let us imitate the courage, let us
emulate the virtue, and let nothing of the trials frighten us, let nothing
throw us into tumult; for these are


bonds of piety, these are wings of philosophy, these make men strong and
unconquerable, these give more boldness of speech toward God, they draw
greater goodwill, they make the grace pitch its tent greatly. So that we may
enjoy all these things, let us bear, I beg, all the things that fall upon us,
glorifying God through all things, because to him glory, honor and might are
fitting, together with his only-begotten Son and the Holy Spirit, now and
always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
