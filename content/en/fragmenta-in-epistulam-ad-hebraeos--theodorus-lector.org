0 Hebr Prolog As he was writing not to unbelievers who had acquired an
implacable hatred toward him, but to those who had shared in all the things
that must belong to those who believe; then not simply to chance persons, but
indeed to those who had displayed confirmation in the works of faith and
exactness of virtue, as what is set in the epistle also shows. So that the
epistle also had to be delivered to them as of Paul having written to them,
just as indeed it also came to be in the case of the rest; for the things
written could not even benefit them unless this came to be. And besides these
things, what is written toward the end of the epistle also shows what I say:
for I exhort you, he says, brethren, to bear with the word of exhortation; for
I have written to you in few words. But to whom was he writing that "I have
written to you," if those were not the ones to whom it was necessary that the
letter be delivered? Then he adds: know that the brother Timothy has been
released, with whom, if he comes sooner, I shall see you. You see that the one
who also served the epistle of the letter was evident, with whom he clearly
promises that he will also see them if he should return sooner. What then is
the cause of this <of his name not having been prefixed>? Quite evident and
exceedingly clear. Barnabas and he, together with those around the blessed
Peter, divided the preaching, not in order that these should teach some things
and those others; for one was their aim 201 but in order that these should
lead those from the Gentiles to the faith, and those the ones from the Jews,
because of the rivalry still prevailing then from the custom in the law of the
Jews, who would not endure mixing with those from the Gentiles, having used
this division more by economy. So then these were styled apostles of the
Gentiles, and those of those from the circumcision; but those who had
believed, as is likely, thought them to be common teachers and apostles. So
when he was writing to those from the Gentiles, reasonably as their apostle he
sends to them; but when toward those from the Hebrews, no longer.
Hebr 1:2-3a Through whom he also made the ages. The age is no nature having
being in hypostasis, but is understood to be a certain interval. So it is
conceived from something that has a beginning of being until that one's end,
or of some other that has likewise received a beginning; for we do not
understand temporal intervals in their own hypostasis, but we conceive them
together with those who have received a beginning of being, receiving from
those the impression of both its beginning and its end. Not as when we say of
God that he merely "was," who since he always was, it is not possible for us
to conceive intervals attending upon his life. Therefore to say maker of the
ages is nothing other than to say eternal, lying above every interval, having
his own existence unbounded. For the maker is prior to the things made, and
for that which has a beginning an interval must necessarily be conceived
together. And just as when the blessed David says: he who exists before the
ages, he does not wish to say this, that he preexists things that do not
subsist, but in order to say that he is above every interval, always having
being. So, when he says: through whom he also made the ages, he does not wish
to call him maker of things that do not subsist, but as always being and cause
of all things that have received a beginning. Who being the radiance of the
glory and the impress of his hypostasis. Well he did not say "of God" but "of
the glory," by the striking quality of the name not even permitting us to busy
ourselves about the things of that nature, as being owed to be glorified only
by us; using the comparison of the radiance for what he especially thought
necessary, but by the addition dissolving the incongruity of the comparison;
for he preserves, he says, the exact impress of the nature, so that whatever
you may understand that hypostasis to be, understand this one also to be this,
bearing the exact impresses of that one by differing from it in nothing.
2 Hebr 1:3b That is, not only to call him maker of the things that are, but
indeed also that he makes them with a great abundance of power, as though all
things were suspended upon his voice. For this differs in nothing from him who
calls the things that are not as being, or also from the narrative of the
blessed Moses saying: and he said, let there be light, and there was light;
let there be a firmament, and there was a firmament.
Hebr 1:7-8 The one signifies the swiftness of service, the other the
capability of ministry. And having said that they have come to be these things
from there, he presented that from there they also have the power to do these
things. And it is distinguished toward "who makes" the "God" and "throne" and
"scepter of kingdom," all of which are symbols of the highest honor and
dignity; for "God" signifies the highest nature, and the throne and the
scepter from human things also present the stability of this; then also "unto
the age of the age"; for "who makes" shows that those who have come to be have
begun from a time, but this presents the eternity of the kingdom.
Hebr 1:12 From here one can understand that when the old covenant discourses
to us about the divine nature, it does not speak about the Father alone in a
delimited way, as the heretics think, trying to take "I am God, and there is
no other besides me," and as many such things, upon him alone. But on the
contrary, as many things as it says as about God, setting forth the highest
nature, it speaks thus as being able to fit similarly both to Father and to
Son because of the communion of nature. Since how did the apostle draw the
second testimony upon him, having nothing that could be significative of the
property of the person, just as also the former? Or has he not clearly done
the same also in that to the Romans, where he says: for it is written, as I
live, says the Lord, that to me every knee shall bow? For one would not find
that testimony either having any such thing which indicated these in a
delimited way.
203 Hebr 1:14-2:1 That they are ministering spirits he had from the testimony
set forth, and he himself then joined on the form of the ministry, saying that
they do all things as those who minister for the sake of our salvation; for it
is no small thing toward addition to know the need of the ministry. Therefore
we must give more abundant heed to the things heard, lest we drift away.
Therefore, he says, we must allot zeal to the things spoken by him, guarding
lest we ever receive some turning aside from the better things.
Hebr 2:3-4 He showed also by the facts the great difference of these toward
those, having said there "word," but here "salvation"; since that was a giving
of ordinances only, but here both grace of spirit and release of sins and
promise of the kingdom of heaven and pledge of immortality. Whence also he
justly said "so great," showing by the addition its magnitude, and there given
through the angels, but here through the Lord. And since there were miracles
among those, so that it might not seem to have been lessened even in this, he
well added "God also bearing witness with signs and wonders and various
powers," saying it by way of increase so that in this too the surplus of the
grace compared with the law might appear; for there it came to be toward the
need only, but here many even of the strangers are healed through us, whatever
passions it might happen to hold them. So great among us is the abundance of
the miracles. And the dead also are raised, and we also punish certain ones
often if need be, making them blind by rebukes alone, bringing deaths upon the
ill-disposed. Having then set the difference side by side and shown the
superiority by the variety and the many modes, he added the greater thing
which no longer belonged to those in the law: and distributions of the Holy
Spirit; for each of the believers sharing in the Spirit was proper to those in
the grace. And well upon all he added "according to his will," that is, of God
having once willed and approved to use much munificence concerning us, so that
his grace concerning us is also unchangeable, we suspecting no change of the
things once given, similarly to the former things.
204 Hebr 2:9-10 They suffer something most ridiculous here, exchanging "apart
from God" and making "by the grace of God," not attending to the sequence of
the scripture, but from not understanding what he ever meant by "apart from
God," indifferently wiping out that and setting what seemed to them easy for
understanding. For what lay before Paul here, that he should add "by the grace
of God"? And what sequence was leading him to this? For it is not his custom
to set "by the grace of God" simply, but altogether from some sequence of
argument; for either saying the thing according to himself he adds this, as
"by the grace of God I am what I am," <or> when it lies before him to speak of
God's love of man concerning men, and that he has done all things though we
were not worthy to obtain, such as is "for by grace you have been saved," to
which he adds: and this not from you, it is the gift of God; not from works,
lest anyone boast, clearly presenting through these that he is discoursing
about the grace of God, which he happened to display concerning all men. But
here what lies before him is clearly that he discourses about Christ, of what
sort he is and what difference he has toward the angels, from which he was
carried into saying, in respect of what he seems to have been made less than
them and that it was because of death. What then was the need here of "by the
grace of God"? For it does not lie before him to speak of his goodness
concerning us. And this the sequence of what follows rather shows; for having
said that apart from God he tasted of death, the divinity having been in no
way harmed toward this, and for this reason from his own nature he received
this lessening for a little, so that from here he appears also sharing the
honor because of the conjunction with the other. Since then it was consequent
that those hearing these things think the indwelling of the God Word at the
time of the passion to be superfluous, contributing nothing to the things set
forth if indeed apart from that one he tasted the experience of death, he
adds: for it was fitting for him, for whom are all things and through whom are
all things, in bringing many sons to glory, to perfect the pioneer of their
salvation through sufferings. But neither was the divinity unserviceable, he
says; for the things that were its own were fitting, and whatever was fitting
to it; for the matter of the passion was in no way fitting to it. This "it was
fitting" clearly established the "apart from God"; for as of that not being
fitting he himself says the fitting, showing at once also what things he was
then doing, and what these were. For it was fitting for him, for whom are all
things and through whom are all things; clearly saying this of the God Word,
since he had given a share of the sonship to many and led them into this
glory, to show the pioneer of the salvation of all, our Lord and Savior Jesus
Christ, the man taken by him 205, perfect through the sufferings, so that both
the nature of this one and the grace of that one appear.
Hebr 3:12-14 For neither now, he says, was it fitting to counsel you, that
having stood away from the worse things you might incline toward the better
through faith upon Christ; for my word comes to be toward those already having
believed. So that it was fitting to exhort you the same things, that you might
remain in what once seemed good. This then he says, that those who have
believed and have partaken of the Spirit have become partakers of the
hypostasis of Christ, having received as it were a certain natural communion
toward him. What remains then is to guard this beginning in an unimpaired
purpose.
Hebr 3:16-18 He wishes to show that all who went out through Moses perished
through unbelief, so as from the parallel to frighten more greatly those
toward whom he was making the word. <But those contradicting these paid
attention> neither to the sequence of what follows, of "and with whom was he
vexed?" and "and to whom did he swear?", being read as a question, but
requiring the same answering; nor to that, that "and who having heard
provoked?" could then be said according to the very sense, if indeed few from
many had undergone this. For we say of things being done "who did them?"
cutting off the part from a multitude, when those who have done them happen to
be fewer than those who have not done them. But here two only, Joshua and
Caleb; so that it did not even have any sequence to say who the provokers
were, two only being from so great a multitude. ** thinking the number of the
two to harm the assertion in nothing.
Hebr 4:1-2 It is fitting to fear, he says, lest we ourselves, having received
the promise of the entrance into the rest, fall short by a wretched purpose;
for let no one think the promise of the things to come sufficient for him,
just as it was not for those; for they were not joined according to faith to
the things promised. Whence it is to be read thus: not having been mixed by
faith with the things heard, that he may say, with the promises of God that
had come to be toward them through Moses.
206 Hebr 4:4-7 This is the work of true rest, not to run back again upon the
old things, undergoing change and transfer. For just as God is said to have
rested from making the world, having once given an end to its construction, so
it is fitting also that the one entering into rest not run back upon the old
things, looking askance at the toils of virtue <upon> transgression of
ordinances; for to these change and the removal from the constitution must
necessarily follow. The "today," he says, is neither indefinite, nor indeed
can it be said upon the interval outside a day, as though "today" fitted upon
each day. And this I thought it just to note not only for the sake of the
apostolic clearness, but also of those wishing to take "I have today begotten
you" upon the generation of the only-begotten's divinity, who declare "today"
to be indefinite, not even considering this, that "today" could not be said if
there were no day. For that "today" would not even be called indefinite, the
apostle clearly showed saying: again he defines a certain day in David,
saying, or also elsewhere: exhort yourselves, he said, according to each day,
until "today" is called. First he showed that "today" would not be said
outside the days, by saying "according to each day," and thus adding "until
today is called." Then not even thus does he appear to be discoursing about
"today" as indefinite, and able to fit to the things that have passed, and
able also to the things to come. For what does he say? Thinking "today" to be
said to you according to each day, thus take care to remain upon the faith;
for as "today" signifying the present day, so he counseled them, using the
signification rather toward the benefit of the exhortation.
Hebr 6:18 The "through two unchangeable things," both by the one who promised
being God, in no way able to lie, and by having made the promise with an oath.
207 Hebr 6:20 Adding that Christ has become for us an eternal high priest by
bringing all who believe in each generation through himself to God upon the
hope of the resurrection.
Hebr 7:3 He was relating later how the temple had been constructed, and he
says there are two tents, but into the first the priests entering always
perform the things ordained, and into the second the high priest alone once in
the year. And wishing then to teach what is signified from these he adds: the
Holy Spirit making this clear, that the way of the holies has not yet been
manifested. Just as then it was not the nature of the place that was
inaccessible, upon which it happened that the inner house was constructed, but
the construction and the ordinance and the consecration of the place made the
place inaccessible, yet he said this was a symbol of the heavens, so here too
wishing to show Christ high priest according to the order of Melchizedek, he
speaks the things according to that one not setting forth the nature, but
setting the narrative according to him from the divine scripture and from that
making the likeness appear. He calls him then without father and without
mother, as the divine scripture not relating his parents; whence he added
"without genealogy," showing that he is not speaking the nature of the man but
the narrative of the divine scripture. Then he joins: having neither beginning
of days nor end of life, not in the nature, but in the divine scripture. And
since these things could also be said upon another (for the divine scripture
has mentioned many, having said neither the parents nor set out the genealogy,
and especially as many as we have learned to have come to be outside the
Israelite race) he well added "but made like the Son of God, he remains a
priest continually"; for this no longer, together with the rest, was upon
another. And he makes appear from the narrative according to the scripture how
the rest also belonged to him, which indeed also this. For Christ was without
father by the generation of the human nature, and without mother by the
generation of the divine existence, but indeed also without genealogy; for
what genealogy would there be of the one who is from the Father only? And that
he has neither beginning 208 of days nor end of life, this too is clear, but
here upon the facts, and there upon the narrative. The remaining a priest
continually he took from the divine scripture by saying: you are a priest
forever according to the order of Melchizedek, as though that one also were
ministering as priest continually. And he called him a continual priest as not
having passed the priesthood to successors, which happened to come to be in
the law. Whence he also said: but made like the Son of God, although the
consequent thing was to say this one was made like to that one (for the first
is not made like to the second) but since with this one there is the truth of
the facts, and with that one nothing such is present, but by the form of the
narrative he well said that one was made like, as the divine scripture having
wished to show us upon the narrative a likeness of the one who would be.
Hebr 7:20-22 The difference of this one toward that is shown, he says, from
receiving the priesthood with an oath; for those came to be without oaths
because they also had to cease at some time from the priesthood, but this one
with oaths, since he was going to remain upon the dignity. Thus he received a
dignity much greater than those in the law, since he was also going to furnish
greater things to those who approach. For "surety" he says thus as by having
been appointed guaranteeing the gain to us, since it is also thus; for having
risen first, according as he also calls him high priest, guarantees to us the
likeness of the resurrection.
Hebr 9:1-2 From here he begins to say how the things in the law are symbols
and types of the things in the grace, and how it is possible to discern these
things as contemplated beforehand in those, showing at once by the comparison
also the superiority of these, how great it is. And he makes the beginning of
the teaching in a narrative. He says then that the first covenant also had
bounds and ordinances of a ministry owed to be rendered to God; for
"justifications of worship" he calls the ordinances about these things. 209
And the worldly holy place. He reasonably calls the temple here holy, in which
it happened that the ministry was rendered to God, or also the tent, since
they had this formerly in the rank of the temple; for likewise they called
that a temple as God happening to be in it, and the one later by Solomon. And
this one can learn from the first of Kingdoms clearly saying: and Samuel was
sleeping in the temple of the Lord where the ark of God was, the temple not
yet having been built by Solomon. And just as commanding Moses to construct
the tent as a symbol of the world according to a certain type, God ordered him
to make an enclosure from the veils, divided in the middle by another; so also
the temple later came to be according to the same type.
Hebr 9:28 Now, he says, having appeared, when it happened that sin was
prevailing, he necessarily received the death that was prevailing because of
sin, but then, sin having as is likely been dissolved, it is necessary that he
also appear without passion; for "apart from sin" he says this, that sin no
longer prevailing, thus he himself also will then appear outside every human
passion.
Hebr 10:5 These things have been said by those in Babylon wishing to say to
God that you did not demand sacrifice of me but only to hearken to you and to
do your will; and immediately the things of the return were thereafter
unambiguous for me. Having then changed it as from the person of Christ he
says it thus, saying instead of "ears," "a body you prepared for me."
Hebr 10:26-27 Those who have here transferred toward the worse from the better
must, having risen, be delivered thereafter to punishment, not then admitting
repentance. For he does not take away repentance upon the present life, but by
there being then no receiving of pardon for the one who has remained here upon
stumbling and has received no perception upon the stumbling, accomplishing
every sin with a certain irrationality with much pleasure.
210 Hebr 11:1-2 Knowing the question about faith among the Jews especially
from a certain sequence, he has used many proofs about it. For those put
forward the law and the justification from its works, but these faith, saying
this, that even if one is held in countless evils, having believed only in
Christ he receives a short way of all: the release, being counted worthy of
the justification from him. Whence having defined it, both so as to make it
akin to the things said before, and so as to be able to show it more appearing
among all the virtuous and ancient men, he adds: for in this the elders were
attested.
Hebr 11:19 He obtained correspondingly to his own faith; for having believed
in the resurrection he received him back who had died through certain symbols.
For having come to be in much expectation of death and suffering nothing was a
symbol of the one who would truly rise, inasmuch as having tasted of death for
a little he rose having suffered nothing by death. "In a parable" then instead
of "in symbols."
Hebr 12:1 He did not say simply that there is one time of recompense for all
(for this was not what lay before him) but in order to show that having both
done so many things, and also suffered so many things through faith, they
nevertheless still wait for the payment of the things promised, not being
displeased, if indeed they will receive them with us who are later, which
showed their endurance greater, if indeed even after death they still wait for
those who ought to contend similarly to them so as with those to obtain the
enjoyment of the good things. And of witnesses here he speaks not of those who
have suffered, but of those who bear witness toward the faith.
Hebr 12:12-13 Where are those who say that the apostle in this epistle takes
away repentance through saying: for it is impossible for those who have been
enlightened and the rest to renew again unto repentance? How then do they
understand these things? How does he command the slackened hands and the
paralyzed knees to be set upright? And what sort of healing of the lame 211
does he who does not admit repentance of those who have sinned wish to come to
be? For that he writes these things to the faithful is clear from what he says
toward them: but remember the former days, in which having been enlightened
you endured a great contest of sufferings.
Hebr 12:17 He does not wish through these to take away repentance, but to
teach that it is not possible for those who have <not> displayed the fitting
correction in season to recover it later, when the time of recompense is going
to be brought correspondingly to each one's manner; for he has not been eager
to say things contrary to himself, and these so from nearby. And one would
know from the example what he says. For first he mentioned Esau, who was then
despondent when he did not obtain the blessing, but remained upon the badness
of his manner similarly even after this. Then it is not that having repented
he did not obtain forgiveness of sins; for he was not asking this then, but a
blessing which, having been given to the brother according to the worth of the
manner, it was in no way possible to be taken away again and given to this one
again. Yet one can also find this, that the tears were not entirely
unprofitable for him. At any rate the father appears after this to have been
affected over him, and to have given him certain blessings. Thus he does not
wish through these to take away repentance.
Hebr 12:26 Well from the voice of the prophet he establishes both the change
of the things now and the stability of the later; for to have promised to
shake makes clear their transfer altogether as going to be, but by the
addition of "once" he makes clear the unchangeableness of the things after
this.
Hebr 13:7 Leaders: those among them who proclaimed the word of piety and were
perfected by Jews there. And they were many, not Stephen only and James who
was killed with the sword, but also James the brother of the Lord, and very
many others delivered to silence.
Hebr 13:16 He said well-doing unto praise of what comes to be, and fellowship
because it comes to be concerning those of the same faith, as it being
necessary to consider 212 such as partners of the things present to us, thus
choosing with zeal to suffice for their needs.
Hebr 13:22 Instead of: persist in the reading, bear the toil of attention.
