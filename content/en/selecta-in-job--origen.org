ORIGEN'S SELECTIONS ON JOB.
=12.1032= CHAP. I. "The horsemen made three heads for us and surrounded
the camels." He has made the camels, then, come to be under the horsemen; but
the asses and the yokes of oxen under those who take captives; and he hated
the sheep; therefore he brought fire down upon them; but he also plotted
against the sons, and sought an opportunity to destroy them. If he had found
them reading, he would not have touched the house, nor would he have had a
place to kill them; if he had found them praying, he could have done nothing
against them; but when he found a convenient time, he prevailed. What was the
time? There was drunkenness, there was a drinking-party. And as to the wind
touching the corners, attend: that in the broad places of the corners the
hypocrites stand and pray; and everyone who has not set foot on a straight
road uses the corners; and a broad corner is the much and relaxed wickedness.
CHAP. XIX. "How long will you make my soul weary?" Just as heavy bodies
laid upon the bodies of animals make those that bear them grow weary; so there
are certain heavy words, which when one hears, he grows weary as if pressed by
the weight of the force of the words, spoken reproachfully and harshly, or for
insults; as even now of those spoken by Bildad, Job, as if perceiving a heavy
burden, having grown weary in his soul, says: "How long will you make my soul
weary," and as a heavy load will you weigh down the words against me? You see
that the words of the tyrant of the Shuhites are tyrannical, which Job, having
heard, says the things set forth. For nothing else so reaches a man's soul as
a hard word; for often someone who has endured tortures brought upon the body
has not endured words. The perfect athlete, however, even if growing weary,
yet endures the harsh words; for to endure insult is the mark of a great man;
wherefore among his own brave deeds the Apostle says: "Being reviled we bless;
being blasphemed we entreat." And now it is Job, the noblest of the athletes,
who says these things; whose book contains nothing else especially =12.1033=
than his courage and his endurance; but nevertheless he perceives the toils
which he had toiled from the hard word of those who had come to visit him. And
since the heaviness of the word, like some burden laid upon the soul, prepares
it to grow weary, therefore he says: For what reason, O friends, do you add to
the pains of the body the labor of the soul, pricking more with the words than
the sufferings the body? "You speak against me." Often one speaks against
another doing it badly; but when he has lost all shame, he speaks to that
one's face, not refuting, nor speaking the truth, but reproaching and
insulting. "I will cry out, and nowhere is there judgment." Now if I cry out,
nowhere does my judgment come; I am still competing. As if he said: As some
athlete in the stadium I have cried out, and nowhere does my judgment arise;
for I am still wrestling. If I do not finish the wrestling, the judgment of
the crown does not come. And we say these things, lest we accuse God, because
Job suffers so many things, and nowhere is the judgment, that is, the sentence
of freedom, and of deliverance from the terrible things. CHAP. XX. "If his
gifts go up to heaven." And the heretic, whenever he prays, whenever his
sacrifice goes up by high-speaking, whenever he seems to have been firmly
established, then he shall perish utterly; for his prayer is reckoned to him
as sin. And we churchmen who know his errors shall say: Where are such men,
dreaming and fashioning errors for themselves? They do not rest their mind
upon the truth, going astray; and they become as a dream that has flown away,
which shall not be found. Something of this sort David also sings: "But the
enemies of the Lord have failed. As soon as they were glorified and exalted,
failing as smoke they failed;" and again: "As a dream of those who are
awakened." "Wealth gathered unjustly shall be vomited up." I know also another
wealth gathered unjustly, that in the words of the heretics, who have
teachings contrary to the doctrines of the truth, things in their belly; which
are not sweet, but "gall of an asp," that is, wicked; as it is said in
Deuteronomy in the song of Moses concerning the wine of their teaching: "For
their vine is of the vine of Sodom." But the good God will not allow them to
have wealth gathered unjustly always in themselves, but will make them vomit
it up. For an angel, by the beneficence of God, will drag them out upon the
very house that was badly built; which is the church of those who work
wickedness. He adds then: "From his house," and what follows. =12.1036= But
see how much also is laid up for the heterodox because of the impious
doctrines. Therefore let us take heed to ourselves, lest ever we be spoiled in
plausible arguments from heretical teaching, and fall away from the mystery of
the Church. "Let him not see the milking of nomads." And the heterodox too
does not see the milking of nomads. And nomads are souls pastured under the
good shepherd, and finding pasture. And the doctrines against God have nothing
sweet, but astringent and dense. "For he has crushed the houses of many
helpless ones." And the heterodox, if he find you somewhere weak in the faith,
weak in the sound teaching, despoils this one. And it is truly possible to say
concerning the teacher of the heterodox, that he has crushed the houses of
many helpless ones; and he seizes the dwelling of the easily deceived, and
after these things he does not set it up; for he cannot set up the falsehood.
"Let a day of wrath come upon him." On which the divine justice is moved
against him; whether of the one from the circumcision who has acted impiously
toward God, or of the heterodox, or of the impious in whatever way, his
portion is that shown in the things previously said. CHAP. XXI. "Why do the
impious live, and have grown old also in wealth?" The phrase, "they have grown
old in wealth," he said instead of, they are not newly rich, but from youth
and until old age they have continued being rich. "Taking up a psaltery and a
harp." Another happiness of fathers, the gladness of the children, which
flutes and harps and drums fulfill, by which the ears are piped to, and the
pleasures of the soul are aroused, and those present are enchanted by the
unchaste melody; which the children of the impious work wantonly, laying their
hands upon the stretched strings, and clapping to the sound of the
instruments. "They have completed their life in good things." That is, no
interval of prosperity came to them, but in a complete life they continued
luxuriating in the bodily goods; and the end of life became for them the limit
of happiness, their well-being having been extended until the end. "And they
slept in the rest of Hades." Instead of, resting and being in good cheer until
death, they go down to Hades untormented. Or, that not even death itself
becomes bitter to them; but as those who fall asleep, so they die. Or, that
the others in living suffer evils and are distressed, but having died they
rest; but these of whom the discourse is, having passed through all their life
in good cheer, thus came to the rest in death. CHAP. XXII. "Is it not the
Lord who teaches?" And this doctrine is sound, that a true teacher of virtue
cannot =12.1037= be a man; for "he who teaches man knowledge," it is also
written in the Psalms, that it is no other but God. And God teaches, shining
upon the soul of the one learning from him, and illuminating the mind with the
true light, his own Word. Therefore, even if just men teach us, having
received a teaching grace, again it is the Lord who through them teaches us;
and even the very understanding and the opening of our hearts for the
reception of the divine lessons comes according to the divine grace. "Or
making a concern of you, will he refute you?" To this we would say, that God
does not refute the impious because he is anxious lest he himself be harmed in
some way; but making a concern of the one provided for and benefiting him.
"You took pledges of your brothers for nothing." And he assumes this
concerning Job from this, that he knew him to be rich, and suspected him to be
rich from some such sources; therefore he says: "You took pledges for
nothing;" that is, being owed nothing. And stretching further again another
sin of his he says. "And you took away the clothing of the naked." It is
possible at times to take garments, not of a naked man, but of a rich man, who
has many clothes stored away. If however someone has one tunic, and I strip
this tunic on the pretext of demanding what is mine, not having pitied that
naked man, I have taken away the clothing of the naked. And many such things
happen among us, when we lend, when we have debtors. Often our debtor is
exceedingly poor, not being able to repay; and we, having shut up our
compassion, and not seeing that he feeds a wife, and children, and is scarcely
fed from the few obols provided to him, demand bitterly, sometimes even having
surplus ourselves. These things then he says accusing Job, that "You took away
the clothing of the naked." And perhaps he also names the not giving a garment
to the naked a taking away; for he too is a robber and a greedy man who does
not clothe the naked, and who does not feed the one needing bread. "And you
have admired certain faces." This too is another sin; for it is a sin also to
admire a face. So-and-so, because he is rich, I admire his face; but the poor
man I trample, since he has no dignity. "The Almighty shall be a helper to
you." Here Eliphaz speaks a sound doctrine; for he thinks that Job suffers
because of sins, and that having endured he is purified through the very
suffering; and being purified, as gold in a furnace, he is shown approved,
having put away everything alien, and having become unmixed with all
wickedness =12.1040=. He says then, that if you also endure the things that
have befallen you, God will render you clean as silver that has been tried by
fire; and having become clean, you will have boldness, as having put away the
sins, and you will lift up your face cheerfully toward heaven, making
supplications with an uncondemned conscience; and if you pray anything and
promise to God, he himself will strengthen you to bring to an end the promises
toward him; for he is "the one who works in us both to will and to work for
his good pleasure." Instead however of, "And he will give you to pay the
vows," Theodotion rendered, "And you shall determine a word, and it shall
stand for you;" that is, everything that you may arrange and form will not
fall through; and to speak simply, he will furnish you all the conduct that
befits a just man; and all your ways will be made prosperous, being guided by
the divine light; or also your deeds will be splendid and glorious. "Because
he humbled himself, and you shall say: He was arrogant." He seems to speak as
concerning another; but the word is toward Job. For this reason, he says,
these things shall be for the one who repents, because he humbled himself, and
said that he justly underwent what he has suffered, since he was arrogant.
"And he will save him who stoops with the eyes." That is, the humble-minded
one, who looks down because of a bad conscience and lack of boldness, just as
the tax-collector who was justified in the Gospels. CHAP. XXVII. "And Job
still adding to the preface said." The devil took authority over Job and
tested him night and day; first through the things outside; later through the
body itself; after these things also through the friends. For when he was
testing him through the wife, and he knows that Job has not been defeated, he
brought three friends, not enemies, not having plotting doctrines; but true
words, yet not well, nor at the right time, brought forward; and he wished
through these to make the just man utter something against Providence; to act
impiously because of the toils; to lose heart because of the pains; but this
firm adamant, see what he says after the words of the three, when Eliphaz
finished three discourses, Bildad three, Zophar two. "For I am not conscious
to myself of having done unfitting things." This Paul also later uttered, and
added: "But I am not by this justified." Great is the boldness of the just man
both concerning the former life, and concerning the things at the time of the
circumstance, so that he said: I am conscious of nothing to myself.
CHAP. XXIX. "And the stout ones ceased speaking." Or according to the
edition of the rest "the rulers." These, yielding =12.1041= the word to the
just man, themselves were silent in his presence. "Having put a finger upon
the mouth." It will seem then also to be a sign of silence, to put a finger
upon the mouth; but perhaps, since the hand is taken for works, it shows that
their work muzzled their lips, and as it were some bond lay upon the mouth;
for they perceived that they are not worthy to speak, Job being present, who
has done greater works, they themselves having done few; for the finger
signifies the least action and energy. "And I had put on righteousness." Job
wove a fair tunic for himself, that he might put on righteousness instead of a
tunic. See also his garment: righteousness the tunic; judgment the cloak.
CHAP. XXXII. "Having feared to announce to you my own knowledge." He
professes however to have "knowledge," or, according to the rest, "gnosis,"
how men suffer in life; how some unjust prosper. I saw those doing injustice
and transgressing, thriving, and my feet were almost shaken, if I look only at
this life; but if I consider the future, my feet stand. Therefore I said: Hear
me. Other copies after "For I will speak, you hearing," have also these
verses: "Behold I have heard your words, I have given ear as far as your
understanding." And the other interpreters have rendered thus: "Behold, I
awaited your words; I listened as long as you were thinking, as long as you
were examining words, and until I reached you, I was considering." According
then to this edition and the other of the copies, Just as, he says, I in
quietness heard all that you said, and waited until the conclusion of your
words; and until you emptied all your own prudence; you too hear my words. But
according to the copies with us the sense is this: Hear me, he says; for I
will speak nothing overblown, but things you can examine. "And I will
understand as far as you," instead of, I will not use empty sounds, but I will
speak as you can understand. Or he says that, I will not sooner cease
speaking, before you also examine my words, whether I too can understand "as
far as you," that is, as far as you also consider, or I even surpass your
understanding. "For I am full of words." He said well, "I am full;" for he who
is not full of words, and comes to speaking, will speak empty things as empty,
according to what is said: "For the fool will speak foolish things, and his
heart will consider vain things." "For the spirit of the belly destroys me."
Just as a woman becomes pregnant, and when that which is being born has been
formed in her she comes to birth-pangs, until she give birth =12.1044= to what
was conceived; according to these things it happens also in the case of the
soul that loves learning; for she conceives the word, which being announced
she forms, and types it in order; and when after typing and forming, she hands
it over perfected, then she gives birth; and until she give birth, she is
weighed down in birth-pang. "I will speak, that I may rest, having opened the
lips." He who happens upon an intelligent hearer, and is full of words,
speaking rests, as it were emptying and casting off the surplus. CHAP. XXXV.
"Look up to heaven." He looks up to heaven not who has raised the bodily eyes,
and seen heaven; for this looking up even dogs and asses have. No lover of the
world looks up to heaven, but he who does not love the world, nor the things
in the world. And again the clouds are not so high from us, as if I see the
life of Moses, and his pleasing of God, and my own smallness and lowliness, so
I see the cloud high from me; if I come to Jesus the son of Nave, to the lives
of the prophets, if I examine their deeds, then I do what is written, and I
learn how high the clouds are from me. "If you have sinned, what will you do?"
Do you then, he says, know, if you have sinned, what you will do toward
loosing what has been sinned? If you have sinned in words, heal with better
words; if you have sinned in works, healing yourself with works that differ;
but if the lawless deeds are many and incurable, therefore for the incurable
things, which we cannot heal ourselves, Jesus Christ came from the heavens,
that he might heal the incurable, and that there might come to pass, "Blessed
are they whose sins have been forgiven." "He who sets in order the
night-watches." See that Elihu goes through praises of God and hymns, saying
concerning him, "He who sets in order the night-watches." Do you wish to see
how God sets in order night-watches? This whole age is night, it is darkness.
The light is kept for you; for now you see through a mirror, but you are going
at some time to see the light. Therefore all the age is night, and there is
need of guards appointed for this night, that the guards, those over the
nights, may keep those in nights from the robbers, from the wild beasts, from
the enemies. Who are the guards? The angels who encamp. "He who separates me
from the four-footed of the earth." And according to the rest, "teaching us
above the cattle of the earth, and making us wise above the birds that fly
through the air;" for man alone has been honored with reason. According then
to the word of creation we have been separated from all the animals; but if
you are a sinner, a fornicator, downward-inclined, you have not been separated
from the four-footed of the earth, but you have been compared to the cattle,
and you have become as =12.1045= a horse and a mule, in which there is no
understanding, and a horse mad for females. CHAP. XL. "Nay, but gird your
loin as a man." The perfect contests are laid up for those who have competed
well after this life; yet none the less even already the grace of God gives
earnest to the athletes. Therefore, when Job had competed the greatest things;
when, having cast off the possessions, he glorified the one who had given;
when, having put away the children, he gave thanks to the one who had taken
them; when, having fallen sick so as to rain worms from the body, he has not
been defeated by the toils; God gives him preludes and earnest of the prizes,
having spoken to him through cloud and whirlwind. Having heard then the former
words, it being needful to answer toward God, he is silent, as not having
boldness; for he did not yet know, "Moses spoke, and God answered him with a
voice." God however gives him boldness to speak; and such is his kindness,
that he takes up the person not of one judging, but of one being judged with
the man. And other Scriptures have taught this; as, "Come, and let us reason;"
and, "My people, what have I done to you?" Already God gives him a prize, that
he asks a man, that he inquires of him, that he gives authority of the answer.
If he had not been victorious, he would not have received; if he had not
endured, he would not have heard: "Gird your loin as a man," and do not be
silent; but be a man toward the questions; for as much as someone's loin is
not girded, nor has been bound tight, but is loosed, he cannot reasonably
answer God. Let no one then answer God first, unless he become a man perfect
in the spiritual stature and gird the loin. "Do not reject my judgment." Do
not think that these things which you have endured have been judged to me
without reason, nor reject my judgment. Let us remember this saying, as
useful, whenever God judges us to suffer something, to cast off a child, to
lose office, to fall sick in the body, to undergo whatsoever of these; it is
not possible for us to suffer any of these, God not judging. Since then none
of these happens apart from the judgment of God, it is said to the one who has
suffered: "Do not reject my judgment;" that is, Do not deny what I have judged
concerning you; he means the present oracle. "Do you think I have otherwise
given you an oracle, than that you might appear just?" I make a defense to
you: you were just, but you were not manifest; you had endurance; I needed to
furnish an occasion for your endurance to shine; you had courage; but it was
hidden in you; the toils that befell showed your courage. Let us remember
these things in the times of circumstances, and we shall seem =12.1048= to
hear God present and saying to us: "Do you think I have otherwise given you an
oracle," that is, to allow the temptation, or that your righteousness might be
clear to those who do not know? "Take up then height." And it is possible also
to understand the verses thus: You have defeated the tempter; take up your
height; take up your power; be crowned; put on comeliness. The insolent one
campaigned against you; but you humbled the proud one, and turned him far away
with shame with his phalanx. CHAP. XLI. "Will you play with him as with a
bird? or will you bind him as a sparrow for a child?" He describes the end of
a great and strong and perfect soul, that the dragon has been handed over to
it, as a sparrow to a child, not even able to harm the child, because even
bound the child leads it wherever he wishes and carries it about; so by the
grace of the Lord, as a sparrow under the hand, Satan has become bound to the
infants and those who suckle, from whose mouth God has perfected praise. "And
nations feed on him." Just as the Lord is the food of the faithful, so the
dragon of the impious; "For you have given him as food to the peoples, the
Ethiopians;" that is, to the sons of darkness, to those in ignorance. For
according to each word which we receive, we either eat from the dragon, if we
receive a vain hearing, or from Christ, if we receive a word that builds up
and benefits. "And every floating thing having come together shall not carry
one hide of his tail." And to speak more clearly: Men, who use the bodies as
ships, cannot of themselves put to death the last things of the dragon and the
extreme things; but with divine power watching over, we can both carry him
wholly and put him to death. And each of us puts to death, as far as it lies
with himself, the devil, according to: "And God will crush Satan under your
feet swiftly." "Who would enter into the folding of his breastplate?"
Breastplate and chest are named, where the ruling part is. His breastplate is
not simple; he is deceitful, he is folded; but a holy one can refute him, and
show that he is folded; for to the holy one he is not folded. "And a spirit
shall not pass through him." The "shall not pass through" is not that it does
not go through and pass by, but that nothing escapes him; all must come
through him. Let us therefore prepare ourselves, not to be held by him. "And
his eyes are the appearance of the Morning Star." His eyes at any rate are not
Morning Stars, but "appearance of the Morning Star;" for he transforms himself
into an angel of light. He pretends to be a heavenly star; he pretends to be a
symbol of day. "As lamps burning." I think that all =12.1049= the impious
introducers of godless doctrines are the mouth of the dragon; and there would
also be something tropically understood besides this, whence the words that
destroy. If ever you have heard a word on the pretext of Christianity accusing
the Creator, say for instance, advocating the things of Marcion, the things of
Valentinus, the things of Basilides; whenever they speak ill of the Maker of
heaven and earth, whenever something similar, you will see that from the mouth
of such men firebrands come out. Let us therefore keep ourselves, lest those
lamps burn us; lest the hearths of the fire touch us. "Before him destruction
runs." Destruction is as it were some forerunner of his and escorts him;
wherever he comes, his destruction runs before, then thus he himself enters.
If someone does not first receive destruction, he does not receive the dragon;
for when someone sins, then, itching in the ear, he seeks teachers, being
destroyed; and seeking teachers, after having been destroyed he finds the
dragon, and receives this one filled with so many evils into his own soul.
CHAP. XLII. "And the Lord gave double." He then who has undergone every
temptation receives as prizes of his endurance all things double compared with
the former; but the Lord gives prizes not double only, but a hundredfold; and
he would not have received double of the former, if he had not shown the
virtue double.
