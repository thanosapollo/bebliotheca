On the Salvation of the Soul.
=60.735=
Beloved, as many of you as have left behind the vain and perishing affairs of
this life, strive, so that you may not turn your mind back to them again. For
wealth passes away, and glory is destroyed, and beauty withers, and all things
are changed, and they perish as smoke, and they pass as a shadow, and they
vanish as a dream. For this reason Solomon said: Vanity of vanities, all is
vanity; and David says: Man walks in an image. Yet in vain are all those
troubled who strain over the affairs of the present life; truly they are
troubled in vain, they are thrown into tumult in vain, they are tossed in
vain, gathering and treasuring up the things that after a little while are
left behind; which we cannot take with us, but leaving all behind, we shall go
naked as we were born to the fearful Judge.

Even if we gather all the treasures, naked, dark, pitiable, downcast, with
necks laid bare, humbled, crushed, fearful, trembling, gloomy, in pain, with
our face to the ground, and covering even this with shame: thus we shall go,
thus we shall rise, thus we shall stand at that terrible and bitter court that
respects no person, where angels tremble, where fearful thrones are set, where
the books of deeds are opened, where the river of unquenchable fire, where the
untamed worm, where the unlit darkness, where the Tartarus that gives no
warmth, where the gnashing of teeth, where the unceasing tear, where the
unsilenced groan, where the inconsolable mourning, where there is no laughter,
but lament; where there is no gladness, but groaning; where there is no
luxury, but judgment; where there is no voice, but trembling. It is truly
fearful to hear, beloved, but more fearful to see all creation standing and
giving account for word, for deed, for thoughts, of those in the night, of
those in the day, of the things we have sinned.

Great is the fear then, brothers, great the distress, such as never was nor
shall be until that day. Then angels run about, the trumpets cry, the stars
fall, the sun is darkened, the heavens are rolled up, the earth is shaken, the
powers run ahead, the Seraphim cry out, the things above, the things below,
the earthly, the underworld are thrown into tumult, the tombs are opened, the
bodies rise and are gathered, the tribunal is made ready. Great is the fear,
unspeakable the trembling, incomprehensible the distress that comes then;
great is that storm, great the pain, hard the crisis, unceasing the tumult,
great the wailing. Hear then Daniel saying: =60.736= I was watching in a
vision of the night, until thrones were set, and the Ancient of days sat. His
garment was white as snow, the hair of his head pure as wool; his throne a
flame, his wheels burning fire. A river of fire was drawing before him; a
thousand thousands stood by him, and ten thousand times ten thousand
ministered to him; the court sat, and books were opened. Then my spirit
shuddered. I Daniel saw, and the vision of my head troubled me. Alas! the
prophet, seeing the vision of the coming judgment, shuddered! And what then
shall we endure, when we come to the things themselves? when from the rising
of the sun to its setting we are gathered and stand naked, showing to all the
burden of sins upon the neck? Then the tongues of the blasphemers are set on
fire, and there is none to cool them with dew; then the teeth of those who
slander are crushed by severe angels; then the hands of the money-lovers hang
and tremble, and being scraped they are in pain; then the mouths of the
babblers are stopped by the fire; then the eyes of those who wink are dug out
without pity.

Where then are parents, where brothers, where father, where mother, where the
kinsmen, where the friends? where the pomp of kings, where the authority of
rulers, where the madness of tyrants, where the pride of judges? where the
menservants, where the maidservants? where the adornment of garments, where
the colors of pearls, where the display of gold, where the clink of silver?
where the ornament of rings, where the shoe that binds the feet, where the
silken garments, where the linen? where the meats, where the unguents and the
vain incense? where the guarded treasures? where the adorned beds? where those
who overlook the poor? where those who turn their eyes from those in need?
where those who think themselves wise? where those who drink wine with drums
and dances? where those who laugh continually, and sneer at the devout? where
those who wear out their slaves, and despise the fear of God? where are those
who disbelieve the punishment, and live as if immortal? where are those who
say, Let us eat and drink; for tomorrow we die? where are those who say, Give
me today, and take tomorrow? where are those who say, Let us enjoy the things
here, and look to the things there? where are those who say, God is loving
toward man, and does not punish sinners? O how much will those who say such
things repent! how much will they beat their breasts, and there is none who
pities them! how much will they groan, and there is none who redeems! how
much, cutting themselves, =60.737= will they say, Woe, woe, that we mocked
ourselves! woe, that we destroyed ourselves!

We were taught, and we did not attend; we were admonished, and we despised;
they bore witness to us, and we did not believe; hearing the Scriptures we led
ourselves astray. Just is the judgment of God; for we receive things worthy of
what we have done. Woe to us, that for a passing pleasure we are punished
eternally, for a little time's neglect we are sunk in the eternal fire, for
vain glory we have fallen from the glory of God, for a little luxury we have
been deprived of the luxury of paradise, for wealth that perishes we have lost
the wealth of the kingdom! In the vain age we enjoyed ourselves, but in it
those who did not enjoy themselves rejoice. Now those who fasted live in
luxury, those who contended dance in the heavenly bridechamber, those who
scattered their wealth reap in gladness, those who wept rejoice eternally,
those who despised earthly things have received the heavenly; but we wretched
ones alone have been given over to punishment as we deserved; and now we cry,
and there is none who pities; we groan, and there is none who saves.
Therefore, so that we too may not speak such words with those fools in the age
to come, come, let us forestall the thief of our souls; let us run, while we
have time; let us groan, let us repent, let us be awakened, I beg you, from
the sleep of our sloth, from the weight of our sins and our neglect; let us
lift hands to him who is able to save us, and let us say: Lord, save us, we
perish. Let us only make haste before the sun sets, before the door is shut,
before night overtakes us. For once the festival is dissolved, no one trades;
once the theater is dissolved, no one is crowned, no one contends. Therefore
let us run; for there is need of a race, brothers, and of a fierce race, that
we may arrive, that we too may not knock and hear: I do not know you; let us
put on swift shoes, let us be a little ashamed.

How much do we dishonor the Master? how much do we provoke the Benefactor? He
does good, and we each day ignore it; he has compassion, and we set it aside,
he feeds, covering us he provides, and we transgress his commandments each
day, and we are not ashamed. Let us be ashamed at last; for the time has drawn
near, and the day has arrived, and we must give account for our whole life.
Let us therefore cease at last from immoderate luxury, and from shameful
laughter, so that we may not weep bitterly there; let us cease reviling and
hating and wronging the brothers; let us cease treasuring up, living wantonly
and committing fornication; let us give ourselves to prayers, to petitions, to
readings, to fasts, to repentances; let us show a new life, let us confess,
let us turn back; for it is the time of turning. Let us repent, let us weep,
let us show God a repentance full of care, and let us hate sin; let us labor
here a little, I beg you, so that we may not be punished much there; let us
contend before the time, brothers, so that we may not be tormented eternally.
The time is short, but the judgment is long, and the end is near, and the fear
is great, and there is none who pities. For each will seek the time which he
spent badly, and will not find it. Woe to the slanderer, because he will seek
drops of water while burning, and does not find them! woe to the unbeliever,
because he will be punished eternally! woe to the negligent, because he goes
to a severe judge! woe to the one who does not contend, because he is handed
over to severe angels. He who loses gold finds other gold; but another time of
repentance he cannot find. Let us not spare, brothers, our own bodies, but let
us wear them down, since Blessed are those who hunger and thirst, blessed are
those who mourn.

For the body is clay; and there will come an hour and a day fearful and evil
and inexorable, =60.738= and earth will go to earth, and dust becomes dust
again. Let us be sober, beloved, I entreat you, let us travel the road, let us
lift our heads; for the hour will come, and it will truly come, of our
departure; and let us not deceive ourselves. Suppose, beloved, we live in
luxury, suppose we are rich fifty or even a hundred years; and after these,
disease and old age, and after these what? Weakness and death, and that
terrible hour which is expected and shuddered at and neglected. Great fear
comes then, brothers; and it is a great thing to see a soul being separated
from the body; great is the distress of that hour, when the tongue cannot
speak the word clearly, when we turn our eyes this way and that continually,
and we recognize the friends and brothers standing by us, and we cannot speak
to them. We hear those who lament us, and we cannot console them; we see the
children mourning and weeping and we go bearing their pain. And why do I say
children? For in that hour there is no care for children, nor for brothers,
nor does any other thought hold us, except the thought of our own trespasses,
and how we shall meet the judge, and what sentence we shall receive, and what
place then will receive us. Then as we ponder these things, suddenly severe
angels stand over us. Then we, seeing them, if we are found unprepared, how
shall we be thrown into tumult, trying indeed to flee the bed, but not being
able, looking toward them with pitiable eyes, and with a gloomy face
entreating, imploring, begging and saying: Have mercy on me, angels and
fearful attendants of the throne of God, and do not carry me fruitless and
unclean to the Judge; do not separate me, a sinner, from the body; do not, I
beg, I entreat, be persuaded, be prevailed upon, and leave me a little time to
repent, to groan, to mourn, to do alms, since I have spent and consumed my own
life badly. The angels, hearing these things, say to us: O wretched soul, you
have lived all your days in neglect, and now you want to repent? O miserable
soul, your sun has set, your time is finished; God has commanded that you be
separated from your body. Come and be condemned in eternal fire according to
your deeds; for there is no longer hope of salvation for you, but eternal
punishment. Hearing these things, beloved, and believing that they are true
and not myths, let us contend before that hour; even if we have been
accustomed to sin, yet let us cut these things out through repentance. Let us
not be led astray, brothers, there is judgment and eternal punishment,
unquenchable fire and a worm that does not end, outer darkness and Tartarus,
gnashing of teeth and great weeping, as the Lord mentioned in the Gospels; for
he is true who said, Heaven and earth shall pass away, but my words shall not
pass away. Therefore let us fear and shudder, all who have lived in sins, and
let us be eager to be found with the saints through repentance. Do not say, I
stole, I murdered, I committed fornication, and God does not receive me; say
none of these things; for he receives all, as the thief, as the publican and
as the harlot; only let us not despair, I beg you, let us not be slothful. Let
us knock through repentance and say: Open to us, Lord, to the unworthy and
sinners; for your holy name's sake do not turn away from us, but be prevailed
upon, and do not deprive us of your kingdom. For you are God of the hopeless,
and salvation of all who flee to you; and yours is the kingdom and the power
and the glory with the unoriginate Father and the all-holy and good and
life-giving Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
