On the synaxis of the archangels.
=59.755=
It is necessary to praise angels. For they too, hymning the Creator, render
him gracious and well-disposed toward men. And I mean angels, our allies,
those who campaign against the opposing powers, of whom one is the archangel
Michael, who arrayed himself against the devil over the body of Moses, and
stood against the prince of the Persians for the freedom of the people; of
whom is that one who turned the ass aside, and restrained Balaam from his
unseemly madness; and since the seer did not understand what was being done,
the voice of an irrational creature brought the rational one to his senses; of
whom is the one who drew the sword, and urged Jesus the son of Nun to join
battle with the enemies; of whom is the one who smote the hundred and
eighty-five thousand of the Assyrians in a single night, and joined death to
the sleep of the barbarians; of whom is the one who transposed the prophet
Habakkuk with an aerial leap, that he might give the prophet Daniel, in the
midst of the unfed lions, a taste of food. These then, and such as these, are
against the enemies, who also prevail as champions against the passions. Of
such sort also was the wondrous and divine Raphael, who by the dissection of a
single fish was stopping a demon-possessed girl, and for a blind old man was
crafting the light. But neither are those who served the New Testament worthy
of silence, of whom a great part is the archangel Gabriel, who spoke to
Zachariah, and exacted the unbelief with silence, because he did not believe
that the barren woman would conceive on account of the economy that was to
come; who announced as good news to the reverent Mary the unwedded
birth-pangs; who leading the shepherds to the cave, and announcing the
uncontainable one in a manger, the uncircumscribed one in swaddling-clothes,
sending up with them a concordant song of doxology: Glory to God in the
highest, and on earth peace, among men good will. Another hinders the magi
from returning to the tyrants; and they were ministering on earth to the one
who created; another admonishes the betrothed of the Virgin not to put away as
corrupted her who was incorrupt; another suggests to him, on account of
Herod's infanticide, flight and return. Even though they beheld the uncreated
one in flesh, they ministered in a slavish manner. For they marveled at his
strength, by which the tempter fell, and according to the voice of David,
Taking him up on their hands, they carried him out of the range of the stones.
For the Jews, it says, took up stones, that they might cast them at him.
Others ran together to the Passion; others after the resurrection, sitting at
the empty tomb, said to faithful women, Why do you seek the living among the
dead? And after the ascension they escorted the disciples as bodyguards,
loosing Peter's bonds, lightening Paul's dangers on land and sea, mediating
for the faith of Cornelius, striking the slayer of the just, Herod, when he
was speaking greatly. Are they not then worthy of the greatest honor, those
who steer our life? For not only were they appointed to guard nations =59.756=
angels, as the oracle cries, When the Most High, it says, divided the nations,
when he scattered the sons of Adam, he set the bounds of the nations according
to the number of the angels of God; but also to each of men an angel was
allotted.
The word is not mine, but of the divine Scripture. For when the maidservant
Rhoda announced to the apostles that Peter, having escaped Herod's prison,
stood before the doors, they, not believing, said, It is his angel. And the
Savior also bears witness to these things, speaking about the small ones
according to this life: See that you despise not one of these little ones. For
I say to you, that their angels in


heaven always behold the face of my Father who is in heaven. And over each
Church Christ set guardian angels, as he says revealing to John: Say to the
angel of the Church of Smyrna: I know your poverty and your affliction; but
you are rich. What do you say, O Master? I am rich, being poor? Why do you
enrich me with an empty name? For if I am rich, I am not poor; but if I am
poor, how am I rich? For great is the distance of wealth and poverty, as you
yourself testify, having set a great gulf between rich and poor. The rich man
turns away from the beggar, the beggar looks askance at the rich. The things
are contrary, the life is uneven. For the one groans under sufferings, the
other cackles at pleasures; this one prays to obtain bread, that one overflows
with gluttonies; this one has not even a cotyle of wine, that one, reveling
and being drenched, belches out his drunkenness; this one is wrapped in a rag,
that one is adorned with varied garments; this one is cast on the ground,
keeping vigil, that one on a bed snores, dreaming of luxury; this one asks for
some coin, that one is heaped with gold. For he who treasures up, but does not
distribute, resembles a beggar awaiting a rich burial. But they do not even
equally enjoy the seasons. For winter is harsh to the beggar who has no
warmth, and summer is wretched to the rich man who from satiety cannot bear
the heat. How then is the same man poor and rich? True is the word, and the
one who utters it. Poor through the want of money, but rich through the
treasures of faith. And it is better to be poor in the things of this life,
but rich in the spiritual; for of the former the end is corruption, of the
latter the gain is inalienable.
For this reason the Son says, Do not treasure up treasures on the earth,
calling us to treasure up the heavenly things, to feed the poor, to visit the
sick, to cover the uncovered, to release destitute debtors, and to demand the
loan from God. For he who has mercy on a beggar lends to God. For these things
angels, beholding, report to the judge of all; and he commands, saying to them
for the one who works the good, Your prayers and your alms have gone up for a
memorial of you before God. To him be the glory and the power, now and always,
and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
