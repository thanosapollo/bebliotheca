OF THE GREAT ATHANASIUS AN ORDINANCE OF THOSE WISHING TO LIVE IN
STILLNESS.
Such is the monk, and thus the monk ought to be, abstaining from earthly
affairs; and not only so, but also a soldier of Christ, immaterial, free
of care, outside of every commercial thought and practice. In these
things let the monk be, especially he who has left behind all the matter
of this world; and toward the fair beauties of stillness the ascesis,
sweet the life, delightful the practice. Do you wish, therefore,
beloved, to take up the solitary life, and to run off toward the
trophies of stillness; leave there the world and the cares of the world,
and the principalities and powers over these, that is, be alone,
immaterial, passionless, outside of every desire, so that, having become
alien to the entanglement from these things, you may be able to keep
stillness well. For unless someone lifts himself out from these things,
he would not be able to accomplish this way of life. Hold to scant and
easily-despised foods, not many and easily-entangling, or rather
unentangling. And if even as for the sake of hospitality a thought
should arise concerning costly things, leave this there, do not be
persuaded by it at all; for through this the adversary lies in wait for
you, to make you depart from stillness. =28.848= See that you do not
desire to have wealth toward distribution of the poor; for a good word
is better than a gift; for this too is a deceit of the evil one, often
toward vainglory and throwing the mind into a cause of busyness. And
concerning garments, do not desire to have many garments; but provide
only those sufficient for the necessity of the body. If there is need of
foods or of garments, do not be ashamed to take the things offered to
you by others; for this is a form of pride. And if you yourself also
abound in these things, give to the one who lacks; thus God wishes his
own children to be managed. Having the necessities of the present time,
do not be anxious for the coming time, such as of one day, or of a week,
or indeed of the present time; the time itself will furnish the
necessities. But rather seek especially the kingdom of the heavens.
Always take thought for your own benefit, and acquire the manner of
stillness. Do not love to dwell with material and distracted men; either
dwell alone, or with immaterial and like-minded men; for he who dwells
with material and distracted men will altogether himself also share in
their entanglement, and will serve human commands, and vain talkings,
and all the other terrible things, anger, grief, madness of materials,
fear of scandals, parental cares, kinships of affection. But decline the
meeting with these, lest they ever destroy you from the stillness in the
cell, and lead you about their own entanglements. And if even the cell
in which you sit is easily-entangling, flee, and do not spare even it,
lest you be drawn into love of it. Do all things, practice all things,
so that you may be able to keep stillness, and to have leisure, and to
be eager to be in the wills of God, and in the struggle of the unseen.
If you cannot easily keep stillness in your own parts, give your purpose
toward sojourning abroad, and hasten your thought toward this; become
just as some excellent merchant, testing all things toward stillness,
and the things quiet and useful toward it. Nevertheless I say to you,
love sojourning abroad; for it frees you from the entanglements
according to your own country, and makes you enjoy only the benefit
according to stillness. Flee the sojourns in a city, and persist in the
desert, if possible altogether. Do not go to meet into a city; for you
will see there nothing useful, nothing beneficial to your way of life.
Do not fear their sound, or be startled; nor flee the stadium of your
benefit. Endure without fear, and you will see the greatnesses of God,
the rest of all full-assurance unto salvation. If you have friends, flee
their continual meetings; for meeting them at long intervals you will be
well-pleasing. And if you perceive through these very ones harm coming
to you, do not approach at all; for you ought to have as friends those
who are beneficial and who take part with your way of life. Flee the
meeting of wicked and =28.849= quarrelsome men; dwell with none of
these; but decline also their base purposes. Let peaceful men, brothers,
and spiritual holy fathers, be friends to you. Do not lodge with
distracted men, nor have a banquet with them; lest they ever drag you
about their own deceits, and lead you away from the science according to
stillness. And if someone of those walking according to the love of God,
having come to you, should invite you to eat, and you wish to go, go
indeed, but return more quickly into your cell. If possible, do not
sleep outside, outside of your cell; so that the grace of stillness may
remain with you always, and you will have the worship of the purpose in
it unhindered. Do not become a desirer of good foods; for the soul that
lives in luxury, while living, has died. Do not fill your belly with
various foods, lest a longing for these come to you, and it produce in
you the longing concerning a table. For it has been said not to be
deceived by filling of the belly. And if you also continually see
yourself invited outside your cell, decline. The continual sojourn
outside your cell is harmful, it takes away the grace, darkens the mind,
withers the longing.
