1.1 Virginity has already become for many a subject of speeches for display,
furnishing countless occasions to the more ambitious to parade panegyrically
in speech and to take pride in the graces belonging to the achievement. But
that this practice is solemn and honorable to all by whom the good is judged
in purity, more than all the things that excel in virtue, I myself also will
not deny, and as far as I am able, just as those who make the crown-winners at
the stadia illustrious by the proclamation, so I myself will not cease
proclaiming the preeminence of the wonder; but the proper praise of virginity,
and the one most of all fitting, I say naturally coexists with those who
accomplish it, and is recognized rather from the very appellation that is
fitted to the thing; for the custom of men knows to call the unwedded
uncorrupted. The excess of this great gift is shown even by this alone, that
though there are many and great things accomplished through virtue, this alone
of all has been honored with the name of the incorruptible.
21.2 But if the pleasant is often mixed in with need (for want knows how to
sweeten all things, making palatable by the intensity of desire whatever is
found alongside need), the need is not to be rejected because of the enjoyment
that follows; nor indeed is pleasure to be pursued as the principal aim, but
selecting the useful from all things it is proper to overlook what delights
the senses.
22.1 ... the body has been composed; for that hot should be mixed with cold
and that there should be a mixture of wet toward dry is unreasonable, these
being joined to the opposites by the kinship through the middle of the pairs.
And by some such subtle craft he would demonstrate the account while treating
of nature, saying that each of these, standing diametrically distant from its
contrary by nature, is joined to the opposites through the kinship of the
neighboring qualities. For since the cold and the hot occur equally in wet
things and in dry, and conversely the wet and the dry are likewise constituted
in the hot and the cold, the sameness of the qualities appearing equally in
the opposites produces through itself the conjunction of the contraries. But
why should I go through each in detail, how these both have been cut apart
from one another by the opposition of nature and again have been united, being
mixed with one another by the kinship of the qualities? Only, the reason we
recalled what was said is that he who had understood the nature of the body by

23.6 If you see a human life standing in the midst of death and life, choosing
what is useful from either side for philosophy, neither accepting the
inactivity of death in its eagerness concerning the commandments, nor having
set its whole foot upon life because of alienation from worldly desires, but
remaining more inactive than the dead in those things in which the life of
flesh is tested, yet toward the works of virtue, through which "those who live
by the Spirit" are recognized, truly ensouled and active and strong, look to
this as the rule of life. Let this be for you the mark of the divine life, as
the ever-visible stars are for pilots. Imitate both his gray hair and his
youth, or rather imitate his old age in a young man and his youth in old age.
For neither did time dim the vigor and energy of his soul when his age had
already declined toward old age, nor was his youth active in those things by
which youth acting is recognized, but there was a wonderful mixture of
opposites in each age, or rather an exchange of properties, the power in old
age being youthful toward the good, and the youth in the young man being
inactive toward evil. And if you will also seek out the loves of that age,
imitate the intensity and fire of the divine love of wisdom, with which he
grew from infancy and which lasted until old age. But if you are unable to
look toward him, as those more afflicted in the eyes toward the sun, then look
away toward the choir of the saints stationed under him, those who shine in
life toward imitation of those who have come to be according to age; God has
set this as a mark for our life.
