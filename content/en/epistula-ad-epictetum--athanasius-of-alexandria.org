from those things which they themselves wrote. For from this also it is
possible for you to see the absurdity: if the Word is homoousios with the body
that has its nature from earth, and the Word is homoousios with the Father
according to the confession of the Fathers, the Father himself also will be
homoousios with the body that came to be from earth. And why do you still
blame the Arians who say the Son is a creature, when you yourselves say even
the Father is homoousios with the creatures, and pass over into another
impiety, claiming that the Word has been changed into flesh and bones and hair
and sinews and a whole body, and has been altered from his own nature. For it
is time for you to say openly that he has come to be from earth; for the
nature of the bones and of the whole body is from earth. What then is this
great madness, that you even fight against yourselves? For in saying the Word
is homoousios with the body, you signify one thing toward another, but by the
Word himself having been changed into flesh you imagine a change. And who will
still endure you even merely uttering these things? For you have turned aside
into impiety more than every heresy; for if the Word is homoousios with the
body, the mention and the need of Mary is superfluous, since the body could
exist even before Mary eternally, just as the Word himself also is, if indeed
according to you he is homoousios with the body. And what need was there of
the sojourn of the Word, that he might either put on what is homoousios with
himself, or, having been changed from his own nature, become a body? For the
Godhead does not take hold of itself, that it might also put on what is
homoousios with itself, nor did the Word who redeems the sins of others sin,
that, having been changed into a body, he might offer himself as a sacrifice
for himself and redeem himself.
5 But it is not so, far be it; for he takes hold of the seed of Abraham, as
the Apostle said; whence he was obliged to be made like the brothers in all
things and to take a body like ours. For this reason at any rate Mary also is
truly set down, that from her he might take this and offer it as his own on
our behalf; and Isaiah, prophesying, showed her, saying, Behold the virgin;
and Gabriel is sent to her, not simply to a virgin, but to a virgin betrothed
to a man, that from the betrothed he might show Mary to be truly a human
being; and Scripture mentions the giving birth and says, she wrapped him in
swaddling-clothes, and breasts were blessed which he sucked; and a sacrifice
was offered, as of the one born having opened the womb. And all these things
were marks of a virgin giving birth; and Gabriel also announced the good news
to her securely, saying not simply that which is born in you, lest a body
introduced into her from outside be supposed, but from you, that that which is
born might be believed to be from her by nature, this too being clearly shown
by nature, that it is impossible for a virgin to bear milk who has not given
birth, and impossible for a body to be nourished with milk and wrapped in
swaddling-clothes unless it was first born by nature. This is what was
circumcised on the eighth day; this Simeon received into his arms; this became
a child, and grew, becoming twelve years old, and came to the thirtieth year.
For it is not, as some supposed, that the very essence of the Word, being
changed, was circumcised, being unalterable and unchangeable, the Savior
himself saying, See me, that I am and I have not been altered, and Paul
writing, Jesus Christ yesterday and today the same and unto the ages. But in
the body that was circumcised and carried and ate and grew weary and was
nailed on the wood and suffered, there was the impassible and bodiless Word of
God. This was what was laid in the tomb, when he himself went to preach even
to the spirits in prison, as Peter said.
6 Which especially shows the mindlessness of those who say the Word has been
turned into bones and flesh. For if this were so, there would have been no
need of a tomb, for it would itself have gone

through itself, the body, to preach to the spirits in Hades. But as it is, he
himself went to preach, while Joseph, wrapping the body in linen, laid it away
at Golgotha; and it has been shown to all that the body was not the Word, but
was a body of the Word. And this, having risen from the dead, Thomas handled,
and saw in it the marks of the nails, which the Word himself endured, seeing
them nailed in his own body, and though able to hinder, he did not hinder, but
even made the properties of the body his own as his, the bodiless Word. At any
rate, when the body was struck by the servant, he said as himself suffering,
Why do you beat me? And though untouchable by nature, nevertheless he said, I
gave my back to scourges and I did not turn my face away from spittings; for
what the human of the Word suffered, these things the Word, being with it,
referred to himself, that we might be able to partake of the Godhead of the
Word. And it was a paradox, that he himself was the one suffering and not
suffering: suffering, because his own body suffered and he was in the one
suffering; not suffering, because the Word, being God by nature, is
impassible. And he himself, the bodiless one, was in the passible body, and
the body had in itself the impassible Word, doing away with the weaknesses of
the body itself. And he did this, and it came about thus, that receiving our
things and offering them as a sacrifice he might do them away, and thereafter
wrapping us in his own things he might make the Apostle say, this corruptible
must put on incorruption and this mortal put on immortality.
7 And these things did not come about putatively, far be it, as some again
supposed, but in very truth, the Savior having become man, the salvation of
the whole man came about. For if according to them the Word was in the body
putatively, and what is said putatively is a fantasy, then even the salvation
and the resurrection of men is found to be said by seeming, according to the
most impious Manichaean. But indeed our salvation is not a fantasy, nor of a
body only, but truly the salvation of the whole man, soul and body, has come
about in the Word himself. Human by nature, then, according to the divine
Scriptures, was that which is from Mary, and true was the body of the Savior;
and it was true, because it was the same as ours; for Mary is our sister,
since we all also are from Adam. And no one would doubt this who remembered
what Luke wrote; for after the rising from the dead, when some thought they
were not seeing the Lord in the body from Mary, but instead of this were
beholding a spirit, he said, See my hands and my feet and the marks of the
nails, that I myself am he; handle me and see, that a spirit does not have
flesh and bones, as you behold me having. And having said this he showed them
the hands and the feet, from which also those who have dared to say that the
Lord has been altered into flesh and bones can again be convicted. For he did
not say, as you behold me being flesh and bones, but having them, lest the
Word himself be supposed to have been changed into these, but that he himself
having them both before the death and after the resurrection might be
believed.
8 These things being thus, as to the demonstration it is superfluous still to
take up the other points and to exercise anything about them. For the body in
which the Word was is not homoousios with the Godhead, but was truly born from
Mary, and the Word himself was not changed into bones and flesh, but came to
be in flesh. For what is said in John, the Word became flesh, has this
meaning, as it is possible to find this also from the like; for it is written
in Paul, Christ became a curse for us; and just as he did not himself become a
curse, but because he took upon himself the curse on our behalf, he is said to
have become a curse; so also he became flesh, not having been changed into
flesh, but because he took up flesh on our behalf and became man. For to say,
the Word became flesh, is equal again to saying, the Word became man,
according to what was said in Joel, I will pour out from my Spirit upon all
flesh; for the promise was not as far as the irrational creatures, but is
toward men, for whose sake the Lord also became man. And this saying having
this meaning, reasonably all who thought that the flesh from Mary existed
before Mary, and that the Word had a human soul before her and had always come
to be in it before the sojourn, will condemn themselves. And those who said
that the flesh is not capable of death, but is of the immortal nature, will
also cease. For if it did not die, how did Paul hand on to the Corinthians
what he also received, that Christ died for our sins according to the
Scriptures; and how did he even rise at all, if he did not first die? And
those who even at all conceived that a tetrad can come about instead of the
Trinity, if the body should be said to be from Mary, will blush greatly; for
if, they say, we say the body is homoousios with the Word, the Trinity remains
a Trinity, nothing foreign being brought upon it of the Word; but if we say
the body from Mary is human, of necessity, the body being foreign according to
essence and the Word being in it, a tetrad comes about instead of a Trinity
because of the addition of the body.
9 Saying these things thus they do not understand how they fall upon
themselves. For even if they do not say the body is from Mary, but that it is
homoousios with the Word, no less than what they pretended, lest they be
thought to think it, they will be shown according to their own notion to be
saying this, a tetrad. For as the Son, being homoousios with the Father
according to the Fathers, is not himself the Father, but is called homoousios
as Son toward Father, so the homoousios body of the Word is not itself the
Word, but another toward the Word; and being another, their Trinity will
according to them be a tetrad. For it is not the true and really perfect and
undivided Trinity that receives addition, but the one invented by these men.
And how are they still Christians, inventing another besides the God who is?
For again even in their other sophism it is possible to see the folly. If
because it is so and is said in the Scriptures that the body of the Savior is
from Mary and human, they think a tetrad is said instead of a Trinity, as if
an addition were coming about because of the body, they wander far,
equating the thing made with the maker, and supposing that the Godhead can
receive addition; and they have not known that the Word did not become flesh
for an addition of Godhead, but that the flesh might rise, nor did he come
forth from Mary that the Word might be improved, but that he might redeem the
human race. How then do they think that the body redeemed and made alive
through the Word makes an addition into Godhead for the Word who made it
alive? For rather a great addition came about for the human body itself from
the Word's communion and union toward it; for from mortal it became immortal,
and being soulish it became spiritual, and having come to be from earth it
passed through the heavenly gates. The Trinity, however, even when the Word
has taken a body from Mary, is a Trinity, receiving neither addition nor
subtraction; but it is always perfect, and in Trinity one Godhead is known,
and thus in the Church one God is preached, the Father of the Word.
10 From this then those who once said that he who came forth from Mary is not
himself Christ and Lord and God will also thereafter keep silence. For if God
was not in the body, how, as soon as he came forth from Mary, was he called
Emmanuel, which is interpreted, God with us? And how also did Paul, if the
Word was not in flesh, write to the Romans, from whom is the Christ according
to the flesh, who is God over all, blessed unto the ages, amen? Therefore let
those also who formerly denied that the crucified one is God confess that they
have gone astray, being persuaded by the divine Scriptures, and especially by
Thomas, who after seeing in him the marks of the nails cried out, My Lord and
my God. For the Son, being God and Lord of glory, was in the body that was
ingloriously nailed and dishonored; and the body suffered, being pierced on
the wood, and blood and water flowed from its side, but being a temple of the
Word it was filled with the Godhead. For this reason at any rate the sun,
seeing its own Craftsman enduring in the body that was being insulted, drew in
its rays and darkened the earth. And the body itself, having a mortal nature,
rose above its own nature because of the Word in it, and it has ceased from
the corruption according to nature, and having put on the Word who is above
man it has become incorruptible.
11 And as for some imagining and saying that, just as it came about upon each
of the prophets, so also the Word came upon some one man from Mary, it is
superfluous to exercise, their mindlessness having an evident condemnation.
For if he came thus, why is this one from a virgin, and not himself also from
a man and a woman? For thus also each of the saints was begotten. Or why, the
Word having come thus, is not the death of each said to have come about on our
behalf, but this one's only? And why

when the Word sojourns upon each of the prophets, is it said of the one from
Mary alone, as having sojourned once at the consummation of the ages? Or why,
he having come as he came in the saints before, have all the others, having
died, not yet risen, but the one from Mary alone rose on the third day? Or
why, the Word having come likewise as upon the others, is the one from Mary
alone called Emmanuel, as of a body filled with Godhead having been born from
her? For Emmanuel is interpreted, God with us. Or why, if indeed he came thus,
when each of the saints eats and drinks and grows weary and dies, is it not
said as he himself eating and growing weary and having died, but only in the
case of the one from Mary? For what this body suffered, these things have been
said as of him himself suffering; and of all the others it is said only that
they were born and begotten; of the one from Mary alone it has been said, and
the Word became flesh.
12 From which it is shown that toward all the others the Word came to be for
the sake of prophesying, but from Mary the Word himself, taking flesh for
himself, came forth a man; being by nature and by essence Word of God, but
according to flesh from the seed of David and from the flesh of Mary having
become man, as Paul said. This one the Father showed both in the Jordan and on
the mountain, saying, This is my Son, the beloved, in whom I have been well
pleased. This one the Arians denied, but we, recognizing him, worship, not
dividing the Son and the Word, but knowing that the Word himself is the Son,
through whom all things came to be and we were redeemed. Wherefore we also
wondered how there came about among you at all a contention about things so
evident; but thanks be to the Lord, that as much as we were grieved when we
came upon the records, so much we rejoiced at the end of them. For they
withdrew with agreement and made peace upon the confession of the pious and
orthodox faith. This at any rate, after I had first considered many things,
has persuaded me also to write these few things, reckoning lest from the
silence grief instead of joy should come about for those who from the
agreement furnished us a pretext for rejoicing. I ask then first your piety,
and second those who hear, to receive these things with a good conscience, and
if anything is lacking toward piety, to correct this and make it known to me;
but if, as from one unskilled in speech, it has not been written according
to worth nor perfectly, that all pardon our weakness concerning speaking.
Greet all the brothers with you; all those with us greet you; may you continue
in health in the Lord, beloved and truly most longed-for.
