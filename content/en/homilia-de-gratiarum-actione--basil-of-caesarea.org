through certain channels of the passages at the eyes the weight of the
moisture is discharged. Hence certain sounds, and dizziness, and darkenings
come on at unexpected hearings of grievous things, the head being whirled
about by the vapors which the contraction of the heat toward the depth sends
up. Then, I think, as the cloud into a drizzle, so the thickness of the vapors
is dissolved into a tear. Hence there is also a certain pleasure for those who
grieve in lamentation, the burdening thing being emptied from them unawares
through weeping. And experience of what happens confirms the truth of the
account. For we have known many who in incurable disasters forcibly endured
not to let the tear drop; then some fell into incurable affections, apoplexies
or paralyses, and others even wholly expired, as though a weak support, their
power being broken under the weight of grief.
For what one may see in the case of a flame, that it is choked by its own
smoke when the smoke does not pass out but coils about it, this they say
happens also in the case of the power that governs the living being; for it is
withered by the painful things, and is quenched, when no exhalation takes
place toward the outside. Let not then the lovers of grief put forward the
tear of the Lord as an advocacy of their own passion. For as the food which
the Lord ate is not for us a starting point of luxury, but on the contrary the
highest bound of self-control and sufficiency; so also the tear is not a
legislation toward lamenting, but a most seemly measure set forth, and an
exact rule, according to which it is fitting, remaining within the bounds of
nature, to bear grievous things solemnly and with seemliness. Neither then to
women nor to men is the love of mourning and much weeping permitted, but only
so much as to be downcast at grievous things and to let fall some small tear,
and this quietly, not roaring nor wailing, nor rending a tunic, or pouring
dust upon oneself, nor committing any other such unseemliness of the things
practiced by those who are uneducated toward the heavenly things. For the one
purified by the divine teaching must be fenced by right reason as by some
strong wall, and bravely and steadfastly ward off the assaults of such
passions; and not, as one sitting down in some place =31.232=, receive with
the low and yielding part of the soul the crowd of passions flowing in. For it
belongs to an unmanly soul, one that has no tension from hope toward God, to
be violently broken and to fall under grievous things. For as worms are born
most of all in the softer woods, so griefs grow in the softer characters of
men. Was Job adamant in heart? Were his inward parts made of stone? Ten
children fell for him in a brief turn of time, crushed by one blow in the
house of cheer, at the time of enjoyment, when the devil shook the dwelling
down upon them. He saw a table mixed with blood; he saw children brought forth
at different times, yet going under the end of life in common.
He did not wail, he did not tear out his hair, he did not let out any ignoble
voice, but he uttered that celebrated thanksgiving hymned among all: The Lord
gave, the Lord has taken away; as it seemed good to the Lord, so it has also
come to pass; blessed be the name of the Lord. Was the man without sympathy?
And how? he who says concerning himself: I wept over every one in affliction.
But was he lying when he said these things? And yet the truth bears witness
for him, that besides the remaining virtues he was also true; For a man, it
says, blameless, righteous, god-fearing, true. But you, with certain songs
made toward gloom, lament besides, and by wailing strains you practice melting
away your own soul; and as the invention and the costume of the tragedians is
their own,

with which they occupy the theaters; so you think it also belongs to the
mourner that a fitting figure should be a black garment, and unkempt hair, and
darkness in the house, and filth, and dust, and a gloomy strain, always
keeping the wound of grief fresh in the soul. Leave these things to be done by
those who have no hope. But you have been taught concerning those who have
fallen asleep in Christ, that It is sown in corruption, it is raised in
incorruption; it is sown in weakness, it is raised in power; it is sown a
natural body, it is raised =31.233= a spiritual body. Why then do you weep for
the one who has gone out to change his garment? Do not lament yourself as
being deprived of some helper toward life; For it is good, it says, to hope in
the Lord rather than to hope in man. Nor bewail him as having suffered
terrible things. For a little later the trumpet from the heavens will wake
him, and you will see him standing at the judgment seat of Christ. Leave then
these low and uneducated voices. Woe is me for the unexpected evils; and this:
Who would have thought these things would come to be? and, When would I have
expected to hide in earth the head dearest to me?
For even if we hear another saying these things, it is fitting that we blush,
we who have been taught the necessary affections of nature both from the
memory of things past and from the experience of things present. Neither then
untimely deaths, nor any other misfortunes falling unexpectedly, will ever
strike us who are being trained by the word of piety. For example: I had a
son, a young man, sole successor of my life, consolation of old age, ornament
of the family, flower of his peers, support of the house, who was at the very
most graceful point of age; this one is snatched away and is gone; and he has
become earth and dust, who a little before was uttering a sweet sound to hear,
and was a most pleasant sight to the eyes of the one who begot him. What then
shall I do? Shall I rend my garment, and allow myself to roll on the ground,
and to wail, and to take it hard, and to show myself to those present like
some child crying out under blows and gasping? Or, attending to the necessity
of the things that come to be, that the law of death is inexorable, and passes
alike through every age, and dissolves all composite things in turn, shall I
not be astonished at what has happened? nor shall I have my mind overturned as
one dashed down by an unexpected blow, having long ago been trained beforehand
that, being mortal, I had a mortal child, and that nothing of human things is
firm, nor is it the nature of them to remain completely with those who have
acquired them? But even great and illustrious cities, distinguished both by
the splendor of their buildings, and by the power of those who dwell in them,
and by the remaining plenty both in the country and in the market, now bear in
ruins alone the tokens of their ancient dignity. And a ship, often saved from
the sea, and ten thousand times sailing swiftly, and carrying a countless
cargo for the merchants, has vanished by one assault of wind. And armies that
have often prevailed in battles over their adversaries =31.236=, when their
prosperity was overturned, have become a piteous sight and tale.
And whole nations and islands that have come forward to a great measure of
power, and have raised many trophies by land and many by sea, and have
gathered much wealth from spoils, have either been consumed by time, or having
become captives have exchanged freedom for slavery. And in general, whatever
of the greatest and unbearable evils you may name, life already has the
examples in advance. As then we distinguish weights by the turns of the
balance, and we test the difference of gold by rubbing it against the stone;
so, bringing our memory back to the measures set forth for us by the Lord, we
would nowhere fall out of the bounds of temperance. Whenever then some
unwanted thing befalls you, first of all, by the mind made ready beforehand,
not to suffer the disturbance, and then by the hope of the things to come to
make the present things lighter. For as those weak in the eyes, turning their
sight away from things too bright, rest upon flowers and grasses; so the soul
also must not look continually down upon the grievous, nor lie close upon the
present distresses, but turn its eye round toward the contemplation of the
things that truly are good. Thus the always rejoicing will be accomplished for
you; if your life always looks toward God, and the hope of the recompense
lightens the grievous things of life. Have you been dishonored? But look
toward the glory laid up in the heavens through endurance. Have you suffered
loss? But gaze upon the heavenly wealth and the treasure which you have stored
up for yourself through good works. Have you fallen from your fatherland? But
you have as fatherland the heavenly Jerusalem. Have you lost a child?
But you have angels, with whom you will dance around the throne of God, and
you will be gladdened with the eternal gladness. Thus, setting the expected
goods against the present distresses, you will keep for yourself the
grief-free and untroubled state of the soul, to which the legislation of the
Apostle calls us. Let neither the bright things of human affairs produce in
your soul immoderate delights, nor let the grievous things by gloom and
contractions humble its high and lofty spirit. For unless you have been so
trained beforehand concerning the things of life, you will never live a
waveless and untroubled life. And this will easily be accomplished for you, if
you have the commandment as a housemate, the one that exhorts you to rejoice
always; sending away the troublesome things of the flesh, and gathering the
gladnesses of the soul; and going beyond the sensation of present things, and
stretching your thought toward the hope of the eternal things, of which even
the imagination alone suffices to fill the soul with gladness, and to house
the =31.237= angelic exultation in our hearts; in Christ Jesus our Lord, to
whom be the glory and the power unto the ages. Amen.
