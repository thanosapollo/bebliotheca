* On the Body and the Soul

Of our father among the saints Athanasius, On the Body and the Soul.
A body that has the composition from the elements receives also dissolution,
and vanishes. Therefore the bodies both of men and of animals happen to be
easily corruptible and easily mortal, being composed of four elements, I mean
earth, sun, water, and air, and they themselves remain neither unaging nor
unfading as bodies, but they die just as the bodies also, all bodies having
been made from earth. But the simple and uncompounded among things are
well-enduring, and they remain undissolved until the consummation; such as the
sun, soul of man, light, luminaries, air, waters, fire, spirit, and all the
simple things, and in its own. And concerning the soul we speak thus: that
there are five passions in it, which are called psychic passions. And they are
these: anger, grief, fear, care, and envy. He therefore who has the reasoning
prudent can master them all. And the soul exists by itself, as a burning fire.
As the fire boils the things thrown into the cauldron, so also the fiery soul
thins and consumes the foods sent through to the belly. And even if it is
called soul, it is not so called because the essence is thus cold, as some of
the simpler have supposed; but because from much heat =28.1433= it dries up
the moist things, as one may be fully assured also from the operation of the
sun; for being fiery and hot it dries mud, and draws up all moisture. And that
the soul is hot by nature, be persuaded therefore from those who are dying;
for in the hour in which the soul is separated from the body, all the members
are found ice-like, and no less than a wintry affliction. For as long as the
soul has the food in abundance, the body flourishes and thrives; but whenever
it is in want of food, the body itself instead of food holds out toward the
heat of the soul. Just as neither a bronze vessel, that is a cauldron upon a
furnace, can bear without water the violence of the fire, for it is scorched
and is completely dissolved; so also the cauldron of the body, not having the
food, being licked away by the fiery soul is left altogether. If you wish to
try to learn from someone how the soul is disposed in the body, ask him: Tell
me, does the soul of man dwell in the whole body, or in one place? And if he
says to you that in the whole body, say to him: And how do they cut the hand
or the foot of the man, and the soul is not cut, but the man still lives and
does not die? And if he says to you that in one place, say to him: And how are
the other members of the man without soul? For everything that does not have a
soul is dead, and I do not know what you are saying. But I will tell you in
what place, and in what manner, and where the soul rests; it is established in
three necessary places of the body; and as the ray of the sun always occupies
one place, and enters the house, yet lights the whole house, so also the soul
dwelling in three places of the body lights the whole house of the body. And
it dwells in the heart, in the hinder part of the head, which they name
cotylon, and in the royal veins. In these three parts the soul dwells.
Therefore in the hour when the head is struck, or the heart, or the royal vein
pours out much blood, immediately the soul is separated from the body, and the
body is found dead. And the soul, dwelling in the three parts, imparts to the
whole body the illuminations of its own power.
