* Letter to John of Jerusalem
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

A letter inscribed to John the bishop of Aelia.
To the most reverend brother papa John, Epiphanius the bishop greeting in the
Lord.
We ought, beloved, not to be carried by the conceit of the lots, but ... 282 ...
for the divine scripture says: (Paul to Cor. 10:10) nor murmur as some murmured
and perished by the destroyer (cf. 10:9 they perished by the serpents) ... ...
and the God of peace will do with us according to his loving-kindness unto the
crushing of Satan under the feet of us Christians and the driving away of every
wicked pretext unto the bond of the unhypocritical love of Christ and of peace
and of right faith and of truth not being split from us. And since I heard that
some murmured against us, that in our going over upon the holy place of Bethel
to be gathered with your honor, as we came into the village called Anautha,
having seen a lamp burning and having asked we knew there was a church in the
place, and going in to accomplish a prayer we found a dyed veil on the door on
which there was painted some man-like idol-formed thing, which they said perhaps
was the impression of Christ or of one of the saints, for I do not remember
having seen, and knowing that it is an abomination in a church for such things
to be, I tore it and advised to clothe in it a poor man who had died, and those
who murmured said: "he ought to have exchanged the veil from his own before he
split it", although I had promised that instead of it I would send 283 another,
and I delayed to send because I had to seek of necessity, for I expected it to
be sent from Cyprus, now then I have sent what I found. Deign therefore to
command the presbyter of the parish to receive from the reader what has been
sent, and I entreat you, command that such things not be spread in the churches;
for it is fitting for your honor to care concerning all things and to be exact
concerning the things that profit the church of God and the peoples entrusted to
you.
