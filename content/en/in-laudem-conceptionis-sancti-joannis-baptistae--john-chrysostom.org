* In Praise of the Conception of Saint John the Baptist

Encomium on the conception of Saint John the Prophet, Forerunner and Baptist.

=50.787=

1. Moses the great, the servant of God, the chief of the prophets,
the traveler of the sea, the charioteer of the air, who set the table of the
manna, the lawgiver of God, who received the sacred tablets, the first
introducer of what is profitable for life: this man, so great and of such
stature, constructed a tabernacle for the people of Israel, according to the
pattern which he was taught by God on the mountain. And all the things, each
in turn, that were enclosed within the corded precinct, he made holy and
sacred; but the innermost of these was both inaccessible and not to be
entered, called the Holy of Holies =50.788=.

By this pattern of the tabernacle Solomon also built a temple, which surpassed
all the temples upon earth not only by the costliness of the material, nor by
the variety of the craft, but also by the form of the building, so that they
should never come to desire those among the Egyptians. And he makes the temple
after the image of the whole world, both the sensible and the intelligible.
For just as there is earth and heaven, and in the midst this firmament as a
partition, so God commanded that one also to be made; and dividing this temple
into two, and setting a veil in the midst, he granted that what was outside
the veil should be accessible to all, but what was within, inaccessible to all
and unseen, except for the high priest alone, as the angel commanded =50.789=,
speaking to Moses, to say to the sons of Israel: In the seventh month, on the
tenth of the month, humble your souls; that is, concerning the feast of
tabernacles; for then, at the feast of tabernacles, once in the year, the high
priest alone entered into the Holy of Holies to burn incense. In this feast of
tabernacles, then, Zacharias also entered to burn incense according to his
course, and he saw the angel standing at the right of the altar of incense.
And Zacharias was troubled when he saw, and fear fell upon him. And the angel
said to him: Do not fear, Zacharias; for your petition has been heard, and
your wife Elisabeth will bear you a son, and you shall call his name John; and
there will be joy and gladness for you; and many will rejoice at his birth.
For he will be great before the Lord, and he will drink neither wine nor
strong drink, and he will be filled with the Holy Spirit even from his
mother's womb. And he will go before in the spirit and power of Elijah, to
turn hearts of fathers toward children, and the disobedient to the prudence of
the righteous, to prepare for the Lord a people made ready.

Did you see a promise that surpasses prophetic power? This prophecy of
Zacharias bound and released the tongue: for it bound it when the birth was
disbelieved; and it released it when the birth came to pass. O the wonder!
John from the womb an evangelist, from the belly a wonder-worker,
foster-brother of the wilderness, and table-companion of the angels, the one
after the law among the prophets, a second Elijah, and a most true herald,
driving out error by the zeal of the Spirit, the forerunner and baptist, who
says: Repent, for the kingdom of heaven has drawn near; the son of Zacharias,
and the offspring of the barren woman, and the perfect accomplishment of the
people's prayers; the genuine introducer of repentance, and advocate of the
faithful who confess; the fulfillment of the law, and pioneer of grace, and
reproof of the impiety of the Jews; the confirmer of the heavenly doctrines,
and incorruptible judge of the people's faults; he who was born beyond hope
from a barren woman without offspring, and by grace the bridesman of Christ
who brought water out of the rock; the one who reined the streams of Jordan,
and ambassador of the cleansing of sinners; the one who knew beforehand the
incomprehensible mystery, and prophet of the presence of the Invisible; this
is John, who was born according to promise to Zacharias in deepest old age. O
the wonder! For behold, in the works themselves were shown the leapings of
John in the womb, which he made known. The world saw the gladness which the
Forerunner painted with his feet in the womb. But what honor shall we pay to
the righteous one, being surrounded on every side by his achievements? For he
announces good tidings while being carried in the womb, he works wonders when
he is born, he advances while training in the deserts, he is nourished by the
Spirit as he is led forward, he mediates between law and grace, he preaches a
baptism of repentance, he baptizes the Master when he arrives, while baptizing
he beholds the glory of the Trinity, and he bears witness of the grace to the
Truth, crying out he points out the one who was prophesied, confessing him
Master, and stronger than himself; he leads those who believe toward him,
teaching what is profitable to the crowds; he admonishes even tax-collectors
who are being baptized, he also rebukes Herod who is acting lawlessly, and he
also receives a blessed end.

With what words then shall we marvel? What tongue shall we set in motion among
these things? What of these shall we omit, and what shall we be able to say
concerning them? For great is the sea of praises, great also is the breadth of
the achievements; even the mere experience of the facts astounds those who
speak. But let Zacharias also come now, the father of the righteous one, who
was both condemned to silence and spoke again; he will narrate his own affairs
to us most clearly, how he spoke when the tongue had been held, how John is
born, and the sentence of the angel is undone, how the punishment is taken
away, and the healing comes. For the evangelist, introducing a brief
proclamation concerning Zacharias and Elisabeth his wife, =50.790= first
indicates their names and their nature and their dignity; then he adds praise
of their way of life and a trophy of their good-doing, in which he also shows
the condemnation of the silence, and makes known the release of fluent speech.
And hear from here the evangelical words: And they had no child, he says,
because Elisabeth was barren, and both were advanced in their days. And now I
see not only their childlessness, but I also consider the ancient image of the
righteous, I mean Abraham and Sarah; and I learn also another barrenness that
gives birth, and a childbearing of grace and of nature, a strange mystery of
generation; for this also is the manner of this promise. And an angel of the
Lord appeared to him, standing at the right of the altar of incense; and
Zacharias was troubled when he saw, and fear fell upon him. And the angel said
to him: Do not fear, Zacharias; for your petition has been heard, and your
wife Elisabeth will bear you a son, and you shall call his name John. Fair is
the possession of the promise, great is the gift of the vision, truly
Abrahamic is the boast; for as to him the conception of Sarah, so also to this
man the pregnancy of Elisabeth was announced beforehand.

But the prediction is equal; the order of the facts is not equal. For Abraham
suffered nothing terrible from the promise, but Zacharias after the good
tidings is found speechless, not having believed a faithful angel, and
receiving silence as the penalty. And Zacharias said, he says, to the angel:
Whereby shall I know this? for I am an old man, and my wife is advanced in her
days. What is this, Master? I offer the supplications to God, making atonement
for the whole people, and you yourself announce to me that a son will be born
to me? This is impossible. For old age is weak, and nature is feeble, and my
spouse is a barren rock; and how shall this be to me? Yet even if, as you have
said, a son will be born to me, what benefit will there be from him to the
people? And the angel: Great salvation will there be through him for the
people, and for all the nations; for he himself will turn all from error, and
will point out to them the way of salvation, becoming a mediator of God and
men. For he will go before in the spirit and power of Elijah, and he will be
called prophet of the Most High. This one will go before the face of the Lord,
saying: Behold the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world. 2. And
Zacharias answering said to the angel: Whereby shall I know this? And the
angel said: Since you seek a sign of the birth, and you disbelieve my words,
take a sure pledge: I am Gabriel, who stands in the presence of God, and I was
sent to speak to you, and to announce these things to you as good tidings. And
behold, you shall be silent, and not able to speak, until the day when these
things come to pass, because you did not believe my words, which will be
fulfilled in their season.

The judgment is unyielding to the one who has erred, and the wrath of the
punishment admits no delay. For truly the angel ought not to have been
disbelieved, nor the altar held in contempt, nor the time of the incense
reckoned a time of deceit; for the angel appeared to him while he was offering
incense. He was not reminded of the history of Abraham; for then also
barrenness and old age beget a child, and the things said were shown
everywhere easy to believe; for he had the wonder of such a childbearing;
whence the angel says to him, You shall be silent, and not able to speak,
until the day when these things come to pass, because you did not believe my
words. O the humane punishment, which rather worked a correction! for the
angel chastens the very voice that had slipped. For when he came out he was
not able to speak to them; and they perceived that he had seen a vision in the
temple; and he was making signs to them, and he remained mute. For the
evangelist also wrote well "he remained"; for the silence was waiting for the
voice being born, Zacharias for John, the old man for the child. O the wonder!
=50.791= the Word sojourns, and the voice proclaims beforehand; the Master
comes, and a servant is sent in advance. But why do I now, running ahead,
speak of the preaching of the wilderness, when I have another strange and
great voice of the preludes? For there are first works, higher than these
words. For still the Baptist, dwelling in the womb of his mother, still held
by the bonds of nature, still wrap[ped] in the maternal swaddling-bands, cries
out by proclamation the presence of the Master. For it came to pass, he says,
when Elisabeth heard the greeting of Mary, the infant leaped in her womb. He
is not yet born, and he speaks by leapings; he is not yet permitted to cry
out, and he is heard through works; he does not yet learn life, and he
proclaims God beforehand.

. Truly others have labored, and we have entered into their labors; the
prophets cried out beforehand, and they were not silent when they were struck;
a catalogue of patriarchs was in danger; tyrants arrayed themselves against
them; kings made war; the populace was angry; the whole world contended
against the contests of the righteous; from here they were brought before
tribunals, and from there they were beaten with scourges; from every side
punishments and tortures. But John, announcing as good tidings an end of the
winter to those who were in such a gloom of life, still bound in the
swaddling-bands of nature, pointed out to us the saving spring; and hastening
to proclaim with the voice him whom he made known with his feet, he was
forcing the bounds of nature; for he was startling Elisabeth who was slow to
give birth; he leaped, at once dancing and being impatient at the things by
which he was held in the womb; he cried out against the circuit of the months;
he shook the womb as an unjust prison, and he cried out by the leapings,
speaking such things to Elisabeth: You do wrong, O mother, being slow to give
birth, holding back the joy of the inhabited world, and hindering the
forerunner; you carry a prophet in the womb, you hold a royal soldier; you
hobble the swift runner of piety; you do wrong, O mother; you do not know what
is coming, you are ignorant of the things that will be. The light is arriving,
release the dawn; the sun will rise, let the morning star shine beforehand;
the Word is at the doors, and there is need of the voice; the bridegroom is
near, and it is right that the bridal chamber be spread in the Jordan; there
is need of blood that redeems the world, and I must from afar point out the
lamb. The race is swaddled in sins; he has come who unbinds those who are
fettered. There is need of a summoner, that all, having learned, may go to
meet the Master; and behold you hold the forerunner in the womb. Consider
then, mother, Adam held in Hades, the eternal groanings of Eve; for still the
blood of Abel cries out, appealing against Cain for the murder; and the very
elements groan with him. For this reason you were altogether delivered from
the passion of childlessness, that he who is from the barren woman might make
known him who is from the virgin; for this reason I leaped in the womb, that
the devil might no longer dance in the world.

Such things then John, not yet born, indicated to Elisabeth through the
leapings =50.792=. And the time was fulfilled for Elisabeth to give birth, and
she bore a son, and her neighbors and kinsfolk heard that the Lord had
magnified his mercy with her, and they rejoiced with her. And it came to pass,
on the eighth day they came to circumcise the child, and they were calling him
by the name of his father Zacharias. And his mother answered and said: He
shall be called John. And they made signs to his father, as to what he would
wish him to be called; and asking for a tablet, he wrote, saying: John is his
name. And they all marveled. And his mouth was opened immediately and his
tongue, and he spoke blessing God.

At any rate the infant is born, and the voice of the father returns; for so
the voice of John is shown crying out, because even the name itself has
appeared as an operation of a voice. For I, he says, am a voice of one crying
in the wilderness, prepare the way of the Lord; make his paths straight. For
having received the illumination of the Holy Spirit, he pointed out the Maker
to the things made, and he pointed out the Fashioner with his finger to all,
crying out with a great voice: Behold the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin
of the world. Bear therefore fruits worthy of repentance, and do not begin to
say in yourselves: We have Abraham as father; for I say to you, that God is
able from these stones to raise up children to Abraham. But why is our word
today also stretched out together with the warm zeal of the infant? Let us
hold back, if it seems good, the tongue for now at least, which has leaped
enough with the infant.

For it is good to measure the interpretation to the time, and to take delight
in what has been said, but also to wait for the contemplation of what follows.
For the Baptist did not force the bounds of nature by the leaping, but he also
himself waited the fitting time, knowing that the Master after him will
fulfill this also for us, crying: The earth shall be filled with the knowledge
of the Lord, as abundant water covers the seas, and from the risings of the
sun to the settings my name has been glorified, and in every place a pure
sacrifice is offered to my name. For no longer according to the law is there
one temple that receives the sacrifices, that is honored in them; but
throughout the whole inhabited world Churches send up glory to God sincerely.
There a single priest of the year was mortal, and filled with every sin; now
we have received as high priest of our souls Jesus Christ, Who did no sin, and
no deceit was found in his mouth. For we have, he says, a great high priest
who has passed through the heavens, our Lord Jesus Christ; to whom belongs all
glory, honor, and worship, with the Father and the all-holy Spirit, now and
always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
