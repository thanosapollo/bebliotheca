HOMILY On the apostolic saying that says: This know, that in the last days
there shall be grievous times.

=56.271=

αʹ. I am weak and poor, and unskilled in words of teaching; but when I see
your assembly, I forget the weakness, I am unaware of the poverty, I do not
know the inexperience; for such is the tyranny of your love. Wherefore even
more eagerly than those who are in plenty I set before you the table of
poverty. And you are the cause of this generosity, rousing those who have
grown slack by the eagerness of your hearing, gaping toward the hearing, and
hanging from the speaker's tongue. So too the chicks of swallows, when they
see the mother flying toward them, leaning out from the nest and making their
necks hang down, so receive the food from her.

So you too, looking toward the speaker with much eagerness, receive the
discourse brought to you from the tongue, and before the words have leapt from
our mouth, the mind has snatched what is said. Who then would not count both
you and us blessed for these things, that we speak Into ears that hear? The
labor is common, and the crown is common; the gain is common, and the reward
is common. For this reason also Christ blessed the disciples, saying: Blessed
are your eyes, for they see, and your ears, for they hear. Grant me to say
these words to you also, since you too display the same eagerness: Blessed are
your eyes, for they see, and your ears, for they hear. But that your ears hear
is clear; that your eyes also see, just as the disciples saw then, this I
shall try to prove, so that your beatitude may not be by half, but complete.
What then did the disciples see then? Dead men rising, blind men recovering
sight, lepers being cleansed, demons being driven out, lame men walking, every
fault of nature being set right. These things you too see now, if not with the
eyes of the body, yet with the eyes of faith. For such are the sights of
faith: they see the things that do not appear, and they discern the things not
yet done. Whence is this clear, that faith is sight of things not seen and
proof? Hear Paul saying: Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the
proof of things not seen. And this is the marvel, that these eyes of the flesh
see the things that are seen, but do not see the things that are not seen; but
the eyes of faith do the complete contrary: they do not see the things that
are seen, but they see the things that are not seen. And that they do not see
the things seen, and that they see the things not seen, Paul made clear,
saying thus: For the momentary lightness of our affliction works for us a far
more exceeding eternal weight of glory =56.272=, while we look not at the
things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen. And how could one
see the things that are not seen? How else, except with the eyes of faith? So
also elsewhere he says: By faith we understand that the ages were framed.

How? for we did not see. That the things which are seen were not made of
things which appear, he says. Do you wish me to bring yet another witness,
that the eyes of faith see the things that are not seen? Once writing to the
Galatians Paul said: Before whose eyes Jesus Christ was openly set forth among
you, crucified. βʹ. What are you saying, O blessed Paul? Did the Galatians see
him being crucified in Galatia? Do we not all confess that the passion took
place in Palestine, in the midst of Judea? How then did they see him being
crucified


the Galatians? With the eyes of faith, not with those of the flesh. Did you
see how the eyes of faith see the things that do not appear? From so great a
distance, then, and after so great a time they saw Christ being crucified. So
you too see the dead rising; so today you saw the leper being cleansed; so you
saw the paralyzed man rise, and you saw more than the Jews who were present.
For they, though present, did not receive the miracle, but you, though absent,
received the faith. So I justly said to you, Blessed are your eyes, for they
see. And if you wish to learn from elsewhere also that the eyes of faith see
the things that are not seen, but pass by the things that are seen; for they
would not otherwise see the things not seen, unless they despised the things
spoken of; hear Paul discoursing about Abraham, that with the eyes of faith he
saw the son Isaac being born, and so he accepted the promise.

For what does he say? And being not weak in faith, he considered not his own
body now dead. Great is the power of faith. For just as the reasonings of men
are cowardly and weak, so faith is strong and mighty. He considered not his
own body now dead. Do you see how he left the things that are seen? how he did
not look to old age? And yet it lay before his eyes; but he was looking with
the eyes of faith, not with those of the body. Therefore he did not see the
old age, nor the deadness of Sarah; he considered not The deadness of Sarah's
womb. Here he hints to us at the barrenness. For the weakness was double, one
from age, the other from the weakness of nature. For not only was the body
useless for childbearing because of age, but the womb itself had also died,
and the workshop of nature, even before old age, was useless because of the
barrenness. Did you see how many hindrances? The old age of the =56.273= man,
the old age of the woman; the barrenness more useless than old age; for this
too is especially a hindrance of childbearing. But nevertheless he passed by
all these things, and with the eyes of faith he rose up toward the heavens,
having as the greatest proof of the things promised the power of the one who
promised. Therefore Against the promise of God he did not stagger through
unbelief, but was strengthened in faith. For faith is a kind of strong staff,
and a safe harbor, freeing from the wandering of reasonings, and giving the
soul rest in much quiet. Blessed are your eyes, for they see; for it is
necessary to return again to this very saying. And yet the Jews also saw the
things that were happening then. But he does not bless this outward sight; for
this by itself does not see the miracles, but the inward. They saw the blind
man and said: This is he, this is not he; let us call his parents. Do you hear
them doubting? do you see that the sight of the body is not enough for the
contemplation of the miracle? Those present and looking said: This is he, this
is not he; but we who were not present do not say, This is he, this is not he;
but It is he himself. You have learned that absence does no harm, when there
are eyes of faith, and that presence does no good, when there are not eyes of
faith. For what did seeing profit them? Nothing. For we have seen more clearly
than they. Since then your eyes see a contemplation, and your ears hear a
hearing, which Christ blessed, come, let us set before you the pearls of the
Scriptures.

For just as Christ did not resolve the questions for the Jews, but even
increased the obscurity, since they were not attending; so also for you, since
you attend, it is necessary to bring the hidden things into the midst. For the
disciples also came and marveled, saying: Why do you speak to them in
parables? And he said, Because seeing they do not see. Therefore since you,
not having seen, have seen, it is necessary not to speak to you in a parable;
and since Hearing they do not hear. Therefore since you, not having heard
then, hear now no less than you heard then, it is necessary not to


deprive you of this table. For Christ blessed these no less than those; For
you have seen, he says, and have believed; blessed are they who have not seen,
and have believed. Do not then be sluggish toward virtue, because you were not
born in those times, but in this one. For if you wish, you have not been
damaged; just as indeed many even of those born then, since they did not wish,
were not benefited. γʹ. What then is that which was read today? This know,
that in the last days grievous times shall set in. Paul writes again to
Timothy. Fearful is the threat; but let us rise up; for he hints to us at
these times, and those after these, and those around the consummation itself.

This know, that in the last days grievous times shall set in. Short is the
saying, and great the power. For just as spices display their fragrance not by
quantity, but by nature; so also the divine Scriptures furnish us all the
benefit not by the quantity of the words, but by the power of what is
contained in them. So also the nature of incense =56.274= is fragrant even by
itself; but when you throw it into fire, then it displays all the pleasure
from it. So also the divine Scripture is most pleasant even by itself; but
when it lays hold of our soul, as if falling into a censer, it fills the whole
house with fragrance. This know, that in the last days grievous times shall
set in. He speaks of the consummation. What then is that to you, O blessed
Paul? and what to Timothy? and what to those who were hearing then? For a
little later they are about to die, to be snatched away from the coming evils
and wicked men. But I do not look only at the present things, he says, but I
also foresee the things to come. I do not spare only the present flock, but I
also agonize and fear for the one that is to come. For we scarcely take
thought for the men who are with us, but he takes much pains even for those
not yet born. So also an excellent shepherd, not only when he sees the wolves
coming upon the flock and drawing near the sheep, then foretells, but he also
reports them when they are far off. So also Paul, like an excellent shepherd,
seated on the high place of the dignity of prophecy, and with prophetic eyes
from above foreseeing the beasts running down, at the very consummation
rushing and stretching out against the flock, he foretells and testifies
beforehand, so that he may both prepare those not yet born to be sober, and
wall the whole flock with the prophecy.

For a loving father also, building a house for his children, often builds it
so splendid and great, not so that it may become useful to them only, but also
to grandchildren and those after them. So also a king, throwing a wall around
a city that is loved, makes it safe and strong and lasting, not that it may
serve in his generation only, but that it may become useful to all those after
as well, fitted not toward the engines of that time, but toward the assaults
that come later. So Paul also did. For since the apostolic writings are walls
of the Churches, through them he makes safe not only those who were then, but
also those who will be later. And he constructed this circuit so strong and
unbreakable, and drove it around the whole inhabited world with all safety, so
as to free those then, and those after them, and those now, and those who
follow afterward until the coming of Christ, from all the siege of the
enemies. Such are the souls of the saints: affectionate, caring, overshadowing
paternal goodwill with their affection, and conquering the love of nature, and
surpassing those birth-pangs; for they are of the Spirit and of divine grace.


δʹ. Do you wish me to show from elsewhere again, that the saints do not care
for their own affairs, nor fear only for those present, but also for those who
will be afterward? The disciples came to him, he says, as he sat on the
mountain, men already grown old and about to depart from the present life
after a little time. What then do they ask? what do they agonize over? what do
they fear? for whose sake do they bring the inquiry to the teacher? is it for
the things in their own life, or for the things in those times? By no means.
But passing by all those things what do they say? What is the sign of your
coming =56.275= and of the consummation of the age? Did you see them too
asking about the consummation of the age, and taking thought for the men who
will be? For the apostles do not look to their own things, but to those of
others, both all in common and each privately. Peter then, the chief of the
choir, the mouth of all the apostles, the head of that company, the protector
of the whole inhabited world, the foundation of the Church, the warm lover of
Christ; for Peter, he says, do you love me more than these? For this reason I
speak the praises, that you may learn that he truly loves Christ; for care for
the servants is the greatest proof of affection toward the Master. And I do
not say these things, but the beloved Master himself: If you love me, he says,
feed my sheep. Let us see then whether he truly has a shepherd's presidency,
whether truly care, whether he truly loves the sheep, whether he is truly
affectionate toward the flock, so that we may learn that he loves the shepherd
also; for he said this was a sign of that. This Peter then, having thrown away
all that he had, the net, all that was in the boat, and having left the sea,
the craft, the house.

Let us not look at this, that these things were few, but that they were all he
had; and let us praise the eagerness. For she who cast down the two denarii
did not lay down a great bulk of money, but displayed a great wealth of
purpose, just as this man also, in much poverty, furnished a great abundance
of eagerness. For what to another were lands, and slaves, and houses, and
gold, that to him was the net, and the sea, and the craft, and the boat. Let
us not then look at this, whether he left few things, but whether he did not
leave all. For this is what is sought, not to lay down few things or many, but
to bring in nothing less than one's own power. Having then left all, both
fatherland, and house, and friends, and kinsmen, and even safety itself; for
in this way he made the Jewish people his enemy; For already, he says, the
Jews had agreed, that if anyone should confess him Christ, he should become
out of the synagogue. Whence it is clear that he did not doubt, nor fear
concerning the kingdom of the heavens, but was strongly persuaded, both from
the very proof of the facts, and before the proof of the facts from the voice
of the Savior, that he would by all means inherit it. For having said, We have
left all, and have followed you, what shall we have? Christ answered them,
that You shall sit upon twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel.

And I have prepared these things, so that when I show him agonizing for his
fellow-servants, you may not say that he feared for himself. For how would he
have feared, when the very one who was about to crown him had declared
concerning the crown and the prizes? This Peter then, who left all, who was
confident concerning the kingdom of the heavens, when a certain rich man once
came and said to Christ, What shall I do that I may inherit eternal life? and
Christ answered him, If you wish to be perfect, go, sell your possessions, and
give to the poor, and come, follow me; then when that man was grieved at this,
and Christ saying to the disciples, See how hardly the rich enter into the
kingdom of the heavens; amen, amen I say to you, that it is easier for a camel
to go through the eye


of a needle =56.276= to enter, than for a rich man into the kingdom of God;
Peter, the one without possessions, the one confident concerning the kingdom,
who did not fear for his own salvation, who was clearly persuaded concerning
the honor laid up for him there, having heard these things said: Who can be
saved? What do you fear, O blessed Peter? what do you agonize over? what do
you tremble at? You have thrown all away, you have left all; the word is about
those who are rich, the saying is an accusation of them; but you continue
living in poverty and lack of possessions. But I do not look to my own, he
says, but I seek also the advantage of others. For this reason, being
confident concerning his own, he brought the inquiry concerning the others,
saying, Who can be saved?

εʹ. Did you see the care of the apostles? how they are one body? did you see
how Peter feared both for those present and for those to come? So also Paul.
Therefore he said: Know, that in the last days grievous times shall set in.
And elsewhere he does this again.

For when he was about to depart from Asia, and to be led to Rome, and from
there to go abroad to heaven; for the death of the saints is not death, but a
removal from earth to heaven, from the lesser things to the better, from the
fellow-servants to the Master, from men to the angels; since then he was about
to go away to God the Master of all, and he had ordered all his own affairs
well. For in the time that he was with the disciples, he set the teaching
before them with all accuracy, and he says: I am clean from the blood of all,
and I have left out nothing, he says, of the things that ought to be brought
in for salvation. What then? since he had made his own affairs safe, since he
was not about to be accused by the Master for the times that were his, did he
then neglect the souls after these things? By no means; but as one about to
give account even for those, so he says those things too to them with all
accuracy, and the very words which we shall read again: Take heed, he says, to
yourselves and to all the flock. Did you see how he was bound to their care?
For each of us cares for his own affairs, but the one who presides for those
of all. Therefore he says concerning the teachers: Who watch for your souls,
as those who shall give account. Fearful indeed is the judgment-seat, to give
account for so great a people; but, as I was saying, having called them he
says: Take heed to yourselves and to all the flock, in which the Holy Spirit
has set you as shepherds and overseers. What has happened? for whose sake do
you exhort? do you foresee some terrible thing? do you foresee something
grievous? is there some danger, some disaster, some war? Speak; for you have
stood higher than we; and you do not look only at the present things, but you
also foresee the things to come. Speak then for whose sake you command these
things and exhort. I know, he says, that after my departing grievous wolves
shall enter into the flock.

Did you see what I was saying, how he does not agonize and fear only for the
times that are his own, but also for those after his departing? For wolves
shall enter, he says; and not simply wolves, but Grievous wolves, not sparing
the flock. The war is double: absence of Paul, and onset of the wolves;
neither is the teacher present, and those who destroy will press upon them.
And consider the malice of beasts, and the device of wicked =56.277= men; they
watched for the absence of the teacher, and then attacked the flock. What
then? do you leave us without a protector, and only foretell the terrible
things, but devise no consolation? But if you do this, you rather increase the
cowardice, and cast down the spirits, and loosen the sinews, you paralyze the
hands of the hearers. For this very reason he first reminded them of the
Spirit: In which the Holy Spirit has set you as shepherds and overseers. Even
if Paul goes away, he says, yet the Paraclete is present. Did you see how he
winged their soul, reminding them of the divine teacher, through whom he
himself also was strong when present? For whose


sake then did he cast them into fear? That he might cast out slackness again.
For the one who advises must do both, neither letting the hearer be confident,
lest he become more slack; nor again only frightening, lest he fall into
cowardice. Having then reminded them of the Spirit, he cast out the cowardice;
and having spoken of the wolves, he cast out the slackness. Grievous wolves,
not sparing the flock. Take heed to yourselves. I have kept back nothing, he
says; remember me. For it is truly enough for confidence to remember Paul. And
he does not simply speak of the memory of himself, but the memory of the
achievements. For that he does not simply speak of the memory of himself, but
that remembering they may emulate, hear what he added: Remember me, that for
three years night and day I did not cease with tears and all those
lamentations, admonishing each one of you. I do not wish you simply to
remember me, but also the time and the admonition, and the zeal and the tears,
and all those lamentations.

For just as in the case of the sick the relatives, whenever having stretched
out many and long speeches they do not persuade them to take the foods and
medicines of those who are ill, weep, so as to bend them more; so Paul also
did in the case of the disciples; whenever he saw the word of teaching growing
weak, he brought in the healing from the tears.

ςʹ. And who would not have been ashamed seeing Paul weeping and lamenting,
even if he were more unfeeling than the stones? Did you see how there too he
foretold the things to come? And here he does this same thing, saying: This
know, that in the last days grievous times shall set in. And for whose sake
does he say it to Timothy, and does not say: Let those who are going to come
into being afterward see, that grievous times shall set in? But know you, that
you may learn that the disciple too, like the teacher, cares for those who
will be. For if he did not care, he would not have put the thought into him
similarly to that one. So Christ also does; for when the disciples came,
wishing to learn the things about the consummation, he says to them: And you
shall hear of wars. And yet they were not about to hear. For the body of the
faithful is one. And just as those who were then hear about those later, so
also we learn about the things that happened then. For as I said, we and they
are one body, bound to one another with accuracy, even if we hold the last
rank of the members; and time does not separate this body, nor place; for we
have been bound to one another, not by wrappings of sinews, but clothed on all
sides with bonds of love. For this reason he also discourses with them about
us, and let us hear their things. And it is worth seeking this too, why in the
world =56.278= he says that the gloomy things are crowded together toward the
ends of the present life.

For elsewhere also he says: In the last days some shall depart from the faith;
and here again he says: In the last days grievous times shall set in. And
Christ, proclaiming things in agreement with these, said: For at the
consummation you shall hear of wars and rumors of wars, and famines and
pestilences. For whose sake then at the consummation the greatness and the
gathering of the disasters? Some say that creation, growing weary and weak,
just as an aged body draws many diseases, so also itself growing old draws
many disasters. But the body comes to old age according to a weakness of
nature and a law; but the pestilences and the wars and the earthquakes are not
because of the old age of creation. For it is not because these created things
grow old that these diseases exist; For famines, and pestilences, and
earthquakes in places; but because the mind of men is about to be corrupted;
for all these things are chastisements of sins, and medicines of human
diseases. For the human diseases also are intensified then. And for whose sake


are they intensified then? he says. It seems to me, that since the court is
delayed, and the accounts are about to come, and the judge has not yet
arrived, those who are about to give account become more slack.

Which is what Christ also says about the wicked servant, that from this he
became more slack. For my Lord delays, says the servant, and for this reason
he beat his fellow-servants, and threw the master's substance into confusion.
For this reason also Christ, when the disciples came together and wished to
learn the day of the consummation, did not tell it, making us continually in
agony by the uncertainty of the things to come, so that each, always expecting
what is to come, and being in hopes of the coming of Christ, may be more
zealous. For this reason someone exhorts saying: Do not delay to turn to the
Lord, nor wait from day to day, lest you be destroyed as you delay. The end is
uncertain, he says, and it is uncertain for this reason, that you may always
be zealous. For this reason as a thief in the night, so the day of the Lord
comes; not that he may steal, but that he may make us more secure. For he who
foresees the thief lives in watchfulness, and having lit a lamp watches
continually. So then you too, having lit the light of faith and of right
conduct, keep the lamps bright in continual watchfulness. For since we do not
know when the bridegroom comes, we must be prepared continually, so that when
he comes, he may find us sober.

ζʹ. I wished to stretch out the discourse; but even these things the weakness
of the body scarcely allowed me to say, because of which I have been torn from
you this long time. For the time is long to me, not by the number of the days,
but by the measure and the disposition of the soul. For to those who love even
the short time of separation appears long and unspeakable. For this reason
Paul also, having withdrawn a little from the Thessalonians, said: We,
brethren, being taken from you for a short time, in presence, not in heart,
endeavored the more abundantly to see your face. And if Paul, who knew how to
philosophize most of all men, did not bear it for a short time of an hour, how
shall we bear so many days? But he, no longer bearing it, having the remnants
of the illness, I ran to you, thinking I would apply the greatest medicine,
the meeting with your =56.279= love.

For to me even more useful than physicians' hands, and more profitable than
all the consolation from there, is to enjoy your love; of which may it be to
revel continually, by the prayers and intercessions of all the saints, unto
=56.280= the glory of our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom and with whom to the
Father be glory, honor, power, together with the Holy Spirit, now and always,
and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
