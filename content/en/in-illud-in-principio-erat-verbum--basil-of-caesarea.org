Homily 16.
On that saying, "In the beginning was the Word".
Every voice of the Gospels is more sublime than the rest of the teachings of
the Spirit, inasmuch as in those He spoke to us through the servants the
prophets, but in the Gospels the Master conversed with us in His own person.
And of the evangelical preaching itself the most loud-voiced, who has uttered
things greater than every hearing and higher than every mind, is John, the son
of thunder; whose preface of the evangelical writing we have just now heard
read: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word
was God." These things I know many even of those outside the word of truth,
who think highly of themselves for worldly wisdom, both have marveled at, and
have dared to mix into their own compositions. For the devil is a thief, and
he carries our things out as tales to his own interpreters. If then the
fleshly wisdom so marveled at the power of the words, what shall we do, we the
disciples of the Spirit, if we hear them as a side-matter and reckon the power
that is in them to be some small thing? And who is so sick with insensibility
as not to be struck with amazement at such beauty of thought and such
unattainable depth of doctrines, and to desire their true comprehension? But
it is not hard to marvel at beautiful things; to come into precise
understanding of what has been marveled at, that is hard and hard to attain.
Since even of this sensible sun there is no one who does not highly =31.473=
praise it, greeting its greatness and its beauty, and the due measure of its
rays, and its gleaming light; yet if he more violently contends to send the
casts of his own eyes against the disk, not only will he not see the things so
eagerly sought, but he will go away having ruined also the precision of his
sight. Some such thing I think the mind suffers, when it contends to make a
precise examination of the words set before us. "In the beginning was the
Word." Who will conceive worthily the things concerning the beginning? What
power of words will be found equally able to set forth what has been
conceived? About to hand down to us the things concerning the theology of the
Son of God, he gave no other beginning to the account than the beginning of
all things. The Holy Spirit knew those who would fasten upon the glory of the
Only-begotten; He foreknew those who would put forward to us the sophisms
devised for the overthrow of the hearers. That "If He was begotten, He was
not"; and, "Before He was begotten, He was not"; and, "He received His
hypostasis from things that were not."

Such things tongues sharpened by persuasive speech utter, beyond every
two-edged sword. So that it might not be permitted to anyone to say such
things, the Holy Spirit, anticipating through the Gospel, says, "In the
beginning was the Word." If you hold this utterance, you will suffer nothing
terrible from the evil craftsmen. For if that one says, "If He was begotten,
He was not," you say, "In the beginning He was." "But," he says, "how was He
before He was begotten?" Do not let go of the "was"; do not abandon the "In
the beginning." The peak of a beginning is not grasped; the outside of a
beginning is not found. Let no one cheat you by the many senses of the word.
For there are many beginnings of many things in this life; but one beginning
over all, the one beyond. For "the beginning of a good way," said the Proverb.
But the beginning of a way is the first motion from which we start the
journey, of which what is behind can be found. And, "The beginning of wisdom
is the fear of the Lord." There is also something set before this beginning;
for the beginning of taking up the arts is the elementary instruction. So the
fear of the Lord is an element of wisdom; but there is something older than
this beginning, the state of the soul of one not yet made wise and who has not
taken up the fear of God. Political powers also are called beginnings, the
eminences of offices. But these beginnings are beginnings of some things, and
each is relative to something.

For the beginning of a line =31.476= is the point; and the beginning of a
surface is the line; and the beginning of a body is the surface; and of speech
according to composition the beginnings are the elements. But that beginning
is not such. For it is bound to nothing; it is slave to nothing; it is
contemplated with nothing; but it is free; without a master, released from
relation to another; unsurpassable by thought, which it is not possible to
overstep with reasonings, of which it is not possible to discover what is
beyond. For if you contend with the imagination of the mind to run past the
beginning, you will find it running ahead of you and meeting your reasonings
first. Let your mind run as far as it wishes, and be stretched toward the
things above; then you will find it having wandered countless ways and having
stepped much on emptiness, and coming back again to itself, because it cannot
make the beginning lower than itself. Because always the beginning is found
more outward and greater than what is conceived. "In the beginning," then,
"was the Word." O the wonder! How all the utterances were yoked to one another
with equal honor! The "was" has equal force with the "In the beginning." Where
is the blasphemer? Where the Christ-fighting tongue, that says, "There was
once when He was not"? Hear the Gospel: "In the beginning He was." And if He
was in the beginning, when was He not? Shall I groan at their impiety, or
abominate their ignorance? "But before He was begotten, He was not." For do
you know when He was begotten, so that you can apply the "before" to time? For
"before" is a temporal utterance, setting one thing before another in
antiquity. And how is it reasonable that the maker of time should have His
generation subject to temporal names? "In the beginning," then, "He was." If
you do not depart from the "was," you will give no entrance to the wicked
blasphemy. For just as those at sea, when they ride at two anchors, despise
the swell, so you too will laugh at this wicked turmoil that comes from the
spirits of wickedness into life and shakes the faith of many, if you have your
soul moored in the security of these words. And our mind seeks, who was in the
beginning? "The Word," he says. What kind of word? The human word? Or the word
of the angels?

For the Apostle also hinted to us that the angels have their own =31.477=
tongue, saying, "If I speak with the tongues of men and of angels." But of
"word" there is also a twofold sense. For one is that which is brought forth
through the voice, this which after being brought forth perishes in the air;
another is the inner word, existing in our hearts, the conceptual. And another
is the technical word. See that the homonym of the utterance never trips you.
For how was the human word in the beginning, when man received the beginning
of his coming-to-be somewhere below? Before man, beasts; before man, cattle,
all creeping things; all that is of land and all that is of water, birds of
heaven, stars, sun, moon, plants, seeds, earth, sea, heaven. Therefore the
human word was not in the beginning, nor even that of the angels. For all
creation is lower than the ages, having received the beginning of being from
the Creator. And the word in the heart is itself younger than each of the
things conceived. But hear the Word in a manner befitting God. For speaking to
you of the Only-begotten, he called Him Word. Just as a little later he will
also call Him light, and life, and resurrection, and having heard "light" you
are not brought down to this sensible light visible to eyes, nor having heard
"life" do you understand this common life which even the irrational animals
live; so also hearing "Word," guard lest by the weakness of your mind you be
carried down to earth-bound and low thoughts. But search out the meaning of
the saying.

Why Word? That it might be shown that He came forth from the mind. Why Word?
Because He was begotten without passion. Why Word? Because He is the image of
the Begetter, showing in Himself the whole Begetter, having portioned off
nothing from there, and existing perfect in Himself; as also our word images
our whole thought. For what we have conceived in the heart, these we have
brought forth in the utterance, and what is spoken is an image of the thought
in the heart. For from the abundance of the heart the word is brought forth.
And our heart is like some spring, and the word brought forth like some stream
flowing from this spring. So much then is the outflow as what first wells up;
and as is the hidden, so much also is the appearing. He said Word, then, that
he might set forth to you the passionless generation from the Father, and
theologize to you the perfect existence of the Son, and through these show the
timeless conjunction of the Son with the Father.

For our word too is an offspring of the mind, being begotten without passion;
for it is neither cut =31.480= nor divided nor flows; but the whole mind
remaining in its own constitution brings the whole word into subsistence
complete; and the word having come forth contains in itself all the power of
the begetting mind. Taking then as much as is pious from the utterance "Word"
toward the theology of the Only-begotten; whatever you find appearing
discordant and unfitting, decline this and pass beyond it by every device. "In
the beginning was the Word." And if he had said, "In the beginning was the
Son," with the name of Son the notion of passion would have entered you. For
since among us those who beget beget in time, and beget with passion,
therefore anticipating, he said Word, correcting beforehand the unseemly
assumptions, that he might keep your soul unwounded. "And the Word was with
God."

Again the "was," because of those who blaspheme that He was not. Where was the
Word? Not in a place; for the uncircumscribed are not contained by place. But
where was He? With God. Neither is the Father in a place, nor is the Son
caught in some encompassing and admitted circumscription; but infinite is the
Father, and infinite the Son. Whatever you conceive, and wherever you go with
your spirit, you will find it filled with God; everywhere you will find the
hypostasis of the Son co-extended. "And the Word was with God." Marvel at the
precision of each utterance; he did not say, "The Word was in God," but, "with
God," that he might set forth the distinctness of the hypostasis. He did not
say, "in God," lest he give a pretext for the confusion of the hypostasis. For
wicked also is that blasphemy of those who attempt to mix all things, and say
that the subject is one, Father and Son and Holy Spirit, and that differing
names are applied to the one thing. Wicked is the impiety, and to be fled no
less than that of those who blaspheme that the Son of God is unlike in essence
to God and Father. "And the Word was with God." Then having used the utterance
"Word" to set forth the passionlessness in the generation, he quickly
also remedied the harm that comes to us from "Word." And as if snatching Him
from the slander of the blasphemers, "What," he says, "is the Word? The Word
was God." Do not for me spin out technical differences of words, nor from your
evil craft smear some blasphemy on the =31.481= teaching of the Spirit. You
have the declaration; submit to the Lord. "The Word was God; this one was in
the beginning with God." Again he recapitulates in few words all his theology,
which the evangelist handed down to us concerning the Only-begotten. Who is
this? This one, the Word who is God. For since he articulated for you the
thought concerning Him, as having stamped on your soul through the teaching
the things unknown, and having housed the Word, Christ, in your heart, he
says, "This one." What kind of "this"? Do not look outward, searching round by
the demonstrative utterance for the one being pointed out to you, but enter
into the hidden things of your own soul, and this one whom you were taught as
God who is in the beginning, who came forth as Word, who is with God: having
recognized him and been struck with awe, and having worshipped your own
Master, the one established in you through the teaching, know that this one
was in the beginning; that is, always with God His own Father.

Preserve these few utterances for me, having stamped them like a seal on your
memories. These will be a most unbreakable wall against the assaults of those
who plot; these are a saving safeguard of souls for those who hold them out.
If someone comes to you and says, "Not being, He was begotten; for if He was,
how was He begotten?" turn away the blasphemy against the glory of the
Only-begotten as a voice of demons. And yourself, returning, come to the
utterances of the Gospels: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was
with God, and the Word was God. This one was in the beginning with God." Say
the "was" a fourth time, and you will abolish their "was not." Let these
remain unshaken foundations of the faith. Upon these we shall build, God
granting, also what remains. For it is not possible to discuss all things with
you at once, lest by the immoderation of the discourse we make useless for you
what has been laboriously gathered. For the mind, being too weak to grasp all
things at once, suffers like a stomach that because of excess of satiety
cannot bring what has been sent down to digestion. I pray then that you be
sweetened by the taste, and benefited by the assimilation. And I myself stand
ready for you for the ministry of what remains, in Christ Jesus our Lord, to
whom be the glory and the power unto the ages of ages. Amen.
