=14.1308= The character of the diction of the Epistle inscribed to the Hebrews
does not have the unskilled quality in word of the Apostle, who confessed
himself to be unskilled in word, that is, in phrasing. But the Epistle is more
Greek in composition of diction, everyone who knows how to judge differences
of phrasings would confess; and again that the thoughts of the epistle are
wonderful, and not second to the acknowledged apostolic writings, this too
everyone attending to the apostolic reading would agree to be true. To these
after other things he adds saying: And I, declaring, would say that the
thoughts are of the Apostle, but the phrasing, and the composition, of someone
who remembered the apostolic things, and as it were wrote as scholia the
things said by the teacher. If therefore any Church has this Epistle as
Paul's, let it be well thought of also in this, for not in vain have the
ancient men handed it down as Paul's. But who it was who wrote the Epistle,
the truth God knows. And the history that has come down to us is of some
saying that Clement who became bishop of the Romans wrote the Epistle; and of
some that Luke who wrote the Gospel and the Acts.
