** Epilogue
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1-h1
:END:

The pious, faithful, innocent, chaste man
sacrifices to God the Father
the gifts of conscience,
with which the blessed mind abounds within.
Another cuts back his money,
from which the needy may live.
We consecrate swift iambics
and whirling trochees,
lacking holiness
and not able to relieve the poor.
Yet God approves
a pedestrian song and hears it kindly.
In a rich man's house
furniture is placed through every corner:
a golden cup gleams
nor is a basin polished from bronze wanting,
there is also a clay pot
and a heavy and ample silver dish,
some things are of ivory,
some are hollowed from oak and elm.
Every vessel becomes useful
that is suited to the master's use;
for they furnish the house
both those bought at great price and those prepared of wood.
Me, in the paternal hall,
as a worn little vessel for perishable
uses Christ fits
and allows to remain in a corner's part.

Lo, a clay gift,
we enter within the palace of salvation.
Yet even to have offered the lowest
service to God is profitable.
Whatever that may turn out to be,
it will help to have sounded Christ with the mouth.
