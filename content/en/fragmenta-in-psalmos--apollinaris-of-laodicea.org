Ps 7,8. 9a When the judge has arrived, he says, many nations circle him
gathered together; making the church known and announcing, the Lord says they
will be gathered to him, and when this has come about the judge who has handed
over the kingdom will turn back above and toward the height. 2 Ps 8,1 And the
Word shows that the winepress, just as also the harvest in the Gospel, is the
gathering of the fruits at the consummation, and the fruits of human beings
are works and reasonings according to Isaiah: "And I," he says, "come to
gather their works and their reasonings." 3 Ps 8,5®9 But you, he says, the
maker of such great works, take thought for a human being who is altogether
small, and you made remembrance of him when he had fallen away from you and
you visited him when he had been brought down into weakness; and his making
was precious to you even from the beginning, and according to the earthly body
he had been made a little lower than angelic glory. For glory was about him as
one reigning on earth over the works made by you and holding all the
irrational nature under himself, not only the land-dwelling and easily taken
but also the aerial and the watery, each of which escapes the hands of a human
being, the one through motion in the height, the other through concealment in
the depth and travel through waters, yet it is caught by wisdom and mind and
the earthly one takes the things in heaven and in waters; from which it
appears that according to the intellectual he surpasses the irrational and
unthinking nature of animals, yet is a match for the intellectual and
angelic, being a partner with the irrational according to the bodily.
4 Ps 15,4cd He speaks the manner of the new piety, that without bloods, and he
shows the change in the Holy Spirit according to which we are no longer the
former ones nor still sons of human beings upon the same names but sons of
God. 5 Ps 16,6 For having been heard even formerly, he says, I need to be
heard by him now also. For Paul says we pray with thanksgiving; for the memory
of former things prepares the thanksgivings that follow.
6 Ps 16,10®12 He narrates the work and figure of those who set upon him, the
work a murderous plot and flattering words toward an ambush, and that they are
very sleek in body, fenced about with fat, and looking askance with the eyes
like a lion rushing to the hunt and sitting hidden in wait. Such was Saul
toward David, saying that he had found favor in his eyes and ordering him to
stand by and having him as attendant, yet suspecting him and attempting to
kill him from an ambush. 7 Ps 16,14c A hidden punishment has been prepared for
them, he says, which will pass inside their belly according to what is said of
Judas: "Let the curse enter like water into his inward parts and like oil in
his bones."
8A Ps 26,4 Again he marks off the love toward God through that toward the
temple dedicated to him. For the one who loves God is gladdened at the places
dedicated to him, not exhausting the longing upon the sight of these (for it
is a small thing to take delight in handmade works), but just as someone also
gladly looks at a garment of the beloved and a book as being raised through
these to the memory of that one, so the

lover of God is raised toward God also through a house and a vessel dedicated
to God. One thing then and this alone I sought, to dwell in God's temple,
which
is what Anna does, and to exult in the beauty of the building, to look toward
this. A house of God then had not yet been constructed. When David sang these
things, he raised the word to the tent and left it to those able to hear
spiritually to lift the word up to the spiritual house.
9 Ps 32,6 In such things the theology of the one God appears, encompassing the
Trinity. Then God is theologized as creator, and the creation is an operation
of the Word and of the Spirit; and it is clear that the voices are human where
also a mouth is spoken of from which word and spirit, yet otherwise understood
in the case of God according to the uncompounded and wholly bodiless, that is,
the Word being with God who is, and the Spirit being, the one being a
manifestation of the Father (named Word according to this), the other a
participation of the Word as spirit from a mouth. And having said heavens and
powers of heavens he does not divide the creation, of those by the Word, of
these by the Spirit (for the things operated upon the Trinity are undivided),
but he phrases the same thing differently, as in "Jacob went into Egypt and
Israel sojourned in the land of Ham" an entrance is not divided as for Jacob
as
another, and a dwelling as for Israel as another; for from parallelism it
shows
the same, and this custom we would find in many places. 10 Ps 33,7 Showing
himself he says he became poor, was in anguish in the flight, escaped this
danger by the will of God, as the device of the madness would not have had
power otherwise unless according to the will of God.
11 Ps 33,9 He calls taste the experience of the things, which was present to
him then also. What then has come about in him according to the kindness of
God, not to be overlooked when pursued by a foreign king but to be saved from
two most grievous and stronger enemies, this he adds as common to all as not
coming about upon him alone but also going to be upon the others, as many as
join themselves to God by the hope toward God. In this the blessed must be,
not
in some other thing, which David says in "Why do you love vanity and seek a
lie? and know that the Lord has made his holy one wonderful." 12 Ps 33,20 He
commands to look to the end and to take courage in the deliverance from the
afflictions, not to be distressed from the very beginning whenever it happens
that one is afflicted. For the soul must be prepared thus, not as not going to
toil but as not going to be defeated by the toils.
13 Ps 34,1®3 He figures the word about God into a whole likeness of a human
being just as also Isaiah, in order that he may show that the impossible in
him
is possible for God, claiming that he wage war and arm himself with shield and
spear and shut off the assaults of the enemies with a wall in the midst. And
this too Isaiah shows in Christ, foreseeing him coming from the punishment of
the unjust and the garment bloodied from the slaughter of the foreigners ("Who
is this who has arrived from Edom, redness of garments from Bosor, and the
things following?"), so that the things referred godwise to the Father it is
clear both belong to the Son and are fulfilled unto the Son. 14
Ps 34,5 Being made unseen falls to those, just as dust driven by wind, and
that
they are thrust out not by a human hand because the just rather suffer this
from the unjust but that this comes about through angels, as the Lord says
that
"The holy ones will go out and will choose out from the midst of the just the
unjust and will separate them." For now the discordant things are mixed
together, but then they will be divided

according to the differing of the manner according to what was said again also
by the Savior that "And there are many mansions with my Father." 15 Ps 34,6
The
roads, he says, of the impious are dark, they do not know how they advance.
And
this he claims to be accomplished, praying that the practice of the unjust be
held by darkness, inasmuch as it also belongs to darkness, and that they fall
down having slipped, because they also travel a slippery road (for there is
nothing in injustice that holds to a support), and that they become unseen
under
angels, being pursued, which they themselves do against the just. 16 Ps 34,13
He flees, he says, to his own strength, which is God. For someone seeks God by
fasts and humiliations, turning the whole soul to invocations of God according
to the leisure from bodily pleasures, when they occupy the mind against the
prayer and slip in at the bosom of the soul.
17 Ps 35,6 For the things ordained by God are firm and unshaken, such as what
was said to Noah that he would no longer bring a flood for the destruction of
living things, which agrees with the word about God's long-suffering. For the
firmness of the word also has been defined through all, being some great and
manifest thing. And so also as many other things as have been ordained and
defined according to the righteousness and goodness of God have a solid and
unshaken remaining forever; such also is "Once I swore in my holy one if I
will
lie to David." To this also "For the gifts of God are without regret" tends.
18
Ps 35,7a Again he compares the height of righteousness to mountains. And this
has a threat of the repayments, that it is impossible, righteousness being
overseer of the things that come about, for anything to come about contrary to
the just, so that even those who now escape the punitive repayments or do not
obtain the repayments will altogether carry them later; and this is
unchangeable
with God, as Paul said with confidence that God the just judge will give back
to him the crown of righteousness on that day. 19 Ps 35,10a For the things
unto
life are supplied in abundance by God, without the supply of which life would
be destroyed. 20 Ps
35,10b He is lit, he says, by the light from the heavens under the one who set
the sun for light of day; but these things are in common to just and unjust,
at
which he also marveled at the philanthropy, the ungrateful not being separated
from the enjoyment of the things from God, but the exceptional already and
proper to those belonging to God.
21 Ps 36,7 He exhorts those who are quiet to await God, not being troubled by
the present things but hoping in the things to come according to the apostolic
"Rejoicing in hope, enduring in affliction," and Jeremiah's "It is good for a
man when he takes up a yoke in his youth. He will sit alone and will be
silent,
he will give a cheek to the one who strikes him, he will be filled with
reproaches." 22 Ps 36,9 He commands to look far and not to be struck by the
present things, and to foresee destruction of the wicked, and a holding of the
good; and these things also at the end, that is, also in another age, since
the
present one does not have the outcomes of these, but the things on the earth
are rather prevailed over by the wicked. Wherefore also "The whole world," he
says, "lies in the wicked one." 23 Ps 36,14ab He says that the wicked move
against themselves the things they prepare against the just, as if someone
having drawn a sword should be slain by it, and that the plot will be vain
like
a bow broken by the hand of an archer. And upon David this has been fulfilled
also bodily, that they would strike David with their own sword which they drew
against David. 24 Ps 36,27
Such an exhortation again from the prediction, to flee from evil, and to
practice the action of the good in hope of the eternal quiet, and a reasoning
upon the matter that the overseer is just and does not overlook those who
belong to him.

25 Ps 37,4a He compares his much-toiling and much-suffering character to a
body
altogether being ill-treated, such as also in Isaiah "From feet to the head
there is no soundness in him but wound and bruise, or a swelling threat";
these
then are signs of wrath, and the occasion of the wrath the sins, so that the
sufferings lead to a sense of the sins and according to this benefit the souls
just as also the bodies when they are led from insensibility into sense. 26 Ps
37,12 He laments a standing-away of friends and a rising-up of enemies, which
things are also seen in the sufferings of the Savior, the disciples having
been
scattered by fear, and the wicked having used all bitterness and malice
against
him, toward whom he also fulfilled the silence here predicted.
27 Ps 37,14 Having, he says, the just things to speak against those who
calumniate, yet knowing that the word would not be for benefit, I bore the
false-speakings without resentment. And I was silent as not hearing and as not
having to refute; for what would I even say and answer? Unto nothing, as also
the Lord said: "If I tell you, you will not believe; and if I ask, you will
not
answer." And when human beings do not hear, the word is useless toward those,
but useful toward God who hears. 28 Ps 37,=22.23= Human beings would not say
these things for themselves, but Christ in place of human beings for human
beings and, suffering as just from unjust, bringing their things upon himself,
would say them; where "Why have you forsaken me?" and where "Do not forsake
me," and "Do not stand away from me," as upon him at least this is impossible.
"For I am not," he says, "alone, since my Father is with me." He asks the
salvation of human beings as his own, having something also proper to the
asking as far as concerns the human in mixture with godhead (and this was the
passible connatural with flesh), and having also the dignity of the nature
that
stands above, by which he also fulfilled the things of the asking.
29 ps 38,4 And hearing, he says, and reckoning the bold and abusive things of
the enemy and seeing with how much authority of words he uses to whom it was
not even fitting to speak, I was kindled over the dishonored righteousness. 30
Ps 38,8a I do not have, he says, in myself anything worthy of expectation, but
with you and expecting your things I wait, holding out in the present toils in
hope of goods to come. 31 Ps 38,9b I bear, he says, being reproached now and
not
even answering back, knowing somehow thus that God wills me to be trained by
the reproaches. Thus David himself bore being abused by Shimei, saying that
the
Lord told him to curse David.
32 Ps 39,2®4b He speaks upon himself the waiting of the just from the age and
the petitions, through which they will hear God complete the ascent from Hades
and the firm life unto an age and the support in this in gladness and
brightness and hymns of God, such as was also in 29 "I cried to you, and you
healed me and you brought up my soul from Hades, you saved me from those going
down into a pit," and the things following; in which he clearly referred the
things of Adam to himself. And having brought on the resurrection at the end
he
says "That my glory may psalm to you and I may not be pierced." 33 Ps 39,4cd
For
the things of Christ extend unto the salvation of all, neither having come
about through him nor standing in him alone. Wherefore Paul says "If the dead
are not raised, neither has Christ been raised." For if the end for the sake
of
which the cause would have come about were not the resurrection of Christ, and
fearful is the mystery, a dissolution of death and the ascent of the souls
from
Hades which also is the true hopes unto God; about which hope Paul speaks,
joining faith to it and bringing on the love toward the one who has so greatly

been gracious and stretching the goods unto an age: "These three remain," he
says; "faith hope love." 34 Ps 39,5 In this, he says, a human being is
blessed,
in making hope in God and seeking the eternal things, not being baited by the
things that appear for a short time nor turning aside into a lie from the
truly
good. For everything is a lie which human beings think to provide through
themselves, and only true is what is from God, which he also showed through
the
whole economy of human beings, having refuted all the human things, and having
brought on the perfect good from himself. 35 Ps 39,12 Saying himself he shows
the church, and he presents us as needing mercy, that we may remain in the
grace; and the asking of the mercy is strong, because it is said to pass not
unto human beings but unto him. For thus we human beings obtain the things
from
God, because Christ in our place is the one receiving the good unto us, as he
says "And I appoint a covenant to you just as my Father appointed to me." 36
Ps
39,14 For there is no power unto salvation for the church of Christ except
that
from God, because neither weapons nor money nor circuits of walls nor a
holding
of land nor any such thing is in us as in Jews, so that it is wonderful and
not
less than any of the other wonders that unarmed human beings, being warred
upon
by countless nations and unprepared and not defending themselves, both remain
and increase; such was the wonder of the bush burning and not being burned up,
and this is an imprint of Christ conquering through suffering. For the church
that suffers and is not dissolved carries in itself all the proofs of the
wisdom and the evident marks of the help from above.
37 Ps 39,18 And unto these ends, he says, the church will advance, having
first
become poor and having come through much poverty, that in the world, and
having
been enriched by the power from you and having been glorified by the help from
you which she asks not to delay. And through the asking he will not delay, as
"The one who is coming will come and will not delay" is said. 38 Ps 40,1 This
one also is joined on unto the same subject. For the person of the Savior and
the memory of the passion and the prediction of the resurrection and of the
return into heaven. Just now then he said "I am poor and needy," and it would
be brought on consequently as said by him exceptionally unto him.
39 Ps 40,2a Even if understanding is a good especially about all those who
become poor in the world through righteousness, and understanding is in
considering such poor to be rich, yet the one who knows and understands the
one
who is truly rich, but poor through us, is truly blessed; and all blessedness
follows the knowledge of God. 40 Ps 40,5 Again the one who suffers for human
beings speaks the sins of human beings as his own and seeks upon himself the
mercy upon these unto us, who looses the contempt of those who are ignorant
upon him by showing forth the glory, upon him according to his own power, upon
us according to the grace of Christ. 41 Ps 40,6 For this was through zeal, he
says, first for the unseen, then for the seen Jews, to cast out from the
beginning according to nature the true king, having reckoned to come into a
holding of the inheritance that did not belong; wherefore they also said "Come
let us kill him, and the inheritance will be ours." 42 Ps 40,9 They hasten, he
says, to put the just one to death, not knowing that his death is a sleep
coming
about unto resurrection and introducing not a taking-away of the life but a
translation into heaven according to "His life was taken up from earth." 43 Ps
40,10 There will be, he says, the resurrection dissolving the gathering of the
enemies upon us and the betrayal of the friends. For already a disciple also
has risen up, being counted worthy of peace from me and numbered among those
trusted and sharing food, yet being a serpent he was convicted, wanting to
take
hold of a heel. And of this the Lord himself also made mention as said unto
him, saying "But that the scripture may be fulfilled, the one who eats my
bread
has lifted his heel against me." 44 Ps 40,13

Exceptionally upon Christ we hear the innocence, the purity from all evil,
which the creator sought from human beings who had come to be for this, that
they be worthy of the maker and remain in the good because all the makings of
God are very good, and seeking he found it in none of those from Adam, but in
the one who is according to flesh from Adam alone and who has not shown forth
the like things of that one's transgression but has the divine form remaining
even with the fleshly covering, according to which also the heavenly voice
bears witness: "This is my beloved son in whom I was well pleased." And the
remaining before God and eternal confirmation is that in heaven, which also in
the fifteenth has been predicted after the resurrection in "You will fill me
with gladness with your face." For he has gone up into heaven to be manifested
to the face of God for us, bringing near in himself what was separated and
alienated from the beginning.
45 Ps 41,3 From this it is possible for the understanding one whom the
inscription calls to understand that a human being has come to be outside the
face of God, that upon God he may seek the restoration unto the face of God.
Reasonably then he seeks the strong one, having fallen into weakness according
to "Have mercy on me, Lord, because I am weak"; whence it is necessary that he
has fallen into the present weakness. For not loving to be sufficient with his
own small strength nor being able, he seeks not to be left behind of the truly
strength. 46 Ps 41,4 Of those who have altogether forgotten, he says, the
return unto God, considering the seeking of God vain and reproaching those who
do this, I was led forth into tears, being with a most unpleasant grief
instead
of the pleasant food according to what is said in Lamentations: "He fed me
with
ash," not standing away from the desire through the separation and the
reproach, yet being burdened over the righteousness being reproached.
Necessary
then also is the annoyance of the wicked and the reproaches, since the tears
from these are not harmful to the just. For upon these is the beatitude:
"Blessed
are those who mourn because they will be comforted." Straightway then also
that
of the comfort. 47 Ps 41,6 Let not the present things grieve you, he says, O
soul, nor trouble you, but let the hope unto God comfort that we will obtain
the goods worthy of thanksgiving and will have the things of salvation in his
appearing, which according to Paul is to await the appearing of the Lord and
his kingdom. These then are understanding things about the things to come and
the promises according to law, going higher, and what was said after these by
Paul † prophets of whom the better is to depart and to be with Christ; and the
things after this a narration of the things still grieving. 48 Ps 41,7 The
separation of the desired melts a desiring soul. And he says he has been
separated from God, until he does not yet see paradise, his own foundation
from
the beginning, but still dwells the land of Jordan and the mountain Hermoniim
most small as toward that, and is as it were troubled by squalls of winds and
strikes of waves, by the successive misfortunes brought on like torrents. 49
Ps
41,9 He teaches that the goods belong to the age to come, calling this day
into
which God has promised to set his mercy according to what was said in 89 "Fill
us in the morning with your mercy"; and the now, he says, unto gladness, when
it is night and darkness, is the converse through hymns toward God and the
stretching-out through prayers. 50 Ps 41,10 The words of petition are said
then
also the things of the petition in odes. And since the petition must be
brought
near with thanksgiving, the ode is set before the prayer. For no one being
ungrateful upon the former things is strong in the askings of the things to
come. And the asking seeks a divine remembrance from God. For not being in
memory, he says, with you I would spend time gloomy and be afflicted by the
unjust. And it is clear that forgetfulness does not touch the divine nature,
being a change of memory, because the divine is unchangeable. For this reason
he also asks "Why have you forgotten me?" For no one about an involuntary

passion of forgetfulness says "Why," but he presents a voluntary
forgetfulness,
the carelessness about a human being unworthy of care from God.
51 Ps 42,1 This psalm is inseparable from the former, not even divided by an
inscription, holding also to the same power of the return unto God, adding
also
the guidance through the light that has shone, of the one who says "I have
come
as light into the world, that everyone who follows me may not remain in the
darkness," and by this shining preparing comfort for the one who is grieved,
just as the one before it made the comfort of the grievous things by the
expectation of the things to come. Since then according to nation and
according
to man the portion of the just is warred upon, and it cannot obtain the things
according to desert unless it take the heavenly judge and protector, as if the
suit toward the opponents were a care to that one who also judges all justly.
Him he also calls upon inasmuch as he does not judge from hearing but brings
on
the just from knowledge. For this reason he also asks the escape from a nation
and from an unjust man. 52 Ps 42,2a As not having some other thing toward
strength he says this and obtains God, such as is also "I will love you, Lord
my strength"; then he speaks here also about those who stand against. 53 Ps
42,2 If to prevail is from God, reasonably he says that not to be prevailed
over is a thrusting-away from God, being most grievous to the one who puts
forward this help alone, and the "Why" not as of God doing something without
cause or in vain (for this comes about toward our contest) but as of the
contest needing to have an end through the mercy and of comfort being owed to
be given back to those who mourn from the one who comforts those who mourn.
For
there is no reason that God overlook the just, whence seeking the things from
God he says "Why" as that "It befits you to take hold of your own and not to
permit pleasure to the opponents; why then is your thing not completed, but
what is from us abounds," as also "Until when, Lord, will you forget me unto
the end?" is said and "What profit is there in my blood, in my going down into
corruption?" For even if the ill-treatment is necessary for some time, yet not
unto all, and if according to ourselves we are reasonably ill-treated, yet
according to the mercy of God at least we ought to obtain help, so that the
training give back a peaceful fruit to those trained through it, that through
the philanthropy of God. And upon the Lord "Why have you forsaken me" is said
altogether, when even what is according to him is not worthy of forsaking;
wherefore also the appearing of the heavenly help is swift about the suffering
body through that about us. 54 Ps 42,4c He has shown the brightness by the
addition of the kithara and the love in the address taken up twice. 55 Ps 42,5
This is the reminder of the comfort. For even if we are burdened by the
present
things, he says, yet let us give thanks upon the things to come, and already
by
the expectation let the grievous stand away, as we expect God as salvation of
the human being who looks toward him. 56 Ps 43,5 You, he says, we have as
king,
God who provides for the salvation of our people, and we know that this is
going to be yours, and we take courage in your will.
57 Ps 43,6 You, he says, we have as a horn unto defending against all enemies,
and your name upon us will give a trampling to those who rise up and hope to
prevail over us. 58 Ps 43,7 For I do not prepare myself, he says, with bow and
spear. For this reason I do not take courage in the weapons, since neither did
these suffice for the fathers nor has the success been completed according to
the strength of these but the work is beyond all human preparation. 59 Ps 43,8
He says the former teachings toward the things that happen later. And we have
been taught that from you the salvation from the opponents has always come
about for us, and we have seen their power having fallen into shame. 60 Ps
43,9
For this reason, he says,

we boast once for all upon you through the whole of time and we bring up
thanksgivings to your name by which we are saved, as also it is said through
Ezekiel: "Not for you do I make a victory of Israel but for my name." 61 Ps
43,=10.11= But the hopes, he says, these are firm and unmoved from which we
will not stand away through the things that have happened, yet the things that
have happened are now contrary to these. For we have been left behind, he
says,
having been thrust away from you and you not being present to our armies, and
the shame that then held the wars has turned about unto us, and we have become
lessened beside those over whom we formerly prevailed, and we have been seized
by those whom we used to seize. For in this also it has been shown rather that
the power existed for Israelites from God, in that when left behind they are
defeated, just as also for the human being the being left behind has become an
occasion of death, showing that the resurrection is not otherwise than from
God. 62 Ps 43,12 We have been held by cowardice, he says, not able to defend
against those who eat us as sheep, a cowardice which Moses also threatened
would hold them upon the lawlessnesses, that a voice of a carried leaf will
pursue them; and we have become, he says, captives and have been scattered
into
the nations, we who formerly were led out from the nations and had a holy
land.
This is a dissolving of the honor just as for a human being of the forming:
"You are earth and unto earth you will go away," so also about the people
Isaiah says: "We have become as from the beginning, when you did not rule us
nor was your mercy called upon us." 63 Ps 43,13a They were sold for nothing,
as
Isaiah also says, and he says also that they were sold by their sins, as also
upon a human being Paul says: "But I am fleshly, sold under the sin." And sale
is the alienation and the coming to be under human masters instead of God,
which he also threatened to Jeroboam and to the people with him through Shimei
the prophet, that they will become slaves to the Egyptian king, to Shishak,
and
they will know the slavery of God and the slavery of the kings of the earths.
64 πς 43,15 For such a thing is a parable, that they be remembered upon the
curses, as Jeremiah says against the false prophets: "They will take a curse
upon him in the colony, of Judah, in Babylon, saying 'May the Lord do to you
as
he did to Zedekiah and as to Ahiath whom the king of Babylon fried in fire
through the lawlessness which they did in Israel.'" Grant these things now
especially and justly; being eaten as sheep when they led Christ as a sheep
unto slaughter, and sold to the nations as they bought the Lord betrayed for
silver and in a certain manner set forth unto death, and being laughed at just
as they made laughter and a shaking of heads upon the Lord crucified. 65 ps
43,18®21 This alone is left to Israel unto hope of the recalling of those
left,
which the prophecy seeing beforehand says, not to be changed toward the
customs
of the Greeks according to the idolatry which they agreed not, nor as from an
enjoined road to turn aside from the piety toward one God even though so
humbled and exiled and set under the authority of those who put to death;
which
was exceptionally kept by those about Ananiah and Azariah and Mishael, and
they
endured fire for the sake of not receiving worship. A not pious thing is
somehow present also to those left of the Jews according to the present time;
at any rate the not going to idols, or they do not altogether leave behind
even
