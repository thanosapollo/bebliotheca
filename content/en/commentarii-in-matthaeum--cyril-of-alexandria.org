1 Mt 1, 1 The Hebrews used to say Iao for salvation, but he said of Christ
because the ancient kings and priests were anointed with the oil through the
horn. But our Jesus Christ was not named Christ because he was anointed with
the horn, but because of the divine Spirit; for truly and properly he himself
had the Holy Spirit. 2 Mt 1, 1 He mentions Abraham, since that one first
became a type of the two peoples who were going to believe in Christ; for
while in uncircumcision he believed, and after being circumcised he remained
faithful again. And he mentions David especially, since he too became a type
of Christ; for just as when Saul had been cast out as rejected David was
appointed second as a king well-pleasing to God, so also when the first man, I
mean Adam, had been cast out through the disobedience and no longer ruled the
rest at all, a second Adam was brought in, Christ the king of all, in order
that what was lost in Adam might be recovered for men through Christ, a king
not only of earth but also of heaven; and he is called son of man by nature as
having all that Adam had, being now God. Abraham, while in uncircumcision,
believed God; then, having received the promises and been circumcised, he kept
the faith unimpaired. Reasonably, then, the saying will be raised to another
contemplation concerning the church of Christ, both that from circumcision and
that from the nations; for he was, as is likely, a type of the two peoples who
have believed in Christ. But there uncircumcision was prior to circumcision;
for uncircumcision is a work of nature, but circumcision of command and law.
Therefore that preceded this, and after these things circumcision returns to
it, since nature together with law is also better than being divided from one
another apart. But David too was a type of Christ; for when Saul had been cast
out, David was brought in instead. So also when Adam through the disobedience
had been driven from Eden, the second Adam was brought in instead, so that he
might restore the first Adam to the ancient rank. 3 Mt 1, 2 Therefore he makes
the names of the genealogy from men, since "the woman is the glory of man,"
being an accompaniment and not a beginning of the man. 4 Mt 1, 12 Zerubbabel
built the temple that had long ago been built from Solomon but burned by the
Babylonians. And the Lord is the true builder of the rational temple that had
fallen apart through the transgressions and been burned with an alien fire,
which we kindled not only by preparing the intellectual part of the soul to
follow slavishly the fleshly mind, but also by kindling through activity the
shameless matter of the passions. But Zerubbabel also has in his hand a stone
adorned with seven eyes. There is also for the Lord a stone, the faith in him;
and in the hand, because the faith of Christ is made manifest in the practice
of the commandments. And the hand is clearly a symbol of practice. So the
Lord, bearing the stone in his hand, teaches us to have the faith in him that
is put into practice, adorned with the seven operations of the Spirit. 5 Mt 1,
16 And the fact that he did not say "through whom he was born" perhaps, but
"from whom," overturns the heresy of Apollinarius who says that Christ passed
through the virgin as through a tube and took nothing from her. For the
evangelist says that Jesus was not born through her, but from her, that is,
from her all-pure bloods; such also is the saying of the apostle: for he says
"God sent his Son, born of a woman," but not through a woman. 6 Mt 1, 20 The
angel called her wife, in order to free the virgin from every suspicion toward
another person; for if of Joseph her as wife


calls her, of him who has no experience of her, then the virgin has not even a
thought of suspicion toward any other. 8 Mt 1, 21 If he has a people even
before being born of Mary and saves, it is clear that it is as God. 9 Mt 1, 24
After the betrothal she conceived, in order that she might seem to have borne
from him and in order that she might be genealogized from him and might also
have a guardian in the plots. 10 Mt 2, 3–6 It was not the voice of the magi
that disturbed him, but that it was being noised about by all those learned in
the law and those who trust the words of the prophets; for the magi were
seeking a king, but the Jews were reporting that Christ had been born.
Therefore, leaving the magi aside, and having called the Jews, he asked where
the Christ is born, whom you now report, having heard the magi. And the
enemies of the truth even unwillingly speak the truth and interpret the whole
prophecy in vain; for they did not add what follows. And he calls the magi
ambassadors, since in a certain manner they came to the king of Israel, in
order that they might treat and peace might come to be between them and the
Israelites and there might come to be of the nations and Jews "one flock, one
shepherd" of both. And what was this, that his goings forth are from the
beginning, from the ends of the inhabited world they come to see the manger.
11 Mt 2, 6 Israel first was Jacob named, when he had seen the ladder and the
angels going up and coming down upon him through it and had wrestled with the
one who appeared to him and heard from him: "Your name shall no longer be
Jacob, but Israel." And by this name the whole people of the Jews was called,
as by one both exceptional and divine and distinguishing them from the other
nations. And Israel is mind seeing God. Therefore the church from the nations
also was named Israel not according to flesh, but according to divine grace.
12 Mt 2, 7–11 And did the magi arrive at the same time as the birth and see
Christ in swaddling-clothes, or sitting in a house with the mother? I think
the truer [account] is that in swaddling-clothes. Therefore also, as we said
above, the star appeared before the birth of the Lord. And from this it is
clear that it also anticipated the arrival of the magi. 13 Mt 2, 9 The star
stood, one unmoving fixture, in order that the magi might proceed no further;
yet it did not come down from heaven and stand upon earth, nor did it fix
itself above the house, having a custom not to make its course according to
the motion of the others, but making a certain strange course and appearing at
other times in other regions, then only it stood unmoving. The star was
unmoving and standing, but it was not, having left the heaven, traveling near
the earth, nor was it fixed upon the house; for it did not make the same
motion as the remaining stars, but a certain strange and extraordinary one. 14
Mt 2, 20 He no longer says, Flee, but Go, hinting at ease after the trial; and
by saying the soul of the child he overturns the opinion of Apollinarius who
says that the Lord took flesh without mind and without soul. Yet in the
economic descent of Christ into Egypt and the return from there again into
Judea God recapitulates the captivity of the people and ransoms him from a
plot, leading him back again to his own. 15 Mt 2, 23 Yet it is worth noting
that Nazareth lay in the extremities of Judea, and Christ by dwelling in it
shows that because of the Jews' murderousness he was going to leave Judea and
pass over to the nations, for the sake of having given those who love learning
an occasion of faith in Christ. Nazareth lay in the last parts of Judea. And
Christ, having left Judea and gone away to Nazareth, showed plainly that he
will leave Jews and transfer the worship to the nations. And at the same time
he will also give those who love learning a reason to believe in him. 16 Mt 2,
23 And if Nazorean is interpreted holy, or according to some


flower, this name is found among many; for Daniel names him "holy of holies."
And Isaiah: "a rod from the root of Jesse and a flower from it," and the Lord
himself says concerning himself in the Song of Songs: "I am a flower of the
field, a lily of the valleys." 17 Mt 3, 2 Kingdom of the heavens is the
justification through faith and the sanctification through Spirit; therefore
also elsewhere: "the kingdom" of the heavens "is within you." 18 Mt 3, 6 The
baptism of John did not grant remission of sins, but taught men to run to the
baptism because of the sins. 19 Mt 3, 7 As more learned in the law than the
others they came later to John, whom he calls offspring of vipers because of
the likeness of manner; for just as those, when they are about to come out
into light, burst the mother's belly and go away leaving her dead, in the same
way these too left their own mother, Jerusalem, as if dead, and were being
saved from the killing of Christ, because of which they underwent the
incurable evils for a time under Romans, which he calls coming wrath. 20 Mt 3,
8–9 And one might say that fruits of repentance are primarily the faith in
Christ, and besides this also the evangelical way of life which is "in newness
of life" and delivered from the thickness of the letter. Do not then, he says,
put forward the accustomed excuses; that God is able from these stones to
raise up children of Abraham, he added. 21 Mt 3, 9 For just as he turned the
wife of Lot to stone, so it is possible for him also to make men from stones,
just as also Adam formerly from earth. 24 Mt 3, 10 He says axe is Christ,
"sharper than a two-edged sword," who is going to cut off the unbelieving Jews
and alienate them from the honor and communion of the patriarchs, whom he also
called root, who also remained, the fathers of old who pleased God, those
about Abraham and as many as were holy of old; for the unbelievers from them
were cut off as unfruitful branches, but the root remained, upon which those
from the nations were grafted. And Irenaeus says axe is the Word of God, as
also Jeremiah: the word of the Lord "as an axe cutting a rock." For why do I
say, he says, that you are about to fall away? For this very reason he had not
even stood off from the root. 25 Mt 3, 10 And he likens the Word of God to an
axe; for Jeremiah too says thus: the word of the Lord "as an axe cutting a
rock." Thus it lies upon the root, in order that by fear it may make you
better and prepare you to bear fruit. 26 Mt 3, 10 What does he call axe?
Nothing else than that which is going to cut from the patriarchs those who
boasted in vain and worked nothing good; for those who have become unfruitful
from such must be cut off and thrown into fire. 27 Mt 3, 11 And the blessed
Baptist bound together with the meaning of the Spirit the operation and
meaning of the fire, not saying that we shall altogether be baptized in fire
through Christ, but signifying the life-giving operation of the Spirit through
the meaning of the fire. 28 Mt 3, 12 He snatches the righteous into the city
above, but the sinners to be burned up. 29 Mt 3, 16 The ancients even before
Christ used to hold those baptized by them in the water until they had
confessed their own sins, but Christ, being sinless, immediately went up; for
he was not baptized as repenting, but as cleansing sins and sanctifying the
waters. 30 Mt 4, 1 He was being led by the devil by permission. 31 Mt 4, 3
Christ did not obey the devil so as to make the stones loaves, because it was
not for his benefit; for not in order that he might believe and become as he
was formerly an angel did he


ask this; for he is unrepentant, but in order that he might draw Christ into
vainglory. Which the Savior, knowing, did not obey him. 32 Mt 4, 3–4 Wishing
to draw Christ into the passion of love of glory, Satan did not say to him
Eat, but Make a sign. And this he was doing, not in order that he might be
benefited, but in order that, as I said, he might draw him into vainglory;
which Christ, knowing, did not obey him. With this aim he also did not consent
to the Pharisees wishing to see a sign from him; for they were not approaching
him with an undoubting heart as God, but were testing him as a man. Let this
then be an unfailing rule for the saints toward unbelievers or those who test,
not to love glory in anything useful. 33 Mt 4, 12 The withdrawing of Jesus
belongs to teaching. Clearly the word teaches that one must not come to the
school unless first he is baptized and obtains Holy Spirit and fasts wholly
and conquers every kind of trial, as the type of the truth began in Christ and
comes to be through Christ. 34 Mt 4, 12–16 And he did not withdraw through
cowardice, but teaching us by the things he did to withdraw from those who
persecute. And he withdraws from Judea to the nations, showing that not only
when Jews insult God himself does he stand off from them, but also when they
sin against the holy prophets. And a great light is Christ our Lord and the
brightness of the evangelical preaching, not indeed the law, which had been
likened to a lamp. Therefore a lamp was always burning in the tent because of
the shortness of the law's gleam, having strength to send out its own light
only as far as the Jewish boundaries. Therefore the Gentiles were in darkness
as not even having the lamplight. 36 Mt 4, 17 In some copies "repent" is not
set down. And if the Savior says the same things as John, one is the God who
sent both. And perhaps John, as "preparing" for God "a people made ready,"
first says repent, but Jesus, having received things already prepared no
longer needing repentance, does not say repent. And not preaching against law
and prophets, but, John having completed the old, he began to preach the new,
himself becoming beginning of it. Therefore upon John "he began" is not
written; for he was an end. And that the one preaches in a desert, the other
among a people. And the kingdom of the heavens is not in a place, but in a
disposition; for "it is within us." And see whether John preaches a kingdom of
heavens drawing near, [and] Christ a king which he will hand over "to God and
Father." 37 Mt 4, 23 And if someone asks what the difference is of disease and
weakness, we will answer that weakness is the temporary irregularity of the
body, but disease is a disproportion of the elements in the body. And this too
must be noted, that the inheritance of the tribe of "Zebulun and Naphtali"
stretched as far as Sidon, a Gentile city, and that Jews were already dwelling
mixed with the nations. And "Zebulun" is interpreted good journey and
blessing, but "Naphtali" a relaxed stem, that is a plant stretching out. And
these things have become those who have believed in Christ; for journeying
well they were counted worthy of the divine blessing and were broadened in all
good things, those who were formerly in Galilee, which is interpreted rolling
down, that is those rolling down the pits of destruction. 38 Mt 5, 9 A
peacemaker is the one who shows the battle of the scriptures that appears to
others to be a harmony, of old to new, of legal to prophetic, of evangelical
to evangelical. Therefore having imitated the Son of God he will be called
son, having received in deed the "spirit" of "adoption." 39 Mt 5, 9 And
peacemakers are not only those who make enemies friends, but also those who
tame anger and evil desire, and who live rightly in deed and word through
unfailing faith, yet also those who re-teach the unbelievers and lead them to
the faith as the enemies of God


making them friends to him. Blessed are such as having imitated the Son of
God; for they will be called sons, having partaken of immortality, or they
will reign with Christ at his second coming. 40 Mt 5, 9 Peacemakers are also
those who persuade the unbelievers to believe, as making the former enemies of
God at peace with him. Peacemakers are also called those who persuade the
unbelievers to believe in God. 41 Mt 5, 13 He calls salt the prudence of which
the apostolic word is full, which, sown in our souls, settles in us the word
of wisdom, which because of its savor and grace has been likened to this; for
in the same way that without salt neither bread nor relish is eatable, so
without the apostolic understanding and teaching every soul is both foolish
and unsavory and not sweet before God. 43 Mt 5, 14 And the saying a city
cannot: those established upon the faith as upon a high mountain ought not to
speak the word secretly or with cowardice. 44 Mt 5, 14 And they themselves
having become light by participation, when you live you are no longer
yourselves, but the light lives in you, Christ, who is able through word to
illuminate even the whole world. 46 Mt 5, 18 In place of the fleshly worship
the Lord introduced that "in spirit and truth"; and perhaps what the Jews were
not even doing fleshly, these things now the disciples of Christ also do
spiritually. Therefore he says: one iota or one tittle shall not pass from the
law, until all things come to be. Clearly the Jews did not do all things, or
even if they did, they ceased from doing, no longer being allowed to do them,
in one way through fear of the kings, in another also because the temple had
been destroyed, in which alone the sacrifices had to be performed. 47 Mt 5, 19
A fighter against God, he says, will be called the one who sets aside the
least of the commandments in the law. Therefore he is also set aside by God as
standing against him and legislating in opposition. 48 Mt 5, 19 The one who
sets aside one of the commandments of the law is set aside by God as a fighter
against God and legislating against God. And now from the evangelical law he
receives the punishment which law of old had not defined. Therefore also
Christ says correspondingly, "that I did not come to destroy the law, but to
fulfill." For what was lacking there, here he filled up, as in the law it was
said: "you shall rise up from before a gray head" and "if you see the beast of
your enemy fallen under the load, raise it with him." If someone transgressed
these, there was not a punishment defined by the law. Which Christ, filling
up, says that in the kingdom of God such a one will be set at nought. This
then he calls a least commandment, upon which there was not a punishment
brought from transgression. 49 Mt 5, 19 Therefore also the law of Moses
accepts the cloven hoof, in order that through riddles he may teach us to
journey through both, through word and through practice. And I think the
cloven hoof in the law also signifies this, that we journey through word and
practice and neither act without reason nor have word without practice. And if
someone wishes one of such things to be isolated, let it be practice
henceforth and not word; for unbridled word has often pushed those who have it
over cliffs, but practice never. 50 Mt 5, 23 The saying if you bring your gift
and what follows shows this, that God invented repentance as a way of
salvation and an escape from punishment for those who sin, and he says that
the healing of the one who has been grieved will be an overturning of
punishment. Since the one who does not love his brother does not love the
Lord, reasonably he does not receive the one who is in grief toward the
brother as not truly approaching him. 51 Mt 5, 23–24 Here the Savior teaches
us who sin to escape the punishment. Repentance then upon the sins and the
appeal to the one grieved by us and the asking of forgiveness of the sin both
heals the brother and God. 52 Mt 5, 25–26 Yet the quadrans signifies the
fourth of the hin measure. This according to the speech of the Hebrews is two
obols,


which Luke, making clear, named "lepton." And let us be delivered both from
the enemy and avenger, that is the devil, while we are in this way, and from
the passions of dishonor, which are adversaries of ours; for no other of us is
an adversary, unless we ourselves become adversaries of ourselves through
these and the conscience does not condemn us as despising its reminder, lest
we be handed over to God the judge of all, convicted by the conscience; and he
"will hand over to the officers," that is to the punishing angels, and we
shall be required the penalties upon every fault, both small and great. 53 Mt
5, 25 Carefully then the Lord here names adversary the one who attempts to
take away something of the things that belong to us. And we are well disposed
to him, if we keep the command of the Lord who said "to him who wishes to go
to law with you and take your tunic, leave him the cloak also" and likewise
upon every such matter. 54 Mt 5, 25–26 What he says is of this sort: before
judgment be fair-minded. Since even if you are accused and imprisoned, you are
not released until you pay even to an obol; for this the quadrans shows. And
what he says is of this sort: for he exhorts to be fair-minded and not to
trust in the judgment; for if something should happen contrary to hope and you
should be held by the judge, you will not be released until you pay the last
obol; for this is called quadrans. 55 Mt 5, 25–26 Let someone be supposed, he
says, having made certain charges against you before one of those appointed to
rule, then having pointed you out to those who lead to the court he causes you
to be carried off; while then, he says, you are with him in the way, that is
before arriving at the judge, give work, instead of: do not hesitate to set
all zeal, in order that you may be delivered from him. And if this does not
happen, he will hand you over to the judge. Then when you are found liable for
the debts, "you will be handed over to the officer," that is to the
collectors, and those, having shut you in, will require even "the last
lepton." Therefore we who are upon the earth are all liable for faults. Yet of
each the adversary and accuser is Satan; for he is an enemy and avenger; while
then we are in the way, that is until we have not yet arrived at the end of
the life here, let us be delivered from him, let us loose the charges against
ourselves, let us snatch the grace through Christ which frees us from every
debt and sets us outside of penalty and of punishment and fear, lest perhaps
having the stain unwashed we be carried off to the judge and be handed over
"to the officers," that is the punishers, whose harshness none could escape.
And he will rather be required the penalties upon every fault, small and
great. Far from these will be those who test the time of Christ's coming and
have not been ignorant of the mystery upon him. 56 Mt 5, 28–30 Perhaps there
are some who have received such a gift so as not to desire. Yet Christ, as
having strengthened the nature of men, also commands the things above law as
now being possible; for even if for those before Christ this was impossible,
yet for the faithful it is easy to accomplish, to cut out the desires. 57 Mt
5, 28 The one who has looked at a woman and desired is then judged, when the
flesh of the woman appears to him beautiful unto desire and he sees her
fleshly and sinfully; for the one who through love of the chaste sees the
beauty does not consider the flesh beautiful, but the soul. 58 Mt 5, 29–30
Having said if your eye scandalizes you and what follows, he calls eye the
thought of desire, which he says is to be cut out. 59 Mt 5, 29–30 Eye and hand
are understood as the friend. For if you love someone thus as in the rank of a
right eye and think him useful as in the rank of a right hand and he harms
your soul, cut these off from you and be separated far from them; for when you
do not save one another, but rather both perish together being together, it is
expedient that, being separated, even the one be saved.


61 Mt 5, 31 The one who casts out the chaste woman gives her license to be
married to another, which is a form of adultery as if the union had not been
loosed; for the bills of divorce do not loose the marriage before God, but the
unseemly act. 62 Mt 5, 33–37 The saying you shall not swear falsely and what
follows. If some shameless person lays an oath upon the saints, in place of
the oath there will be for them the yes and the no. 63 Mt 5, 33–35 Therefore
he forbids us to swear by heaven and earth, in order that we may not give to
the creation the dignity above the creation, making it God; for those who
swear, he says, "swear by the greater," as the apostle said. And he also
forbids the oath by "Jerusalem," since the earthly "Jerusalem" is a type of
the "Jerusalem above" and God swears by himself alone, which is of his own
glory. Therefore as the likeness overpasses us we ought not to swear by
ourselves, that is by our own glory; for we are not free as God is, but we are
under the authority of God. 66 Mt 5, 42 Be, says Christ, not ready toward
receiving, but toward giving; for the one separates us from God, but the other
is naturally able to join, and especially when the one asking happens to be
worthy and the request just. 67 Mt 5, 44 Let us love the enemies not insofar
as they are adulterers or murderers, but insofar as they are men; for to sin
belongs to activity, not to essence; therefore neither is sin a work of God.
68 Mt 5, 44 Perhaps someone might say, how does the blessed Paul curse
Alexander the coppersmith? To whom we say he cursed him not as a private
enemy, but as an enemy of the gospel, teaching that one must always war
against the enemies of God. But perhaps someone, bringing against us the
blessed Paul as cursing Alexander the coppersmith, might persuade us not to
love the enemies; to whom it is possible to say that Paul curses not as a
private enemy, but as of Christ and of the gospel, teaching that one must
always war against such. 69 Mt 6, 1 The one who does virtue for the good
itself has a boast above the world. 70 Mt 6, 2 This has been said from a
metaphor of the trumpet in the camps; for those, whenever they wish to gather
the army together, sound the recall, but the hypocrites do not wish to gather,
but to be made a spectacle to the many. 71 Mt 6, 7–8 Therefore Christ commands
that the prayers be made through a short form, since he knows the mind to be
easily carried aside and wandering off through vain thoughts and cares,
especially at the time of prayer. And he charges to ask God soberly and
quickly for what one seeks, not reporting all things whatever he wishes; for
this belongs to extreme madness; for God knows even before we ask of what
things we have need. And battology is called much-speaking from a certain
Battus a Greek who made long and many-lined hymns to the idols and having
tautology. Battology is that outside the good. The ancients call much-speaking
battology from someone so named who made many-lined prayers to the idols and
saying the same words through other and other expressions. 72 Mt 6, 10
Economically as man Christ is called king although being God by nature. The
faithful then pray that he also exist for the unbelievers, in order that
having believed in Christ they may call him their own, through whom they will
partake of the perfect goods, now having only the earnest and the partial
good; for then the passions would not be at work when passionlessness is
present, death is gone, corruption unseen, when the life reigns in us and
incorruption has the power. Christ, being God by nature and man by nature, is
economically called king, that is as man. Therefore the righteous pray that
this will exist for themselves, and still also for the unbelievers, in order
that seeing and believing in him they may have him as their own king. 73 Mt


6, 14 Jesus also as God is able to forgive sins, but as the disciples being
still imperfect he has not yet handed over the word concerning his own deity
purely. 74 Mt 6, 16 One must then not appear gloomy nor having anything of
the. charge of love of glory, but wash it off as being a filth of the soul. 75
Mt 6, 16–17 One must the ruling part to men while fasting. 76 Mt 6, 21 What
someone has set before himself, this he both thinks and works most readily and
is eager to accomplish; which of the heart is found more clearly, leading the
word out from the intelligibles upon the sensibles and saying. 79 Mt 6, 24 A
good master is the un-money-loving manner, but a bad one the one who loves
money. Therefore one must attach to the one; for it is not possible to please
the two, being of a different and opposite habit. 80 Mt 6, 25 Since the soul
and the body are more honorable than the foods and the garments, one must also
despise the lesser things, and one must take care also of the more honorable,
I mean of soul and body. 81 Mt 6, 25 The soul is more honorable than the
substance of the body as image of God and inbreathing, but the body is an
instrument of it and a fellow-worker toward the finest things. We must then
take thought of both, of the body together and of the soul, and of the body to
such an extent as is sufficient for it and that the soul not be hindered; but
to it one must always give the things proper and take care in every way and
lead it up through the virtues toward its creative cause. 82 Mt 6, 33 To those
seeking the things of the soul God also gives the things suitable to the
bodies, in order that we, not taking thought for such things more than is due,
may not be hindered toward the finest things. 83 Mt 7, 2 Those being trained
toward piety apply the bridle from good prudence to the tongue, permitting it
to speak only those things that do not harm. Teaching us this one of the wise
used to say: child, "if" there "is to you" a word "of understanding, answer;
but if not, let a hand be upon your mouth." For "death and life are in the
hand of the tongue," that is in authority, "and those who hold it will eat its
fruits." And since to hold the tongue is not very easy, from above the
psalmist also teaches us this, saying: "Set, Lord, a watch for my mouth." Let
us therefore not speak against one another nor condemn the brothers; for the
one governed by understanding does not look to the sins of others, but gazing
upon his own evils he drops a good tear upon them, in order that he may
receive the remission from God; for it is impossible that those who are men
and so weak should not altogether fall into a few unseemly things. And not to
fall at all belongs by nature to God alone. And it is necessary for us who are
in sins not to take pleasure in the evils of others, but to take thought how
we ourselves may come to be outside of the base things. 84 Mt 7, 5 Cast out
first the beam from your eye, that is show yourself clean of the great sins
and then you will be a counselor to the one who stumbles a little. And if you
do not cast out the beam, but condemn the one who has the speck, you will
reasonably hear the apostolic saying: "you who say not to commit adultery
commit adultery," you who say "not to steal, steal." Therefore the wise man
will be a teacher to himself, but he will not condemn another. For this also
