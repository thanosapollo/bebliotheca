OF ATHANASIUS, PATRIARCH OF ALEXANDRIA, FROM THE DISCOURSE AGAINST THE LATINS.
But not thus those of Christ, but just as he, saying: "When the Paraclete
comes, whom I will send to you from the Father, the Spirit of truth, who
proceeds from the Father;" from this shows the Father alone as the projector
of the Spirit; so also these, obeying the teacher, say that this proceeds from
the Father alone. And if anyone says that nowhere is the Spirit said to be
from the Father alone, let such a one hear that the one writing about this
does not otherwise bear witness than according to the teaching of our most
divine Fathers. For just as they say that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the
Father, taking the cause from the Lord's own mouth; so also the subsequent
writers and composers declare accordingly. Yet none of them says that this
proceeds also from the Son and through the Son; unless perhaps someone should
speak of the other outpouring, that is, the manifestation and sending of the
gifts of the Spirit. For toward this, although the Holy Spirit is manifested
from the Father for distribution, so also from the Son, as the canon of truth
teaches. And since all, saying the same thing in harmony and as one according
to the tradition of the Lord, and nowhere does anyone appear disagreeing, it
is quite clear that from the Father alone the Spirit has existence; if indeed
such =28.825= procession constitutes existence. For just as when all say, the
Son is offspring of the Father, it is also understood that the Son is from the
Father alone according to the pre-eternal generation; so also when all say the
Spirit is a projection of the Father, it is also understood that the Spirit is
from the Father alone, according to the pre-eternal projection; if the Son is
from the Father alone, then also the Holy Spirit is from the Father alone. But
according to the later outpouring, that is, the distribution and supply toward
the worthy, just as the Spirit is from the Father, so also through the Son and
from the Son. Just as we also glorify the later generation of the Son, that
is, his sojourn toward us, not from the Father alone, but also from the
Spirit. And let the voice of the archangel persuade you, saying to Joseph the
husband of Mary: "Do not fear to take Mary your wife; for that which is
begotten in her is of the Holy Spirit." And if you wish to learn that some of
our great saints also declared that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father
alone, take already as witness for this the great sun of our Church, Cyril, or
rather of the whole world; for in his discourse On the Trinity he presents it
clearly somehow thus. The all-holy, he says, and august Trinity, existing
before all ages and forever, is undefiled and eternal, incorporeal,
incomprehensible, uncircumscribed, but encompassing all things, and holding
together the whole, good, provident, creator of all things, visible and
invisible, impassible, unalterable, unchangeable, incomprehensible to the mind
of men and angels and to created nature; consubstantial in itself, but
incommunicable according to essence to all creation; known only by itself, by
no nature in this world and beyond this world; a monad in three hypostases,
that is persons, and a Trinity in a monad of deity, that is known as one
essence. Of this holy and august monad, the three worshipful hypostases are
known and believed: in an unoriginate, uncaused, unbegotten, eternal Father;
and in an only-begotten Son, begotten ineffably and impassibly and
unspeakably, before all the ages, from the very essence of the Father, and
therefore consubstantial and equal to the Father, apart from fatherhood,
having the Father as beginning and cause in a generated way; and in the
life-giving Holy Spirit who is worshipped, proceeding from the Father, that is
from the essence of the Father. Not in a generated way as the Son, lest there
be two Sons in the Trinity; but proceeding, as has been said, from the Father
alone, and from the mouth; but appearing through the Son, and having spoken in
the holy prophets and apostles. Yet, as I said, existing of the essence of the
Father himself and of the Son, and having consubstantiality with Father and
Son. And it too is incommunicable, just as the Father and the Son, toward all
creation according to the account of essence. Do you see how from the Father
alone is the Holy Spirit according to Saint Cyril, or rather according to all
divine Scripture. And let no one tell me from this =28.828= those cold words,
or little arguments, that the celebrated Athanasius and Cyril, and Gregory of
Nyssa besides, have become causes of the addition. For Athanasius speaks thus:
The opponents say the Spirit is from the Father, and proceeding from the Word,
who is from the Father; and Nyssa, having said that the Son is immediately
from the first, that is from the Father, next says: The Spirit through the one
who is immediately from the first; and the most wise Cyril, they say, has
clearly said that the Holy Spirit is from both Father and Son. These are the
problems of the wonder-workers of the rotten addition. But I, "taking every
thought captive unto the obedience of Christ," though I am unlearned, somehow
clarify these things thus. Athanasius says the Spirit is from the Father, and
proceeding from the Word, who is from the Father, just as also from the
co-eternal Father himself, even as the Son is thus sent, and is given by the
Spirit to the faithful, as also from the Father himself; and take the
testimony from the Word and Son himself, saying, "The Spirit of the Lord is
upon me, because he has anointed me. He has sent me to preach good news to the
poor." Therefore the Spirit both anointed the Son and sent him; but also the
Father is sent from both Son and Spirit. And how this is, hear the Father
himself saying thus through Isaiah the prophet: "Hear me, Jacob and Israel,
whom I call; I am God, the first, I also am after these things; and besides me
there is no god. I by my hand founded the earth, and my Spirit established the
heaven. I commanded all the stars. And now the Lord has sent me." Therefore it
is the Father here who is saying: "He has sent me." Do you see the equality of
honor of the three persons of the supreme deity? When the Holy Spirit is sent
from both, he is sent as consubstantial with Father and Son. And when the Son
from the Father, as also consubstantial with the Spirit. And when the Father
from the Son and the Spirit, as consubstantial. When therefore the great
Athanasius said the Spirit proceeding from the Father, he made clear whence
the Spirit has existence, namely from the Father alone. And when he said again
thus the Spirit from the Father, as from the Word who is from the Father, he
made clear the equality of honor of Father and Son, to shame in this the most
godless Arius, who said the Son was a creature and not God; as if saying these
things to him: O most godless Arius, thus the Spirit proceeding from the
Father shines forth from the Son blasphemed by you and is given to the
faithful according to his operation and gift, just as also from the co-eternal
Father himself. And that this has such a manner, see what this Father,
worthily named of immortality, writes to bishop Serapion. For saying the same
things, and =28.829= philosophizing about the same, he says there too thus:
For just as the Son is the only-begotten offspring of the Father, so also the
Spirit given and sent from the Son, and it too is one and not many, nor one
from many, but it itself alone Spirit. For since the Son, the living Word, is
one, there must be one perfect and full sanctifying and illuminating life,
being his operation and gift, which is said to proceed from the Father, since
from the Word who is confessed from the Father it shines forth and is sent and
given. Just as the Son is the only-begotten offspring of the Father, he says,
so also one projection of the same Father is the Spirit and not many, nor from
many, that is, having received existence from many. But as it itself alone is
Spirit and projection, so also this is from the Father alone and from one. For
since the Son, the living Word, is one, as from one Father, there must also be
one perfect and full sanctifying and illuminating life, which was the Holy
Spirit, who works his own gift, and divides individually to each as he
wills. Which gift is for this reason said to proceed from the Father, that is
to be poured out
and sent, since from the Word who is confessed from the Father it shines forth
and is sent and given. The saint, having first philosophized dimly about the
pre-eternal procession of the Spirit, having shown it alone as a projection,
as from one projector and Father, through what follows demonstrates to us very
clearly also the outpouring under time, as existing from both Father and Son.
And what there the phrase "Thus the Spirit from the Father, as from the Word
who is from the Father" can mean, so also here: "Which is said to proceed from
the Father, since from the Word who is confessed from the Father it shines
forth and is sent and given." And in the same manner also Gregory of Nyssa
says the Spirit through the one immediately from the first; that is, together
with the Son conjunctly and at once, and not after the Son lagging behind in
existence. And having said that the Son is immediately from the first, do not
suppose that the Spirit itself is not also immediately from the first. For
just as the Son is immediately and most nearly from the first, that is from
the Father, so also the Spirit is immediately from the first, according to the
pre-eternal existence. And the Father is first not according to time, nor
according to rank, God forbid! but inasmuch as he is cause of the Son and of
the Spirit, just as also greater and unoriginate, as uncaused, as root of
those from him and fountain. For just as he alone is unoriginate toward the
Spirit and the Son, so also of these the same is the only cause; but the
Spirit toward the Father is not unoriginate, nor uncaused, just as neither is
the Son himself according to the beginning in time. Not only the Spirit, but
also the Son is unoriginate just as the Father. First cause and only is the
Father of the Son and of the Spirit. But do not seek also a second, O orthodox
one, nor indeed a third; lest, cutting and dividing the same and one essence
in an ill-employed =28.832= way, you be condemned as heterodox. The Father
therefore is first inasmuch as greater, and greater inasmuch as unoriginate;
and unoriginate inasmuch as beginning of the co-natural hypostases. The
Spirit, and the Son, immediately from the greater, that is from the Father.
For at once the Son is truly and really from the Father, and the Spirit from
the Father. Not the
one earlier, the other after him; but at once the generation of the Son, at
once also the procession of the Spirit shines forth, and the Spirit is sent
and given through the Son, as blessed Athanasius says, just as also the Son
through the Spirit, as has been said. Likewise also that of blessed Cyril, I
mean the "from both," is to be understood according to this very sense: the
Spirit proceeding from the Father shines forth from both, is sent and given,
of Father and Son. And a little later: Two generations, as I already said
above, I have learned concerning the Word; one from the Father and beyond
time, and one from the Virgin and under time. Likewise I have also been
initiated into two outpourings concerning the Spirit; one the processional and
beyond time, and one the transmissive of goodness. But of the first generation
of the Son, and of the first procession of the Spirit, the Father alone is
cause. But of the other generation under time, and of the other transmissive
outpouring, the Father is not the only cause, but also the Son and the Holy
Spirit. From both therefore the Spirit, Father and Son, is poured forth for
distribution; not according to existence, God forbid! since two causes are
introduced, differing greatly from the one co-natural and equally honored.
