for your sake. I knew even before this how great a wealth I have, but I
marveled even now. I was far away, and a city was being transplanted. Because
of one man the sea became a city. Women, men, children of unripe age, women
carrying children, dared the sea, despised the waves. No slave feared his
master, no woman remembered her natural weakness. The marketplace became a
church, all places everywhere for our sake. For whom did you not instruct? You
received an empress dancing along with you; for I will not hide her zeal. I do
not say this flattering an empress, but serving piety; for I will not hide her
zeal. For she did not take up weapons, but achievements of virtue. I was being
led away then, you know how. For one must speak of the painful things too,
that you may learn the good; but learn how I was led away, and how I returned.
They that sow in tears shall reap in gladness. Going they went and wept,
casting their seed; but coming they shall come in gladness, bearing their
sheaves. These words became deeds. With thanksgiving you received him whom in
grief you had sent forth. And not even in a long time; after one day all was
loosed. For the delay happened for your sake, since God from the beginning had
loosed it.

4. I tell you the secret. I crossed the sea alone, bearing the Church; for
love is not straitened. The ship was not cramped: For you are not straitened
in us. I was going away, anxious for your affairs, separated in body, but
joined in mind; I was going away, entreating God, and committing your love to
him; I was going away, I sat alone anxious for your affairs, deliberating
about the departure alone. Suddenly, when it was still an untimely hour, this
most God-beloved woman sent a letter on the first day, saying these words (for
I must speak her words too): Let not your Holiness think that I knew what had
been done. I am innocent of your blood. Wicked and corrupt men contrived this
device; and of my tears God is witness, to whom I minister as priestess. What
a libation did she pour out? For her tears became a libation. To whom I
minister as priestess. The priestess, self-ordained, sacrificing to God and
pouring out tears and confession and repentance, not for a priest, but for the
Church, for a people scattered. She remembered, she remembered both the
children and the baptism. I remember that through your hands my children were
baptized. These things the empress; but the priests, all of them, from envy,
did not know the place where I was lodging. And the marvel to tell: she,
trembling as for a child, went about everywhere, not in body, but by her own
escort of soldiers. For she had not found the place where I was staying. She
sent everywhere, anxious lest he be murdered, lest he be killed, and we lose
the prey. This only, and I display what is from myself. I seek only, and they
do not prevail, =52.446= The enemies went about everywhere spreading nets,
that they might catch him and bring him back into their hands. Then she also
entreated, and grasped the imperial knees, making her husband a partner of the
hunt; as Abraham did Sarah, so she her husband. We have lost the priest, she
says, but let us bring him back. There is no hope for us of the kingdom,
unless we bring that man back. It is impossible for me to have fellowship with
any of those who did these things; shedding tears, beseeching God, displaying
every device. You too know with how much goodwill she received us, how she
embraced us as her own members, how she said that she too was eager together
with you.

For these words did not escape your good sense, that you received the mother
of the Churches, the nurse of the monks, and the protectress of the saints,
the staff of the poor. The praise of that woman becomes glory to God, a crown
of the Churches. Shall I speak of her warm longing? Shall I speak of her
honor-loving zeal concerning me? Late yesterday evening she sent, saying these
words: Say to him: my prayer has been fulfilled; I demanded the achievement; I
have been crowned more than by the diadem; I have received back the priest, I
have given back the head to the body, the helmsman to the ship, the shepherd
to the flock, the bridegroom to the bridal chamber.

5. The adulterers have been put to shame. If I live, if I die, I no longer
care. See the achievements of the trial. What shall I do, that I may give you
a worthy return of love? A worthy one I cannot, but what I have, I give. I
love you, ready to pour out my blood for your salvation. No one has such
children, no one such a flock, no one a field so flourishing. I have no need
of farming; I sleep, and the ears of grain grow tall. I have no need of toil;
I am at rest, and the sheep prevail over the wolves. What shall I call you?
Sheep or shepherds, or helmsmen, or soldiers and generals? I make all the
names true of you. When I see the good order, I call you sheep; when I see the
care, I address you as shepherds; when I see the wisdom, I name you helmsmen;
when I see the courage and the vigor, I call you all soldiers and generals. O
toil, O care of a people; you drove out the wolves, and you will not grow
careless. The sailors who were with you

:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3
:END:

who were steering the ship with us wished to sink the ship. I marveled at your
understanding. I say these things, not anointing you for faction. For theirs
is faction, but yours is zeal. For you did not think them worthy to be
destroyed, but that this be prevented both for your sake and for the Church's,
lest it be submerged again. For your courage did not allow the storm to come
about, but their purpose worked the surge. And I reckon not by the end, but by
their purpose. A man standing at the altar, entrusted with the care of so
great a =52.447= people, bound to still the painful things, you increased the
storm, you drove the sword against yourself, consuming your own children in
purpose, even if not in deed. But God prevented it. So I marvel at you and
praise you, because after the war and when peace has come you look to how a
perfect peace may come about. For the helmsman must have concord with the
sailors; for if they are at faction, the vessel is sunk. You have achieved the
peace after the grace of God; I will make you partners of the security.
Without you I will do nothing, and then also not without the most God-beloved
Augusta. For she too cares and is anxious and does very many things, so that
what has been planted may remain firm, so that the Church may remain
unstormed. I praised therefore both your =52.448= understanding, and the
emperors' care. For they do not care so much about war as about the Church,
not so much about the city as about the Church.

Let us entreat God, let us petition her, let us remain in the prayers; and let
us not, since the terrible things have been loosed, become more slack. For
this reason until today we pray that the terrible things be loosed. Let us
give thanks to God, as we were then courageous, so also today earnest. And for
all these things let us give thanks to God, to whom be the glory and the power
together with the Son, with the good and life-giving Spirit, now and always,
and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
