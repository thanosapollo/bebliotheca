Rom 1:1
And some puzzle in vain, whether the title "called" is to be added to the
leading word so that it may be slave, called, or to the following as apostle
called, the sense not being out of tune according to either, inasmuch as all
are called both commonly those called into the faith and this grace and those
brought up by election into the apostolic rank. According to another then he
is called both as slave and as apostle. Rom 1:21 He seems then more simply to
set up the word toward Greeks, as making the proof according to one form of
impiety only, idolatry; yet not even to those who look more exactly at what he
says does he appear to do this, but much rather broadening the rebuke against
them as lacking no form of impiety. Rom 3:2 The first, he says, not in order
that he may say some second and third, but because this is first, encompassing
of all goods; for what would be better than to believe the words of God? Rom
3:4 And perhaps someone more simply and more fittingly to the things set forth
would interpret the word, taking the saying not according to a denial but
according to a hypothesis by addition of one syllable, that even if all men
love the falsehood before the truth, no less God remains true and just, both
promising such things as befit God, and accomplishing everything said,
whenever it is fitting
