* On Almsgiving

Of Chrysostom, On Almsgiving. =60.747= Again there has been given to us by our
Lord and God and Savior Jesus Christ a season for casting the seeds of
almsgiving into your ears; again Christ has granted us to imitate the sower
who sows his seed upon the good ground and receives a hundredfold. For behold,
the proclamation of the hands has sounded the trumpet; behold, the theater of
almsgiving has been assembled. Let them therefore be called into the midst,
the lovers of God and the lovers of honor and the lovers of the poor; let the
lovers of the crowns be called. For God is present, the bestower of the
prizes, receiving a little money from those who love the poor, and granting
them the kingdom of heaven. Let none of you, I beg, be deprived of such grace;
let no one, for a little money, overlook this great and supermundane gift,
neither poor nor rich, neither slave nor free, neither wise nor unlearned,
neither man nor woman; but let us all, I beg, with eagerness buy the kingdom
of heaven. And let none of the poor say to me: I am a beggar; for it is
necessary first to take hold of them, since they think their excuse appears
acceptable before God. For what does the poor man often say? I have no money,
I do not have enough for daily food, I toil and am worn out the whole day,
that I may scarcely be able to survive; I carry loads on my shoulders, I
carpenter, I hammer, and I waste away the strength of my body; and you sit
condemning me, that I should give my labors to strangers? For tell me, do you
altogether call them your labors, and do you think the strength of your body
is your own possession? And do you not believe that if you give a little of
what you call your labors, you are giving alms to him who granted you the
strength and power of the work? Rather give a little less to your body, that
you may grant the great treasure to the soul. For if, having ten obols, you
give nine to the body and one to the soul, is your whole life overthrown on
that account? If you have fifty obols, and give five to God through the
helpless, and the forty-five to yourself, you have strengthened the soul by
faith, you have also empowered the body through the prayer of others, and you
have received grace to work again, and again to gain many times more, and you
have become richer than the rich toward almsgiving. But you will certainly say
to me: And what, he says, shall I gain from this? That you are no longer named
poor, but a lender to God.

And whence, he says, will this come about for me? Believe the Scripture that
says: He who has mercy on a poor man lends to God; and you have to hear from
him: Inasmuch as you did it to one of these least, you did it to me. Have you
not often, sitting in the marketplace, seen a rich man passing by, and blessed
him, saying, Blessed is this rich man, because he has no need of the supply of
others, but rather even lends? And that man lends to men, but you to God; that
man =60.748= takes from each the fruit of labors with toil and with fighting
in sin; but you receive from God without toil, without care, with joy a
hundredfold; that man receives for this short life, but you for the unending
life inherit eternal life. And if you do not believe me, believe God who has
assured you with an oath, saying: Amen I say to you, you shall receive a
hundredfold, and shall inherit eternal life. Do not therefore think yourself
diminished by the gift to the poor; for you do not become poor from sharing
with the poor. If the poor man has mercy on a poor man, he does not become
poorer, but praiseworthy, and he rather secures his poverty. Have you heard
the Prophet saying: I was younger; for I have also grown old; and I have not
seen the righteous forsaken, nor his seed seeking bread? and again, All the
day the righteous has mercy and lends, and his seed shall be for a blessing?
For a blessing, why? Because he who sows upon blessings also reaps upon
blessings; for if you take care of your garments, he will also take care of
your soul. For nothing so clothes the soul as the covering of the widow and
orphan and poor man. For it does not let the soul be seen naked before God on
that fearful and terrible day; but clothed with a robe of light through
almsgiving it appears before God with all glory.

For God says through the prophet: Break your bread to the hungry, and bring
the homeless poor into your house; and then your light shall be early, and
your healings shall quickly rise, and your righteousness shall go before you.
Did you see how great a reward is stored up for you through a little bread or
one obol? Did you learn how much light the fruit of almsgiving brings you? Do
you wish to learn how great a treasure the small gift of the poor has procured
for us? Do you wish to see what poor people, bringing poor things, inherited a
place of great treasures? Listen. For we do not assure you from the New
Testament only, but also from the Old, that you through love of the poor may
gain the heavenly treasure. A famine once fed upon men throughout all the
earth, and so great a famine that every day a river of men and beasts was
given over to death, not in a short time, but through three whole years and a
half, so that the whole earth was also destroyed. And the cause of the evil
was the idolatry of the Israelites, and the lawlessness of Ahab and Jezebel,
through which Elijah, being a man, was able to set bolts upon the heavens,
saying: As the Lord lives, there shall be no rain except by my mouth. And it
did not rain upon the earth for three years and six months. Then at last the
lawless king, perceiving that the prophet Elijah had become the minister of
this sentence, went about seeking to kill him. So that for this reason the
prophet, having fled into the land of the foreigners, found a widow woman, a
remnant of the famine, having a little flour and a little =60.749= oil, on
which she had hanging all the hope of her own life and of her children. And
the prophet Elijah says to her: Bring me a little bread in your hand. And the
woman said to him: As the Lord your God lives, I have no cake, but only as
much as a handful of flour in the house, and a little oil in the cruse; and
behold I am gathering two sticks, and I go in to make it for myself and my
children, and we shall eat and die. Does any of us today drive on into such
poverty? God forbid. But from this remnant of death the widow first made a
cake for the prophet, and brought it out to him, despising herself and her
children. And what recompense did she receive for this poor almsgiving? Hear
plainly what the prophet says: Thus says the Lord: The jar of flour shall not
fail, and the cruse of oil shall not diminish. Until when, prophet? Until the
Lord gives rain and dew upon the earth.

For this reason the prophet says: Blessed is the man who trusts in the Lord;
and his hope shall be in a time of drought, and he shall not be diminished nor
fail. And the word of the prophet became a deed, and neither did the jar fail,
nor did the cruse diminish. Are you poorer than she, O man? Does so great a
famine press upon you, that you should not do almsgiving? It is not so, God
forbid; but you both have and are able, whoever you are, and especially if you
are a believer. And toward the body you are lavish, but concerning the soul
you are niggardly. Almsgiving is food of the soul; and just as bread and wine
are food and gladness of the body, so also almsgiving and prayer are healing
and joy of the soul. And what does a man give in exchange for his soul, except
almsgiving only? And if you do not know, learn also from the widow in the New
Testament, that through two obols she gained the whole kingdom of heaven,
having received God himself entire as the praiser of this poverty, her
fruit-offering having gone up even as far as the heavens. Did you not see the
Savior in the holy Gospels accepting the widow who cast the two mites into the
treasury, and granting her the heavenly crown? For he saw the fragrance of the
soul, he saw the poor woman a lover of the poor, he saw her who lacked money
contributing together with those who did not lack. There were many who were
casting much into the treasury; and God and man together was present at what
took place, the same not judging according to the face, and searching the
hearts of men. And what sentence he utters concerning these, let us hear:
Amen, amen I say to you, that this widow has cast in more than all. For I do
not accept all that is cast in, he says, but I also prove the purpose of those
who contribute. Amen, amen I say to you, that this widow has cast in more than
all. Why? Because these brought from their abundance, but she brought in her
whole livelihood. Therefore since he judged justly, let us also all say to
God, as the Prophet said: You are righteous, O Lord, and your judgments are
upright; because you prove the purpose of men, and you do not circumscribe the
kingdom by the multitude of things offered. For you do not attend to the
things offered, but to the disposition of those who offer. Let none of you
therefore hesitate, I beg, even if it be two mites, even if ten obols, to cast
them into the treasury of the widows and orphans; for so also the holy
Scripture teaches, that according as one has purpose, he is acceptable before
God. Let not therefore that table become a stranger to your fruit-offering;
but have a share with it, that you may receive a share with those who receive,
mercy from the merciful God who said: Have mercy, that you may receive mercy.
For just as it is believed that there is an angel from heaven who receives the
prayers of the people and offers them to God, and you are eager together with
all to render the prayers to God, that your gift of prayer may be accepted
together with all; so believe that in these things also there is an angel who
receives what is given by you to the poor, and offers these to God. And who is
this angel, but the one who brought the prayers and the alms of Cornelius to
God, and said to him: Cornelius, your alms and your =60.750= prayers have gone
up for a memorial before God? And he was formerly a Greek; but after this,
from the time the angel spoke to him, he became a believer, and so they were
accepted. How much more yours, who love Christ?

And we ought then also to exhort the rich. Especially the many are lovers of
the poor, and of themselves they do this; for they have well practiced being
watered by such men, those teachers before us, to bear fruit; but nevertheless
since they also demand an account of the poor among us, I will not shrink from
exhorting them, and leading them by the hand toward almsgiving, guarding these
two things, that they may not be accused before God, nor I be slandered before
the angels; and how, I say. For if I do not deposit the words of the divine
Scripture upon your good table, as silver upon the banker, I am immediately
accused by God, and I hear from him: You ought to have deposited my silver
with the bankers, and I coming would have required my own with interest. For
this he also accused in the holy Gospels the one entrusted with the denarius,
who hid it. And how the prophet calls it silver, hear: The words of the Lord
are pure words; silver tried in the fire, proven, purified to the earth seven
times. So also God says to the prophet: Son of man, testify to this people;
and again otherwise, Comfort, you priests, my people; speak into the ears of
Jerusalem. Having therefore received such charges I exhort you all to come to
almsgiving, lest I also be slandered by you at that hour, if, which God
forbid, someone should hear from the Judge on that fearful day: Wicked
servant, why did you not have mercy on your fellow-servant? and now how do you
wish to receive mercy? And again: Repent now, because there is no time of
repentance there; lest someone being accused at that terrible hour should say,
that I was not taught by the shepherd, I did not learn what a man shall give
in exchange for his soul, nor did he tell us that almsgiving atones for sins,
he did not interpret for us the Scripture given him by you which says, Blessed
is he who considers the beggar and the poor; in an evil day the Lord will
deliver him; but he only separated us from Jewish unbelief, and again forbade
the superstition of the Greeks; from whose lawlessness also, having been
taught, we stood away. For if he had also trained us toward almsgiving, we
would have had the fruits of this also to show.

For this reason also I do not cease exhorting with boldness, but I admonish
and testify to rich and poor together, men and women alike: spare your souls,
and ransom them through money from the wrath that is coming; lighten the
burden of sins through almsgiving; have mercy, that you may receive mercy. For
almsgiving has the power of holy baptism; and just as the holy washing
cleanses away the sins of men, so also almsgiving cleanses the faults of the
soul of those who have mercy. The word is not mine, but the divine Scripture
teaches, saying: Alms and faithfulness are; sins are purged away. The money,
even if we do not wish it, remains here; but the soul, even if we do not want
it, is conducted away from here, undergoing the accounts of the sins. For this
reason also our Lord Jesus Christ said in the Gospels: What shall a man be
profited, if he gain the world, but lose his soul? or what shall a man give in
exchange for his soul? Hear then, I beg, this saying, that in peace it is
despised, but in necessities it shows the power of the law of God. For the
hearer often hears: What shall a man be profited, if he gain the whole world,
but lose his soul? and he runs past the power of this saying. And if he
despise the saying in peace, he learns in necessities the power of the one who
spoke it. For what does God do, when he sees someone clinging to money, but
turning away from almsgiving, and choosing to suffer all things rather than
give up the money? In necessities he teaches him to know the law of God; and
how, hear:

Consider with me a merchant going to sea, and having his mind only on =60.751=
money, choosing to suffer all things rather than lose one obol, bringing ten
thousand oaths against his soul, so as not to give up anything of his own. But
such a man, harsh and niggardly, in the hour of necessity, when he is
violently storm-tossed and in danger on the sea; for the surge comes on, the
sea raises triple waves, the waves are lifted up as far as heaven; then
immediately the love of money withers, and he who endured every toil in order
to gather the money makes jettison with his own hands of all that he has, and
grants the wealth to the sea. But ask him immediately, What are you doing, O
man? Why are you mad? Why do you throw the money uselessly? The sea does not
keep your deposits; it is not as the earth which, having received the seeds,
gives you many times more; for it does not receive seeds and give sheaves. Why
then do you lend uselessly to an ungrateful element? Why do you trust
unfaithful waves? Why do you give your cargo as a deposit to the sea that does
not repay? You were not willing to lend to God, and you throw the money to the
wild sea? What then does that man immediately answer? I wish to save my soul,
rather than gain the whole world. Now you have learned, What shall a man give
in exchange for his own soul? now you have learned the saying of Christ, where
exhorting he said, What shall it profit a man, if he gain the whole world, but
lose his soul? But did the law not train you? Let fear at least train you.

Did the teaching of God not train you? Let the madness of the sea train you. O
the inconsistent act; O the necessity that trains all things; the sea, using
force, takes all, but God, exhorting toward the kingdom of heaven, does not
even take a part? You trust the sea that promises you nothing good, and you do
not obey God who promises you a hundredfold and grants it? Do you wish that I
should also set before you another image of such necessity? For it is needful
that your soul be benefited from every side, both through the fearful =60.752=
things and through the good. Consider then also those who traffic on land, who
do and transact all things in order to gain something, who dare with an oath,
and are full of every perjury in order to take one obol more, that such men
set aside the laws of God. Prophets preach, and those men shut their ears;
teachers cry out, and those men sneer at the admonition. And what then happens
in the case of these? For not in the case of all, but of these. For often such
a merchant falls among robbers; and he who gaped toward wealth, and was
insatiably clothed with the passion of love of money, immediately strips off
such a disease, and becomes lavish in giving; for he sees the sword which the
robber carries, and the passion flees; the robber comes on, and that man is
found at last, like some lover of honor, offering his own things with
entreaty. What then does he say to the robber then? Take all, and only grant
me my soul. Let this man also hear: Now you have learned what a man will give
in exchange for his soul. To a robber who wishes to take all you give with all
entreaty, to him who harms you in all things; but to God who wishes to take a
part you do not lend, to him who richly provides you all good things, both
earthly and heavenly; but you do not even fear God as much as the robber? And
you fear a sword being bared, but you despise the fire of gehenna that is
threatened.

Do not, I beg, let any of you become such; for you are not, especially being
lovers of Christ; for you know all things that will happen to men. Secure your
souls before the departure from here, and recognize him who has said in the
holy Gospels: Blessed are the merciful, for they shall receive mercy. To him
therefore, the God who has mercy and feeds and does not reproach, let us send
up glory, with the Father and the all-holy and life-giving Spirit, now and
always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
