of horses above corruption; for this is a myth and painted noise, unless
someone, as you, having knowing tones, will allegorize the sowing of the males
to be incorrupt by the transmission of the race, one after another kindling
nature. Not this for you, nor may you take such a reward on our account, but
may you obtain a vehicle of life running into the paths of incorruption. And
since thereafter even the insolences of the people, having been reared
together with the drunkenness of the tyrant, had a resident disorder of the
members, and the City was almost in labor with all the citizens mixed together
after the manner of a Centaur; for being cut and badly mixed they were busied
with their own confusions, coming as a physician Galenic in the proper sense
and palpating the disordered arteries, you yourself did not overlook the
throbbing disease, and you used in no way a cutting division, nor again the
astringent tones, but with a softening and speaking drug you persuaded them
from good order alone not to suffer this beastly passion, but rather to have
the rational nature above, and to throw down and well


kick out the beast-formed monster of disorder, lest somehow the manger-fed
disorder of the wits, turned aside from the beaten path, take the mind
wandering with itself. Thus most excellently you bring together the divided
manners of the demagogue ranks, and having composed a certain composite living
thing from opposite reasonings you fitted them into unity, and you joined the
manners leaping away from one another into one, and you alone accomplished
what nature did not have the means to accomplish. Wishing to join a memory
profitably to this greatest deed, the City writes for you the image from
below, for if for the one above you had another painter. And since, after the
manner of a householder, you have laid waste all the places within us and,
having emptied the resident veins of the passions by your pious purgative, you
have shut in the harm of the inward cruelty, and thereafter even the clouds of
Thrace from without raised up for us winters of battle, and here the
Scythian-nursing Charybdis, seeming to be silent, was lying in wait in a
robber's way, and there again the Slav wolves running out together were
joining the surge of the sea to the land, and mixed with the bloods from them
the stream was becoming red from violence, and from that side, as if
contending still more, the hard-to-meet sight of the Gorgon of Perseus was
brought face to face; and all the world was mixed together at once. But it was
necessary that you be general of the battle; perhaps another in so great a
confusion would not easily have endured to steer, but it having seemed good to
you so as to bear every load, if indeed you should be able, in place of all,
you forbade your own cares and made the cares of all your own. And often
wishing to stretch the bow and to strike Charybdis into the Gorgon, the
stone-making image of the beholders, having turned you held back the hurried
shot; and indeed, as you were shooting a missile against her, the
plunder-loving wolves were drawing you back; and in a little time, assembling,
you were enduring one triple-danger battle against them. And when these swords
of necessity, sharpened by common assaults against us, were divided in a short
time by your cares, a winter thereafter was mastering not only the affairs,
but also the course of time; in which especially the extensions of the night
give birth to entries for cares, you were setting your mind to cut out, as far
as was reachable, the circumstances from the ends of the sun; for you knew
Persia as the spring of the evils. And indeed, shaping the matter, you
pretended to present yourself to the places before the city, not as something
pleasant and especially when the time was winter, for those accustomed to have
abundance, but so that the affairs of the citizens might in no way be
entangled with your counsels, nor again that the decrees of your plans become
evident to harmful spies; for a spy, having slipped in with a crowd as with a
cloud, is covered and remains hard to find. There then, most mighty one,
gathering the whole mind of yourself and feeding it with wise leisure, you
read all <the> ordinances of the laws that are determined from arms and from
affairs. But the hidden decrees of your aim were starting-points of words for
those accustomed to speak, among whom the tongue, having slipped from the
inner door, flees and shouts in a wandering way; yet you understood these
things to have pardon. For it seemed somehow from a reasonable necessity of
the love toward you that the community was distressed, not having the tongue
provoked from envy, for cares are not envied by the jealous, but not bearing
to look upon the absence, even if they often happen to have been benefited.
There, imitating the Elijah of old and entering the deserts, you were feeding
not on food, as you showed, but on cares; for there was no work of warlike
arrays which you did not go after by the leisure of your plans, forming,
ordering in front, preparing, prescribing, and fashioning the images of the
battle for others, for yourself, for the army, for the nations, so that one
might say, having taken it in brief, gathering the contests before the
contests and doing the battle completely before the battle. Others then will
write, to whom it seems dear, mountains, ravines and narrow passages and the
rocky and hard-to-pass projections through which you passed as a swift-running
breath, as neither is it possible to strike with rocks arrived from missiles,

after toilsome works, as in a side-work, the disaster of Dvin, if indeed it is
fitting to liken to a disaster that which is being sacked for a pious master
or being saved for impious Chosroes. Already I approach the city of
Darartasis; and this they say is the northern of the Persian boundaries, and
to have the position toward the South as much as it lies opposite to us, and I
will form in the running word the turning-post against it in a short by-run.
For Ardashir, being a slave in fortune, they say, having made the Parthians
revolt from the then empire by a tyrannical and bold sword, set Persia on the
throne in reverse in evils upon the captive throne which he seized. And they
collect years, as they say, ten with six having run through sixties, in which
the bitter race of this seed from bloods was in labor with a child of bloods;
for in so great an extension of time that hard-to-loose binding of that
murderous chain had no change, but from children to children this bond, mixed
in slaughters, was interwoven together, and every hostile man-slayer among
them sowed a child toward a father-slaying offspring, and that one again, as
the substance of a viper, sent the inheritance back to the descendants; and a
stream was poured, flowing the murders upon, until having been widened into an
unmeasured pouring it stood, having put Chosroes into the depth. For it was
necessary, I think, that they, emulating the life of Jocasta by a parallel
envy, run up toward like disasters. That one then was founding this city into
a high tower, into an impregnable place, into a wall, as it showed, of sin;
for there Chosroes had both the magi and his own protectors, the coals,
reasonably held by a terrible suspicion lest you snatch the august captives.
But you yourself having come as the speed of lightning and having fashioned
for them a feigned slaughter, for you yourself do not wish even a slaughter of
barbarians, unless they should draw the swords against themselves, and having
set many engines of the instruments, rams, tortoises, stone-sending slings,
and having fenced the phalanx and having enclosed their whole wall with a new
wall, you took all within from fear alone. First then, most mighty one, of all
the spoils you burned to coals the gods of Persia, offering as acceptable
first-fruits to your God the treasures of the forefathers of Chosroes; for
quenching them you were burning Chosroes, not as an enemy of the fire of
substance, for you are wholly fire joined to God, but as wishing to bring even
the creation free and saved to God. For even the fire was released from
pollutions, having been more insulted by the worship, and it has as honor the
fair dishonor, having been purified by your pious taking-down, and it endured
to be unfortunate profitably, well turned to ash rather than badly kindled.
Thus having quenched all that fire you left nothing, except as much as it was
necessary to speak, a small spark for the burning of Chosroes.
