CATECHESIS 2 TO THOSE BEING ILLUMINATED, in Jerusalem, concerning repentance
and remission of sins; and concerning the adversary; and a reading from the
prophet Ezekiel: As I live, says the Lord, I do not desire the death of the
sinner, as that he should turn and live; and the rest.

1. A fearful thing is sin, and lawlessness a most grievous disease of the
soul, cutting through its sinews, and becoming a cause of eternal fire; an
evil of free choice. And that we sin of our own free choice the prophet
somewhere says plainly: I planted you a fruitful vine, wholly true; how were
you turned to bitterness, the foreign vine? The planting is good, whose fruit
is evil by choice; and for this reason the planter is blameless, but the vine
shall be burned with fire, because it was planted for good and bore fruit for
evil. For God made man upright, according to Ecclesiastes, and they sought
many reckonings; and the Apostle also says: For we are his workmanship,
created for the good works, which God prepared beforehand, that we might walk
in them. The Creator then, being good, created for good things; but the things
well created by him were turned to wickedness of their own free choice. A
fearful thing is sin, but not unhealable; fearful for the one who holds it,
easily healed for the one who believes. Suppose for me someone holding fire in
his hand: while he holds it, the coal burns; put away the coal, and you put
away the burning with it. And if anyone thinks that in sinning he is not
burned, to him Scripture says: Shall one cast fire into his bosom, and his
garments not be burned? So also sin cuts through the sinews of the soul,
breaks the bones of the mind, and covers over the brightness of the soul.

2. But someone will say: What ever is sin? Is it a living thing? Is it an
angel? Is it a demon? What is it that works? It is not an enemy brought in
from outside, =33.412= but an evil shoot growing from you. Look straight with
your eyes, and there is no desire. Hold what is yours, and do not seize what
belongs to others, and robbery falls asleep. Remember the judgment, and
neither fornication nor adultery nor murder has strength against you; but when
you forget God, then you begin to spring up fountains of evils.

3. But you are not only the originator of this, but another instigator is
originator too, the devil. He suggests to all men, but he does not prevail
over those who do not obey. For this reason Ecclesiastes says: If the spirit
of the one who rules rises against you, do not leave your place. Shut the
door, and keep it far from you, and it will not harm you. But if you receive
the seed of desire, it grows through your reasonings, it strikes roots, it
unbinds the bonds of your mind, and it brings you into a pit of evils. But you
often say: I am faithful, and a demon does not get the better of me. Yet a
root once found has often split a rock. Do not receive the seed, for it breaks
through faith; before it blossoms, root the evil out from the root; do not be
slack at the beginning, and later be busied with axes and fire. When an eye
trouble begins, treat it in good time, that you may not be blinded and then
seek the physician.

4. The originator of sin then is the devil, the begetter of evils. And Jesus
the Lord said this, not I; he himself said of him: that from the beginning the
devil sins; before him no one sinned. He sinned, not receiving sinning from
nature, for sin would turn back upon him who fashioned him; but having been
fashioned good, he became a devil, taking the name from his deed. Being an
archangel, he was called devil from his slandering; being a good servant of
God, he became Satan, for Satan is the interpretation of devil. These are not
my teachings, but we were helped by Ezekiel. He, taking up laments over him,
says: You were the seal of likeness, you were born in the paradise of God; and
a little after: You were born blameless in your days, until injustices were
found in you. It was not brought in from outside; you begot the evil. And
because of your sins you were wounded, and I cast you to the ground. The Lord
again in the Gospels says: I was beholding Satan =33.413= as lightning fallen
from heaven to the earth. Do you see the agreement of the Old with the New?
He, having fallen, led many away with himself; he casts desires upon those who
obey him; from him come adultery, fornication, and every evil there is.
Through him our first father fell by disobedience, and instead of a paradise
bearing fruits of itself he received a thorn-bearing ground.

5. What then? someone will say: we are lost; sin is a fearful thing, and is
there no salvation? We have fallen, and there is no rising? We have been
blinded, and there is no looking up? We have become lame, and there is no
being made straight of foot? That I may say briefly just now: we have died,
and there is no rising? Shall he who raised Lazarus four days dead and
stinking not be able to raise? Shall he who poured out his precious blood for
us not be able to deliver us from sin? Let us not despair of ourselves,
brothers; let us not cast ourselves away, as though it were something terrible
not to believe in repentance. For he who does not look for salvation adds the
evils; but he who has hoped in the cure afterwards easily spares himself. A
robber who does not expect the amnesty increases toward desperation; having
hoped, he often moves toward repentance. Further, the snake puts off its old
age; shall we not put off sin? And land tilled, though thorny, was changed to
fruit-bearing; shall we, being taught, not be made better? Nature admits
salvation, but it seeks the choice.

6. God is a lover of man, and no little one. Do not say: I have fornicated, I
have committed adultery, terrible things have been done by me; will God then
forgive? Hear what the psalmist says: How great is the multitude of your
goodness, Lord! Your sins gathered together do not overcome the multitude of
God's mercies; your wounds do not overcome the medical skill. Only give
yourself over through faith; tell the physician the injury; say as David said:
I said: I will confess against myself my sin to the Lord, and you forgave the
impiety of my heart.

7. Do you wish to see God's love of man, if you do not quite believe the words
of Scripture, having just now come into the catechesis? Do you wish to see
others so saved, that you may believe that this is possible also in your case?
Do you wish to see God's great love of man? Adam the first-formed disobeyed
God. Could he not bring death upon him at once? But the most loving of men
casts him out of paradise, yet settles him over against it, that seeing he may
be saved through repentance. Cain the brother-killer, inventor of evils,
originator of murders, the first envious one and who slew his brother, with
what is he condemned? Groaning and trembling you shall be upon the earth.
Great the sin, small the punishment.

8. True love of man indeed, but still small. Reckon the things about Noah. The
giants sinned with lawlessness =33.416= so great that a flood was about to
come. In the five hundredth year he speaks to Noah; in the six hundredth he
brings the flood upon the earth. Do you see the breadth of God's love of man
stretched out over a hundred years? Could he not have done at once what he did
then? But he purposely extended it, giving an opening for repentance. Do you
see God's goodness? Would that those then had repented! and they would have
enjoyed God's love of man.

9. Come then also to the others saved through repentance, even if among women.
You have fornicated, you have defiled the body, you have become useless; is
there then salvation? you will say. Look, O woman, to Rahab, and take courage
for salvation. She who fornicated openly and publicly is saved. How was she
saved? She believed; she said only: Your God, the God in heaven and upon
earth. Your God: for she did not dare to say my God, because of her
wantonness. Imitate the faith, and receive the salvation. And do you wish to
know that she was saved? It is written in the Psalms: I will be mindful of
Rahab and Babylon. O the greatness of God's counsel and love of man, that
harlots are remembered in the Scriptures! But not simply: I will be mindful of
Rahab and Babylon, but: with those who know me. There is salvation both for
men and for women.

10. Even if a whole people sins, it does not overcome God's love of man. The
people made a calf, and God did not depart from his love of man; men denied
God, but God did not deny himself. These are the gods of Israel; and again the
God of Israel is the savior. And not the people alone sinned, but Aaron the
high priest also; Moses is the one who says: And the anger of the Lord came
upon Aaron, and I entreated, he says, for him, and God forgave him. Moses
entreats God for a high priest; and the Only-begotten Son asking does not
entreat God? Then he did not hinder Aaron, who stumbled, from entering the
high priesthood; and will he hinder you, who have come from the nations, from
coming to salvation? Repent you likewise, and the grace is not barred to you.
Set forth a manner of life that sin cannot touch; for God is truly a lover of
man, and all time cannot tell out God's love of man; nor if all tongues came
together could they tell even a part of God's love of man. But we speak a part
of what is written, and we do not know how much even the angels received
forgiveness for; for he forgives also those, since one alone is without sin,
he who cleansed the sins. And about them this is enough; we turn next to the
things about ourselves. =33.417=

11. Shall I present to you also other types of repentance? Will you come also
to David? Do you wish to see the blessed one? Take the type of repentance. The
great one fell. After his rest, walking at evening upon the housetop, he saw
carelessly, and he suffered what is human. The sin was accomplished, but
thankfulness of heart did not die with it. Nathan the prophet came, a reproof,
a swift physician. God was angry, he says, and you sinned. The common man
spoke to the reigning king; the king clad in purple was not puffed up, for he
attended not to the one speaking but to the one who sent him; the ring of
soldiers standing about did not dazzle him, for he saw the angelic one; for
beholding the invisible he waited. And he says to the one who came, answering
through him to him who sent him: I have sinned against the Lord. Do you see
the humility of a king? Do you see confession? For how would he have known
that it was a reproof? how would the many have known? The thing was done
swiftly; no prophet was present, no reproof was present; and yet he confessed
the evil. Because he confessed with gratitude, he had the swiftest cure. And
again Nathan, who reproved him: And the Lord has put away your sin. Do you see
the swiftest change of wrath? Nevertheless you have, by provoking, provoked
the enemies of the Lord. You had many enemies, but temperance guarded you; now
you have provoked them through the sin committed.

12. But even if that one said most of all, The Lord has put away your sin, the
king did not depart from repentance: he wrapped himself in sackcloth instead
of purple, and instead of gilded thrones he sat upon ashes and the ground; and
having ashes for food, as he himself says: For I ate ashes like bread. He
melted with tears the eye that had desired. The rulers begged him to eat
bread, and he did not obey; he extended the fast to the seventh whole day.
Kings confess thus; do you, the common man, not owe confession? And after the
rebellion of Absalom, though many were the ways of flight, he flees by the
mount of Olives, scarcely calling upon the Redeemer. When Shimei cursed him,
he says: Let him alone, that the Lord may look upon my humiliation.

13. Do you see that it is a good thing to confess? Do you see that there is
salvation? Solomon also fell; but he says, or rather speaks: Late I repented.
And Ahab became most lawless, an idolater, a madman, the killer of prophets, a
stranger to piety, a desirer of fields not his own. But when he killed Naboth,
Elijah the prophet came, and merely threatened, and he wrapped himself in
sackcloth and rent his garments; and what does the loving God say? Have you
seen how Ahab is softened before =33.420= me? Scarcely in days he yielded to
the burning word of the prophet; for, he says, I will not bring the evil in
his days. And after the forgiveness he did not depart from wickedness; and the
one forgiving forgave, not looking to the future, but rendering what was due
to the present moment. For it belongs to a just judge to deal justly with each
of the things that have come to pass.

14. Again Jeroboam stood by the altar. His hand withers, because he commanded
the prophet to be seized. In that very trial, recognizing the working of the
one who came, he says: Entreat the Lord, and my hand be restored. Because
Jeroboam said, Entreat, the prophet healed him; can Christ then not heal you?
Manasseh also became most lawless, he who sawed Isaiah in two; what more
lawless than that? He went away a captive to Babylon, suffering things worthy
of his own choice at the hands of the enemies. But what does Scripture say?
Manasseh was humbled, and he confessed to the Lord, and the Lord heard him,
and brought him back into his kingdom. He who sawed the prophet was saved
through repentance; and do you disbelieve repentance?

15. Do you wish to know this strong weapon of salvation, how great confession
is even against the enemies? Hezekiah turned back a hundred and eighty-five
thousand by confession. And this is still a small thing: he undid the decree
of God through repentance. For when he was sick Isaiah says: You shall die and
not live. What hope, what salvation or expectation, with the prophet speaking
so? But he did not depart from repentance; but mindful of what is written,
When you turn and groan, then you shall be saved, he turned to the wall, and
stretching his mind through prayer toward heaven, he says: Lord, remember me;
for your remembering me is sufficient for a cure. You have not been subjected
to times, but you yourself are the lawgiver of life; for we do not live
according to birth and the interweaving of stars, but as you will. And to him
for whom living had come round to despair, fifteen years of time were added
for life; and the sign: the sun turned back ten steps. But for Jesus the sun
was eclipsed, not retracing its steps, but leaving off, and showing the
difference of the two. And he was able to undo the decree of God; and Jesus
does not grant remission of sins? Turn also yourself and groan; shut the door;
pray that it be forgiven you, that the consuming flames be taken from around
you; for confession =33.421= is strong enough to quench even fire, and strong
enough to tame even lions.

16. But if you disbelieve, consider what once came down with those about
Ananias. Into the furnace of fire what springs broke forth? How much water,
and in what measure, would it have taken to quench the flame rising over
forty-nine cubits? But where the flame rose exceedingly high, there the
fountain of repentance was poured out abundantly, as they said: You are
righteous in all things you have done to us; and: We have sinned, and we have
transgressed, and we have dealt unjustly. Then repentance stopped the flames
of the furnace; and do you disbelieve that it can quench also the fire of
gehenna? But someone of those who answer quickly will say to me, that the
account is out of place for those present: because those about Ananias were
unwilling to commit idolatry, God gave the paradoxical power. But dealing with
such an objection, and seeing the abundance of the examples, I come next also
to another type of repentance.

17. For what notion do you have about Nebuchadnezzar? Have you not heard from
the Scriptures that he was most impious, most savage in manner, having a
lion-like choice? Have you not heard that he brought out the bones of kings
from their tombs into the light? Do you not know that he led the people away
captive? Have you not heard that he blinded the king's eyes, after showing him
his children being slain? Have you not heard that he carried off the cherubim,
I do not mean the intelligible and heavenly ones, but those fashioned in the
temple, those over the mercy seat of the ark, between which the Lord spoke?
This Nebuchadnezzar tore the veil of holiness, overturned the altar, and
taking all the vessels carried them off to an idol temple; he burned the
temple itself. How many punishments was he worthy of, for killing kings, for
burning the holy things, for carrying the people captive, for storing the
sacred vessels in temples of idols? Was he not worthy of ten thousand deaths?

18. But you have seen the greatness of his evils; come then to God's love of
man. He was made a beast then, he who had a savage and most cruel choice; yet
he was made a beast not that he might perish, but that he might be saved
through repentance. He had the claws of birds, for he was a plunderer of the
saints. He had hair like lions, for like a lion he was seizing and roaring. He
ate grass like an ox, for he was beast-like, not knowing him who gave him the
kingdom. His body was bathed with the dew; for after what was done =33.424= in
the midst of the furnace with those about Ananias, when it played like a
breeze of dew, he saw and did not believe. But later he repents, chastened, as
he himself says: I Nebuchadnezzar lifted my eyes to heaven, and I blessed the
Most High, and I praised him who lives for the ages. And because he had been
scourged for many years, he praised for the ages him who lives, and confessed
him who gave him the kingdom, and knew the king of those reigning. And though
he sinned much in deeds, yet confessing in words alone, he enjoyed the
unspeakable love of man of God. He, the most lawless of all, by divine
judgment and by the love of man of him who chastens, again put on the royal
diadem, and again received the princely thrones.

19. If anyone then among you from the nations is present, who has ever
blasphemed against Christians, or plotted against the holy Churches in the
persecutions, let him have Nebuchadnezzar as the outline of salvation; let him
confess likewise, that he may meet with the like amnesty. If anyone has been
defiled through desires in passions, let him take up the repentance of blessed
David; if anyone has denied like Peter, let him be ready to die again and
again for the Lord Jesus. For he who did not grudge him who wept the
apostleship will not grudge you the evangelical mysteries. And for women let
Rahab be the outline for salvation; for men the varied example of the things
said of the men of old.

20. But all of you, looking to God's love of man, become men of good hope; not
that we may fall again into the same sins, but that, having enjoyed the
redemption and done things worthy of the grace, we may be able to wipe out the
handwriting against us by good works; through the power of the Only-begotten,
the Son of God, and our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom to the Father is the
glory, together with the Holy Spirit, now and always, and to all the ages of
ages. Amen.
