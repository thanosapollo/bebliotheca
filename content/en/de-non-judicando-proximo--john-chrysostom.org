On the Jews' saying, "You have a demon," and on, "Judge not according to
appearance."
=60.763=
Just as the light-bearing moon, whitening the dim things of night, and
holding forth its beauty as a torch to sailors and travelers on land and
sea, makes the journey unhindered for each; so also the teacher of the
Church, after the manner of the moon, flashing the radiances of the
knowledge of God through speech, escorts both those who in the sea of this
life pass by the saltiness of pleasures, and those who walk the
Christ-bearing way, toward the truth, Christ. And divine Scripture also
knows how to name the teachers of the Church who live in a reverent way of
life under the image of moon and stars, as the prophet David says: The moon
and the stars, which you founded.
It is possible to see more clearly from the Apostle's words the difference
of the saints named in the order of sun and moon and stars: There is one
glory of the sun, he says, and another glory of the moon, and another glory
of the stars, and star differs from star in glory. Come then, let us also,
shining a little after the manner of a very small star, clearly point out
to you the lightnings of the evangelical meanings. The Jews then, he says,
were saying to the Lord: You have a demon; but he: Judge not according to
appearance, but judge the righteous judgment. O Jews, you say that I have a
demon; compare my works with yours, and having searched in the rule of your
conscience, say, who then has a demon, I, or you? Judge not according to
appearance, but judge the righteous judgment. You, having passed through
the Red Sea as through a grass-bearing plain, and in the wilderness
received water from the rock changed into unspeakable sweetness, and quail
falling from mid-air; and after the gift of so many and many more other
goods, when Moses had run up the mountain, having made a calf, you
worshipped the legion of demons; but I, having come, drove the legion of
demons from humanity. Who then has a demon, I, or you? Say. Judge not
according to appearance, but judge the righteous judgment. You sacrifice
your sons and your daughters to the demons, but I raised the son of the
widow and the daughter of the ruler of the synagogue, who had died; the one
lying in a coffin I healed by the touch of a hand, sending the healing
power toward the one lying within; the other I raised from death by a word,
as from sleep. Who then has a demon? I, or you? Judge not according to
appearance, but judge the righteous judgment. You, having killed Naboth by
the fabricated words of Jezebel, and having dug up the vineyard, the
begetter of gladness, made it a vegetable-bearing field, food of the weak,
as the Apostle says, For he who is weak eats vegetables; but I, having in
the twinkling of an eye healed and made sound those who, like low
vegetables, had fallen under sickness, imparted the food of heavenly bread,
and again cultivated them into the same order, saying: I am the vine, and
you are the branches. Who then has a demon, I, or you? Say. Judge not
according to appearance =60.764= but judge the righteous judgment. You,
having sawn Isaiah with a wooden saw, dissolved him; but I, having fitted
together humanity dissolved by death through participation in the wood of
the cross, raised it up. Who then has a demon, I, or you? Say. Judge not
according to appearance, but judge the righteous judgment. You, having
thrown the prophet Jeremiah into a pit of mire, choked him; I drew out the
prophetic word that had died in the


mire of your pleasures, and established it in the souls of those obedient
to me. Who then has a demon, I, or you? Say. Judge not according to
appearance, but judge the righteous judgment. You killed Zechariah the
great between the temple and the altar; and Zechariah is interpreted,
memorial of God; but I, having come, this Zechariah, the memory of God, who
was killed by you between the temple and the altar, that is, between body
and soul (for the body is a temple of the soul, as also the soul is an
altar of God, in which the offering of reasonable worship and the incense
of prayers is sent up); having made this memory of God alive, I housed it
in the hearts of men, so that all who received it might serve as priests to
God. Who then has a demon, I, or you? Say. Judge not according to
appearance, but judge the righteous judgment.
And the disciples of the Lord also remember this saying. For when they were
charged by the scribes and Pharisees not to teach in the name of Christ:
Did we not strictly charge you not to teach in the name of Jesus? and
behold, you have filled Jerusalem with your teaching; then they answer,
reminding at the same time of judging unjustly, and saying: If it is just
to hear you rather than God, judge. See also the prophet, beloved, in the
psalm just now sung to us, speaking most plainly about the Lord's incarnate
presence and about not judging unjustly: God stood, he says, in the
assembly of gods, and in the midst he will judge gods. And who would the
God be who stood in the assembly of gods, if not altogether Christ, who
stood in the synagogue of the Jews, who were once gods, to whom he
answered, I said, You are gods? For as far as concerns the loving-kindness
of God, he named them gods; but as far as their own wickedness goes, they
were dying as men, and falling as one of the rulers; rulers, not of the
earthly men, but of those who have fallen from the heavenly vaults, about
whom Paul also says: Our wrestling is not against blood and flesh, but
against the principalities, against the powers, against the world-rulers of
this darkness, against the spiritual hosts of wickedness in the heavenly
places. God then stood in the assembly of gods, and in the midst he will
judge gods. He will distinguish gods from the Jews who were once gods. And
the Holy Spirit says to them: How long will you judge injustice, and accept
the persons of sinners? and the Lord says: Judge not according to
appearance, but judge the righteous judgment. Then again the Holy Spirit:
Judge for the or =60.765= phan and the poor. See, I pray you, how concerning
Christ it introduces all things, teaches all things, wishing to bring to
light the injustice done by them: For judge, it says, for the orphan and
the poor, justify the humble and the needy.
But perhaps someone of the hearers will say and show us to be at a loss as
to this, that the Lord has been called an orphan. For the "poor," it is
easy to learn from the Apostle's words: Who for our sake became poor, being
rich; but who would an orphan be, if not altogether one who has neither
father nor mother? But hear the Apostle saying about Christ: Without
father, without mother, without genealogy. Without father on earth
according to humanity is the Lord, and without mother in the heavens
according to divinity. Judge for the orphan and the poor, him who became
poor for our sake. For he became all things to all, more than the Apostle,
that he might gain all. Justify the humble and the needy. Learn from me,
that I am meek and humble of heart. But what does it say? They did not
know, nor did they understand; They walk about in darkness. They did not
know the God Word become flesh for our sake; for the Word became flesh, not
by being changed, but inasmuch as he tabernacled among us. They did not
know the eternal form of the divinity in the image of the servant; they did
not know; for if they had known, they would not have crucified the Lord of
glory. But neither did they understand; for they did not understand the
power of his miracles, of how many symbols they had


images; they did not understand the treasure hidden in the field, that is,
the divinity hidden in flesh, and lodging in the heavens with the Father;
they did not understand the things that happened in the case of Lazarus;
they did not understand the stone rolled away from the tomb, that he was
saying the foolish heart of men is to be moved away; they did not
understand the tomb itself, and Lazarus lying within four days dead, of
what he was a type, that in the image of a body the grave was containing
within the mind that had died and been made dead. For truly our body was a
tomb before the coming of Christ, as he says in =60.766= the Gospel:
Whitened tombs. Truly we were tombs filled with dead sins, having within
the mind put to death by pleasures, the four-days-dead and already
stinking, the mind composed of four generic virtues, prudence, justice,
courage, temperance. Likewise also the body is composed of four elements,
hot, cold, wet, and dry. They did not know, nor did they understand; for
not having received Christ the light, they walk about in darkness. For the
light also shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not overcome it.
Let all the foundations of the earth be shaken; not of the earth that is
trodden, but of that concerning which he says, Earth you are, and to earth
you shall go. For the foundation of the body is the soul; when it has gone
out, as when a foundation has given way, the house of our tent falls. Let
all the foundations of the earth be shaken, that is, let the souls be moved
and transferred from idolatry to the knowledge of God. Then, Arise, O God,
it says, judge the earth. Arise, you who were crucified, arise, you who
bound our sins in the image of thorns upon your own head, Arise, O God,
judge the earth. He said the earth, not the heaven.
Do not fear, beloved. If you have been born again of water and Spirit, you
are heaven; For as many as were baptized into Christ, you put on Christ. As
is the earthy, such also are the earthy; and as is the heavenly, such also
are the heavenly. Arise, O God, judge the earth, for you will inherit among
all the nations. You are the Christ, to whom the Father has given authority
over all, authority to judge all. For The Father judges no one; for he has
given all judgment to the Son, that they may honor the Son, even as they
honor the Father. You will inherit among all the nations. For the Father
said to you: Ask of me, and I will give you nations as your inheritance,
and the ends of the earth as your possession. To God then let us give glory
in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
