III. An exhortatory discourse of the same on self-control.
=64.17=
Those who, sitting at certain lessons, display diligence while the
compulsion of the teacher is upon them: the children's industry is
reckoned as coming from fear of the teacher. But whenever, with the
teacher absent, they hold fast to the care of what is good, the
diligence is of a pure choice. We too are pupils of the lessons of
fasting. But when, with the fast present, we display philosophy, we are
industrious as if from a pedagogue's training. But now, when this has
withdrawn for a little, those who practice self-control about
immoderate satiety have the pure praise of their judgment, as
philosophers without compulsion, as enduring by choice, as knowing how
to bind fast the good things which they stored up by their previous
labors. For he who goes out into immoderation of foods does not know
what he gathered while fasting, does not know the gains he collected,
but as having nothing stored within, as having treasured nothing in
himself, he is opened toward immoderation of food; he does not set
bolts upon the appetites, he does not secure himself with doors of
measure. And this is no wonder. For such is every poor man: he has no
care for doors and bolts, he is not given over to thoughts of plots, he
sleeps with an unanxious mind, as having nothing that would provoke
robbers, as a destitute man who cannot be robbed. But those who possess
a rich house, through a guard, as treasures being stored within,
through doorkeepers watching the entrance. We too have grown rich by
the fast; and the proofs of the wealth are clear: we have acquired
pleasures as slaves, we have become masters of the belly, to which we
formerly were enslaved; we have acquired a bodiless life, we have set
forth a new house of self-control, whose foundations we have so far
laid. But the work needs joining together, it requires a tightening
tension. Drunkenness belongs to those that loosen, not those that bind;
to those that paralyze the bases, not those that strengthen them.
No one shakes a foundation which he has well bound; no one opens a
treasury of wealth unguarded. But a faster's soul is the storehouse of
some proven rich man, and it needs exact guarding, lest it fall into
some robbery; for a soul is robbed as a house is. David =64.18= had the
house of the soul full, but he was plundered by robber-pleasure; for
he left the entrance open, and did not secure it with the bolt of
sobriety. And what is the neglect concerning sobriety? The broad
relaxation of the body. For it came to pass, he says, at the time of
evening, and David rose from his bed, and walked upon the roof in the
house of the palace; at evening, and he rose from sleep and walked;
sleep, and after sleep a walk of one desiring relaxation. A broad walk
for gluttony. What then was the harm? And he saw, from the roof, a
woman bathing; and the woman was very beautiful in appearance. And
sending, he shamed her.
To see is blameless; for no one would accuse the eyes because they see
what is seen. But the blameless thing has an accusation that has
traveled toward sin; for he did not see the form and look aside, but he
was as it were nailed to the beauty; he fixed the gaze upon the body,
he baptized the pupils in the spectacle; he no longer had maidens in
his eyes, but harlots. So great an evil is even the impulse of a
glance, if you let it go unbridled. So even now we have authority
toward food, just as then David had to use his eyes; but the going
out of sin puts the guiltless under accusation. What then? Let the body
be soothed with food; for the laborer is worthy of his food. But let
Paul be present with us as we dine, and if he should see us somewhere
disorderly toward the food, like a physician he will cry out to those
who are disorderly: Having sustenance and coverings, let us be content
with these. Sustenance, not luxury; sustenance, not the overturning
that comes from drunkenness.
Luxury once stirred Israel to an idolatrous dance; for drunkenness took
the mind captive. For the people sat down, he says, to eat and drink,
and they rose up to play. Let us therefore keep the sufficiency of
need; let the body be tended with food; seeking the consolation of
ease, let us not be broken by the burden of stuffing; let us have
authority over measured eating; let us turn aside from the unseemliness
of drunkenness, and we shall not have danger; let us obey Paul as he
exhorts: not to be drunk with wine, in which is dissipation, but to be
filled in the Holy Spirit; to whom be the glory unto the ages. Amen.
