* On the Testaments and the Coming of Christ

But they addressed as god whatever came to be, in their folly; so then
also faithful Aaron for the empty-minded people, when he sent forth the
calf from the midst of the fire, exposing the outrage, and one fashioned
another new god for mortals, a formation of the mind; and sinful craft
attended, and wealth that rejoices in evil, and time fixed the shame,
increasing it with formations and sacrifices and feasts; few indeed
dissolved them, those whose mind was a defender, who said nothing of god
which the mind alone had not seen, but the more † with words,
supposedly, † following the lights. While the course of wickedness was
still small, and while for me also the law led the way, which lay
unwritten within, a self-taught distinction of good and of evil. But
when the hollows and the broad bounds of the earth were filled with
lawlessness, and the mire boiled over with insolence, and the formation
previously tamed by many disciplines, by tongues being divided and by
waters and by rains of fire, was not willing to shake off the bonds of
the first wickedness, but was always held in the strong knots of the
flesh, devoted to lewdness and battles and idols, then indeed, this
greatest wonder of the counselor Word, God led the people from Egypt in
the hands of Moses the far-famed servant on a journey with great
wonders, and all the nations were shaken; a pillar of fire going before
led the way, and of a cloud drawing them unsigned through the
wilderness. And once in the desert mountains, a greatest wonder, coming
down from heaven under the recesses of a holy cloud, consorting with the
holiest servant alone, by lightning, trumpets, and shakings holding the
people off, he gave the law; and he engraved on tablets from both sides
both the hidden and the open, as much as the unwearied people had
strength, so that he might cut it off from the laws of the nations and
the worst. As a father of his child, under light hands, guiding the
newly set sole of the tender foot, first lifting it a little from the
ground on high, cooing sweetly and granting courage with his eye, but
then holding him back, and drawing foot upon foot he leads him in front
and seems the course and steps of the child, so also Christ the Lord,
caring for the senseless mortal, first cast the idols far away, and
delayed the sacrifices, yet still allowed them, and set him within the
law, softening wickedness; but at last the sacrifices no longer stood,
but yielded, once the people had been tamed. And the law guarded me, as
a guardian some inheritance for the child of a great father; but when
the time of the child came, it yielded willingly, and the best son
received the inheritance. Thus God. For he has the custom to persuade,
not to rage at his day-creatures; and force is without honor for me. For
even some helper of sufferings does not bring all the painful things at
once upon the sufferers, but gave one by desire, another by disease,
thinking kindly, so as to save even the one in toil, doctoring with
pleasant things the better counsels of the art.
As when some temple-builder raising a stone circle upon the air with
polished leaning stones first set supports below; then of them he brings
a stone above, and winds circle upon circle; but when he has bound it,
he has cast the support outside, and the circle is firm, drawn around in
naked air: so the law was a support of a more perfect law. Hold God to
be the beginning of all things. Think nothing in life to be firm.
Honoring parents, fear God most of all. Teach yourself not to speak what
is not lawful. Hasten to please God by works and by words. Desire the
life that has no end. By yielding yourself to friends you will be
conquering. And adorn the mind more than the form. Being mortal, do not
think great things at all. Track always the doors of the wise. Open your
mouth, child, to the word of God. Never forget his memory at any time.
Pray to God soberly each day. Entertain strangers, lest you become a
stranger to God. Bridle the soul-destroying impulses of the passions.
Secure the body with fetters soberly. Bear conscience as a rod for
yourself. Clearly give leisure in the God-inspired Scriptures. Comfort
the griefs of the poor. Wish well for neighbors above yourself. Be eager
to have friends rather than much wealth. For they are far more glorious
than gold. Hate falsehood, and love the truth. O child, keeping these
things you are saved in a godly way.
