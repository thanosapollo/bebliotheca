ORATION XVII.
To the magistrates of Nazianzus who were in distress, and the governor who was
angry.
A. My belly, my belly I ache, =35.965= and the sense-organs of my heart
seethe, says Jeremiah somewhere in his own words, the most compassionate of
the prophets, bewailing Israel being disobedient, and being alienated from the
loving-kindness of God, naming his own soul a belly, according to the laws of
the figure. For thus I find it in many places of Scripture, either as hidden
and unseen (for the same thing belongs both to soul and to belly, the being
hidden), or as receptive and, so to speak, yielding the foods from the word;
for what food is in the body, this word is in the soul. And sense-organs
perhaps the motions and thoughts of the soul, and of these especially as many
as are from sensation, by which the just man is torn, and is heated, and is in
impulse, and is not even restrainable because of the boiling of the Spirit;
for this is the seething, a kind of eagerness mixed with passion. And if one
should take some of the bodily sense-organs, he will not take it off the mark:
both of eyes, and of ears, not only being burdened by the worse both sights
and hearings, but also longing to see and to hear the better things through
compassion. Yet however these things may be understood, the just man aches and
seethes, and does not bear moderately the sufferings of Israel, whichever way
you wish to understand these: either the bodily, and of what is seen bodily,
or the spiritual, and of what is understood spiritually. Since the same
prophet also seeks a fountain of tears, and longs for a last lodging-place,
and welcomes a wilderness, that he may empty the much of the grief, and be
lightened somehow of the inner wound, having lamented Israel in quiet.
B. And the same also the divine David, not long before, being distressed at
the things concerning himself: Who will give me, he says, wings as of a dove,
and I will fly away, and I will be at rest? He seeks wings of a dove, either
as lighter and swifter; for such is every just man; or as sketching the
Spirit, by which alone we flee the terrible things, that he may come as far as
possible from present evils. Then the medicine in the straits, hope: I waited,
he says, for God who saves me from faintheartedness and from tempest. Which
then he appears doing also elsewhere, very quickly adding the healing to that
which grieves, and furnishing us a good lesson both in word and in deed of
greatness of soul toward the terrible things: My soul, he says, refused to be
comforted. Do you see words of =35.968= acedia and of despair? Did you not
fear as concerning David being incurable? What do you say? You do not receive
comfort? You despair of consolation? No one will heal you? not word, not
friend, not kinsman, not one counseling, not one sharing the pain, not one
narrating his own things, not one speaking the ancient things, not one setting
beside the present, as many as were also saved from harder evils? Has
everything been thrown down? Is it gone? Has it been cut around? Has all hope
perished? Must one only lie, awaiting the end? These things the great David,
who is broadened in afflictions; who even when a shadow of death was circling
rose up with God. What then shall I suffer, the small, the weak, the earthly,
the one not so great in the spirit? David is dizzy, and who is saved? What
help should I find when I am in hardship, or what comfort? To whom shall I
flee when I am in a strait? David answers you, the great healer, who also
charms away evil spirits through the Spirit in him. To whom you will flee, do
you wish to learn from me, and you yourself do not know? Who is the one
strengthening the slackened hands, and comforting the loosened knees, and
leading through fire, and saving through water? You need no battle-array, he
says, no weapons, no archers, no horsemen, no counselors, no friends, no help
from without. You have in yourself the alliance, which both I and everyone who
wishes have. One must only will, only set out. Near is the comfort, in your
mouth, and in your heart: I remembered God, he says, and I was gladdened. What
is readier than memory? Remember you also, and be gladdened. O the easy-to-get
of the healing! O the speed of the cure! O the greatness of the gift! God
remembered does not only lull faintheartedness and grief, but also works
gladness.
C. Do you wish to hear also other words of loving-kindness? If having turned,
he says, you groan toward the Lord, then you will be saved. Do you see
salvation yoked with the groan? And while you are still speaking, he will say:
Behold I am present; and he will speak to your soul: I am your salvation.
Nothing is in the middle of the asking and the attaining; no gold, no silver,
no stones of the translucent and precious, none of the things by which men are
bent toward mildness. When Zephaniah says, as speaking from God being angry
and being harsh: I will make their ways utterly =35.969= desert, so as not to
be traveled. Their cities failed because of there being none, nor being
inhabited; when he threatens the most dreadful things, when he casts down with
despondency, when he works gloom through the threat, immediately he brings on
also the light of the better hope, and he restores me with these words: Only I
said: Fear me, and receive instruction, and you shall not be utterly destroyed
from her eyes; and still with brighter and more loving-kind things a little
further on: In that time the Lord will say: Take heart, Zion, let not your
hands be slack. The Lord your God is in you, mighty to save you; and he will
bring upon you gladness, and he will renew you in his love, and he will gather
the crushed, and he will save the oppressed, and he will receive in the
rejected. These things both the holy ones and the reasonings wish, and my word
demands. But receive words of prudence, as the divine Solomon says, that you
may not, having fallen into a depth of evils, despise, nor be swallowed rather
by your own ignorance than by the difficulty that holds you.
D. There is a kind of circle, brothers, of human affairs, and God educates us
through the opposites; just as he composed all things wisely and bound them
together, so wisely conducting the whole and governing by his unattainable and
unsearchable judgments, and among all things also our own. For the whole, so
to speak, is moved around the Unmoved, and is shaken, in the Word in no way
(for he himself stands in all things and is unmoved, even if he escapes our
weakness), but by the things that meet and befall each day. And this is a
doctrine of God both ancient and firm, that darkness is his hiding-place
poured before our eyes; and that the many things of his administration are not
clearly seen, except as far as in dim riddles and appearances; either checking
our pride, that we may know that we are nothing toward the true and first
wisdom, but may incline toward him alone, and always seek to be flashed upon
by the radiances from there; or through the irregularity of the things seen
and being overturned transferring us to the things that stand and remain. Yet
nothing is unmoved, as I said, nor uniform, nor self-sufficient, nor similar
unto the end, neither being of good cheer, nor being despondent, not wealth,
not poverty, not power, =35.972= not weakness, not lowliness, not dominion,
not the present, not the future, not our own, not the other's, not small, not
greater, not whatever one might say. And this equality inequality has, the
change concerning all things. For all things easily go round, and change
place, and are set against; so that one should rather trust breezes and
letters upon water, than the prosperity of men. And envy stops good fortune,
and pity stops disaster; and this wisely, according at least to my word, and
wondrously, that neither the aching be without consolation, nor the faring
well be without education.
E. And it belongs to temperate men, whoever are educated by disasters, and
being purified as gold by fire say: It is good for me that you humbled me,
that I might learn your justifications; as humiliation giving birth to the
knowing of the justifications. And they suffer the thing of Peter, who called
upon the salvation when he was about to sink; and they draw nearer to God
somewhat more through the aching, and they make the benefactor their own
through the affliction. Since a soul that is weary is near God, and the
needing turns toward the one able to give, who perhaps is also despised
because of the ungrudging of the gift. For this let us look up, brothers, in
every time and every circumstance let us put forward the good hope, neither in
good cheer let us throw away the fear, nor in the painful things the good
hope. Let us remember also in fair weather the storm, and in winter the
steersman; but let us not be exhausted by the afflictions, nor become evil
slaves, confessing to the Master when he does good, but not attaching
ourselves when he educates; and yet sometimes toil is better than health, and
endurance than ease, and visitation than neglect, and turning than permission.
I will say briefly: neither through the disasters let us fall, nor through the
satiety let us insolently insult.
F. Let us be subject both to God and to one another, and to the rulers upon
earth; to God because of all things, to one another because of brotherly love,
to the rulers because of good order; and =35.975= so much the more, as they
are milder and better. It is a terrible thing to spend up gentleness by the
continuity of pardon, lest we ourselves also be required to pay the penalty of
their harshness, having loosed a calm with a wind, and bringing gloom upon
light, and mixing wormwood into honey. This too is one of our laws, and this
of the praised, and most beautifully arranged by the Spirit, who approved the
possible with the good, and legislated, just as slaves must obey masters, and
wives husbands, and the Church the Lord, and disciples shepherds and teachers;
so also to be subject to all superior authorities, not only because of the
wrath, but also because of the conscience, as being payers of tribute; and
not, doing evil, to hate the law, nor to wait for the sword, but being
purified by the fear to be praised by the authority.
G. One and the same is the plumb-line; but it spares the straight, and cuts
around the superfluous. One sun; but it lights the healthy sight, and dims the
weak. Do you wish that I say also something of our own, having taken heart?
One Christ; but he is set for a fall and a rising; a fall for the unbelieving,
and a rising for those who believe; and to some he is a stone of stumbling,
and a rock of offense, as many as did not know, nor understand, but walk
through in darkness, either worshipping idols, or seeing through only as far
as the letter, and neither willing nor being able to be shone above this; and
to others a cornerstone, and a praised rock, as many as are bound together by
the word, and have stood upon it; or if you wish, that pearl, which the good
merchant buys with all that he has. And we suffer some such thing, brothers,
not setting right our own things, but being distressed at the authority; as if
someone should blame the president of the games as wretched, himself from
wrestlings; or the physician as ignorant and rash in using cuttings and
burnings, himself being in a bad state, and needing the harsher medicines.
These things of mine toward those who are ruled are both consolation at once
and admonition; these things the poor shepherd setting in order the small
flock, with whom when he is rejoicing to be of good cheer together, and when
he is downcast to groan together, is the law of my shepherding. =35.976=
H. But what of you, the potentates and rulers? For already the word passes
over to you, lest we seem to be altogether unequal, and to exhort the others
with what is fitting, but to yield to your power, as if shrinking from our
freedom according to Christ by shame, or by fear; or to care more for the
others, but to neglect you, of whom it is also more worthy to take thought,
inasmuch as the turn also carries toward greater things whichever way it may
have, and the thing set right is more; for the contrary be far from both us
and the word. What then do you say? And what do we agree toward one another?
Will you receive the word with boldness? Even the law of Christ subjects you
to my power and to my tribunal. For we too rule; and I will add, that even the
greater and more perfect rule; or else the spirit must yield to the flesh, and
the heavenly things to the earthly. You will receive the boldness, I know,
because you are a sheep of my flock, a holy one of the holy, and a nursling of
the great Shepherd, and well led from above by the Spirit, and being
illuminated by the light of the holy and blessed Trinity equally with us. For
this my word toward you is also short and concise.
I. You rule together with Christ, you also administer together with Christ;
from him is the sword to you, not for work, but for threat; which may you keep
a pure offering to the one who has given it. You are an image of God; and you
lead by hand an image, being administered here, and being transferred toward
another life, into which we shall all pass over, having played a little in
this of life, whether prison, or stadium, or sketch, or shadow. Honor the
kinship; reverence the archetype; become with God, not with the world-ruler;
with the good Master, not with the bitter tyrant. That one was a man-slayer
from the beginning; that one also struck the first man through the
disobedience, and brought in the toilsome life, and legislated both the
punishing, and the being punished, through the sin. But you, man of God,
remember of whom you are a work, and where you are called, and how many things
you have, and how much you owe, from whom you have word, law, prophets, the
very knowing God, the not despairing of the things expected. Imitate through
these things the loving-kindness of God. This especially man has as divine,
the doing well. It is possible for you to become God having toiled nothing; do
not let go the season of deification. =35.977=
J. Others empty money, others flesh to the spirit, and they are put to death
to Christ, and they rise up altogether from the world; others consecrate to
God the dearest things. And you have altogether heard the sacrifice of
Abraham; who more eagerly gave to God the only-begotten, the one from the
promise, and unto whom the promise, than he received from God from the
beginning. But of you we ask none of these; bring in one thing in place of
all, the loving-kindness by which God rejoices more than by all the others
together; a gift of one's own, a gift unblemished, a gift calling forth the
generosity of God. Mix gentleness with the fear; blend hope with the threat. I
know many things even goodness setting right, shaming into the due return,
when, force being possible, having granted, we shame the one being pitied by
goodwill. Let nothing persuade you to be unworthy of the rule; let nothing
shut out pity and mildness, not time, not a potentate, not fear, not hope of
greater offices, not boldness having the more. Acquire the goodwill in the
necessary times, the one above; lend mercy to God. No one has regretted of
those who have brought something in beforehand to God. He is great for return,
especially rewarding with the goods from there those who have laid something
down beforehand, and have lent in advance; and there is a time also with the
things here toward faith of the later.
K. Yet a little, and the world passes by, and the tent is taken down. Let us
trade the season; with the things that do not stand let us buy the things that
remain. Each of us is in penalties; and the dust bears many the debts. Let us
pardon, that we may be pardoned; let us forgive, that we may ask forgiveness.
You see in the Gospel: A debtor of many talents is brought in, and he is
released from the debt; for he is brought to a good master. And he is
pardoned, but he does not pardon; for he is a slave even in choice; but of the
loving-kindness which he obtained concerning the greater, this he does not
share with the fellow-slave concerning the less, nor, if not by any other,
following the example of greatness of soul concerning what is another's; and
the master is indignant. And the next I will be silent; yet it is better by
the whole, =35.980= to bring in beforehand from here the goodness of the
account there.
L. What do you say? Do we have you by these words, of which you have often
confessed to be a lover, O fairest of rulers, and may it be added, also most
mild? Or must I also bring to you this gray hair instead of a supplication,
and the number of the years, and this long and unspotted priesthood, which
perhaps even angels reverence, serving purely the most pure, as worthy of
their own service? Do these things persuade? or shall I dare something
greater? And the aching makes me daring. I bring Christ to you, and the
emptying of Christ which was for us, and the sufferings of the passionless,
and the cross, and the nails, by which I was loosed from the sin; and the
blood, and the burial, and the resurrection, and the ascent, or also this
table, to which we approach in common; and the types of my salvation, which I
accomplish from the same mouth from which I am ambassador of these things
toward you, the holy mystagogy that carries us above.
M. If not through one of these two, yet through all, give both to us and to
yourself the grace, and to the Church according to your house, and to this
fair fullness of Christ; which consider to be ambassador together with us,
even if it yields the embassy to us, as more reverend through the one who
honored, and at the same time is constrained by the law of the rule. In this
one thing be well defeated; conquer us by loving-kindness. Behold I bring to
you my suppliants before God, and angels, and the kingdom of the heavens, and
the recompense from there. Honor my faith, which I both was entrusted with and
believed; that yours also may be honored concerning the greater and more
perfect things. And the sum =35.981= of the word, you also yourself have a
Lord in heaven; may you obtain concerning yourself such a judge, as you
yourself become to those who are ruled. And may we all obtain both the good
things from here, and the more bearable from there, in Christ Jesus our Lord,
to whom is the glory and the power, to whom honor and kingdom, with the Father
and the Holy Spirit, as it was, and was before, and will be, both now, and
unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
