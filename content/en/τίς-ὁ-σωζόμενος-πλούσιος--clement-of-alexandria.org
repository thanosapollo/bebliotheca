* Chapter 1
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Those who bring laudatory speeches as gifts to the rich would justly, in my
judgment at least, be deemed not only flatterers and illiberal, as pretending
at a great price to confer ungracious favors, but also impious and
treacherous: impious, because, leaving aside the praise and glorifying of the
only perfect and good God, from whom are all things and through whom are all
things and unto whom are all things, they attach this honor to men who roll
about in a prodigal and filthy life and who, the chief point, lie under the
judgment of God; treacherous, because, when the very abundance is of itself
enough to puff up the souls of those who possess it and to corrupt them and
make them stand off from the way by which it is possible to attain salvation,
these men further astound the minds of the rich, raising them with the
pleasures of immoderate praises, and preparing them once for all to despise
all affairs except the wealth on account of which they are admired,
transferring fire onto fire, as the saying is, pouring conceit upon conceit
and laying upon wealth, already a heavy thing by nature, a heavier burden, of
which it was more needful to take away and cut around, as of a perilous and
deadly disease; for to the one who is exalted and magnified there is fixed, as
a counterpart, the change and fall toward the low, as the divine word teaches.
But it seems to me far more humane than to serve the rich in an illiberal way
and to praise them to their harm, to join in helping their life and to work
out their salvation in every possible way, on the one hand asking this of God,
who firmly and gladly grants such things to his own children, and on the other
healing their souls by the word through the grace of the Savior, enlightening
them and leading them on to the possession of the truth, which he who attains
and is made illustrious by good works will alone carry off the prize of
eternal life. And prayer too needs a soul vigorous and persistent, measured
out until the last day of life, and the manner of life needs a disposition
good and lasting and stretching out toward all the commandments of the Savior.

* Chapter 2
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h2
:END:

It is likely that the cause of salvation seeming harder for the rich than for
the moneyless among men is not something simple, but varied. For some, hearing
at once and readily the voice of the Lord, that a camel will more easily slip
through the eye of a needle than a rich man into the kingdom of the heavens,
despairing of themselves as not going to live, granting everything to the
world and hanging upon the life here as the only one left to them, have stood
off still more from the way there, no longer inquiring either whom the Master
and Teacher addresses as the rich, or how the impossible among men becomes
possible. Others have understood this rightly and fittingly, but, neglecting
the works that bear upon salvation, have not prepared the needed preparation
for attaining the things hoped for. I say both these things concerning the
rich who have perceived both the power of the Savior and the manifest
salvation; but of those uninitiated in the truth I care little.

* Chapter 3
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3
:END:

Those then who are disposed in a way that loves truth and loves the brethren,
and who neither insolently despise the rich who are called, nor again fall
under them because of their own love of gain, must first by the word take out
of them the empty despair and show, with the needed explanation of the oracles
of the Lord, that the inheritance of the kingdom of the heavens has not been
cut off from them completely if they obey the commandments; then, when they
learn that they fear a fearless fear and that the Savior gladly receives them
when they are willing, then also to show beforehand and initiate them how and
by what works and dispositions they may enjoy the hope, as it is neither
impossible for them nor, on the contrary, accruing at random. But in the very
way it is with athletes, that we may compare small and perishable things with
great and immortal, let the one who is rich according to the world reckon this
also in his own case. For of those, the one who has despaired that he will be
able to win and obtain crowns has not even enrolled himself for the contest at
all, while the one who has thrown this hope into his mind, but does not admit
toils and exercises and suitable foods, has gone through uncrowned and missed
the hopes. So also let one who has put on this earthly wrapping neither from
the start have himself proclaimed out of the contests of the Savior, being
faithful at least and seeing together the greatness of the philanthropy of
God, nor again remaining untrained and uncontending hope to share the crowns
of immortality without dust and without sweat; but let him submit himself,
bringing himself to the word as trainer, and to Christ as president of the
games; and let his food and drink appointed be the new covenant of the Lord,
and the exercises the commandments, and grace and adornment the good
dispositions, love, faith, hope, knowledge of truth, fairness, gentleness,
compassion, dignity, so that when the last trumpet signals the end of the
course and the departure from here as from the stadium of life, he may stand
by the prize-giver with a good conscience, victorious, acknowledged worthy of
the fatherland above, into which he returns with crowns and angelic
proclamations.

* Chapter 4
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4
:END:

May the Savior then grant us, beginning the word from this point, to
contribute the true and the fitting and the saving things to the brethren,
first toward the hope itself and second toward the leading-on of the hope. And
he grants to those who ask and teaches those who request and looses ignorance
and shakes off despair, introducing again the same words about the rich, which
become interpreters of themselves and safe explainers. For there is nothing
like hearing again the very sayings which until now have been troubling us in
the gospels, as we listened without testing and mistakenly by reason of
childishness.

/As he was going out him him Barnard. to him MS. into the way, someone came up
and knelt saying: Good teacher, what shall I do, that I may inherit eternal
life? And Jesus says: Why do you call me good? No one is good except one, God.
You know the commandments: do not commit adultery, do not murder, do not
steal, do not bear false witness, honor your father and your mother. And he
answering says to him: All these I have kept from my youth from my youth
inserted by Segaar; see pp. 286. 290. And Jesus looking upon him loved him and
said: One thing you lack; if you wish to be perfect, sell as much as you have
and distribute to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven, and come,
follow me. And he, becoming gloomy at the word, went away grieving; for he was
one who had many possessions and fields. And Jesus looking around says to his
disciples: How hardly shall those who have possessions enter into the kingdom
of God. And the disciples were amazed at his words. And Jesus answering again
says to them: Children, how hard it is for those who trust in possessions to
enter into the kingdom of God; a camel will easily enter through the eye of
the needle rather than a rich man into the kingdom of God. And they were
exceedingly astonished and said: Who then can be saved? And he looking upon
them said: What is impossible with men is possible with God. Peter began to
say to him: Behold, we have left all and followed you. And Jesus answering
says: Amen I say to you, whoever leaves his own and parents and brothers and
possessions for my sake and for the sake of the gospel, shall receive a
hundredfold. Now in this time to have fields and possessions and houses and
brothers with persecutions unto where? unto where? Stählin. unto somewhere MS.
restrain Barnard. And in the coming life life Ghisler. life MS. is eternal;
and and Ghisler. the first shall be last, and the last first./

* Chapter 5
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5
:END:

These things have been written in the gospel according to Mark; and in all the
other acknowledged ones as well, perhaps a little of the words is changed in
each place, but all display the same concord of mind. And we must, knowing
clearly that the Savior teaches his own nothing in a human way, but all things
by divine and mystic wisdom, not hear the things said in a fleshly way, but
search out and learn thoroughly the mind hidden in them with the worthy
inquiry and understanding. For even the things that seem to have been made
plain by the Lord himself toward the disciples, of the things spoken in
riddles, are found to need no less but still more attention even now, because
of the surpassing excess of insight in them. And where even the things
supposed to have been opened by him to those inside and to those themselves
called by him children of the kingdom still need more care, surely then the
things that seemed to have been brought out simply and for that reason were
not even questioned by those who heard them, yet that bear upon the whole end
itself of salvation, but that have been veiled with a wondrous and
superheavenly depth of thought, it is not fitting to receive with the ears in
a surface way, but sinking the mind down upon the very spirit of the Savior
and the secret of his intent.

* Chapter 6
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h6
:END:

For our Lord and Savior has been asked with pleasure a question most suitable
to him, the life about life, the savior about salvation, the teacher about the
chief point of the doctrines taught, the truth about true immortality, the
word about the paternal word, the perfect about the perfect rest, the immortal
about sure immortality. He has been asked about these things for the sake of
which he also came down, which he trains, which he teaches, which he provides,
in order to show the subject of the gospel, that it is a gift of eternal life.
And he knows beforehand as God both what he is going to be asked and what
someone is going to answer him. For who more than the prophet of prophets and
Lord of every prophetic spirit? And having been called good, taking from this
first word itself the keynote, he begins the teaching from this point, turning
the disciple toward God the good and first and only steward of eternal life,
which the Son gives us having received it from him.

* Chapter 7
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h7
:END:

Therefore the greatest and most crowning of the lessons toward life must be
laid down in the soul from the beginning at once, to know the God who is
eternal and giver of eternal things and first and highest and one and good
God. Whom it is possible to acquire through knowledge and apprehension; for
this is an unchangeable and unshaken beginning and foundation of life,
knowledge of God who truly is and who grants the things that are, that is the
eternal things, from whom both being belongs to the others and the receiving
of remaining. For ignorance of this one is death, and the recognition of him
and appropriation and the love toward him and assimilation is the only life.

* Chapter 8
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h8
:END:

This one then he first commands the one who is going to live the true life to
recognize, whom /no one recognizes except the Son and he to whom the Son may
reveal/; then after that to learn the greatness of the Savior and the newness
of the grace, that according to the apostle /the law was given through Moses,
grace and truth through Jesus Christ/; and the things given through a faithful
servant are not equal to the things granted by the genuine Son. If at any rate
the law of Moses were sufficient to provide eternal life, in vain the Savior
himself comes and suffers for us, running through humanity from birth to the
sign, and in vain the one who has done all the lawful commandments /from
youth/ asks immortality on his knees from another. For he has not only
fulfilled the law, but even beginning at once from the first age; since what
great or over-brilliant thing is an old age barren of the wrongs which
youthful desires bear, or boiling anger, or love of money? But if someone in
the skip of youth and the burning heat of the age has furnished a mind ripe
and older than the time, this one is a wondrous contestant and distinguished
and gray in intent. But nevertheless this man, being such, is accurately
persuaded that for him nothing is lacking toward righteousness, but that he
wholly still needs life; therefore he asks it from the only one able to give;
and toward the law he has boldness, but he entreats the Son of God. He changes
station /from faith to faith/; as one tossing unsafely in law and lying at
anchor dangerously he shifts harbor to the Savior.

* Chapter 9
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h9
:END:

Jesus then does not refute him as not having fulfilled all the things from the
law, but even loves him and welcomes him beyond measure for the obedience in
the things he had learned, yet says he is incomplete as toward eternal life,
as not having fulfilled perfect things, and a worker of law, but idle of true
life. Those things too then are good (who says they are not? for the
/commandment is holy/) up to a certain training with fear and preliminary
education, for one advancing toward the legislation of Jesus, the highest, and
grace, but the fullness of /the law is Christ unto righteousness for everyone
who believes/, not making them slaves as a slave, but making sons and brothers
and joint-heirs those who accomplish the will of the Father.

* Chapter 10
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h10
:END:

/If you wish to become perfect./ He was not then perfect yet; for nothing is
more perfect than the perfect. And divinely the /if you wish/ made clear the
self-determination of the soul conversing with him. For the choice was upon
the man as free, and the gift upon God as Lord. And he gives to those who are
willing and overly eager and asking, in order that in this way the salvation
may become their own. For God does not compel, for violence is hostile to God,
but he furnishes to those who seek and provides to those who ask and opens to
those who knock. If then you wish, if you truly wish and are not deceiving
yourself, acquire what is lacking. /One thing is left to you,/ the one, the
mine, the good, that which is already beyond law, which law does not give,
which law does not contain, which is proper to the living. At any rate the one
who had fulfilled all the things of the law /from youth/ and had exulted in
the oversized things was not able to add this one thing to the whole, the
special thing of the Savior, in order to receive eternal life, which he longs
for; but being displeased he went away, vexed at the command of the life for
which he was entreating. For he did not truly want life, as he claimed, but
wrapped himself only in the reputation of a good choice, and he was able to be
busy about many things, but the one, the work of life, he was unable and
unwilling and weak to complete; which is the sort of thing the Savior also
said to Martha, busy about many things and drawn about and troubled in a
serving way, and blaming her sister because, leaving the serving, she sits
beside his feet keeping a disciple's leisure: /you are troubled about many
things, but Mary has chosen the good portion, and it shall not be taken from
her./ So also he commanded this man, leaving off the busyness about many
things, to cling to one and sit beside it, the grace of the one who adds
eternal life.

* Chapter 11
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h11
:END:

What then was it that urged him to flight and made him desert the teacher, the
entreaty, the hope, the life, the things previously toiled? /Sell your
belongings./ And what is this? Not what some receive readily, that he commands
to throw away the substance that belongs to one and to stand off from
possessions, but to exile from the soul the opinions about possessions, the
sympathy toward them, the excessive desire, the flutter and disease about
them, the cares, the thorns of life, which choke the seed of life. For neither
is it a great and enviable thing to be in want of possessions to no purpose,
not upon a principle of life (for so those who have nothing anywhere, but are
deserted and beggars of the things for the day, the poor thrown along the
roads, /not knowing/ God and /the righteousness of God,/ would by this alone,
the being extremely in want and without resource of life and lacking the least
things, be most blessed and most dear to God and the only ones having eternal
life), nor is it new to renounce wealth and grant it to the poor or to
fatherlands, which many have done before the descent of the Savior, some for
the sake of leisure for words and dead wisdom, others for empty fame and
vainglory, Anaxagorases and Democrituses and Crateses.

* Chapter 12
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h12
:END:

Why then does he command as something new and proper to God and alone
life-giving, which did not save those before? And if the /new creation,/ the
Son of God, discloses and teaches something special, he does not enjoin the
apparent thing, which others have done, but something else signified through
this, greater and more divine and more perfect, the stripping of the soul
itself and the disposition of the underlying passions and the cutting out and
casting out of the things alien to the intent by the roots. For this is the
lesson proper to the faithful, and the teaching worthy of the Savior. For
those before, having despised the things outside, let go and destroyed the
properties, but the passions of the souls I think they even further strained;
for they came to be in disdain and boastfulness and vainglory and contempt of
the other men, as themselves having worked something beyond man. How then
would the Savior have advised those who are going to live forever the things
that will harm and ruin toward the life which he promises? For that too is so:
someone can, having unloaded the possession, no less still have the desire and
the appetite for money melted in and living with him, and have thrown away the
use, yet being in want and at the same time longing for what he squandered be
grieved doubly, both by the absence of the service and by the presence of the
regret. For it is unreachable and impossible that one needing the things
necessary for living not be broken in intent and keep a distraction from the
better things, trying to furnish these somehow and from somewhere.

* Chapter 13
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h13
:END:

And how much more useful is the contrary, that one who has acquired enough
neither suffer ill himself about the possession and succor those to whom it is
fitting? For what fellowship would be left among men, if no one had anything?
And how would this dogma not be found plainly contrary and fighting against
many other and good dogmas of the Lord? /Make for yourselves friends from the
mammon of unrighteousness, in order that when it fails, they may receive you
into the eternal tents./ /Acquire treasures in heaven, where neither moth nor
eating makes unseen nor thieves dig through./ How would someone feed the
hungry and give drink to the thirsty and cover the naked and bring in the
homeless, things for which he threatens fire and the outer darkness to those
who have not done them, if each one himself were beforehand lacking all these?
But in fact he himself is guest with Zacchaeus and Levi and Matthew, the rich
and tax-collectors, and he does not command them to let go the money, but
imposing the just use and taking away the unjust he proclaims: /today
salvation has come to this house./ So he praises their needful use, that even
with this addition he commands the fellowship, to give drink to the thirsty,
to give bread to the hungry, to receive the homeless, to clothe the naked. And
if it is not possible to fulfill these needs except from possessions, and he
commands to stand off from the possessions, what else would the Lord be doing
than advising to give the same things and not to give, to feed and not to
feed, to receive and to shut out, to share and not to share, which is of all
things the most irrational?

* Chapter 14
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h14
:END:

Possessions that also benefit the neighbors are not then to be thrown away;
for they are properties as being acquirable, and possessions as being useful
and prepared by God for the use of men, which lie beside and have been put
under like some matter and instruments toward good use for those who know. The
instrument, if you use it with art, is artistic; if you fall short of the art,
it enjoys your lack of music, being itself without blame. Such also is wealth
as an instrument. You can use it justly; it serves through toward
righteousness; someone uses it unjustly; again it is found a servant of
injustice; for it is by nature to serve, but not to rule. One must not then
blame that which from itself has neither the good nor the bad, being without
blame, but that which is able to use these both well and badly, from whichever
it may choose, being cause according to this very thing, and this is the mind
of a man, even having in itself a free criterion and the self-determination of
the handling of the things given; so that one should not make the properties
vanish rather than the passions of the soul, which do not allow the better use
of the belongings, in order that having become beautiful and good he may be
able to use these properties also well. The renouncing all belongings and
selling all belongings must then be taken in this way as spoken upon the
passions of the soul.

* Chapter 15
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h15
:END:

I at any rate would say that too: since some things are within the soul, and
some outside, and if the soul uses them well, these too seem good, but if
wickedly, wicked, does the one who commands to alienate the belongings decline
these, of which when they have been taken away the passions still remain, or
rather those, of which when they have been taken away the properties also
become useful? He then who has thrown away the worldly abundance can still be
rich in the passions, even when the matter is not present; for the disposition
works what is its own and strangles and presses the reasoning and inflames
with the desires grown with it; nothing then has come of it for him to be poor
in money while rich in the passions. For he did not throw away the things to
be thrown away, but the indifferent things, and he cut himself off from the
serving things, but he kindled the matter of vice inborn by the want of the
things outside. One must then renounce the belongings that are harmful, not
those which, if someone understands the right use, are able also to help
together; and the things managed with insight and temperance and piety help.
The damaging things must be thrust away, but the things outside do not harm.

So then the Lord also introduces the need of the things outside, commanding to
put away not the things of living, but the things that use these badly; and
these were the weaknesses and passions of the soul.

* Chapter 16
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h16
:END:

the wealth of these being present is deadly to all, but when it has perished,
saving; having provided the soul clean of this, that is poor and naked, one
must thus already hear the Savior saying: /come, follow me./ For he himself
already becomes a way for the one pure in heart, but into an unclean soul the
grace of God does not slip in; and unclean is the one rich in desires and in
labor-pains with many loves, even worldly ones. For the one who has properties
and gold and silver and houses as gifts of God, and serving the God who gives
from them unto the salvation of men, and knowing that he has acquired these
for the brothers rather than for himself, and being better than the possession
of them, not being a slave of the things he has acquired, nor carrying these
about in the soul, nor marking out and circumscribing his own life in these,
but always working through some beautiful and divine work too, and if he must
ever be deprived of these, able to bear even the release of them with a
gracious intent equally just as also the abundance, this is the one blessed by
the Lord and called poor in spirit, an heir ready of the kingdom of heaven,
not able to live as a rich man;

* Chapter 17
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h17
:END:

but the one who carries the wealth in the soul, and instead of a spirit of God
carries gold in the heart or a field, and always making the possession
measureless, and looking each time at the more, bent down and shackled by the
hunting-nets of the world, being earth and going to go away unto earth, from
where can he desire and take thought of a kingdom of heavens, a man wearing
not a heart but a field or a mine, who will of necessity be found in these in
which he chose? /For where the mind of the man is, there also is his
treasure./

And the Lord knows treasures as twofold, the one good, for /the good man from
the good treasure of the heart brings forth the good,/ and the wicked, for
/the bad from the bad treasure brings forth the bad, because from the overflow
of the heart the mouth speaks./ As then treasure is not one with him as also
with us, the one that gives the sudden great gain in a finding, but also a
second, the unprofitable and unenvied and hard to acquire and damaging, so
also wealth is one of good things, and one of bad, if at least we know wealth
and treasure as not hanging apart from one another in nature. And the one
wealth would be acquirable and to be put around, and the other unacquirable
and to be thrown away; and in the same way also poverty is blessed, the
spiritual. Therefore Matthew also added: /blessed are the poor;/ how? /in
spirit./ And again: /blessed are those who hunger and. thirst for the
righteousness of God./ Therefore wretched are the contrary poor, without share
of God, and still more without share of human possession, and without taste of
the righteousness of God.

* Chapter 18
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h18
:END:

So the rich who will enter with difficulty into the kingdom must be heard in a
scientific sense, not awkwardly nor rustically nor fleshly; for it has not
been said so. Nor does salvation lie upon the things outside, neither if these
are many nor if few, or small or great, or glorious or inglorious, or
well-reputed or ill-reputed, but upon the virtue of the soul, faith and hope
and love and brotherly love and knowledge and gentleness and freedom from
conceit and truth, of which the prize is salvation. For neither will someone
live because of beauty of body or on the contrary perish; but the one who uses
the body given purely and according to God will live, and the one who corrupts
the temple of God will be corrupted. And someone ugly can be wanton and
someone can be temperate according to beauty; nor does strength and size of
body make alive, nor does want of the limbs destroy, but the soul using these
provides the cause upon either side. Bear then, he says, when struck on the
face, which someone even being strong and in good condition can obey, and
again someone being weak can transgress by incontinence of intent. So also
someone being without resource and without livelihood might at some time be
found drunk with desires, and one rich in money sober and poor in pleasures,
persuaded, intelligent, pure, chastened. If then that which is going to live
is especially and first the soul, and around this virtue growing saves, and
vice puts to death, it is already clearly evident that this, being poor of the
things by which someone is corrupted by wealth, is saved, and being rich of
those things which wealth wears down is put to death, and let us no longer
seek the cause of the end elsewhere except in the state of the soul and the
disposition toward obedience of God and purity and toward transgression of
commandments and a gathering of vice.

* Chapter 19
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h19
:END:

He then is truly and beautifully rich who is rich in the virtues and able to
use every fortune in a holy and faithful way, and the bastard rich is the one
rich according to flesh and who has transferred the life into the outer
possession that passes and is corrupted and becomes now of one, now of
another, and in the end of no one anywhere. Again in the same way there is
also a genuine poor and another bastard poor and falsely named, the one poor
according to spirit, the proper thing, and the other according to world, the
alien thing. To the one then not poor according to world and rich according to
the passions, the one not poor according to spirit and rich according to God
/stand off,/ he says, /from the properties belonging in your soul that are
alien, in order that having become pure in heart you may see God, which also
through another voice is to enter into the kingdom of the heavens. And how
shall you stand off from them? By selling. What then? Shall you receive money
instead of properties? Having made an exchange of wealth toward wealth, having
turned the apparent substance into silver? By no means; but instead of the
things formerly belonging in the soul, which you long to save, introducing in
their place another wealth that makes godlike and that furnishes eternal life,
the dispositions according to the commandment of God, from which there will
remain for you wage and honor, continuous salvation and eternal immortality.
So you sell well the belongings, the many and surplus and shutting the heavens
against you, exchanging for them the things able to save. Let the fleshly poor
have those and needing these, and you, having received the spiritual wealth in
return, would already have treasure in heavens./

* Chapter 20
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h20
:END:

The man of many possessions and lawful, not understanding these in the right
way, nor how the same man can be both poor and rich and have possessions and
not have them and use the world and not use it, went away gloomy and downcast,
leaving the post of the life which he was able only to desire, but not also to
attain, himself making the difficult impossible for himself. For it was
difficult that the soul not be led about nor struck by the luxurious things
present with the obvious wealth and by the blooming enchantments, but not
impossible even in this to lay hold of salvation, if someone should transfer
himself from the perceptible wealth to the intelligible and God-taught and
should learn to use the indifferent things well and in a way of his own and as
he would start toward eternal life. And the disciples too at first themselves
became very fearful and struck. Having heard, why then? Was it because they
themselves also had acquired many possessions? But even these very little nets
and hooks and the serving little boats they left long ago, which were their
only things. Why then in fear do they say: /who can be saved?/ They heard well
and as disciples of what was said parabolically and unclearly by the Lord and
they perceived the depth of the words. For the sake of want of possessions
they were of good hope toward salvation; but since they were conscious in
themselves of not yet having put away the passions completely (for they were
newly learned and recently enlisted by the Savior), /they were exceedingly

Therefore you, the solemn and powerful and rich, must altogether set over
yourself some man of God as a trainer and helmsman. Revere even one, fear even
one, practice hearing even one who speaks boldly and astringes and at the same
time heals. For it is not beneficial for the eyes either to remain unchastened
the whole time, but also to weep and be bitten at some time for the sake of
the greater health. So also for a soul nothing is more destructive than
continuous pleasure; for it is blinded from the melting, if it remains unmoved
by the word that speaks boldly. Fear this one also when he has been angered,
and be grieved when he has groaned, and revere him when he stops anger, and
anticipate him when he begs off punishment. Let this one keep watch many
nights for you, being an ambassador for you toward God and charming the Father
with accustomed supplications; for his bowels do not hold out against his
children when they ask. And he will ask purely, being preferred by you as an
angel of God and being grieved by nothing from you, but for you; this is
unhypocritical repentance. /God is not mocked/ nor does he attend to empty
words; for he alone examines marrows and kidneys of heart and hears those in
fire and hears out those entreating in the belly of a sea-monster and is near
to all who believe and far from the godless, if they do not repent.

And in order that you may take courage, having so repented truly, that a
sufficient hope of salvation remains for you, hear a myth not a myth, but a
true account, handed down about John the apostle and guarded by memory. For
when the tyrant had died he passed from the island of Patmos to Ephesus, and
being called he went also upon the neighboring nations, in some places to
appoint bishops, in some to fit together whole churches, and in some to allot
some one lot of those signified by the spirit. Having come then also upon one
of the cities not far, whose name too some say, and having given the brothers
rest in the other things, looking at last upon the appointed bishop, seeing a
young man sufficient in body and graceful in look and hot in soul, /this one,/
he said, /I deposit with you I deposit with you Stählin (from Eus. and Max.
Conf.). I deposit MS. with all eagerness upon the church and with Christ as
witness./ And when he received and promised all things he also stretched the
same things again and testified. Then the one departed to Ephesus, and the
elder taking the young man handed over into his house nourished him, held him
together, warmed him, at last enlightened him; and after this he let go of the
more care and guarding, as having set over him the perfect phylactery, the
seal of the Lord. And to the one who had taken relaxation before the season
there attach some idle and broken age-mates, habituated to evils; and first
they lead him under through costly feastings, then somewhere going out also by
night upon clothes-stealing they take him along, then they thought it fit to
work together also something greater. And he little by little was being
accustomed and through greatness of nature, standing out like a hard-mouthed
and strong horse from the straight road and biting the bit, was being carried
more greatly down the ravines. And having despaired completely of the
salvation in God he no longer thought anything small, but having done
something great, since he had once perished, he thought it fit to suffer equal
things with the others. Taking up these very ones then and putting together a
robber-band, he was a ready robber-chief, most violent, most blood-stained,
most hard. Time in the middle, and some need having fallen they call John
back. And he, when he had set in order the other things for the sake of which
he had come, /come then,/ he said, /O bishop, give back to us the deposit
which I and Christ Christ Eusebius. savior MS. deposited with you upon the

astounded, thinking he was being slandered about money which he had not
received, and he was able neither to believe concerning things which he did
not have nor to disbelieve John; but when he said /I demand the young man and

come into the place he is caught by the advance-guard of the robbers, neither
fleeing nor begging off, but crying: /for this I have come, lead me to your
ruler./ Who until then, as he was armed, was waiting; but when he recognized
