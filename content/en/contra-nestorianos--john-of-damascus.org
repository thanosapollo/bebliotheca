14 Scripture says that "whoever confesses that Jesus is the Son of God, God

God," the only one, the one.

according to you he has undergone incarnation and becoming-man.
16 Micah the prophet concerning the one born of the Virgin in Bethlehem speaks
thus: "And his goings-forth are from the beginning, from the days of
eternity." How are this one's goings-forth from the beginning, from the days
of eternity, unless he himself also is God, pre-eternal?

17 The Lord, bearing witness to his own pre-eternal existence, says: "Amen,
amen I say to you, before Abraham came to be, I am," and again: "And no one
has ascended into heaven except he who came down from heaven, the Son of man
who is in heaven." How was he who is of the seed of David, the son of Abraham,
before Abraham came to be, unless as God? And how is the same one son of
Abraham unless as having become man in the last times? "No one has ascended
into heaven except he who came down from heaven, the Son of man, who is in
heaven"? How has a Son of man come down from heaven and is in heaven? And how
does "he who is in heaven" ascend into heaven, unless because the same one,
being God and man, inasmuch as God comes down condescendingly and is incarnate
and becomes man and does not depart from the heavens, nor yet from the
Father's bosom, but inasmuch as he has become man, "he goes up where he was
before" as God, as he himself again, the unlying one, the hypostatic Truth,
has said: "If then you behold the Son of man going up where he was before"?
For as God he is both pre-eternal and uncircumscribed, even if he is said to
have come down (for we call descent in his case the condescension and
humiliation and the manifestation upon earth), but as having appeared man he
is circumscribed and under time.
18 John the Forerunner and Baptist says: "This is he of whom I said: After me
comes a man who has come to be before me, because he was first of me. And I
did not know him, but that he might be manifested to Israel, for this I came
baptizing in water," and "Behold the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the
world." Understand how, calling him a man and naming him a lamb, he does not
say that another is the one who takes away the sin of the world, and has
assigned this great and truly extraordinary and God-befitting dignity to him.
And he says he is before him and first, although coming after, I mean
according to the time of the generation according to the flesh. For even if
Emmanuel is late-born as man, yet he was before every age as God. Of him
therefore is both the recent humanly and the eternal divinely. Wherefore also
Zacharias, the father of John, prophesying to his own child says: "And you,
child, shall be called prophet of the Highest; for you shall go before the
face of the Lord to prepare his ways."
19 The Son from the Virgin asked his disciples saying: "Who do men say that I
the Son of man am?" Then when others were saying another and guessing him one
of the prophets, not accepting them as not speaking the truth, he asked again:
"But you" who know more than the others "who do you say that I am?" Then the
all-excellent Peter, the chief summit of the apostles, not contemplating a
naked or fleshless Word, nor a mere and God-bearing man, but Son of God and
God by nature and by nature become man, and one Christ being both this and
that: "You are the Christ," he said, "the Son of the living God." He did not
say "Christ" without the article, but "the Christ," the one expected, the one
prophesied, and he did not stop at this, but added "the Son of the living
God." He did not say "a Son," but "the Son," the only-begotten, the one, the
pre-eternal, and he heard: "Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jonah, because flesh
and blood did not reveal it to you, but my Father who is in the heavens." "For
no one knows the Son except the Father."
20 And the disciples, astonished at the divine sign of the walking upon the
sea, said: "Truly you are Son of God." But if he is bastard and adopted, not
"truly." Let the blessed disciples then be accused by you.
21 If there is one nature of the holy Trinity and one energy and all things
upon it are common except the hypostasis, and God the Word was united to the
flesh

ensouled with a rational and intellectual soul by relation and energy and not
according to hypostasis, then no less were the Father and the Holy Spirit also
united to it. And if the union of Father and Son and Holy Spirit toward the
flesh is equal and one, let him who was born of the Virgin be called Father
and Son and Holy Spirit. For what is the arbitrary allotment that the Word
alone is also named Son of God? But if the Son alone had toward him the union
according to relation and energy, he will be of different energy from the
Father and the Holy Spirit, which is absurd. But if the union has come to be
according to hypostasis, nothing absurd will follow; for the Son is of
different hypostasis toward the Father.
22 Blessed Paul says: "One God and Father, from whom are all things, and one
Lord Jesus Christ, through whom are all things." For what reason is Jesus
Christ ranked immediately with the Father, no one intervening? Then wherever
shall we place the Only-begotten, raising a man into his place, and this man,
as you say, being energized by him and honored through him? If you say that,
just as Christ was born in Bethlehem, but was called Nazarene because of the
dwelling in Nazareth, so also God the Word was called man because of the
dwelling in a man, then he has not become flesh nor man, nor would he
reasonably be named man, but fleshly and mannish as Nazarene; for Christ has
not been called Nazareth, but Nazarene.
23 If because of the dwelling in a man and a relative union the Word of God is
named man, then let the Father and the Holy Spirit also be named men; for the
fullness of the holy Trinity dwells in us through the Spirit. And indeed all-
wise Paul says: "Do you not know that you are a temple of God and the Spirit
of God dwells in you?", and Christ himself: "If anyone loves me, he will keep
my word, and my Father will love him, and we will come to him and make our
abode with him." But the Father is nowhere called man, nor yet the Holy
Spirit, because of dwelling in us. Therefore neither is the Son named man
because of the dwelling in a man, but as having truly become man according to
essence.
24 If, since the Word was in Christ, he became a worker of signs, you perhaps
say he is one of the holy prophets; for he also worked the divine signs
through the hand of saints.
25 It is written that Christ has been sanctified by the Father and has
received the coming of the Spirit. How then does he baptize in Holy Spirit and
fulfill the things that befit the divine nature alone? For he is the bestower
of sanctification. And showing this as his own good he says: "Receive Holy
Spirit. If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven; if you retain those
of any, they are retained." He is sanctified then inasmuch as man, but he
sanctifies inasmuch as God, being one and the same.
26 It is written concerning Christ: "Who in the days of his flesh, having
offered prayers and supplications to him who was able to save him from death
with strong crying and tears, and having been heard from his piety, although
being a Son, learned obedience from the things he suffered, and having been
perfected became to all who obey him the cause of eternal salvation." Still
also the Lord himself: "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" If these
things were said concerning a mere man, then of one in distress and making the
onset of the trial unbearable and overcome by fear and not noble in manner nor
having a manly mind (for they are the words of one at a loss from faint-
heartedness: "My soul is exceeding sorrowful unto death," and "now is my soul
troubled," and "Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass from me"), these
words either of one not knowing the divine will or of one opposing the divine
will; but if as concerning God stripped of humanity, they are altogether
unfitting and incongruous. But concerning God incarnate and become man

and healing our passions by the proper passions of his own flesh and leaving
us a pattern, they are very well achieved. For this is what "in the days of
his flesh, although being a Son" makes clear. For Spirit-bearing Paul is
astonished at the mystery and at God's economy toward us and the philanthropic
condescension toward us, that "although being a Son" "in the days of his
flesh" he endured all these things in our nature, that he might nerve it
against the passions and teach us in trials to look toward God and call him to
aid, and to what perfection and to what prizes the endurance of passions and
obedience issues. Wherefore also blessed Peter wrote: "For what glory is it,
if when you sin and are buffeted you endure? But if when you do good and
suffer you endure, this is grace with God, because Christ also died for us,
leaving us a pattern, that you should follow in his steps." Therefore he is
not man only nor naked God, but Christ being God incarnate "in the days of his
flesh" both suffered and did these things "leaving us a pattern," since how
does he rebuke Peter who said: "Be it far from you, Lord; this shall never be
to you," when he was foretelling the passion to them.
27 If Jesus should be said "to advance in wisdom and stature and grace," do
not guess a mere man, but God become man and incarnate, permitting his own
flesh through its own things to be of its own nature, lest the becoming-man be
thought a portent.
28 Paul dares even not to call Jesus a man, not as not being man, but as being
God also. He writes then to the Galatians: "Paul an apostle not from men nor
through a man, but through Jesus Christ," and again: "I make known to you the
gospel that was preached by me, that it is not according to man; for I neither
received it from a man nor was I taught it, but through a revelation of Jesus
Christ." It is written: "Not an angel, not an ambassador, but the Lord himself
saved us." Paul says: "Become imitators of God as beloved children and walk in
love, as also Christ loved us and gave himself up for us." He who was
sorrowing and praying against the cup, how "gave himself up," if he was only
man?
29 Blessed Peter says: "Christ then having suffered in flesh." For what reason
did he add "in flesh" unless knowing that he was God impassible in the
Godhead? If a mere man suffered for us, how "is the word of the cross the
power of God?" and how is "he who has counted his blood a common thing"
condemned, as "having trampled on the Son of God"?
30 How does Christ say that he goes up himself "where he was before"? For
neither as God does he go up (for he is uncircumscribed, and going up and
coming down belong to a circumscribed nature) nor as man was he in heaven
before going up, but he goes up as man "where he was before" as God; for the
same one is God and man together.
31 It is written: "God so loved the world that he gave his only-begotten Son,
that everyone who believes in him may have eternal life." What excess of love,
if he gave a common man adopted as son by grace for us? And how is he only-
begotten who has been honored with adoption and is not son by nature, when
Scripture says: "I said: You are gods and all sons of the Most High," and "as
many as received him, he gave them authority to become children of God"? But
just as, there being many gods by position and grace, there is one who is God
by nature, so also, many having been honored with adoption, there is one who
is Son of God by nature, the Only-begotten, whom he gave for us.

32 Most divine John has cried out saying: "No one has ever seen God; the only-
begotten Son who is in the bosom of the Father, he has declared him." And who
is the one who declared, unless the one born of the Virgin and who consorted
with men? "This is our God, another shall not be reckoned beside him. After
these things he was seen upon the earth and consorted with men." How was he
seen upon the earth "and consorted with men" unless by becoming man? For he
himself said: "No one has ever seen God."
33 The apostle says: "The first man is of earth, earthy; the second man, the
Lord, is from heaven." For he did not bring the body from heaven, but being
God and by nature Son of God he came down "from heaven" and became man, a
second Adam, as a second beginning of those being refashioned and being trans-
elemented toward incorruption through the resurrection. Wherefore he also
says: "The first Adam into a living soul, the second Adam into a life-giving
spirit."
34 The Lord says to his own disciples: "Go, make disciples of all the nations,
baptizing them into the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy
Spirit." And most divine Paul says: "As many as were baptized into Christ were
baptized into his death." The Lord mentioned one Son saying: "And of the Son,"
not: And of the sons. But the apostle says we have been baptized into Christ
and his death. According to you then the natural Son has been expelled and the
one adopted through grace has been brought in. Or you have been baptized into
a tetrad. For we have not, God forbid; for we have been baptized into the name
of the Father and of the Son who was incarnate and became man and was
crucified in flesh and rose, and of the Holy Spirit, into a consubstantial and
undivided Trinity. For the natural Son of God and God the Only-begotten, even
having become man, has remained no less consubstantial with the Father, as
also the symbol of the Church teaches:
35 "We believe in one God the Father almighty, maker of heaven and earth, of
all things visible and invisible. And in one Lord Jesus Christ the only-
begotten Son of God, begotten of the Father before all the ages, light from
light, true God from true God, begotten not made, consubstantial with the
Father, through whom all things came to be; who for us men and for our
salvation came down from the heavens and was incarnate of the Holy Spirit and
Mary the Virgin and became man, and was crucified for us under Pontius Pilate
and suffered and was buried and rose on the third day according to the
Scriptures and ascended into the heavens and sits at the right hand of the
Father and is coming again with glory to judge living and dead, of whose
kingdom there shall be no end." See how the multitude of the holy and God-
bearing fathers said one Lord Jesus Christ, the one begotten of the Father
before the ages and not made, the consubstantial with the Father, the light
from the light, the true God from true God, who was incarnate and suffered in
flesh and rose and ascended into the heavens and sat at the right hand of the
Father and is coming again to judge living and dead; for most emphatic is the
"and coming again," as he has already come from the heavens, when by a
voluntary emptying he was incarnate and became man. For they did not say
another who was begotten of the Father before the ages and another who was
incarnate of the Virgin, but one and the same.
36 But you will say: Do we then say that the Word himself from God the Father
suffered for us? Entirely so, yet in flesh and not in Godhead. If indeed Paul
is true saying concerning him: "Who is the image of the invisible God,
firstborn of all creation, because in him

all things were created, the visible and the invisible, whether thrones or
lordships or principalities or authorities; all things have been created
through him and unto him; and he is before all things, and in him all things
hold together, and he is the head of the body, the Church; who is beginning,
firstborn from the dead, that he himself might become in all things
preeminent." For see, see even very clearly, he says that the image of the
invisible God, the "firstborn of all creation," visible and invisible, the one
through whom are all things and in whom are all things, has been given as head
of the Church, and is also firstborn from the dead, "who endured a cross,
despising shame." For we do not say that a man simply, honored by conjunction
with him (I know not how), as you say, is given for us, but it is himself the
Lord of glory who was crucified. "For if they had known," he says, "they would
not have crucified the Lord of glory."
37 How then has the same one both suffered and not suffered? For if he has not
suffered at all as God, yet is said to have suffered, how would he be God?
Therefore the one who suffered would be understood as only the one of the seed
of David. And yet how is it not of a feeble mind to say these things? For the
Father did not give a common man for us, honored by relative conjunction and
adoption; for he writes thus: "God so loved the world that he gave his only-
begotten Son, that everyone who believes in him may have eternal life." Since
then he willed to suffer for the salvation of the world, but this was
impossible without flesh because he is by nature impassible as God, he assumed
the flesh receptive of suffering and being God became also man, that he might
be able to suffer. So that both belonged to him: to the flesh the suffering,
but to the Godhead the not-suffering; for the same one was in the same God and
man together. Weak then is every device of examples toward a clear imaging of
the mystery, yet at least toward a faint imagination we say this: Just as iron
or some other such matter, having consorted with the assaults of fire,
receives it and is in travail with the flame, but if it should happen to be
struck by one striking, the matter receives the harm, but the nature of the
fire is not at all injured by the one striking, so you will understand also in
the saying that the Son suffered in flesh, but did not suffer in Godhead.
38 Since we find the prophetic and evangelical and apostolic voices concerning
Christ discoursing sometimes as concerning a man, sometimes as concerning God,
sometimes as concerning God and man together, in what way shall we say these
do not contradict one another, unless we should say that the natural Son of
God and God has by nature become man and is after the becoming-man the same
both God and man together? For thus, "even if he was crucified from weakness
of flesh" according to the apostle, "yet he lives from the power of God" and
has been raised by the Father and raised himself and has been taken up and
goes up by his own authority and has sat "at the right hand of the Father in
the heavenly places above every principality and authority and lordship and
every name that is named."
39 If the flesh united to him ineffably has not become immediately the Word's
own, how would it be understood as life-giving? "For I am," he says, "the
living bread that came down from heaven and gives life to the world. If anyone
eats of this bread, he will live unto the age; and the bread that I will give
is my flesh for the life of the world." If these things were not said by him
who had come down himself, we would have said they were said as from a man
adopted. But since he says, "I am the living bread that came down from
heaven," how would his flesh be of another besides him? And if it is also
written: "Amen I say to you, unless you eat the flesh of the Son of man and
drink his blood, you do not

have life in yourselves," we understand that the Son of God and Son of man is
one, being the one eternally as God, and having become the other in the last
times as having become man. Since how is he both Son of man and has come down
from heaven?
40 Blessed Paul writing to the Hebrews says: "In many parts and in many ways
of old God having spoken to the fathers in the prophets, in these last days
has spoken to us in a Son." See that the prophets also were deemed worthy of
adoption, but this one, in whom God has spoken to us in the last times, is not
an adopted son, but by nature; wherefore he is also distinguished from the
prophets. And it is clear; for he says: "Whom he appointed heir of all things,
through whom he also made the ages." How does he become "heir of all things"
who is the one "through whom he made the ages," unless he is God and man? And
inasmuch as God, he is maker of the ages; but inasmuch as man, he is appointed
heir; "Who being the radiance of the glory and the impress of his hypostasis,
bearing all things by the word of his power, having made purification of our
sins through himself, sat at the right hand of the Power in the heights,
having become so much better than the angels as he has inherited a more
excellent name than they." See that he himself is "radiance of the glory and
impress of the Father's hypostasis," "who bears all things by the word of his
power," this one is appointed heir of all and "has inherited the name" higher
than that of the angels. How then is the same one both "radiance of the glory"
and bears all things and is appointed heir of all things and of the name,
unless the same one being God has become man? For these things are not said
concerning another and another, but concerning one and the same Son of God, in
whom "he has spoken to us in the last times."
41 Upon all these things, what is great and new and hidden, which was not made
known to the generations? Or what riches or what unspeakable wisdom or what
excess of love, if the salvation has come to be through a man? Or how would
the mystery of the economy be called emptying and becoming-man and
incarnation, if we have not been saved through a Son of God and God by nature,
but by one of those under corruption who by grace obtained with us to be
saved? And if the natural Son of God and God has not in truth become man and
accepted to suffer all things, where is the emptying? How did he humble
himself, and what incarnation is the not-becoming flesh? And if now the
heavenly things with the earthly worship a mere man and bend the knee, how
have things not been established for the worse? For of old men only served the
creation, but now according to you, together with the earthly and the under-
earth, the heavenly things also. But you, having hope upon a man and
worshiping a man saved by grace, would justly be named man-worshipers. But we
worship and serve and "believe in one God the Father almighty and in one Lord
Jesus Christ the only-begotten Son of God, begotten of the Father before all
the ages; light from light, true God from true God, consubstantial with the
Father, begotten of the Father before the ages and in the last times incarnate
of the Holy Spirit and Mary the ever-Virgin, and become man and crucified and
suffered and buried and risen and ascended into the heavens and sitting at the
right hand of the Father, and coming again with glory to judge living and
dead; of whose kingdom there shall be no end. And in the Holy Spirit."
42 And that he who was born of the holy Virgin and crucified is himself the
pre-eternal Son of God, the divine choir of the prophets first bears witness.
For David has cried out saying: "Your throne, O God, is unto the age of the
age." See, he discourses toward God: "A rod of uprightness is the rod of your
kingdom. You loved

righteousness and hated lawlessness; therefore God, your God, has anointed you
with oil of gladness beyond your fellows." See clearly he says that the God
whose throne is unto the age of the age will be anointed by God and Father,
the Father as God, but he himself as God as also being incarnate and being
anointed as man. And Micah: "And you, Bethlehem, land of Judah, house of
Ephrathah, are by no means least among the rulers of Judah; for from you shall
come forth a ruler who shall shepherd my people Israel, and his goings-forth
are from the beginning, from the days of eternity." Who had the going-forth
from the beginning, unless concerning the one of whom John the Theologian
says: "In the beginning was the Word"? And again David: "O God, give your
judgment to the king and your righteousness to the king's son." And after a
little: "And he shall humble a slanderer and shall continue with the sun and
before the moon, generation of generations." Do these things not clearly show
the pre-eternal existence of Christ? And Baruch: "This is our God, another
shall not be reckoned beside him. He found out every way of knowledge and gave
it to Jacob his servant and to Israel his beloved. After these things he was
seen upon the earth and consorted with men." And John the Theologian and
Evangelist: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the
Word was God. This one was in the beginning with God. All things came to be
through him, and without him came to be not even one thing that has come to
be." And after a little: "And the Word became flesh and tabernacled among us
and we beheld his glory, glory as of an only-begotten from a Father, full of
grace and truth." John bears witness concerning him, and has cried saying:
"This was he of whom I said: He who comes after me has come to be before me,
because he was first of me." He was first as God, that is; for as man Christ
was born after John. And again: "No one has ever seen God; the only-begotten
Son who is in the bosom of the Father, that one has declared him." And who has
declared, unless Christ? He himself then is "the only-begotten Son who is in
the bosom of the Father." And John the Forerunner: "And I have seen and have
borne witness that this is the Son of God." To say "the Son of God" and not to
say "a son of God" without the article signifies him, the one Son of God, the
Only-begotten. And the Lord himself to Nicodemus: "No one has ascended into
heaven except he who came down from heaven, the Son of man." How did the Son
of man come down from heaven, unless being Son of God and God by nature he
became son of man and humbled himself? For this is the descent; for the divine
is not of a nature to pass from places to places, for it is uncircumscribed.
And again the Lord: "For God did not send his Son into the world that he might
judge the world, but that the world might be saved through him. He who
believes in him is not judged; but he who does not believe has already been
judged, because he has not believed in the name of the only-begotten Son of
God." And again John: "He who comes from above is above all; he who is of the
earth speaks of the earth. He who comes from heaven is above all." And after a
little: "The Father loves the Son and has given all things in his hand. He who
believes in the Son has eternal life; but he who disobeys the Son shall not
see life, but the wrath of God remains upon him." All these things have been
said concerning Christ. And again the Lord: "My Father works until now, and I
work. For this reason then the Jews were seeking the more to kill him, because
he was not only loosing the sabbath, but also was saying God was his own
Father, making himself equal to God." And again the Lord: "The bread of God is
he who comes down from heaven and gives life to the world." And after a
little: "I am the bread of life; he who comes to me shall not hunger, and he
who believes in me shall never thirst." And again: "Everyone who beholds the
Son and believes in him has eternal life." If then Christ has come down from
heaven,

how is he another besides God the Word, the Son of God? And if faith in him is
eternal life, how is he not God by nature? For not those who believe in a
creature or in a prophet have eternal life, but those who believe in the true
God alone. And again: "Not that anyone has seen the Father, except he who is
from God, this one has seen the Father." And again: "I am the living bread
that came down from heaven." And again: "If then you behold the Son of man
going up where he was before." If the same one is not Son of man and pre-
eternal Son of God, how did he go up where he was before? For he went up as
man, where he was before as God, the same one being God and man together. And
again: "I lay down my soul, that I may take it again. No one takes it from me,
but I lay it down from myself. I have authority to lay it down and I have
authority to take it again." Who has authority of life and death, unless God
alone? And again: "I and the Father are one." A man will never say: I and God
are one, nor: I and an angel are one, but neither would an angel say: I and
God are one, or: I and a man are one. And an exceptional creature will never
say: I and the Creator are one. For upon creatures a man might perhaps say: I
and an angel are one, according to the created, according to the servant; but
a creature is one with the Creator in nothing. And after a little, making a
comparison of himself toward those who are gods by position and not by nature,
he says: "Is it not written in your law: I said: You are gods and all sons of
the Most High? If he called those gods, to whom the word of God came, and the
Scripture cannot be loosed, of him whom the Father sanctified and sent into
the world, do you say that you blaspheme, because I said: I am Son of God?"
And showing that he is God and consubstantial with the Father, from the
likeness of the works he says: "If I do not do the works of him who sent me,
do not believe me; but if I do, even if you do not believe me, believe my
works, that you may know and believe that I am in the Father and the Father in
me." As he also says above: "For as the Father raises the dead and makes
alive, so also the Son makes alive whom he will," "because he is Son of man.
Do not marvel at this, that an hour is coming and now is, when the dead will
hear the voice of the Son of God and those who hear will live." Having said:
"As the Father raises the dead and makes alive," he added: "So also the Son
makes alive whom he will," showing that the Son is life-giving by his own
authority and by nature as also the Father and not by grace. Then he adds:
That this Son of God who is life-giving by nature "is Son of man." He did not
say: He is called Son of man, but "he is," and "do not marvel at this," he
says, that "a Son of man makes alive." For this Son of man is Son of God not
in name nor by grace, but by nature, since he is life-giving by nature as the
Father is God by nature as also the Father. And again to Martha: "I am the
resurrection and the life. He who believes in me, even if he dies, shall
live." And again: "He who has seen me has seen the Father. Do you not believe
that I am in the Father and the Father in me?" And again: "This is the eternal
life, that they may know you the only true God and Jesus whom you sent." When
Philip said: "Show us the Father, and it is enough for us," the Lord, knowing
that it is impossible to see God with bodily eyes and again that the knowledge
of him is knowledge of the Father as of one consubstantial (for of
consubstantial things there is one and the same knowledge), says to him more
rebukingly: "Have I been with you so long a time and you have not known me,
Philip?" For he knew him as man, but he is rebuked as not having known him as
God by faith and with the eyes of the mind. For if he had known him as God, he
would have known the Father also; for he who has seen him, God that is, has
known the Father also. For he who has known him as man only has not already
known the Father also; for the Father is not man.

And this knowledge, that is the knowing God the Father and Jesus himself as
God, is eternal life. And indeed not to know this is eternal death. And Thomas
said of him who was handled Lord and God, and he himself is charged as not
having believed before seeing, but those who have not seen and have believed
are blessed. And Paul the apostle: "When God shall judge the world according
to the gospel through Jesus Christ, who is God over all, blessed unto the
ages."
And in the letter to the Galatians: "But when the fullness of the times came,
God sent forth his Son, become of a woman, become under law." And in that to
the Hebrews: "Wherefore, holy brethren, partakers of a heavenly calling,
consider the apostle and high priest of our confession, Jesus, being faithful
to him who made him, as also Moses in all his house. For this one has been
counted worthy of more glory than Moses inasmuch as he who constructed the
house has more honor than the house; for every house is constructed by
someone; but he who constructed all things is God. And Moses indeed was
faithful in all his house as a servant, for a testimony of the things that
would be spoken, but Christ as a Son over his house; whose house we are." See
how, having said Jesus was made as man and having compared him with Moses, he
says he has been counted worthy of more glory than Moses, and so much as the
creator than the thing created and a son than a slave. For Moses was a God-
bearing servant, but Christ God and Master dwelling within; whose house we
are. "For we are a temple of God, and the Spirit of God dwells in us." Jesus
then is God and maker, God by nature; for those who are gods by grace and
participation are not already also creators.
43 All these things said concerning him who was born of Mary and crucified, do
they not most clearly show him God and man most emphatically to those who have
a mind? But the darkness of empty glory lies like a rheum upon the
intellectual eyes of those who hereticize, not allowing the illumination of
the gospel to shine through to them. For they cannot believe, receiving glory
from men and not seeking the glory of the only God. But we, to whom it has
been given through God, having fled the superstitious error, to come to be
with God, thus we understand, thus we think, and thus we preach: One God the
Father, one God the Son, one God the Holy Spirit, one God the three understood
with one another, God each by himself, one God the three, the one because of
the monarchy, the other because of the properties. For there is one nature in
the three hypostases, and the things from him have their reference toward one.
Wherefore the three are one God, but the nature, that is the Godhead, is
perfect in each of the hypostases, wherefore also each by itself is perfect
God. The three are one God; for God and his Word and his Spirit are one God,
since also a man and his word and his spirit are not three men, but one. Each
is perfect God; for the Word of God and his Holy Spirit are not anhypostatic
powers nor poured into air, but each is both enhypostatic and perfect, that
God may not be composite, composed of imperfect things as perfect, but one
perfect in three perfect, superperfect and pre-perfect. And we believe and
without hesitation preach that the only-begotten Son himself, begotten of the
Father before the ages, and Word consubstantial with the Father, God by
nature, through whom all things came to be, in the last times by good pleasure
of the Father was conceived in the all-undefiled and all-blameless womb of the
holy Virgin as a kind of divine seed and was incarnate unchangeably and
unalterably, that is, hypostasized in himself flesh ensouled with a rational
and intellectual soul, and became perfect man while remaining what he was,
perfect God. For the holy Virgin did not conceive a man, but God by nature,
the pre-eternal Son and Word of God, to be incarnate from her. For upon each
of the women a conception

comes to be of seed deposited from a man, and the conception is of the man's
seed; and the seed is the hypostasis, the woman at the conception supplying
her own bloods and a joining coming to be, and the bloods being hypostasized
in the man's seed, and the seed itself becoming hypostasis for them, and the
seed becoming a common hypostasis for itself and for the woman's bloods.
But upon the holy Virgin it was not so; for the conception was not from a
man's seed, but after the consent of the holy Virgin the Holy Spirit came upon
her according to the word of the Lord which the angel spoke, purifying her and
furnishing a power receptive of the Godhead of the Word, and then there
overshadowed upon her the hypostatic Wisdom and Power of God the Most High,
the Son of God, consubstantial with the Father, as a kind of divine seed, and
he compacted for himself from her holy and most pure bloods flesh ensouled
with a rational and intellectual soul, himself having become hypostasis for
it. Thus being God from the Father by nature he also became man by nature from
a mother. And the same one is this and that, truly perfect God and by nature
perfect man without falsehood, one hypostasis in two natures undividedly. For
we do not confess a man deified, but God incarnate and become man; "for the
Word became flesh." And we proclaim the same one whole God and whole man,
whole God with his flesh and whole man with his more-than-divine Godhead. And
we dogmatize one nature of God the Word incarnate. And we know and proclaim
two begettings of the natural Son of God and God, one from the Father pre-
eternal, bodilessly and without cause, and one in the last times for our
salvation from the holy Virgin, of him incarnate and become man, both bodily
and temporal. Wherefore we also preach the holy Virgin Theotokos, as having
begotten God who was incarnate from her properly and truly. And we know her as
Christotokos (for she begot Christ), but since god-stricken Nestorius used
this for the abolition of the Theotokos title, we do not name her
Christotokos, but from the greater we name her Theotokos; for other mothers of
prophets and kings are also Christotokoi, but only the holy Virgin Mary is
Theotokos. And we know two natures of the one Christ the Son of God and two
natural wills and energies; for he works in each form and wills that which it
has as proper, with the communion of the other. And we serve a Trinity, not a
tetrad, even after the becoming-man of the Son of God, one Father, one Son of
God and of the Virgin, the same God and man, and one Holy Spirit, unto the
ages. Amen.
