* Homily on the Resurrection of Christ
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of our father among the saints Epiphanius, bishop of Cyprus, a discourse on
the holy resurrection of Christ.
Lord, bless.
The three-day Sun of righteousness has risen today, and has enlightened all
creation; the three-day and pre-eternal Christ, the cluster, has blossomed,
and has filled the inhabited world with gladness. Let us behold the dawn that
has no evening; let us rise before dawn today, and we have been filled with
the light of joy. The gates of Hades have been opened by Christ, and the dead
have risen as from sleep. Christ has risen, the resurrection of those who have
fallen, and he has raised Adam with himself; Christ has risen, the
resurrection of all, and he has delivered Eve from the curse; Christ has
risen, the resurrection, and having adorned the one who was formerly without
adornment, and the things, he has made them bright; the Lord has been awakened
as one who sleeps, and having struck all his enemies he has put them to shame.
He has risen, and has granted joy to all creation; he has risen, and the
prison of Hades has been emptied; he has risen, and he has transferred the
corruption of nature into incorruption; Christ has risen, and he has restored
Adam to the ancient dignity of immortality. Let the new creation which is in
Christ be renewed by the resurrection; let us who are without adornment be
adorned, the new world which is in Christ; the Church which is in Christ is a
new heaven today: a heaven more beautiful than the visible heaven. For it is
not girt about with a daily sun that sets, but with the Sun whom this servant
sun, being ashamed, set at the cross, the Sun of whom the prophet says, "The
Sun of righteousness shall rise upon those who fear Jesus," the Sun whom the
Church has put on as ever-shining, on whose account the Scripture says: "The
sun went out upon the earth, and Lot," as a type of the law, "entered into
Zoar," which is interpreted smallness. This is the Sun who makes wise the
unwise; this is the Sun who has been founded in the firmament together with
our faith. Through this Sun of righteousness, Christ, the Church has become a
heaven; not having a moon that waxes and wanes, but having ever-shining grace;
not causing certain wandering stars to rise, but bringing up newly-illumined
stars from the font; not having rain-bearing clouds, but having theological
teachers; not hanging upon turbid waters, but founded upon the sacred
doctrines; not sending up a wintry rain, not enrolling men by the cries of
birds, but by the voices of teachers.

This is the day which the Lord has made; let us exult spiritually, and let us
be glad in a manner befitting God =43.468= in it. This is for us the feast
above all feasts; this is the feast concerning which the Holy Spirit exhorts,
saying: "Keep the feast among those who cover densely and also keep warm, even
unto the horns of the altar"; this is the feast of the whole world, a
dedication and a salvation together. This feast is the crown and acropolis of
all feasts; this is the day which God blessed, he sanctified it, because on it
he rested from all his works, having perfected the salvation of those on earth
and of those under the earth together. On it he brought to rest the rites and
dances of idols and of brute beasts; on it he brought to rest the power of all
adversaries. On this he brought to rest feasts and demonic assemblies; on this
he brought to rest the fumes and sacrifices of idolatrous bloods; on this he
brought to rest the error of impiety; on this he brought to rest the power of
the tyrant, and the sting of the people; on this he brought to rest legal
sacrifices and new moons;


on this he set new bounds and laws for creation; on this he brought to rest
the Passover of the law, and of the Jews; on this he fulfilled every type,
shadow, and prophecy. Our Passover, the true Passover, Christ has been
sacrificed, and the new creation which is in Christ, the new faith which is in
Christ, new laws, the new people of God; new, but not the old Israel, and a
new Passover; a new and spiritual circumcision; a new and bloodless sacrifice;
a new and divine covenant. Be renewed today, and renew a right spirit in your
hearts, that you may receive the mysteries of the new and true feast, and may
delight today in the delight that is truly heavenly, and may go away having
been enlightened with the mysteries of the new Passover that do not grow old,
instead of the old typical mysteries; that you may understand how great is the
interval, and the difference of our things from those of the Jews, and what
comparison there is of the types toward the truth.

Let us begin the things of the resurrection of Christ's Passover, and the
contemplation, from the things here. Moses the lawgiver was once sent from God
from a high mountain for the salvation of a people, toward the type of the
law; the Lord too was sent, the lawgiver, and God from God, and a mountain
from a mountain of heavenly mountains, for the salvation of our people, that
of the truth. But Moses redeemed from Pharaoh and the Egyptians; but Christ
redeemed us from the devil and from demons. But Moses killed, having buried in
the sand the one who was wronging the Jew; Christ too put the devil to death,
having sent him away to the abyss. Moses made peace between his two brothers
who were fighting; Christ too made peace between his two peoples, having
united the heavenly things to the earthly. There the daughter of Pharaoh,
going to bathe, found, having taken =43.469= Moses; but the Church of Christ,
also a daughter, in being baptized receives Christ, not a three-month-old from
a basket, as Moses, but a three-day one from the tomb instead of Moses. There
typically and by night Israel kept the Passover; here we celebrate the
Passover brightly and by day. There toward the evening of the day; here toward
the evening of the times of the age. There the doors of the doorposts were
anointed with the blood; here the hearts of the faithful are sealed with the
blood of Christ. There the sacrifice was nocturnal, and by night was the
crossing of the Red Sea; here is salvation, and bright is the Red Sea of
baptism, and of the fire of the Spirit that kindles; in which also the Spirit
of God is truly borne together, and appears, and upon the same water, in which
the head of the dragon is crushed, and of the ruler of the demonic
dragon-peoples of the devil. There Moses baptized Israel with a nocturnal
baptism; there a cloud covers the people; but the power of the Most High
overshadows the people of Christ. There, when the people had been saved,
Miriam of Moses danced; but here, when the people of the nations is being
saved, the Church of Christ with all her Churches celebrates. There Moses
flees to the rock of creation; here the people flees to the rock of the faith.
There the legal tablets are shattered, signifying together the passing of the
law, and its growing old; but here the divine laws are kept unbroken. There a
calf was molten for the punishment of the people; but here the Lamb of God is
sacrificed for the salvation of the people. There a rock is struck with a rod;
but here Christ the rock is pierced in the side. There water comes out from
the rock; here blood and water well forth from a life-giving side. Those
received quail-flesh from heaven; but we receive the dove of the Spirit from
on high. Those ate temporary manna, and died; but we eat bread unto eternal
life. But those things, as old and shadowy, have grown old, and have been
brought to an end; but the things of our people increase, and bloom, remaining
forever.


This is the prototype of our Passover; this is the shadowy crossing of the
legal matters. Thus understand the things of the feast; thus consider the
things of Moses and of the prophets concerning the day, that you may be
persuaded of the resurrection. For all are girt about with the veil of
unbelief. It has many types, and ones that cannot be circumscribed, of the
resurrection from the dead, and of the return to life. A trustworthy witness
of this is the slaughter of Isaac; a type of this is the pit of Joseph, into
which he was thrown by his brothers, from which he came up immortal; a type of
this is the pit of Jeremiah, from which he once came up from corruption and
from the mire. A type of Christ's resurrection is the sea-monster of Jonah,
from which he came forth after three days. You have also another type of the
prison in Hades, the prison of Joseph, where the adulterous synagogue shut him
in, from which he came forth unharmed and after three years, as Christ after
three days from the dead. =43.472= With whom also Daniel prefigures the pit of
the lions as the tomb of the Savior, from which Jesus came up living, as one
delivered from the lions of Hades and of death. Through these refute the Jews;
through these rebuke them; thus make a defense for the passion of Christ, and
the resurrection.

These weapons the word grants you today for alliance; these mysteries the
feast teaches you in addition. Then what also after these things? Do we alone
celebrate, or shall we also be concerned for the neighbor? Shall we keep
festival, or is the word also concerning others for us? Of necessity let there
come to be, of our feast, some common groaning of the Church toward God that
is well-pleasing to God and also acceptable, and let us remember our own
brothers who are in our necessities, who are being examined in desert
necessities. Let us become as bound together with those who are bound; let us
become as suffering together with those who suffer the necessity; because if
one member suffers, all the members suffer together. Let us therefore have
compassion on our brothers, our members; some through money, some through
words, and others through well-doing, but all through supplication toward God
on their behalf. Let there be today, I beg, a common embassy of all toward
God, because our captivity too is common. Let the petition be common, I beg,
because the chastisement is common. Let us hear the one who says, "Pray for
one another that you may be healed." Let us hear Christ saying, "If two or
three shall agree unto prayer, concerning every request which they shall ask,
it shall be done for them." Great, brothers, is the weapon of the prayer of
the Church; great, brothers, is a wall toward God, the prayer of agreement,
and especially of a faithful people of captivity. Let no one dare to say that
God does not hear sinners. He receives rather the petition of the humble, and
especially of those who are being worn down for his name, being scourged,
being imprisoned, being afflicted, being reproached by enemies, and who have
not denied the faith of Christ. From among whom there are many men and many
women in our present people of Christ who have become confessors, and who are
quickly heard by him. Great is the weapon, beloved, of their prayer; for
Christ respects them all, through whom they also are in danger every day.
Therefore, taking courage in their prayers, and looking toward their boldness
toward God, let us pray commonly and earnestly for our brothers in
necessities, because often a kingly loving-kindness in the feasts grants to
many condemned men remission and redemption. Great, I say again, brothers, is
the prayer of the Church, and especially the faith of captivity.

=43.473= For indeed a king too often respects the entreaty of a people, and
grants condemned men to a crowd that importunes him. Therefore we ask all your
love earnestly to remember our brothers who are in necessity; and at that most
dread hour itself, in which we shall receive the priceless pearl of the body
of Christ


in our hands. Thus let us pray earnestly for our brothers, and let us say to
Christ: You yourself, the only good God then and now, and the Master who loves
mankind, who through the Passover delivered Israel from the slavery of Egypt
and through the blood of the lamb granted him freedom, you yourself even now
through your undefiled body, and your precious blood, grant to your world
freedom from the bitter slavery. You who received the weeping of the sinful
harlot, receive today also your Church and the groaning of captivity; you who
received the entreaty of the faithful robber, receive the petition of your
faithful people; you who received the repentance of Peter, and the groaning,
receive also the weeping of us the poor; you who did not turn away the tears
of the Canaanite woman, receive also a small Church making embassy on behalf
of a great captivity, and crying toward you, God, today and saying: O God, who
rose after three days from the dead, raise up your faithful people from the
enemies; you who raised Adam from among the mortal, raise the horn of the
Christians; you yourself who then and now are God, who took on the form of a
servant, redeem your humble people from slavery; you who deigned to become an
infant on our behalf, save from the sword the multitude of our infants.

You yourself who then were God, who sojourned with a mother in Egypt, call
back the mothers and the children from the long sojourning; you who were sold
for many of your own will, stop the selling of the people of the Churches; you
who for our sake received upon the back the scourges of Pilate, and threats,
you yourself who grew weary for our sake from the journey, stop the toils and
hardships of your people; you who cried upon the cross, "I thirst," refresh
souls that terribly thirst and hunger; you who were condemned with the lawless
by the lawless, deliver us also from the counsel of the lawless; you who were
stripped as a malefactor by the lawless, you yourself, Master, who were bound
by the lawless, loose those who have been fettered in bonds; you who comforted
your undefiled mother from the lamentation of the cross, and from the weeping;
you yourself who then cried to the myrrh-bearers, "Rejoice," you yourself even
now cry to your Churches, "Rejoice"; you yourself who were nailed by the
precious nails in the hands and the feet, loose the hands and the feet of
peoples bound with iron. Yes, Master who loves mankind, who said: "My soul is
exceedingly sorrowful unto death," deliver your people from the sorrow and
from the death; you yourself who were pierced in your side by the spear, crush
the spear of the enemies with your mighty hand, and remember, Master, as
=43.476= of your faithful robber, also of your people; you who poured out the
undefiled blood on our behalf, stop the confusion of our bloods. Save your
people, Master; spare your inheritance. Awake; why do you sleep, Lord? Why are
you long-suffering toward the enemies? Why do you turn your face away from us?
Arise, and do not thrust away unto the end; do not overlook completely.
Remember your cross, remember your people, remember your compassions. You who
of your own will were tempted forty days for our sake, Master, remember your
peoples who are being tempted in deserts, those in pathless and waterless
places; remember those with us, and before us, O good one who loves mankind.

Help those together, come before those; visit those at last; those who are
more pitiable than all, those who are more humble than all upon the earth;
those in deserts who have been given up as lost by men; who were driven far
from your hand; who were greatly chastised by your wrath; who were greatly
rebuked by your anger; who before death were filled as dead men from among
men; who were condemned to dwell with wild beasts; whose violence no one sees,
except


only your unsleeping eye; whose necessity you, Master, know, and no one else
*** unto the age; so then, having been separated from those through whom they
were parted, having been made far from the end of the inhabited world, having
at last been forgotten of the churches, not knowing the day of the feasts. Do
a little mercy to those, Master; for I do not ask much. Come upon those, O
God, who chastised those; come upon those who were once your children, because
they too were once your children, because they too were once Christians, and
they too were once your flock, and they too were once your members, and they
too once partook of your undefiled body, and now they partake of the bodies of
irrational beasts. Therefore then, Master, look upon them in your mercies and
compassions. See the violence of those pitiable ones, and give
loving-kindness; see the stripping, and give the rescue; see the deep
desolation, and work your compassion. For even if you chastised justly,
nevertheless heal; even if you delivered them over worthily, nevertheless
gather them. You scourged, comfort; you delivered over, redeem. Even if we
have often sinned, nevertheless remember us again, and be persuaded again by
our petition, and especially by our brothers who are in deserts, and
mountains, and caves, and the holes of the earth, in pathless places,
Christians of yours, and your peoples. For even if we are in churches with the
faithful, yet those are in deserts with the enemies; even if we are bright in
the feasts, yet those are in great darkness. Great is their misfortune; great
is their suspicion; terrible is their wound, bitter is their calamity; their
destruction is beyond telling, great is their sting, great is their tragedy,
Master. Do not leave them outside unto the end, but together =43.477= with the
chastisement show also your loving-kindness, that the Jews may not boast unto
the end, that the nations may not say concerning us: "Where is their God?
Where is their Christ? Where is their cross? Where is their hope? Where is the
faith of the Christians?" That they may not say these things, they shall
quickly behold your mercies toward us; "Let your compassions quickly overtake
us, Lord, because we have become exceedingly poor, because we have been
exceedingly humbled, because we have been exceedingly diminished." So then let
the groaning of those who have been fettered enter before you; the tears of
the innocent infants who are being slaughtered will importune you; the
lamentation of mothers who are being made childless will beseech you.

Saying these things we ask, we do not fight, Master; we ask, but we do not go
to law toward you. We ask, because we are reproached by the lawless, because
we are worn down by the Greeks; we do not fight, but we confess. We weep,
because the rest also are in danger. For even if we are sinners, nevertheless
we are Christians; even if humble, nevertheless a people of your faith; even
if unworthy of loving-kindness, nevertheless we are sheep of your Church
gathered into one. This supplication we the poor offer to you on behalf of a
multitude of a people of captivity, in your three-day resurrection, priests
and peoples, young men and virgins, elders with younger ones, infants with
mothers, every soul of those who believe in you; redeem them all from every
threat that lies upon them, and make them worthy of your kingdom, by the grace
and loving-kindness of your only-begotten Son and Master God and our Savior
Jesus Christ, with his all-holy and life-giving Spirit; now and always and
unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
