* Fragments on the First Epistle to the Corinthians

1 Cor 15:1-2 Since certain people in Corinth were saying that the soul is
corruptible and the resurrection of the body superfluous, Paul convicts their
deceit, and says: the gospel, through which you were called from the deceit of
many gods unto knowing the true God, through which you are established and
have salvation, I make known to you. I make known to you the gospel of God, in
order that you may know that the memorial concerning the resurrection of the
dead is not mine, nor anyone's.
1 Cor 15:3-4 He handed on to me the gospel, because the Savior, having
sojourned among us and borne a body in truth, died and was buried and rose
again in order that the dead might rise. Then, lest the simpler be disturbed
at the death of Christ, he adds: according to the Scriptures: for he suffered
such things as the prophets foreannounced concerning him. Since then he died
and was buried and rose again, it is consequent that human beings rise through
him who rose on their behalf, he having become the cause of the rising. But if
Christ is said to have died and to have been buried, this is to be understood
of his body, not of his soul. Since then they believed in the resurrection of
the Savior, they are blameworthy for not having accepted along with the
resurrection of the Savior also the resurrection of all.
1 Cor 15:5-9 Of this his resurrection the first who companied with him are
eyewitnesses worthy of testimony, then the five hundred. These things, he
says, I hand on, which those deceiving you are not able to contradict. Still,
even if the dead body rises, it rises incorruptible and spiritual: wherefore
he did not appear to all, but to those he willed. Thus after the assumption he
was seen by Paul, who, using moderation, calls himself an abortion, conscious
that he came from repentance unto being an apostle, and as 7 not even being
worthy of the appellation, as a persecutor. Yet even if formerly such a one,
now a teacher, having this from the gift.
1 Cor 15:10-11 Even if formerly a persecutor, yet now a teacher by the gift of
God, which did not abide in him in vain, inasmuch as, having been strengthened
by it, he bore the contests more abundantly on behalf of the pious faith. And
the whole uprightness which he had from choice he attributes to the grace
given him from God.
1 Cor 15:12-17 If anyone says that the dead are not raised, since of
necessity, Christ having risen, all rise, does he not necessarily say that
Christ also has not been raised? But if he has not risen, see how many
absurdities spring up: we who have said this will be found false witnesses of
God; even if you have believed foolishly, as you, together with the rising of
the Lord, yet also through faith in him who rose, supposing to have been freed
from sins, you have not put away your sins.
1 Cor 15:18- He who has not accepted that the soul of man is immortal and for
this reason disbelieves the resurrection, pitiable is such a one, depriving
himself of eternal life, for which the holy ones, becoming worthy of rewards,
are rich. Even if some disbelieve, yet we look for a resurrection, knowing
that Christ has become the firstfruits, the resurrection of those who have
fallen asleep: through whom all will be made partakers of immortal life, for
it is consequent.
1 Cor 15:21-22 Since, because of Adam's transgressing the law, all who are
from him have died, so also they will be made alive in Christ, who knew no
sin. But certain of the heretics say that only the soul of the just is made
alive in Christ, because it is not ** for the making alive will be of all who
have died in Adam.
1 Cor 15:23-24a Since the resurrection of Christ, being before all, will be
first in honor: then next the faithful are ranked in the presence of Christ
according to the analogy of the faith they have, after which is the end, thus
distinguished from the beginning, but that for the sake of which all the other
things tending unto 8 salvation exist, itself existing for the sake of no
other, being last, desirable of itself and chosen for itself.
1 Cor 15:24b-26 By kingdom here he means those who are ruled, that is, his own
flesh, which the Son handed over to the Father, having made it better, no
longer ruled as the form of a servant but as being the flesh of the Word,
which is of him, of the Son, having shown forth in it the form of God. For
then, the Son having received back the glory he had before the world was, they
will be ruled as by one king, Father and Son. Christ must therefore reign for
the progress of all, until all who are enemies because of sinning are put
under his feet, he destroying every tyrannical power, after which death also,
the originator of evils, is itself destroyed, since every soul that is united
to him and undergoes death subjects to death the one united to wickedness.
1 Cor 15:27-28 These things were said both against the mythology and deceit of
the Greeks and against those asserting that the gods are old wives' tales, and
because, the church being subjected, he himself is said to be subjected,
appropriating to himself the persecutions and the sufferings and the
subjection of the church.
1 Cor 15:29 Those from Marcion baptize the living in place of the dead who
have been initiated, not knowing that baptism saves only the one who has
received it. But the apostle calls the bodies dead, on behalf of which we are
baptized: for by their own reason these do not live without soul, but from the
souls joined with them have their living. Yet Abraham also named the soulless
body dead.
1 Cor 15:30 If the soul is not immortal, if the body is not raised, then also
vain is it to hazard oneself for piety.
1 Cor 15:32 So far as he came according to a human reckoning, I fought with
beasts at Ephesus, I would not have shrunk from this, being willing on behalf
of the truth, if–the dead are not raised.
9 1 Cor 15:33 Do not be led astray, Corinthians, as though the rational soul
does not remain in continuance: for those who deceive you corrupt themselves,
supposing it to be corrupted. With which the body is placed around, they say
that one must live in luxury: for tomorrow we die, there being no other life
besides the visible one, like the irrational beasts, in which the soul
vanishes together with the body, because it was also sown with it. Therefore
one must not endure the base discourses corrupting the opinions of the simple.
1 Cor 15:34 One must keep awake according to the understanding, in order that
it not happen to sin in consenting to the rotten deceit concerning the
resurrection. And well also the wake up, justly, as not all doing this: for
the wise for doing evil, keeping awake about harmful things, wake up unjustly,
having supposedly put away the sleep of ignorance.
1 Cor 15:35-40 He who speaks in Christ to the one doubting about the
resurrection of the dead, as without understanding, says: fool, if you doubt
that the dead body of a human being is raised, know that the seeds you sow,
having died, you cast into the earth, persuaded that they would not change
into plants unless, having died, they were sown. Different is the body being
raised from that which you sowed: for you cast a grain into the earth, but God
raised a stalk. And to one of the questions, the one saying how are the dead
raised, corresponds the discourse about the things sown: but to the other
question, the one about with what body do they come, he renders the proof from
division, and, having divided the bodies into earthly and heavenly, he said
that according to the heavenly ones, sun and moon and stars, is the glory of
the human bodies being raised.
1 Cor 15:41 Like in the glory of the luminaries and stars, not all of those
who raise their bodies, but only those who have lived well and come to their
senses: for even if the bodies of the evil are raised incorruptible, yet they
are deprived of the glory of the heavenly ones. And it is to be noted that:
the glory of the bodies being raised is substantial even as that of the
luminaries.
10 1 Cor 15:42-43 Just as the rational soul, not being virtue or vice, is
receptive of both of these, so also our body, not being corruption or
incorruption, receives at different times the substantial qualities inherent
in it. Accordingly then not corruption is sown, and it is raised not
incorruption, but in incorruption. And to the one sown in corruption there
follows that in weakness and in dishonor a soulish body is sown, and likewise
to the one raised in incorruption that in power and in glory a spiritual body
is raised.
And when we say an incorruptible body, we do not say a fifth substance, as
some supposed concerning the stars, which we say is always incorruptible, but
we call incorruptible that which has come to be from a corruptible one,
incorruptible by the grace of God. Thus unchanging and unalterable, not
according to change ** for such a thing underwent it, but by enduring unto
long-lasting life, either being punished or honored.
1 Cor 15:44-46 Since the body is sown from the interweaving of the male with
the female, reasonably also dishonor and weakness will be about it, having
chanced to be a body of a soul corrupted in it, of the preceding quality, it
rises by the power of God, having incorruption and power and honor, having
been shown to be a spiritual body, being an instrument of the soul, no longer
of the soul simply, but of the Holy Spirit, by participation, the soul having
risen and as it were having been changed into spirit. And since the soul
remaining in the passions, reasonably also the body of such a soul is called
soulish. But if the soul rise above the passible state, it becomes spiritual,
and the body of such a soul, with which it is interwoven, is called spiritual.
Since the soul, progressing, ascends unto the spiritual, he said first to be
the soulish body, then the spiritual. And it must be understood that the body
of those raised from the dead is likened to the body of Christ, spiritual,
having come to be after the resurrection. Wherefore we do not give heed to
those saying that such a body rises as Adam had, soulish: for the life of the
first Adam prepared the human being to live unto a living soul, but the life
according to Christ, not unto a living soul but unto a spirit making alive.
And the spiritual making alive is congenital to those being raised from the
dead. And first and last he said fittingly, yet not first and second man, in
order that we may learn that certain have become intermediate, in manner of
life, between the first and the last man.
11 1 Cor 15:51 Since the contemplation concerning the resurrection has depth
of understanding, fittingly he says: behold, I tell you a mystery: all indeed,
being about to die, we shall fall asleep, but the just alone we shall be
changed, shining forth as the sun: for these alone, changing unto the better
according to soul and body, will be changed. For it is not consequent, in a
variant reading carried according to some other text, to say thus: not all
indeed shall we fall asleep, but all shall we be changed, because of what is
immediately added, and we shall be changed. For if all are changed, it is
superfluous that it be said also we shall be changed.
1 Cor 15:52-53 The dead will be raised, of their body, which was formerly
corruptible, the incorruptible coming to be. But some say here the we shall be
changed i.e. such as certain others being, by the dead, which, he says,
incorruptible we are raised in our bodies, the souls being changed according
to a better and more divine quality, being altered. But another says the
sinners are the dead, being raised incorruptible unto having endured unto the
eternal punishments, but those who have lived according to virtue being
changed from glory to glory: for it is indeed mortal and corruptible, yet not
the reverse. Wherefore the corruptible puts on incorruption, and the mortal
puts on immortality, in the season of the resurrection of the dead.
1 Cor 15:54-56 Just as, virtue being present, wickedness does not exist, so
also, immortality being present, death is made to vanish. Wherefore, the
mortal having put on immortality, death, having been conquered, is swallowed
up, so that it is said to it: where, O death, is your victory, in which,
conquering, you were making dead? Where, O death, is your sting, with which,
wounding, you were strong? For it stings by casting sins, according to the
reasonings, into those struck by it, and it conquers those sinning actively.
And the usage lies in Hosea not in those very words: for i.e. your judgment
the apostle said victory.
1 Cor 15:58 Since the enemy death has been done away, standing faithful
continue steadfast, working continually and indivisibly the work commanded to
you 12 by the Lord, fearing nothing, being of good courage, as your labor in
the Lord is not vain, an eternal reward being laid up for those always doing
the work.
1 Cor 16:2 On one of the sabbaths, the day of the Lord's resurrection,
therefore lay up in store among yourselves whatever your ability is able to
put aside, without toil and without necessity, in order that, having come, I
may find you ready for the giving, in order that the beginning of preparing
the things for the collection may not come to be with my having come.
1 Cor 16:8-9 In Ephesus, then, he wrote this epistle to the Corinthians. And
he says he remains there for a reasonable cause: for a door surpassing and
very effectual for the hearers has been opened to him: whence he had many
opposed demons, wandering, and men, conjurers and deceivers: for then the evil
become many in the opposing, whenever they see the word of faith prevailing
among those who receive it: and it is his custom, with boldness, to call the
word of piety a door.
1 Cor 16:10-11 When those being taught are steadfast and immovable concerning
piety, the teachers sojourn among them without fear, not having accepted lest
some negligence come to be about the hearers. Wherefore he says: attend to
yourselves, in order that Timothy may be of good courage to come to you, not
having feared, lest you be turned away. He used this signification from the
fearing, and in the next epistle he said: I fear lest somehow, as the serpent
deceived Eve in his craftiness, so your thoughts be corrupted. For this
Timothy, concerning whom I write the things set forth, is a servant of the
genuine ones of God, working the work of the Master similarly to me. Wherefore
let him not be despised by you, but let him be received genuinely with love,
and also be sent forward: for I wait in Asia until he return to me with the
brothers.
1 Cor 16:12 Since Apollos was bishop of Corinth, he withdrew from them because
of the schisms among them and was with Paul, and now, being entreated, did not
journey together with the epistle: and he states the cause for which he did
not go: 13 that it was not the will. Then, encouraging them, he writes: but he
will come when he has opportunity, the opportunity of his sojourn with you you
providing, when there are no longer schisms among you.
1 Cor 16:13 There follows for the one rightly watching, in not giving sleep to
the eyes nor slumber to the eyelids, the standing firmly in the faith, after
which there follow the act like men and be strengthened, as an athlete and
soldier of Christ, the one doing all things with love toward God and neighbor.
1 Cor 16:14-16 Firstfruits they are called, either because they believed
before the others, or because they excel in piety, or because they take
precedence in birth, who, because of the humility of mind inherent in them,
not having been ordained, but led to this by their own choice of themselves,
appointed themselves unto ministry. Wherefore subject yourselves to such ones,
having them as an image and example, in order that you also may appoint
yourselves unto the service of the saints.
1 Cor 16:17-18 With Stephanas, Fortunatus and Achaicus filled up what was left
wanting by you, being of the same choice as he, and refreshed my spirit and
yours. And the one thinking and doing the things of piety refreshes a holy
spirit: for the spirit seeks the good things as to be: in the case of the
evil, soul, but in the case of the saints, spirit is said of that which leaps
beyond in willing the beautiful. Therefore one must recognize those living
well and thinking well by thinking and doing the same things. Thus also Origen
holds that the spirit has something more than the soul in virtue, even if both
soul and spirit.
1 Cor 16:19- Stirring them up sufficiently unto love he writes that the
churches of Asia greet you and all those spending time in it, holy and
reverent men.
1 Cor 16:21 Lest some be deceived writing from the appellation of the apostle,
he makes this a sign of the epistles of himself and not of himself, the 14
being marked with his own hand, and what is written is with his holy right
hand. If anyone does not love the Lord, let him be anathema. maran atha. But
he does not love the Lord who does not keep his commandments: wherefore this
one also is anathema, knowing that the Lord has come who judges the works of
human beings, saying: I love those loving me. But if he loves those loving
him, consider what he will lay hold of those not loving him.
