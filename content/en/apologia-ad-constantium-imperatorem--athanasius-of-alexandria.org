* Apology to the Emperor Constantius

:CUSTOM_ID: h1

Of Saint Athanasius, archbishop of Alexandria, an apology to the emperor
Constantius.

1 Knowing you to have been a Christian for many years, and God-loving from
your ancestors, I now make my defense concerning myself with good courage; for
using the words of the blessed Paul, I make him my ambassador to you on my own
behalf, knowing that he is a herald of the truth, and that you are a lover of
his sayings, most God-beloved Augustus. Now concerning the ecclesiastical
matters and the conspiracy that was formed against me, what was written by so
many bishops is enough to bear witness to your reverence; and the things of
the repentance of Ursacius and Valens are also sufficient to show to all that
none of what they set up against us had truth. For what can others so much
bear witness as these have said in writing? "We lied, we fabricated, and all
the things against Athanasius are full of calumny." And as an addition toward
clear proof, if you would deign to learn, that while we were present the
accusers proved nothing against Macarius the presbyter; but when we were
absent they did by themselves whatever they wished. And such things, first the
divine law, and then also our laws, have declared to have no force. From these
things then you know that your piety too, being a lover of truth and
God-loving, sees us free from every suspicion, and shows those who arose
against us to be calumniators.

2 But concerning the slander that was made against me before your humanity on
account of the most pious Augustus of blessed and eternal memory, Constans
your brother (for this the enemies keep repeating, and they have even dared to
write it), the first things are enough to show that these too are not true.
For if it were some others who were saying this, the matter would be open to
judgment, and there would be need of much proof and of refutations to the
face; but if those who fabricated the first things themselves also composed
these, how are these not reasonably shown from those to have been fabricated
too? For this reason they again speak by themselves, thinking they can snatch
away your godliness. But they have failed; for you did not hear as they
wished, but by your long-suffering you have given room for us also to make our
defense. For not to be moved at once and to demand punishment was nothing
other than, as a just emperor, to wait also for the defense of the one
slandered; and if you deign to hear it, I have taken courage that in these
things too you will condemn those who were rash and did not fear God, who
commanded that no lie be spoken from the mouth to a king.

3 I am truly ashamed even while making a defense about such things, of which I
do not think even the accuser himself, while we are present, will still make
mention. For he knows accurately both that he himself is lying, and that I was
neither mad nor out of my mind, so that I might even be suspected of having
conceived such a thing. Therefore I would not even have answered others if
they asked, so that those who hear might not, even during the time of the
defense, have their mind in suspense; but to your piety I make my defense with
a clear and great voice, and stretching out my hand, what I have learned from
the Apostle, "I call God to witness upon my own soul"; and, as it is written
in the histories of the Kings: "The Lord is witness, and his Christ is
witness" (and grant me also to say), I never spoke ill of your piety before
your brother of blessed memory, Constans, the most pious Augustus. I did not
provoke him, as these have slandered; but if ever, when we came in to him, he
himself mentioned

your humanity, and he mentioned it also when those around Thalassus came to
Pitiabion, and we were staying in Aquileia; the Lord is witness how I
mentioned your godliness, and said those things which God will reveal to your
soul, so that you may condemn the calumny of those who slandered me before
you. Grant me who have said these things pardon, most humane Augustus, and
give me much forgiveness. For that Christ-loving man was not so easy, nor was
I of such stature that we should confer about such things, and that I should
slander a brother to a brother, or speak ill of an emperor before an emperor.
I am not mad, O emperor, nor have I forgotten the divine voice that says:
"Even in your conscience do not curse a king, and in the store-rooms of your
bedchamber do not curse a rich man; for a bird of the heaven will carry away
your voice, and the one who has wings will report your word." And if even the
things said in private against you emperors are not hidden, how is it not
unbelievable that, with an emperor present and so many standing by, I spoke
against you? For I never saw your brother alone, nor did he ever speak with me
alone; but I always went in with the bishop of the city where he was, and with
others who happened to be there; and we saw him in common, and again we
withdrew in common; Fortunatian the bishop of Aquileia can bear witness about
this, Father Hosius is sufficient to speak, and Crispinus of Patavium, and
Lucillus of Verona, and Dionysius of Elia, and Vincent the bishop in Campania.
And since Maximin of Trier and Protasius of Milan have died, Eugenius who
became magister can also bear witness; for he stood before the veil, and heard
what we asked of him and what he himself deigned to say to us. These things
then, even if they are sufficient toward proof, grant me nonetheless to
narrate also the reason of the journey abroad, so that from these things too
you may condemn those who slander us in vain.

4 When I left Alexandria, I did not go up to the camp of your brother, nor to
any others, but only to Rome, and having set before the Church the things
concerning myself (for this alone was my care), I was at leisure for the
assemblies. I did not write to your brother, except only when those around
Eusebius wrote to him against me, and I had necessity, while still in
Alexandria, to make a defense; and when, after he had commanded me to prepare
volumes of the divine Scriptures, I did this and sent them; for I who am
making a defense must speak truth to your godliness. When three years had
passed, then, he writes in that year commanding me to meet him; and he was in
Milan. And I, asking closely the cause (for I did not know it, the Lord is
witness), learned that certain bishops, having gone up, asked him to write to
your piety so that a synod might take place. Believe, O emperor, it happened
thus, and I do not lie. Going down then to Milan, I saw much humanity; for he
deigned to see me, and to say that he had written and sent to you asking that
a synod take place. And while I was spending time in the aforesaid city, he
sent for me again into Gaul, for Father Hosius was coming there also, so that
from there we might travel to Sardica. And after the synod, while I was
spending time in Naissus, he writes; and going up I then stayed in Aquileia,
where the letters of your godliness overtook me. And being called from there
again by the blessed man, and going up into Gaul, thus I came to your piety.

5 What place then, or what time, does the accuser name, in which he slandered
me as having said such things? or in whose presence did I go mad so as to
utter such things as he falsely charged me with having said? or who is it that
advocates for him and bears witness? For what his eyes have seen, these he
ought also to speak, as the divine Scripture charged. He will find no witness,
then, of things that did not happen; but I, that I do not lie, have as witness
together with the truth also your piety. For I ask, knowing you to be

most retentive of memory, to recall the words which I then reported, when you
deigned to see me; first in Viminacium, second in Caesarea of Cappadocia, and
third in Antioch; whether I even spoke ill before you of those around Eusebius
who had grieved me, whether I slandered any of those who had wronged me. And
if I did not slander even those against whom I ought to have spoken, what
madness did I have to slander an emperor to an emperor, and to set brother
against brother? I beg you, either cause me to be refuted while present, or
condemn the slanders, and imitate David who says: "Him who secretly slanders
his neighbor, this one I chased out." For as far as it lay with them, "a mouth
that has lied has destroyed a soul." But your long-suffering has conquered,
furnishing freedom of speech for a defense, so that they may also be able to
be condemned as contentious and calumniators. These things then concerning
your most pious brother of blessed memory; for you are able, according to the
wisdom given you by God, to anticipate the many things from these few, and to
know the fabricated accusation.

6 But concerning the other slander, whether I wrote to the tyrant (for I do
not even wish to speak the name), I beg you, as you will and through whom you
may approve, examine and inquire; for the excess of the slander puts me beside
myself, and leads me into much confusion. And believe, most God-beloved
emperor, that often reasoning with myself I disbelieved whether anyone had
gone so mad as even to lie such things. But since this too was being repeated
by the Arians, and they were boasting as having given a copy of a letter, I
was the more beside myself, and spending sleepless nights I fought as toward
the accusers present; and I suddenly let out a great cry, and I prayed at once
groaning with tears, to find your ears favorable. But even thus, having found
them by the grace of the Lord, I am again at a loss what beginning of the
defense I shall make; for as often as I attempt to speak, I am hindered
because of the shock of the matter. For concerning your blessed brother there
was at least a plausible pretext for the calumniators, that we were deigned
worthy to see him and that he asked your brotherly disposition concerning us,
and that he often honored us when present, and sent for us when absent. But
the devil Magnentius, the Lord is witness and his Christ is witness, I neither
know nor was I at all acquainted with him. What intimacy then is there of the
unknown toward the one who does not know? what pretext drew me to write to
such a man? What preface of the letter did I set, writing to him? That: You
did well in murdering the one who honored me, whose benefactions I would never
forget? and I accept you for having destroyed our acquaintances, Christians
and most faithful men? and we admire you for having slaughtered those in Rome
who received us genuinely, your blessed aunt, the true Eutropia, and that
genuine Abuterius, and Speirantius the most faithful, and many other good men?

7 Is it not even merely to suspect these things of the accuser a mad thing?
For what again persuaded me to have confidence in him? What secure disposition
of his did I see? That he destroyed his own master, and became unfaithful
toward his own friends, and broke oaths, and was impious toward God, inventing
sorcerers and enchanters against the judgment of God? And with what conscience
did I say "rejoice" to this man, whose madness and cruelty grieved not me
alone, but also all our inhabited world? Great thanks indeed and much
did I owe him from these things, that your blessed brother filled the churches
with offerings, and this man murdered him while he was sending them. And the
polluted man, seeing these things, was neither ashamed, nor did he fear the
grace given to the blessed man through baptism; but like some avenging and
diabolical demon he raged against him. For the blessed man, then, this has
become unto martyrdom; but that man thereafter as a prisoner according to
Cain, groaning and trembling

was pursued, so that he might also imitate Judas in his death, becoming
executioner against himself, and might make a double punishment against
himself in the judgment after these things.

8 To such a man the slanderer thought I had become a friend; or perhaps he did
not even think it, but as an enemy he fabricated it unconvincingly; for he
knows accurately that he has lied. And I wished that he, whoever he is, were
present here, and that I might ask upon the truth itself (for what we speak as
with God present, this oath we Christians have) which of us rejoiced while the
blessed Constans was living, and who rather prayed; and the first slander
shows it, and this is clear to everyone. And if he himself also knows
accurately that of those so disposed, even if someone loved the blessed
Constans, he did not become a friend to the one who arose against that man;
and if he was disposed otherwise than we were, I fear that the things which
the hater of that man conceived, these he has falsely charged against me.

9 I then, being astonished at this, am at a loss what one making a defense
ought to say; and I condemn myself to ten thousand deaths, if even any
suspicion at all should arise against me concerning this; but to you,
truth-loving emperor, I make my defense with confidence; I beg you, as I said
before, examine; and especially having as witnesses the ambassadors once sent
from that man to you; and they are Sarbatius and Maximus the bishops, and
those with them; and Clementius and Valens. Learn, I beg you, whether they
brought letters to me; for these would have furnished a pretext for me too to
write to him. And if he did not write, nor even knew me, how did I write, not
being acquainted with him? Ask whether, having seen those around Clementius, I
did not remember the man of blessed memory, and according to what is written,
wet my garments with my tears, thinking of his humane and Christ-loving soul.
Learn how, having heard of the cruelty of the beast, and having seen those
around Valens coming through Libya, I feared that he too might dare to make
trial and as a robber murder those who loved and remembered the blessed man;
of whom I set myself second to none.

10 Fearing this then for those who thought thus, did I not rather pray
concerning your humanity? and did I love the one who murdered that man, and
grieve toward you, who are the brother and who avenge that man's death? But
did I remember that man's lawlessness, and forget your benefaction, which even
after the death of the blessed man you deigned to make known by letters would
be such toward me as it was also while that man was still alive? With what
eyes did I look upon the murderer? Or how, praying for your salvation, did I
not think I saw that blessed man also? For brothers are mirrors of one another
because of nature. For this reason, seeing you also in him, I would never have
slandered; and seeing that man again in you, I would never have written to the
one who arose against him, but rather I prayed concerning your salvation. And
witnesses of these things are first of all the Lord, who heard and granted you
the whole empire from your ancestors; and witnesses also are those then
present, Felicissimus who became duke of Egypt, and Rufinus, and Stephanus, of
whom one was catholicus, and the other was magister there; and Asterius the
comes, and Palladius who became magister of the palace, and Antiochus and
Evagrius the agentes in rebus. For I said only: Let us pray concerning the
salvation of the most pious Augustus Constantius; and all the people at once
with one voice cried: Christ, help Constantius; and he continued praying thus.

11 That I never wrote to that man, then, nor ever received from him, I have
called God to witness and his Word, his only-begotten Son our Lord Jesus
Christ; and as for the accuser, grant that he too be asked about this in a few
things, from where he came to this. Would he say he

has copies of a letter? for the Arians have worn themselves out repeating
this. First then, even if he should show the letters like ours, he does not
yet have what is secure; for there are forgers who have often even imitated
the hands of you emperors; and the imitation does not furnish authority to the
letters, unless those also who are accustomed to write such things bear
witness to the letters. This then I wish to ask the slanderers again, who ever
furnished these, and from where these were found; for I too had those who
write, and he again had those who receive from the bearers and give them to
him. Ours then are present; deign to call those too; for they can certainly be
alive; and learn about these letters, examine as with truth present with you.
For this is a safeguard of emperors, and especially of Christians; with this
it is safe for you to reign, as the divine Scripture says: "Mercy and truth
are a guard to a king, and they will surround his throne in righteousness."
Putting this forward, the wise Zerubbabel conquered; and all the people cried:
"Great is the truth and it prevails."

12 If then I had been slandered before others, I would have called upon your
piety as the Apostle then called upon Caesar and the plot of the enemies
against him ceased; but since they have dared to accuse before you, whom shall
I call upon from you? the Father of the one who says, "I am the truth," that
he may incline your heart toward favor. Master Almighty, King of the ages, the
Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, you through your Word have given this empire
to your servant Constantius; shine into his heart, so that, knowing the
calumny against us, he himself may receive the defense favorably, and may
cause all to know that his ears have been secured in truth, and according to
what is written: "Righteous lips alone are acceptable to a king." For thus you
have also caused it to be said through Solomon that the throne of the empire
is set right. Ask then at all events, let those who have accused learn that
you care to learn the truth. And if they will not show the calumny by the
color of the face; for this is a proof of the conscience, and it is written:
"Of a heart that is gladdened the face blooms; but when it is in griefs it
looks sullen." Thus conscience convicted those who plotted against Joseph; and
the wickedness of Laban against Jacob has been shown from the face. You see at
any rate their suspicion, as they flee and hide, and our freedom as we make
our defense. For the judgment now is not about possessions, but about the
glory of the Church. The one struck with a stone seeks the physician; but the
blows from slander are sharper than stones; "Slander is a club, and a sword,
and a pointed arrow," as Solomon said; and these things truth alone can heal;
and when this is overlooked, the wounds increase more terribly.

13 For these reasons all the things of the Churches everywhere have been
thrown into confusion. And they invented pretexts, and bishops of such stature
and of many years have been exiled, because of communion with me. And if this
had happened only up to this point, there was some good hope; for you are
humane; but so that the evil may not also pass on after these things, let the
truth prevail with you; and do not allow a suspicion to arise against every
Church, as if Christians, and especially bishops, were counseling and writing
such things. Or if you do not wish to inquire, it is just that we too be
believed rather as we make our defense than those who slander. For these as
enemies work wickedness, but we in agony furnish the proofs. Therefore I also
wonder how we speak with reverence, but they have had such shamelessness as
even to lie to an emperor. But examine for the sake of the truth, and as it is
written, "searching search" while we are present, from where they say these
things, or from where the letters were found. But neither will any of ours

be convicted, nor would any of his say so; for it has been fabricated. And
perhaps it is no longer fitting to seek further, for they do not even wish it,
lest the one who wrote these also be found of necessity. For the slanderers
alone know him, and no one else.

14 And since they have also accused concerning the great church, as if an
assembly had taken place there before it was completed; come, I will make my
defense again to your piety concerning this too; for those who are disposed
with love of enmity toward us drag us into these things. Yes, it happened, I
confess; for in saying the first things I did not lie, and this now I will not
deny. But the matter is otherwise again than as they have accused. And grant
me to say, we did not celebrate a day of dedication, most godly Augustus; for
this was truly unlawful to do before your command; nor did we come to this
from preparation; nor was any bishop or other cleric called for this, and much
was still lacking even to the work. Nor did the assembly take place from a
proclamation, so that these might find a pretext for accusing. But all know
what happened; hear it nonetheless with your own equity and long-suffering.
For it was the feast of Pascha, and the people were very many and so great as
Christ-loving emperors would pray there might be of Christians city by city.
The churches then being few and very small, there was no little tumult, as
they asked to come together in the great church, and that all pray there also
for your salvation; which also happened. But though I begged them for the time
to hold back, and somehow with distress to assemble in the other churches,
they did not obey, but became ready to go out of the city and come together in
the desert places in the sun, thinking it better to bear the toil of a road
than to keep the feast with grief.

15 For believe, O emperor, and concerning this again receive the truth as
witness; that in the assemblies of the Forty Days, because of the narrowness
of the places and the great multitude of the peoples, very many children, and
not a few younger women, and very many old women, and not a few young men,
being crushed, were carried away to the houses; and God granting it, no one
died; but all murmured, and asked on account of the great church. And if even
in the days before the feast such distress happened, what would have happened
on the feast itself? Altogether, I suppose, things still harsher than these.
But it was not fitting that grief instead of joy, mourning instead of
gladness, weeping instead of the feast, should come about for the peoples;
knowing this and especially having the pattern of the fathers. For the blessed
Alexander, the other places being narrow, and while building the church then
thought greater, the one called Theonas, assembled there because of the
multitude, and assembling he did not neglect the building. This I have also
seen happening in Trier, and in Aquileia; for there too in the feasts, because
of the multitude, while the places were still being built, they assembled
there; and I did not find such an accuser. But even your blessed brother, in
Aquileia, when there was such an assembly, assembled. Thus I too have done,
and it was not a dedication, but an assembly of prayer. You then, I know well,
being God-loving, accept the eagerness of the peoples, and forgive me for not
hindering the prayers of so great a people.

16 And I wish to ask the accuser again concerning this, where was it lawful
for the people to pray, in deserts, or in a place of prayer that was being
built? Where was it fitting and holy for the people to answer the Amen, in
deserts, or in the already mentioned Lord's house? And you, most God-beloved
emperor, where would you have wished the peoples to stretch out their hands
and pray concerning you? where Greeks also stand as they pass by, or in the
place that bears your name, which already, or rather even together with the
foundation, all name a Lord's house? I know that you prefer your own place;
for you smile, and by smiling you signify this. But it ought, says the
accuser, to have happened in the churches. Small

then they are and narrow, and all, as I said before, are so toward the
peoples. Then how was it fitting for the prayers to happen? and how was it
better, part by part and divided, for the people with dangerous crowding, or,
there being already a place able to receive all, to come together in it and
for one and the same voice to come about with the agreement of the peoples?
This was better; for this also showed the one soul of the multitude; thus God
also hears swiftly. For if according to the promise of the Savior himself, "if
two should agree concerning anything whatever they may ask, it will come about
for them"; what, if when so many peoples have come together one voice comes
about, saying the Amen to God? Who then did not wonder? Who did not call you
blessed, seeing so great a people come together in one place? How did the
peoples rejoice seeing one another, who formerly came together in divided
places? This gladdened all, and grieved only the one who slandered.

17 I wish at any rate to anticipate his other remaining objection. For the
accuser says: The work was not yet completed, and prayers ought not to have
taken place; but the Lord said: "But you, when you pray, go into your
store-room, and shut the doors." What then would the accuser say? Or rather
what would the prudent and truly Christians say? For ask these, O emperor;
since it is written concerning those that "The fool will speak foolish
things"; but concerning these, "From every prudent man take counsel." The
churches being narrow, and the peoples being so many, and wishing to go away
into the deserts, what ought one to have done? For the desert is without a
door, and is a thoroughfare of those who wish, but the Lord's place is both
walled and furnished with doors, and shows the difference of the pious and the
profane. Would not, O emperor, every prudent man whatever nod to this together
with your piety? For they know that here prayer is lawful, but there a
suspicion of disorder; unless perhaps, places not existing, those who pray
would inhabit the wilderness alone, as Israel was; but even for those, when
the tent came about, the place of prayer was thereafter bounded. O Master and
truly King of those who reign, Christ, only-begotten Son of God, Word and
Wisdom of the Father, since the people prayed for your humanity, and through
you entreated your Father the God over all, concerning the salvation of your
servant the most pious Constantius, I am accused. But thanks to your goodness,
that for this I have also been slandered in your laws. For I would have been
slandered more greatly, and it would have been truly a charge, if we passed by
the place which the emperor built, and sought the desert for prayer. How would
the accuser have babbled then, how would he have been persuasive saying: He
despised your place; what is happening is against his mind; he laughed as he
passed by; he showed that the desert fills the need of the place; he has
hindered the peoples who wished to pray. These things he wished to say, these
he sought; and not finding them he is vexed, and thereafter fabricates words.
For if he said these things he would have put even me to shame; just as now he
does injustice, taking up the manner of the devil and watching those who pray;
therefore he also failed, reading beside the mark the thing of Daniel; for the
unlearned man thought that even under you the things of the Babylonians
prevail, and he did not know that you are a friend of the blessed Daniel; and
you worship the same God as he, and you do not hinder, but you wish all to
pray, knowing that it is the prayer of all that you be saved and reign in
peace continually.

18 I then lament these things too toward the accuser; but you, most
God-beloved Augustus, may you live through many circuits of years and
accomplish the dedication. For the prayers that have taken place from all
concerning your salvation do not hinder the festival of the dedication. Let
not the unlearned lie about this; but let them learn from the Fathers, and let
them also read the Scriptures; rather

let them learn from you, for you are a lover of letters, that Joshua the son
of Jozadak the priest and his brothers, and Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel the
wise, and Ezra the priest and scribe of the law, while the sanctuary was being
built after the captivity, and the Tent-pitching having come on, and this was
a feast and a festival and a great prayer in Israel, gathered the people
together with one mind into the broad place of the first gateway toward the
east, and they prepared the altar of God, and there they offered, and there
they accomplished the feast. And thereafter they thus offered the sacrifices
according to Sabbath and new moon, and the peoples offered up their prayers.
And the Scripture says plainly that these things happened and the temple of
God was not yet built; but rather, as they prayed thus, the house that was
being built advanced; and neither because of the expectation of the dedication
were the prayers hindered, nor because of the gatherings of prayers that took
place has the dedication been impeded; but the people also prayed thus; and
when the whole house had been completed, they made the dedication, and they
offered toward the dedicating, and all kept festival over the finishing of the
work. And this again both the blessed Alexander and the other Fathers have
done; for having gathered and completed the work, they gave thanks to the
Lord, accomplishing a dedication. This it is fitting for you also to do, most
learning-loving emperor; for the place is ready, having been purified
beforehand by the prayers that have already taken place, seeking the presence
of your piety; for this is what it lacks toward the complete adornment. This
then may you fulfill, and may you render the prayer to the Lord, for whom you
have also made the house; for this is the prayer of all.

19 Come then, let us see the other slander also, and grant me to make a
defense concerning it too. For they have dared to slander this as well, as if
I had opposed your commands, so as not to leave the church. I wonder then at
those men, that they do not grow weary in the calumnies; but I nonetheless do
not grow weary even thus, and I rather rejoice as I make my defense. For by
how much the defenses are many, by so much the more can those men be
condemned. I did not oppose a command of your piety, may it not be; for I was
not of such stature as even to oppose a curator of a city, much less so great
an emperor, and concerning this there is no need of so many words from me; for
the whole city bears witness for me. Grant me nonetheless to narrate this
matter too again from the beginning; for having heard, you will wonder, I know
well, at the easiness of the enemies. Montanus the Palatine came bringing a
letter, as if I had written, that I should come into Italy and that what I
think is lacking for the ecclesiastical things might be able to be fulfilled.
Thanks then to your piety, that it deigned, as if I had written, to nod
assent, and has made provision of the road so that I might come this way and
accomplish it without weariness; but I have wondered again at those who lied
to your ears, that they did not fear that the lie is proper to the devil, and
that those who lie are alien to the one who says: "I am the truth." For I did
not write, nor will the accuser be able to find such a letter. Even if I ought

21 For this reason then I too, since the letter was from slander and did not
even have a command to come, knew that it was not the choice of your piety
that we come to you. For not to command to come at all events, but even to
write as if I had written, and as wishing to set right the things that seemed
lacking, although no one was saying so, it was clear that the letter that was
brought was against the mind of your gentleness. All knew this; this I also
made plain in writing; and Montanus knows that I was not declining to come,
but I thought it unseemly to come as if I had written, lest the calumniators
again find a pretext even in this, as if I had become a trouble to your
godliness. Indeed I prepared myself, and he himself knows this, so that, if
you should deign to write, I might go out at once, and overtake by eagerness
what was commanded. For I was not mad so as to speak against such a command of
yours. Your piety then not having truly written, how did I oppose when I was
not commanded? Or how do they say I did not obey, although there was no
command? how is this not also a calumny of the enemies fabricating what did
not happen as having happened? I fear that even now, while I am making my
defense, they may repeat that I did not deign to make a defense; so easy am I
before them for being accused by them; and they themselves are swift to
calumniate and to despise the Scripture that says: "Do not love to slander,
lest you be taken away."

25 All asked that letters of your piety be shown. And a report alone from an
emperor has the same force as the things written, especially when the one who
brings it is confident of it and writes what was commanded; but since they
neither said plainly that there was a command, nor, what I asked, did they
send in writing, but were doing everything as from themselves; I confess, and
I say this with freedom of speech, I became suspicious toward them. For many
were the Arians around them, and they ate with these and took counsel with
these; and they did nothing with freedom of speech, but they practiced
attempting ambushes and deceits against me. And they did nothing as of an
emperor having commanded, but as being asked by enemies, they convicted
themselves. For this also made me ask letters from them still more, since what
they attempted and planned was also suspicious; and because it was unseemly,
after I had entered with so many written documents, to withdraw from the
Church without letters. When Syrianus had promised, then, all assembled in the
churches with joy and without care. But after twenty-three days of the promise
he comes upon the church with soldiers, and we were praying as usual; for thus
those who entered saw, since it was a vigil of an assembly that was to take
place. And such things happened that night as the Arians wished and promised
before they happened; for the general came having them with himself; and they
themselves were the leaders and counselors of such an assault. And this is not
unbelievable, most God-beloved Augustus; for they did not escape notice but it
has been proclaimed everywhere. I then, seeing the assault, having first asked
the peoples to withdraw, then I too after them, God covering and leading (for
this also those then with me have seen), withdrew; and from that time I
remained by myself, having freedom of speech and a defense both first of all

toward God and then also toward your piety, that I did not flee leaving the
peoples behind, but I have as witness of the persecution the assault of the
general; which especially all also wondered at. For he ought either not to
have promised or, having promised, not to have lied.

26 Why then did they counsel thus, or why did they attempt to lie in wait with
deceit, when it was possible both to command and to write? For an emperor's
command has great freedom of speech. But the wish to escape notice made even
clearer the suspicion that they did not have a command. And what unseemly
thing did I ask, truth-loving emperor? How would one not say that such a
request is reasonable for a bishop? you know, having read the Scriptures, how
great a charge it is for a bishop to leave the church, and to neglect the
flocks of God. For the absence of shepherds furnishes a pretext of assault to
the wolves against the herd. And this the Arians and all the other heretics
sought, so that by our absence they might find room to deceive the peoples
into impiety. If then I had fled, what defense did I have before the true
bishops, or rather before the one who has entrusted the herd? And this one is
he who judges all the earth, our true King of all and Lord Jesus Christ, the
Son of God. How would someone not reasonably have transferred the neglect of
the peoples onto me? And how would your piety not also have blamed me justly,
saying: Why, having entered with letters, do you withdraw without letters, and
have you left the peoples behind? And how would the people itself reasonably
on the day of judgment have thrown their own neglect onto me, saying: He who
visited us fled and we were neglected, there being no one to remind? If they
said these things, what would I have answered? For such a blame the shepherds
of the ancients also had through Ezekiel. Knowing this the blessed apostle
Paul also charged each of us through the disciple, saying: "Do not neglect the
gift that is in you, which was given you with laying on of the hands of the
presbytery." Fearing this I too did not wish to flee, but to have a command,
if indeed it was the will of your piety. But I neither received what I
reasonably asked, but even now I have been accused before you in vain; for I
neither opposed a command of your piety, nor will I now try to enter
Alexandria, until your humanity wishes this. And this too I have said in
advance, so that the calumniators may not again find a pretext even concerning
this to accuse us.

27 Seeing these things, I did not condemn myself, but having this defense
also, I was hastening toward your piety, knowing your humanity, and having in
memory your unlying promises, and being confident that according to what is
written in the divine Proverbs: "With a humane king righteous reasonings are
acceptable." And already having set upon my road and gone out of the desert, a
certain report came about suddenly; which at the beginning seemed to be
unbelievable, but after these things was shown true. For it was being repeated
everywhere that Liberius the bishop of Rome, and Hosius the great of the
Spains, and Paulinus of the Gauls, and Dionysius and Eusebius of Italy, and
Lucifer from Sardinia and certain other bishops and presbyters and deacons
were exiled, because they did not endure to subscribe against us. And these
were exiled, but Vincent of Capua, and Fortunatian of Aquileia, and Heremius
of Thessalonica, and all the bishops throughout the West, have suffered not
ordinary force, but even very much necessity and terrible insults, until they
promised not to be in communion with us. Then while we were also wondering and
being at a loss at these things, behold again another report overtook us
concerning those in Egypt and the Libyas, that some bishops, nearly ninety,
were persecuted, and the Churches were handed over to those who confess the
things of Arius; and sixteen were exiled, and of the others some were put to
flight, and some were forced to play a part.

For so great a persecution was said to be there, that, while the brothers were
praying in Alexandria at Pascha and on the Lord's days in a desert place near
the cemetery, the general with a multitude of soldiers more than three
thousand, carrying arms and naked swords and arrows, came upon the Christians,

28 And the other things that happened will perhaps be thought unbelievable,
and will astound all because of the excess of the abomination. But it is
necessary nonetheless to speak, so that your Christ-loving zeal and godliness
may learn that the slanders and calumnies against us have come about for no
other reason than that they may cast us out of the Churches, and may be able
to bring in their own impiety instead. For of the true and many-yeared

catechumens, and those who have the first places of the council, and those

whether anyone is blameless; but as the most impious Jeroboam did, the one who
gave more gold was named bishop. And it did not matter to them even if he
happened to be a pagan, only if he furnished gold. And the bishops from
Alexander, monks and ascetics, were exiled; but those wise about slanders
corrupted, as far as lay with them, the apostolic ordinance, and defiled the
churches. Great indeed did their calumnies gain, so that it might be possible
for them to act unlawfully and to do such things in your times, so that
against them is what is written: "Woe through whom my name is blasphemed among
the nations."

29 Such then being the things that were repeated, and the affairs of all being

your piety; and if your humanity should learn what has happened, it will
hinder it from happening thereafter; for it is not of a godly emperor to wish

lamented, I confess, I turned back again into the desert, reasoning this which
your godliness also sees; that if we are sought, so that being found we may be
sent on to the prefects, there is a hindrance of not coming to your humanity.
And if those who did not wish to subscribe against us have suffered such and
so many things, and those of the laity who do not wish to be in communion with
the Arians were commanded to die; it is not doubtful that altogether newer and
ten thousand deaths will be invented against me by the calumniators; and after
death the enemies will thereafter move with whom they wish and what things
they wish, lying against us more greatly, as no longer having those who refute
them. For I did not flee fearing your piety (for I know your forbearance and
your humanity), but seeing from the things that are happening the wrath of the
enemies, and reasoning that fearing lest they ever be convicted for what they
did against the mind of your nobility, they will do all things so as even to
kill. For behold, your humanity commanded only that the bishops be cast
outside the cities and the province; but the wonderful men, daring something
more than your command, have exiled beyond three provinces into desert and
unaccustomed and fearful places old men and bishops of many years. For some
from Libya were sent to the Great Oasis, and some from the Thebaid to the
Ammoniace of Libya. And I did not flee again fearing to die; let none of them
condemn me of cowardice; but because it is also a command of the Savior, that
when we are persecuted we flee, and when we are sought we hide, and not give
ourselves over to an obvious danger, nor by appearing to kindle still more
the wrath of those who persecute. For it is equal to murdering oneself and
again to give oneself to the enemies for them to murder.

But to flee, as the Savior charged, is to know the time and truly to care for
those who persecute, lest having gone even as far as blood they become liable
to the "You shall not kill"; and especially those who slander us wish even
the suffering. For what they have now again done shows that this is their zeal
and that their choice is murderous. And having heard, I know well, most
God-beloved Augustus, you will wonder; for the daring is truly worthy of
astonishment. And how great this is, hear in a short word.

33 The Son of God, our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, having become man for us,
and having abolished death, and having freed our race from the slavery of
corruption, granted us besides all the other things also to have on earth an
image of the holiness of the angels, virginity. Those then who have this
virtue the catholic Church has been accustomed to call brides of Christ. These
even Greeks, seeing them as a temple of the Word, wonder at; for this reverend
and heavenly profession is truly accomplished among no one except among us
Christians alone. For this especially is also a great proof that among us is
the real and true godliness. These also before the others your most pious
father of blessed memory, Constantine the Augustus, honored; these also your
piety writing often named honorable and holy. But now the wonderful Arians who
also slander us, because of whom also very many of the bishops were plotted
against, having the judges themselves as servants and obeying, having stripped
these, caused them to be hung upon the so-called hermetaria, and thirdly they
scraped their sides so much as not even those who are truly criminals have
ever suffered. Pilate then, serving the Jews of that time, pierced one
side of the Savior with a spear; but these have even conquered the madness of
Pilate, because they scraped not one but both; for the members of the virgins
are especially proper to the Savior. All then, even when some merely report
it, shudder at such a hearing; but these alone not only did not fear to strip
and scrape undefiled members, which the virgins have dedicated to our Savior
Christ alone, but even, what is worst, being reproached by all because of such
cruelty, instead of blushing they allege this to be a command of your piety;
so bold are they toward all things and wicked in choice. For not even in the
persecutions that happened was such a thing heard of as having been done. And
if such a thing had ever happened, still it was not fitting under you who are
a Christian either that virginity suffer such insult and dishonor, or that
these men throw their own cruelty onto your piety; for to heretics alone is
such an evil proper, to be impious toward the Son of God, and to offend
against his holy virgins.

34 And such and so many things having again happened from the Arians, I was
not then mistaken in being persuaded by the divine Scripture that says: "But
hide yourself a little, a little, until the wrath of the Lord pass by." And
this again has become a pretext of my withdrawal, most God-beloved Augustus,
and I did not refuse either to go away into the desert, or, if necessity
should come about, to be let down through a basket from a wall. For I endured
all things, and I dwelt with beasts, and to go about you, awaiting a time for
these words, and being confident that the slanderers will be condemned, and
your humanity will be shown. O blessed and most God-beloved Augustus, what did
you wish? that I come while those who calumniate us were inflamed and seeking
to kill, or according to what is written, that I hide a little, so that in so
great an interval the calumniators may be condemned as heretics, and your
humanity will be shown? And what, O emperor, did you wish me to be seen by
your judges, so that, even if you wrote only as far as a threat,

those men, not understanding your mind, but having the Arians sharpening them,
might kill from your letters and ascribe the murder to you because of the
letters? It was not fitting either that I desert to them, and give myself over
to bloods, or that you who are a Christ-loving emperor be inscribed over
murders of Christians, and these of bishops.

35 Better then has it been to hide, and to await this time. Yes I know that
you too, knowing the divine Scriptures, nod and accept me as having done this.
Behold at any rate, those who sharpened having ceased, your godly forbearance
has appeared, and it has been shown to all that you were not from the
beginning persecuting the Christians; but those men were the ones who made the
churches desert, so that they might sow their own impiety everywhere, because
of which we too, if we had not fled, would long ago have been plotted against
by them. For those who did not refuse to speak such slanders against me before
so great an Augustus, those who attempted such things against bishops and
virgins, it is quite clear that they were also hunting our death. But thanks
to the Lord who has given you the empire; for all have been assured concerning
both your humanity and their wickedness, for the sake of which I also fled at
the beginning, so that I may address these things to you, and you may find
whom you will treat humanely. I beg you then, since it is also written: "A
falling answer turns away wrath"; and, "Righteous reasonings are acceptable to
a king"; having received this defense also, give back to the fatherlands and
to the Churches all the bishops and the other clerics, so that the wickedness
of the slanderers may be shown, and you both now and on the day of judgment
may have freedom of speech to say to our Lord and Savior and King of all Jesus
Christ: "I have lost none of yours"; but those who plotted against all are
these; and I was vexed over those who have died, and the virgins who were
scraped, and the other things that happened against the Christians; but I
brought back those who were exiled and gave them back to their own Churches.
