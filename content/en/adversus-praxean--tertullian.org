* Chapter 1
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

The devil has variously rivaled the truth. He has aimed at times to shake it
by defending it. He vindicates the one Lord, the almighty maker of the world,
so that he may make a heresy even of the one. He says that the Father himself
descended into the virgin, himself was born from her, himself suffered,
finally that he himself is Jesus Christ. The serpent has slipped from himself,
because tempting Jesus Christ after the baptism of John he attacked him as the
Son of God, sure that the Son has God even from those very scriptures from
which he was then constructing the temptation. If you are the Son of God, say
that these stones become bread. Likewise: If you are the Son of God, cast
yourself down from here; for it is written that he commanded his angels
concerning you, the Father of course, to take you up in their hands, lest
anywhere you dash your foot against a stone. Or did he reproach the gospels
with a lie, saying, Let Matthew and Luke see to it; but I approached God
himself, I tempted the almighty himself at close quarters. Therefore I both
approached and tempted. But if he were the Son of God, perhaps I would never
have deigned. But he himself rather is a liar from the beginning, and if he
suborned a man from his own, as Praxeas. For this man first brought this kind
of perversity from Asia into Rome, a man restless also otherwise, besides
puffed up with the boast of martyrdom on account of only a simple and brief
weariness of prison, when even if he had given his body to be burned, he would
have profited nothing, not having the love of God, whose charismata also he
overthrew. For the same man then compelled the Roman bishop, who was already
acknowledging the prophecies of Montanus, Prisca, Maximilla, and from that
acknowledgment bringing peace to the churches of Asia and Phrygia, by
asserting false things about those prophets and their churches and by
defending the authorities of his predecessors, both to revoke the letters of
peace already sent and to cease from the purpose of receiving the charismata.
Thus Praxeas managed two businesses of the devil at Rome. He expelled prophecy
and brought in heresy. He put the Paraclete to flight and crucified the
Father. The Praxean tares had sprouted here also, sown over while many were
sleeping in the simplicity of doctrine; thereafter they were exposed, through
whom God willed, and even seemed torn up. Finally the former teacher had given
security concerning his own amendment, and the handwriting remains among the
psychics, among whom the matter was then done; from then on, silence. And we
indeed afterward the acknowledgment of the Paraclete and its defense separated
from the psychics. But those tares had everywhere then shaken out seed. So for
some time it lay hidden by hypocrisy with sly vitality, and now it has broken
out again. But it will also be rooted out again, if the Lord will, in this
voyage; if not, on their day all adulterous fruits will be gathered, and with
the rest of the scandals they will be burned with inextinguishable fire.

* Chapter 2
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h2
:END:

And so after a time the Father born and the Father suffered, God himself, the
Lord almighty, Jesus Christ is preached. But we both always and now more, as
better instructed through the Paraclete, the leader of all truth of course,
believe indeed one God, yet under this dispensation which we call οἰκονομία,
that of the one God there is also a Son, his own Word, who proceeded from him,
through whom all things were made, and without whom nothing was made. This one
sent by the Father into the virgin and born from her, man and God, Son of man
and Son of God, and surnamed Jesus Christ; this one suffered, this one dead
and buried, according to the scriptures, and raised by the Father, and taken
up into heaven sitting at the right hand of the Father, to come to judge the
living and the dead; who thereafter sent, according to his promise, from the
Father the Holy Spirit the Paraclete, the sanctifier of the faith of those who
believe in the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit. That this rule has run
from the beginning of the gospel, even before all earlier heretics, much more
before yesterday's Praxeas, both the very posteriority of all heretics and the
very novelty of yesterday's Praxeas will prove. Whereby equally against all
heresies it is already from here prejudged that whatever is first is true,
whatever is later is adulterous. But with that prescription safe, still
everywhere for the instruction and fortification of certain people a place
must be given even to retractions, or lest every perversity seem to be
condemned not examined but prejudged, especially this which thinks it
possesses mere truth, while it thinks the one God must not otherwise be
believed than if it says the same one both Father and Son and Holy Spirit; as
if even so all were not one, while all are from one, through the unity of
substance of course, and nonetheless the sacrament of the οἰκονομία is kept,
which arranges unity into trinity, directing three, Father and Son and Holy
Spirit, yet three not in status, but in grade, nor in substance, but in form,
nor in power, but in species, but of one substance and one status and one
power, because one God, from whom both those grades and forms and species are
assigned in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. How
they admit number without division the treatises proceeding will demonstrate.

* Chapter 3
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3
:END:

For all the simple, not to say the unwise and unlearned, which is always the
greater part of believers, since the rule of faith itself transfers from the
many gods of the age to the one and true God, not understanding that he must
be believed one indeed, but with his own οἰκονομία, they take fright at the
οἰκονομία. They presume the number and disposition of the trinity to be a
division of the unity, when unity deriving the trinity from itself is not
destroyed by it, but is administered. And so they already toss it about that
two and three are preached by us, but presume themselves worshipers of one
God, as if unity irrationally collected did not also make a heresy, and
trinity rationally expended did not constitute the truth. We hold the
monarchy, they say. And so even the Latins express the very sound vocally, and
so moderately that you would think they understand the monarchy as well as
they enunciate it. But the Latins are eager to sound monarchy, even the Greeks
are unwilling to understand οἰκονομία. But I, if I have plucked anything of
either tongue, know that monarchy signifies nothing other than a singular and
unique empire; yet not to prescribe that a monarchy, because it is of one,
therefore he whose it is either has no son, or has made himself a son to
himself, or does not administer his monarchy through whom he will. Nay I say
no domination is so of one itself, so singular, so a monarchy, that it is not
also administered through other nearest persons, whom it itself has provided
as officials for itself. But if he whose monarchy it is also has a son, it is
not at once divided and ceases to be a monarchy, if a partner of it is also
taken up, even a son, but it is nonetheless principally his from whom it is
communicated into the son, and while it is his, it is nonetheless a monarchy,
which is contained by two so unique. Therefore if even the divine monarchy is
administered through so many legions and armies of angels, as it is written:
Thousands of hundreds of hundreds of thousands stood by him, and thousands of
hundreds of thousands appeared to him, and he has not therefore ceased to be
of one, so that it ceases to be a monarchy, because it is procured through so
many thousands of powers: what sort of thing is it that God should seem to
suffer division and dispersion in the Son and in the Holy Spirit, allotted
second and third place, so consorts of the Father's substance, which he does
not suffer in so great a number of angels? Do you count members and pledges
and instruments born from the Father's substance, and the very force and whole
census of the monarchy, as an overthrow of it? Not rightly. I prefer that you
exercise yourself toward the sense of the thing rather than toward the sound
of the word. For that is to be understood by you as the overthrow of the
monarchy, when another domination of its own condition and proper status and
thereby a rival is brought in over it, when another god is brought in against
the Creator, with Marcion, with several, according to the Valentini and
Prodici; then unto the overthrow of the monarchy, when unto the destruction of
the Creator.

* Chapter 4
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4
:END:

But I who do not derive the Son from elsewhere, but from the substance of the
Father, doing nothing without the Father's will, having obtained all power
from the Father, how can I destroy the monarchy from the faith, which I keep
in the Son as delivered by the Father to the Son? Let this be said by me also
for the third grade, because I think the Spirit not from elsewhere than from
the Father through the Son. See therefore lest you rather destroy the
monarchy, who overturn its disposition and dispensation constituted in as many
names as God willed. But it so remains in its own status, although a trinity
is brought in, that it even has to be restored to the Father by the Son, since
the apostle writes of the last end: When he shall have delivered the kingdom
to God and the Father. For he must reign until God puts his enemies under his
feet, namely according to the psalm: Sit at my right hand, until I put your
enemies as a footstool of your feet. But when all things shall have been
subjected to him except him who subjected all things to him, then he himself
also will be subjected to him who subjected all things to him, that God may be
all in all. We see therefore that the Son does not hinder the monarchy, even
if today it is with the Son, because it is also in its own status with the
Son, and with its own status it will be restored to the Father by the Son. So
no one will destroy it under this name, if he admits the Son, to whom it is
established both that it was delivered by the Father and by whom it is someday
to be restored to the Father. By this one chapter of the spostolic epistle we
could already have shown that the Father and the Son are two, besides from the
names of Father and Son; also from this, that he who delivered the kingdom and
he to whom he delivered it, likewise he who subjected and he to whom he
subjected, must be two.

* Chapter 5
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5
:END:

But because they want the two to be one, so that the same is held Father and
Son, the whole concerning the Son must also be examined, whether he is, and
who he is, and how he is, and so the thing itself will claim its own form with
the scriptures and their interpretations patronizing. Some say even Genesis in
the Hebrew so begins: In the beginning God made for himself a Son. That this
may not be firm, other arguments lead me from God's own disposition, in which
he was before the constitution of the world up to the generation of the Son.
For before all things God was alone, himself to himself both world and place
and all things. Alone, however, because nothing else extrinsically besides
him. Yet not even then alone; for he had with him the reason which he had in
himself, his own of course. For God is rational, and reason was in him first,
and so all things from him. Which reason is his own sense. This the Greeks
call λόγον, by which word we also name speech. And therefore it is already in
the usage of our people through simplicity of interpretation to say that the
Word was in the beginning with God, when it rather befits reason to be held
more ancient, because God was not sermonal from the beginning, but rational
even before the beginning, and because the Word itself also consisting by
reason shows it prior as its own substance. Yet even so it makes no
difference. For even if God had not yet sent forth his Word, he nonetheless
had him with that very reason and in that reason within himself, silently
thinking and disposing with himself what he was soon going to say through the
Word. For thinking and disposing with his own reason he was making it Word
which he was treating by word. And that you may understand this more easily,
recognize it first from yourself, as from the image and likeness of God,
whereby you also have in yourself reason, who are a rational animal, made of
course by a rational artisan not only, but also animated from his own
substance. See, when silent you yourself converse with yourself by reason,
that this very thing is done within you, it meeting you with speech at every
motion of your thought, at every pulse of your sense. Whatever you have
thought is speech; whatever you have sensed is reason. You must speak it in
the mind, and while you speak, you suffer the Word as interlocutor, in whom is
this very reason by which thinking with him you speak, through whom speaking
you think. So there is in you in a certain way a second, the Word, through
whom you speak by thinking and through whom you think by speaking; the Word
itself is another. How much more fully then is this done in God, whose image
and likeness you also are counted, that he has in himself even by being silent
reason and in reason the Word? I can therefore not rashly have laid down in
advance that even then God before the constitution of the universe was not
alone, having in himself likewise reason and in reason the Word, whom he was
making second from himself by agitating within himself.

* Chapter 6
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h6
:END:

This force and this disposition of the divine sense is shown in the scriptures
also under the name of sophia. For what is wiser than the reason of God or the
Word? Therefore hear sophia also, as a second person established. First the
Lord created me the beginning of ways for his works, before he made the earth,
before the mountains were set; but before all the hills he genervit me,
establishing and generating in his own sense of course. Then know her standing
by from the very separation. When he was preparing, she says, the heaven, I
was present with him at the same time; and when he was making the strong
things above the winds, which are the clouds above, and when he was setting
its fountains safe, which are under heaven, I was with him compacting, I was
she in whom he rejoiced; but daily I was delighted in his person. For as soon
as God willed to put forth into their own substances and species those things
which he had disposed within himself with the reason and Word of sophia, he
first put forth the Word itself, having in itself indivisibly its own reason
and sophia, so that through him all things might be made through whom they had
been thought and disposed, nay even already made, as far as in God's sense.
For this was lacking to them, that they should also be known and held openly
in their own species and substances.

* Chapter 7
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h7
:END:

Then therefore the Word itself also takes its species and ornament, sound and
voice, when God says, Let there be light. This is the perfect nativity of the
Word, while it proceeds from God; established by him first for thought in the
name of sophia, The Lord established me the beginning of ways; then generated
for effect, When he was preparing heaven, I was present with him; thereafter
making him equal to himself from whom by proceeding he was made Son,
first-begotten, as begotten before all things, and only-begotten, as alone
begotten from God; properly from the womb of his own heart, according to what
the Father himself also testifies: My heart has belched forth a good Word. To
whom thereafter rejoicing likewise rejoicing in his person: You are my Son,
today I have begotten you, and before the morning star I have begotten you. So
also the Son from his own person professes the Father in the name of sophia:
The Lord established me the beginning of ways for his works; but before all
the hills he generated me. For if here indeed sophia seems to say she was
established by the Lord for his works and ways, but elsewhere through the Word
it is shown that all things were made and without him nothing was made, as
also again: By his Word the heavens were confirmed and by his spirit all their
forces, by that spirit of course which was in the Word, it appears that one
and the same force is now in the name of sophia, now in the appellation of
Word, which received the beginning of ways in God's work, and which confirmed
heaven, through which all things were made, and without which nothing was
made. Nor longer about this, as if he were not himself the Word even in the
name of sophia and of reason and of every divine mind and spirit, who was made
Son of God, from whom by coming forth he was generated. Therefore, you say,
you grant that the Word is some substance, constructed by the tradition of
spirit and sophia? Plainly. For you do not want him to have a substantival
being in the thing through property of substance, so that he can be seen a
thing and a certain person, and so as constituted second from God he may take
to make two, Father and Son, God and Word. For what is, you will say, a word,
except voice and sound of the mouth, and as the grammarians hand down, air
struck, intelligible to hearing, but some empty and inane and incorporeal I
know not what? But I say that nothing empty and vacant could have proceeded
from God, as not brought forth from empty and vacant, nor that that lacks
substance which proceeded from so great a substance and made so great
substances; for he himself also made the things which were made through him.
What sort of thing is it that he himself should be nothing without whom
nothing was made, that the empty should have worked solids and the vacant
fulls and the incorporeal corporals? For even if something can sometimes
become diverse from that through which it is made, yet nothing can be made
through that which is vacant and empty. Is the Word of God an empty and inane
thing, who was called Son, who himself was surnamed God? And the Word was with
God, and God was the Word. It is written: You shall not take the name of God
in vain. This certainly is he who constituted in the figure of God did not
think it robbery to be (??) equal to God. In what figure of God? in another of
course, yet not in none. For who will deny that God is a body, even if God is
spirit? For spirit is a body of its own kind in its own figure. But even if
those invisible things, whatever they are, have with God both their own body
and their own form, through which they are visible to God alone, how much more
will that which was emitted from his own substance not be sin(??) substance?
Whatever therefore the substance of the Word was, that I call a person, and
for him I claim the name of Son, and while I ag(??) the Son, I defend a second
from the Father.

* Chapter 8
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h8
:END:

This ai who shall have thought that I introduce some προβολή, that is a
bringing forth of one thing from another, which Valentinus does, producing one
Aeon after another from an Aeon, first indeed I will say to you, that truth
does not therefore not use that word and the thing and census of it because
heresy also uses it; nay heresy rather received from truth what it might
construct for its own lie. Was the Word of God brought forth or not? Fix the
step here with me. If he was brought forth, know the προβολή of truth, and let
heresy see to it, if it has imitated anything from truth. Already now it is
asked who how uses some thing and the word of it? Valentinus distinguishes and
separates his προβολάς from the author, and so places them far from him, that
an Aeon does not know the Father; finally he desires to know, and cannot, nay
is even almost swallowed and dissolved into the remaining substance. But with
us the Son alone knows the Father, and he himself has set forth the bosom of
the Father, and has heard and seen all things with the Father, and what he has
been commanded by the Father, that he also speaks; nor has he accomplished his
own will, but the Father's, which he had known from nearest, nay from the
beginning. For who knows the things that are in God except the spirit who is
in him? But the Word is constructed of spirit, and so to speak, the body of
the Word is spirit. The Word therefore is both always in the Father, as he
says, I in the Father, and always with God, as it is written: And the Word was
with God, and never separated from the Father, or other than the Father,
because, I and the Father are one. This will be the προβολή of truth, guardian
of unity, by which we say the Son was brought forth from the Father, but not
separated. For God brought forth the Word, as the Paraclete also teaches, as a
root a shrub and a fountain a river and the sun a ray. For these species also
are προβολαί of those substances from which they proceed. Nor would I hesitate
to call the Son both shrub of the root, and river of the fountain, and ray of
the sun, because every origin is a parent, and everything that is brought
forth from an origin is offspring, much more the Word of God, who even
properly received the name of Son; yet neither is the shrub distinguished from
the root, nor the river from the fountain, nor the ray from the sun, just as
neither the Word from God. Therefore according to the form of these examples I
profess that I say two, God and his Word, the Father and his Son. For both
root and shrub are two things, but joined; and fountain and river are two
species, but undivided; and sun and ray are two forms, but cohering.
Everything that proceeds from something must be second of that from which it
proceeds, yet it is not therefore soparated. But where there is a second,
there are two, and where there is a third, there are three. For the Spirit is
third from God and the Son, as fruit from the shrub is third from the root,
and a stream from the river third from the fountain, and the tip from the ray
third from the sun. Yet nothing is alienated from the matrix, from which it
draws its properties. So the trinity running down from the Father through
interwoven and connected grades both in no way obstructs the monarchy and
protects the status of the οἰκονομία.

* Chapter 9
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h9
:END:

Hold everywhere this rule which I have professed, by which I testify that the
Father and the Son and the Spirit are unseparated from one another, and so you
will recognize what is said and how. For behold I say that the Father is
another, and the Son another, and the Spirit another. Some idiot or perverse
person takes this saying badly, as if it sounded diversity and from diversity
stretched a separation of Father and Son and Spirit. But I say this of
necessity, when they contend that the same is Father and Son and Spirit,
flattering against the οἰκονομία of the monarchy, yet not by diversity the Son
another from the Father, but by distribution, nor by division another, but by
distinction, because the Father and the Son are not the same, or another from
another by measure. For the Father is the whole substance, but the Son a
derivation of the whole and a portion, as he himself professes: Because the
Father is greater than I. By whom he is also sung diminished in the psalm, a
little less than the angels. So also the Father is another from the Son, while
greater than the Son, while another who generates, another who is generated,
while another who sends, another who is sent, while another who makes, another
through whom it is made. It is well that the Lord also using this word in the
person of the Paraclete did not signify division, but disposition. For I will
ask, he says, the Father, and he will send you another advocate, the Spirit of
truth. Thus another Paraclete from himself, as we also another Son from the
Father, to show the third grade in the Paraclete, as we the second in the Son,
for the observation of the οἰκονομία. The very fact that they are called
Father and Son, is it not one thing from another? For surely all things will
be what they are called, and what they will be, that they will be called, and
the diversity of words cannot mix itself at all, because neither can that of
the things whose words they will be. Yes, yes, no, no; for what is more than
this is from evil.

* Chapter 10
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h10
:END:

So he is either Father or Son, and neither is the day the same and night, nor
the Father the same and the Son, so that both are one and each the other,
which those vainest Monarchians want. He made himself, they say, a son to
himself. Nay the Father makes a son, and the son a father. And those who are
made from one another can in no way be made for themselves from themselves, so
that the Father makes himself a son to himself, and the Son presents himself a
father to himself. What God has instituted, he himself also keeps. The Father
must have a son, that he may be a father, and the Son a father, that he may be
a son. But it is one thing to have, another to be. For example, that I may be
a husband, I must have a wife, I will not be a wife to myself. So also, that I
may be a father, I have a son, I will not be a son to myself, and that I may
be a son, I have a father, I will not be a father to myself. For the things
which make me if I shall have had them, then I will be a father, if I have a
son, I will be a son, if a father. Further if I myself shall be one of them, I
no longer have what I myself shall be, neither a father, because I myself
shall be the father, nor a son, because I myself shall be the son. But insofar
as I must have one of these, be the other, so far, if I shall have been both,
I will not be the one, while I do not have the other. For if I myself shall be
the son, who am also the father, I no longer have a son, but I myself am the
son. But by not having a son, while I myself am the son, how shall I be a
father? For I ought to have a son, that I may be a father. Therefore I am not
a son, because I do not have a father, who makes a son. Equally if I myself am
the father, who am also the son, I no longer have a father, but I myself am
the father. But by not having a father, while I myself am the father, how
shall I be a son? For I ought to have a father, that I may be a son. Therefore
I will not be a father, because I do not have a son, who makes a father. This
will be the whole ingenuity of the devil, to shut out one from the other,
while concluding both into one under favor of the monarchy he makes neither to
be had, so that both the Father is not, who namely does not have a son, and
the Son is not, who equally does not have a father; for while he is Father, he
will not be Son. Thus they hold the monarchy who contain neither Father nor
Son. But nothing is difficult for God. Who does not know this? And who is
ignorant that the impossible with the age are possible with God? And God has
chosen the foolish things of the world, that he may confound wisdom. We read
all things. Therefore, they say, it was not difficult for God to make himself
both Father and Son against the form handed down to human affairs. For it was
not difficult for God that a barren woman should bear contrary to nature, as
neither a virgin. Plainly nothing is difficult for God. But if we use this
sentence so abruptly in our presumptions, we shall be able to invent anything
we like about God, as if he has done it, because he could do it. But not,
because he can do all things, therefore it must also be believed that he has
done even what he has not done. But whether he has done it must be asked. God
could, if he had willed, have equipped man with wings for flying, which he
also granted to kites; yet not, because he could, did he at once also do it.
He could also have extinguished Praxeas and all heretics at once; yet not,
because he could, did he extinguish them. For there ought to be both kites and
heretics, there ought also that the Father be crucified. By this reason there
will be something even difficult for God, namely whatever he has not done, not
because he could not, but because he would not. For for God to be able is to
will, and not to be able is to be unwilling, but what he willed, he both could
and has shown. Therefore because, if he had willed to make himself a son to
himself, he could, and because, if he could, he did, then you will prove that
he both could and willed, if you have proved that he did.

* Chapter 11
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h11
:END:

But you will have to prove as openly from the scriptures as we prove that he
made his Word a son for himself. For if he names a Son, but a son will be no
other than he who came forth from him, but the Word came forth from him, this
erti(??)son, not he himself from whom he came forth. For he himself did not
come forth from himself. Further you who say the same Father and lily, you
make the same both to have brought forth from himself, and to have come forth
what God is. If he could have done it, yet he did not do it. Or exhibit a
proof which I demand like mine, that is that the scriptures show the same Son
and Father as with us Father and Son are distinctly demonstrated: distinctly,
I say, not dividedly; as I bring forth said by God, My heart has belched forth
a good Word, equally you against it should oppose that God said somewhere, My
heart has belched forth me a good Word, so that he himself is both he who
belched and what he belched, and he himself who brought forth and who was
brought forth, if he himself is both Word and God. Behold I propose that the
Father said to the Son, You are my Son, today I have generated you. If you
wish me to believe that he himself is Father and Son, show it so pronounced
elsewhere: The Lord said to himself, I am my Son, today I have generated me;
likewise also, Before the morning star I have generated me; and, The Lord I
have established me the beginning of ways for my works, but before all the
hills I have generated me, and if there are any others in this manner. But
whom was God the Lord of the universe fearing so to pronounce, if the thing
was so? Or was he fearing that he would not be believed, if he had simply
pronounced himself both Father and Son? Yet one thing he feared, to lie; but
he feared himself and his own truth. And therefore believing God true I know
that he has not otherwise pronounced than he disposed, nor otherwise disposed
than he pronounced. You further would make him a liar, and deceitful and a
deceiver of this faith, if, when he himself was a son to himself, he was

in flesh, while this also must be asked, how the Word was made flesh, whether
as if transfigured into flesh, or having put on flesh. Nay having put on. For
the rest God must be believed unchangeable and unformable, as eternal. But
transfiguration is an interemption of the former. For everything whatsoever is
transfigured into another, ceases to be what it had been, and begins to be
what it was not. But God neither ceases to be nor can be another. But the Word
God and the Word of the Lord remains unto the age, by persevering of course in
his own form. Whom if it does not take to be configured, it is consequent that

and seen and handled through flesh, because the rest also demand to be so

made flesh, there will already be one substance Jesus from two, from flesh and
spirit, a certain mixture, as electrum from gold and silver, and he begins to
be neither gold, that is spirit, nor silver, that is flesh, while one is

be neither God; for the Word has ceased to be, who was made flesh: nor man

neither; a third far other is than either. But we find him directly set forth
both God and man, this very psalm suggesting, Since God man was born in her,
