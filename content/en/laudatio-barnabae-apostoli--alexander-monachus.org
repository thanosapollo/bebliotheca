* Encomium on Barnabas the Apostle
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Encomium of Alexander the monk on Saint Barnabas the Apostle, written at the
urging of the priest and key-keeper of his venerable church, in which is also
related the manner of the revelation of his holy relics.

Your paternal love of learning, O best of fathers and most proven of ascetics,
has set before us who are poorest a very great subject of discourse; for it
has proposed to us for praise the divine and truly thrice-blessed man, most
distinguished among the apostles, the son of consolation and luminary of the
inhabited world, Barnabas the much-hymned. But I, setting my own lack of
training against this excessive subject, long declined obedience, dreading the
undertaking; for what speech can attain to apostolic perfection? For not only
to me has such an attempt been made impossible, but it is hard even for the
many; for I think that all human nature falls short of narrating the good
deeds of the great apostle Barnabas; and if anyone should attempt to say
something about him, he will fall far short of the measure of the worth of the
things, even if he happen to be very wise and learned and most powerful in
words, and he will be found plunging into a depth of wonders. Each of the just
has received from the Holy Spirit some one gift or another, but the apostles
received the whole treasure of the gifts together and displayed every virtue.
How then shall I, the wretched one, sunk under a myriad of passions, be able
to swim through the apostolic sea? For I am weak-voiced, to speak
scripturally, and slow of tongue, and I am not eloquent, that I might narrate
the superhuman virtues of the divine apostle Barnabas; for he accomplished all
the virtues together with precision, as no one else accomplished any of the
rest. For this reason I long deferred obedience, knowing my own unworthiness
as not sufficient for the narration of the man's most glorious achievements.
But having recently remembered the God-inspired Scripture, which says that a
disobedient son shall be in destruction, but the obedient shall be outside of
it, I barely with much fear raised myself to the fulfillment of your command,
considering it better to be charged with deficiency than to be condemned for
complete ingratitude. Only contend with me in your prayers that the all-holy
Spirit become a helper of my speech; for what is said by us who are nothing
will, I think, please even the one being praised, as once the offering of the
two mites of the poor widow pleased his Master; for it is not the quantity of
what is given that is acceptable to God, but what is offered with a good mind,
whether small or much, is holy. In praising Barnabas I shall praise the sacred
priesthood of all the apostles together; for those whose calling is one and
whose glory is the same, and whose rank is one and whose dignity is the same,
and whose contests are equal and whose crowns do not differ, and whose way of
life is common, of these the prizes too are of equal honor. Come then, if it
seems good, drawing the divine Barnabas into the midst through speech, let us
weave praises for him as far as is possible; for I am a most ardent lover of
the man; and I know and am persuaded that to you too he is most sweet even
when simply

named, the apostle. Let us then honor him according to our power; for to speak
according to power is a proof of goodwill; but to speak according to worth let
us concede that it has the victory over every speech. Let then the great among
apostles, Barnabas, be hymned by us the least; let the son of consolation be
praised by all who believe in Christ; let him who was honored by the Father
and called by the Son and perfected of the Holy Spirit be glorified by all
creation. Barnabas, the great orator of the church, the trumpet of the
evangelical preaching, the voice of the Only-begotten, the lyre of the Spirit,
the plectrum of grace. Barnabas, the mighty soldier of the wars for Christ,
who in the body put to flight the bodiless tyrant with all his apostate army,
the pious trainer of good lessons, the unerring guide of the flock of Christ,
the rational paradise of God, who possesses in himself the flourishing plants
of every virtue, the many-flowered shoot of faith, the most fragrant rose of
love, the unfading flower of hope, the most fragrant ear of grace, the
fruitful branch of the life-giving vine, the honey-dropping cluster of
immortality, the unshaken breakwater of patience, the cross-bearing hoplite of
self-control, the fearless general against the Christ-killers, the heaven-high
offshoot of the crucified Christ. Barnabas, the son of consolation, the
teacher of piety, the pillar and roof of the faith, the unshaken tower, the
unbreakable foundation, the indissoluble base, the immovable rock, the
calm-bringing harbor of those storm-tossed, the consolation of all who are
afflicted, the faithful and prudent steward, the excellent architect who
accomplished the ethereal course and an angelic life on earth, the protector
of the churches, the helper of the poor and the provider and nourisher of the
needy, the comfort of widows and most careful father of orphans. Barnabas, the
treasury of the mysteries of Christ, the steward of the orthodox doctrines of
the holy church of God, the untrafficking physician of the sick, the unfailing
gladness of the healthy, the watchful guardian of the flock of Christ, who
walks on earth and has his citizenship in the heavens, who cried out in the
world the doctrines of piety and received in the heavens eternal crowns, the
God-bearing guide of the nations and God-blessed herald of the churches, the
meadow of the fragrance of Christ, the rose-garden of the heaven-breathed
virtues, the fruitful field of the gifts of Christ, who on earth pursued the
incorruptible way of life, living better on earth as the bodiless angels do in
heaven, who shone in ascesis and advanced in contemplations and was magnified
in wonders, the precious pearl of virginity, the choice beryl of chastity, the
pure topaz of temperance, who has Christ speaking in himself, the vessel of
election of God, who left the world and counted all things in it as refuse,
that he might gain Christ alone, the King of the ages, who took up his own
cross without hesitation and followed Christ eagerly as a true disciple, the
putter-to-flight of demons and wounder of the devil, who walked the
four-cornered inhabited world with unwearied journey as a good and
master-loving minister, and brought all the nations through the gospel to the
faith of Christ, who prepared himself as a dwelling of the consubstantial
Trinity and became a temple of the God over all. Barnabas, the noble ornament
of the Cypriots and unconquerable champion of the inhabited world, who loved
Christ exceedingly and laid down his soul for him each day and reigned with
him unto ages of ages. Speech has grown weary, longing to lift this divine and
thrice-desired Barnabas the

apostle with praises, and it has not yet touched the preface; for the wondrous
man is unapproachable to praises. Therefore leaving the discourse of encomia
as unattainable, I shall set before your holiness a few of the things that
have come down to us concerning his life and consummation, from the Stromateus
and from other ancient writings, and thus we shall put an end to the
narrative, yielding to the God-inspired Scripture to crown magnificently the
head of the celebrated man; for it says: "And Barnabas was a good man and full
of the Holy Spirit and of faith." What could ever be equal or near to this?
This thrice-blessed man then sprang from the blessed tribe of Levi, from which
were Moses and Aaron, the great prophets of God and first of the people of
Kohath, tracing his race from the kindred of Samuel the prophet. And his
ancestors, because of a circumstance of wars, came upon the land of the
Cypriots and dwelt in it kindly; and they were devout according to the law and
very rich, whence also in Jerusalem they had sufficient property and a most
beautiful field near the city, adorned not only with all kinds of
fruit-bearing plants, but also most conspicuous by the size of its buildings;
for from the time they heard Isaiah the prophet saying: "Blessed is he who has
seed in Zion and kinsmen in Jerusalem," the children of the Hebrews, taking
the prophecy in a fleshly sense, each of those who were well-off hastened to
have a possession in Jerusalem. When this just man was born in Cyprus, as his
parents saw that he was well-favored to God, they immediately named him
Joseph, honoring the child with the name of the patriarch; and the nobility of
his manners ran together with the title of the name; for Joseph is interpreted
addition of God; for the just man received an addition of grace from God, that
he might arrive at apostolic perfection; again Joseph is interpreted glory of
God; for he became through his excellent way of life a glory of God; and let
no one think the saying to be hyperbole, but let him attend that it is of the
divine Scripture; for Paul says: "A man ought not to cover his head, being
image and glory of God." If then the vessel of election called the common man
image and glory of God, what would one say concerning the man most perfect
according to God? When Barnabas grew and became a boy, his parents brought him
up to Jerusalem and handed him over to learn the law accurately and the
prophets at the feet of Gamaliel; and he had Paul as fellow-student, who was
then named Saul. And Barnabas advanced daily both in learning and in every
virtue; he had not yet however been enrolled in the course of the Levites
because of the deficiency of his age; for he was still an ephebe. And he did
not depart from the temple, serving with fasts and prayers night and day; and
he recited the law and the remaining scriptures so that he had no need of
reminder from the letters; and he held quiet so dear, as the mother of
temperance; and he fled, loathing harmful conversations, being a pure and
unmixed and undefiled image; and he was illustrious to all through virtue. At
that time it happened that the Lord came to Jerusalem and healed the paralytic
at the Sheep-pool, and worked many other signs and wonders in the temple.
Seeing these things the blessed man was astonished and immediately coming
forward he fell at his feet and begged to be blessed by him. And Christ who
searches hearts, accepting his faith, received him kindly and granted him his
divine converse; and he was kindled still more toward love of the Lord. And
quickly reaching the house of Mary, the mother of John who is called Mark, who
was said to be his aunt, wherefore they also called Mark the nephew of
Barnabas, he said to her: "Come," he said, "O woman, see the things which

our fathers desired to see; for behold, a certain Jesus, a prophet from
Nazareth of Galilee, is in the temple, working wonders magnificently, and as
it seems to the many, he is the Messiah who is to come." And the wondrous
woman hearing these things and leaving what was in her hands, reached the
temple of God, and seeing the Lord and master of the temple, she fell at his
feet, begging and saying: "Lord, if I have found favor before you, come into
the house of your servant and bless by your entrance your household." And the
Lord nodded to the request; and she received him when he came, rejoicing, into
her upper room. From that day then, whenever the Lord came to Jerusalem, he
rested there with his disciples, there he made the Passover with his
disciples, there he initiated the disciples through the partaking of the
ineffable mysteries. For a word has come to us from elders that the one
carrying the jar of water, whom the Lord commanded the disciples to follow,
was Mark, the son of this blessed Mary; and the Lord said "to such a one" by
economy, as the fathers say, interpreting this passage, teaching us through
the riddle that with everyone who prepares himself the Lord lodges. In that
upper room then the Lord made the Passover; in it he appeared to those about
Thomas, risen from the dead; there after the ascension the disciples went up,
coming from the Mount of Olives with the remaining brothers, being in number
about one hundred and twenty, among whom were Barnabas and Mark; there the
Holy Spirit came down in fiery tongues upon the disciples on the day of
Pentecost; there is now established the great and most holy Zion, the mother
of all the churches. Then Barnabas followed the Lord, returning from Jerusalem
to Galilee; and when many were coming to the Lord from everywhere and
believing in him, then he said to the disciples: "The harvest is much, but the
laborers are few." Then he appointed the seventy disciples, of whom the first
and leader and chief was the great Barnabas. But let no one, hearing that the
apostles laid this title upon him, think unlearnedly that he received this
title without divine inspiration; for Peter laid this name upon him, through a
revelation of the Holy Spirit, he who also through a revelation of the Father
received the theology of the Son; and just as James and John were called sons
of thunder from their virtue, so also Barnabas was called son of consolation
from his virtue, having become a consolation of all through excess of
holiness. And hearing the Lord teaching and saying: "Sell your possessions and
give alms; make for yourselves purses that do not grow old, a treasure
unfailing in the heavens," delaying nothing, immediately selling all the
property left to him, which was of great value, by his parents (for they had
departed this life), he distributed it to those in need, leaving for himself
only that field for his own sustenance. After the passion and the resurrection
and the ascension of the Lord and the coming of the Holy Spirit, the divine
Barnabas being kindled still more with love toward the Lord and selling even
that field and taking sufficient money, bringing it all laid it at the feet of
the apostles, leaving nothing at all of it for himself, stirring all the
disciples to like virtue by his own example. And he spoke and disputed with
Saul, wishing to bring him to faith in the Lord. But Saul, ostensibly leaning
on the exact way of life according to the law, mocked Barnabas as having been
deceived, and blasphemed the Lord, calling him son of the carpenter and a
private man and

a rustic and one who died a violent death. But when he saw the mighty works of
the wonders done through the apostles and the multitude of the people being
added daily to the word of faith, he was bitten in his soul. And attacking
with the Libertines and Cyrenians and Alexandrians the great orator of the
church, Stephen, and not being able to withstand the wisdom and the Spirit by
which he spoke, he turned to madness; and becoming full of wrath, he raised
against him the disorderly of the people, and having killed him, he raised a
great persecution against the church in Jerusalem. But as he was going thus in
disorder toward Damascus for the harming of the faithful, the Lord tripping
him up cast him on his face; and falling to the earth he recognized whom he
was persecuting, and being blinded in his sight he looked wholly up to the
height of heaven. And returning to Jerusalem he sought to join the disciples,
and all fled from him, fearing his great cruelty. But the great Barnabas,
meeting him, said: "How long, Saul, are you Saul? Why do you so boldly
persecute the benefactor? Cease destroying the fearful mystery long ago cried
out by the prophets and revealed in our times for our salvation." Hearing
these things Saul fell at the feet of Barnabas, crying with many tears and
saying: "Forgive me, guide of the light and teacher of the truth; for I have
known by the trial of your words the truth; for him whom I in blaspheming
called son of the carpenter, I now confess to be the Only-begotten Son of the
living God, consubstantial and of the same glory and of the same throne,
co-eternal and without beginning together; who being the radiance of the glory
and the imprint of the hypostasis of the invisible God, in these last days,
for us and for our salvation, emptied himself, taking the form of a servant,
that is a perfect man from the holy virgin and Theotokos Mary, without
confusion, without change, without division, without separation; and being
found in fashion as a man, he humbled himself, becoming obedient unto death,
even death of a cross; who also rose from the dead on the third day and
appeared to you, his apostles, and was taken up into the heavens and sat at
the right hand of the Father and comes again with glory to judge the living
and the dead, and of his kingdom there shall be no end." Hearing these things
the divine Barnabas from the blasphemer and persecutor was astonished and from
joy his face became as a morning flower; and embracing him and kissing him, he
said: "Who taught you, Saul, to utter such God-inspired words? Or who
persuaded you to confess Jesus the Nazarene as Son of God? Or whence did you
learn such accuracy of heavenly doctrines?" And he, bowing and weeping, said
with much contrition: "The Lord Jesus himself, often blasphemed and persecuted
by me the sinner, taught me all these things; for as if to the abortion he
appeared also to me, and I still have his divine and sweet voice ringing in my
ears; for surpassing all goodness, he said to me, as I lay pitiably on my
face, rather making a defense than accusing: 'Saul, Saul, why do you persecute
me?' And I with shuddering and fear answered: 'Who are you, Lord?' And the
Lord with much gentleness and compassion said to me: 'I am Jesus the Nazarene,
whom you persecute.' And I, astonished at his unspeakable forbearance, begged
him, saying: 'What shall I do, Lord?' And he immediately instructed me in all
these things and still more than these." Then the great Barnabas taking his
hand led him to the apostles, saying: "Why do you flee the shepherd, supposing
him to be a wolf? Why do you chase the helmsman as a pirate? Why do you loathe
the champion as a traitor? Why do you send away the bridesman as a robber of
the bridal chamber? For the church is a spiritual bridal chamber, whose
shepherd and helmsman and

champion the Lord ordained through himself." Then Paul related to them what
had happened to him on the road, and that he had seen the Lord and that he had
spoken to him, and how in Damascus he had spoken boldly in the name of the
Lord; and he was teaching with them the word of the Lord in Jerusalem. And he
was very heavy to the Jews, because he who yesterday was persecuting Jesus
today preaches him Son of God, and they took counsel to kill him. And the
apostles learning this sent him to preach in his own country. And of those
scattered at the affliction of Stephen, coming to Antioch and evangelizing the
Lord Jesus, it was heard about them in Jerusalem. Then the apostles sent the
blessed Barnabas, as great and powerful, to the most holy church there to
shepherd the flock of Christ; who arriving contributed much to those who had
believed, and through his God-inspired teaching a sufficient people was added
to the Lord. From there, led by the Holy Spirit, going out, he went through
evangelizing all the cities and countries, until he came to the greatest Rome;
for he before any other of the Lord's disciples preached in Rome the gospel of
Christ. And when many believed and honored him exceedingly, casting off the
glory of men, fleeing secretly, he went out of Rome; for again this blessed
man surpassed all the men of that time in humility, having accomplished it to
the utmost; and this is clear to all from the history concerning him. For
though the God-inspired Scripture everywhere names him first, he himself
yielding the first places to those about him, embraced the second rank,
accurately imitating the Lord who said: "Learn from me that I am meek and
humble in heart." And Barnabas reaching Alexandria toward Egypt and speaking
there the word of God, went out, going through in order all the cities, until
he came to Jerusalem. From there going out again, he came down to Antioch and
seeing the grace of God and the church multiplied, he rejoiced greatly. Then
he went out to Tarsus, seeking Paul, and finding him, he brought him to
Antioch; and having spent a whole year there and discipling a sufficient
people and first calling the disciples Christians there, and taking what was
for distribution to the poor from the church, they went up again to Jerusalem
after fourteen years of the saving passion, as Paul writes, saying: "After
fourteen years I went up to Jerusalem with Barnabas." And having fulfilled the
ministry and received the right hand from the apostles, that they should
preach to the nations, and those about Peter to the circumcision, they came
down to Antioch, having with them Mark as attendant. And being sent out from
Antioch by the Holy Spirit, they came to Cyprus; and having traveled the whole
island from Salamis to Paphos, evangelizing and working wonders, when they
also blinded Elymas and enlightened the proconsul, and having discipled
sufficient people, then those about Barnabas putting to sea from Cyprus came
to Pamphylia; and Mark seeing the apostles going together toward the dangers
for the gospel, and that where they were about to be honored, leaving it, they
went toward the war against the unbelievers, shrinking at the terrible things,
being a youth, cowardly and incomplete toward contempt of death, leaving the
apostles, returned to Jerusalem to his own mother. And when the apostles
Barnabas and Paul fulfilled the work to which they were called, having endured
countless contests for Christ, they came to Antioch; from where they were sent
by the grace of God to the nations. And there was need for them to go up again
to Jerusalem to the apostles about Peter, because of the false apostles who
were teaching the disciples

to be circumcised and to keep the law. And Mark seeing them honored by all and
that after so many outrages and dangers they were strong and whole, condemning
his own cowardice, wept bitterly; and he was ashamed to approach Paul, but he
approached Barnabas with tears and fell at his feet, begging to receive
forgiveness for the past and promising prayer for security of the future and
saying that he is ready to endure every form of death for the name of our Lord
Jesus Christ. And this great man in virtues, Barnabas, being bent by the many
tears, exhorted him to cease from weeping, saying: "Let the will of the Lord
be done. Only be you ready to fulfill your promises." And taking in writing
the things decreed by the apostles in Jerusalem, they came down to Antioch and
were rejoicing with the brothers; and Mark followed Barnabas, not with
boldness. After these things it seemed good to those about Barnabas and Paul
to go through all the cities and visit the brothers. Then Barnabas coming
forward exhorted Paul that Mark should become their fellow-traveler, being
ready to contend even unto death for the faith in Christ; but Paul in turn
exhorted that this man not be taken along with them. From this came a pretext
for them to be separated from one another, the Lord arranging this for what
was beneficial; for God was about to appoint Mark a shepherd and teacher of
peoples and nations. But let no one foolishly take toward his own passion what
is said in the sacred history of the Acts concerning Mark, that there was a
paroxysm; for the apostles of Christ were not carried out into a passion of
anger and wrath, far be it; for how could they, who crucified the flesh with
the passions and the desires, who cry with a great voice to all who believe in
Christ and say: "Let all bitterness and wrath and anger and clamor and
blasphemy be taken away from you with all malice." For we have known from the
divine Scripture a difference of paroxysms; for it says: "And let us consider
one another unto a paroxysm of love and good works." There was then a paroxysm
for the apostles, that is a zeal of God. For Paul sought exactness, befitting
apostolic perfection, while Barnabas honored the humane; taking Mark then, he
sailed to Cyprus, and going through the whole and discipling a sufficient
people, he came to Salamis; and there he spent time working wonders and
preaching the kingdom of God, and a great crowd was added to the Lord. And he
reasoned with the Jews in the synagogue every sabbath, showing from the
scriptures that the Christ is Jesus. And all revered him because of the divine
grace poured around his face; for his form was angelic and his appearance
ascetic; and he was meeting-browed, having cheerful eyes, not looking grimly,
but nodding down piously; a solemn mouth and seemly lips, dripping a sweetness
of honey (for he never uttered anything beyond what was needed); a gait
composed and without vainglory, and in a word the apostle Barnabas was wholly
throughout a pure pillar of Christ, flashing forth every virtue. And while he
remained in the city of the Salaminians and taught the word of God, Jews came
from Syria and contradicted the things said by him, blaspheming; and they
raised a crowd against him, saying that he says nothing true, but also that
the Jesus he speaks of was a certain deceiver and opponent of God, setting
aside the law and the prophets and the sabbath; and they were seeking an
opportunity to kill him. And the holy apostle of Christ, Barnabas, gathering
all the brothers, said to them: "You know how I was with you the whole time,
admonishing each one and exhorting to remain in the grace and in the faith of
our Lord

Jesus Christ, and to keep his commandments and do them and to abstain from
every wicked deed; for we must all stand before the judgment seat of Christ,
that each may receive the things through the body, according to what he has
done, whether good or bad; for the fashion of this world is passing away and
the Lord is about to come from heaven to judge the living and the dead. Do not
then be negligent, knowing that in what hour you do not think, our Lord comes.
Suffer hardship then and establish your hearts with hope, because the coming
of the Lord has drawn near. Remember how many things I have spoken to you,
saying that the things of the present life are of short time, whether the good
or the painful, and all will quickly pass away, but of the age to come all
things alike are eternal and without end; for neither will the kingdom of the
heavens ever pass away, nor does the judgment have an end, but it always
remains, punishing the sinners with immortal and unceasing punishments. Strive
then to be found blameless and spotless in that day, that you may not fall
into that Gehenna which is immortal and endless. Remember how many signs and
wonders God did among you through me, his servant, and pray for me; for I am
already being poured out and the time of my departure has come, as our Lord
Jesus Christ has made known to me. I have fought the good fight, I have
finished the course, I have kept the faith, now there is laid up for me the
crown of righteousness, not only for me but for all who contend through his
name." And having said these things, he prayed with them all; and there was
sufficient weeping of all, the apostle having said that the time of my
departure has come. And Barnabas taking bread and a cup, and performing all
the liturgy, partook with the brothers of the eucharist of the mysteries. And
after these things taking Mark and withdrawing privately, he said to him: "On
this day I must be perfected into the hands of the disobedient Jews; but you
going out of the city toward the west will find my body; and having buried
this, go out from Cyprus and go to Paul and be with him, until the Lord
arranges the things concerning you; for your name is about to be magnified in
the whole inhabited world." After these things Barnabas entered the synagogue
and taught the Jews, persuading them concerning the Lord Jesus, that this is
the Christ the Son of the living God. And the Jews from Syria being filled
with wrath, rising up laid hands on him and put him in a dark house in the
synagogue until deep evening; and bringing him out and torturing him
sufficiently, they led him by night outside the city and there the lawless
ones stoned him; and lighting a great pyre, they threw the blessed man there
so that not even a relic of him should be found. But by the providence of God
the body of the apostle remained unharmed and the pyre injured it in nothing.
And Mark according to the things commanded him going out of the city toward
the west with certain brothers secretly, they brought together the relic of
Saint Barnabas and burying it in a cave about five stades from the city, they
withdrew, making a great lamentation over him. And there was at that time a
great persecution against the church in Salamis, and all were scattered one
elsewhere; and thereafter the tomb of the holy apostle Barnabas became
unknown. And Mark sailing from the land of the Cypriots came to Paul at
Ephesus and announced to him concerning the consummation of Barnabas; and he
hearing wept with a great weeping over him, and he kept Mark with himself.
After these things Peter, departing for Rome according to a revelation of God,
took Mark with him, in a manner making him his child; there he composed the
evangelical history; which Peter reading and

being pleased, knew it to be God-inspired; and ordaining Mark, he sent him as
most sufficient to Alexandria toward Egypt and Libya and the Pentapolis; who
arriving evangelized to them Christ Jesus; and a great number of those who
believed turned to the Lord. And having spent nine years teaching among them
the word of God, he was perfected by martyrdom and was laid in Alexandria.
After a long time, Christianity having been spread and Christian emperors
having reigned over the empire of the Romans, God worked no ordinary powers in
the place where the relic of the holy apostle and noble martyr Barnabas was
laid; for many of those who had unclean spirits, passing through the place,
crying with a great voice, came out, and many paralyzed and lame and held by
various diseases and torments, coming, slept at the place, who were all
healed; and there was great joy in the city of the Salaminians; and that it
was some divine power working in the place they knew, but what was the cause
of so great an unstinting grace they did not know; and the natives named that
spot the Place of Health. And when the blessed Marcian laid down the rule, Leo
of divine lot received the empire; this man had a son-in-law, one Zeno by
name, an Isaurian by race, who also reigned after him. At that time there was
found in the most holy monastery of the Acoemetae a certain devil of a monk,
as Judas among apostles, named Peter, by trade a fuller; and this man, turning
away from the holy synod at Chalcedon, championed the doctrines of the
Eutychianists. Him those of that holy monastery, as a destroyer and corrupter
and enemy of the apostolic doctrines, drove from the monastery; and he
reaching Constantinople emulated the life of flatterers, going about from
house to house and stuffing his belly; and finding some of those in office who
were of his abominable heresy, he attached himself to them and through them
becomes known to the emperor's son-in-law, who was then a patrician and count
of the excubitors; and putting on a face of piety, he was with him

saying with deceit: "If the bishop of this city is not succeeded, it is
impossible for the people to keep quiet." at the same time also arranging for
him a great quantity of gold, if he should obtain what was asked; for he also
revealed to him the hidden things of his own purpose. Then the Fuller
persuades those who were sick with the same things as he and some theatrical
people and other wicked men of the populace, and he reports to the emperor,
lying all manner of terrible things against the bishop; but his cleverness
profited nothing, the emperor shielding the apostolic doctrines. And when he
who left the things here was transferred to the unaging kingdom, Zeno becomes
successor of the empire. And immediately the aforementioned of Antioch brought
a petition to the emperor, asking for the Fuller as bishop; which indeed
happened, the gold persuading all those of the imperial court to advocacy for
him. Immediately then together with the ordination he anathematized publicly
the holy synod at Chalcedon; and wishing to please the Apollinarists, who were
sick with theopaschism, he invented a most wicked innovation of saying in the
Trisagion at the end of the hymn: "Who was crucified for us." The holy bishops
and our fathers learning these things, were stirred up nobly against his evil
doctrine; and first they attempted through hortatory letters

to call him back from the pit of impiety, but when they saw him contradicting
and rather growing bold against the orthodox faith, then they pronounced
against him and all the bishops throughout the inhabited world anathematized
him. And having come to this point of the discourse, I would gladly ask those
of our own who have accepted this innovation without examination, by
simplicity of reasoning and not by wickedness of mind. For what reason, my
brothers, leaving in this part the orthodox teaching of the fathers, have you
accepted the innovation invented by the heretics? It was necessary to
understand that our holy fathers did not, following cleverly devised myths,
hand down to us to sing this hymn, but from a more divine revelation, revealed
not to one and a second only, nor in some secret place, but at midday, to all
the Christ-loving people of the queen city of the Constantinopolitans, in the
time of our holy thrice-blessed father and bishop Proclus, the most proven
among teachers. And everything revealed to us from God by divine grace is
altogether without deficiency and all-perfect unto salvation, not needing
addition or subtraction from human reasonings according to what is said in the
God-inspired Scripture: "The word which I command you today, keep exceedingly
to do it, you shall not add upon it, nor take away from it." For in truth to
add something to the divine oracles or to take away is unsafe and dangerous;
for we are not correctors of God, but subjects. And there is much to say about
this to our own people and to show that it is extreme idiocy to accept the
innovation of the heretic, even if they do not think the things of the
heretic. Thus then Peter the Fuller being anathematized, as has been said, by
all the bishops and Zeno having fled from the empire because of the uprising
of Basiliscus, went as a fugitive to unknown places. And when Zeno returned to
his own empire, seeking Peter the Fuller, he restored him as bishop of
Antioch, the anathemas against him in no way being loosed, and he banished the
blessed Calandion the patriarch to Oasis. The Fuller having taken the throne
by force, being now desperate and having no hope, used the tyranny without
fear, or rather without God, killing and confiscating and banishing all who
did not wish to communicate in his impiety. But these things I shall pass by,
being many and needing a writing of their own, and I shall go to what is
urgent, showing to all the immeasurable grace of the holy apostle Barnabas and
how much care and concern he has for his own country. Not being satisfied then
with the countless evils which the Fuller did throughout the East, he also
laid hands upon the provinces in no way subject to him; for having once bought
with gold the easiness of the emperor and of those about him, he even despised
the law of God himself; at any rate he planned in every way to harm the
God-loving and faith-loving and orthodox Cypriots, since keeping ancestral
piety they did not wish to communicate in his impiety, and he attempted to
seize the from the beginning and from above most holy and free and apostolic
throne of Cyprus and to make it under himself; and he makes a report to the
ruler, full of false speaking, that the word of God, he says, has been sounded
from Antioch in Cyprus and the church of Cyprus ought to be under the throne
of Antioch, since it happens to be an apostolic and patriarchal throne; but
the apostate did not escape notice being a heretic, falsifying the things
defined at Ephesus at the holy synod under Cyril. But the herald of piety, the
most holy among apostles Barnabas, convicted his folly, becoming a helper of
the country in due time. For the bishop of the Salaminians having been ordered
to reach the

imperial city and to be judged before the ecumenical patriarch against those
of Antioch, was numbed with fear, suspecting the intrigues of the Fuller; and
Anthemius was the wondrous man, most orthodox as could be and made illustrious
by an unstained life, but of little account toward debate with those opposing.
Being then at a loss and distressed about the journey abroad, by night there
stands over him, as he slept in a private place, someone having a godlike face
and flashing gleams of light, clothed in a robe of light in a priestly manner,
and he says to him: "Why are you so grieved, bishop, and what is this
listlessness about you and why has your face fallen? You shall suffer nothing
terrible from the adversaries." And saying these things, he departed from him.
And the bishop becoming awake with fear, fell on his face with many tears and
begged God, saying: "Lord Jesus Christ, Son of the living God, if you spare
this your church (and I know that you spare), and if this vision has been from
you, deign to double and triple the sight for me, that I may be fully assured
that you are with me." Then he used prayer more earnestly, meeting with no
one. And on the following night again the same man stands over him in the same
appearance and in the same form, saying to him: "I have already told you that
none of the things threatened to you will happen; go then eagerly to the queen
city, suspecting nothing." And saying these things, he withdrew. And the
bishop rising thanked the Lord again, speaking to no one. And he awaited also
the third vision, and on the coming night the same man stood over him, saying
to him more sternly: "Until when do you not believe my words, which will be
fulfilled in these days? Go eagerly to the queen city and you return with
glory to your throne; you shall suffer nothing terrible from the opponents,
God shielding you through me, his servant." The bishop answered, his mouth
being opened: "For who are you, my lord, who speak these things beside me?"
And he: "I am," he says, "Barnabas the disciple of our Lord Jesus Christ, who
was set apart by the Holy Spirit for the apostleship of the nations with Paul
the apostle, the vessel of election; and this is the sign for you: go," he
says, "outside the city toward the west five stades to the place called of
Health (for through me God works the wonders in that place), and dig at the
carob tree and you will find a cave and a coffin in it; there my whole body is
laid and a gospel in my own hand, which I received from Matthew the holy
apostle and evangelist. And since your adversaries plead, saying up and down
that the throne of Antioch is apostolic, plead you also against them that my
throne too is apostolic and I have an apostle in my country." Saying these
things, he went away; and the bishop rising and worshiping the Lord, gathered
all the sacred clergy and the Christ-loving people, and went out with a
showing of the cross to the place shown to him with much preparation; and
making a prayer, he ordered the place to be dug; and digging a little, they
found a cave, blocked with stones, and rolling these away, they found the
coffin, and uncovering this, they found the precious relic of the holy and
glorious apostle Barnabas, breathing a fragrance of spiritual grace; and they
found also the gospel, lying upon his breast; and sealing the coffin with lead
and making a prayer and worshiping, they withdrew, the bishop having stationed
at the place devout men to glorify God with evening and morning hymnodies. And
he himself taking the distinguished of the bishops set out for the queen city
and was lodged in the episcopal residence; and the presence was announced to
the emperor

of him and he assigned the patriarch to hear with the resident synod the
things between the parties and to declare what seemed right. And the council
being assembled and the matter being moved, those of the opposite side using,
as they thought, their own rights, said that the throne of Antioch is
patriarchal and apostolic, and the other provinces ought to be under it. And
they seemed to those present to speak reasonably. But the blessed Anthemius,
holding a little, replied, saying: "But my throne too, O best of men, is
apostolic from above and from the beginning, honored with freedom, and I have
an apostle whole of body in my country, Barnabas the thrice-blessed in holy
praises." This being spoken, there was no longer a place for contradiction,
the bishops confirming the word by silence; and those of the opposite side
stood mute with shame, astonished at the reasonableness of the answer.
Learning these things, the emperor, sending for the bishop of Cyprus with
haste, inquired about the revelation of the holy apostle Barnabas; and he
hiding nothing of the truth, related all in order to the emperor; and he
hearing rejoiced greatly, wondering at the rich grace of God, that in the
times of his empire he had done so great a wonder. Then he drove away from him
the bishop of Antioch, ordering that henceforth the bishop of Cyprus not be
troubled at all on account of this matter. Then he exhorted the blessed
Anthemius the bishop, saying: "Since our Lord Jesus Christ in the times of
your holy hierarchy has been pleased to reveal his holy apostle Barnabas,
order that the gospel found be brought here to me at once, granting me in
this, your child, a grace as great as possible; for you will have me also from
now on obeying your fatherhood in all things." And the bishop nodding, sent
one of the bishops with him with a most faithful man of the emperor; and
taking the gospel, they brought it up to Constantinople, having the tablets of
thyine woods. And the emperor taking it and kissing it and adorning it with
gold, laid it in the palace and it is kept until today; for on Great Thursday
of Pascha each year they read the gospel in it in the oratory of the palace.
And the emperor honoring the bishop greatly, dismissed him to Cyprus with much
money and a command, charging him to raise a church to the holy apostle
Barnabas in the place where his precious relic was found; and many of the
magnates also gave him money for the building of the church. And he reaching
the land of the Cypriots and gathering a multitude of craftsmen and workmen,
did not hold the building as a side work, but raised to the apostle a church
most vast, splendid in its devices, more splendid in the variety of the
decoration, girt about outside with porticoes in a circle. And along the aisle
of the church toward the southwest, he made a great court, having four stoas;
and building small houses on this side and that of the court, he ordered the
monks who perform the divine liturgies in the church to dwell in them; and
bringing the aqueduct from far off, he made it gush richly, building a most
beautiful reservoir in the middle of the court, so that those dwelling in the
place and the strangers might have unstintingly the enjoyment of the stream.
And he built many other lodgings in the place for the rest of the strangers
sojourning; and one could see that spot, imitating in beauty a certain small
city and very delightful. And he laid the holy coffin of the apostle on the
right of the altar, adorning the place with sufficient silver and marbles; and
they ordained that the day of the glorious memory of the thrice-blessed
apostle and noble martyr Barnabas be each year, according to the Romans on the
day before the third of the Ides of June, and according to

the Constantinian Cypriots in the month Mesori, which is also the tenth, on
the eleventh, and according to the Asians or according to the Paphians in the
month Plethypatos, which is also the ninth, on the nineteenth, gathering and
performing the spiritual liturgies, to the glory of the Father and of the Son
and of the Holy Spirit, because to him is the glory unto the ages of ages
amen.
