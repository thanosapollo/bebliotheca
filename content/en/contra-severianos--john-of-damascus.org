* Against the Severians

:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

RESOLUTION against those who say, "If Christ is two natures, it is
necessary to speak of three natures of God and of man, and of as many
energies," as Severus used to claim.
=95.225= For this wretched Severus, having seized the throne of Antioch,
was trying to champion the heresy according to Manes and Apollinaris and
Eutyches, stirring up, as far as he was able, the peace of the Churches.
And having been driven out of Antioch as a seditious man, he burst upon
Alexandria after the manner of a hurricane; where also, another tempest
having blown against the people, a certain man called Julian, bishop of
Halicarnassus of Asia, and a most eager champion of the Eutychian false
doctrine, confused everything and threw it into turmoil. For Severus
saying that Christ is one nature, and accepting the difference in
Christ, but Julian asserting one nature according to Severus, yet doing
away with the difference, the unholy ones became causes of all
disturbance and tempest, "not understanding, as the saying goes, either
what they say or concerning what they make assertions;" taking as an
example of the divine union the human being from soul and body, and they
say: Therefore you confess three natures. But let the ignorant and
uneducated know that we say two natures: not of God and of flesh, nor
yet of God and of soul, but of God and of man. For neither is the nature
of the part properly the nature of man. For soul and body are parts of
man; and the divisions and subdivisions concerning them are parts of the
parts; and the unconfused parts of Christ are divinity and humanity. But
soul and body are not parts of Christ, but parts of the part. For parts
of the parts of man, in the case of the soul, are rational substance and
incorporeal quality having in itself mind, and sensation, and reason; of
which one is the ruling, another the spirited, and another the
desir[ing]; and of these, one is conceptual and discursive, both
memorial and deliberative, =95.228= another both appetitive and
attractive, and another defensive, and many other things are subdivided
under these.
But let it be left aside for now to inquire whether these are in the
soul as parts, or as powers; and in the case of the body again a
division is made into homogeneous [and heterogeneous]; and of these
still into head and hands and feet; and these into bones and flesh and
sinews; and these into four elements; and these into matter and form.
And the sons of physicians philosophize many other things also
concerning natural powers; of which they name one attractive, another
drawing-down, another alterative, and another excretive; and many things
also concerning sensation and imagination, and concerning the vital and
organic spirit that holds the living being together, which exists, as
they say, as other than the rational. Just as, then, it is sufficient
for presenting all these things to say flesh, or body of a human being;
and it is sufficient, having said human being, or nature of a human
being, both to make clear and to present all the things from which he is
composed, in which he is also known. But they, not in order to declare
him a complete human being, but in order to slander the Lord, and to
alienate him from our nature, and either that he did not assume our own
at the beginning, or that he did not keep intact what he received from
us. The book of the holy and blessed John Mandar of Damascus, called the
Pandect, has reached its end.
