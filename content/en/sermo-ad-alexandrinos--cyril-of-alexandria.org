* Sermon to the Alexandrians
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of the same Saint Cyril from the first homily that one must not say a God-bearing
man, but God become man.
Great truly was the blessed Moses; for he heard God saying "I know you beside
all, and you have found grace with me;" but he was as a household servant
faithful in a house; the prophet Samuel was worthy of wonder, but he was
numbered "among those who call upon" the God of all; the blessed prophets have
become holy, yet they confess the measure of slavery, and they filled the
economy fitting to household servants, ferrying the words from above and from
God to those of that time, rather even to us ourselves; for they have always
cried These things says the Lord; and somewhere Christ himself also said
concerning the blessed Baptist "Greater among those born of women than John the
Baptist has not been raised;" and he, although according to the unlying voice of
the Savior having obtained the upper order in justice, did not ignore the
superiority, he confessed the master, he yielded the conquering to the glory of
Christ; for he said clearly "This was concerning whom I said I A man comes after
me, of whom I am not worthy to loose the strap of his sandals." therefore of
very many saints having had the Word of God in themselves, concerning none of
those is it written that "The Word became flesh and tabernacled in us," upon
none of the holy prophets will we find the powers above dancing; for of whom
tell me having been born did "a multitude of a heavenly 460 army" make the
thanksgiving odes for us? upon Emmanuel only they have said "Glory in highest
places to God, and upon earth peace, in men goodwill;" therefore in many, as I
said, holy ones God having dwelt, once upon a completion of the ages the Word of
God is said to have sojourned, once he became man, and concerning one the
prophet says "Behold the virgin will have in womb and will bear a son, and they
will call his name Emmanuel;" one and only has "redeemed us from the curse of
the law, having become a curse for us;" one Son the Father has given for us; one
Son has set his own soul for us; one was crucified and lived again, having
trampled the power of death. therefore we recognize the Word of God having come
to be in flesh according to truth, or at any rate having been united to the
flesh; and we do not say Emmanuel is a God-bearing man; and we confess that
being God by nature, having taken a form of a slave, he has become also a son of
man; and he is the same both God together and man.
