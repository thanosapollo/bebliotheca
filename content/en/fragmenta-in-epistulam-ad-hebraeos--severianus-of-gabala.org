* Fragments on the Epistle to the Hebrews
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

The heretics say that the epistle is not Paul's, and they bring as first proof
of this that his name has not been placed first as in the other epistles;
second, that the phrasing is different, that is, foreign compared with Paul's
and the construction. One must then know that Paul was hated by the Jews as
teaching apostasy from the law, and for this reason, having also been in
danger in Jerusalem and barely escaping, he was sent to Rome. Writing then
things beneficial to the Hebrews, he does not prefix his own name, so that
they may not, by the hatred toward him, be deprived of the benefit from the
epistle. And he writes to them, as the blessed Severianus says, in the
language of the Hebrews, which was also translated by one of the disciples, by
Luke, or rather by Clement, of whom he also makes mention. For this reason the
phrasing is foreign. And 346 this has been inquired into by those before us,
and Eusebius son of Pamphilus, having become more historical than those before
him and with him, mentioned the inquiry, and that it seemed good to our
fathers, the ancient bishops, that the epistle is Paul's. Hebr 1:1-2
In many parts, according to the differences of the times in which the things
concerning us were administered, as in the generation of Adam, in the things
concerning Cain, in the things concerning Noah, in the things concerning
Abraham, in those before the law, in those after the law; for many are the
parts of God's administration on our behalf. And in many ways, because another
commandment was given to Adam, another to Noah, another to Abraham, and
through Moses another, and through the prophets various ones. He has spoken to
us in a Son, i.e. through a Son; for not in him as in an instrument, but
through him as one who administered flesh, so that the hearing of the Jews,
beginning from there, and as it were laying hold by a kind of touching of the
Word, might thus be led up to the pre-eternal existence. For having first
said: he has spoken to us in a Son whom he also appointed heir (i.e. made), he
led the word up to the pre-eternal existence. And he does this continually,
now ascending to the theology, now descending to the incarnation. Hebr 1:3α
Seeking to present more clearly that the Word has been begotten from the
essence of the Father, he mentioned the radiance; for the radiance is from the
essence of that of which it is radiance, and is continuous, and from it, and
never conceived without that of which it is radiance. But since the radiance
shows the nature lesser than that of which it is radiance and not in its own
hypostasis, he presented this by another expression, saying: and the impress
of the hypostasis, having set the first as proof of being from the essence
without partition, and the second, that he is not without hypostasis. For just
as John, having named him Word, added: he was with God and was God, so also
Paul, having said radiance, added: and impress of the hypostasis. 347 Hebr
1:3β This is what Jeremiah says: strong are the works of his words. Hebr 1:3ξ
Even if the Word became flesh, yet he was in the glory and nature of the
Godhead and did not depart from the highest thrones of God the Father; and
though he was made a little lower than the angels because of the measure of
humanity (for man's nature sits beneath the dignity of the angels), even so he
was above every name that is named. Hebr 1:7-8 Just as in the case of the
radiance and the impress he imitated the mind of John, who named him Word and
said he was with God, so also here he imitates John. Just as that one said:
this one was in the beginning, all things came to be through him, i.e. this
one was, these things were not, so also Paul: toward the angels, he who makes;
but toward the Son, your throne: that he may show: what is made was not, but
the one who has an eternal throne was always with the Father. Hebr 1:12-14 He
does not speak of a change of nature taking place, but by comparison, that it
is more possible for these to perish, and they as it were perish, so far as it
is by juxtaposition with the Son's eternity. For the Lord also, foretelling
the second coming, says that stars will fall, but no longer sun or moon or
heaven. But to which of the angels has he ever said? Leaving aside the whole
creation he speaks of what is higher than it, the angels; for if there is a
difference of the Son toward angels in that they came to be and he did not
come to be, how much more toward all the invisible things? And one must note
that through "but you are the same" he made clear both the pre-eternal and the
unchangeable of the nature, and through saying "are they not all ministering
spirits for service" he made clear that the Son is not a minister but a
fellow-worker. 348 Hebr 2:2-3 He does not mean the law, but that which was
often administered through angels. It was confirmed to us by those who heard.
This is like that in the epistle to the Galatians, I went up according to
revelation, and I laid before them the gospel, lest somehow I run or had run
in vain. And it was confirmed not because we needed it, but because all the
others were fully assured that what I preach, I preach well. Hebr 2:5-9 He
means the age to come. Last he added at once: but now we do not yet see
all things subjected to him; but we see Jesus, who was made a little lower
than the angels. So that "what is man that you remember him?" he says belongs
to Jesus; for the things of common humanity belonged to him. But as the Son
himself says, "out of the mouth of infants and nursing children you have
prepared praise because of your enemies, and I will see the heavens, the works
of your fingers," no one would say that the man of whom God remembered had
done the preparing of praise out of the mouth of infants and nursing children
because of your enemies, and I will see the heavens, the works of your
fingers; this one remembered man and made him a little lower than the angels.
And who is the man? Jesus. Because of the suffering of death made a little
lower than the angels. Hebr 3:13 Just as he introduces the present age as one
day. Hebr 4:12 He means a division of soul and spirit, when the soul suffers
along with the body, but the grace of the Holy Spirit pulls the other way
toward the heavenly things. Hebr 6:1-2 Since the beginning of Christ is from
Judaism (for he lived according to the law), for this reason he says: leaving
this let us be carried on 349 to perfection, knowing that according to the
order of Melchizedek the one who is going to be a priest without law must be.
From dead works, and faith toward God, of baptisms. For Christians earthly
things are dead. It is absurd then, he says, having departed from this way of
life, whether that according to faith, or from having been put to death to all
things, to come again to the baptisms according to the law. The Lord when he
came preached repentance, saying: repent; for the kingdom of the heavens has
drawn near; and repentance from dead works. Thus then it is to be read: of
repentance from dead works, i.e. of the repentance from the dead works. What
he says then is of this sort: one must not, having left the repentance from
dead works and the faith toward God and the baptism (which he named in the
plural because of the multitude of those deemed worthy; and he has said of
baptisms also the teaching, and the laying on of hands through which the
ordinations, and the hope of the resurrection and the rest) run back to the
law. And he prepares this beforehand, since the priests in the law defended
the law, but Melchizedek was outside the law; and the one who came was
according to the order of Melchizedek. One must not, he says, having left the
one according to the order of Melchizedek, defend the priesthood in the law,
so that the things in the law may stand. And he called the things in the law a
foundation, since it has become a prelude of piety for men. Hebr 6:6 The
apostles raised the dead. And this was the power of the resurrection. He said
making a public example, because if baptism is a mystery, once is enough.
But if the thing happens a second time, it is contempt and a making a public
example. Hebr 6:7-8 He took an example, not with respect to baptism (for how
was he forbidding a second baptism?), but the power of the Holy Spirit that
benefits often. Hebr 6:17 Having said above the full assurance of hope, he
made clear that God granted Abraham full assurance, not the one through works
that belongs to the manner of life, but the one through economy. 350 It is
impossible for God, having sworn, to be changed; it was impossible for the
power of the promise to be changed. Hebr 7:1-2 He does not simply mention how
Melchizedek met Abraham when he had prevailed over the Assyrians, and he gave
him tenths of all, but showing that the forefather of the Levitical tribe used
Melchizedek as priest.
Better then is the priesthood without law than the priesthood in the law. Hebr
7:3 Without father, without mother, without genealogy according to the account
of the priesthood, since in the Levitical tribe it was apparent who was whose,
of a priest or of a priest's wife. And there were times of the priesthood, and
some had yearly courses, and others served as priests through the whole of
life. And both of these are found among the priests in the law, even if not in
the same times. He calls Melchizedek without father and without mother and
without genealogy, having neither beginning of days nor end of life according
to the account of the priesthood; for he neither had a priestly race, nor
began from somewhere as succeeding another, nor another from a course, nor as
having a law to be priest through the whole of life. And in general all the
things said are by comparison of the priests in the law. And it has been said
concerning Melchizedek: but made like the Son of God, he remains a priest
continually. And how does Melchizedek remain a priest? What is said is a
question, and the solution is this: just as he calls the law Moses, so also
here Melchizedek he means in one place the man, in another the priesthood
itself. If then Melchizedek has been made like through the priesthood that is
according to Christ, he remains continually; not the man, for he is mortal,
but the dignity of the priesthood. Hebr 9:11 Just as the tabernacle was
constructed in the time of Moses so that the services might take place, so
this tabernacle is greater, the constitution of grace, the body of Christ
having Christ as head. 351 Hebr 11, By faith concerning things to come Isaac
blessed Jacob and Esau. He set Jacob first then Esau, not according to the
sequence of birth, but according to the order of the blessing. Hebr 11:38 Of
whom the world was not worthy. He does not say this about all but about these
last ones whom he also knows as martyrs. He mentions those who turned camps to
flight and who received grace because of the usefulness of the people. Hebr
12:1 Laying aside every weight. Weight is the sin of enjoyment according to
the flesh, from which the easily surrounding sin is born. And he called easily
surrounding the sin that easily stands around us and leads us to its own will.
Hebr 12:22-23 For a festal assembly and a feast of angels is the glory unto
God, and to the church of firstborns enrolled in the heavens and to God the
judge of all. Hebr 12:27 This is what Paul says elsewhere: for the form of
this world is passing away, so that the temporary things may pass and the
eternal things may come. Hebr 13:10-13 That is, those in the law do not have
this gift of which we partake. And outside the camp, i.e. outside the legal
things and the Israelite camp.
