TO THE SAME
"Suddenly" is that which is brought out contrary to hope and from the hitherto
unapparent into the manifest. But in the case of the love of man according to
Christ I think theology also hints at this, that the Superessential has come
forth out of the hidden into the manifestation among us, having taken
substance humanly. But he is hidden even after the manifestation, or, that I
may speak more divinely, even in the manifestation. For this also of Jesus is
hidden, and the mystery according to him has been drawn out for no word nor
mind, but even when spoken remains ineffable, and when understood unknown.

TO THE SAME
How, you say, is Jesus, who is beyond all, arranged with all men essentially?
For he is not called man here as cause of men, but as being himself, according
to the whole essence, truly man. And we


do not define Jesus humanly; for he is not man only (nor superessential, if
man only), but truly man, the one who is distinctively a lover of man, the
Superessential having taken substance from the essence of men, beyond men and
according to men. And he is no less overfull of superessentiality, he who is
always superessential; indeed by the excess of this, even having come truly
into essence he was given substance beyond essence, and he operated the things
of man beyond man. And a virgin conceiving supernaturally makes it clear, and
unstable water holding the weight of material and earthly feet and not
yielding, but being held together toward the undiffused by a supernatural
power.

What remaining things, being very many, would one go through? Through which
the one who sees divinely will know beyond mind even the things affirmed
concerning the love of man of Jesus, as having the power of a preeminent
negation. For indeed, that we may speak in summary, he was not even man, not
as not being man, but as from among men being beyond men, and having become
truly man beyond man, and for the rest having done the divine things not
according to God, nor the human things according to man, but, God having been
made man, having conducted for us a certain new theandric activity.

TO DOROTHEUS THE LITURGIST
The divine gloom is the "unapproachable light" in which God is said to dwell,
being both invisible by reason of the exceeding brightness and unapproachable
by the same, through excess of superessential outpouring of light. In this
comes to be everyone who is deemed worthy to know and to see God, by the very
not seeing and not knowing; coming to be truly in that which is beyond sight
and knowledge, knowing this very thing, that he is after all things sensible
and intelligible, and saying prophetically: "Thy knowledge was made wonderful
from me; it was made strong, I shall not be able toward it."

Just as then also the divine Paul is said to have known God, having known him
as being beyond every intellection and knowledge, wherefore he also says that
his ways are untraceable and "his judgments unsearchable" and his gifts
indescribable and his peace surpassing "every mind," as having found him who
is beyond all, and having known this beyond intellection, that he is beyond
all, being cause of all.

TO SOPATER THE PRIEST
Do not think this a victory, holy Sopater, to insult a religion or opinion
that does not appear good; for not even if you refute it with judgment are the
things of Sopater already noble; for it is possible that both you and others,
in the many false and apparent things, the true, being one and hidden, escapes
notice. For neither, if something is not red, is it already white; nor, if
someone is not a horse, is he of necessity a man.

But you will do thus, if you are persuaded by me: you will abstain from
speaking against others, and you will speak truly on behalf of the truth, so
that the things said are in every way irrefutable.

TO POLYCARP THE HIERARCH


I do not know that I have spoken against Greeks or against others, thinking it
enough for good men if they should be able both to know and to speak the true
itself by itself, as it really is. For when this, whatever it is, is rightly
demonstrated according to the law of truth and stands unmixed, everything that
is otherwise and that pretends to the truth will be refuted, being other than
what really is and unlike, and seeming that rather than being. It is
superfluous then for the discloser of truth to fight against these or those.
For each says that he himself has the royal coin, and perhaps he has some
deceitful image of some portion of the true. And if you refute this one, the
other, and again the next, will contend about the same. But when the true
itself has been set down as a right account and has remained irrefutable
toward all the others, everything that is not so in all respects is thrown
down by itself by the unconquered standing of what really is true. This then,
as I think, having known well, I did not hasten to speak against Greeks or
against others, but it is enough for me (and may God grant this!) first to
know concerning truth, then, knowing, to speak as one ought.

But you say that the sophist Apollophanes abuses me and calls me a parricide,
as using the things of the Greeks against the Greeks unhallowedly. And yet it
was more true for us to say to him that Greeks use the divine things
unhallowedly against the divine, trying through the wisdom of God to drive out
the divine reverence. And I do not mean the opinion of the many who remain in
the things of the poets in a material and passionate way and worship "the
creature rather than the Creator," but even Apollophanes himself uses the
divine things unhallowedly against the divine; for by the knowledge of the
things that are, which is well called by him philosophy and by the divine Paul
wisdom of God, the true philosophers ought to have been led up to the cause
both of the things that are themselves and of the knowledge of them.

And that I may not refute the opinion of others or his own contrary to what
seems, Apollophanes, being wise, ought to have seen that the heavenly order
and motion could never otherwise have been turned aside, unless it had him who
is its being and holder-together and cause moving it to this, who makes all
things and changes them according to the sacred word. How then does he not
revere the God of the whole, known to us even from this and really existing,
admiring him for the all-causing and super-ineffable power? When sun and moon
are bounded by him, according to a most supernatural power and standing,
together with the all, toward the wholly unmoved, and all things stand upon
the same signs for the measure of a whole day; or, what is even more than
this, if, while the wholes and the stronger and containing things were thus
being carried, the things contained were not carried round with them; and when
some other day is nearly tripled in continuity, and in twenty hours in all
either the whole runs back the contrary courses of so much time and turns
about again by these so very supernatural counter-revolutions, or the sun,
having gathered together in ten hours his own fivefold motion in his own
course, analytically again runs the whole of it back in the other ten,
treading a certain new path. This is what reasonably struck the Babylonians
with amazement and subjected them to Hezekiah without battle, as to one equal
to a god and surpassing men. And I do not of course mention the mighty works
in Egypt or certain other divine signs that have come to be elsewhere, but the
common and heavenly things and those that were


triumphed in the all and among all.

Apollophanes will certainly say that these things are not true. Yet this is
especially carried in the priestly reports of the Persians, and still the magi
celebrate the memorials of the triple Mithras. But let it be permitted him
through ignorance or inexperience to disbelieve these. But say to him: What do
you say concerning the eclipse that came to be at the saving cross? For both
of us then, being present together at Heliopolis and standing together, saw
the moon falling upon the sun paradoxically (for it was not a time of
conjunction); and again from the ninth hour until evening standing over
against it supernaturally at the diameter of the sun. And remind him of
something else also; for he knows that we also saw the falling itself
beginning from the east and coming as far as the solar limit, then running
back, and again both the falling and the clearing not from the same, but
having come to be from the diametrically opposite.

Such are the supernatural things of that time, possible only to Christ the
all-cause, who does "great and extraordinary things, of which there is no
number."

These things, if it is lawful for you, say, and if possible, Apollophanes,
refute even me who was then both present with you and saw together and
examined all together and admired together. Indeed even then Apollophanes
begins, I know not from where, a prophecy, and said to me as comparing the
things coming to be: These, O noble Dionysius, are exchanges of divine things.

Let so much be said by us as according to a letter. But you are sufficient
both to fill up what is lacking and to lead the man wholly to God, being wise
in many things and perhaps not thinking it unworthy to learn meekly the
superswise truth of our religion.

TO DEMOPHILUS THE THERAPEUTES
The histories of the Hebrews say, O noble Demophilus, that even that holy
Moses was deemed worthy of the theophany through much meekness. And if ever
they record him outside the divine vision, they do not cast him out from God
before the meekness. And they say that the Lord was angered with wrath at him
when he was acting very arrogantly and straining against the divine counsels.
But when they make him proclaimed in the dignities judged by God, he is
proclaimed from the preeminent imitation of God of the good.

For he was exceedingly meek, and for this he is called servant of God and more
worthy of the vision of God than all prophets. But even when certain rash men
were contending against him and Aaron over high-priesthood and ethnarchy, he
stood above all ambition and love of rule and yielded to the one judged by God
the presidency of the people. But when they also rose up together against him
and, reproaching him concerning the former things, were threatening and were
already almost attacking, the meek one cried to the Good for salvation, and he
spoke out very reasonably, that he was without cause of all evils to those
ruled; for he knew that the one who converses with God the Good must have been
stamped toward the most similar possible, as far as attainable, and must be
conscious in himself of the works that love the good.

And what made David the God-father beloved of God? For being good, even good
toward enemies: "I have found," says the Super-good who loves the good,


"a man according to my heart." And yet a good ordinance had also been given
even to take thought for the beasts of burden of an enemy. And Job was
justified as holding to guilelessness, and Joseph did not avenge himself on
the plotting brothers, and Abel simply and without suspicion went along with
the brother-killer. And theology proclaims all those who are good-formed, not
thinking the evils beforehand, not making them beforehand, but not even being
altered from the good by the evil of others, but on the contrary making even
the evil good in a godlike way and spreading even upon them their much
goodness and calling them reasonably toward the like.

But let us look up toward the steep, not announcing the reasonableness of holy
men, not the goodnesses of man-loving angels, who pity nations and intercede
for them with the Good and who rebuke the destructive and evil-working
multitudes and who are grieved at evils but rejoice at the salvation of those
being called over to the good things, nor whatever else theology hands down
concerning the good-working angels; but receiving in quiet the good-making
rays of the really good and super-good Christ, let us be light-led by them
upon his divine good-workings.

Is it not then of an unspeakable and beyond-intellection goodness, that he
makes the things that are to be, and that he has brought all of them forth
toward being, and that he always wishes all to become near to himself and
communicants of his things according to the fitness of each? And what of this,
that he even holds erotically to those who have gone away, and contends, and
begs not to be deemed unworthy of those loved, and endures them when they are
coy and accuse at random, and he himself makes defense? And he rather promises
that he will heal, and still, when they are away, nevertheless when they
approach he runs toward them and meets them and, having wholly clung around
them wholly, he embraces them and does not accuse concerning the former
things, but loves the present and keeps a feast and "calls together the
friends" (that is, the good), that the dwelling may be of all who rejoice.
(But Demophilus, and if anyone else is hostile to the good, is very justly
rebuked and taught the noble things and made good.) For how was it not
necessary, he says, that the good one rejoice at the salvation of the lost and
at the life of those who had been put to death? Indeed he even lifts upon his
shoulders that which has with difficulty been turned from error, and he raises
the good angels to gladness, and "he is kind upon the ungrateful," and he
makes his sun rise "upon evil and good," and "he lays down his soul for" those
who flee away. But you, as your letters show, even kicked away the one who had
fallen before the priest, the, as you say, impious and sinner (I do not know
how, being present against himself). Then he was begging and confessing that
he had come for healing of evils, but you did not shudder, but even with
boldness you insulted beforehand the good priest for being merciful to one
repenting and for having justified the impious; and at last: "Go out," you
said to the priest, "with those like him," and you leapt in (it not being
lawful) toward the inner shrines and you packed together the holy of holies,
and you write to us that the sacred things that were about to be corrupted I
saved by forethought and I still keep them undefiled.

Now then hear our things: it is not lawful for a priest to be corrected by the
liturgists above you or by the therapeutes of your own rank, even if he should
seem to act impiously toward the divine things, even if he should be convicted
of having done some other of the forbidden things. For if disorder and lack of
order is an outgoing from the most divine both bounds and ordinances, it has
no reason that for the sake of God the God-delivered order should be
overturned. For


God "was not divided against himself; for how shall his kingdom stand?" And if
"judgment is of God," as the oracles say, and the priests are angels and
interpreters, after the hierarchs, of the divine judgments, be taught the
divine things fittingly for you from them through the middle liturgists, when
it is time, through whom you were also deemed worthy to be a therapeutes. Or
do not the sacred symbols also cry this? For not simply have the holy of
holies been set apart from all, but the order of the sacred initiators
approaches them more, then the arrangement of the priests, and following these
that of the liturgists; but for the appointed therapeutes the gates of the
inner shrines have been assigned, at which they are also initiated and stand,
not for a guard of them, but for order and for knowledge of themselves rather,
approaching the people more than the priestly ones. Whence the holy order of
the sacred things legislates them to partake of the divine things, having
entrusted the distribution of them to others, that is, to those more inward.
For those standing about the divine, to speak symbolically, altar see and hear
the divine things being unveiled clearly to them, and going forth in a
good-formed way upon the outside of the divine veils they manifest according
to worth to the subordinate therapeutes and to the sacred people and to the
orders being purified the sacred things that have been well kept undefiled,
until, having leapt in upon them tyrannically, you forced the holy of holies
to be paraded out to you, though they were unwilling. And you say that you
have the sacred things and keep them, and yet you have neither seen nor heard
nor have you anything of the things belonging to the priests, just as you have
not known the truth of the oracles, wrangling about them every day toward the
overthrow of those who hear.

And if some ethnarch undertook what had not been commanded him by a king, he
was justly punished. And if someone standing by of those ranked under him
dared to re-examine the ruler who was justifying some or condemning (for I do
not yet say to insult at once and to cast out of the rule)? But you, O man,
are so bold against the meek and good one and against his hierarchical
legislation?

And these things it was necessary to say even when someone undertaking beyond
worth nevertheless seemed to do likely things; for not even this is attainable
for anyone. For what out-of-place thing did Uzziah do, offering incense to
God? And what Saul, sacrificing? And what the tyrannical demons, truly
theologizing Jesus? But every overseer of what is another's is proclaimed out
by theology, and each shall be in the order of his liturgy, and the high
priest alone shall enter into the holy of holies, and once in the year, and
this in all the hierarchical purity according to law. And the priests shall
cover the holy things, and the Levites shall not "touch the holy things, lest
they die." And the Lord was angered with wrath at the rashness of Uzziah, and
Miriam becomes leprous, having undertaken to legislate for the lawgiver; and
the demons leapt upon the sons of Sceva. And "I did not send them," he said,
"and they ran," and "I did not speak to them, and they prophesied," and the
impious one "who sacrifices to me a calf as one who kills a dog." And to speak
simply: the complete justice of God does not endure the lawless. And when they
say, "in your name we did many powers," "I do not know you," he answers,
"depart from me, all you workers of lawlessness."

So that it is not lawful, as the sacred oracles say, even to pursue the just
things not according to worth. And each must attend to himself and not


think the higher and deeper things, but consider only the things commanded to
him according to his worth.

What then, you say, must not priests who act impiously or are convicted of
some other of the out-of-place things be corrected, and shall it be permitted
only to those who boast in the law to dishonor God through the transgression
of the law? And how are the priests disclosers of God? For how shall they
announce to the people the divine virtues, not having known the power of them?
Or how shall those who have been darkened give light, and how shall they
impart the divine spirit, not having believed even whether there is a holy
spirit, in habit and in truth?

But I will make defense to you toward these things (for Demophilus is not an
enemy, nor will I endure you to be overreached by Satan); for each of the
orders about God is more godlike than that which has stood further away, and
those nearer to the true light are at once more full of light and more
light-giving. And do not take the nearness locally, but according to the
God-receiving fitness. If then the order of the priests is the light-giving
one, he who is not light-giving has fallen away wholly from the priestly order
and power, and much more he who is unlighted. And such a one seems to me bold,
undertaking the priestly things, and he neither fears nor is ashamed, pursuing
the divine things contrary to worth, and thinking that God does not know the
things which he himself has been conscious of in himself; and he thinks to
deceive him who is called by him father in a false name, and he dares to speak
his accursed blasphemies (for I would not say prayers) in a Christlike way
upon the divine symbols. This man is not a priest, he is not, but a foe,
deceitful, a mocker of himself and a wolf armed in a fleece against the divine
people.

But it is not lawful for Demophilus to correct these things. For if theology
justly commands to pursue the just things (and it is just to pursue them,
whenever one should wish to assign to each the things according to worth),
this is justly to be pursued by all, not contrary to their own worth or order,
since even to angels it is just that the things according to worth be assigned
and marked off, yet not by us, O Demophilus, but through them to us from God,
and to them through the still preeminent angels. And, to speak simply: in all
the things that are, through the first to the second the things according to
worth are assigned by the well-ordered and most just providence of all. Let
those then who have been ranked by God to rule over others also assign after
themselves to the subjects the things according to worth. But let Demophilus
mark off to reason and to anger and to desire the things according to worth,
and let him not do injustice to his own order, but let the overlying reason
rule the things that lie under. For if in the marketplace, seeing a household
slave railing at a master and a younger at an elder, or even a son at a
father, and attacking and laying on blows, we would also seem to act impiously
if we did not run to the stronger and help, even if perhaps those had been
first wronged, how shall we not be ashamed, overlooking reason being wronged
by anger and desire and being cast out of the rule given from God, and raising
in ourselves an impious and unjust disorder and faction and lack of order?
Reasonably our blessed legislator from God does not think him worthy to
preside over the church of God who has not already presided well over his own
house, for he who has ordered himself will also order another, and he who
another, a house also, and he who a house, a city also, and he who a city, a
nation also. And to speak simply, as the oracles say: he who is "faithful in
little is also faithful in much," and he who is unfaithful in little is also
unfaithful in much.


Do you then mark off to desire and anger and reason the things according to
worth, and to you the divine liturgists, and to these the priests, and
hierarchs to the priests, and to the hierarchs the apostles and the successors
of the apostles. And if somewhere even among those someone should fall away
from what is fitting, he will be set right by the holy ones of the same rank,
and order will not be turned about upon order, but each shall be in his order
and in his liturgy.

So much to you from us for the knowing and doing of your own things. But
concerning the inhumanity toward the man who is, as you say, impious and
sinful, I do not know how I shall weep the crushing of my beloved. For of whom
do you think you were ranked by us to be a therapeutes? For if not of the
Good, then wholly you must be alien both from us and from our whole therapy;
and it is time for you both to seek God and other priests and among those to
be made a beast rather than to be initiated, and to be an implacable servant
of the dear inhumanity. Have we ourselves then been completed toward the
wholly holy, and do we not need the divine love of man upon ourselves? Or do
we sin the double sin, as the oracles say, according to the impious, not
knowing in what we stumble, but even justifying ourselves and thinking that we
see, but truly not seeing? "Heaven was astonished at this," and I shuddered
and I distrust myself. And if I had not met with your letters (as I ought not,
know well), they would not have persuaded me, if certain others had thought it
right to persuade me concerning you, that Demophilus thinks that the good God
is not upon all and a lover of man, and that he himself does not need the one
who pities or saves, but he even deposes the priests who have been deemed
worthy by goodness to bear the ignorances of the people and who know well that
they themselves also are compassed with weakness.

But the thearchic sacred initiator has gone another way; and this although set
apart from the sinners, as the sacred oracles say, and he makes the most
reasonable shepherding of the sheep a demonstration of the love toward
himself. And he calls evil the one who did not forgive the fellow-servant the
debt nor impart even partially of the very much goodness that had been given
him, and he judges him to have the enjoyment of his own things; which it is
necessary that both I and Demophilus fear. For even to those who act impiously
toward him, in the very suffering, he brings forth forgiveness from the
Father, and he rebukes even the disciples, because they thought it right to
judge without pity even for impiety the Samaritans who had driven him out.

This then is the thousand-times-said thing of your bold letter (for you din it
up and down), that you did not avenge yourself, but God: through evil, tell
me, the Good?

Away! We do not "have a high priest who cannot sympathize with our
weaknesses," but he is also "without evil" and "merciful." "He will not strive
nor cry out," and he himself is "meek" and "he himself is propitiation
concerning our sins." So that we shall not accept your unenviable impulses,
not even if you should take up Phinehas and Elijah ten thousand times. For
hearing these things, Jesus was not pleased with the disciples who were then
without share in the meek and good spirit. For even our most divine
sacred-institutor teaches "in meekness those who oppose themselves" to the
teaching of God. For those who are ignorant must be taught, not punished, just
as we do not chastise the blind, but lead them by the hand.

But you, even to the man beginning to look up toward the light, striking him
on the temple, shoved him back, and you kicked away boldly the one approaching
with much shame


(this is the thing worthy of much shuddering), whom Christ, being good, seeks
when he wanders upon the mountains and calls when he flees away and, when he
has been found with difficulty, lifts upon the shoulders. Do not, I beg, let
us counsel so evilly concerning ourselves, nor let us thrust the sword into
ourselves. For those who attempt to do injustice to some, or on the contrary
to do good, have not in every case done to those what they wished, but having
housed with themselves evil or goodness they will be full either of divine
virtues or of untamed passions. And these will be followers and
fellow-travelers of good angels both here and there, with all peace and
freedom from all evils, and they will be allotted the most blessed lots into
the age that always is, and they will always be with God, the greatest of all
goods. But those will fall away from the divine peace and from their own at
once, and both here and after death they will be together with the untamed
demons.

For which reason we have much zeal to come to be with God the Good and to be
with the Lord always, and not to be set apart together with the evil from the
Justifier, enduring from ourselves the things according to worth, which I fear
most of all things and I pray to be without share in all evils. And if you
wish, I will also mention a divine vision of a certain holy man; and do not
laugh, for I will speak truth.

Once when I was in Crete the holy Carpus hosted me, a man, if any other, most
fit through much purity of mind toward the vision of God. And indeed he did
not even undertake the holy rites of the mysteries unless first a sacred and
well-disposed vision appeared to him according to the pre-initiatory prayers.
He said then that once someone of the unbelievers had grieved him; and the
grief was that that one had led astray someone from the church toward the
godless, while the cheerful days were still being celebrated for him. He ought
to have prayed over both in a good-fitting way and, having taken God the
savior as helper, to have turned the one and conquered the other by goodness
and not to have ceased admonishing through all of life, until today, and thus
to have led them to the divine knowledge, as if already even the things in
dispute were being judged for them and those who were boldly acting without
reason were being compelled by lawful judgment to be temperate. But not having
suffered this first in himself, I do not know how, then having infused a
certain great hostility and bitterness, he went to sleep being in so bad a
state; for it was evening. And about midnight (for he was accustomed at this
time to be awake by himself toward the divine hymns) he rises, not having
enjoyed even the sleeps themselves, though they were many, and always being
cut off, outside of disturbance. But standing nevertheless toward the divine
converse he was unhallowedly distressed and was impatient, saying it was not
just if godless men should live and pervert "the straight ways of the Lord."
And saying these things he begged God by some thunderbolt to stop unpityingly
at once the lives of both.

And having said these things he said he seemed to see suddenly the house in
which he stood shaken first, divided from the roof into two in the middle, and
a certain many-lighted fire before himself, and this (for the place seemed
thereafter open to the sky) being carried down from the heavenly space as far
as himself, and the heaven itself opened, and upon the back of heaven Jesus,
with unbounded angels standing by him in human form; and these things were
seen from above, and he himself was amazed. But Carpus, having bent down
below, said he saw even the floor itself torn toward a certain gaping and dark
chasm, and those men, against whom he was cursing, before him at the mouth


of the chasm standing, trembling, pitiable, all but being carried down by the
unsteadiness of their own feet. And from below from the chasm snakes were
creeping up and moving under about their feet, at one time dragging them away,
coiling about together and weighting and drawing, at another time striking
from under with the teeth or with the tails or tickling from under, and
through all devising to throw them into the gaping; and there were also
certain men in the midst of the snakes, joining in the attack against the men,
shaking them together and shoving from under and striking down. And those
seemed to be toward falling, partly unwilling, partly willing, being forced
little by little by the evil and at the same time persuaded.

And Carpus said that he himself was pleased looking below, and that he
neglected the things above, and that he was impatient and made light of it
that they had not already fallen, and having often set upon the matter and
been unable, he was both grieved and cursed, and having looked up with
difficulty, he saw the heaven again, as he had also seen it before, and Jesus,
having pitied the thing coming to be, rise from the superheavenly throne and
come down as far as them and stretch out a good hand, and the angels taking
hold together with him, one from one side and another from the other, holding

TO TITUS THE HIERARCH
The holy Timothy, O noble Titus, I do not know whether he has gone away
unhearing of any of the theological symbols that have been distinguished by
me. But in the Symbolic Theology we have clarified for him all the things of
the oracles concerning God that seem to the many to be marvel-tales. For they
smear a terrible absurdity upon the incomplete of the souls, whenever the
fathers of the ineffable wisdom through certain hidden and daring riddles
disclose the divine and mystical truth, untrodden for the profane. Wherefore
we many also disbelieve the words concerning the divine mysteries; for we view
them only through the sensible symbols that have grown upon them. But it is
necessary also, having stripped them, to see them become naked and pure by
themselves. For thus viewing we would revere, seeing a "fountain of life"
poured into itself and standing upon itself, and a certain one power, simple,
self-moved, self-acting, not leaving itself, but being a knowledge of all
knowledges and always viewing itself through itself.

We thought it necessary then that for him and for others there be unfolded by
us, as we were able, the manifold shapings of the symbolic sacred-fashioning
concerning God. For the things outside of it, of how much unpersuasive and
fabricated marvel-working have they been filled? As, upon the superessential
theogony, fashioning a womb of God generating God bodily, and a word poured
forth into air from a manly heart belching it out, and describing a spirit
being breathed out from a mouth, and hymning for us in a body-fitting way
theogonic bosoms embracing a son of God, or fashioning these things


in a plantlike way and putting forth certain trees and shoots and flowers and
roots, or fountains of waters bubbling up, or light-generations leading forth
radiances, or certain other sacred-writings disclosive of superessential
theologies; and upon the intelligible providences of God or gifts or
manifestations or powers or properties or lots or remaining or processions or
distinctions or unions, fashioning around God a man-fashioning and a
many-shapedness of beasts and of other animals and of plants and of stones,
and putting around him womanish ornaments or barbarian armings, and setting
before him pottery and a melting-furnace as for some artisan, and spreading
under him horses and chariots and thrones, and preparing certain cook-like
feasts, and fashioning him as having drunk and being drunk and sleeping and
being hung over.

What would one say of the angers, the griefs, the oaths of every kind, the
repentances, the curses, the wraths, the many-formed and crooked sophistries
of the promising of sin; the gigantomachy in Genesis, according to which he is
said from fear to plot against those powerful men, and these having devised
the building not upon injustice of certain others, but for the salvation of
themselves; and that counsel in heaven being prepared upon deceit and
deception of Ahab; and the material and harlot-like many-passions of the
bodies, and whatever other sacred tokens of the God-fashioning that dares all
things there are, appearing as things put forth of the hidden, and multiplied
and divisible of the unified and indivisible, and typical and many-formed of
the unformed and untypical; of which if someone should be able to see the
well-fittedness hidden within, he will find all things mystical and godlike
and filled with much theological light.

For let us not think that the apparent things of the tokens have been
fashioned for themselves, but that they have been put forth of the ineffable
and unseen-by-the-many science, so that the all-holy things may not be easy
for the profane; but that they may be unveiled only to the genuine lovers of
the deity, as putting away all the childish imagination upon the sacred
symbols and being sufficient to pass over by simplicity of mind and fitness of
contemplative power toward the simple and supernatural and super-seated truth
of the symbols.

Besides one must also think this, that the tradition of the theologians is
twofold, the one ineffable and mystical, the other manifest and more known,
and the one symbolic and perfective, the other philosophic and demonstrative;
and the unsayable is interwoven with the sayable. And the one persuades and
binds down the truth of the things said, but the other acts and seats in God
by the undaught mystic-leadings.

And indeed not even according to the rites of the most holy mysteries did the
sacred mystics of the tradition among us or of the legal one abstain from the
God-fitting symbols; but we also see the all-holiest angels bringing forth the
divine things mystically through riddles, and Jesus himself theologizing in
parables and handing down the God-working mysteries through a typical
table-setting. For it was likely not only that the holy of holies be kept
undefiled for the many, but also that human life itself, being at once
undivided and divided, be illumined with the divine knowledges in a way proper
to itself, and that it mark off the impassible of the soul toward the simple
and most inward spectacles of the godlike images, and that it tend the
passible of it connaturally and at the same time stretch it up toward the most
divine by the pre-devised


fashionings of the typical symbols, since such veils are connate, and they
make clear, as many as, having heard a clear theology even outside of
coverings, fashion in themselves a certain type that leads them by the hand
toward the intellection of the said theology.

And even the world-working of all that appears has been put forth of the
invisibles of God, as both Paul and the true account say. Wherefore the
theologians also oversee some things politically and legally, and some things
purgatively and undefiledly, and some things humanly and in a middle way, and
some things supermundanely and perfectively; and at one time from the laws
that appear, at another time from the unapparent ordinances, according to what
is fitting to the underlying sacred letters and minds and souls. For the whole
account lying before them and through all things has not a bare history, but a
living completion.

We too therefore must, instead of the popular assumption concerning them, pass
within the sacred symbols in a sacred-fitting way and not dishonor them, being
offspring and impressions of the divine characters and manifest images of the
ineffable and supernatural spectacles. For not only the superessential lights
and the intelligible and simply the divine things are diversified by typical
symbols, as the superessential God is called "fire" and the intelligible
oracles of God "fired." Moreover even the godlike orders of the intelligible
and at once intellective angels are described in various forms and in
many-shaped and fiery figurings. And one must take the same image of fire said
of the God beyond intellection in one way, and in another way according to his
intelligible providences or words, and in another way upon the angels, the one
according to cause, the one according to existence, the one according to
participation, and other things otherwise, as the contemplation according to
them and the scientific arrangement bound them. And one must not confuse the
sacred symbols as it happens, but unfold them fittingly to the causes or the
existences or the powers or the orders or the dignities, of which they are
also disclosive tokens.

And that we may not write beyond what is due, one must go already upon the
inquiry itself that has been set forth by us. And we say that every food is
perfective of those being fed, filling up their incompleteness and their want
and healing the weak and guarding their life and making it bloom again and
renewing and granting them a vital well-feeling and, simply, a driver-out of
the distressing and of the incomplete and a supplier of their gladness and of
their completeness.

Well then is the superswise and good Wisdom hymned by the oracles as setting a
mystic mixing-bowl and pouring forth its sacred drink, or rather before these
setting forth the solid foods and with a high proclamation itself, in a
good-fitting way, reaching after those who need it. The divine Wisdom then
sets forth a twofold food, the one solid and abiding, the other liquid and
poured forth; and upon a mixing-bowl it supplies its providential goodnesses.
Let the mixing-bowl then, being circular and opened out, be a symbol of the
unbegun and unending providence of the whole, unfolded at once and going about
upon all. Yet since even going forth upon all it remains in itself and has
stood in unmoved sameness and is seated wholly undeparting from itself, the
mixing-bowl itself also has stood remainingly and fixedly. And Wisdom is also
said to build a house for herself


and in it to set forth both the solid foods and the drinks and the
mixing-bowl, so that it is clear to those who compare the divine things in a
God-fitting way that the cause of being and of well-being for all is also an
all-complete providence, and it goes forth upon all and comes to be in the all
and contains all things, and again the same in the same according to
preeminence is nothing in nothing according to nothing; but it has been
exempted from the whole, itself being in itself identically and everlastingly,
and having stood and remaining and always being according to the same and
likewise, and in no way coming to be outside itself, nor leaving its own seat
and unmoved remaining and hearth; but even in it working-good the whole and
all-complete providences, and going forth upon all and remaining upon itself
and always standing and being moved, and neither standing nor being moved,
but, as one might say, having the providential activities in the remaining and
the remaining in the providing, connaturally at once and supernaturally.

But what is the solid food and what the liquid? For the good Wisdom is hymned
as both granting these and providing. The solid food then I think bears a
token of the intellective and abiding completeness and sameness, according to
which the divine things are participated according to a standing and possible
and unified and undivided knowledge by those intellective senses, to which the
most divine Paul, having received from the Wisdom, imparts of the really solid
food; but the liquid, of the diffusive inflow that at once also hastens to go
forth upon all, and still through various and many and divided things leads by
the hand those being fed, in a way proper to them by goodness, upon the simple
and unshaken knowledge of God. Wherefore the divine and intelligible oracles
are also likened to dew and water and to milk and wine and honey, through
their life-generating power as in water and growth-making as in milk and
re-enlivening as in wine and purifying at once and guarding as in honey; for
these things the divine Wisdom grants to those who approach, supplying and
overflowing an inflow of ungrudging and unfailing feastings. This is the truly
to feast, and through these it is hymned as life-making at once and
child-nurturing and renewing and perfective.

cause of all goods, is said to be drunk, through the overfull and
beyond-intellection complete and unspeakable unmeasure of the feasting, or to
speak more properly, of the well-being of God. For as among us drunkenness

TO JOHN THE THEOLOGIAN, APOSTLE AND EVANGELIST,
CONFINED IN THE ISLAND OF PATMOS
I greet you, the holy soul, beloved, and this is for me toward you more
particular than toward the many. Hail, truly beloved, very beloved to the
really lovable and desired and beloved. What is wonderful, if Christ speaks
truly and the unjust drive the disciples out of the cities, assigning to
themselves the things according to their worth, and the accursed being
separated and going away from the holy? Truly the visible things are manifest
images of the invisible. For not even in the ages that are coming will God be
cause of the just separations from him, but those who have wholly separated
themselves from God. Just as we also see the others already from here coming
to be with God, since, being lovers of truth, they withdraw from the
attachment of the material things, and in all freedom from all evils and in
divine eros of all the goods they love peace and the sanctification, and from
the present life they begin the coming one, living as citizens in an
angel-fitting way in the midst of men with all impassibility and divine naming
and holiness and the other goods.

I would never then be so mad concerning you as to think that you suffer
anything, but I believe that even the passions of body you perceive according
to judging them only. But those who do you injustice and think to confine the
sun of the Gospel I, justly accusing, pray that, having left these things
which they do upon themselves, they may turn toward the good and may draw you
to themselves


and may partake of the light.

But not even the contrary will deprive us of the all-shining ray of John, we
who now shall meet with the memory and renewal of your true theology, and a
little later (for I will speak, even if it is bold) shall be united to you
yourselves. And I am in every way trustworthy, having both learned from God
the things foreknown to you and saying that you will also be released from the
imprisonment in Patmos and will return to the Asian land and will do there
imitations of the good God and will hand them down to those after you.
