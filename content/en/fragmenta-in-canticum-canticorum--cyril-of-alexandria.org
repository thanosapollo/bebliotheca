Of Saint Cyril of Alexandria, Fragments on the Song of Songs.
Myrrh poured out is your name. It makes clear also the name called upon
all the nations of the Only-begotten, according to which all the nations
breathe fragrance toward him, bearing his appellation. Therefore maidens,
and so on. Paul also was a maiden, saying, I can do all things in Christ
who strengthens me. Therefore he loved exceedingly, saying: Who shall
separate us from the love of Christ? But how did they draw him? By
following in his footsteps, and by partaking of the gospel myrrhs. But if
we run having [cod. having], the word of the =69.1280= young men, we
shall walk after the Lord our God. What kingdom does he hail as a
treasury in present things? They made me keeper of the vineyards, and so
on. Perhaps she also accuses herself, that she did not keep her own
vineyard, letting it be ravaged by everyone passing by the way; and for
it to be laid waste out of the thicket, and for a solitary wild beast to
ravage it. Perhaps she also glorifies God, that, not having been able to
keep her own vineyard, by neither tilling nor keeping it she advanced so
far as to be able to keep many vineyards. Then by the sons of her mother
he means those of the Synagogue, the Scribes and Pharisees, who warred
against those believing in Christ, commanding to keep the worship
according to the law; for this is the vineyard. But I, he says, did not
keep it; for whatever things were gains to me, these I have counted loss
for Christ, as the blessed Paul says. As one veiled beside the flocks of
your companions. He calls the apostles his companions. For he said: No
longer do I call you servants, but friends; for you are friends. But by
veiled she means covered; Paul saying, that Until this day, whenever
Moses is read, a veil lies upon their heart. The voice then, the
Synagogue repenting, to Christ: Make yourself plain to me, so that not
[cod. to me], shepherding the Churches amid your companions, I remain
holding the veil upon my heart. If you know not yourself. That is, if you
do not repent, he says, you will be found behind the nations; as not
having fruits of righteousness, remaining fruitless, and behind the
flocks that I gathered for myself. I have likened you to my horse. That
is, having received my faith, O Church, you were likened to the apostles;
concerning whom it is, You shall mount upon your horses, and your riding
shall be salvation; who also were arrayed against the chariots of the
noetic Pharaoh. Your cheeks are as a turtledove. That is, he presents her
modesty toward the bridegroom by means of the turtledove; for the cheeks
of the turtledove are red.
But to the Synagogue the opposite: The look of a harlot has come to be
for you; you were shameless toward all. By neck he means the submissive
part of the soul; for how shall a beautiful world, disciplining its neck,
bear the yoke of Christ? Then silver in Scripture is customarily the
=69.1281= divine teaching; but gold, the divine gifts. But all things,
before the sojourn of Christ, were likenesses and shadow of the things to
come, according to the Apostle, of good things. But Christ having risen
has granted the perfect things to men; but before the resurrection we had
likenesses, from the praises [it is so in the codex] of him, both of the
law and of the prophets. By nard and by an alabaster vial of stacte the
burial of Christ is signified; which Nicodemus, bringing, wrapped his
body. But the bride bears the two Testaments as breasts, between which
Christ encamps. He says she is beautiful, not as before saying only
among women, but now also as near to himself; the bridegroom redoubles
his praise of the bride, praising both her contemplative and her
practical part. But by eyes he means those in the mind and in the heart;
and as of a dove, the far-seeing, according to which it is taken also of
the Spirit. He says then that the bride received a mind that beholds God
alone. But the eyes are also the guileless and sharp-sighted teachers of
the Church. I am the flower of the field, a lily of the valleys. As the
bride, having received the rain from above and having brought forth
virtue, likens herself to the flower of the field; and she shows how much
the sojourn of Christ profited, so much indeed that he showed even the
valleys, unsuited to every sowing, full of such flowers. For he calls a
valley that which was emptied of the demons that dwelt in it of old.
Make my bed among the apples. Guard me with the evangelical words; for
these breathe Christ fragrantly to us. But, he says, may I too be set in
order with his saints; for as he himself is an apple, so those who are of
one body with him and partakers of him are apples. His left hand, and so
on. The law is the left hand; the Gospel the right. Or also the present
life is the left hand, the life to come the right, which will embrace me
when he says to those on the right hand: Come, you blessed of my Father.
And he says elsewhere: Length of life and years of life are in her right
hand; and in her left hand, riches and glory. Her right hand at least is
the knowledge of divine things, from which is eternal life; but her left,
the knowledge of human things, from which are riches and glory. He says
then, that My mind excels the human things; and the divine knowledge will
cover me. For it has been said again also: Prize her, that she may
embrace you.
He shows then that the same one is both our bridegroom and our archer,
and that the purified soul is both bride and arrow; as an arrow then it
directs its aim toward the good. But as a bridegroom he takes her up into
communion of the incorruptible eternity, length of life and years of
life, granting through the right hand; but through the left, the riches
of the eternal goods and the glory of God, of which those who seek the
glory of the world become without share. For this reason he says, that
His left hand is under my head, by which the arrow is directed under the
=69.1284= mark; but his right hand, taking hold of me for himself and
drawing me, makes me light for an upward course; and being sent there,
and not separated from the archer, rather to repose. Then he turns the
word to the daughters of the Jerusalem above. But the word is an
exhortation brought in under an oath, that love abound and increase
always, until he make his own will active, who wills all men to be saved
and to come to the knowledge of the truth. By the left hand he hints, as
it seems, at the good things of the present life, of which he already has
the enjoyment; but by the right hand, at the expectation in promises of
the things to come; the enjoyment of which, as has been said, is laid up,
he says, in promises and hopes. Good also is the saying, to be embraced
by the right hand, but by the left to rest the head; for the things of
the present life, even if they be thought very good and desirable, must
be subordinated to the head of the perfect soul, yielding only the
necessary use to the body, as the pillow to the head. But the things of
the age to come, since as divine they stand above the human nature, he
hinted through the embrace at that which excels. Perhaps, however, since
the hands are a symbol of actions, and the actions of the left hand
signify the things of the body, but those of the right hand, the things
of the soul; the right having received the better lot, the necessities of
the body are contained. For as the embracing hand is both above and holds
the embraced thing within its arm, so the actions appointed toward the
mark of the coming life, encircling every irrational movement of the
body, guard the life well ordered and bound together; nor is even one
able to plot against the soul embraced by the bridegroom.

These things for the clearness of those who are led on dramatically, as
far as, Your voice is sweet, and your countenance is comely. And it is
clear that the soul, the bride of the Word, that is, the Church of
Christ, apprehends the voice as divine before she understands it; which
we the faithful experience, before we understand the legal and prophetic
voices, being struck with awe as full of divine grace. Such is that, The
voice of my kinsman, appointed for the appearing from afar of the Word;
beholding him touching great contemplations, together with not despising
even the lesser things, she says: Behold, he himself comes leaping upon
the mountains, bounding over the hills. But through the whole discourse,
some things are said of the bridegroom as present; some things as though
he were sought by the bride; since also of the problems at times we seek
some, at a loss for the solution; but at times we partake of the
solution, the bridal Word illumining our hearts. Then again we are at a
loss in other things, and again he appears to us; and this many times,
until, having been perfected, we may obtain of the bridegroom not only
his coming to us, but also his making his abode. And the Church longs,
being forsaken amid temptations, but he is manifested to her in the
gifts. Therefore he says: Behold, he says, this one comes leaping
=69.1285= upon the mountains.
But he comes also upon the nets spread by the evil one near the Church;
which, tearing, he teaches to tread upon them with contempt. At the
crossing of the Church through every storm of temptation, the signs in
the air are shown, and that the summer draws near, concerning which in
the Psalms: The summer and the spring. For flowers were seen by her, and
the complete cleansing, named the time of the harvest, has drawn near.
Peering through the nets. But perhaps he also made clear the apostles by
the fisher's craft. These things then the Church hears of the Word
through the prophetic windows and the legal nets, receiving the dawn of
truth, the typical wall of the teaching still standing, I mean the law,
which makes the shadow of the good things to come, not showing itself the
very image of the things; behind which the truth, standing, holding the
place, afterward shines forth. Or he spoke also of the harvest of the
cutting of the noetic reaping; concerning which the Savior says: The
harvest indeed is great. In the shelter of the rock, holding to the
outwork. In the shelter of the rock, holding to the outwork; safety being
made clear through the outwork; where having come to be, he says, she
will obtain every help. Turn away. She asks the bridegroom to leave
behind the fruitless Synagogue, and to come to those who from the hollow
and of old humbled and idol-worshipping soul have mounted to a heavenly
height. Upon my bed by nights I sought. He makes clear the women who came
on the first of the sabbaths, deep at dawn, to the tomb of Jesus, and did
not find him.
The then, upon the bed, or from the bed, he says; or she calls the tomb
of the Lord her own bed, according to which we are buried together with
him. But she did not find him, having heard: He is not here; for he is
risen. And the angels keeping watch found her, whom also she asks: Where
have you laid the Lord? But passing by those questioned, he met her,
saying: Rejoice. Therefore he says, as though a little I passed by from
them until I found him, and I will not let him go. For this one held his
feet, and heard: Touch me not. But by the house of the mother he says the
synagogue of the apostles is meant, into which, having gone, she
proclaimed the resurrection of Christ. Having burned as incense myrrh and
frankincense. Myrrh indeed, when she has been buried with him through the
frankincense; but frankincense, when, rising together with him, she has
been made partaker of his Godhead. And not by these alone has the
ecclesiastical soul been made fragrant, but also by varied contemplations
of knowledge. For he who divides with the utmost exactness and searches
the things that are finely even to the smallest detail might be said to
be in a manner a perfumer, thinning and bringing to powder all the
sayings of the sweet fragrance; which now the bride is said =69.1288= to
burn as incense.

But perhaps also he who lives not according to the flesh but according
to the Spirit, in no wise fattening his heart, preserving the varied and
manifold fragrance, makes a fragrance from all with all of those now so
called powders of the perfumer. But one might say also that the holy and
ecclesiastical soul, once the child forsaken of God, ascends from the
desert, from the synagogue of the nations; having left behind the
doctrines and words and deeds, the deserts of God, and ascending to the
things of God. But let the spiritual union be understood as gold, the
precious and divine one. For the ark in the desert was both within and
without gold, left for the manifestation of the divine union. But the
purple, the multitude of those called to the kingdom, treads upon it. But
when someone believes, he receives Christ in his heart, who is a precious
pearl. But also the litter, he says, has been made from the daughters of
Jerusalem for love alone: For God so loved the world, that he sent his
Only-begotten Son; that everyone believing in him should not perish, but
have eternal life.
From the nations, he says, is the Church by that: Come out and see. But
he calls the day of the passion the day of the wedding, on which he
wedded the Church through his own blood. Her silence signifies the
obedience in all things according to: Be silent and hear, O Israel. Or
also two fawns, the moral and the dogmatic teaching, the two breasts of
the bride; from which those nourished by them see with keenness toward
God. But the nostrils, the fragrance of the apostolic and evangelical
words, which we graze until the consummation. Come from Lebanon, bride.
He teaches us whence the bride, and that she comes from idolatry; for
Lebanon is an idol-struck mountain. From there you shall come, he says;
but you shall pass through the law; for, being ignorant of it, you were
catechized in the mystery of Christ. You have wounded our heart, my
sister, my bride. That is, you have led us into longing for yourself by
one word of the confession, which you held rightly, seeing with your
inward eyes; for, having submitted to the yoke upon your neck, you made
the confession. These things are said to her by the friends of the
bridegroom, angelic, I say, powers. For since the faculty of sight is
twofold, one seeing the truth, and the other wandering among the vain
things; since the pure eye of the bride has been opened upon the nature
of the good alone, while the other is idle; for this reason the friends
bring the praise to one of the eyes; calling her sister for the kinship
through impassibility, but bride for the union with the Word. Since then,
he says, your eye is one in =69.1289= seeing the One, and your soul one,
because it is not divided toward different dispositions, and the posture
of your neck has the perfect, having lifted upon itself the divine yoke
(for this is the yoke upon your neck, that is, the yoke of the Lord); for
this reason we confess that you have wounded us with the wonders; which
is that you wrought in us a certain soul and mind toward the
comprehension of the light by yourself. For in you we contemplate the sun
of righteousness as in a mirror; but through the second saying they add
credibility to what is said.
A garden enclosed. It has been closed to the world, but it has been
opened to the heavenly bridegroom. But the fountain has been sealed by
the Holy Spirit, with whom we are anointed after baptism. For the virtues
are signified by the aromatics. But the woods of Lebanon, he says, are
the prophets. But by the myrrh and the aloes, that Christ, having been
buried, communicated with the saints who had gone before; for, having
gone down into Hades, he led them up. In the cross, he says, taking upon
himself death for men; but the heart keeps watch, according to which as
God he despoiled Hades. That is, what has your kinsman made, or what do
you have from your kinsman? The lips dripping myrrh he calls the lips
confessing the death. His legs are pillars of marble. But these would
also be bases. For those being built are built up upon the foundation of
the apostles and prophets. Accordingly he praises the legs after the
belly, which he says are pillars of marble founded upon bases of gold.
But the pillars of the Church are, for instance, Peter and John, whose
foundation Christ was called gold. But they are marble, whom Paul called
steadfast; because of the steadfast and unmoved, and bearing and propping
up the common body of the Church by the brilliant life and the sound
word. But also to love God from the whole heart, and the neighbor as
oneself, is, as it were by pillars of marble, to bear the common body of
the Church. For he who has been perfected in these two commandments is
made a pillar and steadfastness of the Church; so that by these two
achievements the whole body of the Church is propped as it were upon
certain legs, the golden foundation of the base according to faith making
the thoughts unswerving and immovable and firm in every good.
I to him the faith, and he to me the kingdom. But he feeds in the purity
of the Scriptures and of the holy doctrines. From those disobedient to
you turn =69.1292= away your eyes, and incline toward me; for they
themselves, he says, have roused love in me [cod. you]. The work of a
craftsman. But he names Christ the Craftsman as the Maker; for as he
became obedient, so also the Church. And he says also her sobriety and
her subjection; this soul of hers being subjected to faith, but the body
to sobriety. But he makes clear also her priesthood, saying: Not lacking
the mingled draught, that is, the blood of Christ; for this is in the
hands of the priests. But by her neck he means the deacons of Christ,
because they bear for Christ the head of the Church. For they bear his
holy body in a pure conscience, confessing his mysteries. Therefore he
has spoken of ivory, of what sort Paul says the deacons are, reverend,
not attending to much wine. A king bound in the tresses. Or in another
way, he says, being led by the bond of the love of the peoples into a
royal priesthood. Or also in your delight, in your love, because of being
counted among some even of the righteous. But the breasts are likened to
the clusters, for those having the spiritual gladness. Or that I bring
forward the confession, and upon me is his return; either at the second
coming, or even from the people of the Jews. Therefore the food of the
bride is sanctified, Christ dining with her. That is, the Church prays
that the sojourn of the Savior come to be in Judea, according to the
providence of him who gives him to men, that he may be suckled by the
Mother of God. Having found you. That is, outside Jerusalem, where he was
crucified. Or he means the burial, which Joseph and Nicodemus carried
out. But by the henna he means the saints. Her battlements are
battlements of fire. The walls, he says, of the Church are fire.
To the spiritual Solomon a vineyard came to be; he means the Church. But
Beelamon is interpreted: Among those who have believed. But who have
believed? Those who have received his commandments. The one sitting in
the gardens oversees the Churches; but the companions attend, and do not
=69.1293= judge; but all judgment the Son makes. But the bride desires to
hear: Enter into the joy of your Lord. But she deems him worthy also to
cast out the wicked, saying: Flee, come to the saints.
