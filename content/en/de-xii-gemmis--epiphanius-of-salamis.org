* On the Twelve Gems
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of our father among the saints Epiphanius, Bishop of Cyprus, to
Diodorus, Bishop of Tyre, a book concerning the twelve stones that are
in the vestments of Aaron.
PROLOGUE
1.1 The first stone is the sardius, thus called the Babylonian; it is
fiery in appearance and bloodlike, resembling a pickled sardine;
therefore it is also called sardios, taking its name from the
appearance. It occurs in Babylon toward Assyria. The stone is
translucent; and it has a healing power, which physicians use for
swellings and other wounds caused by iron. There is also another
sardonyx which is called molochas; it is emollient of fatty tumors, and
it happens to be of the same appearance, somewhat greenish, but it is
most substantial rather toward the beginning of spring, when the
beginning of the Passions.
1.2 The stone topaz, red in appearance beyond the carbuncle; it occurs
in Topaze, a city of India, by those who once quarried stones there, in
the heart of another stone which the quarrymen, having seen it bright,
and having shown it as alabaster to certain people, sold for a small
price. And Thebans presented it to the queen of that time; and she,
taking it, set it in her diadem in the middle of her forehead. And the
stone has a test of this kind: when rubbed on a medical whetstone it
does not give off a juice red according to the color, but milky; and it
fills as many mixing-bowls as the one rubbing it off may wish, and it is
not lessened at all from its former weight. And this juice from it is
also useful for a disease of the eyes; and when drunk it counteracts
dropsies and those wasting away from sea-grape.
1.3 The stone emerald. This is also called prasinon; it is also green
in appearance, and there is a certain difference among them; for some
call them Neronian, others Domitian. And the Neronian is bitter, very
green in appearance, translucent and gleaming. And it is called
Neronian or Domitian for this reason, that they say Nero or Domitian
put oil in sufficient vessels, and from the rust over time the oil was
made green, and from this the rock, being watered, blossomed more
abundantly in color. But others say that a certain Nero, a craftsman of
the ancient tablet-makers, or stone-workers, discovered the most
essential quality of the emerald, and from this it is called Neronian,
and others Domitian. But there are also other emeralds, the first in
Judea very like the Neronian, and the other in Ethiopia, which is also
said to have come into being in the river Pishon. And Pishon is the one
called Indus among the Greeks, and Ganges among the barbarians. And they
say that the carbuncle also occurs in it; for there, says Moses, is the
carbuncle and the green stone. And the power, they say, of the stone,
that is of the emerald, is for mirroring a face. And among the
myth-makers it is also said to be prognostic.
1.4 The stone carbuncle. This has a sharp crimson appearance. It occurs
in Carthage of Libya, which is called Africa. But others say this stone
is found thus, not observed by day, but at night from afar after the
manner of a torch, or of a sparking coal, and as shining from a
distance, and from this those searching for it, recognizing it, easily
find it; but when it is being carried it is impossible for it to escape
notice, for in whatever garments it may be hidden its gleam appears
outside the wrapping, whence it has also been called carbuncle. And the
ceraunius stone is a little similar to this, because it is also found
from the same place.
1.5 The stone sapphire, purplish, like the appearance of the dark
purple of the murex. And there are many kinds of this; for there is the
royal, gold-spotted; but this is not so much admired as the one that is
purple throughout. And this too is said to be in India and Ethiopia.
Therefore they say that among the Indians the precinct of Dionysus has
365 steps of sapphire stone, even if to most people it is unbelievable.
And the stone is marvelous and most beautiful and most graceful.
Therefore they also set it in ornaments and necklaces, especially kings.
And it is pain-dispelling; for when rubbed it also heals the wounds that
arise from blisters and tumors, with milk, being smeared on the
ulcerated places. And it is also written in the Law that the vision
shown to Moses on the mountain, and the legislation given, is said to
have come to be upon a sapphire stone.
1.6 The stone jasper. This is emerald-like in appearance; and it is
found by the banks of the river Thermodon, and around Amathus in Cyprus.
But there is a numerous kind called Amathusian; the appearance of the
stone is of this sort, it is greenish after the emerald, but duller and
dimmer; and within it has a green body, resembling verdigris of copper
that has veins in four rows. And we have heard this to be of illusion,
as the mythologists say. And another is more sea-grey than the sea,
deeper in bloom and in dye; and another in the caves, on Mount Ida in
Phrygia, resembling that from the blood of the murex, more translucent,
as if resembling wine or yellower than amethyst; for it is not of one
color nor of the same power; but one is more porous and whiter, neither
gleaming very much, nor again falling short. And another is like the
water of crystal; and it is said by myth-makers to be a remedy of
illusions. And it was found among Iberians and shepherds of the
Hyrcanians in the Caspian land. And another jasper not very shining,
green, which has lines in the middle. And another jasper, the one called
ancient, like either snow or sea-foam. The myth-makers say that the wild
beasts in the field fear this, and the phantoms.
1.7 The stone ligure. We have in no way come to know the finding of
this, neither among naturalists, nor among any ancients who have been
concerned about these things. But we found a stone thus called
langourion, which some in the clear dialect call lagourion; and perhaps
I think this is the ligure, since the divine scriptures altered the
names otherwise, as the emerald prasinon, and because somehow in the
naming of these stones they did not mention also hyacinth, although it
is a conspicuous and honored stone; so that we take it into mind whether
perhaps the divine scripture calls this the ligure. Hyacinth then has
various appearances; for the deeper the stone is found in color, so much
more essential it is than the others. And hyacinth resembles wool, or is
somewhat purplish. Therefore the divine scripture also says that the
priestly garments have been adorned from hyacinth and purple. And the
first stone is called thalassites, the second rhodinos, the third
nativos; the fourth is called channiaios, and the fifth perileukios.
And these are found in the inner barbarian land of Scythia. And these
stones, besides being very precious, also have such an activity: when
thrown into coals of fire they themselves are not harmed, but they
quench the coals; and not only this, but if someone taking the stone
wraps it in linen and sets it upon coals of fire, not even the covering
linen itself is burned, but it remains unharmed. And the stone is said
to be useful to women in childbirth toward good offspring; and it is
also a driver-away of phantoms.
1.8 The stone agate. This was supposed to be the one called perileukos,
which is found under the hyacinth; and it is marvelous, somewhat bluish
in appearance, having a white circumference outside, after the manner of
marble or ivory. And this too is found around Scythia. And there is from
these stones an agate having the color of a lion. And when rubbed with
water and smeared upon the bite of a wild beast it turns away the venom
of a scorpion and of vipers and of such things.
1.9 The stone amethyst. This along its circumference is deeply
flame-colored; and it is whiter from the middle, sending forth a
wine-like appearance. And its form is various; and perhaps it too occurs
in the mountains of Libya; for one of them is similar to a pure
hyacinth, and another to amphikochlos. It occurs on the shores of the
sea of the same Libya.
=1.10= The stone chrysolite. Some have called this chrysophyllon. It is
gold-gleaming; and it is found in the two-stoned well beside the wall of
Achaemenid Babylon; for they call Babylon and that well Achaemenid,
because, they say, the father of Cyrus the king was called Achaemenes.
And it is also gold-sprinkled. This, when rubbed and drunk, is healing
for those with stomach and bowel complaints.
=1.11= The stone beryl is somewhat grey-blue, sea-dyed, having also the
appearance of the more watery hyacinth. And it occurs about the end of
the mountain called Taurus. And if someone should wish to refract this
opposite the sun, it appears as glass having translucent millet-grains
within. And another beryl is similar to the pupils of the eyes of the
dragon. And there is yet another again like wax; and this appeared about
the outlet of the river Euphrates.
=1.12= The stone onyx. This stone has a very yellow color. And they say
that the brides of kings or also of rich men delight in this stone, who
also, having turned it into their own drinking-cups, possess this stone.
And there are also other onychites called by the same name, which are
similar to honey-yellow wax; and some say they have been solidified from
dripping water. And they call them onychites in a natural-historical
way, since the nail of well-bred men is made like marble together with
the appearance of blood; and others somewhere also call the marble stone
from Docimium onychite, falsely so named, because of the purity of the
whiteness.
