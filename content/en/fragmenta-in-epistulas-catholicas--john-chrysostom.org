CHAPTER I.
Count it all joy, my brothers, when you fall into various trials. For
affliction is a kind of unbreakable bond, an increase of love, an occasion of
compunction and piety. For hear him who says, If you come to serve the Lord,
prepare your soul for trial. And Christ again said, In the world you have
affliction; but take courage. And again, Narrow and afflicted is the way. You
see affliction praised everywhere, everywhere taken up as being necessary for
us. For if in the contests outside no one would take a crown without this,
unless he also fortify himself with toils and observation of foods and a
prescribed diet and vigils and countless other things, much more here. Blessed
is the man who endures trial, because having become approved he will receive
the crown of life, which the Lord promised to those who love him. Having
sufficiently exhorted them to undergo the trials with joy, in order that
approved work and perfect endurance may come to be; and these are perfected
when they are done for themselves, and not done for something else; through
another exhortation he attempts to persuade them to accomplish the matters at
hand; saying through a promise that the one who endures trial is blessed. For
thus he will become a man approved, trained through all things, conducting the
contest athletically. And to the one thus appearing out of the grim things, a
crown of life will be given, prepared by God for those who love him. Thus he
who by enduring trial despises painful things and death itself will receive a
crown of eternal life. You ask, what is the material of which God prepared a
crown of life for those who love him? There are "the things God prepared for
those who love him," which through greatness of divinity are not subject to
sight and hearing, nor have they gone up even upon the bare thought of a man.
Then desire, having conceived, bears sin; and sin, having been completed,
brings forth death. For while we are doing the sin, being drunk with the
pleasure, we do not so perceive. But when it has come to be and has taken an
end, then especially, the =64.1041= pleasure having been wholly quenched, the
bitter sting of the thought comes in, opposite to women in labor. For in their
case, before the birth, the toil is great and sharp pangs cut them with pains;
but after the birth there is relief, the pain having gone out together with
the infant. But here it is not so; but while we are in labor and conceiving
the corrupted thoughts, we are glad and rejoice; but when we have brought
forth the wicked child, sin, then seeing the shame of what was born, we are in
pain, then we are cut more grievously than women in labor. Therefore I exhort
you not to receive at all, especially at the beginning, a corrupted desire;
but if we have also received it, to smother the seeds within. And if we have
been slack even up to this, to kill again the sin that has gone out into deed,
through confession and tears, through accusing oneself; for nothing is so
destructive of sin as accusation and condemnation. Pure and undefiled religion
before God and the Father is this, to visit orphans and widows in their
affliction, to keep oneself unspotted from the world. This is that by which we
can be made equal to God, in having mercy and compassion. When therefore we do
not have this, we have been deprived of the whole. He did not say, If you fast
you are like your Father; for none of these things is with God, nor does God
work any of these things,


but what? Become compassionate as your Father who is in the heavens. This is
God's work. If therefore you do not have this, what do you have? I desire
mercy, he says, and not sacrifice.
CHAPTER II.
My brothers, do not hold the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ of glory in
respect of persons. For what reason do you think greatly of yourself, he says?
Or why again does another cheapen himself? Are we not all one body, both great
and small? When therefore according to the head we are one and members of one
another, why do you puff yourself up with arrogance? Why are you ashamed of
the brother? For just as he is your member, so also you are his, and in this
there is much equality of honor. Have you not distinguished among yourselves
and become judges of evil thoughts? Being of the same mind toward one another.
The poor man has arrived; become according to him in mind; do not cast down a
greater bulk because of wealth; there is not poor and rich in Christ. Do not
then be ashamed because of the outward wrapping, but receive him because of
the inward faith. Hear, my beloved brothers, did not God choose the poor of
the world, ri =64.1044= ch in faith, and heirs of the kingdom which he
promised to those who love him? But you have dishonored the poor. But we, as
if we had been wronged in the greatest things, thus insult those who beg, we
turn away; do you not give something, and you also grieve? Admonish, Paul
said, as brothers, do not insult as enemies; he who admonishes his brother
does not do this publicly; he does not parade the insult, but privately and
with much attention, both in pain and bitten with tears and lamenting. Let us
then furnish it from a brotherly mind, let us admonish from a brotherly
purpose, not as pained at the giving, but as pained at his transgressing the
commandment unto gain. For if after giving you insult, you ruin the pleasure
of the gift. But when you neither give and you insult, how much evil have you
not worked upon that wretched and miserable man? He came as one who would be
pitied by you, but having received a mortal blow he went away, and wept the
more. For when he is forced through want to beg, and through begging is
insulted, see how great is the punishment for the one who insults. He provokes
the one who made him; for if he himself left him poor for you, that you might
have to tend him, and you insult the one who is poor for your sake, of how
great ingratitude are these things? of how great thanklessness is this deed!
Do not the rich oppress you, and they themselves drag you into courts? Do they
not themselves blaspheme the good name that was called upon you? Bear the
greedy seizures nobly. They destroy themselves, not you. They deprive you of
money, but they strip themselves of the goodwill of God and of the help; and
he who has been deprived of that, even if he wrap himself in all the wealth of
the inhabited world, is poorer than all. So speak and so do, as those about to
be judged through a law of freedom. For it is better to frighten you with
words, that you may not be pained by the deeds. Mercy boasts against judgment.
Almsgiving is a kind of best art and a protectress of those who work it. For
she is a friend of God, and always stands near him, easily asking grace for
those she wishes, only if she is not wronged by us; and she is wronged when we
work her from plunder, so that if she is pure, she gives much boldness to
those who send her up; and she intercedes even for those who have offended, so
great is her strength, and for those who have sinned; she bursts the bonds,
looses the darkness, quenches the fire, puts the worm to death, drives away
the gnashing of the teeth; =64.1045= by this the gates of the heavens are
opened with much fearlessness. And after a little: For she is truly a queen,
making men like God; For you shall be, he says, compassionate, as your Father
who is in the heavens; she is winged and light, having golden wings; having a
flight that greatly delights the angels.


There, Wings of a dove overlaid with silver, and her hinder parts in the
greenness of gold. As some golden and living dove she flies; having a gentle
eye, a tame eye; nothing is better than that eye. The peacock is beautiful,
but beside her a jackdaw; so this bird is something beautiful and wonderful.
She looks above through all; she is surrounded with much of the glory of God.
She is a virgin having golden wings, wrapped about, having a white face,
tame. She is winged and light, standing beside the royal throne. When we are
judged, she suddenly stands by and appears, and snatches us out of the
punishment, wrapping us with her wings; God wants this rather than
sacrifices. So also faith, if it does not have works, is dead by itself. But
someone will say, You have faith, and I have works; show me your faith from
your works; and I will show you from my works my faith. You believe that God
is one, you do well; the demons also believe, and shudder. For even if
someone believes rightly in the Father and in the Son and in the Holy Spirit,
but does not have a right life, there is no profit to him of the faith unto
salvation. Therefore when he says, For this is eternal life, that they may
know you the only true God, let us not think that what is said is enough for
us unto salvation; for we need also a life and a most pure way of living.
Faith is a great thing and saving, and without it it is never possible to be
saved; but it is not enough by itself to work this. But there is need also of
a right way of living; so that for this reason Paul also exhorts those
already counted worthy of the mysteries, saying: Let us be eager to enter
into that rest; let us be eager, he says, as if the faith were not enough,
but the life also needing to be set forward, and much eagerness to come to
be. For there is truly need also of much eagerness in order to enter into
heaven. For if those who suffered so many hardships in the wilderness were
not counted worthy of a land, and were not able to obtain a land, because
they murmured, and because they committed fornication, how shall we be
counted worthy of heaven, living indifferently and slackly? We need then much
eagerness. And again. Faith without works is dead. Reasonably; for just as a
beautiful and blooming body, when it does not have strength, but is like
things that have been painted, so is right faith without works.

CHAPTER III.
Let not many become teachers, my brothers; knowing that we shall receive a
greater judgment. Since teaching without doing not only brings no profit, but
also much damage and condemnation to the one who administers his own life with
such great inattention, cutting out the contentiousness of those who do not
wish to work, he forbade teaching to those who teach without work, laying on a
great judgment. For he for whom the things are not is condemned, just as also
he is perfect who does not stumble in work and word of the teaching, and is
able also to bridle the whole body. For if he teaches these things, and
defines the faith as right words together with splendid works agreeing with
the faith; clearly he bridles his whole body, not allowing it to have any
friendship toward the world. So also the tongue is a small member and boasts
greatly; behold how great a forest a little fire kindles; and the tongue is a
fire, the world of unrighteousness. Therefore guard the pupil more than the
tongue; the tongue is a royal horse. If then you put a bridle on it and teach
it to walk in good rhythm, the king rests upon it and sits upon it; but if you
leave it to be borne unbridled and to leap, it becomes a vehicle of the devil
and of the demons. But no one of men is able to tame the tongue; an
unrestrainable evil, full of death-bearing poison. The tongue is a sharpened
sword. But let us not bring wounds upon others; but let us


cut off our own putrefactions. Let the wise and understanding among you show
from the good manner of life his works, in meekness of wisdom. Let us cleanse
the filth from the ears of the soul. For just as filth and mud the ears of
the flesh, so also the worldly and thick narratives, and those concerning
interest and loans, more grievously than all filth stop up the hearing of the
mind, or rather not only stop it up, but also make it unclean.

CHAPTER IV.
Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you; cleanse hands, you sinners,
and purify hearts, you double-minded. Be wretched and mourn; let your laughter
be turned into mourning, and the joy into dejection. For he who after sinning
uses repentance is worthy not of mourning but of blessings, unto the choir of
the righteous; For say you your sins first, that you may be justified. But if
=64.1049= after sinning he is shameless, he is not so pitiable for having
fallen as for lying fallen. And if not repenting over sins is grievous, being
puffed up over the sins, of what sort of punishment is it worthy? For if he
who is lifted up over right deeds is unclean, he who suffers this over sins,
of what sort of pardon will he obtain?

CHAPTER V.
Come now, you rich, weep howling over your miseries that are coming upon you.
Your wealth has rotted, and your garments have become moth-eaten; your gold
and your silver have rusted. Let us lay hold of the narrow way; how long
luxury? how long ease? have we not been filled with being slack, laughing,
putting off? will not the same things be again, table and satiety and
extravagance and money and possession and buildings? And what is the profit?
death; and what is the end? ash and dust, coffins and worms. You lived in
luxury upon the earth, and lived wantonly; you nourished your hearts as in a
day of slaughter. What then? is luxury forbidden? and very much. Why then was
it created for partaking? because he also created bread, and the excess is
forbidden; because he also created wine, and the excess is forbidden. He
commands us then not to refuse the food as unclean, but as loosening the soul
through excess. For every creature of God is good, he says, and nothing is to
be thrown away when it is received with thanksgiving. Be long-suffering also
you, establish your hearts, because the coming of the Lord has drawn near.
Long-suffering toward one another, endurance toward those outside. For one is
long-suffering toward those whom it is possible also to avenge; but he endures
those whom he is not able to avenge; for this reason in the case of God
endurance is never said, but long-suffering in many places. If then even here
they seem to have been abandoned, nevertheless there they will enjoy much
glory. But when those who puff themselves greatly see those who are whipped by
them, those who are despised, those who are mocked, these being near God, then
they will lament and howl, when he leads the pitiable and miserable who have
suffered countless terrible things and have believed, into such great
splendor. But before all, my brothers, do not swear, neither by heaven, nor by
the earth, nor any other oath. What then if someone demands an oath, and
brings on necessity? Let the fear of God be stronger than the necessity.


Since if he is going to put forward such excuses, you will keep nothing of
the things commanded. And yet in the case of the laws of men you nowhere dare
to put this forward, nor to say it, but willing =64.1052= and unwilling you
accept the things written. And otherwise you will never even undergo
necessity; for he who has heard the beatitudes before and has prepared
himself such as Christ commanded, will undergo no such necessity from anyone,
being revered among all and august. What is the excess of the yes, and of the
no? The oath is not upon the swearing falsely; for no one needs to learn
that, that it is of the evil one and excess, but the contrary; but the excess
and the more is the oath. Elijah was a man of like passions with us, and he
prayed with prayer that it not rain, and it did not rain upon the earth three
years and six months; and again he prayed, and the heaven gave rain, and the
earth sprouted its fruit. In order that even thus, wasting away with the
famine, they might come to the Creator; in order that the famine might become
for them an occasion of salvation. For nothing, he says, is able to
discipline, except famine, in order that even thus being disciplined, they
may go toward the Creator of all. And after a little: The word of the prophet
went out, and at once the air was changed, the heaven became bronze, not
changing the nature, but bridling the energy. At once the elements were
reshaped. The word of the prophet fell like a violent fever into the flanks
of the earth, and all things at once were dried up, and all things were made
desolate, and disappeared. My brothers, if anyone among you is led astray
from the way of truth, and someone turns him back, let him know that he who
has turned a sinner from the error of his way will save his soul from death,
and will cover a multitude of sins. And how must one turn him back? just as
in the case of farmers the seeds are thrown down once, but do not remain
throughout, but need much preparation. And if, not having stirred up the
earth, they do not wrap the things thrown down, they have sown for the birds
and for all the seed-picking birds. So also we, if we do not wrap the things
thrown down by continual remembrance; for the devil snatches away, and our
slackness destroys. And the sun dries, and rain floods, and thorns choke. So
that it is not enough, having thrown down once, to be rid of it, but there is
need of much attendance, scaring away the birds of heaven, cutting out the
thorns, filling the rocky places with much earth; preventing every harm and
walling it off and destroying it. But in the case of the earth the whole
becomes the farmer's; for that lies soulless, being ready only to suffer; but
in the case of this spiritual earth it is not so at all. The whole is not of
the teachers, but if not the more, at least the half is of the disciples. It
is ours then to throw down the seed, and yours to do the things said.

=64.1053= ON THE FIRST EPISTLE OF ST. PETER.
CHAPTER I.
A little now, if it is needful, having been grieved in various trials, that
the proving of your faith, much more precious than gold that perishes, but is
tested through fire, may be found unto praise and glory and honor at the
revelation of Jesus Christ; whom not having known you love. The righteous are
afflicted, that they may be crowned; the sinners, that they may pay the
penalty of the sins. But not all the sinners pay a penalty here, in order that
the many may not disbelieve the resurrection; not all the righteous are
afflicted, in order that you may not think wickedness to be praiseworthy, and
hate virtue. Knowing that not with perishable things, with silver or gold, you
were redeemed from your vain


ancestral manner of life, but with precious blood as of a lamb unblemished
and unspotted, Christ, foreknown before the foundation of the world, but
manifested at the last of the times through you who through him believe in
God. We are a creature of God, but through sin we have become of the devil;
then, we having become of the devil, the Savior buys us out with his own
blood, that is, You were bought with a price. For we were bought with
precious blood. For just as a revered and good householder, a wicked servant
having many sins, not having judged him worthy of his own good house and of
his own diet, would sell him to someone, in order that he might discipline
him. Then if he sees him having been punished under a wicked master and
saying, I go to my former master, because it was better for me then than now;
he gives him the price again, and buys him out, in order that he may become a
people for his own possession. Thus then God also reclaims us. And how are we
his own and not his own? as created things, his own, but as having become
alien through having sinned, not his own; do not think that you are God's
own, if you are a sinner; for the devil acquires you; you are that one's
property. Man of sin, son of perdition, that one buys you, not with precious
blood, but with dishonorable; for he buys with sin; he buys with fornication;
for he is unclean. He buys with murder; for he is a blood-stained killer; For
he was a man-killer from the beginning, and he has not stood in the truth,
and these are his coins.

CHAPTER II.
But to those who disobey, a stone which the buil64.1056 ders rejected, this
has become for a head of a corner, and a stone of stumbling and a rock of
scandal. This saying is referred to Christ; for he himself also in the Gospels
brings forward this prophecy saying, Did you never read, a stone which the
builders rejected, this has become for a head of a corner? And after a little:
A stone which the builders rejected; he means the Jews, the Scribes, the
Pharisees, that they were rejecting him saying, That you are a Samaritan, and
have a demon; and again, This one is not from God, but he leads the crowd
astray.

CHAPTER III.
And his lips of not speaking deceit. And deceit is the lie, and deception and
slander, and accusations that do not have the true; and these things are
somehow dear to the enemy of the truth, that is to Satan, the father of the
lie; For he is a liar, as the Savior says, and he has not stood in the truth.
And the holy must decline his plunderings rather the things of God, who is
truth. Let him turn away from evil, and do good. For it is not in us not to
receive at all into the mind any of such things; for such thoughts somehow
fall upon it very often; but if someone is wise, he is carried away from them,
and he grants the leanings to the better things that have the beneficial very
much; this, I think, is the, Turn away from evil.

CHAPTER IV.
Hospitable toward one another, without murmurings. If you receive as Christ,
murmur nothing nor be ashamed, but rather even take pride in the matter; but
if you do not receive as Christ, do not even receive. He who receives you, he
says, receives me. If you do not receive thus, you do not even have a reward.
Abraham was receiving men passing by as wayfarers, as he supposed;


and he was not commanding all things to the servants, but he was also
commanding his wife to mix the flour, although having 318 houseborn. But he
himself wished to have the reward together with his wife; not of the expense
only, but also of the service; thus one must display hospitality, doing all
things through themselves, in order that we may be sanctified.
CHAPTER V. Shepherd the flock of God among you, not as lording it over the
lots. For Christ says: He who wishes to be first among you shall be a servant
to you; for he who will humble himself, this one will be exalted. What do you
say? if I humble myself, then I shall be high? Yes, he says; for such is my
power, I am well-resourced and well-devised at furnishing the opposites
through the opposites; do not fear; to my will the nature of things follows; I
do not follow nature. Be humbled therefore under the mighty hand of God, that
he may exalt you in due time. Not simply the he will exalt, but he added in
due time; teaching them to wait for that exaltation which is truly in the age
to come. Peace to you all who are in Christ. Amen. See how when he dictates
the things to be done, he marks them with the prayer; setting the peace as
seals of the things laid up.

ON THE SECOND EPISTLE OF ST. PETER.
CHAPTER I.
Grace to you and peace be multiplied in knowledge of God, and of Jesus Christ
our Lord. For nothing is equal to this; for this reason we pray and say,
asking the angel of peace; and everywhere we ask peace in the churches, peace
in the prayers, in the litanies, in the greetings. And once and twice and
three times and often the president of the Church gives it, Peace to you. Why?
Because this is mother of all the goods; this is the substance of the joy. For
this reason Christ also commanded the apostles, as they enter into the houses,
to say this at once, as a symbol of the goods; For entering, he says, into the
houses say: Peace to you. For if this is not, all things are superfluous; and
Christ said to the disciples, My peace I give to you. This makes a way before
for love. And this very thing, having brought in all eagerness, supply in your
faith the virtue, and in the virtue the knowledge, and in the knowledge the
self-control, and in the self-control the endurance, and in the endurance the
piety, and in the piety the brotherly love, and in the brotherly love the
love. What are these, except the things said before? Virtue, knowledge,
self-control, endurance, piety, brotherly love, love, which must not only be
present for us, but also abound. For if the presence benefits, much more the
abundance. And what is the benefit from them, except to have boldness in the
day of our Lord Jesus Christ? And he means his second coming; when he comes to
judge living and dead. And it is a great and truly exceeding good to have
boldness at the divine and fearful tribunal. He for whom these are not present
is blind, being shortsighted. Hearing these things then, let us wall ourselves
from every side and let us attend to the things said, and let us =64.1060=
cleanse ourselves of the worldly things. But if we do some things, and neglect
others, there will be nothing more for us; for if not thus, but in that other
way we perish. For what does it differ if we are corrupted not through wealth,
but through slackness, if not through slackness, but through cowardice? since
the farmer too, whether he loses the seed in this way or in that, likewise
mourns.


CHAPTER II. And cities of Sodom and Gomorrah, having reduced them to ashes,
he condemned with overthrow, having set them as an example of those about to
be ungodly. Do you wish to learn the cause through which these things
happened then? there was one sin, grievous and accursed; yet those then
persisted among boys; and for this reason they paid this penalty. And he had
a rebuke of his own lawlessness; a voiceless beast of burden, having spoken
in a human voice, hindered the prophet's madness. If the grace of God worked
upon an ass and in vain, through economy and the benefit of the Israelites,
it is quite clear that it will decline to work upon us, but it will endure
even this for our sake.

ON THE FIRST EPISTLE OF ST. JOHN.
CHAPTER III.
He who does the sin is of the devil; because the devil sins from the
beginning; for this the Son of God was manifested, that he might loose the
works of the devil. Since the devil, having turned before all, has come to be
in the sinning, everyone who works sinfully is named from him. For he takes
the lead in the one who is sinning through excess of wicked thoughts, as in
the case of Judas. But someone will say that the devil comes to be in those
who sin of those who have sinned before him, in the giving him place. To which
it must be said that doing the sin is the same as sinning, in the giving place
to the devil. For he gives him place, having been led by desire after
receiving him; accomplishing the sin in deed. For this is what doing it means;
and it is well also that he said, doing, but not, having done; the one who has
repented being no longer of the devil, but only the one still working it. For
thus also the slave of sin is the one who does it, but not the one who has
done it. For how, He who does the sin, he says, is a slave of it? But whoever
has the life of the world, and sees his brother having need, and shuts his
compassion, how does the love of God remain in him? =64.1061= When you see a
poor man, do not run past, but at once consider who you would be, if you were
that one; what would you not have wished all to do for you? Little children,
let us not love in word nor with tongue, but in work and truth. For it is not
enough to come forward, but one must do this with abundance and an ungrudging
mind; or rather not with an ungrudging only, but with a bright and rejoicing
soul.

CHAPTER IV.
He who does not love has not known God, because God is love. What sort of love
does he mean? The sincere, not that up to words, but that from disposition and
purpose, and from sharing pain out of a pure heart. For there is a love also
of the wicked; as robbers love robbers, and murderers murderers. But this is
not from a good conscience, but from a bad.
