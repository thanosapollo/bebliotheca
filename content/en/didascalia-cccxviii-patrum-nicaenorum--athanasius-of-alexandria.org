* Instruction of the Three Hundred Eighteen Nicene Fathers
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Faith of the holy three hundred and ten and eight holy God-bearing Fathers who
were at Nicaea; and a teaching most wondrous and saving concerning the Holy
Trinity. Bless.
We believe in one God, Father almighty, maker of all things visible and
invisible; and in one Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of God, the one begotten of
the Father as only-begotten; that is, from the essence of the Father, God from
God, light from light, true God from true God; begotten, not made,
consubstantial with the Father, through whom all things came to be in heaven
and on earth; who for us men and for our salvation came down from the heavens,
and was incarnate, suffered and was buried, and rose on the third day, and
ascended into the heavens; and is coming again to judge living and dead; and
in the Holy Spirit, the Lord, the life-giver. But those who say: There was
once when he was not; that from things that are not, or from another essence
or hypostasis, they assert the unchangeable and unalterable Son of God to be;
these the holy and catholic Church anathematizes from this holy faith which is
at Nicaea, which was set forth by the holy Fathers for the enlightenment of
souls, and that one may know what is the cause of each saying of the bishops
who confessed in it, the number three hundred eight and ten, or rather it
being an ecumenical synod. And further, following the faith written above, we
anathematize the faith of Sabellius which says that the same is Father and
=28.1638= Son and Holy Spirit; for they err; that the Son and the Spirit are
incomplete; as that the Father himself is the Son; likewise also the Holy
Spirit; as he says there is one person; but three names. For these things
happen to be alien to the orthodox faith; for we know the Father as perfect
God, likewise also the Son as perfect, and in the same way also the Holy
Spirit; one kingdom, one essence, and one Godhead. And further we anathematize
the faith of Photinus, which says that from Mary and henceforth the Son is;
and that he was not before this; but is spoken of in the Scriptures by way of
foreordination; but from Mary he is according to the Godhead. And these things
we know to be alien to the catholic faith; for the Son, having been begotten,
always existed and was in the Father, as it is written; for there never was a
time when the Son was not with the Father; but the Father was, always having
the Son; for it is impossible for the Father to be called so by advancement,
but always Son with Father, as we said before: In the beginning was the Word;
and the Word was God; begotten with the Father, as has been written above. And
these things concerning Father and Son. But concerning the Holy Spirit we
believe thus: a divine Spirit, a holy Spirit, a perfect Spirit, Paraclete; who
spoke in the law and the prophets, and the apostles; who came down upon the
Jordan. And concerning the incarnation of the Son we believe thus: that he
assumed a perfect man from the holy Theotokos Mary, through the Holy Spirit,
not from the seed of a man; body and soul in truth and good pleasure; for thus
he came perfect, to save man; and that he suffered, and that he was buried,
and on the third day =28.1639= he rose; and that he was taken up into the
heavens; and sat at the right hand of the Father, deifying the man whom he
took; and is coming to judge living and dead. And if somehow the divine
Scriptures speak by way of foreordination, we understand it as being fulfilled
in him as of a creature according to the flesh. Further we anathematize those
who do not confess that there is a resurrection of the dead, and all the
heresies. And there was also anathematized in the things written above the
godless faith of the Arians, in believing and saying, begotten and not made;
for they themselves say that the Son is a creature. And to complete all the
parts of the faith, still confessing these things we say: that we believe in
one baptism, in one Church, in resurrection of the dead, in the kingdom of the
heavens, in eternal judgment. We confess God the Father and the Son and the
Holy Spirit; Father incomprehensible, inconceivable, unbounded, and beyond
thought; for God is spirit; and by his will and truth and good pleasure
appearing and speaking to prophets and to the righteous: to Adam, to Noah, to
Abraham, to Isaac, to Jacob, to Samuel also and David, to Elijah and Elisha;
and to the rest of the prophets altogether; he himself being God of the New
and of the Old Covenant. And as many things as the Scripture says, eye of the
Lord, ears of the Lord, and as many such things as the Scripture says
concerning God, are true; and thus we believe; but incomprehensibly and
inconceivably; and that every man is according to the image of God. And the
life of the sons of this same catholic Church, and especially of bishops and
clerics and monks and of the remaining Christians, and as they command their
sons: first themselves living thus and teaching; thus by grace you have been
saved. But grace wishes them to be of their own free will also of every fair
and good thing; and zealots of the orthodox faith, worthy; and to prepare
himself; and it is evident to keep these things also; and you shall love our
Lord Jesus Christ from your whole heart, and from your whole soul; and your
neighbor as yourself; we shall not murder; we shall not commit adultery; we
shall not corrupt boys; we shall not practice sorcery; we shall not steal; we
shall not bear false witness; we shall not cause division; let us abstain from
what is strangled and from blood, and from greed. See, O man, lest anyone
deceive you away from this faith; since he teaches you apart from God. These
are the manifest sins; but the commandments that appear as least; of which we
shall also give account; that the monks be separated from women above all;
neither to come into conversation with them, nor to see them, nor to seek
honor, lest he sing of them; for damage of soul come to be; if your heart
commits adultery, you sin greatly. And again to be on guard not to be
double-tongued or double-minded, not a liar, not a slanderer, not walking
unseasonably, nor wandering about, not shameless, nor irascible, nor given to
wine, or a lover of money, not proud, not self-willed, not bringing a rotten
word, nor an oath =28.1640= at all whatsoever; but the yes, yes, and the no,
no; and if somewhere there is need to say something in conversation, know. I
speak truth; I do not lie. Do not take the reverend name of God upon an oath,
nor anyone with an oath, as the holy Gospel said; for all these faults are not
fitting, nor do they suit monks; but even to cast out from the Church the one
who does not keep watch in these things. Further, that he not uncover himself
before anyone at all, except by necessity of sickness, in a bath, or through
an affliction, or in the depth of waters. Nor insult a brother with raka, or
fool; and not to share in pagan feasts; not to keep the sabbaths, as the Jews
do; not to practice magic; not to practice sorcery; nor to cooperate in these
things for sickness or affliction or in a display; not to go to an enchanter;
nor to put phylacteries on oneself; nor to purify by ritual, neither from
oneself nor from anyone else; but always keep the body from all shamefulness
and wantonness; nor to have women brought in together (φ. συνεισάκτους) as
some have proposed as beloved; perhaps at the last they will be found hated in
it, and more bitter than gall; do not have enmity in your soul against anyone,
and your prayer become unacceptable; do not pray together with heretics or
with pagans, and your prayer become unacceptable; do not transgress the Lord's
fast, that is Wednesday and Friday, unless you are still burdened with
sickness; apart from the week of holy Pascha and of Pentecost, and of the
Lord's feasts, the twelve; but keep the remaining days exactly. Not to break
the fast without necessity; or even if a brother come to sojourn with you,
give love because of the toil of the road; but you yourself do not taste until
the ninth hour. And I mean the appointed fast, that is before all the holy and
glorious Forty Days, and of the remaining time Wednesday and Friday; but the
remaining days, that is Monday, Tuesday, and Thursday, if you are able to fast
from your own choice, your reward will be great; for on these days even
besides the hour a distinction is made for a notable friend; and there is no
condemnation because of love; but the appointed fast by the holy apostles,
judgment comes; and he who disobeys it perishes with the gluttons; whose god
is the belly and the glory of the world, and the pleasing of men. Do not fast
the sabbath the whole day; for it is unseemly to fast the whole day of the
sabbath until sunset; but it is permitted until the sixth or seventh hour; and
what is beyond is a Jewish heresy; and once: let not the sun set on a sabbath,
except only on the great sabbath in the night of Pascha; but the fast of
Wednesday and of Friday and of the holy Forty Days is appointed until the
ninth hour; and if you do anything beyond according to your own choice, you
will have the reward. And when fasting and lying down, see that you not be
puffed up in conceit and pride; for puffing-up is a snare of the devil,
=28.1641= through which he fell from heaven; and likewise through it he casts
down men, ensnaring them. Let no one deceive you into fasting on the Lord's
day, or on a feast of the Lord at all; nor to kneel; for it is not in the
ordinance of the Church, as we said before. And see that some Marcionites or
another heresy not deceive you into fasting on sabbath or the Lord's day. And
do not neglect the synaxes, the psalmody, and the reading, and the undefiled
mysteries; for with such sacrifices God is well pleased. Prepare yourself
worthy of the undefiled communion of the precious body and blood of our Lord
Jesus Christ, that you may not eat and drink unto condemnation. Do not neglect
to wash the feet of monks coming to you; this commandment will be required of
those who have been proud concerning it, even if they be bishops; for their
Lord first washed the feet of the disciples, commanding them to do thus. Do
not become a lover of money, or greedy of base gain. Do not wish to acquire
much wealth, silver or gold much, or only provision for the sake of foods and
garments. And give the surplus to the poor; for it is better to serve God in
poverty and in humility than in wealth and deceit; for the friends and slaves
of mammon cannot please God. Therefore do not trade at all. But there are many
villages that do not sow; and they dwell not having a craft, compelling them
to trade; and they remain monks, they toil; yet at least do not take interest.
Love every man; and be at peace with all, apart from heretics; for the heretic
is a wolf of the flock of Christ, and a forerunner of the Antichrist. And if
you have money or foods, share with him who has not; but if you have not, you
shall not be blamed. If someone come to borrow something from you, and you
have it, give it to him; and with longsuffering take from him the principal
only; and you shall be righteous. And do not deal deceitfully before the Lord;
for if you have produce, or something else, and one who lacks seeks to borrow
from you of necessity, and because you would not take interest, you sell more
dearly that you may gain a difference, that is doubling; the Scripture blames
you saying: God catches the wise in their craftiness. For consider and see,
that lending in necessity to the neighbor and not taking; instead of one
interest three and four principals; but he receives ten with God. Be humble
and quiet, trembling always at the words of God. Do not become quarrelsome;
nor strike a man much, except only a child in letters for discipline and
admonition of the Lord; and that with observation; only through fear
moderately a few and light blows. Look lest somehow through you a murder come
to be, and you lose your soul in one hour; and the good things you think you
have from youth become unacceptable before God, as the gifts of Cain; for many
are the occasions of death. Do not sneer at a man; nor abhor any poor man,
observing the garment in a poor figure; not using soft garments; not in the
clothing of one who appears righteous; not approaching in filthy garments; and
wearing sackcloth before men; =28.1642= and appearing a man-pleaser. But if
you have a sack, that you may mourn your sins, see that you not appear to men;
but let it be inside your garment on your flesh toward the taming of the
disorderly passions. Do not wish to have glory and praise from men. Do not
grow hair at all; for the Apostle cut off such a form; do not shave the beard;
nor use barbers, being shorn. If you are able also to fast a week, show it to
no one; and your reward will be great. And abstain from meats; not as
abhorring the creatures of God, but cutting off the body from much food, and
making the same unworthy of the good foods, and instead of the earthly
desiring the heavenly goods; for everyone who is moderate with abundance of
foods helps the body; but it is good to be continent in all things because of
much flesh. Do not take wine at all; or only to taste in thanksgiving; and
bless God who created it; for if you also, like the honorable Timothy, through
much self-control are about to fall into sickness, use a little wine; for the
little is a medicine of healing; but using it much you will surround yourself
with both weaknesses; but if up to two or three cups; but not beyond. If you
are able to travel unshod, you will do nobly; but if there is need of
footwear, your sandals shall be plain and do not wish to be bound with adorned
sandals, in a worldly form. Be content with the things at hand moderately. And
if there is surplus meat and wine, give them to the poor, orphans, widows, the
powerless, strangers, the sick, and those in prison; and your reward will be
great. If you are in a foreign place, do not wish to boast yourself; but
become equal with your brothers, using vegetables, and other foods. And if
there is need, it is necessary also to partake of fish. And again I say: if
you fall ill and take it, it is not a sin; but if you are heavily overcome in
the sickness, and you eat, desiring the little; because of the weakness of
your flesh there is no condemnation; it is a defeat for you; but do not reckon
it a sin; but be grieved, that you were cut short of your way of life, and you
were loosened from the firm reasoning. And if you are also compelled to use a
bath because of the sickness both once and twice, there is no charge; but
being healthy you have no need to bathe; even if you are a priest; but rather,
the body being healthy, wish a pallet on the ground and to force yourself in
all things. And before all things know a craft; or work in the field; that you
may not eat bread idle; rather work with your handiwork, that you may have to
give to the poor and to strangers, to orphans and to widows; and if you dwell
among brothers, and have an ancestral plot, gathering fruits justly, and not
from injustice and greed; first offer your firstfruits to the priests in the
Church; then refresh widows and orphans, and the remaining powerless, from
your just labors; not from interest and greed. And if you sit alone, do not
insult the ascesis of stillness; if you keep stillness in a monastery, and you
acquire a plot and trade and keep stillness; but you mock and are mocked.
Sitting in a monastery, do not wish to be idle, and to be =28.1643= fed by
others; that you may have the daily food. And if you have other young men
about you, first teach humility; and do not wish them to be teachers. If you
see souls being saved through you, be eager to complete of the five talents
which you have received, to receive back the double. Take careful thought for
the souls about you; and always constrain the younger ones each of them to
keep to himself, in conversation and in the association of the table, and of
the synaxis of the psalmody; and when they sleep, that each have a sack
hidden, that in the prayer of the night he be found ready; be eager to keep
vigil in psalms and hymns, and prayers in measure; and by day to pray in due
measure, and by night likewise; not to linger in the prayer, and to babble;
but to chant with measure; that the souls not be distressed. And wish to
advance the boys silently. Do not enter a tavern at all; but if necessity come
upon you, being in a foreign place, and you have need of food; send and buy;
and in a church eat and drink, in a place set apart. And if there is not a
church in the place, or a church of the orthodox, and necessity come upon you
to enter an inn; do not wish to remain where there are women, nor in a tavern,
nor with those who go into a theater, or who are found at a hunt; if you have
the power, do not even pray with them; but if necessity come, and you are
permitted to them; those who permitted will see to it from the greater ones;
but you, keeping watch of these things, training yourself. These commandments
suit monks and priests, and all who practice self-control, both widows and
true Christians who live in honorable marriage; but if someone happens to be a
priest, he ought to keep the =28.1644= priesthood together with the remaining
clerics with these commandments; for such a one wishes to be sober, diligent
in the prayers, forbearing, kind, a lover of orphans, a lover of the poor, a
lover of strangers, a lover of the good, quiet, firm in the faith and in word
and in judgment, abstaining from every evil deed, abstaining from women, a
prototype, being diligent for the Church, for the synaxis of the psalmody; not
to neglect the flock; not to abhor anyone; not a striker, not to be given to
wine, not a lover of money, I speak according to the Apostle; not a respecter
of persons, so as to convict in transgressions both small and great, his own
and strangers; not to allow the sheep of the flock to become scabby; but
rather to cleanse them, first through repentance, then again to gather them
into the fold, that is in the Church; to the widows, and to all the laymen, to
the catechumens, to the laymen and monks, and to those living in honorable
marriage, to charge them to repent; and this not as of a different law, and
each word being distinguished; but it is one; being distinguished however in
all Christians; and to charge the women not to speak at all, not even one word
in a whisper; nor to chant together with monks, nor to respond together;
except only to be silent and to pray to God through intercession and an
honorable way of life; that they not have bright clothing, nor gold-spangled
garments, or ornaments of pearls; nor their faces to appear fully neither in
the marketplace nor in church. These are the saving teachings of the holy
three hundred eight and ten God-bearing Fathers, which lead into eternal life
those who believe in the holy and consubstantial Trinity; to whom belongs all
glory, honor and worship unto the ages. Amen.
