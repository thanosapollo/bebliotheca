Encomiastic Homily.
On the holy martyr Lucian.
=50.519=

1. What I feared yesterday has come to pass, and has now reached its
end, =50.520= and the feast having gone, the multitude has also
departed from us =50.521= together with it, and our gathering has
become smaller. And I knew that this would certainly happen; yet I did
not on this account withdraw from the exhortation; for even if not all
who heard yesterday were persuaded, still not all disobeyed; and this
is no small thing for us by way of consolation; therefore neither
today will I withdraw from this counsel; for even if not through us,
at any rate through you they will certainly hear what is said. For who
could bear in silence such great indolence of theirs, or grant them
pardon and an excuse, who, having seen their mother after so long a
time, and having enjoyed the good things in her, withdrew, and could
not endure to turn back a second time, and imitated not the dove of
Noah, but the raven, and this while the surge still remains, and that
winter, and a more grievous storm being raised every day, and this
holy ark lying in the midst, calling all, drawing them to herself, and
furnishing much safety to those who take refuge? For she does not beat
back the assaults of waters or of waves, but the continual uprisings
of the most irrational passions, and she destroys envy, and she puts
down arrogance.

For neither will the rich man here be able to look down on the poor,
hearing from the divine Scriptures that All flesh is grass, and all
the glory of man as the flower of grass; nor will the poor, seeing
another who is rich, be caught by envy, himself also hearing another
prophet saying: Fear not when a man becomes rich, or when the glory of
his house is multiplied; for when he dies he will not take all things,
nor will his glory go down with him. For such is the nature of this
abundance: it does not change place with those who have it, it does
not journey abroad with those who possess it, it does not stand by
them when they are judged there and give account, but it is altogether
cut off by death; and many it has even abandoned before death;
unfaithful is the use, unstable the enjoyment, dangerous the
possession. But the things of virtue are not such, nor those of
almsgiving; but this treasure is inviolable. Whence is this clear? He
who philosophized concerning this wealth and said that His glory shall
not go down with him after him, this same one also taught us
concerning the treasures of almsgiving, which always remain and are
never plundered, saying thus: He has scattered, he has given to the
poor; his righteousness remains forever. What could be more
paradoxical than this? The things gathered are lost, and the things
scattered remain, and very reasonably. For God receives the latter,
and from the hand of God no one can snatch them; but the former are
stored in human treasuries, where they lie under many plots, where
envy and jealousy are great. Do not then neglect, beloved, the sojourn
here; for if some despondency troubles you, here it is banished; if
cares of life, they run away; if irrational passions, they are
quenched; but from the marketplace, and the theaters, and the other
gatherings outside, we withdraw home dragging many cares, and
despondencies, and diseases of soul. If you spend time here
continually, you will certainly put off the evils you received
=50.522= from outside; but if you leap away and flee, you will
certainly lose the goods you acquired from the divine Scriptures, your
abundance being little by little drawn away from the assemblies and
conversations outside. And that these things are true, when you
withdraw from here make haste to see those who were absent today, and
you will see how great is the interval between your cheerfulness and
their despondency. A bride sitting in the bridal chamber is not so
beautiful and graceful as a soul appearing in Church is wonderful and
glorious, breathing spiritual myrrh. For he who comes here with faith
and zeal goes away having received countless treasures; and if he only
opens his mouth, he has immediately filled those who are with him with
all fragrance and spiritual wealth; and if countless terrible things
fall upon him, he will bear them all easily, having received from here
from the divine Scriptures a sufficient resource of endurance and
philosophy. And just as he who stands continually upon the rock laughs
at the waves, so he who enjoys continual assembly, and is watered by
the divine words, having set himself as upon a rock of the right
judgment of things, will not be caught by any of human affairs, having
set himself above the inrush of the things of life; and having reaped
much benefit and refreshment not from counsel only, but also from
prayer, and from paternal blessing, and from the common gathering, and
from the love of the brethren, and from countless other things, he has
thus withdrawn, carrying countless goods home. See then how much
blessing you will enjoy today, and how great a loss they will undergo.
For you will go away receiving a martyr's reward; but they, besides
being deprived of this gain, will undergo another loss as well,
dragging a great heap of cares from unprofitable sojourns. For just as
he who receives a prophet in the name of a prophet will receive a
prophet's reward, and he who receives a righteous man in the name of a
righteous man will receive a righteous man's reward; so he who
receives a martyr in the name of a martyr will receive a martyr's
reward; and the reception of a martyr is to come together for his
memorial, to share in the narrative of his contests, to marvel at what
has happened, to emulate the virtue, to bring his deeds of valor
to others; these are the guest-gifts of the martyrs; thus one receives
these holy ones, just as you also have done today.

2. Yesterday then our Master was baptized with water, today the
servant is baptized with blood; yesterday the gates of heaven were
opened, today the gates of Hades were trampled. And do not marvel if I
called martyrdom a baptism; for here too the Spirit flies down with
great abundance, and there comes a taking away of sins and a wondrous
and paradoxical cleansing of the soul; and as those baptized are
washed with the waters, so those who suffer martyrdom are bathed in
their own blood; =50.523= which indeed has also happened in the case
of this man. But before the slaughter it is necessary to speak of the
devil's cunning. For that one, having seen that the saint had laughed at
every kind of punishment and torment, and that neither by lighting a
furnace, nor by digging a pit, nor by preparing a wheel, nor by
bringing him up onto the wood, nor by casting him down cliffs, nor by
throwing him to the teeth of beasts, had he been able to overcome the
saint's philosophy, invented another more grievous manner, and he went
about seeking to find a punishment, so that it might become at once
both most sharp and most long. For since of punishments some that are
unbearable furnish a very swift release, while those that are more
prolonged cut short the pain, he was eager to find a torment having
both at once, both length and an incurable excess of pain, that both
by the intensity and by the quantity of time he might overthrow the
firmness of his mind. And what does he do? He delivers this saint to
hunger; but you, having heard hunger, do not pass simply over what is
said; for of all deaths this is the most grievous. And witnesses are
those who have had the experience; may we not indeed have the
experience; for we have been well taught to pray not to enter into
temptation. For like some executioner sitting within upon the inward
parts he tears all the members, eating the body from every side more
violently than any fire and beast, furnishing a continual and
unspeakable pain. And that you may learn how great hunger is, even
mothers have often tasted of their children, not bearing the force of
this evil; and the prophet, making a tragedy of this misfortune, said:
The hands of compassionate women have boiled their children. Those
whom mothers bore they ate, and the womb that bore became a tomb of
the children that were born, and hunger overcame nature; or rather not
nature only, but also purpose; but it did not overcome the nobility of
this saint. Who would not be struck hearing these things? And yet what
is more powerful than nature? And what more quickly turning than
purpose? But that you may learn that nothing is stronger than the fear
of God, purpose appeared more vigorous than nature, and hunger
convicted mothers and made them forget the pangs of birth, but it was
not able to trip up this saint, nor did the torment overcome the
philosophy, nor did the punishment master the courage; but he remained
stronger than any adamant, taking delight in good hopes, and glorying
in the subject of the contests, having a sufficient consolation in
the occasion of the struggles, especially hearing Paul each day
saying: In hunger and thirst, in cold and nakedness; and again, Even
unto this present hour we both hunger, and thirst, and are naked, and
are buffeted; for he knew, he knew clearly, that Man shall not live by
bread alone, but by every word that proceeds through the mouth of God.
But when the abominable demon saw that he was not yielding to so great
a necessity of the matter, he made the torture still more grievous.
For taking things sacrificed to idols, and filling the table with
these, =50.524= he arranged that they be set before his eyes, that the
ready availability might dissolve the vigor of his eagerness.

For we are not so captured when the things that bait us do not appear,
as when they lie before the eyes; since one would more easily master
the desire of women by not seeing a well-formed woman, than by
continually gazing at her. But nevertheless the just man mastered this
ambush too, and what the devil thought would dissolve his courage,
this especially anointed him for the contests; for not only did he
suffer nothing from the sight of the things sacrificed to idols, but
he even more turned from them and hated them; and what we suffer in
the case of enemies, that the more we look at them, so much the more
we hate them and turn from them, this he also suffered then in the
case of that abominable sacrifice; for seeing it more he loathed it
and leapt away, and the continual sight rather threw him into hatred
and aversion of the things set before him; and while hunger cried out
greatly from within, and the things set before him ordered him to take
hold, the fear of God held back his hands, and prepared him to forget
nature itself; and seeing a table abominable and accursed, he
remembered another table, the fearful one full of the Spirit, and he
was so kindled, as to choose to undergo and suffer all things, rather
than taste of those abominable foods. He remembered also that table of
the three children, who being taken young, and being destitute of all
protection, in a foreign land and a barbarian country displayed such
philosophy, that their courage is sung even until now. And the Jews,
even having their own land, acted impiously, and spending time in the
temple they worshipped idols; but those young men, having been carried
away into the barbarian land, where there were idols and the occasion
of all impiety, continued keeping the ancestral way of life. If then
the captives, and slaves, and youths before grace displayed such
philosophy, he says, of what pardon would we be worthy, if we were not
even able to reach the same virtue as they?

3. Reckoning all these things then he laughed at the devil's
wickedness, he despised the villainy, and yielded to none of the
things seen. When then that abominable one saw that nothing more had
come about for him, he brings him again into the court, and torturing
him he put continual questions; but he answered to each question, I am
a Christian, only. And when the executioner said: Of what fatherland
are you? I am a Christian, he says; What occupation have you? I am a
Christian; What ancestors? and he said to all things, that I am a
Christian; with this one bare word striking the devil's head, and
furnishing him continual and successive wounds. And yet he had also
partaken of the education from without, but he knew clearly that in
such contests there is need not of rhetoric, but of faith; not of
cleverness of speech, but of a God-loving soul; one word, he says, is
enough to turn the whole phalanx of the devil. And to those who do not
examine accurately the answer seems inconsistent; but if someone
=50.525= attends with the mind, from this too he will see the martyr's
wisdom. For he who said, I am a Christian, made clear also fatherland,
and race, and occupation, and all things; how, I will say: The
Christian does not have a city upon the earth, but the Jerusalem
above; For the Jerusalem above, he says, is free, which is our mother.
The Christian does not have an earthly occupation, but belongs to the
citizenship above; For our citizenship, he says, exists in the
heavens; the Christian has as kinsmen all the saints and
fellow-citizens; For we are fellow-citizens of the saints, he says,
and of the household of God.

So that with one word he taught with accuracy both who he was, and
from where, and of whom, and what he continued doing. And with this
voice he ended his life, and went away bringing the deposit sound to
Christ, and through what he suffered exhorting those after these
things to stand firm, and to fear nothing except sin and denial only.
Knowing these things then, let us also in the time of peace practice
the things of war, that when the war stands over us we too may set up
a splendid trophy. That man despised hunger, let us also despise
luxury, and let us pull down the tyranny of the belly, that if a time
should stand over us seeking such =50.526= courage from us, having
been trained beforehand in the lesser things, we may appear splendid
upon the wrestling-grounds. Before rulers and kings that man spoke
with boldness; let us also do this now, and if we sit in gatherings of
rich and distinguished Greeks, let us confess the faith with boldness;
let us laugh at their error. And if they attempt to magnify their own
things, and to pull down ours, let us not be silent, nor bear it
meekly, but uncovering their shame, let us hymn all the things of the
Christians with much wisdom and boldness; and as the king the diadem
upon the head, so let us also carry about everywhere the confession of
the faith.

For the crown is not so accustomed to adorn him as faith and the
confession of it are accustomed to adorn us; and let us do this not
through words only, but also through the deeds themselves, displaying
through all things a life worthy of the confession, that we may not
put the doctrines to shame by the meanness of the works, but
glorifying our Master through all things we may enjoy both the honor
here and that there; which may it be that all of us obtain, by the
grace and loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom and
with whom to the Father be glory, might, and honor, together with the
Holy and life-giving Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of
ages. Amen.
