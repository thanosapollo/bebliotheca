On the saying, The Jews gathered a council, and said: "What shall we do?" On
Holy and Great Monday.
=59.525=
The dearest children of the Church are like nursing infants, who each day draw
the food of milk from the mother's breast, and seek it every day. We too are
like a poor mother whose springs of milk have shrunk from fasting. And just
as, whenever one sees a child-loving mother barren of milk, and wept over by
her own child, putting the breast to it not so that she may feed the infant,
but so that she may still its crying: so we too must consider ourselves,
suffering the same things and doing the like. For we also, seeing this new
company of the Church's children standing round about, and waiting for the
rational milk of the word, let down the breast of our mind: not so that we may
feed them (for we are unskilled and poor in speech), but so that we may still
their longing and their burning heat. But come, beloved, let us run down to
the herald of the Sun, that we may see the treacherous ambush practiced
against the Lord by the Jews.
For the Jews gathered a council, he says, and said: What shall we do? These
things the evangelist John was just now uttering to us. For even if he does
not now converse with us by mouth, yet by the Spirit he was crying out to the
ears of those who stand here. For they gathered, he says, the council, and
said: What shall we do? Good is the, They gathered; but evil is the, Council:
not toward love, but toward a fight; not calling to peace, but goading to war.
They gathered the council, not that they might glorify God, but that they
might dishonor Christ. They gathered the council, not that they might build up
faith, but that they might cast away the cornerstone. They gathered the
council, not that they might fold the straying sheep, but that, having
murdered the shepherd, they might scatter the flock. They gathered the
council, not that they might receive in faith the snow-shining pearl, but that
they might, as they supposed, darken the luminescent stone by unbelief. They
gathered the council, not that they might receive with good will the trea
=59.526= sure hidden in the field, but that they might ungratefully pull down
the temple of God. They gathered the council, not that they might harvest the
honey-dropping clusters of the vine, but that, spreading the true vine upon
the tree of the cross, they might make themselves climbing-vines of
destruction, and us climbing-vines of salvation. O council gathered by the
devil against Christ; O senate filled with wickedness; O council of
bloodthirsty wolves rather than of God-bearing men.
Why do I say, of savage wolves? They are worse even than savage lions. For the
lions, seeing no sign in the case of Daniel, but looking upon the man and
honoring his piety, bridled their untamed mouths. But the Jews, having become
spectators of such and so many signs, were armed all the more out of envy
toward murder, and said: What shall we do? O diligent inquiry of wickedness; O
groan and lament born of envy; O wailing and beating of the breast born over
evils. Not even Rachel weeping for her children sighed so. What shall we do?
Whereas the wretches ought to have rejoiced, whereas they ought to have kept a
public day of gladness, because the dead have come to life, because life has
been manifested, because death has been loosed, because the devil has been put
to shame, because the host of demons


has been brought to nothing, because humanity has been set free, because the
resurrection of the dead has been proclaimed to all: now, at such goods, they
do not rejoice, but they lament, and they take counsel to destroy the author
of so many goods. And what do they say? What shall we do? There was nothing
else to say, except that, having repented, we should turn back, and falling
before him we should believe. For what else could they have said in repenting,
except this? What shall we do, because we have transgressed the commandment of
God, because we have not kept the law given to us, which says to us, that The
Lord God will raise up for you a prophet from your brothers, like me: you
shall hear him in all things, whatever he shall command you. But now they do
not say toward repentance, What shall we do? but, What shall we do? how shall
we kill the righteous one? So Israel is mind-dis =59.527= tressed. Did you not
hear even this, that You shall not kill the innocent and the righteous? Were
the ancient bloods of the prophets not enough for you? For even until now they
cry to God, waiting for vengeance from him. But still adding to those bloods,
you condemn innocent and righteous blood, and you say, What shall we do? O
ungrateful mind, and counsel filled with folly.
Their four-days-dead man has come to life; the bonds of sins were being torn
apart; and they were arming themselves against him for treacherous murder.
Whereas they ought, turning the saying around, to have said, What shall we do,
because we have insulted a holy one, because we have not believed in the child
of God? What shall we do? He scraped away the hail-spots of lepers with the
word as a ship is scraped, and we did not believe in him. What shall we do? We
have known those blind from birth given eyes by him, and we did not believe in
him. What shall we do? We have known five thousand filled to satiety in the
wilderness from five loaves by him, and we did not believe in him. What shall
we do? He dried up springs of blood with the hems of garments, and we did not
believe in him. What shall we do? A paralytic laid on a bed, having the
disease for thirty-eight years contemporary with his own life, he raised by a
word, and we did not believe in him. What shall we do? We have seen him
walking on the sea, as upon dry ground, and we have known him silencing by a
nod of his hand the violence of contrary winds and incurable tumults, and we
did not believe in him. But they said, What shall we do? not in repenting, but
in doing evil. Their land has painlessly brought forth the dead living, like
white-robed and ripe ears of grain, and they were groaning. Did you not reap
what you sowed, Jews? Was anyone of you burdened by receiving the Lord? Having
suffered what loss were you burdened? In what were you wronged by God? Why do
you hinder the dead from life, driven by envy? Why do you wish to break up the
ever-flowing channels of the honey-streaming spring, of which those who
=59.528= drink do not thirst? Why do you kill your own sick, wishing to
destroy their physician? But of all these the cause is most wicked envy. O
envy, begetter of murder, invention of the devil, prince of death; O envy, who
neither wishes those envied by you to live, and who punishes more those who
have you; O envy, housemate of the devil; O envy, enemy of friendship,
opponent of peace, adversary of love, offspring of the devil, dark-colored
flower; those who feed on you work the murder of Christ; and you from the
beginning envied Adam in paradise, and when he had tasted sweet pleasure you
handed him over to most bitter death; for death was not in the world, unless
you, envy, had given it birth. And the divine saying bears me witness, saying:
God did not make death, nor does he delight in the destruction of the living.
But by the envy of the devil death entered into the world. You also stripped
Cain of the white-robed garment of brotherly love, and armed the knife of
treacherous murder, and taught him to murder, and at once taught the wretched
man to tremble. You, dwelling in Pharaoh, withered the newborn infants of the
Hebrews like grass. You, O envy, when being done good, stir still more against
those who do good; just as


Saul, helped by David, and worked upon by you, was armed all the more toward
murder.
Having you, the sons of Jacob sent the most beautiful and splendid Joseph into
Egypt that whirls in darkness, and they sold the free man as a slave; and not
even the dreams frightened them; for a divine vision declared him to be an
image of Christ; for he was worshiped in visions by eleven stars; since the
traitor Judas also, moved by envy, hanged himself. But we, fleeing envy the
begetter of death, rising up let us glorify the God who is worshiped in
Trinity; for to him belongs the power, the honor, and the glory, and the
magnificence, now and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
