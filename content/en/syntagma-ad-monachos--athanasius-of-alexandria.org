OF OUR FATHER AMONG THE SAINTS ATHANASIUS, ARCHBISHOP OF ALEXANDRIA, a
compilation of teaching to monks and to all Christians, both clergy and laity.
The life of the sons of the catholic church, and especially of the solitaries
or monks in her, is as follows. 1.2 We are saved by grace, but grace wills
that her own children and sons of wisdom be of their own choosing, and that,
having tested every good practice, they be zealous for the good and so act.
1.3 Therefore make yourself ready as worthy of this right faith, beloved,
having set these things before you to keep. 1.4 They contend, together with:
You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul,
and your neighbor as yourself. 1.5 You shall not murder, you shall not commit
adultery, you shall not fornicate, you shall not corrupt children, you shall
not practice sorcery, you shall not cause division. 1.6 Abstain from what is
strangled and from what is sacrificed to idols and from blood. 1.7 And these
are the obvious sins; but the commandments that appear lesser, of which we
shall also give account, are these. 1.8 First, those who live as monks and the
continent must be withdrawn from women, and neither come into conversation
with them, if possible, nor be eager to see them, lest some harm come if the
heart commits adultery through the seeing of the eyes. 1.9 And take care not
to be double-tongued, not double-minded, not a liar, not a slanderer, not
unseasonably distracted, not shameless, not a rover, not without feeling, not
stubborn, not bringing a rotten word out of the lips, nor an oath at all
whatsoever; but yes yes, no no; and if somewhere necessity arises, to say in
conversation, "Know," or "I speak truth," or "I do not lie." =1.10= Do not take
the reverend name for an oath, nor any other oath, as the gospel said. =1.11=
For all these things it is not fitting nor suitable to do, but it also casts
out of the church the one who does not keep watch, and some of them it will
even kill. 2.1 And further, do not strip yourself before anyone, except by
necessity in a bath on account of a passion occurring in great weakness. 2.2
Do not say to a brother "raca," nor "you fool." 2.3 Do not share in the feasts
of the nations; do not keep sabbaths. 2.4 Do not practice magic, do not
practice drugs, nor let another do these things for you for a disease or the
pain of a passion. 2.5 Do not go to an enchanter, nor put an amulet on
yourself, nor purify yourself by ritual, nor indeed do these things for
yourself, nor let them be done for you by another. 2.6 Keep the body from
every shamefulness and wantonness. 2.7 Do not have a woman brought in to live
with you, as some have given them the names of beloveds, though perhaps on the
contrary they will be found hateful to them. 2.8 Do not have a coldness toward
anyone, for the prayer of the one who has a coldness is not accepted by the
Lord. 2.9 Do not pray together with a heretic, nor with the heathen. =2.10= Do
not transgress a fast, that is Wednesday and Friday, unless you have been
burdened with disease, except only Pentecost and the Epiphany. =2.11= Keep the
Forty Days of the holy church and the week of holy Pascha with careful
observance. =2.12= Break the fast when a brother has come to stay with you,
not the appointed fast (Wednesday and Friday, and the Forty Days and that of
the Passion), but the one from your own choice, that is of Monday and Tuesday
and Thursday. =2.13= Do not fast on Sabbath and the Lord's day, except the
great Sabbath of holy Pascha. =2.14= But the fast of Wednesday and Friday
until the ninth hour is appointed. =2.15= And if you do anything more, this is
from your own choice; and if you are also able to do more, you exercise
yourself nobly. =2.16= But while fasting and living this way of life, see that
you are not puffed up; for puffing up is a snare of the devil, through which
he fell from heaven, and likewise thereafter, trapping men through it, he
casts them down. =2.17= Let no one deceive you into fasting on the Lord's day
at all, nor kneeling at all, nor in Pentecost; for it is not a rule of the
church. =2.18= And do not endure that some Marcionites or another heresy
deceive you into fasting the Sabbath as their own practice and that of the
Lord's day. =2.19= Do not neglect the assemblies. =2.20= Make yourself ready
as worthy of the mysteries, lest you come together unto judgment. =2.21= Do
not neglect to wash the feet of brothers coming to you; for such a commandment
will be sought concerning those who have been proud against it, even if they
are bishops; for our Lord himself first washed, commanding them to do so. 3.1
Do not become a lover of money, not greedy of shameful gain, not a lover of
possessions, not a lover of wealth; for such cannot please God. 3.2 And do not
trade at all. 3.3 There are many lands that do not sow, and those who dwell
there, if they do not have crafts, are forced to trade, even if they are
monks, and they trade badly, yet necessity does it. 3.4 Do not take interest.
3.5 Do not buy up anything in advance at less than the current price. 3.6 Love
every man and be at peace with all, and with those with whom you do not pray,
if it is possible as far as it depends on you, apart from heresy. 3.7 Having,
give a share to the one who has not; and if you have not, you will not be
blamed. 3.8 If someone asks of you, give for use, and take from him the
principal, when he has it, without interest. 3.9 Do not deal deceitfully
before the Lord. =3.10= For if you have produce or silver, and someone seeks
to borrow from you, if because you do not take interest you sell more dearly
so that you may buy cheaply at the time, that is at a lower price, God catches
the wise in their craftiness; for consider and see that instead of one
interest you will find yourself taking three or four interests. 4.1 Be humble
and quiet, trembling always at the words of the Lord. 4.2 Do not be
contentious, do not strike a man, except only your little child for
instruction, and that with careful watch, looking lest through you a murder
come about, for there are many occasions of death. 4.3 Do not sneer at any
man. 4.4 Do not abhor anyone because of appearance. 4.5 Keep your clothing
with care, neither using soft garments, nor going forth in clothing that
appears to be the garb of the righteous, that is in filthy ones. 4.6 And if
you have sackcloth that you may mourn your sins, see that your sackcloth not
appear, but let it be hidden within. 4.7 And do not clothe yourself in skins,
lest you receive glory from men. 4.8 Do not grow hair at all; for the apostle
cut off such a form. 4.9 Do not shave the beard, and do not use barbers when
cutting your hair. 5.1 If you are able even to fast a week, show it to no one.
5.2 Abstain from meats, not as abhorring them but as checking the body from
luxury and considering yourself unworthy and reaching after the heavenly
instead of the earthly; for everyone who is moderate in the abundance of foods
helps his body toward continence. 5.3 Do not take wine at all, or only to
taste and bless the one who created it. But if you also, like Timothy, because
of the great way of life fall into diseases, use a little wine; for the little
is a medicine of healing, but using much of it you will wrap yourself in both
weaknesses; but if you use it, up to one cup or two, and no more. 5.4 If it is
possible for you to go unshod, you will be nobly led; but if there is necessity
to be shod at all, let your sandals be plain. 5.5 And do not wish to be lifted
up in ornamented sandals, forms of harlotry. 5.6 Be content with what is at
hand. And if you have a more abundant practice as to meats and wine, and you
go abroad, do not wish to boast yourself, but become equal to the brothers, as
when they are using vegetables and boiled dishes. 5.7 And if there is need to
eat garum or even to partake of fish, if you wish, up to this only, and again
take up the first ascesis. 5.8 Again if you fall ill and take an egg, it is
not a sin. 5.9 But if you are overcome in the illness and take of fowl and
meats, it is a defeat for you, yet do not reckon it a sin, but grieve that you
were checked from your way of life and were loosened from the firm reasoning.
6.1 And if you are forced in illness, use the bath once and twice; but being
healthy you have no need of a bathhouse. 6.2 You who are a monk and a priest,
be willing to sleep on the ground, if you are healthy. 6.3 And before all,
take up a craft. 6.4 Work in the field, that you may not eat idle bread. 6.5
Rather have from your hands toward giving rest to brothers and strangers and
if possible widows and orphans and the moderate. 6.6 And if you dwell among
men and have a paternal plot or farmland to work, gathering fruits justly and
having nothing of injustice, first offer the firstfruits to the priests, then
be willing also to give rest to widows and the rest from just labors, not from
interest or surplus or tradings or extortions. 6.7 But if you sit in a
monastery, do not insult the ascesis of the monastery. 6.8 If you possess a
plot while withdrawing in a monastery, it has not been a withdrawal for you,
but you mock and are mocked. 6.9 And sitting in a monastery do not wish to be
idle and to be fed by others; but you must either look to a craft or do labor,
that you may somehow provide the daily food. =6.10= And if you have younger
ones about you, first be humble-minded and do not wish to be a teacher. =6.11=
But if you see souls being saved through your manner of life, wishing to
complete the double of the five talents which you received, take care for the
souls about you. =6.12= And constrain the younger ones that each of them live
privately both in conversation and in manner of life, except only at table and
assembly and psalmody. =6.13= And in sleeping, let each be wrapped in his own,
and let each have his tunic or a hidden sackcloth, that if you pray somewhere
at night they may be found ready. =6.14= Be willing to keep vigil moderately
in psalms and prayers, and by day to pray frequently. =6.15= But do not linger
in prayer or cry out and babble, but psalm moderately, lest the souls be
distressed. =6.16= And be willing to train the children to be silent. =6.17=
Do not enter a tavern at all, but if necessity arises when you are abroad and
you have need of food or drink, send and buy, and eat and drink in a church,
in a place of it that is withdrawn. =6.18= But if there is no church in the
place nor a house of the orthodox, and necessity comes upon you and you enter
an inn, be willing to remain in an inn where there are not women nor a tavern,
and this with grief. 7.1 If then, having kept the aforementioned commandments
and having kept yourself from the aforementioned faults, you finish the course
in faith and purity and way of life, according to what has been said, you will
procure for yourself a great and good rank. 7.2 These are the commandments and
safeguards of the aforementioned faith, which if you do and keep the faith,
you will be a disciple of our Lord Jesus Christ. 7.3 But if anyone contradicts
the aforementioned, he is contradicting God; for he will handle the divine
scriptures and consider the tradition of the church, and he has to understand
that concerning all these things the Lord commands. 7.4 But we, that we may
not speak at length, have not set out the testimonies. 7.5 And these
commandments are for monks and the rest of devout Christians. 8.1 But if some
priest wishes to serve as priest with exactness, together with the
aforementioned commandments, let him be sober, diligent, patient of evil,
kind, a lover of orphans, a lover of the poor, a lover of strangers, a lover
of the good, quiet, firm in faith and mind and word, abstaining from a woman,
not neglecting the flock, not abhorring anyone. Not to show partiality,
rebuking before all. 8.2 Do not let a scabby sheep remain in the flock, but
first cleanse it with repentance, and then gather it in the fold. 8.3 Know
each as one must shepherd, what to command to the widows, what to monks, what
to the laity, what to the catechumens. 8.4 And command women in church not to
speak at all, but in all things to be silent and pray, and not to make
themselves splendid in gold or clothing, and their faces not to appear, not to
kiss the hands of men. 8.5 And let the catechumens pray separately or outside
the veil. 8.6 And let all the people hear the word of the Lord in fear and
silence. 8.7 And not only will those who sin pay the penalty, but also those
who rejoice over those who sin. 8.8 And concerning the offerings the priest
ought to be sober; for if he takes from a soldier who has shed blood, or who
has extorted, or who has stolen, or from a trader, or a perjurer, or from a
rich defrauder, or from a tax-collector who exacts extra, or a usurer, or a
grain-hoarder, or from any sinner, such a priest, if he takes from such,
offers from them to God what is lame and blind. 8.9 But for you the layman it
is not permitted to judge the priest, except if you see a transgressor, flee
from him. =8.10= If you keep these things, O priest, you procure for yourself
a good rank and much boldness that is in Christ Jesus our Lord, with whom to
the Father with the Holy Spirit be the glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
