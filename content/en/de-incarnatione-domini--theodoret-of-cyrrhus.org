Ηʹ. That the incarnation of God is full of loving-kindness. And since these
things, and the other countless and hard to tell kinds of the divine economy,
benefited few, while the rest of men were incurably disposed, then indeed,
then the great and unspeakable mystery of the economy comes to be; for the
Word of God himself, the creator of all creation, the uncontained, the
uncircumscribed, the unchangeable, the fountain of life, the light from light,
the living image of the Father, the radiance of the glory, the impress of the
hypostasis, takes up the human nature, and makes new his own image corrupted
by sin, and renews the statue aged by the son of wickedness, and shows it more
graceful than the first, not fashioning this from earth as of old, but himself
submitting; not changing the divine nature into human, but joining the human
to the divine; for remaining what he was, he took what he was not; and this
the blessed Paul teaches us crying: "Let this mind be in you, which was also
in Christ Jesus, who being in the form of God did not count it robbery to be
equal with God, but emptied himself, taking the form of a slave." From which
it is quite clear that the form of God, remaining what it was, took the form
of a slave; and he calls form not the appearing of man only, but all the
nature of man =75.1428=; for just as the form of God signifies the essence of
God, for the divine is without form and shapeless, and no one unless raving
would say that the incorporeal and incomposite has a form and a division of
members,

essence of man.

Θʹ. Refutation of the impiety of the heretics. But some of those who think the
contrary of piety, since they try by the apostolic sayings to shoot down the
doctrines of truth, Arius and Eunomius affirming that a soulless man was taken
up by God the Word, and Apollinaris one with a soul but deprived of mind, I
know not what he understands the human soul to be; but Marcion and Manes, and
that remaining company of impiety, deny the whole mystery of the economy
together. And the unspeakable conception and pregnancy of the holy Virgin they
think to be a myth and a fiction; and they declare that the godhead was
covered with a fantasy of a body, and in this way appeared as a man to men. It
is necessary to set forth to the pious a clear account of the mind of the
apostolic sayings: "who being in the form of God," he says, "did not count it
robbery to be equal with God, but emptied himself, taking the form of a slave,
becoming in the likeness of men, and being found in fashion as a man." Each of
the aforesaid heretics, appropriating these, constructs the falsehood of the
abominable doctrines. Arius and Eunomius and Apollinaris and those after them
declare that the form of the slave and the fashion and the likeness of man
signify the appearing of our nature; but those of the more loathsome phalanx
take the fashion and the likeness as some shadow and image and fantasy
resembling a body.

Ιʹ. Interpretation of, "who being in the form of God." And we shall at once
refute the insanity of both; for he names form of a slave the essence of the
slave, as we have shown; for if the form of God makes plain the essence of
God, it is quite clear that the form of the slave also is significant of the
essence of the slave; but "becoming in the likeness of man, and being found in
fashion as a man," the Apostle has set not as names of the nature, but of the
activity; for since the Master Christ, having our nature, did not submit to
our wickedness, but was free of all, as the prophet cries, that "he did no
lawlessness, nor was deceit found in his mouth"; and John the citizen of the
desert bears witness with him saying: "Behold the lamb who takes away the sin
of the world"; the blessed Paul necessarily said that he came to be =75.1429=
in the likeness of man, and was found in fashion as a man, being delivered
from the wicked activity of men. Therefore also elsewhere he said: "For what
the law could not do, in that it was weak through the flesh, God, sending his
own Son in the likeness of flesh of sin, and for sin, condemned sin in the
flesh, that the ordinance of the law might be fulfilled in us who walk not
according to flesh but according to spirit."

See how through these he looses the unclarity of those. "God," he says,
"sending his own Son in the likeness of flesh of sin"; he did not say simply,
in the likeness of flesh, dissolving the blasphemy of the impious doctrines;
for the grace of the Spirit foreknows all things; but "in the likeness of
flesh of sin," that we may learn that he set "likeness" because our Saviour is
delivered from all sin; for having become man as to nature, he became man
according to sin; therefore in the likeness of flesh of sin he condemned sin
in the flesh; taking up human nature, but not receiving the yoke of the sin
that tyrannizes in men, but throwing off all its mastery, and showing that it
is possible in human nature to get the better of the


darts of sin; thus he condemned sin in the flesh, exposing its weakness, and
stopping the tyranny, and so teaching men to conquer; therefore the blessed
Paul added, That the ordinance of the law might be fulfilled in us who walk
not according to the flesh of the law but according to spirit. Therefore we
have been justified, sin having been condemned in flesh; and our Saviour
condemned sin in flesh, becoming in the likeness of flesh of sin; taking up
the human nature, but not submitting to the sin that of old was master of
this. Thus the divine Paul in few words dissolved the whole swarm of the
heretics, exposing the madness of Arius and Eunomius in the beginning of the
aforesaid words: "For let this mind be in you, which was also in Christ Jesus,
who being in the form of God did not count it robbery to be equal with God,
but emptied himself, taking the form of a slave." For he did not say, becoming
in the form of God, but "being in the form of God." Nor did he say He did not
count it robbery to be equal with himself, or equal with angels, or equal with
the creation, but equal, he said, with God the Father, who begot, the
beginningless, the unbegotten, the boundless, the Master of all.

Arius then and Eunomius received from here a strong refutation of their own
impiety, and Sabellius also and Marcellus and Photinus, who deny the three
hypostases and confuse the properties of the godhead, are from here convicted
as they blaspheme; for other according to hypostasis is the one being in the
form of God, and other is that one in whose form he is =75.1432=; and another
again is the one who did not count it robbery to be equal with God, besides
that one with whom he is equal, but he did not seize the equality toward him.
And besides these also the falsely named Paul, who denies the generation of
the Saviour before the ages, and uses a Jewish mind, and confesses only that
from the Virgin, from these same words reaps the shame worthy of the impiety;
for he is taught by the divine Paul the Word of God who took, and the human
nature that was taken, the pre-existing form of God, and the form of a slave
assumed from that at the end of the ages. And Apollinaris again is taught
together with Arius and Eunomius that the Word of God the unchangeable was not
turned into a nature of flesh, but taking up our essence he accomplished our
salvation. And we have shown, through what we have said, that form of a slave
is named the essence of man; for if the form of God is essence of God; for the
Divine is unformed and without form; and simple and incomposite and shapeless,
the form of the slave also would reasonably be understood as clearly essence
of a slave; and essence of a slave, that is of a man, is held by the
sober-minded to be not the appearing body only, but all the nature of man. And
besides these the ringleaders of the impiety, and the first who sprouted the
blasphemy against piety, and who put on themselves the name of Christians, but
overshot all the polytheistic deceit of the idols, Marcion, I mean, and Manes,
and the initiates and neighbors of their pestilential seat, through these very
words learn their own madness; for not receiving the generation according to
flesh of the Lord and the incarnation, they hear the most divine Paul teaching
that the form of God took the form of the slave; and the form of the slave was
not some fantasy, nor a shadow, nor an aerial phantom, nor is it named any
other such thing, but the nature of the slave. And if they should put forward
against us what follows, "becoming in the likeness of man, and being found in
fashion as a man," and that in the letter to the Romans, "in the likeness of
flesh of sin," first let us refute their mindlessness; for if "in the likeness
of man, and being found in fashion as a man,"


signifies some human fantasy, and the form of the slave is nature of man, the
Apostle sets contrary things; but if the words of the Apostle are not
contrary, let us learn that the form of the slave is essence of a slave. And
becoming in the likeness of men, and being found in fashion as a man, we shall
understand thus, that our Lord Jesus Christ, having our nature, was not equal
to us in all things; for he too was born of a woman, but not similarly to us;
for he sprang from a virginal womb; and he was a complete man as we; but he
had =75.1433= more than we the indwelling and union of God the Word; and he
had flesh both ensouled and rational as we, but he did not endure the
movements of sin as we, but in a body warred upon by sin he dissolved the
tyranny of sin; for this reason becoming in the likeness of man, and being
found in fashion as a man, he humbled himself, becoming obedient unto death,
and death of a cross; and the very name of humiliation makes plain the taking
up of the humble nature. And besides, discoursing about the one being in the
form of God, he added: "Becoming in the likeness of men, and being found in
fashion as a man," teaching that the incorporeal Word of God was seen as a
man, taking up human nature; therefore he also set "as a man," that we may not
think some change of the invisible God has come to be, but may believe that
taking flesh both ensouled and rational, being God he became in the likeness
of man, and was found in fashion as a man; thus the great champion of piety, I
mean the blessed Paul, dissolved the varied and different mouths of the
heretics. But here the Word has carried us, refuting the stupor of the
heretics, and making plain to the nurslings of piety the doctrines of the
truth. Time then to return whence I went out.

ΙΑʹ. For what reason God the Word took up the human nature. Therefore the
Maker, pitying our nature warred upon by the Evil one, and struck with the
bitter darts of sin, and sent off to death, defends the image, and contends
against those who war, not using the godhead with bare power, nor striking the
adversaries with the authority of the kingship, not levying angels, nor taking
the archangels into alliance, nor arming thunderbolts or fire-winds against
the opponents, nor appearing with the cherubim upon the mainland and
judicially giving sentence against our adversaries [cod. mendose
καταψησάμενος], but becoming one of those liable and warred upon, and hiding
the magnificence of the godhead with the cheapness of the manhood, and
anointing the seen man for wrestling, and crowning him when he conquered; and
from childhood teaching the virtue, and leading him to the peak of
righteousness, and keeping him unconquered, and free of the darts of sin, yet
allowing him nevertheless to come under death, that he might expose the
injustice of sin, and dissolve the power of death. For if death is the penalty
of those who have come under sin, he who is delivered from this altogether was
clearly just to enjoy life, but not death; therefore sin, having been
defeated, and having condemned the victor to death, and having brought out
against the one who had conquered the same vote which it always brought out
against the defeated, was caught doing injustice. For so long as it sent off
the subjects to death, as doing just things =75.1436= it was allowed to do
this; but when it subjected the guiltless and unaccountable and worthy of
crowns and proclamations to the same penalties, necessarily thereafter as
unjust it is cast out of the authority. And teaching this


the blessed Paul said: "For what the law could not do, in that it was weak
through the flesh, God, sending his own Son in the likeness of flesh of sin,
and for sin, condemned sin in the flesh, that the ordinance of the law might
be fulfilled in us who walk not according to flesh but according to spirit."
And what he says is such: The aim, he says, of the law was to justify the
nature of men; but it was unable to do this, not from its own weakness, but
through the ease of those who heard; for being inclined toward the pleasure of
the flesh, they fled the toil of the legal things, and went over to the
sensualities of the body; for this reason, he says, the God of all, sending
his own Son in the likeness of flesh of sin, that is in a human nature, but
delivered from sin, and for sin, condemned sin in the flesh, exposing its
injustice, that it subjected the unaccountable and free of sin to the
penalties of the sinners; and he did not do these things in order to justify
the man whom he took up, but in order that, he says, the ordinance of the law
might be fulfilled in us who walk not according to flesh but according to
spirit. For the benefaction of our Saviour goes upon all the nature of men;
because just as we both shared in the curse with the forefather Adam, and have
all come under death as he, so we both appropriate the victory of the Saviour
Christ, and shall share the glory, and shall enjoy the kingship together. And
of these the blessed Paul is witness, reminding of the old and of the new, and
showing that a dissolution of the former things has come to be, the
righteousness of our Saviour.

ΙΒʹ. Just as we shared in the death of Adam, so also in the life of the Lord.
"For if by the trespass of the one," he says, "the many died, much more the
grace of God, and the gift in grace of the one man Jesus Christ, abounded unto
the many." And after a little: "So then as through one trespass unto all men
unto condemnation, so also through one ordinance justification of life; for
just as through the disobedience of the one man the many were constituted
sinners, so also through the obedience of the one the many will be constituted
righteous." These things he also teaches more clearly in the letter to the
Corinthians saying: "For just as in Adam all die, so also in Christ all will
be made alive." From which it is quite clear that the victory of our Saviour
is our victory; for the defeat of our forefather also became a common defeat;
=75.1437= and it is necessary, just as we shared the fellowship with that one,
that we likewise enjoy the good things together with him who was taken up from
us, and was crowned on our behalf; therefore the divine Apostle also said,
that "Whom he foreknew he also predestined to be conformed to the image of his
Son, that he might be firstborn among many brothers; and whom he predestined,
these he also called; and whom he called, these he also justified; and whom he
justified, these he also glorified." And from another place he says: "And if
children, also heirs; heirs of God, and fellow-heirs of Christ; if indeed we
suffer with him, that we may also be glorified with him." And elsewhere: "If
we endure, we shall also reign with him." Therefore on behalf of all our
nature the God Word of God took up our firstfruits, that leading it through
all virtue, he might provoke the antagonist to wrestling, and might show the
athlete unconquered; and might crown this one, and proclaim the defeat of that
one, and prepare all to take heart against him; therefore in the sacred
Gospels he said, at one time: "I saw Satan fallen as lightning from heaven";
and at another: "Unless one enters into the house of the strong man, and


binds the strong man, how will he plunder his vessels?" calling the human
nature the house of the strong man, as having deserted to that one, and having
endured to do everything commanded by that one, and having drawn on a slavery
of its own choosing. And elsewhere also, "Take heart," he says, "I have
conquered the world." And from another place: "Now is the judgment of this
world, now the ruler of the world will be cast out; and I, if I am lifted up
from the earth, will draw all to myself." And going on he says this more
clearly:

ΙΓʹ. That the economy of the Saviour is a common benefaction of men. "And
concerning judgment, that the ruler of this world has been judged"; and again:
"The ruler of this world comes, and in me he has nothing"; for being delivered
from every cause, he had nothing of the seeds of the devil. Therefore he also
stopped the tyrannizing, and cast him out, and made him to be trodden by those
who of old were enslaved, charging, and saying: "Behold I give you authority
to tread upon serpents, and scorpions, and upon all the power of the Enemy."
And that we may see the very wrestling toward the devil, let us come to the
history of the Gospels. Jesus was led up by the Spirit into the wilderness
after the baptism, to be tempted by the devil. And it was not God the Word who
was led up, but the temple from the seed of David taken by God the Word; for
the Holy Spirit did not lead God the Word away into the wrestling toward the
devil, but the temple which he fashioned in the Virgin for God the Word. He
continued without food forty days, and nights equal in number; for he did not
wish =75.1440= to overstep the measure of those who of old had fasted, lest
the adversary flee the battle toward him, lest recognizing the one hidden he
flee the wrestling toward what appeared. For this reason after the aforesaid
number of days he shows the passion of the human nature, and allows hunger to
take place, giving that one a hold through the hunger; for he did not dare to
come near, seeing many things around him coming to be as befits God; for
angels also danced when he was born, a star having risen guided magi, the
chiefs of the same phalanx, to worship, and he saw him from a child pursuing
all righteousness, abhorring vice, loathing wickedness; and these things [cod.
this] according to the prophecy that had come to be toward him by the prophet,
that "Before he knows good or evil, he disobeys wickedness so as to choose the
good"; and John also cried: "Behold the lamb of God, who takes away the sin of
the world." The Father from above bore witness: "This is my beloved Son, in
whom I was well pleased." The grace of the Spirit came upon him. These things
and as many such as struck the devil did not let him come near to the athlete
of our nature; but when he received the assault of hunger, and saw him needing
human food, and not able to endure further than the men of old, he comes near,
thinking he has found a very great hold, and believing he will conquer easily.

ΙΔʹ. In what manner the Master Christ contended against the devil. And just as
in wars one wishing to shoot down him who is covered on every side with the
full armor, surveying the whole with the eyes, searching from afar, seeking a
limb that has been bared, that throwing the dart there he may strike the
adversary, so the devil, seeing Christ armed with all righteousness, and
seeking a suitable place, that he may hurl the dart there, when he saw the
hunger appear, comes near boldly as having found what was sought, as having
seen in him the passion of the forefather. For having deprived that one
through eating of the painless life, he yoked him to sweat and hardship and
death; he comes near then


saying: "If you are Son of God, say that these stones become bread." And he
would not have done this, if the Saviour had not submitted to the passion of
hunger; and one may learn this clearly from the things after these; for when
he was defeated having wrestled, and learned through the trial that this is he
whom all the prophets proclaimed beforehand, he did not dare to bear even the
sight of him as he came near, but at once ran away, at one time crying, "What
have we to do with you, Son of God? Have you come before the time to torment
us?" and at another, "I know you who you are, the Son of God, I adjure you not
to torment me." Thus he was afraid, and confessed him judge; then before the
trial he had not used such =75.1441= words, but coming near with confidence he
said: "Say that these stones become bread." For I heard, he says, the voice
that came from above calling you this, but I disbelieve until I take the trial
as teacher. Persuade me through the deeds that what you have been called you
truly are. For if I learn this, I will flee, and run away, and decline the
wrestling toward you; for I know how great is the difference of me and you.
Show then the wonder and the wonderworking, teach the maker of the wonder:
"Say that these stones become bread." Hearing these things he hides the
godhead, and discourses from the human nature: "Not upon bread alone," saying,
"shall man live, but in every word going out through the mouth of God." I am
able, he says, to be fed without bread. For not only by breads is the life of
men held together, but a word of God suffices to nourish all the nature of
men; thus the Israelite people was fed, gathering the manna forty years, and
enjoying a hunt of birds bound by the will of God; and Elijah had ravens as
entertainers; and Elisha fed the company with wild herbs; and why must I
recount the old things? This John who just now was baptizing in the Jordan,
and who passed all his age in the wilderness, is fed with locusts, and with
the fruit of wild bees; it is nothing unlikely then that we also be
entertained by God with a strange food, and not need bread. Hearing these
things the devil was pained as having been defeated, but he did not give up
the victory, having heard that he was a man; therefore he brings upon him a
second and again a third trial, saying at one time, "If you are Son of God,
throw yourself from above down"; and at another showing the kingdoms of the
inhabited world, and promising to give these in return, if first he should
receive the worship from him; but when again he reminded him of the old laws,
saying, "It is written, You shall worship the Lord your God, and him only
shall you serve," and taught that he will not endure to offer the divine honor
to any other, and reminded him of other words also of God and teachings
declaring not to tempt the God of all, that one ran away, not bearing the
shame of the defeat, and fearing, and trembling, and expecting to be cast out
of the tyranny. For having emptied all his darts, and having brought on all
the wrestlings of his deceit, he found the athlete unwounded and unconquered.
And as having come near to Adam, he did not find what he expected; but angels
coming near to the victor, who were watching the wrestling from afar, as some
lovers served, danced, praised the athlete, crowned, proclaimed, rejoiced at
the freedom of their fellow-slave men, were glad seeing the defeat of the
adversary.

ΙΕʹ. That if he did not take up a mind, the victory against the devil is
nothing toward us. Against Apollinaris. These things refute the vain talk of
Apollinaris, who =75.1444= says that God the Word dwelt in the assumed flesh
in place of a mind. For if the nature that was taken up did not have a human
mind, it was God who contended toward the devil, and God who bound on the
victory; and God having conquered, I have enjoyed nothing of the


victory, as having contributed nothing toward this; but I am also stripped of
the gladness from here, as exulting over trophies that are another's; but the
devil boasts, and vaunts, and thinks great things, and is solemn, as having
wrestled with God, and having been defeated by God; for even to be defeated by
God is great for him; because if God the Word was in place of a mind in him
who was taken, the devil also would use reasonable pleadings, and would say
fittingly: I, Master and maker of all, did not take up the battle toward you;
for I know your worth, I understand the authority, I recognize the mastery. I
confess the slavery, even if I am sick with apostasy; and I yield the victory
even to angels, and to all the heavenly companies, of whom once I the wretched
one also was one; but I took up the battle toward this one, whom you fashioned
from clay, and made according to your image, and honored with the word, and
set as a citizen of paradise, and showed as master of earth and sea; this one
until the present day I continue conquering, and striking, and sending off to
death; having led this one into the stadium, and having ordered him to wrestle
with me, become yourself a spectator and president of the wrestlings; and if
you wish even to do the things of a trainer, teach him to wrestle, show him
the holds of victory, anoint him as you will, only do not fight with him as he
wrestles. For I am not so rash, and out of my mind, as to attempt to wrestle
with you the Maker. These things the devil would have said justly to the
Saviour Christ, if it was not a man, but God, who was wrestling in place of a
man. For if there was not a human mind in him, God being in place of a mind
was doing the things that belong to the mind; God was the one who hungered
together with the body, and God the one who thirsted, and grew weary, and
endured all the other human sufferings. And if God is the one who wrestled,
and conquered, I too have been deprived of the victory, and God is the one who
accomplished all the righteousness. If, as those who confess the quibbling of
Apollinaris say, since it was impossible even in a human [mind] to fulfill the
laws of righteousness, God the Word did not take this up.

Ιςʹ. That those who sin have a defense, if as being weak God the Word did not
take up the mind. And saying this, first they attach no ordinary inability to
God himself, if, as they say, it was not possible, the human mind being
present, to justify the man. And then they also open a door of defense to all
who sin and transgress the divine laws. For they would say fittingly to the
God of all: We have not committed things unpardonable, Master, nor things
worthy of punishment, having received a weak mind as leader, and one not able
to keep your =75.1445= laws. And patriarchs bear witness, and prophets, and
the ranks of men dear to God, both those before the law, and those after the
law, those in the unwedded state, those under a yoke, those in wealth, those
in having nothing, who were not able to fulfill your commandments because of
this one; and these things while being helped by your all-holy Spirit. And why
must one say the many things? You yourself, O Master, have come to be in
flesh, and you took up our flesh, but you threw away the mind as hindering the
acquisition of virtue, and easily receiving the deceit of sin, and you did not
endure to take it; and you became in place of a mind to the flesh, and in this
manner you accomplished the righteousness. In this manner you have conquered
sin. For you are God, doing what seems good by will, changing the affairs by a
nod. But we, having the human mind, which you did not wish to take up,
necessarily are under sin, not having had strength to follow your steps. For
what is a human mind toward power of God and wisdom of God, and light, and
righteousness, and life, and the other activities of your essence, which like
rays and brightnesses rise from your


nature? These things those who choose to be enslaved to sin would fittingly
say, if God the Word would truly have taken man up together, having contrived
the sinlessness in this manner.

ΙΖʹ. Construction, that the taking up of the human mind is reasonable. But let
us leave their garrulity for the present; and let us return to the subject at
hand, and let us show that the economy of our Saviour has come to be of
necessity. For since the whole man was deceived, and the whole came under sin,
and before the body the mind received the deceit; for the consent of the mind
first sketches the sin, and then thus through the activity the body gives it
form, fittingly the Master Christ, wishing to raise the fallen nature,
stretches a hand to all, and raises what lies, the flesh, I mean, and the
mind, which had come to be according to the image of the Creator; for it is
both invisible, and unseen, both unapproachable and incomprehensible, not even
knowing itself, and besides also uncircumscribed; if we should look to the
imagination of the reasonings, honored with leadership and authority, adorned
with arts and sciences, a certain small and young creator, or, to speak more
truly, an imitator of the Creator, king of the seen creation, or an image of a
king, collecting tributes from earth, from sea, from air, from sun and moon
and stars, from heaven and clouds, from sheep and oxen and the other cattle,
or rather reaping all their fruits; for the things that are seen came to be
through him; for God has no need of these. He did not then dishonor the one so
honorable, the one needing healing; nor did he take up what is corrupted, and
enslaved to disease and old age and death, and overlook the rational and
immortal and what had come to be according to the image, and this while it was
in a bad state, as they say; but he made new the whole nature that had grown
old =75.1448=. And did he accomplish the new of it, and leave the aged and

burial, he loosed that old and long-timed power, and promised the incorruption
to those bound by the corruption. For having rebuilt the temple that had been
destroyed, and having raised it, he showed to those lying and awaiting his
resurrection the promises as true and firm. For in which manner, he says, the
nature taken up from you, by the indwelling and union of the godhead, obtained
the resurrection, and putting away the corruptible together with the
sufferings passed over into incorruption and immortality; so you also will be
delivered from the hard slavery of death, and having thrown off the corruption
together with the passions, you will put on the impassibility. Therefore he
also sent out the gift of the baptism to all men through the apostles. "Having
gone =75.1469=," he says, "make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them
into the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit." The
baptism is a certain sketch and type of the Masterly death. "For if we have
become," says Paul, "grown together with the likeness of the death of his Son,
we shall also be of the resurrection."

ΚΘʹ. Proof from the writings of the Apostle concerning the complete nature of
man. Thus having been born the Master Christ, thus having been reared, thus
having worked wonders, having suffered because of these things, having been
crucified, having died, having sent the sacred disciples as heralds to all
men, he was taken up into the heaven; and these things in summary in the
things written to Timothy, the Apostle taught us saying: "Confessedly great is
the mystery of piety, God was manifested in flesh, was justified in Spirit,
was seen by angels, was proclaimed among nations, was believed in the world,


was taken up in glory." And he yoked the manifestation to the flesh, and the
justification according to the silliness of the heretics; and he was justified
by the cooperation of the Spirit. Is then the Spirit that justified better
than the Son who was justified? But these things are no longer not so; for
what is ours was justified through the God manifested in it, who being
inseparably joined to it, both trained it in the peak of virtue, and kept it
without taste of the darts of sin, and uninitiated, and above the devilish
deceit; and having allowed it to taste of death for a little, at once
delivered it from the tyranny of that one, and imparted to it his own life,
and led it up into heavens, and seated it at the right of the majesty, and
granted it the name that is above every name, having given it his own worth;
and having taken the appellation of its nature, the Word of God before the
ages was well pleased to be named Son of man. "For no one," he says, "has gone
up into the heaven, except he who came down from heaven, the Son of man, who
is in the heaven." And it was not he from the seed of David who came down from
the heavens, but the maker of the [heavens], the Word of God above time, who
is before the ages; and through the union toward the human, he receives the
name of the Son of man; and elsewhere again he names him thus: "If you see the
Son of man going up where he was formerly"; not the form of the slave, but the
form of God; and again: "Because he is son of man," he says, "do not wonder at
this, that an hour comes in which those in the tombs will hear his voice, and
those who have done the good things will come out, unto a resurrection of
life; and those who have practiced the worthless things, unto a resurrection
of judgment." And this is not proper of the bare manhood, but of the godhead
that is active, and of the appearing manhood through the joining and union
toward the godhead.

Λʹ. That the form of the slave is named Son likewise through the joining. Thus
God the Word appropriates the cheapness of the form of the slave =75.1472=,
and being God, he wished to be named man. And just as he partook of the low
things of the man, so he imparted the high things to him. For the infant of
the Virgin is called Emmanuel, even what is wrapped in the swaddling-clothes,
and draws a breast, and is fed with milk, and is called Angel of great
counsel, and wonderful counsellor, and mighty God, and one having authority,
and ruler of peace, and Father of the age to come, and Son of the Highest, and
Saviour, and Lord, and Creator of all. "For there is one," he says, "Lord
Jesus Christ, through whom are all things." And indeed the names Jesus and
Christ are significant of the economy; and the economy came to be not before
the creation, nor immediately after the creation, but upon the last of the
days; since then the name Christ makes plain not him who was taken only, but
also the Word who took together with him who was taken (for this is
significant of the God and of the man); Paul assigns even to what is seen,
through the union toward what is hidden, the making and ordering of the all;
therefore also elsewhere he names the Christ God over all; "From whom," he
says, "is Christ according to flesh, who is God over all." Not because the
descendant of David is himself by himself God, and God over all, but because
he was a temple of the God over all, having the godhead united and joined to
himself.

ΛΑʹ. That there are two natures, but one the person of Christ. For this reason
"Jesus Christ yesterday and today, the same and unto the ages." For we do not
divide the economy into two persons, nor do we proclaim two sons instead of
the Only-begotten and


dogmatize, but we have both learned and teach that the natures are two; for
godhead is other, and manhood other; other is that which is, and other that
which became; another is the form of God, and another the form of the man; and
another is that which took, and other that which was taken; other is the
temple that was destroyed, and other is God who raised this when it had been
destroyed.

ΛΒʹ. That it is not holy to say a mixing, but a union upon Christ. We do not
then confuse the natures, nor do we dogmatize a mixing of maker and thing
made, and by the name of the mixing we bring in the confusion together; but we
both recognize the nature of God the Word; and we know the essence of the form
of the slave, and we worship each nature as one Son; for the one is named
Christ by the other, and never was the form of the slave, being bare, and
stripped of the godhead, called this by the teachers of piety. But those who
say the mixing bring in the confusion together with the mixing; and with the
confusion the change =75.1473= is brought in together; and change entering,
neither would God remain upon his own nature, nor man upon his own. For it is
necessary that each stand out of the bounds of the essence, and that neither
God be recognized as God, nor the man as man. And this not even upon the
construction of the man would one of good mind take. For we do not say that
the soul has been mixed with the body, but that it has been united, and
joined, and dwells, and is active. And neither would one say the soul mortal,
nor the body immortal, unless altogether raving, but we distinguish the each
[nature], and we recognize one living being composed from those [cod. from
that]; and we name each nature with divided names, the one soul, and the other
body, and the living being constituted from both we call by another name; for
we name it man. These things then taking as the image also upon the economy,
let us be delivered from that blasphemy, and leaving the mixing, let us
continue using the name of the union and joining and fellowship; dogmatizing a
distinction of natures [cod. of nature], but a union of person. Thus we shall
refute the blasphemy of Arius and Eunomius, attaching the things said and done
lowly by the Saviour Christ to the form of the slave; and assigning the high
and God-befitting and great things to the high and great godhead that
overpasses every mind.

ΛΓʹ. That the taking up of our nature into heavens hosted beforehand for us
the gifts of the Spirit. But it is time to run back upon what is at hand.
Having been taken up then into heavens the Master Christ, and having offered
himself as some pledge of the peace of men to the Father, he sends to men the
grace of the Spirit, as some earnest of the good things that were promised, a
trainer, and gymnast, of the contestant of piety, an unsleeping keeper of
those who believe, a light of those who come near both unquenchable and
without evening, a healer of the wounds of the soul, a physician of the wounds
that come to be from sin, a general teaching to excel against the devil,
making those who creep on the ground winged, teaching the earthly the
citizenship of the heavens, to despise flesh, to care for soul, to spit upon
the present things, to desire the things to come, to see the things expected
through faith, to think nothing in the life splendid, to laugh at glory, to
overlook the streams of wealth, to see the bloom of a body as a flower
withering, not to be in pain when poor, not to be distressed when sick, to
rejoice when wronged, to be glad when plundered, to bear the terrible things
steadfastly, to pray for the adversaries, =75.1476= to bless those who curse,
and all


philosophy simply to pursue. These things the grace of the Spirit taught,
these things it educated earth [cod. and before earth] and sea, these things
even barbarians philosophize after the sojourn of their Saviour, these things
mainlanders, these things the soldiers, these things those who dwell the
farthest parts of the inhabited world.

ΛΔʹ. An exhortation of thanksgiving, and a turning away from excess. Let us
hymn then the supplier of the unspeakable good things, who led us up from the
last irrationality into the former nature, who became poor through us, that we
might become rich by that poverty of his; and with him his genuine Father, who
so loved the world, as to give his only-begotten Son on behalf of it, that
everyone who believes in him may not perish, but may have eternal life; and
with Father and Son the Holy Spirit, in whom being baptized we receive the
earnest of the gift; through whom we are enlightened in the souls, through
whom we are taught the economy, through whom we are trained in the theology,
through whom we are delivered from the irrationality, through whom we have
been freed from the deceit, and have known the truth. And let us cease being
busybodies about the nature of the unbegotten, whether he himself also is good
and just, and whether it is possible for someone to be not having been
begotten or become; let us cease being curious about the generation of the
Only-begotten, both seeking the manner, and judging for unbegotten and
begotten, and measuring the unmeasured things; let us cease tracking badly the
procession of the Spirit, and seeking to learn things known only to Father and
Son and Holy Spirit; let us remain in the bounds which we received; let us not
transfer bounds which our Fathers set; let us be content with the teaching
given by the Spirit. Let us not wish to overstep the knowledge of Paul, who
said he knows in part, and prophesies in part, and sees the truth in a mirror
and in a riddle; let us await the enjoyment of the good things hoped for. Then
we shall learn the complete things, when we shall not be harmed through
arrogance, when we shall not fall through pride, when we shall live in
impassibility. Therefore in the present let us remain in the teachings of the
fathers, lest seeking greater things, we also fall out of the lesser; which
the =75.1477= forefather Adam has suffered; for that one also, having desired
to become God, lost besides even being an image of God.

ΛΕʹ. That one must say Theotokos and anthropotokos. Therefore let no one be
sick with unbelief concerning the theology, let no one limp concerning the
economy, but let him confess the Christ born from a Virgin as God and man
together according to each; for this reason also the holy Virgin is named by
the teachers of piety both Theotokos and anthropotokos; this on the one hand
as having borne him who by nature is like [a slave]; and that on the other, as
the form of the slave having also the form of God united. Thus through
theology and economy let us hymn him who made known to us the mystery that was
hidden, and having constructed ourselves as temples for God through the purity
of the life, let us receive him as inhabitant; and being enlightened by his
rays, let us walk decently as in day, awaiting the blessed hope and appearing
of the glory of our great God and Saviour Jesus Christ, with whom to the
Father together with the Holy Spirit be glory and power unto the ages of the
ages. Amen.
