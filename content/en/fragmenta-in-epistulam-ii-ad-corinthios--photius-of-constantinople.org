One of the consolations, by their own account: one, that he did not rebuke
them in vain in the epistle, and they corrected what they had been rebuked
for; second, that they gave rest to Titus. He himself says: at the joy of
Titus, showing that much thanks they acknowledged in him, that very Titus;
for from his rejoicing he shows both the excess of the rest and the excess
of his thanksgiving toward them. Wherefore also he prefers this consolation
to the former consolation. And he says these things rather, drawing them
toward the love of Titus; for he was about again to send him to them with
the epistle. And what was the rest of Titus, in which he also rejoiced? Or
their very correction, in which also Paul was consoled: their obedience in
all things, and their hospitality with reverence and faith, and their
reception; for the honor and reverence toward the herald of piety is
itself also a proof of piety. 2 Cor 7:14-15 I boasted, I was not ashamed. Or
the two words must be taken as one clause of speech, and the sequel of the
former must be given as the cause, supplying a comma at I boasted; or,
which seems to me rather, each word is to be represented as its own
thought, and must be read with a full stop. And then the meaning of each
word, taken by itself, would be this: I boasted, he says, as: I boasted
well and fittingly. I boasted: I found my boast made perfect. Then: I was
not ashamed: I was not deceived in my hope, I was not ashamed in those
things in which I expected such things. And having said these things he
showed both by the figure of the words and by their utterance the joy, and
the consolation he had. So mightily was he able to set forth through words
what he willed. Then: and our boasting, which is upon Titus, or the boast
concerning Titus made toward you; for this the preceding seems to
indicate, the but as in all things we spoke in truth; for as in all
things, he says, so also concerning Titus. Or rather, as also we think
together with you: our boasting concerning you upon Titus, that is, made
before Titus; for the things said before require this meaning: his spirit
has been rested. I boasted, I was not ashamed. And the but as in all
things in truth will not be contradicted; for as, he says, the things I
said to you I said in truth, so also the things I said to Titus about you
turned out in truth. And so far was not even one of the things I said to
him about you found false, that his heart was more abundantly than the
others, instead of more than the others, toward you, and so on. In
everything I take courage in you, even if I should need to boast before
Titus, even if before any other whatsoever; or in every matter, even if I
must rebuke and turn away from the one who has whored, even if I must
receive and recall the one who repents, even if anything else must come to
be, in everything I take courage in you. 2 Cor 8:1-5 That according to
power, I testify. In this passage he testified to the Macedonians some six
good deeds: first, that according to power. And great is it to make
alms according to how much one is able, but he also says something
greater, that even beyond power, that is, depriving themselves. And third,
that of their own choice, that is, they did not need another's exhortation
for this, but they themselves chose the good from themselves. Fourth, that
with much exhortation, which is of a hot and overflowing purpose of
almsgiving; for not as laying favor upon others, but as themselves gaining
great things, they exhorted, they besought and did not let go, themselves
beseeching; for beseeching us, he says. Fifth, that even beyond the hope
of Paul. Behold where he raised the matter: and more magnanimously, he
says, than even I they were disposed; for I, he says, did not myself hope
that they would lavish so much upon the alms. And he was not satisfied
even with these, but upon the completing of the circuit he laid the
greatest of all. For what, he says, was it that they did, according to
power and beyond power and of their own choice and exhorting and beseeching
and beyond hope? What, O greatness of virtue and almsgiving and worthiness
of the praises of Paul! What was it they did? They gave themselves, he
says, first to the Lord and to us through the will of God. They did not
give a part, and hold back a part for themselves, but themselves wholly to
the Lord, as if they left not even the means of living to themselves, but
giving all, they furnished all their own providence and care to the Lord.
Wherefore Paul, considering the greatness of the good deed, adds: through
the will of God, instead of: marvel not, man; they had God as their fellow
worker, having drawn him through their choice. And where God works with
them, nothing is impossible. Behold also how at the end he called it grace
and fellowship, in that they surrendered themselves wholly. 2 Cor 8:8 And
testing the genuineness of your love. For of love toward God, or, if you
will, also of love toward Paul, almsgiving toward the neighbors is the
manifest proof and test; for I am persuaded, he says, both that God and
that we through him are loved by you, but the fuller presentation and
confirmation of the genuine love is shown through almsgiving toward the
neighbors. 2 Cor 8:14 In the present season, he says; for in that one you
are not able to show forth any of these things. And otherwise: in the
present season accomplish the equality; for in the one to come you will
receive many times more. And the seeming loss is temporary, in the present
season, he says, but the unspeakable wealth is everlasting. That also the
abundance of those. For praiseworthy indeed it is to begin from
themselves, having been admonished only toward the willing, but greater is
that the very willing should also spring from one's own judgment, and to
show themselves of their own choice. If then, he says, you showed forth
the greater, accomplish also the lesser. 2 Cor 8:18-20 Whose praise is in
the gospel. The "is" is lacking, which, having conceived, one must hear
according to the common construction, and also in what follows, as: whose
praise is in the gospel. And not only does he evangelize and preach
wondrously and worthily of praise, but also he has been ordained, instead
of: but also he has been ordained. By whom? By the churches. For what? To
be our fellow traveler in the preaching. Then not only in the preaching is
he ordained, for one must also take the "not only" according to the common
construction, but also in this grace and so on, that is, that as he is
ordained to be our fellow traveler and to preach the gospel, so also to
minister in the alms. Wherefore indeed also he is now sent upon such an
affair, as he said above: for this he has been ordained by the churches,
and it is not now improvised by us, but being ordained as our fellow
traveler he was co-ordained also to minister and serve in this grace being
ministered. 2 Cor 10:12-13 For we do not dare to class or compare ourselves
with some. We do not dare, he says, nor even to number ourselves with those
who commend themselves, nor in comparison with them to differ from them a
little. But "to dare" he calls also to become as one of them, and to have
any likeness whatever to them, showing the thing's absurdity and their
abomination to God. Wherefore we do not dare, he says, because great
punishment awaits such. And saying this he at once advises the Corinthians
also to flee such a disease, and cutting and separating them from these.
And if we dared to commend ourselves as those, we would not have been able
in the word, being absent through epistles, to remain such as we happened
to be also present, but we would have changed hither and thither, and
becoming different and different in the epistles, and we ourselves present
and altering ourselves with the needs and the seasons, now flatterers, now
terrible, and of those ruling slaves, as ministers of their will, and of
the humble masters and lawgivers; for he who commends himself does all
things looking to his own gain, always altering and transforming himself
toward his own advantage. But neither are we as one of them, nor do we have
toward them any fellowship or likeness; for we do not dare to become such.
How then are we different absent through epistles, and different present?
For this is not ours, but of those who commend themselves. And we do not
commend ourselves anything, but we ourselves measure, that is, we speak
precisely, we examine, making the very conscience the examiner, whether
anything has been done that ought not, whether anything unfitting has been
conceived. And we also compare them, whether perhaps we do anything against
what we preach, what we have promised, whether anything discordant with the
former life. And we compare ourselves not with those who understand, not
as making, he says, the outsiders who understand a theater of our own
comparison and examination, but setting ourselves before ourselves as
judges and arbitrators, which is of a judgment without assessors. And the
false apostles, commending themselves, do all things wholly the opposite
toward display and the pleasing of the multitude. Wherefore also they
neither measure themselves nor examine themselves, but it is enough for
them for all blessedness to please the multitude of those who understand
with them. Wherefore we neither, as those, boasted nor will boast unto the
immeasures, but whatever we shall say and do and write, according to the
measure of our rule we also say and do and write. And he himself does not
say "our measure," but having said measure he adds: of which measure he
divided to us, showing that even the measure itself is from God. Or even
thus: lest you think that, having said measure, I myself defined the
measure of a rule for myself, but I say that measure, of which measure,
that is, through which, God divided to us. And what is that? And many
other things, but for now that through the preaching to come as far as you
and to reach you, this measure we have from God, through which he divided
and ordered us to reach as far as you and to preach the gospel. Or the
whole passage can be said from the "for we do not dare" as far as "we do
not understand with" upon those boasters, and the "but they themselves in
themselves" said as the cause of those who commend themselves, as: for
they themselves in themselves, measuring and comparing themselves, do not
perceive, boasting and deceiving themselves; for as he who is wise beside
himself is strongly foolish, so also he who is great and high beside
himself is laughable and last of all. 2 Cor 11:4-7 Another Jesus he
preaches, who ought to have been preached and whom we did not preach. Or
another gospel, which you ought to have received and did not receive. Which
you did not receive, which you ought to have received and did not receive.
Or even thus: through the leading into the absurd he turns away their
attention toward the false apostles, as: for if another Jesus, for the
false apostle says this one coming, that is, coming in and entering from
outside; if then this one, he says, preaches another Jesus whom ought to
have been preached by us and whom we did not preach, or another spirit you
ought to have received from us and did not receive, or another gospel you
ought to have received and from us did not receive, reasonably would you
give heed to them. And if this is impossible, for neither would the false
apostles themselves dare to say this, nor would you yourselves, how is it
not very absurd for us to be preferred over those, who were deprived of
nothing, nor over the superexcellent apostles? You bear well. The "you bear
well" is not of one praising such a judgment, away with it, but of one
refuting out of abundance; for shortly before he called the bearing of
these and giving heed to them corruption. What then is it that he says?
Without pretext is your deceit, he says, when both we and the false
apostles seem to say the same things, from us you fall away, but to those
you add yourselves; for you will not have pretext of the deceit the novelty
and the altered into perfection of the dogmas. For novelty is accustomed
to tickle and to draw the simpler; but not even this pretext was left to
you. And if even unskilled in speech, but not in knowledge. Speech and
knowledge do not always signify the same things with the divine Paul, but
sometimes he says speech for the having of a gift of teaching, and
knowledge for understanding indeed the word of the preaching, yet not being
able also to benefit others and to teach. Sometimes he says speech for
having been exercised in Greek wisdom, and to partake nothing of the
divine, but only to speak at length, and knowledge for having been perfected
in the theology of God, so as both to know it precisely and to be able to
teach others and to bring them to Christ. But elsewhere again speech simply,
as we are accustomed, the uttered word, and knowledge the perception in the
dogmas. An example of the former: for to one is given a word of wisdom, and
to another a word of knowledge; of the second, that now set before us:
unskilled in speech, but not in knowledge; of the third: you were enriched
in him in all speech and all knowledge. But in all things having been shown
forth we are; for the "we are" is lacking, as: in all things we have been
made manifest. And what have we been made manifest to you? That we were
deprived of nothing of the superexcellent apostles, that we are not
unskilled in knowledge, but able to teach and to perfect in the faith. And
simply in all things we have been known to you, and there is for you no
accusation against us nor pretext of ignorance, unless perhaps you wish to
take this as a pretext of sin, to receive nothing from me. Rebuking them he
says this instead of: unless perhaps you have known to reckon our good
deeds, and those in which others boast, these of ours toward sin, and that
in which I took pains to exalt you, this turns about into an accusation
against us. And this is also of Paul trusting very much in his life and his
conduct according to God, that he shows those to have nothing at all to
accuse

in his own rebuking their ungraciousness and wickedness, that having
nothing at all to bring charge, but being even worthy of admiration as
righteous, nevertheless they loved the false apostles no less, if not even
more than Paul. What does God know? That I love you, he says. Why then do
you not receive me? That I may cut off, and so on. It is not wonderful, he
says. Why? Because they must altogether imitate the teacher, who being an
angel of darkness is transformed into an angel of light. It is a word of
folly to speak, we not being fools, nor doing the things of the fool. For
the fool is he who boasts in vain and vaunts himself in emptiness, who with
being a fool both speaks words of folly and does the things of the fool.
But he who says of himself both great things and true and for the benefit
of the soul seems indeed to speak words of folly as far as the likeness of
the utterance of the words, but is not a fool, but even very prudent, and
doing the things of the most prudent, because he also disdained an
unreasonable slander for the salvation and benefit of others. Wherefore the
saying a little before is not against: would that you bore with me a
little, of one about to speak a word of folly, and with the now saying:
lest someone should think me a fool. Lest someone, he says, should think me
a fool; for not for myself but for you am I about to say what I am about to
say. And if not, receive me also as a fool; for I do not turn away even to
be thought a fool for your salvation, for even the things I am about to say
work toward your support and the benefit of the soul. I speak as
bereft of reason, instead of: even if I seem to some to be beside myself in
speaking. I above; for what at all is madness to say of myself above the
false apostles that I am a minister of Christ? Shipwrecked three times,
interpreting he says, that three times the humanity was shipwrecked: once
in paradise through the transgression, second in the flood, third after
receiving the law. A night and a day. By night here the apostle means the
sojourn in the dark error before the presence of Christ, and by day the
sojourn after the presence of Christ in the luminous baptism. To boast now
is not profitable for me, instead of: but to boast other things besides
those of my infirmity is not profitable for me. For he said a little
before: if I must boast, I will boast of those of my infirmity. Then,
setting between a figure of the infirmity, he adds, giving back to the
aforesaid, as: but those things which are not of infirmity but rather of
height and power, these, he says, are not profitable for me. How then does
he say, saying these things: I would not? For the sake of your salvation I
accept also what I do not will. And observe how he dispenses with all
wisdom: he is not altogether silent in order to benefit, and he does not
speak unveiled, but even this one shadowed, in order to flee the boasting,
and then, with the necessity from the false apostles lying upon him,
instructing through this that they say nothing great about us, even if it
be true, except from great necessity. Whether in the body I know not,
whether outside the body I know not, he said a second time, not as one
would suppose, speaking superfluously and placing both upon the same
catching away, but the one he places upon the catching away toward the
heavens, and the other again upon the catching away from there, that into
paradise; and even if he placed it upon the same catching away, this would
not be superfluous speech; for announcing a strange and surpassing thing to
be ignorant of the manner he persists with persistence. Having been caught
up such a one, not as far as the third of the air above our head, as some
imagined, but as far as the third heaven. He says well: I shall not be a
fool; for it is not of a fool, upon salvation and the benefit of souls and
with necessity lying upon him, to proclaim aloud the grace given him from
God, but rather of one very prudent and humane to pass it over for the sake
of the suspicion of a conceit of salvation of men. The "and by the excess
of the revelations" must be heard from the "for I shall come to visions and
revelations of the Lord." An angel of satan, the one served to the opposing
demon, a man; but a thorn, what such were bringing upon him trials and
distresses. And this is of humility, to confess that he did not bear easily
the plots, and that often having besought he did not succeed; for the
"thrice" means "often." I have become a fool saying these things, he says.
And he does not say this in negation, for he himself knows that he said
nothing of a fool, but he says this as in questioning and self-wondering
toward them, as: having gone through these things, did I seem to you a
fool? And the cause of this, he says, runs back upon you: for you
yourselves compelled me, welcoming false apostles. If only I myself was not
made burdensome to you. If only I myself did not yield, doing all things
for your sake, nor slighted you. Lest in this you be overcome; lest in this
you think unrighteousness; and forgive me this unrighteousness. And the very
seeking proof was a sin; for to tempt, and especially after so many things,
was ungracious and of the ungrateful and of the unstable. For through our
sins and infirmities the strong and sinless one was crucified. Or out of
infirmity, as the faithless were saying: others he saved, himself he is not
able to save; or out of infirmity, as it is natural for you to think: for
if you still seek proof, then also the cross will be thought to have been
endured out of infirmity. Out of the power of God, instead of: as God, as
powerful, as willingly and what he suffered having suffered. Or thus: even
if he was crucified, he says, as having taken up the passible and infirm
nature, but he lives, risen from the dead, as bearing in himself the
almighty and divine nature. And for we also are weak in him must be heard
either that what we suffer we suffer through him, or that we seem to you to
be weak from not punishing and avenging you. But even this we do in Christ;
for he himself taught us to bear and to be patient, but as to bear and to be
patient, so upon those having unfeeling and not repenting to show forth the
avenging and chastening power; for so also he himself does. But we shall
live with him, that is, we shall be shown to you living, not simply but with
Christ himself; and not even this simply living, but having the life from
his power, through which he himself also trod down death and despoiled hades
and rose and lives. And this life and power are manifestly avenging upon
those doing the works of the wicked one, as saving upon those performing
repentance. Wherefore he says that you not despise us, as seeing us
persecuted and suffering myriad things; so also Christ was despised being
crucified, but risen he did all things whatsoever he willed. And we also
what we suffer we suffer through him, so that even if we suffer, that one
lives and is able, and we with him, as: he willing, we also both live and
are able to do what we will toward you, and especially toward you, because
we have many just charges against you as you disbelieved. Are we not
tempting ourselves, if we are in the faith? Are we not proving ourselves?
For to still seek proof of the Christ speaking in me, and not yet to be
firmly persuaded that Christ speaks in the teacher, what is it other than
both to tempt ourselves whether we are faithful, and to doubt even about
ourselves? But rather it is altogether to be ignorant that Christ is in
you; for if Christ is not in the teacher, much more neither in the
disciples. But how, he says, do you not recognize that Christ is in you,
who also enriched you with various gifts? Or through your works having
presented yourselves unapproved and you fell away from these? And he says
this in order to say that, if you received and have gifts and power against
unclean spirits, unless you made yourselves unapproved and were deprived of
these, do not I much rather have authority against those who choose to
perform the wills of the unclean demons, who are you? Or even thus: do you
seek proof in me? You tempt yourselves foolishly, you prove yourselves if
you are in the faith. For as it seems, you fell away even from this, since
you would not have sought proof. Then, as of a lioness the vehemence of the
rebuke: or do you not recognize, he says, yourselves that Jesus Christ is in
you? Instead of: I recognize you thus that Christ is in you; and if you
yourselves are ignorant of this, then the charge is yours. Therefore you
have made yourselves unapproved. And at the same time he also reproaches, as
if I know Christ to be in you, unless you have become unapproved, how do you
still seek proof of him in me? And it is manifest that the passage can be
received in three ways: according to counsel and advice, as other things;
according to self-wondering and refutation; according to rebuke and
reproach, as now it lies. And I pray to God that you do no evil. How then do
you both hope to be seen approved, and again pray that it not come to be? I
pray to be made better and that I need not the punishment upon you, nor be
shown approved in it. This I pray, in this I rejoice. But if you persist in
sin despising the punishment, being pained and heavy in spirit nevertheless
I shall punish; for easy for me is this to hope. Rejoice, having learned
that what I threatened I threatened not that I might do, nor do I will
this, but I even pray that it not come to be; and I threatened in order
that, corrected, you suffer nothing grievous. Wherefore rejoice. Be
perfected, as it were you are made perfect in virtue, and do the other
things; for these, you accomplishing them, you will not only suffer nothing
terrible, but also the God of love and peace will be with you.
