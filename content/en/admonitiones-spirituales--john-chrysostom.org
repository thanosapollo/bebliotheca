Spiritual admonitions.
=62.741=
It is time then for you to come to your senses and shake off all things. Remember that today
or tomorrow you are to see the heavens, and to behold the angels, and to stand before
the fearful judgment seat, and to come before God in his court, in the tribunal of
temperance and righteousness and truth, and to give an account of all your life. If you
wish to see the Holy One, sanctify yourself; if you wish to see the Holy and Undefiled,
purify yourself first. From the moment one comes into the fear of God, he has dedicated
his whole self to God, and no longer has authority over himself; for to the law of God
=62.742= he has enrolled himself. Let him therefore deny himself and his own wills, and
follow God. How great a sea has the conduct of this flesh raised in life? How many waves
has it worked? How many tumults? How many winds? How many shipwrecks? How many lawless
deeds and sins? How many disasters and tragedies? How many evils in all the cities? How
many in houses? How many in the marketplace? What is its power? Or what is its strength?
It has all things full of fear. Its birth is fear, death is fear, its life is fear, the
things after death unspeakable and beyond telling. The turning of a balance is in our
heart; there the scale tips =62.743=. Knock, make haste, that it may be opened to you
quickly; remember that not long hence you leave behind all that is seen, heaven and earth,
men, affairs; remember that you are nothing, neither in body nor in soul. A small care has
troubled you, a small anxiety has struck you with fear, a small affliction has cast you
down, a small pain has thrown you into confusion, a mere word has thundered against you.
And why will you not hate this life? But I tell you that you ought to migrate, and to pitch
yourself a tent above at the gates of heaven, and there to live in solitude, and to entreat
God until death, that he may receive you graciously, and open to you in due season quickly.
Below on earth let the body bend the knee to God; and above let the soul entreat
continually, casting itself upon its face; let them contend which shall labor more in the
petition. For you, then, the world has died; for you the coming judgment has come; as much
as you can, thrust this away, and remember that.

It is better to reckon the evils of the life to come than the goods of the present; those
procure for you the fear of God, but these even plunder the fear you have. In spiritual
matters we are all orphans; we have no helper except God, and our own earnestness.
Therefore, having spat upon all things, let us take thought for ourselves. Remember
yourself and the evils that surround you; remember your sins at last, and your judgment;
remember that you are a man, mortal and weak and wretched, filthy and unclean; remember
that you are a man living in a life of affliction and full of dangers, that has killed
many, both good and bad, wise and unwise, rich and poor. Come, bring, that you may eat and
drink, and you have not even the security of one morsel. Remember that you are a man, bones
brought low from head to feet, unable to bear one day's toil, or one night's vigil;
remember how many times you have offended God in works and words and thoughts; remember
that you wrestle with a lion and a dragon day and night; reckon how many souls there are
in Hades, because they despised the fear of God, begging to obtain a little relief, and not
obtaining it, but in the lowest parts of the earth lamenting their fornications and their
sloth and their luxuries, their wickedness and their word-fights. Remember that not far off
you must die and be judged, and do not cease reciting this to your own soul. Write this
little saying in your heart; bind it about your neck. It is better than a royal diadem;
this leads to punishment, that procures eternal life. Meditate on these things; be in them
continually. If you are in these, you will never be found in the contrary things; but if
you are not pricked to the heart in these, with what shall your heart be salted? For even
if the day of the Lord is near, let us fear and keep quiet. He who wishes to be blameless
in works and words and thoughts, what else ought he to do, except continually to keep quiet
and pray and be silent and despise all things upon the earth? For he who wishes to
accomplish this makes himself a god upon the earth, according to him who said, Become
imitators of God. See, we have been made children by God; what is more just than that a son
should imitate his father? If you are a genuine child, follow the paternal commandments,
and especially as they are just and profitable.

He who has a battle against demons, what ought he to do, except pray without ceasing? He
who is in danger every day in soul and body, what else ought he to do, except to be in
anguish and to entreat God? But you say, The passions tyrannize me, and fear is a tyrant.
A tyrant opposes a tyrant. He only shakes the earth, and all tremble. Who does not fear the
river of fire? One day of the darkness is beyond all the evils in life; therefore despise
the things here as beyond consolation. Has someone insulted you, or struck you? Hope in the
Lord, that he will give you a great reward for your patience. From hope comes faith, from
these charity; because for a small word and a small patience the Master grants you paradise,
he crowns you as an athlete for a cold drink; for your faith is of great worth. Make virtue
a bride for yourself, and self-control and temperance, your living children, going before
you =62.744= into the kingdom of heaven.

Rising in the morning let your soul keep vigil toward the Lord, and departing in the
evening, let it rest upon a spiritual bed, upon pious thoughts, upon the hope of God, upon
dispassion and purity; because the commandments of God are light and life and rest,
blessing and righteousness; but the injunctions of Satan are darkness and death, punishment
and sin and a curse. It is in you to follow either these or those. If you follow the Lord's
commandments, all good things are yours, the light, the life, the rest, the blessing, the
crown of righteousness; but if you obey those of Satan, the contrary of these meets you.
What fool then chooses shame and reproach with eternal punishments, instead of glory and
praises and immortal delight? Arise, hasten, run, press on; the marriage is prepared, the
oxen have been slain, the fatted things wait ready for you, the guests at the feast, as it
is written, The righteous shall wait for me, until you repay me. Make haste, that you may
overtake it. Behold the joy, behold the rest, behold the light, behold the life, behold the
glory, behold the delight, behold the true and eternal things, behold the great and
marvelous, behold all things as you wish, or rather even beyond what you wish. Only know
that God has made his holy ones marvelous, has glorified his saints, has made those who
fear him a boast, has exalted those who love him. Let us not envy ourselves, with so many
goods set before us, such a hope. Others for the sake of a little gain contend, make war,
go to sea, run risks; we who are about to inherit new heavens and a new earth, why do we
lose heart? why do we hesitate? why are we corrupted in this matter? We do all things that
produce passion, that provoke both body and soul; our luxury has no peace; sleep, walking,
all things are full of stumbling. Who has lived as he wished? What of this life is not
burdensome? This flesh of ours does not come to terms with us; when it is healthy it makes
war, when it is sick it afflicts. Who has gone out of this life without grief? Who has not
tasted the salt waters of this bitter sea, of the successive waves? Who has not groaned
over them? How many great men has the present life tripped up, and snared as they were
running well? And it is no wonder; for demons are the ones at work, the devil is the one
lying in wait.

Knowing these things then, let us repent with our whole heart, lest our pleasures be turned
into lamentations and tears; let us help ourselves, let us tear our soul away from this
world, and even from our own flesh; let it find itself a cell at the ends of the earth,
that it too may cry to the Lord: Have mercy on me, the solitary one; behold, I have left
all things and followed you; behold, I have denied all things for your fear. Let our
compunction reach to the clouds, let our groanings draw near to the heavens, let our
prayers and our alms come before God. Let us serve him with all truth, with reverence and
dread; let us do all things, that we may not repent there; let us endure all things, that
we may also enjoy the goods of God, that we may acquire the greater boldness, that we may
be counted worthy of more honorable gifts, that we may not be seen second to any of the
saints. Great is the trial of our life, and no one knows it; great is the storm, and no one
perceives it.

Let us give place to the working of the passions, and to the sin that dwells in us
according to the Apostle; let us give place to the provocations of men; let us give place
to this flesh and its desires, let us give place to this world and its hardships, let us
give place to all the temptations, to all the afflictions; let us hide from them; for we
are pursued by them, and we are all sought out to be killed by demons, by affairs, by men
stirred up by Satan. Let us attend only to ourselves and to God, having given over this
body of ours to those who wish to strike it; for it has been found worthy of many blows.
Let us stretch our heart upward, pouring out our whole soul before God, that we may be
saved and have mercy and live, leaping and delighting and rejoicing, unto the ages of ages.
Amen.
