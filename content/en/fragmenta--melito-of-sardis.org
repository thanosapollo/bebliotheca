Melito's Little Book to Antoninus 1.1 And in the book to the emperor he
records that such things as these have happened against us in his time. "For
what has never happened before is now being persecuted: the race of the godly,
driven by new edicts throughout Asia. For the shameless informers and lovers
of what belongs to others, having their pretext from the decrees, openly
plunder, seizing by night and by day those who do no wrong." 1.2 And after
other things he says: "And if this is done at your command, let it be done
well; for a just king would never counsel unjustly, and we gladly bear the
prize of such a death. But this one petition we offer you: that you yourself
first come to know the workers of such contention, and judge justly whether
they are worthy of death and punishment, or of safety and quiet. But if this
counsel and this new decree, which is not even fitting against barbarian
enemies, is not from you, much more do we beg you not to overlook us in such
public plunder." 1.3 To these he adds again, saying: "For our philosophy first
flourished among barbarians, but having blossomed among your nations in the
great reign of Augustus, your ancestor, it became especially an auspicious
good for your empire. For from that time the power of the Romans grew to
greatness and splendor; of which you have become, and will be with your son, a
hoped-for successor, guarding the philosophy that was reared with the empire
and began to rule together with Augustus, which your ancestors also honored
along with the other religions. And this is the greatest proof that our word
grew together for good with the empire that began well: that nothing base has
befallen from the rule of Augustus, but on the contrary all things splendid
and glorious according to the prayers of all. Alone of all, persuaded by
certain envious men, Nero and Domitian wished to set our word in slander, from
whom it has also come about that the lie of the informer's charge has flowed
by irrational custom against such people. But your pious fathers corrected
their ignorance, often rebuking in writing many who dared to make innovations
about these things; among whom your grandfather Hadrian is seen writing to
many others, and also to Fundanus the proconsul, governor of Asia, and your
father, while you were administering all things with him, wrote to the cities
that they should make no innovation concerning us, among which also to the
Larissaeans and to the Thessalonians and Athenians and to all the Greeks. And
you, having concerning these things the same mind as they, and much more
humane and more philosophical, we are persuaded, will do all that we ask of
you." 2.τ εχ εοδεμ οπερε 2.1 Melito, bishop of the Sardians, after many of the
things presented by the same Justin, says: "We are not servants of stones that
have no perception, but worshippers of God alone, who is before all and over
all, and of his Christ, who is God the Word before the ages," and what
follows. 3.τ Melito's Extracts 3.1 And in the Extracts written by him, the
same man, beginning in the preface, makes a catalogue of the acknowledged
writings of the old covenant; which it is also necessary to list here, and he
writes thus. "Melito to Onesimus the brother, greeting. Since you have often
asked, using zeal toward the word, that extracts be made for you from both the
law and the prophets concerning the Savior and all our faith, and you also
wished to learn the exact account of the ancient books, how many they are in
number and of what sort in order, I was eager to do such a thing, knowing your
zeal concerning the faith and your love of learning concerning the word, and
that you prefer these things above all with longing toward God, striving for
eternal salvation. Going up therefore into the East and arriving even at the
place where it was preached and done, and learning accurately the books of the
old covenant, I sent them to you, having set them down in order; of which the
names are: five of Moses, Genesis Exodus Numbers Leviticus Deuteronomy, Joshua
son of Nun, Judges, Ruth, four of Kingdoms, two of Chronicles, Psalms of
David, Proverbs of Solomon which is also Wisdom, Ecclesiastes, Song of Songs,
Job, of the prophets Isaiah, Jeremiah, of the twelve in a single book, Daniel,
Ezekiel, Esdras; from which I also made the extracts, dividing them into six
books." 4.τ Melito's On the Pascha 4.1 In On the Pascha, then, beginning, he
indicates the time at which he was composing in these words: "Under Servilius
Paulus, proconsul of Asia, at which time Sagaris was martyred, there was much
inquiry in Laodicea concerning the Pascha, which happened to fall in those
days, and these things were written." 5.τ Melito's (On the Devil and the
Revelation of John) 5.1 "Melito, then, the one in Asia, says that he is a type
of the devil who rose up against the kingdom of Christ," and having mentioned
this alone he did not work out the passage. 6.τ Melito's On the Incarnation of
Christ 6.1 Melito in the third discourse On the Incarnation of Christ ...
answered, saying: "There is no necessity for those who have understanding,
from the things which Christ did after the baptism, to establish the true and
not imaginary character of his soul and of his body, of our human nature. For
the things done by Christ after the baptism, and especially the signs, made
plain his divinity hidden in flesh, and gave assurance to the world. For being
God and at the same time a perfect man, the same one gave us assurance of his
two substances: his divinity through the signs in the three-year period after
the baptism, and his humanity in the thirty years before the baptism, in
which, because of the incompleteness according to the flesh, he hid the signs
of his divinity, although he was true God existing before the ages." 7.τ
Melito's On the Pascha 7.1 Of Melito bishop of Sardis, from the discourse on
the Passion: "God has suffered by an Israelite right hand." 8α.τ Melito 8α.1
For who does not know the books of Irenaeus and of Melito and of the rest,
proclaiming Christ as God and man? 8β.τ Of Melito bishop of Sardis, On the
Bath 8β.1 "And what gold, or silver, or bronze, or iron, when fired, is not
baptized in water? One of them so that it may be brightened through the color,
another so that it may be strengthened through the dipping. And the whole
earth is washed by rains and rivers, and having been washed it is well
cultivated. Likewise also the Egyptian land, washed by a swelling river,
increases the crop and fills the ear, and yields a hundred measures through a
good washing. And indeed the air itself is washed by the descents of the
droplets. The many-flowered iris, mother of the rains, is also washed,
whenever she arches rivers along the streams, calling them with a
water-leading wind. And if you wish to behold the heavenly things being
baptized, come now to the ocean, and there I will show you a new spectacle: a
spread-out sea, and a boundless sea, and an untried depth, and an immeasurable
ocean, and pure water, the baptistery of the sun, and the lamp of the stars,
and the bath of the moon. And how they are washed mystically, learn from me
faithfully. The sun, having opened the course of the day with fiery
horsemanship, becoming fire-like by the whirling of the course and kindled
like a torch, and having burned through the middle zone of the course, so
that, if he were seen near, he would burn up the earth with ten ray-shooting
lightnings, being put to shame he goes down into the ocean. Just as a bronze
sphere, full of fire within, flashing forth much light, is washed in cold
water, sounding greatly, and being brightened from the gleam; and the fire
within is not quenched, but flashes forth again, rekindled: so also the sun,
fired like lightning, not dying at all, is washed in cold water, having the
fire unsleeping; and having been washed with a mystical baptism, he is greatly
gladdened, having the water as food; and being one and the same, he rises to
men as a new sun, strengthened from the deep, purified from the bath; and
having driven out the nocturnal darkness, he has begotten a bright day. And
according to his course, both the motion of the stars and the nature of the
moon also work; for they are washed into the baptistery of the sun, as good
disciples; for the stars with the moon pursue in the track of the sun, having
a pure gleam. And if the sun with stars and moon is washed in the ocean, why
is Christ also not washed in the Jordan? King of the heavens, and ruler of
creation, sun of the rising, who appeared both to the dead in Hades and to
mortals in the world, and this sun alone has risen from heaven." 9.τ Of the
blessed Melito of Sardis εχ οπερε ινξερτο 9.1 "For as a ram he was bound, he
says concerning our Lord Jesus Christ, and 'as a lamb he was shorn and as a
sheep he was led to slaughter and as a lamb' he was crucified, and he bore the
wood upon his shoulders, being led up to be slaughtered as Isaac by his
father. But Christ suffered, and Isaac did not suffer; for he was a type of
the Christ who was going to suffer. [But also the one who became the type of
Christ furnished astonishment and fear to men. For it was possible to behold a
new mystery, a son being led by the father onto a mountain to slaughter, whom,
having bound his feet, he set upon the wood of the offering, preparing with
haste the things for his slaughter. And Isaac is silent, fettered as a ram,
not 'opening his mouth', nor uttering with a voice. For not fearing the sword,
nor being terrified at the fire, nor being grieved at the suffering, he bore
it, enduring the type of the Lord. Isaac was therefore set forth in the midst,
foot-bound as a ram, and Abraham standing by and holding the naked sword, not
ashamed to kill his son." 10.τ Of Melito of Sardis εχ οπερε ινξερτο 10.1 "In
place of Isaac the righteous a ram appeared for slaughter, that Isaac might be
loosed from bonds. That one, being slaughtered, redeemed Isaac; so also the
Lord, being slaughtered, saved us, and being bound he loosed, and being
sacrificed he redeemed."

11.τ εχ οπερε ινξερτο 11.1 "For the Lord the lamb was as the 'ram, which
Abraham saw caught in a plant sabek'; but the plant showed forth the cross,
and that place Jerusalem, and the lamb the Lord 'entangled' for slaughter."
12.τ εχ οπερε ινξερτο 12.1 "'Caught by the horns', the Syrian and the Hebrew
say hanging, as more clearly typing the cross, but also the 'ram' makes this
precise; for he did not say lamb, young, as Isaac, but 'ram', as the Lord,
perfect. And just as he called the holy cross 'a plant sabek', that is, of
release, so also Ezekiel at the end called 'water of release' that which types
the holy baptism. For two things have been established that furnish release of
sins, the passion through Christ and baptism."
