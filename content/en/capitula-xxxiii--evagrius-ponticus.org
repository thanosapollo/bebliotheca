Of Evagrius the Monk, Thirty-Three Chapters in Sequence.
Αʹ. Jaundice is a most evil habit of a rational soul, according to
which it sees both God and the contemplation of the things that have
come to be erroneously. =40.1265=
Βʹ. Opisthotonos is a vice of rational nature, according to which it
becomes hard to bend toward virtue and the knowledge of God.
Γʹ. Gangrene is a boiling and movement of irascibility disturbing the
rational part of the soul.
Δʹ. Blindness is ignorance of the intellect, not applying itself to
the practical virtues and to the contemplation of the things that have
come to be.
Εʹ. Paralysis is difficulty of movement of a rational soul toward the
practical virtues.
ϛʹ. Gonorrhea is slackness of a rational soul, according to which it
is accustomed to cast off the words of spiritual teaching.
Ζʹ. Sitting-down is an inability of a rational soul, according to
which it is accustomed to cast off the virtues that contribute to the
formation of Christ.
Ηʹ. Leprosy is unbelief of a rational soul, according to which it is
not fully assured when it handles the words.
Θʹ. Ecstasy is an inclination of a rational soul again toward vice
after virtue and the knowledge of God.
Ιʹ. Crushing is a most evil habit of a rational soul, according to
which it pursues the virtues not for the sake of the good itself.
ΙΑʹ. Truncation of the nose is deprivation of the virtue that
perceives the fragrance of Christ.
ΙΒʹ. Cutting of the ear is hardness of a rational soul dragging
against spiritual teaching.
ΙΓʹ. A freckle is a falling-away from spiritual contemplation, or an
untrue understanding of the contemplation of the things that have come
to be.
ΙΔʹ. Stammering is a passion of the rational part of the soul,
according to which, being scandalized at the things that come to pass,
it is unable in everything to give thanks to God.
ΙΕʹ. Lameness is an inability of rational nature toward the working of
virtue.
Ιϛʹ. Dropsy is an irrational turning-aside of the soul, according to
which, the virtues having been quenched, vice and ignorance prevail.
ΙΖʹ. An ant is a practical man preparing his own food in this age.
ΙΗʹ. Hyraxes are unclean nations that have received the commandments
of our Savior Christ.
ΙΘʹ. Locusts without a king are rational souls not shaken by death and
nourished by the seeds.
Κʹ. A gecko is a practical intellect, leaning upon virtues and
dwelling in the knowledges of the heavenly powers.
ΚΑʹ. A lion's whelp is a passionless man who has become king of the
beasts that have come to be in him. =40.1268=
ΚΒʹ. A cock is a spiritual man filled with knowledge and evangelizing
to other souls the day that has come to be from the intelligible sun.
ΚΓʹ. A he-goat is a practical man rightly leading the disorderly
thoughts in him.

ΚΔʹ. A leech is an unclean nature sucking out the just blood from the
rational souls.
ΚΕʹ. Hades is ignorance of rational nature occurring according to
deprivation of the contemplation of God.
Κϛʹ. Woman is folly calling rational souls to uncleanness.
ΚΖʹ. Earth is a most evil habit gathered from impiety and lawlessness.
ΚΗʹ. Fire is a vice of rational nature, destructive of the virtues of
God.
ΚΘʹ. Water is false knowledge, extinguishing of true knowledge.
Λʹ. A mocking eye is an unclean nature mocking the knowledge of God
and dishonoring the wisdom in the things that have come to be.
ΛΑʹ. Ravens are holy powers, destructive of the vices.
ΛΒʹ. Ravines are rational souls hollowed out from vice and ignorance.
ΛΓʹ. Eaglets are holy powers entrusted to cast down the unclean.
