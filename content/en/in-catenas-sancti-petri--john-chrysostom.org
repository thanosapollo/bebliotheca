* On the Chains of Saint Peter
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1

Discourse on the veneration of the precious chain of the holy and chief of the
apostles, Peter. 1 As many as have become possessed by divine love of the
chief of the apostles, and have been eager to keep festival for him; as many
as, taken in his spiritual nets, have been illumined by the light of his
theology, having set up a common festival today, let us steer the tongue
toward the same praise. 2 For many causes one might find his memory a ground
of festival worthy for praise, and not least even through the apostolic
dignity and the course of the preaching and the teaching of the Gospel, and
the admonition and exhortation by letters among the nations from the
dispersion of the Jews, for whom there was more care and concern to him as one
both born and come from them; all of which, being judged and taken as occasion
of praises, will altogether raise the one being praised, and will bring him up
to the very peak of eulogy, for it is both holy and just, also because of him
who has come for our salvation from the legal writing, that the apostle be
mirrored with the fitting honors, be magnified; and no less also through the
theophany shown to him in the last of the times, of the Only-begotten Son of
God himself, who spoke with him immediately and made him an eyewitness and
initiate of his own incarnation. Upon which he also promised beforehand to
build his own church as a prize of the God-befitting proclamation, and
promised the keys of the heavens as an earnest of the faith concerning him,
and that the completion of those bound and loosed by him upon the earth be
voted in the heavens. Yes indeed, also as one who obeyed when the brother had
called him readily, and as one who had come to Christ together with that call,
and who received the name Cephas as a kind of sign and seal of the faith
toward him, and as one wholly blended from that point with the Teacher and as
it were fired, and for this reason showing the warmth as no other of the
apostles in many things many times. 3 These things, even if by much greatness
they overpower human minds, and appear greater than the power of words, will
not altogether go toward the wholly unattainable and impassable. For he who is
above all apostles will also condescend even as far as our last edge, not only
through the sympathy which, using it before every other, he imparts to us of
the same race, but also through the imitation toward the Teacher, who even
having condescended as far as the form of a slave, trained his own disciples
to be moderate-minded and to humble themselves together with the humble. 4 For
bright also is the feast of his consummation and illumined with much light of
the Spirit: being fixed to a cross, a ready ascent toward him, hands spread
out at full stretch and imaging the Master's passion, a form of crucifixion
not upright but reversed, a riddle of reverence toward the Teacher; the chief
always through shame yielding the first places to him and setting his own
lowliness against that one's height, and for this reason choosing and
preferring the last and inferior things. And lacking nothing of such
brightness also is the festival that has come today [φ. 157 ῃο], both showing
beforehand the causes of the things at the end and of the lifting up upon the
cross, no less announcing the sufferings, because of which the cross too was
sweeter to him rather and more beloved than all; for of his precious chains


it shows forth the manifestation and veneration, in which being bound he
dissolved the many-twined devices of the first-evil one, and snatching those
bound by him he redeemed them from intelligible death. 5 Having these as a
ground of festival, and knowing them as causes and procurers of the
crucifixion at the end, and seeing these and that one having happened and been
united around one and the same apostle, we shall not reckon the present
festival far from the sacred consummation; but having assembled one through
both today, let us make it bright with honors from words, as far as possible,
with boiling faith. 6 This as all-famous and all-holy and a God-awarded
light-bringer of the whole world let us adorn in sacred melodies. This as
conferring the grace of the Holy Spirit and illuminating souls fitted for this
let us honor with encomia according to power. For thus it would be ours to
have shown ourselves lovers of the apostolic praises, and to draw the one
being honored also toward pity [φ. 158 ρο], and to be rich in a helper of the
eulogy toward him. For I earnestly beg that the grace of the tongue be granted
me from him, which the fire-bearing power of the Spirit, having forged it and
filled it with heavenly and unspeakable teaching, magnificently made the
many-sown races of the nations inside its own net. But having come here in the
discourse and having cast the seeing of the mind toward the sea of apostolic
deeds, the discourse, filled with dizziness and vertigo and turned back upon
itself, in concert with the most loud-voiced Isaiah I say: "Woe is me, I say,
for being unclean and having unclean lips" I touch clean subjects, no occasion
granting me the hope of daring toward such things. 7 Therefore having let
myself down upon the abyss of apostolic compassion, with a short tongue I
shall try to swim through the praises concerning him. Let the things about
birth and fatherland and the fathers who brought him around be set aside for
now; they too being solemn and worthy for hearing, but occupying the mind and
not allowing the discourse to stretch on toward the better things, of which
the memory is beneficial and saving. 8 Many are this apostle's God-signs [φ.
158 ῃο] and the multitude of wonders and the other things, as many as the
grace of the Spirit dwelling in him both worked and did through him, which, if
they are written one by one, for it is good concerning him to use also the
theological words, I think not even the strength of words among us itself
would suffice; even if it should play the youth toward this as much as
possible. This man, after the supernatural and unspeakable seating of the
Only-begotten Son of God on the paternal throne, was with and lived with the
remaining company of the apostles, and made the preaching of the Gospel common
with them; and at one time he stood with them at the tribunal and law-courts;
at another he even suffered together with the others in the dishonors and
scourges from the Jews, and there was no place where he was not joined to the
apostles and sharing in their sufferings. 9 The book of Acts then, going
through many dangers concerning him, into which he both fell and was held, and
making memory of many wonders, such as the healing paradoxically worked in the
lame man, the inexorable death of Ananias and Sapphira, the raising of Tabitha
from the very nooses of death, the change and transfer of Cornelius through
angelic visitation, the recovery coming to the sick through the shadow of the
body; and how many afflictions, imprisonments, standings at tribunals he
endured; and how having been scourged for Christ he rejoiced having been
counted worthy to be dishonored for his name; those with frankness


public speeches toward the Jews; and how from an unlettered and private
mouth words full of divine wisdom went out; the earthquake that came through
prayer, and the gathered multitude drawn toward faith. 10 These things and as
many as follow them that he both suffered and did, being referred to a bulk of
multitude not easy to narrate, having cut short and contracted, it proclaims
one danger of all the hardest and hardest to escape, worked with
wonder-working, having both much cruelty of the Jews against him and the speed
of the alliance from God. 11 For that Herod, descendant of the one who first
reigned in Jerusalem, as being of both the Jewish seed and worship, and
jealous at the overthrow of the paternal customs and laws which had been
overturned by the teachings of the apostles and were being undermined toward
destruction, being kindled by the angers of those of the same race [φ. 159 ῃο]
and granting them if he should use still more the wrath and rage against the
apostles. First he had killed James the brother of John by the sword, the
writing indicating his beast-like and inhuman character through the sword. For
it was possible for him even by another death to sacrifice the apostle or even
to strike him down with punishments and penalties nearly of death; but so that
that man's judgment, even more savage than beasts themselves, might be posted
as a pillar, and this man's ready yielding toward slaughter might be known,
after the manner of a sheep being dragged to slaughter, he received the
consummation through the sword. 12 Thus then the sacred James has been
consummated, and by a martyr's death he sealed the apostolic course, having
drunk that cup which of old the wisdom of God foretold him to drink, and
signified beforehand that he would be baptized with his own blood. And seeing
that this was pleasing to the Jews, he added to seize Peter also, whom also
having arrested he placed in prison, having delivered him to four quaternions
of soldiers to guard him. Because the days of unleavened bread were at hand,
wishing, after the Passover, to bring him to judgment to the people. Through
all things the writer [φ. 160 ρο], hinting at the blood-loving and murderous
character of Herod, used such words; for nothing just was done before his
eyes, nothing lawful, nothing seemly and of good report. But as many things as
were toward the pleasing of those of the same race, as many as were toward the
service of their profane choice, these were for him both said and done. 13 For
to seize the apostle and deliver him to a prison and to set death before his
face was the conclusion of the Jewish pleasing; for they thought that, if they
should take this man away and put him out of the way, they would easily
thereafter get the better of the other apostles. For he was the one blowing
against and fighting against in sanhedrins, in law-courts, in synagogues, and
proclaiming the name of Jesus expressly and accomplishing the exceptional
things by the invocation of this one. 14 And it is fitting to observe how this
man too has been eager to be like the scribes and high priests who raged
against Christ. For those men said that his arrest should not come to be in
the feast, lest there be an uproar in the people, as being about after the
feast altogether to lead him to the cross. But here after the Passover [φ. 160
ῃο] this man was to be brought to the (vote) unto death. Therefore of
necessity, these being of the same mind, they used toward the apostle the same
words and deeds also which they used also toward the Teacher. 15 And he marked
off the guard of the soldiers into four tetrads, and not in one place, but in
more and standing a little apart, so that both the security might be stronger,
and for the apostle, if it should happen that he run from the bonds and escape
the nearer guards, he might become both detected and easy to take under the
others.


But Peter, he says, was in the prison sleeping between two soldiers and bound
with two chains. "But prayer was made earnestly for him by the church." 16 For
what reason was he being guarded over so many days, and not, while the wrath
was still boiling and the Jews were thirsting for his death, did they bring
this man at once upon the chief? According to the mind of the writer, because
of the days of unleavened bread, in which the Jews, purifying themselves and
being separated from every out-of-place deed, were completing the feast of the
Passover, with guiltless hands, touching the legal sacrifice. But according to
another manner, Herod, filled with boundless rage against the apostle,
appointed his imprisonment to an extension of days [φ. 161 ρο], so that he
might be tortured and punished still more, and be fenced about with distresses
and afflictions, both by the heaviness of the chains, and by the putting to
death from hunger he hoped, and by the inhuman disposition of the soldiers
sleeping with him. 17 For how many bold and threatening words would they have
brought out toward him through the midst of these days? how many reproaches?
how many outrages and dishonors? here as overthrowing and turning upside down
the things of the Jews of another tribe, there as speaking against royal
commands; and at one time being slandered as factious and a cause of uproars,
at another being reviled as an opponent of the Mosaic laws. And there is a
time also when he was struck and tortured with fists and slaps and knuckles,
and one would not disbelieve that they were not also binding and pressing him
more violently and more painfully with the chains; so that from all things the
onset and perception of the pains, becoming greater for him, might most
quickly release him from living. But they were being convicted through all
things as writing on water and boxing at air. For the apostle, showing himself
rather more firm by the stretching out of the imprisonment, and being pricked
and raised as by certain goads [φ. 161 ῃο] by the terrors circling him, was so
far from weakening and being worn down by these, as the guards were being
covered and put to shame by the saving and courage-making gladness upon him.
18 These things being thus accomplished upon him, the prayer that came from
the church was being carried up toward the heavens; and at once an angel was
sent out from there and, standing by the prison, filled this with light, so
that the chief might have the vision of the wonders being worked unmixed. And
having pricked this man in the side: "Rise," he says, "in haste." And lest
something out of place run under him according to thought, as even Satan
himself sometimes transforming himself into an angel of light, and stealing
the sense in the appearing guise, together with the word his chains fell off
from his hands, so that the words might be confirmed by the deed, and that he
might not think the appearing a rebellious power or a non-existent fantasy,
but a true angel and a substantial vision having the unlying self-credible. 19
And these things having thus advanced for the apostle, since he was still
fearful and trembling, not only being tossed by doubts about the one who
appeared, but also about how he might go through the appointed guards of the
soldiers, [φ. 162 ρο] who were using much security about him and watching
sleeplessly over the matter, the one who appeared says: "Put on your sandals
and throw around your cloak and follow me." For since still, he says, the
royal and at once Jewish fear, and the surrounding and keeping of those
guarding in a circle, have made you shrinking and stunned, and for this reason
you are not willing easily to believe the things said by me, I fill you with
so great a help and alliance, and I beat every fear from your mind, so that
you even throw around the cloak itself without any dread, and put on the
sandals. O the swiftest succor from God and the apostolic intercession toward
him! O greatest prayer in the heart, which as from


some sling of the mouth of the Church sent out toward the heavens, mortally
wounded the heart of Herod, and saved the chief from his hands! Bravo of
apostolic grace and of hope substantial and true, through which, having the
divine as well-hearing, he was snatched from the very nooses of death! And if
someone bringing a counter-argument should be at a loss, why in the world the
Teacher did not make the apostle snatched from the Jewish hands [φ. 162 ῃο]
toward the very coming-to-be of the arrest, but conceded that he be surrounded
also by bonds and prison and a running of days and maltreatment, let him know
that the alliance from God of his being arrested did not so drive the enemies
into perplexity and wonder, as, having taken this man in their hands, and
having fettered him with chains and secured him with guards, to save him out
of the midst of these inescapable and unfleeable nets. 20 For there, being
easy to take for those arresting, he would perhaps have seemed to have worked
some fantastical art, by being far from arrest, and the wonders of the apostle
were in mockery. But here, both bound with chains, and being guarded by four
quaternions of soldiers, and sleeping between two soldiers, and nothing
lacking of as many things as know how to drive the condemned toward death,
thus he has been counted worthy of the help from above, and obtains a
paradoxical redemption; by as much as this is more wonderful and more
paradoxical than that, by so much is being saved out of the midst of the
terrors more extraordinary and more awful than not even coming to a trial of
them. But even after so great a power of wonder-working and an angelic urging
he did not know that what was happening through the angel was true [φ. 163
ρο]; but he thought he was seeing a vision. For the weakness of human nature
was still being convicted, and what was happening above nature and
unspeakably, being measured by the natural sequence, was judged an
insubstantial vision. What then did the angel who appeared? Since he saw him
still being storm-tossed by the wave of unbelief, and guessing that none of
the things accomplished under him had come to be paradoxically, he went
through the first and second guard of the soldiers thus with him noiselessly,
so that those guarding did not even perceive his passage, some divine nod
having bound their eyelids of sleep toward not understanding what had
happened. 21 And when they arrived at the iron gate leading into the city, it
was opened to them of itself, God working wonders here too and little by
little lifting out the apostle's fearfulness and showing him ready toward what
followed. 22 And when going out they went forward one street, the angel having
departed was invisible; and he, having come into perception of the wonder, and
having reckoned both the wonders formerly accomplished about him in the
prison, "and the things now supernaturally marvel-worked" and comparing these
with those and examining them against each other and having been confirmed [φ.
163 ῃο] as to the truth and firmness of the vision and that what was seen was
not a fantasy, but a clear outcome of a deed, he sent forth such a voice with
thanksgiving: "Now I know truly that the Lord has sent out his angel and has
delivered me from the hand of Herod and from all the expectation of the people
of the Jews." 23 What do you say, you who are above apostles? Now you know?
Now you have believed? Now you rejoice at the wonder? Where has the unlying of
the hopes flown from you? How have the things of that warm confession flowed
away. And the keys of the kingdom of the heavens and the being called a
support of the church, where? And where is that burning and fiery longing, and
the unseverable union and joining even as far as death. Did the things of so
great a faith come to emptiness? Did such hopes of yours dissolve into
breezes. Did you perceive the marvel-works accomplished by the Teacher? Have
you forgotten the wave on the sea, and how with the right hand he saved you
when you were sinking? Do you not still hold echoing


his words saying: "He who believes in me, the works that I do he too will do,
and greater than these he will work"? Were you not present with him when he
prepared those already [φ. 164 ρο] dead to leap by a command? when he was
furnishing the seeing energy to those deprived of light? when he was rising
from the tomb and showing the guards dead, and keeping the seals unharmed?
when the power of the signs had been granted you through the coming of the
Spirit? when the lame man at the Beautiful Gate? when Aeneas? when Tabitha?
the one you were making sound of foot and good at running? and the other you
were redeeming from the long illness? and of her you brought back the soul
that had flown away? Having become a sharer of so many and so great wonders,
and some having seen, and some having done yourself, and having taken much
trial of the power of God, now you know that the Lord has sent out his angel
and has delivered you from the hand of Herod? If you were not confirmed by the
wonders that had come before, how will you be confirmed by the one? If the
multitude of these did not furnish you a confirmation of the truth, how will
the single one soothe your reasoning as it is being waved? Yes, he says, for
the things of the divine power are not such as to be easily grasped and to be
led easily toward faith. For this not only does not increase [φ. 164 ῃο] the
wonders, but even lessens them and diminishes them and wipes the characters of
them out of the soul; but what has been tested often with torture and doubtful
reasonings, and has produced the fantastical even more than the true, as the
unstable and weak of the soul makes even the things terrible beyond sense hard
to receive as to what is being worked; it knows, after that much and varied
tossing of the reasonings, toward the very end to send in and deepen in the
souls the faithful and true, and to grant a fixed and strong permanence. 24
From here having come to the house of the faithful gathered together and
having struck the gate, he was being recognized by the servant-girl and was
being reported to those inside, but he was being disbelieved, as she was
bringing good news of his presence, so that she was even charged with madness
and they thought not him, but his angel, to be present. 25 And when the gate
had been opened and he was going in toward the inner part, both ecstasy and
amazement held them, seeing the unhoped-for of the redemption, and he at once
was narrating the seizure by Herod, the imprisonment in prison, the binding
through iron chains, the angelic appearing [φ. 165 ρο], and how through that
one's vision the chains were falling from the hands, and he himself following
him passed the guards of those watching and had gone through the gate opened
of itself. 26 At which things having been terrified and struck with amazement
at the paradoxical of the narrative they were not able to remain upon
themselves, the joy having been mixed with the wonder and being besieged by
these two passions. For such are the things of God, neither preparing the
hearer to be loosened by too much good cheer, nor to fall out through the
wonder toward the overly amazing and ecstatic; but through the he raises the
soul and transfers it toward gladness; and through the contracting he holds
back and checks the impulse of going forward and through both he keeps the
hearer in the settled. 27 But you, consider for me the unpuffed and
unambitious, and the moderation of mind in him toward the paradoxical things.
For he did not trumpet the things of the wonder, which perhaps another someone
would have suffered; he did not, as if parading the successes, proclaim these
upon the housetops, he did not from excess of joy triumph the things about
himself to the church, he did not set them before converts, nor before
acquaintances, but having shaken with the hand those in the church [φ. 165 ῃο]
he quietly announced to these alone, with whom also to James and those with
him.


28 So that having heard from what sort of bonds he was unhoped-for delivered
they might give themselves more toward the preaching of the faith, being
sinewed by the hope of the wonders, and at the same time those who had just
now stood away from the Jewish bad-faith, and had been illumined by the light
of the knowledge of God, might be strengthened and called back, of the doubt
about the divine faith lurking in them, and might remain more fixed toward the
good. But O hardness of soul, which having gone through the very intelligible
senses of Herod, has made him wholly disobedient and unreasoning toward the
power of the apostolic wonders. 29 For he ought, having been confirmed of the
paradoxical redemption of the apostle, both from the guards, and from the
chains, and from the sealed gates, and that it did not happen through some
negligence of those grieving his soul, to have been ashamed, to have been
covered, and if not expressly, because of the mad character of the Jews
against the apostle, to have wondered at the marvel-work, yet at least by
himself and in the soul, having been struck at this, to have held back from
the slaughter of those guarding. 30 But he, so that the beast-like and inhuman
in him might even more be increased, brings those without charge under the
vote of death, and this [φ. 166 ρο] although they had not at all been slack
about the guard and the security; for that he was not able to use the wrath
against that man, and to fill his own belly with apostolic flesh, he madly
brings death against those guarding; and if that they had been condemned
because of him, the apostle did not, having worked a wonder, snatch these from
death. The account of the solution has a double word: one, that they used the
apostle inhumanly and beast-like, sleeping with him and bringing the scourges
from words, sharper than the bonds, and raging and thinking against him no
less than Herod; and another, that he did not use such beastliness toward the
chief alone, for otherwise they would even have been counted worthy of
philanthropy, having once slipped in such a fall. 31 But since they were using
the same rage also toward the remaining apostles, and they threw others of the
faithful around with persecutions and punishments and all kinds of tortures,
and they were fixing around them on every side traps of death, for this they
were condemned unto death, paying a recompense of an old and congenital and
incurable evil. 32 This is the most conspicuous trophy of the chief of the
disciples against the devil [φ. 166 ῃο]. This is the most supernatural
marvel-work of the wonders accomplished toward him. What first shall I wonder
at of this apostle? the fiery of the longing toward Christ? But O the
revelation that came to him from above through the Father! The wishing to die
together with the Teacher, and not even when he had died to be torn away
together? But O the triple questioning after the resurrection, through which
he not only made clear that one's heart-deep love toward him, but also hinted
beforehand the things of the end through the youth and the old age and the
alien girding, indicating the form of the reversed crucifixion. 33 This man,
having received by abundance of faith the revelation from God and Father,
clearly proclaimed Christ Son of the Living God and confirmed the true of the
two natures. This man, being burned with teacher-like longing when Jesus was
hinting beforehand from afar the things of the passion. "Be it far from you,
he says, Lord, this shall not be to you." And having been rebuked as thinking
the things of men and not the things of God, he was being taught silence about
this. 34 This man also was sending the Teacher away from the boat, being
struck beyond measure by the excess of the wonder, and putting forward as
cause of the casting out that he himself was a sinner. This man [φ. 167 ρο]
also was present with Christ being transfigured, and


was eager to build tents upon the mountain, turning aside the murderous
impulse of the Jews. Was it not the same man who in the time of the passion
was also promising to have died together with Jesus? and had taken off the ear
of the murderer with the sword? showing in all things the boiling and fiery of
the faith? Was it so that he might not even be carried toward the overly
unsympathetic and without bowels, that he was conceded to fall into denial,
and to heal the sin with weeping? And what else? I leave aside the cowardice,
the flight, the unbelief, the entrance into the tomb itself, the good news of
the myrrh-bearers toward him, the appearing of the Savior toward him in a
special way, the wonder-working upon Tiberias, and the other things, as many
as both before the passion and after the resurrection were worked and done to
him both unbelieving and believing; which were not conceded in vain, but as
far as it came to human grasp. Sometimes recovering the slack and weak of the
apostle; sometimes beating off the austere and bold of that one's judgment and
through both teaching neither to make promises greater than human weakness,
nor to fall down and be stunned [φ. 167 ῃ0] and shrink; when it is needed to
share the passion with the Teacher, and to show the slavish of the
discipleship, but to remain in bounds of midness, as far as to keep away from
unconsidered warmth and small-souled lowliness. 35 And I shall not shrink from
saying this also about him, that he is known also as a witness, as he himself
says, of the sufferings of Christ, and a sharer of the glory about to be
revealed; that also to the Jews who believed in the dispersion he set down
fitting spiritual exhortations through letters, through which he showed them
love and a support of the faith in Christ, and hinted that his presence would
be from every side, in which he also signifies beforehand the transfer from
this tent and reminds of this only toward zeal and guarding of the divine
commandments; and he makes them fearful and trembling by the example of the
flood and by the condemnation and tartarizing of the angels who sinned,
besides which he also reminds of the second coming, in which he says the
heavens will pass by, and elements being burned will be dissolved. 36 This man
the Only-begotten Son of God also blessed and [φ. 168 ρο] the God and Father
of this one crowned and the kindred and Paraclete Spirit, having sanctified,
showed a God-inspired instrument. This man the ranks of the intelligible
angels praised. This man the chorus of the apostles wondered at beyond measure
and as their own mouth they both embraced and hymned. This man those who have
believed in Christ, having as guide and teacher, do not strike against the
stumbling-blocks of unbelief. This man we also name a heaven-reaching and
many-lighted pillar of the intelligible Israel and a helmsman of souls being
storm-tossed in the sea of bad-faith. This man we also honor beyond measure as
having pushed aside the shadowy of the law and we hymn as having changed
station toward the light of the truth. Truly this man is the foundation of the
apostles, the mystagogue of the heavenly things, the guide of the unspeakable
things, the support of those being shaken, the restorer of those falling down,
the keeper of those who have been fixed, the warmest guide of repentance,
Peter the great wonder of the whole world, the boast of the church, the solemn
speech of the disciples, the ornament of the orthodox, the embellishment of
the theologians, the mouth of Christ, the heavenly mind, the purest tent of
the Trinity, the reconciler of those who have offended, the hand-leader of
those living solemnly, the shield-bearer of those running well, worthy of
every heavenly and earthly praise and of every eulogy. 37 Of this man the
shadows and the handkerchiefs were places of flight of illnesses. And the
chains that touched the precious body, by as much as they happened nearer of
the touching, by so much they shared more ungrudgingly in the working of the
wonders;


those all-venerable chains, the all-honored, which bound that apostolic and
blessed body, which squeezed his wonder-working hands, which were filled from
him with the divine grace, which gush the wonders river-wise, which redeem the
weary from illnesses, which sanctify those who embrace them in faith, which
mystically cleanse the wrinkles of the souls, which check the diseased flows
of the fleshes, which are stretched together with the aerial height reaching
as far as the heavens themselves, and as from some anchor are extended from
the Master's and divine throne. These the ruler of the rebellious [φ. 169 ρο]
powers shuddered at and still even now fears and binds much, withdraws and is
contracted. These also the multitude of the aerial ones, fearing and
