=93.725= Chapter 1. "And it came to pass." According to the history the
prophet laments the captivity of Jerusalem that happened then, but
prophetically and more truly the one that befell her under the Romans because
of the lawless daring against Christ. For there they were recalled from
Babylon, but after the capture by the Romans no longer. And the things written
are also referred to a soul that has fallen from virtue, and has been carried
down into the forms of vice, and has therefore come into confusion; for
Babylon is interpreted confusion; and has been taken captive by the
intelligible enemies. "She became as a widow." Having been deprived of the
bridegroom Word. "Multiplied." She who was first [formerly] full of good
thoughts. "Ruling in countries." She who was prevailing over the vices has
become tributary to them. "Weeping she wept." In the onset of the enemies. And
it is to be known that a soul that sins comes to be in night, being far from
the Sun of righteousness. "Upon the cheeks." Just as we understand eyes, so
also cheeks of a soul are spoken of, through which she is fed the [ρεπονενδυμ
ῃιδετυρ τροφὰς] powers of the word, grinding these down and chewing the cud.
"And there was none who comforted her." According to the history, because the
wrath was upon all, and no longer did one comfort the other. And it is still
[is] to be understood, that the holy angels who formerly loved the soul, when
she gives herself over to vice, leave her without comfort. "All who loved."
The inhabitants of Jerusalem transgressing the divine laws, and false
prophets; and the unclean demons who formerly pretended to love such a soul
rejoice at her fall after the manner of enemies. "Judah was removed." From the
place of virtue to that of vice. "And from multitude." For having been
stripped of the glory she had, she is delivered into slavery. =93.728= "She
sat among nations." For having been added to the adversaries, she was far from
the rest of God. "They overtook her." For a man turning aside from the way of
God becomes subject to the passions, the unclean demons, he says, afflicting;
since then the passions pursue the soul, and the demons who work them, the
Apostle commands saying: Flee fornication. "The ways of Sion mourn." The law
commanded that all the males appear to the Lord three times of the year, at
the Passover, at the feast of Firstfruits, and at Tabernacles; in these three
feasts then the Jews from everywhere come together to Jerusalem. According to
the history then, with no one coming to Sion because of the razing of the
city, the ways mourn. And toward the sense, the teachings through which a man
enters into virtue are in dejection, the appointed time [perhaps eagerness] of
those who learn having been cut off. "All the gates." The teachers, who
neither themselves enter into the kingdom of God, and hinder those who are
coming. "Her priests." Toward the letter, because there are none who offer;
and toward the sense, the priestly manners of virtue, because no one runs to
them. "Her virgins." The sound and blameless manners, not walking according to
authority because of the corrupting tradition of the teachers. "Those who
afflict her became." The Babylonians were lifted up, and the criminal demons.
"Her infants." Even those not yet judged through sin suffer for the punishment
of those who begot them. Or he also calls the saints infants, being distressed
because of the sins of the many, or also infant souls those who have just
begun to be godly, and have faltered about the course of virtue, and have
turned back into vice. "Before the face." Those taking captive, throwing the
babes in front, compel them to walk; and so also the demons do, driving the
soul together into what they wish. "All her comeliness." Toward the letter the
temple, the legal worship; and toward the sense, the


knowledge of God because of the onset of sin. "They became." Whether the
priests, or also those otherwise leading the people, no longer having those
who offer anything to them. And toward the sense, sin having dried up like a
burning heat those who were about to advance in learning, the teaching manners
do not find pasture. "And they went." Being driven together, he says, into
captivity; they no longer had strength. Or also thus, they grew weak, not
being able to look against and stand against sin. "Jerusalem remembered." They
came, he says, into remembrance of the days before her humiliation, =93.729=
of those when they dwelt in Jerusalem, and had the offerings; or also thus:
They came into remembrance of their own sins, because of which she came into
captivity. "All the desirable things." Toward the letter, the temple, the
assemblies, the sacrifices, the offerings, the sacred vessels. And toward the
sense: She remembered, he says, her desires, following which she became a
captive. "Her enemies seeing laughed." For the demons are accustomed, after
they have cast down the soul, then also to laugh at her. "She sinned a sin."
Everyone who sins, it is clear, works the sin; but to sin a sin is said of the
one who sins in knowledge. And he hints at the Jews, that the Lord who was
proclaimed beforehand, and expected by them, having come they crucified. "All
who glorified her." Toward the letter, the synagogue taken captive, and toward
the sense, the soul sick with sin, moved from the right standing. "They
humbled her." Either the demons, who formerly lifted her up, when the
synagogue sacrificed to them, whom she later also turned away from, or also
the souls of the saints, who from disposition love Jerusalem, and glorify her
when she does well, but turn away when she sins. "For they saw her shame." For
through the severe punishment the sins also were uncovered and made manifest,
because of which she also underwent the punishments. "And she herself
groaning." The synagogue no longer serving demons; and such a soul coming into
awareness, and groaning, and in a way wishing to step back from the sins. "Her
uncleanness before her feet." i.e., they are defiled. "She did not remember
her last things." If she had remembered, he says, that there is recompense,
she would have had to humble her own pride, so that she might not fall under
the punishment; for the overweening is humbled, and the humble is exalted.
"See, O Lord." The saint appropriates from there the faults of those who sin,
and offers the petition on their behalf. "He stretched out his hand
afflicting." Toward the letter, first the Babylonian, and later also the
Romans took the desirable vessels, and all that was noteworthy in Jerusalem.
And toward the sense, vice dared, he says, it spread itself against the soul,
and darkened her desirable knowledge of God. "For he saw nations." God
commanded that Moabites and Ammonites not enter into the assembly of the Lord;
when Jerusalem then was made desolate, these also entered into the temple. And
toward the sense, certain unclean thoughts made their way into the inner
sanctuary of the soul. =93.732= "Seeking bread." Either the perceptible
because of the siege, or the word that nourishes the soul. "They gave the
desirable things." In the siege they offered every choice thing for the
purchase of foods, so that they might at least only live, and feed the failing
soul. Or also thus: having betrayed the desirable knowledge of God, they
wished to hear something from the false prophets, whereby to recover their own
despondency. "All you who pass by." While Jerusalem was standing, travelers
often walked through the middle, and rested in her. When she was razed then,
they passed by outside her. He says then, that Wayfarers who run past me from
outside, my word is toward you; have compassion on me for so great a wound.
And toward the sense he says: You who do not walk the straight and lawful way,
seeing my pain, turn back to knowledge of God. "Having spoken in me." Having
threatened with a rebuke of truth; and when also to the


Synagogue: Behold your house is left to you desolate. "From his height he sent
fire." According to his height, he says, so he made exact the punishment
against me; and it also shows after the ascension of the Lord the chastisement
brought upon Jerusalem through the Roman war. "In my bones." For it reaches as
far as joints and marrows, thoughts and notions. "He spread out." The
inescapable punishment. "He turned me back." From the good things to the
worse. "The Lord gave me." i.e., he showed me desolate. "The whole day."
Continually being punished. "It was watched." Nothing, he says, of my sins was
silenced. "In my hands." What I sowed through the deeds, these I also reaped.
Because each one bears his own burden. "My strength grew weak." I was not able
to act against the adversaries. Or also thus: Strength of the synagogue is the
law, which was not able to help her. "Because the Lord gave." i.e., he showed
me having done things worthy of pain. "I shall not be able." My standing was
shaken, that is my support. "He took up all." Toward the letter, the rulers
and the teachers of the law; and toward the sense, the manners of purpose,
which sin, standing against and prevailing, makes vanish. "He called upon me."
He means a time of darkness. For when the intelligible light fails, the
darkness comes in instead. "To crush." So that every good manner of mine might
vanish. "He trod a winepress." i.e. He gathered all my =93.733= sins into one,
which having trodden he made wine; concerning which the Psalmist says, that A
cup is in the hand of the Lord of unmixed wine, that is of wrath. For the sins
being examined, and as it were squeezed out, drip divine wrath, which those
who sin are given to drink. "My eye brought down water." The thinking part of
the soul in a way weeps, and groans over the sins. "He who turns my soul."
Toward the letter, that until now Jews are in distress, and toward the sense,
the manner of the memory of God, which turns the soul from death to life. "My
sons became." The various deeds of our enemy the devil becoming more
prevailing. "Sion spread out." From astonishment she had her hands slack,
having thrown away all good deeds. "There is none who comforts her." Neither
law, nor prophet. "The Lord commanded." The Lord, he says, charged. That those
who afflict you are round about, be sober; but he turned aside into slackness.
Or thus: A commandment of the Lord, he says: Behold I have set before your
face the life, and the death. Since then Jacob chose death rather than life,
those who afflict him became round about him. "Into one sitting apart." i.e.:
Beyond the enemies, in the midst of whom she happens to be, she has become
abominable. And thus after the captivity she remained in her own uncleanness,
not repenting. "For I called my lovers." Her lovers are false prophets, and
omen-watchers, and the unclean demons. And they are called her lovers, either
because she herself loves their deceit, or because they themselves, pretending
to love her, became procurers of the divine wrath, having been able to help
her in nothing. For this is what "They reckoned me falsely" signifies. "My
priests." These things happened in the siege. And toward the sense, the
teachers, who were judged unworthy of the word of God, who did not become
faithful stewards of the salvation of the peoples, did not find those being
taught, who are their food, the people having slipped together with the
impiety of the teachers. "My belly." i.e., all my inward parts. And belly and
heart, according to the laws of tropes, are the digestive and vital powers of
the soul, through which her senses are fed and exercised. "Outside a sword
made me childless." Toward the history, those going out from the siege the
Romans killed; and toward the sense, The onset of the invisible enemies, he
says, from outside, that is not being connatural to us, killed my good
thoughts; just as from within the impulse of our choice toward the worse; for
this is what death in the house shows. =93.736= "All my enemies." Enemies of
the soul are the


unclean demons, who are also called her lovers. For being enemies, they
pretend to love; who after making her sin also attempt at her fall. "You
brought on a day." That of the punishment. "Woe, woe." The doubling signifies
the intensity of the pain. "Let all enter." May there come to pass, he says,
the examination of those who ward off evils from me [perhaps according to]
your truth; for the face of God is the truth. "Glean them." Gleanings are the
small clusters, which often even escape those who harvest. Those then who
glean pick these out, examining the vines exactly. As then the demons set upon
me bitterly; or also as the false teachers and false prophets who deceived me
have caused my sins to be examined exactly, so examine them exactly according
to your judgment, O Lord. {1Chapter 2.}1 "How he darkened." How, as with a
certain gloom, he delivered her to the onset of the enemies. "He cast down
from heaven to earth." The heaven-fitting, he says, comeliness of Jerusalem he
changed into dishonor, thrusting her away from his continual visitation. "And
he did not remember." He calls the temple a footstool of God, as the divine
visitation reaching into it from heaven, or also the Israelite people, who had
been counted worthy of the knowledge of God. For heaven is called a throne of
God, as God resting upon the most pure rational beings there. And the earth is
his footstool, as his knowledge also reaching unto us according to the analogy
that falls to us. "All the beautiful things." Toward the letter, all the legal
worship; and toward the sense, the manners of piety. "He took down." He means
a taking-down, the movement from the foundations. "The strongholds." Toward
the letter, the temple, or also the powerful in her; and toward the sense, he
cast the unconquerable virtues down to the earthly. "He profaned her king."
Toward the letter, the powerful in her; and toward the sense, he showed, he
says, her manners according to excellence to be unclean. "Every horn."
According to history, all the tribes, or all the powerful; and toward
contemplation, every victorious manner of the soul. "He turned back." Either
he turned the right deeds of Israel himself toward the lesser, that is toward
the left; for behind the right are the left; or God held back his own power,
and did not help them when they were being warred upon by the enemies. "And he
kindled." The capture through Romans. The wrath of God then as fire kindled
against Jacob its examining power. =93.737= "He stretched his bow." He means
the complete turning-away of God; which coming to pass, timely blows are
brought by the enemies. "And he killed." Toward the letter their sons, and the
daughters, and whatever honorable living thing was among them; and toward the
sense, the better longings of the soul. For this is what the turning-away of
God does. For his visitation happens to be life. "In a tent of the daughter of
Sion." According to history, in the temple in which they were murdered; and
toward the sense, in the restings of the soul. "The Lord became." They
themselves having set themselves as enemies of God through the lawless acts
against him; for God does not hate. "He destroyed the." Toward the letter, the
walls, and the temple, and the houses, he says; and toward the sense, the good
thoughts that fence the soul. [And otherwise:] That no longer among Jews after
the daring against Christ is there a prophet, nor a revelation, nor a lawful
priestly service. "Being humbled." After the history, he means those taken by
sin. "And he spread out as a vine." Either he says this, that the Synagogue
formerly stretched out as a vine he delivered to the enemies; or thus, that he
laid her open to those who plunder as an uncovered vine. "He destroyed his
feast." Of Jerusalem the legal feasts; and of the soul the joy upon the
better. "The Lord forgot." He despised, he says, even his own legislation,
which charges to keep feast and sabbath. "And he provoked." He allowed them to
be squeezed still more, either himself being provoked, or also himself
provoking at the prosperity of the enemies. "The Lord


rejected." The legal, so that the spiritual of the New Covenant might be
brought in instead. And if it is taken of the captive soul, because she
appeared unworthy of the worship of God. "He shook off." He cast her away
sharply. "They gave a voice." The enemies rejoicing still more. "Wall of a
daughter." He judged [perhaps destroyed] a wall, the law. "He did not turn
back." He prolonged the compound [perhaps the crushing]. "And he mourned." He
did not spare to throw down to be trampled those who sin against him
persistently. "They were stuck." Those entrusted with the teaching were sunk
down into earthly matters. "He destroyed." He made her commands vanish, so
that the one wishing to enter is at a loss, and seeks. "They sat upon the
earth." They were leveled to the ground having minded earthly things. "They
were silent." They lost the teaching dignity, they were made deaf. "They
brought up." According to the history the mourning =93.740= form, as in
captivity. And according to contemplation, from choice, he says, they
preferred earthly matters to their own salvation. "They brought down to
earth." They brought down to earthly matters the synagogues, or also the souls
able to be saved. "They failed in tears." That piety was not troubled by the
Gentiles, but they were corrupted by the bastard sons. "To the mothers." The
sinful souls to the Churches. "Where is grain." Where is the boldness of the
mystery? They say these things, as not being counted worthy of the mysteries
because of the sins. "In the fainting." That is under sin and [perhaps Unto]
death. "In the pouring out." And yet being in mothers' bosoms, they still fail
by famine of teaching. "What shall I witness to you?" What good shall I
witness for you? "Or what shall I liken to you?" What sin shall I compare
toward yours? For truly exceeding is the slaughter against the Lord. "Because
it was magnified." The vengeance of your sin. "Who shall heal you?" i.e., no
one, except God alone. "Your prophets." The false prophets foretelling false
things, and the teachers introducing false opinions. "And they did not
reveal." They did not make the true manifest to you, he says, so that having
known that you do unjust things, you might turn, and not become a captive.
"And they saw for you." False prophecies, and vain opinions, thrusting you out
from God. "They clapped." According to history, those viewing the fall of the
people, being struck clap hands, and hiss moving the head; and toward the
sense, men walking outside of godliness, godless, or also unclean demons do
these things, rejoicing at the fall of those formerly in virtue. "Is this the
city." Of the temple, because of its being conspicuous everywhere; and of the
soul they will say, Is this she who is called perfect, and glorious in virtue?
"They opened upon you." They hissed, they set upon in a beastly way; for
hissing is of beasts. "And they gnashed." To gnash is to grind the teeth. "Yet
this is the day." The confusion from sins; and it was expected, because God
foretells, and threatens those who sin. "The Lord did." He judged justly. "He
completed a word." He brought to an end the eternal judgments, not to keep
silent in sins. =93.741= Yet also through Moses he testified to the
Israelites. "Arise." Be stirred, he says, and examining according to yourself
understand, and anticipate the beginnings of the watch, that is the just
ordinances. i.e., Practice the just ordinances, and propitiate God. And the
"in night" he says because of the onset of the enemies. "Pour out as water."
Stretch still more your eagerness toward God. "Lift toward him." Of those
fainting at the beginning of every virtue, as, someone perhaps began
almsgiving, he did not have the one who urges and teaches, he remained there
fainting. Another of self-control, and so on. For having been cut off from
progress, they remain in what they are, or also stepping back fainting in the
famine. "See, O Lord." For the Lord had said, that such affliction has not
happened, nor will be. "If they shall eat." After the things that happened
according to the history, he means the risings of the Churches against the
children. "A gleaning." The children were roasted, he says, or also boiled,
whom they ate in the siege. And toward the sense, Satan who from the beginning


practiced murder laid the vital parts of the virtues, which are marrows, under
the wrath. "They shall be murdered." If, he says, you show those able to
propitiate you and to teach as having been taken away, the nurslings of the
Church shall be murdered; and the "in the sanctuary of the Lord," That the
manners [perhaps places] of holiness seem to stand, but the power has been
denied. And these things you will contemplate after the history. "They slept
unto." Toward the letter, those who ought to rest, slept themselves [perhaps
put to sleep] unto earth. And toward the sense, Both the inexperienced and the
skilled rested in the places of turning-away; for to go out is to turn away.
That he might say, having gone out of the bounds of godliness, having minded
earthly things. "My virgins." Even those living in incorruption and manliness,
he says, were dragged under by sin. "In a sword." In the overseeing of the
enemy. "And in famine." In the absence of the word. "You called." Toward the
letter he says thus: Those of all the surrounding country fled to me Jerusalem
as a strong city, just as when they ran together in a feast. And toward the
sense the soul says: That you turned your face away from me, you prepared my
enemies to keep feast. For he calls sojourners the enemies who sojourn with
us. "And they did not become." For unless one should fall into complete sin,
he is not delivered to sharp wrath. "As I prevailed." Toward the letter,
Having grown strong, he says, in the sin I prepared you to bring a multitude
of enemies =93.744= upon me. And toward the sense: I was puffed up [perhaps I
was puffed], and I multiplied the enemy manners. {1Chapter 3.}1 "I am the man
who." Upon the person of the Lord because of the measures of the emptying. And
upon the prophet he confesses well-mindedly the poverty and changeableness of
nature; for evil is not by nature, but by change. "By the rod of wrath." From
the person of the Lord according to what was said: Your wrath was established
upon me. And from the person of the prophet: The enemy, he says, the devil
used against me the rod of his wrath, the change into sin; whence also having
taken me, he led me away into darkness, and not light. And these things he
puts forward from the common of humanity. "Yet in me." Not only, he says, was
the enemy content to lead me away into darkness, but he also punished me. And
"the whole day," i.e., all the time in which it came under him. "He made old
my flesh." From the person of the Lord, the falsehood, he says, was built
against me, for they lied much against him. And from the person of human
nature, Not only, he says, was the enemy content to punish me, but he also so
built around against me, that I was not able to escape. "In dark places." This
David first announced beforehand concerning the Lord because of the tomb. And
if from the common of the soul, it is said, that he filled her with darkness,
having deprived her of the intelligible light. "He built up." Upon the Lord,
that those who cast him into the tomb thought he would no longer rise. And
upon the soul, because of the guard that came against her. "He made heavy my
bronze." Upon the Lord, because of the seals that were placed on his tomb; and
upon the soul, because of the collars of sin, which the enemy put around her.
"And even that I shall cry." Having multiplied, he says, the practices of the
sins, he blocked the petition toward God. And the soul says these things,
because she also worshiped idols. "He built up." From the Church, a people of
Jews, he says, of every kind became to hinder the teaching of the preaching;
and this he also likens to a lion and a bear because of the wrathful; and at
least, he says, as far as it was in him, he set me vanished. And from the
person of the soul, The enemy, he says, shut my goings. "A bear troubled." A
bear is sin. And lying in wait, setting upon variously and in a beastly way.
"He himself to me as a lion." The bear toward fear is less than the lion. The
devil then first troubling the soul =93.745= through sin, himself thereafter
attempts to swallow her by terribleness. And "in hidden things," i.e. cunning,
first troubling through sin. "He pursued." i.e., he laid low. "He stretched a
bow." The


people of the Jews, using every kind of reviling and slander against the Lord;
and the devil also having taken the soul under his hand. "He brought into my
kidneys." Against the Lord, having demanded [demands] unto death, and against
the soul the devil, the death-bearing counsels against the soul. "He became a
laugh." The Lord, to that of the unbelievers; and the soul to the multitudes
of the demons. "Their psalm." i.e., gladness, joy. "He filled me." For they
also gave him gall to drink on the cross; and bitterness also of the soul is
sin. "And he cast out with a pebble." The Lord, he says, because he answered
nothing to Pilate. And the soul says, that The devil by the cunning of sin
dimmed my senses. "And he thrust away." The people of the Jews, not wishing to
make peace with their own Savior; and the devil, having stood the soul away
from the peace of God. "And I said, It has perished." These things the Savior
says concerning the people of the Jews, who formerly was his inheritance, and
a hope of boasting. And the soul also says, that My sins having been
multiplied I supposed my recall not to be. "I remembered." The Savior says,
that Having remembered my voluntary emptying, and the sufferings which I
endured, and having these, and keeping them in myself, I endure, so that by
long-suffering I may gain even the Jews. And from the person of the one who
has sinned you will understand thus: Of poverty, of the change; for after the
change the soul is delivered to persecutions of sin. "Of my bitterness." Of
the things that happened to me in the country of sin. "And my soul will talk
against me." Practicing these things in herself she will condemn herself.
"This I will set." The memory, he says, of the condemnation I will store in my
own heart. "Therefore I will wait." For he who knows that he sinned from
choice waits for the discipline of God, and the mercy from him. "And he will
wait." Firmly, he says, he will hope in the help of Christ and Savior. For he
himself also is the salvation of the Lord according to Symeon saying: That my
eyes have seen your salvation. "He will sit alone." The school of youthful
deeds. "He will sit." He remains in the truth; for this =93.748= alone is, and
sin is many, and of many parts. "And he will be silent." i.e., he will put
such a manner to rest. "Because he took up." The yoke clearly. "He will give
to the one who strikes." According to the one who said: If anyone slap on the
right, turn to him also the other. And toward the sense, to the rebuke
according to God. "He will be filled." In this age, so that not in the one to
come. "And he did not answer." He himself, he says, did not prepare Adam to
sin, and those from him; for God did not legislate sin, but we underwent the
change by choice. "To humble." The word holds to what is above; that God
declared [perhaps did not declare] the adversary to be humbled under the feet.
And the bound of the earth, that is those bound with the cords of their own
sins. "To turn aside judgment." To turn the judging part into opposing the
Most High. "To condemn." God, he says, did not command, so that one be
condemned from the adversary in being judged toward him. "Who said thus?" The
manner of the disobedience, and the sower of the deceit. "From a mouth." But
only the good. "If a man will murmur." Let no one, he says, blame God as cause
of sin, because the man having sinned by choice is able also to repent; and
the excuses are vain. "He was searched out." For the Judge is unerring. "Upon
our hands." That is, having lifted the souls from the earthly, let us offer to
God; as a certain offering having offered our own souls through the deeds. "We
have sinned." Since we embittered, you were not propitiated, i.e., We have
suffered just things. "You covered." You covered over, he says, as the clouds,
so that the prayer not pass through to you. "To shut my eyes." The
turning-away, he says, of your face worked for me an absence of light, and as
for those who shut their eyes darkness came upon me. "You set us in the midst
of the peoples." Perceptible, or intelligible, because of the from sins


betrayal. "They opened upon us." Reviling, or seeking to swallow. "Fear and
astonishment." From the various onsets. "Lifting and crushing." Either through
lifting, he says, and boastfulness we were crushed, or that the enemies being
lifted against us crushed us. =93.749= "My eye was swallowed." It encompassed
[ῃιδετυρ δεεσσε ῃοχ, σκότος], he says, my seeing part. And I lament seeking a
waking. "My eye." In fine parts, he says, it will have, and it searches
[perhaps will see and will search] the seeing part of the soul the falls
beyond the healings through the deeds; for daughters of a city, he means the
deeds of the way of life. "Hunting." According to tyranny, having been wronged
in nothing beforehand. "They put to death." The Jews then having stretched out
the author of life, and the demons the soul in their subterranean counsel.
"And they placed." Jews, so that they might cover the tomb, and the demons
against the soul the weight of the sins, persuading to despise the better
things. "Water was poured over." Against the Lord the various afflictions, and
against the soul, the billows of sin. "I said: I have been thrust away." The
Lord because of the passion having become man speaks humanly according to that
in the psalms: I became as a man without help. And the soul of the sinner,

judged, O Lord." From the person of Christ, that the Jews paid the penalties
worthy of the faults against him, having been delivered to Romans. And from
the person of those saved through Christ, because of the resurrection, that he
condemned the ruler of this age. "You redeemed." From the person of Christ
because of the resurrection. And the one being saved, he says, toward him,
that You redeemed me, having counted me worthy to pay my debts through
yourself. "You saw, O Lord, my tumults." Which the Lord endured from the Jews,
or also which the soul from the invisible enemies. "I heard." Prophetically
the things set forth are referred to the person of the Lord concerning what he
suffered from Jews; and they are also said from the person of the soul
entreating the Father himself against the invisible enemies. "Their seat."
That at one time they pretend to make peace, putting inflation, or also
carelessness, under the soul, as having come into passionlessness, but they
suddenly rise up against her. "Look upon." i.e., upon their aim; for whatever
they do, they do unto destruction of soul. =93.752= "You will repay them a
shielding of my heart a toil." Shielding my heart, he says, you will repay
toil to the enemies. You yourself will pursue in wrath; for of no other is it
to pursue and put to flight the criminal demons, than of our Lord Jesus
Christ. {1Chapter 4.}1 "Gold." The evangelical teaching. "It shall be
changed." The Mosaic law, and good, because it alone is God-breathed for them,
although there are many laws among men. "Stones were poured out." They became
common, each upon the purpose of the way of life that is set forth. And the
word has a cry also against the demons who work these things. "Those lifted in
gold." From an image of those being weighed in a balance he took the word.
Those being weighed against, he says. For this is what "lifted with the gold"

shattered, being crushed by the assault of sin? or from not remaining, in the

people." The souls that received their corrupting lessons. Hunted from

the captives from the hardship. "They were dried." Having been cut off from

those who had known, but were deprived of the true food, which is the word of
orthodoxy. "They went pierced from produce of fields." From worldly deeds. For
a field is contemplated in many places as the world. "Hands of women." Toward
the history, one of these was a certain Maria, who being pressed by the
violence of the factionists, always seizing what was being provided, even ate
the flesh of her own child from the famine. And toward contemplation, the
seeming hands of the Churches, the false prophets, and false teachers, whom
they begot in the faith, they held in trials, and perhaps for the sake of
silver, or some other thing they put their souls to death. "They became." The
God-traffickers for their own gain are said to eat the souls of men according
to that in the Psalms: Those who eat my people in the eating of bread. "In the
crushing." Because of the many sins the people being allowed to come under
such leaders. "And it ate her foundations." The firmness, the standing of the
soul; and these things happened according to histories. "They did not
believe." Those according to excellence in virtue, the royal priesthood. "All
who dwell." The multitude sheltered by God through all; for the other is
desert. "That he shall enter." Because the Lord said: Gates of Hades shall not
prevail against her, as then they also did not prevail, even if they were
allowed to trouble for a time. "From sins." That is, not from weakness of the
mystery, but from sins of those entrusted to administer it. "Of those who pour
out blood." These things happened to Jerusalem under Romans because of the
blood of Christ. And he says generally concerning those who crucify again the
Son of God, and make a spectacle by overturning the once-happened redemption
through empty voices. "They were shaken." Upon Christ because of the fear
=93.756= of the Jews even the pious among them were moved. And upon the
general, of the support, he says, her equal-to-angels were moved in their
goings. "They were defiled." For he who is not able to stand against sin is
polluted from it. "They touched garments." As many, he says, as were baptized
into Christ, put on Christ; he says then, that they were in danger even about
the faith itself. "Depart." Exhort one another to depart from the unclean. For
"Call them," i.e., each admonish the neighbor. "Because they were kindled."
Because they became subject to the wrath. "Say among the nations." Say to
them, he says: Do not be added to sojourn among the nations, i.e., Do not mix
together with the aliens.


"Face of the Lord." This is the portion, and the lot of those worthy of God,

also practice things not worthy of his contemplation. "Face of priests." i.e.,
they were not ashamed. "Elders they did not pity." Those grown old in virtue
they did not pity, but the heresiarchs, and the leaders of the Jews, even
turned these aside. "Our eyes failed." Toward the letter those in the siege
say, that While we were living our eyes were spent by the famine. And toward
the sense: And yet, he says, having tasted of life, we were confused by
darkness. And those who after the knowledge came under sin say these things.
"To the help." Those in the siege, that they hoped in vain to hold out against
Romans; and those in the sin, that having left, he says, the confidence of
God, they looked in vain to their own help. "We looked out." Having looked
toward human help we were insolent. "We hunted." Toward the history, that in
the siege they did not allow the babes to go out into the markets; and toward
the sense, Fine things, he says, of our thoughts we deprived of not being led
by impulses of the pleasures in the broad ways; it is clear then, that even if
we keep ourselves in every way, and do not hope in God, we are led captives.
"The time has drawn near." The word is from the saints, appropriating the
things of those who suffer through sin. "Those who pursue us." Toward the
history, Babylonians and Romans; and toward the sense, demons; For our
wrestling is not against blood and flesh. =93.757= "Upon the mountains."
Toward the letter, that even those fleeing in the mountains the enemies
overtaking destroyed; and toward the sense, Upon the overweening, he says,
deeds they were kindled. "In a desert." In inhuman manners. "Spirit of a
face." i.e., He himself is our life; he himself is our soul." "He was taken."
For because of our sins he was delivered. "Of whom we said." To whom we
confessed, that being sheltered by him we shall be guarded from the
adversaries. "Rejoice and be glad." Idumea is always taken upon the oldness of
sin. "And even upon you the cup of the Lord shall pass." The prophet signifies
to the oldness of sin a cup, the taking-away through the cross. So that
according to the drunkenness, and fall, even her destruction is stretched out
once for all excessively. For "you shall pour out" signifies her destruction
being stretched out unto the age. When also the last enemy is abolished,
death. "Your lawlessness has failed." Through the death of the Lord according
to flesh. "He shall not add." This does not fit the history; for after
Babylonians they were again deported by Romans, and until now they are in
exile. You will understand then, that the law had a succession, but the grace
is without end. "He visited your lawlessnesses." She who of old was mixed in
the oldness of sin, and called by her name. {1Chapter 5.}1 "Our inheritance."
After the history understand thus: The freedom according to nature became
subject to sin because of the disobedience from choice. "Our houses." The
distribution of the just ordinances. For the one obedient to him is plundered
by the enemy. "We became orphans." The word of the teaching. "Our mothers."
The churches not widows, but as widows because of the temporary affliction.
"Our water." For the gift granted by God the invention of sin subjected to
sale. "Our woods." When the sons of Israel inherited the land of the promise,
placing upon the kings of the nations crafted woods upon the neck, treading
upon them they killed them. What then we did to the enemies being helped by
you, these we have suffered being overlooked. And toward the sense you will
understand thus: The unsold, he says, yoke of the Son of God is given back
after an exchange of monies. "We were pursued." For in the yoke being
lawlessly despised, the rest also goes away, and thereafter the persecution of
sin stands over. =93.760= "Egypt gave." While Romans were besieging Jerusalem
Assyrians sent foods to the


camp, and Egyptians fighting with them mercilessly killed the Jews. And toward
the sense you will understand thus: Egypt is taken as a subject of sojourning,
and Assyrians, of captivity. The sojourning then can be taken upon the vanity
of this age, and Assour upon the working-out of the worse things. "Our
fathers." For you, says the Lord, are sons of those who murdered the prophets,
and you then fill up the portion of your fathers. And according to the
contemplation, those who went before are a manner; for there are
preconceptions of sins, whose memory after they have passed is stretched out
torturing the conscience. "Slaves ruled." The manners being slaves preferring
sin rule over the choice; for by nature the choice is also mistress of every
counsel. But whenever she is changed into sin, and prefers the contrary to the
good, the preferred sin with its deliberations rules over her. "There is no
one redeeming." For no one is naturally able to redeem, except God alone. "In
the souls." Toward the history: those being besieged, taking risk, went out
secretly of the wall, if somehow they might even obtain herbs for food. And
toward the sense, to stand against sin signifies the contest even unto death.
"From the face of a sword." According to the history, he calls desert the
nations that warred against the Jews, being of old desert. And toward the
sense, from the untamed, and inhuman sin. "Our skin." For those of Christ
crucified the flesh with the passions and the desires. "They were drawn
together." From a most violent, and intense famine, whether the perceptible
according to the history, or the intelligible according to the contemplation.
"Women in Sion." Toward the history, using them shamefully; and toward the
sense, the persecutors of the Church. "Rulers in hands." In the hands of those
who write against the truth. "Elders." For when the truth is being written
against, the elders also are dishonored together with her. "Chosen." Lamenting
over the things that happened. "And young men." So strong in the faith, as
even to be crucified through Christ. "And old men." According to the history,
those sitting in the gates of Jerusalem, and embittering the people, in these
very places, he says, =93.761= they were taken away. And bringing the word up
to the martyrs you will understand, that from an exit of this age they ceased,
that is they were perfected. "The crown fell." Jews say this because of the
law, and those of the Church because of those who drag down the mystery of the
cross. "About this they darkened." For darkness was furnished to us from
despondency by the desert of the country, the beasts that love pleasure of a
desert having abounded in it; but it did not become desert of you, who are
present everywhere and overseeing. "Foxes." The heterodox most bitter in
cunning. "But you, O Lord, unto the age." That is, recover us sometime as
eternal. "Why unto victory?" Why are you silent unto our being conquered? "He
will leave us." Stretching out the abandonment. "Turn us back." Since through
a multitude of sins we are cast away, the prophet asks propitiation, through
which the turning happens eagerly toward the Lord. "And renew us." Inasmuch as
also according to foreknowledge you preordained the salvation of man.
