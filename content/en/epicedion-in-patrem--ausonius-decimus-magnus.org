#+BEGIN_QUOTE
Epicedion on his Father edited by Hugh G. Evelyn-White
#+END_QUOTE

After God I have always cultivated a father and I have owed a second reverence
to my begetter, therefore after this veneration of the highest God follows an
epicedion of my father. a title

dedicated by Greek authors to the honor of the dead, not ambitious, but
religious: which I commend to my reader, whether he is a son or a father or
both, nor do I exact that he praise; but I demand that he love, nor indeed do I
now praise my father, because he does not need it and I ought not to burden one
who has finished with the delight of the living, nor do I say except what those
recognize who were present at a part of his age. And I think it of the same
piaculum after his death to say the false and to be silent of the true, these
verses have been written under his image and no less brought into the series of
my little works. All my other things displease me; this I love to have reread.

My name is Ausonius, not last in the art of healing
and, if you knew my times, I was first.
I cultivated neighboring cities by fatherland and by house,
Vasates the fatherland, but Bordeaux by the hearth,
a double curia and each senate had me
without a share of the office, a partaker in the name,
not rich nor needy, I lived sparing without filth:
food, habit, manners I always had the same,
unready in Latin speech, but the Attic tongue
sufficed with voices of a cultivated eloquence.
I offered help of an unbought art to all who asked
and my office was with piety.

I was eager to furnish a judgment of the good concerning me:
I myself never, with me as judge, pleased myself.
Offices owed to many with a diverse cultivation
I distributed by persons, merits, time,
I kept from lawsuits: I did not increase, I did not diminish the thing;
by me as informer no one, but neither as witness, perished,
I never envied; I fled to desire and to canvass;
to swear or to speak the false I held equal.
A faction did not join me to itself, nor a conspiracy:
I cultivated friendships with a sincere treaty,
I knew him happy not who had what he wished,
but who did not desire things not given through fate.
not an accoster, not garrulous, seeing things meeting,
I did not go to things hidden by doors and a veil.
Fame which could lacerate the life of the good
I did not invent and, if I had known it true, I was silent,
anger far, vain hope far, anxious care far
and in the goods of men false joys far.
Gatherings avoided and tumults sworn off
and always the feigned friendships of princes,
I never thought it praise to have failed in nothing
and I put good manners before laws.
Ready to be angry I hastened to hide the motion

and I gave myself punishments for lightness.
A concordant marriage through nine lustra without crime
I had one: we brought forth four children.
The first died nursing; and he who was last of age
died in a rude puberty not rude.

The greatest came to the highest peak of honors,
prefect to the Gauls and Libya and Latium,
tranquil, clement, serene in eyes, voice, mouth,
in his begetter a boy in mind and spirit.
Of this one I saw both a son and a son-in-law as proconsul;
that he himself would be consul was a sure hope to me.
A daughter possessed matronal grace, whose
praise was outstanding both as a bride and as a widow,
who of a son and a son-in-law and a grandson-in-law of all together
saw houses illustrious in a multiple honor.
I myself neither affecting nor a decliner of honors
am named prefect of great Illyricum,
this large indulgence of fortune persuaded me
the numen having been adored to seek an end of life,
lest any day with a fatal bite bind
the inviolable space of a fortunate life.
I obtained and the prayers were heard: hope, vows, fear
having been put to sleep by a calm end I leave to others,
among grieving friends, but I not sad,
I have lain with the judgments of the funeral disposed.
Ninety years without a staff, with a whole body
I have spent, entire in all offices,
whoever will read these, you will not disdain to say:
such a life for you, such as the vows for me.
