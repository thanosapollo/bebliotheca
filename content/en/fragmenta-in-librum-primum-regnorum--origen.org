* Fragments on the First Book of Kingdoms
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

1 Since divine scripture was about to foretell the destruction of the sons of
Eli, and that the ark of God would be taken by the foreigners, it treats the
hearing beforehand, lest anyone suppose God to be harsh. For this reason, as I
said, it narrates that the wickedness and sin of the sons of Eli was not
diminished through the threat and the prediction against them, but rather
increased.

2 Eli was pious in himself; for he was not anxious primarily about the
salvation of his children but about the ark, lest it should ever come under
the foreigners; which indeed also happened.

3 So that the foreigners may know that neither was God defeated through the
Israelites nor have they conquered him by their own power, but the sins and
impieties of the Jews have caused the defeat. And Dagon has fallen before the
ark, not in order to worship, for he was not worthy, but in order to be
shattered; which indeed also happened.

4 Just as in the case of humans hand and foot and eye and ear and whatever
such thing is named are significant of the members of our body, but in the
case of God "hand" is the creative, "eye" the overseeing, and "ear" the
hearing, and "foot" that of presence whenever he is effecting something; so
also the wrath of God is called the discipline against those who stumble, not
a passion of God, and repentance is the transfer of God's economy from one
matter to another. For what we do when we repent, that is, we flee those
things over which we have repented, and we pursue other better things, this is
called repentance of God. Why? Because the prophets had to speak more
corporeally about God to those heaviest in understanding, so that it might be
received; for those who thought the statues which they carved and cast were
gods, and already even irrational animals, how would they have received the
things said about God through spiritual thoughts and words? And again: If God
knows all things beforehand, he is not wrathful or repentant from the things
he knows; therefore wrath or repentance is not a passion of God, but the work
of wrath is punishment and that of repentance likewise a standing-away from
the former matter, and a transfer into the other economy. Thus also concerning
a sword it is said: "sword, sharpen and slaughter"; thus also a hand of iron
has been named († Solomon: "that it may deliver you from the hand of iron")
and of a sword (as David says: "into the hands of a sword"). Thus also the
wrath of God is said to be angry and sin is said to have a face according to
David, and the wrath of God has a face, as in the thirty-seventh Psalm, and
the rivers clap with hands. But neither do rivers have hands nor do sins have
a face. Let us not then, because of the things said corporeally about God, as
the ancients could receive them, think God to be a body or that he is filled
with passions. {The easily-turned character of human judgment is not to be
attached to God; for we ourselves, of ourselves, work out the turns of the
divine economy that justly come upon us, showing ourselves through our deeds
unworthy of the honor granted by God. Having therefore condemned Saul and
wishing to hand over the things of the rule to David and to keep the kingdom
for his seed because of the king of all creation who would be born from him
according to the flesh, he used human words when conversing with humans, and
said: I have repented that I anointed Saul as king, i.e. I wish to give the
beginning of the kingdom to another, and to stop this one from the power. The
"I have repented" on account of Saul, that is, he made himself so unworthy of
the kingdom that, if I had a repenting nature, I would have changed my mind
about him. Why then was he anointed? Because then he was worthy. But having
turned again he became unworthy.}

5 From these things is shown what I said before, that repentance of God is
called a transfer of God's economy from one matter to another. {The word shows
that even if a prophet and a righteous man is the one praying on behalf of
others, he is not otherwise heard, unless that one shows the repentance in
deed. For thus the petition of the righteous has force, being put into
operation through the one who has sinned. In order then that the matter be
brought to an end, both must run together, both the prayer of the righteous
and the deed of the one repenting.}

6 Esau "was reddish, as a hairy hide", having the deadness from sin and
murderously raging against Jacob. But David was reddish with beauty of eyes,
in order to indicate the seeing faculty of David.

7 How a man, and how intelligent in word, and how also a warrior, the one
still shepherding sheep? He did not then speak from himself when he said this,
but having foretold the things that would be as present, and he bears witness
to David of the things he was going to be, except for "the Lord is with him"
and "good in appearance", which belonged to him from childhood and remained,
the one by nature, the other by grace.

8 Since Saul was powerful, having a multitude helping him wickedly against
David, and the two alone were being pursued, Samuel and David, the grace of
God makes Saul prophesy not only as many things as were against himself, but
also the things on behalf of David, and throws him naked, signifying
beforehand that he will be stripped of the kingdom.

9 He now calls "profane" not the unclean, but those that are not holy, by way
of distinction from the holy loaves set forth on the table. For the table had
six loaves being changed continually on behalf of the twelve tribes of Israel,
which they called presence-loaves, so that each of the loaves was offered on
behalf of two tribes, and the six on behalf of the twelve tribes. What good
then came from this? That the tribes of Israel were joined toward concord. And
what better thing was signified beforehand? That the blessed apostles were
sent two by two by the Savior to preach first among the tribes of Israel, so
that the number of the twelve disciples together was six. {A lay loaf, not
because those who are not holy are profane, but because they are compared to
the holy.}

10 "Today he will be sanctified because of the vessels" he said more
obscurely, but it signifies this. My young men have been sanctified and have
kept from a woman both yesterday and the third day; for this is his saying
"and the young men have been purified". And if it was not so, he was
altogether being sanctified through eating the bread of the presentation. The
priest, persuaded by what was said, gives David of the loaves, which it was
not right for others to eat except the priests alone. And this belongs to the
prophecy shown through deeds, that, the royal tribe being one, that is Judah,
and the priestly tribe another, I mean the Levitical, they were going to be
joined and the priestly dignity and the royal were going to be made one in the
coming of our Savior from David, priest and king together, the one "according
to the order of Melchizedek".

11 Neessar: it indicates either that because of sin Doeg the Idumean remained
before the Lord being treated and confessing, or being demon-possessed like
Saul, whose mules he was feeding.

12 Saul bears the type of the evil spirit that ruled Israel then when they
said: we have no king. For also the people of old, having set at nought the
Lord who was king over them, asked that Saul be given them as ruler.

13 No one urinates standing and this toward a wall, unless he is at leisure;
he therefore says this also of the unworried man expecting to suffer nothing,
if he is of Nabal.

14 The ventriloquist did not bring Samuel up, for it is impossible, nor having
come up has he spoken truth. But who this one is and through what power he
came up, seek.

15 David was besieging Jebus. And Jebus was the name of what is now Jerusalem,
when it was under foreigners. The Jebusites therefore, knowing that David was
a lover of the poor, set from above from the wall blind and lame to insult
him.
But he, having taken Jerusalem, so far abstained from treating badly those
maimed in body, that he even laid down a law of death for his soldiers, if
anyone should attempt to kill a blind or lame person. For "everyone who
strikes," he says, "a Jebusite lame or blind, let him be touched with a
dagger," that is, let him be killed; for even if they hated the soul of David,
yet they obtain sparing. Therefore he is deemed worthy of the honor from all
who say: "lame and blind shall not enter into the house of the Lord," those
who insulted David, of whom he was sparing.

16 It is to be noted that Nathan the son of David is born to him when he had
taken and inhabited Jerusalem. And this is Nathan whom the blessed Luke
genealogizes, while the blessed Matthew genealogizes Solomon his brother. And
from here, I mean from the mentioned sons of David, the one is said through
one genealogy, the other through another.

17 So that from here too it is shown that what God does not do but allows to
happen, he is said to have done. It is therefore agreed that the uprising of
Absalom and the railing of Shimei and his throwing stones at David was not a
work of God. And what I say is shown more clearly by David's saying: let him
curse, because the Lord has said to him, that he may see my humiliation, and
the Lord will repay me good things instead of his curse on this day. For it is
clear that what was happening did not please God; and what did not please him,
he would not have commanded to do.

18 If in the zeal of Israel and of Judah Saul attempted to destroy the
Gibeonites, with whom Joshua the son of Nun and all the people made covenants,
and because of this seven of Saul who had been born from his daughters were
killed, for what reason are the tribes not in the charges, but those who had
done no injustice have paid the penalty? For this is unjust. Unless someone
should say that Saul, being king, was unable to resist the Israelites and the
tribe of Judah, and not to break the covenants of the fathers toward the
Gibeonites. And I would add to these that it is God's economy that the whole
race of Saul be consumed, as much as was ever suspected of claiming the royal
dignity and because of this of rising against David and his children. And "the
Gibeonites exposed them to the sun before the Lord on the mountain" i.e.
having been killed they did not bury them, but left them to lie under the sun.
And "they were exhausted," it says, "and Dan son of Joa of the descendants of
the giants took them." He altogether wants to say that the giant, taking those
set under the sun, did away with them already exhausted by the solar flame.
{The account concerning the Gibeonites has been unknown, unless perhaps we
should say that, when he sat in Gibeah to make war, Saul also routed the
foreigners and destroyed with them the Gibeonites also. And the narrative is
in the thirty-second and thirty-third number of the First Kingdom and the
thirty-seventh, where it says: "and he warred round about against all his
enemies" and what follows. And what the saying means is this: Saul did
injustice against him, and from his house has come the vengeance of the
injustice upon Joshua. But those who obtained being from him are not wronged
when they are taken together with the one in the beginning. For from where
they had being, from there they have not-being, and if they were worthy of any
good through themselves, receiving this later.}

19 It is asked how David, having forgiven Shimei, bids Solomon to kill him, he
who does not remember injuries, and meek, but also Joab who had labored with
him.
Why then were these things thus arranged? Being wise, David knew that Solomon
would neither leave Shimei alive for fear of the uprising, for as from royal
dignity he is going to fight with Solomon about the kingdom, nor Joab, as
having established the son of David as king outside David's mind, as having
therefore also killed Abner, though he had labored with the father in many
wars and succeeded. Foreseeing these things, David taking the lead orders
Solomon to do what Solomon would altogether have done of himself anyway, so
that when the aforementioned are killed by him (that is, by Solomon), Solomon
may have pardon as having fulfilled a father's wish and not himself having
been moved against them of himself.

20 And having turned, it says, he slept. And the angel of the Lord returned a
second time and touched him. Perhaps they were symbols of powers ministering
food fitting for a prophet, to the one withdrawing from human things and
worthy of the teaching from such, so that after these he might be able to
hear: arise and eat, and might find at his head unspeakable mysteries, of
which the barley cake hidden in ashes was a type. Of which also a second time
according to the angel's command he partakes, the angel having said: arise,
eat; because the way from you is great. "The ravens therefore brought bread in
the morning to Elijah" (corresponding to the things spoken by the Lord to
Moses, in which was: "in the morning you shall be filled with bread and you
shall know that I am the Lord your God"), and "meat in the evening"
(similarly: "toward evening you shall eat meat").

21 Power of Christ Jesus making men sons. And if anyone is a man of power, he
is a son of Christ, clearly. But sometimes the powers under Christ cannot make
sons; and if they do make them, they do not make men but infants.

22 Spadones are eunuchs; and he says this about the three youths and Daniel,
as they were from this Hezekiah. He also teaches through this ode the one
approaching God to have the hopes in him alone and to boast in him, not to be
arrogant but to be humble-minded. Inasmuch as he as God has the knowledge of
all, he renders to each according to his practices. And he himself is master
of both death and life. And he bids one to know the Lord, clearly through good
deeds, wherefore he adds doing judgment and righteousness in the midst of the
earth. Inasmuch as he is a just judge of all the earth, he himself is
strength, he himself is exaltation, he himself is a horn of salvation of those
who love him.
