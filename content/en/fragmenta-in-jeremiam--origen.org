* Fragments on Jeremiah

1 For the righteous man is not in youth, since "being perfected in a little he
fulfilled long times." For toward one who is not in blameworthy ways is the,
Do not say that I am younger: of which sort was Rehoboam, "having left the
counsel of the elders" and having followed that of the younger, wherefore he
also did not keep the kingdom such as he received it.

2 The kettle kindled from beneath from the face of the north is kindled from
beneath: for this one is at once enemy and avenger. Wherefore the one who has
sinned, whether in blasphemy "is delivered to Satan, that he may be taught not
to blaspheme," or in fornication is delivered "to Satan for the destruction of
the flesh, that the spirit may be saved in the day of the Lord." And whom does
he kindle from beneath? Those who dwell, but not those who sojourn in the
land. For he sends forth "fiery darts" upon those who do not keep their own
hearts with all watch, so as to be set on fire by whatsoever sin.

3 Concerning those who will strike them he speaks of a lion and a wolf and a
leopard. And it seems that the plot against the deeds comes to be from those
tropically called lions, but that against knowledge from those who wish to
harm the seeing faculty of the soul: for the leopard is said especially to
leap at the eyes, and the lion, as it is related, when aged ranges about the
cities wanting to hunt men. And it is said that even from his bones when cut
or pounded fire comes out. And perhaps Nebuchadnezzar would be both lion and
leopard, having taken the city and as a leopard taking out its eyes, the king
with the foremost, and later also having cut out the eyes of Zedekiah: and his
bones would be understood as Nebuzaradan, having burned the city.

4 He teaches to examine clearly what the place concerning sacrifices is, and
the oracles give starting-points saying: "a sacrifice to God is a broken
spirit," and "sacrifice a sacrifice of righteousness." But those not so
disposed, but excited about bodily sacrifices, the Savior turned saying: "but
if you had known what this is: I want mercy and not sacrifice, you would not
have condemned the guiltless." And that such legislation is about mystical
things, Paul taught saying: "who serve a pattern and shadow of the heavenly
things."

5 Turtle-dove and swallow of the field, sparrows: the intelligent hearers and
those sufficient to speak the better things, kept to making their own entries
in season, so that what is said may reach those who hear, the entry of the
swallow running together with the entry of the turtle-dove.

6 Aquila and Theodotion rendered: that he who formed all things is himself,
and Israel is the rod of his inheritance. And if Israel is the rod of his
inheritance, and he has also received the nations as inheritance according to
the, "Ask of me, and I will give you nations as your inheritance," the nations
also will be Israel.

7 God justly does not hear those who have not heard him: but the demons will
not be able to save justice for those who burned incense to them, when the
time of evils comes upon them. When therefore God does not hearken, it is a
terrible thing to seek help from demons. But one must cling to God, who has
turned away because of the things sinned, yet does not overlook the much and
persistent refuge upon him.

8 He did not say: I will not listen to you, but to them, showing that even
then if they repent, if you ask on their behalf, I will listen.

9 He called the abandonment an energy: for God lifting his own tent is said to
destroy. Besides, a destruction of a hand worthy of God is the changing from
vice to virtue according to the, "I will bring my hands upon, and I will burn
you to purity," the former stains in us from sin having been destroyed. Such
also is the, I will scatter them." For since they were impious toward me in
concert, I will scatter their worst assembly and agreement, just as he also
divided the tongues of those building the tower, having hindered increasing
agreeing impiety. And the, they were made childless, they destroyed my people,
holds an accusation of the rulers and of the priests: for these, being in the
rank of fathers to the


peoples, became deserted of children, substituting impiety instead of pious
teaching. And perhaps it is not even possible to destroy a people without
first having made one's own soul childless of the offspring according to
virtue: for no one being good destroys another.

10 And the souls of the ungodly would be widows, having lost the bridegroom
Christ. When these perish, mothers also are distressed, those who "begat them
in Christ" and "were in travail until Christ be formed in them" and after the
milk nourished them with solid food toward young men. And the multitude of the
widows is not as "the stars of heaven," but as the sand of the sea. And
according to the letter, when very many fell in war the women happened to be
widows, and the mothers were distressed.

11 He commands not to take up a burden of sins nor to say: "as a heavy load
they were heavy upon me," so that we may become kings and rulers sitting
firmly upon the throne of David, the church of Christ, so that it may be said
concerning us: "you set upon their head a crown of precious stone." And such
also have mounted upon chariots and horses, bridling the body and checking the
irrational motions, so that the city also will be inhabited unto the age, and
from every side, because of the virtue of the leaders and the excellent
constitution of the inhabitants, those bringing gifts will come to it, both
from the cities of Judah, those who Confess to God, and those encircling
Jerusalem, the Vision of peace, and from the land of Benjamin, the Son of the
right hand (and right-hand things are all those worked according to virtue),
and from the plain, which has nothing "exalted against the knowledge of God,"
and from the mountain, the evangelical word, and from that toward the south,
which is set against the one blowing from the north. For these will also bring
a sacrifice "holy, well-pleasing to God," the consecration of the soul, and
rational whole-burnt-offerings, burning up through the Spirit all matter
liable to the eternal fire. Of whom "the prayer is as incense and the lifting
of the hands an evening sacrifice." Who also offer frankincense, which is
interpreted Whitening, casting off every gloomy disposition, so that "who is
this that comes up having been made white" fits them: for thus they will also
be able to bring praise into the house of the Lord, "not having spot or
wrinkle or any such thing" rubbed against the church of Christ. And if no such
thing comes to be among us we shall endure the things of the threat that is
brought on.

12 The house built with righteousness the apostle made clear saying: "you are
God's husbandry, God's building." And he also says that "another foundation no
one can lay besides the one laid, which is Christ Jesus. And if anyone builds
upon" and what follows. Therefore through the one who taught, the one who
believes had Christ Jesus as foundation. And someone builds upon, if well,
"gold" the doctrines of the truth, "silver" a saving word, "precious stones" a
building from virtues: but if badly, building upon Jesus wicked things, I mean
"wood, hay, stubble," how is he not impious? Upon whom comes the woe, he who
builds his house not with righteousness. He who makes a high building, but not
according to the word of God and truth, does not make upper rooms in judgment.
Correspondingly understand also those who teach either truth or "knowledge
falsely so called." For example Paul builds a house, the church, with
righteousness, but upper rooms Timothy and Luke and such in judgment.

13 And you would say also concerning the false teachers that it has been said
consistently with the things before. For as Solomon says: "he who works
treasures with a lying tongue pursues vanities, and will come into a snare of
death." For such build houses for themselves in the name of a church, which is
"of those who work wickedness," to whom it is said with irony: you have built
for yourself a well-measured house, airy upper rooms: in which they thought
they had built refreshment. And through the windows you think you have
enlightened your buildings with a light of knowledge, and have used undecaying
woods for the building of the house, and you anoint with red ochre imitating
the


blood of Christ. But you will not reign, even if you were provoked in the
cedar of your father. The father of the sinner is a cedar, a certain high,
proud, opposing tree, the devil or the ruler of wretched words. And those who
have built such things will perish by famine: for they will neither eat nor
drink, nor have they known righteousness, not having known Christ: and to do
the just things is, he says, proper to those who know God. And of those not
such, neither are the eyes good, not seeing the truth, nor the heart filled
with false doctrines, through which stumbling others they murder their souls,
pouring out innocent blood with a lying and deceitful tongue. And this is
worse than slaughter through the sword.

14 This Jehoiakim Jeremiah calls Jeconiah, and this one's father called
Eliakim, Pharaoh Necho named Jehoiakim, whom the Babylonian having killed
threw out before the gate, as the present prophet says and Josephus in the
tenth of the Antiquities. And the seal-impression the Others rendered seal.
And a seal, or a bezel-ring, in the right hand of God becomes everyone who
through repentance takes up again that according to the image: for the right-
hand things of God are understood as good works, of him who sets the sheep "on
the right." Which seal indeed the father gave to the prodigal son when he
returned. Which if Jeconiah had become, he became a type of piety to those
under him: but he did not become so. Yet he seemed to obey the prophet who
said: "he who goes out to go over to the Chaldeans who have shut you in will
live": for he went out taking also his mother and the powerful, having
deserted to the Babylonians. And if, he says, he had also shown the repentance
complete so as even "to become a seal of the righteousness in circumcision"
according to the divine apostle, so as according to this to seem in the right
hand of God, he would altogether have been delivered to pay the penalty of the
things formerly sinned, even if a more moderate one. For having been chastened
in Babylon in prison and bonds, he again under Evil-merodach passed over to
ease, becoming table-companion of the king and thus ending his life in a
foreign land.

15 He forbids not simply to hear a prophet, but words of prophets brought
forward in imitation. For the one who hears a prophet such as Moses hears not
him but the Lord speaking through him. But the false prophets make vain: for
by not turning they make the hearer vain, speaking a vision of the heart. For
a mind applying itself to things without God's help speaks a vision of the
heart, not from the mouth of the one who supplies speech and enlightens. And
there is a time when vision of heart and word of the Lord's mouth run
together: whence he does not simply blame the vision of the heart, but when
the other is not present. And they run together in the one who says: "I pray
with the mouth, and I pray also with the mind."

16 He prophesies concerning a shaking and wrath of the Lord going forth upon
the ungodly. The anger was not turned away, not simply but until he does it,
as of one who turns away once he has done it. And what "the word of the Lord"
has not done, the anger of the Lord does. And what the anger of the Lord does,
David says in the, "Lord, do not convict me in your anger." Therefore he does
not cease convicting, until he does that for which he convicts, as neither
does the wrath until it has fully trained. And he will not only do, but also
set and confirm. And the from the undertaking of his heart, being a riddle,
will, he says, be understood at the last, namely concerning the things said
about both anger and wrath. Thanks then to him who has revealed to us the
things hidden until the present.

17 For this reason they did not escape me, having pushed my people into
wickedness. For he who even gives a prophet to know the things everywhere,
much more knows himself. For "the Spirit of the Lord has filled the inhabited
world": and "in him we live and move and are," as Paul said to the Athenians.
Such also is the, Do I not fill heaven and earth, says the Lord? For by his
power he draws near to all. And prayers then are not made as toward God being
far off. And concerning the Son it is also said: "he was in the world, and the
world came to be through him." For he himself is also the one who says: I am a
God who draws near: for he also said: "where two and three are gathered into


my name, there am I in the midst of them," he who also says: "behold I am with
you all the days until the consummation of the age."

18 For as of our soul nothing of the body is deserted, but where there is
sensation there is also soul, and it reaches over the whole body, so also
nothing is empty of God. And yet filling all things he does not fill the
sinner, for he has been filled with unclean spirits: and it is impossible for
such a one to be filled by God, unless freed from the others that fill him.
For he fills the worthy in heaven and on earth. For "the Lord's" is not the
earth and all the things in it, but "the earth and the fullness of it," as
there being in it some who are not fullness of earth. For fullness of the
earth are those filled from the one who said: Do I not fill heaven and earth?
says the Lord.

19 Since the false prophets also misused the "thus says the Lord,"
impersonating the true prophets, there was need of signs distinguishing both.
There was then, according to the apostle, a gift of discernment of spirits,
and the one who had this discerned spirits, both those that are divine and the
wicked, just as a money-changer the coin both genuine and counterfeit. And
apart from the catholic science even what is now said suffices for
discernment. For my word, he says, is not empty and food of the irrational,
but as it were wheat and food of the rational.

20 And why did he say the chaff toward the wheat, but not toward barley: for
the Lord set forth breads, the barley ones to the more irrational, and the
wheat ones to the rational. Wherefore even now he did not say: what is the
chaff toward the barley, but toward the wheat, having set forth precisely the
rational food. And there is also another sign, that the words of the Lord are
as an axe cutting rock. When the hearer being convicted is burned so as to
say: "was not my heart burning in me," the word is fire and wheat: but if he
is delighted hearing the things according to pleasure, chaff. But <also> the
word of God is an axe, able to split not only woods but also rock, not that
understood according to Christ, but the opposing one which must be cut
according to the, "rocks will be crushed before the Lord."

21 Just as the one who has bought a garment, if it happens, from the one from
whom he ought, has this lawfully, but the one who has stolen has it but not
justly, so of those teaching from Scripture those who bring the words with a
bastard and stained soul steal: concerning whom it would be said: "all who
came before me are thieves and robbers, and the sheep did not hear them." Such
is the one using an evangelical word, not disposed in faith toward it nor
living according to it, but using with an alien choice the announcement of the
word: for he is a thief and it will be said to him: "you who preach not to
steal, do you steal."

22 What the whole world is, this according to a certain analogy is man alone.
For you will find one called heaven, when he bears "the image of the
heavenly," and another earth, if he has "the image of the earthy" (for to the
one who has sinned it has been said: "you are earth"), another having rivers
in him "leaping unto eternal life," if he has most watering words, another
having a salty sea, full of waves, having "a dragon which God formed to sport
with it." And part by part you will find one called sun, and another moon and
stars, since "so is the resurrection of the dead." And a little later: But now
the parable says he is a fig exceedingly wicked: for virtue sweetens him
through ripening, so that he is a soul whole and spirit and body sweet of the
quality from virtues being put into him, but wickedness works him evil and
inedible. But if someone seeks figs not exceedingly good, he seeks these
outside the mysteries of godliness and the temple of God, just as also those
not exceedingly wicked. For if he comes upon the discipleship itself, before
the face of the temple of the Lord there are figs exceedingly good (such as
Christ knows how to make) or wicked: for neither did the outer wicked fig cast
the words of God behind. About the temple of God then and the divine doctrines
two baskets lie. And the one who chooses knows how he ranks each, not enduring
to mix a quality of a wicked fig


among those exceedingly good ("for a little leaven leavens the whole lump")
nor the contrary. And perhaps the basket of the wicked figs is Gehenna of the
eternal fire, and that of the good the kingdom of the heavens. And a little
later: But when did the prophet see the baskets? After the captivity, he says.
For if you see the sojourn of the Savior and the captivity of the people and
us who are alien by nature and outside Jerusalem now through the faith toward
God and sweetness of the truth becoming good figs, you will see the basket of
the good figs: but if you behold those of the circumcision after this
captivity, you will see the basket of the wicked and inedible figs. And it is
not enough that the figs be exceedingly good, but they must also be likened to
the early ones: for there is no late fig to which the one being saved is
compared.

23 Christ is builder and architect, concerning whom it has been said in
prophets: "this one will build my city, and will turn the captivity of my
people." The Lord then says: and I will build them and I will not pull down.
For God being good pulls down certain buildings: for the building in us of the
unclean spirits must be dissolved and thus a temple built for God from virtues
and right doctrines, that his glory also may be seen in it. And he is also a
farmer planting and grafting those who are worthy. For the Savior says as a
root holding up all the branches: "I am the true vine, you the shoots, and my
Father is the farmer. Every shoot remaining in me and making good fruit, my
Father prunes that it may bear more fruit: and every shoot remaining in me not
bearing: and fruit, my Father cuts out and casts it into fire." Not among the
Gentiles, but among the faithful is the "remaining in him and not bearing
fruit": such is the one who says he is faithful and errs with license, who is
reasonably cut out: but he prunes that which bears fruit, that even if it
overlooks something as a man it may correct it.

24 This, as we were saying, the Seventy set first of the visions against the
nations: but the Others the contrary. And Elam is an Arabian nation lying
toward the Red Sea.

25 For they were proud of archery, not having learned to sing: "for I will not
hope upon my bow, and my sword will not save me." And he threatens to move
nations from every side against him, whence he calls winds from the places
from which they were setting out. Each of us analogously to his constitution
is either an Elamite of the same name or an Idumean or a Moabite or an
Egyptian or an Israelite. And it is possible to change from one nation into
another better or worse: for the psychic nations know how to fall over into
one another. Yet each of us analogously to the things he has stored up will
drink the cup of the Lord's wrath unmixed or mixed: for we have all sinned
more or less. And as Israel became named from what he did, having prevailed
toward God in the wrestling, so some are Cast-aside Blameworthy Elamites: for
thus they are interpreted. And those are cast aside whom according to the
apostle "God delivered to a reprobate mind, to passions of dishonor." And Paul
also makes clear who the winds are about which the word is, saying: "that we
may no longer be tossed and carried about by every wind of teaching in the
dice of men, toward the method of error." And the Cast-aside is carried about
by every wind, whether of teaching or of anger or of desire. And there are
four generic winds, troubling the soul of man, desire, fear, pleasure, grief,
to which an Elamite left by God is delivered. But what is the bow of Elam, in
which is the beginning of the dominion, David himself said singing: "behold
the sinners stretched a bow, they prepared arrows for a quiver, to shoot in a
moonless night the upright in heart." And a little later: But the Savior
having sojourned brought the four winds upon the Elamites circling us, that he
might scatter these. For opposing powers are delivered to other opposing
powers for punishment, as Pharaoh to Nebuchadnezzar: for if you shut up
venomous serpents,


the weak are eaten by the stronger under famine, until the strongest of all,
filled from the serpents he has eaten, becomes the one called basilisk, having
venom from sight alone that dries a fruit-bearing tree. Thus the wicked powers
are punished by one another, until last is left "the last enemy of Christ,
death," a basilisk concerning which he says: "upon asp and basilisk you will
tread." For Christ being stronger than the basilisk has trodden upon it and
has given us "authority to tread upon serpents and scorpions."

26 There is a certain Babylonian man and a Babylonian infant, and one must
contend against these, that is either against great passions or against those
now beginning. Yet "blessed is he who having seized dashes against the rock
(Christ) the infants of Babylon," before they come forward into men. And still
more blessed to utterly destroy Babylonian seed, namely a Confusing thought
(and opposite to this is seed concerning which it has been said: "blessed is
he who has seed in Zion" and "blessed are those who sow upon every water,
where ox and ass tread"). Not only a certain man nor a child, but even only
seed: for it can bear fruit when farmed. And what is the water upon which one
must sow the good seed? The bath of regeneration. There "ox and ass tread," ox
the clean and Israelite, ass the one of unclean race and Gentile. And these
animals are symbols of both the word announced to the Israelites and that
preached to the nations.

27 And the Babylonian holds a sickle not to save, but to cut off from the holy
land and to harvest churches: whom before we suffer this let us destroy,
having made vanish the words of the heretics. Such was Paul saying: "having in
readiness to avenge every disobedience," he who says: "pulling down every
height raised against the knowledge of God." Then he witnesses to the Hellenic
word a power, as that all the nations yield to this as seeming somehow to be
demonstrative.

28 He says a sheep as of one that has wandered. And in a gospel he also says:
"the son of man came to seek and to save that which was lost." And in the
parable only one of the hundred was lost, which the sojourning shepherd came
to find, who also having taken it upon the shoulders restored it to the number
of the ninety and nine. "For we all are one body" and one sheep: one is a
foot, another a head, another something else, and the shepherd having come
gathered "bone to bone and joint to joint" and having united took it up to his
country. And the unity comes to be through love and truth and good choice. By
his own word then he united all. But if some appeared disobedient, they
acquired an incurable passion. One then is the whole Israelite according to
the true kinship, "for we all are one body and one bread and we partake of the
one Spirit." And a wandering sheep is the one not attaining the hunt of what
is fitting in both word and deed, either by not seeking or by not finding. But
the one who knows and does the things of the knowledge does not wander: and
the one who wanders is driven out by lions. For "our adversary the devil walks
as a roaring lion seeking whom he may swallow." And already also "whelps of a
lion roaring to seize and to seek from God food for themselves" plot against
the righteous, wanting to drive us out from the mountains of peace. But as
David taking the lion by the beard throttled it, so let us ask the spiritual
David, Christ, having taken the lion and all the council of the beasts to
destroy them. And now he takes two generic lions, both the Assyrian and the
Babylonian. According to the history in the fourth of Kingdoms there are two:
for the Assyrian removed the sons of Israel "into Assyria until this day," and
the Babylonian the sons of Judah "into Babylon." Yet he did not now say first
and second, but first and last. For first lion is the opposing devil, himself
the "man-killer": and last at the consummation of the age "the man of sin, the
son of perdition, the


one raising himself against every so-called god or object of worship."

29 Israel cannot be restored into his pasture, unless the two kings suffer
what they must suffer for the vengeance of those who have suffered badly. But
do not take the word altogether upon the devil or the antichrist: for in me
let "Satan being crushed under my feet quickly" be destroyed. And when he has
destroyed these two and those between, he will restore Israel into his
pasture: when he will also feed in Carmel and Bashan and in the mountain of
Ephraim and in Gilead. Though there are so many places of Judea and of the
land inherited, he named these only.

30 He calls Babylon a hammer, having worn down those upon earth with her own
greed. And when Solomon was constructing the divine temple, in praise the
third of Kingdoms says that "hammer and axe were not heard in the house of
God." And a house of God is the church, in which a hammer must not be heard.
And the devil is a hammer of all the earth, and there is the one who does not
care for it, as it were some matter struck by it and not suffering. For
"behold," he says, "a man standing upon an adamantine wall, and in his hand
adamant": which adamant is crushed by no hammer. Even if then the devil the
hammer stands over, and a dragon is laid under "as an unyielding anvil," the
adamant in the hand of the Lord and under his covering will suffer nothing.
For the holy one is an adamantine wall, and remaining in the hands of the Lord
impassible, even if caught in the middle of both the hammer and the dragon.
And as much as he is struck, he displays his virtue more brilliant: for the
devil as one ignorant of the nature of stones tests through many blows the one
who is as it were adamant, God alone knowing well the nature of these. If
there were no hammer, there would not be a "beaten trumpet" according to the
law, waking to the feasts of God or stirring to war those who hear. Such the
hammer made Paul through various trials, having suffered nothing from it, but
with a great voice waking to the war of vice. The hammer then works the trial,
and there serves the hammer the one from Cain "a hammer-smith, the smith of
bronze and iron." Such was Judas at the Savior and those who cried "crucify,
crucify him," serving through such the hammer.

31 Vessels of wrath the apostle also knows "fitted for destruction, that he
may make known the wealth of his glory upon vessels of mercy." For he divided
all men into these two vessels, some of wrath, some of mercy: some such as
Pharaoh and the Egyptians, some such as Paul and those who believed. But what
is the treasure of the Lord, in which are the vessels of wrath? Perhaps the
church, in which such often lie hidden. And there will be a time when he opens
the church. For now it is shut, and the vessels of wrath pasture with the
vessels of mercy, and the chaff is with the wheat, and in one net both the
rejectable and the chosen fish. And the Lord opens his treasure at the time of
the judgment, when the vessels of wrath being cast out, the one who is a
vessel of mercy would reasonably say: "they went out from us, for they were
not of us." And the vessels sinning outside the treasure are not yet vessels
of wrath, but lesser: for they are "servants not knowing the will of their
Lord and not doing it." They are then simply vessels reserved for other
things.

32 Some of the captives of Judea will come, he says, evangelizing the
vengeance against Babylon. And he would also speak concerning those turning
from idolatry, which he calls Babylon: who coming into Zion, the Watchtower,
the church, announce the vengeance from the Lord our God.

33 Those in Babylon are many, but those in Jerusalem fewest. For it has been
said: "the Lord your God did not love you because of this, because of being
very many: for you are fewest beside all the nations." For truly "few are
those being saved and entering through the narrow door." And everyone
stretching a bow against the "broad and spacious" Babylon is commanded to
spare


none of her.

34 As many opposing thoughts as someone has against godliness, many are
Babylonians: whom one must destroy as being sinners of the earth. From whom
the one who has been purified comes to be in the city of God who avenges the
insolence of Babylon: for she thought she had prevailed by her own power and
boasted against God. For when the temple was burned, the sacred vessels were
given to the concubines for use. Wherefore with fire he consumed the fruitless
nation as some thicket. In this way then he says: behold I am against you the
insolent woman, says the Lord of hosts.

35 He prophesies the consummation that will come to be all at once: "for as in
the days of the flood" and "in the days of Lot" all were doing the things of
life, "until the flood came and the fire and took all," so will the suddenness
of the consummation be. Of which indeed a symbol was Jericho fallen all at
once by voice and by trumpets alone. And at the time of the saving passion
Babylon suddenly fell, Christ having abolished the doctrines about the Gentile
idols and having delivered those of the nations who believed.

36 Golden is the cup of Nebuchadnezzar for the deception of men, that having
received they may drink, seeing the gold "that it is good for the eyes to see
and fair to consider," but not reckoning the judgment in this. For his
wretched doctrines have been adorned with fine diction, baiting with the
beauty of the phrasing and the technical method. And every poet seeming to be
supreme has prepared a golden cup, having put in a poison of idolatry,
shameful speech, and of all the psychic evils. But Jesus did not take thought
beforehand of a golden cup, lest he ever even seem to be like that one
(wherefore "we have this treasure in earthen vessels"), but of the salvation

her, let each go away into his own land and his own place and his own deed,

38 And Isaiah also declared: "upon a level mountain raise a sign, lift up the

teaching: but also farmers who sow "the tares," and farming "thorns and
thistles," the worldly cares among men. He also has leaders in vice and

41 He calls Babylon a mountain because of the height of the kingdom: which was
from idolatry and the unjust works, through which she corrupted the others,
herself corrupted. And the city was also upon a height because of the

part and lying from each bank beside the river, besides the walls of the city
also being most high. And a hand, the punitive power that takes her down from
her own security and garrison as from certain rocks. And making even her
stones useless through the fire, so that a stone may not be put together

named a mountain, as in Zechariah: "who are you, the great mountain before the

know me." And some of those who campaigned <against> Babylon he called

43 And the earth was shaken, of the men upon it in the preceding times firmly
established and having firmness in the dominions and kingdoms in which they

and Confusion, being shaken casts off such a disposition in order to take up a
spiritual and heavenly habit.

44 For God was the one doing this, he who also through Isaiah said to Cyrus:
"and I will go before you and I will level mountains, I will crush bronze

related to have such gates, and to have been taken from the descents into the

the others holding a festival because of the

Babylon and what follows: or from those assembled in them the garrisons.

45 The, they will incline him and they will thin his vessels, Symmachus said:

time of the punishment, and wickedness the toilsome things that will overtake
this one. And he thinks it right that all around trample upon him: for this is
the, move, namely toward shame and grief, "for God arrays himself against the

46 What is in the hand either presents the scepter or those under the rule or
the hired: for hand-wages is the pay.

47 He will clap with the hands, with which rejoicing over Israel and being
bold against God he clapped. And those "who have trusted in the strongholds"
of the cities, having left these dwelt in rocks lying beside the ravines,
imitating doves according to wantonness and according to mindlessness. For
they nest sometimes in places, from which those who wish easily take away the
nestlings. And wanton also is the one initiated to Baal-peor and mindless the
one attached to the unprofitable and vain idols: of which sort was Moab. For
"a fool said in his heart: there is no God," either being altogether atheist
or reckoning that the one who truly is God does not even exist at all.

48 Jerusalem, as has often been said, is taken over into the church, which is
a city of God built from living stones, from which someone sinning is cast out
being delivered to Nebuchadnezzar, to Satan. For concerning the one who has
fornicated Paul says: "to deliver such a one to Satan for the destruction of
the flesh, that the spirit may be saved in the day of the Lord": and in the
first to Timothy concerning the blasphemers: "whom I delivered to Satan, that
they may be taught not to blaspheme." And let the one cast out of Jerusalem
know that, if he does not do a sufficient time outside the church practicing
what he must, he does not return upon Jerusalem. And someone sinning is cast
out, even if he is not cast out by men. And he must, having come to be
outside, not neglect building a house and planting paradises. For not doing
these nor having filled the symbolic number "of the seventy years" being of
sabbath and rest, he does not return to share with the church, but remains
condemned to be outside Jerusalem. And what it is to take wives, he made clear
who said concerning wisdom: "this I sought to bring to myself as a bride, and
I became a lover of her beauty." And one must love this, as in Proverbs
Solomon introduced saying: "love her, and she will keep you," and "fence her
about, and she will lift you." And understand also other wives besides wisdom,
the remaining virtues. For thus it will be possible to take many wives: from
whom one must beget children, from wisdom a word of wisdom so that you may
also build others, from temperance works of temperance that you may make
temperate by life and word, from righteousness works of righteousness in
fellowship and dealings. But also teaching the righteous you will beget
children from righteousness, and having made sons from the sons and daughters,
sons the divine thoughts and the doctrines,


and daughters the deeds.

49 For when Jerusalem was troubled with the Babylonian lying upon her, as is
likely they were taking refuge upon foreknowledge wanting to know the future.
But the false prophets were asserting they were predicting from a holy spirit:
and of the others some were bringing forward divinations, and others again
dreams. For this reason one must altogether not endure any ungodly
foreknowledge: for if stars perhaps operate, we pray in vain. Wherefore he
says: "let the astrologers of heaven say what the Lord plans concerning you,"
and "there will not be an omen in Jacob, nor divination in Israel." Become
then worthy of divine foreknowledge. For he adds after the "there will not be
divination in Israel": "in due time it will be said to Jacob and to Israel
what God will accomplish."

50 Such things will every such acting elder suffer from the king of Babylon.
For if the one unworthily [someone] partaking of eucharist will take it unto
judgment, how much more the one sitting in a presbytery stained in conscience
and defiling the council of Christ? For who sitting with him, being devout,
will dare to say the "I did not sit with a council of vanity" and the things
upon these?

51 And you would say that he also displays his own goodness, through which he
also brings on the severity to those who happen to be in need of it. Wherefore
after being healed unto what is painful, the city, he says, will be built upon
its height. What height? "a city cannot be hidden lying upon a mountain": of
which sort is the one thinking nothing low or human, pleasures he says and
wealth on earth and little glories. "For she was raised with Christ and seeks
the things above." When you do these things "your city will be built upon its
height."

52 As upon a rare thing he says. And it is a custom of Scripture after the
bitter things to say humane things toward consolation and after the good
things again more bitter, lest "having despised the wealth of the kindness" of
God they store up for themselves "wrath in a day of wrath." Wherefore he says:
"if the wrath comes, it will not depart, unless God should do what he wills."
And God wills, and wrath comes to be, that what God wills may come to be. For
if someone does not will to come to be in the will of God the Word, the wrath
is released upon him. Let us not then need training wrath or anger.

53 For not through a desert, as before, but through the lawful and inhabited.
And tropically also in consolation, that "blessed are those who mourn, for
they will be consoled."

54 Islands far off are those tossed by the waves of life, which are far from
truth before coming upon salvation. And God redeemed Jacob: for "the yoke that
lies upon them has been taken away, and the rod upon their neck," as Isaiah
says. And he also makes clear that he himself delivered us from the diabolic
authority. For what could we do toward the one who said both the other things
and the, "I will take the whole inhabited world in the hand as a nest"? Toward
whom through Christ we say: "where, death, is your victory? where, Hades, is
your sting"?

55 To those worthy of beatitude who weep let the, let your voice cease from

56 It is written also in Baruch: "why is it that you are in a land of enemies?
you were defiled with the dead." For when the righteous tread, it is a land of
holy ones: but when many are sinners, a land of the enemies. And thus, he
says, they will turn so that the turning from a land of enemies is lasting for
your children, those benefited by you and your fruits. And in Exodus it is
also written that "God heard the people, their groaning from the works":

lamenting. And Ephraim is interpreted Fruit-bearing.

57 God hears a voice of weeping of those who repent. And why did he say
Ephraim lamenting over sins? The third of Kingdoms then makes clear that,
Rehoboam ruling them bitterly, when they ought persisting not to revolt, they
cried "we have no portion in David," when Jeroboam led those who revolted,
being of the tribe of Ephraim,


who having made heifers withdrew the ten tribes from the worship of God. Over

words of the confession, and he himself hears a voice saying: from my youth
you are a beloved son, Ephraim.

58 A certain Assyrian Nebuchadnezzar held all men, from whom Christ redeemed

your lawlessnesses, that you may be justified." For there are certain cities
of God and buildings and his husbandries: for Paul also says: "you are God's
husbandry, God's building." For in whom the mysteries concerning God and
unspeakable doctrines are built, in this one is a temple of God and the holy
of holies, and this one is metropolis of all Judea.

60 We are all in a prison, and especially when being tried. Then someone
acquires a field, but not having gone out of the prison: and the acquisition
is by will of God for those who contend well. And brothers are Hilkiah the

of brothers is both peace and portion of God: for Hilkiah is interpreted
Portion of God, and Shallum Peace. And fruit of the portion of God is
Elevation of God: for thus Jeremiah is interpreted. And fruit of the peace is
Grace of God: for this is what Hanamel makes clear. Therefore the grace of
God, happening to be an offspring of peace, offers to the prophet in prison
his own field according to a command of God.

61 This one makes a book of acquisition, which perhaps is the understandings,
the memories, the doctrines, through which we have acquired the field, that is
the toils of the way of life. For the one who will practice righteousness must
know beforehand concerning righteousness, which let it also be said concerning
the remaining virtues. And God takes care that the book be thrown into a
vessel toward greater permanence. For "we have this treasure in earthen
vessels," either our vessels, or the cheapness of the scriptural wording.
Whence "the ruler of this world has been deceived," having despised the word
of godliness, since it was not in golden vessels beautified with diction and
persuasiveness of phrasing: wherefore it also remained more days. For "my word
and my preaching," says the one who has the book of the acquisition, "was not
in persuasive words of wisdom, but in demonstration of spirit and power." And
having done what was commanded Jeremiah gives thanks to God. And see the
obedience of the prophet before the apparent advantage, which he adds,
foretelling the recovery of the land. For the prophecy through works makes the
audible visible and the future as it were present, making clear in the part
the whole.

62 It is not plausible that the prophet brings such thanksgiving upon the

womb" and


is full of a divine spirit. But see the sabbatic number the symbol of rest,

smell of vines or figs, but "of love, joy, peace, long-suffering, kindness,
goodness, faith, self-control." Upon such a field then Jeremiah gives thanks
saying: you who are, Lord. For he looks to the one who truly is, "not looking
at the things seen but at the things not seen."

63 Jeremiah wrote not only prophecies, but also his own sufferings, by the

examples of endurance, the false prophets suffering no such thing because of
the flattery. Let us then prepare in a time of persecution to be ranked with
Jeremiah and with the apostle who said: "in prisons often." And the that the

65 Jeremiah says toward him that the women being led into exile will say:

67 The good man suspects no one to be so wicked as even to murder those who

68 Join the, not of the incense, toward the, the Lord remembered. It is clear

even someone burning incense to God as he must not, becomes a cause of the
land becoming impassable. Even if then you offer the sensible incense

fragrance in agreement: but offering with vice, as it were somehow also

able to bear the stench of those then erring. Wherefore he says in prophets:
"I was silent, shall I also always be silent?"
