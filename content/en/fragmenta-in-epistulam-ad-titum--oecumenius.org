* Fragments on the Epistle to Titus
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Tit 1:12 An oracle of Epimenides the Cretan seer. Callimachus also used the
saying in the hymn to Zeus spoken by him. Tit 1:15 Why then did the law given
to Jews have an observation of unclean things? We say: God did not so
legislate because they were unclean, but thereby cutting off the great luxury
and indifference of the Jews. For the clean and unclean come properly from the
mind of those who take them, since by nature all things are clean to those who
do not split hairs; but to such people all things are unclean; for sin alone
is unclean. Tit 2:3 He means the deaconesses, and by "demeanor" he means the
wraps. Tit 3:10-11 And yet elsewhere he says: lest God perhaps give them
repentance. How then does he here command to refuse? There he speaks of those
who have hope of correction, here of those incurably sick.
