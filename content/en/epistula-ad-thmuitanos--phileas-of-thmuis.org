* Letter to the Thmuitans
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

1 And since we said that Phileas was worthy of much account also for the
sake of the outer learning, let him come forward as witness of himself,
at once showing who he was, and at the same time relating more
accurately than we the martyrdoms that happened concerning him in
Alexandria, through these words. 2 All these examples and patterns and
fair tokens having been laid down for us in the divine and holy
scriptures, the blessed martyrs with us, delaying in nothing, stretching
the eye of the soul purely toward the God over all and taking in mind
the death for piety, clung fast to the calling, having found our Lord
Jesus Christ become man for us in order that he might cut out all sin
and lay up for us provisions of the entrance into the eternal life. For
he did not count it a thing to be grasped to be equal with God, but
emptied himself, taking the form of a slave, and being found in fashion
as a man he humbled himself unto death, and death of a cross. 3
Wherefore also, emulating the greater gifts, the Christ-bearing martyrs
endured every toil and every kind of device of tortures, not once only
but already some even a second time, and all threats, the guards being
ambitious against them not in words only but also in deeds, they did not
give way in their resolve, because perfect love casts out fear. 4 What
word would suffice to recount their virtue and the courage in each
torture? For there being license for all who wished to insult them, some
struck with clubs, others with rods, others with whips, others again
with thongs, others with ropes. 5 And the spectacle of the tortures was
varied and had much wickedness in it. For some, having the two hands
bound behind, were hung about the wood and were stretched in every limb
by certain machines; then thus the torturers, at a command, applied them
through the whole body, not as with murderers upon the sides only, but
they punished also the belly and shins and cheeks with the instruments
of punishment. Others, hung from the colonnade by one hand, were
swinging, having the tension from the joints and limbs more terrible
than every pain. Others were bound facing the columns, not standing on
their feet, but by the weight of the body being forced as the bonds were
pulled up with tension. 6 And they endured this not for as long as the
governor was conversing with them nor while he had leisure for them, but
all but through the whole day. For when he even passed over to others,
he left those who served his authority to watch the former ones, if
perhaps anyone, overcome by the tortures, seemed to give way, and
commanding unsparingly that they should also go to the bonds, and after
these things that they, gasping out their life, being laid down upon the
earth, should be dragged; 7* for they had not even a part of care
concerning us, but both thought and acted as if we were no longer
existing, the adversaries having invented this second torture upon the
blows. 8 And there were some who even after the tortures were lying upon
the wood, stretched through the four holes by both feet, so that of
necessity they were on their backs upon the wood, not being able because
they had the wounds still fresh from the blows over the whole body.
Others, thrown onto the ground, were lying, from the concentrated
assault of the tortures presenting to those who saw them a sight more
terrible than the working, bearing in their bodies the varied and
different devices of the tortures. 9 These things being so, some died in
the tortures, putting the adversary to shame by their endurance; others,
half-dead, being shut in the prison, after not many days, being held by
the pains, were perfected; and the rest, having obtained recovery from
the treatment, by time and by the lingering in custody became more
courageous. 10 So then, when it had been commanded, a choice being set,
either that one who had touched the accursed sacrifice should be
unmolested, having obtained from them the accursed freedom, or that one
not sacrificing should await the sentence unto death, delaying in
nothing they went gladly to death. For they knew the things
predetermined for us by the holy scriptures. For he who sacrifices, it
says, to other gods shall be utterly destroyed, and that You shall have
no other gods besides me. 11 Such are the voices of the truly both
philosopher and God-loving martyr, which before the last sentence, while
he was still under the prisoner's condition, he had sent to the brothers
throughout his own parish, at once relating the things in which he was,
and at the same time urging them to cling fast even after him, who was
about to be perfected almost at once, to the godliness in Christ.
