604 Gal 1:16-22 I did not confer with flesh. I did not turn aside into ease
and luxury, nor, as if I had accomplished some great thing, did I 605 sink
back into slackness and neglect, but I made the course of the gospel
strenuous. Or thus: I did not prefer to see kinsmen or house or fatherland,
but leaving all things (for in this too I am not inferior to those around
Peter and John) I ran at once into Arabia and Damascus and into the preaching.
Nor did I go up to Jerusalem. I did not go up, he says, to Jerusalem in order
to behold the apostles before me and to learn something from them; for he did
go up to Jerusalem from Damascus, as the Acts make clear, but not in order to
become a disciple, but in order that he himself too might preach Christ and
teach the faith. For in the same manner, he says, that I was going away to
Arabia to preach, and again to Damascus, in the same manner also to Jerusalem.
And after three years of my preaching and teaching I went up to see Peter, not
to learn something from him, for already I was evangelizing and preaching. Or
thus: from Peter I did not learn, I only saw him. From James I did not learn,
for him too I only saw. Another of the apostles I did not see. But perhaps
from some other humbler and not so distinguished person? And how is there
room for this, to learn from those who have not even seen my face? For I was
unknown by face to the churches of Judea that are in Christ. For the rest
needed teaching, but they did not teach. Therefore I did not learn from a man.
Gal 1:24 And they glorified God in me. They glorified God in me, not so-and-so
or so-and-so as teacher, but God himself. He was the one who revealed the
preaching in me and taught. Gal 2:2 Why privately? Lest, setting it forth
before all, I scandalize those of the circumcision, who were then newly
planted, and prepare them to fall away entirely from the faith of Christ, and
lose my course, and my contest and my zeal be shown empty. For how is it not
empty to run and to have run, when, running in order to save others, I again
through inconsiderate strictness destroy them? How then does he now rebuke
publicly? Because there was progress, and because some dared to make the
economy into a dogma. Or "lest somehow" can also have been said as a question,
being confirmatory of the fact that I do not run or have not run in vain, but
I was doing all things with security and forethought. 606 Gal 2:3-5 But not
even Titus who was with me, a Greek. Not even Titus, he says, although it was
evident that he had been born of Greek seed, did the apostles compel to be
circumcised, although they saw the false brothers also present; for the "but
not even" is to be taken from the common construction, as: but not even Titus
was compelled, he says, to be circumcised, not even because of the false
brothers who had been smuggled in. What then? Why did he circumcise Timothy,
if he takes no account of the false brothers? He did not circumcise him
because of these either, far from it, but in order to gain those who were
scandalized through weakness of reasonings. For one must condescend to those
who are weak for a gentle healing of the ailment, but to those who start from
malice and wicked purpose one must not yield even the least; for these not
only do not need healing, every healing being too weak once the passion has
been fixed in them, but they also seek to fill the neighbors with their own
disease. Therefore we did not yield to them even for an hour, the divine Paul
also declares. Gal 2:6 For those who seemed added nothing to me. I received
nothing extra, nothing was acquired for me, but just as God ordained those men
apostles, so also me. And he did not prefer them to me; for God does not
accept a person's face, as you do. Gal 2:8-14 For he who worked in Peter for
apostleship. If Paul had said that those around Peter practiced circumcision
by way of economy, he would have seemed himself also to concede it, and thus
he would rather have been shown running along with those against whom he had
the work of fighting; for if Peter does well in conceding, why not you also?
they say. And why do you also bitterly blame what you call an economy? Let
them practice it and do not rebuke; and name it as you please, whether economy
or dogma and strictness. These things they would both have reasoned and said
to Paul, if he had said that those around Peter accepted circumcision by way
of economy; for a soul in agitation about something, taking even a small and
chance occasion, grows more familiar with it and entangles itself. Therefore
Paul very wisely is wholly silent about the economy, but sharply attacks the
practice, and as if they were acting unlawfully he rebukes those who conceded,
in order that by the vehemence of the rebuke concerning them he might frighten
and correct those who were truly acting unlawfully. Therefore he also 607 does
not decline to call them seeming, and condemned, and dissembling, and not
walking uprightly, if only he may confirm the preaching of Christ. Thus they
had confidence in one another for the sake of the preaching, and were bound
with so great a bond that, if it should be necessary, should it so happen,
that through mutual condemnation the word of the preaching be strengthened,
they would not decline even this. And that you may learn this accurately,
consider: Paul through the epistles condemns Peter, and Peter through the
epistle praises Paul, and testifies that these epistles are full of the wisdom
of God and of grace, and cries out that the unstable do not understand the
mind hidden in them but even distort it. And otherwise also Paul, saying these
things about Peter for the sake of the preaching, far more than himself cares
for him and honors him. For what is seeming and not walking uprightly, in the
account of insult, compared with being anathema from Christ? Gal 3:4 Did you
suffer so many things in vain? If indeed it is even in vain. But not also with
loss? For in vain is to have neither received anything extra nor laid anything
aside, but the one who began in the Spirit and because of this fell into many
trials, then changing, not only suffered in vain what he suffered, but also
with loss, having lost the prizes that were laid up for him through the
afflictions and trials for Christ. Gal 3:15 Nevertheless a man's covenant,
once ratified. Well he says "ratified"; for it had been ratified by an oath.
And accurately he says "ratified"; for the unratified is also transferred, as
the law itself into the gospel. Gal 3:17 The four hundred thirty years are
gathered thus: from Abraham's 75th year until 100 is counted 25 years, Isaac
60 years, Jacob 5 years, Joseph 110 years, in Egypt 144 years, together 430
years. Gal 3:-21 But God is one. But God is one, who gave both the law and the
gospel, and mediated and reconciled to the Father, that 608 is, he is the same
who is Christ. For even if the things mediated are two, yet the mediator is
one; for he also ought to be one. Is the law then against the promises of God?
Far from it. He solves an objection; for some might say the law snatched the
justification of the promises and has become against them. But far from it;
for if it were able to make alive, reasonably someone would think it had been
set out against the promises and had stolen their justification, inasmuch as
it had power to justify. But since it has power to do no such thing, neither
to make alive nor to justify, it is clear that it was not given against the
promises; for these will display their own justification, saving and
justifying the one from faith. And see the wisdom; for what someone else would
have brought forward for slander of the law, that it does not make alive nor
justify, this he himself established as a defense against the charge and as
praise. For this reason, he says, the law has not become against the promises,
because it had power neither to save nor to justify. And not only has it not
become against them, but in a certain manner it even cooperated and served
them; for through the things by which it did not save nor justify, it urged
flight to the faith; and through the things by which it drove toward the
faith, it helped the promises to be perfected. Gal 3:22 But the scripture shut
up. The law, he says, as it were took around and shut all into sin, showed
them sinners. How? For it said: cursed is everyone who does not remain in all
the things written to do them. And this is impossible; for not only did not
all do all things, but not even some of them all things, but scarcely the more
distinguished of them the greater part, so that by not doing all things those
too were under curse and sin. All therefore happened to have been shut up into
the sin. Therefore from faith alone they can obtain the promise; for rather
from the law they were also driven into sin and all were shown to be so. Gal
4:4 Born under the law. For thus he came to be under the law, that he was also
circumcised, in order that, he says, he might redeem those who were under the
law and its curse by the cross and by having come to be under the law at all.
609 Gal 4:15 What then was the blessedness? What was it? It is not of one
asking, but as of one marveling and lifting up their faith then and the
blessedness upon it. What was it? How great was it, he says, how marvelous?
From how great a blessedness have you fallen away? Or as a question: what was
it? Remember, he says, what your blessedness was; for I used to count you very
blessed, but now why do I rebuke? For this I am hated. And why do I rebuke?
For your salvation. For if this were not so, how, when you furnished no other
pretext, would the one who praised and blessed have come to blaming? Gal 4:23
But the one from the maidservant. Do not then let your being kinsmen of
Abraham according to the flesh puff you up and persuade you that you will also
be heirs of the promise. Rather let this very thing teach, that if you do not
become children of Abraham according to promise, that is, members and of the
same body with Christ (for this one is seed of Abraham from promise), you are
outside the promise. Gal 4:25 To the present Jerusalem, that is, to the one
here on earth, so that both stand apart from the one above and free. Gal
5:16-17 Walk by the Spirit and desire. Virtue is set against vice, lest when
vice has provoked, the fleshly will be borne at once as if loose and unbridled
and downhill toward sin. Again vice is set against virtue, lest the working of
virtue be altogether without toil and without reward, and as if allotted to us
from nature and from necessity, and as accomplished by mere thinking
without all zeal and contest. But he himself says it briefly: that you may not
do whatever you would. Neither the evils, he says, altogether easily and
unhindered, nor the goods wholly readily and with neglect; for the things done
thus would not even be good. Gal 5:24 And "but they" is also to be understood
as i.e. "but these", putting a circumflex on the hoi; and these are those of
whom I spoke, who crucified the flesh of Christ, and so on. 610 Gal 6:2 And
thus you will fulfill the law. For the law of Christ, the one through the
works, was shown to be of this kind; for he himself took up our sins and bore
the diseases. And the excess, that he even accepted a cross and death for us.
Gal 6:4 But let each prove his own work. For if someone should prove his own
work and search himself accurately, he will rather have the boast toward
himself and not against another, that is, he will have the boast against
himself, as he will condemn himself and not others. Or he will have the boast
toward himself, that is, seeing himself diminished and in want, he will blame
himself and will stand off from the condemnation of others, knowing that he
does not even always keep the same conduct of his, but even sometimes falls
from this too. Gal 6:9 Or not growing weary, that is, not growing numb nor
standing off from well-doing, but throughout life casting down the spiritual
seed with intensity; for the crown belongs to those who contend to the end.
Gal 6:17 Let no one cause me troubles. Let no one call me out to a defense of
the things I do. For even being silent I have a greater defense through the
works; for the marks of Christ, that is the blows and scars for Christ,
themselves cry out, both the course and zeal for him and the unhypocritical
and pure and unshrinking character of the preaching. Or: let no one cause me
troubles. Until when, he says, being shaken, do you furnish me afflictions and
troubles? Be put to shame if by nothing else, then at least by the marks of
Christ which I carry about in the body because of you and because of the
preaching toward you. And having said marks of Christ, at the same time he
reminds that that one too suffered for you; therefore in every way it was
fitting to stand off from the law and to be established in the faith.
