631 Col 1:17 And he himself is before all things. He did not say: he came to
be before all things, but: he is before all things, that is, whatever you may
conceive, you will find him pre-existing and pre-subsisting, eternally
co-existing with the Father and the Spirit. Col 1, Having made peace. The
hyperbaton: having made peace through himself, that is, having wrought it by
his own act, and not having procured the peace for us through others. And in
what manner did he work this? Through the blood, he says, of his crucifixion;
for having been crucified for us and through the crucifixion having poured out
his own blood for us, he made peace and reconciled us. Col 1:21 And enemies in
mind. Instead of: we did not do the wicked works unwillingly and by some
snatching-away, but with deliberation and having prefigured in our reasonings
the enmity toward God; thus we proceeded to the deeds hostile to him.
Therefore unspeakable is the divine philanthropy, that even being such he both
reconciled and received us. Col 1:24 And I fill up. Christ, having been struck
and mocked and scourged and crucified for us, did not simply fill all things
but even beyond mind, and with unspeakable word he filled them out completely.
We have all therefore become debtors, in return for those myriad and
world-saving sufferings, ourselves also to endure and to bring in sufferings
in exchange. And afflictions for Christ and for his body, that we may fill up
what Christ suffered for us. But indeed to fill these up, that is, to suffer
equally and likewise and to lack nothing, is impossible. But whatever we may
suffer is a shortfall toward the afflictions of Christ. For how could even
that the Master suffer for a slave be filled up through a slave for the
Master? For this is not equal nor like, far from it. Or the sinless for
sinners and those who have offended through those in sins for a benefactor and
sinless one? And a myriad. Therefore whatever one may suffer, wishing to fill
up, he fills up a shortfall of the 632 afflictions of Christ. Therefore also
the blessed Paul, who ran more than all for Christ, who suffers myriad
terrible things each day, knowing this and teaching, said. Col 2:17 It is
possible to punctuate in two ways, both placing a slight stop at body, and at
of Christ. Col 3:23 To work is from the common construction, by a shift, as
working for the Lord and not for men. Col 4:3 That God may open. The common of
the syntax: that God may open to us a door of the word to speak the mystery of
Christ as I ought to speak, for which I am also bound, that I may make it
manifest. But the divine Paul brought forth the word according to hyperbaton.
Col 4:6 Let your speech always be in grace. Let it be seasoned as with salt;
for just as almost nothing of the things that nourish the body is palatable
without salt and is distributed into well-juiced nourishment, so also the
teaching word that nourishes the soul, if it is not seasoned with grace, will
neither nourish nor be assimilated. Col 4:16 That also in that of the
Laodiceans. There was certainly something of the things written also tending
toward the correction of the Laodiceans. Or that they would attend more to
themselves, seeing the faults of others being exposed. And that from Laodicea.
Not the one from Paul to the Laodiceans, for he would have said: and the one
in Laodicea, but the one from the Laodiceans to Paul; for there was certainly
something in it benefiting the Colossians. Col 4:17 He writes concerning the
teacher, that they may be rebuked and, being exhorted, may obey, knowing the
things made known to him by Paul. And perhaps 633 he was their bishop, or had
been entrusted with another ministry of souls. Therefore he says: a ministry
in the Lord and not according to man. Col 4:18 Rather he wrote the very
wording of the greeting with his own hand, that they might feel something more
seeing the hand of Paul. A greatest exhortation toward bearing trials nobly is
to know and always remember that this man is in bonds. Clearly with which they
will fulfill all the things commanded. They need grace in order to be saved.
For what could a man do without grace?
