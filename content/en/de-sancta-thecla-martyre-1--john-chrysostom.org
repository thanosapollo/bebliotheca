* On Saint Thecla the Martyr
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

"Save me from all who pursue me and deliver me, lest he snatch my soul as a
lion, there being none to redeem nor to save." And swift is the help of the
girl; for when she had become invisible suddenly, the suitor went away having
gained only one thing, a horse-race of wantonness. And the bride stood by the
bridegroom somewhere singing: "Justly is my help from God who saves the
upright in heart." Let us too then learn the contests of continence against
the fleshly things, let us campaign against pleasures as the martyr, let us
arm ourselves for flesh against flesh, let us prepare the members beforehand
for the glory of the resurrection, let us not be mastered by delights that
procure the painful things of Gehenna, let us flee the sweet, that the truly
sweet may not run away from us. Let the widow keep goodwill for the dead
housemate as one who will appear again into life. Let her who lives with
marriage be dead toward alien pleasures. Let her who is rotting with
fornications cool the flame of pleasure with the marital. Let the virgin not
seek a more beautiful bridegroom than Christ. Let each, saving the purity
proper to his own rank, procure for the soul the contemplation of the Master
Christ; for "Blessed," he says, "are the pure in heart, for they shall see
God"; to him be the glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
