* On the Eight Spirits of Wickedness
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of John of Damascus concerning the eight spirits of wickedness.
αʹ. Know, brother, that eight are the thoughts that war against the monk,
as the Fathers say. First that of gluttony; second that of evil and
shameful desire; third that of love of money; fourth that of grief; fifth
that of anger; sixth that of acedia; seventh that of vainglory; and that
of pride eighth.
βʹ. You ought therefore to know, O monk, and to attend accurately, by
which passion you are troubled and thrown into tumult by our enemies and
spirits of wickedness, and to which passionate thought your mind gives
consent.
γʹ. And if you perceive that you are troubled by gluttony, and by luxury,
narrow your belly, limiting in weight and measure the luxury and the
drink; have continual remembrance of the departure of the soul, and of
the coming judgment and of the fearful Gehenna; and likewise also of the
longing for the kingdom of the heavens. For thus you will be able to
conquer the pleasure through the belly, and to abhor it.
δʹ. But if again you are held by the spirit of shameful and evil desire,
practice continence of body, and contrition of soul, and vigils, with
intense prayer; besides these have also reserve of speech, and not to
judge anyone or to slander, or to abhor at all. And call to mind also
death, and love thirst; and by no means wish to go among women, or even
at all to see their appearance, and you will be delivered from the
passion.
εʹ. But if you wish to conquer love of money, love possessionlessness, and
incompleteness; and call to mind the condemnation of Judas, and that
through it he betrayed the Lord to the lawless; and that every lover of
money is also called an idolater by the divine Scripture; and that it
removes us from the hope of God; and that the enjoyment of money is
temporary, but for the lovers of money the punishment is immortal;
considering these things then, and seeking nothing more than sufficiency,
you will conquer this passion.
ϛʹ. But if again you are troubled and thrown into tumult from worldly
grief, you ought to pray continually, and to set all hope toward God, and
to practice meditation of the divine Scriptures, and to keep company with
devout monks and those who fear the Lord, and to despise all present
things as not being, and to consider the joy in the heavens, and the
enjoyments of the righteous. And if you are struck by someone, or
dishonored, or driven out, do not be grieved, but rather rejoice; and
then only be grieved, when you sin against God; for thus by the spirit
you will be able to be delivered from the passion.
ζʹ. But when again you are troubled by wrath and by quick temper, take on
compassion, and enslave yourself toward the brethren, and if possible,
wash their feet continually in humility of mind, and seek forgiveness
from every human being, and visit the sick continually, and move the
tongue toward psalmody, and you will very quickly be delivered from the
passion.
ηʹ. But if you wish to conquer acedia, labor at some small handicraft, and
read, and pray frequently with firm hope of the good things; consider
those gasping out the soul, and the violence and the choking of the
sinners, how they are punished and tormented without mercy, and thus you
will have rest from the passion.
θʹ. But if you are terribly tyrannized by vainglory and by the praise of
human beings, you ought to do nothing toward display before human beings,
but to do all your work in secret, no one else knowing, or God alone; and
do not love the praises, nor the honors of human beings, nor the fine
garments, nor the preference, and the first seat; but rather love that
human beings should blame you, and accuse and dishonor you while lying;
and hold yourself more sinful than every sinner.
ιʹ. But if you see yourself warred upon by the demonic passion of the
worst pride, you ought to revile no one, or judge, or despise utterly,
but to reckon yourself the offscouring of all, and to consider
continually, that unless the Lord build =95.84= a house, in vain have they
labored who build; and always to reckon yourself a debtor, and to make
yourself of no account before God and all human beings. And do not be
confident, until you receive the sentence; seeing that one, who even
after the reclining in the bridechamber, is bound hand and foot, and
banished into the eternal darkness; but whether you fast, or keep vigil,
or sleep on the ground, or psalm, or remain, or make many prostrations,
or do some other good thing, do not say that it has come about from my
own labor, or from my own eagerness, but it comes about from the help and
assistance of God, and not from my own zeal. Strive always, brother, that
you may be always simple and blameless; and do not have other things in
the heart, other things in your mouth; for this is deceitful; and pray
continually with tears; and doing thus you will be delivered from the
destructive and wicked fall. Of the passions, some are bodily, and some
of the soul; and we call bodily, gluttony, fornication, drunkenness,
wantonness. And of the soul, hatred toward the neighbor, envy, strife,
vainglory, pride. And these operate in our soul, love and continence
being absent; and the others by fasting and vigil. For then the mind also
receives its own light, and sees God unhindered.
