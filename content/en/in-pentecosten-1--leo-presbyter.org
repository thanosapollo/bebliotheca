It is permitted me, looking to the present season, to break forth more loudly
with that Davidic voice, which no man uttered, but God forged. Which is this,
he says? All you nations, clap your hands, shout to God with a voice of
gladness. Why? Because the Lord of glory went up into the heavens, and the
Holy Spirit came down upon the earth; because the Master's fire was kindled,
and the Master's bread was baked; because the Holy Spirit is confessed as God,
and Marathonius is blinded; because we received the promise of the Holy
Spirit, and we have not tasted orphanhood at all; because the bridegroom
Christ was taken up, we have not endured widowhood. For it is necessary, when
the bridegroom has gone abroad, that the guardian himself be present with her,
since many corrupters draw near upon the earth. So the bridegroom Christ,
having gone abroad, entrusted the whole world to the bride, leaving at once
all the needful things as the girl's provider. What needful things? Wine, the
mystical; oil, that of the chrism; bread, the precious body; income, the
fruit-bearing of the faithful; wool, the wrapping for the newly illuminated;
linen, the whiteness of brotherly love. For seeing this bride's work, the
speaker of Proverbs said: She made double garments and cloaks for her husband.
What double garments? White ones and purple; white the apostolic, not soiled
by impiety, and purple the martyrs', which they dyed with the murex of their
own blood.

And what of Luke, the writer of the apostolic Acts? For it is necessary to
visit him. You heard him crying out: When the day of Pentecost was being
fulfilled, they were all with one accord in one place; and suddenly there
comes from heaven a sound as of a violent wind being borne along, and it
filled the whole house where the Lord's disciples were sitting. And why were
they sitting? Because they had not yet received the variety of tongues,
because besides the rustic speech they knew nothing else. The Lord's disciples
sat before the coming of the Holy Spirit, honoring inactivity, stitching
together the net of the promise, making themselves ready for the hunt of the
rational fish. For the Lord's disciples, sitting, said to themselves: Let none
of us rush to teaching before the coming of the Holy Spirit. We are unlearned
and unlettered, knowing nothing besides stammering; but those who oppose us
are lawyers and philosophers, and ready to overthrow us by rhetoric. The Lord
declared us teachers of the nations [us]; how can we catechize Romans, or
instruct Parthians, or illuminate Galatians, unless we receive the store of
the varied tongues?

While the apostles were perhaps thus doubting among themselves, there came
suddenly a sound from heaven, as you just now heard. A sound was sent from
above, and a crash was not given back from below. Why? That the apostles might
not seem to have the dragon hissing from below, but the Spirit refreshing from
above. A sound came to be, because the law was ceasing; a sound came to be,
because tongues were being turned on the lathe. Why then did a sound run ahead
of the wonder, and the crash work before the tongues? Because also around the
lawgiving the voice of the trumpet sounded great, and † all-dropping † the
fearful water ran together, that they might hear the word with quiet and fear.
But also in the case of Samuel, when the people were testifying, he gave
voices and thunders with much hail. For where there is a movement of divinity,
there also is the tumult of the elements. Know then nevertheless that the Holy
Spirit also works wonders with the Father and the Son, and he blows there
where he wills. For this reason a sound ran ahead. And the house was filled,
because they also filled the whole world as a house, according to the saying
that declares: Where shall I go from your Spirit, and from your face where
shall I flee? and again: The Spirit of the Lord has filled the inhabited
world.

And by inhabited world he means man, as house of the God over all, and world
of the world-maker, and image of the image-writer, and work of the fashioner
of man. For besides man every animal has bent toward the earth and sends its
sense of smell there where it has its existence, but man alone both treads the
earth and contemplates heaven. Why? That he may trample the things below as
earthly, and long for the things above as heavenly; for man alone, that I may
speak more clearly, both is neighbor to <the earth>, and is akin to heaven.
But nevertheless, even if he holds to both, he has acquired the more lordly
from above; whence the head of man has been likened to heaven and bears
that one's shape; for he has a little heaven and two luminaries, according
to Paul crying out: For our citizenship is in the heavens. If then the Holy
Spirit fills the whole world, which is humanity, how is the Holy Spirit a
creature according to Marathonius' madness? For this Marathonius, gone mad,
dogmatized, that the Holy Spirit is a creature.

But O Marathonius, a creature cannot dwell in a creature. And what I say has
this explanation: man is a creature of God, but man is also a house of God. It
is surely clear to everyone that he has God dwelling in him; for what temple
is there which the statue does not nourish? And if he has God dwelling in him,
besides God no one dwells in man. Hear the Lord himself saying: I will come, I
and my Father, and we will make a dwelling with him, and again: I will dwell
in them and walk about, and I will be their God. But Marathonius will say at
once: The Father and the Son dwelt there, not the Holy Spirit, surely? But O
Marathonius, hear Paul saying: Do you not know that you are a temple of God
and the Spirit of God dwells in you? And if man is both a temple of God and a
house of the Holy Spirit, what will you say to these things?

But the children of the Marathonians will say to these things, those who
number Pentecost and do not keep the feast; for how shall they feast if they
do not piously believe, if they do not confess the Spirit as God? They will
say at once that the apostle testified these things about the Spirit as a
concession. Show me the Father himself saying, as he said concerning himself
and the Son, that I will dwell in man, that he says these things also
concerning the Spirit, and I am persuaded. But O Marathonius, and who spoke
concerning the blessed Paul? Was it not the Master Christ? Did you not hear
him saying: since you seek a proof of Christ speaking in me? Do you strike out
Paul, Marathonius, the vessel of election, the myrrh of grace, the mouth of
the church, the one who lends his mouth to God? And from where is this? That
Paul lends his mouth to God, hear the Lord saying to him: Speak, Paul, and do
not be silent, because I have much people here. God says to Paul: Speak, and
you disbelieve Paul speaking concerning the Holy Spirit?

Do you strike out Paul, Marathonius, the Spirit-bearer, the word of God, the
one who bears the marks of the Lord? Hear him enumerating how many trials he
endured for the gospel preaching. Hear: In labors more abundantly, in stripes
above measure, in prisons often, in deaths often. From the Jews five times I
received forty save one, three times I was beaten with rods, once I was
stoned, three times I was shipwrecked, a night and a day I have spent in the
deep; in journeys often, in dangers from nations, in dangers of robbers, in
dangers among false brothers, in dangers in the city, in dangers in deserts.
Do you strike out Paul, Marathonius? You ought not to strike out Paul, the one
caught up as far as the third heaven, and transferred with the body itself
into paradise, and there having seen the robber harvesting the faith, and
having heard unspeakable words.

And he cries these things: I speak truth, I do not lie. And if you wish, O
Marathonius, to receive this proof from the Savior, hear the Lord saying these
things to his own disciples: When they lead you before governors and kings, do
not be anxious how or what you will speak; for it is not you who are speaking,
but the Spirit of your Father who speaks in you. Did you see the God-inspired
working of the Holy Spirit, and that there is one dwelling of Father and Son
and Holy Spirit? Of those then whose dwelling is one, of these also the
essence is one. And if these things are small toward proof, take also the
things just now read out to us; for it is good to set the spiritual luncheon
before late-diners. What then of Luke, the writer of the apostolic Acts? And
there appeared to them tongues distributing as of fire, and it sat upon each
one of them, and they were all filled with Holy Spirit, and <they began to
speak with other tongues as> the Spirit gave them to utter. Hear with
understanding. As the Spirit gave, <not> as it was commanded; as the Spirit
gave, not as it was learning. What is more God-shining than this truth?

The Holy Spirit today, as you heard, unfolded heaven, multiplied the tongues,
enlightened the world, made the apostles bright, astounded the Jews, amazed
the nations, was added to those below, but was not separated from those above;
he went out from the Father, but was not taken away from the Father; he was
sent from the Son, but he did not change place. The difference of the words is
because of the hypostases, not because of the one essence of the Godhead. We
are taught as we are able to hear; we are not able to see as God is. And that
the Holy Spirit was, and was sent, and having come down from Father and Son
was not separated, do not rest on my words, but learn the teaching of the
blessed Peter. You heard him just now speaking to the Jews who were drunk from
envy. A dreadful wrapping he receives; the drunkard is neither counted among
the dead nor found among the living. He pleads without stumbling toward every
tongue, as you see the apostles schooling all without falling. What of those
who are drunk did you behold in the apostles? Do you call sobriety
drunkenness, and name the gift of the Holy Spirit wine-swilling? The nations
bless and you slander? Are the apostles drunk?

Noah and Lot got drunk; those having gotten drunk, the one uttered a curse of
slavish writings, and the other spread a lawless bed of daughter-mixing. The
apostles are not drunk, Jews; and if they are drunk, they are drunk from the
torrent of the Spirit, and not from wine. For concerning them the prophet
said: They shall be drunk from the fatness of your house, and you will give
them to drink the torrent of your delight. But it is possible for <someone> to
say: And with what torrents did the apostles become drunk with the seemly
drunkenness of the soul? Of what torrent? Of the indwelling of the Holy
Spirit. And who is witness of this? The Master Christ, who was pierced in
the side and put forth blood and water, that he might give the one to
drink, ***. For you heard just now the Master Christ crying out: If anyone
thirsts, let him come to me and drink. He who believes in me, as the
scripture said, rivers of living water will flow from his belly.

And this he said, it says, concerning the Holy Spirit which those who believed
in him were about to receive. From this torrent the apostles were given to
drink, and having gotten drunk they let loose rivers of teaching and watered
the whole inhabited world with the divine word, and they nourished the
newly-sprouted ear of piety. For they did not make a lake of Egypt only as the
river Nile, nor did they pour out the grace by a cubit-measure, nor did they
water Havilah alone as the Pishon, nor irrigate Ethiopia alone as the Gihon,
nor fill Assyria alone as the Tigris, nor fatten Augustus' Euphrasia alone as
the Euphrates, but they gladdened every city and fatherland and country and
island together, they filled them through the grace of the Holy Spirit. And
David bears witness to my word, crying out: Their sound went out into all the
earth and their words to the ends of the inhabited world. Therefore the
apostles were not filled with new wine, but filled with Holy Spirit. Whence
also the blessed Peter, reproaching the Jews, said, as you just now heard:
<For not as you suppose> are these drunk, for it is the third hour of the day.

O the paradoxical wonder. At the third hour the Holy Spirit came upon them. At
the first hour the Father formed Adam, at the second hour the Son created Eve,
at the third hour the Holy Spirit multiplied the tongues of the apostles. Let
us then long for this drunkenness; for the Lord stands crying: If anyone
thirsts, let him come to me and drink. O the Lord's abundance. He did not say:
If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink, but If anyone thirsts, let
him be coming to me and drink. This is: Let him draw this drink each day, and
it heals both in night and in day. For it does not stop a bodily burning heat,
but it persuades a desire of the soul; nor does it raise a swelling of the
belly, but it sharpens a longing of the mind; nor does it put in a harm of
cold-drinking, but it provides a gain of the knowledge of God.

Having longed for this thirst the blessed David also cried out saying: In the
manner that the deer longs for the springs of the waters, so my soul longs
toward you, O God. Say you also, O man, with David: In the manner that the
deer longs for the springs of the waters, so my soul longs toward you, O God.
See that you do not lie to God, that you are not found more idle than the
deer. Imitate that deer, run swiftly to the Lord; for the animal is
well-legged. Bear the cruciform horn upon the forehead; for the animal also is
adorned with a projection of horns. Never separate from the apostolic
mountains; for the deer too is gladly fond of mountains. Slaughter the
venom-shooting beasts of the heretics with the pious word [of the heretics'
serpents]; for the deer also ***. Never drink the muddied water of
Spirit-fighting; for it is impossible for a deer to drink muddied water. Step
upon the height of the evangelical thoughts and let your feet be fitted to the
blameless way of piety, that you too may be able to say with a thankful mouth
of the prophet: God who girds me with power and made my way blameless, fitting
my feet like a deer's and setting me upon the high places. And I wished with
much consent also to call the Master Christ a deer; for he himself caused the
cruciform horn of salvation to rise for us.

Whence taking courage we will gore our enemies; for just as the bodily
deer *** from the abysses of the baptismal waters he took the deep-swimming
devil as a many-headed dragon. Whence the prophetic choir, striking up the
victory hymn toward him, said: You crushed the heads of the dragon upon the
water. And just as again the horn of this bodily deer after † the same † of
the animal, being smoked into some house, drives out the beasts lurking
within, so also the cruciform horn of our Savior Jesus Christ after the
Lord's voluntary death drives out the herd of demons from every place
wherever it draws near. Let us then long for it, O friends, and honor the
present feast. Let us honor it through all things; you go away today to strew
roses on the dead: do not in drunkenness bear incense upon the tomb, do not
mix a mixing-bowl. Need of tears, ***; there is need of sympathy, not of
dancing, surely? Need of groanings, not of rattlings, surely? Need of
psalmody, not of kithara-playing, surely? Need of reverence, not of fighting,
surely? Need of encomiums, not of rippings-apart, surely? Need of
thanksgiving, not of indignation, surely?

Today you seek the site-plan, and you promise to draw and to architect and to
build, and on the next day all things have gone to sleep. O disposition that
together with the sprout endures withering. Honor the one who has gone the
road before, do not grieve his soul in the Spirit. The one who died before
struggles how he will prevail upon the judge, and you get drunk so that you
may delight the devil? Imitate the Master Christ, O man. What did the Lord do
when he arrived at Lazarus' tomb? Did he weep or did he dance? Because to our
Lord Jesus Christ himself belongs glory, greatness and magnificence, together
with the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, now and always and unto the
ages of ages. Amen.
