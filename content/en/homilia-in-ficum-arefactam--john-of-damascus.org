* Homily on the Withered Fig Tree
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Βʹ. Of our holy Father John, monk and presbyter of the holy Resurrection of
Christ our God, that is, Mansur, a discourse on the withered fig tree, and on
the parable of the vineyard.
=96.576= The enhypostatic Word of God the Father moves me to speak: he who
did not depart from the Father's bosom, and was conceived uncircumscribed in a
Virgin's womb; who for me became what I am; who, being impassible in the
Godhead, clothed himself with a body of like passions with me; who is borne
upon Cherubic chariots, and on earth has mounted a colt of an ass; the King of
glory, who is hymned as holy by the Seraphim together with the Father and the
Spirit, and who accepts the stammerings of the children from a tongue
inexperienced in evil; being God, and existing in the form of a servant, and
having taken the form of a servant; being immaterial and invisible as God, and
having taken on a visible and palpable body; who came willingly to the
Passion, that he might grant me impassibility. For when he saw the creature of
his hands enticed by the deceit of the serpent, man, whom he fashioned
according to his own image and likeness, and having become in transgression
of his commandment, and subject to corruption, and liable to death, he who is
compassionate by nature did not bear the deprivation of the one he longed for,
but in many ways called him to conversion and repentance, having trained him
as an ungrateful slave, as a childlike son, in many parts and in many ways,
and having devised every manner, that having shaken off the slavery of the
tyrant he might return to the one who fashioned him. But he was powerless
toward conversion, having once enslaved himself to sin, and having been mixed
with earthly desire by choice.
=96.577= Wherefore the supremely good Master, seeing nature weakened, takes it
up. For seeing man disobedient to word and commandments and saving
injunctions, what does he say? I must train the ignorant by deed. I must take
him in hand toward virtue, that having grown accustomed he himself may work
these things. I must be seen, and thus heal the sick. I must turn back the
wandering sheep, and lead it to the ancient dwelling of paradise. How then
shall I turn it back if I am not seen? How shall I lead the one who does not
see my tracks? Therefore he became man, that through what he both did and
suffered he might teach by deed the ignorant the manner of the working of
virtue; that having seen him come down from the Father's bosom to earth for us
by economy, we too might go up from our mother the earth to him by choice;
that he might show the surpassing wealth of his love toward us. For no one can
show greater love than that one should lay down his soul for his friends. How
then shall he who has no soul show the love?
βʹ. Therefore he assumes flesh, that he may be seen on earth, and may dwell
with men. Therefore he assumes a soul, that he may lay down his own soul for
his friends. And I say friends, not of those who love him, but for those
longed for by him. For we hated him and turned away, having served another,
but he stood having his love toward us unchanged. Therefore he ran after us.
He came to those who hated, he pursued those who fled, and having overtaken
them he did not rebuke with harshness, he did not turn them with a scourge,
but as an excellent physician, being insulted by one in delirium, spat upon,
struck, he offered the healings. As the greatness of healing he offered his
divinity to the nature of humanity as a medicine, a most active medicine, an
all-powerful medicine. This made the weak little flesh more powerful than the
invisible powers. For just as iron united to fire is unapproachable, so the
grass of our nature, united to the fire of the Godhead, has become
unapproachable to the devil; and since the disciples of the physicians say
that opposites are the remedies of opposites, through opposites he overthrows
opposites: pleasure, through toils; pride, through humility. For he not only
humbled himself, being rich in Godhead, and becoming man, but even among men
he humbled himself. For who among men is so humble? For he has nowhere to lay
his head. He had no beast of burden, no second cloak, no other tunic. Being
reviled he did not revile in =96.580= return; suffering he did not threaten;
as an innocent lamb he was led to be sacrificed, not striving, not crying out;
being struck he readily gave the cheek to the one who struck; he did not turn
his face from the shame of spittings; being called a Samaritan and one who has
a demon, being persecuted, he endures these things, that we too may follow his
tracks. All these things he accomplished by the Father's good pleasure. For
being from him only-begotten and consubstantial Son, he published his Father's
love concerning us. For God the Father so loved us, that he gave his
only-begotten Son as a ransom for us. O unsurpassable love! An only-begotten
Son co-reigning with him, for disobedient servants, for blaspheming enemies,
who serve the enemy and proclaim him God. O depth of the wealth of the
goodness of God! But the only-begotten Son did not resist; he did not set
aside the Father's counsel. For he himself was the counsel and the will of the
Father. Wherefore as sharer and partaker of the nature (for the nature of
Father and Son is one, one), he ministers as to his own will, and becomes man,
and is made obedient to the Father unto death, and death of a cross, healing
my disobedience.
γʹ. He comes then pressing to suffer, and hastening to drink the cup of death
that is saving for the whole world. He comes hungering for the salvation of
men, and does not find fruit in it; for the fig tree hints at this in parable.
For who eats in the morning? The King, the Lord, the Teacher, hungering in the
morning, does not have the desire of eating. He does not master nature, but
like some incontinent and unrestrained man he rushes irrationally toward food,
at a time that is not fitting. And how does he train the disciples to fast,
and not to be overcome by a passion of desire? It is not so; but just as he
spoke teaching by word through parables, so also he goes through the parables
by deed. He came to the fig tree having hungered. The fig tree sketched the
nature of humanity. Sweet is the fruit of the fig tree, rough the leaves, and
useless, and ready for burning; but the nature of humanity also had the
sweetest fruit of virtue, commanded by God to bear this. But it acquired,
through the unfruitfulness of virtue, the roughness of the leaves. For what is
rougher than worldly cares? Adam and Eve were once naked, and were not
ashamed; naked in simplicity, and in an artless life. They had no crafts and
worldly cares; they did not devise how they would cover the nakedness of the
body; they were not ashamed at possessionlessness; not at the smoothness of
life, but even if =96.581= they were naked in the body, they were covered by
the divine grace. They had no bodily wrapping, but they had a garment of
incorruption; for as much as they were made akin to God through obedience, so
much with the garment of incorruption. But having disobeyed, they were far
removed from the grace that covered them. They were stripped of the ecstasy
and contemplation toward God; they saw the nakedness of their own body; they
longed for the delights of life; they were brought into the plain and
possessionless life; they sewed fig leaves for themselves, and made themselves
girdles; they sought many reasonings, and found the rough and care-ridden and
painful life. For in the sweat of your face you shall eat your bread. Cursed
is the earth in your works, thorns and thistles it shall bring forth for you,
and to earth you shall go. You had an earthly mind, your turning shall be to
earth. You were compared to the senseless beasts; for you did not understand
when you were in honor. You were in God, and you did not know fruit-bearing
virtue; but you preferred the enjoyment of earthly things, having loved the
life of the irrational. You are earth, and to earth you shall go. After the
manner of the irrational you shall inherit death. From this he is also clothed
with coats of skin. For being in the body between life and mortality, formerly
being in the paradise of delight and moving in a royal chamber, later he
acquired a mortal and thick body, able to bear the toils. Truly rough
are the leaves of the fig tree of our nature, the uneven wickedness of our
nature. To this fig tree, that is the nature of humanity, the Savior came
hungering, and seeking from it the sweetest fruit, that is the virtue sweetest
to God, through which salvation comes. But he did not find fruit, but only
leaves, the roughest and most miserable sin, and the evils that grow from it.
Wherefore he answers against it, that no longer shall fruit come from you. For
salvation is not from men; virtue is not from human power. I will work the
salvation, granting the resurrection through my Passion, and I grant the
deliverance from the roughest life. Which indeed he has also done.
δʹ. Then having made the parable through deed, he went into the temple, to
visit the Father's house, and he finds the evil husbandmen, the chief priests,
who sat upon the seat of Moses, having shown themselves wolves, wearing
outwardly the garment of sheep, likened to an unfruitful fig tree, who have
not the sweetest fruit, but roughness of leaves. For see the roughness of
their words. By what authority do you do these things? and who gave you this
=96.584= authority? Do you see unfruitfulness of soul and of unbelief? Instead
of which they ought to have said: Well done, good teacher, that you raised
Lazarus dead four days; that you taught the lame to walk swiftly; that you
granted to the blind the power of sight; that you healed the crushed, and
drove away every disease and every infirmity; that you put demons to flight;
that you teach the way of salvation. By what authority, they say, do you do
these things? O ungrateful ones! And if he should say to you, Do you believe?
If you did not even believe John, though you went out to him like a river
stream, and confessing your sins were baptized in appearance, and you did not
believe, shall I also, if I tell you, be believed? O wicked and unbelieving
generation! You are the evil husbandmen, who devour the vineyard of the Lord
of Sabaoth. Which of the prophets did you not kill? The Father sent you my
servants the prophets, demanding the fruit of the vineyard. A vineyard I
brought from Egypt through Moses, which I planted in a fat land, having cast
out nations, and I allotted by a measuring-line of inheritance; whose roots I
planted with law and word of prophets, and it filled the earth; whose branches
reached to the sea, and the shoots filled rivers of nations. But you took down
her hedge, the help from the law, and the demons who travel the way of life
gather her unguarded. A boar from the wood, the robber, ravaged her, and a
wild solitary boar grazed her down. Of this vine well planted, the
fruit-bearing and true, when the fruit was demanded by my servants the
prophets, one you struck, another you condemned in a pit of mire, another you
stoned.
Behold I myself the son and heir am present; respect my sonship, be put to
shame before the dignity of my paternal nature. I am in the Father, and the
Father in me, and I have come to you. I have pity on my vineyard; even if I
have come down to earth, yet I lodge in the bosom of my Father. Render to me
the fruit of my vineyard. But truly as evil husbandmen you will fulfill the
work of your fathers. They were slayers of the prophets; you will become even
slayers of God. For you lavish yourselves in wickedness. I am the heir, the
cornerstone, whom if you even thrust away, you will be crushed, but I will
join the two peoples, the things that stood apart, the earthly to the
heavenly. Through me one Church of angels and men will come to be. Through me,
being enemies to my Father, you will be reconciled. For I will make peace, and
I will make the libations of the peace my blood, which will be poured out for
the salvation of the world.
εʹ. Hinting these things through the parables, he did not persuade. For they
shut their eyes lest they see, and with their ears they heard heavily.
Therefore the light of the Gospel did not shine upon them. O =96.585= the
absurd hardening of the unholy priests! They were condemning themselves, not
knowing what they said. For they brought a vote against themselves. For being
asked what he will do to those husbandmen, unwillingly they answered truly. He
will miserably destroy the wicked. It is just that the wicked truly suffer,
having become so from his own choice. And he will let out the vineyard to
other husbandmen, who will render the fruit in season. As priests and having
the dignity of the priesthood, and not knowing the true things, they prophesy.
For the vineyard, the people of the Lord, has been given out to the husbandmen
who rendered to the Master plentiful and abundant fruit. For their sound
went out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the inhabited
world. How beautiful are the feet of those who preach peace, of those who
preach the good things! These going as sheep in the midst of wolves, changed
the wolves into sheep, made the Greeks who formerly persecuted into sheep of
Christ, and acquired for the Lord a peculiar people, zealous of good works.
ϛʹ. Come then, brethren, as many of us as have received the name of the faith,
who have been counted worthy to be named the people of Christ, let us not set
aside our calling, let us not defile the faith through unseemly works. For it
is not enough only to hear oneself called faithful, but let us show our faith
by deed. For there were two sons, he says, of one father, and he said to the
one: Go, work in the vineyard. And he promised, but did not fulfill the
promise. Then he says to the other. And he denied in word, but in deed he
completed what was commanded. And the former is blamed, but the second is
praised. Let us too then remember the renunciation and the pledge that took
place in baptism. We have renounced the devil and his angels and all his
worship; let us keep the renunciation; let us not turn back as a dog to his
own vomit. Works of the devil are adulteries, fornications, uncleannesses,
envies, strifes, contentions, hypocrisies, slanders, mockeries, angers,
grudges, condemnations, blasphemies, enchantments, charms. The signs of
unbelief: lack of compassion, attachments, antipathies, love of pleasure,
stinginess, revels, drunkennesses. And the pomp of the devil: prides,
vainglories, self-conceit, elation, puffing-up, display, adornment of the
body. Having denied communion with all these, as we pledged ourselves to
Christ, let us be zealous for the virtues contrary to these: chastity,
temperance, poverty, patience, peace, love, compassion, almsgiving, sharing
with those in need, seemly form and dress and gait, true speech, humility, and
above all the reproach =96.588= of Christ, that having become partakers of his
sufferings we may also share in the glory with him, being offered to God and
the Father as a living, unblemished sacrifice, in the Church of the firstborn,
where is the dwelling of those who rejoice.
ζʹ. But now I will turn the word to you, longed-for bride of Christ: long
worthily for him who longed for you. Open to him the whole treasury of your
heart, that Christ may wholly dwell in you with the Father and the Spirit.
Pour out from it all earthly things, that he may find in it the entrance. For
a house will not receive earth and the breath of air together. As much as you
house the dust, you will drive out the spirit of the air; and as much as you
house the earthly things in your heart, you will exile the Holy Spirit. From
where come fornications? from where adulteries? from where strifes,
contentions, envies, murders, and the whole swarm of evils? not from the
desire of earthly things? Drive away all vainglory, the mother of unbelief.
For you cannot, says Christ, believe in me, receiving glory from men. Banish
from your heart all self-conceit, pride, elation. For everyone high-hearted is
unclean before the Lord. The Lord resists the proud, but gives grace to the
humble. Renounce all stubbornness, self-pleasing, that you may be subject to
the law of God alone. And he himself will guide you to the harbor of his will.
Do not bring another bridegroom into the bridal chamber of your heart. For
your bridegroom Christ is jealous; the sweetest, the only one to be longed
for, all sweetness, all desire. Spread open your heart to him alone, and cry
to him. I am of a wounded heart; your longing has made me beside myself; your
love has changed me, Master. I am bound by your desire. Enter into your inner
chamber; I will kiss your footprints. For I am not worthy to say, Kiss me with
the kisses of your mouth? Dwell in me, and walk about, according to your
true promise, and make me a temple of the all-holy Spirit. Take lordship of
my heart, Master, and inherit it, and make a dwelling with me, together with
the Father and the Spirit. Widen in me your cords, the operations of the
all-holy Spirit. For you are my God, and I will glorify you, with your
unoriginate Father, together with your good and life-giving Spirit, now and
always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
