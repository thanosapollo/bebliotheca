* That This Life Is Like the Sea
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

VI. Of the same, that this life is likened to the sea, and on the text, When
Jesus went up with his disciples into the ship and fell asleep.
=64.19=
Seafarers on the open sea, when they make their voyage blown by a favoring
wind, become cheerful in their faces, and they fashion their look as a
counterpart of the calm. But when the fair weather of the calm lingers and
slackens the sailors, and prepares some to sleep and others even to trust the
rudders of the ship to children, and while the helmsman is without care, and
the sailors have loosed the labor of their craft because of the fair weather,
and suddenly a most savage squall has burst down, the winds have rushed out
unexpectedly upon the open sea, and the sea has begun to grow =64.20= savage
and to roar, and the multitude of the waves to hinder the ship, and the taut
stretches of the many-twisted ropes hung about the mast, whipped by the
violent onrush of the winds, to whistle: then, beloved, a stormy and tearful
clamor rises from the passengers, who expect a death in the deep besides the
natural one.
And one may see the sailors tossed by the storm that surrounds them from
without, but still more unnerved by the cry of those wail =64.21= ing within,
so that the helmsman, become trembling from cowardice, can no longer use the
steering hand for the ship's straight course, and all together with a cry
await the end of life among men. Some such thing happened to the disciples of
the Lord; for turn the discourse with me to the Gospel. Jesus went up with
the disciples into the ship, and he was crossing the sea easily. And so long
as the Master Christ was awake for them, every contrary wind was muzzled, and
the sea too was spread out for them as dry land, and no wave rose against the
Christ-bearing ship. But when, by economy, Jesus slept in the ship according
to the nature of the body, and not according to the dignity of the Godhead
(for sleepless is the eye of the Godhead), then the winds, seeing their own
chastiser and commander asleep, like horses that have grown careless when the
charioteer sleeps, were borne with a violent blast, whistling a wild storm,
against the apostolic ship.
And when a squall had arisen that breathed an unimaginable death, and the sea
was boiling with a gurgling, and was foaming out the wrath of the waves upon
the sand that sets the bound, the disciples, seeing the much-famed storm,
were calling upon Christ, the much-hymned champion; saying what? Master, save
us, we are perishing. The Lord was sleeping in the ship by economy, not
ignorant of what was about to happen, not unable to award a calm; but knowing
that the sea was about to be stirred, he slept for two reasons: that he might
both expose in the storm the unsoundness of the disciples' little faith, and
show them the power of the Godhead.
So one might see, beloved, when the Master had fully awakened in the body,
everything becoming swifter than itself, and the wind fleeing away to its own
cave, and the sea no longer threatening the apostolic ship, but the waves
turning toward worship. Such also is the course of life: things never remain
in the same state, but at one time they are calm with peace and an unfailing
plenty of food, and at another they are stirred like the sea, now by inroads
of barbarians, now by plots of tyrants and tangles of affairs, and by many
other tossings and unrests. For the history just told about the apostolic
ship has this meaning; for nothing was being done by the Lord that did not
hold a mystery. This life is likened to a sea, the human substance to a ship,
bearing as passengers and helmsman the reasonings and the mind. If then this
ship, that is the human substance, is storm-tossed in this life by the
devil's wickedness, and by the opposing demons who raise the waves of the
desires, and who throw the salt foam of the pleasures toward the mind within,
and who shake the helmsman mind with the desires, and sink it toward sin: and
that each of men, shaken by the many-flamed pleasures, was being sunk toward
sin, David the prophet bears witness to my word. For when he was shipwrecked
in respect of chastity, he cried out saying: I came into the depths of the
sea, and a tempest drowned me. For we do not find this written anywhere of
David according to history; for where did David sail? or going into what open
sea was he sunk? But no =64.22= where.
What then is it that he says, if not surely this: being in this life as in a
sea, and shaken by the pleasures as by waves, and having the joints of his
reasonings loosed, receiving within himself the violent wave of desire, the
man having gone slack toward the wife of Uriah, he suffered a shipwreck of
chastity. Therefore he was saying: I came into the depths of the sea, and a
tempest drowned me. Then again, having repented and as it were sobered up
from the depth of sin, holding again the rudder of chastity, and ordering his
own life in chastity, he was praying to God saying: Let not a tempest of
water drown me again, nor let the deep swallow me. Some such thing the
Apostle also, hinting mystically, says of humanity, I suffered shipwreck a
third time. For what does he say? Three times I suffered shipwreck; a night
and a day I have spent in the deep. For humanity was shipwrecked a third
time: once in paradise through the transgression, a second time again in the
flood of Noah, a third time after receiving the law the people fell away
toward idolatry, until Christ, the helmsman of our souls, came, and having
set the sail of the cross in the midst of the earth, he ordered for us a
voyage without storm toward the city of heaven. A night and a day I have
spent in the deep. Night he calls the sojourn in dark error before the
Savior's coming; and day he calls the manner of life in the bright baptism
after Christ's coming. So when all humanity, as we said, was being
storm-tossed by the devil's wickedness, the Word of God, having seen it,
coming into this life, enters into the belly of the ship of the Virgin Mary;
whose image we have found to have come about in Jonah the prophet. For Jonah
is sent to Nineveh, Christ comes into this life; Jonah goes off into the
ship, and he sleeps in the hollow of the ship, and the Word of God for nine
months in the belly of the Virgin Mary was as it were sleeping, silent toward
men. Then the devilish storm of wickedness was being raised still more, as in
the case of Jonah; but when Jonah was thrown into the open sea, that is, when
Jesus came forth from the Virgin's womb into this life, every contrary wind
also was muzzled, and until the whale should reach Hades, he was working many
signs and wonders as he swam through this life. Jonah came into the whale,
and he himself came into Hades; three days and three nights, the same as
Jonah, he spent in Hades.
Jonah is cast out of the whale into Nineveh, and Christ rises from the dead
and comes into this world, he preaches repentance; the Ninevites repent, and
so do the citizens of this world; they fast a triad of days, and the
countless multitudes are saved, as also in Nineveh twelve myriads of men and
many cattle. For when the Lord preaches repentance and faith to this world,
the twelve tribes of Israel are saved. For when the fullness of the nations
has come in, then all Israel shall be saved. And that Jonah was an image of
Christ, the Lord himself says in the Gospel: As Jonah spent in the belly of
the whale three days and three nights, so shall the Son of man also be in the
heart of the earth three days and three nights. To him be the glory and the
might unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
