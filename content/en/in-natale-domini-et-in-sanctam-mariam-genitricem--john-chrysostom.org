* On the Nativity of the Lord and on Holy Mary the Mother
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of the same Chrysostom] on the same day [on the nativity of our Lord Jesus
Christ and on holy Mary the Theotokos.
1 An ineffable sea appears today through the ages, deeper than human inquiry.
I attempted to drain an incomprehensible deep, and the more I search, the
deeper it appears. Having set myself to behold knowledge of unspeakable things
and wisdom, I am plunged in the contest of such a search. The powers did not
know such a mystery, and how shall I endure to narrate the unutterable? Let
the feast keep to itself the questions that surpass every mortal thought in
the intellect. A storm of seas lies before us, unnavigable, having its
substance from unseen depths. Let creation hymn the Creator today, sending up
voices of supplication and a song. A heavenly treasure is beheld in the field;
let us therefore run and look upon such a mystery. See how the earth imitated
heaven; for, vying, it puts forth the sun who is before the morning star. Such
an illumination shines upon the universe, being without night, and a
light-bearing radiance. God-inspired lamps shone forth from heaven, and ran
through all the ends of the inhabited world. Mary receives him who is
uncontained in all things, and bears in her womb him who hung the ages. Now
the grain was sown in the field of grace and sprouted a many-bearing ear of
the knowledge of God. Such a subject amazes those who see it, how the sea is
wrapped about the abyss. The precious pearl is fastened in the shell, and
Joseph sought this as a merchant. A child is suckled in a mother's arms and
feeds the universe, being eaten continually. A rod blossomed a flower and sent
a root into the depth; a star puts forth a star, and a manger greets the
birth. Swaddling-clothes are wrapped in fire and there is no diminishing of
them; animals take the imprint of life, and a cave receives light. Babylon
runs first from Persia bringing gifts, and Ethiopia hastens to receive God
with hands. Angels hymn and shepherds sing, men bring glory to God, making
known peace upon earth. Salome is seized with fear and by her voice she
propitiates the Judge; she confessed the Master who was born and was freed
from her own wound. With her hands she bears the undefiled one and came to
know saving joy. Souls of the righteous exult and choirs of mortals rejoice;
the desert has been recognized as a city, the King having taken up his
dwelling. As the birth is unutterable, so the mother is undefiled; and because
of all these things an ineffable sea appears through the ages, deeper than
human inquiry.
2 O unutterable conception and birth not wrought by the spindle, O matters of
thought and wonders of astonishment, O unwedded virgin and you alone undefiled
after the birth.
3 I see the virgin city taking the King to her bosom, I see a god-filled
temple, him who tabernacled in the highest and performed priestly service in
it, I see a holy ark clasping the covenant, I see a golden jar putting forth
the manna, I see an unbreakable pillar bearing the impress.
4 O the wonder: fire is hewn from dew and a flame is kindled from matter, the
bush is clothed with flame and the mountain admits no diminishing; traces of
God are beheld and no inquiry of search is known.
5 Greater than the heaven that was laid down at the making of the world, I
behold another earthly heaven; that one is borne without contention by the
word of the Lord, and this one is made bright beyond interpretation by the
birth of the Lord; that one puts forth a sensible sun, and this one has shone
forth a noetic sun.
6 Instead of the morning star it showed him who is before the morning star,
instead of the moon the womb that is without a husband, instead of the stars
the purity of the belly, instead of the clouds the wrapping from the body;
truly I see the virgin as a heaven today, flashing more with virtues than the
one at the making of the world.
7 Without the grasp of hands a stone is hewn from a mountain, which the most
wise Daniel told beforehand. A man is not present for union and a mother has
borne the truth; the gate of the palace was seen secured, and within, in the
bedchamber, the King was made known.
8 You were rooted, O virgin, "as a cedar in Lebanon"; for as a paradise of the
Lord you gave forth "fragrance." You were "raised as a palm" in glory; for the
inhabited world has been filled with your sweetness. You "as a fair olive"
brought forth a fruit of glory, and your flower is an aroma of fragrance.
9 You "blossomed as a vine" a cluster, and the gladness of your wine is drunk
and does not fail. You "as frankincense" received frankincense, and "as chosen
myrrh," gold and myrrh were offered to you. You "stretched out branches as a
terebinth" and under its shelter the wandering nations ran.
10 You were raised "as a plane tree" upon water, for you bore the fountain of
life in your womb. You sprouted "as a well-colored rose" among women and sent
forth fragrance "as an uncorrupted lily." You were foreordained "before the
age" to bear God, and "until the age" your childbearing remains. You are the
mother of love.
11 For peoples proclaim your praises and the Church will declare your glory;
for in taking upon yourself a robe of glory, you have borne a son of God, to
whom the Father cries: "You are my Son, today I have begotten you."
12 "The Lord said to me: 'You are my Son; today I have begotten you'"; I have
none after you as you, because of you; for one is from one; "today I have
begotten you" as an anointing of those who contend and a king of those under
tyranny; "today I have begotten you," for a calm of those at war and a glory
of those in despair.
13 "Today I have begotten you," for a vindication of the persecuted and life
of those put to death, for a good character and a more divine amendment;
"today I have begotten you"; I have not preferred a fellow-heir after you or
with you.
14 Ineffably, without flowing, unspeakably, incorruptibly "You are my Son;
today I have begotten you"; I emptied all the things of nature, having you as
myself also, a Son in the bosom; for with equally balanced will I anointed you
King; "today I have begotten you," spreading out the power by the will without
contention, having willed to beget a Son, I begot a perfect God.
15 "Today I have begotten you"; no age was present at the purpose of the
begetter, no mediation of anyone that the offspring might arrive, no thought
of angels, the thought being deeper, no time, no interval, no slowness of
deliberation, but having transferred his whole self into the child. "Today I
have begotten you."
16 "And why did the nations rage and the peoples imagine vain things," when
the Lord sojourned to renew all things, that the unordered world might again
be ordered from above? "And why did the nations rage?" Having willingly chosen
the form from the body, he hastened, having sought the wandering sheep.
17 "And why did the nations rage," when the King is present to master the
rebel, that he might free man from the first corruption? "And why did the
nations rage," when the shepherd is sojourning to stop a greedy throat, that
the serpent might not again bite the ancient side?
18 "And why did the nations rage?" Having woven on the loom of the womb the
garment of the Godhead, he clothed the world with a robe of incorruption. "And
why did the nations rage?" Above, a Son without a mother, and below a babe
without a father; for it was not that a man advanced into God, but God took up
a man.
19 "And why did the nations rage and the peoples imagine vain things?" The
Hellenic nations raged, the Jewish peoples imagined vain things. Why do you
rage and not shudder, and imagine vain things and do not consider that from a
virgin there has come forth a Son who is the Creator of her who bore him?
20 The law showed it beforehand and the prophets proclaimed it beforehand, and
such a mystery did not come about in silence. And you imagine "vain things
against the Lord and against his Christ."
21 If you were ignorant of the matter, O lawless Jew, you would justly also
seek the manner; did you not hear long ago Isaiah crying among the prophets:
"Hear now, house of David, is it a small thing for you to contend with men?
And how do you contend with the Lord? Therefore the Lord himself will give you
a sign: behold, the virgin shall conceive in the womb and bear a son, and they
shall call his name Emmanuel."
22 And you, covering over the truthful voice of the prophet, imagine "vain
things against the Christ"; behold, the virgin has given birth and again
remained a virgin, behold the virgin who contained in her womb, without
contention, the uncontained, behold the virgin who received the fire of the
Godhead in her arms and was not burned as a bush, behold the virgin who bore
as a pillar the pre-cosmic image of God and remained unshaken.
23 Behold the virgin who was "reared by angels," increased, sealed, betrothed,
guarded, sanctified, given in marriage, and enrolled in the house of God. "For
the birth of Jesus Christ was thus." Thus? How was the birth of Jesus Christ?
There was a certain virgin of the tribe of Judah, tracing the Davidic race, of
Joachim as father, of Anna as mother, from the village of Bethlehem, which is
interpreted house of bread.
24 The child was of royal race and was adorned with every virtue, preserving
the beauty from the Davidic root, a pure and inviolable field; she was being
reared nobly in the house of her parents, a palace in bloom, guarded by a
royal seal, a lamp bearing the vision of God, she shone upon the whole world,
she was looked upon as a lady, showing the dignity that is among women.
25 "Set apart in the temple of the Lord as a dove," she is betrothed to a holy
man, Joseph, who was near of kin to the Davidic race. Joseph therefore
receives the kinship of the betrothal and confessed the contract, having been
ignorant of the exchange that was to come.
26 For what does it say? "She was found with child from the Holy Spirit." And
Joseph her husband, being just, reckoned neither to examine, since it was a
cooperation of God, nor to make a public example because of the nobility and
surpassing quality of the maiden, nor to announce it to anyone because of the
power that had overshadowed, but "he wished to put her away secretly."
27 But an angel of the Lord appears in a dream saying to him: "Joseph, son of
David, why do you seek to put her away secretly, the ewe-lamb long ago
proclaimed beforehand by the prophets? Why do you seek to put her away
secretly, the inviolable field that has received a heavenly fruit? Why do you
seek to put her away secretly, her who contained without diminishing the fire
of the Godhead and who shows a presence of God strange beside that upon
Sinai?"
28 But "do not fear to take Mary your wife; for that which is begotten in her
is of the Holy Spirit; and she shall bear a son and you shall call his name
Jesus."
29 O Master, why should I not fear to take the virgin? Standing near I do not
dare to look upon the power that has overshadowed in her, and I should not
fear to take the virgin? She has been wholly transformed into flame; for she
bears in her womb him who burns the peaks of mountains in wrath and who arrays
the bodiless powers with flame; and I should not fear to take the virgin?
30 With a stern gaze she strikes those who look, and denying her race the
child has been transformed into some vision of God, and she bears another
impress of countenance; and I should not fear to take the virgin?
31 You mediated the birth to the virgin; you became an advocate of the
spectacle that is seen. You transferred the maiden into another likeness, you
gave a greeting and she conceived the propitiation, you said "Hail" and the
Word became flesh in her. And you, who among the powers above cannot look the
one who sent you in the face, say to me who lie in a material life: "Do not
fear to take" the virgin?
32 I serve the birth but I do not minister to the manner; I behold the light
but I do not search out the how; I compare the things that are according to me
but I do not examine the things above me; I groan at the bulk of the womb but
I also obey the bound of nature.
33 Shall I put her away? But it is not expedient. Shall I examine? But I
cannot endure it. Shall I search? But I shall not track it out. Shall I go to
the law? But I contradict the King who is declaring in her.
34 Why do you say that "do not fear to take Mary your wife"? What wife of
mine? one not yoked to a husband's yoke but shown forth as a temple of the
Master? You say my wife: why?
35 I am a servant of the virgin, O Master, and not a husband, a guard and not
a lord, a keeper of a deposit and not a spouse, a household servant and not a
bridegroom. If she flees into Egypt, I shall not be left behind; if she gives
birth, I stand by; she suckles and I am astonished. For from the sheer rock
milk of life gushes and she provides food to all.
36 From this holy virgin our Lord Jesus Christ came forth. To him be the glory
and the power unto the ages of ages. Amen.
