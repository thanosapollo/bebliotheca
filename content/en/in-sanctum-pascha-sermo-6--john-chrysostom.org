1.1 Already the sacred rays of the light of Christ are shining, and pure
luminaries of the pure Spirit are rising, and heavenly treasuries of glory and
of godhead have been opened. The long and dim night has been swallowed up, and
the unseen darkness has been consumed in it, and the unsmiling shadow of death
has been overshadowed. Life has been spread over all things, and the whole is
filled with insatiable light; risings of risings occupy the all, and he who is
before the morning star and the luminaries, immortal and abundant, great
Christ, blooms upon all things above the sun. 1.2 And therefore a long and
eternal and unquenchable bright day is at home among us, for all who have
believed in him, the mystical Pascha, served typically through the law, but
fulfilled in power through Christ; the wondrous Pascha, a marvel of divine
virtue and a work of power, truly a feast and an eternal memorial: from
passion, impassibility; from death, immortality; from deadness, life; from a
wound, healing; from a fall, resurrection; from a descent, an ascent. 1.3 Thus
God works great things; thus from the impossible he creates the paradoxical,
that it may be known that to him alone all things that he wills are possible.
2.1 Let Egypt, then, announce the types beforehand, and let the law interpret
in advance the images of the truth, an angel proclaiming the great sojourn of
a great king, where the Egyptian crowd of the firstborn dies, but the mystical
blood saves Israel. All these things are a shadow of the things to come, but
among us are the forms of the images and the fulfillments of the types, and
instead of the shadow the very exactness and confirmation of the truth. 2.2
Therefore the law went before, signifying in type the forms of the truth, and
the type came to be, but the truth was found. There a lamb from a flock; here
a lamb from the heavens. There the sign of blood and the little as a
phylactery of the whole; here the Word <and> the cup filled with divine blood
and Spirit. There a sheep from a fold; here the shepherd himself in place of
the sheep. 3.1 How then do the works not promise the universal salvation of
all things, of which even the types alone are saving? 3.2 Let the heavens of
the heavens keep feast, then, as the divine Spirit cries, recounting the glory
of God, first receiving the paternal risings of the divine Spirit. Let the
angels and archangels of the heavens keep feast also, and let every heavenly
people and army keep feast, seeing the commander of the army above arriving
bodily upon the world. Let the choirs of the stars keep feast also, announcing
him who rises before the morning star. Let the air keep feast, measured by
unmeasured depths and breadths. Let the salt water of the sea keep feast,
honored by sacred footprints and steps. Let the earth keep feast, washed with
divine blood. Let every human soul keep feast, refreshed into the new
regeneration through resurrection. 3.3 This is the Pascha, the common
panegyric of all things, the sending of the paternal will upon the world, the
divine rising of Christ about the earth, the everlasting feast of angels and
archangels, the immortal life of the whole world, a death-dealing blow to
death, the incorruptible food of men, and the heavenly soul of all things, a
sacred rite of heaven and earth, prophesying old and new mysteries, seen
visibly on earth, but understood in the heavens. 4.1 Therefore, being joined
to those initiated with sacred knowledge in the new and the old, such then, as
we have been able to say in brief, is the Pascha


public feast. And that we may also be feasted part by part with the discourse,
feeding not on earthly things but on the heavenly, let us too eat the rational
Pascha with spiritual desire, with which the Lord himself desired to eat with
us, saying: "With desire I have desired to eat the Pascha with you." Come
then, first taking it up in brief let us say what law is and what the need of
law, and why law after Egypt; second, what the Pascha is that sets out from
Egypt, and what the whole economy concerning the Pascha is, and what the all
is, and the mystery concerning the all of the Pascha. 5.2 And we shall first
set down the Scripture itself, that from comparison and from juxtaposition we
may see each of the contemplations. And the Lord said to Moses and Aaron in
the land of Egypt, saying: This month is for you the beginning of months; it
is first for you among the months of the year. Speak to all the congregation
of the sons of Israel, saying: On the tenth of this month let them take a
sheep according to the houses of families, a sheep according to a house. And
if they are few in the house so as not to be enough for the sheep, he shall
take with them the neighbor near him according to the number of souls; each
shall be numbered for a sheep what is sufficient for him. A sheep perfect, a
year old, shall it be for you; from the lambs and from the kids you shall take
it. And it shall be kept for you until the fourteenth of this month, and the
whole multitude of the congregation of the sons of Israel shall slay it toward
evening. And they shall take of the blood and put it upon the two doorposts
and upon the lintel and in the houses in which they eat in them. And they
shall eat the flesh in that night roasted with fire, and unleavened bread upon
bitter herbs they shall eat. You shall not eat of them raw nor boiled with
water, but roasted with fire, the head with the feet and the inward parts.
Nothing shall be left of them until morning; and a bone you shall not break of
it. And what remains until morning you shall burn with fire. And thus you
shall eat it: your loins girded, and your sandals on your feet, and your
staffs in your hands; and you shall eat it with haste. It is the Pascha of the
Lord. And I will pass through in the land of Egypt in this night and I will
strike every firstborn in the land of Egypt in this night from man to beast,
and in all the gods of the Egyptians I will execute vengeance; I am the Lord.
And the blood shall be for you for a sign upon the houses in which you are
there; and I will see the blood and I will cover you, and there shall not be
in you a plague to destroy, when I strike in the land of Egypt. And this day
shall be for you for a memorial, and you shall keep it a feast to the Lord
throughout your generations; a perpetual ordinance, you shall keep it. Seven
days you shall eat unleavened bread upon bitter herbs. And again the Lord said
to Moses and Aaron, saying: This is the law of the Pascha; every alien shall
not eat of it. Every house-servant of anyone and one bought with silver, you
shall circumcise him and then he shall eat of it. A sojourner and a hired man
shall not eat of it. In one house it shall be eaten; you shall not carry out
of that house of the flesh outside. And a bone you shall not break of it. All
the congregation of Israel shall do it. Every uncircumcised person shall not
eat of it. There shall be one law for the native and for the proselyte who
comes near among you. 6.1 Divine Scripture thus mystically foretold the sacred
feast, but we shall already examine each of the things read with accuracy,
searching by your prayers the hidden mysteries of the Scriptures, not
destroying the truth of the things written, but contemplating the exactness of
the mysteries through the types. 6.2 For the divine Spirit also commands Moses
to make the tabernacle "according to the pattern which I shall show you," it
says; thus what is seen typically is also understood mystically according to
the prototype and first-generated pattern. 6.3


Let us say first what this month is, the beginning of months, and why this
month of the Pascha is first among the months of the year; second, what the
sheep taken on the tenth of the month is, and the sheep perfect, a year old,
who the neighbor is and who the nearby one taken in, and why the sheep is kept
until the fourteenth, then sacrificed toward evening: "and the whole multitude
of the congregation of the sons of Israel shall slay it toward evening"; what
the blood upon the two doorposts and upon the lintel is, and why they eat the
flesh in the night, and this roasted with fire, neither raw nor boiled in
water; or what is "the head with the feet and the inward parts"; why "you
shall not break a bone"; and what the unleavened bread with bitter herbs is,
and why "you shall eat the Pascha with haste"; what the loins girded are and
the sandals on the feet and the staffs in the hands, and what is "the Pascha
of the Lord" and "the blood a sign upon the houses in which you are there, and
I will see the blood and I will cover you, and there shall not be in you a
plague to destroy, when I strike in the land of Egypt"; why "seven days you
shall eat unleavened bread"; why the alien shall not eat, but the homeborn
shall be circumcised; and what is "It shall be eaten in one house, and you
shall not carry out of the flesh outside, and a bone you shall not break of
it. No uncircumcised person shall eat of it. All the congregation shall do it"
- who is the congregation - "There shall be one law for the native and for the
proselyte who comes near; one and the same law upon both." 7.1 The types then
and the symbols and the mysteries, accomplished visibly in Israel, but
spiritually brought to completion among us. Having spoken a little about these
things, let us thus bring on the mysteries of the truth: what is the sojourn
of Jesus Christ after the law, and why he came even with a body; what the
Pascha is which he desired to eat with us, and why he who raised the dead by a
word did not also altogether rebuke death while living; and why he even
endured death at all through wood; what the thorn is with which he was
crowned, or what the vinegar which he drank and the gall, and what the opened
side from which blood and water flowed; why he prays that the cup pass by
which he came altogether to drink; who the robbers are who were hanged with
him, and who of the two is in paradise; why the spirit is entrusted into the
hands of the Father, but the body in the new tomb; what was that which would
be "in paradise" and is coming to be "today"; what the three days are which he
remained under the earth, and why the women first see him and he says as he
brings good news: "Women, rejoice." These are the foods of our sacred feast,
this the spiritual table, this the immortal food and delight. And having taken
as provision the bread from the heavens and drunk the cup of gladness, the
very boiling and fiery one, blood indeed typed from above by a warm Spirit,
taking it up in a word <let us say> first what law is and what the economy of
law, for thus we shall know from comparison what the Word is and what the
freedom of the Word. The law through Moses is a gathering of varied and
necessary doctrines, a collection for the common benefit of all the goods in
life, a mystical imitation of the citizenship in heaven, lamps and lampstands
and fire and lights, the honors of the lamp-bearing above. 9.2 The law through
Moses is the prefiguration of piety, the ordinance of well-ordered
citizenship, the hindrance of the first sin, the riddle of the coming truth.
9.3 The law through Moses is the chastisement of Egyptian error, written with
the finger of God, for otherwise the high arm was being kept. 9.4 The law
through Moses is a leader of piety, a guide of righteousness, illumination of
the blind, reproof of the foolish, a pedagogue of infants, a bond of the
ungrateful, a bridle of the stiff-necked, and a yoke of necessity for those
who pull unevenly. 9.5 The law through Moses is an angel of Christ, a
forerunner of Jesus, a herald and prophet of a great king, a wise school of
training,


a necessary gymnasium, a worldly school, a time-limited doctrine, a passing
mystery. 9.6 The law through Moses is a symbolic and riddling epitome of the
coming grace, announcing by images the perfection of the coming truth: by the
sacrifices the victim, by the blood the blood, by the lamb the lamb, by the
dove the dove, by the altars the high priest, by the temple the lodging of the
godhead, by the fire around the altar the whole light of the world from above.
10.1 The law, then, has been thus mystically predetermined for us. And the law
comes necessarily after Egypt, cutting out the invisible evil shoots of a soul
raving with matter and an egyptianizing heart, cutting like a plow the dense
and thick inner thorns of the thoughts, plowing and splitting in furrows the
depths of the soul, making a way before and making the soul new for the
reception of the heavenly divine Word and seed. Therefore reasonably the
divine Spirit cries: "Break up for yourselves fallow ground and do not sow
upon thorns." 10.2 Therefore also the Pascha mystically begins first from
Egypt, the first cutting-out of dark idolatry and the mystical amputation of
godless idol-madness and the nocturnal and just plague of the firstborn seeds
of error. 11.1 Egypt then also, as is confessed, was suffering the plague of
the firstborn, and every first-born people was dying, that the stiff-necked
Pharaoh, not having been disciplined even by the first plagues, might even
thus be disciplined slowly, and that Israel might even in these things be
saved paradoxically, and God be glorified in all. For Egypt, then, the passion
is in a plague, but for Israel the Pascha is in a feast; therefore it is also
named the feast, the Pascha of the Lord. Many other mysteries also of the
coming philanthropy of God; for the whole account of these things stands thus:
Egypt, the great and black, is an image of the dark and deep error, for from
there the first streams of deceit, calves and fishes and birds and beasts and
all such animals being made gods and being theologized. But when the just
wrath from the heavens is revealed and the great anger is spread over all the
earth, then first the superstitious and idol-mad error shall be struck: "For
in all the gods of the Egyptians," he says, "I will execute vengeance; I am
the Lord." And the plague is upon all the firstborn, since, claiming for
themselves the privileges of times, they had things older than all, and the
slaveries of idolatry. And the plague is dark and by night, for dark and
sullen and unsmiling and lightless and savage are the just-judgments of the
dark works and demons. "I will give," he says, "wonders in heaven and upon the
earth, blood, fire, vapor; the sun shall be turned into darkness and the moon
into blood before the great and manifest day of the Lord comes," and: "Not,"
he says, "those who desire the day of the Lord; why is this day of the Lord
for you? And it is darkness and not light. For as a man would flee from the
face of a lion and a bear would meet him, and he would enter into the house
and lean his hands upon the wall and a serpent would bite him, is not the day
of the Lord darkness and not light, and gloom, and there is no brightness in
it?" And "the blood for a sign" is the blooded mystery of the seal of Christ;
and "a sign," not yet the true itself, but a sign is a sign of coming truth.
For as many as have the sign through blood imprinted upon the souls as upon
the houses and at once anointed, all these the destroying plague shall pass
by. "For in which houses," he says, "if the blood is observed, I will cover
you, and there shall not be in you a plague to destroy, when I strike in the
land of Egypt." 15.2 "The blood as a sign," then, is a phylactery; and "in the
houses" as in the souls, for this is of the divine Spirit the from-faith
sacred


dwelling; and "I will cover you" are the unstinting applications of the
outstretched hands of Jesus which cover those who believe. 16.1 Such, then, is
the cosmic and universal mystery of the Pascha; but hear also, as it is
possible for us, as we recount the divine rite part by part. For how the truth
stands concerning these things, God knows, and the Word who ordered the sacred
Pascha in himself and through himself; but let pardon be given us as men, if,
beloved, we should truly go astray. Let us say, then, taking it up from the
beginning, first <what> this month is, a beginning of months, and why the
month of the Pascha is first among the months of the year. 17.2 The account of
the Hebrews spoken in secret things says that this is the time in which the
craftsman and creator God of all things then created the all, and that this is
the first flower of creation, the beauty of the world, when the creator saw
the all-wrought statue moving in due measure according to his own mind. And
they infer it from the good order of the heavens and the good mixings of the
seasons and the good laws of the sun and the risings of light not being
diminished, and also from the putting-forth of fruits and the impulses of
plants and the well-flowering up-runs of trees and the births of flocks
already being born, when already all the earth bears green, and trees also
wear flowers, pregnant toward the putting-forth of fruits, when a farmer,
having loosed a pair from the plow, has rested the bellowing team and, having
cast the divine fruits upon the earth, awaits the heavenly springs from above,
when a shepherd already milks white milk from the flocks and beekeepers strike
the sweet monthly waxes, when a sailor already, gladdened, grows bold against
the sea and by profit-loving arts dares the grey-green wave; that this good
law of all things and the good order of the all and, as one might say, this
good fortune is the first first-fruits, <and> that the beginning of the year
is the sweet pleasure of spring. 17.3 But I do not disbelieve these accounts
either, yet I think, which I have believed even more, that because of the
spiritual feast of the Pascha this month of the Pascha has been held as
beginning and head and first hegemony of all time and age, in which this great
mystery is brought to completion and is consecrated, that as the Lord of all
things both intelligible and invisible is first-born and first-begotten from
the beginning, so also this month honored with the sacred rite has been
generated first of the year and a beginning of every age. 17.4 And this year
is that which the divine Scripture cries: "To proclaim an acceptable year of
the Lord." 18.1 The sheep is the sacred victim. For "he was led as a sheep to
slaughter," it says, "and as a lamb before its shearer is dumb," and John:
"Behold the lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world." And the sheep is
perfect and a year old: perfect as from the heavens, a year old as on earth;
for a year is a measure of time on earth, taking itself up and cycling back
into itself and by the circuit of the period into itself imitating the
unmeasured age. And it is taken on the tenth of the month, and most
symbolically: for the law is an interval of the gospel, and the most summit
doctrine of the law is the Decalogue; and after the ten commandments of the
law then comes the mystical sheep from the heavens. And it is kept the
intervening days, the Scripture hinting at the sojourn of the Pascha in the
body in the midst of time until the passion, and the arrest in which, having
been arrested in those of the high priest, he was being kept. And the nearby
one taken in for the sheep, the neighbor, is I; for by you, O Israel, the
sheep was not contained. Then the sheep is sacrificed toward evening; for also
at the settings of the sun the sacred lamb of God


is slain. And the whole multitude of the congregation of the sons of Israel
shall slay it; for unbelieving Israel has become guilty of that precious
blood, the one of old slaying, the one until now not believing. Therefore the
divine Spirit testifies against them and cries saying: "Your hands are full of
blood." And the blood upon the lintel as upon the Church, and upon the two
doorposts as upon the two peoples. 25.2 For the Savior does not deny that he
was sent first to you, O Israel: "For I was not sent," he says, "to you, but
to the lost sheep of the house of Israel"; but I as a little dog was sitting
by a foreign table, not yet able to eat the bread, but gathering the crumbs
that fall, a parasite of foreign food; but since you did not know the manna
from the heavens, the bread was transferred to me through faith, and the one
who was a dog becoming already a son [...]. 26.1 And the flesh is eaten in the
night; for the light of the world set upon the great body of Christ: "Take,
eat, this is my body." And the flesh roasted with fire; for the rational body
of Christ is fiery: "I came to cast fire upon the earth, and what would I, if
it were already kindled?" The flesh is not raw, that the Word may be easily
worked and well-offered and well-received; nor boiled in water, that the Word
may not be moist nor watery nor dissolved. The head with the feet and the
inward parts, [...] as invisible counsel, feet as man. 29.2 Head with the feet
and the inward parts, beginning and middle and end, holding together and
binding and gluing all things in himself with unbreakable bonds, having become
truly "mediator of God and men." 29.3 Head with the feet and the inward parts,
God, Word, and man on earth. 29.4 Head with the feet and the inward parts, in
height and depth and breadth <**> and the rootings of earth. 29.5 Head with
the feet and the inward parts, law and Word and apostles, law as beginning and
Word as counsel and apostles as feet: "How beautiful are the feet of those who
bring good news of good things." 30.1 And a bone you shall not break of it,
that the resurrection with a body may be known. For "put," he says, "your
fingers into the marks of the nails, that you may know that a spirit does not
have flesh and bones." You shall eat unleavened bread with bitter herbs; for
bitter to you, or the mysteries of bitter works: "Your land is desolate, your
cities burned with fire, your country strangers devour before you, and it has
been made desolate, overthrown by foreign peoples." You shall eat the Pascha
with haste; for it is necessary that everyone about to approach the great body
be watchful and sober. Your loins girded; let the loosed springs through
generation cease, <the> entwinings of fleshes and the filthy pleasures. Your
sandals on your feet: ways cutting straight, supports unlimping; for they are
rootings of truth. 34.2 Your sandals on your feet, which Moses loosed, but
Jesus put on. For "loose," he says, "the sandal of your feet," that the
passing of the law may be shown, "whose thong of his sandals I am not worthy
to loose," that the Word girt round from every side may be indicated. 35.1
Your staffs in your hands, the signs of the divine power, the stays of
rational strength, the rod through Moses, the rod through Aaron, the almond
rod, the rod that cut the depths of the sea, <the rod> that sweetened the
bitterness of springs, the rod upon which the seven holy spirits of God
rested: "A spirit of wisdom and understanding,


a spirit of counsel and strength, <a spirit of knowledge and piety>, a spirit
of fear of God shall fill him." And the Pascha is the Pascha of the Lord. What
clearer than this did the Spirit cry out, that Pascha is not a type, not a
history, not a shadow, but Pascha truly of the Lord? And the blood for a sign,
and a sign of the coming truth, the first-type of the true Spirit, the
imitation of the great chrism. And I will see the blood and I will cover you;
you covered truly, Jesus, from the great destruction, and you stretched out
paternal hands, you hid us within your paternal wings, having made a spring of
divine blood upon earth by your blooded and philanthropic eagernesses, and you
led the threats of wrath away from us, and you gave back to us from above the
first reconciliations. Let those then eat unleavened bread seven days,
rehearsing the hebdomadal period upon the world and eating the [earthly]
lawful food upon earth; "but our Pascha has been sacrificed, Christ," and we
have received the new lump of his sacred mixture, all of us leavened again and
kneaded again by a stronger power with his Spirit. For I myself <**> the very
kingdom of the heavens, having leavened analogously three measures of fine
flour with the mixed leaven. Therefore from this Pascha the alien shall not
eat, for I do not cast pearls to swine, I do not give the holy to the dogs,
but the one who of old was a slave in sins shall have the heart circumcised,
and having been freed from the bitter slavery he shall thus with nobility
approach the mystery and then with freedom eat the Pascha freely. "For Christ
has redeemed us from" slavery and "the curse, having become a curse for us."
It shall be eaten in one house and you shall not carry the flesh outside, for
there is one congregation and there is one house, that is, the one Church, in
which the sacred body of Christ is eaten, and therefore outside the one house
- Church - the flesh shall not be cast out, and the one eating elsewhere shall
be punished as impious and a thief. There shall be one law for the free man
and the proselyte; where Christ is, freedom, equality of speech of all,
equality of law, equality of honor; all were bought with precious blood.
Therefore "you are no longer a slave," no longer a Jew, but free; for all of
us have become free in Christ. Hear then, hear after the types and the legal
economy what and how great is the sojourn of Christ upon earth after <the
law>. What is the sojourn of Christ? Flight from slavery, deliverance from old
necessity, beginning of freedom, honor of adoption, a spring of remission of
sins, truly immortal life in all. Seeing us from above tyrannized by death and
being loosed by bonds of corruption and at once bound, flowing into the
necessary and unreturning road, having come and taken <**> of the first
formation in paternal counsels, he did not entrust the liturgy on our behalf
to angels, not to archangels, but the Word himself took up the whole contest
on our behalf, obeying the paternal commandments. 45.2 And since unmixed
divine Spirit is unapproachable to all things, that all things might not
suffer together with the unmixed in-castings of the Spirit, he himself
willingly contracted himself in himself and gathering and bringing together
into himself the whole greatness of the godhead, he came as great as he
willed, not being diminished in himself nor lessened nor being spent in the
glory; and by excess of paternal power, what he had he did not lose, but
taking on what he did not have, he came as great as he was about to be
contained. 45.3 And since it was necessary that there also come to be some
vessel of the divine Spirit, that the first ones of human nature and essence
might share <**> having filtered and numbered together, having scummed off and
separated what was excessive and turbid, and having shone around and flashed
around and beamed around and fired and prepared as a bridal chamber and made
virgin and, so to speak, made angelic what was pure and translucent and
unmixed, he was formed bodily into the human


image, having the rising spiritual, but taking on the formation bodily. 45.4
Therefore also most mystically Scripture marked him: "Behold," it says, "a
man, East is his name," east as in spirit, but man as in body. "For the Spirit
of the Lord shall come upon you and the power of the Most High shall
overshadow you; therefore also the holy thing born shall be called son of the
Most High." And the generation being paradoxical and divine, reasonably the
Spirit also has marveled saying: "Who shall declare his generation?" 46.1 And
there being four leading names of the godhead, through which the divine Spirit
is especially believed, of lordship and of godhead, of sonship and of eternal
kingship, see that he was honored to take not only these virtues and glories.
And first see his Lord. "The Lord said to my Lord: sit at my right hand." You
see of him the things from the Lord, the Lord; see also his Son: "He shall
call upon me, Father, and I will set him firstborn," and: "You are my Son,
today I have begotten you; ask of me and I will give you nations as your
inheritance." You see his Son and firstborn and only-begotten; see also the
God: "Tall men shall pass over upon you and in you they shall pray and in you
they shall worship and they shall follow bound in fetters, for you are God,
because God is in you." You have seen his God; see also his eternal King:
"Your throne, O God, is unto the age of the age; a rod of uprightness is the
rod of your kingdom; you loved righteousness and hated lawlessness. Therefore
God, your God, has anointed you with the oil of gladness beyond your fellows."
You have seen his King; see with the King also the Lord of the powers: "Lift
up gates, you rulers, and be lifted up, eternal gates, and the King of glory
shall enter. Who is this King of glory? The Lord of the powers, he is the King
of glory." You see of him with the King also the Lord of the powers; see from
those remaining also his eternal high priest: "The Lord has sworn and will not
repent; you are a priest unto the age." 46.2 And if he is both Lord and God
and Son and King and Lord of the powers and eternal high priest, when he wills
"he is also man, and who shall know him?" And the "and man" makes clear also
that he is also God preexistently. And that he may not seem according to the
opinions of men to have sojourned upon the earth as an automatic phantom and
without generation, a spirit, hear also of him the child having come to be:
"Hear now, house of David; is it a small thing for you to give a contest to
men, and how do you give a contest to the Lord? The Lord himself gives you a
sign: behold, the virgin shall conceive in the womb and bear a son and they
shall call his name Emmanuel," and: "I would they had been burned with fire;
because a child has been born to us, whose government is upon his shoulder,
and his name is called messenger of great counsel, wonderful counselor, God
who has authority, ruler of peace, father of the age to come." 47.1 God then
and man this great Jesus sojourned among us, let no one disbelieve, and a
leading Spirit was contained by a human body. 47.2 For Adam still being earth
and clay also contained the first mystical breath of the Father, for the
divine Spirit is not harmed by having been joined to the body by the will of
God; and if the first dust contained the sacred Spirit, an ensouled body
contained the immortal life of Christ. 47.3 If then Spirit alone had
