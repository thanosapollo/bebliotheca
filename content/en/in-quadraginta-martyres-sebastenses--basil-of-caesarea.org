Homily 19.
On the holy forty martyrs.
What satiety of the memory of martyrs could there be for the lover of martyrs?
Because honor toward the good among fellow-servants has proof of goodwill
toward the common Master. For it is clear that he who welcomes the noble men
will not fall short of imitation in like seasons. Bless genuinely the one who
has borne witness, that you may become a martyr by purpose, and may go forth
without persecution, without fire, without scourges, counted worthy of the
same rewards as they. For us it is not one man set forth to admire, nor two
only, nor does the number of those being blessed proceed as far as ten; but
forty men, as having one soul in divided bodies, in one consent and concord of
the faith, displayed both one endurance toward the terrible things, and one
standing-fast for the truth. All like one another, equal in resolve, equal in
the contest. Therefore they were also counted worthy of equal-honored crowns
of glory.

What speech then could attain to their worth? Not even forty tongues would
have sufficed to hymn the virtue of so many men. And yet, even if the one
being admired were one, it would have sufficed to throw down the power of our
words, not to mention so great a multitude, a military phalanx, a system hard
to contend against, likewise unconquered in wars and unapproachable in
praises. Come then, bringing them into the midst through the remembrance, let
us establish the benefit from them as common to those present, having
displayed beforehand to all, as in a painting, the men's deeds of valor. Since
both historians often, and painters, =31.509= signify the exploits of wars,
the ones adorning them with speech, the others engraving them on tablets, and
both have stirred many toward manhood. For what the speech of history sets
beside us through hearing, these things painting, being silent, shows through
imitation. So then let us also remind those present of the virtue of the men,
and as it were bringing their deeds before their eyes, let us move toward
imitation those who are more noble and more akin to them in purpose. For this
is the encomium of martyrs, the exhortation of those assembled toward virtue.
For the speeches concerning the saints do not even accept being enslaved to
the laws of encomia. Because those who eulogize take the beginnings of the
eulogies from the starting-points of the world; but those to whom the world
has been crucified, how can anything in it furnish a starting-point toward
distinction?

The saints did not have one fatherland; for each had set out from a different
place. What then? Shall we call them without a city, or citizens of the
inhabited world? For just as in the joint contributions of a common feast the
things paid in by each become common to those who have contributed; so also in
the case of these blessed ones each one's fatherland is common to all; and all
are from everywhere, exchanging with one another the lands that bore them. Or
rather, why must we seek around for the ones that lie on the ground, when it
is possible to consider what their city now is? The city of martyrs then is
the city of God; of which God is craftsman and maker, the Jerusalem above, the
free, the mother of Paul and of those like him. As for race, the human is
different for each; but the spiritual is one of all.

For God is their common father, and all are brothers, not born from one man
and one woman, but fitted together to one another into the concord through
love from the adoption of the Spirit. A ready chorus, a great addition of
those who from of old


glorify the Lord, not gathered one by one, but translated all at once. What
then was the manner of the translation? These, having excelled all of their
own in greatness of body, and prime of age, and power, were appointed to be
enrolled in the military lists; and through experience in war and courage of
soul they already had the first honors from emperors, being famous among all
through virtue. But when that godless and impious proclamation was published,
not to confess Christ, or to expect dangers =31.512=; and every kind of
punishment was threatened, and much and beastlike wrath had been stirred by
the judges of injustice against those who were pious; and plots and deceits
were stitched together against them, and varied kinds of tortures were
devised, and those who maltreated were inexorable, the fire ready, the sword
sharpened, the cross had been planted; the pit, the wheel, the scourges; some
fled, some yielded, some were shaken; and some, before the trial, were struck
down by the threat alone; others, having come near the terrible things, grew
dizzy; others, having entered the contests, then being unable to last through
to the end of the toils, giving up somewhere around the middle of the
contest, like those storm-tossed at sea, shipwrecked even the cargo of
endurance they already had; then these, the unconquered and noble soldiers of
Christ, coming forward into the midst, while the ruler was displaying the
emperor's letters and demanding obedience, with a free voice, boldly and
manfully, having cowered at none of the things seen, nor been struck down by
the things threatened, proclaimed themselves Christians. O blessed tongues,
as many as sent forth that holy voice, which the air receiving was
sanctified, and angels hearing applauded; and the devil with demons was
wounded; and the Lord enrolled it in heaven.

Each then, coming forward into the midst, said: I am a Christian. And just as
in the stadia those who come forward for contest both speak their own names
together and pass over to the place of the struggle; so also these then,
having cast away the appellations assigned them from birth, each proclaimed
himself from the common Savior. And all did this, the next joining himself to
the one who had gone before. So that there came to be one appellation of all;
for no longer so-and-so, or so-and-so, but all were proclaimed Christians.
What then did the one then in power? For he was terrible and varied, to steal
upon some with flatteries, and to turn others aside with threats. First he
bewitched them with flatteries, trying to unstring the tension of piety. Do
not betray your youth; nor exchange an untimely death for this sweet life. For
it is out of place that those accustomed to excel in wars should die the death
of evildoers. Besides these things he promised money. And he was giving honors
from the emperor, and distributions of dignities =31.513=, and by countless
devices he outgeneraled them. But when they did not yield to this trial, he
passed over to the other kind of engine. He held over them blows, and deaths,
and trial of incurable punishments. And he such things; but of what sort were
those of the martyrs? Why, he says, do you bait us, O fighter against God, to
stand away from the living God, and to serve destructive demons, holding out
to us your goods? What so great a thing do you give, as you are eager to take
away? I hate a gift that is the procurer of loss; I do not receive an honor
that is mother of dishonor. You give money that remains behind, glory that
withers. You make us known to a king, but you alienate us from the King who
is.

Why do you stingily hold out a few of the things from the world? The whole
world has been despised by us. The things seen are not equal in worth to the
hope we desire. Do you see this heaven, how fair to behold, how great? and the
earth, how large? and the wonders in it? None of these is equal to the
blessedness of the righteous. For these


pass away; but ours remain. I desire one gift, the crown of righteousness; I
am set on one glory, that in the kingdom of the heavens. I am ambitious for
the honor above; I fear the punishment in Gehenna. That fire is fearful to
me; but the one threatened by you is a fellow-servant. It knows to revere
those who despise idols. I reckon your blows the arrows of infants. For you
strike a body; which, if it hold out longer, is crowned more brightly, but if
it give out sooner, it is gone, having been delivered from judges so violent,
who, having received the service of bodies, still contend to rule over souls
as well; who, unless you even be preferred to our God, as having been
insulted by us with the last insults, grow angry, and hold over us these
fearful punishments, bringing piety as a charge against us. But you will not
meet cowards, nor lovers of life, nor men easily struck down, for the sake of
the love toward God. We here are ready both to be wheeled, and to be racked,
and to be burned up, and to receive every kind of instruments of torture. And
when that boastful and barbarian man heard these things, not bearing the
men's frankness, boiling over with wrath, he considered what engine he might
find, so as to make their death both long and bitter at once. He found then a
device, and see how harsh. For having surveyed the nature of the country,
that it is icy, and the season of the year, that it is wintry, having watched
for a night in which the terrible thing is especially intensified =31.516=,
and besides then a north wind blowing upon it, he commanded that all, having
been stripped, should die frozen under the open sky in the middle of the
city.

And you who have been tried by winter know well how unbearable is this kind of
torture. For it is not possible to show it to others except to those who have
the examples of the things said already laid up from the experience itself.
For a body that has fallen beside frost first is all livid, the blood being
frozen; then it is shaken and tossed, the teeth chattering, the sinews being
convulsed, and the whole bulk being drawn together involuntarily. And a
certain sharp pain, and an unspeakable toil reaching even the marrows, makes
the sensation most hard to bear for those being frozen. Then it is mutilated,
as if by fire, the extremities being burned. For the heat being driven away
from the limits of the body, and fleeing together toward the depth, leaves
dead the parts from which it has withdrawn, and gives pains to the parts into
which it is crowded, death approaching little by little through the freezing.
Then therefore they were condemned to pass the night in the open, when the
lake, around which the city is settled, in which the saints contended these
things, was as some horse-driving plain, the ice having transformed it; and
having been made mainland by the frost, it granted those who dwelt around to
walk on foot safely over its back; and rivers ever-flowing, bound by the ice,
stood still from their streams; and the soft nature of water was transformed
toward the resistance of stones; and the sharp blasts of the north wind drove
every living thing toward death. Then therefore, having heard the command (and
consider here for me the men's unconqueredness), with joy each having thrown
off even the last tunic, they went toward the death through the frost,
exhorting one another as in a seizure of spoils.

=31.517= For we are not, he says, stripping off a garment, but we are putting
off the old man, who is being corrupted according to the desires of deceit. We
thank you, Lord, casting off sin together with this garment. Since we put it
on because of the serpent, let us put it off because of Christ. Let us not
cling to garments because of the paradise we lost. What shall we render to the
Lord? Our Lord also was stripped. What great thing is it for a slave to suffer
the things of the Master? Or rather, we ourselves are the ones who stripped
even the Lord. For that was the soldiers' daring; those men stripped him and
divided the garments.


Let us then wipe out through ourselves an accusation written up against us.
Sharp is the winter, but sweet is paradise; painful is the freezing, but
pleasant is the rest. Let us wait a little, and the bosom of the patriarch
will warm us. Let us exchange one night for a whole age. Let the foot be
burned, that it may dance continually with angels; let the hand fall away,
that it may have frankness to be raised toward the Master. How many of our
soldiers have fallen in array, keeping faith to a perishable king? and shall
we not give up this life for the faith toward the true King? How many have
undergone the death of evildoers, having been caught in injustices? and shall
we not bear death for righteousness? Let us not turn aside, O
fellow-soldiers, let us not give our backs to the devil. They are flesh, let
us not spare; since we must in any case die, let us die that we may live. Let
our sacrifice be before you, Lord; and may we be received, as a living
sacrifice, well-pleasing to you, being wholly burned by this frost, fair is
the offering, a new whole-burnt-offering, not through fire, but through frost
being wholly fruit-offered.

Giving these exhortations to one another, and each charging another, as
fulfilling some outpost in war, they passed the night, bearing the present
things nobly, rejoicing in the things hoped for, laughing at the adversary.
And there was =31.520= one prayer of all. We forty have entered the stadium,
may we forty be crowned, Master. Let not even one be lacking to the number. It
is precious, that which you honored with the fast of the forty days, through
which law-giving entered into the world. For forty days Elijah, seeking the
Lord in fasting, obtained the vision. And their prayer was of this sort; but
one of the number, having given way toward the terrible things, having
deserted, went off, leaving an unspeakable mourning to the saints. But the
Lord did not allow their petitions to become incomplete. For the one entrusted
with the guard of the martyrs, being warmed nearby from some bath-house, was
watching what was to come, ready to receive those of the soldiers who fled for
refuge. For this too, that there be a bath nearby, was devised, promising
swift help to those who changed. Which thing, however, was wickedly devised by
the opponents, to find such a place of the contest, in which the readiness of
the consolation was going to unstring the standing-fast of those competing;
this showed the endurance of the martyrs more brightly. For it is not the one
lacking the necessities who is enduring, but the one who in abundance of
enjoyment holds out in the terrible things.

And as they were competing, and he was watching what would come out, he saw a
strange spectacle, certain powers coming down from the heavens, and as it were
distributing great gifts from a king to the soldiers; which divided the gifts
to all the others, but left one only unrewarded, having judged him unworthy of
the heavenly honors; who at once, having given out toward the toils, deserted
to the opponents. A pitiable spectacle to the righteous, the soldier a
fugitive, the champion a captive, the sheep of Christ taken by a wild beast;
and what is more pitiable, that he both missed the eternal life, and did not
even enjoy this one, his flesh being dissolved at once in the assault of the
heat. And the lover of life fell, having broken the law for nothing; but the
executioner, when he saw him turning aside and running off to the bath, set
himself in the place of the deserter, and having thrown off the coverings,
mixed himself among the naked, crying the same cry as the saints: I am a
Christian. And having struck those present by the suddenness of the change, he
both filled up the number, and by the addition from himself consoled the grief
over the one who had grown soft, imitating those in array, who, when the one
at the first shield has fallen, at once fill up the phalanx, so that the
locked shields may not be broken for them by the one who is lacking. Something
of this sort he also did.


He saw the heavenly wonders, he recognized the =31.521= truth, he fled for
refuge to the Master, he was numbered with the martyrs. He renewed the things
of the disciples. Judas went away, and Matthias was brought in instead. He
became an imitator of Paul, yesterday a persecutor, today proclaiming the
good news. He too had the calling from above, "Not from men, nor through a
man." He believed in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ; he was baptized into
him, not by another, but by his own faith; not in water, but in his own
blood. And so, as day was beginning, still breathing they were given over to
the fire, and the remnants of the fire were thrown into the river; so that
the contest of the blessed might pass through all of creation. They competed
on the earth, they held out in the air, they were given over to the fire, the
water received them. Theirs is the voice: "We went through fire and water,
and you led us out into refreshment." These are they who have taken hold of
our country, as some continuous towers, furnishing safety from the inroad of
the opponents, not having shut themselves up in one place, but already having
been received as guests in many districts, and having adorned many
fatherlands. And the paradox, they do not visit those who receive them having
been divided one by one; but having been mixed with one another, they dance
unitedly.

O the wonder! They neither fall short in the number, nor admit a surplus. If
you divide them into a hundred, they do not go out of their own number; if you
gather them into one, they remain forty even so, according to the nature of
fire. For that both passes over to the one who kindles it, and is whole with
the one who has it; and the forty both are all together, and all are with
each. The ungrudging benefaction, the grace that is not spent; ready help for
Christians, a church of martyrs, an army of trophy-bearers, a chorus of those
who glorify. How much would you have toiled, that you might somewhere find
=31.524= one who intercedes for you with the Lord? They are forty, sending up
a harmonious prayer. Where two or three are gathered in the name of the Lord,
there he is in their midst. But where there are forty, who doubts the presence
of God? The one in affliction takes refuge with the forty, the one who is
gladdened runs off to them; the one, that he may find release from the hard
things; the other, that the better things may be kept for him. Here a pious
woman is found praying for children, asking the return for a husband abroad,
salvation for one who is sick. Let your petitions come to be with martyrs. Let
the young men imitate their age-mates; let the fathers pray to be fathers of
such children; let the mothers be taught the narrative of a fair mother.

The mother of one of those blessed men, having seen the others already
perfected by the frost, but her own son still breathing from both strength and
endurance toward the terrible things, the executioners leaving him as being
able at least to change his mind, she herself having taken him up with her own
hands, set him on the wagon, on which the rest lying together were being led
to the pyre, truly a martyr's mother. For she did not let fall an ignoble
tear; nor did she utter anything low and unworthy of the season; but, Go, she
says, O child, the good journey with your age-mates, with your
tent-companions; do not be left behind from the chorus; do not appear second
to the others to the Master. Truly a good shoot of a good root. The noble
mother showed that she had reared him with doctrines of piety rather than with
milk. And he who was thus reared was thus sent forth by a pious mother; but
the devil went away put to shame. For having moved all creation against them,
he found all things worsted by the virtue of the men, a night of harsh wind, a
wintry fatherland, the season of the year, the stripping of the bodies. O holy
chorus! O sacred array! O unbroken locking of shields! O common guardians of
the race of men! good partners of cares, fellow-workers of petition, most
powerful ambassadors, stars of the inhabited world, flowers of the


Churches. The earth did not hide you, but heaven received you; the gates of
paradise were opened to you. A worthy spectacle for the army of the angels,
worthy for patriarchs, prophets, righteous men, men in the very flower of
youth, having despised life, having loved the Lord above parents, above
children. Being at the very =31.525= prime of age, they overlooked
the temporary life, that they might glorify God in their own members; having
become a theater to the world, and to angels, and to men, they raised those
who had fallen, they confirmed those in doubt, they doubled the desire of
the pious. Having all set up one trophy for piety, they were also adorned
with the one crown of righteousness, in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom is the
glory and the power unto the ages of ages. Amen.
