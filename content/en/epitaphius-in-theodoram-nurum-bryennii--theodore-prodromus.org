Epitaphs for the wife of the son of the most fortunate Caesar kyr Nikephoros
Bryennios, kyra Theodora
I do not know how I shall now send the speech on its way, and how I might run
the course set before me, and what I am to become amid the present fortunes.
If I gaze into the abysses of the suffering and consider the loss that has
gone before, I measure out other abysses of lamentations, I mix the cup of the
speech with tears, I weave a narrative of wailing, and I am stirred toward
groans from the deep. If I look upon the blessed end of her who has now died
and left this life, I leap with the very ranks on high, and my heart rejoices
in safety and applauds with the hosts above, who make it a common feast and
joy to receive the radiance of so great a soul. I am split, then, and divided
in the middle, and a balance of gladness and of tears tilts me continually
from that side and from this. For when from the beginning even to the end
itself I go over and run through your fortunes, O ornament of womankind,
hearth of good things and truly a great gift of God the Word, a heroic garden
brought you forth and a royal root sprouted you, the fair and seemly
cultivated vine; this one good was the dignity of your birth, but the
world-leading arm of Providence transplanted you and carried you from a
fortunate land into one three times more fortunate and rooted you in golden
gardens, those most imperial gardens, in which the lily blossomed beyond
number, and the whiteness of the roses was boundless, and much ivy crept, and
how great was the bindweed; and it gave you as fair gardeners both her who is
foremost among empresses, Irene all-famed among women, the most yawning abyss
toward gifts, who crowned the very crown with virtues, mistress of the
passions, not of the earth only, and her who is from her and after her in
manners, the Doukas boast, wise Anna, mind itself in person, the hearth of the
Graces, the white-and-red rose blooming in purple, even if now a black calyx
covers it, of my master (stand for me, flood of tears), of my master (do not
break for me, heart), of my master (endure, wretched breast), of my master the
Caesar's consort. Toward these women directing all your life and adding
imitation to nature you were well cultivated and grew together, a young shoot
running up unbent, as a tree-vine, as a young cypress, and to the all-fair
branch sprung from them, bursting with much grace from every side, from name
and form and reverent manner, you were grafted by the bonds of marital laws.
And from this you sprouted a young fruit, heavy on every side with forms of
good things and most of all with the fair name of the grandfather. Such then
were these things of yours; but the fortunes that followed, what bronze mind
or iron mouth will utter them without groans and tears? For he who had come to
your lawful bed, John fair in all things and sweet, was going abroad again
beyond the Halys, campaigning with his mother's brother the emperor, all
iron-clad as a horse-archer and hammer-wrought even to the insteps, and he was
helping to overthrow the cities of the barbarians; but a host of barbarian
diseases was hastening to take you wholly, his city, now kindling whole
furnaces of blasting fevers and burning you, now filling you with successive
shiverings and chilling you like crystal; from which most of your limbs flowed
away, melted in the eighth circuit of the moon, in all of which the disease
was unceasing and the affliction stood fixed upon you, as one thrown into a
river of Tartarus and from there led into the craters of Etna; for the end of
the burning was the beginning of shivering, and the end of the shivering was
the burning again. And in the midst of the cold and of the heat

a temperate springtime grace came forth, neither flaming and fiery as summer,
nor shivering and icy as cold, but mixed of both evenly and well; but you were
burned with the fierce summer of fevers, and you froze also with the cold of
paroxysms, and you had no toil-loosing spring in the middle; rather, that I
may speak more exactly, you had autumn in the midst of these, terribly wasting
away all your little flesh. And for others wholly dead the body is thus both
undone and flows away; but you, still breathing and present to life, were
assigning your dust beforehand to kindred earth. O all-great mind, angelic
heart, you saw your whole body flowing away and yourself buried the greater
part of your limbs, and no womanish word was sent forth from you, nor did the
disease even soften you, nor, with your reasoning checked by the suffering,
did you let go thanksgiving toward God, but you set against them nobly and
all-wisely: against the fevers, hope toward Him, being for you another dew of
Hermon; and against the shiverings, in turn, desire for Him, which you had
reared hot as a flame of fire. One thing was the grieving, one the biting and
afflicting and laying the great mind on its back and turning it: that the
sweet consort was not present, nor therefore in the last things both to be
seen and to see, and a kindred flood of kindred tears to flow as from kindred
springs. Whence you earnestly entreated the Divine to grant so long a term of
days, to award you so long an addition of life, until you should see the sight
of your consort, that you might lay down the breath with this one alone, whom
you breathed beyond the air itself. And the things came out for you according
to prayer; for you saw him and were sweetly entwined, not with fingers, but
with the shapes of fingers, nor even with hands, but with the bones of hands,
nor even with flesh, but with the remnants of flesh; you wept, he wept in
return, he wailed back to you, clinging he filled the neck with tears, with
tearing he rent the golden hair and scratched the fair cheek for you, and in
what concerned himself he had already died before you. And these things are
mortal and the laws of mortals; but what is from here is as an imitation of
the angels themselves; you ask the holy habit of the monastics; you long for
the thing and longing you receive it, ah, with what heat of heart and with how
burning a brand of desire; and having kindled a bright lamp of the soul you
are led as a fellow-bride with the wise virgins, and having broken away from
the earthly bridegroom you are joined to the spiritual Bridegroom himself in a
new joining of mystical communion. Shall I say the greater? You do not even
come as far as a sight toward him, the consort below, that you may keep
unstained to the end both the fleshly marriage and the marriage in God; and
the awe-inspiring thing: in the midst of holy psalms you give the spirit to
God and the Bridegroom, yourself having closed the folds of the eyes, yourself
having decently composed the hands; rather, first you give forth the breath,
and then your mouth is silent of speaking. O blessed and revered end: you
lived after the manner of angels in the present life, you went out after the
manner of angels from the present life, with the angels now you have your
dwelling. O all-famed imperial hearth, in which the philanthropic head was of
old, let another call you a fortunate house; but I will declare you a place of
weeping; for you (I am silent now of the long labors and I speak the greatest
and the chief) first boast the imperial dust of the all-great all-ruling
Alexios and of the greatest empress Irene, of the Doukas-grown
myriad-sprouting branch, and the wise dust of the all-wise Caesar (if there is
any wise dust, and not dust only) and now the dust of the fair empress who is
a gift of God by her name, even if she was renamed near the end, fittingly
called Aikaterina. You are a floor of Hades and a corpse-bearing place and a
cold guardian of imperial bones. But O woman famed round about for beauty, and
more seemly still in the virtues and simply a dwelling of every good,
all-reverend bride of God, all-fair bride, ask the Divine earnestly and
entreat that He quench the coals of the labors for your consort, and from his

heart pluck out every thorn of grief, and assign to the child an untold course
of days and cycles of long life, so that the father, sweet John, seeing the
fair son Nikephoros, may look upon you who bore him as in an image.
