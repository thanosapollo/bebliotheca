On repentance. Sermon 3.
=60.705=
Let us set forth, beloved, the medicines of repentance, that we may run
to the harbor of salvation. We have been wounded by sin; let us be healed
by repentance. Through sin the devil wounded; through repentance Christ
healed. That one, through sin, laid down the roots; the Lord cut sin out
by the root with the axe of repentance. For behold, it says, the axe is
laid to every tree. Every tree therefore that does not bear good fruit is
cut down and thrown into the fire. The devil sowed sin as tares; Christ,
with the sickle of repentance, mowed down the tares-like sin. Sin is an
evil passion, but repentance is a good thing. Let us therefore flee the
fearful one, and cherish the longed-for one. Through sin the devil leads
into Gehenna; through repentance Christ leads by the hand into the
kingdom. Have you sinned? Be still; for it is not so terrible to have
sinned, but remaining in the sin is evil; for the one admits of a
defense, but the other carries off unpardonable punishment.

Since even Peter, having denied, sinned, and Paul, persecuting Christ,
transgressed the law, and David, having worked adultery and murder, did
things worthy of death. Yet even if they sinned as men, they repented as
saints; and if they fell unawares into the nets of sin, yet taking away
the blight of faithlessness from the eyes of the mind, they ran to
repentance, that through it they might carry off the forgiveness of their
offenses. Come then, let us too run to repentance. For even if the devil
has loaded us with sin, yet the Lord Jesus Christ, who was born of the
holy Virgin Mary, lightens us, taking upon himself through repentance the
burden of sin. For behold, it says, the Lamb of God who takes away the
sin of the world. For the devil laid sin upon men; the Lord took up the
sin from men. That one gave the sin, that on account of the sin he might
destroy man; the Lord took the sin, that he might set free the outrage of
human nature. Before the coming of Christ the devil was fearful; before
the cross, sin was unbearable; but the cross now has crushed the stings
of sin. Appearing upon earth, the Lord Jesus put the devil down like
lightning upon the field of the earth. For I saw, it says, Satan fallen
like lightning from heaven.

The death of Christ ruined the kingdom of death. For even if Death
reigned from Adam until Moses, even over those who had not sinned in the
likeness of the transgression of Adam, who is a type of the one to come;
yet after the death of the Lord, death has become a plaything even for
infants. Therefore all, after the resurrection of Christ, mocking the
pride of death, say: Where is your sting, O death? Where is your victory,
O Hades? Before the cross even the counsel of the serpent breathed death;
but after the cross, after the sufferings of Christ, behold, both old men
and women, lads and youths, as treaders of grapes in the wine-vat, so
they too tread upon serpents and scorpions and upon all the power of the
enemy. I have said these things, that I might show the fall of sin and
the power of repentance. Do not then sharpen against yourself the sting
of sin; let not your sloth become a whetstone for sin. You are not
condemned because you have sinned, but you are punished because, having
sinned, you did not repent. For just as the Lord, knowing the weakness of
your body, entrusted the medical art to men, and made the earth put forth
herbs suited to healing, so also, knowing the soul's liability to slip,
and the wounds of sin, he gave repentance as a safeguard of souls, and in
the divine and =60.706= spiritual books he set the occasions of
repentance as herbs, that each of us, having from there the plaster of
healing, might flee the passion, applying it to himself through faith.
But let not our repentance stop at words; for it is unseemly that
repentance should be woven in words, yet the Church of Jesus Christ our
Lord not be built up in deeds. Confess your offenses, O man; declare your
sin, that you may receive the forgiveness of your sin. When you declare
it, you receive the gift of the forgiveness of offenses. Let David come
as witness, who says: I said, I will declare against myself my
lawlessness to the Lord, and you forgave the impiety of my heart.

Now if you tell your offense, you find your pardon; for the present time
is a season of loving-kindness; but in the age to come, even if you
lament, you will lament in vain, and though you are remorseful you will
not enjoy the forgiveness. For in Hades who shall confess to you? And
even if you confess the offense then, you will profit yourself nothing.
For the rich man, seeing the want of Lazarus turned into abundance, and
his own substance fallen into boundless poverty, groaned, yet was not
pitied; he lamented and wailed, but did not enjoy consolation; he sought
mercy, but did not receive it, because he himself, when asked, was
unwilling to share. He was rich here, but poor there; here he puffed
great things over wealth, there he uttered pitiable things over poverty;
here he had torrents of wines and springs of myrrh, there, seeking even a
drop of water, he did not receive it, but flowed this way and that, being
melted by the immaterial fire. Let us too then run to repentance; for it
is a time of repentance. Let us quench the flame of sin with the dew of
repentance. Do not, giving heed to the long-suffering of God, acquire an
impenitent heart; let not the kindness of God harden your mind. For the
kindness of God gathers you and leads you to repentance. See then that
according to your hardness and impenitent heart you do not store up for
yourself wrath in the day of wrath and of the revelation and just
judgment of God, who will render to each according to his works. Have you
sinned? Repent; do not by sloth make the passion incurable for yourself.
Darkness is changed when light appears; and sin vanishes when repentance
is seen. Become a farmer of yourself: pluck out sin as tares, harvest
lawlessness as a thorn, cut out sin as a branch of the wild olive, as a
wild and fruitless plant. Demons rejoice when they see sin; angels exult
when they behold repentance. For the angels of God, it says, according to
the Lord's voice, rejoice over one sinner who repents.

Do not strip yourself, O man; do not throw away the full armor of the
Holy Spirit, lest you become easy prey for the enemies. With the shield
of repentance receive and shake off the arrows of desire. Fornication is
sweet, but harmful; chastity is harsh, but profitable. Fornication has
the nature of the body cooperating; chastity has the grace of the Spirit
working with it. Where then there is fornication, there the devil dwells;
but where there is chastity, there the Lord Jesus Christ rests. For this
reason the blessed Paul says: If anyone destroys the temple of God, God
will destroy him; and again he says: Shall I then take the members of
Christ and make them members of a harlot? May it not be. Let us then stay
the rush of sin with the anchor of repentance, lest the successive
contrary wind of desires prepare us to suffer shipwreck. But sailors
suffer shipwreck in waters, while sinners are sunk like lead in the river
of fire that flows before the judgment-seat of Christ, not with =60.707=
chariots and horses, but with reasonings and purposes. Let us then revere
the goodness of Christ, let us be ashamed at his sufferings, let us not
make his bruises vain, let us not prefer shameful pleasure to his good
pleasure and commandment, let us not repay the benefactor with evils, let
us not take his members and make them members of a harlot; for we are not
our own masters; we were bought with a price. And who sold us, or who
bought us? The devil sold us through sin; Christ redeemed us through
righteousness. And how did he buy us? By giving gold, or silver? No, but
his own blood, which is beyond gold and a precious stone of great worth;
for he bought all our sins. And they are many, and in varied and
different ways they trouble our salvation.

What sins? Those of the law; yet not because the law is sin, God forbid;
but because apart from the law I did not know sin =60.708=. Since then
the law made me, as a transgressor, liable to a curse, for this reason
Christ Jesus came and redeemed me from the curse of the law, having
become a curse for us. Let us not then prefer the common providence of
the devil to the economy of Christ; let us not, to please sin, run past
the beauty of repentance. If you fear the threat, take refuge in the
commandment; if you wish to escape the coming judgment, turn away from
evil, and do good; that is, put away the sin, and become a worker of
repentance, that he who does not will the death of sinners so much as
that they should turn and live, receiving the eagerness of your soul, may
grant you the forgiveness of sin. And we, for what has been said, send up
praise and glory to the Father and to the Son and to the Holy Spirit, now
and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
