HOMILY XI.
=63.523=
A homily of the same, spoken when another, a man exceedingly old, had spoken
first, concerning Eleazar and the seven boys.
αʹ. How fair is this spiritual olive tree! Though its branches have grown old,
it has borne us ripe fruit. For the plants of the earth are not such as the
trees of the Church. Those, when they have come to old age, then put off the
greater part of their leaves, and the fruit which they may bear, this they
bear both out of season and sparse; but these, when they have come to old age,
are then especially laden with fruit. Which indeed one may see also in the
case of him who has spoken today; wherefore I also resolved to keep silence.
For it was necessary, when so many old men were present and knew how to speak,
that the young man should keep quiet. Thus also the divine Scriptures instruct
us. For when they converse with a young man they say: Speak, young man, if
there is need of you, scarcely twice if you are asked; sum up many things in
few. But to the old man it does not converse with such limitations, but
leaving him it permits him to run even without such bounds; and again,
admiring the pleasure of the things said, whenever they are spoken with
understanding, it spoke thus: Speak, elder, for it becomes you, and with
accurate knowledge, and you will hinder
=63.524=
musical things. What is, And you will hinder musical things? It shows from
this that flute and lyre and pipes are not so sweet to those who hear as the
teaching of an old man brought forth with accurate knowledge. For comparing
pleasure with pleasure, it says that this is far more tyrannical than that,
and that the one prevails, and the other yields to it; wherefore it also says,
You will hinder musical things; that is, it will not let (read: you will not
let) them appear, you will overshadow them, you will throw them into shade. On
account of these things, then, we too ought to have been silent and to listen,
and this man to speak and teach; but what I have often said, this I will say
now also: I can bear neither your tyranny, nor these men's compulsion;
wherefore again I strip for the accustomed courses, handling a toilsome thing,
speaking, with much ease, not through my own power, but through the eagerness
of you who hear. Since even the other day the word, having been carried down
into so great a depth, was not drowned, and crossing such long seas, nowhere
suffered shipwreck for you; and the cause: nowhere reefs, nor shoals, nor
crags, but everywhere it found a sea calmer than a harbor; and as if by some
west wind of the longing of your hearing standing at the stern, toward a
fair harbor
=63.525=
it was escorted. For at once it leaped forth from our tongue, and all received
it with upturned
hands, although it had much difficulty; for such was the nature of the
thoughts of the other day; but nevertheless both by the excess of eagerness,
and by stretching the understanding with all accuracy, you consoled our toil
and made the difficult things easy. For you did not permit it to fall upon
rock, nor among thorns, nor upon the road, but you received all into a fat and
deep-soiled field, into the depth of your understanding. Wherefore also each
day we see the standing crops waving, nourished instead of a west wind by the
breezes of the Spirit, and the theatre becoming splendid for us each day. For
this reason also today I wished to take hold of the things that remain. But
what am I to do? The chorus of the Maccabees stands before my eyes, and by the
brightness of their own wounds illuminating my understanding, it calls our
tongue to their own beauty. But let no one condemn the word for untimeliness,
because, though the day of the contests is tomorrow, today we weave the
crowns, and before the time of the wars we proclaim the trophy. For if when
marriages are being celebrated those who gather both before the appointed day
weave bridal chambers and adorn the houses with crowns and hangings, much more
shall we do this, inasmuch as this marriage is also more spiritual, not a man
joining a woman to himself, but God joining the souls of men. Wherefore one
would not err in calling the soul of the martyrs a bride, a spiritual bride;
since they also bring in blood as a dowry, a dowry never spent. But let the
time of the encomia wait for us until tomorrow; but today we shall set right
the weaker of our brothers. For since many of the simpler, lame in
understanding, being swept away by the enemies of the Church, do not have the
fitting opinion concerning these saints, nor do they similarly enroll them in
the rest of the choir of the martyrs, saying that they did not pour out their
blood for Christ, but for the law and the letters in the law, having been
slaughtered for swine's flesh: come then, let us set right their
understanding; for it would be shameful, celebrating a festival, to be
ignorant of the subject of the festival.
βʹ. In order then that those who are sick with such things may not, in the
common pleasure of all, alone be in pain, but may embrace the contestants with
a sincere mind, and see them with pure eyes, having today drawn out what is
lurking in their understandings, we shall prepare them to meet tomorrow with
sincere judgment and a pure mind toward the spiritual festival. For I am so
far from refusing to enroll them with the other martyrs, that I even say these
are
more splendid. For they contended then, when the bronze gates had not yet been
broken, nor the iron bar taken away, when sin still held sway, and the curse
was in bloom, and the citadel of the devil still stood, and the road of such
virtue was still untrodden. For now even quite young lads and many virgins in
many places of the inhabited world, tender and unwed, have stripped against
the tyranny of death; but then, before the coming of Christ
=63.526=
even the exceedingly righteous trembled at it. Moses, at any rate, fled
through this fear; and Elijah ran away from here a journey of forty days; and
the patriarch Abraham for this reason advised his wife to say, I am his sister
and not his wife. And why need I speak of the others? For Peter himself so
feared death that he could not even bear the threat of a doorkeeper; for it
was fearful and unapproachable, its sinews not yet having been cut out, nor
its power dissolved. But nevertheless these men then, when it still breathed
so fearfully, conquered it and threw it in wrestling. For since the sun of
righteousness was about to rise at last, what happens in the case of the day
happened then also. For just as even when the sun has not yet appeared, the
dawn appears bright to us, the rays not yet becoming clear, but the light of
the rays illuminating the inhabited world even from afar: so indeed it
happened then also. For since the sun of righteousness was about to be
present, at last the darkness of cowardice was being dissolved, and he not yet
being present according to the flesh, but being near and in the preludes, and
already laying hold of the very affairs. That they displayed much courage,
then, having contended in such times, is clear to everyone, I suppose; but
that they also received the wounds for Christ, this I shall now try to show.
For on whose account did they suffer, tell me? On account of the law, he
says, and of the letters set down in the law. If then it should appear that
Christ is the one who gave that law, is it not quite clear that having
suffered for the
law they displayed all that endurance for the lawgiver? Come then, let us
prove this today, that it is Christ who gave the law. Who then says these
things? The one who knows these things clearly, both the old and the new,
Paul, the teacher of the inhabited world; for writing to the Corinthians he
speaks somehow thus: Moreover, brethren, I would not have you ignorant, that
our fathers were all under the cloud, and all passed through the sea, and all
were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea, and all ate the same
spiritual food, meaning the manna, and all drank the same spiritual drink,
meaning the water from the rock. Then showing that Christ was doing these
miracles, he added: For they drank of a spiritual rock that followed; and the
rock was Christ. And reasonably; for it was not the nature of the rock that
sent forth the water and those rivers, but the energy of Christ striking the
stone prepared the springs to be given forth. For this reason he also called
it a spiritual rock, and said it followed; but the sensible one does not
follow, but is fixed in one place; but the power present everywhere and
working wonders in all things, that also split the rock. But if the Jew should
not bear these words, come then, let us take him with his own weapons,
conversing with him not from Paul and Peter and John, but from the
prophets, that he may learn that the facts are with him, but the meanings with
us.
γʹ. Which of the prophets then says these things, that he himself gave the Old
Covenant? Jeremiah, who was sanctified from the womb, who shone in youth.
Where and when? Hear their words, and be instructed clearly from the things
said. What then are the words? Behold, days
=63.527=
are coming, says the Lord. From the very preludes he immediately raises the
hearer, and wakes the thoughts of those who hear, showing that the words are
not his, but of the God who sent him. Behold, days are coming. Then he shows
that he is conversing with us about future things. How then, if he is
conversing about future things, did he himself, he says, give the Old? Wait
and do not make a disturbance, and then you will see clearly the brightness of
the truth. For when these things were being said, the law had been given and
transgressed, but the New not yet. Having then clearly deposited these things
in your understandings, receive the solution of the things that puzzle many.
Behold, days are coming, says the Lord, indicating the present time, and I
will make a new covenant with you, not according to the covenant which I made
with your fathers. I ask the Jew, I ask the ailing brother, who gave the new
covenant? Everyone will certainly say that Christ did. Therefore this one also
gave the old; for he who said, I will make a new covenant, not according to
the covenant which I made, showed that he himself also made that one;
therefore of both covenants there is one lawgiver. And when did he make the
old, tell me? In the day of my taking hold of their hand to lead them out of
the land of Egypt. See how he showed both the ease of the exodus, and his own
affection, and the fearlessness of the conducting then, and that he himself
worked all the wonders in Egypt. For by saying, Of my taking hold of their
hand to lead them out of the land of Egypt, he indicated all the miracles; for
that exodus came about through those paradoxical signs. And they did not abide
in my covenant, and I neglected them, says the Lord. That then of the new and
of the old covenant there is one lawgiver, is clear from this.
But here if anyone has accurately observed what was said, he knows that it is
full of no ordinary difficulty; for speaking the cause on account of which he
is about to give another covenant, he called it new, that is, paradoxical; For
I will make, he says, a new covenant, not according to the covenant which I
made with their fathers, because they did not abide in my covenant; and I
neglected them, says the Lord. And yet on this account they ought to have been
punished, and to have paid the last penalty, and to have undergone the
unbearable vengeance, because having enjoyed so many wonders, and having
received a law, they in no way became better. But here he does not demand a
penalty of them, but even promises things greater than the former. It was
necessary then today also to bring the solution upon this difficulty, but
since the word is also pressing us toward another thing, and we wish to train
you, so that you may not learn everything from us, but also find it from
yourselves, I leave this for you to seek and find. And if we see you having
sought, but not having found, then we ourselves also will stretch out hands;
and so that the finding may become easier for you, I already announce to you
also the apostolic passages in which especially it is possible to find the
treasure and the solution of this difficulty; having set this chapter in the
epistle to the Romans, and to the Galatians, and to the Hebrews, he solved it.
And as many as are lovers of toil will be able, going through the epistles, to
track down this solution, if in these intervening
=63.528=
days you do not give yourselves to untimely gatherings nor to profitless
chatter, but, nailed to the care of the things said, you dig up the treasure.
Leaving this unsolved for the present, we shall go on to what follows. And
what is what follows? That this is the covenant which I will make with you,
says the Lord; giving my laws into their understanding, and upon their heart I
will write them. And they shall not teach each one his fellow-citizen, and
each one his brother, saying, Know the Lord; because all shall know me from
small to great of them, because I will be merciful to their injustices, and of
their sins and of their lawlessnesses I will remember no more. Having spoken
of an old covenant, which he gave; having spoken also of a new, which he is
about to give, he sketches also its beauty, and shows its characters, and sets
the distinctive marks one after another, that you may learn how great is the
difference of the new toward the old, the difference, not the contrary; how
great the superiority, how great the brightness, how great the lightning of
the gifts and of the grace. δʹ. What then are the characters of the new?
Giving my laws into their understanding, and upon their hearts I will write
them. For the old law was written on tablets of stone, and since the first
tablets were broken, he engraved others again, and having carved the letters
there, Moses came down, having the nature of the tablets akin to the
insensibility of those who received them. But the new is not so; for tablets
were not engraved when the new covenant was being given, but how and in what
manner? Hear Luke narrating: They were all with one accord in one place, he
says; and suddenly there came a sound from heaven as of a rushing mighty wind;
and there appeared to them tongues distributing themselves as of fire; and it
sat upon each one of them, and they were filled with Holy Spirit, and began to
speak with tongues, as the Spirit gave them. Do you see how the prophet
clearly proclaimed this beforehand from above, having spoken thus: Giving my
laws into their understanding, and upon their hearts I will write them.
For the grace of the Spirit given by God to sit in their understandings made
them living pillars. and it overshadows the understanding with his glory;
wherefore he also said that he was sent to preach, not in wisdom of word, lest
the cross of Christ be made void; and again, God made foolish the wisdom of
this world; and he turns the cross up and down. For since, he says, Jews ask
for signs, and Greeks seek wisdom, but we preach Christ crucified, to Jews a
stumbling-block, but to Gentiles foolishness, but to them that are called,
both Jews and Greeks, Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God; because
the foolishness of God is wiser than men, and the weakness of God is stronger
than men; naming the cross foolishness of God and weakness of God, not because
it is foolish; for what is wiser than this? nor because it is weak; for what
is stronger than this? but reporting the suspicion of the unbelievers, which
they had concerning the matter. Wherefore also above he said: For the word of
the cross is to those who are perishing
=63.529=
foolishness, but to those who are being saved it is the power of God. But one
must not bring the judgments of the matters from those who are perishing;
since the sick also think honey is bitter, but this is the charge of the
sickness, not the accusation of the honey. So also the cross seems to those
outside to be a foolish thing, but it is not. Then showing that not only is it
not foolish, but the matter is even exceedingly wise, and not only not weak,
but even exceedingly strong, he compares the achievements from it both with
the creation and with the old and with the outside wisdom; and he shows that
what neither the inside wisdom found nor the outside, nor did many of men
learn from the creation, nor did they gain from the old, these things that
which seemed to be this foolish and weak was able to do, that which seemed,
not that which is. Wherefore, having the proof from the facts, he strips with
much boldness, first against the outside wisdom, saying, Where is the wise?
What is, Where is the wise? As if he said: Where are the things of the
philosophers? where those of the rhetors? where those of the sophists? where
those of the speech-writers? All things have gone and perished and been put
out of the way. For so splendid has the victory become, that their things no
longer even
appear. For this reason, these having become thus invisible, and as if some
dust had been stirred up, so he asks and says: Where is the wise? A cross
appeared, and all those things were dissolved; the preaching sounded, and it
was torn more cheaply than a spider's web. Where is the wise? Where the boast
of words? where the beauty of eloquence? where the cleverness of sophisms?
where the rush of discourses? where the sharpened tongue? where the gatherings
and the councils? All those things were swept away and perished and were
corrupted and are gone and have turned their backs. Where is the scribe? Where
are the things of the Jews, he says? the preaching prevailed over those too,
and as a sun hid a shadow. For what the law was not able in so great a time in
one nation, these things the cross accomplished everywhere with more
abundance, having dissolved sins, having granted righteousness, having made
men holy, having taught knowledge of God, having led them by the hand toward
heaven.
Then again leaving the Jewish things, he comes to the Greek: Where is the
disputer of this age? Here he hints at the heretics and the syllogistic men,
who before these things were sharper than swords, but when the cross appeared,
they were cut more weakly than all clay. Has not God made foolish the wisdom
of this world? Again he strips against the Greek. What is, He made foolish? He
showed it to be foolish; for it was. And of them and of their sins and of
their lawlessnesses I will remember no more. But the prophet of the Old
sketches the brightness for us; and the apostle, since he was fighting against
Jews, sets both in parallel; having said above, Not on tablets of stone, but
on tablets of a fleshy heart; and here, Not of letter, but of spirit; for the
letter kills, but the spirit gives life. εʹ. On a sabbath someone gathered
wood, and was stoned.
=63.530=
Did you see how the letter kills, that is, the law punished? learn how the
spirit gives life. Someone comes in full of ten thousand evils, having
fornicated, having plundered, having defrauded, having committed adultery,
having gone through every wickedness, already deadened by sin; the grace of
the Spirit takes him into the font, and makes the one who has fornicated a son
of God, and gives life to the one deadened by sins. This is, But the spirit
gives life. And how does it give life? Not demanding accounts of the
trespasses according to the prophet's: I will be merciful to their injustices,
and of their sins and of their lawlessnesses I will remember no more. Ask the
Jew again, where this happened in the law? But he would not be able to show
it. For both the one who gathered wood was stoned, and the woman who
fornicated was burned, and Moses for one sin fell out of the land of promise;
but under grace those who have worked ten thousand terrible things, enjoying
baptism, are given life, and no penalty of the trespasses is demanded of them.
Wherefore Paul also said: Be not deceived; neither fornicators, nor the
effeminate, nor sodomites, nor adulterers, nor thieves, nor covetous, nor
drunkards, nor revilers, nor robbers shall inherit the kingdom of God. And
such were some of you; but you were washed, but you were sanctified, but you
were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and in the Spirit of our God.
Did you see how the prophetic shines, that I will be merciful to their
injustices? how the apostolic lightens, that The spirit gives life? Do you
wish to learn also the other, the apostle Paul saying how in a brief turn of
time he went around the whole inhabited world? hear him saying: So that from
Jerusalem and as far as Illyricum I have fulfilled the Gospel; and again: But
now having no longer a place in these regions, and having a longing to see
you, as, if I go into Spain, I hope to see you, if I first be in part filled
with you. And if one apostle in a brief turn of time ran through the greater
part of the inhabited world, consider how others also netted it all. Wherefore
he also said: Of the Gospel which was preached in all creation under heaven;
interpreting that prophetic, That all shall know me from small to great of
them.
But that Christ gave the law is clear from these things; and that having been
slaughtered for the law they poured out their blood for the lawgiver, this too
is clear; and I exhort your love henceforth to meet the festival with much
eagerness; like bees leap out from the hives upon the wounds of the martyrs,
and embrace their tortures, not shrinking from the length of the
road. For if Eleazar the old man dared fire, and the mother of those blessed
ones in extreme old age endured so many pains: what defense would you have,
what pardon, not even crossing a few stades for the spectacle of those
contests? ...
