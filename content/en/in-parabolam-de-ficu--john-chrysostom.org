On the parable concerning the fig tree, a homily.
=59.585=
1. The eye that sees either a flowering tree or a water-bearing spring is
drawn by the very sight, and turns the mind of the heart there. Likewise also
the one who hears through words deeds and histories stretches the
understanding toward those things, so that such a one is in body in this
place, but in understanding in that place, where the word of the history turns
the matter. Something of this sort has happened also to me, beloved. For
having heard the evangelist narrating the things that happened in Bethany
concerning the fig tree, I am here in body; but in understanding I imagine
myself to be there; and I think now to follow the Master Jesus, and to see the
fig tree before the Master's command flowering and leafy, but after the Lord's
command grim and dried up. For in the morning, he says, as Jesus was returning
into the city, he hungered. And seeing a fig tree, he came upon it, and found
nothing on it except leaves only; and he says to it: Let no fruit come from
you any longer forever; and immediately =59.586= it was dried up. For this was
just now read to us. The wonder strikes me; my understanding is divided.
For when I wish to narrate the matter of the history, immediately along with
the history the wonders draw me toward themselves. And just as whenever
someone sees a golden jar having precious stones within, bearing mysteries
written in themselves, he wishes indeed to look at the jar because of the
brilliance of the form, but is drawn the other way by the stones within; for
he hastens both to look around at the beauty of the stones, and to know the
mysteries written in it: so also I, seeing indeed the history of the letter
brilliant and very beautiful, am held there in mind; but I am drawn the other
way again by the contemplation of the spirit, which promises to show me
wonders greater than the history. For in the morning, he says, as Jesus was
returning into the city, he hungered. O inexpressible and untellable mystery!
He hungered who feeds the whole inhabited world; he hungered who from five
loaves fed five thousand to satiety; he hungered who from clay =59.587= worked
living eyes; he hungered who walked upon the waves of the sea, and did not dip
his feet into the water; he hungered who by thought alone turned the water
into wine; he hungered who drew souls out of their dwellings, and housed them
again by a word. And how does Isaiah say, The eternal God will not hunger, nor
will he grow weary? But that Christ is God is surely clear to everyone who
believes the Scripture that says: In the beginning was the Word, and the Word
was with God, and the Word was God. How then did God hunger? The form of the
servant hungered; but the will of the Master in him fed five thousand. The man
who was seen came upon the fig tree; but the divinity understood in him was
everywhere. He saw the fig tree fruitless as man, he dried it up by a word as
God. He said, Let no fruit come from you any longer forever; and the deed
followed the saying; and immediately the fig tree was dried up. The saying was
human, but the deed divine. But much-sought for us, beloved, is the
contemplation concerning the fig tree, interpreted by many, as it seems to me,
not accurately; yet I will tell you the solutions of those who went before,
and we will bring forward the exploits we have labored. For the mind lying in
the history of the letter is hard to grasp, having the brilliance of the
mystery hidden in much depth.
For what necessity was there that the fig tree be dried up by the word, and
not that it bear fruit by a word? For he who dried it by a saying was able
also by a saying to make it fruitful. And what did the fig tree even sin,
someone might say, when it was not its season, Mark the evangelist saying that
it was winter at that time? And unless there is some mystery beyond man being
accomplished in it, the Lord will be found praiseworthy in one way, but
blameworthy in another; for praiseworthy, because he dried it by a word; but
blameworthy, because he cursed it unjustly. But we must understand from now
on, beloved, of what the fig tree bore an image; for there was no deed done by
Christ that did not have a mystery. There was wholly no work coming to be from
him that was not exceedingly useful; nothing that did not cry out the truth;
nothing that did not move the mind toward the reception of heavenly things.
This fig tree the many of the interpreters have said to be likened to the
synagogue of the Jews, upon which, he says, the Lord came, seeking in it fruit
of faith; and he did not find it, except only flourishing with words of
prophets and law as with leaves; wherefore he also dried it, saying: Let no
fruit come from you any longer forever.
But I try to speak against this interpretation, beloved; for the accuracy of
the thought is not so. For how would the Lord have cursed, who says, Bless,
and do not curse? and how would he who says, The Son of man did not come to
destroy, but to seek and to save the lost, have cursed and dried up the
synagogue? And if someone still speaks against and contends, saying that the
fig tree is accurately likened to the synagogue of the Jews, let him say how
the synagogue, dried up from the roots, sprouted such a fruitful branch, the
sweetest Paul. And we have known many others also who turned from the
synagogue of the Jews; for Stephen too who was stoned by the Jews was from
that synagogue, and Aquila and Priscilla and many others, whose catalogue of
names we now decline to speak because of the multitude of the words. And that
God did not utterly reject the people of the Jews, let Paul bear witness for
me saying: I say then, Did the Lord utterly reject his people? May it not be;
for I also am an Israelite, of the seed of Abraham, of the tribe of Benjamin.
The Lord did not reject his people. But indeed he also promises salvation to
that people in the last of the days in saying: And when the fullness of the
nations has entered, then all Israel will be saved. For even if the Jews have
become an enemy of God, they have become so through us, that by their
disobedience we might be shown mercy, as =59.588= Paul bears witness for me
saying, As regards the Gospel, they are enemies through us; but as regards the
election, beloved, because of the fathers; for the gifts are without
repentance and the calling holy. The word concerning the fig tree must
therefore be sought, beloved, of what it bore an image. But if you, O Adam,
remember, when you were stripped in paradise, of what tree you took leaves and
sewing them together wove a covering for yourself, you will see how justly he
dried this one; for did you not, taking leaves of a fig tree, cover your own
shame? Christ therefore came, and dried by the word the fig tree still
flowering in you, the covering of shame; he took away your poverty, and gave
you the wealth; he took away your coverings of shame, and gave you a
snow-shining robe, woven of water and Spirit; he dried the leaves of the fig
tree, and restored to you the spoils of the soul. What spoils? Those which a
serpent plundered in you in paradise, the life equal to angels, the luxury of
paradise, the robe of incorruption.
2. But let us return to the heading. For in the morning, he says, Jesus having
returned into the city, hungered. In the morning, after the night of error had
run past, and dawn, Christ, had shone the light of the resurrection upon the
world; for Christ is dawn, as the prophet says: As a ready dawn we shall find
him. In the morning Jesus returning. In the morning, after death of dark form
had been made to vanish by the light of the glory of Christ, and the sun of
righteousness had shone golden rays in the hearts of darkened men; in the
morning, after he had spread out the warm rays of the divinity, and warmed the
souls made dead by the devil; For there is none who shall be hidden from his
heat. For by most cold sin the devil made dead the hearts of men. Whence the
prophet prays saying: Kindle my reins, and my heart. For all humanity was
bound by a frost of impiety. Wherefore all the prophets with one accord prayed
to God saying: Turn, Lord, our captivity, as torrents in the south; that is,
just as a warm south wind, having blown, brings the frozen waters back to the
former nature of water, so also us who have been made dead by the sin of the
devil, as a warm south wind having blown, he renews unto the ancient nature of
incorruption.
This warm blowing of the south the bride of Christ, the Church, also invokes
in the Song of Songs saying: Awake, north wind, and come, south; blow through
my garden, and let spices flow. In the morning, he says, returning. Fair is
the "Returning," that it may signify that our Lord, having come again, leads
Adam who fell from paradise back into the same place. In the morning Jesus
returning upon the city, hungered. Having now heard "city," do not look around
below, but look up toward the Jerusalem above, toward the heavenly place; for
Our citizenship is in the heavens. In the morning Jesus returning upon the
city, hungered, not the food of men, but the life of men; For my food is that
I may do the will of my Father; that they may believe in him whom he sent; For
he who believes in him, as he himself said, shall not die forever. In the
morning Jesus returning upon the city, hungered, and seeing a fig tree, he
came upon it. For if he had passed by the fig tree, the serpent would have had
his nest in the fig tree immortal; but he came upon the fig tree, and dried
this one. Under such a fig tree the Lord saw Nathanael being; For before
Philip called you, being under the fig tree, I saw you. The prophet,
foreseeing this fig tree about to become fruitless, said: I will go up into a
people of my sojourning, because a fig tree will not bear fruit. Upon such a
fig tree Zacchaeus went up to see Jesus; to whom he said: Make haste, come
down. [There the serpent is twined in curves, there he nested, there he has
been hidden. Make haste, come down.] And the evange =59.589= list said
sycamore in the case of Zacchaeus wittily, that he might signify that the
broad and spacious way is interwoven with folly, whence the worst fate is
born. The Lord came upon the fig tree.
This fig tree bore an image of the broad and spacious way; for the plant of
the fig tree is broad-leaved, and sin is itching, leading away through the
broad and spacious way into destruction. Sweetest is the fruit of the fig
tree; sweet is pleasure, dashing those who approach it into a slip of sin.
Flee its taste, O beloved; for it is sweet in the taste, but bitter after the
deed. For honey drips from the lips of a woman who is a harlot, and for a time
it oils the throat; but afterward you will find it more bitter than gall, and
sharper than a two-edged sword. Pleasure is a harlot, she converses with you
sweetly at the beginnings, secretly inserting in the converse the bitterness
of death. Such also was the serpent, sweet in the word, and bitter in the
deceit; sweet the food, but bitter the fall. Flee therefore, beloved, the
harlot, who converses with you sweetly, and brings in bitter wounds; flee
pleasure as Elijah fled Jezebel. The Lord therefore came from heavens; he
hungered to find the life of men; he found the broad and spacious way of this
life standing; he came upon it, and finds it flourishing with leaves, that is,
flowering with doctrines of sin, but no longer having fruit of death. Why?
Because it was not the season; for the evangelist added this: It had not, he
says, fruit; for it was not the season. For how was it still able to bear
death as fruit, Jesus having arrived, and proclaiming resurrection to the
world? It was no longer its season to bear death as fruit; For death reigned
from Adam until Moses. that is, of the law. For Scripture, he says, knows to
call the law Moses, as in the Gospel: They have Moses and the prophets. The
Lord said to it, No further and Let no fruit come from you any longer forever;
that is, Before my presence you bore death as fruit; behold I have arrived,
this is the resurrection. I having arrived, Let no fruit come from you any
longer forever. And immediately the fig tree was dried up; and that which was
written was fulfilled, Death was swallowed up into victory. Where, death, is
your sting? where, Hades, is your victory? For the evangelist, having said
that it was winter at that time in which the fig tree flourished with leaves,
hinted that sin flourished, because the devil was a winter lying upon all
humanity. For before the presence of the prince of peace, the race of men was
tossed by various winds, driven toward idolatries and bloodsheds and revels
and lusts; but our Lord Jesus =59.590= Christ having come, the peace of the
world, stilled the reefs of the opposing spirits troubling the salt-bearing
sea of the passions of this life, and quenched the foam-bearing waves of the
pleasures. And having dried the fig tree, he planted the faith of the cross
flowering, and flashing life to all; of which cross the roots were set into
the earth, but the branches were stretched up into the heavens; whose leaves
are unaging, and the flower unfading, and the fruit immortal; of which wood
David also makes mention, saying: And he shall be as the tree planted beside
the outlets of the waters, which will give its fruit in its season, and its
leaf will not fall away. Of this wood Wisdom also makes mention, saying: It is
a tree of life to all who hold fast to it. Jeremiah, having seen this wood
with prophetic eye, about to be hewn by the Jews against Christ, spoke out
toward the face of the Jews, saying to them: Come, and let us cast wood into
his bread, and let us wipe him out from the land of the living. The wood is
the cross, the bread the body of Christ. For the Lord taking bread said: This
is my body, which is broken for you for the forgiveness of sins.
Our Lord Jesus Christ, having gone up upon this scaffold of the cross, and
having stretched out his hands, and having driven out the powers flying in the
air, was preparing for us the unhindered journey into heaven, and stretching
out for us a long ladder, the faith, from earth to heaven; and the paradox, if
you hear it, beloved, the tree of the cross stood, the true vine was spread
out upon it, she who says, I am the true vine; and it became for the devil a
death-bearing tree-vine, but for us a life-bearing tree-vine, checking the
burning heat of our sins, and granting us a shadow of rest. The one sitting
under its shadow says that which was spoken by Solomon: In his shadow I
desired and sat, and his fruit is sweet in my throat. In this shadow the
blessed David, taking courage, said: And in the shadow of your wings I will
hope. Until when? Until my lawlessness has passed. O wondrous deed and new
mystery! The spring lay upon a tree, and the cross was watered by it; for
having been pierced with a spear in his side, he sent forth blood and water;
with the blood purpling the cross, but with the water watering its roots, that
it may ever bloom immortal fruits for those who believe.
O blessed tree, which kings honor, and governors reverence, and men and women
and every nature and creation of men possess as a phylactery; which demons
shudder at, and the devil trembles; which angels hymn, and heavens bless; and
Churches glorify Christ crucified on it! to whom be the glory and the power,
now and always, and unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
