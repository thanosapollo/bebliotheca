OF THE SAME, CANONICAL LETTER TO LETOIUS AMONG THE SAINTS, BISHOP OF MELITENE.

This too is one of the things that contribute to the holy feast, that we
should consider the lawful and canonical stewardship toward those who have
offended, so that every sickness of the soul that comes about through some sin
may be healed. For since this catholic feast of the creation also, according
to the appointed period of the yearly cycle, being fulfilled each year in the
whole world, is celebrated upon the resurrection of the fallen; and a fall is
sin, and resurrection is the raising up from the fall of sin: it would be well
on this day not only to bring to God those who are being recast through
regeneration by the grace of the washing, but also those who through
repentance and conversion are returning again from dead works to the living
way, and to lead these too by the hand toward the saving hope from which they
were alienated by sin. And it is no small work to steward the words concerning
these things in a right and approved judgment, according to the Prophet's
charge, which commands that one must steward words in judgment, so that, as
the oracle has it, the righteous may not be shaken for ever, and may become an
everlasting memorial.

=45.224= For just as also in bodily healing the aim of medicine is one, to
make the sick man well, but the form of the care is various; for according to
the variety of the sicknesses the therapeutic method too is applied
correspondingly to each of the diseases: so, there being much variety of the
passions also in the disease of the soul, the therapeutic care will
necessarily become manifold, working the healing in proportion to the passion.
And that there may be some skilled method concerning the problem before us, we
shall take up the account thus. There are three things observed concerning our
soul according to the first division: the rational, and the desiring, and the
spirited. In these are both the right deeds of those who live according to
virtue, and the falls of those who drift off into vice. Therefore it is
fitting that one who is about to apply the corresponding therapy to the
diseased part of the soul should first examine in what the passion consists,
and then apply the therapy suitably to what has suffered, so that it may not
be that, through inexperience of the therapeutic method, one part is the one
that has fallen sick, and another the one that receives the therapy: just as,
indeed, we see many physicians, in inexperience of the part that first
suffers, aggravating the disease by the very things with which they treat it.
For when the sickness is often by a predominance of heat, since for those
harmed by an excess of cold what warms and restores is useful, they, applying
without examination to those burning in an excess of heat the same thing that
reasonably helped the former, have made the passion hard to heal. Just as,
then, the understanding of the property of the elements has been judged most
necessary for physicians, so that there may be, for each of those well or ill
disposed, a correction of what has been disposed contrary to nature: so let us
also, having gone back to this division of the things observed in the soul,
make the generic contemplation the beginning and basis of the corresponding
therapy of the passions. Since, then, the property of the soul's movements is
divided threefold, as we said, into the reasoning and the desiring and the
spirited: the success of the reasoning part of the soul is the pious
conception concerning the Divine, and the science that distinguishes the good
and the evil, and the opinion that has a clear and unconfused judgment
concerning the nature of the things that lie before us: what among beings is
to be chosen, and what is abominable and to be rejected. And again, from the
contrary, the vice in this part will certainly be observed whenever there is
impiety concerning the Divine, and want of judgment concerning what is truly
good, and an exchanged and mistaken conception of the nature of things, so as
to set light as darkness and darkness as light, as the Scripture says.

The virtuous movement of the desiring part is that the longing be raised
toward what is truly desirable and truly good; and that if there is in us any
erotic power and disposition, the whole of it be occupied there, in being
persuaded that nothing =45.225= else is by its own nature an object of
appetite except virtue and the nature that is the fountain of virtue. But a
turning aside and sin of such a part is when someone transfers the desire
toward substanceless vainglory, or toward the bloom painted upon bodies:
whence come love of money, and love of glory, and love of pleasure, and all
such things as hang upon this kind of vice. And again, the success of the
spirited disposition is hatred toward evil, and war against the passions, and
that the soul be whetted toward courage, in not cowering before the things the
many count fearful, but standing against sin even unto blood; and to despise a
death-dealing threat, and painful punishments, and severance from the most
pleasant things, and once for all to become stronger than all the things that
by some habit and preconception hold the many in pleasure, fighting in the
front for the faith and for virtue. But the falls of such a part are obvious
to all: envy, hatred, wrath, insults, brawls, the contentious and vengeful
dispositions, which stretch resentment out to a great length and whet many
toward murders and bloodshed. For the untrained reasoning, not finding where
it may use the weapon profitably, turns the edge of the iron upon itself, and
the defensive weapon given us by God becomes destructive to the one who uses
it badly.

These things, then, having been distinguished in the manner stated, whatever
sins touch the reasoning part of the soul have been judged by the Fathers more
grievous, and worthy of a greater and more lasting and more laborious
conversion: as if someone has denied the faith in Christ, or has been shown to
have deserted to Judaism, or to idolatry, or to Manichaeism, or to some other
such form of godlessness. He who has rushed upon such an evil willingly, and
then has condemned himself, has as the time of his repentance the whole of his
life. For never, when the mystical prayer is being performed, is he counted
worthy to worship God with the people, but he shall pray by himself; and he
shall be altogether alien from the communion of the holy things; and at the
hour of his departure, then he shall be counted worthy of the portion of the
holy thing. And if it should happen contrary to hope that he live, he shall
live out his life again in the same judgment, remaining without share in the
mystical holy things until his departure. But those who have been maltreated
with tortures and harsh punishments have been censured for a stated time, the
holy Fathers having used philanthropy toward them, as of a soul that had not
come to be in a fall, but of the bodily weakness not holding out against the
outrages. Therefore the forced and painful transgression also has been
measured, in the conversion, by the measure of those who have offended in
fornication. But those who go off to sorcerers, or diviners, or those who
promise to work certain purifications and avertings through demons, these are
asked with accuracy and examined, whether, remaining in the faith in =45.228=
Christ, they were brought by some necessity to that sin, some ill-treatment or
unbearable loss having produced this impulse in them; or whether, altogether
despising the witness that has been believed among us, they ran to the
alliance of the demons. For if they did that by a rejection of the faith, and
so as not to believe that the God worshipped by the Christians is God, they
shall clearly be subjected to the judgment of those who have transgressed. But
if some unbearable necessity, overpowering their smallness of soul, led them
to this, being struck aside by some deceived hope, the philanthropy shall be
upon them likewise, after the likeness of those who were not able to hold out
against the tortures in the time of confession. And of the sins that come
about through desire and pleasure, the division is of this kind: the one is
called adultery, the other fornication. Now it has pleased some of the more
exact that even the offence according to fornication be counted as adultery,
because there is one lawful coupling, both of a woman toward a man and of a
man toward a woman. Therefore everything that is not lawful is altogether
unlawful, and he who does not have what is his own clearly has what is
another's; for one helper has been given to the man by God, and one head has
been fitted to the woman. Therefore if someone should acquire for himself his
own vessel, as the divine Apostle names it, the law of nature permits the just
use.

But if someone should turn outside of his own, he will altogether come to be
in what is another's; and what is not his own is another's to each, even if it
does not have a confessed master. Therefore fornication too has been shown, to
those who examine the account more exactly, not far from the offence according
to adultery, the divine Scripture also saying: Do not be much toward a strange
woman. But nevertheless, since some condescension toward the weaker has come
about from the Fathers, the offence has been distinguished by this generic
division, so that fornication is and is called the fulfillment of desire that
comes about for some without injustice to another; and adultery, the plotting
and the injustice against what is another's. And in this they also reckon
bestiality and pederasty to be, because these too are an adultery of nature.
For the injustice comes about toward what is both another's and against
nature. This division, then, having been made in this kind of sin also, the
therapy is catholic: that the man become, from remorse, clean of the
passionate madness concerning such pleasures. But in the case of those defiled
in fornication, no injustice is mixed into this sin; for this reason the time
of conversion has been appointed double for those defiled in adultery, and in
the other forbidden evils, bestiality and the madness against males; for in
such cases the sin is doubled, as I said. One is that which consists in the
injustice against what is another's. And there is a certain difference in the
account of repentance even among those who offend according to pleasure
=45.229= of this kind. For he who has of himself set out toward the confession
of the sin, by the very fact of having accepted, of his own impulse, to become
the accuser of hidden things, as already having begun the therapy of the
passion, and having shown a sign of the change toward the better, comes under
more philanthropic penalties. But he who has been caught in the evil, or
unwillingly convicted by some suspicion or accusation, comes under a more
severe conversion, so that, having been purified with accuracy, he may thus be
admitted to the communion of the holy things. The canon, then, is of this
kind: that those defiled in fornication be for three years altogether cast out
from the prayer; and for three years share in the hearing only; and in another
three years pray with those who fall down in conversion, and then share in the
holy things. But in the case of those who have used conversion more earnestly,
and who show by their life the return toward the good, it is permitted to the
one who administers the ecclesiastical stewardship toward what is beneficial
to cut short the time of the hearing, and to bring him more quickly into
conversion, and again also to cut this time short, and to restore the
communion more quickly, however he may discern by his own testing the
condition of the one being healed.

For just as it is forbidden to cast the pearl before swine, so it is among the
absurd things to deprive of the precious pearl the one who has already become
a man through purity and passionlessness. And the lawlessness that comes about
according to adultery, or also the other kinds of uncleanness, as has been
said before, will in all things be healed under the same judgment in which the
pollution of fornication also is; but it is doubled in the time. And the
disposition of the one being healed will be observed in his case also, in the
same way as in those who have been brought together with the defilement of
fornication, so that their participation in the good may come about either
more quickly or more slowly. It remains after these things to set the spirited
part of the soul forward for examination, whenever, having slipped from the
good use of spirit, it falls out into sin. And since many things are worked as
sin according to spirit, and all of them evils, it has somehow pleased our
Fathers not to be too exact in the others, nor to think it worth much zeal to
heal all the offences that come from spirit; and this although the Scripture
forbids not only a mere blow, but also every insult, or blasphemy, and
whatever else of this kind the spirit works; and they have made the safeguard,
through the penalties, only of the pollution according to murder.

And such an evil has been divided by the difference of the voluntary and the
involuntary, among which voluntary murder is, first, that dared from
preparation by one who has made himself ready for this very thing, that he may
work the pollution. Then that too has been counted among the voluntary,
whenever someone in a brawl and a fight, striking and being struck, brings the
blow of the hand upon some vital part. For he who has once been overpowered by
spirit, and is granting the impulse of anger, would take nothing into mind at
the time of the passion of the things able to check the evil. =45.232= So that
the result of murder from the brawl also is referred to a work of choice, and
not to a miss. But the involuntary have clear marks: whenever someone, having
his zeal toward some other thing, from a miss does one of the irreparable
things. In these cases, then, murder is extended to a triple time for those
being healed through conversion from the voluntary pollution. For they are
three times nine years, nine years having been appointed for each degree, so
that he spend a nine-year time in the complete exclusion, being barred from
the Church; and remain as many years again in the hearing, being counted
worthy only of the hearing of the teachers and of the Scriptures, and of the
assembling with the people; and in the third nine, praying with those who fall
down in conversion, thus come to the participation of the holy thing; clearly
in such a case also the same observation will stand with the one who stewards
the Church, and according to the measure of the conversion the extension of
the penalty also will be cut short for him, so that instead of nine years
there come to be, at each degree, eight or seven or six, or even five only, if
the greatness of the conversion should overcome the time, and should surpass,
by the zeal of the correction, those who in the long appointed term cleanse
themselves from the stain somewhat more sluggishly. But the involuntary has
been judged pardonable, yet not praiseworthy. And I have said this so that it
may become clear that even if someone comes to be unwillingly in the
defilement of murder, the canon has declared him, as already become profane
from the pollution, rejected from priestly grace; and as much time as there is
of the purification upon bare fornication, so much also has been judged good
to have upon those who have murdered unwillingly, the choice of the one
repenting being tested in this case also, clearly; so that if the conversion
should become trustworthy, the number of the years need not be observed in
every case, but he may be brought by a shortening to the restoration of the
Church and to the participation of the good. But if someone, not having
completed the time appointed from the canons, should depart from life, the
philanthropy of the Fathers commands that, having shared in the holy things,
he not be sent empty of the provision for that last and long sojourn abroad.

And if, having shared in the holy thing, he should return again to life, he is
to wait out the appointed time, coming to be in that degree in which he was
before the communion given him according to necessity. And the form of
idolatry (for so the divine Apostle names greed): I do not know how it has
been left unhealed by our Fathers. And yet such an evil seems to be a passion
of the third constitution in the soul. For the reasoning, missing the judgment
of the good, imagines the good to be in matter, not looking up toward the
immaterial beauty, and the desire flows toward the things below, drifting away
from the true object of appetite. And the contentious and spirited disposition
takes many starting-points from such a sin. =45.233= And to speak the whole,
such a disease agrees with the apostolic definition of greed. For the divine
Apostle declared it not only idolatry, but also a root of all evils; and yet
this kind of disease has been overlooked, unexamined and uncared for; for
which reason such a sickness also abounds in the Churches, and no one inquires
closely concerning those being brought into the clergy, whether they have been
defiled with this kind of idolatry. But concerning these things, because they
have been passed over by our Fathers, we think it enough to heal, as far as
may be, by the public word of teaching, purging the greedy sicknesses through
the word as some passions of plethora. And we count only theft and
tomb-breaking and sacrilege as passions, because the tradition concerning this
has come to us thus from the sequence of the Fathers. And yet in the divine
Scripture both excess and interest are among the things forbidden, and the
bringing of what belongs to another to one's own possession from some
domination, even if such a thing happens to come about under the pretext of
business.

Since, then, as far as it concerns us, a canon trustworthy unto authority, we
shall now add to what has been said the canonical judgment upon the things
confessedly forbidden. Theft, then, has been divided into robbery and
house-breaking; and there is one aim in both, the taking away of what belongs
to another; but there is much difference between them according to their
intention. For the robber also takes murder as an ally of what he is eager
for, preparing himself for this very thing with weapons and many hands and the
opportune places; so that such a one is to be subjected to the judgment of the
man-slayers, if through remorse he should bring himself back to the Church of
God. But he who by a hidden filching appropriates what is another's, and then
by confession has made his offence plain to the priest, will heal the sickness
by zeal toward the contrary of the passion: I mean by furnishing what he has
to the poor, so that by letting go what he has he may become manifest as being
clean of the disease according to greed. And if he should have nothing, and
should have only the body, the Apostle commands that such a passion be
expiated through bodily labor. And the wording is thus: Let the thief steal no
longer; but rather let him labor, working the good, that he may have to give
to him who has need. And tomb-breaking itself also has been divided into the
pardonable and the unpardonable. For if someone, sparing the substance, and
leaving the hidden body inviolate, so that the unseemliness of nature not be
shown to the sun, should make use of certain stones that have been set forward
in the tomb for the construction of some work, this too is not praiseworthy;
yet custom has made it pardonable, whenever the transfer of the material comes
about toward something more honorable and more of common benefit. But to
search through the dust from the earth-turned flesh, and to stir the bones, in
hope of gaining some of the ornament buried =45.236= together with them, this
has been condemned under the same judgment under which bare fornication also
is, as has also been distinguished in the account that went before; the
steward clearly watching the healing of the one being treated from the life
itself, so as to cut short the term of the penalty appointed from the canons.
And sacrilege, in the ancient Scripture, was not even judged more tolerable
than the condemnation for murder. For alike both the one caught in murder and
the one who filched the things dedicated to God underwent the punishment
through stones. But in the ecclesiastical custom I do not know how some
condescension and accommodation has come about, so that the purification of
such a disease has been counted more tolerable; for in a lesser time than
adultery the tradition of the Fathers has arranged the penalty for such
persons.

Everywhere, and in this kind of offence, it is fitting to look at this before
all, what the disposition of the one being healed is, and not to think that
the time suffices for healing; for what healing would come from the time? but
the choice of the one who is healing himself through conversion. These things,
O man of God, we have composed for you with much zeal from what was at hand,
because one must obey the commands of the brethren, and we have sent them with
zeal. And do not cease from offering to God the accustomed prayers on our
behalf. For you owe, as a well-disposed son, to the one who begot you
according to God, the tendance of old age through the prayers, according to
the command that orders to honor the parents, that it may be well with you,
and you shall be long-lived upon the earth. And it is clear that you will
receive the letter as a priestly token, and you will not dishonor the
guest-gift, even if it should be somewhat smaller than your magnanimity.
