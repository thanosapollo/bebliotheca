according to hypostasis of extreme union. For he is one, who is that
eternally, even if he became this later.

γʹ. Today things unheard of are sounded in the ears of men. For the man who is
seen is attested as Son of God, as only-begotten, beloved, and consubstantial.
Unlying is the testimony; true is the proclamation; the Father who begot him
himself utters the proclamation. Let David be present, and let him strike the
God-sounding lyre of the Spirit, and now let him sing more clearly and more
distinctly the saying which of old, looking from afar with prophetic and pure
eyes upon the incarnate coming of God the Word toward us, he prophesied as
about to be: Tabor and Hermon shall rejoice in your name. Hermon first, having
heard the name of sonship clearly attested to Christ by the Father, rejoiced,
when just then the Forerunner, as mediator of old and new, went forth to the
Jordan to baptize, being sent to publish the treasure hidden in the desert,
shining as in darkness to the world, the unapproachable light, and escaping
those who are short-sighted; when in the midst of the Jordan the water of
remission stood, cleansing the world, not itself being cleansed; when by the
Father's voice thundering from heaven the one being baptized is attested as
beloved Son; and as a dove, the one attested is pointed out by the Spirit. But
now Tabor rejoices and is glad, the divine and holy mountain, the high one,
now worthily gladdened no less by glory and brightness than by its aerial
height. For it vies with heaven in grace. For he upon whom in that heaven the
angels cannot fix an unwavering eye, on this mountain the foremost of the
apostles see shining with the glory of his kingdom; on this the resurrection
of the dead is confirmed, and he is shown as Lord of dead and living, having
sent forth Moses from the dead, and bringing Elijah living as a witness, the
fire-breathing charioteer who of old drove from earth upon an ethereal path.
On this even now the chiefs of the prophets prophesy, declaring the Master's
departure through the cross. Whence it leaps, and rejoices, and imitates the
bounds of lambs, having been made to hear from the cloud, the Spirit, the same
testimony of sonship, the life-giving Christ attested by the Father. For this
is the name that is above every name, in which Tabor and Hermon rejoice. This
is my beloved Son. This is the gladness of all creation; this is honor for
men, and a boast that cannot be taken away. For he is a man, even if


not a mere man, the one who is attested. O the gladness =96.552= granted us
beyond mind! O the blessedness beyond hope! O gifts of God that conquer
desire! O graces that do not yield to the measures of asking! O ungrudging and
supernatural giver, who possesses a supernatural magnificence! O gift worthy
not of the receiver, but of the giver rather! O strange exchanges! O him who
gives power and receives weakness! O him who shows a man without beginning, in
that the beginningless one begins to be created bodily! For if man is deified
in that God is humanized, and the one God and man is shown; then the same,
being man, is without beginning in the Godhead, and being God, begins in the
humanity.

δʹ. Of old then on Mount Sinai smoke, and storm, and gloom, and fire covering
that extreme condescension terrified, proclaiming the lawgiver unapproachable,
and showing in shadow his hinder parts, and publishing the master-craftsman
from his own creatures. But now all things have been filled with light and
radiance. For the craftsman and Lord of all himself has come from the Father's
bosom, not departing from his own establishment, that is, from his seating in
the Father's bosom, but condescending to the servants, and taking the form of
a servant, and becoming man in nature and in fashion, that God uncontainable
might be contained by men, showing through himself and in himself the
brightness of the divine nature. For formerly in the union of his own grace he
made man God, when he breathed a spirit of life into the one newly fashioned
from dust, and imparted of the better, and honored him with his own image and
likeness, and made him a citizen of Eden, and made him a companion of angels;
but since we darkened and confused the likeness of the divine image with the
mire of the passions, the one compassionate toward us has shared a second
communion, much more secure and more paradoxical than the former. For
remaining himself in the superiority of his own Godhead, he partakes of the
worse, deifying the human in himself, and the archetype is mixed with the
image, and he shows his own beauty in it today. And his face shines as the
sun; for he is identified hypostatically with immaterial light, and from there
he has become sun of righteousness; and the garments are whitened as snow; for
they have been glorified by clothing, not by union, by relation, and no longer
according to hypostasis. And a cloud of light overshadowed, painting the
brightness of the Spirit. For somehow thus the divine Apostle said that the
sea bears the image of the water, and the cloud of the Spirit. And all things
are full of light and exceeding bright to those receptive of light, and not
having the soul defiled with the filth of conscience.

εʹ. Come then, and let us too emulate =96.553= the obedience of the disciples,
and eagerly follow Christ as he calls, and shake off the crowd of the
passions, and confess the Son of the living God without shame, and having
become worthy of the promise, let us go up in love to the mountain of the
virtues, and become spectators of the glory, and hearers of the unutterable
things. For truly blessed, as the Lord said, are the eyes of those who see,
because they see, and the ears, because they hear, things which many prophets
and kings desired to see and hear, and did not attain the desire. Come then,
having unfolded as far as possible the sayings of the divine oracles, let us
set a table for us the good banqueters, who stretch all their appetite toward
the divine things. Let us set a table suited to the desire. Let us set a table
of divine oracles seasoned with the grace of the Spirit, not priding itself on
the wisdom of Hellenic words; since we have not been much initiated into the
knowledge of these, but relying on the grace of him who gives


a clear tongue to the stammerers to speak.

ϛʹ. In Caesarea of Philip (this is Paneas, once most renowned among cities,
which was named the city of Philip Caesar; and called Dan in the divine
Scripture; for David, it says, numbered the people from Dan to Beersheba; in
this the Master, having come with the servants, dried the fountain of the
hemorrhaging woman's bloods), in this, having gathered the first assembly of
his own disciples, and the rock of life having improvised the seat upon a
certain rock, he asked his disciples, saying: Whom do men say that I the Son
of man am? Not being ignorant of the ignorance of men, he who knows all things
asked, but wishing to dissolve this by the light of knowledge, as a certain
gloom lying upon the noetic eyes. And they said that some announced him as
John the Baptist, others Elijah, others Jeremiah, or one of the prophets. For
since they saw so great an abyss of wonders, they supposed he was one of the
prophets of old, raised from the dead, and from there had been counted worthy
of such grace. And this is clear; For Herod the tetrarch, it says, heard the
report of Jesus, and said to his servants: This is John the Baptist; he has
been raised from the dead, and therefore the powers work in him. Trying to
dissolve this suspicion, and to grant to the ignorant the true confession, as
a gift, a gift most excellent of all, what does he do, in whose hand all
things are possible? As man he puts forward the question; as God he secretly
makes wise the one first called, and who first followed, whom by his own
foreknowledge he predestined as worthy president of the Church. To this one
=96.556= as God he breathes, and through him he speaks. And what is the
question? But you, whom do you say that I am? And Peter, burning with fiery
zeal, and being God-borne by the Holy Spirit, said: You are the Christ, the
Son of the living God. O blessed mouth! O all-blessed lips! O God-bearing
mind, worthy of the divine initiation! O instruments through which the Father
uttered! Truly you are blessed, Simon, son of Jonah (for this the unlying one
declared), because not flesh, not blood, not human thought, but my Father in
heaven revealed to you this divine and unutterable theology. For no one knows
the Son except the one known by him alone, the Father who begot, and the Holy
Spirit, who also knows the depths of God. This is the faith unwavering and
unshaken, upon which as a rock the Church has been established, of which you
have worthily become namesake. The gates of Hades, mouths of heretics,
instruments of demons, will run against this, but will not prevail; they will
arm themselves, but will not sack it. Their blows have become and will be the
arrows of infants. Their tongues will be weakened, and will be against
themselves. For he who sets himself against the truth fashions evils against
himself. This he acquired by his own blood, and he entrusts it to you as a
most faithful servant. Guard this unshaken and unstormed by your prayers; that
it will never be overturned, will not be shaken, will not be sacked, the
confidence is sure. Christ spoke, through whom heaven has been made firm and
earth has been founded, and remains unmoved. For by the word of the Lord the
heavens were made firm, said the Holy Spirit. But we ask that the surge be
calmed, the tumult be shattered, and a calm and waveless peace be awarded to
us. Beseech this of Christ, the undefiled bridegroom of this Church, who
appointed you key-bearer of the kingdom of the heavens, who granted you the
charge of binding and loosing, whom with God-speaking mouth you proclaimed
unlyingly Son of the living God. O divine and unutterable things! He himself
announced himself Son of man, and Peter, rather the one speaking in him,


proclaimed him Son of God. For he is truly God and man; he was not called son
of Peter, nor of Paul, nor of Joseph, nor of any father, but of man; for he
who had no mother in the heavens had no father upon the earth.

ζʹ. Wishing then the word to be confirmed by deed, and knowing what he is
about to do, the all-working wisdom and power of God, in whom all the
treasures of knowledge are hidden. There are some, he said, of those standing
here, who shall not taste death until they see the Son of man coming in his
kingdom. If then, as he said of one, =96.557= There is someone of those
standing here, we would have supposed it to signify the same as, If I will
that he remain until I come, what is that to you? said concerning John the
Theologian, as remaining without tasting death until the coming of Christ; for
thus already some of the exceedingly wise have taken that. But since the word
indicated more who would see, and moreover the deed followed, it will not give
room to those who wish to understand the interpretation of the word thus.
There are then some of those standing here, he says; for what reason some, and
not all, were called to this vision? Were not all disciples and apostles? Did
not all, being called, follow likewise? Did not all obtain the equal gift of
healings? And how were not all counted worthy also of this sight beyond sight?
Is not the Master no respecter of persons? All were disciples, but not all
were blinded by the disease of love of money. All were disciples, but not all
had the sharpness of the eye taken away by the rheum of envy. All were
disciples, but not all were traitors. All were apostles, but not all were
pierced with a noose through despair, and correcting evil with evil. All were
lovers of Christ, but one a lover of money; this Judas Iscariot, one alone was
not worthy of the vision of the Godhead. For let the ungodly be taken away, it
says, that he may not see the glory of the Lord. Being left then alone of the
rest, the envious and jealous one was kindled into greater madness. But it was
necessary that all who would later be spectators of the sufferings should
become beholders of the glory. From here he takes the chiefs of the apostles
as witnesses of his own glory and brightness; three in number, hinting at the
august mystery of the Trinity; and upon two or three witnesses every word
shall be established. Thus he shuts off from the traitor the charges of the
betrayal. And he reveals to the apostles his own Godhead. For seeing Andrew
remaining below with the rest, this one could not say, under pretext of
defense, that he was led to the selling of the Master because he did not
obtain the vision. For this reason Andrew remained below, and all the rest of
the chorus of the apostles, being separated in place bodily, but united by the
bond of love, spending time below with the body, but following the teacher
above with longing, and with the motions of the soul.

ηʹ. And after six days, Matthew and Mark the divine evangelists write; but
Luke the most wise: It came to pass after these words about eight days.
Properly and truly eight, and six days, are announced by the heralds of the
truth. For the saying is not disagreement, =96.560= but agreement brought
forth from the same Spirit. For it is not they who speak, but the Spirit of
God who speaks in them. For when the Paraclete comes, he says, he will teach
you, and will remind you of all things. Those then who said after six days,
subtracting the extremes, I mean the first and last, numbered the middle ones;
but he who numbers eight days has made both these and those countable. For
thus and thus it is customary for men to number. And six is taken as first and
perfect number. For this is completed from its own parts. For of this three is
the half, two the third, and the


sixth one, which being gathered together conclude it perfect. From here those
skilled in these things have named the number six perfect. But also in six
days God by a word worked the system of all things seen. And it is likely that
those who beheld the divine glory, that which is beyond all, the only
hyper-perfect and pre-perfect, were perfect. For become, he says, perfect, as
your Father in heaven is perfect. And eight; for it also bears the type of the
age to come. For the present life is concluded in seven ages. And the eighth
has been announced as the life to come, as Gregory great in theology said,
interpreting the Solomonian saying, give a portion to the seven, saying to the
present life, and also to the eight, to the one to come. And it was necessary
that on the eighth the things of the eighth be revealed to the perfect. For as
the truly divine and God-speaking Dionysius said, thus the Master will be seen
by his perfect servants, in the manner in which he has been beheld by the
apostles on Mount Tabor. You have the numbering of the days.

θʹ. But why did he take Peter, and James, and John? Peter, wishing to show the
testimony which he truly testified, attested from the Father, confirming also
his own declaration, that the heavenly Father revealed this testimony to him,
and as president, who would receive the helm of the whole Church; James, as
about to die for Christ before all the disciples, and to drink his cup, and to
be baptized with the baptism on his behalf. John, as a virgin of theology and
a most pure instrument, that having seen the timeless glory of the Son of God,
he might thunder, In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God,
and the Word was God; whence he has also been called son of thunder.

ιʹ. And why does he lead the disciples up onto a high mountain? The divine
Scripture tropically names the virtues mountains. And of all the virtues the
summit and acropolis is love. For in this perfection is defined. For if one
should speak with the tongues of men and of angels; even if he have faith so
as to move mountains, and all knowledge, and know all the =96.561= mysteries,
and give his body to be burned, but have not love, he has become sounding
brass, or a clanging cymbal without articulation, and will be counted as
nothing. One must then leave the things of dust to the dust and having risen
above the body of humiliation, and having been led up to the highest and
divine lookout of love, thus behold the unseen things. For he who has come to
be at the peak of love, going out of himself in a certain manner, contemplates
the invisible, and flying over the obstructing gloom of the bodily cloud,
and having come to be in the clear sky of the soul, gazes more clearly upon
the sun of righteousness, even if he has not been able to be wholly filled
with the vision.

privately and to pray. For quiet is the mother of prayer, and prayer is a
manifestation of divine glory. For when we close the senses, and come to be
with ourselves and with God, and having been freed from the outer circuit of
the world, come to be within ourselves, then we shall see clearly in ourselves
the kingdom of God. For the kingdom of the heavens, which is the kingdom of
God, is within you, Jesus our God uttered. Servants then prayed in one way,
and the Master in another. For servants approach the Master in fear and
longing through petition, and prayer becomes for the mind a provider of
departure toward God and of union, feeding it through itself and strengthening
it. But the holy mind united according to hypostasis to God the Word, how
shall it pray? And how shall the Master stand begging? Or it is clear that
appropriating our person, and training us, and making a way toward the ascent
to God through prayer, and teaching that prayer becomes


a provider of the divine glory, and showing that he is not opposed to God, and
honoring the Begetter as his beginning and cause, and yielding to the flesh to
go through its own nature, that through itself it may both be nerved, and be
trained, and come to be in initiation and habit of the better things. And
moreover also baiting the tyrant who watches carefully whether he might be
God. For this the power of the wonders proclaimed. For this reason everywhere
he mixes the human things with the divine, hiding the hook as in some food.
For thus he who baited man with the hope of Godhead will reasonably be baited
by the assumption of flesh. For seeing him flashing with prayer, he also
remembered Moses whose face was glorified. But Moses was glorified with the
glory coming from without, whereas the Lord Jesus did not have the adornment
of glory as acquired, but from the innate brightness of the divine glory.

ιαʹ. But what the invocation is, let the prophet David teach you, addressing
thus clearly: He shall call upon me: You are my Father, my God, and the helper
of my salvation. Father as of God and Son before the ages, and of him who
shone forth from the Begetter's essence; but God and helper of salvation, as
of him who was incarnate, and is renewing our =96.564= nature in himself, and
raising it to the ancient beauty of the image, and bearing in himself the
common person of humanity. Wherefore he also added: And I will make him
firstborn. For he was named firstborn among many brethren, who shared flesh
and blood likewise with us. As God the Word: being always Son, he did not
become so later; but as man, he is said also to have become later, that the
property of sonship may remain unchanged in him. For he himself having become
flesh, the flesh having become of the Son of God from the first moment of
existence through the union according to hypostasis, he himself is said to
have become this, being divinely, becoming as man.

ιβʹ. Taking then on Mount Tabor those who excelled in the height of the
virtues, he was transfigured before them. He is transfigured before the
disciples, he who is always likewise glorified and shining with the lightning
of the Godhead. For having been begotten from the Father without beginning, he
possesses the natural ray of the Godhead without beginning, not receiving
being later, nor again glory. For he is from the Father, but without beginning
and timelessly, possessing the brightness of his own glory; and having been
incarnate he is the same, remaining in the same of the divine radiance. And
the flesh is glorified together with the bringing from not-being into being,
and the glory of the Godhead is also called glory of the body. For Christ is
one, this and that, consubstantial with the Father, and connatural and of the
same race with us. If then the holy body has never existed without share in
the divine glory, but from the extreme union according to hypostasis has been
perfectly enriched with the glory of the invisible Godhead, so that the glory
of the Word and of the flesh is one and the same, yet the glory being
invisible in the appearing body was invisible to those who cannot contain the
things unseen even by angels, prisoners of the flesh. He is transfigured then,
not taking on what he was not, but revealing what he was to his own disciples,
opening their eyes, and making seeing men of the blind. And this is, He was
transfigured before them. For remaining himself in identity, other than what
he appeared before, he was now seen appearing otherwise to the disciples.

ιγʹ. And his face shone as the sun, of him who with much authority made the
sun a torch-bearer; of him who created the light before the sun, and afterward
crafted the sun as a luminary, a receptacle of light. For he himself is the
true light, eternally begotten of true and immaterial =96.565= light, the
enhypostatic Word from the Father, the radiance of glory, the natural imprint
of the hypostasis of God and Father; of this one the


face shone. What do you say, O evangelist? Why do you compare the things truly
incomparable? Why do you set beside and parallel the things truly
unparalleled? Did the Master shine as a household servant? Did the unbearable
and unapproachable light flash as this sun seen by all? But I do not parallel,
he says, nor compare the only only-begotten, and by nothing to be likened, the
radiance of the divine glory, but speaking to prisoners of flesh, I bring as
an example whatever of bodies is most beautiful and brightest, not altogether
most like; for it is impossible for the uncreated to be imaged without
remainder in the creation; but as the sun is one, and has two essences, both
of the light which was generated first, and of the body that lagged behind in
the creation, and through the whole body the light is united indivisibly, and
while the body remains upon itself the light is spread to all the ends of the
earth, so also Christ, being light from light without beginning and
unapproachable, having come to be in a temporal and created body, is one sun
of righteousness, one Christ, known in two natures indivisibly. The holy body
then is circumscribed. For standing on Tabor, it did not reach outside the
mountain; but the Godhead is uncontained, being in all things, and beyond all.
And the body shines as the sun; for the flashing of the light has come to be
of the body. For all things have become common of the one incarnate God the
Word, both the things of the flesh and of the uncircumscribed Godhead; but it
is one thing from which the boasts of glory are common, and another from which
the passions are known as common. And the divine prevails, and imparts of its
own brightness and glory to the body, itself remaining without share of
passion even in the passions. Thus his face shone as the sun; not that it was
not brighter than the sun, but as much as those seeing were able to behold.
For how, if he had shown all the radiance of the glory, would they not have
been burned up? His face shone as the sun. For what the sun is among
sensibles, this God is among intelligibles. And his garments became white as
the light. As the sun is one thing; for it is a fountain of light, not able to
be seen clearly; and another is the light that comes from it to earth; for it
is seen and looked upon by the energy of God's wisdom and love of man, that we
may not be altogether without share of the good things; so the face shines
most clearly as the sun, and the garments are whitened as light, made splendid
by a sharing of the divine light.

ιδʹ. These things being accomplished thus, that he might be shown Lord of old
and new covenant, and that the mouths of the heretics whose throats are an
opened tomb might be stopped, and that the resurrection of the dead might be
believed, =96.568= and that he attested from the Father might be believed
Master of living and dead, Moses and Elijah stand by as servants to a Master
in glory, and speaking with him they are seen by their fellow-servants. For it
was necessary that they, having seen the glory and boldness of those who were
fellow-servants, but servants of God, should be struck by the Master's
philanthropic condescension, and emulate more, and be nerved toward the
contests. For he who knows the fruits of the toils would most easily dare the
contests. For the desire of gain knows how to lead toward unsparingness of the
body. For as soldiers, and boxers, and farmers, and merchants take hold of the
toils with much eagerness, and dare the surge of the sea, and do not heed
beasts and pirates, that they may obtain the longed-for gain, and as much as
they see those who have toiled before luxuriating in the gains, they are
whetted much more toward endurance of the toils; so also the spiritual
shield-bearers of the Master, and boxers, and farmers, and merchants, not
aiming at earthly gains, nor desiring flowing goods, when they behold with
their own eyes the things lying in hopes, and


those who have toiled before enjoying the luxury of the hoped-for good things,
will be sent more eagerly toward the contests, not arraying themselves against
men, nor beating the air, nor leading plough-oxen under the yoke, and with
these cutting the furrows of the earth, nor sailing the swell of marine seas,
but contending against the principalities, against the powers, against the
world-rulers of the darkness, and rejoicing to be struck, and gathering wealth
by being stripped, and setting the helm of the cross against the triple-waves
of the world and the spirits of wickedness that move them, and driving away
beasts as it were roaring and seizing by the power of the Spirit, and sowing
the word of piety in the hearts of men as in certain furrows, and reaping for
the Master a plentiful ear. But let us return to the matter at hand.

ιεʹ. Then Moses, as is likely, uttered. Hear, O noetic Israel, things which
the sensible Israel has not been able to hear. The Lord your God, the Lord is
one. For one God is known in three hypostases. For one is the essence of
Godhead of the one attesting, and of the Son attested, and of the Spirit
overshadowing. This is the one now attested by the Father, the life of men.
This hanging upon wood the ungrateful will see, and they will not believe in
their life. From here Elijah also answered: This is he whom of old I beheld as
in a thin breeze, bodiless, by the spirit. For no one has ever seen =96.569=
God, according as he is by nature. And what one has seen, this he has beheld
in the spirit. This is the alteration of the right hand of the Most High.
These are things which eye has not seen, and ear has not heard, and have never
gone up upon the heart of man. Thus in the age to come we shall always be with
the Lord, seeing Christ flashing with the light of the Godhead.

ιϛʹ. And what of Peter, having become spectator of this divine revelation? As
one inspired by the spirit, It is good for us to be here, he said to the Lord.
For who exchanges gloom for light? You see this sun how beautiful, how fair,
how sweet, how longed-for, how flashing and exceeding bright, and life how
sweet and lovely, to which all hold, and do all things so as not to miss this;
how much more do you think the light itself, from which all light is
illumined, more longed-for and sweeter, the self-life, from which all life is
made alive, and is imparted, in which we all live, and move, and are? how much
more lovely and sweeter? Not at all does desire, nor thought, guess the
measure of the superiority. It conquers every comparison, and is not
overpassed by measures. For how could the uncircumscribed be measured, and the
incomprehensible by thoughts themselves? This light has the victories against
all nature. This is the life that has conquered the world; how then is it not
good not to be separated from the good? Not unreasonably then did Peter speak;
but since all things are beautiful in their season, and there is a season for
every matter, Solomon uttered; and it was necessary that the good not be shut
up only to those there, but that the good be poured out and travel upon all,
that is those who believe, so that more might be those who would share the
benefaction; and this the cross, and passion, and death were about to
accomplish, it was not good for him who would redeem his own creature by his
own blood, for which he had also been incarnate, to remain there. If you had
remained on Mount Tabor, the promise toward you would not have come to
fulfillment. For you would not have become key-bearer of the kingdom; paradise
would not have been opened to the thief; the insolent tyranny of death would
not have been abolished; the palaces of Hades would not have been given for
plunder; patriarchs, and prophets, and righteous would not have been released
from the recesses of Hades; nature would not have


been clothed with incorruption. If Adam had not sought deification before the
time, he would have attained the desire. Do not seek the good things before
the time, O Peter. There will be a time, when you will receive this vision
unceasing. The Master did not appoint you adorner of tents, but of a worldwide
Church. Your disciples, your sheep, which the good chief-shepherd entrusted to
you, have brought your words to work, having crafted a tent for Christ, and
for Moses and =96.572= Elijah his servants, in which we keep festival. Peter
did not speak these things by reasoning, but by inspiration of the Spirit who
foretells the things to come. For not knowing what he says, says Luke the most
divine. And Mark also adds the cause: For they were, he says, exceedingly
afraid. As Simon was speaking these things, Behold a luminous cloud
overshadowed, and the disciples were held with greater fear, seeing Jesus the
Savior, with Moses and Elijah, having come to be within the cloud.

ιζʹ. Of old then the God-seer entered the divine gloom, hinting at the shadowy
of the law. For the law had a shadow of the things to come, not the truth
itself, hear Paul writing. And then Israel was not able to gaze at the glory
of Moses' face that was being abolished; but we with unveiled face behold as
in a mirror the glory of the Lord, being transfigured from glory to greater
glory, even as from the Lord the Spirit. From here also a cloud, not of gloom;
for it did not threaten; but of light overshadowed. For the mystery hidden
from the ages and the generations has been unveiled, and a continuous and
everlasting glory is shown. Wherefore also Moses stands by with Elijah,
fulfilling the person of law and of prophets. For he whom the law and the
prophets proclaim, this one is found, the life-giving Jesus. And Moses images
the company of the saints long fallen asleep, and Elijah of the living. For
the Lord who is transfigured is of living and dead. Moses entered into the
land of promise; for Jesus the inheritor led him in; and things which of old
he beheld typically, he is clearly illumined with today. For this the
light-shining of the cloud hints.

ιηʹ. And a voice came from the cloud saying: This is my beloved Son, in whom I
am well pleased, hear him. A voice of the Father came from the cloud of the
Spirit: This is my beloved Son; this one who is, and is seen as man, who
yesterday became man, the humble one who consorts with us, whose face now
shone; this is my beloved Son, the pre-eternal, the only only-begotten of
only, who came forth timelessly and eternally from me the Begetter, who from
me, and in me, and with me, always is, and does not lag behind in existence.
From me, as begotten from my essence and hypostasis from a paternal cause;
wherefore also consubstantial; in me, as being begotten inseparably and
without departure; with me, as existing a self-complete hypostasis, not an
uttered word poured into air; wherefore also beloved. For what son is beloved,
as the only-begotten? In this one I am well pleased. For by the Father's good
pleasure his Only-begotten and Word has been incarnate; for by the Father's
good pleasure =96.573= he worked the worldwide salvation in his only-begotten
Son; the Father's good pleasure in the only-begotten Son composed the
conjunction of all things. For if man has been by nature a small world,
bearing in himself the bond of every essence both visible and invisible, being
both this and that, truly the master, and craftsman, and ruler of all was well
pleased in his only-begotten and consubstantial Son that there should come to
be a conjunction of Godhead and of humanity, and through this, of all
creation, that God may be all in all. This is my Son, the radiance of my
glory, the imprint of my hypostasis, through whom I have also made the angels,
through whom heaven has been made firm, and earth has been founded; who bears
all things by the word of his


power, and by the spirit of his mouth, the life-originating and ruling spirit,
that is. Hear him. For he who receives this one receives me, who sent him, not
as a master, but as a father. For as man he is sent, but as God he remains in
me, and I in him. He who does not honor my only-begotten and beloved Son does
not honor the Father, me who sent him. Hear him; for he has words of eternal
life. This is the conclusion of the things accomplished, this is the power of
the mystery.

ιθʹ. Then what? He sent Moses and Elijah away to their own places, and he is
seen only to the apostles, and thus they come down from the mountain, speaking
to no one anything of the things seen or heard. For this the Master commands.
For what reason, and for whose sake, I will say. Knowing, I think, the
disciples as imperfect; for they had not yet obtained the perfect sharing of
the Spirit; that grief might not fill their hearts; that the rage of envy
might not madden the traitor. Come then, and let us too bring the words to a
close with the subject.

κʹ. But you, always remember the things that have been said, bearing in the
heart the beauty of that divine vision, always sounding with the paternal
voice, This is not a servant, not an ambassador, not an angel, but my beloved
Son, hear him. Let us hear him then saying: You shall love the Lord your God
with all your heart. You shall not murder; but you shall not even be angry
with your brother without cause; be reconciled with your brother first, and
thus coming offer your gift. You shall not commit adultery; but you shall not
even busy yourself with another's beauty. You shall not swear falsely; but you
shall not even swear at all; and let your Yes be yes, and your No, no; and the
excess is a finding of the evil one. You shall not bear false witness. You
shall not deprive; but also give to him who asks you, and do not turn away him
who wishes to borrow from you, and do not hinder him who takes your things.
Love your enemies, bless those who curse you, do good =96.576= to those who
hate you, and pray for those who despitefully use you and persecute you. Do
not judge, that you may not be judged. Forgive, and it will be forgiven you,
that you may become sons of your Father, perfect and merciful, as your Father
in heaven, because he makes his sun rise upon evil and good, and rains upon
just and unjust. Let us keep these divine commandments with all watch, that we
too may luxuriate in his divine beauty, being filled with his sweetest taste,
now as far as is possible for those burdened with this earthly tent of the
body, and afterward more clearly and more purely, when the righteous shine as
the sun, when having been released from the necessities of the body, they will
be as angels with the Lord incorrupt, in the great and manifest revelation
from the heavens of him our Lord, and God, and Savior Jesus Christ, to whom be
the glory and the power now, and always, and unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
