will begin then from the letter written by him to speak against each of the
things not rightly written. He has written that he believes in Father God
Almighty, and in his Son the only-begotten God, our Lord Jesus Christ, and in
the Holy Spirit; and he says he has learned this manner of piety from the
divine scriptures. And I, whenever he says this, accept the things said
exceedingly, for this is the common manner of piety of all of us, to believe
in Father and Son and Holy Spirit; but whenever, not having aimed at the
divine power, he says in a more human way to us through some artful theory
both the Father Father and the Son Son, it is no longer without danger to
praise such a theory. For through such a theory it happens that the heresy now
invented by them is increased, which I think it easy to show clearly from his
own words. For he said that one must think the Father to be truly Father and
the Son truly Son and the Holy Spirit likewise. 66 For it is impossible that
three hypostases, being, be united to a monad, unless first the triad should
have the beginning from a monad. For the holy Paul said that those things are
summed up in a monad which differ in nothing from God in the unity; for in
unity the Word and the Spirit alone belong to God. 67 If then the Word should
appear having gone forth from the Father himself and having come to us, and
the Holy Spirit, as Asterius also confessed, proceeds from the Father, and
again the Savior says concerning the Spirit that "he will not speak from
himself, but as many things as he hears he will speak, and he will announce to
you the things coming. He will glorify me, because he will take from what is
mine and will announce to you", does not the monad appear here clearly and
manifestly yet by an ineffable word, being broadened into a triad, but in no
way enduring to be divided? For if the Word proceeds from the Father, and the
Spirit also is itself confessed to proceed from the Father, and again the
Savior says concerning the Spirit "he will take from what is mine and will

that the eternity according to spirit has been united to the Father. 71 For if
the examination of the spirit should come to be alone, the Word would
reasonably appear to be one and the same with God; but if the addition
according to the flesh should be examined in the case of the Savior, the
Godhead seems to be broadened by activity alone, so that reasonably there is
truly an indivisible monad. 72 For Asterius declared the Father and the Son to
be one and the same according to this only, according to which they agree in
all things. For he said thus: and because of the accurate agreement in all
words and works "I and the Father are one". 73 If then he himself says these
things "I went forth from the Father and have come", and again "and the word
which you hear is not mine, but of the Father who sent me" and "all things
whatsoever the Father has are mine", it is clear that reasonably he also said
that "the Father is in me and I in the Father", in order that the Word saying
this may be in God, and the Father in the Word, because the Word is power of
the Father. For a trustworthy witness has called him "power of God and wisdom
of God". Not therefore because of the accurate agreement in all words and
works, as Asterius said, does the Savior say "I and the Father are one", but
because it is impossible that either a word of God or God be partitioned from
his own word. Since if Asterius thinks the Savior has said this because of the
agreement in all things, and does not wish, attending to the second economy,
to learn the truth, it is necessary to remind him how sometimes [the]
according to the [your] appearing it is possible to see disagreement. For thus
the sayings teach us. For what agreement at the time of the passion of him
saying this "Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass", and also bringing
on that "yet not as I will, but as you". For it was of one not agreeing first
to say "let this cup pass"; and the thing brought on also seems to have
nothing holding to agreement, for he says "not mine, but yours be done,
Father, will". You hear how the letter makes clear a disagreement according to
the appearing, of the one wishing. and of the one not wishing. For that the
Father wished is clear from the things of which what he wished has come to be;
and that the Son did not wish is clear through the things by which he
declines. And again "I do not seek" he says "my will but the will of the
Father who sent me". How then does he say that the Savior has said "I and the
Father are one" because of the agreement in all things. 74 How can the Son
have agreement toward the Father or the Father toward the Son, when the Son
says "all things whatsoever the Father has are mine"? For it was of one openly
taking advantage of the Father for the Son to say "all things whatsoever the
Father has are mine". For this reason, having omitted to say all things
whatsoever the Father has are common, he said "all things whatsoever the
Father has are mine". And yet it was not proper to the one who agrees to speak
thus, but: all things whatsoever the Father has are common. For if the Acts of
the apostles, praising the


agreement of those then coming to the faith, said "all things were common to
them", and in the case of men able to agree one ought to think that all things
are common, how much more ought the Father and the Son to partake of
fellowship, divided into two hypostases? But now in saying "all things
whatsoever the Father has are mine" the Son appears taking advantage of the
Father; and in alleging that he is not even lord of his own word, but that the
Father is lord of this also (for "the word which you hear" he says "is not
mine but of the Father who sent me"), he shows the Father taking away the
child's own things. And both according to the opinion of Asterius appear not
to have been said consequentially. For the one who agrees ought not to snatch
away the rights belonging to the other, for this at least is greedy, but to
think that the things belonging to each are common. So that whenever we look
to the human flesh, we will not find the Savior to have spoken as Asterius has
written, "I and the Father are one", for it is not because of the accurate
agreement in all words and works, as he himself has written, that the Savior
has said "I and the Father are one". For if this were so, he would altogether
have said: I and the Father agree with one another in all things. But now he
said "I and the Father are one". Therefore if in those things there was some
disagreement, and it is necessary that the Master speak truly, one ought to
know the Savior accurately, that whenever he says "I and the Father are one",
then he says this not looking to the man whom he has taken up, but to the Word
who has come forth from the Father. For if some disagreement should seem to
be, this ought to be referred to the weakness of the flesh, which the Word,
not having formerly, has taken up. And if unity should be spoken of, this
appears as belonging to the Word. Whence he reasonably said not only "I and
the Father are one", but also that: "so long a time I am with you, Philip, and
you say: show me the Father", clearly not with these eyes, but with the
intellectual ones able to see the intellectual things. For both the Father and
his Word are invisible to the eyes of the flesh. Not therefore because of the
agreement in all things did he say this to Philip. 75 For he himself confesses
saying "the Father is in me and I in the Father"; and that he has not said
this simply nor aimlessly is clear also from another apostolic saying; for the
one who said "one Lord, one faith, one baptism" said "one God and Father, who
is over all and through all and in all". You see that not even here does he
stand away from the agreement, but here also he has suffered the same. For
having said "one Lord" he again said "one God", in order that whenever he
mentions the one Lord he may include also the Father, and whenever he speaks
concerning the Father he may bear witness that the Word of God is not outside.
76 And if you wish also to hear another prophecy of the same confirming one
God for us, "I God" he says "first, and unto the things coming I am"; for the
I is indicative of one person, for the two sayings signify one person for us.
For having said "I" he also brings on the "am", so that through the two parts
of speech, both pronoun and verb, the monad of the Godhead is borne witness.
And if there should be need also of another testimony, I will again furnish
the same prophet to him saying "I first and I after these things, and besides
me there is no God". If Asterius thinks the Son divided in hypostasis from the
Father as a son of man, being scandalized from the human flesh which he took
up for us, let him show us the one saying these things; for here too the
saying spoken is of one person. Who then is the one saying "there is no God
besides me"? And let him hear also another prophecy saying "there is none
righteous and savior besides me". If he should think there are two Gods, it is
necessary that he confess the other not to be righteous nor savior. And if he
is not righteous nor savior, how can he still be God? For he declares one to
be righteous and savior. And again "before me" he says "another has not come
to be, and after me there will not be, I God, and there will not be one who
saves besides me". And if he wishes also to hear another prophetic


saying spoken perhaps somewhere toward him and those likewise disposed
concerning Godhead with him, let him hear Isaiah himself saying "repent, you
who wander, turn in the heart, and remember the former things from the age,
that I am God and there is none besides me". He did not say: I God, in order
that also through the addition of the article he might show clearly that God
is one. And what does Hosea the prophet? Does not he himself also bear witness
to the same things, saying "I led you up from Egypt and you shall not know a
God besides me, and there is none who saves besides me"? And again Malachi
says "did not one God create you? is there not one Father of you all?" But
Asterius will say somewhere that David has said nothing concerning this,
although being oldest after Moses of the other prophets, and that for this
reason he is in doubt, whether it is proper to think there are two Gods
divided in hypostasis or not. Therefore in order that he may not say this, I
think it consequent to show him also himself saying the same things as the
holy ones aforementioned: "hear," he says "my people, and I will speak to you;
Israel, and I will bear witness to you. If you hear me, there shall not be in
you a recent God, nor shall you worship a strange God. For I am the Lord your
God". He who shows himself and says "I am", is he not clearly saying that
there is only one God, that is, himself? 77 What then? If, not attending to
the Spirit, we should think the monad to be indivisible in power, shall we not
go wrong, when the Word clearly teaches us "you shall worship the Lord your
God and him only shall you serve"? And he proclaims the same also through the
Gospel according to Mark; for when a certain one scribe came to him and
inquired what was the first of the commandments, he answered him saying thus
"first of all: hear, Israel, the Lord our God is one Lord, and you shall love
the Lord your God from your whole soul and from your whole strength. This is
first; and a second like it: you shall love your neighbor as yourself. There
is no other commandment greater than these. And the scribe said to him: well,
teacher, you have said upon truth that God is one and there is none besides
him". But the scribe, thinking he has learned piety through the law, appears
praising the saying of the Savior "hear, Israel", who says "the Lord your God
is one" and confirming by an oath that it has been well said; for "upon truth"
he says "you have said that God is one and there is no other besides him". But
those who boast that they know the mysteries of the new covenant, these even
wish to fashion a second God separated in hypostasis and power from the
Father. 78 And that the divine scripture knows to call the monad Lord and God
has already become clear also from the things aforementioned, through which
God said to his own servant Moses "and God said again to Moses: thus shall you
say to the sons of Israel: the Lord the God of your fathers, the God of
Abraham and the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you". You
see how, showing us here one person, he addresses the same as Lord and God.
And again likewise the scripture says "and the Lord spoke all these words
saying: I am the Lord your God, who brought you out from the land of Egypt,
from a house of slavery. There shall not be for you other gods besides me";
you hear how through the pronoun he declares that there is only one God. And
again a little later "I am the Lord your God" he says, saying that he himself
is Lord and God. And what do we learn also through another scripture? "And you
shall know today" he says "and you shall not be distorted in mind, that the
Lord your God, this one is God in the heaven above and on the earth below, and
there is none besides him". And again in the same Deuteronomy "hear, Israel"
he says "the Lord our God is one Lord; and you shall love the Lord your God
from your whole heart and from your whole soul and from your whole mind". And
again in the same "see, see that I am, and there is no God besides me; I will
kill and I will make to live; I will strike and I will heal". How then does
Asterius, pretending to follow the holy scriptures simply and fearfully, not
know this part saying "the Lord your God, this one is God in the heaven above
and on the earth below, and there is not still besides


him", and that "he is one" and "besides him there is no other"? 79 How then
will the holy prophet Jeremiah not openly refute him as teaching otherwise?
For prophesying to us the things concerning the Savior he said thus "this is
our God, another shall not be reckoned toward him. He found every way of
knowledge, and gave it to Jacob his child and to Israel the one beloved by
him. After these things he was seen upon the earth, and consorted with men".
80 So that even if someone says that, constructing a first God and a second,
as Narcissus has written in those very words (for neither does the one saying
"let us make man according to our image and according to likeness" allow it),
that he himself and his Father are two, we have heard in part from the

for this reason should wish to divide in power the
Word from the Father, let him know that the prophet who wrote as of God having
said "let us make man according to our image and according to likeness",
himself has written "and God made the man". 81 For having met with a letter of

down "in the last of the days" for our salvation and having been born from the
virgin Mary, took the man. I believe then in God Almighty; and in Christ Jesus
his Son the only-begotten, our Lord, who was born from Holy Spirit and Mary
the virgin, who was crucified under Pontius Pilate and buried and on the third
day rose from the dead, who went up into the heavens and sits at the right of
the Father, from where he comes to judge living and dead; and in the Holy
Spirit, holy church, forgiveness of sins, resurrection of flesh, life eternal.
We have learned from the divine scriptures that the Godhead of the Father and
of the Son is indivisible. For if someone separates the Son, that is the Word,
from the Almighty God, it is necessary that he either think there are two Gods
(which has been thought to be


alien to the divine teaching) or confess that the Word is not God (which
itself also appears alien to the right faith, the evangelist saying "and the
Word was God"). And I have learned accurately that the Son is, as power,
indivisible and inseparable from the Father. For the Savior himself, our Lord
Jesus Christ, says "the Father is in me, and I in the Father" and "I and the
Father are one" and "he who has seen me has seen the Father". This faith,
having received it both from the divine scriptures and having been taught by
the ancestors according to God, I both proclaim in the church of God and have
now written to you, having kept the copy of this with myself. And I ask that
you write the counterpart of this in the letter to the bishops, in order that
some of those who do not know us accurately, attending also to those things
written by them, may not be deceived. Farewell.
