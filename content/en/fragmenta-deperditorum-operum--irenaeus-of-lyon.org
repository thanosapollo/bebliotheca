1 I adjure you who will transcribe this book, by our Lord Jesus Christ, and by
his glorious advent, at which he comes to judge the living and the dead, that
you collate what you transcribed, and correct it carefully against this
exemplar from which you transcribed; and that you likewise transcribe this
oath, and set it in the copy. 2 These doctrines, Florinus, that I may speak
sparingly, are not of a sound mind; these doctrines are at variance with the
church, wrapping those who are persuaded by them in the greatest impiety;
these doctrines not even the heretics outside the church have ever dared to
declare; these doctrines the presbyters before us, who also consorted with the
apostles, did not hand down to you. For I saw you while still a boy, in lower
Asia with Polycarp, conducting yourself splendidly in the royal court, and
trying to win a good name with him. For I remember the things of that time
more than those that have happened recently, for the lessons from childhood,
growing together with the soul, are united to it, so that I can tell even the
place in which the blessed Polycarp sat and discoursed, and his goings out and
his comings in, and the character of his life, and the form of his body, and
the discourses which he made to the multitude, and his association with John
as he reported it, and that with the rest who had seen the Lord, and how he
remembered their words; and concerning the Lord what things they were that he
had heard from them, and concerning his mighty works, and concerning the
teaching, as Polycarp, having received them from the eyewitnesses of the life
of the Word, reported all things in agreement with the scriptures. These
things also then, through the mercy of God that had come upon me, I listened
to earnestly, noting them down, not on paper, but in my heart; and always
through the grace of God I ruminate on them genuinely. And I can bear witness
before God that if that blessed and apostolic presbyter had heard anything of
this kind, he would have cried out and stopped his ears, and according to his
custom said, O good God, unto what times have you kept me, that I should
endure these things, and he would have fled even the place in which, sitting
or standing, he had heard such words. And from his letters also, which he sent
either to the neighboring churches, strengthening them, or to some of the
brethren, admonishing them and exhorting them, it can be made plain. 3 For the
dispute is not only about the day, but also about the form of the fast itself.
For some think they must fast one day; some two, some even more, and some
forty; they measure their day by hours of day and of night. And such a variety
of those who observe has not come about now in our time, but also much earlier
among those before us, who, as is likely, holding not to exactness, have
carried the custom according to simplicity and private habit on into later
time; and none the less all these both made peace, and we make peace with one
another; and the disagreement of the fast commends the concord of the faith.
... And the presbyters before Soter who presided over the church of which you
now are leader, we mean Anicetus and Pius, Hyginus and Telesphorus, and
Xystus, neither observed it themselves, nor did they permit it to those after
them. And none the less they themselves, not observing, were at peace with
those from the parishes in which it was observed, who came to them, although
the observing was rather contrary for those who did not observe; and never
because of this form were any cast out. But they themselves, the presbyters
before you, not observing, used to send the eucharist to those from the
parishes who observed. And when the blessed Polycarp sojourned in Rome in the
time of Anicetus, and they had some small differences with one another about
certain other matters, they immediately made peace, not contending with one
another about this chapter. For neither could Anicetus persuade Polycarp not
to observe, inasmuch as with

John the disciple of our Lord, and the rest of the apostles with whom he had
lived, he had always observed; nor indeed did Polycarp persuade Anicetus to
observe, who said that he ought to hold the custom of the presbyters before
him. And things being so, they communed with one another; and in the church
Anicetus yielded the eucharist to Polycarp, out of respect clearly, and they
parted from one another in peace, the whole church having peace, both those
who observed and those who did not observe. 4 In whatever one could do good to
his neighbors, and does not, he will be reckoned a stranger to the love of the
Lord. 5 Will and energy of God is the creative and providential cause of every
time and place and age, and of every nature. Will is, of the rational soul,
the reason that is in our power, as being its self-determining faculty. Will
is an appetitive mind, and a deliberative appetite, inclining toward what is
willed. 7 And not to bend the knee on the Lord's day is a symbol of the
resurrection, through which by the grace of Christ we have been set free both
from sins and from the death that was put to death upon them. And from
apostolic times such a custom took its beginning, as the blessed Irenaeus, the
martyr and bishop of Lyons, says in the discourse concerning the Passover, in
which he mentions also Pentecost, in which we do not bend the knee, since it
is equivalent to the Lord's day, according to the cause stated concerning it.
8 For just as the ark was gilded within and without with pure gold, so also
the body of Christ was pure and translucent; adorned within by the Word, and
guarded without by the Spirit; that from both the brightness of the natures
might be shown. 9 If we always speak well of the worthy, and never ill of the
unworthy, we too shall obtain the glory and kingdom of God. 10 It is proper
and truly befitting to God to be propitious, to have mercy, to save his own
works, even if they are being borne down into the danger of corruption; For
with him, he says, is the propitiation. 11 The work of the Christian is
nothing else than to practice dying. 12 We then have believed that bodies also
rise. For even if it is corrupted, yet it is not destroyed; for the earth,
receiving the remains of these, keeps them, like seed being fattened and
intertwined with the richer part of the earth. Again, just as a bare grain is
sown, and at the command of God who created it, flourishing, it is raised
clothed and glorious, not before it has died and been dissolved and mixed with
earth; so that we have not believed the resurrection of the body in vain; But
even if it is dissolved for a time, because of the disobedience that came
about from the beginning, it is set as into a furnace of the earth, to be
formed again, not such as being corrupted, but pure, and no longer being
corrupted; so that to each body its own soul will be restored, and having put
this on it will not be distressed, but will rejoice, having remained pure,
having journeyed with a just bridegroom, and not a treacherous one; having in
all things with all ... it will receive, not altered, nor having passed over
from passion or disease, nor will it receive the bodies as glorious, but as
they died in sins or in right deeds; and of what sort they were, such, having
come to life again, they will put on, and of what sort they come to be in
unbelief, such they will faithfully be judged. 13 For Greeks, having seized
slaves of catechumen Christians, then compelling them to learn from these
something supposedly secret about Christians, these slaves, not having how to
say what was pleasing to those compelling them, inasmuch as they heard from
their masters that the divine communion is blood and body of Christ,
themselves supposing it to be in very truth blood and flesh, said this to
those inquiring. And they, taking it that this is actually performed by
Christians, even published this to the other Greeks, and compelled the martyrs
Sanctus and Blandina to confess it through tortures. To whom Blandina spoke
freely and aptly, saying, How could those endure these things who do not even
partake of the meats that are allowed, because of discipline? 14 How is it
possible to call the serpent, which was made by God by nature without reason
and without mind, rational and

capable of speech? If it used of itself, by its own working, the speech and
the discernment and the understanding and the answer of the things said by the
woman, then every serpent also has not been forbidden to do this. And if again
they shall say that by divine counsel and economy this one addressed Eve with
a human voice, they set God as cause of the sin. But neither is it permitted
to the evil demon to grant speech from non-being into being to an irrational
nature; since he would never have ceased, for deception, conversing with men
through serpents and beasts and birds and leading them astray. And how, being
a beast, did he hear the commandment given by God to the man, and to him
alone, mystically, when not even the woman herself had learned this? Why did
he not rather attack the man, and not the woman? And do not say that he ran
down upon her as being weaker; on the contrary she appeared more manly, as
helper of the man, in the transgression of the commandment. For she also alone
stands against the serpent, and after a certain resistance and contention,
having been outwitted, ate of the tree; but Adam, having fought nothing at
all, or contradicted, partook of the fruit given by the woman; which is a
proof of complete weakness and of an unmanly mind. For the woman, having been
overthrown by a demon, is pardonable; but Adam, as having been defeated by a
woman, will be unpardonable, as having himself received the commandment from
God in person; for the woman, having heard the commandment even from Adam
himself, was disposed to think little of it, either as not having been deemed
worthy that God should speak to her also, or also as doubting, perhaps even
thinking that the commandment of Adam was given to her of his own. The serpent
found her by herself, that he might be able to converse with her privately.
And seeing her eating from the trees, he proposed the eating of the tree, or
not eating. And if eating, it is clear that she was also in a corruptible
body. For everything that enters into the mouth goes out into the draught. If
then she was corruptible, it is clear that she was also mortal. And if mortal,
that was no longer a curse, nor a sentence, the voice of God that says to the
man, that you are earth, and to earth you shall go, as the truth of the facts
has it. And if again the serpent saw the woman not eating, how did he bring
over to eating her who had never eaten? And who was it that informed even this
murderous, bloodstained serpent, that the sentence from God to them concerning
death would not come to fulfillment, who said, in the day you eat, you shall
die by death. And not only, but that together with the athrexia the eyes of
these also would be opened, who formerly did not see? And by the so-called
opening they made an entrance for death. 15 These things of old Balaam,
speaking through parables, was not known; and now Christ, being present and
fulfilling these things, was not believed; whence, foreseeing and wondering,
he says: O, O, who shall live when God shall set these things? 16 Explaining
the law from above to the generation after those who were taken away in the
wilderness, he teaches Deuteronomy; not as giving another law, besides that
long ago appointed for their fathers; but summing up this same one; that
having heard the things that happened to their fathers, they may fear God from
their whole heart. 17 From whom Christ was prefigured, and was recognized, and
was born; for in Joseph he was prefigured; and from Levi and Judah according
to the flesh, as king and priest, he was born; and through Simeon he was
recognized in the temple; through Zebulun he was believed among the nations,
as the prophet says: land of Zebulun; and through Benjamin, Paul, having been
preached into all the world, he was glorified. 18 And this not idly, but that
through the number of the ten men, having Jesus with himself he might appear
as helper, as also from an agreement that had come about with them. And since
he was not willing to take part in what they were doing as they idolized, they
bring the charge upon him; because Jerubbaal is called a judgment-seat of
Baal. 19 Take to yourself Jesus the son of Nun. For it was necessary that
Moses lead the people out of Egypt, and that Jesus lead them into the
inheritance; and that Moses as law take rest,

but Jesus as word, and an unlying type of the hypostatic Word, address the
people; and that Moses give the manna to the fathers as food, but Jesus the
grain, now the firstfruits of life, a type of the body of Christ, as also the
scripture says, that then the manna of the Lord ceased, after the people ate
the grain from the land. 20 You shall not go with them, nor shall you curse
the people. He does not hint concerning the people, for all were laid low; but
because of the mystery of Christ being shown beforehand. For since he was
going to be born of the fathers according to the flesh, the Spirit teaches the
man beforehand, lest going according to ignorance he curse the people. Not as
if a curse were strong contrary to the will of God, but for a showing of the
providence of God, which he had toward them because of the forefathers. 21 And
this one had mounted upon his ass. The ass had a type of the body of Christ;
upon which all men, resting from toils, are borne as by a vehicle. For the
Savior took up the burden of our sins. And the angel who appeared to Balaam
was himself the Word; and he had a sword in his hand, clearly, the authority
which he had from above. 22 God is not as a man. He shows that all men lie,
being carried about; but God is not so; for he always remains true,
accomplishing whatever he wills. 23 To render vengeance from the Lord to
Midian. For he who no longer speaks in the Spirit of God, but, contrary to the
law of God, setting up another law of fornication, this one will no longer be
reckoned as a prophet, but as a soothsayer; for not remaining in the
commandment of God, he received a wage worthy of his evil contrivance. 24 Know
that every man is either empty or full; for if he does not have the Holy
Spirit, he does not have knowledge of the Creator, he has not received the
life, Jesus the Christ, he does not know the Father who is in the heavens; if
he does not live according to the word, according to the heavenly law, he is
not temperate, he does not do righteousness; such a one is empty; but if he
has made room for God who said, I will dwell in them and walk among them, and
I will be their God, this one is not empty, but full. 25 The little boy then
who led Samson by the hand will be prefigured as John the Baptist, showing the
people the faith toward Christ. And the house into which they were gathered is
signified to be the world, in which the alien and unbelieving nations dwelt,
sacrificing to their idols; and the two pillars, the two covenants. The
resting then of Samson upon the pillars, the taught people coming to know the
mystery of Christ. 26 And the man of God said: Where did it fall? and he
showed him the place; and he cut a stick, and threw it there, and the iron
floated. Which was a sign of the leading-up of souls through wood, upon which
he suffered who is able to lead up souls that follow his own ascent. Of which
that also is a token, that many souls went up and appeared in the bodies,
together with the descent of the holy soul of Christ. For as the lightest wood
became submerged, and the heaviest iron floated; so when the Word of God by a
union, the natural one according to hypostasis, was united to the flesh, the
heavy and earthy was taken up into the heavens by the divine nature after the
resurrection, having been made incorruptible. 27 The Gospel according to
Matthew was written toward Jews; for these greatly desired a Christ from the
seed of David. And Matthew, having still more vehemently such a desire, was
eager in every way to furnish them full assurance that the Christ was from the
seed of David; therefore he also began from his generation. 28 The axe to the
root, he says; rousing toward knowledge of the truth, and purifying by fear,
and preparing to bear ripe fruit. 29 See a grain of mustard shown through a
parable, and a heavenly word, sown in the world as in a field, after the
manner of seed, having in itself the fiery and austere of the power. For he
was proclaimed judge of the whole world; this one, hidden in the heart of the
earth, in soil, and in a three-day period born a greatest tree, stretched out
his own branches

to the ends of the earth. From this the twelve Apostles, having come forth,
having become fair and flourishing branches, became a shelter to the nations,
as to birds of heaven, under which branches all, having been sheltered, as
birds coming together under a nest, partook of the edible and heavenly food
coming forth from them. 30 But now just as through the disobedience of the one
man, who was first formed from untilled earth, the many were made sinners, and
cast away the life; so also it was necessary that through the obedience of one
man, who was first born of a virgin, many be justified, and receive back the
salvation. 31 Josephus says that when Moses was being brought up in palaces,
having been appointed general against the Ethiopians, and having conquered, he
took the daughter of that king; since by desire toward him she betrays the
city to him. Why then, of the two who spoke against, was she alone required to
pay the penalty? First, because the woman's fault was greater; for both nature
and the law subject the female to the male; and Aaron had some moderate
pardon, as older in time, and as having been deemed worthy of the
high-priesthood. And besides, since the leper was held to be unclean according
to the law, and Aaron was root and foundation of the priests, that the
reproach might not pass through to the whole race, he did not bring upon him
the equal punishment, but through the sister he both frightened and
instructed. For the suffering so pained him, that this having been received
first by her, he himself entreated the one who had been wronged to loose the
calamity by intercession. And he did not neglect, but immediately offered the
supplication. Then the philanthropic Lord taught that he did not instruct her
condemnatorily, but paternally; for he said, If her father, spitting, had spit
into her face, would she not be ashamed? Seven days she shall be separated
outside the camp, and after these things she shall enter. 32 Since some, I
know not from where they have been moved, take away by half the creative power
of God, saying that he is cause only of the quality concerning matter, having
said that matter itself is ungenerated, come let us ask them, what then also
... unchangeable; therefore matter is unchangeable; if matter is unchangeable,
and the unchangeable is not altered according to quality, it is not made into
a world. Through which it appears superfluous for them that God impose
qualities upon matter, matter wholly not admitting alteration, being
ungenerated according to it. Further if matter is ungenerated, it has
altogether been made according to some quality, and this unalterable, it would
not be receptive of more qualities; nor would it be made into a world; and not
being made into a world, it puts God wholly outside of creating. 33 And he
baptized himself, he says, in the Jordan seven times. Not in vain of old was
Naaman, being a leper, cleansed when baptized, but for a showing for us; who,
being lepers in sins, through the holy water and the invocation of the Lord
are cleansed of the old trespasses, being spiritually reborn as newborn
children, as also the Lord said; unless one is born again through water and
spirit, he shall not enter into the kingdom of the heavens. 34 If the body of
Elisha, having died, raised a dead man, how much more shall God who has made
alive the dead bodies of men bring them to the judgment? 35 True knowledge
then is the understanding according to Christ, which Paul calls the wisdom of
God in a mystery, the hidden, which the psychic man does not receive, the word
of the cross, of which if one should taste, he would not come near the vain
disputations and word-fights of the puffed up and inflated, who enter into
things they have not seen. For the truth is without figure and the word is
near you in your mouth and in your heart, as the same apostle says, eum... to
those who obey. For it makes us like Christ, if we know the power of his
resurrection and the fellowship of his sufferings. For this is the choice of
the apostolic teaching and of the most holy faith that has been handed down to
us, which the unlearned receive and the little-learned taught, who to the
endless genealogies

not giving heed, but rather being eager about the correction of life, that
they may not, having been deprived of the divine Spirit, fail of the kingdom
of the heavens. For the first thing is to deny yourself and to follow Christ,
and those who do these things are borne to perfection, having fulfilled all
the will of the teacher, becoming sons of God through the spiritual
regeneration and heirs of the kingdom of the heavens, which seeking first they
will not be forsaken. 36 Those who have followed the second ordinances of the
apostles know that the Lord has established a new offering in the new
covenant, according to Malachi the prophet: Because from the rising of the sun
even unto the setting my name has been glorified among the nations, and in
every place incense is brought to my name and a pure sacrifice; just as also
John in the Apocalypse says: The incenses are the prayers of the saints; and
Paul exhorts us to present our bodies a living sacrifice, holy, well-pleasing
to God, our rational worship. And again: let us offer a sacrifice of praise,
that is fruit of lips. These offerings then are not according to the law,
whose handwriting the Lord, having wiped out, took from the midst, but
according to spirit, for in spirit and truth one must worship God. Because the
offering of the eucharist also is not fleshly, but spiritual and in this pure.
For we offer to God the bread and the cup of blessing, giving thanks to him
that he commanded the earth to bring forth these fruits for our food, and
having completed the offering here we call upon the Holy Spirit, that he may
show this sacrifice and the bread the body of Christ, and the cup the blood of
Christ, that those who have partaken of these antitypes may obtain the
forgiveness of sins and eternal life. Those then who celebrate these offerings
in the memorial of the Lord do not come to the doctrines of the Jews, but
serving spiritually they shall be called sons of wisdom. 37 The Apostles
appointed that we must not judge anyone in food and in drink and in respect of
a feast or new moon or sabbaths. From where then are these fights, from where
the schisms? We keep feast, but in leaven of malice and wickedness, tearing
apart the church of God, and we observe the outward things, that we may cast
away the better, the faith and love. These feasts then and fasts we have heard
from the prophetic words to be displeasing to the Lord. 38 Christ who before
the ages was called Son of God appeared in the fullness of the time, that he
might cleanse us, who were under sin, through his blood, presenting us as pure
sons to the Father, if we render ourselves obedient to the discipline of the
Spirit. And at the end of the times he is going to come to abolish all that is
evil, and to reconcile all things, that there may be an end of all the
defilements. 39 And he found a jawbone of an ass; It is to be noted that no
longer did the divine scripture, after the fornication, speak of the things
accomplished by him the, The Spirit of the Lord leapt upon him; for thus the
sin of fornication is sinned against the body, as against a temple of God,
according to the divine Apostle. 40 This signifies the persecution against the
church from the nations still remaining in unbelief. But he who suffers these
things hoped that there will be vengeance against those who make war. And
through what is the vengeance? First through fleeing to the intelligible rock,
and second through finding a jawbone of an ass. And a type of the jawbone is
the body of Christ. 41 If we always speak well of the worthy, and never ill of
the unworthy, we too shall obtain the glory and kingdom of God. 42 By prophecy
it was signified in them that the people, having become a transgressor, will
be bound with the cords of its own sins. And the bonds being loosed of
themselves signifies that having repented he will again be loosed from the
bonds of sin. 43 It is not easy to change a soul held by error. 44 And they
killed Balaam son of Beor with the sword. For he who no longer speaks in the
Spirit of God, but against the law of God

handed down to us, which the unlearned receive and the little-learned taught,
who to the endless genealogies not giving heed, but rather being eager about
the correction of life, that they may not, having been deprived of the divine
Spirit, fail of the kingdom of the heavens. For the first thing is to deny
yourself and to follow Christ, and those who do these things are borne to
perfection, having fulfilled all the will of the teacher, becoming sons of God
through the spiritual regeneration and heirs of the kingdom of the heavens,
which seeking first they will not be forsaken. 36 Those who have followed the
second ordinances of the apostles know that the Lord has established a new
offering in the new covenant, according to Malachi the prophet: Because from
the rising of the sun even unto the setting my name has been glorified among
the nations, and in every place incense is brought to my name and a pure
sacrifice; just as also John in the Apocalypse says: The incenses are the
prayers of the saints; and Paul exhorts us to present our bodies a living
sacrifice, holy, well-pleasing to God, our rational worship. And again: let us
offer a sacrifice of praise, that is fruit of lips. These offerings then are
not according to the law, whose handwriting the Lord, having wiped out, took
from the midst, but according to spirit, for in spirit and truth one must
worship God. Because the offering of the eucharist also is not fleshly, but
spiritual and in this pure. For we offer to God the bread and the cup of
blessing, giving thanks to him that the earth

he commanded to bring forth these fruits for our food, and having completed
the offering here we call upon the Holy Spirit, that he may show this
sacrifice and the bread the body of Christ, and the cup the blood of Christ,
that those who have partaken of these antitypes may obtain the forgiveness of
sins and eternal life. Those then who celebrate these offerings in the
memorial of the Lord do not come to the doctrines of the Jews, but serving
spiritually they shall be called sons of wisdom. 37 The Apostles appointed
that we must not judge anyone in food and in drink and in respect of a feast
or new moon or sabbaths. From where then are these fights, from where the
schisms? We keep feast, but in leaven of malice and wickedness, tearing apart
the church of God, and we observe the outward things, that we may cast away
the better, the faith and love. These feasts then and fasts we have heard from
the prophetic words to be displeasing to the Lord. 38 Christ who before the
ages was called Son of God appeared in the fullness of the time, that he might
cleanse us, who were under sin, through his blood, presenting us as pure sons
to the Father, if we render ourselves obedient to the discipline of the
Spirit. And at the end of the times he is going to come to abolish all that is
evil, and to reconcile all things, that there may be an end of all the
defilements. 39 And he found a jawbone of an ass; It is to be noted that no
longer did the divine scripture, after the fornication, speak of the things
accomplished by him the, The Spirit of the Lord leapt upon him; for thus the
sin of fornication is sinned against the body, as against a temple of God,
according to the divine Apostle. 40 This signifies the persecution against the
church from the nations still remaining in unbelief. But he who suffers these
things hoped that there will be vengeance against those who make war. And
through what is the vengeance? First through fleeing to the intelligible rock,
and second through finding a jawbone of an ass. And a type of the jawbone is
the body of Christ. 41 If we always speak well of the worthy, and never ill of
the unworthy, we too shall obtain the glory and kingdom of God. 42 By prophecy
it was signified in them that the people, having become a transgressor, will
be bound with the cords of its own sins. And the bonds being loosed of
themselves signifies that having repented he will again be loosed from the
bonds of sin. 43 It is not easy to change a soul held by error. 44 And they
killed Balaam son of Beor with the sword. For he who no longer speaks in the
Spirit of God, but against the law of God setting up another law of
fornication, this one will no longer be reckoned as a prophet, but as a
soothsayer. For not remaining in the commandment of God, he received a wage
worthy of his evil contrivance. 45 God of the age, named God to the
unbelievers, that is Satan. 46 John, having been born, looses the silence for
Zechariah. For he did not soothe, he burdened the father, the voice having
gone forth to be silent; but just as, having been disbelieved, it bound the
tongue, so, having been made manifest, to give the father the freedom; to whom
also it was announced and he was born. And a voice and a lamp is forerunner of
word and of light. 47 And for what is "in the city of David" also added, if
not that through the promise made by God to David, that from the fruit of his
belly there shall be an eternal king, he may announce it as fulfilled.
