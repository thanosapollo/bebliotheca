=39.600= CHAP. VIII. Concerning the foreknowledge and self-determination of
the Holy Spirit. After the divine nature and energy of the Spirit of God, the
oracles discourse most clearly indeed concerning his self-determination and
willing distribution.
1. That not ministerially, but authoritatively, he does and furnishes all the
gifts as he wills, as many as the unspeakable and supersubstantial and only
nature would grant; and he works as many things as he wishes, and blows
=39.601= where he has chosen; and he wills what the Father and Son will; and
he blows likewise where both the Father and the Son will; filling the
ungrudging one will of the Father and of the Son and of himself; and leaving
nothing, not even of those that have a faint life, deserted of his own
providence. For presiding over his own making, as being good of himself, and
as fellow-worker of the good Creator, he looks upon all things, and overlooks
nothing, being all-sufficient, and distributing divine gifts. For toward "the
one good" envy never arises in anyone concerning anything; but rather by
benefactions he shows the authority. And on this account we say that there is
one will, and one authority and power of the Trinity. And of those whose
willing, and authority, and energy is one, one also is the godhead. For that
which works all things according to its own authority, and is present in the
particulars, belongs to the one and unstained nature over all, having in
nothing what is lacking, nor doing what it does not will; but neither doing
what is not pleasing to the Father and the Son. And in the manner in which,
among the other proofs, it shows the Son indistinguishable toward the Father,
also the saying, "Whatever the Father does, these things the Son also does
likewise;" so also the Holy Spirit, together with the other things, also works
all things and grants gifts as he wills and blows where he has chosen. For
presenting this, and theologizing the Spirit clearly, the Apostle wrote: "That
we may see the things granted to us by God." And of all these things the same
man would again be a faithful witness, writing to the Corinthians in the first
Epistle: "There are distributions of gifts, but the same Spirit; and there are
distributions of ministries, and the same Lord; and there are distributions of
workings, but the same God who works all things in all. But to each is given
the manifestation of the Spirit toward what is profitable. For to one is given
through the Spirit a word of wisdom, and to another a word of knowledge
according to the same Spirit, and to another faith in the same Spirit, and to
another gifts of healings in the one Spirit, and to another workings of
powers, and to another prophecy, and to another discernment of spirits, and to
another kinds of tongues, and to another interpretation of tongues. But all
these things the one and the same Spirit works, distributing to each privately
as he wills." And one must not =39.604= pass by, first, that because of the
Holy Spirit's equality with the Father in all things, he mentions him first,
then the Son, and afterward God and Father who works all things in all; and
second, that having said above that God works all things in all, he adds,
setting out one harmony of testimony concerning the Father and his Spirit:
"But all these things the one and the same Spirit works, distributing to each
privately as he wills;" that from this he may show the Holy Spirit to be a
self-determining God; having undertaken no unfitting thing, nor innovating the
order, nor missing what is well; but keeping unhurt the knowledge of the
faith, and the purity of theology; and not insulting the undivided nature and
dignity, as those who do not number together, but


subnumber to the Father the Son and the Holy Spirit. For he did not simply and
of himself reverse the sequence of the names; but Christ speaking in him,
destroying the supposition of the ill-disposed and undistorted hearts, teaches
variously the truth of the equally powerful, and equally honored, and
consubstantial Trinity, having said at one time in the same Paul: "But the
same God is he who works all things in all;" and at another time in the
Evangelist saying: "Baptizing them into the name of the Father, and of the
Son, and of the Holy Spirit;" and now in Paul, as has been said before: "But
all these things the one and the same Spirit works, distributing to each
privately as he wills." And he also advocates this chapter in the Epistle to
the Hebrews, which has it thus: "God also bearing witness with signs and
wonders and various powers, and distributions of the Holy Spirit, according to
his own will." And masterly sayings in John have: "The Spirit blows where he
wills;" and, "The Spirit is the truth." And it is fitting, he says, to hymn
him with the first places of godhead, but not upon second places; that you may
have security concerning the life. For that which benefits likes to be thanked
and served; which even those outside found to say without deceit: "Pay back
the benefactor with kindly exchanges." For if, he says, the self-determining
is better than one under authority, already also man, being self-determining,
would be declared among you better than the Holy Spirit. Alas for the terrible
things! The Macedonians, receiving the tokens of the divine power and nature
as if of a created thing, have devised deceits, and have written introducing
persons of an orthodox man and of a Macedonian; and of the orthodox man they
now cut short the proofs, now do not even bring forward as many as they ought;
at another time making him at a loss concerning the contradictory and
demonstrative answer; or also dissolving and relaxing, as they pretend, with
persuasive words =39.605= the truth and the well-tuned; and simply in nothing
setting the oracles which were used with the dignity that is fitting; turning
the argument to what seems good to them, and bringing themselves in as
flourishing about their own aim, and stringing the argument without drawing
breath, and introducing themselves as victors of all with a piety-seeming
irony; they pull down, as they at least think, the orthodoxy. And the voice of
the Macedonian, pretended as having come toward an orthodox man, has this
manner: You were saying: God works; the Holy Spirit also works; and from the
working and working, you wished the equal honor and equal power to belong to
the Holy Spirit and to God. But I, that physicians and all men take "to work"
upon things of different kinds and differing in nature and in definitions,
whether upon hot waters, or upon things eaten, or upon herbs, I let go; but
this only I show, that the divine word has taken it also upon the opposing
power, the Apostle having said concerning the Antichrist: "Whose coming is
according to the working of Satan in all signs and wonders of falsehood." See
then, he says, the name of working being taken homonymously or synonymously
upon many, to what you have brought down the Holy Spirit; and you did not
reckon that God has both made and worked a world from things not being into
being; but the Holy Spirit, a distribution of gifts in those created by God.
For it is written: "Distributing to each privately as he wills; not indeed
granting." And how, O Macedonian, are these things not contrary to the
theological thoughts? And how is the hypocrisy not self-caught from itself?
For first, since it is confessed by all that the verb "works" is predicated of
different essences and things, for what reason, when the discussion is about
the divine and manifest energy of the Holy Spirit, did you bring forward into
the midst the things you brought forward, and from this insult rather than
praise the Spirit of God? Next, it is not because the word "works" is taken
homonymously or synonymously both of the Divine, and of herbs, and of Satan;
but one must attend to the persons and to the difference of the energy; and
make


the judgment of the words and things concerning the divine Spirit toward God,
who has been hymned by the word of working, as lies before us, but not toward
the earthly things and toward the demon that has been thrown down. Since the
Holy Spirit is mentioned with the reverence befitting God in the whole context
found concerning him in each passage; but the creatures and the demon,
worthily of themselves. Which you the Macedonian, not keeping, nor
distinguishing the hostility of the demon, and the friendliness =39.608= of
the Holy Spirit, to the things you say from your surplus you bring down, at
least as far as it is in you, the incomparable glory of God the Father, who
has been praised with like diction upon divine things, and of his Spirit; and
you have reasonably escaped notice ranging yourself among those who think
soundly. But attend rather, that in the things lying before us, before God the
Father and the Son, the Holy Spirit was first mentioned in a manner befitting
God; and reckon from this that the Trinity worshipped in a monad has no
difference, and that it cooperates with itself unspeakably. Then consider
yourself, that to each through the Holy Spirit and from the Holy Spirit are
given the mentioned divine gifts, of which no gift of a divine donation of its
own is left remaining from outside, and he who does not obtain them, as being
given by God, is more wretched than the wretched; and he who does not so write
and speak remains incurable, neither himself being benefited, nor benefiting
others, but even harming; and that all these things the one and the same
Spirit works, that is, both does and furnishes; and he who works unto these
will also work unto all, and is without need; and he who is without need is
impassible; and he who is impassible is God. Or then if it is said concerning
God the Father: "The Lord is mighty in war;" and, "God is known in Judaea,"
and as many such things; will he not be understood as mighty, and energetic,
and manifest in other things? And consider likewise, that the expressions
speaking thus strip off every heretical persuasiveness, and variously
guarantee his divine self-determination, of one mind with the Father and the
Son: "According to his own will;" and, "He distributes to each privately as he
wills;" and that God the Father jointly bears witness to these things with
signs and wonders, and the Savior saying: "The Spirit of truth, who proceeds
from the Father;" and, "The Spirit is the truth;" and Peter, announcing the
Holy Spirit as God expressly to Ananias thus: "Why has it been agreed with you
to lie to the Holy Spirit? You have not lied to a man, but to God." And know
that also, that you have not escaped notice, having knavishly omitted in your
vain and most harmful little work, or rather your blasphematorium, the leading
part of the divine passage. For it contains thus: "The one and the same
Spirit;" and thus it adds what you alone brought in distortedly: "Distributing
to each privately as he wills;" instead of, granting, and distributing, and
uncovering authoritatively the heavenly goods. And it is not of right mind and
faith, along with misinterpreting, still also to mutilate the oracles. For the
containing, "One and the same Spirit," shuts you off from both separating him
from the identity toward the one, and highest, and steering all things; for
this most uncontemplatable and most incomprehensible one differs in the
godhead not even a little, except only in the account of fatherhood; because
he proceeded by nature from him, and not by a creative energy, and from
saying: If Spirit of God, and Spirit of Christ has been written, two Spirits
will be understood =39.609=. And it has been said not much above, for what
cause it has been so oracled. And do not be ignorant also

that the oracles set out do not signify that the person granting is one, and
the one distributing another, as in the case of men who are able neither to
know all things, nor to act without toil, and on this account give some things
without inquiry, while others distribute according to accurate testing, lest
somehow either those who supply should not suffice, or those who minister
themselves should not gain. For the blameless, and impassible, and
ever-moving, all-sufficient and ungrudging God, who provides not toward what
we are worthy to receive (for we are not even worthy


from any good work), but toward what it is fitting for him to give; knowing
all things, and being able to do all things, as has been said also among the
Greeks: =39.612= For no one will think another mind better than this, Such as
I think; by a nod alone, and not by handicraft or hardship, always supplies
abundantly to each the good and ungrudging gifts; and not now yes, now no; nor
sometimes more, at another time less; but continually according to the same
things and in the same way in all concurrence (for how not?) of both his own
Son Word and his one Holy Spirit. For none of the things that are is rejected
by him; seeing that he makes the sun rise upon righteous and sinners alike,
and to those who have worked unto the eleventh hour he supplies the same wage
which he agreed with those who dig and tend the vineyard from dawn; nor is
anyone formidable to him, that through want, or through not giving richly, he
should war against him; nor is anyone else found differing according to
lordship, that he should grudge this one being equaled with those belonging
=39.613= to him (which it is not even holy to say, except of wretched
hypotheses and hypostases); nor has he let go him with whom he will be angry;
for "You spare," it says, "all, because all things are yours, Master who loves
souls; and your incorruptible Spirit is in all." And these things not being,
what is left for you to sophize? As again whenever the Son, or the Holy
Spirit, is said to furnish divine gifts, it is quite clear, as by paternal
good pleasure; for how not so? Therefore hear Paul who taught the Romans:
"That the love of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy
Spirit given to us;" and, "As many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are
sons of God;" and again: "He therefore who supplies to you the Spirit, and
works powers in you;" and to the Corinthians the first: "But if you are led by
the Spirit, you are no longer under law;" and to the Galatians: "For the fruit
of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, kindness, goodness, faith,
meekness, self-control. Against such things there is no law." Wherefore also
here he said before: "There are distributions of gifts, but the same Spirit;"
that is, by ellipsis of speech and cutting, the Spirit that grants; and thus
he added: "The one and the same Spirit, distributing to each privately as he
wills;" so as to prove that he both grants and distributes, of one mind, it is
clear, with the one whose Spirit he is, and unto whom all things are referred,
as was written to the Romans: "That from him, and through him, and unto him
are all things." And attend also to all the other things which the oracles
have said before concerning him. For if you had attended to all, and if you
had believed God the Father himself, and his only-begotten Son, and still also
the so many saints, whose eyes of understanding were so awake as the Cherubic
ones, whom we, by how much more we are ashamed and honor, by so much more we
receive rightly the contribution and teaching that has come from them in deed
and the faith that has been shown; and if you had not from the beginning
followed in part the shield-bearers of Arius, whom may it not be for us even
to name, nor to learn, blushing at them, as having put around you a name of
bishop, you being before this a deacon of the orthodox and apostolic Church;
you would not have made the comparison of the things said concerning the Holy
Spirit toward the things said concerning the creatures, and you would not have
given more suspicion to those who are impious with you, nor would you have
used abuse, that I may not say blasphemy, conquering the moderate; and you
would have known that he was present with and cooperated unspeakably when all
=39.616= things were being created, as being the hypostatic Spirit of God; and
God and Father once was well pleased, as has been said above, that through the
Son Word all things should be brought from not-being, both the Father himself
and his sanctifying Spirit being co-understood (for how not?) unto the
creating; but in the case of men, or even of angels, there is need of many
persons and hands unto doing anything perfectly and with surplus. And he who
ordained that he be mentioned together in our regeneration of baptism
guarantees these things; as being present with


and acting with, and not being ranked below the unspeakable nature, he too is
mentioned. Thus then these things, and not otherwise, and yet I will pass by
saying more. But also through the Spirit God distributes, as through his Word
he creates. This does not cast him out of the godhead, as neither the Son;
rather indeed it also declares even through these things that he is God. Since
to those created through the Son, through the Holy Spirit the goods toward
salvation and spoken everywhere are furnished. For the one hangs upon the
other, and for the thing created it is more advantageous to be provided for
than to have been created, and to be counted worthy of the flowing multitude
of divine gifts from heaven; and everywhere it has been preferred not to come
to be, rather than, having come to be, to have failure of the inexhaustible
goods from above; which even when given are not seen, as also the giver both
suffices through ages, and is somehow unknown. For no eye has seen them, nor
has ear heard, or has it yet come into thought; and every height of those
reasoning about them, and every beauty of those collecting, lies before our
knowledge, however the faith may approach what is fitting; for where the true
itself, or the accurate, is not yet capturable, one must look toward what is
possible. Therefore an unerring voice said concerning the one who has become
unsharing of these: "It were good for that man if he had not been born." You
yourself therefore have already brought yourself through accusation, and have
turned round upon yourself the charge which you chose against us; and simply
you have seemed to differ in nothing from those who beat the air, having been
led on to stitch together, as the saying is, certain difficulties, and to
bring unwisely into the midst, between divine things and narratives, the need
not requiring it, certain other material things, and indeed the opposing one,
and the one thrown away, and such things as you said, putting them under. And
you are far from those who have done anything fair and useful, and none of the
Christians who have practiced the Scriptures rightly and without passion, and
to whom at once it belonged to excel in the grace given by the divine Spirit,
would accept you into the things worked out by you in a soul-harming way. For
according to your attempt, perhaps every Scripture that has an indication of
theology will be cast out. And come, if these things are not true, =39.617=
let us consider thus gently and moderately. For I shall appear accurately, and
not simply from disputed proofs, but from confessed ones, and I shall teach
easily, and you shall learn: Paul, who has the blessed zeal concerning the
great God and our Savior Jesus Christ hotter than fire, as the saying is,
writes that Christ has become a minister. And this benefactor himself said
manifestly concerning himself: "I received a commandment, what I should say,
and what I should speak;" and, "I do nothing from myself." And elsewhere it
has been said: "The law was given through Moses; grace and truth came through
Jesus Christ." And yet we understand these things to have been written thus
faithfully and in a manner befitting God, because of the one will of the Holy
Trinity, and because all things are referred unto the Father. As therefore no
variation of godhead is conceived upon the Son (for he neither ministers, as
one of another kind in nature to one of another kind; nor by a sinking of
power, but as Son Word to a genuine Father; nor does he receive a commandment,
as being ignorant of what is due and of the paternal will, but as has been
said before), so also the divine Spirit. And in the manner in which the things
of God the Word are referred unto God the Father, in this, and in no other,
the things of the Holy Spirit. So that by the things with which heretics
lifted up their mind, by these they would have moved against themselves the
slander upon boastfulness and evil opinion. The things of the impious and
unfitting slander did not stop for them as far as these. For trying to trouble
the unstained glory concerning the godhead of the Holy Spirit, as if no good
belonged to them from him, and at the same time also to cast us into
accusation, these same men who have used certain men as teachers do not
hesitate to coin also this, word for word, that we, taking into divine dignity
the passage that declares, "The Spirit blows where he wills," bring him down
into human nature; since after other things it has: "So shall everyone be who
has been born of the


Spirit." But they themselves, he says, neither raise the Spirit into divine
dignity, nor drag him down into the nature of the rest; for he holds the
middle rank, being neither God, nor one of the remaining things. And who in
truth will not indict them of so great daring? Come then, attend with the
mind; for I will speak through a few things. Especially he did not say: So is
the man, the man simply; but, "So shall everyone be who has been born of the
Spirit;" instead of, he who has been counted worthy to be regenerated in the
divine font, and to become, as =39.620= has been made clear in catholic
writings, a partaker of divine nature. And these things are near to, "Let us
make man according to our image and likeness;" and to, "That you may become
according to the image of him who created us." And as the highest Father, and
his Son, are not dragged down from these holy and above-us voices into human
nature; so neither is the Holy Spirit, from the sayings spoken before, and
from his philanthropies concerning us. Next it seems to them, what is still
worse, that the Holy Spirit is neither God, nor one of the remaining things.
It remains to think him non-existent. And otherwise: If he is not, as they
allege, God, he is subjected to the common account of the creatures. And no
such token is found in Scriptures, nor has it been oracled that he is of a
middle nature; but quite the contrary, that he is Spirit of God, and who
proceeds from the Father, and a divine Spirit. It remains that he is God, both
from the voices of Peter and Paul, and from every thought of the Scriptures
and from the word "proceeds." For being undefined, it shows most divinely the
eternal and inseparable character of his nature toward him from whom he comes
forth unspeakably. And indeed also this: If he is none of the other things, as
this of theirs is true, he is God. Besides: If no created thing is found in
one hypostasis, as was proved in the first book, but the Holy Spirit is one
and only of the henad, that is monad, of essence; so then they would be
understood as cheating from the very things they wrote, even if they seem to
be clever at saying what they would wish, and not even one thing of their brow
against us would be procured; for human boastings are quickly refuted. A wise
man has distinguished thus concerning each of them: "Do not sophize many
things, lest you be struck out of your wits." And Stephen also in the Acts
somehow thus reproaches them: "Stiff-necked, and uncircumcised in heart and
ears, you always resist the Holy Spirit; as your fathers, so also you." And he
said their fathers were Arius, Macedonius, the destructive dyad. I come then
upon another chapter.
=39.621= 2. That he set the prophets and apostles to shepherd the rational
flock, and to take care of it; and he sent authoritatively, and made them, as
he willed, to speak according to likeness of the Father and the Son, as being
Spirit of God, existing through procession, not through making, and as being
unadulterated Spirit of wisdom and understanding, co-ruling with the Father
who leads all, and with the Son. But a nature of a creature is not of this
rank. And that these things are true, both the books of Moses persuade,
saying: "Who will give all the people as prophets, when the Lord gives his
Spirit upon them?" and the apostolic Acts, discoursing such things as these:
"There appeared to them tongues distributing as of fire; and it sat upon each
one of them, and they were all filled with Holy Spirit, and began to speak
with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them to utter;" and again from the
divine Paul: "Well did the Holy Spirit speak toward your fathers through the
prophet." And again, having sent for the elders of Ephesus in Miletus, he
says: "Attend to yourselves, and to all the flock, in which the Holy Spirit
set you as bishops, to shepherd the Church of the Lord, which he purchased
through his own blood." and again: "And they went through the Phrygian and the
Galatian country, having been forbidden by the Holy Spirit to speak the word
in


Asia; and having come according to Mysia, they attempted to go into Bithynia,
and the Spirit of Jesus did not allow them." As God who foreknows all things,
and is self-determining, he did not allow them in one place to speak, and in
another to go, because of what is profitable; doing a thing equal to the Son,
who said: "Do not carry a wallet, or a staff, or sandals;" and again the
apostles and the elders say: "For it seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us
to lay no further =39.624= burden upon you, except these, of which it is
necessary that you keep yourselves from things sacrificed to idols, and blood,
and what is strangled, and fornication; from which, keeping yourselves, you
will do well." What has seemed good to one under authority, and not a lord, no
one accepts; but whatever may seem good to a king and to those appointed by
him, this is law, bringing praise to the obedient, and danger to those who
disobey. For "disobedience," it says, "works death; but obedience, eternal
life." For a king is stronger, when he is angry at a lesser man. As therefore
a king is accustomed to send down his own legislations to the nearest great
rulers, to whom he has given the reins of those ruled; and those, bringing all
into knowledge, to type that they should bow to the things ordained by the
king, and threatening danger to those who transgress; so also the apostles,
whom God set first, knowing the self-determination of the Spirit, and the
consubstantiality toward the Father, used to send: It seemed good, he says,
such and such things to the Holy Spirit as to a king, and one in authority,
and guardian of the race; and to us also, as having been set apart to preside
over the faith and the Churches. "From which, keeping yourselves, you will do
well;" but having wounded it, he says, you will suffer the contrary. For they
remembered the Savior having said to them in the Gospel according to Mark:
"For you are not the ones speaking, but the Holy Spirit." And so that no one
may suppose that the chapter we now pursue is small, but may believe that the
same will belongs to the Holy Trinity, and command, and energy, which things
the same godhead also follows for it; Paul, who first became learned in law
and a persecutor, and later was shown as mystagogue of the unutterable economy
itself, declares things that agree concerning the Father and the Son, =39.625=
having written to the Corinthians the first: "But some God set in the Church
first apostles, second prophets, third teachers;" and elsewhere: "But I say,
not I, but the Lord, that a woman not be separated from a man;" and to the
Galatians: "When God was well pleased, who set me apart from my mother's womb,
to reveal his Son in me, that I might preach him among the nations;" and
elsewhere: "As we have also been approved by God to be entrusted with the
Gospel, so we speak, not as pleasing men, but God who tests our hearts." And
no less also the Savior thought it right to say to the disciples: "I chose
you."
3. That he legislates and commands declaratively to each of the works what is
fitting, as a king who has taken up a gentle and lawful leadership, and
awarding boundless peace to the whole heaven and world, and having fitted the
leading with the guardian. And to legislate and to judge belongs only to the
royal nature of the wholes; but of a made thing, to complete under law and
judgment. For Paul the vessel of election defined, as we already said, having
written: "For the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus freed me from the
law of sin and of death;" and the apostolic Acts, having it thus: "While Peter
was considering about the saying, the Spirit said to him: Behold, three men
are seeking you. But rise, go down, and go with them, distinguishing nothing;
because I have sent them;" and again: "The Spirit said to Philip: Approach,
and

join yourself to this chariot;" and again from Agabus the prophet: "These
things says the Holy Spirit: The man whose this belt is, thus they will bind;"
and Paul:


"Behold, now I go bound by the Spirit;" which is near to =39.628= "Paul, a
prisoner of Jesus Christ;" and again: "These things says the Holy Spirit: Set
apart for me Paul and Barnabas unto the work to which I have called them;"
like to, "And the Lord said: Enter into the city, and there it will be spoken
to you what you must do." And again the Spirit said to Peter: "Rise, go into
the house of Cornelius." And indeed in Epistles Paul writes to Timothy: "But
the Spirit says expressly." See then, not only that through such a saying Paul
again declared the Spirit a legislator (for those who legislated in a worldly
way also used the word "expressly"), but that other testimonies also show the
equality of the Holy Spirit's nature and authority toward the Father. For in
like manner it has been written in many places concerning God and Father:
"These things says the Lord Almighty." But heretics, to whom all true things
are contradicted, conceiving nothing worthy of the divine energy, but spending
time about empty imaginations, and whatever they may seize upon tightly,
these, as certain resistant things, they bring forward as firm and set
against. He said, he says, also concerning an angel, Zechariah, when he had
seen the vision concerning the lampstand: "The angel who speaks in me." So
that the Holy Spirit can supposedly be understood as an angel. But from itself
their paltriness is managed away. For the angel was not from himself, nor his
own words, but what he was commanded by God and his Spirit, he said,
ministering. And it contains thus: "And I inquired, and said toward the angel
who speaks in me, saying: What are these things? And the angel who speaks in
me answered, and said toward me: This is the word of the Lord toward
Zerubbabel, saying: Not in great power, nor in strength, but in my Spirit,
says the Lord Almighty." Next the Son was called angel, and the Spirit, in the
