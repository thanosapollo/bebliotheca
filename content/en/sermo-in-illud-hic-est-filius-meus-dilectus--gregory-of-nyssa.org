* Sermon on This is my beloved Son

=46.1109= Why having left believing do we technology the faith, and fill the
desire of the common ill-wisher? For the devil, the common destroyer of
humanity, having reasoned that when a calm is living among us, the things of
piety bloom, and when the faith holds, a good life holds, and we become friends
of the Creator, that we may not come upon whence that one fell out, see what he
has done, and how knavishly he has rhapsodized the war against us. The
machine-stitcher of evils, and wise in deceits, and various in plots, and rich
in the wicked machines, prepares some in a pretext of piety to campaign against
the faith, and through them he has thrown into all the pious an untimely
contentiousness, and a soul-destroying war, and he has campaigned the members of
the Church against one another. Let us then turn this wise thing against his own
head, and let us change his gladness =46.1112= into pain, and an unconsolable
mourning.
Let us mourn the peace, which that one hates. Let us leave fighting the yoke,
and weighing the words of the doctrine. Let us cease from wishing to be teachers
of the teachers. Let us hate word-fighting unto the overthrow of those who hear.
Let us believe as our fathers have handed down. We are not wiser than the
fathers; we are not more exact than the teachers. In peace God has called us,
not in battle. As we have been called, so let us remain at the mystical table,
in which we partake of the heavenly things. Let us not become at the same time
table-fellows, and plotters of one another; not here communal, and outside
plotters.
The Lord was called firstborn from the Virgin, not because he summoned some
second of like kind, but because he is firstborn incomparably toward the sons of
men. For neither because the Apostle said concerning the Lord's beginningless
generation from the Father, Who is the firstborn of all creation, did he already
also write a brother of the Lord. Nor again did the same, having called him
firstborn from the dead, already also introduce a blood-relative of the Lord who
similarly trampled Hades. But the Lord is firstborn from the Father and from the
Virgin and from the dead, that through all the unique dignity of sonship might
be known. For the Master's birth is incomparable; for where the mother is a
virgin, how is a birth interpreted?
