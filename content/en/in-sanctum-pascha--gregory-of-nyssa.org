OF GREGORY BISHOP OF NYSSA A DISCOURSE ON THE HOLY PASCHA
The poor among men,
being lovers of feasts and greeting the festivals with eager soul and proud
bearing, even if they do not suffice for themselves from home toward the
brightness they pursue, collecting every luxury from relatives and
acquaintances, prepare themselves unlacking for the need at hand. Which I
think I myself suffer toward the present day; for having nothing magnificent
of my own to contribute in the praises at hand toward the sacred ode which we
just sang, I will take refuge. And using the starting-points from there I will
pay the debt, weaving onto the scriptural saying also my own things, if indeed
there happen to be any encomia of thanksgiving toward the Master from the poor
slave. David then was saying just now, and we with him: Praise the Lord, all
you nations; laud him, all you peoples. He calls every man from Adam to the
hymn, leaving no one =9.246= outside the calling, but the westerners, the
easterners, those on either side, if anyone is neighbor of the Bear and
inhabitant of the south, he leads them all together with the psalm. And
elsewhere he converses in part with some of men, either calling the holy or
raising the children to hymn, but in the present he gathers nations and
peoples to the psaltery. For when according to the apostle the fashion of this
world has passed away, and Christ as king and God appears to all, having fully
persuaded every unbelieving soul and bridled a blasphemous tongue, and has
stopped the vanity of the Greeks and the error of the Jews and the untamed
garrulity of the heresies, then indeed, then all the nations and the peoples
from of old, having bowed, will send up an unconquerable worship, and there
will be some wonderful symphony of doxology, the holy hymning as is customary
and the impious entreating from necessity; and then truly the victory-hymn
will be sung in concert by all, both the conquered and the victors; then also
the one responsible for the disturbance, the scourge-worthy servant who
imagined the Master's dignity, will be seen by all being dragged by angels to
punishment, and all the servants and co-workers of his wickedness will be
subjected to the fitting and just chastisements, and one king and judge will
appear, a common Master confessed by all, and there will be a settled quiet,
as when a ruler is seated on a tribunal and the herald signals the silence,
and the peoples, straining both sight and hearing, wait for the hearing
=9.247= of the speech. For this reason Praise the Lord, all you nations; laud
him, all you peoples. Praise him as powerful, laud him as loving mankind,
because those who had fallen and were dead he made alive again, and he renewed
again the vessel that had suffered, and the unpleasantness of the remains in
the tombs he formed in love of mankind into an incorruptible living being, and
the soul that left the body before [four] thousand years he brought back as
from a long sojourn into <the> own house, being in no way made a stranger by
time and forgetfulness toward its own instrument, but going toward it more
quickly than a bird flies down upon its own nest.

For let us speak the things proper to the feast, that we may keep festival
correspondingly and fittingly to the matters; for the unfitting and alien,
besides benefiting nothing, is disorderly and monstrous not only in the words
concerning religion and piety, but also in the things outside and of the
wisdom of the world. For who is so mindless and over-ridiculous an orator, as,
having been called to the brightness of a wedding, to stand away from the
fitting and polished words that share in the delights of the


graces of the festival, and to wail mournful songs and to lament over the
bridal chambers the evils from tragedy, or conversely, having been ordered to
fulfill the customary things for one of those who have died, to forget the
suffering and to brighten himself toward the gathering full of dejection? And
if in worldly things order and skill are fair, much more surely in the great
and heavenly things it is more fitting. Christ then has risen today, God, the
impassible, the =9.248= immortal (and hold a little, O pagan, letting go the
rash laughter, until you hear all), having suffered not toward necessity, nor
having been forced to the descent from heavens, nor having found the
resurrection as an unexpected benefaction beyond hopes, but knowing the end of
all the matters and thus having made the beginning. Having in the eyes of the
Godhead the knowledge of the things lying ahead, and before coming down from
heavens seeing both the disturbance of the nations and the hardness of Israel
and Pilate sitting in judgment and Caiaphas tearing his own garment about
himself and the factious people inflamed and Judas betraying and Peter
fighting in defense and after a little himself being transformed by the
resurrection into the glory of incorruption, and having every future thing
inscribed in the knowledge, he did not postpone the grace toward man nor put
off the economy, but just as those who see the weak man being dragged along a
ravine, knowing that it is possible both to be rolled in the mire of the
torrent and to bear the blow of the stones being pushed together by the water,
nevertheless because of the fellow-feeling concerning the one in danger do not
shrink from the entrance, so also our Savior who loves mankind voluntarily
accepted insolent and dishonorable things, that he might save the one
perishing from deceit. He came down into life, since he knew beforehand also
the ascent as glorious; he allowed what was human of himself to die, since he
also knew the raising beforehand; for he was not daring recklessly as one of
the common men, committing the outcome to the unclarity of the future, but as
God he was managing the matter at hand upon a stated and known end.

=9.249= This then is the day which the Lord has made; let us exult and be glad
in it; not with drunkenness and revels, not with dances and drunken parties,
but with the God-formed thoughts. Today it is possible to see the whole
inhabited world as one house gathered into symphony of one † matter and having
neglected every customary affair, but having been recast by one signal into
the zeal of prayer. The highways do not have the travelers, the sea today is
empty of sailors and voyagers, the farmer, having thrown away the spade and
the plow, has adorned himself into the form of the one keeping festival, the
shops are at leisure from commerce, the annoyances have been loosed as winter
when spring has appeared, tumults and disturbances and storms of life have
withdrawn before the peace of the feast, the poor man adorns himself as rich,
the rich is shown more splendid than the custom, the old man runs as a youth
toward the sharing of the gladness, the weak man even forces the disease, the
child keeps festival perceptibly by the change of clothing, since it cannot
yet do so intellectually, the virgin over-rejoices in the soul, because she
sees the remembrance of her own hope bright and thus honored, the married
woman rejoices keeping festival with the whole fullness of the house; for now
for her both the housemate and the children and the household-slaves and all
who are at the hearth exult. And just as the swarm of bees, the new and newly
born, first from the hiding-place or from the little baskets toward the air
and the light =9.250= flying out upon one branch of a tree is molded together
all at once and joined, so on this feast whole families run together toward
the hearths. And truly the present is well referred by imitation toward the
coming day; for both are gathering of men, that of the universal, this of the
partial. And that we may say the truer thing, as far as it bears toward
brightness and


good cheer, this is more gracious than the expected one, since then it is
necessary that those lamenting also be seen, whose sins are uncovered, but now
the well-being is unreceptive of the gloomy things; for both the just man is
glad and the one whose conscience is not clean waits for the correction from
repentance, and every grief is put to sleep on the present day, and no one is
so pained as not to find relief in the magnificence of the feast. Now the
prisoner is loosed, the debtor is released, the slave is set free by the good
and philanthropic proclamation of the church, not being struck unseemly on the
cheek and released by blow for blow, nor being shown to the people as in a
procession on a high tribunal, having insult and blushing as the beginning of
freedom, but being released so decently as he is known. The one still
remaining in slavery is also benefited; for even if the sins are many and
heavy, exceeding excuse and pardon, the master, respecting the calm and
philanthropic character of the day, receives the one who has been thrown off
and is seen among the dishonored, as Pharaoh the cupbearer from the prison;
for he knows that according to the appointed time of the resurrection, whose
likeness we honor in the present, he himself also needs the forbearance and
the goodness of the Master, and lending the mercy here he expects =9.251= the
repayment in season. You have heard, O masters, keep the word as a good one,
do not slander me before the slaves as praising the day falsely, take away the
grief of the afflicted souls as the Lord the death of the bodies, transform
the dishonored into honor, the afflicted into joy, the ones without freedom of
speech into boldness, lead out from the corner those thrown as from tombs, let
the beauty of the feast bloom again as a flower for all. For if a human king's
birthday opens a prison, or a victory-feast, will Christ having risen not
release the afflicted? You poor, greet your nurse; you whose bodies have
wasted and been maimed, greet her who heals your misfortunes; for through the
hope from the resurrection both virtue is pursued and vice is hated, since if
resurrection is taken away one saying will be found prevailing among all: Let
us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die.

Looking toward this day the apostle despises the timely life, and desires the
coming one, and cheapening the things seen he says: If we have hoped in this
life, we are more pitiable than all men. Through this day men are heirs of God
and joint-heirs of Christ. Through this day, what the flesh-eating birds ate a
thousand years ago as a part of the body will be found not lacking, and what
whales and dogs and the sea-animals grazed down will rise together with the
man being raised, and what fire burned through and a worm in tombs consumed,
and simply all the bodies, as many as after generation corruption made to
vanish, will be given back unlacking and intact =9.252= from the earth, and,
as Paul teaches, in the twinkling of an eye the resurrection will be completed
(and the twinkling of an eye is a closing of eyelids); and of this speed
nothing else would become sharper. But you, reckoning humanly and according to
your own power, how many intervals of times do you draw in your soul? First,
that the bones that have rotted through and become earth may be compacted into
hardness and smoothness, and having been united from the crushing may come
together again into a rhythm of harmony and the natural joining; then you
invent the molding-around of the fleshes and the stretched bindings of sinews
and the thin channels of veins and arteries spread under the skin, and an
unspeakable and numberless multitude of souls being moved from certain
unspeakable dwellings, and each recognizing its own body as an exceptional
garment and dwelling in it again swiftly, having an unerring discrimination
against so great a multitude of kindred spirits. For consider the souls from
Adam and the from him


bodies, a multitude of so many houses having been loosed and householders
returning from the long sojourn, and all things being accomplished
paradoxically; for neither does the house being rebuilt delay, nor does the
inhabitant wander and wait at the door seeking where the own and exceptional
things are, but he goes straight upon it as a dove toward its own tower, even
if many and continuous ones are about the same place shining through similar
forms. Whence again the remembrance and the reckoning of the former life and
the thought of each deed, being completed so swiftly with the =9.253= living
being that was dissolved so many ages before? And yet even a man raised from a
heavier sleep is for a little ignorant who he is and where he lives, and
forgets the customary things, until wakefulness, having scattered the
numbness, kindles again the memorial and active power.

These things and such as these, running under the reasonings of the many, fill
the mind with exceeding wonder, and they bring in unbelief together with the
wonder; for since the mind does not find the solution of the things doubted
and sought, nor is it able to rest its own busy-ness upon discovery and grasp,
it goes toward unbelief thereafter, in the weakness of its own reasonings
casting out and setting aside the truth of the matters. Or rather, since the
word advancing by a path has arrived at the question continuously spoken, and
the subject of the present feast is proper and kindred, come, leading the
matter at hand back a little to a fitting beginning, let us try to produce
full persuasion for those who badly doubt toward the things that are clear.

The craftsman of the wholes, having willed to create man, brought him into
being not as a contemptible living being but as more honorable than all, and
showed him forth as king of the creation under heaven. And having chosen this
and having completed such a one, wise and God-formed, and having adorned him
with much grace, did he then bring him into being with such a purpose, that
having been born he might be corrupted and undergo complete destruction? But
the aim is vain, and it is exceedingly unworthy to refer such a thought to
God; for he is thus likened to children =9.254= who build with zeal and
quickly dissolve the construction, their mind ending toward no useful end. But
we have been taught entirely the contrary, that he created the first-formed
immortal, and when the transgression and the sin had come upon him he deprived
him of immortality as a penalty of the fault; then the fountain of goodness,
overflowing the love of mankind and being bent toward the work of its own
hands, adorned with wisdom and skill those whom he was well pleased to renew
[us] into the ancient state. These things both happen to be true and are
worthy of the conception concerning God; for they bear witness to him of the
power as well as of the goodness. But to have oneself impassibly and harshly
toward those ruled and shepherded is not even of good and best men; thus the
shepherd wishes the flock to be healthy for him and almost to be immortal, and
the cowherd by treatments of every kind increases the cows, and the goatherd
prays that the goats be twin-bearing, and simply every leader of a herd longs
for the herd to remain for him and to thrive, looking toward some useful end.
And this being so, and it having been shown from the things just now said by
us that it is most fitting for the craftsman and artisan of our race to remold
the corrupted work, it is clear that those who disobey the things that follow
fight from no other place than through thinking it impossible for God to raise
the dead and dissolved. Truly of dead and unfeeling ones is the mind that
reckons the impossible and impracticable upon God and carries the things of
its own weakness upon the almighty magnificence. And that we may reach their
mindlessness with refuting words =9.255=, let the future and disbelieved thing
be shown from the things that have come to be and that are; you have heard
that the dust was formed and became man; teach me then, I beg, you who claim
to grasp all things by your own wisdom


how the fine dust that was scattered was gathered, how the earth became
flesh and the same matter also made bones and skin and fat and hairs, how, the
flesh being one, the forms of the members and the qualities and touches are
different, how the lung is soft to the touch, livid in color, the liver tough
and red, the heart compressed and the hardest part in flesh, the spleen loose
and black, the omentum white and woven together by nature as a fishing-net.

Let us consider those things also, how the first woman from a small part of
the rib came to be a complete living being like the perfect and first, and the
part sufficed toward all things and the little constituted the whole; the rib
became a head, hands and feet, a winding and varied forming of inwards, flesh
and hairs, eye and nose and mouth and simply all things, that I may not lead
the word far, all things wonderful and paradoxical to us the few. But with God
the reasons of the construction are ready and exceedingly confessed; how then
will they seem to be sound-minded who grant that the one rib became a human,
but from the complete matter of the human disbelieve that the same is rebuilt?
It is not, it is not possible to be busy about the operations of God with
human inventions; for if all things were graspable for us, the better would
not be better than we. Why do I speak concerning God? Not even toward the
irrational of the living beings upon certain powers =9.256= do we have some
comparison, but we fall short of those also. At once horses and dogs and many
others surpass us in running, and camels and mules in power, and the donkeys
in marking of roads, and the sharp-sightedness of the gazelle is not found in
our eyes. Therefore it belongs to the well-minded and sound-minded to believe
the things said by God, and not to demand the manners and the causes of the
operations as overstepping, since it will be said to one of the busy-bodies:
show me by your word the substantiation of the things seen, say by what art he
crafted this many-formed work. For if you discover these things, you
reasonably are at a loss and vexed, because you are ignorant of the recasting
of the regeneration, you who know the reason of the generation. But if those
things are a dream to you and a fantasy and the recognition is from every side
impassable, do not be angry if, being ignorant of the reason of the
construction, you also do not see together the correction of the corrupted;
the same artisan is of both the first creation and the second recasting. He
knows how he will fit together again his own work that has undergone
dissolution into the ancient state.

If there is need of wisdom, the fountain of wisdom is with him; if of power,
he does not need a fellow-worker and helper. This is he who according to the
voice of the wisest prophet measured the water with the hand and the great and
boundless heaven with a span and the earth with a handful. Behold clear images
furnishing a signification of the unspeakable power, producing in our
reasonings a despair of not being able to imagine anything worthy of the
nature of God. He both is and is called almighty (for perhaps you will not
wrangle toward this, but you will grant it as =9.257= conceded to prevail),
and for the one who can do all things nothing is impassable or impracticable.
You have many pledges of the faith forcibly driving you toward agreeing with
the things said by us; first the whole varied and much-compounded creation
shouting more clearly than any proclamation, that the great and wise artisan
is the one who crafted all the things seen; and toward this God being
forethoughtful and seeing from afar the little souls of the unbelievers
confirmed by deed the raising of the dead, having ensouled many bodies of
those who had died. For this reason Lazarus, a four-day dead man, leapt out of
the tomb, and the only-begotten of the widow was given back to the mother,
having been loosed from the bier and the carrying-out toward the living, and
countless others, whom it is now burdensome to number. Why do I speak
concerning God and Savior, when, that those who doubt may be put to shame
still more, even to his servants the apostles to raise the dead


he granted the power? The demonstration then is clear, and why do you
contentious ones furnish troubles to us as to expounders of undemonstrated
words? As one was raised, so also ten, as the ten also three hundred, as three
hundred also the many; for the artisan of one statue will easily be also
craftsman of countless. Have you not seen the engineers, how of the great and
extraordinary buildings they pre-form the shapes and the types in a little
wax, and the reason in the small has the same power in the many and great
constructions? Great is the heaven, an artful workmanship of God; and since
God made man a rational living being, =9.258= that by the grasp of the things
made he might glorify the wise and well-devising maker, you will see the
little sphere of the astronomer, small indeed, but being moved in the hand of
the man of science thus as <the> heaven by God. And the least construction
becomes an image of the great workmanship, and in the small things the reason
interprets the over-bulky and the things overstepping our sense. And toward
what did I go through these things? That you may know that even if you ask me
how the resurrection of the bodies from of old will be, you will hear in
return swiftly: how was the four-day Lazarus raised? For it is clear that the
sound-minded will similarly receive the full persuasion upon the one also in
the many. Supposing God as the one doing it, say nothing impossible, nor think
the wisdom of the ungraspable graspable by your own thought; for neither is
anything infinite to him, and to you the things of the infinite are
unsearchable.

And we shall see this word well, if besides the things said we also examine
the manner of our generation, not that first and most ancient one from God,
concerning which it has been said in the things before, but the one until now
being accomplished correspondingly by nature; for this is impassable and
unapproachable to human reasoning. For how does the seed, being a moist and
unformed and shapeless substance, solidify into a head and become firm into
shins and ribs and make a brain soft and loose and the bone containing it so
hard and resistant, and the varied construction of the living being, that I
may speak briefly and not drag on going through the particulars in detail? As
therefore the seed, being shapeless in the beginnings, is typed into form and
is thickened into bulks, being constructed by the unspeakable art of God, so
it is nothing unlikely, =9.259= but even entirely following, that the matter
in the tombs, which once was in form, be renewed again into the old forming,
and the dust become a human again, just as indeed also at first it had the
generation from there. Let us grant to God to be able as much as the potter is
strong. For let us reckon what this one does; taking shapeless clay he forms
it into a vessel and, setting this toward the solar ray, he dries it and works
it solid, and the thing being molded is a little jar or a plate or a jar; but
when someone has fallen in disorderly and overturned it, it is shattered
having fallen and becomes unformed earth, and the artisan, having willed,
swiftly sets right what has happened and, having shaped clay again by the art,
works the vessel no worse than the one that once was. And the potter thus is a
small creature of the power of God, but God is disbelieved when he promises to
renew the one who has died. These things are of much mindlessness.

Let us consider also the example of the wheat, with which Paul the all-wise
educates the fools, saying: You fool, that which you sow, <is not made alive
unless it dies, and that which you sow,> you sow not the body that will come
to be, but a naked grain, if it happen, of wheat or of some of the remaining
seeds, and God gives it a body, just as he willed. Let us attend accurately to
the generation of the wheat, and perhaps we shall be taught the word
concerning us. The wheat is thrown into the earth, and having rotted through
in the moisture and, as one might say, having died, it ends into some milky
substance, which having been frozen a little becomes a sharp and white =9.260=
point, and having grown as much as to peep out of the earth from the white


it is changed toward the gently greening, then it becomes grass and hair of
the clods, and having been spread upon them and scattered moderately it runs
under from below a many-cleft root, preparing beforehand the under-base for
the coming weight; and just as the masts of ships are stretched from every
side by very many ropes, that they may remain fixed, being pulled against by
equal-balanced hauls, so the cord-like offshoots of the root become holds and
supports of the ears. And when the wheat has stood up into a reed and is
urgent toward the height, God under-props it with knees and knots, as securing
some house with bonds because of the expected heaviness of the hair. Then, the
strength having been prepared, having split the husk he leads forth the ear.
And again there greater wonders; for the wheat grows around it in a row and
each of the grains has an exceptional storehouse, and last the awns have been
projected, sharp and thin, weapons I think against the seed-picking birds,
that being pricked by the points of those they may not damage the fruit. You
see how much wonder-working one grain that has rotted through has, and having
fallen alone with how many it is raised; but a human takes on nothing more,
and what he had, he takes back, and for this reason our renewal appears easier
than the farming of the wheat.

From here pass over toward the thought of the trees, how winter becomes for
them each year in place of death; for the fruit drops off and the leaf falls
and the woods remain dry, deprived of every grace. And when the season of
spring takes hold, a most gracious flower grows over them and =9.261= after
the flower the covering of the leaves comes on, and then as a fair-formed
spectacle it both draws the sights of men and becomes workshops of singing
birds sitting in the petals, and some wonderful grace shines out about them,
so that many have even left a house adorned with gold and with the Thessalian
and Laconian stone, and have set for themselves more delightful the passing of
time under trees. Therefore also the patriarch Abraham pitched the tent under
an oak, not at all lacking a house, but exulting in the covering of the
branches. The life of the reptiles also leads me toward agreement with the
word at hand; for in the season of winter their kindling-power is made dead,
and the time of the six months they lie in the dens altogether unmoved. And
when the appointed time comes and thunder sounds through the world, receiving
the crash as some signal of life they leap up swiftly and through the long
time they operate the customary things. What is this reason? Let the examiner
and knower of the deeds of God say to me and teach me how he grants that the
snakes, being dead, are awakened by thunder, but does not give humans to be
ensouled when the trumpet of God sounds from heavens, as the divine word says:
For the trumpet will sound and the dead will be raised; and elsewhere again
more clearly: And he will send his angels with a trumpet of a great voice and
he will gather together his elect. Let us not then disbelieve the alterations
and the renewals; for the life of plants and of various living beings and
=9.262= of the humans themselves educates us, that nothing of the things in
corruption and generation is in identity, but in alteration and turning. And
first, if it seems good, let us learn thoroughly the change in our ages; we
know of what sort the sucking child is. A little time having passed it takes
the crawling power and differs in nothing from the small puppies, being
supported on four bases; about the third year it becomes upright and sends
forth a half-lisping and stammering voice; then it articulates the word and is
completed as a graceful lad; from that age it goes out toward the youth and
young man; and when down has covered the cheek, a shaggy beard after a little
and another from another; then a man in his prime, rough, enduring of
suffering. And when four decades of years have passed, a beginning of the
return, and grayness gently whitens the head and the strength toward


weakness declines, and last old age arrives, a complete vanishing of the
force, and the body is bent and is hunched toward earth as the over-dried of
the ears, and the smooth is completed as wrinkled, and again an infant is he
who was once a young man and a champion, stammering, being mindless, crawling
likewise upon hands and feet as of old. What do all these things seem to you?
Are they not alterations? Are they not many-mannered changes? Are they not
various newnesses transforming the mortal living being even before death? And
how would our sleep and waking not become for the wise a teaching-place of the
thing sought? For the one is an image of death, and the other an imitation of
the resurrection. Therefore also some of the =9.263= outside wise called sleep
a brother of death because of the likeness of the sufferings that happen from
each; for forgetfulness likewise is upon both and ignorance of the things past
and of the things coming, and the body lies unfeeling, not knowing a friend,
not recognizing an enemy, not seeing those standing around and watching, a
slackened corpse, sharing in no operation, differing in nothing from those
laid away in tombs and coffins.

Thus indeed you plunder the sleeper as a dead man if you wish, you empty the
house, you apply bonds, and no sense of the things being done comes upon him;
and a little later, when some holding-back and abatement of the affection has
come to be, as just now made alive the man stands up, coming little by little
into co-perception of himself and of the matters and taking up the operations
at leisure and as being ensouled by the kindling of wakefulness. And if, the
living being still existing and remaining, so many night and after day
ecstasies, alterations, changes, forgettings and rememberings have been fixed
beside in the life, it is exceedingly mindless and contentious not to believe
God who promises the last renewal, who also crafted the first forming. And
what most of all fences the contradictors and prepares them to disbelieve,
this before all as I think is thinking that a complete vanishing of the bodies
comes to be. But it is not so; for it does not vanish completely, but is
dissolved into the things from which it was composed and is in water and air
and earth and fire. And the prototype elements remaining, and the things from
those having gone over to those after the dissolution, the parts also are
saved in the wholes. And for God it is especially easy =9.264= to craft from
things not being (for thus in the beginnings all things received the
generation), and to generate from existing beginnings is much more surely
easiest and most facile. Let us not then take away the fair hope of men, the
setting-right of our weakness and the second, as one might say, generation
free of death, nor by excess of love of pleasure insult the good and
philanthropic promise of God; for they seem to me, the men contrary to the
subject at hand, to be companions of vice and enemies of virtue, lustful and
greedy and unrestrained in eyes and hearing and smell and in all the senses
receiving the pleasure flowing upon them. And since the word of the
resurrection has the judgment lying ahead, and they hear the sacred books
saying expressly that our life is not unaccountable (but when we have been
renewed toward the second life, we shall all stand beside the tribunal of
Christ, so as under that judge to receive the exchanges according to worth of
the things lived), knowing in themselves most shameful deeds worthy of many
punishments, by hatred of the judgment they take away also the resurrection,
as the wicked of the slaves who have spent the substance of the lord, and
writing down for themselves deaths of the master and destructions and molding
empty reasonings toward their own desire. But not even one of the sound-minded
will think thus; for what is the benefit of justice and truth and kindness and
of every fair thing, and for the sake of what do men labor and philosophize,
enslaving the pleasure of the belly and loving self-control and partaking of
sleep toward a little and arraying themselves toward winter and heat, if


there is no resurrection? Let us say toward these things the words of Paul:
Let us eat and drink, =9.265= for tomorrow we die. If there is no
resurrection, but death is the end of life, take away for me accusations and
blames, give unhindered authority to the man-slayer, leave the adulterer with
boldness to plot against marriages, let the greedy man revel against the
things of others, let no one cut short the reviler, let the perjurer swear
continually, for death awaits the oath-keeper also, let another lie as many
things as he wishes, for there is no fruit of the truth, let no one pity the
poor man, for the mercy is unpaid. These thoughts make a confusion worse than
the flood and cast out every sound-minded word, and sharpen every mad and
robber-like thought; for if there is no resurrection, neither is there
judgment, and if judgment has been taken away, the fear of God also is cast
out together; and where fear does not make sound-minded, there the devil
dances with the sin. And very fittingly toward such ones David wrote that
psalm: The fool said in his heart: there is no God. They were corrupted and
were made abominable in practices. If there is no resurrection, Lazarus and
the rich man are a myth, and the frightful chasm and the unendurable flaming
of the fire and the burning tongue and the longed-for drop of the water and
the finger of the beggar. For it is clear that all these things image-out the
future of the resurrection; for tongue and finger are not thought members of
the bodiless soul, but parts of the body. And let no one think these things
have already been done, but that they are a pre-announcement of the future;
and it will be then, when the recasting, having ensouled the dead, leads up
each of the things lived to the accounts, being composite as before and
constituted through soul and body. And the =9.266= God-borne Ezekiel, viewer
also of the great visions, being led toward what thought then did he see that
great and unfolded plain full of the human bones, concerning which he was
commanded to prophesy? And fleshes at once grew around those, and the things
loosed and thrown about in disorder were glued to one another into order and
harmony. Or is it clear that through such words he sufficiently indicates to
us the coming-to-life-again of this flesh? And they seem to me, those who have
themselves contentiously toward this word, to be not only impious but also
deranged; for resurrection and coming-to-life-again and recasting and all such
names carry the thought of the hearer toward the body that is subject to
corruption. As the soul itself examined according to itself will never rise,
since it does not even die, but is incorruptible and indestructible, and being
immortal it has a mortal partner of the matters, and for this reason with the
just judge in the time of the accounts it will dwell again in the
fellow-worker, that with that one it may receive the common chastisements or
the honors. Or rather that the word may become still more following for us,
let us consider thus: what do we say the human is? The both-together or the
other? But it is clear that the yoking of the two characterizes the living
being; for it is not fitting to drag on in the undisputed and known things.

And this being so, let us also reckon that in addition, whether the things men
do, such as adultery, murder, theft, and every thing that is next to these, or
conversely self-control, restraint, and every operation opposite to vice, we
say to be results of the two, or we confine the deeds to the soul alone? But
in this also =9.267= the truth is clear; for nowhere does the soul, splitting
off from the body, either practice the theft or work the wall-digging, nor
again does it alone give bread to the hungry or give drink to the thirsty or
go urgently without shrinking toward the prison, that it may tend the one
afflicted in the prison, but upon every deed both lay hold together with one
another and complete together the things that come to be. How then, these
things being so, and granting that there will be a judgment of the things
lived, do you tear the one from the other and, the things worked being common
<ones>, confine to the soul alone the


tribunal? And if someone should become an accurate judge of the human faults
and should consider carefully from where the first causes of the sin grow,
perhaps he will find first the body being disorderly in the charges. For
often, the soul being at rest and having an undisturbed calm, the eye saw
passionately things which it was better not to have beheld, and having sent
the disease on to the soul it changed the quiet into winter and a wave.
Likewise the hearings, having heard some unseemly or provoking words, as
through certain pipes of themselves bring in upon the reasonings the mire of
the disturbance or of the unseemliness. And there is a time when the nose also
through the smell and the vapors † disposes great and incurable evils upon the
inner man. And the hands also know through the touch to make womanish the
firmness of a steadfast soul. And as I go upon it thus little by little and
consider, the little body is found responsible for the many sins. And it also
bears the labors for virtue and in the contests =9.268= it labors of the
fair things, being cut with iron and burned with fire and carded with whips
and weighed down with painful bonds and undergoing every maiming, that it may
not betray the sacred philosophy as some fair-towered city encircled by the
war of vice. If then in right-doings it labors together with the soul and in
sins it is not left behind, from where setting out do you lead the bodiless
alone upon the tribunal? But the word is neither just nor of sound-minded
ones. If it sinned alone and naked, punish it alone also, but if it has the
fellow-worker apparent, the judge being just will not release this one. And I
hear the scripture also saying this, that upon those condemned just
punishments will be laid, fire and darkness and worm; which all are
chastisements of the composite and material bodies, but fire would never touch
a soul according to itself, nor would darkness pain it, sharing not in eyes
and the seeing organs. And what would the worm also do, being destructive of
bodies, not of spirits? And for this reason by these following reasonings we
are driven from every side toward agreement with the raising of the dead,
which God will accomplish in the due times, confirming his own promises by
deeds.

Let us believe then the one who says: For the trumpet will sound and the dead
will be raised, and again: The hour is coming, in which all =9.269= who are in
the tombs will hear his voice and will come out, those who have done the good
things into a resurrection of life, and those who have practiced the worthless
things into a resurrection of judgment. For he does not only promise, but also
by the works which he accomplishes each day he teaches clearly that he is
almighty; for neither did he toil in the beginning crafting, nor in
transforming will he be at a loss for wisdom. Let us consider the present
things, and we shall not disbelieve the future; for every operation of God
having a following astonishment and much the wonder and unspeakable, when we
look down upon the likenesses of the fathers and great-grandfathers passing
over accurately into the forms of the descendants and the children becoming
impressions from the forefathers, then indeed I am over-astonished at the
all-wise art of the best-artisan God and Savior, how the imitations of
archetypes that are neither being nor appearing are crafted in an unspeakable
mystery and are remolded, raising some other dead through the operation of the
types. And often also the properties of many faces together are imaged-out in
one body; the nose of the father, the eye of the grandfather, the gait of the
uncle, the voice of the mother, and one human is seen as some plant having
received the grafts of many trees and fruiting countless kinds for those who
gather fruit.

All these things are wonderful and how they come to be is unknown to us, but
they are ready for the craftsman and are accomplished with much ease as we
know. And it is exceedingly out of place and unlearned, that the marks of the
bodies already rotten and corrupted are raised in those now growing each day
and the alien things pass over into others,


but not to agree that the own and exceptional things concerning those who once
possessed them =9.270= are renewed and come to life again, but on the contrary
to cancel and fight and to think the promise a myth, not a word, of the one
who constituted this whole that is seen and adorned it as he willed. But we at
least have believed in the resurrection, sending up glory to the Father and to
the Son and to the Holy Spirit, now and always and unto the ages of the ages,
amen.
