* Preface to the Letters of Theodore Lascaris
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of the megas logothetes kyr George Acropolites. If the hide bears the circuit
of heaven and the narrow type writes the hollow of the earth, this book also
succeeds in enclosing an unmeasured abyss of knowledge, an infinite unbounded
abundant outpouring of wise streams from monstrous veins. As stars it has held
white-composed words fallen out into an unmeasured multitude and all-shining
for those who behold, shining from within upon the nature of men and making
bright their minds. Again as a many-colored sight of flowers the varied
composition of the writings the book shuts within all-brilliant, as the oyster
a pearl, as the calyx a rose, as the sea-nurtured shell the flower of purple,
letters wrought out unto beauty, which is also the profit of the
word-merchants, of Theodore Lascaris the emperor, son of the all-glorious lord
John, whose fame ran out into every land, whose part toward weapons and toward
horsemanship, a mighty hand, a well-natured drawing of the bow, and every
other soldierly art and every other manliness of soul, belongs to other
discourses and studies; but now writings and elegance of words, a composed
labor of letters. Long discourses this man also brings forth, some toward
examination for which nature is the place, some toward unfolding for which a
better definition, and others again boasting the matter of encomia, but all
solemn as being exceptional in thoughts, figures, remaining forms, by which
every discourse is splendidly adorned. And who would sail past their grace, as
the son of Laertes the Sirens, and not moor at these very writing-tablets of
the letters the vessel of his mind? Since the books do not procure destruction
of those who hear, but rather life, not then wasting with time as that of
bodies. And these things indeed concerning the long-composed, but here there
is another grace of writings, an epistolary one too toward friends of words, a
Helicon of the Muses and a new Pieria, a clear-sounding melody and the beat of
dances. What mouth would tell the delight of the song? What discourse would
express the solemnity of the sound? The votary Bacchus was found at once
filled with nectar, a divine drink, as flowing from the pole of the book
itself. Why then do I stretch the discourse long and at the gates of the
chamber I hold back the greedy hearing, which hastens to enter, so as to
suffer a loss not moderate? Enter quickly, quickly within, stranger, revel in
the wise thoughts, and be filled even to satiety of the figures too, lest you
run past any seasoning of the diction; for it will become for you as honey
that sweetens. Fill yourself of the book to the utmost of all and marvel at
the first gushing of the words.
