OF OUR FATHER AMONG THE SAINTS ATHANASIUS A DIALOGUE AT THE
COUNCIL IN NICAEA AGAINST ARIUS.
By the providence of God I was going, having come with my
dearest brothers toward the servants of God longed for by me. And as I was
walking somewhat gently because of the weakness of the body together with the
aforementioned servants of God, one of the men traveling with me asked me
concerning the Lord's saying that states: "My Father who sent me is greater
than I." And I say to the inquiry which he brought forward: For the present
hold back the inquiry concerning this, sweetest of brothers; but when, God
permitting, we return to our own, we shall make the examination of the chapter
most secure. And when I had said this, a sudden holding of piety comes to be
in my heart, and I was saying in myself: Not without purpose did the blessed
Paul, writing to his own disciple Timothy, say: "Exercise yourself toward
piety." For truly a divine treasure filled with unspeakable and incomparable
goods is piety toward the Divine. And I was saying in myself in thought, that
piety is magnificent and glorious, and able to save the one who has drawn near
to its comeliness. And there also entered me a parable of my Lord Jesus Christ
concerning the blessed piety, how it is some greatest thing and filled with
all ambrosial riches, and is rarely found among men, unless perhaps from the
many a few scarcely by a truth-loving judgment attain its incorruptible
beauty. And these too have not by their own power seized this desirable sight
of it; but having set out friendship toward God, having used frequent prayers,
they have received this as a gift from God, so that those who behold its most
fair dignity may take eternal joy into their breast. The Lord says: "The
kingdom of the heavens is like a treasure hidden in a field, which a man
having found rejoiced, then goes and sells all =28.441= that he has, and buys
that field." Then as standing in my heart, it was conversing with me kindly,
and says: Man, even if your sins are many, hold strongly piety toward the
Divine. For through this both the forgiveness of sins is given from God, and
death is conquered, and a man will be able most excellently to drive into the
kingdom of the heavens. And besides these I have also taken into remembrance
this: "God knows to deliver the pious, but to keep the lawless for a day of
judgment, being punished with eternal fire." For this lawless one is also
impious; and from impiety sin comes to be. Therefore the mother of all evils
is impiety, and the nurse of all blameworthy things is mindlessness. Blessed
then is the son of piety, because this one shall be called a son of Christ.
And suddenly we arrived at the place toward which we had made our setting out,
and meeting the genuine ones, we all continued gladly, both we, because we
were counted worthy to behold the saints, and they, because they have seen
brothers. And as we were conversing at length of the things belonging to the
most reverend religion with much good order and sparing, suddenly from the
root of the Christ-fighting Arius a human-formed beast leapt in upon us, and
having listened for a short while to the words of piety, not being able to
bear it, spoke such things: "Teach me your glory, or I will teach you my
mind." And I answered him: "Do not call all into inquiry; for they are great
in the house of God. But if you wish for the present to examine together with
me the smallest one only, most gladly I will grant you this concerning the
question. Bring forward first

the proposition; only let us make the co-examination in a truth-loving way,
and let us not be disposed more contentiously toward the God-inspired words
brought forward by the neighbor." ARIUS said: It seems good to me that there
be defined beforehand by both of us, me and you, for the things inquired, and
that each of us set out what he thinks concerning God and Christ and Holy
Spirit, in summary, so that we may send the words according to a mark, and not
make a vain contest of words, and that the prevailing opinion be manifest, and
likewise also the one being overthrown. For I wish to inquire with you
concerning faith, since I have especially heard that you do not discourse
rightly concerning the faith toward the Divine and piety. ATHANASIUS said: In
a manner befitting God all things have been prepared for you; and what I
think, I say in summary. I believe in one God Father almighty, always being
God Father, and in the God Word, the Only-begotten Son of God, that he
coexists with his own Father, and that the Son is from the essence of God and
Father, and that the Son is equal of the Father, and that the Son of God is
equal in honor, and that with his Father he is present everywhere by the
Godhead =28.444=, and that he contains all things by his essence, and that the
Son of God is contained by none, just as also God his Father; and in the Holy
Spirit, that it is of the essence of the Father, and that the Holy Spirit is
coeternal with the Father and the Son. I say that the Word has come to be in
flesh. AR. said: I also believe in one eternal God, and in his Son, whom God
created before the ages, and made a Son. And all things, as many as the Son
has, not having them he received from God; and that the Son is not equal, nor
equal in honor to the Father, but falls far short of the glory of God, as a
work, he falls short of the power of God; and in the Holy Spirit, which has
come to be by the Son. Behold, I also think these things. ATH. said: For the
rest begin the inquiry with all science; for I see you held in great evils,
even if you especially, having come forward, put us under accusation, as
though we do not think rightly concerning Father and Son and Holy Spirit. AR.
said: How do you say God always Father, who being God alone created his Son by
his own will, as it has been written? ATH. said: Where has it been written
that the Father is alone? AR. said: In Paul the apostle; for he says: "But to
us one God, from whom are all things." ATHAN. said: Read the following sayings
of the Apostle, for he said: "And one Lord Jesus Christ, through whom are all
things." Arius said: You see that the Son is a minister, and not a
self-worker, as he himself said concerning himself: "Unless he sees the Father
doing something; for whatever the Father does, these things the Son also
does." Attend how the Son is a minister and not a creator. ATH. said: See; do
not fight against the truth. For what saying of Paul you seem to hold
concerning God Father, this you will find said also concerning the Son in the
Apostle himself; for he says: "And he himself set in the Church, first
apostles, second prophets, then evangelists, then shepherds and teachers,
toward the perfecting of the peoples, for the work of ministry, for the
building of the body of Christ, until we all arrive at the unity of the faith
and of the knowledge of the Son of God, at a perfect man, at the measure of
stature of the fullness of Christ, that we may no longer be infants, tossed
and carried about by every wind of teaching, in the dice-play of men, in
craftiness toward the method of deceit, but speaking truth in love, we may
grow into him in all things, who is the head, the Christ; from whom the whole
body, being fitted together and brought together through every joint of the
supply," and what follows. You see that the voice is predicated in common of
Father and Son, and you have taken it one-sidedly concerning the Father?
=28.445= AR. said: You have remembered well concerning this the whole passage
of the Apostle; but I consider more trustworthy than the apostolic saying the
previously mentioned Lord's oracle by me. He said

for the Lord, not to be able to do anything from himself, unless he sees
the Father doing something. ATH. said: What did God do in the beginning?
AR. said: Heaven, earth, sea, and all things in them, as it has been written.
ATH. said: Therefore it remains to be shown by you, what sort of heavens and
earth and sea and the things in them God made first, and then thus his Son,
having seen, made similarly to his Father. AR. said: I consider it unlawful to
say that a first world has been created by God, and another by the Son, a
second not being able to be shown, the matter being clear to all, that the
seen world is one. ATH. said: Therefore the Son creates the all from himself.
AR. said: I beseech you, tell me, how from himself? And there is not one who
will say this. ATH. said: Where does the wise man find his counsel? AR. said:
In his wisdom. ATH. said: And Christ is the power and wisdom of God according
to the blessed Paul, yes or no? AR. said: It is confessed that Christ is the
power of God and wisdom of God. ATH. said: Does wisdom then get its wisdom
from elsewhere? AR. said: Let it not be! ATH. said: Therefore it does all
things from itself as wisdom? AR. said: So it is. Therefore the Father created
nothing? ATH. said: Listen, and do not speak rashly things which it is not
lawful either to say or to hear, as though no one were able to turn such
things. But for the present give me an account concerning what I ask. How does
the mighty one work of himself? AR. said: In his power he does the all. ATH.
said: And Christ exists firmly as the power of God? AR. said: It has been
confessed. ATH. said: Therefore Christ being the power of God, what things the
power creates, that is, the Christ, these the Father does. AR. said: How do
the things which Christ creates, these God create? ATH. said: Has the power
been separated from the mighty one? AR. said: Let it not be! For who has ever
seen a combatant being manly in wrestling on his own, and his power by itself?
For if this were so, the prizes, that is the crowns, would have been brought
to both. ATH. said: You have spoken rightly. If then in the case of a man you
have granted this, that neither power does anything separately, nor indeed the
body otherwise works anything, therefore Christ, being paternal power,
inseparably all things which the Father does, these he also does. AR. said:
Let it be so, that the Son creates the all inseparably with God; can he also
=28.448= be equal to God, as you have heard the Savior saying: "My Father, who
sent me, is greater than I"? ATH. said: I will ask you; and you answer me: Did
my Savior utter things contrary to himself? AR. said: Let it not be! For
everywhere the Son of God is in agreement with himself. ATH. said: How then
does the Lord say in another place, "I and the Father are one"? AR. said: You
are Sabellius. ATH. said: Why am I Sabellius? AR. said: Because you said, The
Father and the Son is one. ATH. said: Do not lie against me; for I do not say
thus, that the Father and the Son is one, but that the Lord said: "I and the
Father are one." AR. said: This is the heresy of Sabellius. ATH. said:
Therefore the Lord who has said, "I and the Father are one," was Sabellius? Do
not all Christians know Jesus the Son of God? and you say him to be Sabellius
and a teacher of heresy? AR. said: Let it not be for me to think this! For the
Savior said concerning agreement, that "I and the Father are one." ATHAN.
said: For the sake of what did the Lord speak such things? wishing to present
what? the agreement according to you, or the Godhead according to the right
account? For if the agreement according to you, then he was preparing
witnesses against the Father, lest perhaps a setting-aside come to be for him
by the Father; and if this is so, the Son accuses the Father according to you,
as ungrateful, and not remaining in the covenants of the agreement of the
Father. And if this also is so, then understand where your mind leads you. AR.
said: But what do you wish that we say, that the Father and the Son is one?
ATH. said: The Lord Jesus did not say, "I and the Father

I am one;" but he said, "we are one," and he presented the perfect dyad in
saying, "we are one." And the "one" signified that of the essence; but you,
not having understood, went into absurdity. AR. said: Did the Lord not say:
"The Father, who sent me, is greater than I"? ATH. said: Did the Savior not
say: "I and the Father are one," and again: "He who has seen me has seen the
Father"? But indeed also the Apostle clearly showed the equality of the Son,
which he has toward his own Father, saying: "Who, being in the form of God,
did not count it robbery to be equal with God; but emptied himself." You see
that the Son is equal of his own Father, and not smaller? AR. said: Much
disagreement of the Scriptures appears to me for the present. ATH. said: Let
it not be that a man speak against the divine and God-inspired Scriptures, as
though they utter all these things not with agreement! For as a just yoke, so
all the Scriptures agree with themselves. AR. said: How do they agree? The
Savior said: "The Father who sent me is greater than I =28.449=;" again the
same says: "I and the Father are one;" and otherwise: "He who has seen me has
seen the Father;" through which he signifies his unvarying form, which he has
toward his own Father; as also Paul says that the Son is equal of his own
Father. ATH. said: Man, hear and understand, that that chapter which states:
"My Father, who sent me, is greater than I," has not been unyoked from the
remaining chapters. AR. said: If you show this, which is impossible, perhaps
you will make even me follow your mind. ATH. said: Is the Son of God God, or
not? AR. said: A God created by God. ATH. said: As you wish, answer me
concerning what I ask. Did the Son of God make all things, yes or no? AR.
said: It has been given beforehand that he made all things, as being power and
wisdom of God. ATH. said: Therefore the Son of God contains all things made by
him, whether visible or invisible. AR. said: So it is. ATH. said: Therefore he
is present everywhere by the essence of his greatness. If you say that he is
not present everywhere, then you will say the things that have come to be
greater than the creator. AR. said: He is present everywhere. ATH. said: If
then the Word of God is present everywhere, how do you say this, "My Father
who sent me is greater than I," give me the account. AR. said: I am at a loss
concerning this, but I ask, tell me, by what account did the Savior say the
Father greater than himself? ATH. said: If you do not first tell me what I
ask, I will not tell you what you wish. AR. said: Say what you wish. ATH.
said: Are the angels bodiless, or men? AR. said: Men are in a body, but
angels, being spirits, are bodiless. ATH. said: Who then toil, men or angels?
AR. said: Angels are bodiless, so as, ministering to him, not to take
experience of labor; but to toil is proper to a man, because of the heavy drag
of the body. ATH. said: How then did the Lord toil? for it has been written,
"And Jesus, having toiled from the journey, was sitting." AR. said: You have
led me as you wished from the example of the angels and of the men, not as God
Word did he toil, but in order that it might appear that flesh, which the Word
and God did not have formerly, this he took up; for this reason he permitted
the flesh to suffer its own things, in order that it might appear that it is
flesh. ATH. said: Remember that it has been confessed beforehand that the Word
and God is present everywhere. AR. said: Firmly. ATH. said: If then he is
present everywhere, for the sake of what was he saying, "My Father who sent me
is greater than I"? AR. said: I beseech you, do not shrink from uncovering the
covering from the letter quickly. For I am exceedingly distressed. ATH. said:
=28.452= Who is the one who has toiled, a man, or the God Word? AR. said: A
man. ATH. said: Therefore it is yours to understand for the rest. AR. said: I
have grasped your mind in this word, that the God Word being present
everywhere, it is clear that because of the flesh he said, "My Father, who
sent me, is greater than I," the flesh that walks and toils, and from place to

place goes to another. Truly this is rightly so. ATH. said: For the rest take
into mind for me the saying which you said, that the God Word was created.
Whence shall we take the beginning? I do not dare thus to speak without fear
against the eternal Godhead of his Son. AR. said: I say this from the
Scriptures; but you, whence do you say the Son eternal? ATH. said: From the
God-inspired Scriptures I learned that the Son of God is eternal. AR. said:
What are the Scriptures that state the Son eternal? ATH. said: Paul of famous
name, having come, will testify to the truth: "For his invisible things," he
says, "from the creation of the world are seen, being understood by the things
made, both his eternal power and divinity, so that they are without excuse,
because having known God, they did not glorify him as God, or give thanks."
AR. said: The Scripture says this concerning the Father. ATH. said: Therefore
the blessed apostle Paul accuses those who have not recognized the eternal
Godhead of the God Son of God who appeared through a body, of the one who
ordered the all? for from the greatness and beauty of created things the
creator, having been recognized analogously, ought to be thanked. And it is
manifest that all things that have come to be have come to be through the
Word, and without him has come to be not even one; therefore it was necessary
that you also from the virtuous compacting of the elements analogize the
creator analogously, and become a knower of yourself, that the world is a work
of eternal God, and not of created nature, which is not able to suffice to do
so many things. AR. said: Let the Son be eternal, but also having been created
eternally he has the eternal. ATH. said: If the Son is eternal, he was not a
creature; and if he is a creature, he was not eternal. AR. said: For I do not
say these things from my own authority; the Son said that God created him
first of all things. ATH. said: Was this revealed to you alone, or also to
others, the mystery concerning the Son God? AR. said: "Not of all is the
faith; yet not to me alone, but also to many others this faith has been made
known." ATH. said: Whence was it revealed to you, from God, or from men? AR.
said: From God. ATH. said: Have you become a hearer yourself, or did others
instruct you concerning this? AR. said: They did not instruct me, but many
saints, speaking in the Spirit of God, said that the Son was created. ATH.
said: Who are these? For are we not able to know also? AR. said: You yourself
also know that both the prophets and the apostles spoke in many places that
the Son is a creature =28.453=. ATH. said: I heard you saying a short hour ago
that the Son said. Just now you have shifted to the prophets and apostles. Yet
instruct me what sort of prophet spoke such things concerning this? AR. said:
And now I say that the Son speaks through the holy prophets and apostles, as
also through Solomon he said: "The Lord created me a beginning of his ways for
his works." You see that before all things God created him because of the
works that were going to come to be through him. ATH. said: Therefore
according to you the pretext of the creatures has become a cause of the Son
being created. AR. said: So it is. ATH. said: Are then the things created by
him more honorable than God? How does it admit? For if that one came to be
because of these, and not those because of this one, according to you, firmly
these are more honorable than that one; but let it not be that we think thus!
For all things are because of the Son, as it has been written: "For it was
fitting," says the Apostle, "for whom are all things and through whom are all
things, in bringing many sons to glory, to perfect the pioneer of their
salvation through sufferings." AR. said: Forgive me for having said that the
Son was created because of the works, yet that he was created before all
things. ATH. said: Who was created before all the created things? AR. said:
The Son, and because of this he has also been written firstborn. ATH. said: If
someone worships Satan, does he do well or badly? AR. said: Impious and
godless, and not even keeping the common wits, is such a one, whom it is not
worthy to call a man, but un-man. ATH. said: God, giving an oracle to Job,
spoke thus concerning Satan, that is: "A beginning of the Lord's formation,
made to be mocked by my angels." You then,

the first work worshipping, worship Satan, as the account has presented. And
if you should wish to be wise, that Satan is a former work of the Son, then do
you say him older than the Holy Spirit? for thus you defined, that the Son has
been created by God; and you confirmed that the Spirit has come to be by the
Son. AR. said: The truth circumscribes me, and not you. Yet since you have led
me in this, I also will shut you in with the many testimonies that state that
the Son is a creature. ATH. said: Bring forth from the treasure the things
stored up by your father. AR. said: My father is God, whom I worship; yet you
answer me concerning this, "The Lord created me," the Lord having confessed
himself to have been created by his own Father. ATH. said: The saying has
another spiritual power within, and it possesses a divine treasure stored in
it. AR. said: I do not set the voice of Solomon upon the saying only; for I am
not without understanding, but I understand that a spiritual meaning is
contemplated by those who have science. Whence =28.456= I also, not being
without a share of the initiation, say thus, that the "The Lord created me" is
according to the saying, but the power in it I see greater which it has, which
you, not having understood, being held by ignorance, are led astray, and it is
proper to a bad man to envy those who are better. ATH. said: What then you
think, tell me also without envy, what seems to you to appear good. AR. said:
For the "The Lord created me" I grasp upon the "he begot me." ATH. said: If
then the Son was begotten, clearly he was not created; and if he was created,
he was not begotten. AR. said: You do not understand this power. ATH. said: Do
not shrink from bearing with me, until I understand, I beseech you. AR. said:
In order that a passion not be understood, he said, "The Lord created me;"
therefore in the case of God the creating is the same as the begetting. ATH.
said: If then in the case of God the creating and begetting is the same,
therefore all the creatures are offspring of God. And if these things are so,
then the Lord is brother of all the things that have come to be through him,
according to you. Where then is the Only-begotten of God? AR. said: The
only-begotten is assigned upon an exceptional and most eminent and greatest
thing. ATH. said: Does it not seem to you that the heaven is greatest? AR.
said: And very much. ATH. said: Therefore according to you the Son is, and the
earth also and the sea daughters according to you. AR. said: Let it not be!
God made one Son only. ATH. said: Does it seem to you that all things by the
same definition of generation are only-begotten, yes or no? AR. said: So it
is. ATH. said: Therefore if the Son was made as also the remaining created
things, how is he Son of God? AR. said: I beseech you, how do you understand
the "The Lord created me"? ATH. said: Be persuaded; I would not have answered
you; but because of those who hear I will speak. Solomon, being just, and
filled with wisdom, was counted worthy in a parable to signify beforehand the
creation of the temple, that is of the body of the Lord Jesus, as the Lord
himself says to the Jews: "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise
it. And this he was saying concerning the temple of his body," as John says.
But indeed also Solomon more freely spoke concerning these very things, taking
them thus: "Wisdom built herself a house, and supported seven pillars. She
slew her own victims, and mixed her own wine into a mixing-bowl, and prepared
her own table; she sent her own servants, calling with a high proclamation
upon a mixing-bowl, saying: "He who is foolish, let him turn aside toward me,"
and to those lacking wits she said: "Come, eat my bread, and drink wine, which
I have mixed for you; leave folly, in order that you may live." Firmly wisdom
is God Word, who because of his unspeakable philanthropy and that of his
Father became poor being rich, in order that we by that one's =28.457= poverty
might become rich, being in the form of God, he came to be in the form of a
slave, and being equal of his own Father, as a slave and fellow-slave he lived
with us, being perfect in Godhead, and perfect in humanity. This one then, the
Word and God, being paternal wisdom and power, being bodiless,

as God also his Father, because of our salvation built the body in the womb of
Mary the Theotokos, without communion of a man, and became, as I said, a man,
and made himself a pattern of every virtue; in order that those who wish to
follow as through a path may have a divine impress, toward which being
likened, they become partakers of divine nature, as the Vessel of election
says: "Become imitators of me, as I also of Jesus Christ." After then his
passion and the resurrection, wisdom, the power, the Word and God, sent the
apostles toward all the foolish, the unintelligent gentiles, those who did not
know God, having set forth a table, that is the holy altar, and upon it
heavenly and incorruptible bread, and granting life to all who partake from
it, his holy and all-holy body, and wine gladdening heart, and producing
sobriety in the soul of each of those who taste from it, having mixed as upon
a mixing-bowl his own blood, having called the called and elect through his
apostles, leaving behind all folly, and being shown citizens of the kingdom of
the heavens, those who obey his voice. AR. said: Since you have led the "The
Lord created me" to the body, well, I also agree with you through the written
testimony. For the rest do you also walk straight toward the truth, and do not
do otherwise. ATH. said: What do you wish I do? What is dear to the truth, I
also agree. AR. said: I will furnish you a testimony which you will not be
able to allegorize; for it is manifest that it states concerning the
substantiation of the Word, as he was made by the Father. ATH. said: If you
give me from the God-inspired Scriptures a testimony concerning the things you
confirm, I am not unmindful; only attend, lest you have just now written
toward your own wish, and I do not bear the things said by you. AR. said: Do
not slander us as not fearing God. It is a voice of an apostle, which I said I
bring forward. ATH. said: Speak from it. AR. said: The most manifest of the
apostles, Peter, testifying concerning the one set before Israel, spoke thus:
"Let all the house of Israel know securely then, that God has made him both
Lord and Christ." Have we at all engraved this voice? ATH. =28.460= said: You
have not engraved it, but you have counter-engraved it. AR. said: You have
seen yourself being circumscribed, and you have turned to insults. Rather, if
you are able, speak toward these things, or desert to the truth. ATH. said:
Read the whole chapter. AR. said: "Let all the house of Israel know securely
then, that God has made him both Lord and Christ." ATH. said: Do not mutilate
the words of the Scripture. For also the body as it was formed from the
beginning, if it remains, is well-formed; but if it comes to be beside a
member, that is a hand, or a foot, or a finger, it is shown unseemly. Say then
also the following of the Scriptures. AR. said: That is, "Jesus, whom you
crucified." ATH. said: Who is the one crucified, the Word God, or the body
which the Word God took from Mary the virgin? AR. said: Do you not confess the
Word to have been crucified? ATH. said: I confess the God Word, having become
man, to have been crucified in flesh. AR. said: What is in flesh? did not the
Word suffer? ATH. said: You confess first what you think. AR. said: Even if
you do not say, I say, the Word was nailed together, and suffered together
with the flesh. ATH. said: It is necessary through a parable to present the
demonstration, whether the Word and God has suffered. AR. said: Say what you
wish. ATHAN. said: Have you ever seen one nailed to a cross? Arius said: Yes.
ATHAN. said: Then until the body was held by the nails upon the cross, was the
soul also held, not being able to withdraw from the body? AR. said: It
withdraws, since the soul is spirit. ATHAN. said: If it withdraws, it is clear
that it is not held upon the cross, that is upon the wood and the nails, and
so it is. For the body being held, the soul, having the free and the unheld,
having left the body,

goes to its own place. AR. said: So this also is. ATHAN. said: If then in the
case of a condemned man you have granted that the soul does not suffer in the
present bodily condemnation, how did you say that the Word and God has been
fixed together with the body? AR. said: Let it be granted that the God Word
has not suffered together with the body. ATH. said: Therefore the apostle
Peter made the word secure, not wishing some to perish by ignorance. For the
sons of Israel thinking the Christ according to us to be a man, he signified
through the testimony that this one is the elect of God; and he says: Even if
