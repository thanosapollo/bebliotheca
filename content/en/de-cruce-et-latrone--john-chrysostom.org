* On the Cross and the Thief
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

On the same day. A discourse of our father among the saints Chrysostom on the
precious Cross and on the thief. Bless, father.
1 Let us begin, on the day of the Cross, the discourse concerning the Cross,
and let us crown the day, or rather let us be crowned by the remembrance of
the day; for it is not from what we utter that the Cross is crowned, but from
what we confess with gratitude we receive the crowns from it. Today, brothers,
the Cross was planted and the world was sanctified; today the Cross was
planted and death was overturned; today the Cross shone forth and the demons
were scattered; today the Cross awarded the prize and the devil was put to
shame; today the Cross shone forth and death was conquered and man was set
free and God was glorified.
2 Today among the Jews the earth was shaken, but among us the inhabited world
is made firm; today the God-fighting daring of the Jews split the rocks; today
the God-loving piety united the inhabited world. For it was fitting that the
things of piety be joined together, and the things of impiety be split, as
David says: They were split, and they were not pricked. Today among the Jews
the sun from the sixth hour until the ninth worked darkness; for the creature
could not bear the outrage against the Maker. For it drew in its rays so that
it might not become a spectator of the God-fighting hatred, not because
outrage touched the Godhead, but because what was done by those who dared it
was done as outrage.
3 Today among the Jews the sun changed its ray; today grace among us makes the
night into day. For it is dear to piety even in darkness to enjoy light, but
to impiety even in light to be filled with darkness. For the faithful even the
night is reckoned as day, but for the unbelieving even the day is changed into
night. Therefore Scripture, speaking of the faithful, says: And night is
illumination in my delight, because the darkness shall not be darkened from
you, and night shall be lighted as day. For the believer, then, even the night
is changed into day, but for the unbeliever even the day is shifted into
night, as the prophet cries saying: They shall grope as the blind a wall, and
they shall walk in the day as at midnight.
4 Today, brothers, Adam was cast out of paradise through disobedience; today
he is brought in again into paradise. And the thief is witness. A thief went
out and a thief went in; he went out who stole contrary to the will of the
commandment, and he went in who stole salvation from the Cross. I know I have
spoken before about the thief, but the discourse of the Cross has various
accounts. That thief is put to death through a tree; this thief gathers
salvation from a tree. The earthly things were not enough for him, but he
plundered the heavenly as well, not bounding them by power, but conquering by
faith. For this is the voice of the Master saying: The kingdom of heaven
suffers violence, and the violent snatch it.
5 On the Cross two thieves, an image of Jews and of Greeks, of those who first
traveled in impiety and afterward came to know the truth. An image of the Jews
is the thief who remained a thief to the end; until the Cross they ran
together with one another the road of wickedness, and the Cross divided the
roads; for from the Cross the two are divided, a road of destruction for those
who disbelieve and a road of salvation for those who believe. There the thief
learned the difference of the roads, that the Lord knows the way of the
righteous, and the way of the ungodly shall perish. 6 And see the discernment
of God, that you too may say with the prophet: Righteous are you, O Lord, and
upright are your judgments. For one sin Adam is banished from paradise; for
one voice of faith the thief is saved; one sin casts that one out of the
delight, one righteousness houses this one in the delight. The thief entered
into paradise. O the wonder. Not even to Abraham was a promise of paradise
given by a voice; for he inherits through faith, but a promise of paradise he
did not receive even before the thief.
7 Here attend carefully, having searched the whole old Scripture and the new;
you will find that none of the righteous appears to have received the promise
by a voice before the thief, not Abraham, not Moses, not the prophets, not
apostles, but before all the thief hears: Amen, amen I say to you, today you
shall be with me in paradise. God called Abraham saying: Go out from your land
and from your kindred and from the house of your father, and he did not say,
that you may inherit paradise, but, Come into a land which I will show you. To
Moses God gave the law, and as fruit of the commandments he promises the goods
upon the earth: If you keep the law of the Lord your God. Nowhere a promise of
paradise before the thief.
8 But it is necessary to inquire for what reason he promised to this one
before the others and before such trustworthy men. Abraham believed God, but
he believed him not appearing in such outrage; he believed him speaking from
heaven, he believed him sending forth the word through holy angels and
bringing the commandment from his own authority. Isaiah also believed, but
having seen him in glory; Ezekiel also believed, but having beheld him upon
the cherubim; the prophets also believed, but each in a different way saw his
own Master in glory, as it was possible for human nature to see. Moses
believed, but he believed God speaking from the midst of the fire and the
gloom and the trumpets and the thunders; these things were enough even to
nerve the unbeliever.
9 But I say these things not despising the prophets, but making a defense for
the one who received paradise. But he sees a savior not upon a royal throne,
not speaking in the heavens, not giving orders through an angel; he saw him in
outrage and worships him in glory; he saw him upon a cross and entreats him as
sitting in heaven. He sees him being condemned and calls the crucified one
king, and says: Remember me when you come in your kingdom.
10 You see him crucified, and you call king the one in the glory of
condemnation? Did you read the law, you who spent your time in lawlessness?
Did you read the prophets, you who spent your time in murders? Did you read
the divine law while sharpening the sword? From where were you taught to
philosophize such things concerning Christ? Jews crucify, they who knew the
law; you who are inexperienced in it call the condemned one God, you worship
the crucified. Whom did he lead into the glory of the kingdom? Let us speak on
behalf of the one who dared, through the facts; for we do not imitate the
outward character-drawing and personification, but we borrow the
starting-points of the subjects from the facts themselves. Who taught you to
say such things concerning him? The law, he says, did not teach me, but the
sun, having hidden itself, taught me. I saw it shuddering at the outrage of
the Cross; I saw also the earth split and not bearing the Christ-killing
beasts; I saw also the rocks split. Rocks were split, but the unfeeling heart
is not dissolved; the rocks were split, and the veil of the temple was torn in
two.
11 What is the veil? As if we should say a curtain; for we must use this
customary word, from which all understand what the veil was, not because I am
interpreting something difficult, but because for many it is unintelligible. A
veil, a woven work made of purple and scarlet and fine linen and hyacinth.
When therefore the Cross was being planted, he says, the veil was torn from
above to below; the rocks were split, the earth was shaken, the tombs were
opened.
12 The double-doored hanging is split because, when there is no king and ruler
in his own house, the veil in every way makes known the authority. And after
the outrage against the most high God, God who dwelt in the temple and the
Holy Spirit went out of the temple, leaving the house desolate: Behold, your
house is left to you desolate. When the King of glory went out, the symbols of
the kingdom were torn; the tombs were burst; he had not yet gone down into
Hades, setting things in order, and the earth could not bear the Master's
presence; the rivers burst, not bearing the one who came down to them from
above; the tombs burst and gave forth men, and they entered into the holy
city, that they might leave the judgment without excuse.
13 These things happened at the Cross, and the Jews were mocking, saying: If
you are the Son of God, come down from the Cross and we will believe you.
Consider the voice of the children of the devil, how they imitate this
paternal voice. The devil says to the Savior on the pinnacle of the temple: If
you are the Son of God, cast yourself down. Likewise also the Jews: If you are
the Son of God, come down from the Cross and we will believe you.
14 And they crucify thieves with him as well, not because they hate thieves,
for they were partners of thieves, but by the fellowship of the thieves
insulting the Master, and so that those present may see that he is judged for
a charge of wickedness: With thieves, they say, let us crucify the one who has
done things like thieves. But Isaiah did not keep silent about the staging,
but having seen the inequality of the passion upon the just one and the
unjust, he said: For the sins of the people he came to death, and he was
numbered among the lawless, that is, with thieves. For the Jews did not hate
thieves, but they wished by the fellowship of the thieves to insult the
passion of Christ.
15 And that they did not hate thieves, Pilate says to them: Whom shall I
release to you, Jesus or Barabbas the thief, who, he says, had been cast into
prison for murder. And they say: Release to us Barabbas. The thieves asked for
the thief and handed the Lord over to the Cross, not prevailing by force but
handing him over by the economy. Pilate says to them: Shall I crucify your
king? And they say: We have no king but Caesar. They denied the kingdom of God
and were handed over to the kingdom of the Romans; since they were ignorant of
what they had, they received what they asked.
16 They brought also thorns upon the Cross, they brought also gall, and Moses,
having foreseen these things, cried out: Crooked and perverse generation, do
you repay these things to the Lord? To him who gave the manna in the
wilderness, to this one you offer gall? Well then does Isaiah also say: I
waited for it to produce a grape, but it produced thorns. To him who made the
good things bloom for you, to this one you offer a crown of thorns. Pilate
spares, and the Jews: His blood be upon us and upon our children.
17 Let us also say the same voice, not from the same mind: The blood of Christ
be upon us and upon our children, for them unto condemnation, for us unto
righteousness. For the blood of Christ is not worse than the Babylonian
furnace; the furnace of Babylon honored the saints and consumed the Chaldeans.
The precious blood knows how to destroy the unbelieving, and to save those who
believe. 18 Light befits the Cross. For this alone dissolved the darkness of
the demons. If there were no Cross, we would be worshiping demons. The Cross
brought down error, the Cross made holiness bloom, the Cross brought a ray of
righteousness. All things are ours from the Cross, and we are Christ's, to
whom be the glory and the power together with the Father and the all-holy
Spirit, now and always and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
