* On the Resurrection of the Lord B
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

A discourse of our father among the saints, John Chrysostom, archbishop of
Constantinople, on the holy Pascha, and on the myrrh-bearers, and on the
newly illuminated, and on In the beginning was the Word.
The Church of Christ among us may always rejoice, but much more in this
day's festival of countless lamps, when Christ himself, the Master of all,
the sun of righteousness, having risen by the resurrection from the bridal
chambers of the tomb, first of all cast his rays upon the race of women,
saying to them: Rejoice. The first voice of the resurrection is Rejoice;
fear was buried and grace was given.
The first voice of the resurrection is Rejoice; and before all he met with
women, and the Lord said to them: Go quickly and tell my disciples that I
have risen from the dead, and I go before you into Galilee; there you will
see me.
He met with women, for where sin abounded, grace overflowed still more. O
the wise dealing of the Lord. He did not send an angel to the disciples,
nor did he himself come, but he sent women; and not simply women, but
faithful women, his own soldiers. These he sent to the disciples, at once
gladdening and reproaching: gladdening, because he who is free among the
dead has risen from the dead; reproaching, because the disciples, being
men, were hiding in cowardice, while women, made bright with boldness,
were announcing the resurrection as good news. Truly then it is timely to
say also of the present Church, because of her many births of those
regenerated by the Spirit: Behold, I and the children whom God has given
me. I call the newly illuminated children because of the harmlessness now
of their disposition, children as being nursed on the milk of the Lord.
Not only children, but clouds and doves also is the rank of the newly
illuminated called. And who is witness of this? Isaiah the prophet. Hear
him saying: Who are these that fly as clouds, and as doves with their
young? And why are the newly illuminated clouds and doves? Clouds, because
they have been parted from the earth, because they range the air, because
they seek the things of heaven, because they bear the intelligible rain
that came down upon the intelligible fleece. Doves, as driving off the
devilish raven; doves, as carrying the sprig of piety in the mouth. So
also another prophet has cried saying: Rejoice, barren one who does not
bear; break forth and cry, you who do not travail, because the children of
the desolate are more than of her who has the husband. The Church from the
nations was called desolate; before her union with the Master Christ she
was desolate, as not having the palm of righteousness, as not possessing
the climbing vine of the cross. But the desolate one has become fruitful,
not advancing by nature, but bearing fruit by God, so as to be able to
say: But I am as a fruitful olive tree in the house of God. We celebrate a
universal feast today, at once fulfilling the law and confirming grace,
because the law was given through Moses, grace and truth came through
Jesus Christ.
Where there is a slave, there is "was given"; where there is a free man,
there is "came to be", because the slave ministers, but the Master acts
with authority. And again: Christ is the fulfillment of the law unto
righteousness for everyone who believes. The law has been fulfilled, grace
has been brought in instead; the Jewish people has grown old, the
Christian people has been made new. Therefore concerning the former it has
been said: That which is becoming old and aging is near to vanishing; but
concerning this:



Your youth shall be renewed like an eagle's. There the shadow, here the
truth; there the unleavened bread, here the bread; there circumcision,
here the seal; there the ark, here the virgin; there the tablets, here the
gospels; there the jar, here the altar; there the rod of Aaron that
blossomed with almonds, here the staff of the cross that bore a robber
into paradise as fruit; there the desert, here paradise; there the land of
promise, here the inheritance of the kingdom of the heavens. Therefore the
present things also imitate the order of heaven by the star-radiance of
the newly illuminated; and stars above and stars below. Earth is heaven
for us today, not because the place has been changed, but because the
former blessing has been handed down. No longer does the dividing wall of
enmity hold, but the pledge of friendship is welcomed with the right hand;
no longer do we hear: You are earth and to earth you shall go, but Our
citizenship is in the heavens.
Truly then it is timely to say, singing in refrain just now: This is the
day which the Lord has made; let us rejoice and be glad in it. Let us
rejoice because the images of the idols have been shattered and the holy
of holies has been given; let us be glad because the grief of Eve has been
quenched and the joy of the virgin has been kindled. This is the day which
the Lord has made. What did he make it? Queen of the other days. Therefore
David also has cried concerning her to the king Christ, saying: The queen
stood at your right hand in a garment shot with gold, clothed and
embroidered. And what is a gold-shot garment? The clothing of faith, for
faith is gold because of the uncounterfeited stamp of baptism. The queen
stood at your right hand, clothed, embroidered. Clothed in what? The
unaging summer-garment of piety. Embroidered with what? The many-tongued
comeliness of the doctrines. This is the day which the Lord has made; let
us rejoice and be glad in it: not with wine that makes drunk, but with a
word that leads to compunction. Being rational, let us receive a word of
gladness. And what is the gladdening word?
The Word of God himself, who gladdens us but scourges heretics.
You heard just now John crying out: In the beginning was the Word, and the
Word was with God, and the Word was God. What will the children of
heretics say, who are without the Word in their religion? In the beginning
was the Word: nowhere a creature, nowhere a work, nowhere under another's
power, nowhere feeble, nowhere the "was not". John has the "was" and Arius
the "was not". Whom am I to believe? John, who reclined upon the breast of
the Lord, or Arius, who suddenly expired in dung-filled places because of
his blasphemy against the Son of God. In the beginning was the Word: he
did not say "came to be", lest he introduce a coming-to-be of the Godhead;
for the existence of the Godhead is not a work of generation, having
neither beginning of days nor end of life, but remaining the same and in
the same state; for the divine nature does not admit change. What then
does David
say? I long to say again with you: This is the day which the Lord has
made; let us rejoice and be glad in it. Truly the present day is full of
gladness. Why full of gladness? Because on this day he blocked Hades and
opened heaven, he spoiled Hades and made life bloom again, he bound the
devil and released Adam, he moved aside the flaming sword and by the
life-giving wood brought the soul of the believing robber into paradise,
he drove out the rams and the gods and prepared the rational lamb to be
divided. And who is witness of this? Paul crying out: Christ our Pascha
has been sacrificed for us. So that we keep the feast not with old leaven,
nor with leaven of malice and wickedness, but with unleavened bread of
sincerity and truth. We have been commanded to keep festival of the
passion that tastes of Christ, and not in the unleavened bread of Jewish
wickedness.
Since the Jews pursue other things, and we other things: they wickedness,
we the blessing. Other things they did concerning the Master Christ: they
slaughtered, we bought as our provision; they pierced the side, we the
mystical milk



drew, they the vinegar we the blood, they the gall we the sweetness, they
the tomb we heaven, they the stone we the throne, they the seal we the
treasure, they the military custody we the angelic presidency, they
Pilate we Paul, they Barabbas we Abraham. This is the day which the Lord
has made; let us rejoice and be glad in it, because the rational lambs,
standing at the Master's manger, are pastured on the body of the Lord. Men
have become angels, slaves free, soldiers kings; those who go about in
the sheepskin of Adam have been clothed with the royal cloak. And may it
be that we all be found worthy of the resurrection of Christ both in this
age and in the age to come, now and always and unto the ages of ages.
Amen.
