To Heraclius the emperor and to the Persian wars and when he was setting out
from the City 1 O Trinity that directs the immaterial generalships of those
above with light-bearing word toward both a fiery and a fixed station, for by
word setting their natures on fire you know how to show immaterial matter,
filling the heavens and burning the aether and binding the inhabited world,
and being present everywhere not being moved, and giving room nowhere and
being contained: wherever a prayer flies up from the depth, give to the dim
instruments of the reasonings a trumpet's sound and a speaking shield. Teach
us to move a well-aimed sword, a tongue against enemies, a sharpened weapon;
guide us where it is possible to write the wonders of your authority. For the
emperor, trusting in your command, set himself against the contests of the
lawless barbarians, for whom it is law to worship the creatures above you who
created; for whom it is genuine to cling to the bastards, and wholly bastard
to attack the genuine; among whom some god is mindlessly reckoned, an armed
horse worshipped in vain, who for the refutation of the deceiving cult is now
worshipped and again whipped. O wretched collapse of heart: how to the one
impiously honored by them do they allot honor and punishment together? And of
the one he had no share when worshipped, but at the other he suffers, being
whipped densely. And water and fire, the contrary natures, they introduce as
gods that destroy one another, worshipping a dissolution of contrary things.
But, O general of the wise counsels, receive my words with goodwill; for they
are free from flatteries, having learned from you not to speak hypocrisies.
Appear then as a true judge of true things of this generalship; become a judge
more than Celtic Rhine for I think you will convict nothing as bastard, become
a judge; but grant pardon, for the losses you undergo through us; for the
tones of the reasonings in us are too weak to relate the various judgments and
divisions of your toils, and the soul's daring through all things, which you
fitted well to the contests, widening the mind in a straitening of affairs. If
they say not unreasonably that prosperities raised to the peak are fallen, let
the rest of Persia's things lie in evils, and conversely of our affairs may
the hardships be turned into prosperity; for the flowing pour of life always
brings reversals and changes, so that the firm thing, saved everywhere, may be
kept for the God of creatures alone. The swift words run through all, painting
the natures by fineness; but when they attempt the course toward you, they run
with longing and delay with fear; and often having painted you they write
again, not writing accurately. Homer, whom they call a spring of words for he
widens the speaking veins, and gushing he waters the reasonings of a youthful
mind and by giving drink he rears them, and remains full though often emptied,
divides <at any rate> the sister and foster virtues in double labors of words;
yet from necessity; for he did not touch a time that showed both courage and
prudence and a common dwelling of those with them. But if he had had your
image and had found the perfect nature as was needful, leaving the many fables
of words, adding the soul's form which you have, he would have shown with all
the virtues joined one four-formed image through you. For if when Nestor
speaks sweetly he likens the mouth to the works of bees, how did he not come
to the peak of wonder seeing in you an immaterial music of wits in the
sweetness? for you do not have the


poison; you pick the useful of every flower for every season, not for spring
alone. You have the sting, as a bee, the laws, but you do not kill, nor strike
into the depth. For if somewhere you find a member worthy of a blow, you hold
out the laws, as a sting, toward fear, but you spare wholly; and the sting
often leaps forward to strike and again is drawn in, and the active thing,
held by compassion, remained idle; for even if it has a sharp impulse, it is
piously blunted through you, and thereafter, as from a philanthropic passion,
the sting drips honey instead of bitterness. But the delight of your good
things led me away in a digression from the matter at hand; whence farming
again toward the very furrows of the word I bring in the seeds. The black
night of the adversaries had been spread over all the inhabited world; for
there was no satiety of Persian greed, but they stretched their appetites
toward murders. Yet toward so great and lightless evening you yourself were in
no way turned toward sleep; for the density of the cares on our account
reasonably turned sleep away from you. And many, painting quietly by
themselves counsels and laws of generalship, were saying that the emperor's
power must stand by in the necessities of battle; but others, as from the
contrary, were counter-generalizing, fighting hard in words, saying it was
unsafe for the power to throw itself into ready circumstances; and some,
forming in their own hearts one mixture from both, were speaking
sophistically, so as both to remain and to run with the contests by the cares.
But the common things of the words were without blame; for the judgments did
not have a wicked manner. But you, general for you were of the whole and these
things yourself more accurately, having given all authority to speak, you made
God judge of the unclear things. And where virgin faith goes forth, from there
she is clothed in white hope. And having celebrated the greatest day on which
the common of the race rose up into a God-inspired and new refashioning,
immediately after it, imaging Moses, you out-general <the> second Pharaoh, if
one would not err in calling second him who is truly first into sin. And
having taken that divine and venerable form of the unwritten writing, which
hands did not write but in an image the Word who forms and fashions all things
himself bears, as he knows, a forming without writing, as a conception without
seed; for it was needful that he, as then without seed, so again be typed
without writing, so that through both, the Word being formed, the faithful
thing of the incarnation might remain, convicting the error of the
Phantasiasts, trusting in this God-written type, you made a divine
first-fruits of the contests. For it was needful that your advocate Word be
wholly present as the trial was being moved. And on the second day of the
feast, throwing yourself immediately into the ships, you were cutting the wet
passage vigorously, and indeed you were sailing past at once the places of
Heraia; for so they named it from the error of the one formerly ruling, having
received a succession, until you turned, piously recasting, the inglorious of
the error into good glory. And I tremble, having remembered these places, to
pass in silence the piety which you worked in them recently; for perhaps even
for those after us the silence will be a loss. But let no one, as I wish again
to run in a digression from the aim the life-benefiting course, hastily hold
the horseman of the word with voiceless reins as a side-work. There was, when
the south wind blew to the contrary, the streams running back intensely, and a
lightless night and boiling waves were working a double necessity of the
squall. But you, most mighty one, were cutting the sleepless evening as was
your custom, if indeed it is fitting to call evening the day through you. And
a voice of sharp lamentations from afar thundered down upon your pious wits;
for a ship, unluckily thrust from the waves, leaped against a rocky freeze,
and a great roaring shout of streams, raising the whole sound to the rocks,
was savagely spitting the foam outside, and the waves, dashed against the
stones, as from violent counter-blows, were sending forth sparks of wet
substance.


And to so great a violence the sailors came as to be nothing more than those
being made dead; for the manner of the necessity was shaping them as dead,
buried in a short time. But immediately hope, and arriving beyond hope, you
shone for them on the sea as a light-bearer, showing forth the light sweeter
than the sun; for the one knows how to burn up bodies, but you piously bedew
hearts. And running out toward the ship itself, laboring you were drawing all
to eagerness, they blushing at a master toiling in so great a self-working of
good things. And the multitude of the swift-running ships too was immediately
running together vigorously into one, and every one then enrolled in arms,
throwing down the bow and letting go the shield, was arming himself toward the
wet grapples. And those who had cut by a word of goodwill the seeds of the
child-producing organs it was possible to see then running together to the
same, in no way cut as to the male nature. Thus each was sweetly hastening to
take part in your sweats; yet all were bringing the grace to you; and all that
was done was being eagerly worked for you, so that the wage of all might run
together to the cause and the starting-point of the deed. For some were
thrusting the hull rooted as from necessity from sea-stones, and the sailors,
taking it with the tensions of the tow-ropes, were dragging the ship as they
could, until having prevailed they scarcely dragged the ship that had fallen
in a net of stones. O compassionate mind and tone of soul and one care that
sufficed for the whole; again you are anxious and again you join battle
against a squall; for neither danger of a sea's onset, nor the squall of the
land, gave a check to your eagerness; but as some wise man in steering of a
squall, seeing the airs of a storm grown savage, then shows the good craft
still more, and having anticipated the triple-waves with reasoning he cuts the
stream of the squall running back up and densely empties the violence of the
wind, so having mastered the necks of life, there being a storm and a
confusion <of the> affairs, anticipating each triple-wave of disasters, here
you cut through, there you empty the violence, and there running piously up to
a height you quenched the surge of land and sea. And Envy, seeing these things
and groaning for he knew he had suffered a great loss seeing so many saved in
a short time, strikes the tip of your foot's toe, having shaped himself, as it
seems, into a stone; and a hot pour of blood was shooting up, dyeing the earth
and calling it as witness; for it was needful that the stigmata remain for you
unto an undiminished grace of piety. But as you snatched that hull of
necessity even from so many waves, so may the Word of God who shelters you
everywhere save through you the whole worldly ship from squall unto the end. 2
Demosthenes, come forward with frankness; words prevail; do not be troubled
now with fear; Philip is not present, but the master; there is no danger, even
if you should suffer silence, all in common and well having been defeated. And
as the words are urgent to run back, I fly again toward the courses from the
beginning. For you gave no check to the ships until, having gone through the
road of the streams, you stood at the so-called Gates, having come as an
unexpected day-runner. From here, as a gush of a spring passage, being moved
from veins in the depth, is joined together and collects the streams, but is
variously divided in the pastures, so having opened the veins of reasoning by
your various and wise division you divide yourself to the pastures of affairs.
For who is general? <the> emperor before the whole. Who will teach thoroughly
the laws of taxiarchy? All were looking well toward you. Who is a secure
counselor of the needful things? Again the inclination of the eyes toward you.
How well monarchy ruling with God; for it was not many-faced anarchy, but
monarchy ruling with God, which warms all and directs by word and overturns
the unordered turnings-aside; for nothing so as the disease of disorder
creeping bites and consumes the substance and lays hold of the vital things in
it. But if I shall wish to relate the various things of the words and the
differences of the cares, as many as you were eagerly undergoing instead of
all, having missed a double


hope I shall be judged as neither describing these things in the word nor
going forward and saying the vital things. You worked the roads as
generalship, and your paths as tactical good craft; for you even table and
food and drinks had been prepared with bows and with shields. And every
practical word of yours was bare, both rhythm and canon of all good counsel.
For having found an army formerly from slackness [...] full of disorder,
neglected, you set them right more quickly both by words and by figures,
typing, dividing, showing, undersigning as a pedagogue of the armed letters. O
mind sufficient, imitating Ocean, both circling the earth and running round
the whole, and watering all and remaining filled. As many other triple-waves
of cares upon others as you went after in a short time, doing, caring, making
ready, writing beforehand, so that you might fit together the multitude of the
army scattered into many parts of the earth for there was no slight fear, lest
the barbarian, not having anticipated, might split the divided ranks of your
army, passing through in the midst and how often the dense counsels, not
having run together with the hopes of the aim, you were undergirding with more
accurate judgments, I will willingly pass by; for you yourself, master, doing
so many things you contrived to escape notice, so that the common might not
share the toil. Yet they came together, as from some mountain having many
projections and positions springs flowing by the much-split passage run
together to the same from the splits. And a wonder fittingly was coming upon
me how the pour of so many reasonings was being soberly ordered by your
reasoning; yet, as it seems, an active Word of God, having stood over these,
not only bent the necks of all, but hearts together. And when all, having run
to your tracks, as from one symphony, hymned your God-supported power, and
bent to earth the necks luxuriant with trophies as aerial flames, which,
having been put together in a well-running variety, are heralds of tactical
movements, seen from afar near by all, and all were intensely proclaiming a
common benefactor and ruling master, you yourself, taking the dread copy of
the God-written type, said shortly: "The relation and the manner of the empire
have fitted you to me as brothers; for we decree authority not so much in fear
as to shine forth in longing; for it is law for us now to bring in against the
inhuman violences, which tyranny armed against the laws, the philanthropic
violence, and to set against so many the one, which always by blameless
necessities constrains the well-set laws. I thus both in manner and in figure;
but this one is common both emperor and master and leader of the armies among
us, with whom it is safer to be general, through whom it is more pious to
conquer; trusting in whom and having arrived even now I arm myself as one of
you toward toils. For it is fitting that we, as his fashionings, go against
enemies who worship creatures, who mixed the tables the unmixed with gore of
bloods by murderous bloods; who stain the unreceiving assemblies of the
passions with most passionate pleasures; who wish to dig out with barbarian
sword the vine planted by the Word; on whose account David spoke in a
God-inspired way, saying blessed is he who dashed the children of Persia, made
level, against rocks." Thus you yourself were recasting all from slackness
into eagerness by word; thus putting God forward everywhere as general and
master you make the power secure. And indeed immediately, anticipating the
postponement, you begin the new exercises of the army. And a certain longing
was coming upon me here to see the delightful forecourts of the battle and to
relate the fear without fear; yet the manner of the contests, bringing in even
the dread seeming, was approaching. For there was an armed rank made accurate,
where trumpets and phalanxes of shields, spears, quivers and arrows and
swords, and a terrible clash of breastplates had been prepared, woven from the
iron fabrics, in which the lamp of the sun, gleaming, by the interchanges as
by counter-shinings was sending forth a lightning-bearing glitter. And when,
drawn up as adversaries, they tightened their


ranks securely, the walls of the armed buildings were seen, and as the whole
armies crashed together shield was thrusting sword and swords the shields
everywhere with violent collisions, and the fashion of the battle was showing
the swords full of many bloods, and all things dreadful, both fear and
confusion, and a nodding toward murders without bloods. For you knew how to
show them soberly before the necessity the <manners> of the necessity, so that
each, taking starting-points of the dangerless slaughter, might remain more
secure. To say each thing then and to write that various novelty of the art,
the mind of my undaring heart grows weak, being shaken by a fear still ringing
in the ear. But as into an ebb from some violence the stream being intensely
thrust forward, so those waves of the army, being moved together with your
running word, were going after either toward your face or the contrary with
face-to-face speed. And you did not shrink from so great a clash, but you were
drawing it and drawing the tones back, you were ordering, counter-ordering,
where by the word the nod of your inclination was turned together. How did you
alone fit yourself to so great a multitude? And how so many races and
differences of many-mannered characters and standings-apart of wits, as your
word struck with well-rhythmed tone, you were drawing not as another mythical
one the beasts toward himself, but toward one judgment, recasting confusions
of so many tongues? Yet it is not unreasonable that the all-holy Spirit was at
work even now, not by a fire-bearing division and contemplation of tongues,
but the Spirit itself again dripping the fitting grace by your tongue; thus
having taught more quickly all the formerly neglected you had them made ready.
And I, not even being a prophet, master, having anticipated I announced
beforehand in the first word that you neglected nothing, but even writing the
advantageous to your generals everywhere you erred in nothing, if indeed in
the needful the thing that stood by you immediately ran together. Your
practical good counsel of wits showed us in no way deceived. From here fears
do not trouble us, cares do not make the eagerness slack, disasters do not
bite, toil does not press; for a pious love, running round us, into the
necessities and circumstances still more constitutes the tones of the little
flesh with sinews. As then a sophist, having joined experience to the medical
words, did not let the disease smolder secretly into a depth of harm, but
immediately cut it out, cutting with the sword, or burned it cautery-wise or
by a purgative, cleansing out its inner causes, frees the body from a
difficult passion, so the body of the army then, having found it harmed from a
harm-loving disease, you held them from the evil greed, cleansing by word the
appetites of the harm. For you knew it easiest to master the adversaries
having rhythmed your army in piety. From here immediately a winged victory
came to you of Persian spoils, bringing victory-prizes before the battle. For
since you had nothing neglected, but you were sending out well-composed
sorties, horsemen swift-running everywhere toward a hunt; those were present
who in a short time were bringing not contemptible kinds of beasts, but ones
standing out and prevailing by daring and often having harmed the sojourn. But
not even these, though being beasts, did you let not take part in your tame
thing. Yet leaving the many now I chose to describe their multitude from one.
There was present some clan-chief of a daring race, leading with him the rank
of the many-haired Saracens and looking round how, escaping notice, he might
come upon your army toward harm. Yet as an ally to a greedy generalship,
wishing to steal, he himself was being stolen in turn, and to your all-day
feet they bring with speed the former general as a prisoner. And when he was
brought to your power, master, that one who had taken the much-groaned
captivity into a free pasture, the one who had unluckily been lucky, if one
must say for having come to you he exchanges fortune, he throws off the bonds,
and having recast he immediately turned all the disasters into joy. And seeing
the exchange more quickly as great he judged the salvation a reversal, the
prisoner hoping to be general again.


Thus your mind is armed variously and often campaigns with the reasonings, and
using sparing rather than a sword it draws even the faithless barbarians. And
these things were being done for you soberly; but for the general of the error
the contraries, who had as work an unseemly sound of instruments and cymbals
and a strange dance of women stirred toward nakedness. But you, general of the
wise panoply, were delighting in psalms from mystic instruments, putting the
divine sound into the heart, and you had seemly leapings of virgins, the
unspoken hopes of your reasonings. That one had the moon as hope, but as your
sun struck more quickly he himself was undergoing an eclipse sharply. That one
had the fire worshipped, but you, most mighty one, the wood being raised. And
of this, it is clear, as it was raised toward a height, the Persian fire was
being kindled in vain. For when the barbarian, having spent time shortly into
winter toward the Pontic clime, anticipated and held the inroads of the road,
and your army, once prepossessed, had the difficult inroads toward the sun,
here you found a most short reversal and a figure of praiseworthy fashioning,
O most mighty one, showing the barbarians a face of a false sortie, but
whetting the mind so as also the cause [...] <! ! ! ! !> your, how having been
turned with good devices you might snatch away the roads from those. And the
impious one more quickly deceived, having set out as toward your face by
crooked and turned-aside paths, you reverse with a two-faced figure and the
second was immediately found first; thus thinking to run forth luckily he,
tripped from you, turns back unluckily. Thus being general you alone
accomplish both a wise fashioning and a wise hypocrisy; and this variety of
the aim still more sent the barbarians into slackness. For no one thus passed
a beast raging savagely by a reverse course, nor does another, driving horses,
showing the reins aslant, pass by uprightly, as you, having mastered the reins
of the army, running under passed by from the reverse, and having thrown the
transgressor by a strange fiction you let him go before the battle into a
standing-against. And as the sixth day was now running out, he had the
messages of his own disasters from unexpected hearings. And a great care of
the shaken wits was holding him, and confusions of reasonings beclouded the
darkened mind. For it is no small thing but a vital one if even some small
thing the army turns aside, many hardships being moved between, reasonably for
the foreign-tribe armies. For from hunger and a many-mannered destruction and
the unclear snares toward harm and the best prepossessions toward battle there
was from here a danger; but still more it was biting them to see the sun, whom
they worship as god of Persia, contrary in the array of the battle. They say
then that Xerxes in a rabid manner, wishing to mix the natures that stand
apart, made water into stone and land into sea. And this one seems to me some
most simple-minded man, who thinking to turn the substances by unordered
changes was working confusions, who having been raised to frighten the
Laconians, as a wonder-worker of contrarieties, makes the streams dry land,
and makes the stones flow, and stirring all and turning and reversing was
begetting not astonishment but infatuation; who was hastening to re-set the
bases of the set and fixed bounds; who came to so great a turning-aside that
he whipped the water revered by him and dear and ancestral, not having from
where he might quench his kindling madness. And these things, what violence
thrusting him? So that water might be cut by a dry-land course and earth be
crossed by the sea passage, so that more quickly then, having gone through an
easier road, he might anticipate the adversaries; and a small toil and an easy
deed he exchanged for so many toils in vain. But you, most mighty one, not
having troubled the natures, nor again having shaken the fixed bounds, going
without toil and walking quietly you passed your adversaries easily. And no
longer was there for the barbarian an easy passage in the cares of


necessity, but from resourcelessness he then turned back the bulk of the
lifting-up into humiliation. For here a judgment then stirred was holding him,
and was persuading him to run back behind your power into a counter-flow, but
he was wary of the contraries of the aim, seeing the outcomes unclear
everywhere; and there from necessity he set out to run up the Cilician inroads
and the position of the road hard to meet and narrowed by cliffs and ravines;
but even here a reversal was holding the judgment again. For the way out of
Armenia was biting him, lest you, having invaded from there, might anticipate
and throw all Persia into confusion utterly. And raging much with the bastard
counsels he had a reasoning nowhere standing firm, but as an easily-rolled
unstable stone is urgent toward a downward depth, and having struck against a
counter-prop was overturned, so the many rollings of this stone, the counsels
having slipped, being thrust by the unstable carryings-out, were being
overturned by the contrary judgments. Yet he was brought under and after the
manner of a dog bound with cords was being dragged from necessity. And
laboring at random and toiling in vain he was driving double courses into one
road. But as, having struck against your tactical good crafts ready and
secured beforehand, he knew the battle sinewless for himself, he is anxious
for theft and was preparing to take the fair night as fellow-worker of the
aim; for he was now making ready toward attacks, seeking a lightless time for
the stealings. But since, seeing the light-bringer that after day makes the
night all-shining and all-bright, he had the lamp as conviction of the theft,
and he now had the moon as contrary, the one formerly worshipped being
impiously used; and somewhere, bearing it hard by himself, the barbarian mind
prayed that the goddess of Persia not run toward increase but rather cease. 3
The goddess of Persia had an eclipse, she had an eclipse both in word and in
deed. And I think, she rejoices for the sake of Persian harm, waning and
ceasing and being diminished; for she always wishes rather to eclipse for them
than to shine forth being impiously honored. But a lamp of the sun of the
master, warming us, even now kindles the words, and a pious Phoebus comes
forth for us, cleansing all from a purgative word; and he abhors the bounds of
the Crooked one, fleeing the crooked riddles of the error. And already, many
things being moved in the midst, there were now three five-numbered days in
which, running out unceasingly into contests, you were assembling the army
toward the arrays. But the barbarian both by race and by the manner did not
have the reins of the mind wholly; for often he promised the battle and came
to so great a shame, that having struck against you daily he was overturned,
daring undaring things and running backward. He was occupying mountains then
as the gazelles, and he was dwelling rocks as the hares from fear. And it was
not an aimless thing of wickedness, and even doing this he was immediately
being caught; for he was running down into hard-to-pass places, so that he
might take a starting-point toward postponement of your battle of the ready
armies. But you yourself were bringing your army made ready toward a land well
spread out, and somewhere the barbarian, having looked from a mountain at the
well-crafted and wise generalship, and astonished at so great an order, fixed
his ranks with fear and remained being made stone together with the stones.
These things are not colored toward favor; but wholly simple are the words of
the truth, which if we should be silent, perhaps the rocks will break out
voices of the things done. And since the cowardice of the barbarian was
furnishing from habit a great postponement for being cowardly and undaring, he
appeared brave only in the seeming of the battle; <but> he had the arrays
unmoved; for they had been related only as painters paint the images, and here
you dare more than the judgment of Alexander, most mighty one, but without
danger. Not as not wishing to run into danger but not wishing to fall by want
of counsel; for the general is secure by good counsels, but is not more secure
by darings. And indeed then between the armies you pitched a tent, giving easy
starting-points toward the battle; and the dinner in the midst was ready for
you by figure and by it alone; for your mind


had not a desire of foods but had the greed toward saving. Thus you were
despising those securely, furnishing both so many and strange materials, so
that through them you might kindle the battle; thus stirring the evildoer, as
a dog, you were hastening to snatch him into war. But you were not persuading
this one to run out at all, neither daring forth nor playing the battle; for
by the neglected seeming your contempt, as I think, was striking the
barbarians still more into agony. From here no one was easily going after or
was leaping out of his own ranks; for all were as if set up as stelae; and the
strange spectacle of your tent was being built against as a fence for the
barbarians, and was being set as a wall for your household. For the justice
that runs everywhere did not let one day for the foreign tribes, but
outstanding tents of barbarians were falling utterly. For a grapple was often
coming to be from approaching in part; yet each of the champions then you
alone were preparing into eagerness, always leaping forth well-strong in the
battles and drawing a bow and holding out a shield and doing all things
instead of all before the whole, through which each was immediately being
stirred to find danger rather than, the master toiling thus, not to share the
toil. And every one being undaring, looking toward you, was recasting the mind
into good daring; for he knows how to bring a work into good action, when a
master is armed before all. And somewhere someone said, speaking to his
fellow-fighter: "Alas for the necessity; the emperor and master is armed
toward battle as one of us and now an iron tunic instead of purple binds the
back and weights the neck, and much dust, mixed with the hair, covers a form
of so great a delight; and he endures this burning sun, wetted everywhere with
hot sweat which a violent toil of the members being bound from necessity
brings forth from the body." As he spoke, he groaned a great groan from the
depth and the tears were flowing down together with the word. And the other
immediately spoke back to him: "But it does not strike my wit so much to see
the master now suffering hard, as this makes me wonder, that he is sweetly
urgent toward the necessities, and reckons the toils to be a delight. For
holding a spear he is made more beautiful than holding the scepters of the
authority; and he bears this shield easily more most sweetly, as I see, than
the crown; and having formerly quenched the murders on our account he is armed
again toward murders on our account. And now stretching a black-sandaled foot
he is more seemly in the unseemly things and in the poor things he remains
