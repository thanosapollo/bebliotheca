OF OUR FATHER AMONG THE SAINTS BASIL, Archbishop of Caesarea of Cappadocia,
HOMILY ON THE HOLY SPIRIT.
The one baptized into the Trinity is baptized into Father and Son and Holy
Spirit; neither into principalities, nor into powers, nor into the rest among
creatures. Let it therefore be clear to him that the Trinity is above
creation; and let him not attempt to bring any of the Trinity down into
creation. Let him also remember the Lord, who breathed the Holy Spirit, and
showed through the breathing that the Spirit is of the divine essence, and not
of created nature. Let him also consider the apostolic exposition concerning
the Trinity, in which we see one operation of Father and Son and Holy Spirit;
as neither the Son nor the Spirit is created and made as to nature. Because
God does not need his own creation in order to render his own operation. And
the Apostle says: "There are diversities of gifts, and the same Spirit. And
there are diversities of ministries, and the same Lord. And there are
diversities of operations, and the same God, who works all things in all."
Therefore the things that God works, these the Spirit works. "All things," he
says, "the one and the same Spirit works, distributing to each as he wills."
Nor let him bring the Spirit down into slavery, hearing that as he wills he
works all the things of God. And again: "Now the Lord is the Spirit. And where
the Spirit of the Lord is, there is freedom." For if he does not say that
deity dwells in us through the Spirit that dwells, when John says, "By this we
know that he is in us, from the Spirit which he has given us," he will lose
all hope. For if God is not in us, we shall obtain neither life nor eternal
goods. And Paul says again: "Do you not know that you are a temple of God, and
the Spirit of God dwells in you?" and again: "Your =31.1432= bodies are a
temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you, whom you have from God." And creation
needs revelation in order to know the things of God; but the Spirit reveals.
"To us," he says, "God has revealed through his Spirit."

And with respect to us he searches, because in him we make the search: "The
Spirit," he says, "searches even the depths of God;" but in himself he knows,
just as our spirit does. "For who among men knows the things of the man except
the spirit that is in him? So also no one has known the things of God except
the Spirit of God." And God gives life; for Paul says: "I charge you before
God who gives life to all things;" and Christ gives life, who says: "My sheep
hear my voice, and I give them eternal life." And we are made alive through
the Spirit, as Paul says: "He who raised Christ from the dead will also give
life to your mortal bodies through his Spirit who dwells in you." One is
therefore given life by God through Christ in the Spirit. And for this reason,
since every operation of the Son is manifested through the Spirit, the Spirit
is named Lord with the Lord's name; and Christ with the name of Christ, as we
were just saying: "Now the Lord is the Spirit." And it has also been said:
"You are not in the flesh, but in the Spirit, if indeed the Spirit of God is
in you. But if anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ, he is not his." Then
he adds: "But if Christ is in you," instead of saying what would follow: But
if the Spirit is in you. Nor is there any divine voice teaching that the
Spirit is made. For "All things came to be through him" concerns the multitude
of creatures. But the Spirit is not in the multitude, but with the pair it
completes the Trinity, and is alienated from creation, and far exceeds it,
inasmuch as it sanctifies creation and gives life, and is an anointing upon
us, and already even in the very flesh of the Lord.

Now our Lord Jesus Christ is surely not sanctified through a creature, as
Peter says: "Jesus of Nazareth, how God anointed him with the Holy Spirit and
with power." Nor does he have power through a creature; and he says: "By the
Spirit of God I cast out the demons." Nor is he led by a creature; for it is
written that "He was led in the Spirit into the wilderness." The dignity of
God the old prophets have cried: "Thus says the Lord;" but the new prophets:
"Thus says the Holy Spirit;" and the apostles: "It seemed good to the Holy
Spirit and to us." Therefore also the Apostle, showing that he utters divine
words, sometimes says: "Or do you seek a proof of Christ speaking in me?" and
sometimes, "I think I have the Spirit of God." And the old Scripture too is
full of the =31.1433= glorification of the Spirit. "By the word of the Lord,"
says the Psalmist, "the heavens were established, and by the Spirit of his
mouth all their power." And in the book of Job it is written: "And a divine
Spirit is that which made me." And just as it is not possible to suppose
another Word besides the Son, by whom the heavens came to be (for they would
no longer have come to be through the Lord, if they came to be through another
word); so neither is it possible to suppose another Spirit, the one that with
the Word establishes the powers of the heavens. And when we hear Spirit of the
mouth, we do not hear it in a human way, but as is fitting in the case of God,
just as also the Word. For neither is the Word a dissolving utterance, but as
the psalmist says: "Forever, O Lord, your Word remains in heaven."

Nor is the Spirit some dispersing breath, but standing and remaining. "For
where shall I go from your Spirit?" says David, it being, that is, and having
filled all things, and being established in divine dignity. For what he said
concerning the Spirit, this he also says concerning the face of God, and
concerning his right hand, saying next: "And from your face where shall I
flee? If I go up into heaven, you are there; if I go down into Hades, you are
present; if I take my wings at dawn, and dwell in the uttermost parts of the
sea; even there your hand will lead me, and your right hand will hold me." And
the voice of God says this concerning himself and the Spirit: "I am in you,
says the Lord, and my Spirit has stood in your midst." Therefore neither is
the Spirit alien to the glory of God, having appeared unspeakably from the
unspeakable mouth; nor is God himself without the Spirit, but it is the Spirit
of God, and with God, being sent from God, and supplied through the Son; as it
is said from the paternal person: "My Spirit, which is upon you;" and it is
said by the Lord that "I will send you a Paraclete;" whom indeed he also calls
"the Spirit of truth." One Father, then, and one Son, and one Spirit also must
be confessed according to the divine tradition, and neither two fathers, nor
two sons; therefore the Spirit is not Son, nor is he named so. For we do not
receive something from the Spirit as we receive the Spirit from the Son, but
we receive him himself as he comes to us and sanctifies: a communion of deity,
and a participation of sonship, and an earnest of the eternal inheritance, and
a firstfruits of the goods to come. Let no one busy himself beyond the sacred
names that have been spoken; nor force the appellation of Son upon the Spirit,
nor generation; but let him be content with the =31.1436= appellation and
likeness according to the Spirit. And since by the grace of the good God, by
the memory of the words of the only-begotten Son of the living God, and of his
holy evangelists and apostles and prophets, who have sufficiently made clear
to us the account concerning the baptism according to the Gospel of our Lord
Jesus Christ, we have been taught that the baptism in fire is convicting of
every wickedness, and receptive of the righteousness according to Christ,
producing hatred of wickedness, and desire of virtue; and through faith we
have been cleansed by the blood of Christ from every sin, and having been
baptized in water into the death of the Lord, we have laid down as it were a
written confession: that we have been put to death to sin and to the world,
and have been made alive to righteousness, and thus having been baptized in
the name of the Holy Spirit, we have been born from above; and having been
born and baptized in the name of the Son, we have put on Christ; and having
put on the new man, who was created according to God, we were baptized in the
name of the Father and were named children of God.

There is need then for us henceforth to be fed with the food of eternal life,
which again the same only-begotten Son of the living God delivered to us,
saying: "Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds
through the mouth of God." And how this comes to be, he taught in saying: "My
food is to do the will of the Father who sent me;" and again: "Amen, amen I
say to you." For the confirmation of what is brought forward and the full
assurance of those who hear, having set this forth a second time, he says that
"Unless you eat the flesh of the Son of man, and drink his blood, you do not
have life in yourselves. He who eats my flesh, and drinks my blood, has
eternal life, and I will rest him on the last day. For my flesh is true food;
and my blood is true drink. He who eats my flesh, and drinks my blood, remains
in me, and I in him." And a little later it is written: "Many therefore,
having heard the word of his disciples, said: This word is hard. Who can hear
it? And Jesus, knowing in himself that his disciples were murmuring about
this, said to them: Does this scandalize you? What then if you see the Son of
man going up where he was before? It is the Spirit that gives life; the flesh
profits nothing. My words are spirit and they are life. But there are some of
you who do not believe. For Jesus knew from the beginning who they were who
believed, and who it was who would betray him. And he said: For this reason I
have said to you that no one can come to me, unless it has been given him from
my Father." And toward the end: "Jesus then taking bread, and having given
thanks, broke it, and gave it =31.1437= to the disciples, and said: Take, eat;
this is my body which is broken for you; do this in remembrance of me. And
taking a cup, having given thanks, he gave it to them saying: Drink from it,
all of you; for this is my blood of the New Covenant, which is poured out for
many for the remission of sins." And the Apostle: "For as often as you eat
this bread, and drink the cup, you proclaim the death of the Lord."

What then are these words, and what is the benefit from them? That, eating and
drinking, we may always remember the one who died for us and was raised, and
thus we may be taught of necessity to keep that apostolic command given to us,
which says: "For the love of Christ constrains us, having judged this, that if
one died for all, then all died; and he died for all, that those who live may
no longer live for themselves, but for him who died and was raised for them."
For the one who eats and drinks, clearly, unto an indelible memory of the
Master's and Maker's obedience even unto death, and of him who for my sake
became what the formed thing was. But we have not been commanded simply to
eat, but we are also exhorted to discern such a body of the Lord; clearly
having put away defilement of flesh and of spirit, and having prepared a
vessel of the spiritual ointment, thus to approach the pure sacrifice, in
Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory and the power now and unto the
ages of ages. Amen.
