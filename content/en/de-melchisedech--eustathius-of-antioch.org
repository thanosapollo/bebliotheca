* On Melchizedek
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Paul expressed Melchizedek as without mother somewhere and without father.
That, then, the greatest of priests, Melchizedek, did not spring up from
earth, no one would be able to contradict; for he was not born from oak nor
from rock, nor, having been taken from earth, is he worked in clay being
formed according to Adam and having both hands fashioned. And after a little
space: What then would one say? For Melchizedek is related to us as without
mother. And yet if he should happen to be like God and the Word and the whole
of him seems to us like him, bearing his image and preserving his character,
he is not without mother, not without father; for Christ has both a mother,
Mary, and the royal lineage of David, and from the root of Jesse the flower
of the whole fragrance springs up for us, and his lineage according to the
flesh is genealogized, but that of Melchizedek is seen to be without
genealogy. Therefore one would say that Melchizedek is without genealogy
because he is not from the seed of Abraham nor was he ever genealogized by
Moses, but is a Canaanite by race and springs from that accursed seed, having
been shown just among those of his own time and not springing from just
ancestors nor from any just seed. It was not lawful, then, to genealogize and
to trace back into the race of Canaan him who inclined to the height of
righteousness, nor was it most fitting to intertwine him who had laid hold of
the height of righteousness with the race that had laid hold of the height of
injustice. Wherefore Scripture says that he is without father and without
mother, considering the ancestors not worthy of the virtue of the just and
temperate man.
That Melchizedek is shown to be a Canaanite by race, it is possible, I
suppose, to infer also from those very regions which he ruled and over which
he was king and which he neighbored; for he was a neighbor of Sodom, but
nearer to Abraham dwelling by the oak of Mamre. And I think <that> he was
also king of that Salem, which is this much-famed Jerusalem. And after a
little space. Therefore if Melchizedek clearly resembles the Son of God, he
will not be without father nor without mother nor without genealogy, but the
things of the flesh according to the nature of the flesh will be
genealogized, and the other, that which is without genealogy, will be seen,
just as indeed also in the case of Christ; for Christ too is genealogized
according to the body, but according to the highest order has an ineffable
generation, as we have already set forth.
2 Of Chrysostom on the same: Many, not having understood the things said
concerning Melchizedek, have said that this one is greater than Christ and
have constituted a heresy for themselves, who are also called
Melchizedekians. Contending then and wishing to show that Melchizedek is
greater than Christ they bring forward to us as witness the Scripture saying:
You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek. For how, he
says, would he not be greater, according to whose image and order Christ
serves as priest? And others, having gone astray, say that he is the Holy
Spirit. But we say that he is a man of like passions with us and neither
greater than Christ, nor than John the Baptist, nor indeed that he is the
Holy Spirit. And that he is not greater than Christ, let those who say this
tell us of what region they say he is; is it heavenly? is it earthly or under
the earth? If heavenly or of some other region, let them hear that every knee
shall bow to him, and the rest. If then he bows the knee, the worshipper
would be less than Christ who is worshipped. But neither do the wretched know
the following verse: being made like the Son of God; just as we too were made
according to the image and likeness of God. But perhaps one of the hearers
asks and says: what then is it that he is called without father and without
mother and without genealogy? To whom we shall say that the Jews have said
that he was born of a harlot and for this reason was not genealogized by
Scripture. To whom we shall say that you have spoken badly. For we find also
others born of a harlot and genealogized; immediately Solomon from the wife
of Uriah, then also Abimelech son of Gideon. For what reason then is he not
genealogized? Since he was a type of Christ and bore his image, for this
reason Scripture omitted his father and mother, in order that in him as in an
image we might behold as in a mirror the Christ who is truly without father
and without mother and without genealogy. For Christ is without father on
earth according to the flesh, without mother in the heavens according to the
spirit according to the divinity; such also is having neither beginning of
days nor end of life. For Scripture did not genealogize Melchizedek, but
omitted his birth and his end, in order that, as we said before, we might
behold this one in that one. For Melchizedek is interpreted king of
righteousness, who is Christ having neither beginning of days nor end of
life; for neither was the God Word begotten in a beginning of days, nor did
he ever cease or die, for Christ remains forever. Thus then Melchizedek
became a just man and truly bearing the image of God in himself; moved then
by a prophetic spirit, having understood the offering about to be brought to
God by Christ, he honored God with bread and wine, imitating the Christ who
was to come. Since then in the synagogue of the Jews the order of Aaron
offered sacrifice to God, not bread and wine, but calves and lambs,
glorifying God with bloody sacrifices, God cried out to Jesus who was about
to be born of the virgin Mary, saying: You are a priest forever according to
the order of Melchizedek, not according to Aaron who serves with bloody
sacrifices, but according to the order of Melchizedek, bringing the offering
of the nations in bread and wine unto perpetuity.
