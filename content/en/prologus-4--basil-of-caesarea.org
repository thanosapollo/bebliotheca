THE RULES AT LENGTH OF THE SAME.
PREFACE.
Since by the grace of God we have come together in the name of our Lord Jesus
Christ, we who have set before ourselves one and the same aim of the life
according to piety; and you are clearly longing to learn something of the
things toward salvation, and there is necessity for me to announce the
ordinances of God, remembering night and day the Apostle saying that For three
years, night and day, I did not cease admonishing each one with tears; and the
present is a most fitting time for us, and this place furnishes quiet and all
leisure from the tumults outside, let us pray with one another, so that we on
our part may give in due season the measure of grain to our fellow-servants;
and you, receiving the word, as the good earth, may give back the fruit in
righteousness perfect and multiplied, as it is written. I exhort you therefore
through the love of our Lord Jesus Christ, who gave himself for our sins, let
us at last take hold of the care of our souls; let us be grieved at the vanity
of the life that has gone before; let us contend for the things that are to
come, unto the glory of God, and of his Christ, and of the worshipful and Holy
Spirit. Let us not remain in this sloth and this slackness, always letting the
present go through sloth, and putting off the beginning of the works toward
tomorrow and what follows, then, being overtaken by the one who demands our
souls, unprepared of good works, let us be cast out from the joy of the
bridechamber =31.892=, and weep afterwards, idle and unprofitable, lamenting
then the time of life that was badly let slip, when nothing more will be
possible for those who repent. Now is an acceptable time, says the Apostle,
now is a day of salvation. This age is of repentance, that of recompense; this
of endurance, that of consolation. Now God is a helper of those turning back
from a bad way, then a fearful and inexorable examiner of human deeds and
words and thoughts. Now we enjoy long-suffering, then we shall know the
righteous judgment, when we shall rise, some unto eternal punishment, and some
unto eternal life, and each shall receive according to his deed. Unto what
time are we putting off the obedience of Christ, who called us into his
heavenly kingdom? Shall we not sober up? Shall we not call ourselves back from
the life according to habit toward the exactness of the Gospel? Shall we not
set before our eyes that fearful and manifest day of the Lord, in which the
kingdom of the heavens will receive those approaching the right hand of the
Lord through their deeds; and gehenna of fire and eternal darkness will hide
those thrust away toward the left through the barrenness of good works? There
will be, he says, the weeping and the gnashing of teeth. But we say we desire
the kingdom of the heavens, yet we do not take thought of the things from
which it is possible to attain it; but, accepting no toil for a commandment of
the Lord, we suppose in the vanity of our mind that we shall obtain equal
honors with those who have stood against sin even unto death. Who, sitting at
home or sleeping in the time of sowing, when the harvest has come, filled his
bosom with sheaves? Who harvested a vineyard not planted and labored by
himself? The fruits belong to those whose also the labors are; the honors and
the crowns are of those who have conquered. Who ever crowns the one who has
not even stripped for the opponent? since it is necessary not only to conquer,
but also to contend lawfully, according to the Apostle; and this is not to
pass over even a little of the things commanded; but to do each thing as we
have been ordered. For blessed, he says, is that servant whom the Lord, when
he comes, shall find, not doing as it happens, but doing thus; and, If you
offer rightly, but do not divide rightly, you have sinned. But we, having
supposed that we have done some one of the commandments =31.893= (for I would
not say that we have done it; for all hold to one another according to the
sound aim of the word, so that in the loosing of the one the rest also are of
necessity loosed together), do not wait for the wrath upon the things passed
over, but wait for the honors upon the thing supposedly accomplished. The one
who, of the ten talents of the deposit, has withheld some one or two, and paid
back the rest, is not known as honest because of the payment of the greater
part, but is convicted as unjust and greedy upon the deprivation of the
lesser. And why do I say deprivation? since the one entrusted with the one
talent, then giving back that very thing whole and unimpaired which he
received, because he did not work in addition upon what was given, is
condemned. The one who honored the father for ten years, but later struck one
blow only, is not honored as a benefactor, but is condemned as a parricide.
Go, says the Lord, make disciples of all the nations, teaching them, not to
keep some things and neglect others, but to keep all things whatsoever I
commanded you. And the Apostle writes accordingly: Giving no offense in
anything, that the ministry be not blamed, but in everything commending
ourselves as ministers of God. For if all things were not necessary for us
toward the aim of salvation, neither would all the commandments have been
written, nor would all have been declared as necessary to be kept. What is the
profit to me of the other accomplishments, if I am about, having said fool to
the brother, to be liable to gehenna? For what is the profit of freedom from
the many to the one held by one into slavery? For he who does, he says, the
sin is a slave of sin. And what is the gain of impassibility in many things to
the one whose body is being corrupted by one passion? Is it then, someone
says, that the multitudes of the Christians, not keeping all the commandments,
will have the keeping of some unprofitable? Toward this it is good to remember
blessed Peter, who after so many accomplishments, and such beatitudes, hears
concerning one thing only: If I do not wash you, you have no part with me. And
I leave aside saying that even that itself had no indication of sloth or
contempt, but was an expression of honor and reverence. And indeed, someone
might say, it is written: Everyone who shall call upon the name of the Lord
shall be saved; so that even the invocation itself of the name of the Lord is
enough to save the one who calls. =31.896= But let this one also hear the
Apostle saying: How then shall they call upon him in whom they have not
believed? And if you believe, hear the Lord saying: Not everyone who says to
me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of the heavens, but he who does
the will of my Father who is in the heavens. Where even the one working the
will of the Lord, but not as God wills, nor doing this in a disposition of
love toward God, has the zeal of the work unprofitable, according to the voice
of our Lord Jesus Christ himself, who said that They do them to be seen by
men; amen I say to you, that they have their reward. Whence Paul the apostle
was taught to say that If I give away all my possessions to feed the poor, and
if I deliver my body that I may be burned, but have not love, I am profited
nothing. And in general I discern these three differences of disposition
toward the unavoidable necessity of obedience. For either, fearing the
punishments, we turn aside from the evil, and we are in the slavish
disposition; or, pursuing the gains from the wage, we fulfill the commands for
the sake of our own benefit, and according to this we resemble the hired men;
or for the good itself, and the love toward the one who has given us the law,
rejoicing that we have been counted worthy to serve so glorious and good a
God, and thus we are in the disposition of sons. Neither therefore will the
one accomplishing the commandments in fear, and always looking with fear at
the penalty of sloth, do some of the things commanded to him and overlook
others; but he will take the vengeance upon every disobedience as equally
fearful for himself. And for this reason he is blessed who cowers at all
things through reverence; and he stands firm in the truth, being able to say:
I saw the Lord before me always, because he is at my right hand, that I may
not be shaken; as choosing to overlook nothing of the things that are due;
and, Blessed is the man who fears the Lord. Why? Because In his commandments
he will delight greatly. It is not then the part of those who fear to pass
over any of the things commanded, or to do them carelessly; but neither will
the hired man choose to transgress any of the things ordered. For how shall he
receive the wage of the work of the vineyard, not having fulfilled all the
things agreed? For even if one of the necessary things is lacking, he has made
himself useless to the owner. Who then will still pay a wage of the harm to
the one who has done injustice? The third was the service according to love.
What son then, having as his aim =31.897= the pleasing of the father,
gladdening him in the greater things, will choose to grieve him for the sake
of the smallest? And much more, also remembering the Apostle, who said: And do
not grieve the Holy Spirit of God, in whom you were sealed. Those therefore
who transgress the most of the commandments, in what part do they wish to be
ranked? neither serving God as Father, nor being persuaded as by one who
promises great things, nor serving as Master. For if I am a Father, he says,
where is my glory? and if I am a Lord, where is my fear? For he who fears the
Lord will delight greatly in his commandments; but through the transgression
of the law you dishonor God, he says. How then, having preferred the life
according to pleasure to that according to commandment, shall we suppose for
ourselves blessedness of life, and equal citizenship with the saints, and the
joys with angels in the face of Christ? Such imaginings are truly of an
infantile mind. How shall I be with Job, I who have not received even a chance
affliction with thanksgiving? How with David, I who have not been disposed
long-sufferingly toward the enemy? How with Daniel, I who have not sought God
with continual self-control and laborious prayer? How with each of the saints,
I who have not walked in their footsteps? What umpire is so without judgment
as to count the victor and the one who has contended nothing worthy of equal
crowns? What general ever called those who did not even appear at the battle
equally into the portion of the spoils with those who have conquered? God is
good, but also just. And of the just man, the recompense according to worth,
as it is written: Do good, Lord, to the good and to the upright in heart. But
those who turn aside into crooked ways the Lord will lead away with those who
work lawlessness. Merciful, but also a judge. For the Lord, he says, loves
mercy and judgment. Therefore he says: Mercy and judgment I will sing to you,
Lord. We have learned upon whom the mercy is; for Blessed, he says, are the
merciful, because they shall be shown mercy. Do you see how he uses mercy with
discrimination? neither having mercy without judgment, nor judging without
mercy. For the Lord is merciful, and just. Let us not then know God by half,
nor take his loving-kindness as an occasion of sloth. For this there are
thunders, for this lightnings, that the goodness may not be despised. He who
makes the sun rise also condemns to blindness. He who gives the rain =31.900=
also rains fire. Those things are of kindness, these of severity; either
through those let us love, or through these let us fear, that it may not be
said also to us: Or do you despise the riches of his kindness, and of his
forbearance, and of his long-suffering, not knowing that the kindness of God
leads you to repentance? But according to your hardness and unrepentant heart,
you store up for yourself wrath in a day of wrath. Since therefore it is
neither possible to be saved without doing the works according to the
commandment of God, nor is it without danger to overlook any of the things
commanded (for it is a fearful arrogance for us to sit as judges of the
lawgiver, and to approve some of the laws and to dismiss others), come then,
contestants of piety, who have honored the quiet and unbusied life as a
fellow-worker of the keeping of the evangelical doctrines, let us set forth a
common care and counsel, so that none of the things commanded may escape us.
For if the man of God must be perfect (as it is written, and the word in the
things before these has shown), there is every necessity to be purified
through every commandment unto the measure of the stature of the fullness of
Christ, since also according to the divine law, even if the maimed be clean,
it is unacceptable for sacrifice to God. Whatever therefore each thinks
himself to be lacking, let him set this forth for common consideration. For
more easily would the hidden thing be found out in the laborious searching of
the many, God, clearly, according to the promise of our Lord Jesus Christ,
granting us the finding of what is sought by the teaching and reminding of the
Holy Spirit. As therefore necessity lies upon me, and woe is to me if I do not
preach the gospel; so also there is equal contest for you if you are slothful
toward the examination, or are disposed weakly and slackly both toward the
keeping of the things handed down and toward the fulfillment through the
works. Because the Lord says: The word which I spoke, that will judge him in
the last day; and, The servant who did not know the will of his Lord, but did
things worthy of stripes, shall be beaten with few; but the one who knew, and
did not, nor prepared himself toward his will, shall be beaten with many. Let
us pray therefore that the stewardship of the word be blameless for me, and
the teaching fruitful for you. As therefore knowing that the words of the
God-inspired Scripture will stand face to face with us at the judgment-seat of
Christ (For I will convict you, he says, and I will set your sins before your
face), =31.901= so also let us attend soberly to the things said, and let us
hasten earnestly to bring the divine teachings forward into work, because we
do not know on what day or hour our Lord comes.
