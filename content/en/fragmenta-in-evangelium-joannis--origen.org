13 There being a twofold beholding, both of sense and of intellect, the one is
apprehensive of bodies and the other of the incorporeal. Therefore we also
call the things underlying the mind and the things beheld by it invisible, not
by not being seen but by not being of a nature to be looked at. For nothing of
bodies is invisible, even if at some time happening outside of sight it is not
seen. For not-being-seen does not signify the invisible, but not being of a
nature toward this. Therefore not-being-seen and the invisible also do not
convert toward themselves. For to the invisible there follows that the
invisible is not seen. For many of bodies, though being visible, are not seen,
either being covered or by not being where these happen to be. This
distinction being rightly held one must take the thing said as about the
invisible. For not even the highest powers see God, not because of their own
weakness but because of the incorporeality of God. But neither is he visible
