A discourse of Chrysostom on the Palms. Lord, bless.
When the Lord drew near to Jerusalem and came to Bethphage toward the
Mount of Olives, he sent two disciples, saying: Go into the village
opposite you, and you will find a donkey tied, and a colt with her;
untie them and bring them to me. And if anyone says anything to you,
you shall say that the Lord has need of them; and immediately he sends
them. This took place so that what was spoken through the prophet
might be fulfilled: Tell the daughter of Zion, Behold, your king comes
to you, meek and mounted on a donkey and on a colt, the foal of a
beast of burden. And yet he had often entered Jerusalem before, but
never with such publicity.
What then was the reason? At that time it was the prelude of the
dispensation, and neither was he himself very well known, nor was the
hour of his suffering near. Therefore he mixed with them more without
display, and rather hid himself. For appearing in this way he would
not have been marveled at, and he would have stirred them to greater
anger. But when he had given sufficient proof of his power, and the
cross was at the doors, he shines forth more greatly from then on, and
does all things with greater publicity, the very things that were
going to inflame them. It would indeed have been possible for this to
happen even from the beginning, but nothing useful or profitable would
have come of it that way. But consider with me how many wonders take
place, and how many prophecies are fulfilled. He said that you will
find a donkey. He foretold that no one would hinder, but that hearing
they would keep silent. And this too is no small condemnation of the
Jews. Those who had never been known to him, nor even appeared before
him, he persuades to give up their own things and not even to
contradict, and this through the disciples. But these, who were
present while he worked wonders, were not persuaded. Do not then
suppose that what happened was a small thing.
For who persuaded those Jews, when their animals were being taken, and
that though they were perhaps poor and farmers, not to speak against
it? Why do I say not to speak against it? Not even to ask, or if they
did ask, to fall silent and yield? For both alike were wonders: both
if they said nothing while the beasts were being led away, and if,
having spoken and heard that the Lord has need of them, they yielded
and did not resist, and that without seeing him himself but only the
disciples. From these things he trains them that he could have
prevented even the Jews who were going to attack him altogether, even
against their will, and rendered them speechless. But he did not wish
to.
And besides these things he also teaches the disciples that whatever
he may ask, they should give, and he commands them even to give up
their very soul and to offer it and not to contradict. For if those
who did not know him yielded in this way, much more must they give way
when he is not present. Moreover he was fulfilling another prophecy as
well, a double one, one through deeds and one through words. The one
through deeds was that of sitting on the donkey; the one through words
was that of the prophet Zechariah. For he said that the king would sit
upon a donkey; and he himself sat and fulfilled it. Another prophecy
again: he was therefore making a beginning, prefiguring what was to
come. How and in what manner? He proclaims beforehand the calling of
the unclean Gentiles, and that he himself will rest upon them, and
they will come to him and see. And prophecy succeeded prophecy. But it
seems to me that he sat upon the donkey not for this reason only, but
also to furnish us a pattern of the philosophic life. For he was not
only fulfilling prophecies, nor only planting the doctrines of the
truth, but also correcting our life through these very things, setting
us rules everywhere of the life of necessity and through all things
setting our life aright.

For this reason also, when he was about to be born, he did not seek a
splendid house, nor a rich and illustrious mother, but a poor woman
who had a carpenter as her betrothed. And he is born in a hut and laid
in a manger, and when choosing disciples he chose not orators and wise
men, nor the wealthy and well-born, but the poor and those from the
poor, obscure from every side. And when setting a table, sometimes he
sets out barley loaves, and sometimes he commands the disciples to buy
from the market according to the very occasion. And he makes beds of
grass, and puts on cheap garments, wrapping himself in nothing
different from the many. And he did not even have a house; and if he
had to move from place to place, he would travel so as even to grow
weary, and when sitting he needs no throne nor cushion, but sits on
the ground, sometimes on the mountain, sometimes by the well, and not
only by a well but even alone, and converses with a Samaritan woman.
Again, setting measures for grief, when he had to mourn, he weeps
quietly, planting everywhere, as I said, rules and limits as to how
far one must go and no further. For this reason also now, since it
happened that some who were weaker needed beasts of burden, here too
he set a measure, showing that one must not be carried after yoking
horses or mules, but use a donkey and go no further, and everywhere
remain within need. Let us look also at the prophecy through words.
What then is the prophecy? Behold, your king comes to you, meek and
mounted on a beast of burden and a young colt, not driving chariots as
the other kings, not demanding tribute, not swaggering and leading
bodyguards around, but displaying great gentleness even from this. Ask
the Jew, then, what king entered Jerusalem riding on a donkey? But you
would not be able to say, except this one only. And he was doing these
things, as I said, declaring beforehand what was to come. For here the
Church is shown through the colt, and the new people, once unclean,
but after Jesus sat upon it becoming clean. And see how throughout the
image is preserved. For the disciples untie the beasts; for through
the apostles both they and we were called, through the apostles we
were brought near.
And since our good standing made them jealous, for this reason a
donkey appears following the colt. For after Christ sat upon the
Gentiles, then those too will come, being made jealous. And Paul,
making this clear, said that a hardening in part has happened to
Israel until the fullness of the Gentiles comes in, and then all
Israel will be saved. That it was a prophecy is clear from what was
said; for the prophet would not have cared to speak with such
precision of the age of the donkey, if this were not so. And not only
these things are shown through what was said, but also that the
apostles claim them with ease. For just as here no one spoke against
the taking, so neither among the Gentiles was anyone of those who held
them before able to hinder. And he does not sit on the bare colt, but
on the garments of the apostles. For once they had taken the colt,
they then give up all things, as Paul also said: And I will most
gladly spend and be spent for your souls. And consider the
tractability of the colt, how, though unbroken and unused to the bit,
it did not rear but was carried along in good order, which itself also
was a kind of prophecy, showing the obedience of the Gentiles and the
sudden change into good order. For the word accomplished all things,
saying, Untie them and bring them to me, so that the disorderly became
orderly and the unclean became clean thereafter. But see the
low-mindedness of the Jews. He worked so many wonders, and they never
marveled at him in this way. But when they saw a crowd running
together, then they marvel.
For the whole city was shaken, saying, Who is this? And the crowds
said, This is the prophet from Nazareth of Galilee. And when they
seemed to be saying something great, then their mind is earthbound and
low and dragged along the ground. And he was doing these things,
making himself known, but at the same time, as I said, both fulfilling
prophecy and teaching the philosophic life, and at the same time also
comforting the disciples, who were grieving because of the death, and
showing that he suffers all these things willingly. But marvel with me
at the precision of the prophet, how he foretold all things; and some
things David, others Zechariah, proclaimed beforehand. So let us also
do, and hymn him, and cast our garments before those who bear him.
For of what would we be worthy, when some wrap the donkey on which he
sat, and others strew their garments under its feet, but we, seeing
him naked, and not even commanded to give back, but to spend from what
remains, are not even so generous? And those follow before and behind,
but we send away even those who approach, we thrust them off, we
insult them. Of how great a punishment are these things worthy? Let us
not then live as beggars, nor become inhuman and cruel toward
ourselves, but let us traffic in this fair business, so that we
ourselves may take it and go our way, and leave it to our own sons,
and attain the good things to come, by the grace and loving-kindness
of our Lord Jesus Christ, to whom be the glory and the power unto the
ages of ages. Amen.
