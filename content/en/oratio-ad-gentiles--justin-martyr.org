Of Saint Justin the Philosopher and Martyr
To the Greeks.
Do not suppose, O men of Greece, that my separation from your customs is
irrational or unconsidered; for I found nothing in them holy or dear to God.
For the very compositions of your poets are memorials of frenzy and of
incontinence. For anyone who attends upon the one who among you is foremost in
education is the most wretched of all men. For first he says that Agamemnon,
cooperating with his brother's incontinence, with intense frenzy and
ungovernable desire, both consented to give his daughter for sacrifice and
threw all Greece into turmoil, that he might ransom Helen, who had been
carried off by a leprous shepherd. And when, the war having taken hold, they
brought captives, Agamemnon himself was led captive by Chryseis; he raised
enmity against the son of Thetis because of Briseis. And the son of Peleus
himself, who bound a river, who overthrew Troy, who mastered Hector, your hero
was a slave of Polyxena; he had been conquered by a dead Amazon; having
stripped off the god-wrought armor, having put on a bridal robe, he became a
victim of love-charms in the temple of Apollo. For the Ithacan son of Laertes
trafficked virtue out of vice; and that he was without good sense, the sailing
past the Sirens made clear, when he could not by prudence stop up his hearing.
Telamonian Ajax, who bore the sevenfold ox-hide shield, having been defeated
in the judgment concerning the arms against Odysseus, was taken by madness. I
do not wish to be educated in these things; for I do not claim such virtue,
that I should be persuaded by Homer's myths. For the whole rhapsody, beginning
and end of both Iliad and Odyssey, is a woman.

But since Hesiod after Homer wrote Works and Days, who will agree with his
idle Theogony? For he says that Cronus, the son of Uranus, deposed his father
from the rule and took the scepters, and, fearing to suffer the like, chose to
devour his children; and that Zeus, stolen away and hidden by the device of
the Curetes, bound his father in chains, and they divided, as the story goes,
Zeus taking the aether, Poseidon the deep, and Pluteus obtaining the portion
in Hades. But Pluteus seized Kore; and Demeter, wandering through the deserts,
sought her child. And the fire at Eleusis raised this myth on high. Again
Poseidon shamed Melanippe as she was drawing water, and he used a crowd of not
a few Nereids, whose names, if we should recount them, we would wear out a
great mass of words. Zeus then is an adulterer in many places: with Antiope as
a satyr, and with Danae he was gold, and with Europa a bull, and he was winged
beside Leda. For the love of Semele both convicted his own incontinence and
Hera's jealousy. For they say he snatched the Phrygian Ganymede to be his
cupbearer. And these things the sons of Cronus did. For your far-famed son of
Leto, who professed the prophetic art, convicted himself of lying. He pursued
Daphne, whom he did not catch; and as he was throwing the discus with his
beloved Hyacinthus, he did not foretell his death. For I pass over in silence
the mannishness of Athena and the womanishness of Dionysus and the whorishness
of Aphrodite. Read to Zeus, O men of Greece, the law against parricides, and
the penalty of adultery, and the shame of pederasty. Teach Athena and Artemis
the works of women, and Dionysus those of men. What dignity does a woman
display, arrayed in arms, or a man decking himself with cymbals and garlands
and women's clothing and celebrating orgies with a herd of women?

For the three-evening Alcides, leader of the contests, the one sung for
courage, the son of Zeus, who slew the mighty lion and destroyed the hydra of
many heads, who put to death the untiring wild boar, who was able to bring
down the man-eating birds as they flew, and who brought up the three-headed
dog from Hades, who was able to take down Augeas' strong wall of dung, who
slew bulls and a deer whose nostrils breathed fire, who took the golden fruit
from the stem, who slew a venomous serpent and Achelous (for what reason he
killed him it is not lawful to say) and Busiris the slayer of strangers, and
who leaped mountains in order to take water that, as the story goes, gave back
an articulate voice: he who was able to do so many and such and so great
things, like an infant, having been overwhelmed with cymbals by satyrs and
having been defeated by a woman's love, was pleased to be struck on the
buttocks by a laughing Lydian woman, and at last, not being able to strip off
the shirt of Nessus, having made a pyre against himself, he took the end of
his life. Let Hephaestus set aside his jealousy, and let him not envy if,
being old and lame in the foot, he was hated, while Ares was loved, being
young and in his prime. Since then, O men of Greece, your gods have been
convicted of incontinence, and your heroes are unmanly, the dramatists'
histories among you have shown the pollutions of Atreus and the beds of
Thyestes and the abominations of the Pelopids, and Danaus killing from envy
and making childless a drunken Egyptus, and the Thyestean feasts which the
Erinyes prepared. And Procne even until now, having been given wings, wails,
and her sister, tongue-cut, the Cecropid, has twittered. For why must one even
speak of the goads of Oedipus, and the murder of Laius and the marriage with
his mother, and the mutual slaughter of his brothers and children at once?

And I have hated your festivals; for there are immoderate fillings there, and
polished flutes calling forth to maddened motions, and overcurious anointings
with unguents, and the putting on of crowns. And by so great a heap of evils
you put an end to shame, and you fill the mind, being driven to bacchic frenzy
by incontinence; and you are accustomed to use unholy and frenzied mixings.
And I would say this also to you: Why are you indignant, being a Greek,
against your child, if, imitating Zeus, he plots against you and has equally
plundered the marriage? Why do you count this one an enemy, and worship the
one like him? And why do you blame your wife living licentiously, while you
have honored Aphrodite with temples? And if these things had been said by
others, it would have seemed a bare accusation and not truth; but now your own
poets sing these things, and the histories among you cry them out.

Come then, O men of Greece, and share in incomparable wisdom, and be educated
by the divine Word, and learn an incorruptible King, and come to know His
heroes, who never work slaughter upon peoples. For our general himself does
not want strength of bodies and beauty of forms, nor the pride of high birth,
but a pure soul, walled with holiness, and the watchwords of our King, and
divine deeds, as of a power through the Word reaching into the soul (O
peaceful trumpet of a soul at war, O place of exile for terrible passions, O
teaching that quenches living fire!), which does not make poets, does not
furnish philosophers, nor formidable orators, but by educating makes mortals
immortal, and mortal men gods, and transfers them from earth to the regions
above Olympus. Come, be educated; become as I am, because I too was as you
are. These things captured me, both the inspired character of the teaching and
the power of the Word; because just as a good enchanter, having made a
terrible serpent creep out from its hole, drives it into exile, so the Word
drives out from the very recesses of the soul the terrible passions of sense,
first desire, through which every terrible thing grows, enmities, strifes,
jealousy, factions, rages, and things like these. Desire having been driven
out, the soul becomes serene and calm. And having been loosed from the evils
flowing around her neck, she goes away to Him who made her; for she must be
restored to the place from which she departed.
