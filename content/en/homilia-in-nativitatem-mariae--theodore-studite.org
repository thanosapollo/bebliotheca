* Homily on the Nativity of Mary
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Ζʹ. Of the same author, a discourse on the nativity of our most holy Lady
the Theotokos.

=96.680=

αʹ. Brightly does creation keep festival today, receiving the Virgin as a
new heaven. A maiden has been born upon earth, the bridal chamber of the King
of heaven having conceived. A young maiden has come into the light, flashing
more brightly than the solar ray. A babe has shone forth from a barren woman,
the most sacred precinct of virginity. Who will not come to meet the
festival? who will not bring festal gifts, things dear to the Virgin? The
fairest gift for those living in virginity is incorruption; for the married,
temperance; for the rich, sharing; for the poor, thanksgiving; for rulers,
gentleness; for kings, righteousness; for priests, holiness; for all in all
things, good order. Since such are the gifts, and with such things the bright
procession, come, let us leap for joy. Let us sing songs of thanksgiving with
David; let us rejoice in the Lord, let us cry aloud to God our Savior. For
the Creator of all things a temple of reception has been built up; =96.681=
because for the maker Word a lodging of sojourning has been made ready;
because for the Sun of righteousness a cloud of light has been spread out;
because for him who wraps the heaven in clouds the veil of a God-woven
garment is raised up; because for him who mingles the seasons with the times
and years a bridal resting-place has been appointed.

βʹ. Dance, maidens; for the birth is virginal. Leap, mothers; for she who
bears is a motherly fruit while keeping virginity. Barren women, be of good
hope as you gaze upon her who was once barren and then bore the child of
God. Nor let the maidens be left behind from the dance, keeping festival of
the birth of her who alone of maidens is both crown and queen. Blow the
trumpet upon the mountains; sound aloud upon the heights; proclaim in
Jerusalem, with Hosea making known to us the command even here. David the
forefather of God, be glad; Isaiah the prince of prophets, be brightened.
And the one, because out of your loins according to the oath the queen comes
forth, from whom God promised to set your fruit upon your throne; the other,
because your prophecy has received its end: A rod shall come forth out of the
root of Jesse, and a flower shall rise up out of the root. The rod is the
Virgin, from whom the unwedded Christ, the flowering and unfading, rises up.

γʹ. But why should I, wretched man, define for this one and that one the
rejoicing, and not mark out the exultation with an all-embracing voice? Let
the heaven above rejoice, and let the clouds drip righteousness. Let the
earth open up and blossom with mercy, and together bring forth
righteousness. Why? Because a tabernacle has been made manifest to the God of
Jacob; because a holy place has been shown forth for the all-holy Word. How
awesome is this place, let the most patriarchal Jacob cry out; this is none
other than the house of God, and this the gate of heaven! O unutterable
hearings! O paradoxical results! What is this new tale? what is this strange
and unutterable parable? If the heaven of heavens is not sufficient for you,
O Word, what is the natural dwelling that suffices him? Do I not fill heaven
and earth, says the Lord? And where for such a one, and so great, and
surpassing every comprehensible subject? for the infinite will not find a
comprehensible place in quantity. The heaven, he says, is my throne, and the
earth the footstool of my feet. And what house shall be built for him who
asks? or what place of his rest shall be found? but nevertheless it has been
found and made manifest. How, and in what manner? For here is what is
sought. Wait a little, beloved, and you shall receive the solution of the
thing desired.

δʹ. As man was about to be brought from non-being into being through the
boundless goodness of God, first the heaven was stretched out, the earth was
spread beneath, the sea was marked off with bounds, and all the things that
complete both were taken together in this one ordering of the whole
adornment. Thereaf =96.684= ter man is royally set in a paradise for the
exercise of virtue; but if the transgression had not advanced, he would not
have been driven out of the life-bearing field. And being cast out from the
garment, if he had not opposed the divine providences, he would not have
sunk into the most passion-laden alteration, venerating polytheism instead of
God the monarch. On account of these things, then, to say it in summary, all
things having been seized by corruption, and God being blasphemed, it
pleased the uncontainable one, that the workmanship of his own hands should
not pass into utter non-existence, to fashion another new heaven and earth
and sea, in which he is contented to be contained through the refashioning of
the race. And these things are the blessed and much-hymned Virgin. O the
wonder! A heaven, bringing up out of her secret treasuries the Sun of
righteousness; an earth, having sprouted out of her undefiled womb the ear of
life; a sea, presenting out of her inward depths the pearl understood by the
mind. Already therefore the newly shining creation of the uncontained one has
been brought in. The royal court of the King of all has been equipped. The
rational lodging of the incomprehensible has been made ready. How
magnificent is this world! How worthy of wonder the workmanship, beautified
with plants of virtues, fragrant with flowers of purity, made glorious with
brightnesses of contemplations, lacking none of the other manifest goods,
worthy, as the saying is, of the inhabitation of God among men! And yet the
former world also is most wondrous of all; for when, he says, the stars were
made, all my angels praised me with a loud voice, and hymned me. But nothing
is yet so befitting God as this blessed and all-wondrous Virgin. And that
this is true, hear the much-enduring Job saying: The heaven is not pure, and
the stars are not unblamable in his sight. But what is purer than her? what
more blameless? of whom God, the light most high and most pure, loved so
much, that through the oversight of the Holy Spirit he was essentially
mingled with her, and from her came forth a perfect man, with the unmixed and
unconfused settlement of his own attributes. O the wonder! The most
philanthropic was not ashamed to acquire his own slave as mother. O the
condescension! The all-good did not refuse to be called the offspring of his
own creature, having fallen in love, that is, with her who is fairer than all
creation, having laid hold of her who is more worthy than the heavenly
powers. Concerning her therefore Zechariah the most wondrous: Rejoice, he
says, and be glad, daughter Zion, for behold I come, and will tabernacle in
the midst of you, says the Lord. Concerning her also Joel the most blessed,
as it seems to me, appears somehow to have cried: Be strong, O earth, rejoice
and be glad, for the Lord has magnified himself to do to you. For she is
carth, upon which Moses the most sacred was bidden to loose the sandal of
the shadowy law for the transition to grace. Earth she is, upon which it is
sung that he was founded in the flesh by the Spi =96.685= rit, he who founded
the earth upon its own steadfastness. Earth she is, receiving no sowing, and
nourishing the nourisher of all fruits. Earth she is, upon which the thorn of
sin did not spring up. But rather through her sprout it has been torn out
root and branch. Earth she is, not as the one cursed before, whose fruits
were full of thorns and briars; but upon her the blessing of the Lord, and
blessed the fruit of her womb, as the sacred word says.

εʹ. But since these things are so, come, let us see whence the ever-living
stream of virginity ran straight; who is the begetter, and who the
mother. Joachim and Anna, the luminous and all-famed pair of the Word, the
harmony more divine-speaking than all unions. For if the branch surpasses
all, surely her root is not less excelling? But for us the well-rooted plant
was thus great-souled and most excellent, but as yet unfruitful of fruit.
Most pure the spring, but yielding no stream. The calamity was
lamentable. The things present were grievous and painful. What then? The
righteous cried, and the Lord heard them, and delivered them out of all
their afflictions. Long before David most prophetically signified such
things: The righteous, he says, cried. This pair has been contemplated by me
as introducing the person of the whole of humanity. I contemplated therefore
humanity bereft of the knowledge of God, the world widowed through
unbelief; since all turned aside, together they became unprofitable,
according to the psalmody. Painful the disorder, downcast the
barrenness. The righteous cried, in their own paradise, that is. For what
reason? Since out of paradise the sorrowful sin had gone forth, there, he
says, the God of all to the first mother: Multiplying I will multiply your
griefs and your sighing. In griefs you shall bear children, and your turning
is toward your husband. What did they cry? The fruit of the womb, that is,
entreating the fruitfulness of the knowledge of God, and it is likely they
prayed thus: Adonai Lord Elohim Sabaoth, you know the reproach of
childlessness. You know the despondency of barrenness. If looking you look
upon the lowliness of your servants, and give to your servants seed of a
child, we will offer it up before you as dedicated. And the Lord heard them,
swift to mercy, slow to wrath, giving in place of Eve Mary the so-named of
the Lord, a ransom so bright and great-souled. The daughter has become a
motherly remedy; the new dough of the divine refashioning, the all-holy
firstfruit of the race, the root of the God-framed branch, the exultation of
the first father. O the benefaction! O the great generosity! Does not the
sun itself now let go brighter rays, somehow perceiving the exultation? Does
not all creation too, as it were, glory in the hope of freedom from
corruption, at the birth of her who incorruptibly bore the liber =96.688=
ator of the world? Consider also what follows. Be a lover of the tapers,
beloved, and do not grow weary of the inquiry. Milk, he says, you shall
milk, and it shall be butter. Suitable was the place of the prayers of the
righteous. Praying in a paradise, they bore a paradise much more blessed
than the former. There the serpent, whispering, easily caught Eve and
deceived her. Here Gabriel the archangel, conversing joyously with Mary,
troubled her, but did not deceive her. There the obedience of the serpent
pre-housed unavenging death. Here the belief of the angel brought in its
stead the ever-joyous life for men. There the decision painful for her who
bears through disobedience; here the overshadowing of the Holy Spirit,
joyous for her who has escaped prophetically the grievous pangs. O the
reversal of the things done! O the newness of the things accomplished! The
ancient things have passed away (let it be said also here), behold all
things have become new. The beginning of a house, according to the most
exact contemplation, and the present festival outshining the sojourn of God
among men.

ςʹ. You have, O lover of beautiful things, the solution of the thing
sought. The debt of the promise has been repaid. Come, speak yourself with
Isaiah the things of Isaiah: O Lord my God, I will glorify you; I will hymn
your name, for you have done wondrous things, a counsel of old, true. For in
a little while you shall see the blessed one entering into the Holy of
holies, as already dedicated to the most all-holy God; then conceiving in her
womb the uncontained one. O the wonder! For the one who holds all things in
his hand to dwell in a little body. And how is he not circumscribed, O you
who war against Christ (that I may overlook for a little)? That, though
according to us, he says, yet for us. And what is this? For not according to
the human form, but according to the nature of the Godhead is the difference
and reversal; as he was suckled at his mother's breasts in a human way; but
he provided life to his mother in a manner befitting God. He spoke with an
articulated voice according to us, but he was proclaimed with divine
authority for us, whence the wonders. He made roads with earthy feet
according to us, but walked on the back of the sea for us. He was crucified
in the flesh according to us, but he turned back the opposing powers upon
the cross for us; of which things the one are of a circumscribed nature,
that which is according to us; the other of the uncircumscribable, that
which is for us; neither destroying the other's natural attributes through
the essential union, but each standing within its own boundary. But you,
fleeing to circumscribe, or rather to depict (for both are the same), you
mingle the natures with the Acephali, and you pack off the economy with the
Manichaeans. It were less a matter of folly for you, to speak the truth, to
Judaize, or, bearing the appellation of Christian, to rage against
Christ. For the one who does not admit that God became embodied neither
depicts, acting consistently with himself; but you, admitting it, yet not
depicting, become inconsistent with yourself, incurring laughter together
with the Judaizer. And I do not yet speak of the outrage; but toward those
the discourse goes this =96.689= far, that not dogmatically but festively we
have assembled. But we, adding yet a few things to the Virgin, and these
unrolling out of those things for which she is named in the God-inspired
Scripture, will conclude in these the discourse, even if unworthily.

ζʹ. Hail, Mary, or rather myriad-fold, because of the countless multitude of
the encomiums. For if one said myriad things about you, he would not reach
your worth. Hail, Lady, as having received the queenship in a manner
befitting the mother of the Lord of all, of whom if one said that all things
are slaves, he would not cast the word amiss. Hail, myrrh of the sea, who in
the briny inclination of life bore flesh as a corpse toward sin, out of whom
sweetness, and altogether desire, he who says in the Songs: I gathered myrrh
with my aromatics. Hail, bush, the fire-plaited wonder, who by way of
privation art untrodden by sin; since also you showed the unhandled plant,
and heaven trodden by earth-born ones through your birthgiving of God.
Hail, ark, the God-built shelter, the treasurer of the newly built world,
from whom Christ comes forth, the new Noah, filling the world above with
incorruption. Hail, rod, the God-planted shoot, the only childbearing one of
all virgins, blossoming from unsown ground the Son, the God and high priest
of all things. Hail, urn, the gold-fashioned vessel, whom every vessel
inhabits, from whom the whole world is manna-fed with the bread of life
baked in the fire of the Godhead. Hail, tabernacle, the vault of the
Godhead, more excellent than the heaven of the arches, from whom God
conversed with men face to face, and from whom propitiation eternal came
into the world. Hail, censer, the gold-bearing vessel, who bears within the
divine coal, from whom the fragrance of the Spirit breathed forth, driving
the smoldering corruption from the world. Hail, table, the God-placed wine,
who fills all with the good participation of virtues, to him who says in the
Songs: Your navel is a turned bowl, not lacking wine. Hail, temple, the
purely wrought dwelling of the Lord, concerning which David says: Holy is
your temple, wondrous in righteousness; from which Christ, making for
himself the body a temple, shows men to be temples of the living God.
Hail, sanctification, the God-springing fountain full of all holiness, from
whom comes forth the Holy One of the holy ones, purifying the world from
defilement. Hail, place of the Lord, the God-trodden earth, who made local
through the incarnation him who is outside all this in the Godhead, from
whom the simple is composite, and the eternal, in time; and the
uncircumscribable, circumscribed. Hail, house of God, the house shining with
divine splendors, whose lintel is the inalienable one of purity; who is full
of the glory of the Lord, and more light-bearing in spirit than the fiery
Seraphim. Hail, gate, looking toward the east, from whom the east of life
for men lessens the setting of death; who art the bush trodden by God,
bearing the keys of virginity. =96.692= Hail, heaven, the habitation more
precious than the space round the world; who art more starry with the lamps
of the virtues, from whom the sun of righteousness rose, making for men a
day of unending salvation. Hail, throne, seen aloft in glory, the ensouled
seat who outlines in yourself the throne of God, and gives God rest above
the intelligible powers. Hail, Cherubim, the mind like fire, who fills the
divine thoughts as eyes, and flashes forth many-tongued graces, and through
whom the unsetting light is ferried over to men. Hail, Mother unwedded, who
alone among mothers art pure, and hast the things of mothers in virginity,
of all wonders the newest wonder. Hail, Virgin who has borne, who alone
among virgins hast given birth, and hast the things of virgins in
motherhood, of all portents the most dread utterance. Hail, royal seal,
imprinting in a mother-fashioned body the King of all things who was made
essential from you; since such as the one bearing, such, that is, is also
the offspring. Hail, sealed book, who art untouched by every
corruption-making device, from whom the Lord of the God-carved law is read
in a virginal manner by himself alone. Hail, scroll of a new mystery, who
art wholly untouched by corruption, in whom the formless Word is painted
with the pen of human form, or rather is made embodied, who was made in all
things like us except sin. Hail, sealed fountain, the gusher of
corruptionlessness, who poured forth the stream of life, Christ, the marks
of virginity in no way defiled; through the participation of whom, made
immortal, we run back to the ageless paradise. Hail, garden enclosed, the
fruit-bearing habitation uninvaded by virginity, whose scent is as a field
full, which the Lord who came forth from you blessed. Hail, unfading rose,
who sweetens the unmeddled, with whom the Lord, having smelled, rested
again, and through whom, blossoming, he made the fragrance of the world
unfading. Hail, fragrant apple, the barren-growing fruit, and beautiful
toward God, who say in the Songs: Make me to lie among apples, for I am
wounded with love. Having gathered her purity, Christ feasted, breathing an
unblemished fragrance to the world. Hail, lily, whose offspring is Jesus, he
who clothes these lilies of the field; the sweet-breathing rose-bed of the
Spirit, from whom Christ clothed himself with an unfading garment from
unsown ground, hiding the garment of Solomon. Hail, flower, more variegated
than every flower-dye, the sweetness of every virtue, from whom comes forth
a flower like to the like according to the motherly likeness, upon whom rest
the seven spirits, as the Word says. Hail, nard, pouring forth and watering
according to the unguent-crafts of purity the aromas, whose diffusion is a
sweet savor to him who says in the Songs: My nard gave forth its scent.
Hail, stacte, who distilled from virginal balsam-making for Christ a stacte
of holiness, or of milk, who sing in the Songs: A bundle of stacte my
=96.693= beloved, he shall lodge between my breasts. Hail, cinnamon, the
aroma going forth from the intelligible paradise of purity, whose scent is
sweet to him who says in the Songs: The shoots of your orchard are a
paradise of pomegranates with the fruits of orchard trees; calamus and
cinnamon with all trees of Lebanon. Hail, daughter, the priestly-censing
maiden, whose undefiled loveliness, and the world-contrary, to him who said
in the Songs: How beautiful are your steps in sandals, daughter of
Aminadab; your belly is a heap of wheat fenced about with lilies. Hail,
sister, named after the good brother and altogether lovable, whose voice is
such in the Songs: You have ravished our hearts, my sister bride, you have
ravished. Hail, bride, whose bridesman is the Holy Spirit, and bridegroom
Christ, who says in the Songs: All-fair is my neighbor, and there is no
blemish in you; come from Lebanon, bride. Hail, ointment, the
myriad-precious token of the virtues, who art scented with the perfumes of
all purity, from whom the Lord came forth of the same name with you. For an
ointment, he says, poured out is your name; from which the royal priesthood
has been anointed. Hail, incense, the prayer-offering directed before the
Lord on behalf of the whole world, who art accomplished of the fragrance of
the Spirit, concerning whom somewhere it is wondrously cried: Who is this
rising up out of the wilderness, as stems of smoke made fragrant with
incense? Hail, pure gold, who art tested in the furnace of God with the fire
of the Spirit, and bear nowhere a wrinkle of evil; from whom the lamp, and
the table, and all the golden things according to the law, by way of
allegorical intimation, take reference to you the gold-named and
many-named. Hail, wood incorruptible, who have never admitted the worm of
sinful corruption, from whom the intelligible altar was framed, not out of
corruptionless woods, but built of undefiled womb for God. Hail, royal
purple, who wove out of your virginal blood the scarlet robe, to him who
says in the Songs: The plaiting of your head is as purple; the king is bound
in the galleries. How were you made beautiful, or how were you made sweet?
Hail, byssus woven, who plaited in mind the depth of divine meanings, and
art unshamed by the opposing enticements: For how, he says, shall this be
to me, since I know not a man? Through whom the purple-and-gold-woven ephod
of him who hierarchizes among the powers above. Hail, jacinth, the
flame-shining fleece of virginity, from which the garment of embodiment has
been mystically hierurgized to God. Hail, light cloud, who bear the earthy
and heavy body, somehow light, as if airy, and cover the bread of life as in
an altar, in whom Isaiah greeted the Lord as sitting. Hail, stainless one,
the untouchable treasure of virginity, who bore the stainless Word, from
whom virginity =96.696= shone forth, drawing the world together. Hail, pure
one, who alone boastingly possess the pure heart. Truly the mountain
acceptable to God, from which the new Israel is sanctified more preciously
and longer than of old. Hail, bedfellow, bearing motherly the
child-nourishing nursing, from whom he who nourishes all things milks
virginal milk. Hail, unstained one, who alone art readily approachable to
God for inhabitation, and hast the dignity of God from birth; wherefore you
are reverenced even by all the heavenly Powers. Hail, fleece, the symbol of
victory about Gideon, from whom the immortal dew, figuratively, has been
drawn off, he who says of himself: Take courage, I have conquered the
world. Hail, field of God, who contained in your womb the uncontained one in
all things; of whom all things are in his hand, and, being present with
you, in your hands; who is both called inconceivable and conceives the
incomparable. Hail, Mother of God, who alone art the handmaid of your own
Son, and both by nature, after whom there is no mother who is a virgin of
blessed virginity, toward the incorruption that ran back to the former
paradise. Hail, receiver of God, the light-bearing element of the divine
boundlessness, whose boundless one, held in a cubit-long womb, holds the
boundaries in himself. Hail, Theotokos sovereignly and truly, the dread
joining-place of men to God, through whom the heavenly things are
harmonized with those on earth, balancing to God the things of man, and to
man the things of God. Hail, pavilion, who art raised up with the beauties
of virginity, to him who says in the Songs: I entered into my garden, my
sister bride. To which he adds for the union with the flesh: Go forth and
see king Solomon in the crown with which his mother crowned him in the day
of his espousals, and in the day of the gladness of his heart. Hail, ewe
lamb, barren according to the conjugal union, and childbearing according to
the divine conception; from whom the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of
the world, came to be present. Hail, cloud of light, shadowing
ambassadorially the new Israel in the wilderness of life; from whom the
ordinances of grace have been heard, from whom the sun of righteousness has
risen, brightening all things with the flashing rays of incorruption.
Hail, lampstand, the golden and well-rooted vessel of virginity, whose wick
is the grace of the Spirit, and oil, the holy body taken from undefiled
flesh, from which Christ is the light unapproachable; who brought in close
to those sitting in darkness and the shadow of death the eternal life.
Hail, full of grace, the thing and name more graceful than all joy, from
whom Christ the unbroken joy is born into the world, the remedy of the
Adamian grief. Hail, paradise, a place more blessed than Eden, whose plant
of every virtue has sprouted up, and upon whom the tree of life has been
made manifest; through the participation of whom we run back to the ancient
dwelling, the flaming sword giving way, as it is written. Hail, city, the
resounding, in Davidic fashion to speak, of the great King, =96.697= in
which the kingdoms of the heavens have been opened, and earth-born ones,
being enrolled as citizens, exult; whose narratives are ecstatic and
marveled at by every tongue and mind, in Christ Jesus who is merciful to me
through you, for the things for which I, wretched and senseless, am in
debt, extolling your boundless praises; for to him belongs all glory, honor
and worship, with the Father and the holy Spirit, now and always and unto
the ages of ages. Amen.
