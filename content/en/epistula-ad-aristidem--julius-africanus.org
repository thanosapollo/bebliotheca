53 ............ Those then who either have been ignorant of the
Gospel history or have been unable to understand it have thickened
their ignorance with an error that gives glory, saying that this
differing enumeration of the names and mixing of the priestly, as they
suppose, and of the royal has come about justly, so that Christ might
be shown justly to have become both priest and king, as if someone
were disbelieving or had taken another hope, that 54 Christ <is> an
eternal priest of the Father, offering our prayers to him, and a
supermundane king, assigning by the Spirit those whom he has freed,
having become a fellow worker in the ordering of the whole. And this
was announced to us not by the catalogue of the tribes, not by the
mixing of the registered families, but by patriarchs and prophets. Let
us not then sink to such pettiness of piety, that we establish the
kingship and priesthood of Christ by the interchange of names, because
the priestly tribe of Levi was joined to the royal tribe of Judah,
Aaron at the beginning taking Elizabeth the sister of Naasson, and
again Eleazar the daughter of Phatiel, and from there they 55 begot
children. The evangelists therefore lied, establishing not truth but a
conjectured praise, and for this reason the one genealogized through
Solomon from David down to Jacob the father of Joseph, while the other
from Nathan the son of David down to Heli, likewise otherwise father
of Joseph. Yet they ought not to have been ignorant that each of the
enumerated lines is the family of David, the royal tribe of Judah. For
if Nathan is a prophet, yet Solomon also is, and the father of both of
them; but prophets arose from many tribes, while priests from none of
the twelve tribes, only Levites. Vainly therefore has the 56 falsehood
been invented for them. Let not such a word prevail in the church of
Christ and of God the Father of exact truth, that a lie is composed
for praise and doxology of Christ. For who does not know also that
most sacred word of the apostle, proclaiming and announcing the
resurrection of our Savior and insisting on the truth, saying with
great fear, that if some say Christ has not been raised <nor has
Christ been raised>, and we both say this and have believed and hope
it itself and proclaim it, we bear false witness against God, that he
raised Christ, whom he did not raise. And if thus he who gives glory
to God the 57 Father fears lest he seem a liar when narrating a
paradoxical work, how would he not justly fear who by false speech
procures a confirmation of truth, composing a glory that is not true?
For if the families are different and no genuine seed is brought down
to Joseph, and it has been said only for the establishment of the one
who was to be born, that the one who will be shall be king and priest,
with no demonstration present, but the solemnity of the words being
carried into a feeble hymn, it is clear that the praise does not
attach to God, being a lie, but judgment belongs to the one who has
spoken, who has boasted of what is not as if it were. In order then
that we may both refute the 58 ignorance of the one who has said this,
and stop anyone from being scandalized by similar ignorance, I will
set forth the true history of what took place. For since the names of
the families in Israel were enumerated either by nature or by law, by
nature through succession of genuine seed, but by law when another
begot a child in the name of a deceased brother who was childless (for
because a clear hope of resurrection had not yet been given to them,
they imitated the coming promise by a mortal resurrection, so that the
name of the one who had passed away might remain unfailing), since
then those who are entered in this genealogy, some succeeded as a
child to a father genuinely, while others were born to some but were
assigned to others by name, the memory of both has come about, both of
those who begot and of those as if they had begotten. Thus neither of
the Gospels lies, enumerating both nature and law. For the families,
the one from Solomon and the one from Nathan, had been interwoven with
one another by raisings of the childless and by second marriages and
by raising of seeds, so that the same men were justly considered at
different times to belong to different men, some seeming fathers,
others being so in fact, and both accounts, being properly true, come
down to Joseph in a complex way, yet accurately. 59 And so that what
is said may be clear, I will narrate the interchange of the families.
For those enumerating the generations from David through Solomon,
third from the end is found Matthan, who begot Jacob the father of
Joseph; but from Nathan the son of David according to Luke, likewise
third from the end is Melchi; [whose son is Heli the father of Joseph]
for Joseph is son of Heli the son of Melchi. With Joseph therefore set
as our point of inquiry, it must be shown how each is recorded as his
father, both Jacob who is from Solomon and Heli who is from Nathan,
[each bringing down a family] and how [first these, namely Jacob and
Heli, two brothers, and before that, how] their fathers Matthan and
Melchi, being of different families, appear as grandfathers of Joseph.
Matthan then and Melchi, having in turn taken the same woman, begot
uterine brothers, the law not forbidding a woman who is widowed,
either divorced or also when the husband has died, to be married to
another. From Estha then, for the woman is handed down as being so
called, first Matthan, who traces his family from Solomon, begets
Jacob; and when Matthan had died, Melchi, who is referred by family to
Nathan, being of the same tribe but of another family, as I said
before, took her while she was a widow and had a son, Heli. 60 Thus we
shall find Jacob and Heli, of two different families, uterine
brothers. Of whom the one, Jacob, when his brother Heli had died
childless, took the wife and begot from her Joseph, by nature and by
reason his own, therefore it is written: "And Jacob begot Joseph"; but
by law he was the son of Heli; for Jacob, being his brother, raised up
seed for him. Therefore the genealogy according to him will not be
invalidated either, which Matthew the evangelist enumerates: "And
Jacob," he says, "begot Joseph"; but Luke conversely: "who was, as was
supposed" (for he also adds this) "of Joseph of Heli of Melchi"; for
it was not possible to declare more distinctly the generation
according to law, and he kept silent about "begot" for this kind of
child begetting to the end, making the reference by analysis as far as
Adam, the son of God. Nor indeed is this unproven or improvised. At
any rate the kinsmen of the Savior according to the flesh, whether
showing off or simply teaching, but in any case speaking truth, have
also handed down these things: that Idumaean robbers, having attacked
Ascalon, a city of Palestine, from a shrine of Apollo which was set up
by the walls, carried off among other spoils Antipater, child of a
certain Herod, a temple servant, as a captive; and because the priest
was unable to lay down ransom for his son, Antipater, having been
brought up in the customs of the Idumaeans, was later befriended by
Hyrcanus the 61 high priest of Judaea. And having gone as envoy to
Pompey on behalf of Hyrcanus and having freed for him the kingdom that
was being cut away by Aristobulus his brother, he himself prospered,
being styled procurator of Palestine; and Herod his son succeeds
Antipater, who was murdered out of envy of his great good fortune,
which Herod was later judged by Antony and Augustus by a decree of the
senate to rule the Jews, whose children were Herod and the other
tetrarchs. These things are indeed common also to the histories of the
Greeks. But while the Hebrew families were still at that time recorded
in the archives, even those traced as far as proselytes, such as
Achior the Ammonite and Ruth the Moabitess and those of mixed stock
who came out together from Egypt, Herod, the family of the Israelites
contributing nothing to him, and being struck by the consciousness of
his low birth, burned their records of the families, thinking he would
appear well born by the fact that no one else would have from a public
writing to trace his family to the patriarchs or [proselytes] those
[also] called geioras [the mixed]. But a few of the careful, having
private registers of their own, either remembering the names or
otherwise having them from copies, pride themselves on the nobility
being preserved in memory; of whom were the aforementioned, called
Desposyni because of their connection with the Savior's family, who
from Nazara and Cochaba, Jewish villages, traveled over the rest of
the land and 62 set forth the genealogy before us from the Book of
Days as far as they could reach. Whether then it is thus or otherwise,
no one else could find a clearer explanation, as I at least think, and
everyone who is fair-minded; and let this be our concern, even if it
is without witness, because we have none better or more true to speak;
at any rate the Gospel is altogether true. .............. Matthan who
