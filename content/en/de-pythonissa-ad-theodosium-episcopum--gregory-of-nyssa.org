LETTER OF GREGORY BISHOP OF NYSSA CONCERNING THE PYTHONESS
TO BISHOP THEODOSIUS
He who said to his own disciples, Seek and you shall find, will surely
also grant the power to find to those who, in love of learning and
according to the Lord's command, search out and seek the hidden
mysteries. For he who promised does not lie, lavishing beyond the
asking in the munificence of his gifts. Therefore Give heed to the
reading, child Timothy. For I think it fitting to use the voice of the
great Paul toward your goodness, and May the Lord give you understanding
in all things, so that you may be enriched In every word and all
knowledge. And now concerning the matters you enjoined: whatever the
Lord may suggest, I have judged it well to serve your eagerness in a
few words, so that through these you may learn that we must serve one
another through love in doing one another's wills.
First, then, since the inquiry concerning Samuel was also set at the
head of the remaining chapters, I shall set it out as briefly as
possible, God granting. =102= Some of those before us were pleased to
take as true that sorceress's evocation of the soul of Samuel, and they
offered some such argument in support of this opinion of theirs: that
Samuel, grieving over the rejection of Saul and always bringing this
before the Lord, namely that Saul, as you willed, has purged from the
people the sorcery that comes from the ventriloquists for the deception
of men, and that because the prophet was distressed that the Lord was
unwilling to be reconciled to the rejected one, they say God allowed
the prophet's soul to be brought up through such magic, so that Samuel
might see that what he had put forward to God on Saul's behalf was
false, when he said that Saul was an enemy to the ventriloquists, the
very man who at that time, through their sorcery, obtained an oracle of
the ascent of his soul. But I, looking to the evangelical chasm which
the patriarch, or rather the Lord of the patriarch, says is fixed
between the wicked and the good, so that it is impossible for the
condemned to pass over =103= to the rest of the righteous, and for the
saints to cross over to the choir of the wicked, do not accept that
such opinions are true, having been taught to believe that only the
Gospel is true. Since, then, Samuel is great among the saints, and
sorcery is an evil possession, I am not persuaded that Samuel,
established in so great a rest of his own, crossed that impassable
chasm toward the impious, either willingly or unwillingly. Unwillingly
he would not have submitted, because the demon could not cross the
chasm and, coming into the choir of the holy, move the saint from his
place; and willingly he would not have done this, because he neither
wished to mix with the wicked nor was able to. For to one who is among
goods, a change from the things in which he stands toward their
opposites is unwanted; and even if one granted that he wished it, the
nature of the chasm does not permit the passage. What, then, do we
reason about these things? The common enemy of all is an enemy of human
nature, and his whole device and zeal is to harm man in the very vital
points. And what other such vital blow against human beings is there as
to be cast off from the God who gives life and to desert of one's own
will to the destruction of death? Since, then, there is in this life a
certain zeal among those attached to the body to have some knowledge of
things to come, through which they hope either for escape from evils or
guidance toward what they desire, for this reason, so that human beings
might not look toward God, the deceitful



nature of the demons devised many ways of knowing the future: augury,
omen-divination, oracles, inspection of livers, necromancy, ecstasies,
possessions, inspirations, and many other such things. =104= And
whatever form of foreknowledge anyone, from some deceit, thinks to be
true, in that the deceitful demon appears, to justify the deceived
opinion of the one who has gone astray. Thus the demon also arranges
that the eagle's flight run together with the hope of the observer, and
the throbbing of the liver, and the derangement of the membranes that
comes from a windy humor, and the twisting of the eyes. And in each
case, according to the observation noted from the deceit, the cunning
of the demon displays it beforehand to those being deceived, so that
human beings, standing away from God, attend to the service of the
demons, through whom they believe such things are worked.
One form of deceit, then, was also that of the ventriloquists, whose
magic was believed able to draw the souls of the departed back again to
the life above. When, then, Saul had come to despair of salvation,
because the whole alien host had been stirred against him in full
force, and had come to this device, that Samuel might suggest to him
some way of salvation, the demon that remained with the pythoness,
through which the woman was customarily deceived, was formed in various
shapes, shadow-like, in the woman's eyes, while none of what the woman
beheld appeared to Saul. For when she had taken hold of the sorcery and
the phantasms were already before the woman's eyes, the demon contrived
this as a pledge that the appearances were true: first he made clear
that Saul himself, =105= hidden in the guise of his disguise, so that
Saul was all the more struck with amazement, as if the woman would no
longer go wrong in anything, since the magical power had not failed to
recognize his private appearance. When, then, she said that she had
seen gods coming up, and a man standing upright in a double cloak, how
will the slaves of the letter establish the matter according to the
history? For if it is truly Samuel who was seen, then in truth those
seen by the sorceress are also gods. But Scripture calls the demons
gods: For all the gods of the nations are demons. Was Samuel's soul,
then, together with the demons? God forbid. Rather, the demon that
always obeyed the sorceress took along other spirits as well, for the
deception both of the woman herself and of Saul who was deceived
through her, and it made the demons be thought gods by the pythoness,
while it itself was formed toward the sought-for appearance and
mimicked that man's voices, and, as one would reasonably reckon from
what appeared, it uttered as in the form of a prophecy the sentence
expected to follow from what was at hand. And the demon, even against
its will, convicted itself by speaking the truth, that Tomorrow you and
Jonathan shall be with me. For if it were truly Samuel, how was it
possible that the one condemned in all wickedness should be with him?
But it is clear that that wicked demon, appearing in place of Samuel,
did not lie when it said that Saul would be with itself. And if
Scripture says =106= that And Samuel said, let such a phrase not
trouble the one who understands, but let him take it as added that it
was the one thought to be Samuel. For we find the scriptural custom in
many places setting forth the seeming in place of the real, as in the
case of Balaam, now saying himself that I will hear what God will speak
in me, and after this that Balaam, knowing that it was pleasing to God
not to curse the Israelites, no longer went, according to custom, to
meet the omens. For the unobservant man will think that there too the
true God was conversing with Balaam; yet the sequel shows that
Scripture so named the god supposed by Balaam, not the God who truly
is. Therefore here too the one who seemed to be Samuel mimicked the
words of the true Samuel, the demon skillfully imitating the prophecy
from what was likely. The matter concerning Elijah needs more
contemplation, yet not in the



question that was set before us. For when he was commanded to drink
water from the torrent, he was being advised by God in a hidden way, so
that the sentence of drought uttered by the prophet against the
Israelites might through him be dissolved again. For to him who was
given to drink only from the torrent, which in droughts would
reasonably dry up, no other comfort for thirst being found for the
prophet, because it was forbidden him to drink from elsewhere, it was
necessary to ask for the rain, so that the water might not fail the
torrent. And the service of the things necessary for life comes to the
prophet from the ravens, God showing through these things to the
prophet that there are many who =107= have remained in the worship of
the true God, whence the food was supplied to the prophet through the
ravens. For they would not have brought him bread of the defiled or
meat sacrificed to idols, so that through these things too Elijah might
be led to let something of his wrath against the impious slacken,
having learned through what was happening that there are many who look
to God, whom it is not just to condemn together with the guilty. And if
bread is served to him in the morning, and meat in the evening, perhaps
what happens bears, as a riddle, upon zeal for the virtuous life: that
to those beginning the life according to virtue, of which morning is a
symbol, one must offer the easily grasped word, but to those being
perfected the more perfect, according to the voice of Paul, who says
that Solid food is for the perfect, those who by habit have their
senses exercised.
As for the veil of Moses, you will not be ignorant of what it looks
toward, once you have become conversant with Paul's letter to the
Corinthians. And as for all that you inquired about the sacrifices, you
will do well to examine the whole of Leviticus more laboriously and,
with greater assiduity, to contemplate the law in these things as a
whole. For thus the part will be understood together with the whole;
for this by itself alone could not be accurately distinguished before
the contemplation of the whole. And in the difficulties raised
concerning the opposing power the solution is evident: that the one who
became apostate was not simply an angel, but had been ranked among
archangels. It is clear, then, =108= that with the principality is also
indicated the subordinate rank, so that the inquiry about how he was
one and is with a multitude has been solved. For when the army under
him apostatized together with him, what was sought has been made clear.
And the last of the chapters inquired after (I mean how the Spirit
comes before baptism), needing a broader examination and contemplation,
we shall, God granting, enclose in a discourse of its own and send to
your honor.
