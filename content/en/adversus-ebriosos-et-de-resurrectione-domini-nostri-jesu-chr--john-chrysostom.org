* Against Drunkards and on the Resurrection of Our Lord Jesus Christ
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Against those who are drunk, and on the resurrection, a discourse delivered on
the holy and great Sunday of Pascha.

=50.433=

1. We have laid aside the burden of the fast, but let us not lay aside the
fruit of the fast; for it is possible to lay aside the burden of fasting, and
to reap the fruit of fasting. The toil of the contests has passed, but let the
zeal of the achievements not pass; the fast has gone, but let piety remain; or
rather, not even the fast has gone. But do not be afraid; for I said this not
proclaiming another Lent to you, but announcing the same virtue to you. The
bodily fast has passed, but the spiritual fast has not passed; this is better
than that, and that came to be for the sake of this. Just as, when you were
fasting, I used to say to you that it is possible for one who is fasting not
to fast, so now too I say that it is possible for one who is not fasting to
fast. Perhaps what is said seems a riddle, but I bring the solution to the
question: How is it possible for one who is fasting not to fast? When someone
abstains from foods, but does not abstain from sins. How is it possible for
one who is not fasting to fast? When someone enjoys food, but does not taste
of sin. This fast is better than that, and not only better, but also lighter;
in that fast many alleged bodily weakness, a grievous itching. I am filled
with the scab, he says, I cannot endure going unwashed, drinking water ruins
me, I cannot taste vegetables. Many such things I used to hear many saying
then; in this fast there is nothing of the sort to say. Enjoy baths, share in
the table, use wine-drinking in measure, and if you are going to taste of
meats, there is none who forbids; enjoy all things, only stand away from sin.
Do you see how easy this fast is for all? It is not possible to allege
weakness of body; the achievement is of the soul, and it is clean. It is
possible both for one not drinking wine to be drunk, and for one drinking wine
to be sober; and that there is drunkenness without wine, hear the prophet
saying: Woe to those who are drunk without wine; and how is it possible to be
drunk without wine? When you do not mix the unmixed wine of the passions with
pious reasoning. It is possible also for one drinking wine not to be drunk;
and if this were not so, Paul would not have commanded this, writing to
Timothy thus: Use a little wine for your stomach and your frequent
infirmities. For drunkenness is nothing other than an ecstasy of the natural
wits, a turning aside of reasonings, a desert of understanding, a poverty of
sense. And these things not the drunkenness from wine alone produces, but also
drunkenness from anger and from unseemly desire. For just as wakefulness
produces fever =50.434=, and toil produces it, and despondency produces it,
and a corrupted humor produces it, and the occasion is different, but the
passion and the disease are one; so also in these cases: wine produces
drunkenness, and desire produces it, and a corrupted humor produces it, and
the occasion is different, but the passion and the disease are one. Let us
abstain from drunkenness; I do not say let us abstain from wine, but let us
abstain from drunkenness; wine does not produce drunkenness; for it is a
creature of God, and no creature of God produces anything evil, but an evil
choice produces drunkenness. And that it is possible to be drunk otherwise
than by wine alone, hear what Paul says: Be not drunk with wine; showing that
it is possible to be drunk otherwise also: Be not drunk with wine, wherein is
excess. Marvelously in a short saying he uttered the whole accusation of
drunkenness. What is, Be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess? We call those
of the young men prodigals, who have inherited a paternal lot, and squander
everything at once, and do not calculate to whom one must give, and when to
give, but simply, both garments, and gold, and silver, and all the paternal
wealth, distribute to harlots and the lustful.


Such is drunkenness; as if it had taken a prodigal youth, having seized the
mind of those who are drunk, and having taken reason captive, it forces us to
pour out all the substance of our thoughts without consideration and without
any exactness. The drunkard does not know what must be said, and what kept
silent, but his mouth is continually without a door; no bolt is set upon it,
nor a door upon the lips; the drunkard does not know how to manage his words
with judgment, does not know how to dispose the wealth of the mind, does not
know how to lay some things aside and spend others, but all those things are
expense and outlay. Drunkenness is a willing madness, a betrayal of
reasonings; drunkenness is a calamity that is mocked, a disease that is jeered
at, a self-chosen demon, and worse than insanity.

2. Do you wish to learn how the drunkard is even worse than the demoniac? We
all pity the demoniac, but we hate this man; we share the pain of that one,
but we are angry and indignant at this one. Why ever? For that one the passion
is an outrage, for this one it is slackness; for that one a plot of enemies,
for this one a plot of reasonings; and the drunkard suffers such things as the
demoniac, he is carried about in the same way, he is beside himself in the
same way, he falls down in the same way, he twists the pupils in the same way,
he convulses lying on the ground in the same way; he sends forth foam from the
mouth, =50.435= and thus pours out putrid saliva, and the mouth is filled with
an unbearable stench. Such a man is unpleasant to friends, ridiculous to
enemies, contemptible to servants, joyless to a wife, burdensome to all, more
joyless than the irrational creatures themselves. The irrational creatures
drink only until they thirst, and they stop desire at need; but this man by
wantonness goes beyond desire, and has become more irrational than the
irrational; and what is more grievous, that a disease so full of evils and
having so many calamities is not even thought to be a charge; but at the
tables of the rich there is rivalry and contest over this unseemliness, and
they strive the more against one another, who will make a spectacle of
himself, who will furnish more laughter, who will more dissolve the sinews,
who will more overthrow the strength, who will more provoke the common Master;
and one may see a stadium and a diabolic rivalry. The drunkard is more
wretched than the dead; for that one lies without sensation, and inactive
toward both good things and evil things; but this one is fit for the working
of evils; and having buried the soul in the body as in a tomb, he carries
about a dead body. Do you see how he is more wretched than the demoniac? how
more without sensation than the dead? Do you wish me to say what is greater
than all these and more grievous? A drunk man cannot enter into the kingdom of
heaven; who says these things? Paul. Be not deceived; neither fornicators, nor
idolaters, nor adulterers, nor the effeminate, nor abusers of themselves with
mankind, nor thieves, nor the covetous, nor drunkards, nor revilers, nor
extortioners, shall inherit the kingdom of God. Did you hear into what chorus
he ranked him? With those who have consorted with harlots, with those who have
fornicated, with the idolaters, with the adulterers, with the revilers, with
the covetous, with the extortioners. What then, he says? is the drunkard and
the one who has consorted with harlots the same? is the drunkard and the
idolater the same? Do not tell me these things, O man; I have read divine
laws, do not demand the accounts of me; ask Paul, and he himself answers. For
whether he is ranked with them or not with them in the punishment, I cannot
say; but that he likewise falls away from the kingdom of heaven as the
idolater does, this I would strongly maintain; and this being confessed, why
do you still demand of me accounts of the measure of the sin? For when he
remains outside the doors, when he falls from the kingdom, when he becomes
destitute of salvation, when he is sent away into eternal punishment: why do
you hold out to me measures of sins and balances and weights? Truly, beloved,
drunkenness is terrible and very grievous. I do not say this to you; God
forbid; for I am quite persuaded that your soul is clean of this disease and
this passion, and a token of the health is the lingering here, the gathering
with eagerness, the sober hearing; for no one


who is drunk with wine can desire the divine oracles. Be not drunk with wine
wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit. This is the fair
drunkenness; stupefy your soul with the Spirit, that you may not stupefy it
with drunkenness; occupy the mind and the reasonings beforehand, that that
shameless passion may not find room. For this reason he did not say, Partake
of the Spirit, but Be =50.436= filled with the Spirit. Fill the mind up to the
brim like a cup of the Spirit, that the devil may no longer be able to cast
anything in; for one must not simply partake of the Spirit thereafter, but be
filled with the Spirit, with psalms, hymns, spiritual songs, with which you
have been filled today. For this reason I am confident concerning your
sobriety. We have a fair cup of drunkenness, there is a cup of drunkenness
that produces sobriety, not paralysis. And what is this? The spiritual cup,
the unstained cup of the Master's blood. That does not produce drunkenness,
that does not produce paralysis; for it does not paralyze strength, but raises
up strength; that does not dissolve the sinews, but tones the sinews; for that
produces sobriety, that is venerable to angels, fearful to demons, precious to
men, beloved to the Master. Do you see how David speaks of this spiritual cup
that lies on this table? You prepared a table before me, over against those
who afflict me; you anointed my head with oil, and your cup inebriating me as
strongest. For lest, having heard the name of drunkenness, you should be
afraid at once, and think it productive of weakness, he added that it is
strongest and powerful. A new manner of drunkenness, it implants strength, it
makes one continent and able; for it flowed from the spiritual rock; it is not
a turning aside of reasonings, but an addition of spiritual reasonings.

3. Let us be drunk with this drunkenness; but let us abstain from that, that
we may not put the present feast to shame; for the present feast is not of
earth only, but also of heaven. Today there is joy on earth, today there is
joy in heaven; for if at one sinner turning there is joy on earth and heaven,
much more when the whole inhabited world has been snatched from the hands of
the devil will there be joy in heaven. Now angels leap, now archangels
rejoice, now the Cherubim and the Seraphim keep the present feast with us;
they are not ashamed of their fellow-servants, but they rejoice together in
our goods. For if the grace from the Master is ours, yet the pleasure is
common to them also. And why do I speak of the fellow-servants? The Master
himself of them and of us is not ashamed to keep the feast together. And why
need I say that he is not ashamed? With desire, he says, I have desired to eat
this Pascha with you; and if he desired to keep the Pascha with us, it is
clear that he desired the resurrection also. When then angels rejoice, and
archangels, and the Master of all the heavenly powers keeps the feast with us,
what reason is left thereafter for despondency? Let no poor man be downcast
because of poverty; for this feast is spiritual; let no rich man be lifted up
because of wealth; for he can contribute nothing from money toward the
pleasure of this feast. For in the outer, worldly feasts, where there is much
unmixed wine, where a table overflowing and gluttony, where unseemliness, and
laughter, where every Satanic pomp =50.437=, reasonably the poor man is
downcast, and the rich man in brightness; why ever? Because the rich man sets
out a lavish table, and enjoys luxury more, but the poor man is prevented by
poverty from displaying the same munificence; but here there is nothing of the
sort; one table both for the rich man and for the poor man, and if someone is
rich, he can add nothing to the table; and if poor, nothing less because of
poverty will the things of communion be disposed; for the grace is divine; and
why do you wonder if for rich and poor? For to the king himself who wears the
diadem, who has the purple, who has been entrusted with the authority of the
earth, to that king, and to the beggar who sits for alms, one


table is set forth. Such are the Master's gifts; he does not divide the
communion by dignities, but by choice and by mind. For when you see in church
a poor man standing beside a rich man, a ruler beside a private man, a cheap
man beside the one who is superior, the one who outside trembles at the power,
inside standing fearlessly alongside the power, understand what it is, Then
the wolf shall feed together with lambs. Scripture then calls the rich man a
wolf, the poor man a lamb. And whence is this, that The wolf shall associate
with the lamb, in the way a rich man with a beggar? Attend exactly. Often a
rich man and a poor man have stood in church; the hour of the divine mysteries
arrived; the rich man is thrust out as uninitiated, but the poor man stands
within the heavenly tents, and the rich man is not indignant; for he knows
himself to be a stranger to the divine mysteries. But O the divine grace! not
only is there equal honor in the church through the grace of God, but even
when they stand, often the poor man takes precedence of the rich man in piety,
and wealth did not benefit the one who has it without piety, nor did poverty
harm the faithful man standing with confidence at the holy altar. And I say
these things, beloved, concerning the catechumens, and not concerning the
simply rich. Consider, beloved, how a master withdraws from the church, and a
faithful servant attends upon the mysteries; a mistress withdraws, and the
maidservant remains; For God does not accept the face of a man. There is not
then in the Church slave and free; but Scripture knows as slave the one
enslaved to sin. For he who does, it says, the sin, is a slave of sin; and it
knows as free the one who has been set free by the divine grace. With the same
boldness both the king and the poor man come to this table, with the same
honor, and often the poor man even with more honor; why ever? Because the
king, entangled in countless affairs, is splashed from every side like a ship,
and many sins are rubbed onto him; but the poor man, caring only for necessary
food, and having taken up the unbusied and quiet life, at rest, sitting as in
a harbor with much safety, comes to the table. Again in the outer feasts the
poor man is in dejection, and the rich man in brightness, not because of the
table only, but also because of the garments; for what they suffer in the
matter of the table, this they endure also in the matter of the garments. For
when the poor man sees the well-to-do man clothed also in a more costly robe,
he is struck =50.438=, and counts himself most wretched of all. But here this
want too has been taken away; for there is one garment among all, the saving
bath; For as many as, it says, have been baptized into Christ, have put on
Christ. Let us not then put this feast to shame by drunkenness; for our Master
honored rich and poor alike, and slaves and masters; but let us repay the
Master for his goodwill toward us; and the best repayment is a clean way of
life, and a sober soul. This feast and this assembly needs neither money nor
expense, but choice and an excellent mind; for such are the wares here.
Nothing bodily is sold here, but hearing of divine oracles, prayers of
Fathers, blessings of priests, concord, and peace and harmony, the gifts are
spiritual, the wage is spiritual. Let us keep this greatest and splendid
feast, in which the Lord rose; and let us keep it both brightly and in a godly
manner; for the Lord rose, and raised the inhabited world together with
himself; he himself rose, having burst asunder the bonds of death. Adam sinned
and died; but Christ did not sin, and died. This is new and paradoxical; that
one sinned, and died, this one did not sin, and died; why? So that the one who
sinned and died might, through the one who did not sin, but died, be able to
strip off the holds of death. So also it happens in the matter of money;
Someone often owes, and not having to pay is held in bonds; another not owing,
but being able to pay, having paid, released the


one liable; so also it happened in the case of Adam; Adam owed, he was held by
the devil, but he did not have the means to pay; Christ did not owe, nor was
he held by the devil, but he was able to pay down the debt. He came, he paid
death on behalf of the one held by the devil, that he might release that one.

4. Did you see the achievements of the resurrection? We died a double death,
therefore let us expect a double resurrection. He died a single death; for
this reason he also rose a single resurrection; how? I say; Adam died both in
the body, and in the soul; he died both by sin and by nature; In the day you
eat from the tree, you shall die by death. And yet he did not die on that very
day by nature, but he died by sin; that death is of the soul, this death of
the body. And when you hear that death of the soul, do not think that a soul
dies; for it is immortal; but death of a soul is sin and eternal punishment.
For this reason Christ also says, Do not fear those who kill the body, but are
not able to kill the soul; but rather fear him who is able to destroy both
soul and body in gehenna; and that which is destroyed exists, but is outside
the sight of the one who destroyed it. But as I was saying, in our case the
death is double; therefore the resurrection also must become double. In the
case of Christ the death is single; for Christ did not =50.439= sin; but even
the single death itself was for us; for that one did not owe the death; for he
was not liable to sin, and therefore not to death either; for this reason he
rose the resurrection from the single death; but we, having died a double
death, also rise a double resurrection; one we have already risen, that from
sin; for we were buried with him in baptism, and we were raised with him
through baptism. This one resurrection is deliverance from sins; and the
second resurrection is that of the body; he has given the greater, expect also
the lesser; for this is much greater than that; for it is much greater to be
delivered from sins, than to see a body rising. For this reason the body fell,
because it sinned; therefore if the beginning of falling is sin, the beginning
of rising is being delivered from sin. We have already risen the greater
resurrection, having cast off the grievous death of sin, and having stripped
off the old garment; let us not then despair concerning the lesser. This
resurrection we too rose long ago, when we were baptized; and those who have
now been counted worthy of the evening of baptism, these fair lambs. Before
yesterday Christ was crucified, but he rose in the night that has passed; and
these before yesterday were held by sin, but they rose together; that one died
in body, and rose in body; these had died by sin, and rose from sins having
been set free. The earth then at this season of spring yields us roses and
violets and the other flowers; but the waters have shown us a meadow more
delightful than the earth. Do not wonder if flowers have sprouted from the
waters; not even the earth according to its own nature, but according to the
command of the Master, yields the growth. In the beginning too the nature of
the waters brought forth moving living things; For let the waters, it says,
bring forth creeping things of living souls, and the command became a work,
and that soulless substance brought forth ensouled living things; so also now,
let the waters bring forth, not creeping things of living souls, but spiritual
gifts. Then the waters brought forth irrational and voiceless fishes, now they
have sent forth rational and spiritual fishes, fishes caught by the apostles;
For come, he says, and I will make you fishers of men; this fishing he was
speaking of then. New indeed is the manner of the fishing; those who fish draw
out from the waters, we cast into the waters, and thus we fished. There was
once also among the Jews a pool; learn what that pool was able to do, that you
may learn the Jewish poverty, that you may learn the wealth of the Church.
There was a pool of waters, and an angel came down there, and


troubled the waters; then after the troubling of the water one sick man went
in and was healed; one only of the year was healed, and immediately the grace
was spent, not because of the poverty of the giver, but because of the
weakness of those receiving. An angel then went down into the pool, and
troubled the water, and one was healed; the Master =50.440= of the angels went
down into the Jordan, and troubled the water, and healed the whole inhabited
world. For this reason there, after the first, the second who went down was
not healed; for the grace was given to the Jews, the weak, the poor; but here
after the first the second, after the second the third, after the third the
fourth, and if you throw ten, or twenty, or a hundred, or ten thousand, or the
whole inhabited world into the pool, the grace is not spent; the gift is not
consumed; the streams are not soiled. A new manner of cleansing; for it is not
bodily; for in the case of bodies when the streams wash more people, they
receive more of the stain; but here the more it washes, the cleaner it
becomes.

5. Did you see the greatness of the gift? Keep the greatness of this gift, O
man. It is not permitted you to live indifferently; set a law for yourself
with all exactness; it is a contest and wrestling-bouts; and he who contends
is temperate in all things. Shall I tell you one manner of achievements,
excellent and safe? Let us cast out of the mind the things that seem to be
indifferent, but that beget sins. For of affairs some are sins, and some are
not sins, but causes of sins; as laughter is not a sin by nature, but it
becomes a sin when it is carried further; for from laughter comes jesting,
from jesting filthy speech, from filthy speech filthy deeds, from filthy deeds
punishments and retributions. Take away then the root from above, that you may
take away the whole disease; for if we guard ourselves in the indifferent
things, we shall never fall into the things that are forbidden. So also to
look at women seems to many to be indifferent, but from this an unbridled
desire is begotten, from the desire fornication, from fornication again
punishment and retribution. So also to live in luxury seems to be nothing
terrible, but from this drunkenness and the countless evils from drunkenness.
Let us then everywhere take away the beginnings of sins. For this reason you
enjoy continual teaching each day; for this reason we accomplish a gathering
for seven days in succession, setting the spiritual table before you, making
you enjoy divine oracles, anointing you each day, arming you against the
devil; for he attacks more savagely now; the greater the gift, the greater
also the war. For if in paradise the devil, seeing one, could not bear it,
seeing so many in heaven how will he bear it, tell me? You have made the beast
savage, but do not be afraid; you have received greater power, a sharpened
sword; with that sting the serpent. For this reason God allowed him to become
savage against you, that through the experience itself you may learn the power
of your own strength. And just as an excellent trainer, having taken some
filthy, sinewless, neglected athlete, having anointed him, having trained him,
having brought him to good flesh, no longer lets him be at rest, but orders
him to go down to the contests, that he may teach him through experience how
much power he has put in him; so also Christ has done. For he was able to take
the enemy out of the way; but that you may learn the excess of the grace, the
greatness of the spiritual power, which you received from baptism, he lets
=50.441= him wrestle with you, furnishing you many occasions of crowns. For
this reason you enjoy teaching for seven days in succession, so as to learn
the wrestling-bouts exactly. And otherwise too what is taking place is a
spiritual marriage; and at marriages the bridal chambers stand for seven days.
For this reason we too have legislated for you seven days here to stand at the
sacred bridal chambers. But there after the seven days they are dissolved, but
here if you wish you stand continually at the sacred bridal chamber; and in
the outer marriages the bride after a former


month or even a second is not so beloved to the bridegroom; but here it is not
so; but as time advances, so much the warmer the loves of the bridegroom, so
much the more genuine the embraces, so much the more spiritual the union, if
we are sober. Again in the case of bodies after youth old age; here after old
age youth, and youth that never =50.442= has an end, if we wish. Great is the
grace, but it will be greater, if we wish; and Paul was great when he was
baptized, but he became much greater after these things, when he preached, he
confounded the Jews; after these things he was caught up into paradise; he
went up to the third heaven. So that it is permitted also to us, if we wish,
to increase, and to magnify the grace given us through baptism; and it
increases through good works, and becomes more splendid and furnishes us the
light more brightly. And if this happens, with much boldness we shall go
together into the bridechamber with the bridegroom, and we shall enjoy the
goods laid up for those who love him; which may we all attain, by the grace
and loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom to the Father and to
the Holy Spirit be glory and worship unto the ages of ages. Amen.
