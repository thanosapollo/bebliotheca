On the Three-Day Resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ, Homily.
=50.821=
Rejoice in the Lord always, beloved brethren; again I will say, rejoice; the
Lord is near, be anxious for nothing; the Lord has been raised from the dead,
and with him a great multitude of saints. Let us therefore keep the feast both
gladly and soberly; for this is truly the day which the Lord has made; let us
rejoice and be glad in it; let us proclaim the resurrection of the Savior, or
rather let us cry out our own salvation; let us proclaim the memorial of the
saving day; let us proclaim the death of the devil, the captivity of the
unclean demons, the salvation of Christians, the resurrection of the dead.
For through the resurrection of Christ Gehenna of fire is quenched, and the
unsleeping worm comes to an end; Hades is thrown into confusion, the devil
mourns, sin is put to death, wicked spirits are driven out, those from earth
run up into the heavens, those in Hades are freed from the bonds of the devil,
and fleeing to God they say to the devil: Where, O death, is your victory?
Where, O Hades, is your sting? And the cause of this holy feast and festival
for us is Christ, who is also the provider of all good things for us. For he
both made us from the beginning, and brought us from non-being into being; he
also now saved us who were perishing; he made alive those who had been put to
death; and he took us away from the tyranny of the devil; he set us free who
were slaves to sin, blotting out the handwriting that was against us; Christ
redeemed us from the curse of the law, having become a curse for us. For which
things it is fitting that we too should say: What shall we render to the Lord
for all that he has rendered to us? Being God, the Only-begotten, he was well
pleased to become man for us, and became obedient unto death, that he might
deliver us from eternal death; the Master of the angels put on the form of a
servant; the Word of God took on flesh, and he appeared as man, who is of the
same form and consubstantial with the Father; and these things he endured,
that he might snatch us from unjust slavery and ransom us from dishonor; for
this reason the giver of our life consented to suffer in the flesh; for
this reason also the fountain of immortality was buried, that he might grant
everlasting life to mortals. And he was present upon earth doing good, and
healing the sufferings of men; but from the God-fighting Jews he received
unworthy recompenses. For our Lord Jesus Christ, through great goodness,
cleansed lepers, granted sight to the blind, healed the lame, drove out
demons, raised Lazarus on the fourth day from the dead, fed five thousand from
five loaves, walked upon the sea, changed the water into wine, healed the
woman with the issue of blood, made alive the dead daughter of the ruler of
the synagogue, and performed many other mysteries worthy of wonder; but the
Jews, moved by envy and the evil eye, at one time stoned the Lord, at another
tried to cast him down a precipice, and at last even led him to the cross. But
our Lord Jesus Christ did not imitate the wickedness of the blasphemous Jews;
but he also gave his back to scourges, according to the prophet, and his
cheeks to blows; and he did not turn away his face from the shame of
spittings; and at last he was led as a sheep to slaughter, and as a lamb
before its shearer is voiceless, not opposing, not answering back. When he was
reviled he did not revile in return, when he suffered he did not threaten; but
he committed himself to him who judges justly. For he did not come at the
first coming to punish and take vengeance on the unbelievers, but he willed
through long-suffering and patience to lead those who had gone astray to the
truth. And learn the Lord's goodness and his great =50.822= kindness. The Jews
blasphemed him, and said to him: You have a demon; but the Lord, being
long-suffering, drove the demons out of men. The Jews spat into the face of
the Savior, but he healed their blind; the Jews stoned Christ, but Christ
granted their lame to run; and throughout he continued doing good to those who
insulted him, and instead of evils granting good things to those ungrateful
and defiled men; and bearing insults without taking offense, perhaps he was
even thought weak, he who is escorted by angels. And lest by saying many
things we seem to lengthen the discourse, let us come to the very heads of the
matters. At last, then, the King of glory was led to the cross and death, and
he who is glorified by the Cherubim and Seraphim, and worshipped by all the
powers and angels, is nailed to the wood; and these things he both endured and
suffered meekly, providing us a pattern, and becoming a teacher of gentleness.
For this reason, then, we too ought to bear nobly the threats of wicked men.
But even hanging upon the cross he displayed greater works and more miracles,
that even so he might stop the frenzy of the God-haters, so that they might
have no pretext for unbelief, nor say that they crucified a mere man. First,
then, Christ consented to be crucified and lifted up into the air, that he
might put to flight the demons in the air; he was hung upon wood, that he
might heal the sin that of old came upon men through wood; and his side was
also pierced with the spear, because of the woman taken from the side of Adam.
For since the serpent deceived Eve, and Eve prepared Adam to transgress (and a
sentence went out against them both, and death reigned from Adam until Moses
even over those who had not sinned), for this reason the side is wounded, that
we may learn that the passion of Christ brought salvation not only to men but
also to women. For Adam was formed first, then Eve. And Adam was not deceived,
but the woman, having been thoroughly deceived, came to be in transgression;
but she shall be saved through childbearing. What childbearing, if not through
the childbearing of Mary? For she brought forth the Savior Christ, not having
come together with a man, as Isaiah bears witness, but the Holy Spirit having
overshadowed her, as Gabriel the archangel announced the good news. For this
reason, then, the side of Christ is also struck, that the things already said
might be accomplished, and the mystery of baptism might be proclaimed, and the
grace that was to come might shine. For blood and water well forth from the
side of Christ, that he might both blot out the handwriting of sin that was
against us, and that we might be cleansed by his blood, and receive paradise
again. O great mystery! The thief repented; there was need of water, that he
might be baptized; he hung upon the cross; there was no other place of
baptism, no spring, no lake, no rain, nor the one who performs the initiation;
for all the disciples had fled because of fear of the Jews; but Jesus was not
at a loss for streams, but even hanging upon the cross he became a maker of
waters.
For since it was not possible for the thief to enter the kingdom without
baptism, and the one who had repented ought not to be without a share in
baptism, the Savior sent forth water and blood from his pierced side, that he
might both free the thief from the evils hanging over him, and show his blood
to have become a =50.823= ransom for those who have their hopes in him. For if
the blood of bulls and of goats, and the ashes of a heifer sprinkling,
sanctifies those who have been defiled toward the purifying of the flesh, how
much more has the blood of Christ our Savior become a purification of all
Christians together? If then one of the unbelievers should say to you, Why was
Christ crucified? say to him: That he might crucify the devil. If he should
say to you, Why was he hung upon wood? say to him: That he might undo the
sin that came through wood in paradise. If he should say to you, Why did he
also wear thorns? say to him: That he might uproot the thorns and thistles of
Adam; for that one was condemned to groan and tremble, and to farm thorns and
thistles. Jesus then, being a lover of mankind, wishing to provide for his own
workmanship, underwent all things for us, that he might free us from
condemnation. For just as he was born through a woman, that he might blot out
the sin that came upon men from a woman, so also he is crowned with thorns,
that he might make milder, through his own obedience, the earth that was badly
farmed by disobedience. And if he should say to you, Why did he drink gall and
vinegar? say to him: That we might vomit out the death-bearing venom of the
dragon; for that gall has become my sweetness, and that vinegar has become my
healing. And if the unbeliever should say to you again, Why did he also put on
a scarlet cloak, and why did they kneel approaching him? say to him: That even
unwilling the Jews might worship him, and even against their will confess his
kingdom upon earth. Now they worshipped in mockery, not knowing what they were
doing; but in the resurrection to come every knee shall bow, of those in
heaven and on earth and under the earth, and every tongue shall confess that
Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father. Amen. And that cloak has
also another riddle; for it not only sketched the kingdom, but also displayed
the bloodthirsty and murderous character of the Jews. And they also gave a
reed in his hand, that their sins might be written.
These things, then, the Christ-fighters did, not knowing the one being
crucified, or rather having been willingly blinded; but creation did not fail
to know its own Master and maker. For while the Savior was still hanging upon
the cross, the sensible sun, having seen the sun of righteousness, Christ,
being insulted by the lawless, not bearing the daring, flees, having darkened
the earth, and thinking it unfitting =50.824= to cooperate and to give light
to eyes that were committing the greatest impiety. And not only did the sun
flee, but the earth also was shaken, not bearing the lawlessness of the things
being done, and showing and teaching that it was God who was being crucified;
wherefore she also did not endure it, but was angry, not wishing to bear upon
herself the God-hating Jews. For Cain, having slain his brother, did not so
defile the earth, nor did the tower-building of the giants so weigh upon her,
nor did the Sodomites, having done unlawful things, so pollute her, nor even
those who fashioned idols from her; nor did the blood of Zechariah and Abel
poured out so weigh upon her as the Jews, having dared this great impiety. For
this reason also the hard rocks were split, that they might learn that this
one is the spiritual and living rock. For they drank, he says, of a spiritual
rock that followed; and the rock was Christ. O the thanklessness of the Jews!
The rocks were split, and these feel nothing; the lifeless things are shaken,
and the living disbelieve; the veil of the temple is torn, that thereafter
their desolation might be shown. For the veil was split, and the things in the
temple were laid bare because of Christ who said: Behold, your house is left
to you desolate. For indeed after the Christ-killing all the solemn things of
the Jews have been made desolate, and the angels who remained by the city and
the temple passed over from there and came to the Church. And many bodies of
the saints who had fallen asleep were raised together with Christ, that we
might learn that Christ, having died, does not rise alone, but raises from the
dead all who believe in him.
This, in summary, is the solemn feast of Pascha, and these are the mysteries
of Christians; we keep festival concerning the resurrection of the dead and
eternal life. Therefore let us keep the feast not in the leaven of malice and
wickedness, but in unleavened bread of sincerity and truth, believing in
Father, and Son, and Holy Spirit, in a Trinity consubstantial, uncreated;
believing in the resurrection, expecting the Lord coming again, no longer in
lowliness, but in glory with heavenly brightness, with shining angels, with
trumpet and fear and joy; joy of the saints and the righteous, but fear of the
unjust and sinners. And may the God of peace count us all worthy of the
resurrection with the saints, found in good works and in orthodox faith, by
the grace and love of mankind of his Only-begotten Son, with whom be glory,
honor, and worship to the all-holy and good and life-giving Spirit, now and
always and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
