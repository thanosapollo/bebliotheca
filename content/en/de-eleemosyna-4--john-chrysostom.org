On Almsgiving. =60.707= Meadows have varied and diverse flowers, some for the
delight of the eyes, some for fragrance, some for care, and each of them is
fitted to man. So also the Church has the reading of the divine Scriptures,
both evangelical and apostolic and prophetic, and the remaining sacred books;
and each of them is fitted to Christ. But indeed upon the meadows outside the
sun rises, and it withers the flowers, and the comeliness of their faces
vanishes; but in this meadow the sun of righteousness rises, and he brings one
to a sound mind, he justifies another, and he has mercy on another. For he has
risen upon us today through the Gospel, saying: Inasmuch as you did it to one
of these my brethren, you did it to me. Again a reminder of the poor, again a
reproach of the rich. I do not slander rank, God forbid, but an unbridled
mind; I do not slander wealth, but those who are rich in a bad way; for wealth
is good for those who administer it well; poverty is good for those who endure
it well. For Abraham too was rich, and he looked down on no one, and Lazarus
was poor, and he never took it ill, and that especially in so great necessity.
Why then did I bring two persons as witnesses? Come, bring me here the blessed
Job, who had undergone the trial of both. And when he was rich, he said, My
house stood open to every man; and when he became poor, he said: The Lord
gave, the Lord has taken away; as it seemed good to the Lord, so it has also
come to pass. How many times did the devil wrestle, and he did not throw the
righteous man? He emptied the quiver of his arrows, and he did not wound the
soldier; he brought up his engines, and he did not shake the tower; he shook
the tree, and he did not gather the fruit; he broke the branches, and he did
not pull up the root; he dug through the wall, and he did not plunder the
treasure. And I call treasure not of gold and silver, but his unshaken faith;
for this treasure of his the devil wished to plunder. Did you see the fruit
multiplied? did you see the faith established as a rock? did you see the tower
standing unbreakable? did you see the soldier drawn up in array, did you see
the devil vanishing? did you see God awarding the prize, did you see the devil
put to shame? What name to call the righteous man I do not know. Shall I call
him an athlete? But he is a many-contested athlete in the wrestlings. Shall I
call him a soldier? But he is mightier in heart. Shall I call him a tower? But
he is higher in spirit. Shall I call him a rock? But he is more solid. Shall I
call him a tree? But he is fairer. Shall I call him a treasure? but he is more
precious. What name I have to set upon the righteous man I do not know. Let
the rich hear, let the poor also hear, how this blessed man in =60.708= his
prosperity administered his own wealth, and in poverty, having endured how
great a trial, his hope was not cut off from God. Do you see that it is good
to be rich for those who administer well, and poverty is good for those who
endure well? Do not tell me that I fear poverty. You fear poverty, and you do
not fear God? You fear beggary, and you do not fear sin? Do not fear poverty,
but sin, the mother of punishment. If you have the king as a friend, he will
enrich you; and if you love Christ and the poor, he will enrich you. He
himself enriches and makes poor, he humbles and he raises up, he raises the
poor man from the earth, and from the dunghill he lifts up the beggar. The
Master asks a commandment, and you do not give it to him? For when you hear
him saying that Inasmuch as you did it to one of these least, you did it to
me, you see that if you give to the poor man, you give to God. If then you do
not give to him, how will you yourself also ask him on that day, that he may
have mercy on you? will he not charge you, that You saw me hungry, and you did
not fill me; you saw me thirsty, and you did not give me drink; you saw me
naked, and you did not clothe me? what then will you say to him? what pardon
will you have? You are clothed in silks and hare-skins and goat-hair, and the
poor man has not a rag; and you eat pheasants or fowls and geese and whatever
is among foods; and the poor man cannot fill his own belly with bread and
vegetables; you have gold-roofed walls and varied Lacedaemonian marbles, and
columns and golden capitals of columns; and the poor man has not a hut. And
sometimes you have the house from plunder, from greed; and you die, and you go
away having nothing, and your house remains a perpetual accuser, convicting
your deeds. For each one who passes by is going to say: This is the house of
that plunderer, of the greedy man; how many widows did he harm? how many
orphans did he wear down? how many goods did he seize? how many poor did he
defraud? Do not tell me that I am a prefect. I do not know rank, nor do I
slander it, but those who use the good badly. It is a good thing to be rich;
for whatever God made, all things are very good. You see a poor man, and you
treat him with pride, and you do not consider that you are a man, and the poor
man is a man; a man, the living being much sought after by God; a man, for
whom heaven was stretched out; a man for whom the sun runs its courses; a man,
for whom the moon knows its settings; a man, for whom the stars give their
light, for whom the sea was spread out, for whom the earth was founded upon
the waters; for whom mountains and glens and hills, for whom groves and woods
and ravines and plains, for whom springs and rivers and lakes, for whom the
earth brought forth herb of grass, and the waters bring forth living souls,
for whom cattle, four- =60.709= footed things and creeping things and birds,
for whom seasons and years and months and days and hours and nights, for whom
angels and archangels, principalities and powers, thrones, dominions, the
Cherubim and the Seraphim. Why do I say many things? For whom the Master
poured out his blood. A man bought with precious blood, and you overlook him?
is he not a man, as you also are, because he is poor? for this do you treat
him with pride? did God not choose the poor? surely not the rich? Peter, the
chief of the apostles, the foundation of the Church, who is truly Peter both
in name and in deed, was he not poor? was he not pitiable and cast off? was he
not mean and easily despised? was he not a fisherman? Likewise James and John
also, were they not poor and fishermen? And Jesus, passing by, saw them and
their father Zebedee mending torn nets. O excess of poverty; they were so poor
that they could not buy nets for the fishing catch, but they were mending the
old ones and striking craft against craft, sewing against fishing. Likewise
Matthew also, was he not a tax-collector? And Jesus, passing by, saw him
sitting at the tax office, and he says to him: Rise, and follow me. And rising
he followed him. And he did not think that he was holding a royal command, but
he thought that the King of those who reign was calling; and he who once
seized what belonged to others has now made himself a stranger to his own
affairs; and he who once was a wolf, now a sheep. Peter threw down the nets,
and received the keys of the heavens. Matthew left the tax office, and
received the Gospel. Likewise Paul also, was he not a craftsman with his
hands? did he not hold a knife, and cut hides? did he not receive letters from
the rulers of the synagogues, that he might come and take the saints captive?
And Jesus appeared to him on the road, and light flashed around him, and he
says: Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me? And he, turning, says: Who are you,
Lord? And he says, I am Jesus the Nazarene, whom you persecute. And falling on
his face he was blinded. Why did he add, Nazarene? That he might not think
that Jesus is one, and another is calling him from heaven. For this the Jews
were saying, that Jesus was not taken up, but that his disciples stole him.
For this reason he said, Jesus the Nazarene; that he might know that it is the
same one then and now. So Paul entered Damascus led by the hand, and he went
to Ananias, and he laid his hands on the eyes of Paul, and he healed him. The
history is long, but I wish to narrate it to you in brief. And looking up he
at once taught in the name of Jesus. And when the Jews heard that Paul was
teaching in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, they sought to seize him. And
the disciples of Paul, seeing this, put him into a basket, and they let him
down through the wall below, and so he fled and went up to Jerusalem. O that
blessed basket, which sent such a provision to the inhabited world. Likewise
the blessed John also, does he not dwell in the desert, and after these things
become a citizen of heaven? did he not eat locusts and wild honey? were not
all poor, both prophets and apostles? and through these poor men did he
prepare the salvation of men? How many kings, how many tyrants, or how many
consuls, how many rulers, how many leaders have died from of old, and all have
been given over to forgetfulness? and these pitiable ones, the cast off, the
mean and easily despised, were not given over to forgetfulness. But this king,
who wears the purple and has the diadem, glory and golden helmets and soldiers
as bodyguards, goes away to the tomb of the fisherman, of the poor man, of the
pitiable, of the easily despised, and he embraces and weeps and prays to the
one who glorified him. Who persuades the king to do these things? Who? I will
tell. Strain yourselves for me, I beg, attend to what is said. He who said to
them, Going, make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of
the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit; and I will be with you all
the days of your life, and unto the consummation of the age; this is the one
who granted them such a craft. Do you see how much power the poor have? Not
only this, =60.710= but even those possessed by demons go away to their tombs,
and they are examined. Do you see that the dead are more powerful than the
living? Do not any longer cast off the poor. The poor man enters your
courtyard seeking alms, and he wastes two hours receiving nothing, and
sometimes you order your slave to throw him out. Do you not know that if you
throw the beggar out, you have driven Christ away? For he himself said that
Inasmuch as you did it to one of these least, you did it to me. Do you see
that if you insult the poor man, you dishonor Christ? For whatever you will do
to the poor man goes back to Christ, as he himself said. These things I say,
and saying I will not cease; For me to speak is not irksome, and for you it is
safe. I will not cease training you, until you become better; for it is
written, Blessed is he who speaks into the ears of those who hear.
Disobedience works death. You see a poor man, and you do not have mercy? how
do you go into the church, and pray? how do you open your mouth, and call upon
God? do you not blush? do you not tremble? do you not shudder? You enter the
church of God to pray? Give alms, that your almsgiving and your petition may
be written down. Sometimes some put forth a petition before the earthly king,
and they spend two hundred gold pieces, and often nothing comes of it for
them, but they hold it as a scrap of paper, and you do not wish your petition
to be signed for two obols? Here the earthly king signs, and often the
signature is contrary; but if the King of those who reign signs, it is
ratified and confirmed. Did you see a petition of two obols being signed? For
this reason there are two basins before the gates of the church; one of water,
in which you wash your hands, and one the hand of the poor man, in which you
wipe your soul. Enter the church, and wash your hands; give an obol into the
hand of the poor man, and wipe your soul. But if you enter and wash and pray,
and give nothing to the poor man, what is the profit? You have wealth, and you
hide it upon the earth, in which thieves come by night, and dig through and
steal your treasure, and rising at dawn you see it plundered. Do you wish not
to be plundered? What I say to you, do this, and you will never be plundered
unto the age. Do you have a treasure? Bury it in the belly of the poor, and be
strengthened; for it can never be plundered. But if you bury it in the earth,
you will lose it; and if you do not lose it, but it is kept, after your death
others also grow rich from yours. What is the profit to you, that you toil and
labor, and others enter into your goods, from which you will not receive a
wage, and they revel? Have you not heard the prophet saying, The rich man
treasures up, and he does not know for whom he will gather them? And again
elsewhere the same says, The fool and the senseless shall perish, and they
shall leave their wealth to strangers; and: Do not fear, when a man grows
rich, or when the glory of his house is multiplied. For when he dies he will
not take all things, nor will his glory go down with him. And in the Gospels
the Lord says to the foolish rich man: Fool, this night they demand your soul
from you; and the things you have prepared, whose will they be? Do you see how
many things those suffer who treasure up for themselves upon the earth. But
treasure up in heaven, where neither moth nor eating destroys, and it remains
inviolable. O wretched and miserable man, you trust the earth, and you do not
trust God, who says, What you did to one of these least, you did to me? Do you
not know that there are some of the poor husbandmen, pitiable, providing the
daily bread from their own labors, and in the time of sowing they have nothing
from which to sow? and he takes his little tunic or his wife's, and often the
very mattock with which he works the land, and he sets it as a pledge, and he
borrows one modius or two or three, or as many as his field can receive, and
having borrowed he scatters it into the earth, and he trusts the earth, and he
waits for an open season, until the fruit comes to be in the earth? and that
man, having borrowed, trusted the earth, and you do not trust God? He trusted
the fellow-servant, and you do not trust the Master? Tell me, what pardon do
you have? These things I say not condemning you of much inhumanity, God
forbid, but anointing you to be ready for the sowing of =60.711= almsgiving.
Do you love gold, the blood-drinker, desired by all, and an enemy to all,
loved by all and plotting against all, the runaway, the thankless? or do you
not know what its company is? Like a shameless harlot it consorts with this
man, and nods to that; it is engaged to one today, and tomorrow it passes to
another, today a ruler, and tomorrow ruled; today rich, and tomorrow poor;
today you judge, and tomorrow you are condemned. Do you see the change of
life, how it runs past as a shadow, and dissolves more cheaply than smoke? is
it not torn apart as a spider's web? I will not cease training you; whether
you do something, or even if you do not, I speak, and speaking I will not
cease; for it is written: Blessed is he who speaks into the ears of those who
hear. Disobedience works death. Imitate at least that poor widow who cast in
the two obols, how she was justified through a good intention. You do not have
two obols to give? Give one. You do not have one? Give bread. You do not even
have bread? At least groan when you have seen =60.712= the poor man, and your
petition has been received. For he who knows the secrets of each one's mind,
he who knows all things before they come to be, knows that, if you had, you
would have given. He wants the intention, he searches the heart and knows; for
man looks to the face, and God to the heart. I beg you therefore, keep the
command which I command you: For in this you are my disciples, if you love one
another. It is the voice of the Master himself, he himself said it to the
disciples; and again: You shall love the Lord your God with all your strength,
and your neighbor as yourself; in these two commandments is the whole law and
the prophets. I beg you then to be toward one another humane, hospitable,
lovers of the poor; especially let us hold fast continually the love toward
God, that we may be able to be counted worthy of eternal life, which may it be
that we all attain, by the grace and loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus Christ,
to whom be the glory and the power with the Father and the Holy Spirit, unto
the ages of ages. Amen.
