On the Prophet Elijah, a Discourse.
=56.583=
1. Formerly the people of the Jews used to pride themselves on the possession
of the prophets, and it was a prophetic field; but now even this very boast
has been taken from them by the Church. For behold, even the bodies of the
prophets are with us. For prophetic husbandmen were not fitting for a
fruitless people; it was not fitting that those who crucified the one
prophesied through them should still keep the prophets, in return for the dead
whom he raised among them, in return for the blind whom he gave sight among
them, in return for the hungry whom he fed among them, in return for the
violence which, because of them, the Master God of all brought upon blessed
Elijah, forcing the prophet to loose the sentence against them. For the
instructor who has gone before leads me to Elijah. Blessed Elijah, seeing the
people of the Jews wantonly abusing the loving-kindness of God, and God
continually enduring their war against God, while they were always being
trained by his long-suffering toward indolence, devises something of this sort
against God's goodness toward them: he pronounces a sentence against them.
Then, knowing that God is put to shame by the zeal of the righteous, wishing
to punish the Jews, and fearing the divine kindness which is at once healed by
small tears, he pronounces a punishment against them, and binds the punishment
with an oath, so that God, being put to shame by the oath, might not loose the
sentence against them. God, he says, is good toward them, and is healed by
small tears from them, and these things train them toward idolatry. As the
Lord lives, he says, there shall not be in these years dew and rain. Then, as
if God were answering him: What then, Elijah, if I am wheedled by them through
repentance, if I see them offering tears, if they repent of their offenses,
shall I not at once give rain? No, as the Lord lives, except through my mouth.
No, as the Lord lives. Those men, he says, are nailed to idols, but I swear by
your Godhead. Show my oath sworn by you to be powerful, so that through this
too they may learn the weakness of the idols. No, as the Lord lives, there
shall not be, he says, in these years dew and rain, except through the word of
my mouth. Not when it seems good to you, he says, will you loose the
punishment (for it always seems good to you to show loving-kindness), but when
I intercede for them, when I judge, when they show sufficient repentance of
soul. God respected the prophetic sentence; he honored the oath sworn upon
himself.
So then the Master of all was constrained, both respecting the prophet and
pitying the people being punished by famine. What then does he do? He punishes
the prophet together with the people, so that he too, being constrained by the
punishment of famine, might counsel something humane concerning the sworn
sentence; and he punishes the prophet together with the people, but he
lightens the punishment for the prophet, not even making the very path of
loving-kindness without wisdom. For he feeds the prophet through birds that
hate their young; for the raven is a parent that hates its young, and does not
feed what it begets. This, then, the prophet David was hinting at when he
said: And to the young of the ravens that call upon him. For since the young
of the ravens, or rather all nestlings, are weak for gathering their own food,
but they have parents who rear the young, yet the young of the ravens are
deprived of this, and nevertheless the race of ravens remains though not fed
by the parents, David, showing the loving-kindness of God, ascribes their
nourishment to God. For the young of the ravens, gaping toward the air, and
not being fed by their parents, have certain small living things which the
divine providence sends, ministering to them, and thus receiving those from
the air they are fed toward growth. For this reason he says: To the young of
the ravens that call upon him.
Since then Elijah took up a hatred of children against the Jews as useless
children, God makes embassy to him through fathers that hate their young, all
but crying this silently to Elijah: Is there in you so great a hatred of
children against the Jews as there is in the ravens against their own young?
But see now the ravens changed; see, O Elijah, their =56.584= loving-kindness:
those who are inhuman toward their own young become your ambitious hosts. It
is absurd, O Elijah, that the ravens should become mediators toward you of the
loving-kindness from me, while you do not become a mediator for the Jews of
the punishment from me against them. Respect the change of the ravens, and
become kind toward the Jews; for I honor your sentence, and I also pity those
being punished. When therefore God saw Elijah not yielding, he again withdraws
the food through the ravens, forcing him toward reconciliation, so that,
forced by famine, he might at least loose the sentence for his own sake. But
he endured even when the ravens were withdrawn: I will starve, he says, only
let me see the impious being punished; let my body perish, being punished with
these by famine. When therefore the food through the ravens failed, and much
time had passed, Elijah, constrained again, moves to another place, and being
crushed by famine he goes away, and becomes a suppliant of a Gentile woman,
and she a widow and poor. And God, wishing him to become more humane, since at
that time association with Gentiles was forbidden to the Jews, anticipating,
announces beforehand what would happen: Arise, he says, from here, and go to
Zarephath of Sidon, and I will command there a Gentile widow woman to feed
you; so that, hearing the word, Gentile, he might loathe the food, and entreat
God for the supply of rains. But he was not even softened at this word, but
ran toward the feeding by the Gentile woman; and what is greater, God prepares
the widow at once, as he hungers, to speak harsh words to him, driving him
from every side toward kindness.
What then does the Gentile woman say to him? As the Lord your God lives. And
from where did the Gentile woman have it to say, As the Lord your God lives?
God, anticipating, shows the prophet to the widow in a vision; for this is
what God was crying to the prophet: I will command there a widow woman to feed
you. God, anticipating, showed the woman the prophet's figure, form, age, the
coming request. Hence after the prophet appeared, the widow was reminded of
the vision; she saw him such as he had appeared to her in the vision. From
this then, knowing the revelation to be divine, at once as the prophet stood
by and asked for food, she cried: As the Lord your God lives; I have no cake,
but only a handful of meal in the jar, and a little oil in the cruse; and I
will go in and make this for myself and my children, and we shall eat and die.
At once words of despair rather than of giving. I have no cake, but only a
handful of meal; this I will go in and make for myself and my children, and we
shall eat and die. Nothing else is left for us, she says, once the handful is
spent, except death. Elijah began to feel something at the word. She, he says,
wrestles with a famine greater than mine; I at least hunger alone, but she
starves with her children. Let me not become the cause of death to my hostess.
What then? As I said, he is softened at the word, and begins thereafter to
carry about in himself a practice of loving-kindness: The cruse of oil shall
not fail, he says, nor the jar of meal, until the Lord gives rain upon the
earth. Already he faintly utters the giving of the rains. Before he was tamed
by famine, he did not grant to God the loving-kindness concerning the rains,
but said: There shall not be in these years dew or rain upon the earth, except
through the word of my mouth. But now he grants it, and already dictates the
prelude of the loosing, but he was dictating it, yet not yet bringing the
request. What then does the fountain of loving-kindness do? Again he brings
against him a third device of loving-kindness: for he commands the soul of the
widow's child to depart from the body. For honoring the prophet, he did not
loose the blessing, nor did he make the cruse fail, so as to drive him by
necessity to loving-kindness. For it would have been an insult to the prophet
if the things of the blessing from him were overturned; and this would also
have harmed the faith of the Gentile widow, as if the blessing =56.585= had
succeeded a little by chance. For this reason God does not overturn the
blessing, but brings a third device against the prophet: for he makes the
widow's child die, thrusting the prophet into the necessity of petition toward
him. For the widow, when the child had died, stood around the prophet with
tears. Would that I had perished first by famine, she says, and died before
your blessing, rather than become a spectator of my son who had died. The
prophet was ashamed at what had happened: Are these, he says to the widow, the
wages of hospitality toward me? Before my reception, blessed with a child;
after my reception, a dirge over the child she loved.
Elijah, being ashamed at what had happened, and turning the matter over in
himself, perceived that what had come about was a craft of the Master of all;
he perceived the divine craft. And what does he say? Woe is me, Lord, the
witness of the widow, with whom I dwell with her! And what is it that he
wishes to say? From you, he says, is the testimony to me concerning the woman;
you, he says, said: I will command a widow woman to feed you, altogether
bearing witness to the woman's pious purpose. You moved me to this woman from
those places, and her whom you have honored with the testimony from you, her
now with the punitive death of her child? Woe is me, Lord, the witness of the
widow, with whom I dwell with her: you have put her son to death! What has
happened, he says, is not a death of nature; what has come about is of your
craft; you are devising against me a necessity of loving-kindness, so that
when I say to you, Lord, have mercy on the widow's son who has been made dead,
you too may answer me: Have mercy on my son Israel. You are thrusting me
toward loving-kindness; I perceive your devices, O Master, so that when I say
to you, Save the widow's son who has died, you yourself may answer me: You,
pity Israel made dead by famine. You ask a favor of me, have a favor asked in
return; loose the sentence of famine, and I loose the verdict of death. When
therefore God saw Elijah, tamed by many things, and become more humane, then
at last he brings more openly toward him the words of loving-kindness, since
he no longer counted the loosing of the punishment an insult. Go, he says.
Words of embassy of God toward the servant; God himself through himself makes
embassy to the servant concerning loving-kindness. Go, he says. Since you have
become more humane, he says, =56.586= receive also my words of
loving-kindness; since you have known me constrained by the birth-pangs of
loving-kindness, hear me now more openly. I am in labor for the loosing of the
punishment; I am urgent toward the supply of reconciliations; I am ashamed at
the tears of those being punished.
All sinners, O prophet, hold a handwritten bond of my loving-kindness; do not
make me appear a liar among them. Hear also the voices of the document, which
you yourself also know: I do not will the death of the sinner, as that he
should turn and live. Elijah submitted his ears; he set his soul under what
was spoken by God. Go, he says, and appear to Ahab. I do not reconcile without
you when I reconcile; you grant the grace to those being punished. I secretly
make embassy to you on their behalf; but you become the open dispenser of the
reconciliations. Go, and appear to Ahab, and I will give rain upon the earth.
You announce the reconciliations; you declare the supply of the rains; and I
subscribe to your sentences. So Elijah was running both unwilling and willing,
and was announcing the rains as good news; but again, seeing their impiety, he
was meditating some grievous sentence of punishment against them. What then
does God do? Seeing him not having room for loving-kindness toward those who
stumble, he separates Elijah from dwelling with sinners. I, he says, O Elijah,
know your zeal; I am put to shame by your way of life; but I also pity the
sinners, whenever they are punished beyond measure; but you are a terrible
avenger of piety, and the race of men continually offends.
I therefore separate you from dwelling with one another. Go up as into heaven,
not being able to bear sinners; but I will sojourn upon the earth. If you
remain long, he says, with sinners, that race of men will be wiped out, being
continually punished by you. What then? Be transferred as into the heavens, O
Elijah; for fire cannot dwell with a reed. I give you sinless ones to dwell
with; I settle you with the choirs of the angels.
You be transferred toward heaven; but I will dwell among sinners, I who can
bear the wandering sheep upon my shoulders, I who say to all the sinners: Come
to me, all who labor; I will not punish you, but I will give you rest. To him
be the glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
