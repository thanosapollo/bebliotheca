ON THE INSCRIPTIONS OF THE STELOGRAPHY
7 It appears, then, that he posits a twofold form of pillars; for he
would not, while receiving that of Jacob with praise, have censured those
of the adversaries as set up impiously; but those portray demon-like
phantasms of idolatry in a heterodox manner; while that of the
forefather Jacob does not display the same business as those. For the
righteous man, having been sounded through by the Holy Spirit, first
sees the very bodily form of Christ being pre-depicted clearly, which is
visible and bound to local circumscription; and second he also recounts
the victory-bearing trophy of the cross, represented in a manner like a
ladder. For on this he was also set up on high, having drawn all to
himself. It is possible, then, for those who consider it to see how by
name some at times call the very passions of the temptations
ladder-rungs, hinting at precipice-girt ascents and descents, while in
shape the ladder somewhat resembles the cross, being fitted together
with upright and transverse woods. When therefore he had beheld such a
vision, taking up the stone on which, having laid his head, he rested,
he set it up as a pillar, a memorial of the history, characterizing an
image of the face that appeared to him, in which he established a
renewal of memory, and calling it a house of God he hinted at the temple
of the Word of God, as the Son of the Almighty himself also made
manifest: "Destroy this temple and in three days I will raise it up."
And already he also named the same a gate of heaven, since through the
temple-work of Christ, being purified in body and soul, we are able to
approach the divine nature. Therefore neither did he pour the oil idly
upon the top of the pillar; but this was indicative of that which Mary
earnestly performed, pouring myrrh from the extremities upon the feet of
Christ; and it was again that same announcement of the burial, as the
Lord himself made clear openly; and this stone images that cornerstone
which the builders rejected, but which became the head of the corner.
8 The divine Scriptures declare two nations and two peoples. And Moses
is witness of this; at any rate in the cosmogony, narrating the life of
Rebecca, he says how she went to inquire of the Lord concerning the
things she had conceived. And at that time he says: the Lord said to
her: "Two nations are in your womb, and two peoples shall be separated
from your belly, and a people shall excel a people, and the greater
shall serve the lesser." For there are many nations and many peoples,
but they name these two especially as peoples, who were counted worthy
to be entrusted with the doctrine of piety, (and he mentions neither
schisms, nor heresies, nor numbered ones) but there are two peoples
extended in many places of the earth, of Christians and of Jews. And the
one who boasts of being greater in times, falling short in virtue, shall
serve the younger; for "a fool shall be servant to a wise man," he said.
9 From this point, then, he prefigured him sitting upon the throne of
the holy one, showing that he has been manifested as co-throned with the
most divine Spirit because of the God who dwells in him continually.
