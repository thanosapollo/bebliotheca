FROM THE INTERPRETATION OF SAINT CYRIL ON THE BOOKS OF THE KINGS
BOOK 1.
=69.680=
And Samuel took one milk-lamb. Of Cyril, archbishop of Alexandria. Do you
see how he made Christ both mediator and at the same time steward of
salvation? And how the mystery upon him, like an unassailable weapon, set
against the inroads of the foreigners, has saved Israel? For he sacrificed
a milk-lamb as a type of Christ, and long ago, while crying through the
voice of the prophets, I was as a guileless lamb led to be sacrificed, I
knew it not.
He watches, moreover, how the power of the sacrifice has rendered the
Father more ready for the aid of those who toil. For he thundered
something great and extraordinary against those in opposition; and to
those offering the sacrifice he gave salvation as a reward, as a type of
the mystery that is in Christ. And now appoint a king over us.
{Of Cyril.} Israel had been weaned from being ruled by God through the
prophets; and he called a man to be king. On this account he also lost the
=69.681= commandment; for first Solomon worshipped the Baals; then after
him Jeroboam set up the heifers. And the Lord said to Samuel: Hearken to
their voice, and so on. {Of Cyril.} When their affairs were going well for
them, and they were in most excellent order, the wretched ones conceived
bitter thoughts against themselves. Shaking off, as it were, the yoke of
the kingship under God, they came to the blessed Samuel, saying: Appoint a
king over us to judge us, and what follows;
Then, when the blessed Samuel proclaimed to them the rights of the king,
and alarmed them exceedingly with many terrors, while those so unsound and
impious contrivances were withdrawing very quickly, they none the less
kept urging, saying: No; but there shall be a king over us. And Saul said
to his servant, and so on. And what shall we carry? and so on. {Of Cyril,
from that on Matthew.} It has always sprung up with those who have been
benefited, that is, with those who have received from anyone the sweetest
things, to wish to render rewards according to their power, and to delight
them with honors equal, or even yet greater; and as proof of gratitude the
earnestness which those so disposed continually cultivate in these things,
in whom a good and grateful disposition may reside. And there was there
one of the servants of Saul. And his name was Doeg. {Of Cyril.} There was
a certain man, an Edomite by race, whose name was Doeg. This man was in
attendance among the guards of the blessed David, and was a herdsman; only
a man bitter and barbarous, and nowhere possessing what is genuine. He
chanced, moreover, secretly to be with Saul, and reported to him David's
movements and all his plans; and he promised at times even to betray him.
And when at some time he saw the blessed David enter the house of God,
when he ate the showbreads, and having chanced upon Abimelech the priest,
he reported to Saul that he was altogether with few men, and that, coming
upon him, he could easily take him and subdue him without trouble. Doeg,
then, betrayed him by reporting to Saul that he had chanced upon Abimelech
in the house of God; but the Master of all was preserving the prophet, and
made the attempt of his malice vain.

BOOK 2.


And it was said to David: You shall not enter here, because the blind and
the lame resisted. {Of Cyril.} Now against the blessed David the blind and
the lame from Jerusalem, that is from Jebus, withstood him; and doing
this, they miserably perished; for he took it. Against Christ the Savior
of all, in the time of his sojourn, resisted the lame and blind
inhabitants of Jerusalem; for they did not know to walk straight, nor did
those not receiving faith admit the divine light to their mind, the
Scribes and especially the Pharisees. And David sent messengers, and took
her, and entered in to her. {Of Cyril.} Do not be readily disturbed
concerning the saints, if at times they too have been in slips; for it is
true that even the most valiant of soldiers, those admired for courage of
body and skill, are at times struck while fighting, only they do not fall
headlong. Otherwise, for I suppose one must add this also to what has been
said: It has been reserved for the philanthropic Savior Christ alone not
to suffer sin; for, being God by nature, he became flesh.
For this reason he also said of himself: The ruler of this world comes,
and in me he will find nothing; and a little after. And I say these things
concerning the sin of David, when he went in to Bathsheba, the wife of
Uriah. But the saying belongs to all in the history; for if he grew weak
as a man, and is found to have suffered something of the unexpected, yet
he showed that the stumbles of the saints have a swift correction. And
David said to Nathan: I have sinned against the Lord. {Of Cyril, from that
on the twentieth psalm.} Here, to my mind, admire the blessed David; ...,
he mourned over the sin. And the son born to you shall surely die.
{Of Cyril, from the commentary on the Psalter, on the verse set before
us.} O Lord, rebuke me not in your wrath. And yet, having heard these
things from the prophet, ... rather than to be pardoned. And he took the
crown of Malchom from the king. {Of Cyril.} He arrayed himself for battle
against the sons of Ammon, and those who had been banded together with
them in their madness, those, I mean, from the race of the Syrians; and he
conquered, and took them by main force; and taking the crown of Malchom,
then finding in it a stone exceedingly great and precious, he set it upon
his own crown. And David went up weeping, and his head covered.
{Of Cyril.} Since before the war with =69.685= Absalom the sin had
happened to the blessed David, the one against Uriah, I mean, there was a
tale throughout, so to speak, all the country of the Jews, and some,
entertaining not implausibly a suspicion about him; for they thought he
was paying the penalties for his transgressions against Uriah; and having
come into collision with God, then surely to be easily taken by those
willing, and everywhere ailing in weakness. Scatter then the counsel of
Ahithophel. {Of Cyril.} Scatter, he says, the counsel of Ahithophel, O
Lord my God. For Ahithophel was deep and bitter and full of counsel, and
able to find ways in every matter, and, besides this, most strategic.
David therefore feared Ahithophel most of all others, because he brought
in always bitter counsels, and knew how to wage war. And behold, Hushai
met him. {Of Cyril.} Hushai being one of his own friends, he persuades him
to pretend defection from him, and to wish to be attached to Absalom, that
he might war against the counsels of Ahithophel without his knowing it;
which indeed came to pass. And thus spoke Shimei when he cursed him.
{Of Cyril.} Having insulted him and all but pelted him with bitter voices,
he went away, having suffered nothing from him at that time; even if
afterward he paid out hard and fitting penalties; only that David
endured, and, with many restraining him, counted silence worth the whole,
from very great and admirable prudence.


For he perceived that, because of his striking against God, as it were, he
had been given over to scourges, and he reckoned the revilings and jests
and laughter from certain men to be his portion of the blow. For where
there is turning away of God and abandonment, even if it be only moderate
from someone, there he who is accustomed to forsake will surely forsake
with many and bitter things. That the Lord bade him curse David. {Of
Cyril.} He makes mention of this in the thirty-eighth psalm; he says, I
gave a reproach to the foolish. And Ahithophel said to Absalom, and so on.
{Of Cyril.} Absalom wished to pursue his father as he fled; and that this
must be done, Ahithophel advised with youthful spirit. But Hushai, by
persuasion withdrawing him from the savagery against his father, urged him
not to run straight at this thing simply, but rather to prepare beforehand
a sufficient force, and to gather a multitude of soldiers, since the
blessed David was able, versed in war, and most warlike of all. And the
woman took and spread the covering, and so on. {Of Cyril.} Therefore, even
if at times good things come to pass through men, let us not give thanks
to them rather, =69.688= but to God, who granted the sweet things through
them. And Ahithophel saw that his counsel was not made good. {Of Cyril.}
The counsel of Hushai had prevailed; and Ahithophel, being exceedingly
grieved at this, ended his life by a noose, himself passing a just
sentence upon himself. And he wept, and thus he said, and so on. {Of
Cyril.} Absalom had made war upon the blessed David, and unexpectedly the
young man had raised the weapons of enmity against him who begat him; and
he was taken and perished. Only the father did not exult over him who had
suffered death, but mourned him as a son.

BOOK 3.
And king David was old, advanced in days. {Of Cyril.} Having endured
unwaged contests, and having been magnified over his enemies, and having
become untouchable by those plotting against him. For he had conquered not
foreigners only, nor put to flight the phalanxes of bloodthirsty
barbarians, but also those rising against him at a season, and those
banding together with those who rose against him. And you shall give to
your servant a heart to hear and to judge your people in righteousness.
{Of Cyril.} For those willing to serve God must be wise and knowing the
things that profit, with mind awakened toward those who use them, and
willing to escape the surge of the present life. For as ships, when they
have rudders, are borne straight to the mark, and escape the assaults of
the waves, even if the sea at times rages upon them; but when they have
none, they are tossed hither and thither easily, and are made drunk with
wanderings, and, dashing upon rocks and stones, are broken; so also the
soul of man, when governed by good counsels, will easily escape Satan the
winter-storm; but thus it renders ineffective also the wicked devices of
those plotting against it. But a heart not having wisdom endures hard the
shipwreck.
And it was pleasing before the Lord, and so on. {Of Cyril.} And it was
pleasing, he says, before the Lord that Solomon asked this word; he was
praised not a little, and was counted worthy of the gift. For the Master
of all, being rich in gifts, inclined to him; for God readily bows to
those asking well. For wisdom and understanding are truly an exceptional
gift from God; with gifts so resplendent even the nature of the angels is
brightened, and the blessed multitude of the spirits above and in the
heavens. For what other possession would come to the soul besides this?
Gold and silver? But this is nothing to us, for they are both transitory
and perishing and


altogether worthless to cast away, and of no power to profit the =69.689=
soul of man. A possession, then, precious and truly lovable for the soul
of man is wisdom and knowledge, that concerning God, of course. And what
you did not ask I have given you, and riches, and so on. {Of Cyril.}
Teaching us what we must ask for, and what to receive as in the manner of
addition; for to him who asked well for wisdom, after giving it, he has
added all the other things. Since he himself is he who said in the
Gospels: Seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness, and all
these things shall be added to you. That your eyes may be open upon this
house. {Of Cyril.} And let your eyes, he says, and your ears be open. But
if the divine Scripture, speaking to us concerning God, makes mention of
parts, or members, it must be known that it speaks to us from what we know
and are by nature; for otherwise we could not understand the things
concerning God. The cause, then, and truly the pretext of the
divinely-inspired Scripture making with us bodily discourses concerning
God is the poverty of mind and tongue in us; for the things concerning him
are altogether unspeakable. And it was not possible for those in tangible
and gross bodies to comprehend any of the things that must be, unless,
receiving their own members by way of examples, they should thus with
difficulty rise to the slender conceptions concerning God. And the queen
of Sheba, and so on. Of Cyril, from that on Luke. A barbarian woman, with
long distances barring her, became superior to hesitation, not that she
might make a collection of riches, but that she might hear the wisdom of
Solomon, speaking parables and riddles, and sketching the beauties and
faults of human characters. Then Solomon built a high place for Chemosh
the idol of Moab. {Of Cyril.} And what could be more grievous of such
out-of-place transgressions than to assign the honor and love due to the
one and by-nature God to herds of demons, or rather to things moulded of
stones and woods, and to build temples for these? And the Lord was angry
with Solomon, and so on. {Of Cyril.} But what was the command given to him
and to all the men under the law, not to intermarry with those of another
race? For he said, You shall not give your daughter to his son, nor shall
you take his daughter for your son; for she will turn aside your son from
me, and he will go and serve other gods. Forgetting, then, such a
commandment, he interwove himself with the daughters of the foreigners.
Since, then, you have made no account of the commandment given, he says,
and, sliding into apostasy, have worshipped the gods of the foreigners,
and, knowing that the creation is my handmaid, being =69.692=, have
apportioned to it the honor and glory that are mine and alone most
befitting, and I will rend your kingdom, and give it to your servant, that
you may learn what you have done from the thing itself that has befallen.
For since, overcome by lusts toward women, Solomon, though exceedingly
wise, all but, and as much as one could say, rent the kingdom of God,
assigning to idols the honor and glory most fitting to him alone; on this
account God most fittingly threatened to rend his kingdom, requiting him
in kind, according to what is written in the prophet Ezekiel: As you have
done, so shall it be to you; your requital shall be requited to you upon
your own head. And the king took counsel and went and made two heifers.9
{Of Cyril} A cause of ruin became to those of Israel Jeroboam, showing
that the kingdom of men was in turn to be destroyed, and to go forth from
being ruled by God through the prophets. For behold, Jeroboam provoked me
the Master, having invented heifers, and put upon them my dignity. And
again: Jeroboam devised in the beginnings the heifers, and commanded to
fulfill upon them the things prescribed by law, the wretched one putting
upon his own inventions the glory proper to God; that he might hold the
kingdom safely himself, but might rob God, as it were, of his own


God [cod. God]. And a vineyard was to Naboth. {Of Cyril.} Isaiah sets
before us the understanding of the vineyard as Israel. But those of his
rulers, of whom Ahab bears the type, desired to have it, but could not
persuade him to betray to them his father's exceptional vineyard, so as to
convert it into a garden of vegetables, teaching the commandments of men
as teachings, and watering it with a muddy subversion. Then what did the
God-hated Jezebel, that is, the Synagogue of the Jews, do, running to the
aid of the griefs of the rulers? By deceit she sends against righteous
Naboth, that is, him who was to come. For Emmanuel has been slain, being
falsely accused. God of hills, God of Israel. {Of Cyril.} For they
supposed that those of Israel had conquered, God being able to save in the
hills only, or also on the mounds; we are defeated, then, he says, because
God of the hills is God of Israel; but if we may come to battle in the
level plains, we shall certainly prevail, the God of Israel weakening in
the valleys. But these are indictments of Hellenic madness, and a terrible
foolhardiness of those who do not know the true and by-nature God.
Therefore the all-powerful God was indignant against the foreigners, and
most rightly, because, =69.693= winning, as I said, against Israel, they
hung the thank-offerings upon their own gods, and in their folly thought
that they had prevailed even over God himself. And he finds another man,
and said: Strike me. {Of Cyril.} Do you hear how he who entreated the
prophet to strike him was pitiably slain, having fallen in with a terrible
beast? And yet, how is it not true to say that it was a fruit of reverence
not to dare to trample the holy and prophet? But disobedience against the
word of the Lord has become a transgression not easy to escape. Thus it is
a transgression not easy to escape to do anything contrary to what seems
good to God, or not to do without delay the things commanded by him. It is
necessary, then, that we finish without shrinking and delay whatever would
please God. But to do anything besides this, or to choose to think
otherwise, carries the transgression of contempt; for such men have
decided henceforth not only this, but even to censure, as though it were
not right at times that God should have taken counsel.

BOOK 4.
And now you have taken the money, and so on. {Of Cyril.} This also Christ
says in the holy Gospels: Carry neither purse nor scri nor sandals, and
salute no one by the way. For one must go to the work with straight aim,
and not even to bless in return to one blessing; for the turning aside
toward others is a loss to earnestness, and to spend idly the season
proper to preachings on things not necessary, nor to gratify friendships
with unprofitable delay.

And they inherited Samaria. {Of Cyril.} These, then, having received the
lot, dwelt in the land of the Samaritans, and perhaps became fathers of
children; and they inclined to the customs of the Jews, having been
terrified by the attacks of the lions. From them have come Sarasar and
Arbeseser, who was also named king, for by him the colonists in Samaria
were at that time governed


from among the Persians. Hezekiah reigned, son of Ahaz king of Judah.
{Of Cyril.} He had advanced in glory and at the same time in strength to
this, as to subdue the nations dwelling near Judah, and indeed to impose
many tributes. And a little after. Then he suffered something of human
things; for from exceeding luxury, and being vanquished by the superiority
of his renown, he somehow conceived high thoughts, and, mastered by the
disease of contempt, rose up even against the divine law itself. For he
supposed that he ought to crown himself also with the glories of the
divine priesthood. For he attempted to enter the divine temple, and
himself by himself to burn incense to =69.696= God, and to perform
lawfully the things prescribed. For this cause he was straightway stricken
with leprosy, and was most dishonored; for unclean according to the law is
he who is disfigured by the affection of leprosy. Where is the God of
Hamath? and so on. That they have taken Samaria out of my hand? {Of
Cyril.} For the barbarian perhaps supposed, having arrogance together with
his tongue, that the master of earth and heaven, and maker and Lord of
all, is one of the falsely-named gods; for such is the custom of those who
know not God: at times to cut in pieces the hope of the saints, and to
dare to say that they shall not be delivered by God. But he did not know
that the falsely-named gods were wrought by the hands of men; while he who
is by nature and truly was made by no one, but rather he himself brought
into real existence the things that once were not. Samaria, then, was
taken, God withdrawing his aid. And, as I said before, even if none of the
gods said of among the Greeks saved his own, this is nothing toward God,
who truly and by nature is and is great. For those obtained by human craft
even merely to be seen; but he holds absolute sway over all, being Lord of
the powers. He rent his garments. {Of Cyril.} Do you see how, having
neglected his own affairs, he is pained for God, and seeks vengeance? He
rent, he says, his garments, and put on sackcloth, and entered into the
house of the Lord

what was the pretext for rending the garments? It was the custom of the
Jews upon blasphemies against God to do this; which also is related in the
Gospels to have been done by Caiaphas. I have cut down the height of his
cedar. {Of Cyril.} Of him, of whom? Of Lebanon, that is, of Judah. By the
height of the cedar he means their kingdom. For the glories of the kingdom
of Hezekiah were destined to be lifted to a height. And I will put, he
says, my hook in your nostrils, and my bridle in your lips. {Of Cyril.} To
the end that he not dare to utter such things. Yet even if he did not
perish with the multitude for the reason aforesaid, for which Pharaoh also
was kept as a spectator while the rest of the Egyptians were punished,
still, having returned to his own land, he found his own sons his
murderers. And I will defend this city for my own sake. {Of Cyril.} For
this befits God as he is. Therefore I will do these things for my own
sake, but not because those helped are worthy. And the angel of the Lord
went out, and smote in the camp of the Assyrians 185 thousand. {Of
Cyril.} And the war is dissolved, while the threats flee away with the
dangers; so that henceforth the inhabitants of Jerusalem, greatly
rejoicing at the aid from God, say: You have turned my mourning into joy
for me. Behold the reversal of the things; consider exactly what he says:
You have turned my mourning into joy for me. Great was the mourning
because of the multitude of those warring; great has the joy become
because of the multitude of those fallen.
