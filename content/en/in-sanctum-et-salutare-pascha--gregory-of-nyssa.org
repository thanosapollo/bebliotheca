OF GREGORY BISHOP OF NYSSA ON THE HOLY AND SAVING PASCHA
The true sabbath-rest that received the blessing of God, in which the Lord
rested from his own works for the salvation of the world, having sabbatized
in the inactivity of death, already has an end and has shown its own grace
both to eyes and to ears and to heart through all these things of the feast
having been completed in us, by what we saw, by what we heard, by what we
received the gladness in the heart. For the thing seen by the eyes was light,
by the cloud of fire through the lamps being torch-led for us in the night,
and the word sounding all night to the ears in psalms and hymns and spiritual
odes, as some stream of joy flowing in through hearing into the soul, made us
full of the good hopes, and the heart, being made bright by the things said
and seen, was stamped with the unspeakable blessed ness, being led by the
hand through the things appearing toward the invisible, so that of those
goods, Which eye has not seen nor ear heard nor has it gone up upon a human
heart, the goods of this rest are an image, confirming through themselves the
unutterable hope of the things laid up. Since therefore this luminous night,
having mixed the gleams from the lamps with the early-morning rays of the
sun, made one continuous day not divided by the insertion of darkness,
=9.310= let us understand, brothers, the prophecy that says that This is the
day which the Lord has made, in which the work is not something heavy and
hard to accomplish, but joy and gladness and exultation, the word having said
thus that Let us exult and be glad in it. O fair commands, O sweet
legislation. Who postpones toward obeying such commands? And who does not
hold even the short delay of the commands a loss? Gladness is the work and
exultation is the injunction, through which the condemnation upon sin is
dissolved and the grievous things are remade into joy. This is the saying of
wisdom, that in a day of gladness there is amnesty of evils. This day has
made a forgetting of the first sentence against us, or rather an
obliteration, not a forgetting; for it has wiped out altogether every
memorial of the condemnation against us. Then the birth was in pains, now the
generation is without travail; then we were born flesh from flesh, now that
which is born is spirit from spirit; then sons of men, now we have been born
children of God; then we were sent away from heavens to earth, now the
heavenly one has made us also heavenly; then through sin death reigned, now
righteousness takes the power in exchange through life; one then opened the
entrance of death, through one also now life is brought in instead; then
through death we fell out of life, now death is destroyed by life; then under
shame we hid ourselves by the fig-tree, now through glory we draw near to the
tree of life; then through disobedience we were banished from paradise, now
through faith we come inside paradise. Again the fruit of life is set forth
for delight according to authority for us; again the fountain of paradise,
being divided fourfold through the evangelical rivers =9.311= waters the
whole face of the church, so that the furrows of our souls, which the sower
of the word cut with the plough of teaching, even become drunk, and the
offspring of virtue multiply.
What then still is it fitting for such people to do? What else than to
imitate the prophetic mountains and the hills with skippings. For the
mountains, he says, skipped like rams and the hills as lambs of sheep.
Therefore come, let us exult in the Lord who has taken down the power of the
enemy and has set up the great trophy of the cross on our behalf in the fall
of the adversary. Let us shout, and a shout is a victory-praise raised by
those who have prevailed against those who have been defeated. Since
therefore the battle-line of the enemy has fallen and that one himself who
had the power of the wicked army of demons is gone and has been made to
vanish and has already passed over into non-being, let us say that Great is
the Lord God and a Great King over all the earth, Who Has Blessed the crown
of the year of his goodness and has gathered us into this spiritual
choir-standing, in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory unto the ages,
amen.
