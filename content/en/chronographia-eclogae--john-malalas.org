An extract from the Chronicles of John the Historian, from Adam to the
reign of Caesar.

The first man from the earth, Adam, was six feet in measure of stature,
together with his own head, so that his stature was ninety-six digits.
His span was fourteen digits. His cubit was twenty-four digits. His foot
was sixteen digits. He lived nine hundred and thirty years. And his wife
Eve bore three sons, Cain, Abel, and Seth, and two daughters, Azura and
Asuam. And Abel was a shepherd of sheep, and Cain tilled the earth, and
each of them offered a sacrifice to God. And God looked upon Abel and
upon his gifts, but upon Cain he did not look; and Cain was grieved and
killed Abel. 232 And the Lord was angry with Cain and said to him,
"Groaning and trembling you shall be upon the earth."

Then Adam, at the command of God, set names for all the four-footed
beasts and the creeping things and the fish and the amphibious
creatures, and for his own children. For his own name and the name of
his wife, an angel of the Lord told him. And his son Seth set in order
the names of the seven stars, having received wisdom from God, and at
the command of God he set names for all the stars, and for the five
wanderers, that they might be known by men; and he called the first
wandering star Cronus, the second Zeus, the third Ares, the fourth
Aphrodite, the fifth Hermes; and the seven vowels from the five stars
and the two luminaries he set forth; and he set forth also Hebrew
letters, for he first discovered them and wrote them down. But the names
of the two luminaries God himself called, sun and moon. These things the
most wise Fortinus the Roman chronicler wrote down.

Then Seth took to wife Asuam his own sister, and begat children, and
there was a very great race, and the earth was filled with men and
women; and Cain took to wife Azura his own sister, and there was from
him a great race. In the midst of these times Enoch the righteous, son
of Jared, was born, and he did not die. But after Adam, after two
thousand and six years, Enoch was translated, being seventh from Adam.
And when the sons of God saw the daughters of men, that they were fair,
they took them as wives and begat sons, and there were giants on the
earth in those days, men of renown from of old. And it came to pass that
from Adam until the sons of God lusted after the daughters of men were
two thousand one hundred and twenty-two years. Plutarchos says that a
sphere of fire was sent down by God into the Celtic country, and burned
up the giants, and the sphere, being carried into the river Cerdanos,
was quenched. This fire the Greeks relate, and say that Phaethon, the
son of the sun, fell from the chariot of the sun to the earth. And the
other giants, seeing those of their own struck on the head, 233 remained
disobedient; and God, being angry, said to them, "My spirit shall not at
all remain," and what follows, as is contained in the Mosaic writings.

And there was a man beloved of God, Noah by name, and at the command of
God he made an ark, and entered in himself and all his sons, and of the
beasts and creeping things and birds. And the flood came for forty days,
and all flesh having the breath of life died. And there were from Adam
to the flood, in the year 2882, ten generations. But after the flood was
stayed, the ark was found on the mountains of Ararat of Pisidia, and its
timbers are in it to this day.

And the race of Noah begat children, and there was a multitude of men
and women, and they made tower-building, who also, knowing the
construction of the ark, first made ships. And from the flood to the
tower-building were years ... and in the midst of the time after the
flood, Arphaxad wrote down astronomy, having found the naming of the
stars of Seth the son of Adam and of his own children, written on a
stone tablet. For the descendants of Seth, being godly and having
foreknown the coming destruction of men, whether alternation, made two
pillars, one of stone and one of brick, and wrote on them all the
heavenly things set forth by Seth their grandfather; reckoning that if
the alternation should come by water, the stone pillar would remain, and
the things written on it, and if by fire, the brick one, and the things
written on it would be known to the men after these things; and a pillar
was found after the flood on the mountain of Seridos, and it is until
now. And thereafter the race of Arphaxad remained until the
tower-building; and Arphaxad lived four hundred and thirty-eight years.

There are therefore from Adam to the tower-building two thousand nine
hundred and twenty-two years; and thereafter those who were building the
tower were seventy nations, who were divided into tongues over the face
of all the earth; and Chus the Ethiopian, of the tribe of Sem, begat
Nebrod the giant, 234 who built Babylon, whom the Persians say, having
been deified and become among the stars of heaven, they call Orion; for
he first showed hunting, and supplied them with beasts for food, whence
he had the primacy.

But in the times above, from the race of Arphaxad there sprang up a
certain wise man of India, an astronomer, Gandoubarios by name, who
first wrote down astronomy for the Indians; and from the same tribe of
Sem, which held Assyria and Persis and the parts of the east, there
appeared a man, a giant by race, Cronus by name, surnamed Domnos by his
own father, after the name of the wandering star Cronus. And he became
powerful, who first showed how to reign and rule and hold sway over the
multitude of men. And he himself reigned over the Assyrians many years,
and subjected all the land of Persis; and he was terrible to all, and
had to wife Semiramis, who was also called Raia among the Assyrians,
because she was boastful and proud. She also was of the tribe of Sem,
the son of Noah. And Cronus had a son Picus, who was called Zeus by his
own father, he too after the name of the wandering star; and the same
Cronus had also another son, Ninus by name, and a daughter, Hera by
name. And Picus, who is also Zeus, took to wife Hera his own sister,
from whom he had a son named Sembelos. But Cronus the forefather,
leaving his own son Picus to reign over the Assyrians, with his own wife
Raia, who is also Semiramis, himself taking the help of brave men, went
down to the West, which was held by no one, and reigned over the western
parts, having become unseen out of Assyria, and he reigned over the
western parts many years. And he had there also a wife, Phyllara by
name, from whom he had a son, Aphros by name, to whom he gave the land
toward Libya to reign over. And Aphros took Astymone and begat a
daughter, whom he called Aphrodite, after the name of the wandering
star. And Cronus had by Phyllara also another son, the philosopher
called Cheiron. And Picus, who is also Zeus, having reigned over the
Assyrians thirty years, 235 leaving also his own mother Raia, who is
also Semiramis, and his own sister and wife Hera, and having made his
own son Belos king of the Assyrians, went down to his own father Cronus.
And Belos reigned over the Assyrians two years and died; whom the
Persians deified.

And Cronus, when his own son Picus came to him to the West, granted him
the kingdom of the West. For he had become weak; and Picus reigned over
the West, who is also Zeus, that is, over Italy, sixty-two years. But
over Assyria after Belos reigned Ninus, the other son of Cronus, who
also took his own mother Raia, who is also Semiramis, to wife, from
which source only the Persians marry their own mothers and sisters,
because Picus also took Hera his own sister. And Ninus, having become
master, built the city Nineveh of the Assyrians, having first reigned in
it, having his own mother as wife. From his race was born Oiozoras the
astronomer, famous among the Persians; who, being about to die, asked to
be consumed by heavenly fire, saying to the Persians, "that if you burn
me with fire, guard what is of my burned bones, and the kingdom shall
not fail from you." And having called upon Orion, he was slain by fire,
and the Persians keep his remains, burned to ashes, until now.

But after Ninus reigned over the Assyrians one named Thyras, whom his
father Zames, the brother of Hera, renamed after the name of the
wandering star Ares. This one became a bitter warrior, who attacked a
certain powerful man of Caukasos, himself also powerful, descended from
Japheth, in the northern parts; whom he murdered, and took his country,
and coming into Thrace, dying there he lies. For him the Egyptians and
Assyrians set up a pillar called Arian, and worshipped him as God, and
to this day they call him Baal, a god, which is interpreted, Ares, god
of war. Daniel the prophet mentions him also. But after the death of
Ares reigned Elames over the Assyrians, 236 and after Lamos, Sardanapalos
the Great, whom Perseus the son of Danae murdered and took the kingdom
from the Assyrians; and having reigned over them, they called them
Persians after his own name. And this Perseus was the son of Picus, who
is also Zeus, and from him he learned magic and the rites of the
abominable cup, whence also, seizing a virgin maiden, he cut off her
head, and having perfected it, he had it for the destruction of all that
resisted him, which he called Gorgo, because it brought sudden death
upon those who saw it.

And the brother of Ninus, Picus, who is also Zeus, reigned over Italy
one hundred and twenty years, holding the West; and he had sons and many
daughters from the most beautiful women, making also certain mystical
apparitions and secretly corrupting them, which women held him as a god,
being corrupted by him. And the same Picus, who is also Zeus, had a son
named Faunus, whom he also called Hermes, after the name of the
wandering star. And when Zeus was about to die, he ordered his remains
to be placed in the island of Crete; and his children built a temple for
him and laid him there, which is a tomb until this day, on which is
inscribed, "Here lies Picus, who is also Zeus, whom they also call
Dias," about which Diodoros the most wise chronicler wrote. But after
the death of Picus, who is also Zeus, reigned Faunus his son, who is
also Hermes, over Italy thirty-five years, a crafty man and learned, who
first discovered the gold mine in the West and the smelting of it; and
knowing the plot of his brothers against him (for they were about
seventy, whom his father Picus, who is also Zeus, had by various women),
he departed, carrying much gold in exceeding abundance, and went to
Egypt, to the tribe of Cham; and they received him with much honor,
wearing a golden robe and proud above all. For he was also a physiologos
and philosophized among the Egyptians, saying to them also certain
oracular things, and they worshipped him, saying that he was the god
Hermes, as foretelling to them the things to come; and he provided them
also with wealth, wherefore they called him also the giver of riches.

But when Stremouninos, king of the Egyptians, of the race of Cham, died,
the Egyptians made Hermes king, 237 and he reigned over them thirty-nine
years in much pride. And after him reigned Hephaistos, who was a warrior
and an oracle-giver. They called him the god Hephaistos; who, going out
to war, fell in with his horse, and being struck on the foot remained
lame. And he received, as they say, from an oracle, that is, from his
own mind, from the star, quickness in making weapons of iron; whence he
became master in wars, whom they also deified. For before this they used
clubs and stones, and made war.

But after Hephaistos reigned Helios his son. And after him Sesostris,
and after him Osiris, and after Osiris, Horos; and after him Thoules,
who, having done many achievements, went to the oracle to ask, saying,
"Prophesy to us, O fire-strong, unerring, blessed, aetherial pole: in
the changing of my first kingdom I was able to subject all things, or
who overcame me?" And there was given him this oracle: "First god, then
logos, and spirit with them; and these all are of one birth, and one,
and going into one, whose might is eternal; but walk with swift feet,
mortal, passing through an uncertain life."

And the names of the seven wandering stars are these: for the one called
Cronus is called Lampon, and Zeus, Phaethon, and Ares, Pyroes, and
Aphrodite, Kallistos, and Hermes, Ostemon. And these things concerning
the seven wandering stars, whose names Seth set forth, having received
wisdom from God. But men, naming their children after their names, were
proclaimed falsely-named gods. And after many kings of the Egyptians
reigned Sosoris, who subjected the Assyrians and Chaldaeans and Persians
as far as Babylon, and all Asia and Europe and Scythia and Mysia. And on
his return he chose fifteen thousand brave Scythian men and sent them to
dwell in Persis, and the same Scythians remained in Persis until now,
who were changed by the Persians into Parthians; who have the dress and
the speech and the customs of Scythians. 238 And in the time of this
Sosoris, king of Egypt, was Hermes Trismegistos, who declared that the
name of the ineffable and maker was three greatest powers, but one
divinity, he said; whence he was called by the Egyptians
thrice-greatest. For it is contained in various discourses of his,
having spoken to Asklepios concerning the nature of God, these things:
"If there were not some providence of the Lord of all to reveal to me
this discourse, neither would such love have held you to seek concerning
this. For it is not possible for the uninitiated to partake of such
mysteries, but listen with the mind. There was one only light, mind, and
there was ever mind of mind, light, and nothing else was; its unity was
ever in itself, being ever with its own mind and light and spirit, it
provides all things. Outside of this, no god, no angel, no daimon, no
other substance whatsoever." For of all he is lord and father and god of
all, by them and in them; for his logos, having come forth, being
all-perfect and maker, falling in fertile nature upon fertile water,
made the water pregnant; and having said these things he began to say:
"I adjure you, heaven, wise work of great God, be gracious; I adjure
you, voice of the father, which he first uttered to his own only-begotten
logos, when he established the whole world, be gracious, Lord."

That Libya was the daughter of Io and Zeus; and Io was the daughter of
Inachos, king of the West, of the tribe of Japheth, whom Picus, who is
also Zeus, ravished; she was married to a certain Poseidon, from whom
Agenor was born; and Agenor coming to Phoenicia took to wife one named
Tyros, and builds a city after the name of his wife, which they called
Tyros, and reigned there. And he had from Tyros a son, Kadmos the
Phoenician, and Syros, and Kilix. And Agenor, dying, divided all the
land he had subjected among his three sons, and Phoenix took Tyros and
its region, calling the land under him Phoenicia after his own name; and
likewise Syros called the land that fell to his lot Syria after his own
name; and Kelix called the district that fell to his lot Kilikia.

239 That Kadmos the son of Agenor summoned from his exile Tiresias the
philosopher and beast-tamer, concerning whom Sophokles says, that
Tiresias saw Pallas bathing and became a woman; and he says that he
sought to know the wisdom of God and was not able; thus then Sophokles
wrote in his writings these things: "That one is of the truth; there is
a God who fashioned heaven and the broad earth, and the flashing wave of
the sea and the violence of the winds; but many mortals, their heart
wandering, have founded for ourselves solaces of evils, images of gods
from stones and wood, wrought with gold, elephantine figures, and making
sacrifices to these and common festivals, we think that we are pious."
For Sophokles seemed to believe in monarchy. And in the times of his son
Phoenix was Herakles the philosopher Syros, who discovered the shell.
For walking about the seaside part of the city of Tyros, he saw a
shepherd's dog eating the shell called kokhyle, and the shepherd, taking
a tuft of wool from the sheep and thinking that the blood flowing from
the dog's mouth stained it, dipped the tuft, thinking that the dog bled.
And Herakles, perceiving that it was not blood, but the virtue of a
strange dye, marveled, and taking the tuft from the shepherd, and
knowing that the blood flowing was from the shell, brought it as a very
great gift to Phoenix, king of Tyros; who, marveling, ordered wool to be
dyed from the dye of the shell, and it to become a royal robe.

For they performed sacrifice and feast to them, saying that their souls
were in the islands of the blessed and were not judged. Now Tharras, the
father of Abraham, was one of the other makers, making gods and selling
them, and introducing the error of images and of idolatry among men
through the images of their ancestors; especially those who found the
writings and the arts, to whom eagerly ran the Egyptians and the
Babylonians and the Phrygians and those from Hellas. For of this
religion there were image-makers and interpreters of mysteries 240 and
initiators, through whom especially this religion was brought to the
Greeks, and from a certain Greek by name, son also of Picus, who is also
Zeus, doing certain mystical things; who, a Greek, taught those dwelling
in Greece, of the tribe of Japheth, the son of Noah; whom Plutarchos of
Chaeroneia, blaming with the old philosophy praised among Greeks and
barbarians, set forth, that some introduce the error of images; but he
himself says that they seemed to deify the lights in heaven, sun and
moon, introducing them as nourishing and increasing all things by the
third partial motion of the five planets and of the rest of the
star-host, and governing the things of generation and of the air. But
Abraham, recognizing the true knowledge of God, and reckoning that the
images which his father made were of dead men, and that they ought not
to be honored as being gods in heaven, but earth and dust, condemned his
own father Tharras, saying, "Why do you lead men astray for gain? For
there is no other god but him who is in the heavens, who made all these
visible things." And he departed to Mesopotamia.

There are from Adam to Abraham three thousand four hundred and
twenty-three years. And in the times of Abraham was also Melchisedek, a
godly man, descended from the race of Sidus, the son of Aigyptos, king
of the land of Libya, from whom also the Egyptians are called; who,
coming down, took the land of the so-called Canaanites, the now-called
Palestine; and having subjected it, he dwelt there, and builds a city
which he called Sidon after his own name. And thereafter from the race
of Sidus, the son of Aigyptos, king of the land of Libya, came down
Melchisedek; who, becoming priest and king of the Canaanites, built a
city in the mountain called Sion, which he called Saleim, which is
interpreted, city of peace; and he reigned in it one hundred and
thirteen years, and died righteous and a virgin, as Iosephos set forth.
From the flood therefore to the tower-building, to Abraham, were one
hundred and twenty-three years, and the knowledge of God came from
Abraham 241 and Isaak his son, and Iakob his grandson, and their seed,
from whom are the Hebrews, from the time of Abraham having taken the
character of the nation through circumcision; and the tribe of Ioudas,
the fourth of Iakob, held the Jews and governed them, whence also they
had their name. And in these times there appeared one of the tribe of
Japheth, Hesiodos by name, who invented the Greek letters, who is also
the theologian of the Greeks.

That in the times of Pharaoh, who is also Achor, king of Egypt, there
appeared a gigantic philosopher of the tribe of Japheth, Endymion by
name, in the land of Karia, who, having mystic prayers and saying them
to the moon, sought to learn from her the divine name in a vision. While
then he was praying, he fell asleep, and heard in a vision, and did not
rise, but his remains stand in the land of Karia sleeping; concerning
whom they say that the moon loves him.

And Persianios reigned over the Egyptians when the exodus of the sons of
Israel took place; and that he, beholding the wonders done by God
through Moses, went away to Memphis, to the famous oracle, and having
made sacrifice, asked the Pythia, saying, "Make clear to me who is first
among you, and whether the God of Israel is great." And there was given
him this oracle: "There shall be, having come to pass beyond the flame
of the great heaven, an aetherial, eternal, immortal fire surpassing,
which every heaven trembles at, and earth and sea and the abyss of
Tartaros; the daimons were riven. This is the God, self-father father,
himself son of himself, thrice-blessed; we are but a small part of the
angels; having learned, go, keeping silence." In the times of Gedaeon
the judge was Orpheus the Thracian, the lyric poet, the most wise and
famous, who set forth the knowledge of God and the creation of the world
and the fashioning of men, saying at the beginning of his discourse that
from his own reflection he set forth nothing concerning God or the
creation of the world, but asked to learn concerning the sun and the
creation, and who was the maker of them. And having said many verses
concerning this, the same Orpheus, concerning the knowledge of God and
the creation of the world, wrote through many verses poetically; and
these are the things he set forth: "that from the beginning the aether
was shown to the world, having been fashioned by God, and here and there
of the aether was chaos, and dark night held all things and covered the
things under the aether, the night preceding the day." And saying in his
exposition that "there is a certain incomprehensible and pre-existing
one and maker of all, both of the aether itself and of night, and of the
creation under the aether, being and covered; and the earth, he said,
was invisible under the darkness, and that the light, breaking through
the aether, illumined the earth and all creation, saying that that light
was the one that broke the aether, the aforementioned, the over-all of
all." Whose name Orpheus, having heard from the oracle, "counsel, light,
life-giver," said the same three names as divine powers in his
exposition: that there is one power and might of the heavenly God, whom
no one can see or know, either form or nature. And from that power all
things came to be at first bodiless, sun and moon and all the stars,
earth and sea, and the things visible in them and all the invisible
things. And the race of men, he said, was fashioned by that same God
from the earth, and received from him a rational soul, as Moses the most
wise also set forth. And the same Orpheus set forth that through the
three names of one divinity all things came to be. These things the most
wise Timotheos the chronicler set forth, saying before how many years
Orpheus spoke of the consubstantial Trinity.
