OF OUR FATHER AMONG THE SAINTS ATHANASIOS
On the eternal existence of the Son and of the Spirit with God, and against
those who Sabellize.
Judaism stands opposed to Hellenism, and neither is pious, but both are
outside the truth. Since even the Babylonians have warred against the =28.97=
Egyptians, and many other impious have been set against impious. It is not
then enough to stand against impiety, unless the resistance against impiety
come to pass through piety. Many and great things against those who worship
idols the Jews have to say, and they speak justly accusing them of serving the
creation rather than the Creator. But not because they refute impiety
will they therefore be confessed to be pious, denying the Son of God, through
whom all things came to be, and charging those who through him worship the
Father with polytheism. Wherefore we have come out from the Greeks, and have
been separated, so as not to be mixed with the unclean idolatries; and we have
come out also from the blasphemy of the Jews, having confessed the Son of God,
and having fled the destructive denial of our Lord, who says: "Whoever shall
deny me before men, I will deny him before my Father who is in the heavens."
And we confess the Son from the Father, and always with the Father, believing
him who says and promises: "Everyone who shall confess me before men, I also
will confess him before my Father who is in the heavens." Seeing this pattern,
even now we have been alienated from those who Hellenize under the name of
Christianity, who dare both to theologize and to worship a work of God. And we
are separated also from those who Judaize and corrupt Christianity in Judaism;
who, denying the God from God, say one God similarly to the Jews; not because
he alone is unbegotten and alone source of Godhead, for this reason saying
that he is only God; but as barren of a Son and fruitless of living Word and
true wisdom. For they think the Word of God to be such as that from a man's
heart, and wisdom such as that in a soul; and for this reason they say God
together with the Word is one person; just as also the man together with his
own word is one man, holding nothing more than the Jews, who did not receive
the evangelist who immediately from the beginning cries: "In the beginning was
the Word, and the Word was with God, and God was the Word." For if God has a
Word in the heart not truly begotten from him, as God from God, how would the
Word be with God? and how would he be God? For the word of the man is not a
man with a man, since it is neither living nor existent, but only a movement
of a living and existent heart; and it is spoken at once, and is not, and
though often spoken never =28.100= remains. But the psalmist from of old has
cried out concerning the Word of God, saying: "Unto the age, O Lord, your Word
remains in heaven;" and in agreement with him the evangelist who confesses the
Word to be God, both evangelizes him as manifested, and announces him as
having arrived, and proclaims him as having become flesh, not including the
Father in the incarnation of the Word; "For the Word," he says, "became flesh,
and tabernacled among us, and we beheld his glory, glory as of the
Only-begotten from the Father, full of grace and truth." You see the Word who
is Only-begotten being manifested in the world, tabernacling among men, glory
to him

being proclaimed, as of the Only-begotten from the Father; not as though he
himself were Father, nor as though the Father together with the Word had
appeared, just as the man appears together with his own word, and it is not
possible for the word to appear unless the one speaking it also be present and
utter. Whom then do we believe? Those who evangelize to us a Son who has
sojourned from the Father, or those who say that the Father together with the
Word is one person? For if one person, how does the one send, and the other is
sent, and is beheld through flesh? For "No one has seen God at any time; the
only-begotten Son, who is in the bosom of the Father, he has declared him,"
and having arrived he himself speaks with the holy voice, granting the vision
of himself, and through this suggesting the knowledge of the Father; "He who
has seen me has seen the Father," not saying that he himself is the Father,
for how, the invisible? but such as the Father; for he had said beforehand:
"If you had known me, you would have known my Father also," plainly declaring
to us the dyad of persons, recognized by unvarying Godhead. Wherefore the one
counted worthy of the vision of the Son is not deprived of that of the Father.
For the begetter did not beget another kind, but such as he himself is. If
then you think there is one thing only, though they are two according to
truth, you know neither the Father nor the Son. What communion then do you
have with the ecclesiastical teaching? And how will you receive the Master's
voices, "I," he says, "and the Father are one"? And again discoursing
concerning the Father, "I am from him," he says, "and he sent me;" and he adds
also the third: "I," he says, "will ask my Father, and he will give you
another Paraclete." When then concerning himself he says "I," and concerning
the Father "He," and concerning the Spirit, =28.101= "another;" how is it not
a clear apostasy to deny the three, and to say that he alone is who says: "I
am not alone, because the Father who sent me is with me." And he uses also a
legal voice, and through this presents two as being, himself and the Father;
"For in your law," he says, "it is written that the testimony of two men is
true; I," he says, "am the one who testifies concerning myself, and the Father
who sent me testifies concerning me." For behold two persons, and if you do
not say that they are two according to truth, saying this you do not even
allow the testimony to stand as being true. For this confirms the testimony,
that it is not done by one only, but by two. For indeed the Father has
testified from heaven saying: "This is my beloved Son;" and the only-begotten
Son testifies that he is Son of God, and has spoken to the Father clearly in
the hearing of men saying: "I thank you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth,
that you hid these things from the wise and understanding, and revealed them
to babes. Yes, Father; for so it became good pleasure before you. All things
have been delivered to me by my Father." But what do they plead to these
things, the servants of the Jews' denial, in order not to be clearly convicted
as acting against the Gospels of our Lord Jesus Christ; and thus two
hypostases appear, one the Father God, and the other the Son a man? Alas for
the impiety! And how does this differ from the apology of the Samosatene? And
let them say how a man dares to utter these things concerning himself. "He who
has seen me has seen the Father"? And how does the evangelist testify
concerning him uttering these things, that he was calling God his own Father,
making himself equal to God? A man is not equal to God, but the God from God
will have equality with the one who begot him according to nature, as Paul
also testifies, not concerning the incarnation, but explaining the things
before the incarnation: "Who, being in the form of God, did not count it
robbery to be equal with God." We know also that the Savior often speaks
humanly; but not when he says: "I am the one who testifies concerning myself,
and the Father who sent me testifies concerning me." For it is not a man who
was sent into the world, the one who says:

"I came forth from the Father and have come;" the one who says: "For God so
loved the world, that he gave his only-begotten Son, that everyone who
believes in him should not perish, but have eternal life;" the one who says:
"I have come down from heaven, not that I may do my will, but the will of him
who sent me." But if you wish to hear him =28.104= humanly, fittingly to the
form seen according to the flesh, and to the bare humanity supposed by those
who heard, hear him saying: "If I testify concerning myself, my testimony is
not true;" according to a man testifying for himself not being trustworthy,
nor being thought so; but when he says: "Even if I testify concerning myself,
my testimony is true, because I know whence I come, and where I go." When,
numbering the Father and himself, he says that the witnesses concerning his
glory are two, he does not speak human things, but things above all human
dignity; and why do I say human? above angelic, above principalities, above
powers, above lordships. For what numbering together of man and God? what
nearness, or what communion? but saying: "I thank you, Father, Lord of heaven
and earth," he seems to you to be a man, well; for the Godhead that has
arrived into the world has the human mixed in. But do not through the covering
of the flesh be ignorant of God, who immediately adds concerning himself, and
says: "No one knows the Son except the Father, nor does anyone know the Father
except the Son, and he to whom the Son wills to reveal him. Come to me, all
who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon
you, and learn from me, that I am meek and lowly in heart; and you will find
rest for your souls; for my yoke is easy, and my burden is light." The Lord
has shut off your calumny; having presented himself as God even upon the human
form, known only by the Father, and knowing the Father, and revealing to those
counted worthy the knowledge of himself and of the Father, he calls us to
himself, promising rest with himself, and commanding us to bear his own yoke.
Saying these things he uncovers the Godhead, interprets the surpassing
greatness covered through the small form, displays the superheavenly authority
veiled by an earthly body. And why must I catalogue the myriad divine voices
of our Lord Jesus Christ? through which reasonably the innovation of the
Samosatene concerning the Savior, as being a mere man, is cast out, and
together is cast out also the unreasoning babble of those who imitate him.
Come then, that we may mention a few testimonies, who is the one saying such
things: "Not everyone who says to me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom
of the heavens"? Not a man's saying, but of God =28.105= showing the genuine
generation from the Father, and for this reason not denying the naming as Lord
concerning himself. For Lord is the one begotten from the only Lord, as also
in Moses it has been said: "The Lord rained brimstone and fire from the Lord."
And at another time the Savior says to the apostles: "You call me Teacher and
Lord, and you say well; for I am;" and again, declaring himself Master of the
paternal house, as being only-begotten Son: "If they called the master of the
house Beelzebul, how much more those of his household." Therefore that the
Lord of all, Jesus Christ, is God, as being begotten from God, is clear to
everyone; and having said, "Not everyone who says to me, Lord, Lord, shall
enter into the kingdom of the heavens," he adds also the Father, clearly
interpreting to us: "But he who does the will of my Father who is in the
heavens." You have then a Son, you have a Father; do not fear the dyad; for it
is not divided in nature, nor has the one who says the two alienated the
offspring from the one who begot; nor because of the other madness of those
who denied that the Godhead of the two is one, for this reason is saying two
also to be fled. One God, because also Father

one; and the Son also is God, having identity, as Son toward Father; not
himself being the Father, but united to the Father in nature; two in number,
but being one perfect essence; two, not from two parts. Perfect the Son has
come from perfect Father, being from one who is, impress of the paternal
hypostasis, not an insubstantial word, but living power, and cause of life
to all, not such as a man's power, through which a man is powerful; for he is
not an offspring of a man, either a son or his power; but the Son of God is
power, so that the Father is perfectly powerful, because Father of power, and
the Son is perfect power, because his offspring. And the Son is wisdom of the
Father, but not as that of man, through which the man is wise, but wisdom from
the wise; concerning whom it is said: "To the only wise God through Jesus
Christ, to whom be the glory unto the ages, amen." But the Father did not
beget. How then is he Father, and of whom? But if he begot, the one begotten
is he who is. And I, he says, confess generation; for the word is generated,
when it is also spoken and known. And how many times, tell me, will the same
one be generated? or will you say many Words of God being generated each time?
and will you imagine God sometimes silent, sometimes speaking, and changing
from silence to speech, and with lips opening, or heart moving? God is not a
man; did you suppose, lawless one, that God will be like you? O man, even if
you hear of eyes of God, even if of the other =28.108= bodily members, do not
think carnally; but through the likeness of the bodily things understand the
incorporeal. For it has been said to you: God is Spirit; so also the Word is
not human, nor such as yours; for he is God, even if it does not seem so to
you, and there are not two Gods, because neither two Fathers, nor is the one
begotten of another essence than the one who begot. For the one who introduces
two beginnings proclaims two Gods. This is the impiety of Marcion, saying a
just God Father of his own Christ, and another good God Father of his own
Christ. Again the one who says God is ungenerated, and another God generated,
himself also says two Gods, because of the difference of essence which he
blasphemously introduces. But where there is one beginning, and one offspring
from it, a most exact and natural image; because also begotten from him; one
God, the Godhead being understood perfect in the Father, and the paternal
Godhead existing perfect also in the Son. Since even through this small
example, which the divine Scripture has made clear, having named Christ image
of the invisible God, you can grasp what is said; let us flee again even here
the unlikeness in the example, that the image of God is a craftsman's work and
of another essence, and let us confess it begotten and consubstantial.
Nevertheless then the one who sees the image of the king sees the king, and
says: Behold, this is the king, and he has not made two kings, nor the image a
part of the king, or the king a part of the image. Thus then one God the
Father, and one Godhead of Father and Son. But as you say, there will be one
composed from two incomplete parts. And against this impiety it is good to
shut the ears, and to wash the soul; for the one who imagines this confesses
neither Son nor Father; since it is not possible to think either Father or Son
incomplete. But it has been said, he says: "That I am in the Father, and the
Father in me;" but the Father is not a Word in the heart of the Son; therefore
neither is the Son a Word in the heart of the Father, but a living Word from
the living Father God, having appeared by eternal generation, being with the
Father without beginning; so that the Father is never conceived as alone. For
always the Trinity is Trinity, and does not receive an addition of Godhead.
Nor is the Son who was not formerly with the Father added later, nor does the
Spirit come upon the Son afterwards. For the things that have come to be from
some beginning are both works and slaves, and are in no way numbered with the
Trinity; "For go," he says, "make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them
in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit." And he
numbered together neither one beginning, nor =28.109= authority, nor power;
for it is impossible for the things not eternal to be numbered with the
eternal, and with the

Godhead the things not partaking of the Godhead are not arranged. How then is
the one begotten in the one who begot? and again the begetter in the
offspring? how is this one in that, and that in this? Because he himself also
is such as that one, and that one such as this. Thus also the two are one, by
not differing, nor being fenced off, nor the Son being thought according to
another and foreign form and character, but being God, just as also the
Father. Since even when he said: "I and the Father are one," the Jews were
angry saying, "That you, being a man, make yourself God." And the Savior
answered confirming what was said by them, as having declared nothing else
than this in saying: "I and the Father are one," that he too is God being Son
of God; "If he called them gods, to whom the Word of God came, and the
Scripture cannot be broken; him whom the Father sanctified, and sent into the
world, do you say, That you blaspheme; because I said, that I am Son of God?
if I do not do the works of my Father, do not believe me; but if I do, even if
you are not willing to believe me, believe the works; that you may know and
understand, that I am in the Father, and the Father in me." Therefore the Son
being in the Father, and the Father in the Son, this is and nothing else, that
the Son is of God, the one begotten from God, being God according to nature,
which the Father is, and showing the paternal form in himself, and being shown
upon the paternal hypostasis. One then, and one, and the dyad is not divided
in nature, being without need in perfection, the Father the whole fullness of
the Godhead, as Father, and the Son the whole fullness of the Godhead, as Son.
Since through both one form is thought, being shown wholly in both. Let no one
deny the life that has been manifested in the world; "Which was," he says,
"with the Father, and was manifested to us." And before the manifestation this
was what it appeared to be when it has been manifested. For he has been
begotten eternally, and exists with his own Father. Let no one think that by
denying the three he finds the monad; but let him think the one in the
Trinity, having the chief point of the faith in baptism, and in the three holy
seals, through which he has been regenerated unto salvation, remembering the
Apostle saying: "One God the Father, from whom are all things, and one Lord
Jesus Christ, through whom are all things;" remembering the Father saying:
"From the womb before the morning star I begot you," and the Son testifying
that he was before the world with the Father, having the glory of the Godhead;
"For glorify me," he says, =28.112= "Father, with yourself with the glory
which I had, before the world was, with you;" asking the glory according to
man, but testifying that he had the glory according to God before the world.
And he had the glory being and existing; for neither has the one who is not
been glorified. And if he was, he had also been begotten; for there is no son
not begotten; but if he has been begotten, he says, he is outside the one who
begot, and is divided; but it is necessary that the Word being in God be
undivided, and not appearing outside of God. For where would he even appear
outside, God having filled all things, according to what is written: "I fill
heaven and earth, says the Lord"? With such imaginations they attempt to
destroy the generation of the Son, and the eternal procession from the Father,
and the existence with the Father, and that the Father exists perfectly by
himself, and the Son also exists perfectly by himself. Let them learn then
that they do not understand, "neither what they say, nor concerning the things
they affirm," circumscribing God by place, and again imagining the Son in
another place, and thinking them divided, the one there, the other here, if
the Son is confessed as begotten and having appeared by himself from the
Father. "What place of my rest?" says God through the prophet. "Heaven and the
heaven of heaven will not suffice you," Solomon says to God; God persuading
men by a very great proof, that no place is receptive of him. "For my hand,"
he says, "made all these things." Therefore the works do not contain the one
who made them, who also before

these was, and grants being to these, and they are established by the very
power of the one who made them. Wherefore Paul says: "From whom are all
things, and through whom are all things, and unto whom are all things;" and
again: "For in him we live, and move, and are." Take away then for me the one
established in place, when you think concerning God, and the Word, and the
Spirit; take away local separations, and do not suspect that the Father being
in one place, and the Son in another, he sends him forth from begetting. For
these false, or rather even godless reasonings, scandalize you concerning the
existence of the Son. And together with the Son the Holy Spirit also has been
denied by you, through all things being held beforehand by one, the Father.
Do not seek a magnitude of place so great, which will be able to contain the
magnitude of God; for the unmeasured is not upon the measured, but with his
span he measures the heaven, as the prophet interprets parabolically; and he
has measured also =28.113= the water with the hand, and the whole earth with a
handful. To whom all things are thus least, as the prophetic parable
moderately indicates, how would he seem to be coextended with the small and
least creation? God has not filled all things by being coextended with all;
for this is bodily, just as if someone should say that the air has filled the
space between earth and heaven; but as power he holds all things together; for
power is incorporeal, invisible, not circling, not being circled. And the same
also concerning the Son, and concerning the Holy Spirit; for through the Son
and in the Spirit God both constituted all things, and holding them together
preserves them. It is impious then both to seek and to think where in the
world God is, or what place the Word has obtained, or what dwelling the Spirit
has been allotted. And if for this reason someone should say that the Son
neither is nor has been begotten, because the place of his essence is
inconceivable and undiscoverable, such a one would say that the Father also is
not at all, nor that God exists; since neither is any place of God found. "The
fool said in his heart: There is no God." Truly fools are such, cheating
themselves with empty imaginations; the words of infants and altogether
mindless, wanting the incorporeal to be taken by the eye and to be encompassed
by place. But let us give them some brief images from the possible
incorporeals, mentioning certain things, which being among us, and appearing
upon the creation, if somehow they might be able through these ascending to
think somehow the incorporeality of the Father, and of the Son, and of the
Holy Spirit; and no longer to imagine places of them, nor to invent local
divisions of the Trinity. The law being incorporeal and spiritual is in the
mind of the one learned in the law; and in the same also the prophets, Gospels
and apostles; and it has been said concerning the legal and prophetic words
"They have Moses and the prophets;" and the Savior says that the one who has
old and new sayings upon his own mind is like a man, a master of a house, who
brings forth from his treasure new and old. And the mind of Moses received
both all Egyptian wisdom, and that given to him from God. And Daniel, having
received all the understanding of the Chaldeans, contained also the various
thoughts of the divine wisdom, which God signifies through Ezekiel: "Are you
wiser than Daniel? was not every secret shown to you." And the prudence of
Solomon is numberless, and the outpouring of his heart, =28.116= as sand
that is by the sea. And seeing so great a multitude of wisdoms around one
mind, let them consider that they are not in place. For how large in
magnitude would a man have to become, to suffice for the reception of so
many, if he had each of the contemplations in divided places? When then
the spiritual things here, being more, do not need more places, but turn
around one and the same mind, let us think also the things above us, and being
incorporeal in themselves, the Father and the Son and the Spirit not needing
places, nor being divided by places; and let no one dare for this reason to
suppose either the Father, or the Son, or the Holy Spirit nonexistent; because
it is not even possible to mark off a place and

assign it to each. But the Father is, having perfect and unfailing being, root
and source of the Son and of the Spirit; and the Son is in the full Godhead,
living Word, and offspring of the Father without need; and full also is the
Spirit of the Son, not a part of another, but complete in itself. And thus the
Trinity, being truly, is joined without interval. For there is nothing that
divides, and it is together eternally; for no age falls in between. And it
displays one and the same form, the Father being beheld in the Son, and the
Son in the Spirit both being thought and being participated in by man. "For we
all," he says, "with unveiled face beholding as in a mirror the glory of the
Lord, are being transformed into the same image from glory to glory, even as
from the Lord, the Spirit." Let us not then imagine three as bodily undivided
parts. For the reasoning is impious, alien to perfect incorporeals. Let us
receive an undivided communion of those existing together with one another
without interval, and of three indeed truly existing, let us think one form,
beginning from the Father, shining in the Son, and being manifested through
the Spirit. For this reason also those who bear the Spirit bear Christ; "For
if anyone," he says, "does not have the Spirit of Christ, this one is not his.
If Christ is in you, the body is dead because of sin," and what follows. And
John: "By this we know that he is in us, from the Spirit which he gave us."
And Paul names our bodies a temple of the Holy Spirit, and says that Christ is
in us. And Christ dwelling into our inner man, according to what is written,
God is dwelling in us; "Do you not know," he says, "that you are a temple of
God, and the Spirit of God dwells in you." Thus learn piously from the divine
words the undividedness of the Godhead, considering one form in three, not
composing one thing =28.117= from three. And they are inseparable also unto
the things beheld of the operation, hearing the Apostle initiating and saying:
"And there are divisions of gifts, but the same Spirit; and there are
divisions of ministries, and the same Lord; and there are divisions of
operations, but the same God who works all things in all." And having
catalogued the gifts he adds: "But all these things the one and the same
Spirit works, dividing to each privately, as he wills." And when all things
are worked by God through Christ in the Holy Spirit, I see an inseparable
operation of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. But not for
this reason, having woven together the from-whom, and through-whom, and
in-whom, do I force the Trinity to make a monad; nor, supposing a man composed
from three, spirit, soul, body, so also God, just as those also dare, nor upon
such an impious supposition do I put forward the inseparable. Nor if someone
should say that as the immanent word, or the one in voice of the one thinking
and speaking, is inseparable, so the Son and the Spirit of the Father. For the
parts of the composite, and the movements of the one being moved, have no
communion with the uncomposite and unchangeable nature. Since also how does
the Father send his part or his movement, sending the Son? or the Son sending
forth the Holy Spirit into the world? Or was the Son not sent by the Father?
He testifies everywhere, and promises to send the Spirit the Paraclete, and
indeed also sends according to the promise. But those who make the Trinity a
monad attempt to adulterate also the sending, just as the generation. For they
say that the Son being within the Father works sanctification of man, that is,
the one and only God himself, that thus the man has been sent, not God from
God. For how, they say, has the Word been sent, not being divided, nor being
separated, of whom he is Word? That then an incarnation of the Word has been
proclaimed, who would contradict? the evangelist crying: "The Word became
flesh," and the Savior himself crying that he has come down from heaven, not
once, but also many times? And indeed that our Lord Jesus Christ is not a man,
the apostle

Paul strongly affirms to the Galatians. But even if a man homonymous with us,
a second man, and not such as the former earth-born, and the one a living
soul, the other a life-giving Spirit. =28.120= For these are both divine and
apostolic words and doctrines, and the works testify to the Godhead being done
with heavenly authority, when he commands elements, and rebukes spirits, and
by power restores maimings, and by will heals diseases, and speaks the
authority, and does not hide it, and death is abolished through him, and life
and incorruption are brought to light, and the corrupted nature of men is
created a second time, and a world is renewed through his presence, and a new
creation appears through the resurrection of Christ, the body of our
humiliation being transformed into conformity with the body of his glory; and
the whole creation being freed from the bondage of corruption into the freedom
of the glory of the children of God. And that the incarnate Lord and our God
Jesus Christ is not the Father, nor, as those would say, the only God, all the
divine Scriptures testify, proclaiming the one who has arrived as Son of God,
and showing him always discoursing concerning the Father, saying he has come
from the Father, and is going to the Father. And there is no need to lengthen
by setting out testimonies; for all, so to speak, the Gospels, and the
writings of the apostles tend to this. And they stir up other Greek
contradictions against us, and they bring upon us the blasphemies from the
Jews, mocking the mystery of the sending of the Word and incarnation. For if
he is sent, and comes down, they say, he will not be with the one sending, nor
is he together with the one remaining above. Let them learn then not to hear
the above and the in-height only bodily, but let them consider something even
newer. For the, "The Lord is high above all the nations," and the, "I will
exalt you, O Lord," and the, "Praiseworthy and highly exalted unto the ages,"
does not present the Lord as being in some place and aloft, nor does it sketch
God as being lifted aloft by men, nor by the others who hymn him, but it calls
the magnificence of the invisible Godhead height. And the one who says: "The
heaven of heaven is the Lord's, but the earth he gave to the sons of men,"
does not allot heaven as a place of dwelling to the Lord, and the earth to
men; but shows the preeminence of God and magnificence. When then the august
and magnificent is said to be heavenly, and oppositely to this the lowly is
said to be earthily and upon earth, a descent of the Word is found, the giving
of himself from the incorporeal magnificence into the cheapness of the body,
he having given himself into bodily formation through love of man and the
saving providence of the whole. And he will be understood as sent according to
this, that by an ineffable will of the incorporeal Father he has =28.121=
given himself to a body. Let not then the bodily voices, that is the descent
and the sending, be brought forward toward a denial of his incarnation,
because God does not come down locally nor is sent; but let them hear the
Apostle phrasing the matter, and at the same time of such likenesses, when he
says: "Who being in the form of God, did not count it robbery to be equal with
God, but emptied himself, taking the form of a slave, becoming in the likeness
of men." This was the coming down, this the being sent into a garment more
humble than his dignity, a stepping down, the putting-on of the human form,
accomplished according to the will of the Father. And if the Word is not
circumscribed by place and body, he will not be torn away from the incorporeal
Father through the mixing toward a body; for the body has received him when he
willed, as the fleshly nature was naturally able to receive God. And he
remains no less upon his own nature, even as also before the incarnation. So
that the descent from heaven was not a change of place from heaven, nor the
sending a separation from the Father who sends.
