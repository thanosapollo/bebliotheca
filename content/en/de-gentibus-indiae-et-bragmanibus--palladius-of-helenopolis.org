Palladius on the peoples of India and the Brahmans
1.1 Your great love of toil and love of learning and love of hearing and love
of God, an ornament of the best of men, has urged us to narrate yet another
matter overflowing with
philosophy. Moved then by your love of hearing, in addition to what has been
said we will further set forth to you also the life of the Brahmans, whose
country I myself have neither visited nor have I met the men. For they are
settled far off both from India and from Serice, dwelling beside the river
Ganges, and I reached only the extremities of India a few years ago with the
blessed Moses the bishop of the Adulenes; and being seized by a wild heat
(such that the water bubbling up from the spring, exceedingly cold, and taken
in vessels, boils at once), having seen this I turned back again, not enduring
the burning heat. And this river Ganges is among us the one called Pishon, the
one lying in the Scriptures, being one of the four rivers said to go out from
paradise. 1.2 And a narrative is current of Alexander the king of the
Macedonians, who in some measure set forth their life; and even for him it
perhaps existed as from a mishearing; for he himself, as I think, did not
cross the Ganges, but having reached as far as Serice, where the Seres produce
the silk, and having set up a stone stele there he inscribed: Alexander the
Macedonian reached as far as this place. 1.3 And I have been able in some
measure to learn the things concerning the Brahmans from a certain Theban
scholasticus, who made the journey voluntarily, but fell into captivity
involuntarily. This man, as he said, had been without talent in forensic
practice and, seized by acedia, so longed to visit the country of the Indians.
1.4 And having sailed with a presbyter he first reached Adulis, then after
that Auxume, in which there was also a small kinglet of the Indians sitting
there. And having spent time there and acquired much familiarity he wished
also to enter the island Taprobane, where the so-called Macrobians are; for on
that island the old men live up to a hundred and fifty years through excess of
the temperateness of the airs and the unsearchable judgment of God. And on
this island also the great king of the Indians dwells, to whom all the
kinglets of the country are subject as satraps, as the scholasticus himself
related to us, having himself also learned it from others; for not even he
himself was able to enter the island. 1.5 For there lie beside this island
(unless what was said is false) some thousand other islands of the Red Sea
that empties into them. Since then the magnet stone that draws iron occurs in
those islands called the Manioloi, whatever ship goes there having iron nails
is held by the nature of the stone, unable to pass by; and specifically the
ships that cross over to that great island are constructed without iron, with
wooden pegs. 1.6 And this island, he says, also has five very great rivers, on
which ships pass through. And as those from there related to him, fruit never
fails in that place of the island; for in the same time, he says, one of the
branches is in bloom, another is turning unripe, and another is even harvested
{neither does a vine turn unripe nor is it harvested}. And it also has palms
and the great Indian nut and the thin aromatic one. And the inhabitants of
that place live on milk and rice and fruit, wool not being produced among
them, nor even flax, but they put about the loins only well-worked sheepskins.
And there are also sheep that are haired without wool, very milky, having very
broad tails. And they also use


flesh-eating of goats and sheep; for a pig from the Thebaid is no longer found
in the parts of India or Ethiopia through excess of heats. 1.7 This
scholasticus then related that from Auxume, finding some Indians crossing in
small boats for the sake of trade, he attempted to go further in; and having
reached near the so-called Bisades who gather the pepper; and that nation is
very small and very weak, dwelling in stone caves, who also know how to climb
cliffs because of the contraction of the place, and they gather the pepper
thus from the bushes; for they are truncated little trees, as that
scholasticus said. And the Bisades too are truncated little men, large-headed,
unshorn and straight-haired; but the remaining Ethiopians and Indians are
black and youthful and woolly-haired. 1.8 "And from there," he says, "being
held by the one in power over affairs I was attempting as having dared to
enter their country; and neither did they receive my defense, not knowing the
dialect of our country, nor did I recognize the charges coming against me from
them. For I too did not know their language, and we matched known signs to one
another only by the distortions of the faces. I for my part guessed the force
of what was said by them from their incriminating voices, from the bloodshot
color of the eyes and the wild gnashing of the teeth and the movements, and
they in turn clearly learned the pitiable state of the soul through the
cowardice of the body from my trembling and agony and paleness of face. 1.9
Held then among them for a six-year period I served, being handed over to the
bakery for work. And the expenditure of their king, he says, was one modius of
wheat for his whole palace, and that I do not know from where it was brought.
Thus, he says, in the six years I was able to note down very much from their
language, through which I also learned the remaining neighboring nations.
=1.10= And I was released from there, he says, in this manner: another king
having made war with the one holding me slandered him to the great king
sitting on the island Taprobane, as having taken a notable Roman man captive
and having this one in most ignoble service. And he, sending one judge and
learning the exact truth, commanded him to be flayed as a wineskin as having
insulted a Roman; for they greatly honor and fear the empire of the Romans as
able even to set foot on their country through excess of courage and
resourcefulness. =1.11= This man then said that the Brahmans are a nation not
withdrawing by choice as the monks do, but they obtained this lot from above
and from the judgments of God, the dwelling beside the river, living naturally
in nakedness. Among whom there is no four-footed animal, no farming, no iron,
no building, no fire, no bread, no wine, no garment, nor anything else
contributing to work or stretching toward enjoyment. And they have air that is
clear and temperate and altogether most beautiful. Worshipping God and having
knowledge, yet not so subtle nor able thus to discern the reasons of
providence, nevertheless they pray without ceasing. And praying they gaze at
the heaven instead of the east, not attending to the turning of the east.
=1.12= And they eat the tree-fruits that happen to be at hand and the wild
vegetables, as many as the earth produces of itself, and they drink water,
being nomads in forests, resting upon leaves. And among them there is much of
the persea wood and the so-called acanthus and certain other local
fruit-bearing trees from which they live. =1.13= And the men dwell on the
Ocean side from there of the river Ganges; for this river empties into the
Ocean; and their women are on this side of the Ganges toward the part of
India. And the men cross over to the women in the month of July and August;
for these are among them the coldest months, the sun having been raised toward
us above the north; and those months are said to be more temperate and to stir
desire. And having spent with the


women forty days they cross back again. When however the woman has borne two
children the man no longer crosses to his own wife for the sake of
intercourse. Having then set up the corresponding body in their stead they
remain continent for the remaining time. And if it happens that a barren woman
is found among them, the man crosses to her having intercourse with her up to
a five-year period; and if she does not give birth, he no longer approaches
her. Wherefore their nation has not been extended into a populous multitude,
both because of the hard living of the place and because of the natural
continence of generation. =1.14= This is the way of life of the Brahmans. And
they say the river is hard to cross because of the so-called odontotyrannus;
for it is an animal exceedingly great, existing in the river, amphibious, able
to swallow down an entire and unharmed elephant; and at the time of the
Brahmans' crossing to their own women it is not seen in those places. And
there are also very great dragons in those rivers up to seventy cubits, as I
myself also saw one skin having a width of two feet. And the ants there are a
palm's breadth, and the scorpions a cubit; wherefore those places are also
hard to travel through. And not every place there has such animals or venomous
beasts, but the uninhabited alone. A great herd of elephants. =1.15= Of Arrian
however, the disciple of Epictetus the philosopher who had also been a slave,
but through natural talent advanced to philosophy in the times of Nero the
king who punished the celebrated apostles Peter and Paul, this man having
related the things concerning Alexander the Macedonian {for he says} I sent
the little treatise which I possessed to the manliness of your nobility,
appending it to my memoir; which reading intelligently and having cared for it
fitly you will live securely. 2.1 Dandamis, the teacher of the Brahmans,
relating the things concerning the Macedonian said: "King Alexander, not
enduring to be king of Macedonia alone nor content with Philip as father, but
even said he was a son of Ammon and, though generation refuted him, he brought
false proofs of nature, and put forward invincible contests; for he rose as
the sun from Macedonia, having ridden over much of the world before he set at
Babylon, and having used Europe and Asia as small districts he came even
surveying and searching out our world." 2.2 And Alexander said these things in
himself: "Wisdom, mother of Providence, partner of the power of all, lying
higher in truth alone, nourisher and craftsman of nature, giver of words,
scout of common knowledge, separated from the unjust, sparing the just, grant
me already to obtain my requests favorably. I have come to see the Brahmans
present here, wise and good, having learned from a certain Calanus of those
here who hastened to me; and having been persuaded I came, and I long that I
may see and learn clearly." 2.3 The Brahmans, themselves also Indians, say
these things to Alexander: "Desiring wisdom you came to us, which first we
Brahmans welcome, because <what> is more kingly in our life? For this you
wished to learn, king Alexander; for the philosopher is not mastered, but
masters, for a man does not rule him. But since until now, being slandered, we
were disbelieved by you, having now tasted true words you came. 2.4 For
Calanus has become a bad man to us, and through Calanus you Greek men came to
know Brahmans {but you did not come to know them fully}; for that man was not
ours, having run away from virtue to wealth; it did not suffice him to drink
water of self-control of the river Tiberoboam and to eat milk, in which a
god-befitting mind is increased; he got wealth, enemy of the soul, and thus
kindled a terrible fire in himself and turned himself from wisdom toward
pleasure. And none of us rolls on coals nor does pain consume our body, but
our food is life, a drug of health. 2.5 And we are without wealth according to
nature and likewise death follows all in our life. And if some of mortal men,
having learned false words, shoot at us with empty


arrows, yet they do not harm our freedom. And to lie and to be persuaded
quickly are equal; for the one who lies wrongs the one he persuades, and the
one persuaded also wrongs, attending to a liar before he learns the truth. For
slander is mother of war and begets wrath, from which they fight and make war.
2.6 But it is not courage to murder men, for it is a robber's work. And
courage is to fight against the turns of the airs with the body naked and to
destroy the desire of the belly and rather to conquer the wars within and not
to be overthrown by desire toward reaching after glory and wealth and
pleasure. Conquer these first, Alexander, kill these; for if you conquer
these, you have no need to fight against those outside; for the fight against
those outside is in order that you may bring tribute to these. Do you not see
that you conquer those outside and have been conquered by those within? How
many kings of folly seem to you to tyrannize among the foolish? Tongue,
hearing, smell, sight, touch, belly, genitals, the whole skin. And many also
from within, like implacable mistresses and insatiable tyrannies, command
endlessly: desires, love of money, love of pleasure, treacherous murders,
{mixings of bodies} homicides, stinginess, divisions, to all these and to many
others besides mortals are slaves, for which they murder and are murdered. 2.7
But the Brahmans, having conquered the wars within, have thereafter been
strengthened and rest, looking at forests and heaven. And we hear the
well-tuned sound of birds and the cry of eagles, and we have been clothed with
leaves and dwell in air. We eat fruits and drink water, we sing hymns to God
and we desire the things to come, we hear nothing of anyone who does not
benefit. Being silent as to the many words, such things we Brahmans live. 2.8
But you say what one must do, and you do what one must not say; and among you
none of the philosophers knows anything, unless they speak; for your mind is
the tongue and the wits are on the lips. You gather gold and silver, you have
need of slaves and great houses, you pursue offices, you eat and drink as much
as the cattle also, and you do not even perceive as private persons do, you
wrap yourselves in soft things becoming like the worms that spin silk. You do
all things fearlessly and you repent of what you do, you speak against
yourselves as against enemies, having authority over the tongue you are warred
upon by it; better than you are those who are silent; for they do not convict
themselves. 2.9 From sheep as captives you borrow wool; you put glory about
your fingers as cult-statues. You wear gold, as the females do, and you
magnify yourselves upon these things, having been crafted in the likeness of
the Creator you beget the wrath of untamed beasts. When you wrap yourselves in
many possessions, you magnify yourselves upon these things, not seeing that
toward the truth they can benefit you nothing; for gold does not raise a soul
nor fatten a body, but on the contrary rather both darkens the soul and wastes
the body. =2.10= But we, having come to know nature toward the truth, also
provide for those things allotted to it; when hunger arrives we heal it with
tree-fruits and vegetables furnished to us from providence, and when thirst
arrives coming to the river we drink water, treading on gold, and we treat
this. And gold does not stop thirst nor console hunger nor treat a wound, does
not heal disease, does not fill greed, but rather even further awakens this
desire that is foreign to nature. And a man who is thirsty clearly desires to
drink and having taken water has ceased from thirst, and the one who hungers
naturally seeks food and having eaten is filled and has ceased from the
desire. Altogether then it is clear that to desire gold is foreign to nature;
for every desire of mortals has ceased when it takes satiety, since this has
been sown together with nature. But the love of money is insatiable because
this is contrary to nature. Then also you adorn yourselves in it and you are
glorified upon this {and you boast in garments} being overly magnified above
the other men. And because of this you make the common things of all into
private things, the one nature like for all


of love of money thereafter cutting it into many opinions. =2.11= Calanus, our
false friend, had this opinion, but he is trampled underfoot by us; and the
cause of many evils is among you honored by all and is honored by you; but
being useless to us he is sent away by us having been made nothing, and all
things that we trample, these the money-loving Calanus admired, your vain
friend, but not ours. And wretched and more pitiable than the miserable he
lost his own soul by loving money. And because of this he did not appear
worthy of us nor worthy of friendship toward God nor {of the freedom among us
nor of the freedom from care in us nor} did he rest in the freedoms from care
in the forests, having luxuriated in these, nor did he have a hope of the
expectation after these things, having killed his wretched soul with love of
money. =2.12= And there is among us a certain Dandamis, who lies in a forest
upon spring leaves, having near a spring as milking an unharmed breast of a
mother." =2.13= Then king Alexander having heard all these things invited
their teacher and president of these words to meet. And they point out to him
from afar the place of Dandamis. And Alexander having come there did not see
him as he passed by; for he was lying in {dense} forests resting upon leaves,
in peace, having a spring near as milking an unharmed breast of mother earth.
When then Alexander did not see Dandamis, the overseer and teacher of the
Brahmans, he sends to him a friend of his own named Onesicritus, saying to
this man: =2.14= "Hasten to the teacher of the Brahmans, the great Dandamis, O
Onesicritus, and either bring him to us or having learned the things
concerning him, where he lives, report to me quickly, that I myself may come
to him." And Onesicritus said to him: "I will do what is commanded with speed;
for yours is to command, and to act is mine." And this man having gone and
having found the great Dandamis said: "Greetings, teacher of the Brahmans, the
son of the great god Zeus, king Alexander, who is master of every man, calls
you, who if you come to him will provide many and good gifts, but if you do
not come will cut off your head." And Dandamis having heard and having smiled
gracefully did not even lift his own head from the leaves, but having laughed
at this man, lying down, answered thus: =2.15= "God, the great king, never
begets insolence, but light, peace, life and water, a body of man and souls;
and these he receives, when fate looses those not tamed by desire. This is my
master and God alone, who turns away from murder, does not work wars. And
Alexander is not a god, knowing how to die. How is he master of all, who has
not yet passed the river Tiberoboam nor Cossoalus nor has set his throne in
Palibothra nor has yet passed Zonenade nor has seen the course of Mesopotamia
{of the sun}, nor has seen the one in Methoria and Carisoboria. And Scythia
does not yet know his name. And if the land there has room for him, let him
cross the Ganges and he will find a land able to bear men, if indeed the land
among them no longer endures to carry this man. =2.16= And as many things as
Alexander promises me and as many gifts as he announces he will provide me,
they happen to be useless to me. And these things happen to be dear to me and
good and useful: a house, these leaves and rich food, the plants by the shore
and water for drink. And the remaining money and affairs gathered with care
(and those gathering perishing together with them) provide nothing else but
grief with which every mortal is filled. And now I sleep upon a bed of leaves
with closed eyes watching nothing; for if I wish to watch gold, I destroy even
my sleep. Earth bears all things for me as a mother milk {and it is} for the
one born. I go to what I wish, I am not forced to care for what I do not wish.
=2.17= And if Alexander takes away my head, he will not destroy the soul, but
a head only that is silent; and my soul will go away to the master, leaving
the body as a rag upon the earth, from where it was also
taken. And having become a spirit


I shall go up to my God, who shut us in flesh having sent us
down upon the earth testing how, having come down, we shall live for him as he
commanded, who when we have gone to him will demand an account, being judge of
all insolences; for the groanings of those wronged become punishment of those
who wrong. =2.18= And let Alexander threaten these things to those who want
gold and fear death; toward us his two weapons have fallen; for the Brahmans
neither love gold nor fear death. Go then and say to Alexander that Dandamis
has no need of your things; because of this he will not come to you. And if
you have need of Dandamis, come to him." =2.19= And Alexander having heard
these things from Onesicritus desired rather to see him, because a naked old
man had conquered him who had taken down many nations. He goes then with
fifteen friends into the forest of Dandamis and having come near him he came
down from the horse and put off the diadem and all the boastfulness about him.
And having entered alone into the forest, where Dandamis was, he greeted him
and said: "Greetings, Dandamis, teacher of the Brahmans and president of
wisdom; I am present to you having heard your name, since you did not come to
us." And Dandamis said: "Greetings also to you yourself, on whose account many
cities are troubled and nations are unsettled." =2.20= And Alexander having
sat by his feet at that hour saw the earth clean from bloods; and Dandamis:
"Why are you present to us, Alexander? What do you wish to carry away from our
wilderness? What you seek we do not have, of what we have acquired you
yourself have no need. We honor God, we love men, we neglect gold and we
despise death, we take no thought of pleasures. But you fear death, you love
gold, you reach after pleasures, you hate men, you despise God." And Alexander
to him: "I have come to learn something wise from you; for they say you
converse with God; for I wish to know in what you differ from the Greeks or
what more than the rest you see or think." =2.21= And Dandamis answered to
him: "I too wish to serve you words of the wisdom of God and to put in you a
god-befitting mind, but you do not have room in your soul so as to contain the
gift of God being served to you from me; for insatiable desires and unfillable
love of money and demonic love of rule have filled your soul, which now fight
against me, because I draw you aside here and you are not murdering nations of
many peoples pouring out blood. And those with you are distressed at me,
because they see a city remaining and men being saved. And you have said you
will come even to the Ocean and after it to another inhabited world and after
that again to another and you will be greatly distressed when you do not have
those whom you will conquer. How then can I speak to you words of God's wisdom
when your understanding is filled with so great boastfulness and immeasurable
desires, which not even the whole world serving was able to satisfy? =2.22=
You were created short and naked and you passed into the world as one man and
having grown why do you slaughter all? That you may take the things of all?
And when you conquer all and acquire every inhabited world, you will occupy
only so much earth as I lying down or you sitting; for being lord of so much
earth you will pass from here, so that we the contemptible without battle and
war have all things equal to you: earth, water, air; and all that I have, I
have justly and I desire nothing. But you, warring and pouring out bloods and
slaughtering men, if you acquire all rivers, you drink water equal to me. And
I am not in agony nor do I bear wounds nor do I raze cities and I have earth
and water equal to you {and I need nothing}. =2.23= This then, Alexander,
learn as wise from me: wish to have nothing as a poor man and all things are
yours and you will need nothing; for desire is mother of poverty, and poverty
is a work of disorder treated with a bad drug, grief, and never finding what
it seeks nor resting upon what it has, but always being tortured over what it
has not acquired. =2.24= And you will be rich {pleasantly} as I am, if you
wish to live with me and if you attend to me and hear my words, and


you will acquire from my goods. For God is a friend to me and rejoicing in his
works I converse, and he becomes living in me. I do not hear bad men. Heaven
is a roof to me, all earth a bed to me, forests a table, fruits enjoyment of
food, rivers servants {of thirst}. I do not eat flesh as a lion, nor do the
meats of other animals rot in me, nor do I become a tomb of dead irrational
creatures; for providence brings me food, fruits, as a dear mother milk to the
one born. =2.25= But you seek, Alexander, to learn from me what I have
acquired beyond the other men or what I know that is wise beyond many. You see
me, I live as I was created from the beginning. I live as I was born from the
mother, naked without wealth and cares; because of this, as many things as God
does, I know and what must come to be, I understand. But you are amazed,
divining {and you are not ashamed} concerning the things that come upon you
day by day, not understanding the works of God shown to you according to the
hour; famines, wars, thunderbolts, droughts, rains and destructions of fruits
I know beforehand; and how and from where these come to be and because of
what, providence furnishes me knowledge. And this also greatly gladdens me,
‖that of his own works God has made me a counselor as a beloved son‖, that God
with his own works {as counselor} has made righteousness toward me‖. If fear
of enemies comes upon a king or another terror, he comes to me as to an angel
of God {seeking prudence}, and I having met with the providence of God
persuade it to give something good to the one present to me; and having
dissolved this fear I send him away with confidence. =2.26= What is better,
tell me, to harm men and to have a name of bad repute or rather to guard these
{and to do good} and to be known as a benefactor? And what befits the sons of
God, to make war and to raze the things crafted by providence or to make peace
and to build up again the things that have been labored and thrown down as a
servant of the craftsman? This authority will not benefit you, king Alexander,
nor the multitude of gold or the many elephants nor the varied and diverse
clothing about you nor the army now surrounding you nor horses and the
spear-bearers and all that you have taken from the others with wars and
battle; but you will be benefited the greatest things, if you are persuaded by
my words and obey my voice. =2.27= And you, Alexander, if you murder me, I do
not fear, {me} saying the things that profit you; for I will go away to my
God, who constructed all things; for he himself knows my cause and nothing has
escaped this one, whose eyes are all the stars, sun and moon, and he himself
will judge also the others who do wrong, whom you will not escape nor will you
have a place where you will flee this one {having risen nor will you pass by
this one's judgment}; so that {king Alexander} do not throw down what God
wishes to be created nor force to make vanish what that one wishes to order,
nor pour out blood of cities nor pass through dead nations {upon them}; for it
is better to live for yourself rather than to kill others and not, having
died, to bless others, and to know that to seize the things of others is
rather a loss than to consider this a gain. =2.28= Why having become one soul
do you wish to dissolve so many nations? Why foolishly rejoicing do you fill
the inhabited world with many evils? Why do you see the misfortunes of others
as your own gains? Why do you laugh at those weeping? Remember me in
wildernesses, the naked and poor, and gain yourself and having dissolved the
wars greet the dear peace of providence and do not seek to have courage in
evils, but to live a carefree life with us; throw off from yourself the
fleeces of the sheep and do not take refuge in a dead covering. Then you will
honor yourself having imitated us, having become such as you were also
created. For a soul is tested toward virtue in wilderness. =2.29= Choose
therefore, O king Alexander, to live our immaterial life; I do not know if you
are so blessed as to be able, having been persuaded by our words, to find
yourself. Now Macedonians wait for you, that they may throw down cities and
slaughter men and plunder their things, and today those who


murder are distressed seeing a nation of others' bloods being saved; for they
are soldiers of their own greed having an unjust pretext. =2.30= When will you
acquire a carefree life, the one allotted to you from God, that you may both
live for yourself and not kill others? Now then hearing these words why do you
delay thereafter to attend to yourself? Or do you still seek to seize and to
trouble nations and to murder men? Both what you have worked, and what you are
about to do. But if you do not endure my words you will be removed from here,
and being in the aether I will see you paying the penalties of these things
and with bitter groanings lamenting, giving the accounts of the things done by
you. And then you will remember the divine words served by me, when horses
taught for wars and multitudes of spear-bearers no longer follow. Then you
will lament groaning seeking the life you lost in vain into disorderly
troubles and wars of innocent bloods, when you have nothing else to see around
you except memory of what you heaped for yourself; for I know the punishments
brought from God justly upon unjust men; then you will say to me there: "You
were a good counselor to me before, O Dandamis"; for there souls will stand by
you that were warred upon in vain unjustly by you. To how many will you
suffice then making defense? And you will be benefited nothing having been
called and having appeared great. He who now wishes to conquer the world then
conquered." =2.31= And Alexander heard him very gladly and was not angered.
For there was also in him a divine spirit, but by some evil demon he was
turned toward murders and troubles. And being greatly struck at the
paradoxical words of Dandamis Alexander answered saying: =2.32= "True teacher
of the Brahmans, Dandamis, even remaking men who happen upon you by the wisdom
of providence, whom I, having learned from Calanus and having longed to meet,
found higher than all men because of the spirit in you. I know that you say
all things truly; for God generated you and he himself sent you down in these
places, in which it is permitted you to be happy being untroubled in all
nature, rich, lacking nothing, enjoying much quiet. =2.33= But I, what shall I
do, who dwell with unceasing fears and am flooded with continual troubles?
Having many who keep me I fear these more than the enemies; the friends are
worse than the opponents, who plot against me day by day more than my enemies,
and I can neither live without them nor again do I take heart being with them;
for those whom I fear, by them I am kept; by day troubling nations, and when
night has come being troubled by my reasonings, lest someone standing over me
overpower me with a sword. Woe is me. And having punished those who disobey me
I am distressed, and not having punished I am despised again. =2.34= And how
is it possible for me to deny these works? For even if I should wish to live
in wilderness, I am not permitted by the shield-bearers. And it is not
permitted me rather, even if I were able, to run away from these things,
having obtained these by the vote. What then shall I defend to God, who
granted this lot when I came to be? =2.35= And you, O elder, a precious
possession of God, in return for the things by which you benefited me and
gladdened me with the words of your wisdom and tamed me from wars, receive my
gifts, which I have brought you, and do not dishonor me; for I am benefited
honoring wisdom." =2.36= And having said these things Alexander to Dandamis
nodded to his servants. And they brought to him gold and stamped silver and
every kind of clothing {costly and sheep} and loaves and oil. And Dandamis
seeing laughed and said to Alexander: =2.37= "Persuade then the birds singing
about the forest to take gold and silver and to sound better; if then you
cannot persuade these, neither would you persuade me to become worse than
them; for what I do not take, neither eating nor drinking {nor do I watch},
some useless possession, nor do I watch a thing harmful to the soul nor will I
now bind my free life from all care nor will I vainly trouble my pure
understanding; may it not be; but neither do I need to buy anything dwelling
in wildernesses; for God gives me all things as a gift, fruits for food and


water for drink, forests for a place of a house, air for the increase of all
things. For God sells nothing for gold, but he grants all things giving good
wits to those who wish to take {these things then are nothing to me}. =2.38= I
have been wrapped in a garment which my mother bore me having from
birth-pangs. I am nourished by air and I gladly see myself {such}. Why do you
force me to put about myself a bond of the whole body? To me freedom from care
is sweeter than all creation and sweeter than honey is the drink from the
river healing the thirst according to nature. And if these loaves too are for
food, why did you burn them about with fire? I do not eat a remnant of fire
nor do I take away another's food; let the fire that tasted them consume them.
And that I may not dishonor you who honor wisdom, I receive the oil. =2.39=
And having said these things Dandamis {took the oil} and having stood up he
went about the forest and having gathered woods he made a heap and having
kindled a pyre he said: "A Brahman has all things and is nourished by
providence." And having kindled the fire sufficiently he poured on, until he
consumed all the oil, and he raised a hymn to God saying: "Immortal God, I
give thanks to you in everything; for you alone truly reign over the whole
providing to your creature all things richly for food; and having crafted this
world you preserve it waiting for the souls which you sent out there, that
having lived as citizens securely you may honor them as God, and those who
disobeyed the ordinances you may hand over to condemnation; for all just
judgment is with you and unbounded life is ready with you; for with eternal
kindness you have mercy on all." =2.40= And Alexander having heard all these
things and having seen, having wondered greatly and having been pricked at the
wise and true words of Dandamis, went away carrying back all the gifts he had
brought except the oil, which the fire consumed. And Dandamis said: =2.41=
"Such are we all {we}, Alexander; but your friend Calanus became a bad man to
us, having imitated our life for a short time, and since he did not happen to
be dear to God, having left us he ran away to the Greeks and contrary to
custom having seen our mysteries and having paraded these out to profane
nations from here he transferred himself into immortal fire. And you are
master of a bad nation of Macedonians, formerly you were reviling Brahmans and
you were commanding these to be destroyed having been persuaded by false
words; which does not befit a king and one who cares to rule nations, to be
persuaded by those who slander. =2.42= For we will give an account of our own
life to God, when we go away to him; for we are his works, all who lay claim
to his righteousness; we despise empty glory which is among the foolish. How
then can you think our things, living badly and neglecting the truly
beautiful? But we the Brahmans, remembering how we were generated, live with
that nature and we see how we can live blamelessly; we live in freedom from
care taking thought about nothing; for care of the matter of life of mortals
separates mind from God; for providence will demand of each of us an account
of his own judgment and we will give penalties of its deeds. Because of this
we rejoice sitting in wildernesses and in the midst of forests, that we may
set the mind upon everything pleasing to God, that the idle talk of the
remaining men may not draw our souls aside from the doxology of God. =2.43=
For blessed is he who needs nothing {of the superfluous}, and who cares for
the glory of the master of all. And empty glory needs all things; for the one
who wishes to please all must be a slave of all. And we have no need to seek
cities; for an assembly is of plotting and a farm of much evil. And God has
created great houses for us, high mountains and shady forests, from where of a
pure nature {memory of that given from God is saved} eating fruits in these we
luxuriate and drinking water from this we are gladdened and we have a most
sweet rest upon leaves and resting upon these we put off the toil. =2.44= How
then, being slaves of many, can you command those free in all things? For you
are disorderly slaves


of your own soul desiring many and various things; for if you wish to
have many garments, you need the shepherd, the weaver, the fuller or even the
reaper. Do not say to me: "I do not wear soft garments"; for it is equal
slavery to take thought over small and great; the one reaching after a little
gold will also desire the more, the one wishing to be king of a small city
will clearly also seize to rule the greater. But you also pride yourselves in
a little shining purple on a garment, though the Indians being all-purple are
our slaves wearing all-purple; and you consider the purple magnificent even if
you use a little; and if the beautiful among you is a little, in truth you are
poor and you marvel at the small things. =2.45= And why do you murder animals
that are children of earth and themselves most useful to you? For some you
shear and you wrap yourselves in their wool, and some you milk and you eat the
milk, and with some you also farm selling the fruits, and upon some having
mounted you also make war seizing things of others; and these you murder
unjustly and such is the wage for the animals among you. You wrap their
fleeces about you outside, and you carry their fleshes inside and you become
walking tombs of dead animals. How then can a soul weighed down with so great
dishonor of out-of-place works receive a mind of God? Leave the meats outside
two days and you will see what will come to be; you will not be able to endure
their smell, but you will even flee outside. How many uncleannesses enter
through these into a soul and pass into the kidneys of those who desire these?
How then can a divine spirit of such a one inhabit the sense? You eat meats,
which fatten a body, but melt a soul, beget wrath, and drive out peace, tame
self-control, but awaken incontinence, gush out vomitings and house diseases;
the spirit of the flesh-eating of mortals withdraws and a murderous demon
dwells. But fruits of tree-nuts and plants of shores left outside give off a
most beautiful vapor and having been feasted on by the wise they conceive a
god-befitting mind and the body is seemly. These God planted as food of
mortals for prudence; but your mind has been destroyed by gluttony, you
breathe the wrath of beasts being filled with fleshes of animals, you are
corrupted vessels of rotten limbs. You are worse than wolves and lions and all
untamed beasts; for wolves if they were able to eat fruits would not seek
meats. Bulls horses deer and another nature of animals very many have a more
just food than you, being nourished with plants of shores and drinking water
and dwelling in the mountains. Because of this strength is dear to these and
the sinews have been fixed strong. Why then do you not imitate these being
nourished by the providence of God? =2.46= But on the pretext of offering of
sacrifices and expenditure of fire you prepare these as food for yourselves;
and as many as are vigorous and lasting, these are not plotted against by you;
for you seek very much nourishment and you spend much because of your greed
and you toil very much upon small animals for a little and vain pleasure. Your
toil is mindless and in destruction. Because of all these things your life is
wretched and unlucky. =2.47= We then do not even drink water willingly when
thirst is not present. And when thirst is present from necessity we ward this
off that comes from nature with springs of waters, which even when we are not
drinking pour the streams. But you invent arts for pleasure of the belly, that
even not hungering through the varied art of the cooks you may burst the
wretched belly furnishing to it the things toward gluttony. You hunt air
through love of pleasure, you sift the sea with nets through your many
desires. You campaign upon the mountains through your greed boasting upon the
prowess of dogs, and you speak evil of beasts crafted by providence and you
call them themselves dwellers of a bad wilderness being displeased against
providence. And some you pursue, and some you wish to hunt, and most you
murder, and the more untamed of these having seized and having shut in cages
you bring into the cities, not that you may sow through them or accomplish
some other useful service in these, but that having outraged the one of your
own kind and work of God, man, you may


destroy him; and this one bound with violent bonds you throw to a loosed beast
and sitting opposite you gaze taking pleasure in the incurable misfortune at
the one being wrestled down by the beasts and laughing; you are eager to
dissolve by a most evil fate the common image of our nature that was molded by
the hand of God; and after this one has been destroyed by it you yourselves
again having slaughtered the beast how do you turn away, and speaking evil you
say: "O bad beast and murderer of men." And the most terrible of all, when the
beast has been filled with bloods and fleshes of men, you yourselves again
having slaughtered this eat and you are found more untamed than the wicked
beasts, eating these themselves and heaping irreconcilable misfortunes for
yourselves in the belly. =2.48= Again having deprived the poorer of lands you
build houses, that being warmed you may digest quietly. And you force your
intestines to be broad, composites of arts from measureless incontinence,
eating of animals and drunkenness with wine. But we pray not even to thirst
for water; for we are drawn aside from truth, when we water a body excessively
with water. But you coming together rejoice upon drinkings of wine, and if you
are not driven mad drinking you do not stop. And after these things you
stretch out hands upon libations and you raise your eyes to the sun having
completely driven out your prudence, the mind being weighed down from the
wine. More fortunate among you rather are those who are mad; for those are
drunk not having bought wine. But you take care for the price of the wine,
that having bought this you may drive out prudence by drunkenness, bringing in
upon yourselves mindless madness, breaking hands against one another and each
filling the neighbor with wounds. And these things you do without perception.
Then thus when the wine has blown away you learn the things done by them from
the pains. And not even thus are you able to guard against the drunkenness.
=2.49= Again having eaten many things you are not able to digest them, but
later having withdrawn you vomit your incontinence through the mouth, turning
nature from below upward; drinking without measure you are filled as vessels
and when the wine has smelled you at last gush this out, foolish, having
turned the body walking on the head instead of the feet as the animals; you
force yourselves to be filled and with insolence you are emptied. Taking on
disease you crush the body and in the things in which you seem to treat the
belly, from there you are pained and always being filled you have been
deprived of every pleasure; for the end of measureless satiety is torture of
body and not health. For you are punished with diseases because of the lack of
measure. And what is the luxury of the body toward happiness of soul? And if
you wish to display that you have many things, grant the superfluous to those
in need among you. But as we hear, you are poor in truth, so that you do not
even give bread to those who ask you, and you are eager to heap numberless
substances for yourselves; for you are slaves of bodies and of insatiable
intestines. =2.50= For because of these passions there are many physicians
among you who empty your greed or choke the much-eating with hunger or with
some other arts bridle the disease and those who formerly poured out much wine
as into a pierced vessel they tame with thirst, not even deeming them worthy
of a drop of water. And those who once had heavy heads now being tortured
continue dry. And then contrary to nature they drank the wine, but now
according to nature they desire water. And then they were bound by insatiable
desire, but now they are racked by the continence of necessity. =2.51= But we
Brahmans do not seek wine, we do not acquire madness of our own choosing; and
water, as much as we wish, we have from divine providence, with which we also
take pleasure. And {through it} being of sound mind we treat the thirst
according to nature not acquiring madness; for we would rather choose death
than to be mastered by drunkenness. To be handed over to a beast is much
rather better than to be thrown from the glory of God through much drinking.
For the one who is drunk is worse than irrational animals, having died in
prudence; for having lost the mind he has been alienated from God. =2.52= And
what of those in glory among you? They are deceived by wealth looking upon
false things


and expecting to have only the things here they wrong one another, and even
murdering the weaker they deprive them even of the few things they have
acquired. And the end of these death awaits. =2.53= And what shall we say
concerning Epicureans, men soaked in myrrh and walking daintily in soft
clothing of women and polluting the air with juices of forced spices? And what
need even to speak concerning Stoic learned philosophers who have loved money?
And what again shall we say concerning the Peripatetic philosophers? All these
are wondrous and great among you, but not among Brahmans. =2.54= And we hear
that a new nature of men has been invented among you, that you cut the males
forcing them to make them female. And the man among you having been generated
becomes neither sowing as a man nor giving birth as a woman, and he lives only
toward his own insolence. Who would not pity you seeing this vanishing among
you? =2.55= And pitying you we benefit nothing by your judgment not running
together with the purpose of the Brahmans. We then hate arrogance and we
love all human nature,
sitting forth as teachers of the truth and pointers of the way
of righteousness to those who wish to be benefited, in this life as in a
very great house living toward all with the soul naked just as also with the
body, that is we are rich with the whole soul toward every man. =2.56= And we
hear that Macedonia forces all things having itself first been forced; for all
are slaves of the soul that urges. But of all these things Brahmans are
inexperienced, being warred upon by no one; for we desire none of the things
among you. =2.57= And you, if you wish, king Alexander, to think our things,
having come to Indians and Brahmans, having seen, dwell naked in wilderness;
for otherwise we do not receive you, unless you first throw off from yourself
all the offices, in which you rejoice and think great things. And words of
providence will touch you, which I had said to you before, and you will love
from your heart all that you then praised having wondered; and if you are
persuaded by me and do these things, no one at all will still make war on you
nor will anyone be able thereafter to take away from you anything of what you
have not acquired; for if you are persuaded by me strongly and securely, no
one will find anything of your life beside you; for forests will thereafter
nourish you, providence furnishing you all things just as also to us, and in
all things you will be rich having imitated Brahmans exactly. And your benefit
will be written down for me unto eternal joy; for this you also asked from me
from the beginning. We then envy no one of those who wish truly to be pious
toward God and who wish to emulate our life, pitying all nature of man."
