* EPIGRAM 1
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:
=1= Have reverence for one who needs guest-gifts and a house,
=2= you who dwell in the steep city, Cyme the fair-eyed maiden,
=3= at the lowest foot of high-tressed Sardene,
=4= drinking the ambrosial water of the divine river,
=5= eddying Hermus, whom immortal Zeus begot.
* EPIGRAM 2
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h2
:END:
=1= May my feet swiftly bear me to a city of reverent men;
=2= for their heart is willing and their counsel is best.
* EPIGRAM 3
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3
:END:
=1= I am a bronze maiden, and I lie upon the tomb of Midas;
=2= so long as water flows and tall trees flourish,
=3= and the rising sun shines and the bright moon also,
=4= and rivers run and the sea washes up,
=5= remaining here upon this much-lamented tomb
=6= I shall tell passers-by that Midas is buried here.
* EPIGRAM 4
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4
:END:
=1= To what a fate did father Zeus give me to become a prey,
=2= nursing me as an infant on the knees of a reverend mother.
=3= The city which once by the counsel of aegis-bearing Zeus
=4= the people of Phricon, riders of wanton horses,
=5= younger men deciding Ares of raging fire, built with towers,
=6= Aeolian Smyrna, neighbor of the sea, shaken by the deep,
=7= through which the bright water of sacred Meles goes;
=8= from there the daughters of Zeus, glorious children, setting out,
=9= wished to make famous the divine land and the city of men.
=10= But they refused the holy voice, the fame of song,
=11= in folly; and one of them, having suffered, shall consider this
again,
=12= he who with insults contrived my fate among them.
=13= But the doom which the god granted me even at my birth
=14= I will endure, bearing unfulfilled things with a patient heart;
=15= nor do my dear limbs remain to stay in the sacred streets
=16= of Cyme, they are eager, and my great heart urges me
=17= to go to the people of foreigners, small though I am.
* EPIGRAM 5
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5
:END:
=1= Thestorides, of the many things unforeseen for mortals,
=2= nothing is more inscrutable than the mind of men.
* EPIGRAM 6
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h6
:END:
=1= Hear, Poseidon, of great strength, earth-shaker,
=2= lord of the wide dancing-place and of tawny Helicon,
=3= give a fair breeze and to see a harmless return
=4= to the sailors who are escorts and commanders of the ship;
=5= and grant that coming to the foot of high-cliffed Mimas
=6= I may meet with reverent and holy mortals,
=7= and that I may punish the man who, deceiving my mind,
=8= angered Zeus of guests and the guest-table.
* EPIGRAM 7
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h7
:END:
=1= Lady Earth, all-giving, giver of honey-hearted wealth,
=2= how then you have been made well-soiled for some of men,
=3= and ill-clodded and rough for those with whom you were angry.
* EPIGRAM 8
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h8
:END:
=1= Seafaring sailors, like to hateful ruin,
=2= like cowering shearwaters, having an unenviable life,
=3= have reverence for the majesty of Zeus of guests who rules on high;
=4= for terrible afterwards is the vengeance of Zeus of guests against
whoever sins.

* EPIGRAM 10
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h10
:END:
=1= Another pine of yours sends forth better fruit
=2= on the peaks of much-folded windy Ida,
=3= where the iron of Ares for earth-dwelling mortals
=4= shall be, when the Cebrenian men hold it.
* EPIGRAM 11
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h11
:END:
=1= Glaucus, watcher of flocks Kuester: dear one, of mortals, MSS., I
will put a word in your heart;
=2= first give the dogs their dinner at the courtyard doors;
=3= for this is better; for he first hears
=4= a man approaching and a wild beast going to the enclosure.
* EPIGRAM 12
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h12
:END:
=1= Hear me as I pray, Kourotrophos, and grant that this woman
=2= refuse the love and bed of young men;
=3= and let her take pleasure in grey-templed old men,
=4= whose season is blunted, but whose heart is eager.
* EPIGRAM 13
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h13
:END:
=1= Children are a man's crown, and towers of a city,
=2= and horses in turn the ornament of a plain, and ships of the sea,
=3= and possessions increase a house, and reverend kings
=4= seated in the agora are an ornament for the people to behold
Ruhnken: and for others, Sources: The Contest of Homer adds the
verse: and a people sitting in assemblies to be looked upon.
=5= and a house is more reverend to behold with a burning fire
=6= on a winter's day, when the son of Cronos snows.
* EPIGRAM 14
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h14
:END:
=1= If you will give a wage I will sing, O potters.
=2= Come here, Athena, and hold your hand over the kiln.
=3= And may the cups and all the dishes be well finished Pollux: be
blackened, be blackened, Life of Homer.
=4= and be well baked and fetch a price of honor,
=5= many sold in the agora, many in the streets,
=6= and much profit gained, and for us too, as I sing for them.
=7= But if you turn to shamelessness and take up lies,
=8= then I call together the destroyers of kilns,
=9= Smash together with Crash and Unquenchable and Shatter
=10= and Crudebake, who would furnish many evils to this craft;
=11= sack the fire-blazing house, and with it let the kiln
=12= all be thrown into confusion, while the potters wail greatly.
=13= As a horse's jaw crunches, so may the kiln crunch,
=14= making all the pottery inside it fine.
=15= Come here also, daughter of the Sun, Circe of many drugs,
=16= cast wild drugs, and harm both them and their works.
=17= And let Chiron also lead here many Centaurs,
=18= those who fled the hands of Heracles and those who perished,
=19= let them strike these works badly, and let the kiln fall;
=20= and let they themselves groaning look upon their wretched works.
=21= And I shall rejoice seeing their ill-fated craft;
=22= and whoever peeks over, may all his face
=23= be burned with fire, so that all may know to do what is fitting.
* EPIGRAM 15
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h15
:END:
=1= We have turned to the house of a man of great power,
=2= who is greatly able, and greatly is fitting Ilgen: thunders, MSS.
always blessed.
=3= Open of yourselves, doors; for Wealth will enter
=4= much, and with Wealth also blooming Cheer
=5= and good Peace; and as many vessels as there are, may they be full,
=6= and may the cake always crawl in a heap over the kneading-trough.
=7= Now a barley one, fair-faced, sesame-filled
=1= And the child's wife shall step down from the chariot for you;
=9= and strong-footed mules shall bring her to this house;
=10= and she herself may weave at the loom standing upon amber.
=12= I will come, I will come yearly, like the swallow
=12= I stand in the porch barefoot; but quickly bring
=13= persai of Apollo Gyiatis.
