There were various opinions in the circumcision among the sons of Israel, of
those against the tribe of Judah and the Christ, these: Essenes; Galileans;
Hemerobaptists; Masbothaeans; Samaritans; Sadducees; Pharisees. These Eusebius
cited in book iv of the History, ch. 22. From these heretics, that is, some
accuse Symeon son of Clopas as being of David and a Christian. And thus he
bears witness being one hundred twenty years old, under Trajan Caesar and the
consular Atticus. And the same man, 208 Hegesippus, says that his accusers
also, when those of the royal tribe of the Jews were then being sought, as
being of it, happened to be caught.

These Eusebius in book iii of the History, ch. 32. From the fifth book, or
last, of the same Work. ON THE MARTYRDOM OF ST. JAMES THE JUST. James, the
brother of the Lord, succeeds to the church together with the apostles, he who
was named by all Just from the times of the Lord even until us. Since many
were called James; but this one was holy from his mother's womb. He drank
neither wine nor strong drink, nor did he eat anything that had life; a razor
did not go up upon his head; he did not anoint himself with oil, and he did
not use the bath. To this one alone was it permitted to enter into the holy
places. For he did not wear wool, but linen. And he alone used to enter into
the temple; and he was found lying upon his knees, and asking forgiveness for
the people; so that his knees became hard like a camel's, 209 because of
always bending on the knee worshipping God, and asking forgiveness for the
people. For because of the excess of his righteousness he was called Just and
Oblias; which is in Greek bulwark of the people and righteousness, as the
prophets show concerning him. Some then of the seven sects among the people,
those previously written by me in the Memoirs, used to ask him what the door
of Jesus was; and he said that this one was the Savior. Of whom some believed
that Jesus is the Christ. But the aforementioned sects did not believe either
in resurrection or that he is coming to render to each according to his works.
And as many as also believed, believed through James. When then many even of
the rulers were believing, there was an uproar of the Jews and scribes and
Pharisees saying that the whole people is in danger of expecting Jesus as the
Christ. They said then, having come together to James: We entreat you;
restrain the people, since it has gone astray after Jesus, as if he were the
210 Christ. We entreat you to persuade all who have come for the day of the
Passover concerning Jesus. For all obey you. For we bear witness to you, and
the whole people, that you are just, and that you do not respect persons.
Persuade then the crowd concerning Jesus not to go astray. For also the whole
people and all of us obey you. Stand then upon the pinnacle of the temple, so
that you may be conspicuous from above, and your words may be easily heard by
all the people. For because of the Passover all the tribes have come together,
together also with the nations. The aforementioned scribes and Pharisees then
set James upon the pinnacle of the temple, and they cried out to him and said:
Just one, to whom we all ought to obey, since the people is going astray after
Jesus the crucified, tell us what the door of Jesus is. And he answered with a
loud voice: Why do you ask me concerning Jesus the son of man? And he himself
sits in heaven at the right hand of the great Power, and is about to come 211
upon the clouds of heaven. And when many were fully persuaded, and glorifying
at the testimony of James, and saying Hosanna to the son of David, then again
the same scribes and Pharisees said to one another: We have done badly in
providing such a testimony to Jesus; but going up, let us throw him down, so
that they, being afraid, may not believe him. And they cried out saying: Oh,
oh, even the just one has gone astray. And they fulfilled the scripture
written in Isaiah: Let us take away the just one, because he is unprofitable
to us; therefore they shall eat the fruits of their works. Going up then they
threw down the just one, and they said to one another: Let us stone James the
just. And they began to stone him, since having been thrown down he did not
die, but turning he set his knees saying: I entreat you, Lord God Father,
forgive them; for they do not know what they are doing. And while they were
thus stoning him, one of the priests of the sons of Rechab son of Rechabim, of
those 212 testified to by Jeremiah the prophet, cried out saying: Stop, what
are you doing; the Just one is praying for you. And one of them, one of the
fullers, taking the club with which he used to beat the clothes, brought it
down upon the head of the just one. And thus he bore witness; and they buried
him on the spot beside the temple, and still his monument remains beside the
temple. This one has become a true witness to Jews and Greeks that Jesus is
the Christ. And immediately Vespasian besieges them.

These Eusebius cited in book ii of the History, ch. 23. From an uncertain
book, but perhaps the last, of the same Work. ON THE GRANDSONS OF JUDE ACCUSED
BEFORE DOMITIAN. And there were still surviving those of the family of the
Lord, grandsons of Jude, who was called his brother according to the flesh,
whom they informed against as being of the family of David. And the evocatus
brought these to Domitian Caesar; for he feared the 213 coming of the Christ
as also Herod did; and he asked them if they were of David; and they
confessed. Then he asked them how many possessions they have, or of how much
money they are masters. And they both said that nine thousand denarii belonged
to them only, the half belonging to each of them; and these they said they did
not have in silver, but in the valuation of land of thirty-nine plethra only.
From which they both paid the taxes and supported themselves by working with
their own hands. Then they also showed their own hands; presenting as witness
of their manual labor the hardness of the body, and the calluses impressed
upon their own hands from the continual work. And being asked concerning the
Christ and his kingdom, of what sort it was and when and where it would
appear, they gave an account that it is not worldly nor earthly, but heavenly
and angelic, to come about at the consummation of the age, when coming in
glory he would judge 214 the living and the dead, and will render to each
according to his practices; upon which Domitian, having condemned nothing of
them, but even having despised them as paltry, released them free, and by an
edict stopped the persecution against the church; and those released led the
churches, as being both witnesses and of the family of the Lord; and when
peace had come, they remained in life until Trajan.

These Eusebius set in book iii of the History, ch. 20.

They come then, the grandsons of Jude just previously mentioned, and they lead
every church as witnesses, and of the family of the Lord. And when deep peace
had come in every church, they remain until Trajan Caesar. Until the
aforementioned Symeon son of Clopas, of the Lord's uncle, having been
slandered by the heresies, was likewise himself accused on the same account
before Atticus the consular. And being tortured for many days, he 215 bore
witness; so that all were exceedingly amazed, even the consular, how being one
hundred twenty years old he endured; and he was ordered to be crucified. Upon
these things the same man Hegesippus, narrating the things concerning those
indicated, adds that until those times the church remained a pure and
uncorrupted virgin, while those who, if there were any, were attempting to
corrupt the sound rule of the saving preaching, were still then lurking
somewhere in unseen darkness.

These Eusebius cited in ch. 32 of the same book. From an uncertain book of the
same Work. And after James the just had borne witness, as also the Lord, on
the same account, again Symeon son of Clopas, of his uncle, is appointed
bishop; whom all put forward as second bishop, being a cousin of the Lord. For
this reason they called the church a virgin; for it had not yet been corrupted
by vain hearings. But Thebuthis begins to corrupt it because he was not made
bishop. He too was from the seven sects among the people; from whom 216 Simon,
whence the Simonians; and Cleobius, whence the Cleobians; and Dositheus,
whence the Dositheans; and Gorthaeus, whence the Gorthaeans; and Masbotheus,
whence the Masbothaeans; from these Menandrianists, and Marcionists, perhaps
Marcianists, see the note, and Carpocratians; and Valentinians, and
Basilidians, and Saturnilians; each differently and otherwise introduced his
own opinion. From these false christs; false prophets; false apostles; who
divided the unity of the church with corrupting words against God and against
his Christ.

These Eusebius brings in book iv of the History, ch. 22. From an uncertain
book of the same Work. It is possible then to hear, says Eusebius, where
above, immediately before the passage now about to follow, after certain
things said by him concerning Clement's letter to the Corinthians, adding,
that is Hegesippus, these things: And the church of the Corinthians remained,
etc. Eusebius also in book iii of the History, ch. 16. And that indeed in the
time of the one indicated, that is Clement, the things of the Corinthian
sedition were stirred, Hegesippus is a trustworthy witness. 217 From an
uncertain book of the same Work. And the church of the Corinthians remained in
the right word, until Primus was bishop in Corinth; with them I associated as
I sailed to Rome, and I spent sufficient days with the Corinthians; in which
we were refreshed together in the right word. And having come to be in Rome, I
made a succession until Anicetus, whose deacon was Eleutherus. And after
Anicetus Soter succeeds, after whom Eleutherus. And in each succession and in
each city it is so, as the law preaches and the prophets and the Lord. These
Eusebius brings in book iv of the History, ch. 22. From an uncertain book of
the same Work. In these times Hegesippus was known, of whom we have already
previously used very many utterances; as setting forth from his tradition
certain things concerning the times of the apostles. In five writings then
this man, having recorded the unerring tradition of the apostolic preaching in
a most simple composition of writing, indicates the time in which he was
known, writing somehow thus 218 concerning those who from the beginning set up
the idols. For whom they made cenotaphs and temples as until now; of whom is
also Antinous, slave of Hadrian Caesar, of whom also the Antinoean contest is
held, which came to be in our time. For he also founded a city named after
Antinous, and prophets, perhaps to be supplied from Jerome, he established in
the temple. See the note. These Eusebius says, and cites, in book iv of the
History, ch. 8. Concerning the same Work, Eusebius book iv of the History, ch.
22. And Hegesippus writes very many other things also, of which we have
already previously made mention in part, setting the histories beside the
times fittingly; and from the Gospel according to the Hebrews and the Syriac,
and especially from the Hebrew dialect he sets down certain things, showing
that he himself had believed from among the Hebrews; and he also mentions
other things as from Jewish unwritten tradition; and not this man alone, but
also Irenaeus and the whole choir of the ancients, called the Proverbs of
Solomon all-virtuous wisdom. And treating of the so-called apocrypha, he
records that some of these were fabricated in his times by certain heretics.
