D. And when he both thought these things and persuaded (for everything toward
pleasure is easy for deceit), they on the one hand were intending as those who
would raise the temple, and with much hand and eagerness they toiled about the
work; they say at any rate that even their women, those who

body to the work and to those laboring, but even carried out the soil in their
bosoms, and sparing neither costly clothing nor the softness of their limbs,
thought they were being pious and judged all things less than the undertaking.
But when they were driven together suddenly by a wild whirlwind and a shaking
of the earth toward one of the nearby sacred places, some set out as those who
would supplicate, others, which is wont to happen in such things, using the
thing at hand toward help, and others otherwise being carried with the
confusion, and falling in together with those running. There are some who say
that not even the sacred place received them, but coming to the gates thrown
open they met them shut by some invisible and unseen power, which works such
wonders toward the consternation of the impious =35.669= and the safety of the
pious; but what all already both say and believe, that as they were forcing
and contending about the entrance, fire stood having met them out of the
sacred place, and some it burned down and consumed (so that something like the
sufferings of the Sodomites happened about them, or the wonder about Nadab and
Abihu who both offered incense strangely and came into danger), and others,
having cut off the vital parts, it left as a living pillar of the threat and
motion of God against the sinners. This then is such, and let no one
disbelieve, because not even the other powers of God; but what is still more
paradoxical than this and more conspicuous, a light stood in the heaven
outlining the cross, and the shape and name formerly dishonored on earth by
the godless is now shown in heaven equally to all, and becomes a trophy for
God of the victory against the impious, higher than every trophy.
E. Toward these things what will they say, the wise of this age, and those who
make much of the things among themselves, those who draw the deep beards, and
who trail for us the elegant cloak? You also narrate against me your things,
you who write the long discourses, and compose the unbelievable histories, and
gape toward the things above, and lie against the heavenly things, and weave
from the motion of the stars the births and the things that happen. Tell me
also you your stars, the crown of Ariadne, and the lock of Berenice, and the
wanton Swan, and the insolent Bull; and if you wish, your Ophiuchus, and the
Capricorn, and the Lion, and as many others as having recognized them for evil
you made either gods or stars. Where do you have this circle in your learning?
And where the star that formerly ran upon Bethlehem from the east, the guide
of your Magi and their introducer? I also have something to say from the
heavenly things; that star declared the presence of Christ; this is the crown
of the victory of Christ. =35.672=
F. These things from the heavenly things and from the things above suffering
together with our things, according to the great harmony and kinship of the
whole; and let the psalm complete the things next for me; that You also
overthrew cities (as those ancient ones upon the same impieties), in the very
lawlessnesses against us, some flooded by seas, others brought down by
earthquake, so as almost to have also the remaining thing to say, that Their
memorial perished with a sound and with a famous destruction; for so great was
their fall, and such the crushing, even of those from the neighborhood, and
especially of those rejoicing at the impiety, that they need much time, if
indeed anyone would even dare this, toward restoration.
G. Was it then thus with earth and heaven; and did air not give a sign at such
times, nor was it then hallowed by the signs of the passion? Let them still
even now display the garments, the spectators and initiates of that wonder,
those then marked with the brands of the cross. For at the same time someone
was narrating these things, whether of our own, or

of those without, or was hearing those narrating, he also saw the wonder
happening beside himself, or in the neighbor, being starred, or seeing that
one such in the garments, more varied than every weaver's mosaic or
over-curious painting. From this what happens? So great a consternation of the
things seen, that almost all as from one signal and one voice called upon the
God of the Christians, and propitiated him with many praises and
supplications; and many not unto delays, but beside the very things that
happened, having run to our priests, and having begged much, both became a
part of the Church, and were initiated into the more perfect things, having
been purified by the sacred baptism, and having been benefited through the
fear.
H. These things then were thus; but he, being goaded and shaken by the rages
little by little, meets the very chief point of his own disasters. For when he
thought the things of the Christians were according to his mind, and from the
things already accomplished hoped that even everything is capturable =35.673=
for one who only wished, and having snatched some prosperity against the
western barbarians, he counsels this one counsel most intelligent and most
philanthropic. Having taken up from here a double army, one of hoplites, and
one of the demons leading him, in which he had more to be confident, he
campaigns against the Persians, having trusted more to unreason of rashness
than to safety of strength; and not even that was the most wise able to see,
that confidence and rashness, even if they should be near in the names, are
separated from one another very much in power, as also manliness which we
speak and unmanliness. For to be confident in the things that must be dared is
of manliness, just as to give way is of cowardice; but where the danger is
more, to go together and to shove, and not to be held back, is of rashness,
just as to withdraw is of safety. And it is not to be set down of the same
reason to guard the things that are, and to take on something of the things
that are not; for the one is especially and first to be honored by those who
have mind; and the other, if it be present with ease, is to be received; but
if it oppose, is to be overlooked. But he who for the sake of acquiring
something of the things hoped for risks all the things that exist is
exceedingly mindless. And he seems to me to resemble a boxer bad in the art,
putting forth his hands before setting the stance well; or a steersman, when
his ship is loosened and not in a seaworthy state, sinking an enemy ship, or
being eager to sink it. Of which things that man seems to me having thought
nothing, to attempt without looking around the things resolved, while the
Roman things were still in labor for him, and badly disposed especially by the
persecution, looking around at the foreign land; and being some Salmoneus
thundering from a hide, looking toward those Trajans and those Hadrians, of
whom the safety was admired no less than the manliness. But he did not think
of that Carus, nor Valerian, who paid the penalties of an unreasoning impulse,
that, what the tragedy says, I may not reproach the fortunes, they were
destroyed on the borders of the Persians, at the peak of prosperity.
I. But anyway these things seemed good, and of the impulse he was, having
brought together into one every wonder-working of divination and sorcery, of
spoken and unspoken =35.676= sacrifice, that in a short time the whole might
be destroyed. And the fair-offering as great and supernatural, O Christ, and
Word, and sufferings of the passionless, and mystery of the whole world! that
the whole race of the Christians be set beside the demons, if he should
prevail of the thing set forth. The first things then of the attempt for him,
even exceedingly youthful, and famous among many of those who think his
things, are these. For as much of the Assyrians as the Euphrates flowing
through, and passing by Persia, is mixed there with the Tigris, this he took
and cut, and having taken some of the forts in much desert of the one who
would hinder, whether having escaped notice through the speed of the attack,
or being thus generalled by the Persians, and being led on little by little
into the forward (for both are said), thus at any rate going forward, and with
the army marching beside, and with the ships through the river both carrying
grain and carrying equipment,

not small, the interval, and he assaults Ctesiphon; of which even to have
become near was judged by him a part of victory through the desire.
J. And from here already, as if sand of feet having been drawn from under, or
a storm having fallen against a ship, the affairs go backward. For Ctesiphon
is a strong fort and hard to take, fortified with a wall of baked brick, and a
deep trench, and the shallows from the river. And another fort makes it
stronger, whose name is Coche, composed with equal safety, as much as is
natural and as much as handmade, being united so much that both seem one city,
these being divided by the river in the middle; for it was not possible either
to take them out by a sudden raid, or to set them beside by siege, or to drive
through and especially with the naval force (for one would have been in danger
from the higher ground, being struck from both sides, and being barred), he
puts it behind himself, and he does it in this manner. Having broken off not
the least part of the Euphrates being among the greatest of rivers, and having
led it around, as much as to be navigable for ships from a canal (of which
they say even ancient traces appear), and having joined this to the Tigris a
little in front, thus he saves the ships given from the river to the river in
safety. Thus then he runs away from the danger from these forts. =35.677= But
as a Persian force having appeared beside him as he went forward, and always
some being added to the existing, thought it must not stand according to the
front, and run the risk without great necessity, it being possible to prevail
from surplus; but from the ambushes and the narrows wherever it was allowed,
striking and shooting, and occupying beforehand the vital parts of the
passage, easily barred him from the forward; at that time he was already in
much perplexity, and not having where he might turn he finds a wicked loosing
of the counsel.
K. For a certain man of those not without repute among the Persians, having
imitated Zopyrus the one against Babylon toward Cyrus, as having offended
indeed something against the king of the Persians, or rather greatest things
and upon greatest, and being especially ill-disposed to the things of that
one, and well-disposed to the affairs of the Romans because of this, and
having taken the being trusted from the fiction; Why these things, he says, O
king? How do you counsel so unsoundly about so great an affair? What is this
ship-borne grain to you and the superfluous burden, the teacher of
unmanliness? For nothing is so hard to fight and contentious as a belly, and
the having the salvation in hands. But if you are persuaded at all by me, you
will let this naval force farewell, and the following dissolution for this
noble army; and you yourself through another way both more well-resourced and
safer, of which I am your guide (and I say I am experienced if anyone else of
the Persian land), will both throw into the enemy land, and having hit the
things according to mind will come back; and you will do well to us at that
time, whenever you take by deed a trial of our goodwill and exhortation.
L. And when he said these things, and having said persuaded, for lightness is
easy to trust, and God especially driving together, all the terrible things
were together; fire had the ships, and the grain was not, and laughter was
added; for the slaughter was almost by his own hand; and the things of the
hopes were gone, and the guide went away together with the promises. And in a
circle the enemies, and the war flowing around; and the advance not easy, and
the food not well-resourced; and the army in despondency, and they had the
king through anger; and nothing of good hope was left; and one as in the
present things seemed alone, to be delivered from the wicked kingship and
generalship. =35.680=
M. The things then as far as this are such; but the things from here not one
account is said, but another is carried together and composed with another,
both of those present likewise at the battle; and of those absent. For some
say that he was shot down by Persians using disordered sallies, and darting
here and there with stupefaction; and that something like happened about him
to that of Cyrus the son of Parysatis, who, having gone up together with the
ten thousand against the

brother Artaxerxes, and fighting youthfully, destroyed the victory by
rashness; and others narrate some such account upon him; Having gone up upon
some hill of the high ones, so as from a lookout to take the army with a view,
and to learn how much was left to the war, when the multitude appeared to him
much, and more abundant than the hope; How terrible, to say, if we will lead
all these back to the land of the Romans; as if one begrudging them the
salvation. Upon which some one of the soldiers having grown harsh, and not
having held the anger, shoved against the inward parts, having disregarded his
own salvation. And as others, that some one of the barbarian jesters dared
this (who follow the soldiers, both a charming-away of grief and a seasoning
for drinks). And there are who even give this glory to some one of the
Saracens. Except he receives a blow truly vital, and saving for the whole
world, and by one cut of a slayer, penalty is demanded of many inward parts
badly trusted. Which I even marvel at, how, thinking from here to know all
things, the vain one, =35.681= this one thing, the blow against his own inward
parts, he was ignorant of.
N. And it is worthy not even to run by this of the man, having upon many a
greatest proof of that one's ill-fatedness. He lay upon the bank of the river,
and was in a bad way from the wound; and knowing many of those before him who
had been thought worthy of glory, that they might be judged above a human,
having been made to vanish from humans by some arts, and through this having
been judged gods, having been caught by desire of the same glory, and at the
same time being ashamed at the manner of the end through the ingloriousness of
the ill-counsel, what does he devise, and what does he do? for not even with
the life is wickedness consumed; he tries to throw the body down the river,
and toward this he used some of his own faithful as fellow-workers and
initiates of the unspoken things. And if some one of the royal eunuchs, having
perceived the affair, and having reported it to the others, had not hindered
the impulse by hatred of the crime, some other new god would even have
appeared to the mindless from a misfortune. But that one having thus been
king, and having thus been general, thus also dissolves the life.
O. And having succeeded to the kingship immediately after that one, the one
proclaimed after that one in the camp itself, and at the peak of the dangers
necessarily seeking the one who would lead, a man conspicuous both in the
other things and in piety, and in form, truly worthy of a tyranny, was able in
no way either to go into hands with Persians, or to go forward, though lacking
nothing of manliness and eagerness, the army having let the hands and the
hopes go slack, and he sought the marching back; and he considered how he
might hit this safely, as having become heir not of a kingship but of a
defeat. If then the Persians, being moderate in the victory (for this is a law
for them to know to measure good fortune), or having feared some other of the
things said, had not turned to agreements, and these so unexpected and
philanthropic, nothing hindered that not even a fire-bearer, as they say, be
left behind for the army; so much did the Persians have them in hand, both
fighting in their own land, and being lifted up besides by the things that had
happened; for it is sufficient to take on something of prosperity, even toward
the hope of the coming =35.684=; but now he, what I said, was of one thing, to
save the army through, and to leave behind the sinews for the Romans; for they
were sinews, even if they fared badly more by ill-counsel of the one who
generalled than by unmanliness of themselves. And they came together upon
these treaties, so shameful and unworthy of the hand of the Romans, that I may
say the most concise; of which if anyone, having let that one go from the
cause, should blame this one, he is to me an exceedingly unfeeling reckoner of
the things then happened. For the ear is not of the one who reaped, but of the
one who sowed; nor the burning of the one who was not able to quench, but of
the one who kindled; and it is time to say the thing of Herodotus about the
tyranny of the Samians, that This sandal Histiaeus sewed, but Aristagoras put
it on, the one who received the things of the one who took them beforehand.

P. What then was left but that the body of the impious be led back to the
Romans, even though he had thus ended? And since there is also for us a
corpse, the one who dissolved the life before him, let us see even here the
difference of both the kings, if this also bears anything toward
well-fatedness or ill-fatedness for those who have departed. The one at any
rate is escorted with public praises and processions, and with these our
solemn things, all-night songs and torch-bearings, with which we Christians
think it right to honor a pious departure; and the carrying-out of the body
becomes a festival with suffering. And if the account is faithful to anyone,
this also was spread to the hearings of the many, that, when the body was
crossing over Taurus being saved through toward his ancestral city, this one
the namesake to those and great-named, a voice from the heights was heard by
some, as of those singing psalms and escorting, of angelic powers I think, a
prize of piety for that one, and an exchange at the tomb. For even if he
seemed to move the right opinion, yet even this =35.685= is the charge of the
clumsiness and ill-opinion of those ruling under, who, having received a soul
simple and not fixed toward piety, not foreseeing the pits, led it away where
they wished, and by a pretext of exactness worked the zeal into vice.
Q. But anyway we, more commonly, both thinking of the father who laid the
foundation of the royal power and faith for Christianity, and the lot of the
dogma that came down upon him, honored as was fitting the body of the one who
lived together with a just kingship, and who dissolved the life with the holy
end, and who left the power behind for us. And as it drew near to the great
and queen city, what need to speak of both the spear-bearing of the whole
army, and an armed array, as happening for the king living, or the outpouring
of the splendid city, most notable of those ever happened or to be? But that
bold and noble one, and beautifying himself with the new purple, and through
this, as is likely, thinking great, becomes himself also a part for that one
of the escorting honor, both giving back and receiving in turn the same grace,
having been forced somewhat, and willing somewhat, as they say. For the whole
army, even if it had been defeated by the present power, yet assigning more of
reverence to the one gone (since we have also been born somehow more
well-disposed to the still fresh sufferings, suffering with them by affection,
and attaching the pity to this), and through this not bearing that this one
not be honored as king, and received, they persuade the apostate, and compel
him together to meet the corpse with the fitting form; and this was, having
unadorned the head of the diadem, and having bowed to the king the fitting
things, thus to escort together to the tomb with those leading, and the
celebrated precinct of the Apostles, who both received the sacred race and
keep it through, almost =35.688= reaping the equal prizes. Thus our one.
R. But for him the things of the campaign were shameful (he was driven by
peoples and cities, and by public and buffoon voices, of which still even now
the many remember), and the return more inglorious. And what is the
ingloriousness? Mimes of laughable things led him, and he was paraded with the
shames from the stage being piped against and danced against, and being
reproached for the denial, and the defeat, and the end. And what of evils did
he not suffer? And what did he not hear with which such men play the young
men, having the insult as an art, until the city of the Tarsians receives him,
I do not know how and in return for what having been condemned to this insult?
Where there is for him a dishonored precinct, and an accursed tomb, and a
temple to be spat upon, and not even visible to pious eyes.
S. And these things I went through as the greatest of his accusations and most
sovereign, not being ignorant, that to two and three of the flatterers about
the palaces, and equal in honor in the impiety (for the others I willingly
pass over), so great a wage of

impiety was given, so that nothing hindered within a little time, they would
have plundered all the land and sea under the Romans, if a favorable end had
not been given more quickly to the affairs; so much did they surpass in
brigandage and greed the hundred-handed ones before; and the rules of the
nations were entrusted not to the most reasonable, but to the most inhuman;
and there was one vote unto rule, the transgression, and the hitting of the
gifts from that one, those who had counseled the worst things about
themselves.
T. And what if I should speak of transfers of judgments and leanings-over
through the middle of night often being changed and overturned, like
ebb-tides; since the noble one even thought it right to judge, making all
things of himself through love of honor? Perhaps I would seem to accuse things
exceedingly small, and to insult the greatest things with the small. Except
let it be confessed, that these things are not worthy of Elysian fields, nor
of the glory of Rhadamanthys there, of which portion those of the same
=35.689= clan and order think him worthy. One of his things I have to marvel
at; for many of those accustomed to him and known, especially from the
sojourns throughout Asia, he called with all eagerness, as being about to do
wonders, of such a kind as he also winged them with the hopes, remembering the
promises; and when they were present, this that, the thefts of the votes, and
the deceits of the faces; mocking some in this manner, others in that, others
in the other; and there are whom even treating kindly with a table, and
drinking much the Friend, and gurgling as some other bait, he sent away
without effect, they not having whom they blame more, that one of the deceit
or themselves of the lightness.
U. And that how is it not praiseworthy of the education of the philosopher,
that he was so without anger, and higher than the passions, according to the
unmoved and unshaken of the kings ever, and who would not, if anything should
happen, turn aside anything of the face, or mark a trace of passion; so that
he filled the palaces with shoutings and shakings when judging, as if he
himself were the one being tyrannized and fined, not defending these things
for others suffering them? These things we will not even think worthy of some
account; but this who of all is ignorant of, that many of the more rustic
coming to him in public, so as to hit some of the things humans need of kings,
striking with the fist in public and leaping upon them with the foot he so
disposed badly, that those were content not to suffer something harder?
V. And the puffings and counter-puffings, which that wonderful one, even
pulling down our things, displayed in turn to the old women, lighting the fire
upon the altar, where will we set of an account? Or is it fair to see the jaws
of the king of the Romans behaving unseemly, and furnishing much laughter, not
only to those without, but even to those themselves, whom he thought to please
doing these things. And did he not hear Athena, his own goddess, that she even
cursed the flutes, on which she learned herself behaving unseemly, having used
the water instead of a mirror. And the toasts and love- =35.692= cups, which
in public he both toasted to the harlots and was toasted in turn, stealing the
wantonness with a pretext of a mystery, how is it not worthy to marvel at?
W. These things experience presented to the others, and the power having taken
the authority beforehand; but to me they were seen even from afar in some
manner, from when I was together with the man at Athens. For he came there
also, just after the things newly done concerning his brother, having asked
this very thing of the king. And the account of his sojourn is double; the
more seemly, according to a history of Greece and of the schools there; and
the more unspoken, and not known to many, so as to be together with the
sacrificers and deceivers there about the things concerning himself, the
impiety not yet having freedom of speech. Then therefore I know I became not a
paltry conjecturer of the man, though I was not one of those well born about
these things. But the

irregularity of the character made me prophetic, and the excess of the
ecstasy; if indeed the best prophet is whoever knows to conjecture well. For a
neck not fixed seemed to me to be a sign of nothing good, shoulders shaking
and being lifted up, an eye being driven and carried around, and looking mad,
feet unstable and shifting, a nostril breathing insult and contempt,
configurations of face ridiculous bearing the same thing, laughs both
unrestrained and boiling, nods and upward-nods with no reason, speech standing
and being cut by breath, questions disordered and unintelligent, answers no
better than these, trampling upon one another and not stable, nor going
forward with an order of education.
X. Why should I write the things according to each? This one I saw before the
works, whom I also recognized upon the works. =35.693= And if some of those
then being together and having heard were present to me, they would not with
difficulty have borne witness; to whom, when I saw these things, I immediately
uttered; What an evil the land of the Romans is nourishing! both having
foretold, and having prayed to become a false prophet of myself. For it is
better than that the inhabited world be filled with these evils, and such a
monster appear, as never before; many floods being rumored, and many burnings,
and shakings of earth, and chasms, and still most inhuman men and beasts both
strange and composite, of which nature made a novelty. In this way then he
also bore an end worthy of the madness; here alone God not having borne the
accustomed long-suffering, where the philanthropic was an evil to many, and
was about to furnish much despondency to those succeeding, and much contempt
to those sinning; as if no one oversees our things, nor is there some steering
or exchange, but the automatic both bears and overturns the whole; which is of
a wicked mind and exceedingly unsafely disposed about the greatest things.
Y. These of us the Galileans, these the narrations of the dishonored; these we
who worship the crucified, these the disciples of the fishermen and of the
uneducated, as they themselves say; these who sit down together with the old
women, and sing psalms together; these who have been melted down by the long
fasts and half-dead; these we who keep vigil in vain, and talk nonsense with
the all-night standings, and throw you down. Where are the grammarians? Where
are the counselors? From some one of the unwise among us, as at any rate it
seems to you, I take the victory-song. Where the sacrifices, and rites, and
mysteries? Where victims both visible and unseen? Where art praised against
the insects? Where wonder-working of foreknowledge and signs of
ventriloquists? Where Babylon the glorious being rumored, and the whole
inhabited world being looked around through a little and accursed blood? And
where the Persians and Medes being held in the hand? And where the gods being
sent forth and escorting, and fighting before and =35.696= fighting together
gods? Where the oracles against Christians, and threats, and the dissolution
of us according to a set date even as far as the name? All things are gone,
have been belied, have flowed apart, the boastings of the impious appeared a
dream.
Z. Hezekiah then the king of Judah, when some king of the aliens came upon him

AA. These things to Christians from you in return for the things through which
you were saved by them, the bad wages? These things did you give back to the
Lord your God? Formerly then, when God still held back and delayed the wrath
on behalf of us, and had not yet kindled out all his own zeal, but still had

his wickedness to break out first, just as indeed a law of the judgment of
God, that he may either save by the repentance or punish more justly; then,
being impatient at the things happening, and laboring toward the future (for
we did not bear his goodness being hidden to his own), we sent forth those
voices toward God, some as calling upon a master, some as imploring toward a
good father, some as if reproaching and pleading toward him, such as the
things of those in pain; Why have you thrust away, O God, unto the end? Has
your anger been angered upon sheep of your pasture? And remember your
congregation, which you acquired from the beginning, which you purchased with
the sufferings of your only-begotten Word, which you thought worthy of your
great covenant, which you even drew into heavens by the new mystery, and by
the earnest of the Spirit; and, Lift up your hands upon their arrogances unto

AB. These things then formerly I both thought and shouted up toward God; but
now of what sort of voices instead of what sort do I take a share! I lament
then the destruction of the impious, and I become philanthropic to those who
hate, and I utter such things; How did they become unto a desolation? suddenly
they failed, they perished through their lawlessness, as dust which a
whirlwind took from under, as chaff being thrown apart by wind, as morning
dew, as a rush of a sent arrow, as an assault of thunder, as lightning

darting through. If then they should now be persuaded otherwise, and, having
ceased from the much error and drunkenness, should pursue the truth, perhaps
even some gain of the fall would come to be for them; since even the being
educated often is unto a good for those suffering; but if they should remain
upon the same judgment, and should still cling to the idols, nor, what even
educates the foolish, should they be made temperate by the disaster. Jerusalem
Jeremiah laments so much, that he even commands a lament to the lifeless
things, and calls forth a tear of the walls. But for these what lament will be
found adequate? Or who would wail the present things, having omitted to weep
the things of the coming punishment, in return for the things in which they
were mindless, and were put far, and served the creation beside the one who
created; and not only this, but even rose up against those serving God, and
stretched up an impious hand and worthy of such evils?
AC. This then, however it is dear to God, thus let it have. Who knows if the
God who looses those who have been bound, and who leads up from the gates of
death the one both heavy and nodding down, who does not wish the death of the
sinner, but the return, who even having enlightened and made intelligent us
sitting in darkness and shadow of death, will sometime take these up also, and
will shepherd =35.701= with the shepherding rod, having put down the heavy and
iron one? But for me the word will run up again toward the same victory-song;
Bel fell, Dagon was crushed, Sharon became marshes, Lebanon was put to shame.
No longer will they say to the fool to rule, to the unmoved and unfeeling
array of the idols; nor will they seek a fly the god of Accaron, or if there
is something more laughable than this; no longer will they look around the
groves and the high things, and every well-haired and shady mountain; no
longer will they sacrifice their sons and their daughters to the demons, which
of old Israel was reproached by the prophets. But what of these to me? I will
turn toward the present and our things. No longer will they look a wicked look
at our sacred houses; no longer will they defile with defiled blood the altars
named of the most pure and bloodless sacrifice; nor will they put to shame the
untrodden things with godless altars; no longer will they plunder and profane
dedicated things, having mixed greed with impiety; no longer will they insult
the gray hair of priests, the solemnity of deacons, the shame of virgins; no
longer will they let loose upon inward parts of holy ones cut up the
defilement of swine, that they may gorge themselves together both of the food
and of the inward parts; no longer will they bring fire to memorials of
martyrs, as if they would hold back other contest by the dishonors against
them; no longer will they consume with fire relics of holy ones, having
dishonored them together with most dishonored bones, and will they give them
to the winds, that they may even deprive them of the honor owed to such; no
longer having put together a seat of pests, will they revel in the blasphemies
against bishops, and presbyters, and still also prophets, and apostles, and of
Christ himself; no longer will they keep festival against us, having shut us
out by a law of the counterfeit education, as if they would together with this
also block our tongues.

righteousness, and a garment of glory instead of a spirit of acedia, and the
great and unspotted tunic Christ, our ornament.

perhaps not even great, if the word is true, that Whom the Lord loves he

But great is the not even

AH. Therefore let us also perceive by deed the divine education. Let us show
ourselves worthy not of the things we first suffered, but of the things we
later hit. Let us make some defense about the disaster that held us, that we
were not given over to the nations as criminals, but were made temperate as
children. Let us not forget the storm in the calm, nor the weakness in the
time of health, nor the captivity, having been saved back into Jerusalem, nor
Egypt after Egypt; let us not make the time of the ill-suffering better than
that of the letting-up; and we will make it, if we appear then contracted and

AI. First, brothers, let us keep festival, not with brightness of body, nor
with changes of clothing and luxuries, nor with revels and drunkennesses, of
which you learned beds and wantonnesses the fruit; nor let us crown streets
with flowers, nor tables with shames of myrrhs, nor let us beautify doorways;
let not the perceptible =35.709= light shine down the houses, nor let them
ring around with flute-concerts and clappings; for this is the law of a
Hellenic holy-month; but let us not honor God with these, nor lift the present
time, with things of which it is not worthy, but with purity of soul, and
brightness of mind, and lamps that light the whole body of the Church, I mean
divine contemplations and thoughts, both being raised upon the sacred
lampstand, and shining down the whole inhabited world. Small to me toward this
is the light, all as many as humans lighting keep festival both privately and
publicly. I have also some myrrh, but with which only priests and kings are
anointed, being both varied and costly, and emptied through us, but composed
by art of a great myrrh-mixer. Would that it come to be for me to strike the
fragrance of this myrrh toward God! I have also a table, this spiritual and
inspired one, which the Lord prepared for me opposite those afflicting me, on
which I rest besides and I revel, and I insult nothing through the satiety,
but I even put to sleep every uprising of passions. I have also flowers, both
more fair-flowered than all spring ones and more lasting, of the field that is
full, which the Lord blessed, the sacred and fragrant shepherds and teachers,
and as much of the people as is both pure and chosen. With these I wish to be
crowned and to parade, having contested the good contest, and having finished
the course, and having kept the faith, according to the sacred Apostle. Let us
take up hymns instead of drums, psalmody instead of the shameful twistings and
songs, a thanksgiving clap instead of theatrical claps, and a well-sounding
action of hands, thoughtfulness instead of laughter, instead of drunkenness a
sensible word, instead of luxury solemnity. And if you must even dance, as a
festival-keeper and lover of feasts, dance, but not the dance of the unseemly
Herodias, whose work is a death of a Baptist;

but that of David upon the rest of the ark, =35.712= which I think to be a

AJ. And second I know I will speak a hard word and hard to receive for the
many; for a human having become in authority of acting badly in turn is wont,
and otherwise even if he has the anger just from the things he has suffered,
least to be persuaded by bridles of a word checking the spirit; but it is
worthy nevertheless both to be heard and to be received. Let us not use the
time greedily, let us not revel in the authority, let us not become bitter to
those who have done injustice, let us not do these things of which we have
judged; but as much as to flee the terrible things having enjoyed the change,
as much as comes unto an exchange, let us hate. Sufficient justice for the
moderate at least is the fear of those who have pained, and the expecting to
suffer these things of which they are worthy, and to be tortured by their own
conscience; for what things one has feared as being about to suffer, these he
has suffered, even if he not suffer, and perhaps is punished more from himself
than from those who will do. Let us not then wish the anger to be measured,
nor let us appear lesser punishers than the worth; but since we are not able
to exact the whole, let us concede the whole; let us become by this better
than those who have done injustice and higher; let us show what the demons
teach those, and what then Christ educates us, who, having the well-approved
in the things he has suffered, has conquered no less in the things being able
he has not done. Let us give back one thank-offering to God; let us increase
the mystery with goodness; let us use the time unto this.
AK. Let us conquer with reasonableness those who tyrannized, and especially
let philanthropy be the thing conceding, and the power of the command, giving
back in turn the equal philanthropy to us, in the things we ourselves need;
for with what measure we measure, we know we are measured in turn; and if
someone even is exceedingly bitterly disposed, let us send away those who have
pained to God, and to the court from there; let us lessen nothing of the
coming wrath through our hand. Let us not think of confiscation, let us not
set beside tribunals, let us not exile from fatherland, let us not maltreat
with scourges, nor let us do any of as many things as we have suffered, that I
may say concisely. Let us make those also more reasonable =35.713=, if indeed
possible, by the example according to us. If for anyone a son has suffered, if
for anyone a father, if for anyone a wife, a kinsman, a friend, or some other
of the honored, let us set the suffering down as paid for all, through the
persuading to bear steadfastly the things they have suffered; this we will
grace them greater than something else. May I say also the greatest of the
things in which we fare well? The persecutors are shouted down by peoples and
cities, in theaters, in markets, in gatherings; the ancient things are
blessed, the new things are pilloried, and even by those who persecuted
together, which is even paradoxical, the gods themselves are dragged down by
them with all shouting-down, as having deceived them much time, and the deceit
having been convicted late sometime; and the yesterday worshiper is today an
insulter. What greater than these do we seek? Now these things even perhaps
small to the wretched. There will be a time, when I will see my insulters, and
the great leader of these, bewailing their own vice, whenever all wickedness
is judged and tortured.
