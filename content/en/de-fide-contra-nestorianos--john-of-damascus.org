* On the Faith Against the Nestorians
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of our father among the saints John of Damascus, a discourse concerning the
faith against the Nestorians.
1 We who have been saved by God through his only-begotten Son, whom he gave as
a ransom for us, ought indeed, according to the word of the Lord, to believe
in Father and Son and Holy Spirit, one God and one essence of Godhead in three
hypostases, and that the Word of God for our salvation became flesh
unchangeably and tabernacled among us, being persuaded by the sacred Gospels;
but, since the sower of the tares, having seized instruments worthy of
himself, confused the people of God and threw them into turmoil, his Holy
Spirit raised up the champions of the truth, the shepherds and teachers as
luminaries in the world, holding forth the word of life, and through them
guided us onto the royal path, who taught us to walk a middle way, turning
aside neither toward the things that seem to be on the right nor toward those
clearly recognized as on the left; following whom with all our power we shall
not go astray.
2 We believe then in Father and Son and Holy Spirit, a consubstantial Trinity,
one Godhead worshiped and served in three hypostases or persons by all
creation, and that the only-begotten Son himself and Word of God, who before
all the ages was begotten of the Father, himself by good pleasure of the
Father became flesh unchangeably of the Holy Spirit and Mary the Virgin,
neither the Godhead being turned into the nature of flesh (for the divine is
unchangeable and unalterable), nor the flesh being changed into the nature of
Godhead; for the divine is unreceptive of any addition.
3 We confess therefore upon our Lord Jesus Christ, the one of the holy
Trinity, two natures, each perfect according to its own definition and
account, lest we introduce change or confusion, but one hypostasis, lest we
bring in a duality of sons and a fourth person into the Trinity; for nature
makes another thing, while hypostasis makes another one and distinguishes a
person. For if, saying three hypostases upon the holy Trinity, we proclaim
three persons, those who confess two hypostases upon our Lord Jesus Christ
must of necessity confess two persons also.
4 Nature then and essence and form indicate the species, that is the
commonality, as Godhead, humanity; but hypostasis and person the particular
and that which exists by itself, as this one and that one; upon the Godhead,
Father, Son, Holy Spirit; upon humanity, Peter, Paul, John.
5 Just as it is impossible for nature and nature to be one nature upon Christ,
so it is impossible for hypostasis and hypostasis to be one hypostasis. And if
it is possible for hypostasis and hypostasis to be one hypostasis and person
and person to be one person, it is possible for nature and nature to be one
nature upon Christ.
6 Just as there is no essenceless hypostasis, yet it is not necessary that
each hypostasis have its own essence, so there is no anhypostatic nature, and
it is not necessary, upon things that are united, that each nature have its
own hypostasis separately.
7 Just as upon the holy Trinity, saying three hypostases, we are not forced to
give each hypostasis its own nature, but the three have one nature and common
essence of the Godhead, and neither is each of them essenceless, nor does each
have its own essence, so upon Christ, saying two natures, we are not forced to
give each nature


its own hypostasis, but the two natures have one common hypostasis of sonship,
that is of the Son of God (for this hypostasis is Son of God according to the
Godhead and son of the Virgin according to the humanity, and neither is each
one of the natures anhypostatic, nor does each have its own hypostasis); and
just as there we say the three hypostases one nature, so here we say the two
natures one hypostasis.
8 Question: Is there an essenceless hypostasis? You will say: By no means.
Give then to each hypostasis of the holy Trinity its own essence. But you do
not give it. And if you say that the same nature belongs to the three
hypostases, I too will say that, even if there is no anhypostatic nature, the
same hypostasis belongs to the two natures of Christ.
9 Question: If there is no hypostasis without person, and there are two
hypostases in Christ, is it necessary to say two persons also?
10 Just as Peter is one hypostasis, but two natures, soul and body, and
according to one nature he thinks, that is according to the essence of the
soul, and according to another he is cut, that is according to that of the
body, yet Peter is one and one hypostasis which both works this and suffers
that, and neither is the soul cut, even if Peter is cut, nor does the body
think, even if Peter thinks, but Peter thinks inasmuch as he has a soul, and
is cut inasmuch as body: so also Christ, being one hypostasis, but having two
natures of the Godhead and of the incarnation, according to his Godhead worked
wonders, and according to his humanity suffered, and neither did his Godhead
suffer nor did the body work wonders, but the one Christ, the one hypostasis,
the one person, both worked these as God and suffered those as man. And just
as upon Peter the soul imparts to the body life, sensation, movement, and the
body receives, yet there is one hypostasis of the giver and of the receiver,
so also upon Christ the Godhead gives, and the humanity receives, and there is
one hypostasis of the giving Godhead and of the receiving humanity.
11 If then the son of the Virgin became son of God by grace, there are two
sonships, another of him by nature and another of him by grace, and not one
Son, but two; for a son by nature and a son by grace will be called brothers,
but would never be called one son, since also all who have been adopted by God
by grace through baptism are one son with the natural Son of God and one
person.
12 But we do not say that the son of the Virgin became Son of God, nor that
the hypostasis of the man became God, but that the Son of God became son of
the Virgin and that the hypostasis of the Son of God was named man.
13 The name God and the name man, likewise soul and body, do not characterize
hypostasis, but nature; even if they are said upon a hypostasis, they do not
indicate the definition of the hypostasis, but of what nature the hypostasis
is. God the Father: "God" does not indicate the property of the hypostasis,
but of what nature he is, while "Father" indicates the property of the
hypostasis. Man Peter: "man" indicates of what nature he happens to be, while
"Peter" indicates the hypostasis properly.
14 Hypostasis is contemplated in the nature and the nature in the hypostasis,
and according to this we say in Christ two natures, Godhead and humanity, and
Christ in two natures, Godhead and humanity; for the whole is in the parts and
the parts in the whole.
15 Parts of Peter are soul and body, and parts of Christ are Godhead and
humanity.
16 It is the custom of Scripture sometimes to name the whole from both of the
parts, as the Lord says: "But rather fear him who is able to destroy both soul
and body in Gehenna."


See, from both he named the whole, that is the man. And from one of the parts,
as David says: "Let all flesh bless his holy name," that he might say: Every
man (for flesh apart from soul does not bless God), and "in seventy souls
Jacob went down into Egypt," and "his soul passed through iron"; for a soul is
not bound without a body.
17 Thus Scripture sometimes calls Christ God, sometimes man, sometimes both
God and man, and sometimes it discourses concerning his Godhead, sometimes
concerning his humanity, sometimes concerning both together. One must
therefore assign the high things to his Godhead, the lowly things to his
humanity, and both these and those to the one hypostasis.
18 To say perfect God and perfect man indicates the perfection of the natures;
it does not signify hypostases. "God" is indicative of nature, and likewise
"man." We know the Son and Word of God, that he is a perfect hypostasis and
that he is timeless and invisible and incomprehensible and intangible and
uncircumscribed and impassible and that passion does not touch him nor insult.
Inasmuch then as he is a perfect hypostasis, he is other than the Father; for
other is the Father and other the Son and other the Holy Spirit, that is
another and another hypostasis, as Gregory the Theologian says: "For there
other and other, lest we confuse the hypostases, but not other and other; for
the three are one and the same in the Godhead," that is in the nature. For
Godhead is a name of nature and not of hypostasis. But to have pre-eternal
existence and the invisible and the incomprehensible and the intangible and
the uncircumscribed and the impassible, the creative, the divine and almighty
will, the almighty energy, all these are not of hypostasis, but of nature; for
they are contemplated likewise in the Father and in the Son and in the Holy
Spirit. And the things contemplated likewise in all the consubstantial
hypostases are natural, <but hypostatic> are the things that separate a
hypostasis from a consubstantial hypostasis, as the unbegotten of the Father
and the begotten of the Son and the proceeding of the Holy Spirit.
19 We know again that the flesh of the Lord has temporal existence and the
bringing from non-being into being and shape and size and form and members and
circumscription and tangibility and human life and movement and sensation and
change from place to place and human will and human energy and hunger and
thirst and sleep and reasoning and fear and agony and being slapped and being
spat upon and being helped by angels and suffering and dying and all the human
things. But all these are of nature and not of hypostasis; for all these are
contemplated in each hypostasis. For having a nose does not indicate
hypostasis, but having a snub nose or a hooked one separates from the
consubstantial hypostases; for having a nose is natural to all men, but having
a snub or hooked nose distinguishes hypostasis and person. Neither then are
the human things raised up to the Godhead nor are the divine things brought
down to the humanity, even if the flesh partook of the divine things; for the
Godhead is imparting, not partaking: it imparts of its own, it does not
partake of those of the flesh. Neither then do the divine things distinguish a
hypostasis nor the human things, but they signify nature and nature.
20 We know again that the flesh of the Lord never existed by itself nor had a
beginning or cause of existence except the Son and Word of God, nor did that
holy body or that undefiled soul become of anyone else except of the Son of
God, and


all the things of it are his and are called his, not of his divine nature,
but, since the flesh is his and not of anyone else, all the things of his
flesh are his and are called his. Therefore we say one hypostasis of the Word
and of the flesh and not two, because it is not other and other, but one. For
if two hypostases, other and other (for hypostasis makes another one, this
particular one) and if other and other, no longer one, but two sons.
21 One then is Christ and not two. For the flesh did subsist, and it is
impossible for it not to have subsisted, but it did not subsist by itself nor
did it become of anyone except of God the Word; for it is not the fact of
subsisting that makes hypostasis and hypostasis, but for each to subsist by
itself, unilaterally and separately and individually, and to become this
particular one and to have its own person.
22 We do not say that the flesh of the Lord does not have hypostasis or person
(for it is impossible not to have them), but we say that it does not have
another hypostasis or person besides God the Word; for of whomsoever the soul
and the body are, he is their hypostasis and person. If then that flesh became
of someone else and not of God the Word, how is there one Son? For we have set
down that hypostasis makes this particular one, as Peter and Paul, and that
Peter and Paul are not one son, and neither is Peter Paul nor Paul Peter.
Therefore if we said two hypostases, another of the Son of God and another of
the son of the Virgin like us, neither is the son of the Virgin a son of God,
nor is the son of the Virgin Son of God except as we are by grace. And it is
impossible for the son by nature and the son by grace to be one son; for son
and son are called brothers, if they are sons of the same father or of the
same mother, but both are in no way one son, since we too who have become sons
of God by grace would be one son with the Son of God, and let the things in
heaven and the things on earth and the things under the earth worship us; but
these things are not so. And that son and son are many sons, the Apostle says:
"For it was fitting for him, for whom are all things and through whom are all
things, in bringing many sons to glory." See, the sons by grace are both many
and not one.
23 We do not say that the Word was changed and became flesh, but that the Word
himself dwelt in the womb of the Virgin as a kind of divine seed and took from
the Virgin from her pure bloods and in himself caused to subsist flesh
ensouled with a rational and intellectual soul, and that flesh existed and
subsisted in truth not by itself but in God the Word, nor did it have seed or
anything else as hypostasis except God alone; for the hypostasis of every
infant is seed, but of this, God the Word. And every flesh becomes of someone,
but this of no one except God the Word. Therefore one hypostasis. Thus we
understand "the Word became flesh," and "he was incarnate" and "he became
man," not that he was changed (for the divine is unchangeable and
unalterable), nor that the flesh was changed into Godhead (for each remained
in the perfect definition of its own nature), but that that holy flesh neither
had a beginning or cause of existence except the Son and Word of God himself,
nor became of anyone else except of him, and that in him it subsisted and in
him the union of the natures came to be, of the divine and of the human,
without confusion and without any change and alteration whatsoever.
24 Concerning the passion, how it is said of the Word. We do not say that the
Word was spat upon or suffered in his divine nature, but, since he himself
became hypostasis to the flesh and the flesh is his and not another's and it
is not other and other but one and the same Son and Christ and Lord, the
passions of his flesh are referred to him; for they are his and not another's.
25 Just as my soul is mine and not another's and my body is mine and not


another's, and all the things of my soul and of my body are mine and are not
another's, but neither are the things of my soul of my body nor are the things
of my body of my soul, but the cutting and flowing belong to the body and not
to the soul, while reasoning and being pious belong to the soul and not to the
body, and both these and those are mine, since I reason with the soul and am
cut in the body: so also God the Word suffers in flesh and works wonders in
Godhead, and neither does Godhead suffer nor does the nature of the flesh work
wonders, even if through the human energy the divine energy was shown.
26 It is impossible then to say or to conceive concerning God, except what he
himself has revealed to us through the holy Scriptures, and it is impossible
to say to God: "Why have you done thus?" "for all things whatsoever the Lord
willed, he did", and one must not say with human reasonings: How is this
possible? "But let God be true, and every man a liar." Since tell me: Whence
do we know that God made all things? How did the Father beget the Son? Or how
is the Holy Spirit not begotten, but proceeds? Or how "did the Word become
flesh?" That the Son is begotten, and the Spirit proceeds, we have received
from holy Scripture, and that "the Word became flesh"; but how, no one knows
except the Father who begot and the begotten Son Word who became flesh and the
Holy Spirit who proceeds.
27 What is the strange mystery hidden from the generations, which the wise did
not know nor the rulers of this age, except the word of the cross, the
foolishness of the preaching, that God became man and was crucified? For
Abraham was a friend of God and died, Moses was a lawgiver and died, Isaiah
was a prophet and was sawn, Jeremiah was sanctified from his mother's womb and
was cast into a pit of mire and died, and John was filled with the Holy Spirit
from his mother's womb and was beheaded, and none of these was reckoned
foolishness, except the word of the cross, that God became man and the Lord of
glory was crucified. This among men is unbelievable and foolish and
impossible, that God was seen and was spat upon. These things to the faithful
are the power of God, that all things whatsoever he wills, he can. This is the
strange and ineffable mystery. For if they had known that the son of the
Virgin is God and Lord of glory, they would not have crucified him. Thus also
the invisible is seen and the impassible suffers and remains no less
impassible. For he suffers by the nature of flesh and remains impassible in
the Godhead; for the divine is impassible, even if it is united according to
hypostasis to the suffering body. One must therefore keep Scripture in
agreement, both that "the Word became flesh and tabernacled among us," and
that he is son of David and Lord of David and son of Abraham, and that he took
flesh and took hold of the seed of Abraham and was before Abraham "and he is
man, and who will know him?" and he is a man coming after John and was before
John, and that the bread of life that comes down from heaven is his flesh, and
that "no one has ascended into heaven except he who came down from heaven" and
the second Adam is Lord from heaven, and that the Son of man, the nature that
heard "You are earth and to earth you shall go," sits upon a throne and is
worshiped by all creation and the Trinity received no addition, and cursed is
everyone who trusts in a man.
28 How can these things agree, except that the Son and Word of God and God by
nature became son of the Virgin, man, two natures, one hypostasis, the same
pre-eternal and before Abraham and John and Lord of David, invisible,
impassible, intangible as God, the same become recently man and as man
visible, the same tangible, passible, spat upon, his divine nature falling
under none of these things.


29 For Scripture says that "he took hold of the seed of Abraham" and that
"from the seed of David according to the flesh," and "son of David, son of
Abraham," because the Virgin was of the Abrahamic race and of the seed of
David and the flesh taken from her by the Word, and that the flesh was of our
nature and not consubstantial with the Father nor did it come down from
heaven; but "the Word became flesh," because he himself became hypostasis to
the flesh and there is one hypostasis; and "he tabernacled among us," in order
to show the difference and unchangeableness of the natures; for other is the
nature of the one tabernacling and other of the tabernacle. For the seed also
takes a body from the bloods of the woman, and other is the hypostasis <of
seed, and other the hypostasis> of woman and the bloods of the hypostasis of
the woman, but not two bodies nor two hypostases of the seed and of the body
taken from the woman by the seed, but one body and one hypostasis in two
natures of soul and of body. Thus also the Son of God was a perfect hypostasis
and, if he took from the holy Virgin flesh ensouled, not two hypostases, but
one hypostasis in two natures; for what there is by nature, this here is above
nature and thought.
30 Thus Christ is God. Wherefore also the Virgin who bore him is Theotokos.
For she who bore God would justly also be called Theotokos, not as having
borne a bare God nor as the Godhead of the Only-begotten having its beginning
from her, but as having conceived God incarnate from her; for if she who bore
is not Theotokos, neither is he who was born God. For he did not become God
after being born from her, but being God he was incarnate from her and was
born God incarnate.
31 For there are other Christotokoi also. For Jeremiah too was anointed and
sanctified from his mother's womb, and John was filled with the Holy Spirit
from his mother's womb, and all prophets and priests and kings are christs of
the Lord and all Christians are christs. "For you went forth for the salvation
of your people, to save your christs." But another Theotokos neither has come
to be nor is nor will be. Justly then and worthily would she be called
Theotokos who alone conceived him who truly is God and Son of God incarnate.
32 There is no hypostasis without person. If then two hypostases, then also
two persons entirely, even if they appropriate one another's actions and
passions.
33 If we shall say one nature of Christ of his Godhead and of his humanity, we
introduce confusion and change of his natures and we shall charge passion to
the divine nature. But if we said two natures of Christ, we keep the union
unconfused and the divine impassible and unchangeable. And if we say two
hypostases, we introduce two sons and we shall worship a tetrad of hypostases,
and the Trinity will be a tetrad, whether we wish or not; for if we shall
count three and one, it is impossible not to say four, and it is impossible
for the things that are and are said not to be numbered. But if we said one
hypostasis of Christ and two natures, all things will be well, as the soul is
said to be in the body and to dwell and the body is said to be a garment of
the soul, and they are not two hypostases, but two natures, and one
hypostasis, Peter perhaps or Paul.
34 For if two consubstantial hypostases, twins of one father and one mother,
like in all things, would not be called one son, how could two hypostases of
different substance, born from another and another nature, become one son?
35 Why ever is the creature co-worshiped with the Creator, if it is of neither
one nature nor one hypostasis? For if the Son of God and the son of the Virgin
like us are of neither one nature nor one hypostasis, how are the two one? For
Peter and Paul, even if they share in nature and in grace and in apostolic
dignity, are not one man or one apostle.
36 Just as upon the holy


Trinity the three hypostases through the natural identity and the perichoresis
in one another are and are called one God, so upon our Lord Jesus Christ the
two natures through the hypostatic identity and the perichoresis in one
another are one Son; for "son" is of hypostasis and not of nature. And just as
there one worship of the three, so also here one worship, since, if there is
neither one nature nor one hypostasis, why one Son and one dignity and
worship?
37 Just as the fired sword is untouchable not through the proper nature of the
iron, but through the nature of the fire united according to hypostasis to the
iron, so the flesh of the Lord is not worshiped through its own nature, but
through being united according to hypostasis to God the Word.
38 If God the Word is master of Christ, how are the two one? For master and
slave would not become one son.
39 If God the Word is master of Christ, they are not of the same dignity; for
mastership and slavery are not one dignity.
40 If it was not the person of the pre-eternal and Only-begotten that was spat
upon, then another person; and if another and another person, they have not
been united even according to person.
41 If the suffering body belonged to the only-begotten Son of God, the passion
also is his; but if of another son, how are there not two sons?
42 The Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit share in all things except the
hypostasis. If then you do not say the union and the incarnation according to
hypostasis, you will say the incarnation of the Father and of the Holy Spirit
also. For if we said the becoming-man according to grace and identity of will
and relation and love, we shall say the becoming-man of the Father and of the
Spirit also; for there is one will and grace and love of Father and Son and
Holy Spirit. And if according to homonymy, he has nothing more than we; "for
he gave us authority to become children of God," and "I said: You are gods and
all sons of the Most High." But the Father also is God and the Holy Spirit.
43 If two hypostases, one from Father only and one from mother only, he will
not be the same without father and without mother.
44 If the union according to grace is called becoming-man, God has become man
many times, even if more and less; for he dwells in us and we are his temple.
45 "My glory I will not give to another," says God. How then shall the son of
the Virgin be co-glorified with God, if he is of another nature and another
hypostasis?
46 Things consubstantial are not compared with one another according to
essence (for no one compares Peter and Paul according to essence; for the
essence is one), but things of different substance are compared according to
essence and not according to hypostasis; for no one says that John is longer
than this horse. Soul and body are of different substance, but of the same
hypostasis. Having members then belongs to the body, and the sense-organs are
of the body, but the sensitive power is of the soul, and they do not on this
account differ from one another hypostatically; for it is also natural to the
body to have members and sense-organs and likewise natural to the soul to have
the sensitive power and to work through the body; for the body is an
instrument of the soul. This difference then is natural and distinguishes
natures. But that Paul is bald, and Peter hairy, the one snub-nosed, the other
hook-nosed, <is a hypostatic difference and distinguishes hypostases>. <And>
receiving spittings and slaps and not receiving does not distinguish
hypostasis; for the whole nature of men is receptive of spittings and slaps,
but the nature of the Godhead is unreceptive. So that when Christ was spat
upon the flesh was spat upon, but the Godhead was unreceptive of the
spittings; likewise also when he was slapped and suffered the nature of the
flesh received


these things, but the Godhead did not receive them. And even when a mere man
is spat upon the body receives the spittings, but the soul by no means, and it
is not on this account another hypostasis of the soul and another person and a
different one of the body. For having members and a visible and spat-upon face
and not having members nor a visible or spat-upon face is of nature; for the
visible and invisible, the material and immaterial, the shaped and unshaped
are differences of nature.
47 What is hypostasis and person? And hypostasis is to subsist by itself and
to become another one, and person is to act individually and separately. But
the <flesh> of the Lord never subsisted by itself and became someone else
besides God the Word, nor did it act separately from God the Word, as it is in
the case of soul and body; for neither is the body another and the soul
another nor is the body <of another> and the soul of another nor do they act
separately, but the soul acts through the body and the divine will and energy
worked through the soul and the body, through the union and will of the soul
and movement of the body.
48 Every hypostasis wills and acts and is moved separately and individually,
and this is a property of hypostasis, and therefore hypostasis and person are
the same. But upon the Godhead alone each hypostasis does not act individually
nor will individually, but there is one will, one energy, one movement of the
three hypostases, and only the unbegotten is a property of the paternal
hypostasis, and the begotten of the hypostasis of the Son and the proceeding
of the hypostasis of the Holy Spirit. Upon our Lord Jesus Christ, if you say
two hypostases, it is necessary that each of them will and act individually
and separately, and they have been united neither according to energy nor
according to another manner. But if they have been united according to energy
and will, there is one hypostasis and one person.
49 We can say that the Virgin bore God the Word and that the Son and Word of
God was not born of a woman. For we know two births of the only-begotten Son
and Word of God, one the pre-eternal bodilessly and divinely from the Father
alone, according to which birth he was not born of a woman and is without
mother, and another at the last from a mother alone with flesh economically
for our salvation, according to which birth he is without father. According to
the first birth he was not born of a woman, but according to the second birth
he was born of a woman; for he did not have from a woman the beginning and
cause of the divine existence, but from the Father alone, and the beginning of
being incarnate and becoming man from the Holy Spirit and Mary the Virgin. For
many things are said according to another and another manner and seem to be in
disagreement to one who does not attend well; for upon the transfiguration of
God one evangelist says that "after six days," and the other that after eight
days, and they disagree, if one does not attend well; but to one who attends
well it is not disagreement but agreement. For he who said "After six days"
counted neither the day on which the Lord spoke nor the day of the
transfiguration, but he who said After eight days counted both this and that.
50 Nature is the commonality of the species, as Godhead, humanity; the name
God is sometimes assigned upon the divine nature, as we say one God, that is
one Godhead, and sometimes also upon hypostasis, as we say: God the Father,
God the Son, God the Holy Spirit, and this we name derivatively from the
nature, and likewise "God." We say then: God is other and man other, that is
another nature of Godhead and another of humanity. And Saint Gregory the
Theologian writes to Cledonius: "For natures are two, God and


man, since also soul and body." And again we say: God the Father and God the
Son and God the Holy Spirit and man Peter and man Paul.
51 Hypostasis is this one and that one, Peter and Paul, and Peter is not Paul
nor Paul Peter, but other and other and two and not one, even if they share
one nature, nor are Peter and Andrew one, but two, even if they share one
nature and the same father and the same mother and the same grace and dignity;
for nothing can make one son except the sameness of the hypostasis.
52 Just as essence and nature are the same among the holy fathers, so also
hypostasis and person are the same.
53 Is the only-begotten and pre-eternal Son of God and the son of the Virgin
of one nature or of two? Of one hypostasis or of two? Is there one will of
Godhead and of humanity or two, another and another? One energy of Godhead and
of humanity or two, another and another? Is natural sonship and sonship
according to grace one or another and another? Is the son of the Virgin a
slave of God? Are slavery and mastership one dignity or another and another?
They spat into the person of the only-begotten and pre-eternal Son, but into
which person? When Gabriel was sent and spoke to the Virgin from the person of
God, was it one person of him and of God? If another and another nature and
another and another will and another and another energy and another and
another hypostasis and another <and another> sonship and another <and another>
dignity and another and another person, how are there not two sons?
54 One must keep all these things entirely: the Godhead impassible and
unchangeable, upon God three hypostases worshiped, and to believe into a
Trinity and to be baptized and not to worship the creature and to keep holy
Scripture, both the old and the new, in agreement.
