Epictetean Gnomologium (from Stobaeus books 3-4) edited by Heinrich Schenkl
#+END_QUOTE

* 1
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

A life entangled with fortune is like a torrent river; for it is turbulent and
mixed with mud and hard to enter and tyrannical and noisy and short-lived.

* 2
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h2
:END:

A soul conversing with virtue is like an ever-flowing spring; for it is pure
and untroubled and drinkable and sweet and communal and rich and harmless and
undying.

* 3
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3
:END:

If you wish to be good, first believe that you are bad.

* 4
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4
:END:

Better to err seldom while confessing to be temperate more often than to sin
seldom while saying one errs often.

* 5
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5
:END:

Chastise the passions, that you may not be punished by them.

* 6
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h6
:END:

Do not so much be ashamed of what comes from opinion as flee what comes from
the truth.

* 7
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h7
:END:

If you wish to be well spoken of, learn to speak well; and having learned to
speak well, try to act well, and so you will reap being well spoken of.

* 8
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h8
:END:

Examine yourself, whether you wish to be rich or to be happy. And if to be
rich, know that it is neither a good nor altogether in your power; but if to
be happy, that it is both a good and in your power; since the one is a timely
loan of fortune, the other † of happiness of choice.

* 9
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h9
:END:

Just as when you see a viper or an asp or a scorpion in an ivory or golden
casket you do not love and count it blessed because of the costliness of the
material, but because the nature is destructive you turn away and loathe it,
so also whenever you see vice present in wealth and bulk of fortune, do not be
struck by the brilliance of the material, but despise the counterfeit in the
character.

* 10
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h10
:END:

Wealth is not of the goods, luxury of the evils, temperance of the goods. And
temperance calls to frugality and the acquisition of goods, but wealth to
luxury and draws away from temperance. It is therefore hard for one who is
rich to be temperate, or for one who is temperate to be rich.

* 11
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h11
:END:

Just as, if you were sown or born in a ship, you would not hasten to be its
helmsman,  [lost]  for neither there will the ship naturally remain with you
nor here wealth, but reason everywhere. What then is natural and kindred to
you, reason, considering this also your own, take care of it.

* 12
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h12
:END:

If born among the Persians you would not hasten to dwell in Greece, but to
fare well remaining there; but being born in poverty, why do you hasten to be
rich, and not to fare well remaining there?

* 13
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h13
:END:

Just as it is better to be healthy squeezed on a small pallet than to be sick
rolling on a wide bed, so also it is better to be of good cheer contracted in
a small estate than to be of ill cheer happening to have a large one.

* 14
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h14
:END:

It is not poverty that works grief, but desire; nor does wealth release from
fear, but reasoning. Having therefore acquired reasoning you will neither
desire wealth nor blame poverty.

* 15
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h15
:END:

Neither a horse at manger and trappings and rugs nor a bird at food and nest
is exalted and prances, but both at swiftness, the one of feet, the other of
wings. You too therefore do not swell greatly at food and shelter and simply
at the external abundance, but at goodness and well-doing.

* 16
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h16
:END:

Living well differs from living luxuriously; for the one comes from temperance
and self-sufficiency and good order and seemliness and frugality, the other
from incontinence and luxury and disorder and unseemliness; and the end of the
one is true praise, of the other blame. If then you wish to live well, do not
seek to be praised with luxury.

* 17
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h17
:END:

Let the measure for you of all food and drink be the first filling of
appetite, and relish and pleasure the appetite itself; and you will neither
take more than is needed nor need cooks, and you will be content with whatever
drink happens to be at hand.

* 18
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h18
:END:

Make your meals not luxurious and gloomy, but bright and frugal, so that souls
may neither be troubled because of bodily things nor, being cheated by bodily
pleasures, grow careless, and then be harmed, the bodies being luxurious at
once but sick later.

* 19
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h19
:END:

Take care that it is not the food in the belly that lifts you up, but the
cheer in the soul; since the former is cast out as refuse and the † praise
flows away with it, but the latter, even if the soul is separated, remains
unstained throughout.

* 20
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h20
:END:

In feastings remember that you receive two, body and soul, and whatever you
give to the body, this you immediately pour out, but whatever to the soul, you
keep throughout.

* 21
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h21
:END:

Do not mix together anger with luxury and bring it forward. Luxury, leaping
into the body, is gone before long, but anger, having entered the soul,
remains for the longest time. See therefore that you are not carried away by
anger and insult the guests amid luxury, but rather with gentleness cheer them
frugally.

* 22
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h22
:END:

It is shameful, sweetening the swallow with the gifts of bees, to embitter
with vice speech, the gift of the gods.

* 23
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h23
:END:

Let it be your care in foods that those serving you are not more than those
served; for it is absurd that many souls should slave for a few couches.

* 24
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h24
:END:

Best, if both in the preparations working with the hands and in the meals
being entertained you share with those serving what is present. But if such a
thing should be difficult for the occasion, remember that not toiling you are
served by those who toil, eating by those not eating, drinking by those not
drinking, eating and talking by those silent, at ease by those contracted; and
you will neither yourself, having become inflamed, suffer something absurd,
nor having made another savage do something harsh.

* 25
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h25
:END:

To contend and be contentious is everywhere unfitting, but especially unseemly
in conversations over drink. For neither would a drunk man teach a sober one,
nor again would a drunk man be persuaded by a sober one. And where the end of
persuasion is not present, you offer yourself in vain to strain.

* 26
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h26
:END:

The cicadas are musical, but the snails voiceless; and the snails rejoice
being moistened, the cicadas being warmed; then dew calls forth the snails and
they come out upon it, but the sun at its height wakes the cicadas and they
sing upon it. Therefore if you wish to be a musical and well-attuned man,
whenever in drinkings the soul is dewed by wine, then do not let it go forth
and be defiled; but whenever in assemblies it is fired by reason, then bid it
prophesy and sing the oracles of justice.

* 27
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h27
:END:

Examine the one conversing with you in three ways, either as better or as
worse or as equal; and if better, one must listen and obey him; if worse,
persuade; if equal, agree; and you will never be caught in contentiousness.

* 28
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h28
:END:

Better, yielding to the truth, to conquer opinion than, yielding to opinion,
to be defeated by the truth.

* 29
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h29
:END:

Seeking the truth you will not seek to win by every means, and having found
the truth you will have not being defeated.

* 30
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h30
:END:

Truth conquers with itself, but opinion with those outside.

* 31
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h31
:END:

Freedom and slavery, the one a name of virtue, the other of vice, and both
works of choice. And of things in which choice has no share, neither of these
touches; but fortune is accustomed to be master of the body and of such things
about the body as have no share in choice. For no one being a slave in choice
is free.

* 32
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h32
:END:

A bad bond of the body is fortune, of the soul vice. For he who is loosed in
body but bound in soul is a slave; but he who is bound in body but loosed in
soul is free.

* 33
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h33
:END:

The bond of the body both nature looses through death and vice through money,
but that of the soul virtue through learning and experience and practice.

* 34
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h34
:END:

If you wish to live without disturbance and with contentment, try to have all
who dwell with you good; and you will have them good if you educate those
willing, and those unwilling, then having subdued them, you set free. For
wickedness together with slavery will flee with those who have fled, and
goodness and freedom will be left behind with those remaining with you.

* 35
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h35
:END:

Better to live with one free man and be fearless and free than to be a slave
with many.

* 36
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h36
:END:

What you flee to suffer, this do not attempt to inflict; and you flee slavery,
so beware of being served by slaves. For enduring to be served by slaves you
yourself first seem to be a slave; for neither does virtue share with vice nor
freedom with slavery.

* 37
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h37
:END:

Just as the healthy man would not wish to be treated by the sick nor that
those dwelling with him be sick, so neither would the free man endure to be
served by slaves or that those living with him be slaves.

* 38
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h38
:END:

If you wish to be without slaves, yourself be released from slavery; and you
will be free if you are released from desire. For neither Aristides nor
Epaminondas nor Lycurgus, being rich and being served by slaves, were
proclaimed the one just, the other savior, the other a god; but because being
poor they released Greece from slavery.

* 39
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h39
:END:

If you wish your house to be well managed, imitate the Spartan Lycurgus. For
in the way he did not fence the city with walls, but fortified the inhabitants
with virtue and kept the city free throughout, so also do you not throw around
a great court and raise high towers, but strengthen those dwelling within with
goodwill and trust and friendship; and nothing harmful will enter it, not even
if the whole company of vice should array itself.

* 40
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h40
:END:

Do not surround your house with tablets and paintings, but paint it with
temperance. For the one is a timely enchantment of another kind, of the eyes,
the other a native and inerasable and everlasting adornment of a house.

* 41
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h41
:END:

Instead of a herd of oxen try to have herds of friends herding in your house.

* 42
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h42
:END:

Just as a wolf is like a dog, so also a flatterer and an adulterer and a
parasite are like a friend. Attend therefore, lest instead of guardian dogs
you unwittingly receive in destructive wolves.

* 43
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h43
:END:

To be eager that the house whitened with gypsum be admired is of one without
taste, but to make the character bright with goodness of fellowship is of one
who loves the beautiful and at the same time loves mankind.

* 44
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h44
:END:

If you admire the small things before the great, you will be despised; but if
you despise the small things greatly, you will be admired.

* 45
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h45
:END:

Nothing is smaller than love of gain and love of pleasure and boastfulness;
nothing stronger than greatness of mind and gentleness and love of mankind and
well-doing.

* 46
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h46
:END:

No one who is a lover of money and of pleasure and of reputation is a lover of
mankind, but only the lover of the beautiful.

* 47
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h47
:END:

Just as you would not wish, sailing in a great and well-wrought and
much-gilded ship, to be sunk, so neither choose, lodging in a house oversized
and luxurious, to be storm-tossed.

* 48
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h48
:END:

Just as a true balance is neither corrected by a true balance nor judged by a
false one, so also the just judge is neither corrected by the just nor judged
among the unjust.

* 49
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h49
:END:

Just as the straight has no need of the straight, so neither does the just of
the just.

* 50
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h50
:END:

Do not judge in another court before you yourself have been judged before
justice.

* 51
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h51
:END:

If you wish to make judgments just, recognize none of those being judged and
pleading, but justice itself.

* 52
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h52
:END:

You will least stumble in judgments if you yourself continue unstumbling in
life.

* 53
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h53
:END:

Better, having judged justly, to be blamed unjustly by the condemned than,
having judged unjustly, to be justly blamed by nature.

* 54
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h54
:END:

Just as the stone that tests gold is no longer itself tested by the gold, so
also he who has the criterion.

* 55
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h55
:END:

It is shameful for the judge to be judged by others.

* 56
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h56
:END:

Just as nothing is more straight than the straight, so neither is anything
more just than the just.

* 57
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h57
:END:

Just as the beacons upon the harbors, having kindled a great flame by means of
a few sticks, work sufficient help for ships wandering over the sea, so also a
brilliant man in a storm-tossed city, himself content with few things, greatly
benefits the citizens.

* 58
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h58
:END:

Just as, if you attempted to steer a ship, you would altogether learn
thoroughly the steersman's art,  [lost]  for it will be possible for you, as
there the whole ship, so here also to steer the whole city.

* 59
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h59
:END:

If it is set before you to adorn the city with offerings, first dedicate to
yourself the fairest offering of gentleness and justice and well-doing.

* 60
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h60
:END:

You will do the greatest good to the city if you do not raise the roofs high,
but increase the souls. For it is better that great souls dwell in small
houses than that lowly slaves nest in great houses.

* 61
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h61
:END:

Do not variegate the walls of the building with stones from Euboea and Sparta,
but rather with the education from Greece adorn the breasts of the citizens
and of those in public life. For cities are well inhabited by the judgments of
men, but not by stones and timbers.

* 62
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h62
:END:

Just as, if you wished to keep lions, you would not care for the costliness of
the cages, but for the action of the animals; so, if you try to stand at the
head of citizens, do not so much care for the costliness of the buildings as
attend to the courage of those dwelling in them.

* 63
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h63
:END:

Just as a good horse-breaker does not feed the good colts and let the
hard-to-rein starve, but feeds both equally, and chastises the one more,
forcing it to be equalized to the other; so also a caring man and one skilled
in political power attempts to make the reasonable citizens, and those of the
contrary sort not to be utterly destroyed, but least grudges food to both, and
educates and more vehemently urges on the one who resists reason and the law.

* 64
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h64
:END:

Just as neither a goose is struck with terror by a honk nor a sheep by a
bleat, so neither let the voice of a mindless multitude frighten you.

* 65
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h65
:END:

Just as a multitude asking indiscriminately does not shame you out of
something of your own, so neither be turned aside by a crowd unjustly putting
you to shame.

* 66
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h66
:END:

What falls upon the city, this contribute in advance; and you will never be
asked for the things that do not fall to you.

* 67
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h67
:END:

Just as the sun does not wait for prayers and enchantments in order to rise,
but immediately shines and is greeted by all, so neither wait you for
clappings and noises and praises in order to do good, but willingly benefit,
and you will be loved equally with the sun.
