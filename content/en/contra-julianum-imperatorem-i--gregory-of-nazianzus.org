ORATION IV. First Invective Against Julian the Emperor.
1. Hear these things, all nations; give ear, all you who inhabit the inhabited
world. For I call all, as from some lookout and a most central vantage, with a
great and high proclamation. Hear, peoples, tribes, tongues, every race of
men, and every age, as many as now are, and as many as shall be. And that my
proclamation may be greater, every power of the heavens, all angels, whose
work is the overthrow of the tyrant: not those who will pull down Sihon the
king of the Amorites, nor Og king of Bashan, small potentates, and harming a
small part of the inhabited world, Israel; but the dragon, the apostate, the
great mind, the Assyrian, the common enemy and adversary of all, who raged and
threatened much upon earth, and both spoke and practiced much injustice
against the height.
2. Hear, heaven, and give ear, earth. For it is time for me to sound together
the same things with Isaiah, the most loud-voiced of the prophets =35.533=.
Except that he calls and bears witness to these things over Israel that set
them at nought; but I over a tyrant, who both set them at nought and fell a
fall worthy of the impiety.
3. Hear also, soul of the great Constantius, if there is any perception, and
as many Christ-loving emperors as were before him; and of him most of all,
inasmuch as, having grown together with the inheritance of Christ, and having
increased it into power, and having confirmed it by time, so as even to become
for this reason the most renowned of all emperors that ever were, (O the
spite!) he was ignorant of an ignorance utterly unworthy of his own piety. He
escaped his own notice, committing to Christians the enemy of Christ, and in
this alone of all things he did not do well in his humanity, both to save and
to make emperor the one who was both saved and reigned badly. And for this he
would be most pleased, as with the overthrow of the impiety, and with the
affairs of the Christians returning to the ancient state, so also with the
word. For I will dedicate a word to God as a thank-offering, more holy and
purer than every irrational sacrifice, not according to that man's accursed
words and nonsense, and the more accursed sacrifices, of which the abundance
and the power was a power of impiety, and an unwise wisdom, that I may so name
it. Since even all the power and education of this age, going about in
darkness, falls far from the light of the truth. That one, then, was such, and
among such, and having such fruits, withering quickly as grass, and falling
away quickly as the green herbs, and going off together with the fathers who
perish with a noise, more conspicuous in the fall than in the impiety.
4. But for me, sacrificing today a sacrifice of praise, and kindling the
bloodless honor of words, who will set around a theater equal to the grace? Or
what tongue will sound together as much as I also wish? What hearing will be
eager together with the word? For not only to the Word is the grace through
word most proper, among the other things by which he is named, and he delights
especially in this title and in the power of the calling; but it is also a
fitting penalty for that man, to be punished by a word =35.536= for the
lawlessness against words: which, being common to all rational beings, he
begrudged to Christians as if they were his own, having thought most
irrationally about words, he who of all, as he supposed, was most eloquent.


5. First, because he wickedly transferred the name to what seemed good, as if
the Hellenic word belonged to the religion, and not to the tongue; and for
this reason, as thieves of an alien good, he drove us from the words; as if he
had also barred us from the arts, as many as have been found among the
Hellenes, and thought this differed for him because of the homonym. Then,
because he supposed he would escape our notice, not about to deprive us of
some first good (we who even greatly despise these words), but fearing the
refutations of the impiety, as if they had their strength in the elegance of
diction, and not in the knowledge of the truth and in the arguments, which it
is more impossible to hold us back from, having a tongue, than to confess God.
Since we also, among the other things, consecrate the word, as we do the
bodies, whenever it is necessary to contend with these also concerning the
truth. So that he who ordered this hindered Atticizing, but did not stop the
telling of truth; and he exposed the rottenness of his own, but did not escape
the refutations; because he even more, having subjected himself to these, did
not know it.
6. For it was not of one who was confident either in the rights concerning the
religion, or in the words themselves, to hold back our words; as if someone
should think himself strongest of the athletes, and should claim to be
proclaimed over all, having ordered that none of the noble contend, nor go
down into the stadium, or having cut off some of the limbs, which is a proof
of cowardice rather than of strength. For the crowns =35.537= are against
those who contend, not those sitting above; and of those using all their
strength, not of those stripped of the greater part of their power. But if you
wholly feared to mix and come to hands, by this very thing you showed the
defeat, and it was mine to conquer without even having contended, you whom I
contended not to have contended. These things the wise emperor and lawgiver
did for us, as if that nothing of his tyranny might be without a share, and
that he might proclaim the irrationality at the beginning of his own reign,
having tyrannized the words before the others. But for us it is good even on
behalf of the words themselves to confess thanks to God, since they have
recovered their freedom; and most of all also to honor him with the other
dedicated things, sparing nothing, neither of money nor of possessions, which,
having become of the time and of the tyranny, the loving-kindness of God saved
through, but to honor him before the others with the words, the just
fruit-bearing and common to all who have partaken of the grace. But the word
on behalf of the words is so much; lest, stretching longer, we fall outside
the season, and seem to be eager about something else rather than that for
which we have come together.
7. Already my word leaps and is let go toward festival, and is gladdened
together with the things seen, and calls all into a spiritual dance, as many
as were given to fastings and weeping and petition, asking night and day for
ransom from the painful things that held them, and making hope that does not
shame a good medicine in the evils; and as many as, having endured great
contests and prizes, and having been struck by many and hard assaults of the
time, became a spectacle to the world, and to angels, and to men, according to
the voice of the Apostle; and wearing out the bodies, but remaining
unconquered in the souls, and being able to do all things in Christ who
strengthens them; and as many as, having put off worldly matter of vice and
power, either having received with joy the plunder of their goods, or having
been unjustly banished from their own, as the saying is, or of men, or of
women, or of parents, or of children, or by as many other names as we are held
by the small kinship, having been separated from these for a little, and
having borne as fruit to the blood of Christ =35.540= the sufferings on behalf
of Christ, would now say and sing in season that, You have caused men to ride
over our heads; we went through fire and water; and you led us out into
refreshment.


8. I call the other portion also into the festival, as many as, confessing the
God of the whole, and being sound in their reasonings up to this point, yet
not entering into the reasons of providence, which often administers the
better things from the contrary, and by kindness calls forth the correction,
but through poverty of soul and lightness, in the arrogant man's being
arrogant, having their reasonings set on fire and kindled, and not bearing the
peace of sinners, as the psalm says, nor enduring the counsel of God, and
being long-suffering toward the end; being always slaves of the present and of
the things seen, are confirmed toward the truth by such wonders.
9. I call also the souls fluttered about the stage of this world and the great
theater; and I call them with the words of Isaiah: Women coming from a
spectacle, come, and having turned the eye of the soul from the outer
wandering, be still and know that he himself is God, exalted among the
nations, exalted in the earth, always in all the wonders and signs which he
made, and in the present ones more clearly.
10. Would that that company also were part of my chorus, which, singing with
us until now to God a song not counterfeit, nor unapproved, but even once
judged worthy of the station on the right, and I believe that it will also
shortly be judged worthy, I do not know what it has suffered, suddenly both
changes key and changes ranks; and not even by the common gladness, which I
wonder at more, does it come to the same; but it sets up a dance of its own,
not some well-rhythmed one, nor harmonious. For so much perhaps even they
themselves will grant to say. But of what sort and of what kind? Even if zeal
is moved to speak, yet faith prevails; and I will hold back the unpleasantness
of the word from reverence of the hope. I still tend my members; I still give
more to the former love than to the present contempt; and for this I become
more long-suffering, that I may reproach more warmly. =35.541=
11. One portion, one race of souls, I ban from the festival, groaning and in
pain, and mourning those perhaps not even hearing, nor perceiving their own
destruction as I lament. For this is the most pitiable of their blow. Yet I
ban them nonetheless, who were sown not upon the solid and unshaken rock, but
upon the dry and barren; and there are those who came to the word
superficially and of little faith, and because of not having depth of earth,
having sprung up at once, and having put forth, that they might please the
neighbors, then by a short assault of the evil one and a little burning of
trials, were dried up and died. And worse than these still, and more to be
banned from the festival, as many as did not even for a little stand against
the time, and against those who take us captive with the evil captivity from
him who went up on high and took us captive well; but from surplus they
appeared both wicked and cheap, not even holding out for a little, nor, some
affliction or trial having come to them, having been scandalized because of
the Word, but for a timely gain, or attendance, or a small power, the
wretched, having sold off their own salvation.
12. And since we have cleaned by the word the fullness of the chorus, come, as
much as is in our power, having purified both bodies and souls, and having
taken up one voice, having been fitted together by one Spirit, let us sing
that victory song which Israel once sang over the Egyptians, covered by the
Red Sea, Mary leading and striking the timbrel: Let us sing to the Lord, for
he has been gloriously glorified; horse and rider he has thrown, not into the
sea (this I change of the song), but wherever it was dear to him, and however
he himself judged right. He who makes all things and transforms them, Amos
said somewhere in his own prophecy, philosophizing most divinely; he who turns
the shadow of death into morning, and darkens day into night, and as it were


through some cycle directing and leading through the whole world, and our
affairs, being shaken together and not being shaken, moved and overturned by
the changes, and being otherwise at other times; but by the order of
providence firm and unmoved, even if it travels through the contraries, clear
to the Word, but unknown to us; he who strips potentates from thrones, and
adorns the unsuspected with a diadem =35.544=. For I take this also from the
divine Scripture; he who puts courage around knees that have no strength, and
crushes the arms of a sinner and a wicked man, and these from another song,
however each comes to the memory, many filling up the song for me, and
collecting the thank-offering; he who gives to be seen both the lifting of the
impious above the cedars, and the taking down into being no longer; whenever
we are able to pass by his impiety with speed and security of foot.
13. Who will sing these things worthily, and will narrate, of those who
narrate the divine things? Who will speak the powers of the Lord, will make
all his praises heard? What voice, or what power of word, will he equal to the
wonder? Who crushed weapon, and sword, and war? Who broke the heads of the
dragons upon the water? Who gave him as food to peoples, to whom he also
delivered him? Who stood a hurricane into a breeze? Who said to the sea, Be
silent, be muzzled; and, In you your waves shall be crushed; and indeed also
having crushed them, not raised and boiling for long? Who granted to tread
upon serpents and scorpions, no longer watching the heel in secret, as the
condemnation, but rising up openly and lifting the head, which they were
condemned to be trodden? Who is he that made judgment and unexpected
righteousness? Who is he that did not wholly let go the rod of the sinners,
must one dare to say, upon the lot of the righteous? Or, what is more moderate
than this, upon the lot of those who know him?
14. For we were not delivered over as righteous (this, few and seldom), that,
as noble athletes, they might shame the tempter; but as sinning we were
condemned, then we were pitied both carefully and paternally, as much as to be
sobered, having been struck, and as much as to turn to him, having been
admonished. For he refuted, yet not with wrath; and he disciplined, yet not
with anger; through both showing the loving-kindness both of the reminder and
of the release. Who is he that made vengeance among the nations, rebukes among
the peoples, the Lord mighty and powerful, the Lord powerful in war. =35.545=
15. I find one voice, one strain, somehow worthy of the present things, which
Isaiah uttered before us, very fitting to the present time, and competing with
the greatness of the benefaction: Let the heaven be glad from above, and let
the clouds rain righteousness; let the mountains break forth gladness, and the
hills exultation. Since even all creation, and heavenly powers, and of such
things, as at least my word has it, perceive together. For it does not only
groan together and travail together, being enslaved to corruption, I mean to
the things below that come to be and pass away, awaiting with outstretched
head the end of these and the revelation, that it itself also may then obtain
the hoped-for freedom, as now bound to these unwillingly through the power of
him who created, but it also is glorified together and exults together with
the children of God when they are glad.
16. Therefore (for I will not hold off from divine voices while interpreting a
divine power), let the desert rejoice, and let it blossom as a lily, the
Church yesterday and the day before a widow, as it seemed, and without a
husband, and everyone until now contracted with the envious and joyless winter
of the impiety; because the Lord pitied his people, and has not abandoned his
inheritance; because he has done wonderful things, an ancient counsel that is
true; and this was, to take pleasure in those who fear him, and those who hope
upon his mercy; because he crushed gates of bronze, and shattered bars of
iron; because


through our lawless deeds we were humbled, but we were called back, and, the
snare having been crushed, we were delivered in the grace of him who called
us, and of God who comforts the humble in heart.
17. Do you see how I weave the song with both divine words and thoughts? And I
do not know how I am both lifted and adorned with things that are others', and
as it were become inspired by pleasure; and I dishonor every low and human
thing, fitting and joining other things to others, and bringing into one the
things of the same Spirit?
15. Formerly, then, the wonders of God were shown, Enoch being translated,
Elijah being taken up, Noah being saved, and saving the seeds of the races,
the world in a small wood, when they fled a flood of the inhabited world, that
the earth might be adorned again with more pious inhabitants; Abraham being
called, and being honored with a child beyond age, toward faith of another
promised seed, and offering the only-begotten as a willing sacrifice, and
receiving a strange victim in place of the child; a paradoxical destruction of
the impious, =35.548= overwhelmed with fire and brimstone, a more paradoxical
theft of the pious, a pillar of salt triumphing the turning back toward the
evil; and Joseph sold, and beloved, and being sober, and being made wise by
God, and being set free, and ruling, and giving grain upon a greater economy;
Moses being judged worthy of a theophany, and being given law and giving law,
and becoming a god to Pharaoh, and leading Israel toward the land of the
promise; and the plagues of the Egyptians being numbered, and salvation of
those laboring in the midst of the Egyptians; and a sea yielding to a rod, and
coming together at a word, and sending some through as dry, and flowing upon
others according to its own nature. 19. And the other things, as many as
followed these, a pillar of cloud overshadowing by day, a pillar of fire
lighting by night, and both leading; bread raining in a desert, a relish sent
from heaven; the one measured to the need, the other even beyond the need;
water from a rock, the one given out, the other sweetened; Amalek warred down
by prayer, and by an unspeakable and mystic figure of hands; and a sun
standing, and a moon being held, and Jordan being cut back; walls shaken down
by a circuit of priests, and a sound of trumpets, and a number having power;
earth and fleece, by turns both rained upon and remaining without rain; power
in hairs, a counterweight of a whole army; a few chosen men lapping, and being
trusted to conquer, and conquering, according to the hopes, the short number
the more thousands. Why must I number each, as many things as through Christ
himself according to his saving presence and incarnation, as many as after him
and through him have been done as wonders by his holy apostles, and servants
of the word? How many books and memories carry these things? =35.549= that
another generation may know, and successions of generations, the wonders of
the power of God. And since it is not possible to make these clear, without
having set forth the greatness of the danger; and this is not possible,
without having refuted the malice of the manner, and from what beginnings and
seeds of vice he fell out upon so great a misery, having increased the impiety
little by little, as the venom of the reptiles and of the beasts as many as
are most bitter, we will leave to books and histories to make a tragedy of all
his things; for we at least have no leisure to rhapsodize things longer than
the present subject; but we ourselves, having gone through a few from many,
will leave as it were some pillar-writing to those after us, having gone with
the word upon the most lordly and most conspicuous of the things concerning
him. 21. He, one and first, having been saved by the great Constantius, who
had just taken over the power from the father (when the soldiery was armed
against those in office, innovating from fear of innovation, and the palace
was being settled through new patrons); and having been saved with his brother
a salvation unbelievable and paradoxical, neither to God thanks


did he have for the salvation, nor to the emperor, through whom he had been
saved; but he appeared evil to both, travailing apostasy for the one, and
uprising for the other.
22. And what it is necessary for me to say before these things: they were
judged worthy of imperial diet and attendance in one of the imperial estates
by the most humane emperor, as remnants of the race being kept for the empire;
at once his inventing a defense of the innovations at the beginning of the
reign, as not having been dared according to that man's judgment; and at once
also seeking to display the magnanimity by the sharing of the empire; and
third, to settle the rule more firm by the additions, which was of one
thinking more kindly than more wisely.
23. And since they spent leisure from all things, the =35.552= empire still
being future and being practiced, and the age together with the hope not yet
bringing them to the second of the offices, they used teachers also of the
other lessons, the divine emperor educating them in all the encyclical
education; and they used also our philosophy more, not that concerning words
only, but as much as has the pious also through the morals; associating with
the most serious of men, and with the most graceful of deeds, and those having
much display of virtue. To the clergy at least, bringing themselves, they
enrolled, so as even to read the divine books under to the people, thinking
this to be for them no less than anything toward glory, but even piety the
greatest adornment of all.
24. And by most costly memorials of martyrs, and ambitions of dedicated
things, and by all the things by which the divine fear is characterized, they
indicated the philosophic and the Christ-loving; the one, even being pious
according to truth (for he was, even if hotter in nature, yet genuine toward
piety); the other, buying out the time, and hiding the malice in a fiction of
fairness. And a proof: for I could not run past the wonder that happened,
being both very worthy of memory, and able to educate many of the impious.
25. They were laboring for the martyrs, as I said, both, and they were
ambitious against each other splendidly, raising the house with much hand and
expense. And since it was not from the same choice, nor did the labor go
forward for both to a like end: for the work of the one was being
accomplished, and was proceeding according to reason, I mean of the elder of
the brothers, as of God readily receiving the offering, according to the
sacrifice of Abel, both brought and divided rightly; and the offering was as
it were some consecration of a firstborn; but of the other, alas for the
dishonor of the impious already from here being witnessed from there, =35.553=
and with small indications proclaiming beforehand the great things, the God of
the martyrs was sending away as the sacrifice of Cain.
26. And the one was laboring, but the earth was shaking off what had been
labored. And the one was more contentious, but it thought it unworthy to
receive foundations of the rotten toward piety, as it were crying out the
earthquake that would be from that man, and honoring the martyrs by the
dishonor concerning the most impious. This work was some prophecy of the later
self-will and madness of the man, and of the dishonor toward martyrs, and of
the lawlessness concerning the holy houses; escaping the notice of the others,
but persecuting the persecutor from afar, and signifying beforehand the wages
of the impiety.
27. O soul wise toward doing evil, yet not fleeing its own torture! O God
proclaiming the future, that he may either cut back the impiety, or show the
foreknowledge! O wonder paradoxical, yet more true than paradoxical! O the
brother-love of the martyrs! They did not receive honor from him who would
dishonor many martyrs; they did not accept a gift from him who would display
many athletes, or rather who would begrudge even the contesting itself; or, to
say what is more true, they did not bear to be insulted alone of martyrs,


the others being about to be set and tended by holy hands, nor to give to the
sophist of vice to boast in the insults; lest from the same hand some
memorials of martyrs be raised, and others be pulled down; and some martyrs be
honored, and others be dishonored, the honor in figure a little anticipating
the true dishonor; and lest, besides the greatness of the insult, he also
think himself wise with himself, escaping notice, as of men, so also of God
who is sharpest and wisest of all, and who catches the wise in their own
craftiness, through the apparent pretext; but that he may know he is being
understood, and may not be lifted above when he is being caught. =35.556=
28. For even if the God of the martyrs did not hold back the impiety, nor at
once dry up or cut back the vice being practiced and being hidden, as a wicked
stream, by the reasons which he himself knew, according to his unspeakable
wisdom and steering, according to which he was also managing the lawless deeds
of the Amorites to be filled up; yet it was necessary both that the malice be
hated, and that the honor be pushed away for the sake of the many, and that
the equality and purity of God concerning all things brought be shown.
29. For he who said to Israel being impious, If you bring fine flour, it is
vain; incense is an abomination to me; and not receiving their new moons and
the Sabbaths, and a great day; for he has no need, being full, of any of the
human and small things, that he may even rejoice in the things unworthily
brought; and abhorring the sacrifice of the lawless, even if it be a calf, as
of a dog, and the frankincense as blasphemy, and shutting out from the temple
and shaking off the exchange of a harlot, and honoring only a sacrifice which
clean hands bring to the most clean, and a mind both high and purified: what
wonder, if he did not even receive that man's honor, being brought wickedly
and with a wicked mind, he who does not look as a man, nor look to the
apparent, but to the hidden man, and to the inward workshop of vice or of
virtue? This then is such; and if anyone disbelieves, we call to witness those
who themselves have seen; and there are many, who both to us and to those
after have sent this wonder on, and will send it on.
30. And when, already going forward into men, they touched the doctrines in
philosophy (as I would they never had!), and they were taking on besides the
power from the word, which for the fair becomes a weapon of virtue, but for
the more depraved a goad of vice, he was no longer able to hold the whole
disease, nor wholly to philosophize the deceit of the impiety in himself
alone; but as fire lurking in matter, even if it has not yet been raised
toward a bright flame, some sparks kindling or smoke from depth indicate
underneath; or if you will, of the springs which, running under some clefts
with breath, then not having breadth, nor a free outlet, blow up in many
places of the earth and sound underneath from below, being pushed up by the
breath, and being held and cut back by the force from above; so he also was
hiding the greater part of the impiety =35.557= under the time and the
tutoring of the one in power (for it was not yet safe to be impious openly);
but there is where he also stripped the secret of his judgment to those
sharper in the impiety or in the understanding; both in the words toward the
brother stretching himself more than was well on behalf of Hellenes (on a
pretext, forsooth, as exercising the weaker word, but it was truly an exercise
against the truth), and rejoicing in all things by which an impious manner is
characterized.
31. And when the loving-kindness of the emperor displays the brother as
emperor, and a not small part of the inhabited world is entrusted, and it was
possible for him according to much authority and license both of words and of
teachers the most destructive


to associate (and Asia was for him the school of the impiety, as much as
marvels concerning astronomy and the nativities, and a fantasy of
foreknowledge, and the magic following these); one thing still remaining was,
that power also be added to the impiety. Not much in the middle, and this the
multiplied lawlessness of the many gives him against us, and the, as one might
say, extreme well-being of Christians, seeking the contrary change, and the
authority and the honor, and the satiety through which we insulted.
32. For in truth it is in danger of being harder to hold the good things than
to acquire them when they are not present; and easier to call back prosperity
that has gone away through =35.560= care, than to save through the one that
exists; and before a crushing insolence leads, the Proverbs say well, and
before glory humiliation; or, that I may speak more clearly, crushing follows
insolence, and good repute humiliation. For the Lord arrays himself against
the proud, and gives grace to the humble, and he who measures all things
justly brings in the contraries in exchange for the contraries. Knowing these
things the divine David also sets as one of the good things even the being
contracted; and he confesses thanks to him who contracted, as of the learning
the statutes from there being added; and, Before I was humbled, he says, I
transgressed; for this I kept your oracle; setting the humiliation as a middle
of transgression and of correction, as being born from this, and the other
having given birth. For sin is mother of humiliation, and humiliation of
turning. We at least, having been lifted, when we were both fair and moderate,
and having been increased little by little, so as to go forward into this
figure and multitude with the leading by the hand of God, when we were
fattened, we skipped; and when we were broadened, we were straitened; and the
glory and power which we collected in the persecutions and the afflictions,
this, doing well, we pulled down; and the next of the word will show.
33. The empire and the life take a limit for the Caesar; for the things in the
middle I will be silent from sparing both of the one who did and of the one
who suffered; of both of whom, reverencing the piety, I do not praise the
rashness. For since it was necessary even to sin something, being humans, this
is what perhaps one would not praise of both; unless here also through the
things by which we will accuse the one, we will release the other from blame.
And he at once is heir of the brother's empire, but not of piety; and a little
later even of him who had made him emperor, the one willing, the other forced
by the common end, and defeated with a defeat evil and destructive for the
whole world.
34. What is this, O most divine of emperors and most Christ-loving (for I am
led on to blame as to one present and hearing, even if I know him much better
than our blame, stationed with God, =35.561= and having inherited the glory
there, and having changed so much, as to have transferred the empire); what
counsel is this that you counseled, differing much in understanding and
sharpness from all, not of the emperors in your time only, but even of those
before? You who were cleaning out the barbarian in a circle, and mastering the

battle are great, but greater and more conspicuous the bloodless; toward whom
even the embassies and supplications from everywhere; to whom some things were
obedient, and others were about to be; and every hoped thing was equal to the
mastered; who was being led by the hand of God toward every both counsel and
deed; of whom the understanding was more wondered at than the hand, and the
hand more than the understanding; and more than the good repute in both, the
piety.

speed of the inhuman humanity? What demon came in together with the plan? How
the

36. Perhaps I even appear to you, brothers, to be impious, and to be
ungrateful in these words, and that I do not quickly bring upon the words of
the accusation the things of the truth. And yet I have sufficiently defended,
even through the very things of which I have accused, if you give the mind to
the accusation; and here alone the accusation has the release. For having said
kindness, I made the defense clear. For who does not know, even of those who
know that man moderately, that for the sake of piety and of the affection
concerning us, and of the wishing every good for us, not only would he have
overlooked that man, or honor of a whole race, or an addition of the empire,
but even the empire itself, and all things that are, and the soul itself, than
which nothing is more honorable to anyone, he would not have exchanged with
difficulty our security and salvation. =35.564=
37. For a hot love of no thing ever so struck into anyone, as into that man
that Christians be increased, and go forward into most both of glory and of
power; and neither nations being mastered, nor the common being well-lawed,
not a multitude of money, not a surplus of glory, not both being and being
named emperor of emperors, not as many other things by which the happiness of
men is known; nothing of all things so gladdened that man, as that through
himself we, and through us he, be of good repute both with God and with men,
and that the power remain for us unoverthrown into all time. For besides the
other things he knew that clearly, thinking about these things more highly and
more royally than according to the many, that with the affairs of the
Christians the things of the Romans increased together, and the power came in
together with the sojourn of Christ, not yet formerly having wholly conquered
into monarchy; and for this he seemed to me to tend our things more, doing
well; who, even if he somewhat pained us, not despising, nor insulting, nor
gratifying some others before us, but so that all might be one, and might
think together, he pained, and that we might not be cut apart, nor stand apart
by the schisms.
38. But as I said, simplicity is unguarded, and the humane with the rotten,
and what is free of vice least suspects vice. For this the future was unknown,
and the fiction was not detected, and the impiety came in little by little,
and two kindnesses came in together with each other, that concerning the pious
race and that concerning the most impious of all and most godless. Who,
blaming Christians for what, and having what of ours not to praise, and having
thought what of the doctrines among the Hellenes extraordinary, and hard to
move by word, and following what example, did he make himself most known from
impiety, and contend with a newer manner with him who displayed him? Since at
least it was not possible to surpass in virtue and in the better things,
having been contentious to appear through the contrary of the unmeasuredness
toward impiety, and of the ambition concerning the worse. The defense then of
the man on behalf of Christians, and toward Christians, is such, and so just
for those at least who have mind. =35.565=
39. And since there are some who, even if they release us from this blame, not
letting go of another, but accusing simplicity, because he entrusted the rule
to the most hostile and most warlike, and constructed the same man first an
enemy, then strong, having laid down beforehand the enmity through the
slaughter of the brother, and having added the power through the empire: it is
necessary also concerning this to go through briefly and to show the humane
not altogether irrational, nor outside the imperial greatness of mind and
forethought. For I would even be ashamed if, having obtained such honor from
him, and having believed him to differ so much in piety, we should not
advocate the just things;


which is owed by us even to those who have benefited nothing, of the
attendants of the Word and of Truth; and these after the release from here,
when we have even escaped seeming to flatter, and the word is free of a wicked
suspicion.
40. For who would not have hoped, if nothing else, to make him milder by the
honors? And who not more just from the trust, in which he was trusted even
contrary to the likely? As of both by a just and imperial judgment, the one
having been censured, the other having been put forward. For he who honored
the second, with things which no one would have hoped, not even he himself who
obtained the honor, it is clear that he did not even punish the former without
just anger; and the one was of that man's rashness, the other of the humanity
of him who had honored.
41. And at once he had the being confident, if one must say the greater, not
so much from that man's trust, as from his own power; by which even Alexander
the great, I think, having been confident, gave not only the living to Porus
defeated, and these having contended so youthfully concerning the empire, but
even the empire of the Indians again; as about to display the magnanimity not
otherwise than thus, by which Alexander being thought it worse to be mastered
than by the weapons; and of the other, if he should take an evil, it being in
his power to master again. And the surplus of the being confident was for him
the humane.
42. And yet why do I contend this, it being possible to prevail strongly even
having been defeated? For if the one who trusted was evil, =35.568= what ever
would the one trusted be beside that man? And if the not having foreseen the
manner is blameworthy, where at least shall we set the vice itself? But truly
wickedness is some unsyllogizable thing; and there is not that by which one
would make the depraved better; when even that man, from the things from which
he was just to appear more well-minded, and if he had some ember of vice to
take this away, from these was kindled into a greater hatred, and was
considering how he might avenge himself on the benefactor.
43. These things Platos taught him, and Chrysippuses, and the bright Peripatos
and the solemn Stoa, and those who throat out the elegant things; these things
the equality of geometry, and the words concerning justice, and that one must
choose rather to be wronged than to wrong; these things the noble teachers,
and fellow-contenders and lawgivers of the empire, whom he collected for
himself from the crossroads and the pits; of whom he did not praise the
manner, but wondered at the fluency; and not even this perhaps, but impiety
alone, as a counselor sufficient both of the things to be done and not, a
teacher.
44. And how is it not worthy to wonder at these, who both in word mold cities,
those not able to be constituted in deed, and all but worship the solemn
tyrannies, and lead the obol above the gods with the brow; and some dogmatize
that there is not even a God at all, others that he does not provide for the
things here, but that the whole is carried randomly and as it chanced, others
to be led by stars and figures of necessity, I do not know by whom being led
and from where; and others to carry the whole into pleasure, and suppose this
to be a limit of human life? And virtue for them is otherwise a decent name,
and nothing longer than the present life, nor any examination of the things
lived here =35.569= later contracting the injustice. For either one of the
wise among them did not understand these things, but was covered together with
deep mire, as the saying is, and unlit gloom of error and ignorance, not even
so much as to look toward the rays of the truth having been purified in the
mind, but wriggling about the things below and the sense, and able to imagine
nothing above the demons, and to be lifted through worthily of him who made;
or if someone looked through even a little, using as a guide word, and not
God, he was swept aside by the more persuasive, and by what more draws the
many through nearness.
45. What then is wonderful, that the one starting from such doctrines, and
being directed by such steersmen, so also appeared evil to him who had
trusted, the one trusted, and to him who had honored, the one honored? For if
one must even through the


accusation make a defense, it does not seem to me so much, being in excess of
pain over the brother, whom at least he knew even as an enemy from the
religion, as not bearing the things of the Christians being increased, and
raging against the piety, to dare against the established things, and to seek
a freedom for the madness. For philosophy and empire, as their word has it,
must come together into the same; not that the cities may cease from evils,
but that they may be filled.
46. And the first of his self-will and madness, to bind himself, and to honor
with the great title (which not a plunder of chance, but a prize of virtue,
either time or a vote of an emperor grants, or a judgment of the senate
council, as of old), and he does not make the one of the empire master of the
measure of the honor. And second (for he knew that he had made the madness
necessary for himself from the things already dared), what does he think? and
where does he go forward of impiety and rashness? O that mad soul! He
campaigns against him himself, and from the west stands
up; =35.572= on a pretext, as about to defend concerning the diadem (for he
still seemed to hide his own madness); but the true, as about to transfer into
himself all the power, and to be wondered at through the ingratitude; and he
was not cheated of the hope.
47. Let them not wonder, those of the words of God, by which the whole is
carried, who do not know the unguessable depth, and do not yield to the
craftsman of the steering, at any rate more understanding than we, and leading
his own where and how he wishes; and wholly toward the better and the healing,
even if those being treated are displeased. By which words that man also was
not lifted into the vice (for the Divine is altogether without blame of evils,
being good by nature, and of him who chose beforehand the things of the vice);
yet he was not held back from the impulse. But with much speed having run
through both his own, and some of the barbarian bank, and having snatched the
passage by escaping notice rather than by prevailing, he comes near the
palace. As those who think his things, having been raised upon the campaign
according to foreknowledge, and of demons promising the future by a movement,
and managing a change for the affairs; but as the word of those who tell
truth, being present according to a set time, yet more unspeakable and deeper,
and hurrying upon death, of which he himself was craftsman, having stolen the
attempt by one of those within; so that the thing happening is not
foreknowledge, but knowledge, and a work of wickedness, but not a benefaction
of demons; who at least how well-aimed in such things, Persia showed clearly.
And let those who attribute that man's speed to the demons cease; unless we
shall add even the being evil itself to these.
48. If then the end of the emperor had not anticipated the attack of the
tyrant, nor the unseen war had more strength than the apparent hand, perhaps
the accursed would have known that he had hastened against himself; and before
=35.573= being sobered by Persians of the shock of the madness, he would have
given justice in the bounds of Romans, of which he badly dared. And a proof:
for still going forward and being unknown, as he thought, he is surrounded by
an army of the most noble emperor sending forward even the flight, as became
clear from the later things; for even already having the power, the prevailing
over this became no small work. But now he, boiling with wrath against the
madness together with the impiety, and having the most wise in snares, O our
vice! at the point of the road dissolves the life, having defended much both
to God and to men on behalf of his own humanity, and through the impulse
having shown to Christians the movement on behalf of the piety.
49. And here some tear comes upon me mixed with joy because of the next of the
word; and as a battle of a river and a sea, and a mixing, and a standing,
standing against each other, and coming together. For from the last things I
suffer the things of the pleasure,


and from the things that went before the tear; not on behalf of Christians
only and of the spite that fell together upon them, either having fallen upon
them from the evil one, and having been allowed by God through causes which he
knows, and perhaps through our lifting needing a cleansing; but also on behalf
of that man's soul itself, and of those snatched together by him toward the
same destruction.
50. For some lament their last blows and the tortures from here; for whom this
living is thought the only, and they do not reach with the mind toward the
things from there, nor think there is some account and repayment of the things
lived here; but they live a life of irrational things, always living the
toward a day and the present, measuring the being happy by this one thing, the
ease from here, and the being miserable by the contrary to those who fall
against them. But it comes upon me to mourn them more of the tortures from
there, and of the punishment laid up for the wicked. And I do not yet say the
greatest, the being thrust out from God itself, how great it is for them into
punishment. =35.576=
51. How shall I not weep the wretched? How shall I not mourn more those who
ran toward than those persecuted? And more than those who deserted into the
vice, him who snatched them together? Or rather, for the one there is nothing
terrible in suffering on behalf of Christ, but even most blessed of all
things, not through the things from there only, but also through the good
repute from here and the boldness, which they granted themselves through the
dangers; but for the others a prelude of the things laid up and threatened,
which they have already suffered; and it was better for them if they had been
punished more long from here than stored up for the courts from there. These
things then because of the law, which orders not to rejoice upon the fall of
the enemy, and demands the sympathetic from those who stand; but I must bring
the word back again toward him.
52. What is so great a contentiousness of the evil? What the love of the
impiety? What the run of the destruction? From where so Christ-hating the
disciple of Christ, who associated with so many words of the truth, and both
said some and heard of the things that carry into salvation? For he did not
anticipate inheriting the empire, and he is bold with the impiety, as even
being ashamed of having once become a Christian, or bearing a grudge over this
against Christians, with whom he shared the name. And the first of his
darings, as those who adorn themselves with that man's secrets (into what
words I am forced to fall!), washes off the bath with blood not holy, setting
against our initiation the completion of the defilement, a pig having been
rolled in mire, according to the proverb; and he purifies the hands, cleaning
off from the bloodless sacrifice through which we share with Christ, both of
the sufferings and of the godhead; and with entrails and sacrifices he settles
the palace, using evil counselors of an evil beginning.
53. But since I remembered entrails, and the man's superstition concerning
such things, or to say misfortune more properly, I do not know whether I
should give over to writing the wonder being rumored, or dis
believe those who say it. For I myself also am balanced in the judgment, and I
do not have toward which I should incline, the things that would justly be
believed being mixed with those not worthy of faith. For the having given some
sign to so great an innovation of the evil and impiety is of the things not
unbelievable, but even often already having happened around the greatest
changes; but even this manner is very worthy of wonder to me at least, and to
all who both wish and think the clean things to be shown cleanly.
54. The thing said, then, that as he was sacrificing the inward parts display
the cross being crowned, which furnished to the others shuddering, and agony,
and a joint perception of our power; but to the teacher of the impiety even
courage


forsooth, as of us having been drawn around and shut in together. For so he
improvised the cross and the circle. This then is my wonder; and if it is
false, let breezes carry it; but if true, again Balaam prophesies, and Samuel
is drawn, or seems, through a ventriloquist, and demons confess Jesus
unwilling, and through the contraries, that it may even be more believed, the
truth is shown. Perhaps it was being managed that that man also be held back
from the impiety through these; for the Divine knows to innovate many and
paradoxical roads of salvation, nodding toward the humane. And what is said by
more, it is not out of manner to believe.
55. He was going down into one of the inner shrines, untrodden and fearful to
the many (as I would he had gone even the one carrying into Hades, before
going forward into such evils), there being present with him also the one
worthy of many inner shrines, the wise in such things, or rather sophist. For
there is even this form of divination for them, to associate with some gloom
and subterranean demons concerning the future things; whether as they rejoice
more in darkness, since they even are darkness, and craftsmen of darkness of
the vice; or as they flee the mixings of the pious above earth, and through
these are weaker. And as the fears strike upon the noble as he goes forward,
and always more and more fearful, they say some unaccustomed sounds, and
smells of the unpleasant, and fire-gleaming apparitions, and I do not know
what nonsense and =35.580= idle talk, struck by the unexpected (for he was
late-learned in such things), he flees down upon the cross, and the ancient
medicine, and with this he signs himself against the fears, and makes the one
being persecuted a helper. And the next things more shuddering.
56. The seal had strength, the demons are defeated, the fears are loosed. Then
what? The evil breathes again, is bold again, again an impulse, and the same
fears, and the seal again, and the demons being still; and the initiate in
perplexity; and the mystagogue from near misinterpreting the truth: We have
been abhorred, saying, we did not frighten; the worse conquers. For he says
these things, and having said he persuades, and having persuaded he leads the
disciple upon the pit of the destruction. And nothing wonderful; for
wickedness is ready for the evil to follow rather than to be cut back by the
better. What things then having said, or having done, or having been deceived
he is sent up, those who both accomplish and are accomplished in such things
would know; but he comes up then both being demon-possessed in the soul and in
the affairs, and with the mad of the eyes indicating those whom he tended;
unless even from that day he was filled with demons, from which he so set
forth wicked things; but then even more conspicuously; lest he have gone down
in vain, and having partaken of the demons, which they call an enthusiasm,
changing the names solemnly. The first things then are such.
57. And when the travail prevailed, and the persecution was breaking out, he
saw something, whether then of a man wise in the vice and excessive in the
impiety, or he was taught by those who were anointing him into this. That the
taking up the war openly, and sitting down before as own-hand of the impiety,
besides being too rash and uneducated, was still also most contrary to the aim
wholly. For we would become more contentious being forced, and would set
against the tyranny the ambition on behalf of piety. For noble minds are wont
to be self-willed toward what prevails by force, and just as a flame fanned by
wind, to be kindled so much more, as it is blown down more vehemently
=35.581=. And this he was finding not from the reasonings only, but he had to
know also by the persecutions that went before, which have made Christianity
more honorable more quickly than weaker, strengthening the souls into piety,
and as hot iron with water, tempering them with the dangers. But if he should
campaign with the art, and should color the forcing with the persuading, or as
with bronze should throw around as bait upon the tyranny the pleasant, the
contest would thus become for him at once both wise and powerful.


58. For besides the other things he also begrudged the athletes the honor of
the martyrs; and for this he devices himself both to force, and not to seem,
and us both to suffer, and not to have honor as suffering on behalf of Christ.
O the simplicity! First if he thought to escape notice on behalf of what the
dangers are, and that he would cover the truth with the sophisms, but not, by
as much as he was inventing against the honors, by so much to make them
greater and more splendid.
59. And second, if he supposed us to be in danger by desire of glory, but not
of the truth. Let Empedocleses play these things among them, and Aristaeuses,
and some Empedotimuses, and Trophoniuses, and a number of such unfortunates;
of whom the one, having deified himself, as he thought, in the Sicilian
mixing-bowls, and having sent himself up from us into the better lot, was
indicated by the dearest sandal thrown out by the fire; and he was not shown a
god after a man, but a vain-glorious man, and unphilosophic after death, and
not even understanding the common things; and the others having hidden
themselves in some inner shrines under the same disease and self-love, then
having been refuted, were not more honored from the theft than insulted from
the not escaping notice.
60. But for Christians the suffering on behalf of piety is sweeter, even if
they escape the notice of all, than for others the being of good repute with
the impiety. For of pleasing men the account is short for us; but of the honor
from God the whole desire; or rather even above this, those at least who are
truly philosophic and god-loving, loving the kinship toward the beautiful
=35.584= through the beautiful itself, not the honors laid up from there. For
this is a second rank of the praiseworthy, to do something for a wage and
through a repayment; as a third, by fear of punishment to flee the depravity.
Our things then are such and so having; and this is easy to show from more to
those who wish.
61. But he, as about to deprive Christians of a great honor (for the many
judge the things of the others by their own passions), this he pursues before
the others, the good repute. And not even magnanimously, like the other
persecutors, does he register the impiety; nor, if not imperially, at least
tyrannically does he think wholly concerning us, that the impious deed may be
magnificent for him, to force a people of the inhabited world, and to
tyrannize a doctrine standing above all doctrines; but very slavishly and
ignobly he does evil to the piety, and bringing the weavings and doublings
concerning the reasonings he introduced them upon the persecution against us.
Therefore, the prevailing having been divided into these two, the persuading
and the forcing, what was more inhuman, he let go to peoples and cities the
thing of the tyranny, of whom the madness is also more uncatchable through the
irrationality, and the inconsiderate carrying upon all things; and this not by
a public order, but by the not cutting back the impulses, having set forth as
an unwritten law the wishing.
62. And what was milder and more imperial, he brings into himself forsooth,
the thing of persuasion; yet he did not save this through wholly. For he did
not have nature either for a leopard to put off the spotted, or an Ethiopian
the black, or fire the burning, or the wicked the


misanthropy, being from the beginning a man-killer, or that man the
wickedness, from which he started against us. But as the chameleon is said to
become of every sort readily, and to take on all colors, except one, the
whiteness (for I pass by Proteus, the Egyptian sophist of the myth), so that
man also was and was becoming all things to Christians, except mildness, and
the humane was very inhuman for him, and the persuasive violent, and the
kindness a defense of the wildness; that he may reasonably seem to force,
failing of the persuading.
63. And this is clear from the things in which the thing of persuasion was
short with him, but more the thing of force at once following; that, as in the
hunts, we may be caught either by the snares, or by the chasings, and one
manner at any rate may master us. And having both thought these things and


divided, he generalships that second thing, and alone securely, even if very
impiously, from the nearest and the hand around him, which is customary to all
persecutors, he begins the vice. For it is not even possible to attempt the
things outside, these not having been made one's own, as not even to lead an
army upon enemies, one that is in faction toward the general.
64. And for this he transforms the palace, some having led out beforehand by
death first, others having pushed aside; not as well-minded to the great
emperor only, but also as more well-minded to the greater, and unprofitable to
him according to both; and he wins over the soldiery, the one through himself,
the other through those in office, which he also supposed more easy of belief,
the one being defeated by the honors, the other being swept aside by
simplicity, and knowing one law only, the wish of the emperor.
65. Or rather a not least part of the soldiery, and as much as he found rotten
and diseased, a slave of the time both then and before; of which the one had
been enslaved, the other was hoping. For he did not take away the whole, nor
did the persecutor through that man give him so much against us; but there
remained above the seven thousand, who did not bend a knee to Baal, nor
worship the golden image, nor were wounded by the serpents =35.588= from the
looking to the serpent that was hung, and was pulled down by the sufferings of
Christ; many of those in powers and heights, whom it was even more likely to
be defeated both through fear and through hopes; and many of those below and
known by number only, whom having struck against he was beaten off, as by some
engine of a noble wall of the things not timely. Except the escaping did not
pain more than the being caught encouraged, as one raging; and the wishing set
beside the hoped as being mastered.
66. And he dares already even against the great standard, which processes with
the cross, and leads the army being raised into height, being a releaser of
toils both and being named according to Romans and reigning, as one might say,
of the remaining standards; as many as are delighted with faces of emperors,
and with spread fabrics in different dyes and drawings, and as many as with
fearful yawns of dragons being blown upon, being poised upon tips of spears,
and being fanned through the trails, speckled with woven scales, a spectacle
both most sweet and shuddering falls upon the sights. And when the near things
were according to mind for him, and he became better than the danger in hands,
as he thought, so already he tries also the next.
67. Most simple, and most impious, and most uneducated in the great things!
You against so great a lot and the worldwide fruit-bearing, which has taken
through all the ends, through the cheapness of the word and the foolishness,
as you yourselves would say, of the proclamation, which conquered wise men,
and stopped demons, and overshot time, the same being at once both ancient and
new, as you marvel some one of the gods; the one for the few, the other for
the many; and the one by the sketch, the other by the completion of the
mystery stored up for its own times? You against the great inheritance of
Christ? (who, and how much, and from where?) the great and not about to cease,
not even if some should rage more than you, but always going =35.589= upon
more and being raised; for I believe the predictions and the things seen;
which as God he made, and as man he inherited; which law typed, and grace
filled, and Christ renewed; which prophets fastened together, and apostles
bound together, and evangelists fitted?
68. You against the sacrifice of Christ with your pollutions? You against the
blood that cleansed the world with your bloods? You a war against the peace?
You a hand against that which was nailed on behalf of you and through you? You
against the gall your tasting? against the cross a trophy? against the death a
pulling down? against the resurrection


an uprising? against the martyr not even martyrs? after Herod a persecutor,
and after Judas a betrayer? except that you did not show the repentance by a

69. Did you not reverence the slaughters on behalf of Christ? nor did you fear
the great contenders, that John, Peter, Paul, James, Stephen, Luke, Andrew,
Thecla, those after them and before them who ran the danger beforehand of the
truth? who with fire, and iron, and beasts, and tyrants contended against
readily, both present evils and threatened, as in others' bodies or bodiless?
For the sake of what? that they might not betray the piety even as far as a
word. Of whom the great honors and festivals; by whom demons are driven, and
diseases are healed; of whom the appearances, and of whom the predictions; of
whom even the bodies alone are able equally with the holy souls, either being
touched, or being honored; of whom even drops of blood alone, and small
symbols of suffering, do equal things with the bodies.
70. These things you do not revere, but you dishonor; you who wonder at the
pyre of Heracles from a misfortune, and =35.592= of the injustices concerning
women; and the butchery of Pelops, the guest-loving, or god-loving, from which
the Pelopidae are notable from the shoulders and the ivory; and the cuttings
of Phrygians, those charmed by a flute, and insulted after the flute; and the

later; you who praise the Laconian youths being carded with the whips, and the
blood upon the altar delighting a pure and virgin goddess; you who lift the
