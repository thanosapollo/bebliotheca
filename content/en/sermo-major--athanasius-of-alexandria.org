exactly both the impassibility of the nature of the Word and the weaknesses
spoken of because of the flesh, it is good to hear the blessed Peter. For this
one would become a trustworthy witness concerning the Savior. He writes
therefore in the letter saying: «Christ therefore having suffered on behalf of
us in flesh.» Therefore also when he is said to hunger and thirst and grow
weary and not to know and to sleep and to weep and to ask and to flee and to
be born and to decline the cup and simply all the things of the flesh, it
would be said correspondingly upon each «Christ therefore hungering and
thirsting on behalf of us in flesh.» And saying not to know and being slapped
and growing weary «on behalf of us in flesh». And being exalted again and
being born and growing and fearing and hiding «in flesh» and saying: «if it is
possible let the cup pass from me» and being struck and receiving «on behalf
of us in flesh». «And wholly all such things «on behalf of us in flesh».» For
indeed the apostle himself has for this reason said «Christ therefore having
suffered» not in Godhead but «on behalf of us in flesh», in order that the
passions might be recognized as not properties of the Word himself according
to nature, but properties of the flesh itself by nature. Let no one therefore
be scandalized from the human things, but rather let him know that the Word
himself is impassible as to nature, and yet because of the flesh which he put
on these things are said concerning him, «since these are properties of the
flesh,» but the body itself is proper to the Savior. And he himself remains
impassible as to nature, as he is, not being harmed from these, but rather
making them vanish and destroying them. 101 From the same discourse. These
things we have necessarily examined beforehand, in order that if we see him
acting or speaking divinely through the instrument of his own body, we may
know that, being God, he works these things. And again, if we see him speaking
or suffering in human fashion, let us not be ignorant that, wearing flesh, he
has become man and thus does and speaks these things. For knowing the property
of each and seeing both being done from one, and understanding, we believe
rightly and we shall never be led astray. But if someone, seeing the things
coming to be from him alone divinely, should deny the body, or also, seeing
the properties of the body, should deny the incarnate presence of the Word, or
also from the human things should think low things of the Word, such a one, as
a Jewish huckster mixing the wine with water, will think the cross a
«stumbling-block», and as of Greeks will consider the preaching «foolishness»,
such things as indeed the God-fighting Arians also have suffered. For seeing
the human things of the Savior they supposed him to be a creature. Therefore
it was necessary also, seeing the divine works of the Word, that they deny the
generation of his body and thereafter number themselves also with Manichaeans.
