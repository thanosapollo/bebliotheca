* On Lazarus and on the Palm Branches
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of Hesychius, presbyter of Jerusalem, a discourse on Lazarus and on the palms.

1 Just as the immeasurable abyss abundantly gushes forth the springs of the
waters, and the springs again send forth unrestrained rivers, and the rivers
visit the earth and make it drunk, that it may bear fruit of bread for eating
and wine for the gladness of men, and men, enjoying these, fulfill what is
written: "The poor shall eat and be filled, and they who seek the Lord shall
praise him": so also the unspeakable loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus Christ,
from the immeasurable abyss of his goodness and loving-kindness, causes his
holy feasts to spring forth for us, in which he worked our salvation. And he
himself causes a feast to spring forth, but the grace of the Holy Spirit that
is at work in the feast fills "the streams of the river," I mean the
God-bearing men, who possess "teaching in the teaching"; and they visit the
intelligible and rational earth of humanity, gladdening the holy church, which
is "city of the great king" Jesus Christ, according to what is written: "The
streams of the river make glad the city of God." And the church, being
gladdened and sweetened from fatness of heart, praises and glorifies the Lord
who reigns in her. Come then, let us also today say what he himself proclaimed
beforehand concerning himself through his holy prophets; let us search,
according to our power, the salvation which our king accomplished for us in
this feast; come, let us understand for what reason not only men but also the
angelic powers hymned him as king.

2 The cause then of the hymnody was the raising of Lazarus according to what
appears, as the gospel says: "And they took counsel that they might kill
Lazarus also, because many of the Jews went away on account of him and
believed in Jesus." And this is the cause of the doxology of the faithful
according to what appears, but secretly and hiddenly the raising of all human
nature. And just as the sign of Lazarus and that raising made men hymn Jesus,
so also the sign of the raising of all human nature prepared the holy angels
to glorify; and as much as the raising of all is greater than the raising of
Lazarus, so much also was the glory then greater. And the consciousness of
those crying out, being astonished at the wonder, moved them to glorify; but
the divine economy, on account of the raising of all human nature, secretly
moved not only men but also the holy angels to marvel at and glorify Jesus.
For they saw, they truly saw and learned and were instructed in a wisdom which
they did not know, the angelic powers, through the church of the faithful,
being manifested from God who created all things, the wisdom hidden in him
before the ages, I mean of the teachings and promises and wonders of the Lord
Jesus, as somewhere the divine Paul says: "That now there might be made known
to the principalities and the powers through the church the many-colored
wisdom of God." For the powers above learn those things which they have not
yet seen, nor heard; for they are creatures, and the creature is not without
need of learning, nor do they know the things that are about to come to be,
unless they be initiated by the holy and august and worshipful Trinity.

3 They learned then and were confirmed in the resurrection of Adam, having
beheld the Lord raising the son of the widow; for the earth became as a widow,
when her first-formed son died. And seeing him again raising the daughter of
the ruler of the synagogue, they were instructed that he will raise Eve also
with Adam. Again, when <he> raised the child of the foreign centurion, they
were taught this also, that he will raise the foreign nations as well. Behold
three differences of the dead. But when he raised Lazarus the four-day dead
from the tomb, the one constituted of four elements, the one who had stunk,
the one who had rotted, the one overmastered by worms, it was made known to
the principalities and the powers in the heavens and to those on earth, that
he will raise all humanity constituted of the four, even if it stank in the
sin of the transgression, even if it rotted in the impiety of idolatry, even
if, as by worms, it was overmastered by demons and taken captive into their
will. These things then the angels having seen secretly and men openly, they
were astonished, and they were struck with amazement, and they began to cry
out and say: "Hosanna in the highest, blessed is he who comes in the name of
the Lord, hosanna in the highest." And they praised him not only as a bearer
of signs, but also as a king who had taken captive all the captivity that from
of old had been taken captive by the devil and by death. On account of these
things and such as these the divine prophets proclaim him king.

4 And since the aforementioned prophets are feast-givers of rational souls,
and just as the months of the year feed human bodies in part, so also they in
part, or rather in common, feed according to the feast and always the souls of
the faithful, and they make Christians drunk with the divine teachings, as
somewhere one of them says: "They shall be made drunk from the fatness of your
house." Come, let us hear, and they will recount to us, all who love the
kingdom of the Lord, what concerning it the great Zechariah, the glorious
among prophets, prophesies to us. Let us understand what the herald
evangelizes, running before the kingdom long beforehand. Come, let us enjoy,
as rational banquet-guests, the words that proceed through the mouth of God
through the spiritual feast-giver Zechariah; for it fell to him to feed us in
this feast, as the Lord says that "man shall not live on bread alone, but on
every word proceeding through the mouth of God." For since grief is hateful
among men, but joy is longed for, he begins from joy, crying out and saying:
"Rejoice greatly, daughter of Zion." "Rejoice greatly," the prophet cries to
the captivity. "O depth of the riches and wisdom and knowledge of God." Why
"Rejoice"? And why "greatly" "be glad and be of good cheer"? Because he has
come who turns you back, as the father of the king who turns you back
proclaimed beforehand, saying: "When the Lord turned back the captivity of
Zion, we became as those who have been comforted; then our mouth was filled
with joy and our tongue with exultation."

5 "Rejoice," he says, because you return from the captivity of the demons and
the exile of this age into the Jerusalem above, which your father Abraham
awaited, as somewhere the blessed apostle Paul says: But you "have come to
Mount Zion and to the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem, and to
myriads of angels, a festal assembly and church of firstborn enrolled in the
heavens, and to God the judge of all, and to the spirits of the righteous made
perfect." From which "pain, grief, and groaning have fled," where is the
dwelling of all the righteous who are being gladdened. Then "be glad and be of
good cheer" and "rejoice greatly, daughter" of this Zion, clearly. "And why,"
she says, "shall I rejoice greatly, O prophet?" "Rejoice," because that
terrible one will no longer take you captive, and "greatly," because not as
the other kings is the one who delivers you, but an eternal king and his
kingdom is eternal, as the angel Gabriel says to his mother and virgin Mary:
"And of his kingdom there shall be no end." And as his kingdom is eternal, so
your salvation is eternal. Therefore then: "Rejoice greatly; proclaim,
daughter of Jerusalem." What shall I proclaim, O prophet? Proclaim to your
king, who came down from the heights of heaven to save you, thanksgiving,
doxology, hymnody. Proclaim: "Hosanna in the highest, from where you came to
save me, blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord." And consider with
me the evangelist, how he proclaims the Christ as double; for in saying
"Hosanna in the highest," he makes clear the high nature of the Godhead, and
in saying "Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord," he signifies the
essence of the humanity, the blessed seed with which the blessed Abraham was
blessed, that in his seed all the nations of the earth should be blessed; "For
blessed," he says, "is the coming kingdom of our father David."

6 Let us take up again the former voice and squeeze it as a prophetic breast;
for it still has good things of milk and contemplation. For he says: "Rejoice
greatly, daughter of Zion; proclaim, daughter of Jerusalem." And for what
reason "daughter of Zion" and not Zion, and "daughter of Jerusalem" and not
Jerusalem? Attend intelligently, I entreat. For just as the names of plants
and of birds and of four-footed creatures and of men possess names according
to the difference of form and of power, so each tittle of the divine oracles
possesses its own power and contemplation. Therefore let us search why ever
the prophet first calls the now faithful Jerusalem "daughter of Zion," then
"daughter of Jerusalem." For he says: "Rejoice greatly, daughter of Zion;
proclaim, daughter of Jerusalem." Zion is interpreted "thirsting"; and this is
the synagogue of the righteous and prophets, who from of old until the coming
of Christ thirsted to be united and to please the divine and worshipful
essence of the Godhead, as the chief of the apostles Peter says: "That they
might become partakers of the divine nature." These, seeking the Lord and
thirsting, were called Zion, as one of them said: "My soul thirsted toward God
the strong, the living;"

7 And when they attained and drew near, or rather appeared and beheld his
face; for so another edition says: they became Jerusalem, "seeing God and a
watchtower of all knowledge." And he calls daughter of this Zion the faithful
in the Lord, who through faith thirst and through the working of the
commandments of the gospel seek to find the Lord, who is hidden in them, as
the Holy Spirit says: "This is the generation of those who seek the Lord, of
those who seek the face of the God of Jacob." And when they attain, they are
called daughter of the aforementioned Jerusalem, contemplating God and looking
toward spiritual knowledge. And the prophet, entreating God through the Spirit
to show forth such a Zion and Jerusalem, says: "Remember your synagogue, which
you acquired from the beginning." Therefore with this your mother Zion,
"Rejoice greatly, daughter of Zion; proclaim, daughter of Jerusalem," crying
out heavenly with your mother: "Hosanna in the highest, blessed is he who
comes in the name of the Lord."

8 And what does the gospel say? "When they drew near to Jerusalem and came to
Bethphage." For that the faithful might eat him, he was coming in the upper
room, "toward the Mount of Olives," which is the faithful church from the
people. "Then Jesus sent two of his disciples," who are two choirs of the
prophets and apostles; for the apostles, discipling the nations, had with
themselves the God-inspired oracles of the prophets, which bear witness to
Christ, in the manner in which Ezekiel, the great among prophets, beheld them:
"A wheel," he says, "in a wheel," flying and running. Jesus saying to them:
"Going into the village opposite"; for he sent them into all the inhabited
world of the nations, because they were until then opposed to the faithful
church from the people. For at a fitting time he sent them "into the village
opposite" of the nations; for it was ready to receive the aforementioned two
choirs of the apostles and prophets. "And to bring to him the colt," who are
the nations, that he might mount and reign over them, as the psalmist says:
"God reigned over the nations, the Lord sits upon his holy throne." "Upon
which no man ever sat"; for they had neither a God mounting and reigning over
them, nor law nor prophets. "Having untied him," he says, "bring him to me";
for the apostles unbound the nations from the darkness, that is from the
ignorance of God, from error, from sin, from the slavery of the alien, from
"the authority of darkness," "of the ruler of this age." And they brought them
to the Lord their creator and fashioner.

9 And behold he sits upon them and reigns as upon the cherubim, they crying
out and saying: "Hosanna in the highest, blessed is he who comes in the name
of the Lord; peace in heaven and glory in the highest"; for so Luke says.
"Peace in heaven," for what reason does he say it? Did the angels not have
peace in heaven? Yes, and they had peace among themselves very much, "the
peace that surpasses every mind," as the most wise Paul says. They had peace
in heaven, but with men they did not have it; heaven then had peace by itself.
But when the angels saw all humanity being raised through the sign of Lazarus,
and the nations being unbound after the manner of the colt and coming to be
reigned over by their fashioner, then heaven also was at peace with men, and
with one accord they cried out and said: "Blessed is he who comes in the name
of the Lord, peace in heaven and glory in the highest." "And they laid the
garments upon the colt," which signify the grace given them from him among the
nations, and through it "they mounted Jesus upon them"; and not only this, but
also through their own virtues, as the evangelist signifies saying: "And
others were spreading their garments in the way." And in this he hints at
their own virtues, which they yoked together with the grace, as the divine
Paul says openly: "Having then been justified from faith, let us have peace
toward God through our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom we have also had the
access by faith into this grace in which we stand, and we boast upon hope of
the glory of God." Thus far the things of grace. "And not only so, but we also
boast in our afflictions, knowing that affliction works endurance, and
endurance proving, and proving hope; and hope does not put to shame, because
the love of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit
given to us." And the "they were spreading on the earth," because bodily and
openly and perceptibly their virtues were beheld before the nations, as also
in another place the apostle says toward these things: "What things you saw in
me and heard and received, these practice." But let us return to the matter at
hand.

10 "And the crowds going before him and those following were crying out," with
praises and branches of olives [and palms], which signify the practical
working of the outer man, and of palm-branches, which make known the spiritual
[and practical] and gnostic and pure working of the inner man. "They were
crying out saying: Hosanna in the highest." For in truth all the crowds of the
righteous and prophets and priests, who are those going before and those
following, cry out by faith and say: "Hosanna to the son of David; blessed is
he who comes in the name of the Lord; Hosanna in the highest." As the
evangelist says: "And this whole thing came to pass, that what was spoken
through the prophet might be fulfilled, saying: Say to the daughter of Zion."
To whom do you say "Say," O prophet? Behold you proclaim, behold you speak. I,
he says, was longing to see and to hear, not I alone but also many prophets
and righteous. But also to those who are found and made disciples, seeing and
hearing the king of Zion, I speak, that they may say and proclaim: "Say to the
daughter of Zion: Behold your king comes to you." And he did not say merely
"comes," but "comes to you," that is, to become yours and to dwell in you.
"Behold your king comes to you, righteous and saving," saving from the slavery
of the enemy those who believe upon him.

11 "Meek and mounted upon a colt, the son of a beast of burden," that is
humble-minded, and in his humility "destroying the chariots of the kingdom of
Ephraim," that is of the devil who reigned over Israel and over the nations.
And he destroyed also according to the letter even the kingdom of the people
of the Jews; for until Christ the anointing and the kingdom of the Jews came,
and it was stopped. "And a horse," he says, "from Jerusalem," that is all the
pride of their rule, through his humble colt. And the patriarch Jacob,
blessing Judah and making manifest the blessing toward his fathers Abraham and
Isaac, which is Christ, says: "Binding his colt to the vine." For to the vine
that migrated from Egypt, that filled all the earth from the teaching of her
prophets and righteous, he bound the unclean nations; for the colt is unclean
in the law, so also the nations. "Binding his colt to the vine." Why? That
they might receive from her the law and the prophets and the knowledge of God
in them, that is Christ, who is blessing of the nations and purification. For
he says: "And to the tendril the colt of his donkey," tendril, that is the
shoot from the vine, who is Christ, having sprouted from the vine of the
synagogue from Egypt, as Jacob himself says to him: "From a shoot, my son, you
went up." And the nations were bound in him inseparably.

12 Therefore let us also be bound to Christ inseparably. If we altogether
contend to be saved and to be benefited from his feasts, he also will raise us
together with Lazarus from our dead works, those that came to be without him.
Thus let us dine with him in Bethany, which is interpreted house of obedience,
thus fittingly let us gather our thoughts from the captivity of forgetfulness
together with the senses of the body, crying out and saying: "Hosanna in the
highest; blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord," to enter into his
own Jerusalem, not in order to suffer as then, but that he may save her and be
glorified by her, as David addresses her saying: "Praise, Jerusalem, the Lord;
praise your God, Zion." He said a second time: "praise" and "praise," with
soul and body, "because he strengthened the bars of your gates," which are
faith, hope, love, meditation, long-suffering, endurance. "He blessed your
sons in you," who are the faithful infants who received the Lord with the
branches, that is with new fruits, those nursing the milk of the teaching of
Christ; for believing in him they at once praise, hymn, glorify, as it is
written: "Out of the mouth of infants and nursing ones you have perfected
praise." Let us become for him a holy vehicle instead of an unclean colt, that
he may mount upon us. Let us yoke for him the chariot of our thoughts, "and he
will reign" over us "unto the ages, and of his kingdom there shall be no end."

13 Let us take a cheerful way of life as olive branches, "to make our face
cheerful with oil," as the psalmist says, and again: "Serve the Lord in
gladness." Let us take up again in the inner man the divine and spiritual
working, as the palms of the palm-trees, and let us follow giving thanks to
him; and instead of the whiteness the right and unstained and simple faith;
and instead of the sharpness the enduring hope, which goads and slays every
thought that is lifted up to take us captive from the knowledge of God; and
instead of the sweetness of the palm the sweet love, which is constituted from
the aforementioned faith and hope and the rest, that what was spoken may be
fulfilled in us: "The righteous shall flourish as a palm-tree; as a cedar in
Lebanon he shall be multiplied." Let us follow him into the holy city, let us
enter with him into the temple, that he may overturn the tables of the
money-changers in us, and may drive away from us the demons who sell the
doves, the thoughts flying in error, together with all who sell in us and buy,
and instead of a cave of robbers may make us, according to the ancient temple,
a holy temple in which Christ dwells and walks, that he may grant light to our
blind, and make the deaf hear and the lame walk, and raise the dead with
Lazarus (for where life dwells, there is resurrection), that with Lazarus and
the crowds and his disciples, and with infants and nursing ones, and with the
holy angels we may cry out saying: "Hosanna in the highest; blessed is he who
comes in the name of the Lord," Christ Lord and God, the "king of the ages";
to him glory, honor, and worship, together with the Father and the Holy
Spirit, now and always and unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
