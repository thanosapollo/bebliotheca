On the three-day resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ, a homily.
=50.821=
Rejoice in the Lord always, beloved brethren; again I will say, rejoice; the
Lord is near, be anxious for nothing; the Lord has been raised from the dead,
and with him a great crowd of saints. Let us therefore feast both gladly and
with temperance; for this is truly the day which the Lord has made; let us
rejoice and be glad in it; let us proclaim the resurrection of the Savior, or
rather let us shout our own salvation; let us proclaim the memorial of the
saving day; let us proclaim the death of the devil, the captivity of the
unclean demons, the salvation of the Christians, the resurrection of the dead.
For through the resurrection of Christ the gehenna of fire is quenched, and
the sleepless worm dies; Hades is troubled, the devil mourns, sin is put to
death, wicked spirits are driven out, those from earth run up into the
heavens, those in Hades are freed from the bonds of the devil, and fleeing to
God they say to the devil: Where, O death, is your victory? Where, O Hades, is
your sting? And the cause for us of this holy feast and festival is Christ,
who is also the procurer of all good things for us. For he both made us from
the beginning, and brought us from not-being into being; he also now saved
those who were perishing; he made alive those who had been put to death; and
he took us away from the tyranny of the devil; he made us who were slaves to
sin free, blotting out the handwriting against us; Christ redeemed us from the
curse of the law, becoming a curse for us. Upon which it is fitting for us
also to say: What shall we render to the Lord for all that he has rendered to
us?
Being God Only-begotten, he was well-pleased to become man for us, and he
became obedient unto death, that he might deliver us from eternal death; the
Master of the angels put on the form of a slave; God the Word assumed flesh,
and he appeared as man, who is of like form and consubstantial with the
Father; and he endured these things, that he might snatch us from unjust
slavery and ransom us from dishonor; for this reason the dispenser of our life
consented to suffer in the flesh; for this reason also the fountain of
immortality was buried, that he might grant everlasting life to mortals. And
he was present on earth benefiting, and healing the passions of men; but from
the God-fighting Jews he received unworthy returns. For our Lord Jesus Christ
through great goodness cleansed lepers, granted sight to the blind, healed the
lame, drove out demons, raised Lazarus four days dead from the dead, from five
loaves fed five thousand, walked upon the sea, changed the water into wine,
healed the woman with the issue of blood, made alive the dead daughter of the
ruler of the synagogue, and did many other mysteries worthy of wonder; but the
Jews, moved by envy and malice, at one time were stoning the Lord, at another
were trying to cast him down a precipice, and at last they also led him to the
cross. But our Lord Jesus Christ did not imitate the wickedness of the
blasphemous Jews; but he also gave his back to scourges, according to the
prophet, and his cheeks to slaps; and he did not turn away his face from the
shame of spittings; and at last he was led as a sheep to the slaughter, and as
a lamb before its shearer is silent, not resisting, not speaking against.
Being blasphemed he did not revile in return, suffering he did not threaten;
but he committed himself to him who judges justly.
For he did not come at the first coming to punish and take vengeance on the
unbelievers, but he willed through longsuffering and endurance to lead those
who had gone astray to the truth. And learn the goodness of the Lord and the
great =50.822= kindness. The Jews were blaspheming him, and saying to him: You
have a demon; but the Lord, being longsuffering, was driving the demons out of
men. Jews were spitting into the face of the Savior, but he was healing their
blind; Jews were stoning Christ, but Christ was granting to their lame the
power to run; and throughout he continued benefiting those who were insulting
him, and instead of evils granting good things to those ungrateful and
polluted men; and bearing reproaches without resentment, perhaps even the one
escorted by angels was thought to be weak. And lest by saying many things we
seem to stretch out the discourse, let us come to the very heads of the
matters.
At last then the King of glory was being led to the cross and death, and he
who is glorified by the Cherubim and Seraphim, and worshipped by all powers
and angels, is nailed to the wood; and these things he both endured and
suffered meekly, furnishing us a pattern, and becoming a teacher of
gentleness. For this reason therefore we also ought to bear nobly the threats
of wicked men. But even hanging upon the cross he displayed greater works and
more wonders, that even thus he might stop the madness of those who hate God,
so that they might have no pretext for unbelief, nor say that they crucified a
mere man.
First then Christ consented to be crucified and lifted up into the air, that
he might put to flight the demons in the air; he was hung upon wood, that he
might heal the sin that of old came upon men through wood; and he was also
pierced in the side with the spear, on account of the woman taken from the
side of Adam. For since the serpent deceived Eve, and Eve prepared Adam to
transgress (and a sentence went out against them both, and death reigned from
Adam until Moses even over those who had not sinned), for this reason the side
is wounded, that we may learn that the passion of Christ brought salvation not
only to men but also to women. For Adam was formed first, then Eve. And Adam
was not deceived, but the woman being deceived has come to be in
transgression; but she shall be saved through childbearing. What childbearing,
if not through the childbearing of Mary? For she brought forth the Savior
Christ, not having come together with a man, as Isaiah testifies, but the Holy
Spirit overshadowing her, as Gabriel the archangel announced the good news.
For this reason then the side of Christ is also struck, that the things said
beforehand might be ordered, and the mystery of baptism might be proclaimed,
and the grace that is to come might shine. For blood and water gush from the
side of Christ, that he might wipe out the handwriting of sin against us, and
that we might be cleansed by his blood, and recover paradise. O great mystery!
The thief repented, there was need of water that he might be baptized; he was
hanging upon the cross, there was no other place of baptism, no spring, no
lake, no rain, no one performing the mystic rite; for all the disciples fled
through fear of the Jews; but Jesus was not at a loss for streams, but even
hanging upon the cross he became a creator of waters.
For since it was not possible for the thief to enter into the kingdom without
baptism, and it was necessary that the one who had repented not be without a
share of baptism, the Savior sent forth water and blood from his pierced side,
that he might both free the thief from the evils that hung over him, and show
his blood as a ransom =50.823= having come to be for those who have their
hopes in him. For if the blood of bulls and of goats, and the ashes of a
heifer sprinkling, sanctifies those who have been defiled toward the
purification of the flesh; how much more has the blood of Christ our Savior
become a purification of all Christians together?
If then some one of the unbelievers says to you, Why was Christ crucified? say
to him: That he might crucify the devil. If he says to you, Why was he hung
upon wood? say to him: That he might recall the sin that came through wood in
paradise. If he says to you, Why did he also wear thorns? say to him: That he
might uproot the thorns and thistles of Adam; for that one was condemned to
groan and tremble, and to cultivate thorns and thistles. Jesus therefore,
being a lover of man, wishing to provide for his own creature, underwent all
things for us, that he might free us from condemnation. For just as he was
born through a woman, that he might wipe out the sin that came upon men from a
woman; so also he is crowned with thorns, that through his own obedience he
might make more tame the earth that had been badly cultivated by disobedience.
But if he says to you, Why did he drink gall and vinegar? say to him: That we
might vomit out the death-bearing poison of the dragon; for that gall has
become my sweetness, and that vinegar has become my healing. But if the
unbeliever says to you again, Why did he also put on a scarlet cloak, and why
did they come kneeling to him, say to him: That even unwilling the Jews might
worship him, and even against their will confess his kingdom upon earth. Then
they were worshipping in mockery, not knowing what they were doing; but in the
coming resurrection every knee shall bow of things in heaven and on earth and
under the earth, and every tongue shall confess that Jesus Christ is Lord to
the glory of God the Father. Amen. And that cloak also has another riddle; for
it not only typed the kingdom, but also displayed the bloodthirsty and
murderous character of the Jews. And they also gave a reed into his hand, that
their sins might be written.
These things then the Christ-fighters were doing, not knowing the one who was
being crucified, or rather having willingly blinded themselves; but creation
did not fail to know its own Master and creator. For while the Savior was
still hanging upon the cross, the sensible sun, having seen the sun of
righteousness Christ being insulted by the lawless, not bearing the daring,
flees having darkened the earth, and considering it unfitting =50.824= to
cooperate, and to give light to eyes that were committing the greatest
impiety. And not only did the sun flee, but the earth also was shaken, not
bearing the lawlessness of the things being done, and showing and teaching
that it was God who was being crucified; wherefore it also did not endure, but
was angry, not wishing to bear upon itself the God-hating Jews. For Cain
having slain his brother did not so stain the earth, nor did the
tower-building of the giants so weigh it down, nor did the Sodomites having
done unlawful things so pollute it, nor even those who fashioned the idols
from it; nor did the blood of Zechariah and Abel poured out so weigh it down
as the Jews, having dared this great impiety. For this reason also the hard
rocks were split, that they might learn that this one is the spiritual and
living rock. For they drank, he says, from a spiritual rock that followed; and
the rock was Christ. O the ingratitude of the Jews! The rocks were split, and
these are without feeling; the lifeless things are shaken, and the living
disbelieve; the veil of the temple is torn, that their desolation might
thereafter be shown. For the veil was split, and the things in the temple were
laid bare because of Christ who said: Behold, your house is left to you
desolate. For indeed after the Christ-killing all the venerable things of the
Jews have been made desolate, and the angels remaining in the city and the
temple departed from there, and came into the Church. And many bodies of the
saints who had fallen asleep were raised together with Christ, that we might
learn that Christ having died does not rise alone, but raises from the dead
all who believe in him.
This, as in a summary, is the venerable feast of Pascha, and these are the
mysteries of Christians; we keep festival concerning the resurrection of the
dead and eternal life. Therefore let us feast not in the leaven of malice and
wickedness, but in unleavened bread of sincerity and truth, believing in
Father, and Son, and Holy Spirit, in a Trinity consubstantial, uncreated;
believing in the resurrection, expecting the Lord coming again, no longer
indeed in lowliness, but gloriously with heavenly brightness, with luminous
angels, with trumpet and fear and joy; joy of the saints and righteous, but
fear of the unjust and sinners. And may the God of peace grant us all to be
counted worthy of the resurrection with the saints, found in good works, and
in orthodox faith, by the grace and love for man of his Only-begotten Son,
with whom glory, honor and worship to the all-holy and good and life-giving
Spirit, now, and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
