* Question 136 from the Questions to Duke Antiochus 47
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

47 The Greeks who do not believe the gospel and cheapen all the divine
scriptures, how ought we to persuade them that there is an uncreated God, maker
of heaven and earth, one and only, and not many gods, as they wandering think?
It is responded: of those who do not receive the divine scriptures, we ought
henceforth to persuade concerning theology from the very nature of the things.
For we see certain essences in the creation existing with one another not
according to nature, but above nature. I say something of this sort. By nature
the essence of the waters is flowing and downward-bearing. How then do we see
the so-called siphons drawing water from the sea the clouds? And the more
wonderful, that coming up salty, these then come down sweet upon earth through
the rains. And how again also the nature of bodies being by nature sinking is
seen unsinkable and unsinking in the waters of Marmaric Pentapolis? And not
only, but also the nature of water and fire being contrary was at the same time,
once in Lycia in the mountain called Olympus, as myriads of men have seen. And
other myriads like these and paradoxical things are seen and wondered at in the
creation; all of which would not have been about to come to be thus above
nature, unless some essence of God were mastering these and permitting these not
to array against one another. And how again do children of Greeks say to us,
when a solid thunder comes to be, all the nature of men trembles, both shudders
and stands out, showing through the form that it is under a master who works the
thunders. And these examples bring toward the simpler of the Greeks unto
knowledge of God. For before the wise among them, from ancient powerful
philosophers, some wise of Greeks spoke with many testimonies concerning piety.
But they also dimly fore-announced the economy of Christ. And at any rate one of
the wise among them many times before the sojourn of Christ says that the young
woman will come having for us a heavenly offspring. And another again spoke
thus: late sometime God comes down upon this much-divided earth, and without a
fault he will become flesh, he will be hung as a condemned one on a wood, and he
will heal incurable sufferings.
