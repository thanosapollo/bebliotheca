ON THE ENJOYMENT OF THE THINGS TO COME, AND ON THE CHEAPNESS OF THE THINGS
PRESENT.
=51.347=
αʹ. Intense is the heat, and heavy the drought; but it has not unstrung your
readiness, nor marred your longing for the hearing. For such a hearer, warm and
wakeful, braced by the love of hearing, would easily bear all things, so as to
fill this good and spiritual desire; neither cold nor drought, nor a crowd of
affairs, nor a multitude of cares, nor anything else of such kind could trip
him up. As therefore the man lying on his back and lounging would not be
awakened by the mild airs, nor by leisure and idleness, nor by ease and
relaxation, but remains sleeping a sleep worthy of much accusation; but you are
not such, but better than those who dwell in the city with us. For you are the
flower of the city, so composed and sober, and always following the things said
with exactness. This theater of mine is more august than the royal courts. For
there the things given, whatever they may be, are dissolved together with the
present life, and it is filled with tumult and disturbance; but here nothing of
such kind, but all safety, and honor freed from disturbance, and authorities
that have no end, nor are cut off by death itself, but become more secure then.
Do not tell me of the man seated on the carriage, lifting his brows, having
many guards, neither the belt and the voice of the herald; do not from these
mark out for me the ruler, but from the condition of the soul, whether he rules
his own passions, whether he gets the better of his maladies; such as, whether
he masters the desires of moneys, whether he has subdued the insatiate love of
bodies, whether he does not melt with envy, whether he is not dragged by the
grievous passion of vainglory, whether he does not fear and tremble at poverty,
whether at the change to the more sullen, whether he has not died of this
dread. Show me such a ruler; for this is authority. But if he rules men and
=51.348= is a slave to passions, I would say such a one is more servile than
all men. And just as one shut up in bed with a fever in his depths, even if the
surface of the body shows nothing of such kind, the sons of the physicians
would say he is feverish above all, even if the laymen do not know; so I too
would say the man who has his soul a slave and captive of the passions, even if
the outward sight shows nothing of such kind, but rather the opposite, to be
more servile than all, having in his depths the fever of evils, and the tyranny
of passions established in his soul; but a ruler and free, and more kingly than
kings, even if clothed in rags, even if he dwells in a prison, even if he is
girt with a chain, the man who has stripped off that tyranny, and is neither
held by evil lusts, nor fearing and trembling at an unreasoning dread of
poverty and dishonor, the things seeming grievous in the present life.
βʹ. These authorities are not bought with moneys, nor do they have their
enviers; the tongue of an accuser does not know her, nor the eye of the
envious, nor the devices of plotters; but dwelling as in some inviolable place,
that of philosophy, she remains forever unhurt, yielding not only to the other
circumstances of things, but not even to death itself. And the martyrs show
these things, whose bodies indeed were dissolved and became dust and ashes, but
their authority lives and works each day, driving out demons, making maladies
ready to flee, giving wings to whole cities, and leading the peoples here. Such
is the power of this authority, not only of the rulers living, but also of
those departed, so that by constraint no one, but by judgment and longing all
come here, and =51.349= are not withered by any time. Do you see that I did not
say in vain that this theater is more august than the royal courts? For the
things there are like leaves withering, and shadows running past; but the
things given here imitate the adamant, rather, are firmer even than that, being
immortal and unmoved and yielding to no change ever, and coming safely to those
who love them, freed from battle and strife, and from envy and courts, and
plotters and calumny. For the things of life have many enviers; but the
spiritual things, as far as they come to more, so much more show the wealth of
themselves. And you may learn this from this discourse. For the discourse which
I pour out to all, if I keep it at home with myself, I become poorer; but if I
pour it out to all, as casting seeds into a clean field, I strain my wealth, I
make my riches greater, making you all wealthier, while I myself in this become
in nothing poorer, but rather richer by far; which is not so with moneys, but
all the contrary. For if, having gold laid up, I chose to distribute it to all,
I could no longer have my former wealth, diminished by this division.
γʹ. Since then so great are the excellences of the spiritual things, and they
have much ease, coming as a gift to all who wish, let us love these rather,
leaving the shadows, and not pursuing the precipices and rocks. For that God
may intensify this love, before the death of the man possessing them he
co-allotted to these things their own death. What do I say? Not when the
possessor of these ends, then they also end, but even while he yet lives these
wither and die, that he might take away from those who love them exceedingly
and are mad about them this grievous frenzy, teaching their nature, and
teaching through experience that they are feebler than a shadow, and by this
dissolving the love. As for example: wealth is not dissolved only when the rich
man ends, but even more it has departed while he lives. Youth, not only having
left the man ended, but even yet breathing, has gone, dissolving in the way of
age, and giving way to old age. The beauty and comeliness together, while the
woman yet lives, have ended, and shifted to unshapeliness; the glories and
dominions again likewise; the honors and authorities are ephemeral and
fleeting, and more mortal than the men having them; and as there are daily
deaths of bodies to see, so also of things. But this has come to pass, that
looking beyond the things present we may hold the things to come, and hang upon
the enjoyment of those, and walking on earth, by longing dwell in the heavens.
For God made these two ages, the one present, the other to come; the one
visible, the other invisible; the one sensible, the other intelligible; the one
having its repose in the body, the other bodiless; the one in experience, the
other in faith; the one in hands, the other in hopes; and he bade the one be a
stadium, the other a prize; and =51.350= to this one he co-allotted trenches
and toils and sweats, to that one crowns and prizes and recompenses; the one he
fashioned a sea, the other a harbor; and the one brief, the other ageless and
immortal. Since then many of men preferred the sensible things to those
intelligible ones, he co-allotted to them the ephemeral and the fleeting, that
having taken them away from the things present by this, he might bind them with
much exactness to the love of the things to come. Then, since those were
invisible and intelligible, and in faith and hopes, see what he does. Having
come here, and taken our flesh, and wrought that marvelous dispensation, he
brings under sight the things to come, persuading the duller minds by this. For
since he came bringing an angelic polity, and making the earth heaven, and
enjoining those things which made those pursuing them like to the bodiless
powers, and made men angels, and called them to the hopes above, and stretched
out small trenches, and bade them fly higher, and to climb the arches of the
heavens themselves, and to strip for combat with demons, and to be arrayed
against the whole phalanx of the devil, having a body, entangled with flesh,
and mortifying the bodies, and banishing the tumult of the passions, and simply
being clothed about with flesh, while the contest was stretched out against the
bodiless powers.
δʹ. Since he enjoined these things, see what he does, how he makes the contest
easy. But rather, if it seems good, let us first say the greatness of the
commandments, and how high a wing he was fashioning, and almost displacing
human nature, he bade all men moor themselves toward heaven. Whereas the law
bids an eye for an eye, he himself says: If anyone strike you on the right
cheek, turn to him the other also. He did not say, bear the outrage only nobly
and mildly, but go forward further in philosophy, and prepare yourself to
suffer greater things than he desires to do; by the abundance of your
philosophy overcome the forwardness of his outrage, that ashamed by your
surpassing gentleness he may withdraw. And again he says: Pray for them that
misuse you; pray for your enemies; do well to them that hate you. Again he
introduced the counsel concerning virginity, saying: He that is able to make
room, let him make room. For since the thing flew away from paradise, and after
the disobedience withdrew, coming down from heaven, he again led it back, as
bringing back a fugitive to her ancient fatherland, and releasing her from long
exile; and having come, he first was born of a virgin, and moved the laws of
nature, honoring her with prefaces, and declaring the virgin a mother. Since
then coming he was enjoining such things, and working a lofty polity, he also
gave prizes worthy of the labors, rather far greater and higher. But these too
were invisible, and in hopes and in faith and in expectation of the things to
come. Since then the commandments are toilsome and lofty, and the prizes and
crowns are in faith, see =51.351= what he does; how he makes the contest easy;
how easy the trenches. How and in what way? By these two ways; one, that he
himself went through them; the other, that he himself showed the crowns and
brought them under sight. For of the things said by him some were command, some
prizes; command indeed, Pray for them that misuse you and persecute you; prize,
That you may become sons of your Father who is in the heavens. Again, Blessed
are you when they revile you and persecute you, and say all evil word against
you lying, for my sake. Rejoice and exult, for your reward is great in the
heavens. Do you see the one, command, the other, prize? Again, If you wish to
be perfect, sell your goods, and give to the poor, and come follow me, and you
shall have treasure in heaven. Do you see another command and prize? For the
one he bade them do, the other he himself prepared, which was reward and
recompense. And again, Whoever leaves houses and brothers and sisters; this is
command; A hundredfold he shall receive, and shall inherit eternal life; this
is crown and garland.
εʹ. Since then the commandments were great, and the prizes of them not seen,
see what he does; he himself displays them through his deeds, and brings the
crowns before sight. For as the man bidden walk an untrodden road, if he first
see someone walking, takes hold more easily and receives more readiness, so
also in the commandments, those who see those who went before easily follow.
That then our nature might follow more easily, taking this flesh, and our
nature, thus he walked it, and showed the commandments through deeds. For the
word, If anyone strike you on the right cheek, turn to him the other also, he
himself did, when the servant of the high priest struck him. For he did not
retaliate upon him, but showed such gentleness as to say: If I spoke ill, bear
witness of the ill; but if well, why do you strike me? Do you see a meekness
filled with awe? Do you see a lowliness having astonishment? He was being
struck, not by some free man, but by a servant, a scourger and a house drudge,
and he answers these things with such fairness. So also his Father said to the
Jews: My people, what have I done to you? Or in what have I grieved you? Or in
what have I vexed you? Answer me. As he himself says, Bear witness of the ill,
so also his Father, Answer me; and as he himself says, Why do you strike me, so
also the Father, In what have I grieved you, or in what have I vexed you? Again
teaching propertylessness, see how he displays it through deeds, saying: The
foxes have holes, and the birds of the heaven lodgings; but the Son of Man has
not where to lay his head. Do you see the intensity of propertylessness? There
was not for him a table, nor a lamp, nor a house, nor a couch, nor anything
else of such kind. He was teaching to bear ill repute nobly, which he showed
through deeds. For when they called him demon possessed and a Samaritan, being
able again to destroy them, and to exact punishment for their outrage, he did
nothing of such kind, =51.352= but even benefited them, and drove out their
demons. And having said, Pray for them that misuse you, going up upon the cross
he did this. For when they had crucified him and nailed him, hanging he said,
Forgive them; for they know not what they do. He said these things not being
slack himself to forgive, but teaching us to pray for our enemies. For since he
showed the teaching not by word only, but also by deed, therefore he added also
the prayer. Let no one then of the heretics, because of his much love for man,
pass judgment of weakness upon the things said; for he himself is he who says,
That you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins.
But since he wished to teach (and the teacher advances the teaching not only by
what he says but also by what he does), for this reason he added also the
prayer; for he also washed the feet of the disciples, not as being less, but
being God and Master he descended to such a height of lowliness.
ʹ. For this reason then he said: Learn from me, for I am meek and lowly in
heart. Again hear in another way the bringing the crowns themselves and prizes
into the midst, and showing them under the eyes. He promised a resurrection of
bodies, incorruption, the meeting in the air, the rapture in clouds; these
things he showed through facts. How and in what way? Having died he rose;
therefore also forty days he was with them, that he might assure them and show
how great our bodies are going to be after the resurrection. Again saying
through Paul, that In clouds we shall be caught up to meet him in the air, and
this he showed by deeds. For after the resurrection, when he was about to go up
into the heavens, while they were present, He was lifted up, he says, and a
cloud received him from their eyes; and as they were gazing, as he went. Thus
then also our body will be consubstantial with that body, as being of the same
kneading; for as the head, so the body; as the beginning, so the end. And Paul
declaring this more plainly said: Who will transform the body of our lowliness,
that it become conformed to the body of his glory. If then it becomes
conformed, it will walk the same way, and likewise be lifted upon clouds. These
things he too awaited in the resurrection. For since the word of the kingdom
was before unclear to those hearing, therefore having gone up into the mountain
he was transfigured before his disciples, opening to them the glory of the
things to come, and as in a riddle and dimly showing what our body will be
like. But then he appeared with garments, but in the resurrection not so. For
our body has no need of garments, nor of roof, nor of house covering, nor
anything else of such kind. For if Adam before the transgression, being naked,
was not ashamed, clothed about with glory, much more our bodies, which will
walk toward a greater and better end, will have need of none of these. For this
reason then he himself also rising, let the garments be lying upon the tomb and
the coffin, having raised =51.353= the body naked, filled with glory
unspeakable and blessedness. These things then knowing, beloved, and taught
through words, and instructed through eyes, let us show such a polity, that
caught up in clouds we may always dwell with him, being saved =51.354= by his
grace also, and enjoying the good things to come; of which may we all be
counted worthy in Christ Jesus our Lord, with whom to the Father, together with
the Holy Spirit, glory, might, honor, worship, now and always, and unto the
ages of ages. Amen.
