150 Of John Chrysostom among the saints: On the apostolic saying: When all
things shall have been subjected to him, then the Son himself also shall be
subjected to him who subjected 151 all things to him; and on I do nothing of
myself; and on the Trinity, and that in honor of the Trinity three patriarchs,
three priests, three kings, three virtues are commemorated. Yesterday,
brethren, the discourse concerning the unending kingdom of Christ took place.
Our discourse having come to the height of the hearing, and having given the
hearers a sufficient demonstration (sufficient, I say, not toward the dignity
of what is proclaimed, but toward the power of the one proclaiming); for there
is nothing such, neither on earth nor in heaven, that can worthily hymn the
much-hymned dignity of the Only-begotten; for many things have been proclaimed
beforehand by the prophets concerning the theophany of the Lord, many things
have been ordained by the Law, many things have been preached by the apostles,
many things have been declared by the evangelists, and no one has been able to
attain to the dignity. And if so many rivers of the Holy Spirit, having
perpetual and pure streams, emptied out human power, yet uttered nothing
worthy of the divine power, where shall a tiniest droplet of speech appear,
when so great a dignity of speech is set before us? A mortal tongue cannot
worthily hymn an immortal nature; the speech of men cannot contain the
world-making Word of God so as to hymn him worthily. What then? We preach as
much as we are able, since as much as we ought we do not suffice. Now in other
contests those who win carry off the crowns, but in the labors concerning
theology those who confess to be defeated carry off the crowns of
right-mindedness; let us therefore be defeated by speaking, and not be
condemned by remaining silent. But since, he says, the unending character of
the kingdom has been shown, because he delivers the kingdom to God and the
Father, that is, the inhabited world 152 which was once ruled by sin, but
later is ruled by grace, and there has been left unexamined the saying, When
all things shall have been subjected to him, then the Son himself also shall
be subjected to him who subjected all things to him, it is necessary to say
what is possible, lest our silence prepare a slip for the ungrateful. And you
must all know, brethren, that we have not left it out willingly, but having
delivered the discourse as complete and examined, we supposed from what went
before that the remainder also had been made clear. For there is one aim and
one argument which both solves what went before and clarifies what is set
forth, that these things were said by the apostle according to economy; and
just as there the delivering of the kingdom to God and the Father does not
agree with the power of the preaching, but the wording is introduced
superficially because of the weakness of those who had taken up the
polytheistic error (for I gave you milk to drink, not meat), so also now this
too is said by the same argument. For everywhere the apostle's aim is to show
that the divine nature nowhere is at variance with itself, nor that the Son
stands against the Father according to the impious supposition of the Jews;
for they were saying that This man is not from God, because he does not keep
the sabbath. He therefore introduces one nature unfactioned, one concord, one
word, one will, one authority, one God in all, that he may cut out the shoots
of the polytheistic error. For we have demonstrated, as the industrious among
the hearers remember, that the same Word who bore witness to the Father's
dignity proclaims with the same words and syllables the dignity of Christ; and
just as there he said, That God may be all in all, so also 153 upon the
Only-begotten: But Christ is all and in all. What then does "He shall be
subjected" mean? He prepares a medicine for the Hellenic disease; he does not
introduce an ecclesiastical dogma,


but casts out a Hellenic suspicion. And if one must use an unconstrained
argument, he calls the subjection of Christ that which comes about through the
Church; for when the Church shall have been subjected, having nothing bastard
or counterfeit, but offering to God a sincere and unadulterated disposition,
then he shall be subjected to him genuinely. The saying, he says, is forced,
and not sufficient to persuade the ungrateful. Therefore, that you may learn
that as many such things as are attributed to Christ are contemplated in the
Church of Christ, I will set before you as witness the Master himself of the
Church; but a little from above, that what is said may be clear. While Christ
was in this world according to the flesh, fulfilling the human economy for the
salvation of the world, he was persecuted by Jews and driven about by the
enemies of the truth, the Savior of the inhabited world, and by those who
enjoyed the benefaction the fountain of the benefaction was treated
ungratefully; but when he had fulfilled the whole economy through the Passion,
he transferred to the heavenly choirs, transferring in the body, yet in truth
filling all things; and having taken up on high the body which he had put on,
he sat at the right hand of the Majesty on high, not as having received some
foreign and alien glory, but clothing the mortal body with so great a dignity.
After the ascension, then, and the release from the sufferings, after that
much-hymned resurrection and the transfer into the heavens, being worshipped
by every army of the super-heavenly, every angelic escort standing by him,
being therefore neither among those who are afflicted nor among those who are
154 persecuted nor among those who are driven about, when he saw Paul raging
against the Church, breathing much wrath against the saints, he who after the
Passion is nowhere persecuted, but lives in so great honor and divine glory,
says to him: Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me? And yet I am not persecuting
you; for you have the presidency in the heavens. Why do you persecute me? He
makes his own the sufferings of the Church. Saul, Saul, why do you persecute
me? Do you see that the persecution of the Church is inscribed upon Christ,
the head of the Church? Just as, then, when the Church is persecuted he says
that he himself is persecuted, so also now Paul, when the Church is subjected,
says that Christ is subjected. Take for me also another demonstration of the
argument; for upon two and three witnesses every word shall be established;
not as though the Master's voice needed another testimony, but because Christ
speaking is faithful to the faithful, yet unpersuasive to the unfaithful by
the testimony; we do not fortify the Master's voice, but we stop the mouth of
heretical ingratitude. This Paul, having learned from Christ himself that
persecution of the Church is referred to Christ, knowing thereafter that
whatever the Church may suffer has its reference to Christ, himself also
following in the tracks of the royal words says somewhere in one of the
epistles: I fill up the things lacking of the afflictions of Christ. Is Christ
then still afflicted again after going up into the heavens? But see what
follows: I fill up the things lacking of the afflictions of Christ in my flesh
for his body, which is the Church. Do you see how Christ makes his own both
the sufferings of the Church and the honor? Did you honor the Church? You
honored Christ. Did you persecute the Church? Even if not in appearance, yet
in purpose 155 you persecuted Christ. Consider how great is the danger of
those who grieve the Church, how great the crown of those who honor the
Church; He who rejects you rejects me, says the Savior. I recognize the voice,
O Master, holding to paternal sequence; for the Father also used to speak such
things concerning his ancient prophets, making his own the insult of the
righteous. For when Abimelech the king of the Philistines was taking Sarah who
was Abraham's wife, God says to him: Restore the wife to the man, for he is a
prophet and he will pray for you. And he, making his defense, says: Lord, I
did not know that she is his wife; in a pure heart I did this. What then does
God say to him? I also knew that you did this in a pure heart; therefore I
spared you from sinning against me.


It has been demonstrated, then, brethren, that all things contemplated in the
Church have their reference to Christ. The apostle therefore says: The Son
shall be subjected, that is, the Church, to him who subjected all things to
him. But there is another road for the ungrateful. What then? he says; the
Church then has not been subjected, but will then be subjected after the
resurrection? The same refutation is recited against you toward the
contradiction, O heretic. For tell me, has Christ not been subjected now,
according to your argument? For the saying, When all things shall have been
subjected to him, then he shall be subjected, would have had a time, if in the
present he was not subjected; but if according to your argument, O heretic, he
is always in subjection and never stands against the Father, the saying When
all things shall have been subjected to him, then he shall be subjected is
superfluous; through a thousand words you bring forward subjection for him,
and again you store up for another time a 156 second subjection of his? If you
hear Christ saying, I came not to do my own will, but that of him who sent me,
immediately you put forth the horn of impiety and say: Do you see how he is
subjected to the Father? If you hear him saying, I do not speak of myself, the
Father has given me commandment, what I should say and what I should speak,
immediately you seize upon the wording, insisting that Christ has no
authority, but is subjected. If you hear him saying, I judge no one, there is
one who seeks and judges, you immediately snatch subjection through the
wording. Showing him therefore through all things to be subjected, for what
reason do you introduce an ungrateful thesis and say: I follow the wording and
not the sense, that then he shall be subjected who is always subjected? What
then does the sense mean? Everywhere the apostle's aim is to make the
polytheistic error vanish and to cast out the faction of the unfactioned
nature and to banish the suspicion of those who think badly. He therefore
shows a subjection not forced, not compelled, not slavish, but the concord
that fits the dignity. But let none of the things previously said seize you,
brother, that I do not speak this of myself, the Father has given me
commandment, what I should say and what I should speak; I fear lest you take
the things said agonistically against the heretics as an ecclesiastical dogma,
having set aside the sense of the preaching. Just as our discourse on the
former day insisted, defending the apostolic sense, that the apostles do not
preach all things dogmatically, but some things they set forth dogmatically,
and some things economically, so I find even the very fountain of the truth,
the Master of apostles and prophets, not speaking dogmatically toward his own
dignity, but 157 economically condescending toward the weakness of the people.
Tell me then, brother, when the Master of the truth says, If I bear witness
concerning myself, my witness is not true, shall I follow the wording? Shall I
follow the saying? Shall I insult the dignity because of the economy? But not
even if I wish to be ungrateful does the Master permit me; for he will shut
out my evil sense, if I follow the wording badly, and he seizes upon my voice
as otherwise theologizing the holy things, as simply confusing and insulting
the dignity; since, he says, I condescended to the Jews and said that If I
bear witness concerning myself, my witness is not true, did you snatch the
wording unto blasphemy? Did you not hear me saying elsewhere: Even if I bear
witness concerning myself, my witness is true? What then, brethren? Does the
truth put forward things contrary to itself? God forbid; but it speaks one way
toward those reared with the truth, and another way toward those cut off from
the truth, not itself changing, but being fitted to the purposes. Why then
does he say to the Jews, I do not speak of myself, the Father has given me
commandment, what I should say and what I should speak? Because they supposed
him to be an enemy of the Mosaic law and to be arranging things contrary to
the Father who through the law honors the sabbath, and the new covenant seemed
to the eye to be at political odds with the old constitution; wishing
therefore to show that he is not at political odds with God nor contrary


preaches he to the law, he says, I do not speak of myself, as you suspect,
but he himself who is worshipped by you has given me commandment, all but
teaching them that if you set these things aside, you do not set aside my
teaching, but you strike against the God confessed among you; 158 since,
brethren, I take the word of Christ so pitiably, so humbly, so
earth-crawlingly, as to receive in a slavish rank the voice that says, I do
nothing of myself, the Father has given me commandments, what I should say and
what I should speak; but do you see into how great a depth of impiety I come,
taking it thus? For I make him not only lower than the Father, but also lesser
than the apostles; and how, listen. Paul says: Now concerning virgins I have
no commandment of the Lord, but I give a judgment. Paul speaks without a
commandment, and Christ can say nothing of himself? Now concerning virgins I
have no commandment of the Lord, but I give a judgment. Do you permit Paul
even without a commandment to give an opinion, yet shut Christ under a yoke of
slavery and follow the wording that says, I speak nothing of myself? Then
therefore, as I said before, he converses one way with those reared with the
truth, another way with those cut off; for did the Jews suppose him to be Son
of God? Did the Jews take into thought God the Word? Did you not hear what
they say concerning him? This man is not of God, because he does not keep the
sabbath. They did not suppose him a man of God, and they were thinking the
dignity of the Only-begotten? For the same men said again concerning the
Savior: We know that this man is a sinner, because he does not keep the
sabbath; having an opinion concerning a sinner and concerning a man cut off
from the truth, did they at all go even into the chance fantasy of divinity?
Toward those so disposed, stepping down in the voice and teaching them not to
contradict the appearing form which according to them was thought to stand
apart from the will of God, he said: I do not speak of myself, he himself has
given me commandment, what I should say and what I should 159 speak; but
toward the sons of the faith see how he puts off the falling voice, and puts
on the authority of the divine dignity and says: You have heard that it was
said to the ancients, You shall not commit adultery; he who looks at the wife
of his neighbor to desire her has already sinned; but I say. Do you see how,
where there is a faithful man, there is bare authority, where the hearer is
suspect, the teaching is shaped toward economy? And when a small child was
pressed in the crowd he said: Disturbance always springs up upon the hearing
of the divine word. For the enemy of souls, the devil, since he sees the word
sending arrows sharpened against his own error, continually troubles the
hearers, that he may escape the refutation of the truth. A certain young
Eutychus muddied Paul's teaching, and here a certain young man muddied the
people's hearing, that he might more exercise the purpose of the hearers; but
we, brethren, fleeing the surge of the waves, let us drive the boat toward the
calm harbor of the truth. For I do not wish the digression, by being
prolonged, to impede what is set before us. Where the hearer is suspect, the
teaching is shaped toward economy; where there is a true companion of piety,
the authority is bare; to the Jews he says, I speak nothing of myself; to
those belonging to the faith: You have heard that it was said to the ancients,
You shall not commit adultery. But I say. The I shows the authority of the
speaker; for everywhere the I bears witness of authority to the one speaking,
as when he says, I am, I am, and until you grow old, I am. And again to the
Jews he says, I came not to do 160 my own will, but the will of him who sent
me. Today's reading of the Gospels solves the difficulty of the heretical
suspicion. For when that leper approached him who was leprous in body but
bright in soul, he says to him: Lord, if you will, you can make me clean. Here
seek the canon of the heretics; he ought then to have said things consistent
with his own voice: I do not will, the Father wills, be cleansed; for I
came not


to do my own will, but the will of him who sent me; did you not hear me
saying, I came not to do my own will? If then a subjected will is forced by
authority (according to the word of the impious), why do you demand authority
from one who is subjected to the Father's authority? But the Savior said none
of these things to the one who approached him genuinely, but having accepted
the sincerity of his faith he gives him also a sincere and genuine salvation;
you approached me faithfully, receive the medicine faithfully; you ascribe the
benefaction to my will, carry off the salvation from my will. Lord, if you
will, you can make me clean; I will, be cleansed. Does not the boundary of
impiety stand for the heretics? Do you see that he is subjected in word, but
from his own will he has authority? And when I say these things, I do not
introduce the will of the Son as cut off from the paternal will. For whatever
you say as the wish of the Son, you say the wish of the Father, and whatever
you say as the wish of the Father, you proclaim the authority of the Son, and
if you say the authority of the Son, you indicate the power of the Holy
Spirit; for the authority is one, the kingdom undivided. But you have omitted,
he says, the discourse concerning the Spirit; for I find from the apostolic
voice that the Spirit is subjected to Christ; 161 for Paul said: And when he
says that all things have been subjected to him, it is clear that it is except
him who subjected all things to him; and if except him who subjected, then is
the Spirit subjected? For he said only that the Father has not been subjected
to Christ. "All things" signifies the creation; it does not insult the
uncreated
nature. He is discoursing concerning the subjection of enemies: The last enemy
is abolished, death. Why then do you place the Holy Spirit in the place of the
subjected enemies? Did the Father introduce a subjection of friends? Did he
mention a subjection of angels? Did he touch a subjection of archangels? A
subjection of enemies. The last enemy is abolished, death. Among the subjected
enemies the Holy Spirit is not. If we the faithful, believing in Christ, shall
reign together, shall the Holy Spirit not even attain the same dignity as the
subjects? At the appearing of the Savior the sons of Zebedee, James and John,
shall sit, and the chorus of the apostles upon twelve thrones, and shall the
Spirit be about the footstool with those who have been subjected? Depart from
the impiety. The discourse is concerning the creation, O man, not concerning
the uncreated nature, not concerning the worshipful dignity. And that you may
take an accurate and examined account of piety, consider how the blessed
David, introducing the chorus of creation hymning, says: Praise the Lord from
the heavens, praise him in the highest, and so on, angels, powers, heavens of
heavens and the water that is above the heavens. Did he mention the Spirit?
Did he number the uncreated nature together with the creation? The three
children around Ananias in the furnace themselves also introduced the choral
station of the creation, 162 saying, Hymn, angels of the Lord, heavens of the
Lord, powers of the Lord, sun and moon, and so on. Did they mention the Holy
Spirit? Did they number the uncreated together with the creation? Paul again,
dividing the invisible things from the things seen, says somewhere teaching:
That in Christ all things were created, both those in heaven and those upon
the earth, both the visible and the invisible, and immediately the invisible
things are numbered, saying: Whether thrones, or principalities, or
authorities, or lordships. For since all the things seen have a confessed
slavery, but among the invisible there are angels and powers and Holy Spirit
and Son and Father, lest by ignorance of the ordering of the heavens you drag
the Master's dignity of the divine Spirit under a yoke of slavery, he does not
divide the things seen nor say: Whether sun, or moon, or stars, all are
slaves; for these have a confessed constitution, that all things are your
servants; since among the invisible there were principalities and angels, and
the worshipful Spirit also was invisible, lest anyone include it among the
invisible things, he enumerates all things above, that he may cast the Lord
out of the household rank,


he enumerates the things that worship, that he may set apart the one
worshipped, whether thrones, or principalities, or authorities; he did not
say: Or Spirit. But let us come to what is set before us. If you will, Lord,
you can make me clean. The leper saw the benefaction welling forth, he saw the
salvation gushing, he saw many who were ill and demon-possessed standing
around the fountain of salvation, and the one leprous in body but clean in
soul puts off the division of mind; for the soul had not been divided with
different colors; for just as leprosy is unpleasant in 163 a body, dividing
the complexion, so a double heart in a soul is unpleasant, being divided
toward faith and unbelief; as much unpleasantness as leprosy brings to the
sight of men's eyes, so much unpleasantness a divided soul brings to the eyes
of God; therefore the Scripture says: Woe to a double heart and a slack soul
and a sinner walking upon two paths. But let us hold again to what is set
before us. I will, be cleansed. O the wonder. I will, be cleansed. There is,
brethren, a saying often put forth from authority, and often also from
arrogance; now to utter it belongs even to a chance person, but for the work
to follow the authority belongs only to the one who is able. If then he said,
I will, and the work did not follow, it was arrogance; but now work followed
the voice; and do not put the word to shame, reverence the work. See how great
a difference there is between the old law and the new grace toward every
constitution. Moses coming down from the mountain with the first legislation
and the first law commands the lepers to be separated from the camp; Christ
coming down from the mountain immediately cleanses a leper. For the one
banishes the passions, the other heals the passions. Christ went up into a
mountain, Moses also went up; the one that he might receive, the other that he
might give a law. Moses went up and found the mountain filled with trumpets,
cloud, thunders, lightnings, storm, gloom, not because these things were
fitting to the glory of the one who appeared, but because these things trained
the people unto reverence; but the grace of Christ, brethren, puts off fear,
casts out slavery; for we did not receive a spirit of slavery again unto fear,
but we received a spirit of adoption. And Moses gave ten commandments, but
Jesus the Master of 164 Moses nine beatitudes. As many as are experienced in
the Scriptures know the truthfulness of what has been said. First beatitude:
Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of the heavens.
Blessed are they that mourn, for they shall be comforted. Blessed are the
meek, for they shall inherit the earth. Blessed are they that hunger and
thirst after righteousness, for they shall be filled. Blessed are the
merciful, for they shall obtain mercy. Blessed are the pure in heart, for they
shall see God. Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of
God. Blessed are they that have been persecuted for righteousness' sake, for
theirs is the kingdom of the heavens. Blessed are you when they shall reproach
you and persecute you and say every evil word against you, lying. Why then
does the law have ten commandments, and grace nine beatitudes? The number of
the ten commandments is equal in number to the plagues against the Egyptians;
ten plagues against Egyptians chastening the arrogance, ten commandments to
the people educating the insensibility; and even the very letter, brethren,
through which the number of the ten is written, I mean the iota, has the shape
and type of a rod; for it was a rod educating both Egyptians and the people.
But Jesus our Lord gives nine beatitudes, having worked from a triple triad a
triple-plaited crown of the beatitudes; since, brethren, the worshipful
Trinity, the royal dignity, the undefiled nature, the unmastered mastery has
its glory in the Trinity, and God honors upon earth both the preaching and the
number of the virtues, when it is equal in number to the Holy Trinity. What I
mean is such as this: many patriarchs; and God of the patriarchs calls himself
God: I am the God of Abraham 165 and the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob;
not that he was not God of Moses or that he was not God of Joseph; for he is
God of all who call upon him in truth; but he honors as in an image by the
earthly things the


heavenly. Three patriarchs in honor of Trinity; three virtues in honor of
Trinity; and now these three remain, faith, hope, love. The creation has been
divided into three, into an order of heavenly and earthly and under-earthly.
Times are cut into three, into the past and present and future. And just as
there are many patriarchs, yet the triad sufficed for the doxology of the
Spirit, so though there are many priests a triad of priests is chosen: Moses
and Aaron among his priests, and Samuel. Many kings, many priests, and three
are those commemorated; For except David and Hezekiah and Josiah, says the
Scripture, all sinned. In a furnace three children; with Christ upon the
mountain Peter, James and John. Many angels in heaven, and three appear to
Abraham. All the days are God's, and three serve the mystery of Christ; which
three? Take Isaac whom you loved, your son, your only-begotten, and go into
one of the mountains; and Abraham went three days and three nights, that he
might show together also the mystery; For just as he received Isaac living
from death; and how, listen. From the time he was taken for sacrifice, he died
to the father; for not the outcome of the work, but the purpose ratifies the
promise. Isaac had been put to death to the father as far as the purpose; the
third day, and he was given back living as a type of the resurrection. Jonah
also could have made many days in the sea, but he does not make except only
the three, that he may show the mystery of the resurrection. 166 Then, he
says, even if you find somewhere such testimonies divided, do you again take
them up as images of the Trinity and of the economy? You do not want these
signs. If I speak, set aside the argument; but if the preaching is divine,
hear from me. But hear also with me, that the three days interpret the Passion
of the Lord. Since concerning Abraham the Savior himself says: Abraham your
father rejoiced that he might see my day; and he saw and was glad. Which day?
Of the death. For the lamb that was delivered from the tree to Abraham had an
image of the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world. For to say, He
saw the day, does nothing other than He saw the death. Whence this? We also
often say concerning men: his day has come, instead of, the end. But a law of
custom is not sufficient to confirm what is said. What then? Let us come to
the Scripture. The blessed David says somewhere: The sinner shall watch the
righteous and shall gnash his teeth upon him; but the Lord shall laugh him to
scorn, because he foresees that his day will come. But these things unto
Abraham; and I will return upon Jonah. For again the three days of Jonah
interpret the saving Passion. This again the Savior himself cries: Just as, he
says, Jonah was in the belly of the whale three days and three nights, so must
the Son of man make in the heart of the earth three days and three nights. But
let us say one thing stronger than the others in addition to what has been
said. For the former people there were three ranks, priesthood, kingship, 167
prophecy; outside these three principles one could not see another principle
among the Jews; kingship, priesthood, prophecy. There was a horn of oil, of
sanctification. The anointing was one, but the gifts were three; the horn was
one, the charisms three; for they anointed priests, kings, prophets. But since
some are ignorant of the matter, lest the discourse running in vain consume
itself and benefit the hearer nothing, hear briefly. There was a horn held by
the prophets, and from the horn were anointed kings, prophets, priests, the
anointing not being changed, but the grace being furnished through the same
horn they were anointed kings, they were anointed priests, they were anointed
prophets; the anointing one, three gifts. And why was the anointing in a horn?
Because it had an image of Christ. Could it not have been in a silver vessel?
Could it not have been in gold? Could so great a gift not have been in some
other of the more precious materials? But it did not come to be, but in a
horn; because, as I said before, it had an image of Christ. For I will make a
horn to spring up for David. And may he who is Lord of the priesthood and of
the


kingship and of the prophecy be with you all. For having taken the beginning
of the chapter on the horn, when the people were pressed, he stopped.
