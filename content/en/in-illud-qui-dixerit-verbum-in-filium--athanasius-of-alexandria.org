the Spirit against them: "They exchanged their glory for the likeness of a
calf that eats grass;" then at last, having dared things unpardonable, they
were struck, as the Scripture says, concerning the making of the calf which
Aaron made. The Pharisees, daring such things even now, have received from the
Savior such a sentence as even the Beelzebul supposed by them has already
received and still has; so that they are eternally consumed together with him
in the fire prepared for him. For it is not as making some comparison of the
blasphemy spoken against himself and that spoken against the Holy Spirit, and
as if the Spirit were greater, and therefore the blasphemy that comes to be
against the Spirit had the greater charge, that he has said these things; may
it not be! For he has already taught that all things whatsoever the Father has
are the Son's, and that the Spirit will receive from the Son, and that it will
glorify the Son. And it is not the Spirit that gives the Son, but the Son
supplies the Spirit to the disciples, and through them to those who believe in
him. The Savior therefore has not spoken thus; but as the blasphemy on both
counts coming to be against him, and the one =26.664= lesser, the other
exceeding, the Lord has uttered these things. For the Pharisees themselves
also were saying both things: and seeing him a man they reviled him: "Whence
has this man this wisdom?" and, "You are not yet fifty years old, and have you
seen Abraham?" And beholding the works of the Father, they not only denied his
Godhead, but in place of this they said that Beelzebul was in him, and that
the works were his. Therefore, as both were being spoken against him, and the
one blasphemy being lesser because of the humanity, but the greater because of
the Godhead, he declared against them, upon the greater, the inescapable
punishment. Indeed, encouraging the disciples and saying that "If they have
called the master of the house Beelzebul," he was saying that he himself was
the master of the house, who was also being blasphemed thus by the Jews. And
if the Jews, saying. "in Beelzebul," reviled no other than the Lord, it is
therefore clear that what is said, "But the blasphemy of the Spirit," is
spoken against the Lord himself, and the Savior, signifying the whole saying
concerning himself, was speaking. For he himself is the master of the house of
all; for again, for the sake of security, one must not shrink from saying the
same. To thirst, to toil, to sleep, to be struck, to eat, are proper to men;
but the works which the Lord did were no longer of men, but of God to do them.
Whenever therefore some, as I said before, seeing these things revile the Lord
as a man, they would be thought worthy of a lesser punishment than those who
transfer the works of God to the devil. For such men not only cast the holy
things to the dogs, but also compare God with the devil, and say that the
light is darkness. Mark at any rate observed that this is the unpardonable
blasphemy of the Pharisees, saying: "Whoever blasphemes against the Holy
Spirit has no forgiveness, but is guilty of an eternal sin; because they were
saying, He has an unclean spirit." And the man born blind, having received
sight, testified: "From of old it has not been heard that anyone opened the
eyes of one born blind. If this man were not from God, he could do nothing;"
and the crowds, marveling at the things coming to be by the Lord, said: "These
=26.665= words are not of one possessed by a demon; can a demon open the eyes
of the blind?" but those who were thought the stout men of the law, the
Pharisees, who broaden the fringes, and boast as knowing something more than
the others, were not even so ashamed; but according to what is written, the
wretches sacrificed to a demon and not to God, saying that the Lord had a
demon, and that the works of God were of demons. And they suffered this for no
other reason than that they might only deny that the one doing these things is
God, and Son of God. For if the eating and the appearance of the body showed
him also a man, why did they not from the works behold that he is in the
Father, and the Father in him? But they did not wish it; rather they
themselves had Beelzebul speaking in them, so as from the human things to name
him man only; but from the works, which were proper to God, no longer to
confess him God, but to deify the Beelzebul in them instead of him, that they
might thereafter be punished with him eternally in the fire. And the
observation of the reading itself also seems to me to furnish some such
emphasis, and to show that both blasphemies reach to him, and that he has
spoken concerning himself both "Son of man" and "Spirit;" that from this he
might show his bodily, and from the spirit he might declare his spiritual and
intelligible and most true Godhead. For he arranged what is able to receive
pardon upon the Son of man, that he might signify his bodily; but he has
declared that the unpardonable blasphemy reaches to the Spirit; that, saying
this in distinction from the bodily, he might show his own Godhead. And this
character I have seen also in the Gospel according to John; when, discoursing
concerning the eating of the body, and having seen many scandalized because of
this, the Lord says: "Does this scandalize you? What then if you behold the
Son of man ascending where he was before? It is the Spirit that gives life;
the flesh profits nothing. The words which I have spoken to you are spirit and
life." For here too he has spoken both concerning himself, flesh and spirit;
and he distinguished the spirit from what is according to flesh, that they,
having believed not only the apparent but also his invisible, might learn that
even what he says is not fleshly, but spiritual. For to how many would the
body suffice for eating, that this should become food even of the whole world?
But for this reason he mentioned the ascent of the Son of man into =26.668=
heaven, that he might draw them away from the bodily notion, and that they
might thereafter learn that the said flesh is a heavenly food from above, and
a spiritual nourishment given by him. "For what I have spoken, he says, to you
is spirit and life;" equal to saying: What is shown and given for the
salvation of the world is the flesh which I wear; but this and its blood will
be given you by me spiritually as food; so that this is spiritually
distributed in each, and becomes for all a safeguard unto resurrection of
eternal life. Thus also the Lord, drawing the Samaritan woman away from the
things of sense, called God Spirit, in order that she might no longer think
bodily, but spiritually, concerning God; thus also the prophet, beholding the
Word who became flesh, said: "The Spirit of our face, Christ the Lord;" that
no one from the apparent might suppose the Lord to be a mere man, but that,
hearing also Spirit, he might know that the one who is in a body is God. Each
of the two, then, would now be clear, that the one seeing the Lord discoursing
the things concerning himself, if looking only at his bodily things, and not
believing, should say: "Whence has this man this wisdom?" sins indeed, and
blasphemes as against the Son of man; but the one =26.669= seeing his works
done by the Holy Spirit, and saying that the one doing such things is not God
and Son of God, but ascribing them to Beelzebul, blasphemes openly, denying
his Godhead. For even in the evangelical saying, as has often been said, by
saying "Son of man" he shows what is according to flesh and his human; that,
by saying "the Spirit," he might declare that the Holy Spirit is his, in which
he worked all things. Whence while doing the works he said: "If you do not
believe me, believe even my works, that you may know that I am in the Father,
and the Father in me." But when about to offer himself bodily for us, at the
time when for this very thing he went up to Jerusalem, he said to his
disciples: "Sleep on now, and take your rest; for behold the hour has drawn
near, and the Son of man is delivered into the hands of sinners;" for the
works made him to be believed true God; but the death showed him truly having
a body. Wherefore he reasonably called the one being delivered Son of man; for
the Word is immortal and untouchable, and happens to be life itself. But
neither did the Pharisees believe these things, nor did they wish to see what
the sons of these were doing; whence the Lord again gently convicts them
saying: "If I cast out the demons in Beelzebul, in whom do your sons cast them
out? Therefore they will be your judges; but if I cast out the demons in the
Spirit of God, then the kingdom of God has come upon you." And here too he
said "in the Spirit of God," not as being less than the Spirit, nor as the
Spirit working these things in him, but that he might again declare that he
himself, being Word of God, himself works all things through the Spirit, and
that he might teach those who hear that by as much as they ascribe the works
of the Spirit to Beelzebul, by so much they speak evil of the one who supplies
the Spirit; for even by saying this he shows that not in ignorance, but
willingly, they pierce themselves through into such inescapable blasphemy. And
they are not ashamed; although knowing that such works are of God, because
they said that these things come to be by a spirit of Beelzebul. =26.672= How
then, daring these things, can they still accuse the Greeks, who fashion idols
and name them gods? For theirs too are like that madness of those men; or
perhaps even the daring of these happens to be greater; because, although they
received a law concerning this, they dishonored God through the transgression
of the law. And what will they do, blaspheming such things, when they read the
prophet Isaiah, and hear that the signs of the presence of Christ are the
recovering of sight of the blind, the walking of the lame, the speech of the
speechless, the resurrection of the dead, the healing of lepers, and the
opening of the hearing of the deaf? Whom then will they wish to be the maker
even of these? For if they say God, they will convict themselves of having
been impious against the Lord; for what the prophet, foreseeing, said, these
things the Lord himself, being present, has done; but if, as having been
forestalled by rashness, they dare to say that those too come to be in
Beelzebul, I fear lest, advancing little by little in impiety, and reading:
"Who gave a mouth to man? And who made the hard of hearing and the deaf, the
seeing and the blind?" and the things like these, they, raging again, may say
that these utterances also are of Beelzebul. For to whom they give the grace
of recovering sight, to this one they must also give the cause of not seeing;
for the Word has said that the same one does both. And certainly, saying this,
they will sometime even think Beelzebul to be craftsman of human nature; for
it belongs to the maker to have also the authority over the things that come
to be. For Moses having said: "In the beginning God made the heaven and the
earth;" and, "in the image of God God made man;" Daniel also speaks freely to
Darius saying: "I do not worship handmade idols, but a living God who created
the heaven and the earth, and has lordship of all flesh;" unless perhaps,
changing again, they should say that not seeing and being lame, and the other
sufferings, come to be from the penalty of the Craftsman; but that Beelzebul
works the dissolving of these and the benefaction toward those who suffer. But
even to examine this is exceedingly simple-minded. For such foolish talk with
impiety belongs to men raging and still more beside themselves; because the
deranged give not even the better things, by comparison, to God, but to
Beelzebul. For it is no care to these men to maltreat the doctrines of the
divine Scriptures, only that they may deny the presence of Christ. =26.673=
But the wicked ought either not even from the bodily things to set them at
nought as a man, the Lord, or plainly also from the works to confess him true
God. But they did all things crookedly: seeing a man, they reviled him as a
man; but beholding divine works, they denied the Godhead, and ran to the
devil, thinking that, daring these things, they would be able to escape, so as
not to be judged by the very Word being insulted by them. The enchanters,
then, and magicians, and sorcerers of Pharaoh, although they had attempted
very many things, nevertheless seeing the signs through Moses, yielded, and
turned their backs, saying that the one doing these things is a finger of God;
but the Pharisees and the scribes, seeing the whole hand of God working, and
beholding things more and greater than those, the things coming to be through
the Savior himself, said that Beelzebul did these things, whom even the
magicians, although he was their own, nevertheless confessed unable to do even
the lesser than these. What excess of their madness, then, is there to tell?
or who, as the prophet has said, will be able to liken anything to their
impieties? For they have even justified the Sodomites from themselves; and
they have outdone the ignorance of the Greeks, and they even surpass the
unreason of the magicians of Pharaoh, and they have comparison only toward the
Ariomaniacs, falling together with one another into the same impiety. For the
Jews, seeing the works of the Father through the Son, ascribe them to
Beelzebul; but the Arians, themselves also seeing the same works, number with
the creatures the Lord who does these things, asserting that he is from things
that are not; and, he was not, before he came to be; And the Pharisees,
beholding the Lord in a body, murmured saying: "Why do you, being a man, make
yourself God?" but the Christ-fighters, seeing him suffering and sleeping,
blaspheme saying: The one undergoing these things cannot be true God and
consubstantial with the Father. And altogether, if anyone should wish to
examine in parallel the malice of both, he will find, as I said before, them
falling together with one another into the valley of the salt of drunken
outrage. =26.676= For this reason the Lord declared that the punishment upon
such things will be unpardonable both for those and for these, saying:
"Whoever speaks against the Holy Spirit, it will not be forgiven him, neither
in this age, nor in the one to come." And reasonably so; for the one who
denies the Son, calling upon whom will he be able to have propitiation? or
what life, or rest, will he expect, who has thrust away the one who says: "I
am the life;" and, "Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will
give you rest?" And these thus paying the penalty, it would be clear that
those who are pious toward Christ, and worship both what is according to flesh
and what is according to Spirit of him, and neither are ignorant that he is
Son of God, nor deny that he has also become Son of man; but also believing
that "In the beginning was the Word," and, "the Word became flesh," will reign
eternally in the heavens according to the holy promises of our Lord and Savior
Jesus Christ himself, who says: "Those will go away into eternal punishment,
but the righteous into eternal life." These things I have written you briefly,
from what I have learned. But do you take these from me not as a perfect
teaching, but only as a starting-point; and thereafter, taking up for yourself
the more exact mind both from the evangelical saying and from the Psalms, bind
the sheaves of the truth; that also, as you bear these, those who say may say:
"And coming they will come in gladness, lifting their sheaves," in Christ
Jesus our Lord, through whom and with whom to the Father together with the
Holy Spirit be the glory and the might, unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
