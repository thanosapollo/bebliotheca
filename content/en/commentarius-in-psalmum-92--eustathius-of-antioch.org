INTERPRETATION OF THE 92ND PSALM
35 It is therefore evident, if the one who anoints shows as God him whose
throne he called eternal, that the one who anointed is clearly by nature God
begotten from God, and the one anointed has received an acquired virtue,
having been adorned with an excellent temple-work from the Godhead dwelling in
him.
36 At any rate the prophet Isaiah, going through the very tracks of his
sufferings, among the rest also adds this with a louder cry: and we saw him
and he had neither form nor beauty; but his form was without honor and failing
beside the sons of men. Then, showing expressly that the forms and sufferings
of seemliness are attached to the man and not to the divine, he immediately
again adds, having put: A man being in a blow and knowing how to bear
infirmity.
37 Again then this is the one who after the insults was seen unseemly,
shapeless, then again from a change having put on seemliness. For neither was
the God dwelling in him led to death after the manner of a lamb and
slaughtered in the way of a sheep, being by nature invisible.
