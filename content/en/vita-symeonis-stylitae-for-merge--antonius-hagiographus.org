* Life and Conduct of the Blessed Symeon the Stylite
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

1 A strange and paradoxical mystery has come to pass in our days. It seemed
good to me the sinner and least Antonius to write unto what I grasp. For of
benefit and of compunction the composition has been filled. Whence I beseech,
incline your ear and hear unto the things I grasp.
2 The one among the saints and most blessed Symeon, being an infant in age,
was shepherding the sheep of his father in the same manner as the prophet
David also; and on a holy Lord's day he would go into the church toward the
oracles of God and he was gladly listening to the holy scriptures, not knowing
what he was hearing. And advancing into age and being pricked by the word of
God he goes off on one of the days into the holy church and hearing the
apostle being read he asks a certain old man: Tell me, father, what is it that
he is reading. The elder says to him: Concerning continence of soul. Saint
Symeon says to him: What is continence of soul? The elder says to him: Child,
why do you ask me? For I see you young in age, but possessing an aged mind.
Saint Symeon says to him: I am not tempting you, father; but I am astonished
at the name of the expression. The elder says to him: Continence is salvation
of soul, guiding into light, leading into the kingdom of the heavens. Saint
Symeon says to him: Teach me, honorable father, the things you say, because I
am unlearned.
3 The elder says to him: Child, if anyone fasts to God unceasingly and renders
all the prayers to him with gentleness, that is, at the third hour one prayer,
likewise also sixth, ninth and twelfth and the things following these; just as
they are also performed in the monasteries. If then you know, child, what you
have heard, reckon these things in your heart; for you must both hunger and
thirst and be insulted and be buffeted and be reproached, groan and weep and
be afflicted and be unwell, be healthy and desire to renounce and be humbled
and suffer many things from men, and thus be comforted by angels; see, you
have heard all these things, may the Lord of glory give you a good mind toward
his will.
4 And having heard these things saint Symeon went out of the church and goes
off into a desert place and sets himself upon his face seven days, weeping and
praying to God, partaking neither of food nor of drink. And after the seven
days, having risen, he comes running into a monastery. And he falls at the
feet of the archimandrite, crying out and saying: Have mercy on me, father, a
humble and wretched man, and save a soul that is perishing and longing to
serve God. The archimandrite says to him: Who are you and from where? And what
is your name? And from where have you come? The blessed Symeon says: By race I
am free, and the name is Symeon, but how I have come here or of what parents I
am, do not ask me, master, I beg you, but redeem for yourself one soul that is
perishing. Having heard these things the archimandrite raised him from the
ground and the archimandrite says to him: If you are from God, the Lord will
guard you from every wicked and deceitful deed and you shall be doing the
service for all, that you may be loved by all.
5 But his parents did not cease weeping and seeking him; and the saint was
spending his time in the monastery serving all and being loved by all and
fulfilling the rule of the monastery. So on one of the days, having gone out
of the monastery, he finds above the well a bucket from which they used to
draw the water, having a

rope; and having loosed the rope he goes off to a private place and binds
himself about over his whole body; and he puts on over the rope a hair tunic
and he went into the monastery and says to the brothers: I went out to draw
water and I did not find the rope on the bucket. The brothers say to him: Be
silent, lest anyone learn of it to the archimandrite. And no one knew that he
was bound about within with the rope. So he remained up to one year and more
having the rope wound in his flesh, and it ate up his flesh, so that the rope
was covered from the putrefied flesh of the righteous one, and from his stench
no one was able to stand nearest him, and no one knew the mystery. And his bed
was filled with worms, and no one knew what had happened.
6 And he would take his food and give it to the poor, no one knowing. So on
one of the days one of the monks goes out and finds him giving his bread and
the pulse which he received to the poor. And all used to fast until evening,
but saint Symeon used to taste from Lord's day to Lord's day. And one of the
monks, having gone in, slandered him to the archimandrite, saying: I beg your
holiness; this man wants to dissolve the monastery, that is, the rule which
you handed down to us. The archimandrite says to him: How does he want to
dissolve the rule? The monk says to him: We received to fast until evening,
but this one eats from Lord's day to Lord's day, and his bread which he
receives, and the pulse, he gives secretly to the poor each day; and not only
this, but also from his body an unbearable stench rises, so that no one is
able to stand nearest him; and his bed has been filled with worms, and we are
not able to bear it; But if you wish, keep this one here and we withdraw, or
send him away to go off whence he came.
7 Having heard these things the archimandrite became astonished, and he
examines his bed, and finds it filled with worms, and from the stench he was
not able to stand there. And the archimandrite says: Behold, even the young
Job. And having seized him he says to him: Why have you done these things,
man? Whence is this stench of yours, why do you lead the brothers astray, why
do you dissolve the rule of the monastery? Or are you a phantom? Go off
elsewhere and die without us; perhaps through you I the wretched one have come
to be tempted? For if you were a true man from sensible parents, you would
have reported to us who your father was and your mother and your race, and
whence you came here. Having heard these things the saint, looking down toward
the earth, was silent, saying nothing, and from his tears the place in which
he stood was filled; and the archimandrite, having become furious for a long
time, says to the monks: Strip him, that we may see whence this stench is.
8 So they wished to strip him and they were not able; for his garment had been
glued from the rotten flesh. So for three days they did not cease soaking him
with lukewarm water with oil, and thus with much pain they were able to strip
him, so that with the garment his putrefied flesh was flayed off, and they
find the rope wound about on his body, so that nothing of it appeared, except
only the ends of the rope; and of the worms that were in him there was no
conjecture; then all the monks became astonished at him, seeing that incurable
wound, and they were reasoning among themselves how and by what device they
might take the rope off him; but saint Symeon was crying out, saying: Forgive
me, my lords brothers, let me die thus, the stinking dog, for according to my
deeds I ought also to be judged; for all injustice and greed has come to be
with me, for I am the

sea of sins. And the monks and the archimandrite were weeping, seeing that
incurable wound; and the archimandrite says to him: You are not yet eighteen
years old and what sins do you have? Saint Symeon says to him: The prophet
David says: Behold, I was conceived in lawlessness and in sins my mother
conceived me; and I have been clothed with the like of all these. And the
archimandrite was amazed at his intelligent answer, that being a rustic how he
was pricked unto the fear of God. And the archimandrite, having called two
physicians, with many toils and pains, so that he was thought to have died,
they pulled the rope from him † from the flesh glued on it, and having tended
him fifty days they benefited him in part. And the archimandrite says to him:
Child, behold you have become healthy; go off where you wish.
9 Then saint Symeon goes out from the monastery; and behold there was a well
lying near the monastery, in which there was no water in it, but a multitude
of unclean wicked spirits dwelt in it; and not only of unclean spirits, but
also of asps and vipers and snakes and scorpions an incomparable multitude;
whence all were afraid to pass through that place. And saint Symeon went off
there, no one knowing, and throws himself into that well, having made the seal
in Christ, and hides himself in the side of the well.
10 So after seven days of his departing from the monastery the archimandrite
sees in a dream an incomparable multitude of men clothed in white and circling
the monastery, holding lamps and saying: Now we burn you here, unless you hand
over to us the servant of God Symeon. And for what cause did you drive him
out, and what did he do, that you cast him out from the monastery, and what is
his fault? Tell us, before we burn you; do you not know what you had in your
monastery? For this one will be found greater than you on that fearful and
dreadful day. And having been awakened the archimandrite, becoming trembling,
says to the monks: As I see, that man happens to be a genuine servant of God;
for behold I suffered many evils in a dream this night because of him; but I
beseech you, brothers, run about for me and find him for me, since let none of
you come here.
11 And having gone out they sought him in every place and not having found him
they came saying to the archimandrite: Truly, master, we did not leave a place
where we did not seek, except the place in which no one dares to travel
because of the multitude of the wild beasts. The archimandrite says to them:
Children, having made a prayer and having taken lamps, go off, go down and
search for him. And having made a prayer above the well for three hours they
let down into the well five monks with ropes, holding lamps; and the reptiles,
seeing, fled into the corners. And saint Symeon, having seen them, cried out,
saying: I beg you, brothers and servants of God, grant me a little, that I may
give over my spirit, for it is enough for me that what I set myself I did not
fulfill. But the monks, having forced him with much force, brought him up from
the well, dragging him as one who had done evil; and they brought him to the
archimandrite. And he, having seen him, fell at his feet, saying: Forgive me,
servant of God, become yourself my teacher and teach me the things which
endurance has and grants.
12 But saint Symeon did not cease weeping and begging God, and having spent
three years in that very monastery he goes out, no one knowing, and goes off
to a desert place, in which sufficient villages were lying near, and nearest
him was a

village called Gelasois, and he builds there for himself a small place of dry
stones, and he stands in the middle four years, being snowed on, rained on,
scorched, and many were going off to him; and his food was soaked lentils and
his drink water. And after these things he made for himself a pillar of four
cubits and he stands on it seven years, and his fame was running everywhere;
and after these things the crowds build for him two folds of dry stone, and
they set a door on the inner fold and they make for him a pillar of thirty
cubits, and he stands on it fifteen years, performing many healings; for many
who were demon-possessed were going there and being healed.
13 And saint Symeon was imitating his teacher Christ, whom also calling upon
he made the lame walk, cleansing lepers, making the mute speak, the paralyzed
run, granting healing to those long-sick, admonishing and exhorting each: If
someone says to you, Who healed you, say you, God healed me; let it not seem
good to you to say that Symeon healed me, since you would find yourself again
in the same pains. And this I say to you: never lie and swear God; and if also
a necessity of swearing comes to you, swear by me the humble one, whether with
truth or with falsehood; for it is a great sin and fearful to swear unto God.
14 Hear a fearful and paradoxical wonder: his mother after twenty years learns
where saint Symeon is and having come running she wished to see him after so
many years and weeping much toward seeing him and she was not permitted to see
him; and as she wished for a long time to be hallowed by his holy hands, she
was forced to climb the wall; and climbing on the wall of the fold she was
broken to the ground, not having been able to see him. And saint Symeon makes
known to her: Forgive me, mother, for now, and if we are worthy in that age we
shall see one another. And as she heard these things, she was burned the more
to see him. And saint Symeon makes known to her: Cease, my lady mother, since
you have come from afar and have toiled because of me the humble one, but set
yourself a little and resting take strength and after a little I see you. And
she, having heard, sets herself at his doorway and immediately she gave over
her spirit to God. And the attendants came to wake her; and they found her
dead and they announced to the saint concerning her. And he, having heard,
orders her to be brought in and sets her before his pillar, and having seen
her with tears he began to say: Lord, the God of the powers, the guide of the
light and charioteer of the Cherubim, who guided Joseph, who empowered your
prophet David against Goliath, who raised Lazarus four days from the dead,
raise your right hand and receive in peace the soul of your servant. And as he
was praying her holy relic was moved and she smiled in the face, and all who
were watching were astonished and were glorifying God; and having laid her out
they buried her before his pillar, that, as he prays, he remembers her.
15 Hear another strange and paradoxical mystery: some were coming to prayer
from a long distance, and a hind grazing meets them, having in the womb, and
one of them says to the hind: I adjure you by the power of the holy Symeon,
stand, that I may seize you. And immediately the hind stood, and having seized
her he slaughtered her and they ate her flesh; but the hide remained; and
immediately their speech was hindered, and they began to bleat as the
irrational animals, and they come running and fall before his pillar begging
to obtain healing, and they filled the hide of the hind with chaff and toward
the recognition of many people the hide was set for a time

sufficient, and the men, having spent a sufficient time in repentance and
having been healed, withdrew to their own.
16 Hear another strange and wondrous thing: a certain woman, thirsting through
the night, wished to drink water; and having taken the pitcher of water she
drinks together with the water a small snake, and having been nourished in her
belly it became large, and her face became as the appearance of green grass,
and many physicians came, that they might heal her and they were not able; and
those in her house, having known the wonders and the healings which the saint
of God Symeon was doing, having taken her they led her to the saint and they
announce to him all the things concerning her and he orders them, saying: Put
into her mouth of this water and of this spring. And having done thus
according to what was commanded, the beast was moved before all, and she
dashes herself to the ground, and it comes out and having set down its head in
the midst of the railings it made a noise, and all were glorifying God.
17 And they changed his pillar, making it of forty cubits, and he was rumored
into the whole inhabited world. Whence the multitude of the Saracens came to
him burning with the faith, and he pricked them unto the fear of God. So the
man-hating devil, having a custom of tempting the saints and being trampled by
them, brought a plague upon his thigh in the same manner as of the blessed
Job, a pain of the so-called plague, and his thigh putrefied and he was
standing thereafter on one foot for a two-year time. And incomparable worms
were falling from his thigh upon the earth, whence those nearest him had no
other work except only to collect them and bring them up to the place whence
they fell, the saint saying: Eat, whence the Lord has given you.
18 And according to the will of God it happens that the king of the Saracens
comes to prayer toward him, and as soon as he went in nearest the pillar to be
blessed by him, saint Symeon, the saint of God having seen him began to
admonish him. And as they were speaking a worm falls from his thigh, and the
king sets his mind, not indeed knowing what it was that had fallen, and having
run he snatches it. And he sets it upon his eyes and unto his heart and he
goes out outside holding it in his hand. And the saint makes known to him,
saying: Come in, put down what you took up, you bring a burden to me the
sinner. It is a stinking worm from stinking flesh. Why do you destroy your
hands, being an honorable man? And when the righteous one had said these
things the Saracen comes in and says to him: This shall be for me unto a
blessing and unto forgiveness of sins. And having opened his hand there was an
honorable pearl in his hand. And having seen it he began to glorify God and he
says to the righteous one: Behold what you said is a worm, it is a pearl of
which there is no price, through which the Lord has enlightened me. And after
these things having heard, the saint says to him: As you have believed let it
be to you all the days of your life, and not only to you, but also to your
children. And having been blessed the king of the Saracens went off to his
own, rejoicing in peace.
19 Hear another mystery. On the mountain on which he stands, from the east a
great dragon was remaining. Whence not even herbage was coming up in that
place. And it happened to that dragon, as he was coming out to cool himself,
that a stick went into his eye, and for a sufficient time from the pain no one
was able to bear his hissing. So on one of the days the dragon comes out from
his hole dragging and comes while all are watching and sets himself at the
doorway of the fold. And suddenly his eye was opened and the stick comes out
from his eye, and he spent three days there, until he was restored healthy.
And thus he went off to his place while all were watching

having wronged no one, but as a sheep thus he was lying at the doorway of the
righteous one, and all were going in and going out, being wronged in nothing
by him.
20 Hear another paradoxical wonder. There was a chief robber in Syria, his
name Antiochus, and his byname was called Gonatas, whose things concerning him
were spoken in the whole world. And from here and from there soldiers were
sent to hunt him and bring him into Antioch, and no one was able to hunt him
because of the great power of his strength. So bears and the remaining beasts
were prepared in Antioch, as he being about to fight with beasts, and the
whole city of Antioch was moved because of him. And they go out to hunt him
and they find him drinking, in a certain village in an inn, and the soldiers
circled the inn. And that one, having learned, began to declaim, and a river
was lying by the village, and that chief robber had a mare and he was
commanding her as a man and having risen he throws his garments upon the mare
and says to her: Go off to the river and wait for me there, and the mare goes
out from the inn biting and buffeting, and having gone off to the river she
was waiting for him. And the chief robber also goes out from the inn having
bared his sword, crying out and saying to the multitude of the soldiers: Flee
lest anyone die, and none of the soldiers mastered him. And having escaped the
notice of all who were guarding him and having crossed the river together with
the mare, and having sat upon her, he reached the fold of saint Symeon. And
having gone in he threw himself before his pillar, and indeed the soldiers
having been gathered in the fold of the saint, the saint says to them: With
our master Jesus Christ two robbers were crucified together, the one indeed
received according to his works, and the other inherited the kingdom of
heavens. And if anyone is able to stand against the one who sent him, let him
come and himself snatch him away, for I neither brought him nor am I able to
send him away. For the one who sent him here himself also claims him, so let
no one any longer revile me the humble one, who has toiled much because of my
many sins. And having said these things to them he sent them away. And as
these were going off the chief robber says: My lord, I am going off. And the
saint says to him: Do you go off again into the same evils? And the chief
robber says: No, master, the Lord is calling me. And having stretched out his
hands unto heaven, he said nothing more, except only: Son of God receive my
spirit in peace. And he wept for two hours, so as to make even the righteous
one weep with those standing by. So setting himself before the pillar of the
righteous one, he immediately gave over the spirit, and the crowds laid him
out and buried him near the fold of the righteous one. So on the next day
there come from Antioch more than a hundred men with swords to snatch him and
they began to shout down at the righteous one: Send away to us the one you
have. And the holy one said to them: Brothers, the one who sent him here is
stronger than you, and having had need of him as being useful, having sent
toward him two fearful soldiers in arms, who were able to thunderbolt your
city with those dwelling in it, he took him, and I the sinner, having seen
their fearful form, having feared I did not dare to speak against them, lest
they kill even me the humble one as opposing God. And those men, having heard
these things from the saint and having learned with what glory the chief
robber gave over the spirit, glorifying God and trembling they went off again
unto Antioch.
21 Hear another fearful and glorious wonder. In the place in which the saint
was, water was not appearing. And the multitude of the animals and of the
people was perishing of those coming in the place of saint Symeon. And the
saint, having made a prayer for seven days, did not speak to anyone, but he
was having bent the knees and praying, so as to think

all that he had already died. And about the fifth hour of the seventh day
suddenly water gushed from the east of his fold, and having dug they found a
form of a cave filled with water. And they built in it seven mouths. And all
were glorifying the God of heaven and of earth.
22 [And a certain queen of the Saracens, being barren, went off begging him
that she might bear. For she was being reproached by her husband each day and
falling before much and begging for sufficient days she was remaining at the
pillar of saint Symeon. So the saint made known to her: Go off to your house
and the Lord has the things advantageous to you to grant. And having gone off
to her house she came together with her husband and immediately she conceived.
And she bore a daughter. And for five years she was neither speaking nor
walking. And she comes with her husband and her child weeping before him, and
saint Symeon answering said to them: Remain and God has to help. And as they
spent seven days they were being benefited nothing and being grieved they were
withdrawing, saying that In any case the Lord did not wish to heal her. And as
they were traveling and having gone off not a little they turn to see the
column of saint Symeon. And behold their daughter suddenly cried out, saying:
Glory to you, saint Symeon. And the parents of the child, having seen,
glorified God through saint Symeon.
23 And hear another mystery. On one of the days as soldiers were traveling, a
certain woman from much desire having come to see the face of saint Symeon by
what pretext to see him; she was devising then to take on a man's form and to
see him. So on one of the days she saw sufficient soldiers going off to the
saint to pray. And having taken on the soldier's form she also was traveling
with them. And having arrived at the place where the pillar of saint Symeon
was, she says to the soldiers with her: Brothers, if you wish, leaving me your
animals go in and be blessed by the righteous one, and when you come out I
also go in again, that I may be counted worthy to be blessed by the righteous
one. And when they had gone in and worshiped, saint Symeon says to them: One
soldier of you was left outside. And they said to him: Yes, master; he guards
our animals. And the righteous one answering said to them: Having gone out say
to him: Do not trouble yourself, for you have been heard and you are blessed
to the Lord. And they, having gone out, were inquiring, exhorting him, What
good has been done by you before God? for behold the righteous one said to us,
Say to the soldier with you who remained outside: do not trouble yourself, for
your petition has been heard and you are blessed to the Lord. And answering he
said to them: I confess to you, brothers, I am a woman and from much desire I
had to see the righteous one, since I am a sinner. And the soldiers having
heard were astonished, blessing God through saint Symeon, and they were going
in peace.
24 And another mystery if you hear. When a sufficient multitude had come †
according to the custom with incense, crying to him: Servant of God, the
people waits to receive your blessing, command, send them away, for behold
they have been waiting a long hour, and they were receiving no answer. For his
spirit was not present. And as I was crying to him persistently and not
receiving an answer the deacon began to lament above at the column, having
thought that he had died. And the multitude of the people having seen began to
groan and to beat a great beating, mourning their sins. And as after a
sufficient hour his holy spirit returned again, and answering he said to the
people: Brothers, a great ship on the sea was being storm-tossed violently at
this hour, having[ta] about three hundred souls; and they cried out

all the souls, exhorting the master God. And crying to me with dreadful oaths
and I, having seen what was happening to them, exhorted the God who is always
long-suffering over our sins to calm the sea, and having given them a hand I
saved them all. And having heard the mystery the multitudes of the people were
glorifying God through saint Symeon. And the righteous one, having blessed
them, sent them away in peace.
25 A female snake had a great swelling come out and from her pains she was
going off for one mile. And the male, sharing the pain of her pains, taking
his female they were coming toward the lord Symeon and having arrived at the
pillar they were separated from one another. For the female, not daring to be
seen by the righteous one, went off to the women's part, and the male, having
gone in among that multitude, was worshiping the pillar, making his head up
and down and begging the righteous one. And the crowds, having seen the
immense size of the snake, fled from him. And saint Symeon, having seen, said
to the crowds: Do not flee, brothers, for truly having a prayer he came here.
For his female is very weak and she went off to the women's part. And he says
to the snake: Take up mud from the earth and bring it up to your wife. And
having set it on, blow on her and he has to heal her. And the snake, having
taken of the mud, was going off toward his female. And the crowds, having
seen, followed him to see what he was about to do. And they see his female
standing upright outside the railings having a great swelling and the snake,
having taken the mud, sets it on her and having blown on her in the presence
of all he healed her. And having taken her he withdrew. And the crowds, having
seen the mystery, glorified God.
26 And another wonder came to pass in his days. 26 For when the great threat
of the fears had come upon the whole inhabited world, the whole East and
Antioch were being brought down to him, begging him to prevail upon God. For
many falls of dwellings were coming to pass and deaths of sufficient people,
so that even the righteous one with the pillar was being shaken as a reed by
wind. And weeping with them he was saying to the crowds: Brothers, all have
turned aside, together they have become useless, there is not even one. You do
not hear me, but unto who will defraud whom your fornications and your
injustices have overflowed. Truly I say to you, brothers: it is easier for me
to speak with the master than with you the faithless. And having commanded the
supplicating to cease, he made a prayer, and again the threat was remaining in
the same things, shaking all things. And he permitted them to cry Lord have
mercy. And as they were remaining for sufficient hours and crying: Give us
peace, he made a great prayer. And after he fulfilled the prayer, answering he
said to the people: Brothers, truly I say to you, that from the whole
multitude except one soul another was not heard; and that I may persuade you,
behold in the midst I make him come. And having commanded him to come in the
presence of all he questions him and says to him: Brother, believe me saying:
from all these you alone were heard; but inform all and tell us, what good has
been done by you. And that one was not wishing, saying: I am a sinner, forgive
me. And as he was being forced for a long time a voice came to him from heaven
saying: Your petition has been heard. And as the rustic was standing among the
whole people he began to narrate to them that I am a man, a farmer, and it is
a custom for me, my labor of the day, whatever I work, I divide them into
three parts. And first I set aside the part of the poor, after the end of the
public tax, and thus that of my expense. Doing this until now I have not
ceased. And all, greeting, were worshiping him. And they were pressing the
man, each one mad to see him.

28 So the blessed one stood on various columns forty-seven years and after all
these things he sought him; and it was the day of Preparation; and he was shut
for prayer and as he had a custom, he spent the whole Preparation and the

softer, I knew that he had died; and having set my face in my hands I wept
bitterly and having bent I kissed his mouth and his eyes and his beard and
having lifted aside his garment I kissed his feet and having taken his hand I
set it upon my eyes. And in his whole body and in his garments there was a
smell of myrrh, so as to be gladdened from that fragrance. And having stood
about a half-hour I attended to his honorable relic and behold his body was
shaken together with the pillar and I heard a voice saying: Amen amen amen,
and from the fear I said: Bless me, Lord, and remember me in your good rest.
29 And I went down and I did not make the mystery manifest to anyone, lest a
tumult come to pass, but through a faithful man I signaled to the bishop of
Antioch Martyrius and to the general Ardaburius. And on the next day the
bishop of Antioch comes with also six other bishops; and Ardaburius also comes
with six hundred names, lest the villages having been gathered snatch his
honorable relic; for thus they were also planning. And curtains came to be in
a circle of his pillar. And three bishops go up and kiss his garments having
said three psalms and they brought up a leaden coffin and they set his holy
relic and they bring it down through machines and then all knew that saint
Symeon had died, so that even all the Saracens were gathered in arms with
camels, they also wishing to snatch the relic. And a confusion of the crowd
comes to pass so that the mountain was not appearing from the multitude and
from the smoke of the incenses and of the tapers and lamps being burned, of
which there was no number. And the voices of the weeping of the men and women
and children were going off even far and the whole mountain was being shaken
from the cry of the birds that had been gathered and had circled the fold of
the saint. So as they brought him down, they set him upon the marble altar,
which he had before his pillar, already having the fourth day having died. And
thus his holy relic was stretched out as having been rested before one hour.
And all the bishops gave him the love; and his face was very bright as

the light; and the hairs of his head and of his beard were as snow. And the
bishop of Antioch wished to take a hair of his beard unto a blessing and his
hand was dried, and all the bishops made a prayer for him, weeping and saying
to the holy relic that Nothing of the members or of the garments is lacking to
you, nor again will anyone take from your holy and honorable relic. And as
they were saying these things with weeping the hand of the bishop was restored
healthy. And then with psalms and hymns they put him in the coffin.
30 And I the sinner was watching before five and six days of his rest a man in
a fearful robe, which I am not able to narrate, and his length being as of two
men and speaking with him a third of the day and giving him the love. And I
wished to narrate to some the things concerning him and my mind was being
snatched and I was being hindered from speaking until he was rested. So as the
man whom I said was coming to him, I was watching, he says, them eating; but
what they were eating, I did not know and they were psalming; but what they
were psalming, I did not know, except only the amen. And fear was holding me
toward the sight of the man.
31 So they set the coffin of the saint upon the carriage and thus with tapers
and incenses and psalmody they carry him off into their city Antioch. And as
they were coming about five miles from the city in a place called Merope the
mules stood and were not being moved; for a paradoxical mystery was there; for
there was on the right of the road a tomb. And a certain man was remaining at
it; and the matter of the man was such. For he was loving a married woman for
twenty years and he was not able to master her. And the woman died, and they
set her in that tomb. And that somehow the hater of the good might gain the
soul of the man, having gone off into the tomb he opens aside the slab of the
tomb and comes together with the dead body. And immediately he was made mute
and was held in the tomb and he was no longer able to withdraw from that
place. And as the wayfarers were passing they were attending to him sitting on
the little steps of the tomb, and each for God's sake was bringing to him, one
water, and one food. And according to the will of God as the honorable relic
was passing on that day and (read ὁ) neither speaking nor hearing, the
carriage standing and the crowd, he came out from the tomb crying out and
saying: Have mercy on me, saint of God Symeon, and having taken hold of the
carriage, immediately his bridles were opened and his wits came unto
understanding. And all, having seen what had happened, glorified God, and from
the cry of the people that place was being shaken. And the man was crying out:
I today through you have been saved, servant of God, for I had been lost by
the sin.
32 And the whole city went out to the meeting of the honorable relic, clothed
in white, with tapers and lamps. And they bring him into the church called of
Cassian. And after thirty days the general Ardaburius commands and they set
him in the Great Church, and from there again according to a revelation of God
a prayer-house temple of the holy and righteous Symeon was built and thus with
much glory and hymns they transferred his holy relic into that prayer-house.
33 And many exhorted, having given gold and silver, that they might take a
blessing from his holy relics. And the bishop granted to no one because of the
oaths which he swore. And many healings are performed in the place where his
honorable relics are laid through the grace given to him by our master Christ.
And the saint of God Symeon was perfected in the month of September on the
1st, in the reign
