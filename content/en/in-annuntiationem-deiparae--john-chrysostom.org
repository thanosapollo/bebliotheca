On the Annunciation of the Theotokos, and against the impious Arius.
=62.763=
On the former Sunday the mystic and interpreter of the uncreated Trinity, the
great Basil, the God-taught orator, the advocate of the truth, the noble
accuser of the lie, who triumphed over the rage of the Arian madness as was
fitting, who speaks with his life before his tongue, and cries with his word
together with his manner of life, the most familiar ornament of this city, the
star of the Church, who arrays himself against the enemies of piety, as
against wolves, as a noble soldier, and uses his tongue, as a sling, against
the impious; the sleepless guardian of this flock, and the all-wise
wall-builder of the holy fold, called this orthodox Church a heavenly choir.
And just as the dove in the time of Noah, having kept the branch of the olive
ever-green upon the mouth, and having brought this fruit-bearing to us; so
also this man lent his own tongue to the Spirit on the Sunday before this, and
with his divine words he shot down Arius, his blasphemy being of the teaching
of the devil. He showed in the midst of the sheep the wolf, as a lamb covering
himself with the fleece of Christianity; and having stripped off his garment
of hypocrisy, he pilloried this one as a ravening wolf, and what was thought
to be a sheep, using the prophetic and apostolic words as stones, he stoned
the beast. And just as =62.764= David, having cast down Goliath, found that
one's sword useful to him for the slaughter of the enemy; so also the new
David of our array pierced Arius with the weapons of Arius.
He set down in the word how God and Father was Father of the Word without
time; how the Son is co-eternal with the Father; how consubstantial, not of
another substance; how fellow-worker, but not under-worker; how maker, but not
a thing made; how Master, not slave; how radiance of the glory of the Father
and impress of the hypostasis; how In the beginning was the Word, and the Word
was with God, and the Word was God; how the Word became flesh not having been
turned, became flesh not having been changed, became flesh not having been
altered, and tabernacled among us, himself having fashioned for himself our
nature as a tent inseparable from his own divinity. Thus then this herald of
the truth proclaimed; for he had Christ himself speaking through him; but
being blasphemed by the Arians.
For this reason the angels from heaven praised him invisibly, as hymning well
upon the earth the consubstantial Trinity worshipped by them, and teaching it
to be glorified; but you from below, leaping with just applauses, were glad,
and you proclaimed the imitator of the apostles worthy of the apostles, as
gush =62.765= ing with the tongue the blessed confession of Peter, as kindling
with the lips the zeal of Paul; and you reasonably called the Church blessed
as possessing such a treasure; for she is truly blessed having such a
treasury.
These things then happened on that day. But I today wish to open for you the
forecourts of the incarnation of the only-begotten Son of God, that you may
behold how God the Word, being consubstantial with the Father, having taken
our substance, willed to become consubstantial also with the slaves; how the
high one bent himself from heaven toward our lowliness; how the rich one put
on our poor first-fruits; how the one without need took what he formed, not
needing; how the maker bound round his own work; how remaining God the lover
of mankind became also man; how the Sun of righteousness, having kindled in
himself the lamp of the body, went about everywhere seeking the lost drachma;
how the good shepherd, having covered his own divinity with the hide of the
sheep, ran about, calling to himself the wandering sheep, and saying to each,
I am the good shepherd.
But it is necessary for us to speak also the voice of the holy Gospels
proclaimed today in the Churches: In the sixth month the archangel Gabriel was
sent by God into a city of Galilee, whose name was Nazareth, to a virgin
betrothed to a man whose name was Joseph, of the house and lineage of David;
and the name of the virgin was Mary. And the angel having entered to her said:
Hail, full of grace, the Lord is with you; blessed are you among women. In the
sixth month, already Zechariah having been condemned to speechlessness because
of unbelief, already Elizabeth having sprouted the unexpected branch, already
John the Baptist having a six-month time in the prison of the womb, already
the forerunner having been prepared beforehand in the places of barrenness,
already the lamp shining in the dark places of the belly, the angel Gabriel
was sent by the Sun of righteousness. Go, he says, into the city of Galilee,
Nazareth, to the virgin Mary betrothed to the carpenter Joseph; for I, the
carpenter of the whole creation, have betrothed this virgin for the salvation
of men. Foretell to her my untroubled presence toward her, that the virgin,
having received me without foreknowledge, may not be thrown into confusion;
teach her beforehand my loving-kindness, for the sake of which I wish to come
forth from her into the world as man, that having learned beforehand what is
being economized, she may not be astonished at what is being accomplished;
proclaim beforehand the economy, that she may not be troubled having seen the
bulk of her womb suddenly being raised. Already accomplish the journey by your
course; for there you will find me where I now send you; there I anticipate
you, remaining here. I both before you and after you enter in to her; you
bring the good tidings of my arrival, and I, having come invisibly, will seal
your voice with the works. For I wish in the virginal womb to renew the race
of men; I wish by condescension to recast the image which I formed; I wish by
a new fashioning to heal the ancient formation. From virgin earth I formed the
first-formed man; but the devil, having taken that one as an enemy, dragged
him about and cast him down, and mocked when my image had fallen.
I wish now from the virgin earth to compact for myself a new Adam, that nature
also may make a fair defense for itself, and may lawfully be crowned against
the one who cast it down, and that the enemy may reasonably be put to shame. O
unmeasured sea of condescension! O infinite height of divine loving-kindness;
the king did not send many angels, or many forerunners; the creator did not
move the armies of the bodiless powers, but one angel only he commanded to
become the forerunner of his presence, that through the teaching of the one he
might make secure beforehand the mother about to receive him.
The angel therefore came to the virgin Mary, and having entered to her said:
Hail, full of grace. He addressed the fellow-slave as mistress, as already
having become mother of the Master. Hail, full of grace. Your ancestress Eve,
having transgressed the law, received a sentence that she would bear children
in sorrows; but to you the address of rejoicing is fitting; that one bore
Cain, having given birth to envy and murder; but you will bear =62.766= a son
providing life and incorruption to all. Rejoice then and dance; rejoice and
trample the head of the serpent; Hail, full of grace. For the curse has
ceased, corruption has been abolished, the gloomy things have withered, the
bright things bloom, the good long ago proclaimed by the prophets is present.
The Holy Spirit was pointing you out, saying through the tongue of Isaiah:
Behold, the virgin shall conceive in the womb, and shall bear a son. That
virgin is you. Rejoice then, full of grace; you pleased the Creator, you
pleased the Maker, you pleased the Fashioner, you pleased the one who delights
in the beauties of souls; you found a bridegroom who keeps your virginity, not
who corrupts it; you found a bridegroom who from much loving-kindness wishes
to become your son. The Lord is with you, and in you the one who is
everywhere, both with you and from you; in heaven the Master, in the deep the
Most High, in all creation the Creator; the Fashioner upon the Cherubim, the
charioteer upon the Seraphim, the Son in the bosom of the Father, the
Only-begotten in your bosom, the Lord, as he himself knows, whole everywhere,
whole in you. Blessed are you among women, because you have now been preferred
of all the virgins together; because you were counted worthy to give
hospitality to such a Master; because you contained the uncontainable who
willed it; because you received the one who fills all things; because you have
become a most pure workshop of the divine economy; because you have appeared a
worthy vehicle of the entrance into life of our king; because you have been
shown a treasury of the spiritual pearl. Blessed are you among women.
The all-holy virgin, having heard these words, and having seen an angelic form
and voice, reasoned with herself [saying], Of what sort might this greeting
be? What then does this address wish? What then do these words signify to me
beforehand? An angel first addressed me, being whom, or of what dignity
happening to be? Am I full of grace? From where, or on account of what? The
Lord with me? For with whom of those who fear him is the Lord not? Am I
blessed among women? For what grace then, or on account of what, and how? What
gift then does this word proclaim beforehand? What wonder then does this
saying foretell? But the angel, having seen her thus troubled, says to her:
Fear not, Mary; for I did not greet you that you might be thrown into
confusion; that you might keep festival, I speak kindly to you. Do not then
have trouble in your soul, you who possess with yourself the peace of the
inhabited world. Fear not, Mary; for you have found grace with God. You have
been adorned above creation, you have been made bright above the heavens, you
have shone above the sun, you have been exalted above the angels; you were not
taken up into the heavens, but standing upon the earth, you drew to yourself
the heavenly Master and king of all. And behold, you shall conceive in the
womb. The work anticipated my word; the mystical conception has become swifter
than my voice; you have within, in your belly, the Lord himself, the creator
of all things, building without toil from your holy and undefiled flesh the
temple of his holy flesh; you have within the painter of nature, painting
again by grace his own image that had grown old. Behold, you shall conceive in
the womb, and shall bear a son, and you shall call his name Jesus; this one
shall be great. For he is great in the divinity, and he shall be great also in
the humanity. And he shall be called Son of the Most High; and the Lord God
shall give him the throne of David his father. For he swore truth to David,
and he will not set it aside.
And what truth did he swear to him? From the fruit of your loins, he says, I
will set upon your throne. That, the oath, receives its end in the present
time. And he shall reign over the house of Jacob unto the ages, and of his
kingdom there shall be no end. No one is higher than this king, and there is
no other more blessed than you who bear him. And Mary said to the angel: How
shall this be to me, since I know not a man? Joseph is my betrothed, not a
husband; for as far as the pledges the contracts concerning marriage have gone
forward; how then without marriage shall the conception be to me? How without
a planter shall I sprout fruit? Upon me first is the course of nature
exchanged? Upon me first is the law of conception rewritten? Upon me alone is
a strange path of childbearing found? Upon me alone are the ancient customs of
generation made new? Teach me plainly, O angel, =62.767= the riddle of the
words, narrate to me the manner of the sayings, interpret to me the power of
your good tidings, lead me by the hand toward the strange childbearing, that I
may learn little by little the mystery I am about to bear, that I may learn
how the impossible things will become possible, that I may learn how the
unmanageable things obtain some device, that I may learn how the things
without resource have been able to find a way, that I may hear how grace
innovates nature toward the better, or how nature serves grace. And the angel
answering said to her: The Holy Spirit shall come upon you. You have learned
the craftsman, do not over-inquire the manner; you have learned the fashioner,
do not over-inquire the work; you have heard concerning the Holy Spirit, leave
then the working to the Spirit itself. The Holy Spirit shall come upon you,
and the power of the Most High shall overshadow you.
This only I was commanded to say to you, that you will bear a son; but how you
will bear, he himself who is being born of you will teach you clearly. I as a
slave brought the word; the Master himself will add the work, who also knows
the manner; I am a minister of royal commands, not an interpreter of divine
counsels; I do not dare to teach you what I have not learned, I do not dare to
interpret what I have not received, I do not dare to make public the matter of
the mystery, I have not strength to make the gospel clear. I cry the wonder,
but I am too weak to speak the manner; I do not seek the things that surpass
all seeking, I do not pursue the incomprehensible, I do not meddle in the
things above me, I do not call to account the grace beyond word, I do not
subtilize the unspeakable generation. Truly the spiritual conception is above
mind, the paradoxical pregnancy above word, and the saving birth above
narration. The Holy Spirit shall come upon you. The Holy Spirit knows what it
works; the Son knows how he fashions for himself the spiritual dwelling. For
the maker of nature does not serve the laws of nature; he himself alone has
the counsel; he himself, as he wills, puts on the form of the slave. The
Father knows the incarnation of the only-begotten Son; for where the Holy
Spirit is, there also is the Son, and where the Son is there also is the
Father. Inseparable is the Trinity, undivided is the Trinity. For as in the
beginning the Father, fashioning man, urged the Son and the Spirit toward the
work, saying, Let us make man according to our image and according to
likeness; so also now when the refashioning has come about, the holy Trinity
is present again unspeakably. For of whom the fashioning is one, of this also
the refashioning again is common. Wherefore also the holy thing being born
shall be called Son of God. For this is truly a holy offspring, this an unsown
shoot, this an all-holy conception. This one is the son of God because of the
divinity, this one also your Son because of the humanity; this one
consubstantial with the Father according to what is thought, this one
consubstantial with you according to what appears; this one the heavenly and
earthly; this one the invisible and the seen; this one impassible in the
divinity, this one also passible in the humanity; and as he is perfect God, so
also Jesus is perfect man. And as having become man he has remained God
unaltered, so also you remaining a virgin shall become also a mother
undefiled; for neither will the virginity war against the birth, nor does the
birth campaign against the virginity.
Hymn then what has come about, and do not meddle with the gift; marvel at the
great-giving, and do not doubt toward the grace; worship the mystery entrusted
to you, and do not over-inquire the strangeness of the wonder. If you believe
sincerely, he himself who is believed will enlighten your soul; but if you
search inquisitively, you will find what you desire in no way. Do you wish to
learn? Learn the truth of the wonder concerning you from a manifest sign. Do
you wish to behold the image of the mystery of your generation? Behold
Elizabeth your neighbor and kinswoman. For behold, he says, Elizabeth your
kinswoman, she also has conceived a son in her old age; and this is the sixth
month for her who is called barren; because no word shall be impossible with
God. The barrenness of Elizabeth was loosed beyond hope, that the loosing of
the affliction might become a teacher of your virginity that is not loosed.
The barren and aged woman conceived the forerunner of the king who shall be
born of you, that you may believe that you have the Lord upon your womb. Go,
observe Elizabeth; and if you do not find her thus, as I said, do not believe
my proclamation.
What then after these words the =62.768= holy Mary, the virgin in body and in
soul, the pious and reverent and obedient, the honor of human nature, the gate
of our life, the procurer of our salvation? Having received the voice of the
angel kindly, she says to him: Behold the handmaid of the Lord, be it to me
according to your word. As a slave I lie before the master's nod; as clay I
lie under the hands of the potter; as the fashioner wills with authority, let
him work in me the will; as he wills with loving-kindness, let him work in me
as a wonder the paradoxical pregnancy. Be it to me according to your word; be
it to me that these your words be both most evident and true things. And the
angel departed from her, who having come down from heaven, catechized,
prepared beforehand the virgin, taught her, as much as she contained; he
himself also heard from her the things he was eager to learn; and again he
went up whence he came down, having left below the one who sent him, and again
finding him above in the heavens worshipped by every angelic host.
What then do you say to these things, O Arius? Tell me, O wretched one, how
was the uncontainable contained in a virginal womb, how being a virgin she
conceived, and again was kept a virgin, and was enriched with the birth of the
Savior, and she herself was co-enriched with the grace. But you would not have
to say. For if the mother of the mystery did not comprehend the mystery which
she bore, how will you be able to comprehend this? If Gabriel the archangel,
having been asked by the mother of the Lord, How shall this be to me, since I
know not a man, was not able to say more, but fled to the Holy Spirit, as
being able to declare nothing else stronger, how will you find to say what
Gabriel was not strong enough to interpret? And if no one is able to narrate
worthily the generation of Christ according to the flesh, which came about in
the last of the days, that generation, the heavenly, the timeless, the
bodiless, the invisible, the impassible, the altogether inexpressible and
incomprehensible, how do you dare to meddle with it? Who furnished you the
seeds of such daring? The angels worship the holy Trinity, crying unceasingly,
Holy, holy, holy Lord of Sabaoth, heaven and earth are full of his glory. They
do not say, as you say: Holier the Father, as older; they do not say with
lowered voices, Holy the Son, as younger; holy the Paraclete, as shorter; but
they offer one and the same hymnody to the equal Trinity. From where then did
you alone, O Arius, learn to speak contrary to the creation against the maker?
But you set aside the testimony of the heavens and of the angels. To whom then
do you abide, tell? Who is pleasing to you as judge of this dogma? Whom do you
wish we should take as an impartial judge? Whose decision concerning piety do
you receive? Do you abide by the Father who more than all knows his own Son?
Then let us hear him crying: This is my beloved Son, in whom I was well
pleased, hear him. If he should say, I and the Father are one, hear him; if he
should say, I in the Father, and the Father in me, hear him; if he should say:
He who has seen me has seen the Father, hear him; for the truth does not lie;
if he should say, The Father who sent me is greater than I, fit the less not
to the divinity of the one speaking, but to the humanity. You have heard,
Arius, the decision of the Father; hear also his only-begotten Son voting with
these words; God so loved the world, that he gave his only-begotten Son, that
everyone who believes in him should not perish, but have eternal life. He who
believes, not he who says: There was when he was not; He who believes, not he
who doubts.
Why then, having left believing, do you attempt to insult, having the judge as
an enemy? But you, Arius, babbler and madman, believe as you will; for if you
do not wish, wretched one, to recognize what is profitable, babble against
yourself. For neither will you wrong the Son, and you will kindle upon your
own head the fire of gehenna. But I, being trained by the holy Scriptures,
hymn the Father, who is always Father; I hymn the Son, who has shone forth
timelessly from the paternal substance; I hymn the Holy Spirit, who proceeds
from the Father, and rests in the Son; I confess Christ, perfect God, and the
same perfect man; I proclaim of his divinity =62.769= the high things, and I
am not ashamed of the low things of his humanity; but with the great and
blessed apostle Peter I cry to him, You are the Christ, the Son of the living
God; and to you be =62.770= longs the glory and the honor and the worship,
together with the Father with the Holy Spirit, now and always, and unto the
ages of the ages. Amen.
