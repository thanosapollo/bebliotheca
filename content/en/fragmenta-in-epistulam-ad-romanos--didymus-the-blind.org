Rom 7 Doing good, the divine apostle became an unstained image for those being
discipled, for which reason he also cried out: become imitators of me, just as
I also of Christ. Therefore what he said is not his according to the proper
sense: but I know that the good does not dwell in my flesh, nor yet wretched
man that I am, who will deliver me from this body of death? But these things
and as many as are of this kind he says, acting in a parenthetical person,
toward the refutation of those who love sin. And just as divine Scripture
knows how to call human beings from their own deeds fleshly, soulish,
spiritual, and by countless other names, so also it knows how to call the
devil not only Satan and adversary and devil and serpent and lion and Beliar,
but also flesh and law and sin and death; for to these we were enslaved
through the disobedience and not to Christ. For which reason he says: when we
were in the flesh, making clear that now they are no longer in flesh, although
they are in flesh; so that he is not slandering the flesh which we wear, but
the flesh called by the same name as it, from which we have also been
delivered because we died in Christ through being buried with him through
baptism. Truly we have been freed from this flesh; for he adds, saying: but
now we have been released from the law of sin, having died in that in which we
were held, so that we may serve in newness of spirit and not in oldness of
letter. Then in this way he began again the personification according to
hypothesis, beginning from What then shall we say? Is the law sin? May it not
be! up to wretched man that I am, who will deliver me from this body of death?
having set forth as an unshaken foundation in the prologue his own defense,
speaking thus: What then? Is the law sin? May it not be! and that the 2 law is
holy, and the commandment holy and just and good. Here he says law not the one
warring against the law of our mind, but the one that exhorts beforehand, the
one that punishes those who sin. For which reason he also voted with it as
pious and just and good. Then in this way he began again to resolve in
sequence what he had said above, prefacing a word of thanksgiving thus: I
thank God through Jesus Christ our Lord. Then toward the condemnation of those
who take such occasions and speak ill of the fleshes we wear, he says
interrogatively: So then do I myself serve with the mind the law of God, but
with the flesh the law of sin? Have I also, Paul, been divided, serving in one
part the law of God, and in another the law of sin? Do I have Christ and
Beliar together and at the same time in myself? Do I, who say I live, yet no
longer I, but Christ lives in me, having lied, have sin living in myself? He
who addressed others, Do you not know that your bodies are a temple of the
Holy Spirit that is in you, and glorify God in your members, do I myself have
nothing good in the body? But he is not such, he is not; may it not be! For
formerly a child of wrath and a vessel of wrath, having changed his manner,
not his nature, he became a precious vessel of election. Since therefore the
personified words fit neither the inspired Paul, nor indeed anyone buried with
Christ through baptism, because they have been freed from the so-called flesh;
for such as these will no longer be condemned because of the disobedience of
Adam, no longer does sin reign over them, nor the death that is through sin;
for they were buried with the life of Christ, who through obedience abolished
the disobedience, and by his own voluntary death, which struck away the death
that came upon us from it; for he became willingly under the law of
disobedience, in order that he might redeem us who were under the law of
condemnation. For this reason every one who has been redeemed said: but thanks
be to God through Jesus Christ our Lord; for what the law could not do, in
that it was weak through the flesh, God, sending his own Son in the likeness
of flesh of sin, condemned sin in the flesh. And that these things have been
contemplated well in this way, the divine apostle adds, saying: There is
therefore now no condemnation to those in Christ Jesus; for the law of the
Spirit of life in Christ Jesus has freed me from the law of sin and of death,
and the things that follow these.

3 I shall attempt then, in brief, to speak also, in passing, upon the very
words spoken according to personification, beginning from: but sin, taking
occasion through the commandment, deceived me and through it killed me. The
name of sin does not indicate a subsisting substance, but a manner and a life
of having missed some thing that is according to reason and right; for just as
sin is a most generic name, predicated of many and different things. But every
sin comes to be neither by reason nor by a commanding law; for if it came to
be right according to law and reason, it would no longer be a sin, having a
defined and ordered account of its coming-to-be. But since the divine apostle
mentions not simply sins, but a most generic sin that has a mind, that has a
law, able to deal craftily, to furnish occasions toward the construction of
evil, he names as most generic sin no other than the very originator and
father of sins, the devil. For which reason the apostle also declared that he
reigns over the sins: let not sin therefore reign in your mortal body. For
just as we call human beings from the craft they practice, so also he calls
the devil himself, as having become first inventor of sin, sin. Since
therefore through the commandment given to Adam he deceived the human being,
and through it Adam, furnishing himself occasions through the commandment. And
this is it: Why is it, he says, that God said, You shall not eat of every tree
in the garden with eating? And on whatever day you eat of it, your eyes will
be opened, and you will be as gods, knowing good and evil. And he deals
craftily with them, to hide the deceit with a greater promise; he promises the
more, in order that he may overthrow the keepers of the commandment from the
bases of virtue by a fall like his own; for having driven the human being mad
with desire to become equal to God, he furnished the occasion of the converse
according to deceit from the commandment that had been given. For which reason
Paul said: but sin, taking occasion through the commandment, deceived me. And
lest some blame the commandment or the God who gave it, he used a neighboring
and kindred saying, speaking thus: but we know that the law is holy and the
commandment holy and just and good. Since therefore not even sin itself, that
is the devil, is sin by nature and substance, he adds, saying: in order that
sin might become sinful beyond 4 measure through the commandment. For that
which is something according to substance will not admit the more and the
less; for it is what it is, always being this which it is substantially. For
that to which something comes to be and is added, whether little or beyond
measure, is other according to substance than the things added to it; for
quantity is not substance but is about the substance, not being substance. The
sin called by the apostle, then, admitting the more in sinning, in order that
sin might become sinful beyond measure. Therefore it also took a beginning of
being sin, insofar as it is sin, the sin not being in the beginning sinful
beyond measure according to having sinned. Wherefore also he signifies the
intensification of becoming sinful beyond measure in a time that will be,
speaking thus: in order that sin might become sinful beyond measure through
the commandment. But if they should say: and how would it be good and just?
And how is the commandment and the law holy, when through the occasion of them
sin became sinful beyond measure? To these things let them hear that it is not
the case that, if something is harmed, already the occasion through which it
is harmed is also base and evil; for neither, because those who gaze at the
sun without method and without measure are harmed in their sight, do we
already say that the sun also is evil; nor, because the devil has become a
sinner beyond measure by having set his hand against the Son of God, do we
already say that the Son of God also is base and evil.

But I know that good does not dwell in me, that is, in my flesh. Now he
expounds it in the person of Adam. And that, having been deprived of having
God in himself, having exchanged death in place of the true life (for not only
the common death of his members held sway, but also the death of disobedience,
which invaded against his soul); for especially the death that held sway
reigned before the advent of Christ, having against all as law and scepter of
his own kingdom the disobedience. But the kingdom of death is not without
beginning or unbegotten; for dying comes about from the living, and someone's
disobeying from the obedience of the subjects. For to will is present with me,
but to work out the good is not; for the good that I will, this I do not do,
but what I hate, this I practice. If only the willing is in us, but the
working out of the good that was willed is not in us, in vain did the good God
send down into the bodies the souls consubstantial with himself; for they do
not do, they do not work, what is dear to him and to them, but only will it;
and perhaps not even this, for it is unclear. But if this is true, I will
speak according to the impious: then the good neither was nor will be in life,
nor did anyone ever work it, if indeed they take it as a denial of the fair
and the good upon every human being, that the good that I will, this I do not
do, but what I hate, this I practice. And from where also in the life of human
beings were 5 names of virtues found? But if there are those who work the
good, even if those who do this should be few, then not all will say: but we
know that good does not dwell in us; for the good that they willed, this they
practice. But if those who work the good work this while being in flesh, and
those who do not do this but the evil are also in flesh, then the cause of the
good not coming to be is not to be fastened upon the flesh, but upon the
softness of soul of these. For if to those who wish to do the good evil is
present as a neighbor, and likewise also to those who wish to work evil the
good is not present, yet having taken thought beforehand and having willed
beforehand they come to such a degree of working, then our bodies are not base
by nature and substance; for counsel and will precede our deeds, and these
belong to a soul that has a self-determining mind, able to incline toward the
actions it wishes. But now it is no longer I who work it out, but the sin that
dwells in me. Through this saying he made a defense, that the flesh is not
base according to nature; for it is not the flesh which we wear that dwells in
us, but the devil who dwells in the lawless. For in the way I say Christ
dwells in every just person who works the good, in the same way I think the
devil dwells in every evil-doer. Therefore we are not evil insofar as we have
flesh, but the sin that dwells in us, being some other besides the flesh and
the soul of the human being; for the devil is said to sojourn especially in
the soul, according to Satan entered into the heart of Judas, and according to
Ananias, why has Satan filled your heart? And it was well also to say no
longer I work it out but the sin that dwells in me; for there is a great
interval between work and pre-working, because the pre-working precedes the
work, as for example of a house or a temple; and the pre-working, that is the
pre-preparation of the material. The sin then that dwells in those who sin,
that is Satan, admittedly pre-works in our reasonings the construction of the
form of the evil that will be, the soul that received him cooperating with him
in the vice, just as for those who sow the womb of the female toward the seed
being brought to completion. And since from the heart go forth evil
reasonings, from the conception of the devil that has taken place, the divine
saying has cried out: casting down reasonings and every height that is raised
against the knowledge of God. Just as therefore the Holy Spirit is said to
toil together with souls that love virtue, according to I toiled more
abundantly than all, yet not I but the grace of God that is with me, so also
whenever a slack soul is persuaded by the 6 promptings of the devil, it has as
fellow-worker toward the evil the devil himself; for he also pre-worked in it
the type of the sin that will be. For which reason it has been said: no longer
I work it out, but the sin that dwells in me.
