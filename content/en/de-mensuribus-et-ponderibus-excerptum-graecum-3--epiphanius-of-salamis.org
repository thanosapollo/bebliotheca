Of Saint Epiphanius concerning measures and weights.
A kor of wheat is thirty modii, which is called chor. Chor is named thus from
the setting of the hills; for a hill is called choria; for the thirty modii,
heaped into a mound, make a camel's load. Thekel [γρ. Lethek] of barley is
fifteen modii, which is called eparma. A kadus of oil is of fifty xestai. And
this is of the work of the oil-press. Mnasis [γρ. Mnasís] is of ten modii of
wheat or of barley of measures [γρ. measure]. A saton is a heaped modius, so
as to be one modius and a fourth, and of liquid fifty xestai. A small gomor of
twelve modii, of fifty xestai. A medimnos is five modii, so as to be four
modii of a measure of twenty-two xestai, and another ʹ. A choenix and an oiphi
are the same; and it is a measure of two xestai and a half and a postemorion,
which is the measure of the two handfuls of the hand. An artabe is of
seventy-two xestai, a complete measure. Three measures of fine flour. Each
measure had a gomor. And the gomor is a tenth of the great measure of the
artabe, that is seven xestai and five (a fifth). Three kana of groats. The
kana he calls little baskets. And chondrites is a kind of wheat cut in two. A
nebel of wine, a measure of one hundred fifty xestai, omyei thus of liquid
three sata. A kotyle is half a xestes; from there those who sell wine or oil
are called kotylistai. A tryblion in form is an oxybaphon, that is a
side-dish, and it holds half of a xestes. An aporyma [γρ. Aporryma] has the
half of the saiton. And the Saiton is of twenty-two xestai. It is in a ceramic
form [ἴσ. of a ceramic]. Saphitha is a measure of twenty-two xestai among
some, and among others eighteen and twenty-four. The great ein of eighteen
xestai, and the holy one of nine. A kampsakes of water of four xestai. A
chous, which is a chouza, the great one of eight xestai, and the holy one of
ʹ xestai. An alabastron of myrrh. An alabastron is bissin, round-shaped,
holding one litra, that is 132 a hemina. A siklon. The siklon is the fourth of
the ounce, which is one nomisma and a half. Medekom he calls the kyathoi; and
the masmaroth he calls the strainers, or [ἴσ. that is] ithmoi [γρ. ethmoi]. A
kyathos, sometimes unto a simple cup, which is of ʹ xestai; and sometimes unto
a triple, which is of three. And an antlentērion [γρ. antletērion] is from a
ceramic, drawing diakara [γρ. through a long] handle. A kyathos has one ounce;
and that of oil one ounce and a half; and that of wine one ounce and a half
ʹ; and that of honey one ounce and a half. The great gomor and the [ἴσ. also
the] lethek; and lethek, as it is said in the prophet Hosea [3, 2]: "I hired
for myself a leketh [γρ. lethek] of barley." And in other copies it says: "A
gomor of barley." So it is the same; for fifteen modii are signified." A
drachma and three grammata. A drax of flour. A drax is the grasp of a hand.
The great ein of eighteen xestai, and the holy one of nine xestai. A kabos of
wheat of five xestai and a half. A metrites [γρ. metretes] of seventy-two
xestai. {1 Concerning weights.}1 Silver pieces and the bronze ones weigh
three grammata. Perfect silver of one hundred litrai. An argyrous and a mna
and manesongkiai [ἀνάγν. ounces of a manes] twenty, which becomes one litra,
eight ounces. An amphoreus of twenty-four xestai. An assarion, a weight of six
ʹ grammata. An assarion is called of sixty lepta. A bag which is also a pholis
[γρ. phollis], of two hundred fifty denarii, becomes three hundred twelve
litrai and a half. A denarius weighs one litra, three ounces. A holy didrachm
of six grammata. A kodrantes, a nummus carrying six ʹ grammata. A lepton saton
carries three grammata. A lepton is a little weight, the fourth of an ounce,
concerning which the Lord says [Matt. 10, 29]: "Are not two sparrows sold for
an assarion?" And assar in Hebrew, being diminished, is so called. A
miliarision and a golden and a half of the silver carries ten ounces. A
nomisma of one hundred twenty-five litrai. Another nomisma of three grammata.
Nummon [γρ. Nummus]. Numa a certain king of the Romans made it, and it was
called after his name. An obol that is not of silver, but made from iron,
carries three grammata. An obol of silver carries three grammata. A holke
carries three grammata. The holy shekel, which is twelve grammata. A stater
carries twelve grammata. A pholis and a bag [γρ. Phollis] carries two argyroi
and a half, two hundred fifty denarii, twelve litrai and a half. I found the
holy shekel in the Numbers [18, 16] thus: "You shall redeem, he says, the
firstborn of five shekels according to the holy shekel, they are twenty
obols." An oxybaphon has eight xestai. A cheme has forty-eight xestai. A
cochliarion has seventy-two xestai. He shows the shekel of four obols. The
obol carries three grammata. So then the holy shekel is of twelve grammata,
and the royal of ʹ grammata. The denarius was of sixty assaria. And the
argyrous denarii. And the argyrous was struck from the beginning as a
nomisma, and it came from the Assyrians. And Abraham into the land of Canaan,
he says [ἴσ. they say], has brought this type. A drachma which has three
grammata. The hair of Absalom they weighed in this shekel at the time when he
was shearing his head, one hundred twenty-five shekels. So then it is thirty
ounces and 133 one, ʹ grammata; by the royal shekel they become one litra
three grammata. The holy shekel is of four obols, of ten grammata; and the
obol carries three grammata. And the weight they used at times to strike in
silver pieces; and it was not of silver? but made, but from iron; so an obol;
this was it. A mna instead of the mane; for in Hebrew the silver is called
mane; and the Italian mna is forty; it is a stater [γρ. it is of forty
staters], that is of twenty ounces, of one litra eight ounces.
