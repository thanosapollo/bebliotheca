Homily III.
On Almsgiving, and on the Rich Man and Lazarus.
=64.433=
1. Great is the benefaction of almsgiving. Nothing else is equal to
almsgiving. No virtue has such power to wipe out sins. For this reason divine
Scripture also says: He who has mercy on the poor lends to God. If you lend
gold to a man, he binds with the wounds and with the writings. But with God it
is not so. You give to the martyrs' shrines, to the poor, and God summons to
repay the loan. What you lent to the poor man, he hastens to loose the surety,
and he does not simply repay, but he repays with much blessing, saying: Come,
you blessed of my Father; inherit the kingdom prepared for you from the
foundation of the world. If you lend to a man, there is never a time when he
repays the loan, nor does he even wish to see your face; God, having borrowed
in the place of those who remain at the martyrs' shrines, and of the poor,
does not repay the loan and send you away, but he even makes the lenders
co-heirs, saying: Come, you blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared
for you from the foundation of the world. When did the Lord prepare it? When?
When you were bringing fruit in the churches and in the martyrs' shrines, when
you were celebrating the memorials of the saints, when you were lending bread
to the poor, =64.436= when you were receiving the poor into your house, when
you were putting a garment around the poor, when you were visiting the sick
and those in prison, when you were doing these things, I was preparing the
kingdom.
2. And see the greatness of almsgiving, that no virtue is ranked with it. For
he did not say that You kept virginity for my sake; he said none of these
things. I was hungry, and you gave me to eat. Great also is virginity, and
fasting, and the remaining virtues; but they fall short of almsgiving. The
five foolish virgins also kept virginity; but since they did not have
almsgiving, they remained outside the bridechamber. For the five wise,
together with virginity, also had almsgiving; therefore they entered into the
bridechamber. But the five foolish kept virginity only, expecting through that
to enter into the kingdom of heaven, and they acquired nothing else, neither
almsgiving, nor anything other. Therefore the Lord also says to them: Depart
from me, I do not know you. Therefore they were also called foolish, because
they kept virginity only, and did not do almsgiving.
3. For just as if someone lights a lamp, and does not put in the oil, it is
quenched; so also if he acquires virginity, and does not feed the poor man
with the oil of almsgiving, he has gained nothing. Great is the business of
almsgiving. If you desire to buy a garment or oxen, you take expenses and
gold, and you go to a fair, and being burned by the heat you sometimes hunger
and thirst. And enduring all these things, you can scarcely attain the aim;
and when you succeed, the seller will not give as you wish, but as he himself
is pleased. You swear, I will not buy for so much; he swears, I will not sell
for so much. And between you there comes about the first sin, as Solomon said,
that: Between selling and buying sin will arise. For of necessity one must
swear falsely, or both. For often neither the buyer stops at one word, nor the
seller. And when with so much toil you acquire what you desire, you do not
have security. For often before it reaches your house, it has either died, or
been lost, or been found worthless, and the toil becomes double for you. But
in the kingdom of God you suffer nothing of this kind: not the toil of travel
and the faintness of burning heat, not the grief of failure; none of these.
But while you sit inside in the house, the poor man comes selling paradise,
and he says: Give bread, and receive paradise; give a worn garment, and
receive the kingdom of heaven. And I do not tell you for how much, lest you
delay, taking poverty as a pretext. Buy paradise for as much as you will. Give
bread. You have no bread? Give an obol, give a cup of cold water. Give what
you will, what you have; I receive all things. Only buy paradise. Imitate that
=64.437= wonderful widow, who gave a handful of flour, and inherited the
kingdom of heaven. And again another widow, the one mentioned in the Gospel,
having nothing else except two mites only, and she cast those into the
treasury, and bought paradise.
4. The one who sells paradise does not traffic in money, but a merciful soul
and a well-disposed heart. Give to the poor man, and acquire God as debtor. He
who has mercy on the poor lends to God. And when, he says, does the one who
borrowed repay? When? When you leave behind the money, the houses, the gold,
the clothing, all the rest, and you go away naked, having left all things
behind; then the one who borrowed repays with thanksgiving. Send your money on
ahead there, and then go yourself. Do not wait for the gold, do not keep it.
Life is uncertain; you do not know when you go out of life. For as a thief in
the night, so death comes. At an hour you do not know and do not expect, you
go; and your money stays here and there you desire a drop of water, that it
may wet your lips, and others here are dividing your living.
5. So also that rich man who was in the fire desired a drop of water, and he
was begging Abraham, saying: Father Abraham, have mercy on me, and send
Lazarus, that with his little finger he may wet my lips, for I am in pain in
this flame. This rich man, when he was on the earth, was clothed in purple,
fine linen and silks; and he went forth carried by a chariot; his horses were
very white, adorned with gold and silver, and their legs set with gold.
Servants running before and following, wearing gold collars, bracelets and
necklaces, and in a word he went forth in great pomp. And at his dinner gold
without measure was serving, and silver beyond telling, costly wine,
pheasants, geese, purple gallinules, partridges, doves, fowl, hares, lambs,
kids, a crowd of cooks making an uproar; others fanning, lest even one speck
of ash settle on his head; others holding silver basins, and clean linens,
stood ready, wiping the tips of his fingers, while he reclined. And see the
inhumanity and the shame.
6. All were toiling; bloody-handed labors, such an uproar, that they might
fill the belly of that one rich man. And after it had been filled with the
costly foods, ivory couches are also prepared for him, goose-down, clean and
soft linens. And since he could not fall asleep because of the heaviness of
his head, others rub his feet, others take care of the house, guarding the
doors and the windows, lest some voice, lest some noises =64.440= wake him.
Such was the pomp of the rich man.
7. And there was a poor man named Lazarus, and he had been laid at the gate of
the rich man, full of sores, and he desired to be filled from the crumbs that
fell from the table of the rich man, and no one gave to him. For the rich man
saw him, and turned away his face, he loathed him, he spat, he ran past, or
rather he looked past him. The wretched man did not say, This man is a human
being as I also am. The same hand created both, he is of my own race and of my
own kind. He did not bend his reasoning, he was not softened toward
compassion. He did not have mercy on the man laid in such sickness at his
gate. For the dogs, coming and seeing him lying thus, and the discharge
flowing around, even of his limbs, showing much compassion, coming like some
sponge they wiped the discharge from his limbs with the tongue, licking
around, as some excellent physicians healing the sores, gaping with much care,
taking exact precaution lest somehow the venom of the teeth arrive and harm
anything of the righteous man. O the cruelty of the rich man! The dogs put
forward the physician's art upon the sick man; those who did not know God, nor
expected a resurrection, were hastening to the healing of the poor man. And
the rich man, who expected to stand at the fearful judgment, did not have
mercy on the poor man.
8. Did you see the rich man's glory here, the luxury? Did you see the poor
man's appearance of little account, and the cheapness of wealth for a little
while? That one was glorious, this one without honor; that one living in
softness, this one laid in sickness. That one living in such wealth did not
remember God; this one laid in such sickness did not blaspheme God. Come then,
let us see also in that age the end of both.
9. It came to pass, he says, that the rich man died, and was carried away into
eternal punishment. And Lazarus also died, and was carried away by the angels
into the bosom of Abraham. And being in the fire the rich man saw Lazarus
resting in the bosom of Abraham, and he recognized him. And he cried out,
saying: Father Abraham, have mercy on me, and send Lazarus, that with his
little finger he may wet my lips, for I am in pain in this flame. Where is the
wealth? where the gold, where the silver? where the silver roof? where the
pomp of the servants? where the wine being poured out, and now you seek and
desire a drop of water? Have mercy on me, father Abraham? O wretched rich man!
When Lazarus lay at your gate, you did not wish to recognize him; and now you
desire Lazarus for =64.441= help? Have mercy on me: your supplication is now
useless. The time of mercy has passed. There, then, there is no mercy. For
judgment is without mercy to him who has not done mercy. Why do you desire
mercy, which you did not acquire on earth? Have mercy on me, father Abraham,
and send Lazarus, that with his little finger he may wet my lips, for I am in
pain in this flame. With what finger, O rich man? the one you did not deign to
have eat at your table? Then you did not wish to look upon his hands, but you
loathed them, and now you beg that they touch your tongue? Have mercy on me,
father Abraham. How do you call him father, not having done the things of a
son? He is father of those who live in the light. There is no agreement of
light with darkness. Do not call him father. For you, according to your
merciless manner, are a son of darkness and of Gehenna. And how do you call
him father, not having had mercy on his son Lazarus?
10. But this great patriarch did not yield to him because of the pain; he did
not add pain, he did not strike him with words; he did not deny human nature.
But with a gentle voice and cheerful in face he answered, saying: Child, you
received your good things on the earth, and Lazarus the evil things. Therefore
this one rests here, and you are in pain. Nevertheless there is a great gulf
between you and us, and no one can cross it from us.
11. Did you see the end of the rich man? did you see how temporary luxury is?
Do not wait to give your things to the poor. Never keep them for tomorrow. For
you do not know what the coming day will bring forth. Why do you heap up
money, which in a little while you leave behind unwilling, and you go away
grieving, and there you desire a drop of water, and others here, dividing your
wealth, make mention of your name in this way. For often even your heirs dig
up the bones, cursing you. Send your money on ahead into the age there that
has no end. The innkeeper there provides the carrying for you free of charge.
Here if you wish to transfer your wealth from city to another city, you hire
cattle, hired camels, mules, sword-bearing guards on the road, lest some
villains fall upon you and plunder your wealth. But into that world, if you
wish to transfer your wealth, you neither suffer toil, nor spend hire, nor
suffer any spite, nor do you even go out of the house. But you sit in the city
in much rest, and the poor man comes to your door, seeking to transfer your
wealth into the age there that has no end. And you do not yield to him of your
own purpose even one obol? Look up, O man, if you wish to have wealth without
end. Give into the hands of the poor, and you will find =64.444= hospitality
being made for you there. Do not fear that the poor are without means; the
surety is rich and well-disposed. For he who has mercy on the poor lends to
God.
12. Let us fear that day, on which the master of the house calls the laborers
to the sowing of the wheat, and he will gather the wheat into the storehouses,
but the chaff he will burn with unquenchable fire. For the master of the house
is the Lord, and the laborers are the angels, who gather the world to the
judgment in that hour and day. And they will gather the righteous into the
eternal tents, which the Lord who was taken up prepared, but the sinners into
the eternal fire. Let us therefore hasten to be found ripe wheat through
almsgiving, that we may enter into the eternal tents. With whom to the Father,
together with the Son and the Holy Spirit, be glory, power, honor and worship
unto the ages of ages. Amen.
