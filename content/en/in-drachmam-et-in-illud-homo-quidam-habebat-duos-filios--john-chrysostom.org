* On the Drachma and on That Saying, A Certain Man Had Two Sons

On the Gospel according to Luke, on the drachma, and on the saying, "A
certain man had two sons."
=61.781=
Again the bosom of Christ puts forth an unceasing stream, a honeyed flood of
love; again the lamp-bearing wisdom of Christ, having kindled a lamp and set
it upon the lampstand of the cross, torches the whole inhabited world toward
piety. Using this lamp, the wisdom of God, having sought the one of the
drachmas that was lost, and having found it, joined it to the nine drachmas
of the angels. And who the woman who has the ten drachmas might be, it is
necessary, beloved, to say. She is the wisdom of God, having the ten
drachmas. Which? Count them: angels, archangels, Principalities,
Authorities, Powers, Thrones, Dominions, Cherubim, Seraphim, and Adam the
first-formed. This drachma of Adam, grazed upon by the devil's wickedness,
and dragged down into this depth of life, and buried under the many-voiced
pleasures of sins, the wisdom of God came and found again. How did she find
it, beloved? She came from the heavens, takes the earthen lamp of the body,
kindles it with the light of the Godhead, sets it upon the lampstand of the
cross, seeks the drachma, and folded it into the court and pasture of the
angels. But perhaps some of those standing here, who have sinned after
baptism, will say: What is that to us, if Adam was saved? How shall we be
shown mercy? How shall we be saved, since after baptism we have broken the
law? To whom we say: You of this sort, do not fear, do not be startled, do
not be troubled, do not lose heart; for it is not that God cared for Adam
and despised you; but if for him there came a woman having ten drachmas, for
you there comes a compassionate father, having two sons, and taking the one
who had wandered into his arms again.
For he became all things to all, that he might gain all. Just now we heard
the Gospel saying: A certain man had two sons; and the younger of them came
to the father and said: Father, give me the substance. And he divided to
them the living. And not many days later the younger son, having gathered
all, went away into a far country; and there he wasted his living, living
prodigally. And when he had spent all, a mighty famine arose in that
country; and he went to one of the citizens, and the citizen sends him into
the fields to feed swine. And he longed to fill his belly from the husks
which the swine were eating; and no one gave to him. And coming to himself
he said: How many hired servants now of my father have bread enough and to
spare, and I perish here with hunger? I will arise and go to my father; and
I will say to him: Father, I have sinned against heaven, and before you, and
I am no longer worthy to be called your son; make me as one of your hired
servants. And the sense of this one must not be sought outside, but in
ourselves. For the parables have been spoken for us, and they are read for
us, that no one may despair of himself, even if he happens to be a great
sinner; but that he may come to God repenting and seeking propitiation from
him, especially if any of you has become a son of God through faith in the
Lord. For as many as received him, it says, he gave them authority to become
children of God.
If then any of you has become his son through faith in our Lord, and has
gone far from him, not in place, but in manner; if the famine has touched
anyone, not of the common bread, but of the heavenly (For bread of heaven,
it says, he gave them; man ate the bread of angels): let him leave behind
the wanton pleasures that are like swine, and no lon =61.782= ger desire to
eat husks. What are the husks? The fruit of a tree. The husks have been
likened to sin; for just as the husks at the beginning of the tasting are
sweet, but after the tasting roughen the throat; so also sin at the
beginning of the tasting is sweet, but after the tasting is harsh. And this
is what is said by the wise Solomon: Do not attend to a worthless woman. For
honey drips from the lips of a harlot woman, who for a time oils your
throat, but afterward is found more bitter than gall, and sharper than a
two-edged sword. If the famine of the heavenly bread has touched anyone, let
him come, let him seek. For everyone who seeks finds, and the one who asks
receives, and to the one who knocks it shall be opened. Knock at the door of
repentance with your unspoken groanings; do not fear it, the door is not a
stranger to you; he died for you. And if you say in yourself: Who is it?
Christ answers you and says: I am the door of the sheep. Say you also: I
have sinned against heaven and before you. If anyone, having received the
heavenly baptism, has sinned, he has sinned against heaven. For the one who
sins before baptism sins on earth; For you are earth, and to earth you shall
go, was said to Adam. But if anyone has put on Christ in baptism, he is no
longer earth, but heaven; For as is the one of dust, such also are those of
dust; and as is the heavenly one, such also are the heavenly. For as many of
you as were baptized into Christ have put on Christ. If then anyone has
sinned after baptism, he has sinned against heaven. Therefore let him come
and say: Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you, I am no longer
worthy to be called your son.
Why? Speak the cause. The heart of mine that was sprinkled with the blood of
Jesus, blossoming rose-like, I have fouled with pleasures; I am no longer
worthy to be called your son. I have provoked you, I have embittered your
Holy Spirit, I have stained the flashing robe of faith with sin; I am no
longer worthy to be called your son. My soul that was set free by baptism,
and made snow-white by illumination, having darkened it with sin, I have
enslaved; I am no longer worthy to be called your son; make me as one of
your hired servants. The senses of my body, drunk with revelry, like frantic
maidservants, have betrayed their own mistress, the soul, to wantonness; but
now, having disciplined these through fasting, I will bring my soul to
temperance; Make me as one of your hired servants. I know now the wandering
of the Enemy, I know his trip-ups, I know the missile-store of his gluttony,
I flee revelry, through which he looses the sinews of temperance, I pursue
the fasting that grants me sobriety; Make me as one of your hired servants.
I too will work in your vineyard; I too will cut away with the sickle of the
Spirit the superfluous shoots of the rational vine that lies within me, or
rather the accusations; Make me as one of your hired servants. I too will
cultivate the sweetest cluster of love; I too will prop up, like stakes, the
commandments of the Lord for my soul, that it may not, being dragged to the
earth, be plundered by a serpent; Make me as one of your hired servants. I
will work to make it look toward the heavens; I will prepare it to stretch
out tendrils of brotherly love, that embracing the poor who are dragged upon
the earth it may bear them up; Make me as one of your hired servants.
Give me again, father, the plow of the cross upon my shoulders; I will
til =61.783= l the furrow of my soul, I will take out the thorn-bearing and
harm-making sprouts of the Evil One, I will sow upon it the heavenly seeds.
For when you rain down mercy from above, the furrow of my soul will bear
fruit, some thirty, some sixty, some a hundred; Make me as one of your hired
servants. Do not thrust me away, father; for the Enemy has not altogether
unnerved me and stripped off the robe of faith. For if he stained the beauty
of my temperance, yet he never bent me to worship his idols; I have not
denied you, my Lord. Even if I sinned in the prime of my youth, yet I
trampled the steam of idols, I overthrew images of demons, I put carved
idols to shame, I blew upon the devil, I spat upon demons. But now I fall at
your footsteps, my Lord, and my God; Make me as one of your hired servants.
If you repent thus, if you confess thus: while you are still far off the
heavenly Father runs to you, falling in spirit upon the neck of your soul,
laying the yoke of grace upon you, and binding the neck of your heart with
the collar of truth; with a kiss he looses your enmity, and says to his own
servants the priests: Bring the first robe. He did not say, Bring a robe,
but, Bring the first robe, that he might show the first dignity of baptism
=61.784= being given to you. Bring him the first robe, and clothe him; and
give a ring, the token of the wealth, the keeper of the treasure, the
earnest of grace, the seal of the Spirit.
And sandals for his feet; that is, With the words of the Gospel bind the
track of his soul; lest the serpent again lick under the heel and drag him
down into the former wandering of sin. And bring the fatted calf. Who is
this, if not altogether Christ, given after baptism to the worthy, as the
Apostle says: In whom dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily? Bring
it, that having eaten we may make merry. Who then among Jews and Greeks will
believe that a calf was slain, and the world was set up? O calf of a Virgin
heifer, holy child of an untilled womb! O calf that was slain and lives, and
is always feasted upon by us, and remains unconsumed!
Therefore, beloved, having let down the anchor of our word into the steady
and fair-weather harbor, let us send you on with the embarkation of the Holy
Spirit, and cutting through the sea of the divine Scriptures with his
well-measured tongue, he will lead you by the hand into the heavenly city in
Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory and the might, together with the
Father, with the holy and worshiped Spirit, now and always, and unto the
ages of ages. Amen.
