OF GREGORY BISHOP OF NYSSA AGAINST ARIUS AND SABELLIUS
My heart has belched forth a good word, the psalmist was just now saying. This
good Word of God those who according to Arius and Achilles send forth the most
impious voice dared to proclaim a creature and a work, and they have drawn
many to their own error; but those who according to Sabellius think the
opposite of these attempt to abolish the hypostasis of the Son, and thinking
to honor the Father himself, who is one, with two names, they address him as
Son-Father. And these two opinions, each of them, have been set like two
snares by those just named; and a certain middle way, narrow and afflicted,
passes through, the one that leads to life according to the Gospel. From this
the many of men, turning aside, become prey of the other; but you seem to me
to have feared the falls of both and to flee even the way of truth itself,
thinking that one must not take in hand a word concerning God. See then that,
being wary of the error of the two, you do not incur a penalty of impiety. The
Savior himself, standing before the true way, has cried saying: I am the way
and the truth; do not turn aside to the right or to the left, so that you may
meet with neither of them. And again: I am the door of the sheep; the one who
does not enter through me, but climbs up from elsewhere, is a thief and a
robber, who also, caught by a snare, will perish. Let those then who have
turned their mind toward the error of both hear, that by nature our Savior
Jesus 3,=1.72= Christ is and is addressed as Son of God, and is not a work
named Son by usage as we are, and that he does not have a beginning, but
is eternal; for this reason also in unending ages he will co-reign with the
Father according to his own hypostasis. But perhaps someone might say:
everything without beginning would also be unbegotten; how would the Son
reasonably be addressed as without beginning, whose very name has an
indication of a beginning? For son is named from having been begotten. And
that begetting has fallen under a beginning, no one would deny; if then he
happens to be a son, it is shown to us from the begetting that he has taken a
beginning. And perhaps he will also have an end, someone might say according
to the philosophical word; and the Apostle also says that he will be subjected
to the one who subjected all things to him and that he will hand over the
kingdom to the Father and God, so that it has been supposed that he, not
existing before being begotten, will again proceed into not being. Ascend for
me upon the secure faith, lest you be snatched, as by a violent blast, by the
deceit of such reasonings, as chaff which the wind casts out, from the narrow
and straight way upon pits of destruction. For if the Father of all is
unbegotten and eternal, both wise and powerful, and the Apostle, evangelizing
the faith toward him, proclaims to all Christ the power of God and the wisdom
of God, how would they dare to bring his begetting under time and suppose him
neither eternal nor without beginning? For if according to the supposition of
some the begetting of our Savior has fallen under a portion of time, still not
only the Son has been dishonored but also the Father, according to the voice
of the Son: You know neither me nor the Father; The one who does not honor the
Son does not honor the Father, the one as not existing before coming to be,
the other as not having power and wisdom before the Son exists. And such
people would consequently suppose that the Father was not even God at some
time before the coming-to-be of the Son; 3,=1.73= for how would he be God, if
he did not possess power and wisdom? But if he is always being God, as has
been decreed for us by the divine scriptures, then he is to be believed as
always having wisdom and power, that is, Christ, with himself. And I remember
having promised before to show you as through a mirror the image of the things
concerning the Father and the Son, since to human word the things according to
the


divine nature have not been subjected. For when God himself † has not spoken
to us according to the proper power of his magnitude, but measures his own
voice to human hearings, so that we may be able with our hearings to receive
the divine voice. For indeed he also measures his unbearable and
incomprehensible magnitude to the sights of men, and being God he has appeared
as man; for he emptied himself, taking the form of a slave, so that we humans
might be able to behold God. As therefore we have been persuaded that, not
being man, he appeared as man, but by nature he was and is God, having
circumscribed his incomprehensible magnitude with a body and through himself
as through a mirror having shown us the whole magnitude of God, so that using
this as a spyglass we may also become beholders of the Father; for being the
image of the invisible God according to the Apostle he has said: The one who
has seen me has seen the Father, so also through the things said in the
scriptures according to the measure of our hearings, which in many ways are
not fitting to the divinity because of weakness of word, we analogously behold
him with the mind; for as God circumscribed by a body has been seen by us
because of the sights, so also the word has been proclaimed smaller than
according to God because of the hearings. And it has been shown, as we said
before, through the scripture itself that a human word would not admit God and
the things according to him. For since there was not left a part of word that
indicates the eternity of the Son, by the preposition as by a riddle 3,=1.74=
the scripture signifies that he is contemporaneous with the Father, saying:
Before the morning star I begot you; and Before all hills he begets me and of
each created thing. Setting forth this part of the word, the scripture shows
him without beginning; for that which is pre-transcendent of all things, and
especially when it says before the ages, turns our mind toward the eternal and
without beginning. How then, always sitting with the Father, he has been
permitted to be Son and to have been begotten, I hold out to you an image of
these things, since the word does not reach the things according to nature:
the ray from the sun. How would you not say that the light also has been born
from the lamp? But as the sun is not first, then the ray, but together with
the sun's being or having come to be the ray is and has come to be, and
together with the lamp's existing the light also existed, and that which is
begotten by the lamp appears not after some time, but being begotten with it,
so God is not first, and afterward his wisdom, but together with the God of
all being (and he was always) there was also the wisdom, that is, Christ; and
yet being begotten by God, the wisdom of the one who exhibits her is
reasonably addressed as offspring. And the Before all hills he begets me
indicates the Savior as always being begotten for humans by the Father; and
the Today I have begotten you, although he has been begotten beforehand and is
always together with him, the scripture, bringing the today under a portion of
time, signifies that he is co-begotten always with those who proceed in
succession, until that which is called today. And if ever the today of this
world should be co-bounded with the ends, he is no longer begotten, but having
been begotten once for all and seated as judge and adjudicator of living and
dead of all, he will reign unto an age with the Father. And so that the The
Lord created me a beginning of his ways 3,=1.75= for his works may not also
implant reasonings in those whose mind is corrupted, this too must not be
passed over. For the translators, having turned aside a little from the Hebrew
exactness of the scriptures into the Greek, said created me, but you would
find, if you searched, made me. Nevertheless even the created me, exactly
understood, is shown as nothing discordant from the made me. For if he had
said only The Lord created me, he would have left a question for some; but
since he added a beginning of his ways for his works, it would be clearly
understood as having said the Lord made me to oversee his works and entrusted
to me a beginning of his ways. For when did the Father have a beginning of his
ways, or when was he not active, or from whom did he receive the being able
and the working? For not always having all things with himself is indicative


of having received from some other, so that some other god older than him is
understood. And Who has given first and it shall be repaid to him? says the
scripture. And if all things are from him, then a beginning of his ways and of
his works will never be found, but he is always active. And if all things came
to be through the Son and without him not even one thing came to be, then it
is openly shown that he always has with himself the working wisdom. And if the
Father always works, and without the Son he has worked nothing, they have
supposed in vain that he was created and at some later time was created, so
that he might work. But the He created me a beginning of his ways signifies
that he entrusted his ways to me, so that they might be under my rule; for he
does not say a temporal beginning, but the ruling one. For the apostles also
say that he made him both Lord and Christ. And if the he made should be named
a work and a creature, there would be two whom he made, one Lord, another
Christ. And that Christ is addressed as king, this is unknown to no one; but
the he made signifies that two rules have been entrusted to him from the
Father, the lordly and the kingly, or rather one rule 3,=1.76= honored with
two appellations, with more. Let both then be persuaded, those who have been
caught in both errors, that always with the Father the Son is both begotten
and without beginning; without beginning in that he is always together with
the Father, begotten in that he has had the Father as cause of being; as we
said concerning the lamp, saying that it is itself cause of the light, yet not
prior to the light, but happens to be contemporaneous; so also he will be unto
the age and will never cease reigning, since he did not even begin, but having
always co-worked with the Father he co-indicates himself through having said:
My Father works until now and I work; and the until now seeks the from which;
and since the from which happens to be in time, having omitted the from which
and said only the until now he indicated that all things have always been
co-worked by him; for he works until now, causing to grow, giving life,
increasing, driving all things as with reins, and he will never cease; for it
will not fit God to be idle, nor will he grow weary working, so as to cease. I
think then that those of Arian error have used as an advocacy the evangelical
voice that says The one who sent me is greater than I, from which they have
supposed him a creature and a work, and the contradicting the one who
addressed him as good teacher, No one is good except one, God, has implanted
in them a suspicion that being a man he has had the name Son by catachresis,
and the No one knows the consummating day and the hour, neither the angels of
the heavens nor the Son in the things said according to Mark, except the
Father alone, I think implants in them a suspicion that the Son has become
alien to the paternal essence. But those according to Sabellius, I and the
Father are one and The one who has seen me has seen the Father 3,=1.77= and
the When he hands over the kingdom to the Father and God, having read without
exact judgment, have fallen out into the greatest error of impiety, thinking
that through a human desertion the Son has come forth from the Father for a
time, and again after the correction of human faults, having been dissolved,
has entered and been mixed back into the Father. Have you not also read, O
Sabellian-minded one, this passage of the apostolic scripture, which would
stand against your seeming? For he says after other things: According to the
working of the might of his strength, which he worked in Christ, having raised
him from the dead and seated him at his right hand in the heavenlies, above
every rule and authority and power and lordship and every name that is named,
not only in this age but also in the one to come. Does not even the
evangelical voice persuade you that the Son is not temporary, but that even
the judgment according to all in the age to come has been entrusted to the
Son? For the Father, he says, judges no one; but he has given all the judgment
to the Son, so that they may honor the Son as they honor the Father; and
somewhere else the Apostle In the revelation of the Lord Jesus, he says, from
heaven with angels of his power


in a flame of fire, giving vengeance to those who do not know God and to those
who do not obey the gospel of our Lord Jesus Christ. See how those who do not
obey will pay a penalty, he says, eternal destruction, when he comes to be
glorified in his saints. And they will pay it to the Son, whom you have
supposed no longer to be. And if you wish to know what in the world is the
When he hands over the kingdom to the Father and God, it must not be hidden
from you, even if you have happened to be hard to receive of the truth. And I
seem to myself to stand against the command from Christ Do not cast your
pearls before the swine, but having imitated God I will share in good hopes
and will not even from the impious 3,=1.78= hide the truth; for he rains
likewise and the light of day is stretched equally upon just and unjust. Then,
being thus loved as a human, will you not inquire the truth? Will you not
submit the hearings of the mind, so that you may be persuaded that there is
not the least opposition toward any other of the things said in the
scriptures, but that the whole happens to be consonant and in agreement with
the whole? Wherefore the Then he himself will be subjected to the one who
subjected all things to him must not be understood according to your
supposition; and When he hands over the kingdom to the Father and God. For if
in all things he was likened to humanity and suffered with us according to the
prophet: He himself bears our sins and is in pain concerning us and He himself
took our weaknesses and bore the diseases, since he has also been clothed with
the flesh that is receptive of these; and For us, he says, he made sin, not
having done it but as having done it, so that we might be justified; and
Having become a curse for us, so that he might redeem us from the curse, and
he has been named accursed, having chosen by sympathy to be synonymously named
with us; if having come to be in all these things he was likened to humanity,
having taken up the very form of the slave, as himself having transgressed,
putting forward his own person on behalf of humanity, he was turning the
Father toward mercy, calling upon him. And as all humanity had not been
subjected to the Son, he himself also as not subjected seemed and was taken,
inscribing the insubordination of the people in the sights of the Father; but
if ever all things should be subjected to the Son (for now we do not yet see
all things subjected to him), he will say: All things have been subjected to
me. And when all have been subjected to him, then he himself will appear
subjected to the one who subjected all things to him; for if we all should be
of Christ, Christ also would be of God and 3,=1.79= would be subjected. But if
we are not yet of Christ, neither is Christ yet of God, the one who is in pain
on our account. When therefore we all become of Christ, then Christ also will
be of God, subjecting through himself to the Father all whom he had formerly
received from him, So that God may be all in all. And since he seems somehow
not to have been subjected to the Father, of those guaranteed by him and of
those whose image he bears not yet having been subjected to him. And when all
things should be subjected to him, then he will appear handing over the
kingdom to the Father and God. And kingdom must not be understood as the
dignity of scepter-bearing; for the Father does not still need royal dignity,
so that he may receive from the Son what he had formerly conceded to him as
for use, recalling this. For such a thing you seem to me to hint. And further
you are impious not only toward the Son, abolishing his hypostasis, but also
toward the Father, showing him as not being king at some time, having conceded
the kingdom to the Son, then about to receive it back from him as needing the
dignity. And he will hand over to him not the dignity, as we said before, of
the kingdom, but those who have been ruled by him and subjected to him. Having
brought this kingdom and handed it over he will say: This is a kingdom, this a
priesthood, a holy nation, a people for a possession for me and Behold I and
the children whom God has given me; for he himself gave them to him, having
subjected all the nations to him; For I will give you nations as your
inheritance, and the ends of the earth as your possession. And know also that
the I and the Father are one does not abolish the hypostasis of the Son; for
they are one in essence, one in dignity, they are one in will, they are one in
mind, but they are not one in hypostasis.


And it will be shown also from the Apostle that the one is not of one and the
same hypostasis, as it is often arranged; for as upon the one who plants and
the one who waters 3,=1.80= but the name being portioned out divides the
hypostasis, each 3,=1.80= will receive his own wage according to his own
labor, so let the one be understood as not being abolishing of the other
hypostasis. Neither of these then having been shown to be discordant with each
other, but having agreed toward the straight faith, cease thinking the things
of Sabellius, turn aside from ill-mindedness, and do good, being persuaded by
the truth. These things alone are also sufficient for you toward a
demonstration of thinking base things. Know also you for me that you have been
unfortunate in the Arius-mad faith of Achilles. Why ever, having left the
straight way, have you driven upon the pits of the dishonor of the Son, having
suspected him a work and alien to the essence of God? Are not even the names
alone sufficient for you toward a demonstration of having been begotten from
the Father, that he has been addressed as wisdom and power of God? That wisdom
and power are unhandmade and unconstructed, but virtues of soul and as
exhalations of a mind rooted under the shoot of wisdom, you would altogether
nod along. But if the wisdom has been created and constructed in some time, as
you said, she who is oldest of all time and created thing, that is, the Son,
see that you may not seem to me to show as discordant from your opinion the
Without him not even one thing came to be. Or if you should say that this is
to be set forth, try also to show this openly to us, that there would be some
other wisdom older than this, through which the second has been constructed.
For without wisdom it is impossible for something to be constructed. For this
reason the wisdom is eternal with the Father and firstborn of all creation, so
that through her all things may be constructed. And the Son has also been
addressed as Word, so that he may be shown begotten and not supposed created;
for a word is born but is not created, being brought forth through a mouth but
being moved from a heart. The Father testifies to me saying: My 3,=1.81= heart
has belched forth a good word; the Son also agrees: I came forth from the
mouth of the Most High; how did my mind bear so great a burden of the daring,
having supposed him a work? You have heard of those who have gone before
concerning the Son; hear then also of the one who has now come upon us, that
the was, lying in the imperfect tense, has the signification of eternity: In
the beginning was the Word. And a beginning of God has not been grasped by
human word. For this reason then, as we have said in the things before,
whatever part of word the scripture has set, the in the beginning, understand
for me a divine beginning that is eternal. And the was is somehow significant
of the same. And the Word shows the consubstantial hypostasis toward the
Father; and so that we may not seem that the Son exists at some time, when he
was unmanifest, being hidden in the Father, and so that we may not suppose an
uttered word and an inner one (for such a word must be in some thought or in a
letter, not having hypostasis by itself), for these reasons John also has said
And the Word was not in God, but toward God, ascribing to the Word its own
hypostasis, existing from the paternal essence. In the same manner also, so
that the craftsmanlike wisdom may not be understood, which has its being in
something, either in a man or in a construction, he has said He was toward
God, so that he may be understood as God existing from God. And besides he has
also received the appellation: And the Word was God; and that God would not
have a beginning, since he would not be God; which not only we, but also
children of philosophers decree, than whom it is not fitting that you possess
lesser knowledge according to God. And so that we may believe him to exist as
God, being himself from himself, the Father formed him humanly, having
circumscribed him with flesh, so that he might manifest the Son to human
sights. And knowing him to exist as being God, do not for me imagine the Son
cheaper or smaller by the having said The Father who sent me is greater than
I. For it was necessary that he, becoming man, 3,=1.82= indicate his own
magnitude with humble-minded words. He himself has said: Let the neighbor
praise you and not your own mouth. For what? Was it not necessary that he,
being humble-minded, show himself lesser than the Father, of


the uncircumscribed and filling all things, him who for the time was being
circumscribed by flesh and measured by human sights? Nevertheless also to call
the cause of being greater than himself is not alien to truth. For if not God,
where is the wisdom? And if not the Father, where is the Son? But see for me
the Son in every way and altogether being humble-minded and at the same time
indicating the divine magnitude, first when he says to the young man who
addressed him as good: Why do you call me good? No one is good, except one,
God. And reasonably indeed he declared this; For as I have appeared to you as
a man, he might say, O young man, but you, having addressed me as a teacher,
as one of the scribes, added the good. And no one of humans is good, but to
God alone the appellation is proper. In vain then you named good him who for
the time has appeared to you as a man. Having thus declared, he indicated
humility to the man saying: No one is good, of those you have supposed; but
having added except one, God, himself and the Father being one, having
mystically then gone through the truth with humility, he showed himself
co-partaker with the Father of the goodness. Therefore the I and the Father
are one and the I in the Father and the Father in me shows him equal to the
Father. For the Son must be perfect from the perfect Father. But perhaps you
might also say this: If the Father being perfect fills all things, what sort
of place is left for the Son, being perfect, to occupy? But I say they have
become receptive and containing of one another; For I, he says, am in the
Father and the 3,=1.83= Father is in me; and those who contain one another
would be equal to one another in magnitude. How then they would come to be in
one another, you have altogether chosen to hear; for the one must be contained
by the other. The contained, at any rate, is in the containing; but the
containing would not again be contained by the contained. Do not think that
God has some emptiness in himself by which he has received the contained. For
this is to be supposed in the case of humans; for neither are we containing of
others, nor again do we occupy into another. And if we should occupy, we will
both contain and be comprehended by an emptiness. But in the case of God some
such thing must be understood: as if in a human soul two sciences or more
should come together at once, medicine and philosophy and as many others, they
are not cramped by one another in the space of mind and soul, the many in
number, having ample room and giving place to one another, and yet filling the
soul and not withdrawing one from the other, so as to furnish a phantasm of
one essence to the most skeptical of men, since they are seated upon one and
the same mind, but they have differed from one another, inasmuch as medicine
is one thing, philosophy another; so then also the Father and the Son, having
occupied according to the same place and having become receptive of one
another and being one, as we said a little before, by hypostasis alone and
appellation the one has differed from the other, and they happen to be in one
another. And as a scent of myrrh, having been diffused in air, both happening
according to the same space, by seeming have been co-diffused, but according
to truth you will find the air one thing, the scent another; and already also
light from the sun and blasts of winds, having grown through one another and
by seeming mixed, have much difference; for light and winds are not least
distant from one another; liken the divinity to these, and having thought
something greater than the demonstration through word you would find the two
3,=1.84= one according to essence and like-mindedness, and the one into Father
and Son, as we said before, formed by hypostasis and appellation. You will
altogether then also move this for me: If the Father has filled all things,
and again also the Son, what sort of place is left for the opposing power? For
to have the contrary with himself would not fit God. And if the ruler of evil
should happen to be somewhere, unless God also should be there, God will then
be shown circumscribed and partial. But I for my part say that he is
everywhere and there is not where God is not, even if the ruler of evil should
happen to be somewhere. For neither are the rays of the sun, upon filths and
gore


being carried, polluted, but rather they happen to be annihilating, drying
them with the heat; so also Our God is addressed as a consuming fire and Fire
will be kindled before him, so that for those dear to him he may be a lamp and
light, but for the enemies a consuming fire; For he will flame, he says,
around his enemies. Wherefore also the ruler of evil, wishing to flee the
fire-bearing bolts of God, runs through every place; and finding God
everywhere he passes through all humanity; and if he finds some boiling with
the spirit, he flees also the burning from them. And having found a descent in
someone who does not have the spirit of God, swept and adorned for a little,
having rested he will be consumed together with the one who has received him;
for likewise an enemy will be seen also the one who has received, making a
friend of the one who lifts himself against God. I think it sufficient toward
demonstration to go through even with these. Nor let this be left remaining
for me, having escaped notice, or even having sailed past by the impulse
toward the other things, as having been crowded by a violent blast, how
together with all things not even the Son knows the day, he who has the Father
in himself and is himself in the Father; but even 3,=1.85= this has an
indication of an excess of humility. For it was necessary that the Son of God
exceedingly humble himself, since he also received the rest of his way of life
most beautifully according to excess. And having said that he has not known,
he indicated being humble-minded to those who hear; and so that he may not
seem to be false, he said: Except the Father alone knows, having denied by the
word to know, but by the power of the riddle having confessed; for if the
Father alone knows, then the Son also knows, himself and the Father being one.
Two successes then have come about for humanity from this, both a hypothesis
of humility and ignorance of the consummation, through which life has been
compacted. And what would be harder to know, the day of the consummation or
God himself and Father? And if he knew the Father, having said: So that you
may know me, as the Father knows me and I know the Father, how would he not
have known the day, of which he himself also has become creator? For without
him nothing came to be. And the word, having been shown by these brief things
how it holds, let it not become a hindrance to anyone toward the straight
faith. You then, those according to Sabellius and Arius, who have come to be
in so great a difference, having recognized from the things said the whole
truth, come together with me toward friendship, and having come into the
between of both snares you will find the way of truth, having traveled which
you will see the eternal life.
