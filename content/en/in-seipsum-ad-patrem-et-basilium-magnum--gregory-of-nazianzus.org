ORATION X.
On himself, and to the father and Basil the Great, after the return from
flight.
A. Nothing is stronger than old age, and nothing more venerable than
friendship. By these I was led to you as a prisoner in Christ, bound not
with iron chains, but with the unbreakable bonds of the Spirit. Until then
I thought myself someone strong and unconquerable, and, O the folly! I
would not give the words even to these my lovers and brothers, so that I
might have leisure, and philosophize in quiet, leaving all things to those
who wished them, speaking to myself and to the Spirit. I was contemplating
Elijah's Carmel, and John's desert, and the otherworldliness of those who
philosophize thus; and I considered the present things a storm, and I was
seeking some rock, or cliff, or wall, under which I would be sheltered.
Let others, I said, have the honors and the toils, others the wars and the
victories; but let it suffice me, fleeing the wars, looking to myself, to
live thus as I may, as one crossing a small sea upon a slender raft; and
acquiring a small dwelling there by the poverty of the way of life here.
The reasoning is perhaps of humbler men, but at any rate of safer ones: to
keep equally far from height and from fall.
B. These things so long as it was still possible to write shadows and
dreams, and to feast the mind on vain fabrications; but now what?
Friendship presented me, and a father's gray hair subdued me; old age of
prudence, a term of life, a safer harbor, and friendship of one rich
toward God, and who makes rich. For already I dismiss the anger (let the
meek hear, and let them be glad); and I look mildly upon the hand that
tyrannized, and I smile upon the Spirit, and my heart is settled, and my
reasoning returns, and friendship, like some flame quenched and withered,
lives again from a small spark and is kindled. My soul refused to be
comforted, and my spirit was in despondency upon me. I said: I will no
longer add to trust friendship. And why should I hope in man? because
every man walks deceitfully, and every brother will trip his neighbor with
the heel, and we were all of the same =35.829= dust and lump, and we have
tasted of the same tree of wickedness; yet another has put forward another
tent, more seemly? and what profit to me, I said, of that friendship, the
zealous and celebrated, which began from the world and advanced to spirit?
What then of the one roof and table? or what of the common teachers and
lessons? what then of the blending beyond brothers, or of the genuine
later breathing-together, if not even so much was granted me as to remain
below in a time of power and height, when the contrary is pursued and
attained by the many, I mean to share in power, and to partake of the
prosperity of friends?
C. Why should I tell all the things of grief and of despondency, which I
call gloom, inventions of the mind? For these things there were, and
things more absurd than these; for I myself will accuse my own either
madness or folly. But now I take up and retune myself to things both much
truer than these and more fitting for us. And that you may know the
genuineness of our change, O admirable one, you not only dissolve the
silence which you blamed, and against which you cried out much, but you
also have the words as advocates. This already belongs purely to our
friendship, and to the Spirit in us. But what is the advocacy? and if I
err in anything, correct it yourself, as you are accustomed also in the
other things. You could not bear to make the spirit of friendship second;
since of the others perhaps we, but of us the Spirit is far more precious
to you. You could not bear that the talent be hidden in the earth and
buried; you could not bear that the lamp be covered for long by the
bushel, because you take this to be my light, and my work. You sought that
Barnabas also be added to your Paul; you sought that Titus also be joined
to Silvanus and Timothy, so that the gift might run for you through those
who genuinely care for you, and that you might fill the Gospel in a circle
from Jerusalem as far as Illyricum.
D. For this reason you bring me into the midst, and you take hold of me as
I withdraw, and you seat me beside yourself; this is my injustice, you
might say, and you make me a partner of the cares and of the crowns. For
this reason you anoint a high priest, and you clothe him with the long
robe, and you put on the mitre, and you lead him to the altar of the
spiritual whole-burnt-offering, and you sacrifice the =35.832= calf of
consecration, and you perfect the hands by the Spirit, and you bring him
into the Holy of Holies to behold, and you make him a minister of the true
tent, which the Lord pitched, and not man. But whether he is also worthy
of you who anoint, and of him for whom, and into whom, the anointing is,
this the Father of the true and really Christ knows, whom he anointed with
the oil of gladness beyond his fellows, having anointed the humanity with
the divinity, so as to make both one; and our God and Lord Jesus Christ
himself, through whom we have obtained the reconciliation, and the Holy
Spirit, who set us into this ministry, in which we also stand, and boast
in hope of the glory of our Lord Jesus Christ, to whom be the glory unto
the ages of ages. Amen.
