On the parable of the one who fell among robbers.
=62.755=
We have heard, brothers, in the Gospel of our Lord Jesus Christ saying that a
certain man was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho, and fell among robbers;
who also, having stripped him and laid blows upon him, left him half-dead. And
a priest and a Levite, passing along that road and seeing him, passed by on
the other side. But a certain Samaritan, coming to the place and seeing him,
was moved with compassion for him, and putting wine with oil and mixing them
he poured it on him, and bound up his wounds, and taking him up upon his own
beast he brought him to an inn, and gave the innkeeper two denarii, saying:
Take care of this man, and if you spend anything more, I will repay you when I
return. Let us then look at the parable of these things, so that,
understanding with a prudent heart, we may come to know the mysteries of God.
The man is Adam, Jerusalem is the heavenly citizenship and the mind, and
Jericho is the world. So long, then, as Adam before the disobedience had the
heavenly mind and the angel-like manner of life, he had unhindered entrance
into the heavenly city Jerusalem, and making his dwelling and sojourn in the
commandments of God, he was overcome by no one, nor was he wounded; but when
he disobeyed God, and did not keep the commandment, but was carried away by
the deceit of the serpent, then he went down to Jericho, that is, to the
earth, and worked the things of the earth; for Jerusalem is interpreted as
ascent, and Jericho as flood. He went down, then, as from Jerusalem to
Jericho, so from the heavenly commonwealth to the commonwealth of the devil's
deceit.
For when anyone keeps the commandments of God, then he has his citizenship in
the heavens, as the Apostle says, But our citizenship is in the heavens. He
went down from glory to dishonor, from a paradise of delight to a
thorn-bearing earth, from life to death; For in the day, he says, you eat of
the tree, you shall die by death; that is, by sin; for the death of the soul
is sin, to disobey God. He went down from the righteousness that
was in paradise, from the holiness that was in the heavens, and came to
Jericho, that is, to the fall of disobedience, to the death of sin. And he
falls among the robbers, that is, among the devil and the powers opposed to
him. And the road is this life, where Adam, walking, fell among the robbers,
and they strip him. And what did they strip from him? The robe of obedience,
the friendship with angels, the unstained glory, the sojourn with Christ, the
delight in paradise, the heavenly life. This robe they stripped from him. And
they laid blows upon him, that is, the sins: fornications, adulteries,
idolatries, sorceries, murders, envies, strifes, wrath, and all the remaining
working of evils. For these works scourge the man; these produce stench and
corruption.
And that this is so exactly, learn from David, how, depicting the wounds of
Adam =62.756= upon himself, he well calls them bruises: My bruises have become
foul and have rotted because of my folly. And every sin works a bruise
and a wound. He was wounded, then, through the disobedience, he was struck
through the lawless deeds, as the prophet says: I was struck as grass, and my
heart was dried up, because I forgot to eat my bread; that is, to keep the
commandment of God. And they left him, he says, half-dead, not as not wishing
to kill him, but as God not permitting it. For I do not will, he says, the
death of the sinner, as I will the repentance. And where do they leave him? On
the road, that is, in this life; for this age is called a road, because all
men pass through it. And the priest, he says, coming upon him on the road, and
seeing him, passed by on the other side. And by priest he means the blessed
Moses and Aaron. For David bears witness, saying, Moses and Aaron among his
priests, and Samuel among those who call upon his name. This wonderful Moses,
then, having become glorious, who punished the Egyptians with the tenfold
scourge, who split the Red Sea and dried it and led the people through it, who
sweetened the water at Marah, who conversed with God through a cloud, who
worked many wonders, traveling the road of this life, and seeing the man lying
wounded, passed him by, and did not raise him up. And likewise also a Levite,
that is, the prophetic order.
For they too, coming later after Moses, and walking the same road of life,
finding Adam a wounded man, did not raise him up. Neither Moses through the
law, nor prophets through signs, healed the wound of Adam. No one raised him,
no one granted him life, no one redeemed him from death, no one staunched the
wound of sin; for they themselves were held in sins. Even if through a
reverent manner of life they became friends of God, yet because they were of
the same flesh with Adam, and were borne from the dead root, they were not
able, being branches, to pull the root up from sin. But a certain Samaritan,
he says, earnest in works, compassionate in purpose, sympathetic toward his
fellow-servants, coming to the place, and seeing him wounded, was moved with
compassion, and put on oil and wine, and bound up his wounds, that is, this
man's sins. Our Lord Jesus Christ takes upon himself the person and image of a
Samaritan. But one of the hearers will say, What? Do you call the Lord a
Samaritan? Yes, I call him a Samaritan, not by the nature of the Godhead, but
by the imitation of compassion. For just as the Samaritan was like the others
in the nature of the body, but in the purpose of compassion was not like them,
but appeared better; so also the Lord, as man in the appearance of the body,
appeared like prophets, like patriarchs according to the man from =62.757=
Mary, but by the power of the Godhead he was shown greater than all; equal in
the human form, not equal in the glory above the world. Those, using neglect
and lack of compassion, passed by the wounded man without pity; but the
Samaritan appeared more compassionate and more pious and merciful. So also
Christ, when patriarchs and prophets overlooked the man who had fallen through
disobedience, alone appeared compassionate and merciful, according to the
prophet who says: The Lord is compassionate and merciful, long-suffering and
of much mercy;
and again, For you, Lord, are compassionate. And just as the Samaritan was not
of the nation of the Jews, but came from another country, so also Christ was
not from the world, but from heaven.
He came upon the earth: being God, he became man for us; being Master, he put
on the form of a servant. He was moved with compassion for us, he came down
from heaven upon the earth, he saw the man thrown down, torn, wounded in
fornications, idolatries, adulteries, murders; he saw, and was moved with
compassion for his own creature, and applied wine with oil; that is, mixing
both he made a soaking, and laid it on the man. What is it, mixing wine with
oil? That is, mixing the Godhead with the manhood, mixing compassion with
salvation, he saved the man. Mixing wine with oil, that is, mixing the Holy
Spirit with his blood, he made the man alive. For when the blood of the Lord
dripped from the side upon the earth, it washed away our bond of sins. And
what is it, He bound up his wounds? That is, he bound the devil, and loosed
the man; he bound the hull, and brought the shipwrecked vessel to life; he
bound and enslaved the powers of the evil one, and set the man free. And if
you wish to understand it in another way also, hear. Mixing wine with oil: oil
he brings on as the word of exhortation, and wine as the astringent, soaking
the teaching that gathers the scattered mind, according to what was said by
the Apostle: Reprove, rebuke, exhort. And he set him upon his own beast; that
is, Christ, taking the flesh upon the shoulders of his own Godhead, bore it up
to the Father into the heavens. For he did not bear up gold, or silver, or
precious stones, but he bore up the man according to the image from earth into
the heavens, into the great and wonderful and spacious inn, into this catholic
Church.
And he delivered him to the innkeeper, to the blessed Paul, the pillar of the
Christians, the genuine innkeeper, giving him two denarii; and through Paul to
the high priests and teachers and ministers in each Church; two denarii, the
Old and the New Testament, saying: Take care of this man, and whatever more
you spend, I when I come will repay you. And what he says is this: Take care,
he says, of the people =62.758= from the nations, whom I have delivered to you
in the Church. Since men are sick, wounded by sins, heal them, laying on
them a plaster, the prophetic sayings and the evangelical teachings, making
them sound through the admonitions and exhortations of the Old and New
Testament, persuading them to stand away from sin, and to leave behind the
deceit of the devil. And if even so they remain uncorrected, persuade them
from your own achievements, bend them through your austere words; become a
type for them, and a good pattern, in word, in work, in manner of life, in
faith, in love, in reverence, so that they may follow your type, and become
imitators of you in the good manner of life. And if you do this, and furnish
some addition of your own in words or works, whatever more you spend, I
returning will repay you; that is, at my second coming of recompense I will
repay you the wage worthy of your labors. For this reason Paul, taking courage
from these promises, says: And I most gladly will spend for Christ, and be
spent out for your souls; meaning the teaching toward the nations and the
ministry of the word. For he is the one building up and establishing the
Churches of God, and healing all men through the spiritual admonitions,
distributing to each what is fitting; he leads our souls into eternal life.
For to all, he says, I have become all things, that I may save all. This is
the good innkeeper of the Church: he receives all and takes care of all; he
does not thrust away a fornicator, nor a drunkard, nor a reviler, he does not
turn away from a plunderer, he does not abhor an idolater, he does not drive
off any other ungodly and unclean person, but he receives all. Just as a
physician washes the wounds, and through the bath of regeneration he scrubs
and cleanses, and offers the astringent sayings, as wine, so that they may not
be led by the sins, or rather vices, committed in ignorance.
And again he heals through exhortation, anointing our souls after the manner
of oil; for this man says: We exhort you, brothers, by the mercies of God, to
present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, well-pleasing, your reasonable
worship. As many of you, then, as are disciples of the words of Paul, let us
keep the commandments of Christ; lest we fall out of the heavenly Jerusalem
and the city of the living God.
And may it be that we, having been healed of the wounds of both soul and body,
stand before Christ in health and perfection of faith, sound and unfailing,
lacking in no good work, and enjoy the good promise in the heavens, by
the grace and love of mankind of our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom to the
Father be glory, together with the all-holy Spirit, now and always, and unto
the ages of ages. Amen.
