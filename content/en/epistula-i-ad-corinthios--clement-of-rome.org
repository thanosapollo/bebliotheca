Of holy Clement
bishop of Rome
the first letter to the Corinthians.
Written from the person of the church of the Romans.

CHAPTER 1.

The church of God that sojourns at Rome to the church of God that sojourns at
Corinth, to those called and made holy in the will of God through our Lord
Jesus Christ. Grace and peace from God almighty through Jesus Christ be
multiplied to you.

Because of the sudden and successive misfortunes and accidents that have come
upon us, we consider that we have been rather late in turning our attention to
the matters inquired into among you, beloved, and to the abominable and unholy
sedition, alien and foreign to the elect of God, which a few rash and
self-willed persons have kindled to such a pitch of madness that your
venerable and renowned name, worthy of love among all people, has been greatly
blasphemed.

For who that stayed among you did not prove your all-virtuous and firm faith?
Who did not admire your sober and gentle piety in Christ? Who did not proclaim
the magnificent character of your hospitality? Who did not bless your complete
and secure knowledge?

For you did all things without respect of persons and walked in the ordinances
of God, submitting to your leaders and rendering the fitting honor to the
elders among you; and you charged the young to think moderate and seemly
thoughts; and you instructed the women to accomplish all things in a blameless
and seemly and pure conscience, loving their own husbands as is due; and you
taught them, being in the rule of submission, to manage the affairs of the
house seemly, being wholly sober-minded.

CHAPTER 2.

And you were all humble-minded, boasting in nothing, submitting rather than
subjecting, more glad to give than to receive. Content with the provisions of
Christ and attending to his words, you had them carefully stored in your
inward parts, and his sufferings were before your eyes.

Thus a deep and rich peace was given to all, and an insatiable desire for
doing good, and a full outpouring of the Holy Spirit came upon all;

and being full of holy counsel, in good eagerness with pious confidence you
stretched out your hands toward God almighty, begging him to become gracious
if you had sinned unwillingly.

Your contest was day and night for the whole brotherhood, that the number of
his elect might be saved with mercy and conscience.

You were sincere and innocent and without remembrance of wrongs toward one
another.

Every sedition and every schism was abominable to you. You grieved over the
transgressions of your neighbors; you judged their shortcomings as your own.

You were without regret in every good work, "ready for every good work."

Adorned with an all-virtuous and venerable way of life you accomplished all
things in his fear; the commandments and the ordinances of the Lord were
written on the breadth of your heart.

CHAPTER 3.

All glory and broadening was given to you, and what is written was fulfilled:
"He ate and drank, and was broadened and grew fat, and the beloved kicked."

From this came jealousy and envy, strife and sedition, persecution and unrest,
war and captivity.

Thus "the dishonorable were raised up against the honorable," the inglorious
against the glorious, the foolish against the prudent, the young against the
elders.

For this reason righteousness and peace are far away, in that each has left
the fear of God and grown dim-sighted in his faith, and neither walks in the
ordinances of his commandments nor lives as a citizen according to what is due
to Christ, but each goes according to the desires of his own evil heart,
having taken up an unjust and impious jealousy, through which also "death
entered into the world."

CHAPTER 4.

For it is written thus: "And it came to pass after days that Cain brought from
the fruits of the earth a sacrifice to God, and Abel also brought from the
firstborn of the sheep and from their fat.

And God looked upon Abel and upon his gifts, but upon Cain and upon his
sacrifices he did not attend.

And Cain was exceedingly grieved, and

his face fell.

And God said to Cain: Why have you become very sorrowful, and why has your
face fallen? If you offer rightly but do not divide rightly, have you not
sinned?

Be still; his turning shall be to you, and you shall rule over him.

And Cain said to Abel his brother: Let us go through into the field. And it
came to pass, while they were in the field, that Cain rose up against Abel his
brother and killed him."

You see, brothers, jealousy and envy worked brother-murder.

Through jealousy our father Jacob ran from the face of Esau his brother.

Jealousy caused Joseph to be persecuted to death and to enter into slavery.

Jealousy compelled Moses to flee from the face of Pharaoh king of Egypt, when
he heard from his own kinsman: "Who appointed you a judge or a decider over
us? You do not wish to kill me, do you, in the way you killed the Egyptian
yesterday?"

Through jealousy Aaron and Miriam lodged outside the camp.

Jealousy brought Dathan and Abiram down alive into Hades, because they
seditiously rose against Moses the servant of God.

Through jealousy David incurred envy not only from the foreigners, but he was
also persecuted by Saul king of Israel.

CHAPTER 5.

But that we may cease from the ancient examples, let us come to the athletes
who have been nearest; let us take the noble examples of our own generation.

Through jealousy and envy the greatest and most righteous pillars were
persecuted and contended unto death.

Let us set before our eyes the good apostles.

Peter, who through unjust jealousy endured not one or two but many labors, and
having thus borne witness went to the place of glory that was due.

Through jealousy and strife Paul showed the prize of endurance; having worn
bonds seven times, having been driven out, having been stoned, having become a
herald in the east and in the west, he received the noble fame of his faith;

having taught righteousness to the whole world, and having come to the limit
of the west, and having borne witness before the rulers, he was thus released
from the world and went to the holy place, having become the greatest pattern
of patience.

CHAPTER 6.

To these men, who lived in a holy way, there was gathered a great multitude of
elect, who, having suffered many outrages and tortures through jealousy,
became a most fair example among us.

Through jealousy women, Danaids and Dircae, having suffered terrible and
unholy outrages, reached the firm course of the faith and received a noble
prize, they who were weak in body.

Jealousy alienated wives from husbands and altered what was said by our father
Adam: "This now is bone from my bones and flesh from my flesh."

Jealousy and strife overthrew great cities and rooted out great nations.

CHAPTER 7.

These things, beloved, we write not only admonishing you, but also reminding
ourselves; for we are in the same arena, and the same contest is laid upon us.

Therefore let us leave empty and vain cares and come to the glorious and
venerable rule of our tradition,

and let us see what is good and what is pleasant and what is acceptable before
the one who made us.

Let us look intently at the blood of Christ and know how precious it is to his
Father, because, poured out for our salvation, it brought the grace of
repentance to the whole world.

Let us go through all the generations and learn that in generation after
generation the Master gave a place of repentance to those who wished to turn
to him.

Noah preached repentance, and those who obeyed were saved.

Jonah preached overthrow to the Ninevites; but they, having repented of their
sins, propitiated God by their entreaty and received salvation, although they
were aliens from God.

CHAPTER 8.

The ministers of the grace of God spoke through the Holy

Spirit concerning repentance, and the Master of all things himself also spoke
concerning repentance with an oath: "For I live, says the Lord, I do not wish
the death of the sinner so much as his repentance," adding also a good
judgment: "Repent, house of Israel, from your lawlessness; I said to the sons
of my people: If your sins be from the earth to heaven, and if they be redder
than scarlet and blacker than sackcloth, and you turn to me from your whole
heart and say: Father, I will hear you as a holy people."

And in another place he says thus: "Wash and become clean, take away the
wickednesses from your souls before my eyes; cease from your wickednesses,
learn to do good, seek out judgment, rescue the one who is wronged, judge for
the orphan and justify the widow, and come and let us be tested, says the
Lord; and if your sins be as purple, I will whiten them as snow, and if they
be as scarlet, I will whiten them as wool; and if you are willing and hear me,
you shall eat the good things of the earth; but if you are not willing and do
not hear me, a sword shall eat you; for the mouth of the Lord has spoken these
things."

Wishing, then, that all his beloved should have a share in repentance, he
established it by his almighty will.

CHAPTER 9.

Therefore let us obey his magnificent and glorious will, and becoming
suppliants of his mercy and kindness let us fall down and turn to his
compassions, leaving behind vain labor and strife and the jealousy that leads
to death.

Let us look intently at those who have ministered perfectly to his magnificent
glory.

Let us take Enoch, who, being found righteous in obedience, was translated,
and his death was not found.

Noah, being found faithful, through his ministry preached a regeneration to
the world, and through him the Master saved the living creatures that entered
the ark in concord.

CHAPTER 10.

Abraham, who was called friend, was found faithful in that he became obedient
to the words of God.

This man through obedience went out from his land and from his kindred and
from the house of his father, so that, leaving a little land and a weak
kindred and a small house, he might inherit the promises of God. For he says
to him:

"Go from your land and from your kindred and from the house of your father
into the land that I will show you; and I will make you into a great nation
and I will bless you and I will magnify your name, and you shall be blessed;
and I will bless those who bless you and I will curse those who curse you, and
in you all the tribes of the earth shall be blessed."

And again, when he was separated from Lot, God said to him: "Having looked up
with your eyes, see from the place where you now are, toward the north and the
southwest and the east and the sea; for all the land that you see, I will give
it to you and to your seed forever.

And I will make your seed as the sand of the earth; if anyone is able to
number the sand of the earth, your seed also shall be numbered."

And again he says: "God led Abraham out and said to him: Look up to heaven and
count the stars, if you are able to number them; so shall your seed be. And
Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him for righteousness."

Through faith and hospitality a son was given him in old age, and through
obedience he offered him as a sacrifice to God toward one of the mountains
that he showed him.

CHAPTER 11.

Through hospitality and piety Lot was saved from Sodom, when the whole region
round about was judged by fire and brimstone, the Master making it clear that
he does not abandon those who hope in him, but sets those who turn aside to
punishment and outrage.

For when his wife went out with him, being of another mind and not in concord,
she was set for this as a sign, so that she became a pillar of salt until this
day, that it might be known to all that the double-minded and those who doubt
concerning the power of God become a judgment and a mark for all generations.

CHAPTER 12.

Through faith and hospitality Rahab the harlot was saved.

For when spies had been sent out by Joshua the son of Nun into Jericho, the
king of the land knew that they had come to spy out their country, and he sent
out men to seize them, that being seized they might be put to death.

So hospitable Rahab, having received them, hid them in the upper room under
the flax-stalks.

And when those from the king stood there and said: "The

spies of our land came in to you; bring them out, for the king so commands,"
she answered: "The men whom you seek did come in to me, but they went away at
once and are going on the road," pointing them the opposite way.

And she said to the men: "I know, I know that the Lord God is delivering this
land to you; for the fear and the trembling of you has fallen on those who
dwell in it. When it comes to pass, then, that you take it, save me and the
house of my father."

And they said to her: "It shall be so, as you have spoken to us. When you
know, then, that we are arriving, you shall gather all yours under your roof,
and they shall be saved; for as many as are found outside the house shall
perish."

And they further gave her a sign, that she should hang scarlet from her house,
making it clear that through the blood of the Lord there will be redemption
for all who believe and hope in God.

You see, beloved, that not only faith but also prophecy came to be in the
woman.

CHAPTER 13.

Let us therefore be humble-minded, brothers, putting away all boastfulness and
pride and folly and angers, and let us do what is written; for the Holy Spirit
says: "Let not the wise boast in his wisdom, nor the strong in his strength,
nor the rich in his wealth, but let him who boasts boast in the Lord, to seek
him out and to do judgment and righteousness"; remembering especially the
words of the Lord Jesus, which he spoke teaching gentleness and
long-suffering.

For thus he said: "Be merciful, that you may be shown mercy; forgive, that it
may be forgiven you; as you do, so it shall be done to you; as you give, so it
shall be given to you; as you judge, so you shall be judged; as you are kind,
so kindness shall be shown you; with the measure you measure, in it you shall
be measured."

With this commandment and these charges let us establish ourselves so as to
walk, being obedient to his words that befit the holy, being humble-minded;
for the holy word says:

"Upon whom shall I look, but upon the meek and quiet and the one who trembles
at my oracles?"

CHAPTER 14.

It is just, then, and holy, men and brothers, that we rather become obedient
to God than follow the leaders of abominable jealousy in boastfulness and

disorder.

For we shall suffer no ordinary harm, but rather great danger, if we rashly
give ourselves over to the wills of those people who hurl us into strife and
seditions, so as to alienate us from what is well.

Let us be kind to one another according to the compassion and sweetness of the
one who made us.

For it is written: "The kind shall be dwellers of the earth, and the harmless
shall be left upon it; but those who act lawlessly shall be utterly destroyed
from it."

And again he says: "I saw the ungodly highly exalted and lifted up as the
cedars of Lebanon; and I passed by, and behold, he was not, and I sought out
his place, and I did not find it. Guard harmlessness and see uprightness, for
there is a remnant for a peaceful man."

CHAPTER 15.

Therefore let us be joined to those who make peace with piety, and not to
those who wish for peace with hypocrisy.

For he says somewhere: "This people honors me with their lips, but their heart
is far away from me."

And again: "With their mouth they were blessing, but with their heart they
were cursing."

And again he says: "They loved him with their mouth and with their tongue they
lied to him, but their heart was not right with him, nor were they faithful in
his covenant."

For this reason "let the deceitful lips that speak lawlessness against the
righteous become mute."

And again: "May the Lord utterly destroy all deceitful lips, the tongue that
speaks great things, those who said: We will magnify our tongue, our lips are
our own; who is lord over us?

Because of the misery of the poor and the groaning of the needy I will now
arise, says the Lord; I will set him in safety, I will speak freely in him."

CHAPTER 16.

For Christ belongs to the humble-minded, not to those who exalt themselves
over his flock.

The scepter of the majesty of God, the Lord Jesus Christ, did not come

in a parade of boastfulness or of arrogance, although he was able, but being
humble-minded, as the Holy Spirit spoke concerning him; for it says:

"Lord, who has believed our report? And to whom has the arm of the Lord been
revealed? We announced before him as a child, as a root in a thirsty land; he
has no form or glory, and we saw him, and he had no form or beauty, but his
form was without honor, failing beside the form of men; a man in blow and in
toil and knowing how to bear weakness, because his face has been turned away,
he was dishonored and not counted.

This one bears our sins and is in pain for us, and we counted him to be in
toil and in blow and in ill-treatment.

But he was wounded because of our sins and has been weakened because of our
lawless deeds; the discipline of our peace is upon him, by his bruise we were
healed.

All we like sheep have gone astray, a man has gone astray in his own way.

And the Lord delivered him up for our sins, and he, because he has been
ill-treated, does not open his mouth. As a sheep he was led to slaughter, and
as a lamb before the one who shears it is voiceless, so he does not open his
mouth. In the humiliation his judgment was taken away.

Who shall tell his generation? For his life is taken from the earth.

From the lawless deeds of my people he has come to death.

And I will give the wicked for his burial and the rich for his death; because
he did no lawlessness, nor was deceit found in his mouth. And the Lord wishes
to cleanse him from the blow.

If you give concerning sin, your soul shall see long-lived seed.

And the Lord wishes to take away from the toil of his soul, to show him light
and to form him with understanding, to justify a righteous one who serves many
well; and he himself will bear up their sins.

For this reason he himself shall inherit many and shall divide spoils of the
strong, because his soul was delivered up to death, and he was counted among
the lawless; and he bore up the sins of many and because of their sins he was
delivered up."

And again he himself says: "But I am a worm and not a man, a reproach of men
and an outcast of the people.

All who look at me sneered at me, they spoke with lips, they shook the head:
He hoped in the Lord, let him deliver him; let him save him, because he wants
him."

You see, beloved men, what the pattern is that has been given us; for if the
Lord was thus humble-minded, what shall we do who have come under the yoke of
his grace through him?

CHAPTER 17.

Let us become imitators also of those who went about in goatskins and
sheepskins preaching the coming of Christ; we mean Elijah and Elisha, and
Ezekiel too, the prophets, and besides these those who have received witness.

Abraham received great witness and was called friend of God, and looking
intently at the glory of God he says, being humble-minded: "But I am earth and
ashes."

And concerning Job also it is written thus: "And Job was righteous and
blameless, true, god-fearing, holding back from every evil."

But he accuses himself, saying: "No one is clean from defilement, not even if
his life is of one day."

Moses was called "faithful in his whole house," and through his service God
judged Egypt through their scourges and outrages; but even he, being greatly
glorified, did not speak great things, but said, when an oracle was given him
from the bush: "Who am I, that you send me? But I am thin-voiced and slow of
tongue."

And again he says: "But I am steam from a pot."

CHAPTER 18.

And what shall we say of David who has received witness? To whom God said: "I
found a man according to my heart, David the son of Jesse; I anointed him in
eternal mercy."

But he too says to God: "Have mercy on me, O God, according to your great
mercy, and according to the multitude of your compassions blot out my lawless
deed.

Wash me still more from my lawlessness, and cleanse me from my sin; because I
know my lawlessness, and my sin is before me continually.

Against you alone I sinned, and I did the evil before you; that you may be
justified in your words, and may overcome when you are judged.

For behold, I was conceived in lawless deeds, and in sins my mother longed for
me.

For behold, you loved truth; the unclear things and the hidden things of

your wisdom you made plain to me.

You will sprinkle me with hyssop, and I shall be cleansed; you will wash me,
and I shall be whitened beyond snow.

You will make me hear gladness and joy; bones that have been humbled shall
rejoice.

Turn your face away from my sins, and blot out all my lawless deeds.

Create a clean heart in me, O God, and renew a right spirit in my inward
parts.

Do not cast me away from your face, and do not take your Holy Spirit from me.

Give me back the gladness of your salvation, and establish me with a governing
spirit.

I will teach the lawless your ways, and the ungodly shall turn to you.

Deliver me from bloods, O God, the God of my salvation; my tongue shall
rejoice in your righteousness. Lord, you will open my mouth, and my lips shall
announce your praise.

Because if you had wished a sacrifice, I would have given it; you will not be
well pleased with whole burnt offerings.

A sacrifice to God is a crushed spirit; a crushed and humbled heart God will
not despise."

CHAPTER 19.

The humble-mindedness and the want, through obedience, of so many and such men
who have thus received witness made better not only us but also the
generations before us, those who received his oracles in fear and truth.

Having therefore taken a share in many and great and glorious deeds, let us
run back to the goal of peace handed down to us from the beginning, and let us
look intently at the Father and creator of the whole world, and let us be
joined to his magnificent and surpassing gifts of peace and his good works.

Let us see him according to the mind and look with the eyes of the soul at his
long-suffering will; let us understand how he is without anger toward all his
creation.

CHAPTER 20.

The heavens, being moved by his administration, are subjected to him in peace.

Day and night complete the course appointed by him, hindering one another in
nothing.

Sun and moon and the choirs of stars according to his arrangement unroll in
concord, without any deviation, the bounds appointed to them.

Earth, bearing according to his will, in its own seasons brings up food in
full multitude for men and beasts and all the living creatures that are upon
it, not standing apart nor altering anything of the things decreed by him.

The unsearchable judgments of the abysses and the inexpressible ones of the
nether regions are held together by the same commands.

The hollow of the boundless sea, gathered according to his fashioning into the
collections, does not overstep the bars set around it, but as he arranged for
it, so it does.

For he said: "You shall come as far as this, and your waves shall be broken in
you."

The ocean, impassable to men, and the worlds after it are steered by the same
orders of the Master.

The seasons of spring and summer and autumn and winter hand themselves over to
one another in peace.

The stations of the winds in their own season accomplish their ministry
without stumbling; and ever-flowing springs, fashioned for enjoyment and
health, without failing furnish the breasts toward life for men; and the
smallest of the living creatures make their gatherings in concord and peace.

All these things the great fashioner and Master of all things commanded to be
in peace and concord, doing good to all things, and exceedingly to us who have
fled for refuge to his compassions through our Lord Jesus Christ;

to whom be the glory and the majesty to the ages of the ages. Amen.

CHAPTER 21.

See, beloved, that his many good works may not become a judgment for us, if we
do not, living as citizens worthily of him, do the things that are good and
well-pleasing before him with concord.

For he says somewhere: "The spirit of the Lord is a lamp searching the
store-rooms of the belly."

Let us see how near he is, and that nothing of our thoughts has escaped him,
nor of the reasonings we make.

It is just, then, that we not desert from his will.

Rather against

foolish and senseless people, who exalt themselves and boast in the arrogance
of their speech, let us stumble than against God.

Let us reverence the Lord Jesus Christ, whose blood was given for us; let us
respect those who lead us; let us honor the elders; let us train the young in
the training of the fear of God; let us correct our women toward the good;

let them show the love-worthy character of purity, let them exhibit the
innocent will of their meekness, let them make the gentleness of their tongue
evident through silence, let them give their love not according to
partialities, but equally in a holy way to all who fear God.

Let our children take a share in the training that is in Christ; let them
learn what humble-mindedness avails with God, what pure love can do with God,
how his fear is fair and great and saving for all who live in a holy way in
him in a clean mind.

For he is a searcher of thoughts and intentions; whose breath is in us, and
when he wishes, he will take it away.

CHAPTER 22.

And all these things the faith that is in Christ confirms; for he himself
through the Holy Spirit thus calls us: "Come, children, hear me, I will teach
you the fear of the Lord.

Who is the man that wishes life, loving to see good days?

Stop your tongue from evil and your lips from speaking deceit.

Turn aside from evil and do good.

Seek peace and pursue it.

The eyes of the Lord are upon the righteous, and his ears toward their
petition; but the face of the Lord is upon those who do evil, to utterly
destroy their memory from the earth.

The righteous cried out, and the Lord heard him, and from all his afflictions
he delivered him."

"Many are the scourges of the sinner, but mercy shall encircle those who hope
in the Lord."

CHAPTER 23.

The Father who is merciful in all things and does good has inward parts toward
those who fear him, and mildly and

gently he gives back his graces to those who come to him with a simple mind.

Therefore let us not be double-minded, nor let our soul imagine things
concerning his surpassing and glorious gifts.

Let this scripture be far from us, where it says: "Wretched are the
double-minded, who doubt in soul, who say: These things we heard also in the
time of our fathers, and behold, we have grown old, and none of these things
has happened to us.

O senseless ones, compare yourselves to a tree; take a vine; first it sheds
its leaves, then a shoot comes to be, then a leaf, then a flower, and after
these an unripe grape, then a standing cluster." You see that in a little time
the fruit of the tree arrives at ripeness.

In truth his will shall be completed quickly and suddenly, the scripture also
bearing witness with it, that: "He will come quickly and will not delay," and:
"The Lord will come suddenly to his temple, and the holy one, whom you
expect."

CHAPTER 24.

Let us consider, beloved, how the Master continually shows us the resurrection
that is going to be, of which he made the Lord Jesus Christ the firstfruits by
raising him from the dead.

Let us see, beloved, the resurrection that comes to be according to season.

Day and night show us a resurrection; night sleeps, day rises; day goes away,
night comes on.

Let us take the fruits; the seed, how and in what manner does it come to be?

The sower went out and cast into the earth each of the seeds; which, having
fallen into the earth dry and naked, are dissolved; then from the dissolution
the greatness of the Master's providence raises them, and from the one it
increases more and brings forth fruit.

CHAPTER 25.

Let us see the strange sign that comes to be in the eastern places, that is,
those about Arabia.

For there is a bird which is named the phoenix; this, being unique, lives five
hundred years, and when it has already come toward release, that it may die,
it makes for itself a shrine of frankincense and myrrh and the rest of the
spices, into which, when the time has been fulfilled, it enters and comes to
its end.

And as the flesh rots a certain worm is born, which from the moisture of the
dead

creature is nourished and grows feathers; then, having become noble, it takes
up that shrine where the bones of the former one are, and carrying these it
completes the way from the Arabian country as far as Egypt, to the place
called Heliopolis.

And in day, while all look on, having flown upon the altar of the sun it sets
them down and thus starts back.

The priests then inspect the records of the times and find that it has come
when the five-hundredth year has been fulfilled.

CHAPTER 26.

Do we then think it a great and wondrous thing if the fashioner of all things
will make a resurrection of those who have served him in a holy way in the
confidence of a good faith, when even through a bird he shows us the greatness
of his promise?

For he says somewhere: "And you will raise me up, and I will confess you,"
and: "I lay down and slept, I was raised, because you are with me."

And again Job says: "And you will raise up this flesh of mine that has drawn
up all these things."

CHAPTER 27.

In this hope, then, let our souls be bound to him who is faithful in the
promises and righteous in the judgments.

He who charged us not to lie will himself far more not lie; for nothing is
impossible with God except to lie.

Let his faith, then, be rekindled in us, and let us understand that all things
are near to him.

By the word of his majesty he composed all things, and by a word he is able to
overthrow them.

"Who shall say to him: What have you done? Or who shall stand against the
might of his strength?" When he wishes and as he wishes, he will do all
things, and nothing of the things decreed by him shall pass away.

All things are before him, and nothing has escaped his counsel,

if "the heavens tell the glory of God, and the firmament announces the work of
his hands; day to day belches a word, and night to night announces knowledge;
and there are no words or speeches whose voices are not heard."

CHAPTER 28.

All things then being seen and heard, let us

fear him and leave the abominable desires of worthless works, that by his
mercy we may be sheltered from the judgments that are coming.

For where can any of us flee from his mighty hand? And what world will receive
any of those who desert from him? For the writing says somewhere: "Where shall
I go and where shall I hide from your face? If I go up into heaven, you are
there; if I go away to the ends of the earth, there is your right hand; if I
spread myself in the abysses, there is your spirit."

Where then shall one go away, or where shall one run from the one who contains
all things?

CHAPTER 29.

Let us therefore come to him in holiness of soul, lifting pure and undefiled
hands toward him, loving our gentle and compassionate Father, who made us a
portion of election for himself.

For thus it is written: "When the Most High divided nations, when he scattered
the sons of Adam, he set the bounds of nations according to the number of the
angels of God. The Lord's portion became his people Jacob, the measured line
of his inheritance Israel."

And in another place he says: "Behold, the Lord takes for himself a nation
from the midst of nations, as a man takes his firstfruits of the
threshing-floor; and there shall come out from that nation holy things of
holies."

CHAPTER 30.

Being then a portion of the Holy One, let us do all the things of
sanctification, fleeing slanders, and abominable and unclean couplings, and
drunkennesses and innovations and abominable desires, and foul adultery and
abominable arrogance.

"For God," it says, "sets himself against the arrogant, but gives grace to the
humble."

Let us then be joined to those to whom the grace from God has been given; let
us put on concord, being humble-minded, practicing self-control, putting
ourselves far from every whispering and slander, being justified by works and
not by words.

For it says: "He who says many things shall also hear in return; or does the
well-spoken think he is righteous? Blessed is the one born of woman, short of
life. Do not become many in words."

Let our praise be in God and not from ourselves; for God hates those who
praise themselves.

Let the witness of our good

deed be given by others, as it was given to our fathers the righteous.

Boldness and self-will and daring belong to those cursed by God; gentleness
and humble-mindedness and meekness among those blessed by God.

CHAPTER 31.

Let us then be joined to his blessing and let us see what the ways of the
blessing are. Let us unroll the things that came to be from the beginning.

For what cause was our father Abraham blessed, was it not because he did
righteousness and truth through faith?

Isaac, knowing with confidence what was coming, was led to sacrifice gladly.

Jacob with humble-mindedness went out from his land because of a brother and
went to Laban and served, and the twelve-scepter of Israel was given him.

CHAPTER 32.

If anyone considers each one by one sincerely, he will recognize the great
things of the gifts given by him.

For from him are priests and Levites, all who minister at the altar of God;
from him is the Lord Jesus according to the flesh; from him are kings and
rulers and leaders according to Judah; and the rest of his scepters are not in
small glory, as God having promised that: "Your seed shall be as the stars of
heaven."

All then were glorified and magnified not through themselves or their works or
the right-doing which they accomplished, but through his will.

And we too, called through his will in Christ Jesus, are not justified through
ourselves, nor through our wisdom or understanding or piety or works which we
accomplished in holiness of heart, but through the faith through which the
almighty God has justified all who have been from the age; to whom be the
glory to the ages of the ages. Amen.

CHAPTER 33.

What then shall we do, brothers? Shall we be idle from doing good and abandon
love? May the Master not allow this to come to be upon us, but let us hasten
with intensity and eagerness to accomplish every good work.

For the fashioner and Master of all things himself rejoices over his works.

For by his most vast might he established heavens and by his incomprehensible
understanding he ordered them; and he separated earth from the

water that contains it and settled it upon the sure foundation of his own
will, and the living creatures that go about on it he commanded by his own
arrangement to be; the sea and the living creatures in it he prepared
beforehand and shut in by his own power.

Above all, the most excellent and vast, man, he formed with his holy and
blameless hands as the impress of his own image.

For thus God says: "Let us make man according to our image and according to
our likeness. And God made man, male and female he made them."

Having then completed all these things he praised them and blessed them and
said: "Increase and multiply."

Let us see that in good works all the righteous were adorned, and the Lord
himself, having adorned himself with good works, rejoiced.

Having then this pattern let us come without reluctance to his will; with all
our strength let us work a work of righteousness.

CHAPTER 34.

The good worker receives with confidence the bread of his work; the sluggish
and slack does not look his work-supplier in the face.

It is necessary, then, that we be eager toward doing good; for from him are
all things.

For he tells us beforehand: "Behold the Lord, and his reward is before his
face, to give back to each according to his work."

He therefore urges us who believe from our whole heart in him not to be idle
or slack toward every good work.

Let our boast and our confidence be in him; let us be subjected to his will;
let us consider the whole multitude of his angels, how they minister to his
will as they stand by.

For the scripture says: "Ten thousand times ten thousand stood by him, and a
thousand thousands ministered to him, and they cried out: Holy, holy, holy
Lord of Sabaoth, all creation is full of his glory."

And we too, in concord, gathered to the same thing in conscience, as from one
mouth let us cry out to him intensely, that we may become sharers of his great
and glorious promises.

For he says: "Eye has not seen and ear has not heard and it has not gone up
upon a human heart, what he

prepared for those who wait for him."

CHAPTER 35.

How blessed and wondrous are the gifts of God, beloved.

Life in immortality, brightness in righteousness, truth in confidence, faith
in persuasion, self-control in sanctification; and these things were all
falling under our mind.

What then are the things being prepared for those who wait?

The fashioner and Father of the ages, the all-holy one himself, knows their
quantity and their beauty.

Let us then contend to be found in the number of those who wait, that we may
take a share of the promised gifts.

And how shall this be, beloved? If our mind is established faithfully toward
God, if we seek out the things well-pleasing and acceptable to him, if we
accomplish the things that belong to his blameless will and follow the way of
the truth, having thrown off from ourselves all injustice and wickedness,
greed, strifes, and maliciousnesses and deceits, whisperings and slanders,
hatred of God, arrogance and boastfulness, vainglory and inhospitality.

For those who practice these things are hateful to God, "and not only those
who practice them, but also those who consent with them."

For the scripture says: "And to the sinner God said: Why do you tell my
ordinances and take up my covenant upon your mouth?

But you hated training and threw my words behind you. If you saw a thief, you
ran with him, and you set your portion with adulterers. Your mouth multiplied
evil, and your tongue wove deceit. Sitting, you slandered your brother, and
against the son of your mother you set a stumbling-block.

These things you did, and I was silent; you supposed, lawless one, that I
would be like you.

I will refute you and I will set you before your face.

Understand these things, then, you who forget God, lest he snatch as a lion
and there be none who delivers.

A sacrifice of praise shall glorify me, and there is the way, in which I will
show him the salvation of God."

CHAPTER 36.

This is the way, beloved, in which we found our salvation, Jesus Christ, the
high priest of our offerings, the protector and helper of our weakness.

Through this one we look intently into the heights of the heavens, through
this one we behold as in a mirror his blameless and most high vision, through
this one the eyes of our heart were opened, through this one our unintelligent
and darkened mind sprouts again into the light, through this one the Master
wished us to taste of the deathless knowledge, "who being a radiance of his
majesty is so much greater than angels, as he has inherited a more different
name."

For it is written thus: "He who makes his angels spirits and his ministers a
flame of fire."

But upon his Son the Master said thus: "You are my Son, today I have begotten
you; ask from me, and I will give you nations as your inheritance and the ends
of the earth as your possession."

And again he says to him: "Sit at my right hand, until I set your enemies as a
footstool of your feet."

Who then are the enemies? The worthless and those who set themselves against
his will.

CHAPTER 37.

Let us then serve as soldiers, men and brothers, with all intensity in his
blameless commandments.

Let us consider those who serve as soldiers under our leaders, how in good
order, how yielding, how subjected they accomplish the things arranged.

Not all are prefects, nor chiliarchs, nor centurions, nor commanders of fifty,
nor the rest in sequence, but each in his own rank accomplishes the things
ordered by the king and the leaders.

The great cannot be without the small, nor the small without the great; there
is a certain mixing in all, and in these a use.

Let us take our body; the head without the feet is nothing, so neither are the
feet without the head; and the smallest members of our body are necessary and
well-used for the whole body; but all breathe together and use one subjection,
that the whole body may be saved.

CHAPTER 38.

Let our whole body then be saved in Christ Jesus, and let each be subjected to
his neighbor, as he was placed in his gift.

Let the strong care for the weak, and let the weak reverence the strong; let
the rich supply the poor, and let the poor give thanks to God, because he gave
him one through whom his lack might be filled; let the wise show his wisdom
not in words but in good works; let the humble-minded not bear witness to
himself, but let him allow himself to be borne witness to by another; let the
one who is pure in the flesh not boast, knowing that another is the one who
supplies him the self-control.

Let us then consider, brothers, of what matter we were made, of what sort and
who we were when we entered into the world, from what tomb and darkness the
one who formed us and fashioned us brought us into his world, having prepared
his good works before we were born.

Having all these things then from him we ought in all things to give thanks to
him; to whom be the glory to the ages of the ages. Amen.

CHAPTER 39.

Foolish and unintelligent and stupid and untrained people mock us and sneer,
wishing to exalt themselves in their minds.

For what can a mortal do? Or what is the strength of one earth-born?

For it is written: "There was no form before my eyes, but I heard a breeze and
a voice;

What then? Shall a mortal be clean before the Lord, or a man blameless from
his works, if he does not trust concerning his servants, and has noted
something crooked concerning his angels?

And heaven is not clean before him; let be, those who dwell in houses of clay,
from which we too are of the same clay. He struck them in the manner of a
moth, and from morning until evening they are no more; because they were not
able to help themselves they perished.

He breathed on them, and they came to an end, because they did not have
wisdom.

But call, if anyone will hear you, or if you will see any of the holy angels;
for anger does away with a fool, and jealousy puts a wanderer to death.

And I have seen fools casting roots, but immediately their manner of life was
eaten.

Far be their sons from salvation; may they be derided at the doors of lesser
men, and there shall be none who delivers. For what has been prepared for
those, the righteous shall eat; but they themselves shall not be exempt from
evils."

CHAPTER 40.

These things then being clear to us, and having stooped into the depths of the
divine knowledge, we ought to do in order all the things the Master commanded
to be accomplished at appointed times.

The offerings and ministries to be accomplished, he commanded not to come to
be at random or without order, but at defined times and hours;

and where and through whom he wishes them to be accomplished, he himself
defined by his most high will, so that all things, coming to be in a holy way
in good pleasure, might be acceptable to his will.

Those then who make their offerings at the commanded times are both acceptable
and blessed; for following the ordinances of the Master they do not go astray.

For to the high priest his own ministries have been given, and to the priests
their own place has been appointed, and upon the Levites their own services
lie; the lay man is bound by the lay commandments.

CHAPTER 41.

Let each of us, brothers, in his own rank be well-pleasing to God, being in a
good conscience, not overstepping the defined rule of his ministry, in
seemliness.

Not everywhere, brothers, are sacrifices of continual offering or of vows or
concerning sin and trespass offered, but only in Jerusalem; and there they are
not offered in every place, but in front of the sanctuary toward the altar,
the thing offered having been inspected for blemish through the high priest
and the aforementioned ministers.

Those then who do anything beyond what is due of his will have death as the
penalty.

See, brothers: by how much more knowledge we have been counted worthy, by so
much more we are subject to danger.

CHAPTER 42.

The apostles were given the good news to us from the Lord Jesus Christ, Jesus
the Christ was sent out from God.

The Christ then from God, and the apostles from the Christ; both therefore
came to be in good order from the will of God.

Having then received charges, and having been fully assured through the
resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ, and having been made faithful in the
word of God with full assurance of the Holy Spirit, they went out announcing
the good news that the kingdom of God was going to come.

Preaching then throughout countries and cities they appointed their
firstfruits, having tested them by the Spirit, as bishops and deacons of those
who were going to believe.

And this not newly; for from many times indeed it had been written concerning
bishops and deacons; for the scripture says somewhere thus: "I will appoint
their bishops in righteousness and their deacons in faith."

CHAPTER 43.

And what wonder is it if those in Christ who were entrusted by God with such a
work appointed the aforementioned? When even the blessed "faithful servant in
the whole house," Moses, noted in the holy books all the things arranged for
him, and the rest of the prophets followed him, bearing additional witness to
the things legislated by him.

For when jealousy had fallen in concerning the priesthood and the tribes were
in sedition over which of them would be adorned with the glorious name, he
commanded the twelve tribal chiefs to bring him rods inscribed with the name
of each tribe; and taking them he bound them and sealed them with the rings of
the tribal chiefs and set them in the tent of the testimony upon the table of
God;

and having shut the tent he sealed the keys likewise and the rods, and said to
them: 'Men and brothers, whichever tribe's rod sprouts, this one God has
chosen for priesthood and for ministry to him.'

And when morning had come he called together all Israel, the six hundred
thousands of the men, and he showed the tribal chiefs the seals and opened the
tent of the testimony and brought out the rods; and the rod of Aaron was found
not only to have sprouted but also to have fruit.

What do you think, beloved? Did Moses not know beforehand that this was going
to be? He knew it especially; but that unrest might not come to be in Israel,
he did thus, that the name of the true and only one might be glorified; to
whom be the glory to the ages of the ages. Amen.

CHAPTER 44.

And our apostles also knew through our Lord Jesus Christ that there would be
strife concerning the name of the oversight.

For this cause then, having received complete foreknowledge, they appointed
the aforementioned and afterward they gave a continuation, so that if they
should fall asleep, other tested men might succeed to their ministry.

Those then who were appointed by them, or afterward by other notable men, the
whole church having consented, and who have ministered blamelessly to the
flock of Christ with humble-mindedness, quietly and without vulgarity, and who
have received witness for many times by all, these we do not think it just to
throw out of the ministry.

For it will be no small sin for us if we throw out those who have offered the
gifts of the oversight blamelessly and in a holy way.

Blessed are the elders who have gone the road before, who had a fruitful and
complete departure; for they do not fear that someone may move them from the
place established for them.

For we see that you have transferred some, who were living as citizens well,
from the ministry that had been honored for them blamelessly.

CHAPTER 45.

Be lovers of contention, brothers, and jealous concerning the things that
belong to salvation.

You have stooped into the holy scriptures, the true ones, those through the
Holy Spirit.

You know that nothing unjust or counterfeit has been written in them. You will
not find righteous men thrown out by holy men.

Righteous men were persecuted, but by the lawless; they were imprisoned, but
by the unholy; they were stoned by those who act contrary to law; they were
killed by those who had taken up an abominable and unjust jealousy.

Suffering these things they bore them gloriously.

For what shall we say, brothers? Was Daniel thrown into a pit of lions by
those who fear God?

Or were Ananias and Azarias and Misael shut into a furnace of fire by those
who worship the magnificent and glorious worship of the Most High? May this
not be. Who then are those who did these things? The hateful and full of every
evil contended in anger to such a point that they threw those serving God in a
holy and blameless purpose into outrage, not knowing that the Most High is
champion and defender of those who in a clean conscience worship his
all-virtuous name; to whom be the glory to the ages of the ages. Amen.

But those who endure in confidence inherited glory and honor, and they were
raised up and became written by God in his memorial to the ages of the ages.
Amen.

CHAPTER 46.

To such examples, then, we too must be joined, brothers.

For it is written: "Be joined to the holy, because those who are joined to
them shall be made holy."

And again in another place he says: "With an innocent man you shall be
innocent, and with an elect you shall be elect, and with a crooked man you
shall twist."

Let us then be joined to the innocent and the righteous; and these are elect
of God.

Why are there strifes and angers and divisions and schisms and war among you?

Or do we not have one God and one Christ and one Spirit of the grace that was
poured out upon us, and one calling in Christ?

Why do we drag apart and tear the members of Christ and rise in sedition
against our own body, and come into such madness that we forget that we are
members of one another?

Remember the words of Jesus our Lord.

For he said: "Woe to that man; it were good for him if he had not been born,
rather than to cause one of my elect to stumble; it were better for him that a
millstone be hung and he be sunk in the sea, than to twist one of my elect."

Your schism has twisted many, has thrown many into faintheartedness, many into
doubt, all of us into grief; and your sedition is persistent.

CHAPTER 47.

Take up the letter of the blessed Paul the apostle.

What did he first write you at the beginning of the gospel?

In truth he wrote you spiritually concerning himself and Cephas and Apollos,
because you had even then made partialities.

But that partiality brought you a lesser sin; for you were inclined toward
apostles who had received witness and toward a man tested among them.

But now consider who have twisted you and diminished the venerable character
of your renowned brotherly love.

Shameful things, beloved, and very shameful and unworthy of the conduct in
Christ, to hear that the most firm and ancient

church of the Corinthians is in sedition against the elders because of one or
two persons.

And this report has gone not only to us, but also to those who are turned
aside from us, so that blasphemies are also brought upon the name of the Lord
through your folly, and you work out danger for yourselves.

CHAPTER 48.

Let us then take this away quickly and fall down before the Master and weep,
begging him that, becoming gracious, he may be reconciled to us and restore us
to the venerable pure conduct of our brotherly love.

For a gate of righteousness opened into life is this, as it is written: "Open
to me gates of righteousness; having entered in them I will confess to the
Lord.

This is the gate of the Lord; the righteous shall enter in it."

Of many gates then that have been opened, this one in righteousness is the one
in Christ, in which blessed are all who have entered and who direct their
journey in holiness and righteousness, accomplishing all things without
disturbance.

Let one be faithful, let one be able to declare knowledge, let one be wise in
discernment of words, let one be pure in works; for by so much more ought he
to be humble-minded, by how much he seems to be greater, and to seek the
common benefit for all, and not his own.

CHAPTER 49.

Let him who has love in Christ do the commandments of Christ.

Who is able to explain the bond of the love of God?

Who is sufficient to declare the greatness of its beauty?

The height to which love leads up is inexpressible.

Love joins us to God, "love covers a multitude of sins," love bears all
things, is long-suffering in all things; nothing vulgar is in love, nothing
arrogant; love has no schism, love does not rise in sedition, love does all
things in concord; in love all the elect of God were made complete; without
love nothing is well-pleasing to God.

In love the Master took us to himself; because of the love which he had toward
us, Jesus Christ our Lord gave his blood for us in the will of God,

and the flesh for our flesh and the soul for our souls.

CHAPTER 50.

You see, beloved, how great and wondrous love is, and of its completeness
there is no explanation.

Who is sufficient to be found in it, except those whom God may count worthy?
Let us then beg and ask from his mercy, that we may be found in love without
human partiality, blameless.

All the generations from Adam until this day have passed; but those made
complete in love according to the grace of God have a place of the pious; who
shall be made manifest in the visitation of the kingdom of Christ.

For it is written: "Enter into the store-rooms a little, so much, until my
anger and my wrath pass; and I will remember a good day and I will raise you
from your chests."

We are blessed, beloved, if we were doing the commandments of God in concord
of love, that our sins may be forgiven us through love.

For it is written: "Blessed are they whose lawless deeds were forgiven, and
whose sins were covered; blessed is a man to whom the Lord will not count sin,
nor is there deceit in his mouth."

This blessing came to be upon those chosen by God through Jesus Christ our
Lord; to whom be the glory to the ages of the ages. Amen.

CHAPTER 51.

As many things then as we slipped and did through certain incursions of the
adversary, let us ask that they be forgiven us; and those also who became
leaders of sedition and division ought to look to the common of the hope.

For those who live as citizens with fear and love wish rather to fall into
outrages themselves than their neighbors; and they rather bear condemnation of
themselves than of the well and justly handed-down harmony.

For it is good for a man to confess concerning his transgressions rather than
to harden his heart, as the heart of those who rose in sedition against Moses
the servant of God was hardened, whose judgment became clear.

"For they went down into Hades alive," and "death shall shepherd them."

Pharaoh and his army and all the leaders of Egypt, both the chariots and

their riders were sunk in the Red Sea and perished for no other cause than
that their unintelligent hearts were hardened after the signs and the wonders
had come to be in Egypt through Moses the servant of God.

CHAPTER 52.

The Master of all things, brothers, is without need; he needs nothing of
anyone except that confession be made to him.

For the elect David says: "I will confess to the Lord, and it will please him
above a young calf putting out horns and hoofs; let the poor see and be glad."

And again he says: "Sacrifice to God a sacrifice of praise and give back to
the Most High your vows; and call upon me in a day of your affliction, and I
will take you out, and you shall glorify me."

"For a sacrifice to God is a crushed spirit."

CHAPTER 53.

For you know, and you know well, the holy scriptures, beloved, and you have
stooped into the oracles of God. We write these things then toward
remembrance.

For when Moses had gone up into the mountain and had made forty days and forty
nights in fasting and humiliation, God said to him: "Go down quickly from
here, because your people, whom you led out from the land of Egypt, have acted
lawlessly; they have quickly turned aside from the way which you commanded
them, they have made themselves molten things."

And the Lord said to him: "I have spoken to you once and twice saying: I have
seen this people, and behold, it is stiff-necked; let me utterly destroy them,
and I will blot out their name from under heaven, and I will make you into a
nation great and wondrous and much more than this."

And Moses said: "By no means, Lord; forgive the sin to this people, or blot me
also out from the book of the living."

O great love, O unsurpassable completeness; a servant speaks freely toward the
Lord, he asks forgiveness for the multitude, or he even claims to be blotted
out himself with them.

CHAPTER 54.

Who then among you is noble, who compassionate, who fully assured in love?

Let him say: If through me there is sedition and strife and schisms, I
withdraw, I go away, wherever you wish, and

I do the things commanded by the multitude; only let the flock of Christ be at
peace with the appointed elders.

The one who has done this will procure for himself great fame in Christ, and
every place will receive him. "For the Lord's is the earth and its fullness."

These things those who live the unregretted citizenship of God have done and
will do.

CHAPTER 55.

And that we may also bring examples of the nations: many kings and leaders,
when a pestilential time had arisen, being given an oracle, delivered
themselves to death, that they might rescue the citizens through their own
blood; many went out from their own cities, that they might not be in sedition
any further.

We know many among us who have delivered themselves to bonds, that they might
redeem others; many delivered themselves to slavery and, having taken their
prices, fed others.

Many women, being strengthened through the grace of God, accomplished many
manly deeds.

The blessed Judith, when the city was shut in, asked from the elders that she
be allowed to go out into the camp of the foreigners.

Delivering herself then to the danger she went out through love of the
fatherland and of the people who were in the shutting-in, and the Lord
delivered Holofernes into the hand of a female.

No less also Esther, complete according to faith, threw herself into danger,
that she might deliver the twelve-tribe of Israel that was going to perish;
for through her fasting and her humiliation she claimed from the all-seeing
Master, God of the ages; who, seeing the humble state of her soul, delivered
the people for whose sake she had been in danger.

CHAPTER 56.

Let us also then make petition concerning those who are in some transgression,
that gentleness and humble-mindedness may be given them, so that they may
yield not to us but to the will of God; for thus the remembrance toward God
and the holy ones with compassions will be fruitful and complete for them.

Let us take up training, at which no one ought to be indignant, beloved. The
admonition which we make toward one another is good and exceedingly
profitable; for it joins us to the will of God.

For thus the holy word says: "Training, the Lord trained me, and he did not
deliver me to death."

"For whom the Lord loves, he trains; and he scourges every son whom he
receives."

"For a righteous man will train me," it says, "in mercy and will refute me;
but let not oil of sinners fatten my head."

And again he says: "Blessed is a man whom the Lord has refuted; and do not
refuse the admonition of the Almighty; for he himself makes one to be in pain,
and again he restores;

he struck, and his hands healed.

Six times he will take you out of necessities, and in the seventh evil shall
not touch you.

In famine he will deliver you from death, and in war he will loose you from
the hand of iron;

and he will hide you from a scourge of tongue, and you shall not fear coming
evils.

You shall laugh at unjust and lawless men, and you shall not fear from wild
beasts;

for wild beasts shall be at peace with you.

Then you shall know that your house will be at peace; and the manner of life
of your tent shall not miss.

And you shall know that your seed is much, and your children as the all-fodder
of the field.

And you shall come in a tomb as ripe grain harvested in season, or as a heap
of a threshing-floor gathered according to the hour."

You see, beloved, how great a defense there is for those trained by the
Master; for being a good father he trains, that we may be shown mercy through
his holy training.

CHAPTER 57.

You then who made the foundation of the sedition, be subjected to the elders
and be trained unto repentance, having bent the knees of your heart.

Learn to be subjected, putting away the boastful and arrogant self-will of
your tongue; for it is better for you to be found small and notable in the
flock of Christ than, seeming according to superiority, to be thrown out from
his hope.

For thus the all-virtuous wisdom says: "Behold, I will put forth to you an
utterance of my breath, and I will teach you my word.

Since I called and you did not obey, and I stretched out words and you did not
attend, but you made my counsels void,

and you disobeyed my refutations; therefore I also will laugh at your
destruction, and I will rejoice when ruin comes to you and when tumult arrives
upon you suddenly, and the overthrow is present like a storm, or when
affliction and siege come to you.

For it shall be, when you call upon me, that I will not hear you; evil men
shall seek me, and they shall not find me. For they hated wisdom, and they did
not choose the fear of the Lord, nor did they wish to attend to my counsels,
but they sneered at my refutations.

Therefore they shall eat the fruits of their own way and shall be filled with
their own impiety.

Because they wronged infants they shall be murdered, and examination shall
destroy the impious; but he who hears me shall encamp upon hope, having been
persuaded, and shall be still without fear from every evil."

Let us then obey his all-holy and glorious name, fleeing the threats spoken
beforehand through the wisdom to the disobedient, that we may "encamp having
been persuaded" upon the most holy name of his majesty.

Receive our counsel, and there shall be things without regret for you. For God
lives, and the Lord Jesus Christ lives, and the Holy Spirit, both the faith
and the hope of the elect, that the one who has done in humble-mindedness with
intense gentleness, without regret, the ordinances and commandments given by
God, this one shall be enrolled and notable into the number of those being
saved through Jesus Christ, through whom is the glory to him to the ages of
the ages. Amen.

But if some disobey the things said by him through us, let them know that they
will bind themselves with no small transgression and danger.

And we shall be innocent from this sin and we shall ask, making the petition
and the supplication intense, that the fashioner of all things may keep
unshattered the numbered number of his elect in the whole world through his
beloved child Jesus Christ our Lord, through whom he called us from darkness
into light, from ignorance into knowledge of the glory of his name,

to hope upon your name, the original of all creation, having opened the eyes
of our heart to know you, the only Most High in the highest, holy, resting
among the holy; who humbles the insolence of the arrogant, who dissolves
reasonings of nations, who makes the humble into height and humbles the high;
who makes rich and makes poor, who kills and makes to live, sole benefactor of
spirits and God of all flesh; who looks upon the abysses, the overseer of
human works, the helper of those in danger,

the savior of those who have despaired, the creator and overseer of every
spirit; who multiplies nations upon earth and from all has chosen those who
love you through Jesus Christ your beloved child, through whom you trained us,
made us holy, honored us.

We claim of you, Master, to become helper and supporter of us; save those in
our affliction, raise the fallen, appear to those in need, heal the weak, turn
back the wandering of your people; feed the hungry, redeem our prisoners,
raise up those who are weak, encourage the faint-hearted; let all the nations
know that you are God alone, and Jesus Christ your child, and "we are your
people and sheep of your pasture."

For you made the ever-flowing constitution of the world manifest through the
things being worked; you, Lord, created the inhabited world, the faithful in
all the generations, righteous in the judgments, wondrous in strength and
magnificence, the wise in creating and intelligent in settling the things that
have come to be, the good in the things seen and kind in those who have been
persuaded toward you, merciful and compassionate, forgive us our lawless deeds
and the injustices and the transgressions and trespasses.

Do not count every sin of your servants and handmaids, but cleanse us with the
cleansing of your truth, and direct our steps to walk in holiness of heart and
to do the things good and well-pleasing before you and before our rulers.

Yes, Master, make your face appear upon us unto good things in peace, that we
may be sheltered by your mighty hand and delivered from every sin by your high
arm, and deliver us from those who hate us unjustly.

Give concord and peace to us and to all who dwell on the earth, as you gave to
our fathers when they called upon you in a holy way in faith and truth,
becoming obedient to your almighty and all-virtuous name, and to our rulers
and leaders upon the earth.

You, Master, gave the authority of the kingdom to them through your
magnificent and inexpressible might, that we, knowing the glory and honor
given them by you, may be subjected to them, opposing nothing to your will; to
whom give, Lord, health, peace, concord, stability, that they may administer
without stumbling the leadership given them by you.

For you, Master heavenly, king of the ages, give to the sons of men glory and
honor and authority of the things that are upon the earth;

you, Lord, direct their counsel according to what is good and well-pleasing
before you, that administering in peace and meekness, piously, the authority
given them by you they may obtain your gracious favor.

You who alone are able to do these things and more good things with us, we
confess to you through the high priest and protector of our souls, Jesus
Christ, through whom to you is the glory and the majesty both now and unto
generation of generations and unto the ages of the ages. Amen.

Concerning the things that belong to our worship and the things most
profitable unto a virtuous life for those who wish to direct themselves
piously and justly, we have written you sufficiently, men and brothers.

For concerning faith and repentance and genuine love and self-control and
sobriety and endurance we have handled every place, reminding you that you
must in righteousness and truth and long-suffering be well-pleasing in a holy
way to God almighty, being of one mind without remembrance of wrongs in love
and peace with intense gentleness, as also our fathers shown beforehand were
well-pleasing, being humble-minded in the things toward the Father and God and
creator and all human beings.

And these things we have reminded so much more gladly, since we knew clearly
that we write to men faithful and most notable and who have stooped into the
oracles of the training of God.

It is lawful then, having come to such and so many examples, to place the neck
and to fill up the place of obedience, that having become still from the vain
sedition we may arrive without any blemish at the goal set before us in truth.

For you will furnish us joy and gladness if, becoming obedient to the things
written by us through the Holy Spirit, you cut out the unlawful anger of your
jealousy according to the petition which we made concerning peace and concord
in this letter.

And we have sent faithful and sober men who have lived among us blamelessly
from youth until old age, who will also be witnesses between you and us.

And this we did, that you may know that every care has come to be for us and
is, that you may be at peace quickly.

CHAPTER 58.

For the rest, may the all-seeing God and Master of the spirits and Lord of all
flesh, who chose the Lord Jesus Christ and us through him unto a people for
his own, give to every soul that has called upon his magnificent and holy name
faith, fear, peace, endurance and long-suffering, self-control, purity and
sobriety, unto well-pleasing to his name through our high priest and protector

Jesus Christ, through whom to him is glory and majesty, might and honor, both
now and unto all the ages of the ages. Amen.

CHAPTER 59.

And send back quickly in peace with joy those sent from us, Claudius Ephebus
and Valerius Bito together also with Fortunatus, toward us, that they may the
sooner report the peace and concord prayed for and longed for by us, that we
too may the sooner rejoice concerning your stability.

The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you and with all everywhere who
have been called by God through him, through whom to him is glory, honor,
might and majesty, an eternal throne from the ages unto the ages of the ages.
Amen.
