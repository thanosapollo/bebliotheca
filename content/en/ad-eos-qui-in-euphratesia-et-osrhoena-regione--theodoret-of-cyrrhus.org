* To those in the region of Euphratesia and Osrhoene
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:
151. Theodoret to those living as monks in Euphratesia, and Osrhoene, and
Syria, and Phoenicia and Cilicia.

Seeing the condition of the Church at the present time, and the storm that has
lately risen against the holy ship, and the violent squalls, and the assault
of the waves, and the deep moonless night, and besides these the strife of the
passengers, and the fighting of those allotted to row, and the drunkenness of
the pilots, and, in a word, the untimely flood of evils, I am reminded of the
laments of Jeremiah, and I cry with him: "My belly, my belly, I am in pain,
and the senses of my heart; my soul is in tumult, and my heart is torn," and I
seek fountains of tears, that by the streams of the eyes I may put away the
great cloud of despondency. For in so wild a winter it was needful that the
pilots too should keep watch, and fight the surge, and take thought for the
safety of the vessel, and that the sailors, having left off their strife
against one another, should dissolve the dangers by prayer and skill; and that
the passengers should sit quietly, and wrangle neither with one another nor
with the pilots; and that they should entreat the Master of the sea, that by a
nod he might change the gloomy things. Of these things no one is willing to do
anything; but as in a night battle, having failed to recognize one another,
and having left the adversaries, we spend all our weapons against ourselves,
and wound those of our own race as enemies, while those standing nearby laugh
at our drunkenness, and revel in our evils, and rejoice =83.1417= to see us
being spent by one another. The causes of these things are those who have
contended to corrupt the apostolic faith and have dared to impose a strange
teaching upon the evangelical doctrines; and who have received the impious
chapters which they sent out with anathemas to the imperial city, and have
confirmed them, as they supposed, by their own signatures, chapters which have
clearly sprouted from the bitter root of Apollinarius. They also share in the
impiety of Arius and Eunomius; and if anyone should wish to look closely, they
are not even without share in the impiety of Valentinus and Marcion. For in
the first chapter he casts out the economy that has taken place for us,
teaching that God the Word did not assume human nature, but was himself
changed into flesh; and dogmatizing that the incarnation of our Savior took
place in seeming and fantasy, but not in truth.

These things are offspring of the impiety of Marcion and of Manes and of
Valentinus. But in the second and third chapter, as though having forgotten
what he set out in the preface, he introduces the union according to
hypostasis, and a coming together according to natural union, teaching through
these names that a kind of mixing and confusion of the divine nature and of
the form of the servant has taken place. This is a birth of the heretical
innovation of Apollinarius. And in the fourth chapter he forbids the
distinction of the evangelical and apostolic voices, and does not allow,
according to the teachings of the orthodox Fathers, the God-befitting voices
to be taken of the divine nature, and the lowly ones spoken humanly to be
attached to the assumed humanity; and from this too those of sound mind can
find the kinship of the impiety. For Arius and Eunomius, asserting that the
only-begotten Son of God is a creature and from things that are not, and a
servant, have dared to attach to his Godhead the things spoken lowly and
humanly by the Master Christ; constructing from this the difference of essence
and the unlikeness. Besides these things, to speak concisely, he proclaims
that the very impassible and unchangeable Godhead of Christ both suffered, and
was crucified and died and was buried. And this is even beyond the madness of
Arius and Eunomius; for not even those who dare to call the Maker of all
things and Creator a creature have run aground on this impiety. He also
blasphemes against the Holy Spirit, saying that it does not proceed from the
Father, according to the voice of the Lord, but has its existence from the
Son. And this too is the fruit of the seeds of Apollinarius; and it also
neighbors the wicked husbandry of Macedonius. Such are the offspring of the
Egyptian, truly more wicked children of a wicked father. These things, which
those entrusted with the healing of souls ought either to make abortive while
they are being carried, or to destroy as soon as they are born, as deadly and
as poisons of our nature, the gallant ones nourish, and they deem them worthy
of much zeal, to the destruction of themselves and of those who endure to lend
them their ears.

But we are eager to keep the ancestral inheritance inviolate, and we keep
untouched and unstained the faith which we received, with which we both were
baptized and baptize; and we confess our Lord Jesus Christ, perfect God and
perfect man, of a rational soul and body, begotten before the ages from the
Father according to the Godhead, and in the last days for us and for our
salvation from Mary the Virgin; the same consubstantial with the Father
according to the Godhead, and consubstantial with us according to the
humanity. For a union of two natures has taken place. Wherefore we confess one
Christ, one Son, one Lord; for we do not dissolve the union, and we believe it
to have taken place without confusion, obeying the Lord who says to the Jews:
"Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up." And if mixing and
confusion had taken place, and one nature had been completed from both, he
ought to have said: Destroy me, and in three days I shall be raised. But now,
showing that God according to nature is one thing, and the temple another, but
one Christ is both, "Destroy," he says, "this temple, and in three days I will
raise it up," clearly teaching that it was not God who was being destroyed,
but the temple. And the nature of the one admitted the destruction, while the
power of the other raised what was being destroyed. And we confess Christ as
God and man, following the divine Scriptures. That our Lord Jesus Christ is
God, the blessed John the evangelist cries: "In the beginning was the Word,
and the Word was with God, and the Word was God; this one was in the beginning
with God; all things came to be through him, and without him came to be not
even one thing that has come to be." And again: "He was the true light, which
lights every man coming into the world." And the Lord himself teaches
expressly, saying: "He who has seen me has seen my Father." And: "I and the
Father are one; and I am in the Father, and the Father in me." And the blessed
Paul in the letter to the Hebrews says: "Who being the radiance of his glory
and the impress of his hypostasis, and bearing all things by the word of his
power." And in that to the Philippians: "Let this mind be in you," he says,
"which was also in Christ Jesus, who, being in the form of God, did not count
equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, taking the form
of a servant." And in that to the Romans: "Whose are the fathers, and from
whom is the Christ according to the flesh, who is God over all, blessed unto
the ages. Amen."

And in that to Titus: "Looking for the blessed hope and appearing of the glory
of the great God and our Savior Jesus Christ."

And Isaiah also cries that "A child has been born to us, a son also has been
given to us, whose government is upon his =83.1421= shoulder, and his name is
called Angel of great counsel, Wonderful, Counsellor, Mighty God, Master,
Prince of peace, Father of the age to come." And again: "Behind you," he says,
"they shall follow bound in fetters, and in you they shall pray, because God
is in you, and there is no God besides you. For you are God, and they did not
know, God of Israel, Savior." And the name Emmanuel is significant of God and
man, for it is interpreted, according to the teaching of the Gospel, God with
us, that is, God in man, God in our nature. And the divine Jeremiah also
prophesies, saying: "This is our God, another shall not be reckoned beside
him; he found out every way of knowledge, and gave it to Jacob his child, and
to Israel his beloved. After these things he appeared upon the earth, and
consorted with men." And one might find countless other voices both from the
divine Gospels and from the apostolic writings, and from the prophetic
oracles, showing that our Lord Jesus Christ is true God.

And that he is also called man after the incarnation, the Lord himself
teaches, conversing with the Jews and crying: "Why do you seek to kill me, a
man who has spoken the truth to you?" and the blessed Paul in the first letter
to the Corinthians, saying: "For since through a man came death, through a man
also the resurrection of the dead." And showing of whom he speaks, he
interprets what was said, speaking thus: "For as in Adam they die, so also in
Christ all shall be made alive." And writing to Timothy he says likewise: "One
God, and one mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus." And in the Acts,
speaking publicly at Athens: "The times of ignorance therefore God
overlooked," he says, "but now he commands all men everywhere to repent;
inasmuch as he has set a day in which he is about to judge the inhabited world
in righteousness, by a man whom he appointed, having given assurance to all,
having raised him from the dead." And the blessed Peter, conversing with the
Jews, "Men of Israel," he says, "hear these words: Jesus the Nazarene, a man
approved by God among you by signs and wonders and powers which God did
through him." And the prophet Isaiah, foretelling the passions of the Master
Christ, whom a little before he named God, calls this one man, saying: "A man
in wound, and knowing how to bear infirmity; this one bears our lawlessnesses,
and is in pain for us."

And I would have collected other agreeing testimonies from the Holy Scripture
and inserted them in the letter, if I did not know that your piety has as its
life the study of the divine =83.1424= oracles, according to the man blessed
in the Psalms. Leaving therefore the collection of the testimonies to your
industry, I will go on to the matters at hand. We confess therefore our Lord
Jesus Christ as true God and true man, not dividing the one into two persons,
but we believe two natures to have been united without confusion. For thus we
shall also be able easily to refute the many-branched blasphemy of the
heretics; for great and various is the error of those who have risen against
the truth, as we shall presently make clear. For Marcion and Manes say that
God the Word neither assumed human nature, nor have they believed that our
Lord Jesus Christ was born from a Virgin; but that God the Word himself was
fashioned into human form and appeared as a man, using fantasy rather than
truth. But Valentinus and Bardesanes accept the birth, but deny the assumption
of our nature, saying that the Son of God used the Virgin as a kind of pipe.
But Sabellius the Libyan, and Photinus, and Marcellus the Galatian, and Paul
of Samosata, say that a mere man was born from the Virgin; but they openly
deny that the Christ before the ages is also God. But Arius and Eunomius say
that God the Word assumed a body only from the Virgin. And Apollinarius adds
to the body also an irrational soul, as though the incarnation of God the Word
had taken place for the sake of the irrational, not for the rational; but the
teaching of the apostles teaches that a perfect man was assumed by perfect
God; for the saying, "Who being in the form of God took the form of a
servant," makes this clear; for form is set down instead of nature and
essence; for it makes clear that having the nature of God, he took the nature
of a servant. Wherefore, conversing with the first inventors of impiety,
Marcion and Manes and Valentinus, we are eager to show from the divine
Scriptures that the Master Christ is not only God, but also man. And making
clear to those who do not know the incompleteness of Arius and Eunomius and
Apollinarius concerning the economy, we declare from the divine oracles of the
Spirit that the nature that was taken is perfect. And we refute the impiety of
Sabellius and Photinus and Marcellus and Paul, using the divine Scripture as
witness and showing that the Master Christ is not man only, but also God
before the ages and consubstantial with the Father. For that he assumed a
rational soul, the Lord himself teaches, saying: "Now is my soul troubled, and
what shall I say? Father, save me from this hour; but for this I came to this
hour." And again: "My soul is very sorrowful even unto death." And elsewhere:
"I have authority to lay down my soul, and I have authority to take it again;
no one takes it from me." And the angel to =83.1425= Joseph: "Take the child
and his mother, and go into the land of Israel; for all who were seeking the
soul of the child have died." And the evangelist: "And Jesus advanced in
stature, and in wisdom, and in grace with God and men." But it is not the
Godhead, which is always perfect, that advances in stature and wisdom, but the
human nature, which both comes to be in time, and grows, and is perfected.

For this reason we say that all the human things of the Master Christ, hunger
I mean and thirst and weariness, and sleep, and fear, and sweats, and prayer,
and ignorance, and such things, belong to our firstfruits, which God the Word
assumed and united to himself, working our salvation. But the running of the
lame, and the resurrection of the dead, and the fountains of the loaves, and
the change of the water into wine, and all the other wonder-workings we
believe to be works of the divine power. So that I say the same one, namely
the Master Christ, both suffers and looses sufferings; he suffers according to
what is seen, and he looses the sufferings according to the ineffably
indwelling Godhead. And the history of the holy Gospels also makes this clear.
And we learn from there that, lying in a manger and wrapped in swaddling
clothes, he was proclaimed by a star, and worshipped by magi, and hymned by
angels, and we discern piously that the rags, and swaddling clothes, and the
want of a bed, and the great lowliness, are proper to the humanity. But the
course of the magi and the guiding of the star and the choir of the angels
proclaim the Godhead of the one hidden. Thus he flees away into Egypt, and by
the flight is delivered from the madness of Herod; for he was also man. But he
shakes, according to the Prophet, the handmade things of Egypt; for he was
God. He is circumcised and keeps the law and offers purifying sacrifices; for
he has sprouted from the root of Jesse. And he was under the law as man; and
he loosed the law after these things, and has given the new covenant; for he
was a lawgiver, and he himself promised through the prophets that he would
give this.

He was baptized by John; this shows what is ours. He is testified from above
by the Father, and is pointed out by the Spirit; this proclaims the one before
the ages. He hungered, but he also filled many thousands from five loaves;
this is divine, that is human. He thirsted and asked for water, but he was a
fountain of life; and the one was of human weakness, the other of divine
power. He slept in the boat, but he also put to sleep the storm of the sea;
this of the passible nature, that of the productive and creative nature that
has granted being to all things. He grew weary having walked, but he also made
the lame sound of foot, and raised the dead from the tombs; and the one was of
the supermundane power, the other of our weakness. He feared death, and he
loosed =83.1428= death; and the one is indicative of the mortal, the other
happens to be significant of the immortal, or rather of the life-giving. He
was crucified, according to the blessed Paul, from weakness; but he lives from
the power of God, according to the same. Let the name of weakness teach that
it was not the almighty, and uncircumscribed, and unchangeable and inalterable
who was nailed, but the nature that was made alive from the power of God,
according to the teaching of the Apostle, died and was buried; both belong to
the form of the servant. He crushed gates of bronze and shattered bars of
iron, and he destroyed the power of death, and in three days he raised his own
temple; these are the marks of the form of God, according to the voice of the
Lord: "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up." Thus in the
one Christ through the passions we behold the humanity, and through the
wonders we understand his Godhead. For we do not divide the two natures into
two Christs. And we know that God the Word was begotten from the Father, and
our firstfruits was assumed from the seed of Abraham and David. Wherefore also
the blessed Paul says, conversing about Abraham: "He did not say, And to your
seeds, as of many, but as of one, And to your seed, who is Christ." And
writing to Timothy, "Remember," he says, "Jesus Christ raised from the dead,
from the seed of David, according to my Gospel." And writing to the Romans:
"Concerning his Son," he says, "who came to be from the seed of David
according to the flesh." And again: "Whose are the fathers and from whom is
the Christ according to the flesh." And the evangelist: "The book of the
generation of Jesus Christ, son of David, son of Abraham." And the blessed
Peter in the Acts, "David," he says, "being a prophet, and knowing that God
swore to him with an oath that from the fruit of his loins he would raise up
the Christ and seat him upon his throne, foreseeing he spoke concerning his
resurrection." And God says to Abraham: "In your seed shall all the nations of
the earth be blessed." And Isaiah also: "A rod shall come forth from the root
of Jesse, and a flower shall rise up from the root, and there shall rest upon
him a spirit of wisdom and understanding, a spirit of counsel and strength, a
spirit of knowledge and piety, a spirit of the fear of God shall fill him."
And a little later, "And there shall be," he says, "the root of Jesse and he
who rises to rule nations; upon him nations shall hope; and his rest shall be
honor." It is clear therefore from what has been said that according to the
flesh the Christ was a descendant of Abraham and David, and was clothed with
the same nature as they, but according to the Godhead he is the Son of God
before the ages and Word, begotten from the Father unspeakably and beyond man,
and co-eternal as radiance and impress and Word. For as word toward =83.1429=
mind and radiance toward the light have an inseparable relation, so the
only-begotten Son toward his own Father.

We say therefore that our Lord Jesus Christ is only-begotten Son of God and
firstborn; only-begotten both before the incarnation and after the
incarnation; but firstborn after the birth from a Virgin; for the name
Firstborn seems somehow to be contrary to the Only-begotten, because
only-begotten is the one called who alone has been begotten from someone, but
firstborn is the first of many brothers. And the divine Scriptures say that
God the Word alone has been begotten from the Father; but the Only-begotten
also becomes firstborn, having taken our nature from the Virgin, and having
deigned to call brothers those who have believed in him; so that the same one
is only-begotten insofar as he is God, and firstborn insofar as he is man.
Thus we, confessing the two natures, worship the one Christ, and offer him one
worship. For we believe that the union took place from the very conception in
the holy womb of the Virgin. Wherefore we also name the holy Virgin both
Mother of God and Mother of man. Since the Master Christ himself is called God
and man by the divine Scripture. And Emmanuel also proclaims the union of the
two natures. And if we confess and say that the Christ is God and man, who is
so simple as to flee the term Mother of man, set together with Mother of God?
For in the Master Christ we set the two appellations; wherefore the Virgin has
been honored and was called full of grace; what person of sound mind then
would refuse, from the names of the Savior, to name the Virgin, who on account
of that one is honored by the faithful? For it is not he who is from her who
is venerable on account of her, but she is adorned with the greatest
appellations on account of the one from her. If therefore the Christ is God
only, and has taken the beginning of being from the Virgin, then from this let
the Virgin be named only and called Mother of God as having begotten God by
nature. But if the Christ is God and man, and the one always was (for he did
not begin to be; for he is co-eternal with the one who begot him), and the
other in the last times sprouted from the human nature, then from both sides
let the one who wishes to dogmatize weave the appellations for the Virgin,
making clear which belong to the nature, and which to the union. But if
someone should wish to speak panegyrically, and to weave hymns, and to go
through praises, and wishes of necessity to use the more august names, not
dogmatizing as I said, but celebrating and wondering as much as possible at
the greatness of the mystery, let him enjoy his longing, and use the great
names, and praise and wonder. For we find many such things among the orthodox
teachers; but everywhere let the moderate be honored. For I praise the one who
said that the moderate is best, even if he is not of our =83.1432= flock.

This is the confession of the ecclesiastical faith; this is the doctrine of
the evangelical and apostolic teaching. For this we will not refuse to die
thrice and many times, the grace of God clearly cooperating. These things we
were also eager to teach those now being led astray; and many times we
challenged them to discussion, being eager to show them the truth, and we have
not persuaded. For suspecting the obviousness of the refutations they fled the
contests; for falsehood is truly rotten, and yoked with darkness. "For
everyone," he says, "who does worthless things does not come to the light,
lest his works be made manifest by the light." Since therefore, having toiled
much, we have not persuaded them to recognize the truth, we have returned to
our own Churches, being both downcast and rejoicing: rejoicing on account of
our own freedom from error, and downcast on account of the rot of our members.
Wherefore I exhort your holiness, earnestly to entreat our Master who loves
mankind, and to cry to him: Spare, Lord, your people, and do not give your
inheritance to reproach. Shepherd us, Lord, that we may not become as at the
beginning when you did not rule us, nor was your name called upon us. See,
Lord, that we have become a reproach to our neighbors, a sneer and a mockery
to those around us; because wicked doctrines have entered into the
inheritance. They have defiled your holy temple, because the daughters of
aliens have rejoiced over our evils. Because we have been divided into many
tongues who formerly both thought alike and spoke alike. Lord our God, give us
peace which we have lost, having neglected your commandments. Lord, besides
you we know no other; we name your name; make the two one, and dissolve the
middle wall of the partition, the impiety that has sprung up. Gather us one by
one, your new Israel, building Jerusalem and gathering the dispersions of
Israel. Let us become again one flock, and let us all be shepherded by you;
for you are the good shepherd, who has laid down his soul for the sheep.
Awake, why do you sleep, Lord? Arise and do not thrust us away unto the end;
rebuke the winds and the sea; and give calm to your Church and deliverance
from waves. These things and such things I exhort your piety to cry to the God
of all; for being good and a lover of mankind, and always doing the will of
those who fear him, he will hear your petition, and will scatter the present
gloom, gloomier than the Egyptian plague, and will grant his own dear calm,
and will gather those who have been scattered, and will receive those who have
been thrust away. And again there shall be heard a =83.1433= voice of gladness
and of salvation in the tents of the righteous. Then we too shall cry to him:
"We have been gladdened in return for the days in which you humbled us, the
years in which we saw evils." And you, having obtained the request, hymning
him shall say: "Blessed is God who has not turned away our prayer, and his
mercy from us. To him be the glory unto the ages. Amen."
