HOMILY III.

=63.473=

Of the same. On the following day, when the emperor had come to the
martyr-shrine of the apostle and martyr Thomas which is situated at Drypia,
and had withdrawn before the discourse, the homily was delivered after his
departure to the multitude.

αʹ. Blessed be God: how great are the powers of the martyrs! Yesterday they
drew the whole city here with the empress, today the emperor with the army,
with much piety, not by putting on bonds, but by the chain of love, a chain
that is never broken. For this is the marvel, not that an emperor has come,
but that he has come with much eagerness, not by necessity but by choice, not
granting a favor but receiving a favor; and he who benefits all throughout the
inhabited world came to enjoy benefaction from these saints, and to reap the
greatest goods. For this reason both he himself laid aside the diadem, and all
the bodyguards, some their shields, others their spears, and leaving that
display behind, all came with a settled mind, as if entering from earth into
heaven, where ranks and splendor and all this stage of dignities are put
aside, and only the display of life shines forth, and the fruits of virtue.
And if here the power of the martyrs is so great, consider how great it is in
the heavens; if in the time of contests and wrestlings their honors are such,
in the time of the recompense how great will the prizes be? For our own
affairs too do not come to an end with the present, but we are journeying
toward another life better than the present, and we walk toward longer hopes
and an immortal enjoyment of goods that have no end.

For God divided our life into these two ages, and he made the present one
toilsome, and the one to come sweet and unfading, so that having toiled a
little here we may enjoy the unfading crowns there. For he wished even from
the very beginning to lead us at once to that life, but we did not allow it,
working much delay by sloth and through ingratitude constructing this long
postponement. And this I will try to make clear from the things that happened
to us from the beginning. For such is God: when he is about to give us
something good, even if we appear unworthy of his munificence, nevertheless he
shows through all things that he himself wished it, and that falling away from
what he wished came about through our sloth. This then he did also from the
beginning. For when he was fashioning man, he did not fashion him at once with
toils, with hardship, with grief; he did not make him mortal, but he was still
outside of despondency and sweats and death. For if from the beginning he had
allotted these to him, he would not later, after the transgression, have
condemned him to them in the rank of punishment and vengeance. Having come
into being, then, without these, he made him shine above the sun, naked of
garments, but clothed with glory. And this too was the greatest sign of his
blessedness, that he needed neither garments, nor covering, nor any other such
wrapping, but had a body above the need that comes from these. And he was
blessed not only in this, but also because even before this he enjoyed
converse with God, and luxuriated in the freedom of speech toward him
=63.474=. And angels trembled, and the Cherubim and the Seraphim did not dare
even to look; but he spoke as friend with friend. For when he made the kinds
of the irrational creatures, he brought them to him, and he himself set names
on all, and the names remained unmoved. And this too is the greatest proof of
the honor from God, not that he commanded him to set the names, but that, when
man moved the law given to him, God did not move the honor which he had given
him before the law, but Whatever Adam called, that was its name. Did you see
how there was no symbol of the present life there? no crafts, no trade, no
buildings, no clothes, no shoes, no roof, no table, no toil, no grief, no
death, no swarm of the other passions, but splendid the preludes, and bright
the gateways, and the beginning journeying toward a better life.

But Adam did not allow it, by unspeakable sloth overstepping the bounds of
what was commanded, and not even enduring to keep from one tree; yet even so
the loving-kindness of God is shown to be great. For such is God's custom:
when we fall away from something through sloth, not to withdraw before doing
and contriving everything, until he leads us back to things far greater than
those from which we fell; which indeed happened then also. For we fell from
paradise, and we received heaven; therefore the transaction became greater
than the loss. But we did not receive it at once; and this too is of his care.
For since the enemy said, You shall be as gods, and puffed them up with these
hopes, and prepared them to expect equality with God, and anointed them toward
madness, and made them take a thought greater than their own nature: healing
this wound from a great abundance of means, he at once lets death linger, so
that by experience they might learn the devil's counsel, and the soul, well
schooled, might keep measure, and then receive back an immortal body. For the
time being, however, he wished the fear of death to be in full force in the
mind, and the thing to appear fearful.

βʹ. For this reason he also allowed Abel to die first, so that the one who had
transgressed might learn by the very sight what death is, and how heavy and
burdensome a thing. For if he himself had died first, he would not have known
the nature of the thing, never having seen another dead; but now, himself
living, and seeing death ruling in another body, that of his child, he learned
more exactly and more clearly the magnitude of the punishment. For this reason
not simply in a stranger's body, but in that of his child he saw it, where
along with the nature of the thing the tyranny made the pain harsher, and
along with the nature the virtue of the child, and along with the virtue the
flower of his age; for not in old age, but in the very prime of youth he
received the end; and besides all these, to suffer these things from a
brother, and from a brother lawlessly and unjustly. For through all these the
mask of death was made fearful and more painful to the sight, and the pain was
raised more harsh for Adam, each of the things said kindling a great furnace
and making the flame more bitter, and schooling him into how great an evil the
devil had thrown him.

For if we, seeing the dead every day, are troubled, are shaken, shrink back,
and not we =63.475= only, but also those who raise their brows and are in
great dignities and puffed up, if they come to the funeral even of some chance
dead man, shrink at the sight, and become humbler than all: what was that man
likely to suffer, seeing the first dead, and in the body of his child, and of
such a child, and not yet having practiced the passion by the succession of
those who die, but being troubled most of all by the strangeness? What was he
likely to suffer, seeing one who perceived neither voice, nor touch, nor
tears, nor wailings, who did not move, who did not share the pain with the
mourning father, who did not do the customary things? Do not look at the
present condition, but consider that then for the first time man saw that dead
one, and he was filled with much dread and much confusion. Yet even with this
God also devised the things of consolation; for he did not wish only the fear
of death to be increased, but that man should also reap some comfort. What
then was this? That from the resurrection. But he did not give it at once; yet
at once he opened its hopes dimly and as in a riddle. For when the fear had
been well increased, and had shaken the mind of man, and it had been shown
what death was, how heavy and burdensome and oppressive, not only from the
sight that then took place, but also from the things after the sight, the
worms, the stench, the ichor, the ash, and all the other things that follow a
dead body: when therefore the fear had been increased, and had shaken the
mind, see how he then also introduces the hope from the resurrection, dim and
unclear, yet he introduces it nonetheless. For Enoch, who came after these
things, he did not let die; and Paul says: By faith Enoch was translated that
he should not see death, and he was not found, because God translated him. He
translated him, and did not let him die; yet he did not as yet make him
immortal, so as not to dissolve the fear, but he remains not having died, yet
he is not immortal, but in the mortal body he granted length of life; opening,
as I said, dimly before the resurrection the hopes. For this reason he also
allowed the first righteous man to die; for besides the cause already said,
one would not err in saying this also. What then is this? That of the
resurrection. For since death was not going to rule continually, for this
reason he made its first foundation and first root to be fixed in
righteousness, so that the foundation might become unsound.

For as sin is the food of death, so righteousness is the undoing and
disappearance of death. He therefore allowed the first who was going to die to
be righteous, conversing with us from the preludes, and hinting at good hopes
and showing that he will not let our race remain in death; therefore he also
laid down a weaker foundation for it.

γʹ. Then after Enoch, in the case of Noah, he showed us again in another way a
sight of resurrection. For how was Noah better situated than those who had
died, shut up in so great an abyss and darkness and prison? Yet those
boundless seas, which covered the peaks of the mountains, did not sink him who
was in the ark; but as if rising from death, so Noah then came out from that
long winter, showing the sight of the resurrection in his own salvation. Again
after him (for the more time advanced, the clearer also the images of the
resurrection were shown) he worked this same thing also in the case of Jonah.
For the three days in the whale were a symbol =63.476= of the three days in
death, and the deliverance from the whale was a proclamation beforehand of the
resurrection of the Master's body; therefore he himself also said, This
generation seeks a sign, and a sign shall not be given it, except the sign of
Jonah; for as Jonah was in the belly of the whale three days and three nights,
so shall the Son of man be in the heart of the earth three days and three
nights. And the birth of Isaac too one would not err in calling a type of
resurrection.

For this reason Paul also, having related the things concerning his generation
from the barren woman, and having said that his father against hope believed
in hope, and that Not being weak in faith, he considered his own body as good
as dead and the deadness of Sarah's womb; but at the promise of God he did not
waver in unbelief, but was strengthened in faith, giving glory to God, and
being fully persuaded that what he has promised he is able also to do;
therefore he also added: Now it was not written for his sake alone, but also
for us, to whom it is going to be reckoned, who believe on him who raised
Jesus Christ our Lord from the dead. It is clear then that the birth of Isaac
was a type of the resurrection of Christ. For if it were not a type, he would
not have added and said: Now it was not written for his sake alone, but also
for us, to whom it is going to be reckoned. For types are not written for
those who receive them at that time in which they come to be, but also for
those who are going to profit from them in the time after. Therefore elsewhere
also he says: Now these things happened to them as a type; and they were
written for our admonition, upon whom the ends of the ages have come. And
Elijah too, having been taken up, opened for us this hope of the resurrection.
But those things were as in types; and when the only-begotten Son of God came
after many and boundless times, he showed us the resurrection in the very deed
through his own body, having released it from the tyranny of death; therefore
he also says, Christ having died and been raised from the dead dies no more;
for the death he died to sin, he died once for all.

δʹ. But in the preludes these things came about dimly; for the hope of the
resurrection was shown to us as in a riddle through Enoch, and the dissolution
of death through Abel; but both of these came about clearly and manifestly and
wholly and in truth through the only-begotten Son of God. Whence also
companies of martyrs sprang up for us, death having been dissolved, and the
resurrection shining. For this reason the present life also has become
toilsome and full of many sweats, so that from this too the coarser of men,
who linger among present things, being pushed, and growing numb at the
burdensomeness of this life, may flee pleasures and the sympathy toward the
present life, and may run toward the love of the heavens, and may press on
toward that day. For the philosophic and high soul will not need the
exhortation from this, but having considered how great the kingdom of heaven
is, or rather, before the kingdom, to be with God and to be with Christ (for
this is greater than every kingdom), he will perceive none of the present
=63.477= pleasures, but will dishonor these, passing them by even more than a
shadow. But since many, enslaved to the flesh, and held by the tyranny of
worldly things, like beasts that lurk in dens, linger among them: cutting off
their sympathy toward these things, he allotted to them many grievous things,
and fears and anxieties and cares and agonies and dangers and cowardice, and
the great swarm of bodily passions, and the siege from the bodies, and many
other things which it is not possible to say in the word; so that at least
shuddering at the cloud of these evils, they may desire to sail down into the
waveless harbor, and to enjoy the continual calm, which does not have evils
mixed with the goods, but pure goods, the goods that truly are. For the things
that here seem to be fair, such as wealth and glory and power, are names of
fair things and mixed with their opposites; but the things there are unmixed
and pure, having blessedness not in names but in facts. Therefore, that we may
obtain these, let us emulate the virtue of the martyrs, the courage, the zeal,
=63.478= the faith, the contempt of present things, the desire of things to
come. For it is possible to accomplish all these even without persecution. For
even if no pyre is set forth, yet a more vehement desire is present; even if
there are no teeth of beasts, yet a wrath more harsh than a beast is laid upon
us; even if executioners do not stand by scraping the sides, yet envy is
present, eating the mind more harshly than any executioner.

We must therefore strip for these passions, and walling them off with the
strength that comes from philosophic reasonings, so pass through the present
life, and be in the contest through all our life; so that having toiled a
little time, we may be crowned continually, and enjoy the unfading goods, and
being always with the Lord, and enjoying that companionship which surpasses
all word and thought; of which may it be that we all partake, by the grace and
loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom and with whom to the
Father be glory, together with the Holy Spirit, now and always, and unto the
ages of ages. Amen.
