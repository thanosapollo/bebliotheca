HOMILY 24.
Against the Sabellians, and Arius, and the Anomoeans.

Judaism wars with Hellenism, and both with Christianity, as the Egyptians and
the Assyrians were enemies to one another and to Israel; so too in wickedness
we find cowardice and rashness set against one another, and both against
manliness. Such is the battle that lies on either side against the orthodoxy
of the confession: on this side that of Sabellius, on the other that of those
who preach the unlike. But we, as we fled the Greeks, and were turned aside
from their wicked idolatry, and condemned their polytheism to be godlessness,
so also fled the blasphemy of the Jews, who deny the Son of God, fearing that
threat: Whoever denies me before men, I also will deny him before my Father
who is in the heavens. Let us flee then, as is fitting, also those who hunt
out things akin to these against the word of truth. For since the devil, wise
to do evil, saw the estrangement of the Christians toward Greeks and Jews, and
that straightway from the names themselves we stand toward them in hostility,
laying our own name upon both, he attempts again to bring in the Jewish denial
and the Greek polytheism. For those who say the Only-begotten to be a work of
God and a thing made, and then worship and =31.601= confess him as God, from
the worshiping of the creature and not of the Creator plainly bring in the
things of the Greeks; and those who deny the God from God, confessing the Son
in name, but setting aside his existence in deed and in truth, renew Judaism
again. For when they confess Word, they liken him to the word within us; and
calling him Wisdom, they say he is like the faculty formed in the soul of
those educated; and therefore one person of Father and Son, since man too is
said to be one, not divided from the reason and the wisdom indwelling in him.
And yet the evangelist cries out straightway in the introductions: And the
Word was God, giving the Son his own existence. For if the Word were in the
heart, how would he be understood as God? and how would he be with God? Since
neither is the word in a man a man, nor is it said to be with him, but in him.
For it is neither living, nor subsistent; but the Word of God is life and
truth. And our word, once spoken, is not; but concerning the Word of God what
does the psalm say? Forever, O Lord, your word abides in heaven. On this side
then is this war; but who and what is the struggle on the other side against
the truth? They grant the existence, and put together that the Son has his own
person, and the Father his own; but they introduce an unlikeness of nature.
And they grant the name of Son as far as the sound, but in truth they bring it
down to the creature, not being persuaded by the voice of the Lord, showing
himself beforehand to him who hastens to the contemplation of the Father. He
who has seen me, he says, has seen the Father. This voice, for those reasoning
rightly, bars the blasphemies of both. For he does not claim to be the Father
himself, seeing he plainly divides the persons in saying: He who has seen me.
For he shows from this his own proper person: has seen the Father, referring
to the paternal person, and plainly dividing from himself, and in saying: If
you had known me, you would have known my Father also. For these things do not
show a confusion of persons, but present the unvarying likeness of the
Godhead. And let the opponents hear the same word: that he who was thought
worthy of the communion of the

Son is not deprived of the Father. For not something else did the begetter
beget, but such as he himself is. Hear, O Anomoean: I and the Father are one.
Hear also you, O Sabellian: I =31.604= went out from the Father, and am going
to him. And let each of you heal his own wound from the evangelical teaching.
And do you take the unity for the unvarying likeness of nature; and do you
take the, From him I went out, and I go to him, for the distinction of the
persons. Let us meet then upon these things, and make peace, and lay down the
long war against piety, casting away these worn-out weapons of impiety, and
reforging the spears into ploughs and the swords into sickles. And do not you
merely say, but follow him who says: I am not alone, because the Father who
sent me is with me. Another then is the Father who sent, and another the Son
who was sent. And again, I, he says, bear witness of myself, and the Father
who sent me bears witness of me. And, In your law, he says, it is written that
the testimony of two men is true. Count the persons, if you will. I, he says,
am he who bears witness. One. And the Father who sent me bears witness of me.
Two. And not I so boldly number, but the Lord himself taught, saying: In your
law it is written that of two men the testimony is true. And you, who war
against God in another form of impiety, saying the Son to be unlike God by
nature, not granting the equality, implanting a separation of life, be
persuaded by Paul saying: Who is the image of the invisible God; and grant to
the living image to hold unalterably toward the archetypal life. Confess the
Son to have a Father, not to be a creature; a maker, not a thing made. And in
the true confession of the Father grant to the begotten equality of honor with
him, remembering the testimony of the evangelist, that he called God his own
Father, making himself equal to God. But the being equal to him who begat is
understood according to nature, not by the measuring of bodily magnitude. And
how did he not count being equal to God a thing seized by robbery, if, as you
blaspheme, he was never equal? And how was he in the form of God who,
according to your word, was never like? But such is the war against us from
either side; and what is the truth? And do not you fear the confession of the
persons, but say Father, say also Son, not uttering two names over one thing,
but teaching a proper conception from each appellation. For it is a grievous
ungraciousness not to receive the teachings of the Lord, who distinctly
=31.605= distinguishes for us the difference of the persons. If I go away, he
says, I will pray the Father, and he will send another Comforter to you.
Therefore the Son is the one who prays, the Father the one prayed to, and the
Comforter the one sent. Do you then not plainly act shamelessly, hearing, I
concerning the Son, That one concerning the Father, Another concerning the
Holy Spirit, and mixing all things, and confounding all things, and putting
all the appellations upon one thing? But do not you too take the division of
the persons as a prize for impiety. For if they are two in number, yet in
nature they are not divided; nor does he who says two introduce an
estrangement. One God, because also Father; and one God also the Son, and not
two gods, since the Son has identity with the Father. For I behold not another
Godhead in the Father, and another in the Son; nor another nature that one,
and another this one. That then the propriety of the persons be made plain to
you, number a proper Father, and a proper Son; and that you be not split off
into polytheism, confess one essence over both. Thus too Sabellius falls, and
the Anomoean will be dashed in pieces. And when I say one essence, do not
conceive two divided from one, but the Son subsisting from the beginning from
the Father, not Father and Son from one essence lying above. For we do not
speak of brothers, but we confess Father and Son. And the

identity of the essence, since the Son is from the Father, not having been
made by command, but begotten from the nature; not cut off from the Father,
but having shone forth perfect, the perfect remaining. And do not run round
about me, as many as either did not follow perfectly the things said, or have
stood round us for provocation, not seeking to receive anything profitable
from us, but watching to lay hold of something of the things said, that you
may say: He preaches two gods, he proclaims polytheism. Not two gods: for
neither two fathers. He who introduces two beginnings preaches two gods. Such
is Marcion, and whoever is like to him in impiety. And again, he who says the
begotten to be of another essence than him who begat, this one too says two
gods, introducing polytheism through the unlikeness of essence. For if one
Godhead is unbegotten, and one begotten, you are he who preaches polytheism,
saying the unbegotten to be contrary to the begotten, and of course setting
the essences contrary: if indeed the unbegottenness is the essence of the
Father, and the generation the essence of the Son. So that you say not only
two gods, but also gods warring with one another; and most dreadfully, giving
the battle not by choice, but by a natural separation, which can never come
into a peaceful accord. But the word of truth has fled clear of the
contradictions on either side. For where the beginning is one, and the thing
from it one, and the =31.608= archetype one, and the image one, the word of
unity is not destroyed. That the Son, subsisting from the Father begottenly,
and naturally stamping the Father in himself, as image has the unvarying
likeness, and as offspring preserves the consubstantial. For neither does he
who gazes at the royal image in the marketplace, and calls the one in the
picture king, confess two kings, both the image and him of whom it is the
image; nor, if, pointing to the one painted in the picture, he say: This is
the king, does he deprive the archetype of the appellation of king. Rather he
confirmed the honor to him through this confession. For if the image is king,
much more of course is he king who afforded the cause to the image. But here
wood, and wax, and the painter's art make the image corruptible, a mimic of
the corruptible, and artificial of the one who made it; there, when you hear
image, conceive the radiance of the glory. And what is the radiance, and what
is the glory? The Apostle himself straightway interpreted it, adding: And the
character of his subsistence. Therefore the subsistence is the same with the
glory, and the character with the radiance. So that, the glory remaining
perfect and being diminished in nothing, the radiance comes forth perfect. And
thus the word of image, received in a god-fitting manner, shows us the unity
of the Godhead. For this one is in that one, and that one in this one. That
this one is such as that one, and that one such as this one. Thus the two are
united, by the Son not differing, nor being conceived in another kind and a
strange character. Again then I say: One and one, but the nature indivisible,
and the perfection unshortened. One God therefore, since through both one form
is beheld, shown whole in both. But indeed I long ago see you disliking the
word, and I almost seem to hear that, dwelling on the things confessed, I do
not touch the much-bruited questions. For every ear is now stirred to the
hearing of the words concerning the Holy Spirit. But I would most of all, as I
received simply, as I committed without curiosity, so to hand on to those who
hear; not always being put to give an account about the same things, but
having those taught persuaded from one confession. But since you have
surrounded us, judges rather =31.609= than disciples, wishing to test us, not
yourselves seeking to receive anything, we are compelled as in a courtroom to
set forth the reply, and always to be examined, and always to say what we
received. But we entreat you not by every means to seek the

thing pleasing to you to hear from us, but what is well-pleasing to the Lord,
and according to the Scriptures, and not warring against the Fathers. What
therefore we were saying concerning the Son, that one must confess his own
proper person, these things we have to say also concerning the Holy Spirit.
For the Spirit is not the same with the Father, since it is written that God
is Spirit. Nor again is there one person of Son and Spirit, since it is said:
But if anyone has not the Spirit of Christ, this one is not of him; and Christ
is in you. Hence some were deceived that Spirit and Christ are the same. But
what do we say? That the proper thing of the nature is here shown, not a
confusion of persons. For the Father is, having the being perfect and lacking
nothing, root and fountain of the Son and of the Holy Spirit; and the Son is
the living Word in the fullness of Godhead, and offspring of the Father
lacking nothing; and the Spirit also is full, not a part of another, but
perfect and whole beheld in itself. And the Son is joined to the Father
indivisibly; and the Spirit is joined to the Son. For there is nothing that
divides, nor cuts the connection from eternity. For no age intervenes between;
nor does our soul admit the conceit of a separation, as though the Only-
begotten were not always with the Father, or the Holy Spirit not co-existing
with the Son. When then at some time we join the Trinity, do not imagine the
three as parts of one indivisible thing (for the reckoning is impious), but
receive the inseparable association of three bodiless perfect things. For
where is the presence of the Holy Spirit, there also is the sojourn of Christ;
and where Christ, there also of course is the Father present. Know you not
that your bodies are a temple of the Holy Spirit in you? And, If anyone
corrupts the temple of God, him God will corrupt. Being sanctified then
through the Spirit, we receive Christ dwelling in us in the inner man, and
with Christ the Father, making the dwelling common with the worthy. And this
connection the tradition of baptism also shows, and the confession of the
faith. For if it were alien in nature, how was the Spirit numbered with them?
And if it came later in times to Father and Son, how was it joined with the
eternal nature? And those who divide Father and Son, and number the Spirit
with the creation, make the baptism imperfect, and the confession of the faith
deficient. For neither does the =31.612= Trinity remain a Trinity, the Spirit
being taken away. And if again one thing more from the creation were added,
the whole creation would enter in with into the numbering with Father and Son.
For what hinders saying: We believe into Father and Son and into all the
creation? For if it is pious to believe into a part of the creation, much more
of course is it seemly to take the whole creation into the confession. But
believing into the whole creation, you believe not only into angels and the
ministrant spirits, but also, if there be any opposing powers, since these too
are a part of the creation, you will join covenant with them according to the
faith. Thus the blasphemy against the Spirit leads you around into impious and
lawless words. For at once you said the things that must not be said
concerning the Spirit, and the abandonment of the Spirit was signified to you.
For as he who shuts his eyes has darkness as his own, so he who has withdrawn
from the Spirit, having come to be outside the one who illumines, is
confounded by the unseeing in the soul. But do not separate the Holy Spirit
from Father and Son; let the tradition persuade you. Thus the Lord taught,
apostles preached, Fathers preserved, martyrs confirmed. Be content to say as
you were taught, and not these wise things to me: It is unbegotten, or
begotten. For if unbegotten, Father; if begotten, Son; and if neither of
these, creature. But I know the Spirit with the Father, not the Spirit as
Father; and I received it with the Son, not named Son. But I perceive the
relation to the Father, since it proceeds from the Father; and that to the
Son, since I

hear: But if anyone has not the Spirit of Christ, this one is not of him. For
if it is not proper to Christ, how does it make proper to Christ? But I hear
also Spirit of the truth. And the truth is the Lord. And when I hear Spirit of
adoption, I come to the conception of the unity by nature toward Son and
Father. For how does he adopt the strange, how make proper the alien? Thus
then I neither make new words, nor set aside the dignity; but those daring to
address it creature I groan and lament, that through a small sophism and a
vile sophistry they thrust themselves upon the pit. For since, he says, these
three our mind has grasped, and there is nothing among beings which does not
fall under this division of beings. Either unbegotten, he says, or begotten,
or created; but neither the first, nor the second, therefore the third. This
your therefore will make you liable to the curse of the =31.613= age to come.
Have you sifted all things? Have you brought all things under division by your
own reasonings? Have you left nothing unexamined? Have you comprehended all
things with the mind? Have you shut in all things with your own grasp? Do you
know the things under the earth? Do you know the things in the deep? Boastings
of demons: I know the number of the sand and the measures of the sea. But if
you are ignorant of many things, and the things unknown are myriads of times
the things known, why do you not with all confess shamelessly also concerning
the manner of the existence of the Holy Spirit the safe ignorance? But I do
not take the leisure to refute the vanity of your reasonings, nor to show how
much in beings your grasp of reasonings has fled past; that I would gladly ask
of those among you, and I determine confidently, that you will sometime repent
of this atheistic wisdom, saying the Holy Spirit a creature. Do you not fear
the unforgivable sin? Or what do you think can be blasphemed more impiously
than this? For from this one voice all the most dreadful things have set in:
estrangement from God in the very nature, the lowering of slavery, ministrant
services, the loss of sanctification, of the thing not present by nature; and
it will be girt about upon the Spirit, to partake of sanctification thus from
the distribution by grace, as also the rest have, whatever things have been
sanctified. And as the manifestation of the Spirit is given us for the profit,
and as it is measured to each according to the analogy of the faith, so it
will be also the Spirit partaking of sanctification, if truly created
according to the supposition of the Spirit-fighters. But let us not grant
their madness unchecked, of those supposing all things have been grasped by
their own mind. Let them answer us then. What ever then is the essence of the
sensible sun? Is it one of the four, or is it compounded of the four? But it
is neither earth, nor air, nor water, nor fire. For these move in a straight
line, some borne upward, some downward. For earth and water slope downward
because of weight; and air and fire move in the upward course because of
lightness; but of the sun the motion is circular. It is not then one of the
four. But neither is it compounded of these, because compounds are held
together by weariness, having their constitution from contraries, the
contraries pulling against one another in motion. But the motion of the sun is
unresting, and for this reason =31.616= also unceasing. So it is not
compounded. Further, in bodies something is either simple or compounded. But
the sun is neither a simple body: for it does not move in a straight line; nor
compounded: for it does not grow weary moving. Therefore the sun is not. Such
are your wise divisions, laughed at by those who have eyes. Again, how do we
men see? Whether receiving the impressions of the things seen, or sending
forth a power from ourselves? But neither do we receive the images of the
things seen (for how does the hemisphere of heaven appear through the small
pupil of the eye?), nor indeed do we send anything from ourselves (for how
again does what is sent forth suffice to be spread over the heaven?). If then
neither do we receive the images of the things seen, nor send forth any power
from ourselves, therefore we do not see. What then? Shall I refute your

syllogisms, or shall I pray for you that the conclusions be true? What does
your turning of the Spirit into a craft differ from such reasonings, which you
display to wretched women, or to the eunuchs who are neighbors of the women?
But do not hear as seeking provocation. If the Spirit is from God, how do you
bring it under the creation? For you will not surely say that all things also
are from God. For as Christ is said of God, not a creature as we also are (for
we are Christ's, but Christ is God's), but otherwise we are said of Christ, as
servants of the Master; otherwise Christ is said of God, as Son of the Father;
so also the Spirit, not because all things are from God, is it also as all
things. For not because also the ministrant spirits, straightway is the Holy
Spirit like them by the appellation. For one is the Spirit in truth. For as
many are sons, but one the true Son; so even if all things be said to be from
God, yet properly the Son is from God, and the Spirit from God. Since also the
Son went out from the Father, and the Spirit proceeds from the Father. But the
Son from the Father begottenly, and the Spirit unutterably from God. Behold
then how great is the danger of belittling the glory of the Comforter. The Son
does not receive the honor toward him while the Spirit is set at naught. For
that one, he says, will glorify me, not as a servant with the creation. For if
he glorified together with all, it would not have been said, That one. But now
the reference to the one shows the glory that is brought in by superiority
over the others. For not as those saying: Glory in the highest to God; but as
he who said: Father, I glorified you, the work which you gave me I finished.
And as the Father glorifies the Son, saying: And I glorified you, and again I
will glorify; so also the Son takes the Spirit into the communion of himself
and the Father. Or let someone show me a greater glory than this, and I grant
all things to be true that the contradictors say. He who sets you at naught
sets me at naught. Why? Because of the Spirit dwelling in them of course. So
that =31.617= he who does not honor the Spirit does not honor the Son; and he
who does not honor the Son does not honor the Father. Thus then the
ungraciousness concerning one of the things believed is a denial of the whole
Godhead. If the Spirit is a creature, it is not divine. But yet, he says, the
divine Spirit made me. And, He filled, he says, Bezaleel with a divine Spirit
of wisdom and understanding. Where then do you find the divine drawing near:
in the creation, or in the Godhead? For if in the creation, you will say also
the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ a creature: for it is written concerning
him: His eternal power and divinity; but if in the Godhead, ceasing from the
blasphemy, recognize the dignity of the Spirit. Thus are you senseless, as not
even from the voice itself to be brought to the worthy conceptions of the
Spirit.
