* Preface
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Two youthful brothers, digger and shepherd two,
whom the first of women first brings forth,
stand at the altar from the fruits of their labors
with first-fruits of gifts to be consecrated to God.
This one deals in things of earth, that one in living things;
with rival vow they sacrifice, differing,
one the offspring of a sheep, but the other of the trench.
God approved the victim of the younger,
rejected that which the elder prepared.
Lo, a voice sent from the highest throne rings out:
'Cain, be still; for if you offer rightly,
yet you do not divide the offerings by right law,
perverse vows will have drawn a black guilt.'
Then the brother, rival of proven holiness,
arms a parricidal hand;
he breaks the kindred neck with the curved hoe,
stains the fresh world with impious slaughter,
to be atoned late, already aging, by the sacred
blood of Christ, by which the slayer fell.
First death began from the wound of the innocent,
then yielded when the guiltless was wounded.
Born through crime, it was dissolved by crime,
what first struck Abel, thereafter Christ;
and it itself was ended, seeking one who has no end.

Therefore from things to come the old tale began
and by the first deed the last thing was marked,
that that rustic beginner of death,
bringing tasteless libations of earth
and reckoning God a god of dead things,
should believe things reduced by the rakes worthy of rites,
fierce rival of the living victim.
I recognize, of course, whom this figure denotes,
who the fratricide, who the envious slayer
who wrongly divides the discipline of the sacred,
while he thinks himself to sacrifice vows more rightly.
Marcion, form of the most corrupt field,
teaches that a duality disagrees from the Spirit,
offering gifts of contaminated flesh
and worshipping the eternal Godhead separately.
If he would be still and not neglect the warning,
quiet brotherhood 1 would love peace,
confessing the one and living God of the living.
This man, dedicating himself to a perishable mystery,
profane, divides the highest substance;
evil and good, as of two separated
kingdoms, he commits two scepters of Gods,
believing him to be God whom he confesses worst.
Bloody Cain, envious of unity,
tiller of the world, squalid sacrificer,
whose offering is filthy and tastes of earth,
earth of the perishable body, a rotting vein,
clotted with dense moisture and dust,
whose nature flourishes by fertile fraud,
fruitful, pouring forth crimes of things harmful,
and kills the life of the soul by the stain of flesh.
Flesh directs weapons against the sister mind,
the mind is tossed in a drunken brain,
from which it gathers succulent rages,
dripping with the frantic poison of the body.
He splits the everlasting God into two Gods,
dares to cut the uncuttable Godhead.
She falls slain, denying the one God,
Cain triumphs by the death of the brother's breath. 1

* Section 1
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h2
:END:

Whither does your rage hurl you, faithless Cain,
blasphemous divider of God? Does one creator
not stand clear to you, and do clouds of split light grow dark?
The unsound sight is always scattered through two
partings, frustrated in its seeing among twin figures.
The double form of the lands mocks you, so that, mindless,
you believe a divided God reigns above the aether.
This filthy world heaps up twin distinctions of good
and evil, but heaven obeys one Lord.
Heavenly things do not therefore hold two kings
because there are two works that stir human cares.
The outer man is earthly, who, seeing such things,
conjectures that there are two godheads of varied things.
While he thinks there is a God who once fashioned the crooked,
and one who likewise, founding, brought in the right, he asserts
both to be highest Gods of unlike nature.
What nature, I ask, can stand double
or reign long, which a fount divided from the citadel
separates, and cuts back by alternate rule of the peaks?
Either there is one God, to whom belongs the highest power of things,
or those which are already two are lessened by unlike height,
further nothing is highest except one with full powers,
since, being distant, while each claims his own by a torn-away
empire, they can hold neither the highest nor all things.
Varied right is not full, because the one does not have
whatever the unlike has; distinction plucks the heap.
We testify God full without part and one,
in whom Christ is, himself also full and one,
who lives and has lived above all things and who will live,
having suffered no partner by a conferred compact.

The highest of powers is the Father, 1 domination of things,
sublime head of virtues, unique fount of the world,
natural peak, author of race and of origin;
from whom all things flow, both light and times and years
and number, who after something gave a second to be;
for one is the first of number, nor can only one
be numbered. Thus, since the Father is not another God,
likewise Christ is not second to the Begetter,
he is prior in number, to whom, being one, there is a unique Son.
He is God, and deservedly God, because first and one,
first in his own power, then first in him
whom he begot; for what does a simple generation differ?
The begetting will always be one, and one begotten from one
before chaos, free of number and of time.
Who would dare to have called two a thing reigning under one
majesty, proper to itself and everlasting backward,
and to cut out the forces of one nature?
Did the Begetter take to himself an adopted one, so that another
of external race should furnish the number of two
and, being distant, introduce a twin godhead?
The true Son stands as form of the true Father and rightly
proves himself one while he keeps the same form.
Not an assumed love associates nor a sworn faith
joins both, but sure piety and one race,
which is God, is recalled to one height.

This way displeases you, Marcion, this faith your
sect damns, having parted heaven for two lords.
What clouds confound you? What sleep
lies on a sluggish talent, to which through phantasms a double
appearance meets, scattered above on a two-way path?
If dull stupor besieges the force of the mind, look at least
at the elements meeting earthly eyes, by which
the hidden thing of God has deigned to show itself in signs.
The foreseeing majesty of the Father had once foreseen
this heresy: that there would be one who would split the ruler
of light and of the world into parts, twinned by a separate kingdom.
Therefore he set a visible specimen and example
for our eyes, lest anyone believe two godheads
command, through the wandering empty forms of the world. 1
One flame through the immense hollow of heaven furnishes
the revolving days; one unique sun weaves the year;
yet he, triple, with no distinction, supported by a threefold
reason, lives, shines, flies, burns,
is driven by motion, burns with heat, then flashes with light.
There are three, of course, at once, light and heat and vigor,
yet one and the same wheel of the star performs these
undivided, keeps so many offices in one course,
and one substance underlies the three things mixed.
I would not dare to compare to God anything as if
equatable, nor to set a servant sign beside the Lord;
but the Father himself willed men to conjecture his great
from the least, for whom it is not to see the steep things.
In the mirror of small things we mark what is not understood,
and it is given to seek the hidden true through things nearest.
No one has seen two suns except under a cataract,
or, if a dark cloak has poured over the serene pole,
whenever an opposing cloud, with fire struck back,
scatters the darts of rays into lying orbs.
Souls too have their own clouds, and a thick air;
there is a cataract, which veils the sight with a watery covering,
lest free meditation penetrate the tender heaven
or grasp with swift senses the one God.
The unsound intent is scattered into split lights,
and builds double altars for twin authors.

If there are two, why then should there not be many thousands
of Gods? Why is Deity content with a twin number?
Or was it not better to pour swarms of Divinities
scattered among the peoples and fill the capacious world
with half-gods, monsters everywhere with no distinction,
to whom wild barbarism slays honors about to perish?
If discordant godheads hold a divided heaven,
it is fitting also to assign to clouds and founts and the resounding
ocean and woods and hills and caves,
rivers, winds, furnaces and metals
their own Gods, each his own rights.
Or, if it is filthy to venerate gentile shades
and it pleases that there be two equal with companion scepters,
come, say, who holds the lands by lot of rule,
who rules the sea-storms by eternal law,
set forth the distinct right of the coheir lords.

'One,' you say, 'sits high in a grim citadel,
author of wickedness, God of crimes, harsh, unjust,
who sowed whatever evil boils in the vicious world,
and, medicating new seeds with snaky juice,
drew the beginning of things from the tinder of death.
He himself, craftsman of the world, made earth, sea, stars,
he himself founded man and packed muddy members,
fashioning what disease may eat, what with much crime
may grow filthy, what a mound may dissolve with shapeless rot.
But to the other belongs love of piety and a mild genius
of healing, restoring men, saving mortal things.
Two Testaments flowed from either prince:
this better one handed over the new, that bitter one the old.'
This, Marcion, is your grave and dialectical voice,
nay, this is the manifest frenzy of a stunned brain.

We know there is a father of crimes, but we know him
yet by no means to be God, nay rather a slave
of gehenna, who is to be damned to Stygian Avernus,
the Marcionite God, grim, wild, lurker,
high of peak, whose snake-bearing head is girt
with black clouds and packed with smoke and fire.
Envy, impatient to bear the joys of the just,
pours over his livid eyes with burned gall.
A dense mane covers his shaggy shoulders with wandering hydras
and green horned snakes lick his face.
He himself with his hand draws back snares bent through
slippery threads into a knot, and with easy binding
weaves twisted fetters and stretches sinews into bonds.
His art is to catch wild beasts, to net brute animals
with toils, finally to set unseen ties in blind
places against the wandering prey.
This is that fierce hunter, who with frequent slaughter
does not cease to strike unwary souls, Nebroth,
who with cunning craft circles the world bristling
with curved windings and wooded crags,
having set about to bind some with fraud and covered wiles,
further to break others by wrestling with giant arms,
to exercise funereal triumphs far and wide.
Shameless death, to what do you not drive mortal breasts?
Man himself (shame, alas!) with the prince of life scorned
venerates his own destruction, himself worships the bloody
butcher and, about to be slain, adores the sword's edge.
To such a point for the wretched, caught by the nectar of sin,
is it sweet to die, so great the pleasure from the plague in darkness!

He who first begot evils is thought to be God,
and he who stained goods with vices and white with black!
Equal is the madness of those whom fame reports
to have consecrated Fever and Scab as goddesses in dedicated shrines.
The inventor of vice is not God: a degenerate angel
created it, conceived in an infamous mind,
who formerly shone as an august star and a huge
splendor from nothing burned with nourished honor.
For all things backward are from nothing, and what is anywhere is made,
but not from nothing God and true Wisdom
and the Holy Spirit, a thing always living and never
begun, but having also wrought airy ministers.
Of their number one most beautiful of face,
fierce in majesty, while increased with excessive powers
he is inflated, while swelling great he lifts himself higher
and displays his fires more boastfully than is allowed,
persuaded himself that he was begotten by his own powers,
had taken matter for himself from himself, by which he first
would begin to be, and that his beginning was born without a prince.
Hence the school meditates to beget a half-silent sect,
which teaches that a tyrant suddenly flashed from darkness,
who as if hiding under eternal night backward
had lived and had always reigned in a covered age.
The rival, as they recount, suddenly put forth his head
in black gloom to corrupt the divine works.
But this our reason denies, to which it is not allowed
to weaken one faith, which has been handed down in the sacred book.

'Nothing,' he says, 'was made without God, but all things through him,
all things, nor is there any other except one made by him.'
But made good from the stock, brought forth for the use
of goodness and serene from the first fount of the race,
soon worse of his own will, while discoloring envy
stains him and goads with bitter stings.
For a spark of hate burned from the tinder of zeal
and sudden grief forged an unjust nature.
He had seen clay, a constructed image, grow warm
with the breath of God, and a lord also set over the
creation, 1 that the nature of soil and sea and pole
might know, serving man, to pour a rich produce
and assign the poured-out to the earthly king.
The beast inflated a swelling with the ferment of a sickening spirit
and, bitter, drew force from acid marrows;
a beast lacking filth, for whom then right wisdom
kept the knotless youth of a long body.
Lo, he folds new knots with a winding breast,
wrapping the shining belly with twisting coils.
The tongue, simple before, flashes with varied art of speaking,
and, split by wiles, resounds with three-forked speech.
Hence the natal head of vices; from that prince
flowed the origin of evil, who first learned to corrupt himself,
soon man, with no master informing.

Last the world, its guardian overthrown, is allotted
ruin, and all the world's gear is shaken.
Not otherwise than when a robber by chance has set out
to strip an unwary traveler, first, unmindful of the prey,
he strikes with the sword the master himself, the knot and delay of the fight,
that, when he perishes, the victor may drag captive garments,
already a rich plunderer from a body no longer opposing.
So the house subjected to man, the richest world,
slipped, of course, into easy ruin when the lord sinned,
and already then, capable of vice, drank in the master's evil.
Then the malignant field dared to bear darnel and light burrs
through adulterated tilth with badly rich clods,
and violated the wheaten crop with empty oats.
Then too fierce lions learned to feed on the innocent
blood of calves, and, the shepherd slain, to tear with the maw
steers already tamed to the yoke,
and the wolf, provoked by complaining bleats,
strove by night to break through the full folds.
Cunning skill imbued every animal with dread theft
and sharpened the twisted senses with deceit.
Although a wall may surround flowering gardens,
and vineyards be fenced with dense hedges,
either the ravaging locust will eat the gemming buds,
or the grape will be torn, shredded by wild birds.
Why should I speak of the fibers of herbs, stained with medicating
poison, having poured the dangers of death-bringing juice?
Lo, a noxious savor boils in tender shrubs,
though nature before had borne harmless hemlocks,
and the dewy flower that clothes green oleander
had given sincere fodder to wanton goats.
The elements themselves also, the sure compact destroyed,
transcend the opposing bound and seize and rush,
all things shaking the world with law-breaking forces.
Battles of the north-winds break the shade-bearing groves,
and the wood falls, uprooted by immoderate storms.
In another part a river, violent with torrential waters,
leaps the opposing banks, the prescribed bars,
and, wandering, lords it far and wide in the overturned fields.
Yet the founder himself did not set so great a rage
in these things at their birth, but a loose license of things
disturbed the quiet laws, the governance broken.

Nor is it wonder if the shaken members of the world are whirled,
if the world's machine, stirred by its own vices, labors,
if an inciting plague wearies the lands:
the life of men gives the example by which the rest may sin,
the life of men, to which whatever madness and error
supply they furnish, that wars may roar, that slack pleasure
may flow away, that lust may boil with impure fire,
that the gulf of avarice may swallow heaps of coins
with wide jaws, which no end of having
tempers, adding vows to piled talents,
for the hunger of gold becomes greater from gold brought forth.
Hence the crop of crimes, and the sole root of evils,
while ornament, pander of dissolving shame, melts out
all the gushes of rivers and hidden metals,
while inept ambition searches the veins of the squalid ground,
scratching the hidden secrets of lurking nature,
if in any pits it may, probing, find shining pebbles.
For woman, not content with inborn grace, feigns
an external form and, as if the craftsman Lord's hand had given
an imperfect face, which the matter still requires either hyacinths
to paint on the height of a brow bound with sewn gems,
or to gird genuine necks with fiery wreaths,
or to hang green berries from heavy ears.
A white pebble of shells is also bound
in shining hair, and the hair stands stiff, woven with golden chains.
It wearies to run through the sacrilegious cares of mothers,
who stain with dye the formations gifted with God's gifts,
so that the skin, smeared with pigments, may lose
what it had been, not to be recognized in a false color.
This the weakly-strong sex does, whose fragile mind
in a narrow breast fluctuates with an easy surge of vices.

What of this, that he himself, head of the woman's body and king,
who rules the weak particle cut from his own flesh,
who governs the tender vessel by his rule,
is dissolved into luxury? You may see old heroes
grow soft in dress, for whom the craftsman had fashioned
rough members and hardened stiff limbs with bones,
but they are ashamed to be men; they seek the vainest things
with which they may shine, that, fickle, they may loosen inborn strength.
They rejoice to take flowing cloaks not from the fleece
of sheep, but from spoils of branches sought from the eastern world,
and to pour over the hard body with checkered stuffs.
Art is added, that threads saturated with recooked herbs
may mock varied forms with a distinct warp;
whatever down of a wild beast is softest to the touch
is combed. This man you may see hunting with swift course
wanton tunics, weaving also the feathery garments
of many-colored birds on new looms,
that man shamefully scattering womanish airs
with fragrant pigments and foreign powder.
Luxury holds all the vigors of our life,
setting in the five senses what the author founded.
For ears and eyes, then nostrils and palate,
a use infected by vicious arts is sought;
even touch itself, which is strong through the whole body,
courts a tender stroking from bland fomentations.
O grief, that the freeborn laws of nature fall,
and the dowries are dragged captive, lust reigning!

Every perverse right flourishes, while whatever the Almighty
had given to be had they turn into contrary pursuits.
Was it for this, I ask, that the watching pupil was set
under the soft eyelid, that it might see the shameful members
of half-men whirled in theatrical dizziness,
defiling wretched sights with foul delight?
Or do the nostrils therefore breathe and send down from the mid
citadel of the brain twin associated openings,
that pleasure, badly joined, may drink the allurements
which a shameless harlot casts from painted hair?
Did God add open ears for the vain modulations
of a lyric girl and the sounds of strings and the banquet-song
of warming wickedness, and bid the path of the voice
penetrate through cavernous passages? Does the savor set
in the moist mouth live for this cause, that medicated dishes
may bait the sluggish gluttony and lively gullet of a gourmand,
that dinners furnished through varied tastes may lead
into night and its own drunkenness may weary the heavy belly?
What would be hard, what soft, what smooth, what rough,
what hot or cold, God willed us to know
at a touch, with the interpreting sense of feeling,
but we spread delights of feathers and linen weavings
and polish the skin with an attenuating couch.
Happy he who has been able to use granted gifts
moderately and to keep a sparing measure of enjoying,
whom the rich appearance of the world and pleasant charm
and surrounding plenty, deceiving with shining things,
does not take, as a child, nor bind to a foolish love,
who detects that under shadowed sweetness a grim poison
hides in the cover of a lying good.

But that was once for us holy and good
at the beginning of things, when Christ founded the world,
for God saw that it was good, as Moses himself,
historian of the nascent world, has testified:
'God saw,' he says, 'that whatever he created was good.'
This I will follow, this I will hold, conceived in a stable mind,
what the holy prophet, by God's inspiring, brought forth,
recalling the first-beginnings of ancient light,
that whatever God and Wisdom made is good.
Therefore the Father is founder of the good, and with the Father Christ,
for God, and God the Father and the Son, are one;
nature indeed makes them one, which stands for both
as one of will, of right, of power, of love.
Yet not therefore two godheads nor two craftsmen
of things, since there is no disagreement of race,
and therefore there is no distance of work, none
of talent; one founder brought forth all good things.
Nothing muddy flows from the fount, nor is a turbid liquid
born, or violated from the origin of the first vein,
but while the uncorrupted liquor washes along the livid
sands, it grows filthy among putrefied contagions.

Did horse, iron, bull, lion, rope, oil
have in themselves the force of crime when they were formed?
That a man is slain, iron is not the cause of the rage
but the hand; nor does the hot madness of the circus
have the horse as author of levity or of rabid uproar:
the mind of the crowd, lacking reason, not the running of horses,
rages; a useful gift perishes by infamous zeal.
So we know the Lacedaemonian wrestling-grounds grew wet with oil
and a quiet juice served for crimes.
Hence daring, hanging on an aerial rope,
climbs steep stages with secure soles;
hence rash bodies will pass wild beasts with a flying leap
and play among the hazards of death.
Public consent gives shows of human blood
and laws bid a venal punishment be prepared,
that the limbs of men, torn by bloody bites,
may delight the people cheerful from a funeral.
It wearies to recount a thousand other raving joys
of the stupid world, which, forgetful of the true Thunderer,
roll human life in wretched errors.
No one raises a mind mindful of the highest Begetter
on high, no one sends sighs to heaven,
nor, recalling the peak of the natal throne, looks back
to the author himself, nor poises hope above the air,
but subjects a mind content to snore in heavy cares
to an unworthy lord and, prone, loves things about to perish
and seeks earthly things with a bent sense.
This is beautiful which the earth bears, which slippery glory
confers, which ruinous pleasure commends,
which, like a wind, stirred, with dust blown out,
passes by, which flies across with the example of a thin shadow.
With these plagues of diseases the powerful robber
presses sick souls, whom, poured among them with silent forces,
the hearts of men drink; he sows from the marrow all
wickednesses and scatters his ministers through the members.
For there a numerous cohort under such a prince
makes war and besieges souls with horrid arms:
Wrath, Superstition, Grief, Discord, Mourning, 1
black Thirst of Blood, Thirst of wine and Thirst of Gold,
Envy, Adultery, Guile, Detraction, Theft;
their faces bristle shapeless and threatening in habit.
Windy Ambition swells, Doctrine is proud,
Eloquence sounds, hidden Fraud weaves knots.
Hence dog-like Fluency barks through the whole forum,
hence cheap Wisdom bears a Herculean club
and displays her gymnosophists street by street.
Idolatrous Religion smears smoky stones with wax
and, pale, grovels at deaf altars.
Alas, with how many armed men the shameless enemy presses
the mortal race, with how great a following the iron-clad leader
wages wars, with how great a rule he triumphs over the conquered!

The Canaanite rises to aid and thickens the columns,
terrible with a helmet, shaking the weights of bristles on his chin
and brandishing the right hand with a heavy spear.
But from another part a raging army burns
of the Amorite king; then thousands of Gergesenes
pour a battle-line flying over the whole field;
these strike from afar, those clash at close quarters.
Lo, Jebusite squadrons boil for battles,
whose golden weapons, stained from the dragon's blood,
flash with death-bringing splendor and shine and kill.
It also pleases you, Hittite, to arm terrific troops
with javelins; but the Perizzite race insults with arrows
of equal valor, but with unlike iron.
Last the king of the Hivites advances a wedge,
wearing a scaly breastplate from a snake's skin.
Leaning on these men the perverse power of crimes
subdues weak minds, which in simplicity,
untaught and rude in war, under a false compact
of grim friendship first believe the columns allied,
and follow Mammon's faith under love of peace.
Soon, easy to be seized to bonds, willing they go under
hard yokes with addicted necks, and choose
to serve the fierce commands of rascal spirits.

That man, increasing patrimonies with superfluous estates
and gaping, the bound scorned, at neighboring little fields,
is led bound with manacles and a thousand chains,
tied behind his back before triumphal chariots,
nor does he feel himself addicted to barbarian reins.
This man, who climbs the heights of windy fame
and is inflated with the hollow honor of popular pomp,
who thinks it the highest and solid good of ambition
to grow by success, to terrify trembling defendants
with the crier's voice, to break the rods on the bodies
of the wretched, to exercise the terrible axes of the laws,
has already given his neck to the noose, is already bound
with a hard fetter and wears away servile chains with his feet.

Believe, captive mortals, whom hostile workhouses
already hold condemned, whom a power of punishments
not understood confines in servitude,
this is that Babylon, this the transmigration of our
race and the horrible victory of the prince of Assur,
which Jeremiah, weeping with a grief-making song,
howled as a city bereft of its own citizens.
Is it hidden, or is it doubtful, that souls from the seed of Jacob
suffer a gentile exile, whom Persian kingdoms
hold captive and compel into their own compacts?
There they unlearn to live by the natal rite
and, stripped of ancestral manners, degenerate into barbarian
rights and follow a new tongue and dress,
and learn to grow filthy from a profaned cult
and abolish nurse Zion from a petulant breast.
Already it irks to remember the fatherland; already they break
mystic instruments and praise the devoted things of an external kingdom.
Was it not better that the fathers had borne the savage empire
of the Memphite hall and sat deep at the left-hand
hearths, placed under the feet of unjust Pharaoh,
ready to serve in mud and chaff,
raw with the filthy belch of immoderate flesh?
To what end did the Lord pour so much help through prodigious
signs, while he by chance saves a rebellious people
with undeserved aid, while he looses necks set under bonds
and checks Egypt with a serpent-rod?
What avails it to have trodden the watery deep, the sea yielding,
on a dusty path, when rocks conscious of the sea
lay open, betrayed under an unknown heaven,
and thirsty mud dried in the mid flood,
if the army once victorious by God's virtue, splitting
the mid darkness with columnar light,
has lost the rich valley of the discovered cluster,
if it does not know how to turn the soil whose honey-flowing
clod, milky, mixes snowy streams,
if it suffers Jericho, tamed with trumpets and sounding bronze,
to rise again into ancient walls,
if it is driven from the banks of the refluent Jordan and already
deserts the allotted lot measured into acres,
finally if it cannot defend a city built with such sweats
and roofs that transcend cloud-born storms,
if it does not know what that stone is
that withstands enemies and is the impregnable guard
of a tower, which no engine leaping with brazen beak
rams, nor iron blows shake?
This corner is at the head of the gate, this holds every
series of stones and firms the constructed thresholds.
He who shall have known it duly inserted in his own walls
through the ramparts, and shall gird himself and his walls
with a triple rampart, standing high from afar on the citadel,
trusting in love of the rock and watching with chaste arms,
him neither the queen of Tyre, nor the Parthian neighbor of great
Euphrates, nor the discolored Indian,
his black temples wreathed with winged arrows, will snatch.
Nay, if the foreign tyrant, gathering thunderbolt giants
to wars, should wish to destroy your camps,
you will be safe, nor will he move you from a firm station,
Charon himself of the world, Marcion's godhead, himself,
who rules vain darkness under the aerial sun.
For whatever the sun looks on is vain; from soluble
elements all things consist, and from a flowing creation.

I am wrong, unless the teacher apostle, pitying, began
and groaned that every creature, subjected to vain labors
not of its own will, serves a perjured robber:
'He errs,' he says, 'who thinks we have a struggle with blood
and flesh and boiling veins and vicious
gall, and that the soul sins from hot marrows.
His own members do not press the mind, nor does iron force

This interval, of course, which lies open between heaven
and the lowest of earth and hangs clouds in an empty gap,
bears the reins of varied powers and under
prince Belial bristles with unjust rulers.
With these robbers we wrestle, as the sacred sentence
of the apostolic mouth, a witness, shows.
Let no one, sinning, accuse the habit of nature or the provocations
of the body; it is easy to bridle rebellious
affections of the flesh and to blunt the excessive pulses
of fragile matter and to tame the conquered bowels.
For the soul is far more excellent, as sent down
from highest aether; if it should wish to shake the subjected
limbs with heavy command and give them over to severe orders,
no force will resist the reigning lord.
Greater is that force in man which inflicts a breathable virus
and strikes the thin mind with thin air.
A Parthian reed does not equally run across the winds,
whose path no sight is able to grasp;
for while the swift thing flies across the winged airs with feathers,
it comes unforeseen, nor does a hiss announce before
the advent of death until it burst the hidden things of the breast,
penetrating secure life with a medicated wound;
but more winged and more medicated is the arrow
which the slippery domination of the shadowy world casts,
eluding the eyes when shot, and driving the inmost
of the heart with the swift crossing of the flying reed.
Nor is the nature of the soul slow or late in beware
of a wound, since God has set in it a fiery
talent, pure, wise, subtle, serene,
mobile, anxious, swift, agile, sharp,
if only, chaste, it may venerate its maker and fight
for him and, sober, trample the conquered world,
tasting nothing from pestilent wealth or falsified
spoils of the lands with foolish delight,
lest, lying under an alien bundle and given to a kingdom,
it be unable to avoid the cunning arrows of the enemy.

But why do I twist every evil of world and of men
to the envy of the malignant enemy, when our evils,
concreted from our own souls, take from the parent heart
what they are, what they can do, their race and head and force?
He indeed is the tinder and cause of our evils,
but he can disturb or deceive only as much
as we wish, who give suggested armaments
to a decrepit lion: the beast grows cold, gnashing futilely,
unless it swell through the suffrages of the human race.
We beget every evil of ours from our own body,
as David, otherwise an excellent father, begot one
crime, Absalom; that father begot a foul thing, but one,
a patricide among harmless offspring,
who dared to draw the sword against the author of his race
(O piety!) and drove standards contrary to his father's standards
and joined arms of one blood.
So our breasts, with the lineage burning, bring forth
dreadful sons, who at once, with bites turned
against us, grow used to live by the punishments of those who beget;
for they depopulate the too fertile bowels of parents
and are nourished by the destruction of the natal stock.
But that man had varied his own progeny with sad
and pious, as highest king and prophet of God
and author of the virgin about to bear, so that Absalom,
brother of Solomon, might sow his crimes among just
pledges and disturb the sweet house with bitter things.
Us nothing worthy of Solomon fills, us degenerate
Absalom alone fills, tearing pious bowels with the sword.

If it is allowed to presume anything from ethics, 1 or if
anything of example from physics, so the viper, they say,
dies together by the teeth of offspring poured through the bowels,
a mother by her own death, not fertile by sex or
distended in the womb from a coupling, but when she grows hot,
stirred by female fire, the obscene thing thirsts with open
mouth for a husband about to die; he inserts his three-tongued
head into the jaws of the spouse and, burning, enters the kisses,
insinuating genital venom by a coupling of the mouth.
The bride, wounded by the force of pleasure, with a bite
breaks the lover's throat, drunk in amid bland compacts,
and drinks the infused spittle while the flesh perishes.
By these allurements the father is consumed, but closed offspring
kills the mother; for after, with the seed grown,
the small little bodies begin to creep in the hot
hiding-places and, vibrated, to strike the moved womb,
the mother boils with an internal crime of piety
and groans at the butcher progeny, conscious of a damned sex,
bursting the obstacles of a fenced birth.
For because no exit of being born lies open, the belly,
tortured by fetuses striving into the light,
is torn and, lacerated, opens a way through the loins.
At last by the nurse's death that flock of pains comes forth,
having scarcely struggled through the entry of life and carving
an origin through crime; the creeping whelps lick the natal
corpse, while an orphan brood is born,
having not experienced the day except as posthumous of a wretched mother.

Not unlike is the conception of our mind: so from a viper's
mouth it drinks in the infused venom
with Belial as spouse, so it swallows kisses with a draught
and snatches them inward, so, burning with the gall of lust,
it is filled with vices, mixed with a husband about to perish.
Then, pregnant, it bears a lethal race, conceived works
of a malignant talent from the seed of the accomplice hydra,
whom it is necessary first to repay with punishments for his own deeds,
for the defilement of a corrupted soul and for the slain world.
Further, cruel wounds themselves tear the soul
with a thousand childbeds, while it brings forth from itself offspring
begotten against nature, frequent sins
namely, and sons fed on a mother's funeral.
Hence that just rebuke of the Lord Christ:
'Is not the father a demon, I chide you, sinners,
did he beget you by a coupling of flesh thirsting for unjust seed?'
Reader, go through the holy volume:
what I speak, you will find the Lord said to the profane,
objecting true things to mortals: 'For piety itself,' he says,
'would prove you begotten from my Father,
and the work of piety.' O blind lust! What is this,
that when the good mind knows itself betrothed to just
chambers, and is called to go as bride to the king's bed,
and of a king always young, with old age driven back,
keeping divine beauty on an eternal face,
it prefers adultery and, cheap, sells itself for tawny
gift to the filthy embraces of a night-dwelling Indian,
having scorned God's progeny poured through the virgin's limbs
and calling sweet sons in a brothel?

I feel how deceit moves a suit against this,
with what tooth resisting thorny calumny fights
and calls us to wrestlings, the true having been challenged.

'If God does not wish evil to be, why does he not forbid it?' he says.
'It matters nothing whether he was author and maker of evils,
or whether he allow the most beautiful works to be turned
into the vice of crime, when he could prohibit; who if he, almighty,
should wish all to act as innocents, neither would the holy will
degenerate, nor would the hand stain itself with any deed.
Therefore the Lord founded the evil which he watches from on high
and suffers and approves to be done, as if he himself had created it;
for he himself created what, though he could shut it out,
he does not abolish and allows to rage with long use.'
Condemn my ears, kindly Father, and close the passages
of a head growing dull, lest the winding, being pervious,
conceive such sounds; it is worth so much to lose
the extinguished office of life from a part of the brain,
if only the soul, happy, immune and without share of the unspeakable
hearing, may keep itself from the stupid ear.
Who would bear these insults cast on God, who remembers
that he himself excels, ennobled by divine gifts?
To be silent of many things, this fact alone proves God
to be kind, that he raises from the tombs men destroyed
by love of the forbidden and bids them be through the age
partakers of his kingdom. If he were author
and savior of evil, he would never after the losses of salvation
and the deaths of sinners wish to bring a living remedy
and restore the lost with a second aid.
To fall is of man, to save is of God: this one perishes by merits,
that one abolishes the work of the perishing and looses the merit.
A huge argument that the Lord who furnishes such things
does not wish evil, nor approve beforehand what he afterward washes.
'Can anyone sin with the Thunderer unwilling,
for whom it is easy to compose in the heart of man the senses
he may please, and to animate all fibers
with chaste pulses and to pour the whole honorable into the veins?'
Do you not know, fool, the force of your liberty given
by the very former? Do you not know from the origin how great
a power has been granted you, a servant, over the world,
and over your own talent and the loosed, unbound
right of will, to which it is allowed to wish and follow
what is pleasing, and to subject the mind to no bond?
Or when he set you as lord over all things he had created
and bade the world serve your
commands, and when he gave fields, pole, sea, rivers, winds,
would he, greedy, have been unwilling to trust you with judgment
over yourself as if unworthy, and have denied liberty?
What sort of thing was it that he who was chosen to be king of the great world
should not himself be king of himself, defiled with a curtailed honor?
For what honor of a lord is there, whose mind is not free,
but one opinion serves an imposed law?
What praise of man, further, or what merit, to live justly
without a sure distinction between either path?
He does not become good of his own will, for whom there is not ready power
to wish another thing and to turn the bent senses of the mind;
and yet he is neither good nor praiseworthy
who is not good of his own will, since from forced goodness
no glory comes, and inglorious virtue is filthy;
nor yet is it virtue, unless, refusing the worse,
it flash out and by a better nature seek the right path.

'Go,' says the parent himself and craftsman and founder of Adam,
'go, man, most noble by the breath of our mouth,
unsubjected, powerful, arbiter of things, arbiter likewise
and judge of your own mind; subject yourself to me alone
by your own will, that the subjection itself may be free
by loosed judgment. I do not compel nor exact by force,
but I warn that you flee the unjust and follow the just.
Light is companion of the just, grim death companion of the unjust.
Choose the thing of life; let your virtue carry you yourself
into the age, let your fault in turn damn you eternally,
and let the permitted license furnish either lot.'
Wandering by this piety and abounding in so great a gift,
he transgresses the proposed right and, knowing, chooses
lethal things and willing, while he believes more useful
to himself what the cunning snake persuaded with God forbidding.
He persuaded, certainly, by exhortation, he did not drive with sharp
command; this the woman, defendant of the crime, answered
the chiding Lord, that she, enticed by ill-working persuasions,
had persuaded the man; the man himself also gladly
consented. Was it allowed to spurn the exhorter by liberty
of a right mind? It was allowed; for God also before
had urged that, willing, he follow the better; but he,
spurning the counsel, believed the savage enemy more.
Now he stands in the middle between the Lord of life and the master
of death; God calls from here, the tyrant from there,
him ambiguous and alternating himself with his own motions.

Receive the famous monuments of things done,
with which history has played beforehand a visible sign.
Lot, fleeing burning Sodom, was hastening to withdraw
all the dear pledges of the house with him, the seat left,
from city-burning clouds, when already the air, fired
with a cloudy sulphur, was veiling the sky
and a crackling shower was kindling the flaming day.
An angel host had prescribed this law to them,
sent by the virtue of God under a double image,
that the whole house should go from the gates and, constant,
direct eyes into the open, nor with light bent behind
see the fires reigning through the walls:
'Let no one, mindful of Sodom, which is the form of the world
to be burned, once he has moved a step from the walls, with face
turned behind his back, look back at the funerals of things.'
Lot, wise, obeys the warnings, but the light wife
by mobility of mind twisted a womanish talent backward
and, callable, stuck to her own Sodom.
Eve had drawn the man to partnership of a dread fault:
this one, sinning, perishes for herself alone; solidified with metal
she stiffened, made a fragile and dissolvable stone,
the woman stands as she had stood before, keeping all things,
carefully fashioned of long salt, sealed,
both beauty and dress and brow and eyes and hair
and face bent toward the back and chin a little
carried backward, stiff monuments of an ancient offence.
She indeed melts, wet with salt sweats,
but she feels no losses of a full form from the fluid
dissolution, and as much as herds of savor
thin the stone, so much moisture suffices for those licking
and reforms the worn skin through the losses.
The sinning woman has deserved to be set with this title,
loosening a weak and fluid mind through slippery
counsels; and, fragile at the heavenly commands, Lot,
having entered the way, keeps a purpose of vow not changeable,
nor looks back at walls collapsed into deep
ash on a pyre, and a burned people
and the manners of the people, archives, rights and forum,
baths, hucksters, lodgings, temples, theaters,
and the circus with its people, and soaking cookshops.
Whatever men do, the fires of the Sodomites wrap
with just fires and damn with Christ as judge.
To have fled these once is enough; our Lot looks
no further, but the fragile wife looks back, and what
she had fled, changeable, she revisits with inverted face,
and stands hardened among her fathers' ashes.

See, for you a sealed proof of liberty,
by which God wished us to know that whatever is to be followed
is set under our rule, and is everywhere left free
to tread either path. Two were bidden to withdraw
from Sodom; one snatches himself away, the other mutters,
he hastens his step fleeing, she on the contrary refuses.
The mind is free for both, but the will unlike for both.
Each one's own desire, snatching, divides hither and thither.
Many a track in the sacred books marks such an appearance.
Look at Ruth of the Moabite race and Orpha together:
that one accompanies the mother-in-law Noomin 1 with faithful love,
this one deserts. And yet, stripped of chambers and of the marital
law and vacant from Hebrew beds and rites,
they were enjoying their own right, but the former rite of shrines
persuaded Orpha to prefer barbarian foreskins
and to nourish the stock of half-wild Goliath.
Ruth, while she is burned through the stubs by rustic heat,
deserved the couch of Boos, 1 and, adopted into a chaste bed,
fruitful, brought forth a Christ-bearing house, Davidic kingdoms,
and mixed mortal origins with God.

I myself often remember that twin brothers in wavering
youth came together to a gaping two-way
and doubted long under a split path which
way was better; when a thorny wood with brambles
narrowed the right, and a rocky path led far
an aerial ridge with a sloping margin,
but a shade-bearing grove through pleasant greens
adorned the left with rich fruits and a wide plain
gave a gently lying way: that one, content
with squalid thorns, crept through steep rocks,
the other indulged himself on the left-hand field;
that one mixed his head with neighboring stars,
this one suddenly fell into muddy marshes.
One nature underlies all, but one exit
does not accomplish all, with the form of choices separate.
Not otherwise than if once through the clear a milky
cloud of doves, slipped, should descend into a field
of fruit-bearing country, where a cunning fowler
has stretched snares and smeared twigs with clinging birdlime,
and has scattered ambushes with pods or deceitful grain.
Deceiving grains entice some, and twisted ties of bristle
bind the greedy gullet,
or soft glue binds around the entangled wings;
but others, whom no love of eating allures,
walk with harmless steps on sterile grass
and beware to turn their eyes to the suspected bait.
Soon when they must fly back to heaven, part seeks the starry
aether free, clapping wings above the air,
part lies captive and, wounded with torn feathers,
fights on the ground and looks up in vain at the winged airs.
So nature pours onto the soil souls of one color
from the founts of heaven, but there, bound
by sweet allurements, they are held, and few, returning,
climb the aether; a viscous pasture baits many
and does not allow them to run through to the upper airs.

Hence the foreknowing Father kindles livid Tartarus with liquid
lead and undermines pitchy ditches of bitumen
of infernal water under dark Avernus,
and under the Phlegethontic flood he ordained that eating
worms grow into perpetual punishments of crimes.
For he knew that vigor from his own breath is in
our bodies and that a soul formed from the everlasting mouth
cannot die, cannot in turn,
polluted with vices, return again to the vaults,
to be sunk deep in the pit of the boiling abyss.
To worms and flames and tortures he gave an immortal
age, lest the penalty perish with old age
when the soul does not perish. Torments pluck and foster
the matter given without end; death itself deserts
eternal groans and forces the weeping to live.

But far off in the regions of paradise the majesty
knowing the future has set diverse prizes
for pure spirits and removed from every stain,
and who have not looked back at Gomorrah's ruins,
but with eyes duly averted have left behind their backs
the darkness, the hastening dangers of the wretched world.
And first they are borne back to the stars with easy flight,
whence the flowing soul had given vigor to constructed Adam.
For because the downward weights of life do not press
a thin nature, nor does an iron fetter delay,
the burning spark, retracing, cuts the thickened air
with swift gliding and surmounts the pole, the journey remade,
hating the prison sites to which it had clung as exile.
Then white-haired Faith receives the returning one in a high
bosom by right of return and delights the foster-child
with tender delights, recounting with complaining mouth,
after many inns of the flesh, the labors she has suffered.
There, stretched on her side on a purple couch,
she drinks breathing odors from eternal flowers
and drinks ambrosial dew from a rosy straw,
and, asked, refuses to the rich smoking at a long
interval, thirsting for rivers and all the rains of heaven,
to insert a finger in the palate,
and to extinguish the peaks of flames with a fearing touch.
Nor would you wonder that in places far distant, between
damned and just souls, conspicuous sights meet
and due exchanges are marked across great
intervals, which the pole divides with the mid world.

He errs, whoever estimates souls by the bound of our
eyes, which a shining cloak wraps with a glassy
barrier, for whom thickened curds weave a mirror
and impede the wandering windows with a drawn-over moisture.
Do rounded drops of dense vigor roll
for the eyes of souls, or do they bristle outward
with eyelid bristles, or are they covered by a shading lash?
Theirs is a living sight, nor a small pupil, but a fire
that shoots through mist and penetrates the vast hidden.
Nothing rusty or solid stands against those watching,
night clouds yield, blackening
clouds yield, the round area of the world stretched before yields.
Nor does the spirit only send aerial gaps through by seeing,
but with light it crosses opposing mountains,
it transfixes the bounds of ocean and the last letter, Thule,
and sends winged eyes into Tartarus.
For us, of course, all colors perish under night
from our sights, and forms are deleted in blind time.
Do those stripped of members and of viscera also lose
the marks of things by recognition, or wander in their steps?
Souls always have one appearance and one color
of air, according as each has the sum of merits, of the left
or of the right: nor does changeable time convert
and vary alternate turns; long and perpetual is
whatever it is, one course rolls its ages.

Do you, having experienced, doubt that souls run through by seeing
things hidden from bodily eyes, when often, in the dew
of rest, the living mind, put to sleep, discerns remote
and distant places, stretching its sight through fields, through stars,
through seas? For it does not separate itself
before death from the living limbs, or flee as exile
from the inmost of blood and flesh and strip itself
from the marrows, or drive abducted life from the breast,
but remaining in the visceral seat it watches all things
with sharp lights and, wherever it has carried around the keen
gaze of light nature, shut out by no barrier of things,
it looks forth at the world set before its eyes
and the filthy element set under the mass of the world.
Meanwhile the earth lies in the way and does not stand against the sights.
Nay, if it turn its face to the star-bearing axis,
nothing running between impedes the fire of the
watchful soul for the gazes, although thickened clouds
grow heavy and the sky blacken with an opposing fleece.
So bodily John, still not loosed from flesh,
sees secrets covered with silent things to come,
but by the gift of sleep, the flesh a little while the go-between,
free for gazing and wandering with sense and eyes through
years arranged in order for coming suns,
he sees the angelic array for the destruction of a world
already now about to be burned, and he perceives with the ear
the sad 1 bellowings of heavy trumpets at the world's end.
These things he, fenced in the prison of members before death,
was seeing, the soul withdrawing, not departing.
Will not the breath without a body much more mark all things
when the wrappings of the body are laid back in a chilling tomb?
Sure faith has it that the rapid furnaces under the night below,
which cook a polluted soul through long ages with everlasting
fire, are set out to the eyes of the remote poor man
across the long void; nor less are the golden gifts
of the just and the ruddy crowns, chaos sundering,
shown from afar to those sunk in the prison of punishments.
Hence after dire sores the unhappy spirit howling
in the plague of guilt is shown to the paradise-dweller,
blessed, and in turn they discern the mutual things of merits.

O God all-begetting, giver of the soul, O God Christ,
from whose mouth God the one Spirit stands,
by your governing I am ruled, with you as prince I lead life,
with you as judge I grow pale and tremble, with you as the same judge
I take hope that whatever I do will be pardonable with you,
however unworthy of pardon I may do and speak.
I confess; forgive willingly and spare the one confessing.
I have deserved every evil, but you, good judge, take away
what I have deserved; favoring, grant better
gifts to my soul at last, when it shall have left
the lodging of this body, built of sinews, skin, blood, gall,
bones, which the inhabitant, corrupted by luxury,
alas, too much embracing, fosters, when the tearful hour
shall have closed these orbs, and the matter will lie
bewailed and the naked mind will enjoy its own eyes,
lest it see some truculent one of the race of robbers,
cruel, rabid, terrible with threatening face and voice,
who may drag me, spotted with the sprinkle of morals,
headlong, as a plunderer, and, as I fall, sink me
in black caves, about to exact all debts of a damaging
life even to the smallest farthing.
There is much habitation in the Father's treasuries, Christ,
distinguished in unlike places. I do not ask a house
in the blessed region; let there be there chaste bands
of men, who, scorning a dusty census,
have sought your riches; let there be, with everlasting flower,
white virginity, castrated, the mind cut back.
But for me it is enough if no face of a Tartarean minister
meet me, nor the flame of greedy gehenna
devour this soul sunk in the lowest furnaces.
Be it so, that grim fire, because thus it is necessary
for bodily stain, swallow me in cavernous Avernus:
at least let the slow fires exhale mild vapors
and let the heat grow tepid with languishing blaze.
Let immense light and temples bound with crowns
glorify others: let a light penalty burn me mildly.
