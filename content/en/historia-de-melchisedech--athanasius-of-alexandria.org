OF THE SAME, WHY MELCHIZEDEK WAS CALLED "WITHOUT FATHER, WITHOUT MOTHER, AND
WITHOUT GENEALOGY."
A history concerning Melchizedek.
In that time there was a queen of Salem, after the name of the city; and she
bore Salaad. And Salaad begot Melchi. And Melchi took a wife named Salem after
her own name; and she bore him two sons, one called Melchi, and the other
Melchizedek. And their father was a Hellene, a prodigal, offering sacrifices
to the idols. And a fitting time came for sacrificing to the idols; for he was
sacrificing in the temple of the twelve gods; and Melchi the king says to his
own son Melchizedek: Take with you some of the serving-boys, and go to the
cattle-fold, and bring me seven calves, that we may sacrifice to the gods. As
Melchizedek was going, then, a divine thought came upon him on the way, and
lifting his eyes to heaven he gazed at the sun, and considered the moon and
the stars; and coming to himself he said: If anyone made the heaven, the
earth, and the sea, and the stars, to him who created them the sacrifice ought
to be given. The signs of heaven make it plain to me that their Creator rests
above them, incorruptible, invisible, immortal, being the only God in heaven
and on earth, knowing the blasphemies of hearts; he is the true God; to him
the sacrifice ought to be given. Let me go then to my father, and I will plead
with him; perhaps he will hear me. And Melchizedek turned back, bringing
nothing. And his father, seeing him, says to him: Where are the calves? And
Melchizedek says to him: Do not be angry, father king, but hear me. And he
said: What you have to say, say it quickly. And Melchizedek says: Come, the
sacrifice which we have to give, let us not give it to these gods; for these
do not appear to me to be gods; but rather let us give sacrifice to him who
rests above the heavens. For he is God of gods. And his father, being angry,
says to him: Go; bring what I told you; otherwise you shall not live. And as
Melchizedek was going again to the cattle-fold, the king of Salem, Melchi,
went in to Salem his wife, saying to her: Come, =28.528= let us give as a
sacrifice one of our sons. And his wife, hearing, wept bitterly; for she knew
that the king wished, under a pretext, to kill Melchizedek, because he had
reproached him concerning the sacrifice. And the queen groaned, and said: Woe
is me, I have toiled and labored in vain. And the king, seeing her, said to
her: Do not weep, but come, let us cast lots; and if the lot falls to me, I
choose whom I wish, and I will give him as a sacrifice to our gods; but if it
falls to you, choose whom you will, and keep him safe. And this he said,
thinking to obtain the lot for the priest; and casting lots, the lot fell to
the queen, and she chose Melchizedek, whom she loved. And the king of Salem,
being overcome by his wife, made ready the son who had fallen to him by lot
for sacrifice; for he feared to offend his gods. And Melchizedek came and
brought the seven calves. And the father, taking the son who had fallen to
him, went to the temple of the idols in the shrine of the twelve gods; and
there gathered for the sacrifice five hundred and three children offered by
their own fathers; and another three hundred from the mothers, and cattle and
sheep without number, and the sacrifice was made ready. And Salem, the mother
of Melchizedek, sitting in her house, cried out with a loud voice and says to
Melchizedek: Do you not weep for your brother, that after so much toil he is
going away to be slain? And hearing these things from her he wept, and says to
his mother: I have need of you only thus far; and rising, he went up on Mount
Tabor. And his mother, rising, went away to the temple of the idols, to see
her own son before he was slain, and her whole race. And Melchizedek, going up
into Mount Tabor, bending his knees, said: O God, Lord of all, who made the
heaven and the earth, I call upon you, the only true God, hear me at this
hour, and command that as many as have come to the sacrifice of my brother
Melchi, the place become Hades, and swallow them up. And God heard
Melchizedek, and immediately the earth gaped open, and swallowed them and
every tribe of Melchi, together with the whole city; and neither man, nor
altar, nor temple, nor beast, nor any creature of the whole city remained, but
all was swallowed. And Melchizedek came down from Mount Tabor, and seeing that
God had heard him, in great fear he returned again to the mountain, and coming
into the thick of the wood, he lived as a solitary there seven years; naked as
he came forth from his mother's womb; and his nails became a span long, and
the hair of his head reached to his loins, and his back became as the shell of
a tortoise. And his food was the fruit of trees, and his drink was dew, which
he licked. And after the seven years a voice came to Abraham, saying, Abraham,
Abraham. And Abraham said: My Lord. And he said: Saddle your beast, and take
costly =28.529= garments and a razor, and go up on Mount Tabor, and cry three
times: Man of God; and a wild man will come. Do not then fear him; but shave
him, and pare his nails, and clothe him, and be blessed by him. And Abraham
did as the Lord commanded him, and went up into Mount Tabor, and stood in the
thickets of the wood; and he cried three times: Man of God. And Melchizedek
came out, and saw Abraham, and was afraid. And Melchizedek said to him: Do not
fear, but say: Who are you, and whom do you seek? And Abraham said: The Lord
commanded me to shave you and to cut your nails, and to clothe you, and to be
blessed by you. And Melchizedek said to him: As the Lord commanded you, do.
And Abraham did as the Lord commanded him. And Melchizedek, coming down from
Mount Tabor after three days, taking a horn of oil, and sealing it with the
word of God, blessed Abraham saying: Blessed are you by God Most High, and
henceforth your name is called among those made perfect, and your name is no
longer called Abram, but your name shall be perfect, Abraham. And again a
voice came to Abraham; and he said, What, my Lord? And the Lord said to him:
Since no one of the race of Melchizedek was left remaining from the earth, for
this reason he shall be called without father, without mother, without
genealogy, having neither beginning of days nor end of life; and being made
like the Son of God, he remains a priest forever; and I have loved him, as I
have loved my beloved Son; because he kept my commandments, and will keep them
forever. Therefore, that he may not seem to have no beginning of days, because
no one knows when he was born, nor the genealogy, neither of father nor of
mother, for this reason he is called without father, without mother, and
without genealogy; and because he was well pleasing to God, he remains a
priest continually. When therefore Melchizedek met Abraham returning from the
slaughter of the kings, he gave him a cup of wine, invisibly adding to it also
a fragment of bread, and to his people, being three hundred and eighteen men,
which is called bucacraton even to this day. In this manner he was made like
the Son of God, but not unto the grace; and thus he became the first type of
the unbloody sacrifice of the Savior, bearing it into the holy offering.
Wherefore he says: "You are a priest forever according to the order of
Melchizedek," since he became a type of the holy offering, having given it to
Abraham and to the three hundred and eighteen. And thus also the holy Fathers
were found in due season in the city of the Nicaeans, who also rightly divided
the faith; whose number, after the likeness of the patriarch Abraham, is three
hundred and ten and eight holy bishops in the synod. And to our God be glory
now and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
