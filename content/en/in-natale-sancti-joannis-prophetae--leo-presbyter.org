* On the Nativity of Saint John the Prophet

:END:

Of Leontius, presbyter of Constantinople, on the nativity of Saint John the
Prophet and Forerunner.
Blessed be the Lord God of Israel, for he has visited and made redemption for
his people; for a dayspring from on high has visited us this day also. For we
were sleeping in sloth, and he woke us by his love of mankind; he shook the
houses, that he might establish us, having healed the wounds; he scourged the
lifeless things, that he might chasten the living; he troubled the earth, that
those in high place might be fully assured. He shook, he did not destroy; he
visited, he did not pass sentence; for whom the Lord loves he chastens, and he
scourges every son whom he receives. An earthquake freed Peter from prison, an
earthquake loosed Paul from his chains, in an earthquake Elijah was taken up
as it were into heaven, an earthquake went before the Master's resurrection.
[For the present day is a resurrection.] Why an earthquake? Where there is a
movement of divinity, there also is a tumult of the elements. Have mercy on
your neighbor, and an earthquake will not trouble you; fast from wickedness,
and God is not angry. Do you also say readily the words of David as you pray:
Lord, do not rebuke me in your wrath, nor chasten me in your anger; do not
flood me as you did the giants, nor burn me as you did the Sodomites, but have
mercy as you did the Ninevites. It is timely, then, for those who perceive the
Lord's chastening to say the prophet's words: The discipline of the Lord opens
my ears.
As many, then, as demand that the divine word be vigorous, they themselves
also owe an obedient hearing, so that both those who speak piously and those
who hear sincerely may be able to reap the many-eared sheaf of faith; for
without faith neither is the echo of hearing widened, nor is the door of the
lips opened, nor is the plectrum of the tongue set in motion, nor are the
pipes of the vocal organs enlarged. And of this Zechariah the priest is
witness, who was chastened for unbelief for the instruction of those who do
not believe, who sought a sign of the archangelic revelation, who demanded a
surety of the Master's announcement. For when the archangel Gabriel had said
to Zechariah, as you heard just now yesterday: Do not fear, Zechariah, for
your petition has been heard, and behold, your wife Elizabeth will bear you a
son, Zechariah answered and said, as you know: Whereby shall I know this? For
I am an old man, and my wife is advanced in her days. Unbelieving indeed are
the old man's words, and full of much contradiction. For to say "Whereby shall
I know this?" is instead of "What proof do you give me as full assurance of
what is said?"
Whereby shall I know this? For I am an old man. How shall I be found a father
of a genuine child? Old age thrusts faith away; the matter is now outside
nature; the generative members have been dissolved; I walk holding a staff,
and you stir up in me the trial of a newlywed? When there is a tomb, then
offspring? Whereby shall I know this? Tell me, O greatest Gabriel. And it is
necessary that those who do not know should also learn that Gabriel is
interpreted "man of God," as one who served him who became man, as one who
made a way before him who appeared, as one who brought the good tidings not
only to the barren woman but also to the virgin. For this is the Gabriel who
also gave Daniel understanding, and made known the Lord's coming with exact
time, and transferred Habakkuk by the hair, and subjected the lions to Daniel
in the den as if they were ascetics. What then of Zechariah? For it is good
never to desert the sequence. Whereby shall I know this? For I am an old man
and my wife is advanced in her days. Whereby shall I know this? See, he says,
the trembling of the limbs, and the old age of both of us bowed down toward
the earth. If one part were still young and one part grown old, there would be
some small hope of what is said; but now the body of both has been wasted, we
wait for nothing else but the sickle of death, as ripe ears of grain. Do you
speak truly, master Gabriel? Give clear signs in advance; entrust to me a sign
as a pledge. I do not receive the promise unless I see a wonder. Whereby shall
I know this? Aaron did not believe unless the rod blossomed; Moses was not
fully assured unless his hand was made white; and Gideon did not understand
unless dew of rains came upon the fleece alone. Whereby then shall I know
this? Give a sign, and I will dance as a young man. Give a sign, lest
<Elizabeth> laugh rashly as Sarah did. Whereby shall I know this? If I am
shown to be a second Abraham, let me have the rights and the distinctive marks
that belong to Abraham. Enter into my house, I will wash your feet; for you
who have made a journey I will set before you a table filled with mystery;
Elizabeth will knead three measures of flour, prefiguring the Church; I will
seek a calf, that you may grant a child. Whereby shall I know this?
Why do you foretell the child's name to me, and enumerate his virtues when he
is not yet present? Show me something contrary to nature, that I may believe
what is above nature. If the heaven is vaulted afresh, I too am made new; if
the sea is measured, my wife also is delivered; if the sun is overtaken, the
dry breasts also will flow with milk; if the moon is walled about, the dry
womb also is moistened. Whereby then shall I know this paradoxical mystery?
For I am an old man and my wife is advanced in her days.
But the angel to Zechariah: Do you demand God as a pledge? Do you put an
archangel to the test on earth? Setting bodily weakness as more certain than
the Master's promise, you are not afraid to examine: Whereby shall I know
this? It is not as if he were sending you into Egypt, that you should fear
Pharaoh's bulk and decline, saying: "Appoint another, for I am weak-voiced and
slow of tongue"? Whereby shall you know this, Zechariah? Does God work nothing
above nature? Or do you seek the sequence of nature, and not the wonder-working
of divinity? Tell me: where have the pillars of the earth been fixed? Where
has the vault of heaven been established? Where are the hollows of the clouds
found? Where are the drops of rain warped on the loom? Where are the clods of
snow quarried? Who charioteers the course of the sun? Who sets the bounds of
the moon's waning? Who numbers the multitudes of the stars? How does the raging
sea revere the sand? How are hot-flowing rivers mixed? Where is the mist of
darkness woven? How is a human being formed in the womb? How is the female
found more timid than the male? How is a God-like soul found all at once in
the body? And Esau and Jacob, whom the whole world did not contain, how did
Rebecca's dark passage keep them without crowding? You say, Whereby shall I
know this, Zechariah? Do you seek the sequence of nature where there is an
operation of divinity? Do you not believe that the barren woman will give
birth? What if you hear concerning the virgin? Did he not, when Samson the
Nazirite had struck a thousand foreigners with the jawbone of an ass, and was
caught in a waterless place and in thirst, and had all but failed, and had
cried out sincerely to God, immediately open the hollow of the ass, and pool
the water, and give the Nazirite to drink? If then in the jawbone of an ass he
displayed such a sign and from a dry bone let rivers of waters flow, will he
hesitate to call those who have grown old in days to childbearing? Whereby
shall you know, Zechariah? Do you not have Abraham and Sarah as an example?
Was he not also an old man, and she barren and failing in the custom of the
purifications because old age had wasted the matter? Have you not heard Hannah
boasting: The barren has borne seven, and she who is many in children has
grown weak? Have you not heard the prophet saying, Zechariah, Signs are for
the unbelievers? You ought not to have refused what was said, Zechariah, you
who have obtained a ministry greater than the prophetic liturgy. But since as
an unbeliever you seek signs from God, bear in your own members a
scourge-bearing sign: you shall be silent and not able to speak. Where the
boldness of rashness is, there also is the bridle of chastening. You shall be
silent: where insult spoke, there is the sentence of rebuke.
God wished, Zechariah, that you should become the herald of such a wonder; for
a commander of the heavenly king's camp is being born, and a forerunner
purifier of the world's redemption from sins. But since you defined the
weakness of the body as more certain than the Master's promise, you shall be
silent and not able to speak, until the day these things come to pass, because
you did not believe my words, which will be fulfilled in their season. You see
that without faith none of the things that ought to be comes about. As soon as
Zechariah heard these words, he went out of the temple at once, bearing as the
wage of unbelief his muzzling. He went in to set others free, and he himself
came out condemned; he who bore the liturgical censer carried about a title of
exile. The people waited to hear some good thing from him, and he made signs:
Let no one come near me; I bear the dread of the Master's indignation.
O the paradoxical things! Zechariah is muzzled and Elizabeth feasts; a tongue
is stopped and a womb swells; the tongue is made barren and the barren woman
becomes a mother; the affection of the womb migrates to the tongue; the voice
is bridled and the seed is set free; Zechariah is silent and John leaps. For
as soon as the barren woman saw the virgin and the morning star perceived the
sun, John leaped greatly in the maternal womb, blaming the slowness of nature:
I am, he says, the Master's herald. Why am I bound by the same laws as my
fellow-servants? Shall I, waiting for the birth, lose the course? Shall the
Master outstrip the <servant> who is coiled about the birth-pangs? Shall the
sequence of nature become a transgression of rank? I have recognized him who
is present and I cannot bear to be silent; I shake the bonds of nature; for I
am eager to preach; I become a swift signal of two paradoxical things; I
trumpet a divine presence and I loose a father's tongue. These are the
interest-yields of the chapters left aside for us yesterday. For every
principal begets interest, which is why the infant too is named a yield; for
the husband lends the seed to the wife as a principal, for the husband is the
head of the wife, and the wife bears the interest to the principal ...
