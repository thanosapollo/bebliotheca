On the resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ; and on the myrrh-bearers.
=62.753=
Even now, as always, the wonders of the Master's resurrection strongly hold my
mind, and the fearful spectacles of the mysteries strike the thought of my
soul with astonishment: that dishonor begot glory, and passion brought
impassibility to men; that a wood of punishment delivered from a wood of
slavery, and nails dug up the many-rooted sin; that a crown of thorns
overturned a universal curse, and a shameful cross released the world from
shame; that a side pierced by a spear became a gate of the world's mysteries,
and a three-day tomb was shown to be a house of life; that a tax-collector
uttered doctrines of theology, and an angel sowed words of joy in the souls of
the women.
I cannot bear to be silent about the Master's paradoxes, even if, by the
multitude of the divine signs, speech is not sufficient to overtake them for
narration; I dance with the robber, I leap with Mary, I obey the evangelist
crying out: Late on the sabbath, as it began to dawn toward the first day of
the week, Mary Magdalene came, and the other Mary, to see the tomb.
And behold, there was a great earthquake; for an angel of the Lord descended
from heaven and rolled away the stone from the door of the sepulchre, and sat
upon it; and his appearance was as lightning, and his raiment white as snow.
And from fear of him the keepers were shaken, and became as dead. Late on the
sabbath, as it began to dawn toward the first day of the week. You heard this
voice a little while ago; in the fearful night, or rather in the great light,
Mary Magdalene came, and the other Mary, to see the tomb.
Good is the zeal of the women, =62.754= evangelical is the course of the women
who came to meet him, showing their manly mind. Perhaps the steps of those who
easily slip were more praiseworthy than those of the apostles. For the men,
having fled, were hiding in houses with fear; but the women, having run down
boldly to the tomb, were mourning the free one among the dead, fulfilling the
Christ-loving purpose with grateful faith. They did not fear the Jews, they
did not think of the keepers, they did not reckon the still flourishing
madness of those who crucified, nor the ready shamelessness of those sitting
nearby toward insults, having contemplated none of the terrible things in
their thought; but as at an altar of the tomb they were approaching with
tears, weeping there, whence he who had stopped Eve's griefs had been raised,
where the whole world was about to keep festival: And behold, there was a
great earthquake.
For an angel of the Lord descended from heaven, came and rolled away the stone
from the door of the sepulchre, and sat upon it. If when an angel descended
there was a great earthquake, how, when the Master of the angels was being
crucified, was the earth not justly shaken at the things dared? And for what
reason an earthquake at the passion, and again an earthquake at the
resurrection? Fear is joined to fear, that the foundations of death may be
wholly overthrown. There was a great earthquake, stones were trembling, and
Jews were dancing. But the feast which they thought they were keeping, this
they received as a perpetual occasion of dejection. They laid hands on the
lamb, but they lost the altars; they consumed the heir, but they fell from the
covenant; they divided the garments, but they were deprived of the wonders;
they sealed =62.755= the tomb, but they lost the


temple. There was a great earthquake; for an angel of the Lord descended
from heaven, came and rolled away the stone from the door of the
sepulchre.
What is this, O angel? As a bodyguard of the risen king did you run down from
on high? Did you come to console the mourning women? Did you come to rejoice
together with the race of men? Let the stone of the tomb lie beside it. Why do
you shake what the Rock, coming near, did not move? As he was born from the
womb, so he came forth from the tomb; for being born he did not trouble the
bars of virginity, he did not move the stone which he kept. He left the tomb
sealed with the stone. I know, he says, I know; but what happened did not
happen simply; I served the nod of my Master; I rolled the stone by economy,
that Peter, stooping down, might see the linen cloths; that the women might
see the place where the Lord lay. He rolled away, it says, the stone, and sat
upon it.
He was keeping the accuser of the Jews, he was guarding the witness of the
resurrection; he was keeping the stone for the conviction of those petrified
in heart. For this also the Lord, exposing the hostility of the
Christ-killers, cried out to them in =62.756= the Gospels: Even if they keep
silence, the stones will cry out. And his appearance was as lightning, and his
raiment white as snow; reasonably. For where no stain of sin is laid, all
things are full of light, resembling the brightness of snow. And from fear of
him the keepers were shaken, and became as dead. At the servant they became as
dead, and at the Creator they were bold. And what follows? Answering, it says,
the angel said to the women, Do not fear, you. O paradoxical things! O strange
narrations! When the soldiers had become as dead, he was speaking to the
women, Do not fear? For it was necessary that the unbelieving guards remain in
the fear, but that the grace of salvation be announced as good news to the
faithful women. Do not fear, you; for Adam was refashioned by the potter of
your nature, as on a wheel, the cross. Do not fear; for the charge unto
condemnation of your mother Eve has been loosed. Do not fear; for before I
roll away the stone, he whom you seek rose with authority; to him be the glory
with the Father and the Holy Spirit, unto the ages of ages. Amen.
