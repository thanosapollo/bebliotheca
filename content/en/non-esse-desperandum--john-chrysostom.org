* That One Must Not Despair
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Concerning that some should not despair of themselves, nor pray against their
enemies, nor give up in not receiving when they ask; and to husbands
concerning peace toward their wives.

=51.363=

αʹ. I have many thanks for you, because with eagerness you received the words
concerning prayer, because you made me bles =51.364= sed. For blessed is he
who speaks into the ears of those who hear. Not from the clappings and the
praises =51.365= only, but also from the things I saw you doing, I was
persuaded. For when I was exhorting you not to pray against your enemies, and
was saying that we provoke God by doing this, and legislate against him (for
he himself said, Pray for your enemies, but we, praying against our enemies,
ask him to dissolve his own law), when therefore I was saying these and such
things, I saw many among you striking their faces and breasts, groaning
bitterly, stretching out their hands to heaven, asking forgiveness concerning
such prayers. Then I too, having stretched my eyes to heaven, gave thanks to
God, because so quickly the word of the teaching brought forth the fruit for
us. For such is the spiritual seed; it does not need years, nor times, nor
days, but if it takes hold of a noble soul, immediately it shows the ear of
grain ripe and complete; which also happened yesterday by you.

I cast down a word of compunction, and there sprouted a groan of confession, a
groan having much wealth of good things. For if that publican, that he might
strike his breast and say, Be merciful to me the sinner, went away justified
beyond the Pharisee, how much boldness is it likely we have acquired, having
displayed so great a compunction in a short time? And yet nothing is worse
than a publican; for this man is the last bound of wickedness; which also
Christ indicating, continually brings the harlots and the publicans as an
example of the last evils. For it is violence that has grown bold, plunder
without rebuke, a shameless manner of greed, a business having no account, a
shameless trade; but nevertheless he who lived with so many evils was able
from bare words to cast off the reproaches, and to receive more than he asked.
For he himself was asking, saying, Be merciful to me the sinner; but God not
only was gracious, but also justified him beyond the Pharisee. For this reason
Paul says: Now to him who is able to do all things exceeding abundantly above
what we ask or think. And yet the Pharisee prayed, and stood in the temple,
and called upon the same God, and said more words, and made the prelude of the
prayer from thanksgiving. Whence then did that one lose even the good things
he had, and this one receive in addition even the boldness he did not have?
Because the manner of the prayer was not the same. For the one was full of
boastfulness and conceit and madness, but this one of much right-mindedness;
for this reason this one, having ten thousand burdens of sins, laid them all
aside, and received righteousness; but that one, having brought up the ship
full of achievements, and of almsgiving, and of fasting, having dashed as
against some reef upon the mind of vainglory and madness, endured the
shipwreck in the harbor itself; for to suffer loss in prayer is to endure
shipwreck in harbor. But this did not happen from the nature of prayer, but
from his own choice.

βʹ. Do you see how prayer does not suffice for our salvation, unless there be
added the praying according to those laws which Christ has laid down? And what
laws has he laid down?

=51.366= To pray for our enemies, and for those who grieve us much. And if we
do not do this, we perish altogether; and it is clear from the example
concerning the Pharisee. For if this man, not having prayed against his
enemies, but having been vainglorious only, paid so great a penalty, what
punishment awaits those who stretch out long and many words against their
enemies? What are you doing, O man? You stand asking forgiveness of sins, and
you fill the mind with wrath? When we ought to be gentler than all, conversing
with the Master, entreating for our own trespasses, asking mercy and
loving-kindness and forgiveness, then we grow wild, and fall away toward
brutishness, and fill the mouth with bitterness? And how shall we be able,
tell me, to attain salvation, putting forward the form of suppliants, but
bringing words of madness, and provoking the Master against ourselves? You
entered to heal your own wounds, not to make those of the neighbor more
grievous; it is a time of propitiation, a time of prayer and groaning, not of
anger; of tears, not of wrath, of compunction, not of indignation. Why do you
confuse the order? Why do you war against yourself? Why do you pull down your
building? He who prays must before all other things have a gentle mind, a
settled understanding, a contrite heart; but he who cries out against his
enemies would never be able to accomplish this; for he is filled with wrath,
and is not able to have a settled mind.

Let us therefore not pray against our enemies, nor even remember our own
achievements, lest we suffer what the Pharisee suffered. For just as to
remember sins is good, so to forget achievements is good. For what reason?
Because the memory of achievements lifts us up toward boastfulness, but the
memory of sins settles the mind and humbles it; and that makes us more
slothful, but this works us more earnest. For as many as think they have
nothing good become more eager toward acquiring the good things; but those who
suppose that much merchandise has been stored up for themselves, taking
courage in the abundance of it, would not display much zeal toward
encompassing more again.

γʹ. Do not therefore remember the achievements, that God may remember them.
For say, he says, your sins first, that you may be justified. And again: I
will not remember your iniquities, he says; but you remember. But for what
reason did God so quickly hear the publican, but allowed Isaac to beg and
entreat him twenty years concerning his wife, and then nodded to the prayers
of the righteous man? For it is necessary to give back to you the remainders
of yesterday's teaching. For what reason then did this happen? That from the
things concerning the publican you may learn the loving-kindness of the Master
who heard quickly, and from the things concerning Isaac you may learn the
endurance of the servant who received slowly, and did not stand away from the
supplication; that, even if you are a sinner, =51.367= you may not despair,
and even if you are righteous, you may not be lifted up. They that are strong
have no need of a physician, but they that are sick. The publican was sick;
for this reason he quickly stretched out a hand to him; but Isaac was
stronger, and for this reason he left him, that he might stretch out his
endurance. But let this word have been said to us from abundance. But for what
reason the woman was barren, it is necessary to say, that, when you see a
Virgin becoming a mother, you may not disbelieve; that, when the Jew says to
you, How did Mary bear? you may say to him, How did Sarah bear, and Rebecca,
and Rachel? For whenever some wondrous and great wonder is about to come to
be, many types run before. And just as when a king is entering, soldiers run
before, so that they may not receive the king all at once unprepared; so also
when a paradoxical wonder is about to come to be, types run before, so that
we, having practiced beforehand, may not be struck all at once, nor be beside
ourselves at the paradox of what is coming to be. This also in the case of
death. Jonah ran before, and trained our mind. For just as after three days
the whale vomited him, not having found in him its own proper and suitable
food; for the proper and suitable food of death is the nature of sin; from
there it was born, from there it was rooted, from there it is also fed. Just
as therefore in our case, whenever we swallow a stone without knowing, then
first the power of the stomach attempts to digest this; but when it finds it
to be food alien to it, having consorted with it further by the digestive
power, it does not destroy that, but loses its own strength, whence it cannot
even hold down the former food, but having grown weak it vomits out that also
together with it with much pain; so also it happened in the case of death. It
swallowed the cornerstone, and was not able to digest it; all its power grew
weak; for this reason together with it it also cast out the remaining food
which it had, having vomited out together the nature of men. For neither will
it be able to hold this down any longer unto the end. For this reason the
barren women also ran before, that the birth might be believed; or rather not
only that the birth might be believed, but if we examine exactly, we shall
find the barrenness to be a type of death itself also.

δʹ. But attend; for what will be said is subtle; for we are about to say how
the womb of Sarah having been made barren leads us by the hand toward the
faith of the resurrection. How then does it lead us by the hand? Just as she,
being dead, rose from the grace of God, and sprouted a living body, that of
Isaac; so also Christ, having been put to death, was raised by his own power.
And that what has been said has not been forced, hear Paul himself saying. For
having said concerning Abraham, that He considered not the deadness of Sarah's
womb, but was strengthened in faith, giving glory to God, and being fully
persuaded that what he has promised he is able also to do, that is, from dead
bodies to cause a living son to be born; then leading us by the hand from that
faith to this he added: It was not written for his sake alone, that it was
reckoned to him, but also for us. Why? To whom it is about to be reckoned, he
says, to those who believe upon him who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead.
And what he says is of this sort: He raised Isaac from dead bodies; so also
the Son =51.368= he raised, having become dead. Do you wish to learn that the
barrenness is a symbol of another matter also? The Church was about to bring
forth the multitude of the faithful; that therefore you may not disbelieve how
the unfruitful, the barren, the sterile bore, the one barren by nature went
before, making a way beforehand for the one barren by choice, and Sarah became
a type of the Church. For just as that one being barren bore in old age, so
also this one being barren bore at the last of the times. And that this is
true, hear Paul saying: But we are children of the free woman. For since Sarah
is a type of the Church, the free woman, for this reason he added, that We are
children of the free woman. And again: So then, brethren, we are children of
promise according to Isaac. What is, Of promise? Just as nature did not bear
him, neither did nature bear us, but the grace of God. And again: But the
Jerusalem above is free, which is our mother; and this is the Church. For you
have come to Mount Zion, he says, and to the city of the living God, the
heavenly Jerusalem, and to the Church of the firstborn.

If therefore the Church is the Jerusalem above, and Sarah is a type of the
Jerusalem above, as he said, that There are two, one bearing unto slavery,
which is Hagar; but the Jerusalem above is free, which is our mother, it is
quite clear that Sarah is a type of the Jerusalem above according to the birth
and the barrenness.

εʹ. I know that the things said are rather subtle; but if we take them,
nothing of the things spoken will run past us. These words then are more
mystical and more dogmatic; but if you wish, I will speak also more ethically
together with these. The woman was barren, that you may learn the chastity of
the husband; that he neither cast her out, although no law then was
preventing, nor having taken another did he bring her in upon the free woman;
which indeed many do on the pretext of begetting children, fulfilling their
own wantonness, and casting some out, and bringing others in; and some even
arming concubines, and filling the houses with ten thousand wars. But that
righteous man was not so, but he remained loving the wife allotted to him by
God, and he was entreating the Master of nature to correct the bonds of
nature, and he did not reproach the woman. And whence, that he did not
reproach? From the Scripture itself. If he had reproached, the Scripture would
have said this also, and would not have been silent; for it speaks both the
achievements of the righteous and the shortcomings, that we may flee the ones
and emulate the others. When at any rate the bride Rachel was wailing toward
his son, and that one rebuked, it has set down both, and the Scripture did not
hide it. For when she said, Give me children, and if not, I shall die, what
does that one say? Am I in the place of God, who has deprived you of the fruit
of the womb? Give me children. Womanish is the request and unreasonable. Do
you say to the husband, Give me children, having run past the Master of
nature? For this reason that one also, answering more strikingly, settled her
unreasonable request, and taught from whom one must ask. But not so, neither
did he himself say anything of this sort, nor did she wail and lament toward
this man. From this we are trained in chastity and faith. For =51.369= to have
asked God shows his faith; and not to have cast out the wife makes the
chastity manifest to us; and neither to have reproached, nor to have
despaired, makes plain both the endurance and the philosophy and the much
gentleness and the affection toward the wife. For neither, as many now do in
such circumstances, fleeing to enchantments and sorceries, those superfluous
and unprofitable and harmful things, and such as destroy the soul, did that
man do, but having left all these things, and having laughed at all human
things, he ran up to the Master of nature who alone is able to correct these
things.

ςʹ. Hear these things, husbands, be trained, wives, let us all imitate the
righteous man. Let nothing be more honorable to a wife than a husband, nothing
more longed-for to a husband than a wife. This holds together the life of us
all, that a wife be of one mind toward a husband; this holds together the
whole world. For just as when the foundation has been shaken, the whole
building is brought down; so also when marriages are in faction, our whole
life is overturned. For see: the world is composed of cities, the cities of
houses, the houses of men and women. If therefore war comes in between the men
and the women, the war has entered into the houses; and when these are
troubled, the cities also become overthrown; and when cities are in faction,
the whole inhabited world also must of necessity be filled with disturbance,
and war, and battle. For this reason God made much providence of this matter;
for this reason he does not permit one to cast out a wife, except for
fornication only. What then if she be abusive, he says, if she be extravagant
and costly, and have ten thousand other shortcomings? Bear all things nobly,
and do not cast her out because of the shortcomings, but correct the
shortcomings. For this reason you occupy the place of a head, that you may
know how to heal the body. For our body also, even if it have ten thousand
wounds, we do not cut off the head. Do not therefore cut off your wife either;
for the wife is in the rank of the body for us. For this reason also the
blessed Paul was saying: So husbands ought to love their wives as their own
bodies. And toward the wives also the same law is for us: love your own head,
O woman; and if you honor, so honor the husband; for not in vain do we make so
great a word concerning the matter. I know of how many good things it is a
cause, that a wife not be at variance toward a husband; I know of how many
evils it is an occasion, whenever these are in faction toward one another. For
then neither wealth, nor good children, nor many children, nor office and
power, nor glory and honor, nor luxury and costliness, nor any other
prosperity would ever be able to gladden a wife or a husband, whenever they
wrangle with one another.

ζʹ. This then let us be zealous for before all other things. Does the wife
have shortcomings? Do what Isaac did; ask God. For if this man by the
endurance of prayer loosed a maiming of nature, much more shall we be able to
correct shortcomings of choice, continually entreating God. If God sees you
enduring because of his law, and =51.370= bearing nobly the faults of the
wife, he will take part with you in the teaching, and will give you a wage of
the endurance. For what do you know, he says, O husband, whether you will save
the wife? or what do you know, O wife, whether you will save the husband? And
do not grow weary, he says, nor despair. For it happens that she also be
saved; but if she remain uncorrected, you have not lost the wage of the
endurance; but if you cast her out, you have sinned one thing first, the
transgressing of the law, and being judged an adulterer before God; For
whoever casts out his wife, he says, except for the cause of fornication,
makes her to be adulterated. And often also having taken another more grievous
than that one, you have worked the sin, but have not enjoyed rest. But if you
also take a better, it does not allow the pleasure from the second to be
intact for you, because of the dismissal of the former, adultery being
reckoned to you; for it is adultery to have dismissed the former. Whenever
therefore you see some difficulty having fallen together, either in marriage,
or in another state of affairs, entreat God; for this alone is the best
loosing of the terrible things that happen to us. For great is the weapon of
prayer. This I have both said often, and I say now, and I will not cease
saying; even if you are a sinner, look toward the publican who did not fail,
who washed off so many sins. Do you wish to learn how great prayer is?
Friendship does not accomplish so much toward God as prayer. And the word is
not mine; for I would not have dared to declare so great a matter from my own
judgment; hear from the Scriptures how. How much friendship did not
accomplish, prayer accomplished. Which of you, he says, shall have a friend,
and having come shall say to him: Friend, lend me three loaves; and that one
answering will say to him: The door has been shut, the children are upon the
bed; do not give me trouble.

For I say to you, even if he will not give him because of his being his
friend, yet because of his shamelessness he will give him as many things as he
needs. Do you see how, as much as the friendship was not able, this the
persistence was able? For since it was a friend who was asking, lest you
suppose that because of this he accomplished it, he said: Even if he will not
give him because of his being his friend, yet because of his shamelessness he
will give him. Even if the friendship will not work this, he says, yet the
persistence will work, as much as the friendship was not able. And where has
this happened? In the case of the publican. For neither was the publican a
friend to God; but he became a friend; so that even if you are an enemy, by
persistence you will become a friend. See also the Syrophoenician woman, and
hear what he says to her: It is not good to take the bread of the children and
give it to the dogs. And how did he do it, if it is not good? By persistence
the woman made it good; that you may learn, that of the things of which we are
not worthy, we become worthy, through persistence.

ηʹ. I have said these things that you may not say, I am a sinner, I am without
boldness, I do not have prayer. That one has boldness who does not think he
has boldness; as he who thinks he has boldness has lost the boldness, just as
the Pharisee; but he who thinks himself cast away and without boldness, this
one especially will be heard, just as the publican. See how many examples you
have, the Syrophoenician woman, the publican, =51.371= the robber upon the
cross, the friend in the parable who asked for the three loaves, and succeeded
not so much through the friendship as through the persistence. If each of
these had said, I am a sinner, I am put to shame, and because of this I ought
not to approach, he would not have accomplished anything more. But since each
of these looked not toward the greatness of the sins, but toward the wealth of
the loving-kindness of God, he took courage, and grew bold, and being a sinner
asked beyond his own worth, and each accomplished what he wished. Let us
therefore remember all these things and keep them, and let us pray
continually, with sobriety, with taking courage, with good hopes, with much
zeal. With as much zeal as others pray against their enemies, with so much
=51.372= zeal let us pray both for our enemies, and for our own brethren, and
we shall altogether attain all the things that are profitable. For the one who
gives is loving toward man, and we do not long to receive as he longs to give.
Knowing all these things therefore, even if we have been brought down to the
last depth of wickedness, let us not even so despair of our own salvation, but
let us approach with good hope, having persuaded ourselves that we shall
altogether receive what we ask, if we ask according to the laws laid down by
him. To him who is able to do all things exceeding abundantly above what we
ask or think, to Christ the all-ruling God of us, is due all glory, honor and
worship, together with the unoriginate Father, and the all-holy and
life-giving Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
