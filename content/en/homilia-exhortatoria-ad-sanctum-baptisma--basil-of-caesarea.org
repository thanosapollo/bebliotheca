HOMILY XIII.
Exhortation to Holy Baptism.

The wise Solomon, dividing the times of the affairs according to life, and
assigning to each of the things that come to pass its fitting season, said: To
all things there is a time, and a season to every matter; a time to give
birth, and a time to die. But I, slightly altering the saying of the wise man,
would say, proclaiming to you the saving proclamation, that there is a time to
die, and a time to be born. And what is the reason of the interchange? That
he, discoursing about the things in generation and corruption, following
bodily nature, set generation before death (for it is impossible for one who
has not first been born to take experience of death); but I, being about to
make the discourse concerning the spiritual regeneration, set death before
life. For through dying to the flesh it comes about for us to be born to the
Spirit, as the Lord also says: I will kill, and I will make to live. Let us
therefore die, that we may live. Let us kill the mind of the flesh, which
cannot be subject to the law of God, that the mind of the Spirit may be
generated strong in us, through which life and peace are by nature wont to
come about. Let us be buried together with Christ who died for us, that we may
also rise together with him who procured the resurrection for us. Therefore
one season is fitting for some things, another for others: one proper to
sleep, and one proper to waking; one proper to war, and one proper to peace;
but the season of baptism is the whole life of men. For neither is it possible
for a body to live without breathing, nor for a soul to hold together without
knowing the one who created it. For ignorance of God is death of a soul.
And he who has not been baptized has not been enlightened. And without light
neither does the eye see its own things, nor can the soul receive the vision
of God. Therefore every time has opportunity toward the salvation through
baptism; whether you say night, or day, or hour, or a moment of time, or
something most brief. But it is surely far more likely that the more kindred
time is more fitting. And what could become more akin to baptism than the day
of Pascha? For the day is a memorial of resurrection; but baptism is a power
toward the resurrection. Therefore on the resurrection day let us receive the
grace of the =31.425= resurrection. For this reason the Church from afar
summons her own nurslings with a high proclamation, that those whom she has
long been in travail with she may then bring to birth, and having weaned them
from the words of the catechesis, she may give them a taste of the solid food
of the doctrines. John proclaimed a baptism of repentance, and all Judea went
out to him; the Lord proclaims a baptism of adoption; and who of those who
have hoped in him will not hearken? That baptism was introductory; this is
perfecting. That was a withdrawal from sin; this is an intimacy with God. The
proclamation of one man, John, was, and it drew all to repentance; but you,
being taught through prophets: Wash yourselves, become clean; being admonished
through psalms: Come to him, and be enlightened; being evangelized through
apostles: Repent, and let each of you be baptized upon the name of the Lord
Jesus Christ for forgiveness of sins, and you will receive the promise of the
Holy Spirit; being received by the Lord himself, who says: Come to me, all who
labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest (for all these things have
run together today toward the reading); do you hesitate and take counsel and
delay? Having been catechized in the word from infancy, have you not yet
consented to the truth?

Always learning, have you not yet come to the knowledge? A tester through
life, a spy until old age, when will you become a Christian? When shall we
recognize you as our own? Last year you awaited the present season, now again
you wait for the coming one. See that you not be found making promises longer
than life. You do not know what the coming day will bring forth; do not
promise the things that are not yours. We call you to life, O man; why do you
flee the calling? To a share in good things; why do you overstep the gift? The
kingdom of heaven has been opened; the one who calls is not false; the way is
easy, there is need of no time, no expense, no business; why do you delay? Why
do you draw back? Why do you fear the yoke, as some heifer unused to the yoke?
It is kind, it is light; it does not chafe the neck, but glorifies. For the
yoke-collar is not bound about the throat; it seeks the one who draws under it
to be of his own authority. You see that Ephraim is accused as a heifer gone
wild, because he wanders disorderly, dishonoring the yoke of the law? Submit
your untamed neck; become a beast of burden of Christ, lest, being let go from
the yoke-collar, and living freely in life, you be easy for the beasts to
attempt. Taste and see that the Lord is kind. How shall I announce the
sweetness of honey to those who do not know it? Taste and see. Perception is
more evident than every =31.428= word toward experience.
The Jew does not postpone the circumcision because of the threat, that Every
soul which shall not be circumcised on the eighth day shall be utterly
destroyed from its people; but you delay the circumcision made without hands,
perfected in the putting off of the flesh, in baptism, having heard the Lord
himself: Amen, amen I say to you, unless one is born through water and Spirit,
he shall not enter into the kingdom of God. And there, pain and a wound; but
here, dew of soul, and a healing of the wound of the heart. Do you worship the
one who died for you? Then consent to be buried together with him through
baptism. If you do not become grown-together with the likeness of his death,
how will you become a partaker of the resurrection? That Israel was baptized
into Moses in the cloud and in the sea, handing down the types to you, and
characterizing the truth that was to be shown at the last of the times; but
you flee baptism, not being typified in a sea, but being perfected in truth;
not in a cloud, but in the Spirit; not into Moses the fellow-servant, but into
Christ who made you. If Israel had not passed through the sea, he would not
have been separated from Pharaoh; and you, if you do not pass through the
water, will not be separated from the bitter tyranny of the devil. He would
not have drunk from the spiritual rock, if he had not been baptized typically;
nor will anyone give you the true drink, if you are not truly baptized. He ate
bread of angels after the baptism; but how will you eat the living bread, if
you do not first receive baptism? He entered into the land of promise through
the baptism; but how will you return into paradise, not having been sealed
with the baptism? Or do you not know that a flaming sword has been set to
guard the way of the tree of life, fearful and flaming to the unbelievers, but
accessible to those who have believed and shining with desire? And the Master
made it turn; for when it sees a believer, it gives its back; but when someone
of the unsealed, it meets him face to face. Elijah was not struck with terror
at the chariot of fire and the fiery horses that came to him, but by desire of
the upward journey he dared the fearful things, and with great joy he mounted
the flaming vehicles, still living in the flesh; but you, not about to mount
fiery chariots, but through water and Spirit to go up into heaven, do you not
run toward the calling?
Elijah showed the strength of baptism upon the altar of the
whole-burnt-offerings, consuming the sacrifice as a whole-burnt-offering not
through fire, but through water. Otherwise the nature of fire fights with
water; but since my =31.429= stically the water

third then was poured upon the altar, it became a beginning for the fire, and
it kindled the flame as oil. Take for me, he says, water-jars, and pour upon
the whole-burnt-offering and upon the sticks. And he said: Do it a second
time, and they did it a second time; Do it a third time, and they did it a
third time; the word showing that through baptism the one being brought near
is made intimate with God, and that a pure and heavenly light shines forth in
the souls of those who approach through the faith in the Trinity. If they were
distributing gold to the church, you would not have said to me: I will come
tomorrow, and you will give tomorrow; but you would already have demanded the
distribution, pressing, and you would have been impatient at the delay; but
since the great-giver holds out to you not fairness of matter, but purity of
soul, you invent pretexts and enumerate causes, when you ought to run toward
the gift. O the wonder! you are renewed, not being melted; you are reshaped,
not being crushed; you are healed, not being in pain, and you do not reckon
the grace. If you were a slave of men, and freedom was proclaimed beforehand
for slaves, would you not have gone to the mistress, hiring advocates, and
calling upon judges, so as by every device to be taken out toward freedom? Or
perhaps you would even have accepted a blow, the last stroke of slaves, for
the sake of the later release from the outrages. But since, being a slave not
of men but of sin, the herald calls you toward freedom, that he may loose you
from the captivity, and make you a fellow-citizen with the angels, and show
you a son of God adopted through the grace, and an heir of the good things of
Christ; you still say that it is not a season for you to receive the things
given. O the wicked hindrances! O the shameful and unfinished occupation!
Until when pleasures? Until when luxuries?
We have lived a long time for the world; let us live henceforth also for
ourselves. What is of equal worth with a soul? What is of equal weight with
the kingdom of the heavens? What counselor is more trustworthy to you than
God? Who is more prudent than the wise? Or who more beneficial than the good?
Who more intimate than the one who created? Nor did it profit Eve to be
persuaded by the counsel of the serpent rather than by that of the Master. O
the absurdity of the words! I have no leisure to be made healthy; do not yet
show me the light; do not yet join me to the King. Do you not say these things
outright? Or rather things more irrational than these. But you, if you were
enrolled among the public debts, and remissions of debts were announced to
those liable, and then there was someone attempting, for spite, to make you
without a share of this forgiveness; you would have been angry and would have
cried out, as being deprived of the portion falling to you of the common
grace. But since not only forgiveness of the things past, but also gifts of
the things to come have been proclaimed beforehand, yourself wronging
yourself, things which not even one of your enemies would have done you spite
in, =31.432= you think you have taken counsel fittingly, and have considered
profitably concerning yourself, you who do not receive the forgiveness, but
die in the debts? And this though you know that the debtor of the ten thousand
talents would have obtained the forgiveness, if he had not himself renewed the
collection for himself from the inhumanity toward the fellow-servant.
Which we too must guard against suffering, whenever we obtain the grace, not
forgiving our debtors, that the gift may remain firm for us. Enter into the
treasury of your soul, stir up the memory of the things you have done. If your
sins are many, do not be despondent at the multitude; For where sin abounded,
grace superabounded, if you receive the grace; for to the one who owes much,
much also will be forgiven, that he may love more. But if your sins are small
and cheap, and not unto death, why do you agonize over the future, you who
have borne the things past not ignobly, and that when not yet being trained by
the law? Now think that your soul stands as upon a balance, drawn this way by
angels, and that way by demons. To which then will you give the turn of the
heart? What with you

will prevail? pleasure of flesh, or sanctification of soul? enjoyment of the
present things, or desire of the things to come? Will angels take you, or will
those who hold you hold you fast? In array the generals distribute the
watchword to those under them, that both the friends may easily call one
another, and toward the opponents, if they are mixed in the clashes, the
separation may be able to be distinct. No one will recognize you, whether you
are ours, or of the adversaries, unless you furnish the intimacy by the mystic
symbols, unless the light of the face of the Lord is marked upon you. How
shall the angel claim you? And how shall he snatch you from the enemies, if he
does not recognize the seal? And how will you say, I am of God, not bearing
the marks? Or do you not know that the destroyer passed over the sealed
houses, but in the unsealed he slaughtered the firstborn?
An unsealed treasure is easy for thieves to attempt; an unmarked sheep is
plotted against without danger. Are you young? Secure the youth with the
bridle of baptism. Has the prime run past? Do not be deprived of the
provisions, do not lose the phylactery, do not think about the eleventh hour
as about the first; seeing that it befits even the one beginning life to have
the end before his eyes. If some physician promised you by certain devices and
inventions to make you young from old =31.433=, would you not have desired to
come to that day, on which you were about to see yourself turning back toward
the prime? But since baptism promises you that your soul will bloom again,
which you have aged, and have shown wrinkled from the lawless deeds, and
stained, you despise the benefactor, and you do not run toward the promise. Do
you not desire to see what the great wonder of the promise is? how a man is
born again without a mother? how the one growing old and being corrupted
according to the desires of deceit is vigorous again, and grows young, and
returns to the true flower of youth? Baptism is a ransom for captives,
forgiveness of debts, death of sin, regeneration of soul, a bright garment, a
seal not to be attempted, a vehicle toward heaven, a procurer of the kingdom,
a gift of adoption. Of so many and so great good things, wretched one, do you
hold pleasure more honorable? For I understand your delay, even if you wrap it
with words; the deeds themselves cry out, even if you are silent with the
voice. Let me, I will use the flesh toward the enjoyment of the shameful
things, I will roll in the mire of pleasures, I will bloody my hands, I will
take the things of others, I will go deceitfully, I will swear falsely, I will
lie; and then baptism, whenever I cease at some time from the evils, I will
receive. If then sin is a good thing, keep it to the end; but if it is harmful
to the one who does it, why do you remain in the destructive things? No one,
seeking to vomit bile, gathers it for himself many times over from a wicked
and unrestrained diet. It is fitting to cleanse the body of the things that
pain it, not however to make the disease greater than the strength. The ship
appears up to a point to bear the weight of the cargoes, but what is beyond
this sinks it. Fear lest you too suffer the like, and having made the sins
greater than the forgiveness, you undergo the shipwreck before the hoped-for
harbors.
Does God not see the things that come to pass? Or does he not understand your
thoughts? Or does he work the lawless deeds together with you? You supposed
lawlessness, he says, that I will be like you. But you, when you are doing
business for the friendship of a mortal man, you draw him near through
benefaction, saying and doing those things in which you perceive him to
rejoice; but when you are being made intimate with God, and hoping to be
received into the rank of a son, doing the things hostile to God =31.436= and
dishonoring him through transgression of the law, from the very things by
which you most of all offend, from these you hope for the intimacy? See that,
on hope of the redemption, having gathered a multitude of evils for yourself,
you heap up the sin, but fail of the forgiveness. God is not mocked. Do not
traffic in the grace. Do not say: The law is good, but sweeter

is sin. Pleasure is a hook of the devil drawing toward destruction. Pleasure
is mother of sin; and sin is the sting of death. Pleasure is nurse of the
eternal worm, which for a season smooths the one enjoying, but afterward makes
the returns more bitter than bile. The delay cries nothing else but these
things: let sin first reign in me, then the Lord too will reign at some time.
I will present my members as weapons of unrighteousness to lawlessness; then I
will present them at some time also as weapons of righteousness to God. Thus
Cain too offered the sacrifices; the first things to his own enjoyment, the
second things to God who created and bestowed. When you are strong in works,
you spend your youth upon the sins. When the organs grow weary, then you bring
them to God, when there is no using them for anything, but they must lie, the
tension having been relaxed through the withering from time. Temperance in old
age is not temperance, but inability of incontinence. A dead man is not
crowned; no one is righteous through inability of evil. While there is power
for you, master sin by reason. For this is virtue: a turning aside from evil,
and a doing of good. But idleness of wickedness is itself by itself worthy
neither of praises nor of punishments. If through age you cease from sin, the
grace is of the weakness. But we praise those good by choice, not those being
barred by some necessity.
And who has fixed for you the bound of life? Who has determined for you the
appointed time of old age? Who is so trustworthy a surety with you of the
things to come? Do you not see infants being snatched, those in the prime of
age being led away? Life does not have one appointed time. Why do you wait for
baptism to become a gift of fever for you? when you will not even be able to
utter the saving words, and perhaps you will not even be permitted to hear
clearly, the disease dwelling in the head itself; not to raise hands to
heaven, not to stand up upon feet, not to bend a knee toward the worship, not
to be taught usefully, not to confess securely, not to covenant with God, not
to renounce the enemy, and perhaps not even to follow understandingly being
initiated, being ambiguous to those present, whether you perceived =31.437=
the grace, or are without perception of the things coming to pass. And when
you also receive the grace knowledgeably, then you have the talent, but you do
not bring the working. Imitate the eunuch. That man found the one catechizing,
and he did not overlook the learning, but the rich man took the poor man up
onto the chariot, the solemn and overbearing took up the private and
contemptible man; and having been taught the Gospel of the kingdom, he
received the faith in the heart, and he did not postpone the seal of the
Spirit. For when they met with water, Behold, he says, water. The word is from
great joy; Behold the thing sought, What hinders me to be baptized? Where the
choice is ready, there is nothing that hinders. For the one who calls is a
lover of man, the minister is prepared, the grace is ungrudging; let the
eagerness be present, and what impedes will not be. There is one who hinders,
the one who blocks the ways into salvation for us, whom we shall conquer by
understanding. He produces the hesitation in us; let us rise toward the
working. He deceives our hearts with empty promises; let us not be ignorant of
his thoughts. For does he not suggest doing the sin today, and persuade us to
store up the righteousness for tomorrow? For this reason the Lord, dissolving
his wicked counsels, Today, he says, if you hear my voice. He says: Today for
me, and tomorrow for God. The Lord cries against: Today hear my voice.
Understand the enemy; he does not dare to counsel a complete standing-away
from God (he knows that this is heavy for Christians to hear), but by
deceptive arts he manages the attempt. He is wise to do evil. He sees that men
live according to the present, and every deed is worked according to what is
at hand. Therefore stealing our today through the management, he leaves us the
hopes of tomorrow. Then, whenever tomorrow comes,

again the wicked sharer of ours comes, claiming today for himself, and
tomorrow for the Lord; and thus always taking away the present through
pleasure, and attaching the future to our hopes, he secretly shepherds us away
from life. Such a cunning of a resourceful bird I once saw. For her nestlings
being easy to catch through tenderness, putting herself forward as a ready
prey, and turning about in the hands of the hunters, she was neither easy to
catch at once for the hunters, nor did she produce despair =31.440= of the
catch in them; but holding them variously by the hopes, by the occupation
about herself, she procured for the nestlings the freedom of the flight, then
at the last she too flew away with them. Fear lest you too suffer the like,
giving up the things that are clear in the hopes about the unclear. Come then,
transfer your whole self to the Lord; give over your naming; be enrolled with
the Church.
The soldier is numbered in the rolls; the athlete having enrolled himself
contends; the citizen, having been registered, is numbered with the tribesmen.
You are liable to all these, as a soldier of Christ, as an athlete of piety,
as having the citizenship in the heavens. Enroll yourself in this book, that
you may be transferred to the one above. Learn, be taught an evangelical way
of life, exactness of eyes, restraint of tongue, enslavement of body, a humble
mind, purity of thoughts, disappearance of anger. Being pressed into service,
add; being deprived, do not go to law; being hated, love; being persecuted,
endure; being blasphemed, exhort. Be put to death to sin; be crucified
together with Christ; transfer the whole love to the Lord. But these things
are hard. And what of the good things is easy? Who sleeping has set up a
trophy? Who living in luxury and being piped to has been adorned with the
crowns of endurance? No one not having run has taken the prize. Toils give
birth to glory, labors procure crowns. Through many afflictions we must enter
into the kingdom of the heavens, I too say; but these afflictions the
blessedness in the kingdom of the heavens awaits; but the labors of sin the
toilsome and gloomy thing of Gehenna awaits. But indeed to one looking
accurately, not even the works of the devil come to the workers of lawlessness
without effort. What sweats of temperance? But the one who fornicates is in a
sweat, the pleasure wasting him.
How much does self-control take away from the body, as much as the abominable
and raging incontinence destroys? And sleepless nights of those who pass the
night in prayers; but far harder are those of the ones who stay awake in
injustices. For both the fear of being detected, and the sting upon the
pleasures, drive away all rest utterly. But if, fleeing the narrow of the way
that leads toward salvation, you pursue the spacious of sin, I fear that,
having traveled the broad way to the end, you may find the lodging following
upon the way. But the treasure is hard to guard. Then keep watch, brother. You
have fellow-workers, if you wish, prayer keeping night-watch, fasting keeping
the house, psalmody leading the soul. Take these along. Let these lodge with
you at the watches of the =31.441= precious things. What is better, tell me,
that we being rich agonize over the guard of the precious things, or not even
to have at the start what we shall guard? No one through fear of deprivations
thrusts away the good things. For thus nothing of human things would even
stand together, if in each of the things pursued we reckoned the failures. For
barrenness lies beside farming, and shipwrecks beside trades, widowhoods
beside marriages, childlessnesses beside the rearing of children. But
nevertheless we set our hands to the works, leaning upon the better hopes; and
committing the outcome of the things hoped for to God who manages our things.
But you in word make much of sanctification; in deed you spend time in the
things condemned. See lest

at some time you repent of the wicked counsels, when there is no profit to
you of the repentance. Let the example of the virgins bring you to your
senses. Those women, not having the oil in the vessels, when they needed to
enter together with the bridegroom, then perceived themselves to be in want of
the necessary things. Wherefore the word also called them foolish, because,
having spent the season of the use of the oil on going about and gathering it
together, they escaped their own notice shutting themselves out of the
brightness of the bridechamber.
Never also you, putting off year from year, and months from months, and day
from day, and not taking the oil the food of the light, fall at some time into
a day which you do not expect, when the resources of living fail you at last;
and there is want from every side and inconsolable affliction, the physicians
having given up, and the household having given up; when being held by
frequent and dry gasping, a fierce fever burning the inward parts and
smoldering them, you will groan from the middle of the heart, but you will not
find the one who shares the grief. And you will utter something thin and
powerless, but there will be no one who hears; and everything spoken by you is
despised as delirium. Who will give the baptism then? Who will remind one
stunned by the passion? The relatives are despondent, those outside the
passion despise, the friend hesitates at the reminder as producing
disturbance, or perhaps even a physician deceives, and you do not despair of
yourself because of being by nature a lover of life. Night and a desert of
those who help; the one who baptizes is not present. Death has stood by; those
who lead away press. Who is the one who delivers? God, who has been despised?
But he will hear then; for you hear him now. Will he give an appointed time?
For you used well the one given.
Let no one deceive you with empty words. For sudden destruction will stand
over you, and the catastrophe will be present like a tempest. A downcast angel
will come, le =31.444= ading away forcibly and dragging your soul bound by the
sins, turning often toward the things here, and lamenting without voice, the
organ of the dirges having been shut off at last. O how many times you will
tear yourself! how many times you will groan! repenting without effect over
the things counseled, when you see the brightness of the righteous in the
splendid distribution of the gifts, and the downcast look of the sinners in
the deepest darkness. What things will you say then in the pain of your heart?
Woe is me, not to have thrown off this heavy burden of sin, the laying-down
being so easy, but to be dragged along with the heap of these evils. Woe is
me, not to have washed off the stains, but to be branded with the sins. Now I
would have been with the angels; now I would have been luxuriating in the
heavenly good things. O the wicked counsels! through a temporary enjoyment of
sin I am tortured deathlessly; through pleasure of flesh I am given over to
the fire. Just is the judgment of God; I was called, and I did not hearken; I
was taught, and I did not attend; they testified to me, but I laughed. These
and such things you will say, bewailing yourself, if you are snatched away
before the baptism. O man, either fear Gehenna, or claim the kingdom. Do not
dishonor the calling. Do not say, Have me excused because of this and that. No
pretext is sufficient toward an excuse. It comes upon me to weep, when I
consider that you prefer the works of shame to the great glory of God, and
through the pleasant of incontinence, being hard to tear away from sin, you
shut yourself out of the good things in promises, so as not to see the good
things of the heavenly Jerusalem. There myriads of angels, assemblies of
firstborn, thrones of apostles, presidencies of prophets, scepters of
patriarchs, crowns of martyrs, praises of the righteous. Desire to be numbered
with those, having been washed and sanctified according to the gift of Christ;
because to him is the glory and the power unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
