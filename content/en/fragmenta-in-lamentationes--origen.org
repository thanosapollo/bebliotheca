1 Jeremiah, the people having been taken captive and being in Babylon, laments
the city and the country and the people over the things that have befallen
them, and he laments marking off certain sections and divisions beginning in
sequence from the letters among the Hebrews. And after finishing the Lament
upon each letter and, after all, the one beginning from Thau, which is the
last letter of the Hebrews, he returns to Aleph and again laments, and he does
this four times going through the twenty-two letters.

2 If we see the soul becoming subject to the devil or also to his angels, and
in a word to the hostile powers, the soul that by nature is contemplative and
able to behold the things that are <and> to attend, we shall somehow
understand the captivity and the one who took captive. But taking refuge upon
Christ, who according to the prophet Isaiah proclaimed "release to captives,"
we shall be freed from the captivity, even if the one who took captive should
reach as far as binding. For Jesus came "to lead out those bound from bonds
and those sitting in darkness from a house of prison."

3 For this reason the Hebrews say that the books of the old scripture are
equal in number to the letters, so as to be every introduction toward
knowledge of God, just as the letters toward all wisdom for those who learn.
And for this reason it is quadrupled, perhaps because the elements of bodies
also stand as four. And after a little: But an edition of Aquila and
Theodotion is not current in the Lamentations, but only of Symmachus and of
the Seventy. And again: But one might inquire how, the title of the book
having Lamentations in the plural, he speaks of this Lament in the singular in
the prologue. But the partial ones are more, divided by the letters, yet all
are brought up into one, being brought up toward one subject, Israel.

4 The weeping from love being blessed; for it belongs to the holy man "to weep
with those who weep."

5 Drawing near to Jerusalem the Lord "wept and said: if you also had known the
things toward your peace." "Jerusalem, Jerusalem, who kills the prophets and
stones those sent to her, how often I wished to gather your children, as a
bird gathers her nestlings under the wings, and you did not wish."

6 If then "blessed are those who weep, because they shall laugh," Jeremiah had
to weep this mystical weeping and according to the "blessed are those who
mourn" to mourn those worthy of being mourned.

7 And the multitude of the people seems, as far as the wording, to show the
Israelite inhabitants, and the multitude of the nations, as we have said
before, the proselytes. But as to anagogy, since the contemplative things are
more akin to the soul and less subject to circumstance than the practical, the
multitude of the people would signify of the blessed Jerusalem the wealth of
contemplations; and the multitude of the nations the large number of good
deeds. And after a little: She who has been multiplied with people sits alone:
by the bride having cast off the bridegroom Word, becoming as a widow, she who
once was full with the nations previously mentioned.

8 As far as the wording it is fitting that Jerusalem be understood to be said
with wonder, because of the change toward the more gloomy, to have sat
isolated, she who once was full of people, so that the isolation may be her
desolation, having had as husband the living protector Word, when she followed
him present and helping, but having been widowed and left behind by him
because of the things sinned. And after other things: These things then toward
the letter; but toward the sense, Jerusalem thriving and full of people and of
nations and ruling countries is the divine soul. And it is no wonder if not
only a house nor a homestead nor the village greater than this that would be
named nor an ordinary city but, differing from all cities and honored beside
God, the perfect soul is styled Jerusalem, a magnitude of notable size being
thrown around her by wisdom as around the greatest city and of every virtue †
coming about manifold buildings. And after

a little: And just as it is possible to see Jerusalem thriving, multiplying
with people and nations and ruling in countries, so, if virtue is liable to
fall, her at some time being isolated and widowed and enslaved, so as even to
pay tributes to the enemy who has overpowered her, according to the soul of
the one who has fallen out of the truth.

9 But what need is there to say also concerning this, that having ruled
countries she is enslaved among the enemies to such a degree that she also
always pays tribute, paying to the evil masters whatever they may command? And
they command nothing useful. And perhaps for each of the things she fails in
here, she pays some tribute to Nebuchadnezzar, who is interpreted Sitting-down
and knowledge of constraint; and not only to Nebuchadnezzar, but also to his
satraps.

10 Upon the first Lament, the one that began from Aleph, we have been able to
save the wording. But upon this one what would someone say, a city being said
to have wept in the night, so that the tears are upon her cheeks? Unless
perhaps someone will say that those inhabiting, being named a city, who also
pay tribute, have wept, having been reminded from what sort of peace and
freedom they have come into how great a slavery and humiliation. How then
shall we also explain the in the night, or that in the time of the common rest
of all men the city, being reminded of the disasters, that is those inhabiting
the city not resting, were weeping, and they were weeping with the tears
flowing in streams upon their cheeks? And the weeping was stretched especially
for them and the tears were pouring, there not being even one comforting them
from so many who loved, and every one who was fond of her setting aside the
friendship toward her and having changed into enmity. Who then, as toward the
wording, are those who formerly loved her, from whom there was no one who
would comfort her? Perhaps the remaining cities of Judea, and perhaps for this
reason loving the metropolis, in a time of circumstance they were not
exhorting. And after a little: These things then toward the letter. But if the
Word, as we set forth in the things before these, should call Jerusalem the
soul of such a sort, this one would be said to weep, having fallen out of her
own country, having come to be in Confusion, and to have a state opposite to
laughing the laugh from joy and cheerfulness, which our Lord promises will
belong to those who have wept the blessed weeping, saying: "blessed are those
who weep now, because they shall laugh." And in Job too he says "the mouth of
true men he will fill with laughter." And perhaps for this reason one of the
patriarchs also is called Laughter. He then who groans because of being in the
tent, as the apostle has made clear saying: "for we also who are in the tent
groan being burdened," longing for the laugh in promise and the divine
cheerfulness, weeps a weeping most nourishing and benefiting the soul.

11 And we think that to love is more divine and, that I may so speak,
spiritual, but to be fond is bodily and more human. And perhaps, no worthless
person loving, it is to be observed that concerning the better ones he says:
there will not be one comforting her from all who love her, but concerning the
lesser: all who were fond of her have set her aside, they became to her for
enemies. For the more deficient both set her aside and became enemies to her;
but the better ones only did not comfort.

12 Toward the letter the sense would be of this sort: Judea changed her own
place, these things having become causes for her of her humiliation (which she
has been humbled, having sinned and having left behind the stature and the
raised-up state which she formerly had while existing in piety), and still
also of her much slavery, which she had been enslaved, having been subjected
to the passions. And having been moved she was settled among nations, where
she has not been able to find rest, all the enemies pursuing her down and
overtaking and overpowering, she having come to be among the nations who were
afflicting her.

13 The sense toward the letter is this: the law saying, "three times in the
year every male of yours shall appear before the Lord your God," those from
the people were eager, after

the temple was built on Zion from the time of Solomon, to accomplish what was
commanded. And the cities of Judea being around Jerusalem, there were roads
from all sides bearing upon Zion. These then the prophet <says> mourn because
of the captivity, being gloomy and existing in all desolation † and in those
times, when there was much gladness and cheerfulness in them, the Jews from
everywhere being eager toward a feast upon the mountain of Zion. And there
being many gates that had been built on Zion from all the quarters, the Word
says that all have been made to vanish, and that the priests groan, clearly
not being able to accomplish the priestly works according to the feasts and
the other days, either by having been taken captive or by there being no one
approaching them and requiring the priestly liturgies. But also the virgins,
he says, of Zion are being led, and it is clear that by the enemy who have
overpowered.

14 But toward the sense: of the watchful and contemplative power being called
Zion there are many roads of the varied doctrines, mystical and physical and
ethical, and perhaps also logical, which bear upon the comprehension and
vision of the things set forth. Whenever then someone, having lost the
discipline of these, is confused, all these roads mourn not being traveled,
there being a festal assembly for the previously mentioned roads when they are
traveled being examined and, that I may so speak, being gone through; but they
mourn when they are made desolate, not being searched nor being gone through,
because those traveling have fallen away and, having been taken captive by the
passions, are distracted about the worse things.

15 Zion, having nothing sweet at all, spends time in bitterness by herself.
For the being made bitter is not now to be taken of some form of anger.

16 The clarity of the wording is of some such sort. Some were afflicting the
country of Judea, or at least wished to afflict it, before the times of the
captivity, but they were not prevailing, God shielding the Jews, when those
were presenting themselves worthy of help. But when they sinned and, besides
not hearing the prophets, the sons of Israel were even killing them, then they
were delivered subject to those who afflict, so that their enemies became a
head, that is, as Symmachus interpreted, rulers. And others perhaps, being
enemies besides those who afflict, either in conception or also in
subsistence, were in prosperity, those abandoned because of the sins having
become subject to those who afflict, the Lord having humbled through the
multitude of the impieties those who had transgressed and having done this to
such a degree that not even for their infants was mercy left, but they went in
captivity, being driven by every one who afflicts.

17 Both those who ruled and those who were gladdened at the Lord's having
delivered, because of the multitude of the impieties, into humiliation those
who had not kept the covenants toward him in the holy oracles.

18 The things of the wording, as far as is in our power, are to be made clear,
as we examine who is the daughter of Zion, and in what she has the comeliness,
and what is the pasture which the rulers of the daughter of Zion do not have,
like rams lacking grazing; and how these, having been deprived of strength, go
before the face of the one who pursues. And we suppose that the mountain of
Zion is mother of the synagogue in it, styled daughter of Zion, and the
comeliness of the daughter of Zion the possession in her robes, which
possession in the time of the plunder, having been taken by the enemies, went
out from these of whom it was an ornament, that is it was carried out (for
this he named more emphatically by the went out), and that the leaders of the
synagogue, because the enemies were pressing and taking captive, lacking the
customary things, have been likened to rams not finding food. Just as indeed
rams being driven, not having been fed, go weakly, yielding to the necessity
of those pursuing after, so also the rulers having become captives, being led
away into the country of those who took captive, were going not in strength
before the sight of the one who pursues.

19 But toward the sense the soul of such a sort is a daughter of Zion: as the
just woman is a daughter of justice and the wise woman a child of wisdom, so
also (since Zion is the

Watchtower) the watchful and contemplatively going-through one would be called
a daughter of Zion. And what else would I duly think her comeliness and
dignity
to be than the wealth of the holy doctrines of the truth contemplated with
wisdom? Whenever then someone, after acquiring comeliness and dignity,
neglects both the holding-together of these and the increase of the divine
wealth, the things that were until then her ornament flow away and go out. And
rulers, those who led the word and guided by the hand toward the acquisition
of the previously mentioned comeliness, like rams, the leaders, of the flock
of sheep not finding pasture, have themselves also come to be in want, having
let go the attention of the things able to fatten, that I may so speak, both
them and the sheep. For this reason having grown weak, having been overtaken
by the one who pursues, who is "our adversary the devil," they go before him
being driven wherever that one wishes to lead. If however the previously
mentioned daughter of Zion should take care of her beauty and of her
comeliness and forethought that the dignity be guarded, she makes cheap and
sneers at the king of the Assyrians, as we have learned from Isaiah saying:
"the virgin daughter of Zion made you cheap and sneered at you."

20 For the one who is reminded in suffering such and such things is not
reminded of the present, but of the past. And after a little: Having raised
such difficulties in the place and seeking a reminder of her past humiliation
and of her rejections, because of her people having fallen into hands of one
who afflicts and the things brought upon her having happened, we consider
whether perhaps humiliation in the proper sense is no other than sin, just as
heightening is success, and rejection comes to be of nothing acting except the
wretched deed; for this is sufficient to reject from God and from every finest
thing according to nature. When then Jerusalem was being humbled, being drunk,
that is those inhabiting her, and beside herself from the sin, she was not
pleased with the failures; but when her retributions have come through her
people having fallen into hands of the one who afflicts, no one helping her,
then she was reminded of the things because of which she was suffering,
grasping those as being causes both of her people having fallen and of her
having been left without help. And after a little: But if the soul of such a
sort is Jerusalem, it is worth seeking her people, at whose falling into hands
of one who afflicts, by there not being a helper, she herself is reminded of
the days of her humiliation, and still also of her own rejections by which she
was rejected, and besides these also of her desires, as many as there were
from ancient days. And see whether the contemplations in the scientific soul
are not the people of Jerusalem, which, by the soul having come to be in
inattention and neglect, falls being thrown down by every one who afflicts.
For the doctrines of those who neglect flow away, being squeezed out by the
sins that have gained place in the one who neglects the better things, having
in no way the help from reason.

21 The sense of the wording is of this sort. Since Jerusalem sinned a sin, she
has come to be in tossing and in disturbance to such a degree that all who
formerly glorified her, having changed, have dishonored such a city, having
seen her unseemliness. And she herself, over the things she has suffered
because of the sins, having fallen into tossing and dishonor from those who
once glorified, groaning has no longer come forward of the sins, but having
lessened them has withdrawn backward. And after a little: But if Jerusalem of
such a sort is said to be the soul, some skilled and clear-seeing one and able
to comprehend "the peace of God that surpasses every mind," since it is
possible from many causes for this one also to sin, it is shown that she has
fallen over into tossing, having stood away from the good things and having
come to be in the worse. And everyone who has fallen out of virtue and lives
with vice is soundly said to be in tossing.

22 It shows the cutting-short of the sins of Jerusalem, no longer "stretching
forward to the things in front" of the failures nor coming to be in the
pasture of the sins, but turning away

to the things behind for her own good.

23 According to the bodily narrative what is now said concerning Jerusalem is
of this sort. The uncleanness of Jerusalem is a hindrance; but of what it is a
hindrance, the prophet has left us to understand. Perhaps then he shows that
her uncleanness is a preventer of her journey. But also being of such a sort
she shows that Jerusalem did not remember her last things. And the one who has
these before his eyes and sees what will meet because of the sins will not
sin. For this uncleanness then there is a hindrance for her; and because she
does not remember her last things, her wonderful and great things, which she
had when she was worthy of being overseen, she brought down and carried into
lowliness, they having formerly been over-swollen and greater than every
praise. But even after all these things there was not the one comforting her
who had brought down her greatest things. And upon these consider the
humiliation of me who am speaking, Lord, since the enemy is magnified over the
things that have come to be for my fatherland. And after a little: It is to be
sought however according to the laws of anagogy, whether it is possible for
uncleanness to exist in someone who is not cause of this. And let us see also
the first thoughts of the Lament. The soul of such a sort, having fallen out,
is also altogether in uncleanness, which uncleanness hinders traveling the
holy road. And just as the holy Moses, stepping upon a holy place, is
commanded by God to take off his sandals, and his successor Joshua, it not
being fitting that those in a holy place have dead hide around the feet, so if
someone is going to travel unhindered, perhaps he ought not only to take off
the sandals but also to wash off the uncleanness that has come to be from the
dust
around the feet. And we think that a symbol of this is the Savior, having
thrown water into the basin, having washed the feet of the disciples, saying
to Peter who did not yet understand the mystery and for this reason was
preventing: "if I do not wash you, you have no part with me," and "what I do,
you do not know now, but you will know after these things." Therefore it will
be necessary, having gathered the uncleanness around the, that I may so speak,
feet of the soul, [and] to ask Jesus that he wash it off and we may be able to
have a part with him. For if this should remain, it is a hindrance for us to
walk the road according to the sound word, the Savior who said: "I am the
road."

24 And it is clear that the healthy soul, or at least the one wishing to be
such, does these things, not so much looking at the present only as seeing
beforehand what will also meet for the future from the things being
accomplished. And if, something useful being thought to come to be for the
present, she should foresee the bitter in the future, having dismissed what
seems to be pleasant she does the opposite, for the sake of not falling after
these things into some hard thing; but if, the present being toilsome, good
hopes succeed what appears harsh, she will voluntarily give herself over to
the roughness of what would have seemed to some to be terrible, for the sake
of obtaining the sweet things that succeed the harsh through having endured
these.

25 And having suffered all these things, he says, she who is more tropically
called Jerusalem did not even have abundance of the one who comforts; for
having turned away the word in her and having become wholly of the passion,
she has lost the helper, who always arms against the pains and against the
things thought terrible the disposition that attends to him. And upon these
see the humiliation of me the citizen: as a kinsman of a soul would say: the
passion of my fellow-citizen, through the love of men I was taught, is my
disaster. Consider then, O Lord, and do not completely leave her who has
suffered the things previously mentioned; do not overlook the enemy for her, I
mean vice having been magnified and poured out against her. And more
tropically the see, Lord, my humiliation can be said by the word existing in
the soul, having been dishonored and humbled and, that I may so speak, having
lost its own kingdom.

26 Desires of Jerusalem then are to be understood as the things in the temple,
and the holy of holies laid up as suited for the priestly liturgy, and the
things otherwise believed to be divine by being stored in the temple, such as
the

golden lampstand and the rest. For those from the people, having been
persuaded that these things were stored in the temple for the glory of God,
preferred them before all, themselves wishing rather to suffer some hard thing
from the enemies than for something of these to become subject to the enemies.
And sanctuary it is custom for those from the circumcision to call the temple.

27 And Jerusalem has suffered something most paradoxical at the time of the
captivity. For she saw nations, whom God commanded not to enter into the
assembly, treading the sanctuary, where it is not lawful even for the
Israelites to enter, only the priests having been counted worthy to come to be
in that place with the not ordinary purifications. And the enemies reached
even as far as the untrodden places, having entered into a place which
scarcely "with much holiness the high priest entered once in the year." And
after a little: But if Jerusalem, just as we set forth before, is the soul of
such a sort, her goods according to nature that are her ornament, through
which she was serving the divine, stored in her places untrodden for everyone
except the high-priestly word, are to be understood to be called her desires.
And upon all these the enemy who has prevailed in the wrestling, having
afflicted her who was not well campaigning for herself, but was worthily
abandoned by the divine help, having stretched himself through his active
power toward the wretched things, has pulled out of the ruling faculty and has
left desolate of the good things the soul that stood away from God. And
nations too according to the place are to be allegorized as the crowd of
vices, having entered into the ruling faculty that was created to be able to
contain God and a true sanctuary of the one who wishes to rest upon a pure
mind. Which nations God wholly forbids to enter into the assembly; for what
else would we think is more properly forbidden by God to enter into a soul
numbered in the assembly of God than the works and the mind of the flesh,
being truly nations hostile to God? "For the flesh campaigns against the
spirit," "the mind of the flesh being enmity toward God"; "for it is not
subject to the law of God; for it cannot even."

28 They were groaning through famine and for the sake of a small bit of food
that turns back a failing soul they were giving the most precious things. And
how were the previously mentioned desires, having been carried off by the
enemies, giving toward purchase of food? But "the enemy stretched the hand
upon all," and having been sated by taking what he wished, he left the rest,
in return for which they bought the food.

29 A prayer from Jerusalem is the see, Lord, my dishonor, she having lost the
ornament through famine. And perhaps the desires are different, primarily the
ones previously shown "upon which the enemy stretched the hand," and second
the things of each one of those from the people; and the common things he
took, but in return for the private each was buying food. But toward anagogy,
just as Esau sold the birthright for the sake of red food, from which he has
become named (and Edom is Red earth; for he also became earthy, having
despised the birthright, but having made the earthy things primary, which
Jacob was pursuing as necessary in a second account because of existing in a
body), so also Jerusalem. But the one gives to worthy Jacob to obtain what
desires he had, even if he did not choose these; but the people of Jerusalem
has itself also given for the sake of food, yet not of a holy man having
prepared this, but of Babylonians who were going to insult the desires that
were given. For Jacob knows how to ornament the things from Esau, but the
Babylonians do not use the things given for their own good. So also a soul
through lack of the necessary needs throws away the divine lessons to those
who wish from curiosity to learn for slander and mockery.

30 He has shown the burning at the capture that fastened upon the strongest
buildings, or the capture of the outstanding citizens in her, being as it were
fired in soul and in the outward things by the disasters. He also shows the
seizure of those lower down into slavery as in a catch of nets, of those who
did not use freedom well. For these, "not stretching forward to the things in
front, were forgetting the things behind"; but they were turning away from
glory to dishonor, from

freedom to slavery, and they have become instead of glorious ones dimmed and
obscure from dishonor and insolence; because of which things the city has
fallen into continual grief.

31 Providence, he says, has not slept upon my impieties; for God as if taking
them into hands through the examination has woven them into the works for the
retribution. And these have become for me as a yoke upon a neck and have laid
on slavery; for the slavery which I was enslaved to Nebuchadnezzar is my
impieties, wherefore I have become without tension, having been delivered to
these whose mastery I cannot stand. And after a little: His yoke is upon my
neck. And it is shown through the present saying that the things said as
concerning our passions <concerning> God must be taken more tropically. For
how else would the impieties of Jerusalem woven together with the hand of God
be understood than tropically? For no one would say these things also bodily.
And how is it not out of place, the neck of Jerusalem being a city being taken
tropically, but the members said as of God not being heard in the same manner?
And let the things now be said as by a soul that has slept into impiety, in
having thrown off the saving yoke of the sound word, which the Savior names
useful, but having slipped under that of the impieties, through which she grew
without tension, having been let go from the divine oversight and having been
delivered to sin that reigns over the evils, whose wills through the unbounded
and infinite of vice she cannot altogether serve and furnish satiety to it.
And sin truly humbles and chokes and makes one look down; and a yoke also is
the conscience, always choking and wounding and not allowing one to lift the
head and taking away all freedom of speech. And a paralysis, he says, of the
strength which I did not use well he also made, and I am undone as I behold
the evils that hold me.

32 He did not take them up out of my midst (for even if I were deprived of
them, yet it was possible to use them for other things), but in my midst he
condemned them to suffer because of the things I was impious, and my chosen
ones have suffered the hardest things, the Lord himself having trodden the
wine of his wrath in a winepress to fullness for me and for the virgin of most
kingly Judah. And he would call strong those powerful in war and shielding the
fatherland, and chosen the well-born or the wise. And the he trod a winepress
makes clear what has been said in Psalms concerning the punishment of the
sinners: "a cup in the hand of the Lord, of unmixed wine full of mixture,"
being tilted from this one who is punished upon that one who is punished,
until "all the sinners of the earth" drink, the lees being left for those
still more sinful than these, into whom last "the lees" will be emptied.

33 And it is not hard to see how the Lord trod a winepress for a virgin
daughter of Judah; for since "what each sows, these he will also reap,"
perhaps then also what he farms, these he also vintages. He then who has made
better wine will be gladdened from his own products, but he who farmed "a vine
of Sodom and a vine-branch of Gomorrah and bore as fruit a grape of gall and a
cluster of bitterness and wrath of asps incurable, all being gathered by the
Lord" into one winepress, his evil works being trodden and squeezed, will
drink correspondingly to such fruits for the sake of vomiting the gall and the
bitterness and the wrath. And he will drink, according to Jeremiah unto
vomiting, but according to Isaiah unto being raised and putting on the kindred
glory; for not in vain does the Lord tread a winepress, and especially for a
virgin daughter of Judah.

34 The soul then, having received from the one who created her many powerful
and chosen gifts, deprived of these through vice and paying penalties,
confesses what she has suffered. And the time called unto being crushed is
that of the retribution, when according to Ezekiel "all the righteous deeds
which the just man has done will not be remembered in the day of his sin." And
mourning the things that have happened she is benefited, those who mourn and
weep having a promise of the comfort and the laugh.

35 He said the cause of the wailings to be there not being the one who turns
back

her soul, or rather the one who recovers according to Symmachus. And he says
that the sons also have been made to vanish, the enemy having been empowered
through the things they were sinning; for when we are pious he has no
strength, for he is weak when we advance and is put to death when we are
perfected and is crushed under the feet "being made fine as dust before the
face of wind, and as mud of broad places being made fine." For analogously to
the things we sin he is both fed and gets power, but when we have ceased from
transgression he is as it were destroyed by famine. And from the opposites,
when we do well we strengthen the divine powers for ourselves and when we sin
we drive them away. Wherefore Symmachus instead of have been made to vanish
rendered desolate. And a contemplative soul would say these things, being of
the word of the truth and through slackness having lost this gladdening word
and all the fruits, styled her sons, because vice hostile against her has been
empowered.

36 Zion he says the mountain, and the people Jacob, and the metropolis
Jerusalem. And God commanded Jacob commandments through which, being
transgressed, he was encircled by enemies, and according to Symmachus and
according to another edition of the Seventy the Lord commanded those who
afflict him to come to be around him, clearly the powers stationed unto
harming.

37 He has shown the unclean of Jerusalem through sin as through a monthly
flux. And in the midst he says either of Zion and of Jacob, or middle of the
whole mountain (for she has been built in a part of it), and of the whole
people, Jacob now signifying this, the twelve scepters. And in two ways
according to wording it is possible to take Zion and Jerusalem, either the
places or the inhabitants. But if Zion is a place, her hands the works, such
as farming and whatever is handmade, he says spread out and not contracted nor
doing the fitting work with hands, as in an idle and desolate country. But if
he means those inhabiting, hands would be farmers and workers, not drawing the
hands together into works because of the things that have stood around. But if
hands of Zion are tropologized, how much more the members of God according to
the scripture. And the mountain itself, so to speak, groans with the nation,
not having for comfort either God or a holy man.

38 Mouth of the Lord is to be taken as the prophets, being of whom Isaiah was
saying: "for the mouth of the Lord has spoken these things." And Jerusalem has
been reproached by the Savior as "having killed the prophets," inasmuch as she
also embittered the mouth of the Lord, having given them over to most bitter
pains. And God "who seeks out the blood of the prophets" delivers her youths
and virgins to the pains from wars and again to captivities. In return then
for what she has suffered, she confesses, furnishing herself as an example to
the others, toward their not suffering similar things, having learned the
justice of God.

39 After the perceptible, understand virgins as those living in incorruption,
and youths as those well-reputed in virtue. For often even the holy ones
suffer together with those liable.

40 Who the lovers are, Ezekiel has shown saying toward Jerusalem, whom he
calls Oholibah: "thus says the Lord Lord: behold I raise up your lovers
against you from around,

sons of Babylon and all the Chaldeans"; for these, formerly being friends,
according to divine providence changed into enmity. Which indeed often comes
to be upon desire turning toward hate; for he says concerning Samaria: "and
Oholah fornicated from me and set herself upon her lovers the Assyrians," and
after a little: "for this reason he delivered her into hands of her lovers,
into hands of sons of Assyrians." And he says also concerning Jerusalem: "and
her sister Oholibah saw, and she corrupted her setting-upon beyond the
fornication of her sister, and she set herself upon the sons of the
Assyrians."

41 In the prophecy then he is in pain in soul as about to suffer most terrible
things; but in the present Lament, as these things having already happened, he
is both burdened and through sins confesses that he has set the kindness of
God as most bitter for himself, so as even for the much-childed to be made
childless, and the most terrible thing, by an enemy hand. And there being a
personification, one must not seek a city's belly that has borne and a heart
that has been undone.

42 Jerusalem divides

the word toward the enemies, whom she wishes to hear, and toward God. But why
does she call the enemies toward hearing? And what does she say to God
concerning them as they have rejoiced? Perhaps then (since "by her and
Israel's trespass salvation has come to the nations unto making them jealous,"
and "when the fullness of the nations has entered, then all Israel will be
saved") she says the first things prophetically toward those from the nations,
and the second toward God. I have been abandoned to suffer in order that you
may take place, and I have become through you an enemy of God, although
according to the election existing as beloved because of the fathers.
Wherefore having heard my groan, understand also the cause of this; for I have
been completely abandoned for your salvation, not having the one who always
comforts me after a trial of evils. For now "a ruler has failed from Judah,
and a leader from his thighs"; for he has come to whom were "the things laid
up," "the expectation of the nations," at whose blood being avenged, there is
no longer the one comforting Jerusalem.

43 That is: let their vice not still further be let go to use, but let it come
to be in your oversight, as if someone should pray that wounds come into the
sight of a physician toward obtaining a complete healing. For this is the all;
for most blessed is not to sin, and second that all our vice be overseen by
God.

44 And to glean is after vintage to vintage the small things of what has been
left, which the poor do. Just as then when you were overseeing carefully, you
were cleansing my vine-branches, taking away all the worse things even to the
smallest "of the grape of gall and of the cluster of bitterness," so glean my
enemies. For this is profitable for me, if at least after them having been
completely saved I too according to your, O Lord, judgment will obtain
salvation; seeing whom I have both been exceedingly grieved and have condemned
myself, needing the oversight from you.

45 Similar in David is the "he will crush all the horns of the sinners." And
he now means the rulers, in whom is the strength of the nation just as in the
horn-bearing of the animals, and because it stands out from the body as the
rulers from the subjects. And Daniel too likened the kingdoms to horns. And
the horn also shows the disdain of the ruling faculty, as in the "do not lift
up your horn into height." Having then turned away his saving hand he made
them easy to capture for the enemies, whom indeed he also called a flame
consuming all. And you would also say every outstanding thought a horn, and
fire the "passions of dishonor," to which the transgressor is delivered,
having lost all the help that encircles according to the "an angel of the Lord
will encamp around those who fear him."

46 For even in the temple the enemies dared the slaughters, God allowing. And
well the as an enemy; for God does not punish being hostile, but wishing to
benefit either those who suffer or those who see. And tent he calls the temple
according to the sanctuary from the beginning, and bow the punishing power as
in Habakkuk: "stretching you will stretch your bow upon the scepters, says the
Lord," clearly the opposing rulers and authorities. And the son, being right
hand of God and Father, contended exceedingly, having also dissolved whatever
idols they stood in a temple of God (for these are a desire of eyes), having
killed instead of the idols the demons who had inhabited.

47 Palaces he says the houses, which Symmachus rendered kingdoms. And he takes
up the laments again, as someone in grief in much pain. And again he shows
that she has taken on a low reasoning and a low thought, the things as
dwellings and forts having been taken down, as filling the ruling faculty. For
virtues are the dwellings, for by them we are sheltered, and fortifications
the undeniable reasonings. For the taking-down is not now of worse "heights,
lifting themselves against the knowledge of God," nor of lowliness according
to which someone "learns the righteous ordinances of God"; for these are of a
nature worthy of joy, but not of Lament.

48 Tent the temple, and vine and vineyard he says Israel according to the "you
removed a vine from Egypt" in David, and "a vineyard came to be for the

beloved" in Isaiah. And David adds: "why did you take down her fence?" and the
things next, and Isaiah: "you took away his fence" and the things upon this.
These things the present Lament also showed. For God, having drawn in his own
guard, delivered to the enemies, an angel no longer "encamping around him"
because of not "fearing God." And he also abolished the times according to law
unto worship, as he says in Isaiah: "fasting and idleness and your feasts my
soul hates." Which he called forgetfulness, but not as a human passion. And he
provoked king and priest, being indignant if, being a portion of God, they
undergo such things from the impious.

49 He delivered the liturgical vessels, no longer being well-pleased with the
sacrifices from the impious according to the "whole burnt offerings of rams
and fat of lambs and blood of bulls and goats I do not wish." And he also
delivered the wall holding together the houses of the city, or rather the
palaces as Symmachus rendered. And the hostile, having taken even the temple,
were then celebrating as upon a complete victory, shouting. And an altar of a
soul is the rational in us, through which the passions are priested being put
to death.

50 For he took away all her safety, having stretched the measure of the
punishment; which indeed Zechariah also has seen.

51 And wall and outwork mourn, the things worthy of mourning, becoming causes
of weeping also for those who see; "for who will scatter the counsel of the
Lord? and who will turn back his high hand?" according to the blessed Isaiah.
And that such a one mourning, whether city or soul, so as to be a trampling
for the enemies, it is likely that angels mourn, those who formerly were
guarding her, others upon others, both greater and lower. For if they rejoice
over those saved, how will they not mourn over those who have fallen?

52 For as having been swallowed by earth or sea they have become obscure. And
king and rulers have also perished and the false prophets and her law by
necessity of slavery, which indeed by choice even before being captured they
dissolved. And gates of a soul are the senses, which when she sins are both
flooded with passions and become earthy. And the powers of the soul that keep
these are also dissolved, mind that reigns having perished and ruling
reasonings in the passions, when both law and order of the soul perish, and
she becomes Babylon being full of Confusion, having nothing providential
helped by God.

53 He relates painful sights that have come to be in the place of gladness;
for by fear they were not even permitting the elders to lament, and they
prepared the first virgins to look toward the earth, lest appearing they
should draw them toward violence from the beauty in which they were first. And
you would also say those who of old were elders in virtue, through which they
were so called, having become clayey and having been settled in irrational
passions, still according to reason doing nothing, and having made earthy the
ruling spirit in them and having made the prudence they had of old worthy of
mourning and having corrupted reasonings, which before they were keeping
uncorrupted.

54 Own glory the prophet says both a temple of God and piety of fatherland.
And famine was accomplishing the dissolution of the infants. And you would say
more highly, that he makes his own the passions of those kindred according to
flesh according to the "who is weak, and I am not weak?" taking up the
difficult things according to soul of those, a withering of true knowledge and
an unstable bearing of the reasoning toward the earthy things and a
deprivation of glory that raises the head according to the "but you, Lord, are
my helper, my glory and the one raising my head."

55 He showed the complete vice, so as to have no testimony of virtue nor even
the one who has transgressed similar things. For what similar sin of the one
far from God and of the one who after drawing near has thrown off? For "if I
had not come," he says, "and spoken to them, they would not have sin."
Wherefore also "in those who draw near to me I will be hallowed"; because "he
who does not know the will of the Lord and does not do it will be beaten with
few; but he who knows and does not do it will be beaten with many." The so
great passion then, which prophets have not been able to heal, needs God
alone. Wherefore he has also worn flesh, the prophets confessing weakness in
the "Lord, who

has believed our report?" And formerly too Jerusalem was saying: "see if there
is a passion according to my passion." He then "who has struck will heal; for
he himself makes to be in pain and again restores." For the who is taken both
of the rare and of the none; for no human is able to heal the blow brought on
by God.

56 A vain prophecy is the one that has no outcome and which vain idols
prepare. And from themselves too some were prophesying supposedly "for the
sake of a handful of barley and a fragment of bread" according to what is
written; who do not know how to refute sins and to save from the captivity
under demons, twisting the souls through fair-speaking. And as of the true the
taking and the word, which they receive from God, is by nature true and joins
to God, so that of the demons is both false and making distant from God and
being thrust out of the inclination from there. And of what sort are the not
vain thrustings-out, as was shown, and takings? It is clear then as the things
thrusting out of error and bringing toward God.

57 For "our enemy the devil goes about as a roaring lion seeking whom he may
swallow" and as a serpent sending the hissings upon us. Only may it not come
to be for him to say as I swallowed, nor to overtake in us a day which the sun
that burns together accomplishes; for it has been said: "the sun will not burn
you together by day." For with pleasures and wrath he burns together and
furnishes error to us as light and knowledge. Singing psalms then we will say:
"if it were not that the Lord was in us, then they would have swallowed us
living," and the "but a lamp of the impious will be quenched."

58 Nothing, he says, did the enemies do from themselves; for it seemed good to
God and it has come to be. Wherefore Nebuzaradan also was saying: "the Lord
your God spoke these evils unto this place, and the Lord brought them on,
because you sinned against him." Everyone then who disbelieves the judgment
will hear these words. All the prophets then were foretelling these things,
and from ancient days Moses in Deuteronomy indicated these things beforehand.

59 There are times when the scripture makes the words much-feeling and gives
perception to the unperceiving, introducing hills leaping and mountains
skipping, and calling hills and stones to witness; which in these is
impossible, the wall having already been taken down by enemies. A wall then
here is both king and rulers and as many as by power walling this around, whom
he counts worthy to pour unceasing tears of repentance, which having fallen
procures for those who see through all. These things, he says, belong to them,
even if he does not again signify the grief of the power that of old was
guarding, showing this hyperbolically. "And in affliction," he says, "they
remembered the Lord from the whole heart." This then do, he says, weeping
night and day and "be pierced upon your beds"; and do not consider sobriety
the release from these; for repentance is unto salvation. And someone is sober
not by ceasing from good things, but from worse. And join with the mind
through all toward Christ, that he may guard you as a pupil of an eye and
become a wall for you.

60 Night is attention-making, the soul being gathered in it in the stillness
of the senses. Then he commands to meditate in the commandments of God and to
say with David: "by night I was talking with my heart, and my spirit was
stirring." And the night being divided into four watches, say with David: "my
eyes anticipated watches," and with Habakkuk: "upon my watch I will stand, and
I will look out, what the Lord God will speak in me." For then you will say:
"that he will speak peace upon his people." Talk then in the night of the
present age; for "your wrestling is not toward blood and flesh, but toward the
rulers, toward the authorities"; "for you do not know when the lord of the
house comes, late or at midnight or at cockcrow or early; lest coming suddenly
he find you sleeping." And again: "for if you knew in what watch the thief
comes, you would have watched and would not have let your house be dug
through."

61 Lift your hands toward him through the working of the good things. The
contest is not about ordinary things, through lack of the divine word your
infants failing, and standing at the beginning of all ways out, of the

commandments of God, and not being able to go forward without God's
cooperation and of the bread that strengthens a heart.

62 The prophet having exhorted, Jerusalem was persuaded and says toward God:
"do not turn your face away from me," "look upon me and have mercy on me," "do
not refute me with your wrath, nor discipline me with your anger." The blow is
greater than human nature. I know having transgressed the law and being
subject to the penalties from there, among which is also the "you shall eat
the offspring of your belly, flesh of your sons and of your daughters." I was
thinking the words were as far as a threat; I knew, Lord, that "your law is
truth." And a slaughter of priest and prophet I did not learn in the midst of
a temple.

63 Upon earth were thrown all those unable through age toward the toils, a
sight exceedingly pitiable, as also slaughter of young men and of virgins †
would that even of these there were slaughters before being led away into a
more bitter life. And ways out of earth are the things of living, in which
they slept not "having watched nor having prayed," but having closed the eyes
of the soul toward sins and folly. And who are these? If children, it is not
very wonderful. But now before these those who seem to have advanced in virtue
and everyone uncorrupted in soul and powerful in war has become subject to
enemies. But you as good make such a life of theirs to cease, and you cut down
all their constitution that is upon evil.

64 Those around me, he says, inhabiting Judea were thinking the feast unto me
a refuge, hoping salvation from there; of whom no one has been left because of
your anger brought upon them. For through the things they sinned, the enemies
have become many and powerful.

65 Terrible things happened to the prophet both before the city was captured
and when this had been captured. Wherefore beginning another lettering, he
bewails himself, seeing poverty and desolation of the city through discipline
of the Lord. And he himself no less has come to be in want of good things, at
once having come to be in circumstance when he was refuting these, so as to
say: "I continued being sneered at the whole day," and at once also sharing in
the disasters of their terrible things and in the pain from sympathy. And he
says also according to David: "a shadow of death overshadowed me." For
whenever he seems for a little to give a pause to the pains, he again runs
back making a continual punishment.

66 The whole day shows according to each day, as in David: "the whole day
warring he afflicted me." And the he turned his hand would also signify that
formerly furnishing the good things to me, now he brings on the disciplines,
as having changed the hand.

67 It signifies the rottenness that has happened to us from the sin.

68 For a persistent disaster produces an old-man's weakness.

69 For he did not let me flee the terrible things, being besieged from all
sides. And he would perhaps also mean the enemy wall thrown around the city.
And he let out the same voice as David who has said: "they sat me in dark
places as dead of an age." And this through the prisons from the enemies, and
at once also through the disasters from God; for darkness of a soul are the
trials. And particularly "Pashhur threw Jeremiah also into the cistern,"

being dark and without light, of which sort was also for David the cave in
which he was hidden from Saul. And the two Laments could also be taken unto
Christ, of whom Jeremiah was a type, having suffered so many things from Jews
and at the end having been delivered to a tomb.

70 He has thrown unescapable evils around me, having laid upon my neck a harsh
and embittering people and a burden greater than according to me. And I was
not heard to be lightened, having often prayed; for the things sometimes
prevent the holy ones from being heard. And he would also mean a yoke and the
iron collars, which instead of wooden ones he put around by command of God,
for a writing is also current: for he made heavy the bronze of my neck. For he
blocked my prayer and having said to him: "do not pray concerning them; for I
will not hear you."

71 For having set out often he was not allowed to decline the ministry. For he
was saying: "I will not name the name of the Lord," and he was as if driven
together by fire. And he also blocks the roads of the sinners, not allowing

to sin. But the prophet is not of this sort, who at least says: "I did not sit
with a council of those playing."

72 Every missile of his, he says, he emptied against me, wounding my desire
and not allowing me to act as I wish.

73 And kidneys in the scripture are the desiring faculty, and arrows the
missiles. And they were then tearing me as a liar, having made me a song. And
having come he was also saying: "I was sitting alone, because I was filled
with bitternesses" and the things next; for he was asking on their behalf, but
they were plotting, and nevertheless it was not possible to decline the
ministry. He was then being made bitter both suffering from them <and> sharing
pain with those who suffer; and being over-burdened for those who are impious.

74 By a pebble he says or by a number, being struck according to each tooth
against the cheek, or by a hard stone. And according to David too he says: "I
was eating my bread with ash," showing that he does not even take bread with
pleasure from affliction. It was not then possible for the soul to be in
peace, herself being disturbed and besides seeing tumults from outside, fights
from within. Wherefore having hoped nothing useful, I let out the voices of
Job: "mercy has declined me, and my hope from the Lord." For it was not
possible to conquer the terrible things standing around.

75 For being good toward all he requires endurance from us. For for the soul
that will seek him, the seeking is good, which would endure not being
disturbed, until the mercy should meet, nor being moved away, until the
salvation of the Lord should appear, unto hoping upon another. And perhaps he
also says she will be still as not being busy about the judgments of
providence. One must then, even if we are ignorant, not throw off the Lord.

76 Because "his yoke is useful and the burden light." Even if then no one
agrees with him about something, one must not be led away together, but even
if he should be isolated toward this, to persevere in the things that have
seemed good and to be firmly settled. For "the Lord settles the
single-mannered in a house" says David, and "I am alone, until I pass,"
clearly the trials, having obtained kindness.

77 For he will humble himself saying: "but I am earth and ash," if indeed he
is truly a man, and he has been filled with the hope that does not shame, of
which sort is also the "my soul was glued to the floor."

78 This also fits toward those in the captivity saying the things in the
seventy-third Psalm: "why, God, have you pushed away unto the end? has your
wrath been angered upon sheep of your pasture?" And in agreement is the "the
Lord will not push away unto the end nor will he be wroth unto the age"; and
he will say to the one who has repented toward worth: "let your voice leave
off from weeping, and your eyes from tears."

79 Through these things we learn that the Lord has not said it nor is cause of
the prisoners of the earth being humbled under the sin, nor that the human
judgments turn aside over against God, for he loves the just and the straight,
nor yet of it becoming a worthy thing to condemn a man; for the opposites, he
says,
cannot have come out of a mouth of the Lord, the good things and the evil; for
neither "does a good tree make wicked fruits, nor a wicked tree good fruits."
The men being wronged by wicked ones then is beside the divine judgment, yet
it comes to be in those overlooked by God, as happened to the Israelites from
the enemies, and in a turning of God it is dissolved. Wherefore those
delivered unto punishment must seek this out.

80 In vain then murmuring we bring the cause up upon the creator; which the
living man does not have to do; for such a choice is of those dead in sins,
unless perhaps it refutes the one living according to sin and blaming God. As
then not having another cause or only ourselves, having judged the things that
have come to be from ourselves let us turn.

81 For it is not possible to turn toward the Lord for the one who has not
known himself; and someone recognizes himself, bringing the mind upon the
deeds, at once overseeing them, and at once also making them noetic. For then
"with unveiled face we mirror the glory of the Lord" and confessing the things
before we will say that "we have sinned and been lawless to stand away from
you" and "that you are just upon all the things you have brought upon us."

82 The taking up hearts upon hands seems to show that as if having taken our
own thoughts in hand let us examine, if indeed

they are clean.

83 And these things are a part of the confession that has already come; for as
darkness from a cloud you brought upon us the circumstances of the terrible
things, and being far from you you pursued, you destroyed those dead to the
sin through the punishment. And since our sins were standing us apart from you
as of a cloud walling through, our prayer was not arriving toward you; for we
did not have "wings of a dove" to fly over and to say: "in God I will go over
a wall." For it was not easy to pass the sin, being both solid and dark and
without share of holy spirit.

84 Symmachus: a fright. With a whole mouth they swallowed us, and we were
brought down into a depth of evils having been filled with fear and
disturbance; "for the sinner falls into a pit which he dug" giving penalties
of the things he sinned. And since "before crushing insolence leads" according
to Solomon, that is pride, we were both lifted and crushed. Whence David
sings: "let a foot of pride not come to me, and let a hand of a sinner not
shake me."

85 And having suffered so many things he is in over-pain for those who have
done them, so "love does not reckon the evil."

86 Instead of my eye has been worn down, and it will not be silent from there
not being a sobering, Symmachus said: my eye persisted, and it did not cease
from there not being a relief. And the Seventy show that although the eyes
have been weakened from a multitude of tears they nevertheless were not
leaving off, he not being able to sober from the weeping, but Symmachus that I
both persisted weeping and was not leaving off; for intense prayer and a
stretching of tears draws God toward mercy.

87 For the tears cut me down, he says, just as an exact vintager a vine; and
according to Symmachus, that the tears sank me, by the metaphor of those
drowning in waters. And enduring I will endure the Lord, until the turning of
his face away from us. And the God stooping through shows the counting us
worthy of the oversight. And he interprets the beside all by bringing upon
all, that is upon their disasters; for the female kind is more pitiable than
that of the males.

88 Or he says the things he has suffered; for he was thrown into a pit and as
far as it came to those he died, but having been saved beyond hope he was
crying with David: "my soul was delivered as a sparrow from the snare of those
hunting"; for it was not possible "to fall upon the earth without the Father
who is in the heavens" according to the voice of the Savior. Either then he
says it through himself or also making his own the passions of the people. But
the freely would reasonably be said concerning him; for what wrong did he do
them, or rather did he not benefit?

89 God judged, having killed the false prophet and having delivered the king,
and upon Christ having delivered the nation unto capture. And the I was upon
their eyes, that I was heavy to them even appearing.

90 He was saying also before: "complete them, and your word will be to me unto
gladness and joy of my heart." These things have come to be after the plot
against the Savior, when they were scattered everywhere; for "all just blood
poured out" came upon them. The to complete then shows the worship according
to them taking an end, and no longer being styled a nation of God.

91 The shielding of heart a toil for them, that you will give them toil unto a
perception of how great you were shielding them, in order that they may seek
you even again. But if not, you will pursue them in your wrath.

92 Beginning the now present fourth lettering he took up again the Lament
especially concerning the notable, saying that the appearing has been dimmed
and has been made to vanish like water poured out.

93 And holy as chosen because of the fathers or because of the holy ones taken
with Jehoiakim, whom he likened to good figs. And after a little: And of ways
out from the holy land "leading away unto destruction" and unto the noetic
Babylonian, "in order that they may be taught not to blaspheme." And you would
also say the Lament upon those changing from piety, if they should be notable
and should lead the others in error, "having left the fountain of the living
water, and having become broken pits which cannot hold." Whence reasonably
they are poured out about the error groaning untimely.

94 Those

who pride themselves on wealth, he says, or even more precious than gold, how
have they so been likened to the cheap and broken, having been led away into
slavery? And he said they were likened to these also when having gone down
"into the house of the potter" he took a vessel and broke it. And Isaiah too
likens them to a city that has fallen, saying "and her falls will be as
fragments of an earthen vessel."

95 He speaks concerning famine of the glorious infants, as Jerusalem not even
similarly to the wild animals having been able to hold out a breast to them.
And the excess of the passion also works a forgetting of nature. And dragons,
or also the Sirens according to Symmachus, you will hear as the wicked
spirits, who have nursed their own young with wicked milk. And of this sort
are those introduced by the teachings of the impious.

96 For how are they not daughters, the ones made womanish, whom also Pharaoh
keeps, killing the males? Who also graze down the teaching, the desert of God,
not being able to fly off according to the ostriches, but always running over
the earth? For the sparrow Symmachus so rendered, and the teachers of these he
not unfittingly called Sirens. For according to the outside myth these through
pleasure were destroying those who happened upon them, just as the dragons
having bitten in the poison. He then reproaches the supposedly good teachers,
that they have not even similarly to these been eager about those who attend.

97 They have suffered these things both being besieged and being led away. And
you would also say that "they endured a famine not of bread, not of water"
according to the prophetic oracle, "but of hearing a word of the Lord."
Wherefore he prophesies concerning their young.

98 Historically he has returned upon the things that happened at the
captivity, but toward the sense he speaks concerning the famine of the
teaching word.

99 For the poor who are being harmed are not so pitiable. And Paul too laments
these, saying "whose is the adoption and the glory." For now practicing empty
things they do not revel in Moses and the prophets, as the "revel in the
Lord." For there is no one for them cooking the nourishing word, nor do they
partake of the fatted calf although they themselves have sacrificed. And ways
out he calls the dispersions or also the turnings-aside of the legal letters.

100 They were nursed formerly upon scarlet, hearing beforehand prophetically
the blood of the Savior that was going to be poured out, and now wearing rags
from the earthy thoughts.

101 Symmachus rendered the as haste, suddenly, and the they did not toil,
which they did not wound. For what is more concise than a divine word that has
said: "behold your house is left to you desolate" and has delivered by the
wishing alone? And the lawlessness of Jerusalem was magnified beyond that of
Sodom. The ones had only the natural law, but the others both a written one
and prophets and as many things as God was working paradoxically in them. And
those sinned against angels and similar humans; but these against the Savior
and redeemer, of old from Egypt, and now from worship of demons, if at least
they received the one who came toward them primarily. For "I was not sent," he
says, "except unto the lost sheep of the house of Israel."

102 Symmachus: her undefiled set apart above snow, brighter than milk, redder
in habit beyond the notable, sapphire their members, darker than soot their
form. He means those who have hallowed themselves to God; for Nazirites the
Having-been-hallowed shows, for so Symmachus and Theodotion in Amos rendered
the hallowed ones, having said Nazirites. Moses too has remembered these; of
which sort was also Samson. And they were also notable among Jews. Wherefore
he compared their clean to snow, and the bright to milk; and they were fired
having been tested by the discipline of the things they were promising to God,
and their chosen (which the fragment shows) have a heavenly way of life; for
of this sort is the color of sapphire. And it also has gilded veins, to which
indeed Symmachus likens their habit, having the uncorrupted and the fiery
according to piety.

103 They have fallen out of the spiritual illumination and have been melted by

among them, having no spiritual well-nourishment. And according to the letter
too, from extreme shame their faces were being blackened, because sinning
similarly to the others they were being led away; since even now upon those

104 For it is better to end quickly in war than to be mastered by famine
through lack of the products in a field. And you would say a sword is also a
saber the word of God; for Paul says: "and the saber of the spirit, which is a
saying of God," by which, says the bride, "I am wounded with love." And David
too says: "gird your sword upon your thigh, powerful one, in your beauty and
in your fairness." Those struck by such a sword it is good to fall under the
feet of Jesus according to the "peoples will fall under you," having confessed
his kingdom, but not to be captured by a psychic famine. For a product of
country is the Christ, the one born in Bethlehem, which is signified House of
bread, of which having become without taste Jews were led away into a country
of demons. And a field blessed by the Lord and exceedingly fragrant, of which
Jacob was breathing, shows the church of Christ.
