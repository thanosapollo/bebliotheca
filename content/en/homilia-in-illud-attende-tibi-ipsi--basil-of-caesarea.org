* Homily on that saying, Attend to yourself
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of our father among the saints Basil, archbishop of Caesarea of Cappadocia, on
Attend to yourself. God who created us has given us the use of speech, that we
may reveal the counsels of our hearts to one another, and that, because of the
social character of our nature, each may share with his neighbor, bringing
forth the thoughts from the hidden things of the heart as from some
storehouses. For if we lived with the soul naked, we would at once communicate
with one another from the thoughts; but since our soul, covered by the flesh
as by a curtain, works out its conceptions, it needs words and names in order
to publish what lies in the depth. Whenever then our thought takes hold of a
significant voice, riding upon speech as upon some ferry, crossing the air, it
passes from the speaker to the hearer; and if it finds a deep calm and quiet,
the word moors in the ears of those who are learning as in fair and unstormy
harbors; but if the tumult from the hearers blows against it like some rough
squall, having been dissolved in mid-air it has been shipwrecked. Make then a
calm for the word through silence. For perhaps it may appear having something
useful of its cargo. The word of truth is hard to catch, easily able to escape
those who do not attend, the Spirit having so arranged that it be condensed
and brief, so as to show very many things in few, and through brevity to be
easy to hold in memory. For by nature the virtue of speech is neither 24 by
unclarity to hide the things signified, nor to be superfluous and vain,
flowing idly around the matters. Such then also is the one just now read to us
from the books of Moses, which you who are labor-loving altogether remember,
unless perhaps through brevity it ran past your ears. And the saying is thus:
Attend to yourself, lest there ever come to be a hidden word of lawlessness in
your heart. We humans are easy toward the sins according to thought. Therefore
he who fashioned our hearts one by one, knowing that the greater part of sin
is fulfilled in the impulse according to intention, first ordained for us the
purity in the governing part. For that by which we most readily sin, this he
deemed worthy of greater watch and care. For as the more forethoughtful of the
physicians secure the weaker of bodies from afar with preventive counsels, so
the common guardian and true physician of souls, what he saw in us most
slippery toward sin, this he forestalled with stronger guards. For the actions
through the body need both time and opportunity and toils and helpers and the
rest of the provision. But the movements of thought are effected without time,
are accomplished without toil, arise without business, have every season
suitable. And somewhere one of those grave and frowning with solemnity,
wearing outwardly a fiction of temperance, often sitting in the midst of those
who bless him for virtue, has fled in thought to the place of sin in the
unseen movement of the heart. He saw in imagination the things pursued, formed
some unseemly converse, and altogether in the secret workshop of the heart,
having painted the pleasure vividly for himself, he worked the sin within
without witness, unknown to all, until he comes who reveals the hidden things
of darkness and makes manifest the counsels of the hearts. Guard then lest 25
there ever come to be a hidden word of lawlessness in your heart. For he who
has looked at a woman to desire her has already committed adultery in his
heart.

Because the actions of the body are interrupted by many things; but he who
sins according to intention has the sin completed together with the speed of
the thoughts. Where then the fall is sharp, swift watch has been given us. For
it testifies: lest there ever come to be a hidden word of lawlessness in your
heart. Rather let us run back to the very beginning of the saying. Attend, it
says, to yourself. Each of the animals has from God who composed all things,
from within, the resources toward the guarding of its own constitution. And
you would find, if you examined carefully, that most of the irrational
creatures have an untaught aversion toward what harms, and again by some
natural attraction are urged toward the enjoyment of the things that benefit.
Therefore also God who educates us has given us this great commandment, that
what those have from nature, this may accrue to us from the help of reason,
and what is accomplished by the irrational creatures without attention, this
may be fulfilled by us through attention and the continuous application of the
reasonings; and that we may be accurate guardians of the resources given us by
God, fleeing sin, as the irrational creatures flee the poisonous among foods,
and pursuing righteousness, as those also pursue the nourishing grass.
Attend then to yourself, that you may be able to distinguish what harms from
what saves. And since attending is twofold: one, to gaze with bodily eyes upon
the visible things, the other, to apply the intellectual power of the soul to
the contemplation of the bodiless; if we should say the commandment lies upon
the activity of the eyes, we will at once convict its impossibility. For how
could anyone grasp his whole self with the eye? For neither does the 26 eye
itself use seeing upon itself, it does not reach the crown of the head, it
does not know the back, not the face, not the disposition in the depth of the
inward parts. It is impious then to say that the commandments of the Spirit
are impossible. It remains then to take the command as referring to the
activities according to mind. Attend to yourself, that is: look around
yourself from every side. Have the eye of the soul sleepless toward your own
guarding. You pass through the midst of snares. Hidden nooses have been
planted from many sides by the enemy. Look around then at all things, that you
may be saved as a gazelle from nooses and as a bird from a snare. For the
gazelle is uncatchable by the nooses through sharpness of sight, whence also
it is named from its own sharp-sightedness; but the bird, with light wing,
when it attends, becomes higher than the plot of the hunters. See then that
you not appear worse than the irrational creatures toward your own guarding;
lest ever, caught in the snares, you become prey of the devil, taken alive by
him unto his will. Attend then to yourself, that is: attend neither to the
things that are yours, nor to the things around you, but to yourself alone.
For we ourselves are one thing, and the things that are ours another, and the
things around us another. We then are the soul and the mind, according to
which we have been made in the image of the one who created; but ours is the
body, and the 27 senses through it; and around us are money, crafts, and the
rest of the equipment of life.
What then does the word say? Do not attend to the flesh, nor pursue its good
in every way, health and beauty and enjoyments of pleasures and long life; nor
marvel at money and glory and power; nor, having thought great whatever fills
the service of your temporary life, neglect through zeal about these things
your primary life; but attend to yourself, that is: to your soul. Adorn this,
and take care of this, so that every filth that comes upon it from wickedness
may be cleared away through attention, and every shame from vice may be
cleansed away, and that you may adorn and brighten it with every beauty from
virtue. Examine yourself who you are, know your own nature; that your body is
mortal, but the soul immortal, and that there is some double

life of ours: one proper to the flesh, quickly passing, the other akin to the
soul, not admitting limitation. Attend then to yourself, lest you remain in
the mortal things as everlasting, or despise the everlasting as passing.
Overlook flesh, for it passes; take care of soul, an immortal thing. Apply
yourself to yourself with all accuracy, that you may know how to distribute to
each what is fitting, 28 to flesh nourishments and coverings, but to soul
doctrines of piety, a noble way of life, exercise of virtue, correction of
passions; not to fatten the body excessively, nor to have been zealous about
the mass of fleshes. For since The flesh desires against the spirit, and the
spirit against the flesh, and these are opposed to one another, see lest ever,
having attached yourself to the flesh, you grant much dominion to the worse.
For as in the swings of balances, if you weigh down one pan, you will
altogether make the opposing one lighter; so also upon body and soul, the
excess of the one makes necessary the lessening of the other. For when the
body is well-off and weighed down with much flesh, it is necessary that the
mind be feeble and slack toward its own activities; but when the soul is in
good condition and through the study of the good things is raised toward its
proper greatness, it follows that the habit of the body wastes away. And this
same commandment is both useful to the sick and most fitting to the strong. In
sicknesses at any rate the physicians charge the suffering to attend to
themselves, and not to neglect any of the things that come toward healing.
Likewise also the word, the physician of our souls, heals through this small
help the soul damaged by sin. Attend then to yourself, that according to the
proportion of the fault you may also receive the help from the healing. Great
and hard is the sin: you have need of much confession, of bitter tears, of
intense vigil, of unceasing fasting. Light and bearable is the fall: let the
repentance also be equalized. Only attend to yourself, that you may recognize
health and disease of soul. For many through too much inattention, being sick
with great and incurable things, do not even 29 know this very thing, that
they are sick. And great is the benefit from the commandment also for those
who are strong concerning the actions; so that the same both heals the sick
and perfects the healthy. For each of us who are being discipled to the word
is a servant of some one action of the things commanded us according to the
gospel. For in this great house, this church, there are not only vessels of
all kinds, gold and silver and wooden and earthen, but also crafts of all
kinds. For the house of God, which is the church of the living God, has
hunters, travelers, master-builders, builders, farmers, shepherds, athletes,
soldiers. To all these this short saying fits, producing in each both accuracy
of the work and zeal of the choice. You are a hunter sent by the Lord who
said: Behold I send many hunters, and they will hunt them upon every mountain.
Attend carefully, lest the prey somewhere escape you, that having caught by
the word of truth those made wild by wickedness you may bring them to the one
who saves. You are a traveler like the one praying: Direct my steps. Attend to
yourself, lest you be turned aside from the way, lest you decline to the right
or to the left; go by the royal road. Let the master-builder lay securely the
foundation of the faith, which is Christ Jesus. Let the builder see how he
builds upon it: not wood, not hay, not reed, but gold, silver, precious
stones. Shepherd, attend lest any of the things belonging to shepherding pass
you by. And what are these? Turn back the wandering, bind up the crushed, heal
the sick. Farmer, dig around the unfruitful fig tree, and apply the things
toward help of fruit-bearing. The soldier,

suffer hardship with the gospel, 30 wage the good warfare against the spirits
of wickedness, against the passions of the flesh, take up all the panoply of
the Spirit; do not entangle yourself in the affairs of life, that you may
please the one who enlisted you. Athlete, attend to yourself, lest you
somewhere transgress any of the athletic laws. For no one is crowned, unless
he contend lawfully. Imitate Paul, both running, and wrestling, and boxing;
and you yourself, as a good boxer, have the gaze of the soul unwandering.
Cover the vital parts with the projection of the hands; let the eye be intent
toward the opponent. In the races stretch forward to the things before. So
run, that you may lay hold. In the wrestling contend against the invisible
ones. Such the word wants you to be through life, not slumped, nor sleeping,
but soberly and wakefully standing over yourself. The day will fail me as I
recount both the pursuits of those who cooperate toward the gospel of Christ,
and the power of the command, how it fits well toward all. Attend to yourself:
be sober, deliberative, a guardian of the present things, forethoughtful of
the future. Do not through slackness let go what is already present; nor, of
things that neither are nor perhaps will be, set before yourself the enjoyment
as if they were in hand. Or is this not by nature the infirmity of the young,
by lightness of mind to think they already have the things hoped for? For
whenever they take hold of stillness, or of night quiet, they fashion for
themselves unsubstantial fantasies, being carried by the ease of thought
toward all things, setting before themselves distinction of life, splendid
marriages, goodly children, deep old age, the honors from all. Then, unable
to halt anywhere in their hopes, they are puffed up toward the greatest of
things among men. They acquire houses fair and great; having filled these with
treasures of all kinds 31, they surround themselves with land, as much as the
vanity of their reasonings may cut off for them from the whole creation. Again
they shut up the abundances from these in the storehouses of vanity. They add
to these flocks, a multitude of household servants exceeding number, civic
offices, governorships of nations, generalships, wars, trophies, the kingdom
itself. Having gone over all these things in the empty fashionings of thought,
through too much mindlessness they seem to enjoy the things hoped for as
already present and lying at their feet. This is a peculiar infirmity of an
idle and slack soul, to see dreams while the body is awake. Therefore the
word, pressing down this flabbiness of thought and the inflammation of the
reasonings, and as with some bridle checking the unsteadiness of thought,
commands this great and wise commandment: To yourself, it says, attend, not
setting before yourself the things that do not exist, but disposing the
present things toward what is beneficial. And I think the lawgiver used this
exhortation also removing that passion from custom. Since it is easier for
each of us to be busy about the things of others than to examine his own, lest
we suffer this, cease, he says, being a busybody about the evils of so-and-so;
do not give leisure to the reasonings to examine another's infirmity, but
attend to yourself, that is: turn the eye of your soul upon your own search.
For many, according to the word of the Lord, consider the speck in the
brother's eye, but do not look at the beam in their own eye. Do not then cease
searching yourself, whether your life proceeds according to commandment; but
do not look around at the outside things, if somewhere you might be able to
find a fault of someone, according to that heavy and boastful Pharisee, who
stood justifying himself, and disparaging the tax-collector; but do not leave
off examining yourself, whether you have sinned at all according to the
thoughts, whether the tongue has slipped at all, having run out ahead of the
mind, whether in the works of the hands anything

of the unintended has been done. And if you find in your own life many sins
(and you will find them altogether, being a human), say the things of the
tax-collector: God, be merciful to me the sinner. Attend to yourself. This
saying will stand by you usefully both when you are sometime splendidly
prospering, and when all of life is being carried with the stream, as some
good counselor bringing a reminder of human things. And indeed also when you
are being pressed by circumstances, it would come in season, being chanted to
the heart, so that you may neither be lifted by conceit toward an overweening
boastfulness, nor fall by despair into ignoble despondency. Do you flourish
with wealth? and think great things upon ancestors? and exult in fatherland
and beauty of body and the honors from all? Attend to yourself, that you are
mortal, that you are earth, and to earth you will go. Look around at those
before you who were examined in like distinctions. Where are those who were
clothed with civic powers? where the most unconquerable orators? where those
who arranged the festivals, the splendid horse-breeders, the generals, the
satraps, the tyrants? Is not all dust? is not all a tale? Are not the
memorials of their life in a few bones? Bend into the tombs, if you will be
able to distinguish, who is the household servant and who the master, who the
poor and who the rich. Distinguish, if you have any power, the prisoner from
the king, the strong from the weak, the fair from the ill-formed. Remembering
then the nature you will never be lifted up. And you will remember yourself,
if you attend to yourself. Again, are you someone of low birth and without
glory, poor from the poor, without hearth, without city, weak, in need of the
daily things, trembling at the powers, cowering before all 33 through
lowliness of life? For a poor man, it says, does not withstand a threat. Do
not then despair of yourself, nor, because nothing to be emulated is present
to you in the present, throw away every good hope; but raise your soul both
toward the good things already provided you by God, and toward the things
stored up later through promise. First then, you are a human, alone of the
animals God-fashioned. Is this not enough for one reckoning soberly toward the
highest good cheer, to have been formed by the very hands of God who composed
all things? then that having been made in the image of the one who created,
you are able through a good way of life to run up toward equal honor with the
angels? You received an intellectual soul, through which you contemplate God,
you behold by reasoning the nature of the things that are, you pluck the
sweetest fruit of wisdom. All the land animals, both tame and wild, and all
that live in waters, and as many as fly through this air, are slaves and under
your hand. Did you not indeed invent crafts, and found cities, and devise both
whatever is necessary and whatever is toward luxury? Are not the seas passable
for you through reason? Do not earth and sea serve your life? Do not air and
heaven and the dances of stars display to you their own order? Why then are
you small-souled, because you do not have a silver-bitted horse? But you have
the sun with swiftest course through every day torch-bearing the lamp for you.
You do not have the gleamings of silver and gold, but you have the moon
shining around you with the countless light from herself. You have not mounted
gold-inlaid chariots, but you have feet, a proper vehicle and 34 connatural to
yourself. Why then do you bless those who possess the stout purse, and who
need others' feet for moving? You do not sleep upon an ivory bed, but you have
the earth more precious than many elephants, and sweet rest upon it, sleep
swift and freed from care. You do not lie under a golden roof, but you have
heaven glittering round with the unspeakable beauties of the stars. These then
are the human things; but the things still greater. For you God among men,
distribution of Holy Spirit, dissolution of death, hope of resurrection,
divine commands perfecting your life, a way toward God through the

commandments, the kingdom of the heavens prepared, crowns of righteousness
ready for the one who has not fled the toils for virtue. If you attend to
yourself, these and still more you will find about yourself; and you will
enjoy the present things, and you will not be small-souled toward what is
lacking. The commandment standing by you everywhere will furnish great help.
For example, has anger overpowered your reasonings, and are you carried by
wrath toward both unseemly words and harsh and beastlike deeds? If you attend
to yourself, you will bring down the wrath as some disobedient and
hard-to-rein colt, laying hold with the blow of the word as with a whip. And
you will also master the tongue, and you will not let the hands loose upon the
one who provoked. Again wicked desires, driving the soul mad, throw it into
unrestrained and unchaste impulses. If you attend to yourself, and remember
that this present sweet thing will come down to a bitter end, and the tickling
now arising from the pleasure in 35 our body, this will beget the venomous
worm that chastises us undyingly in Gehenna, and the burning of the flesh will
become mother of the eternal fire, immediately the pleasures will go away,
having been put to flight, and some wondrous calm within around the soul and
quiet will come to be, as when the tumult of unchaste maidservants has been
silenced by the presence of some temperate mistress. Attend then to yourself,
and know that one part of the soul is rational and intellectual, and the other
passionate and irrational. And to the one by nature belongs the ruling, and to
the others to obey the word and to be persuaded. Never then allow the mind,
having been enslaved, to become a slave of the passions; nor again permit the
passions to rise up against the word, and to turn the power of the soul upon
themselves. And altogether the accurate understanding of yourself will furnish
you sufficient guidance also toward the thought of God. For if you attend to
yourself, you will not need at all from the construction of the wholes to
track out the craftsman, but in yourself, as in some small cosmos, you will
behold the great wisdom of the one who created you. Think God bodiless from
the bodiless soul that exists in you, not limited by place; since neither does
your mind have as primary the spending of time in place, but through the
conjunction with the body it comes to be in place. Believe God to be
invisible, having thought of your own soul, since it too is ungraspable by
bodily eyes. For it is neither colored, nor shaped, nor encompassed by any
bodily character, but is known from the activities alone. So that you should
not seek upon God the understanding through eyes, but having entrusted the
faith to the thought, have an intellectual grasp concerning him. Marvel at the
craftsman, how he bound the power of your soul to the body, so that, reaching
through to its extremities, it leads the members most distant to one common
breath and communion. Consider what is the power given from soul to the flesh;
what is the sympathy going back from flesh to soul; how the body receives life
from the soul, and the soul receives 36 pains from the body; what storehouses
of learnings it has; why the addition of the things coming on does not darken
the knowledge of the things that went before, but the memories, unconfused and
distinct, as engraved on some bronze stele in the governing part of the soul,
are preserved; how slipping toward the passions of the flesh it loses its
proper beauty; and how again, having been cleansed of the shame from vice,
through virtue it runs up toward the likeness of the one who created. Attend,
if it seems good, after the contemplation of the soul, also to the
construction of the body, and marvel how the best craftsman created it a
fitting lodging for the rational soul. He formed man alone of the animals
upright, so that from the very shape you may know that your life is from the
kinship above. For all the four-footed toward the belly

have been bent; but for man the looking-up toward heaven is ready, so as not
to be at leisure for the belly, nor for the passions under the belly, but to
have the whole impulse toward the upward journey. Then having set the head
upon the highest things, in it he established the senses most worthy. There
sight, and hearing, and taste, and smell, all settled near one another. And
thus being cramped around a small space, none impedes the activity of the
neighbor. The eyes at any rate have taken the highest lookout, so that none of
the parts of the body block them, but sitting under a small projection 37 of
the brows, from the prominence above they stretch toward the straight. Again
the hearing has not been opened in a straight line, but by a spiral passage it
takes hold of the sounds in the air. This too is of the highest wisdom, so
that the voice may pass through unhindered, or even rather resound, being bent
around in the windings, and that nothing falling in from outside may be able
to be a hindrance to the sense. Learn the nature of the tongue, how it is both
soft and well-turning and sufficient for every need of speech by the variety
of movement. Teeth, at once instruments of voice, furnishing a strong support
to the tongue, and at once also servants of food, some cutting it, others
smoothing it. And thus going over all things with the fitting reasoning, and
learning the drawing of air through the lung, the guarding of the heat at the
heart, organs of digestion, channels of blood, from all these you will behold
the untrackable wisdom of the one who made you, so that you yourself also may
say with the prophet: Your knowledge was made wonderful from me. Attend then
to yourself, that you may attend to God; to whom be the glory and the power
unto the ages. Amen.
