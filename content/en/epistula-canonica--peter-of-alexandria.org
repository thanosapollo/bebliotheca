OF THE HOLY PETER ARCHBISHOP OF ALEXANDRIA AND MARTYR
CANONS HANDED DOWN IN HIS DISCOURSE CONCERNING REPENTANCE
Concerning those who after many tortures denied Christ.
Since then already a fourth Pascha has overtaken the persecution, it is
sufficient for those who were brought forward and imprisoned, and have endured
incurable tortures and unbearable scourgings and many other terrible
necessities, but afterwards were betrayed by the weakness of the flesh, even
if they were not received from the beginning because of the very great fall
that followed, nevertheless because they have contended much and fought long,
for they did not come to this by choice, but having been betrayed by the
weakness of the flesh, since they also show "the marks of Jesus" in their own
bodies, and already some have a third year mourning, that there be added to
them from their coming forward, as a reminder, another forty days, which also
our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ, having fasted after being baptized, was
tempted by the devil; into which they also, having been further exercised, and
having become more vigorously sober, will watch unto prayers, meditating
thereafter on what was said by the Lord to the one tempting him, that he
should worship him: "Go, Satan, for it is written: Thou shalt worship the Lord
thy God and him only shalt thou serve."
2 Concerning those who after being imprisoned only denied.
But for those who after being imprisoned only and having endured the
afflictions and stenches in the prison as in a siege, but afterwards without a
war of tortures became captives, broken by much poverty of power and by a
certain blindness, a year in addition to the other time will suffice, since
they also wholly gave themselves to be afflicted for the Name, even if they
enjoyed in the prison much rest from the brethren, which things they will
repay many times over, longing to be redeemed from the most bitter captivity
of the devil, remembering especially him who says: "The Spirit of the Lord is
upon me, because he has anointed me, he has sent me to preach good tidings to
the poor, to proclaim release to the captives and recovery of sight to the
blind, to send forth the broken in release, to proclaim the acceptable year of
the Lord and the day of recompense."
3 Concerning those who were not even imprisoned.
But for those who have suffered nothing at all of this kind, nor shown fruit
of faith, but deserted of themselves to wickedness, betrayed by cowardice and
fear, but now coming to repentance, it is necessary and fitting to set beside
them the parable of the unfruitful fig tree, as the Lord says: "A certain man
had a fig tree planted in his vineyard and he came seeking fruit on it and
found none; and he said to the vinedresser: Behold, three years I come seeking
fruit on this fig tree and I find none; cut it down; why does it also make the
ground useless? And he answering says to him: Lord, leave it also this year,
until I dig about it and cast dung; and if it bear fruit; but if not, in the
time to come you shall cut it down." Which taking before


their eyes, and having shown fruit worthy of repentance in the interval of so
great a time, they will rather be benefited.
4 Concerning those who do not repent.
For to those altogether given up and unrepentant, "possessing the skin of an
Ethiopian unchangeable and the spots of a leopard," there shall be said the
word of the other fig tree: "Let no fruit grow from you henceforth forever,
wherefore also it is immediately dried up." There is fulfilled then upon them
also what was said by Ecclesiastes: "That which is crooked cannot be adorned
and that which is wanting cannot be numbered." For unless first the crooked be
set right, it is impossible for it to be adorned, and unless first the wanting
be filled up, it is impossible for it to be numbered. Whence also at the end
there shall happen to them what is said by the prophet Isaiah: "And they shall
look, he says, upon the carcasses of the men who have transgressed against me;
for their worm shall not die and their fire shall not be quenched and they
shall be a spectacle to all flesh." Since also as it was said before by him:
"But the unjust shall be tossed as by a storm, and they shall not be able to
rest; there is no rejoicing for the ungodly, said God."
5 Concerning those who used pretence.
But for those who used pretence after the manner of David who feigned himself
epileptic, that he might not die, not being epileptic, and who did not nakedly
write down the things toward denial, but mocked, under much constraint, as
prudent children of counsel among foolish children, the plots of the enemies,
either as having passed by altars, or as having signed, or as having put
Gentiles in their own place, even if some of the confessors granted them
pardon, as I have heard, since especially with much piety they fled becoming
the very hands of the fire and of the fumes of the unclean demons, since then
it escaped them that they did this in folly, nevertheless six months shall be
laid upon them of conversion in repentance; for thus they also will rather be
benefited, meditating on the prophetic saying and saying: "A child was born to
us, a son also was given to us, whose government is upon his shoulder, and his
name is called Messenger of great counsel." Which, as you know, in the sixth
month of the conception of the other child, who proclaimed before the face of
his coming repentance for the remission of sins, was itself also conceived to
proclaim repentance; for we hear both of them in the first place proclaiming
not only concerning repentance, but also concerning the kingdom of heaven,
which as we have learned "is within us," "the word being near us which we
believe in our own mouth and in our own heart." Concerning which they also,
being reminded, will learn "to confess with their own mouth that Jesus is
Lord, believing in their own heart that God raised him from the dead,"
inasmuch as they hear that "with the heart one believes unto righteousness,
but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation."
6 Concerning slaves forced by their own masters to sacrifice for them. But for
those who have put forward Christian slaves in their own place, the slaves, as
being under their hand and in a manner themselves also imprisoned by the
masters, and threatened by them, and having come to this through fear of them
and having slipped, let them show the works of repentance in a year, learning
thereafter as slaves of Christ "to do the will of God" and to fear him,
hearing especially that "whatever each one does that is good, this he shall
receive from the Lord, whether slave or free."
7 Concerning those who forced their own slaves to sacrifice in their place.


But the free persons shall be examined in repentance for three years, both as
having used pretence and as having forced the "fellow-slaves" to sacrifice,
inasmuch as they disobeyed the apostle, who wishes the masters to do the same
things toward the slaves, forbearing threatening; "knowing," he says, "that
both our and their Lord is in the heavens and there is no respect of persons
with him." And if we all have one Lord who is no respecter of persons, since
also "Christ is all and in all, both in barbarians and Scythians, slaves and
free," they ought to consider what they have accomplished, having willed to
save their own soul, they who dragged our fellow-slaves to idolatry, who could
themselves also have escaped, if "they had granted them what is just and
equal," as again the apostle says.
8 Concerning those who were delivered up and fell, and then thus came forward
themselves to the contest and contended. But for those who were delivered up
and fell, and who have come forward themselves to the contest, confessing that
they are Christians, and have been cast into prison with tortures, it is
reasonable to strengthen them with gladness of heart and to communicate with
them in all things, both in the prayers and in the partaking of the body and
the blood and in the consolation of the word; that having contended more
vigorously they also may be counted worthy "of the prize of the upward
calling"; for "seven times," he says, "the righteous shall fall and rise
again"; which if also all who have fallen had done, they would have shown a
most perfect and wholehearted repentance.
9 Concerning those who of their own will leap into the contest.
And also for those who as from sleep leap themselves into the contest, which
is in travail and about to drag, but who have drawn upon themselves a
temptation of sea-fighting and much surging, or rather also "kindling for the
brethren a coal of sinners," we must communicate with them also, inasmuch as
they come to this in the name of Christ, even if they do not attend to his
words, teaching "to pray not to enter into temptation," and again in prayer he
says to the Father: "And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from the
evil one." And perhaps they are also ignorant of the often-occurring
withdrawals of our Master and Teacher from those who wished to plot against
him, and that sometimes "he did not even walk openly" because of them, and
that also when the time of his passion drew near, he did not give himself up,
but waited until they came upon him with swords and staves; "He says then to
them: As against a robber you have come out with swords and staves to take
me"; who also "delivered him," he says, "to Pilate." After his likeness then
also those who walk according to his aim have suffered, remembering his divine
words, through which strengthening us concerning persecutions, he says: "Take
heed to yourselves, for they shall deliver you up to councils, and in their
synagogues they shall scourge you." "They shall deliver you up," he said, but
not, you shall deliver yourselves up; and "before governors," he says, "and
kings you shall be brought for my name's sake," but not, you shall bring
yourselves. Since he also wishes us to leap from places to places when
persecuted for his name, as again we hear him saying: "And when they persecute
you in this city, flee into the other"; for he does not wish us to desert of
ourselves to the shield-bearers and spearmen of the devil, lest we also become
causes of more deaths to them, as forcing them rather to grow savage and to
complete the death-bearing works, but to wait and take heed to ourselves,
"both to watch and to pray, that we may not enter into temptation." Thus
Stephen first receiving martyrdom in his steps, having been seized in
Jerusalem by the lawless and brought into the council, being stoned in the
name of Christ, was glorified, entreating and saying: "Lord, lay not this sin


to their charge." Thus James second, having been seized by Herod, had his
head cut off with the sword. Thus the foremost of the apostles, Peter, often
seized and imprisoned and dishonored, afterwards was crucified in Rome.
Likewise also the renowned Paul, delivered up more often and endangered even
unto death, having contended much and boasted in many persecutions and
afflictions, in the same city also himself had his head shorn with the sword;
who in the things in which he boasted enumerated that also in Damascus he was
let down in a basket through the wall by night and escaped the hands of him
who sought to take him. For what was set before them in the first place was
to preach the gospel and to teach the word of God; in which strengthening the
brethren "to continue in the faith," they also said this, "that through many
afflictions we must enter into the kingdom of God"; for they sought "not
their own advantage, but that of the many, that they might be saved." And
there was much to say to them on these things toward acting according to
reason, if not, as the apostle says, "time would fail us as we narrate."
10 Concerning clerics. Whence it is not reasonable that those also of the
clergy who deserted of themselves, and fell and then wrestled again, should
still be in the ministry, inasmuch as they left the flock of the Lord and
blamed themselves, which none of the apostles has done. For also he who
exhausted many persecutions, and showed many prizes of contests, the blessed
apostle Paul, knowing also that "it is better to depart and to be with
Christ," adds and says: "But to remain in the flesh is more necessary for
you"; "for looking not to his own advantage, but to that of the many, that
they might be saved," he judged remaining with the brethren and caring for
them more necessary than his own rest; who also "wishes the one who teaches to
be in the teaching, becoming a type of the faithful." Whence those in the
prison who claim the ministry, having fallen and wrestled again, are
altogether without feeling; for how do they ask for what they left, being able
to be useful to the brethren in such a time? As long then as they were without
stumbling, they had pardon for their unreasonable act; but when they stumbled,
as having vaunted and blamed themselves, they can no longer minister.
Wherefore let them rather take thought in humility how they shall finish,
having ceased from vainglory; for the communion with attention and exactness
toward both is sufficient for them, both that they may not seem to be grieved,
grasping with violence at the departure from here, and that some who have
fallen may not make pretext as having been relaxed through the occasion of the
penalty. Who more than all will have shame and reproach, according to that one
who laid a foundation and was not able to finish; "For they shall begin," he
says, "all who pass by to mock him, saying: this man laid a foundation and was
not able to finish."
11 Concerning those who of their own will entered the contest and after many
tortures denied.
For those who first leaped forth in the boiling of the persecution, standing
around at the judgment-seat and seeing the holy martyrs hastening "to the
prize of the upward calling," being eager in a good zeal, gave themselves over
to this, using much boldness, looking especially at those being dragged away
and falling, for whom being heated within and sounding to war down the one who
exalts himself and opposes, they hastened to this, that he might not also
"seem to himself to be wise," in the things in which by knavery he seemed to
conquer, even if he escaped his own notice being conquered by those who
endured the tortures of the scrapers and scourgings, and the sharpness of the
sword and the burnings of the fire and the drownings of the waters. And that
prayers and supplications should be made for those who according to faith go
forth, either


for those punished in prison and betrayed by hunger and thirst, or for those
outside the prison tortured at the judgment-seat with scrapers and scourgings,
but afterwards overcome by the weakness of the flesh, it is worthy to consent.
For to suffer with and to grieve with those who lament and groan for those
overcome in the contest by the great violence of the evil-devising devil,
either for parents or brothers or children, harms no one at all; for we know
that some also through the faith of others have enjoyed the goodness of God,
both for remission of sins and health of body and resurrection of the dead.
Remembering then their many labors, which they previously bore in the name of
Christ, and the tossings, and not only so but also their repenting and
lamenting what was done to them by betrayal in the weakness and deadness of
the body, and further also having been witnessed as having lived well in their
life, we pray together and exhort together for their propitiation with the
other things that are due, through the Advocate who is toward the Father for
us, propitiating for our sins; "And if any one," he says, "sin, we have an
Advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous, and he is the
propitiation for our sins."
12 Concerning those who have given money.
For to those who have given money that they might be altogether undisturbed
from all wickedness it is not to bring a charge; for they bore loss and
destruction of goods, that they might not lose or destroy their own soul;
which others through shameful gain have not done, although the Lord says: "For
what shall a man be profited, if he gain the whole world, but lose or destroy
his soul?" and that: "You cannot serve God and mammon." For they appeared
beyond those serving God, having hated the money and despised it, and they
fulfilled also in this the thing written: "The ransom of a man's soul is his
own wealth." Since also in the Acts of the apostles we have read that those
dragged instead of Paul and Silas in Thessalonica before the politarchs were
released with a satisfaction; for after they had burdened them much for the
Name and troubled the crowd and the politarchs, "having taken," he says, "the
security from Jason and the rest, they released them; and the brethren
immediately by night sent away both Paul and Silas to Beroea."
13 Concerning those who fled.
Whence neither is it to accuse those who left all things for the salvation of
the soul and withdrew, as if others were detained because of them; for also in
Ephesus instead of Paul again they seized Gaius into the theatre and
Aristarchus, fellow-travellers of Paul, who wishing to enter into the people,
since also through him who persuaded and transferred a great crowd to piety
the sedition was taking place, "the disciples did not permit him," he says;
"and moreover also certain of the Asiarchs being friends to him, having sent
to him, exhorted him not to give himself into the theatre." And if some should
persist in cavilling at those who sincerely attend to him who says: "Saving,
save thine own soul, look not behind," let them be reminded also of the
foremost of the apostles, Peter, already cast into the prison and delivered to
four quaternions of soldiers to guard him; who having fled by night and been
delivered from the hand of the murderous Herod and all the expectation of the
people of the Jews, according to the command of an angel of the Lord, "when
day came," he says, "there was no small stir among the soldiers, what then had
become of Peter; and Herod having sought him and not found him, having
examined the guards, commanded


that they be led away"; on account of whom no charge is attached to Peter,
for it was possible for them also, having seen what happened, to flee; as
also all the children in Bethlehem, and in all its borders, if their parents
had known what was about to be, who were slain by the bloodstained Herod
because of the one child sought by him to be destroyed; which child also
itself according to the command of an angel of the Lord escaped, already
having begun quickly to spoil and swiftly to plunder according to the calling
of the name, as it is written: "Call his name, Spoil quickly, plunder
swiftly; because before the child knows to call father or mother, he shall
take the power of Damascus and the spoils of Samaria before the king of the
Assyrians." The magi then as already spoiled and plundered, in subjection and
honor worship the child, opening their treasures and offering to him most
timely and most fitting gifts, gold and frankincense and myrrh, as to a king
and God and man; whence they no longer thought it right to return to the
"Assyrian" king, being helped by providence; "for being warned," he says, "in
a dream not to return to Herod, they withdrew by another way into their own
country." Hence "the bloodthirsty Herod, seeing that he was mocked by the
magi, was exceedingly wroth, and having sent," he says, "he slew all the
children in Bethlehem and in all its borders from two years old and under,
according to the time which he had exactly learned from the magi." With whom
also seeking to kill the other child born before him and not finding him, he
murdered his father Zacharias "between the temple and the altar," the child
having escaped with his mother Elizabeth, upon whom they had no blame.
14 Concerning those who were forced to sacrifice.
And if some have suffered much violence and necessity, having received a bit
in the mouth and bonds, and having remained steadfastly in the disposition of
the faith, and their hands having been burned as they were brought unwillingly
to the unholy sacrifice, as indeed the thrice-blessed martyrs from the prison
wrote to me concerning those in Libya, and also other fellow-ministers, such
persons, the other brethren also especially bearing witness with them, can be
in the ministry, ranked among the confessors, as also those who were made as
dead in the many tortures and were no longer able to speak or utter a sound or
to be moved to resist those forcing them in vain; for they did not consent to
their abomination, as indeed I heard again from fellow-ministers. And there
shall be ranked among the confessors also whoever lives according to Timothy,
himself also obeying him who says: "Pursue righteousness, piety, faith, love,
patience, meekness; fight the good fight of the faith, lay hold of eternal
life, unto which you were called, and you confessed the good confession before
many witnesses."
