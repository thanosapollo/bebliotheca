* The Gouty Man
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

O hated name, O name even the gods abhor: Gout, child of Cocytus, cause of so
much groaning. Megaera the Erinys bore you in the deep-shaded dens of Tartarus
and nursed you at her breasts, and Alecto dripped milk on the bitter infant's
lip. Which of the daimons then sent you of ill name up into the light? You
came as a blight on men.

If the dead still pay among the shades for sins they did in the light, they
should not punish Tantalus with drink, nor Ixion whirled on the wheel, nor
Sisyphus with the stone in Pluto's house. They should simply bind every
wrongdoer to your joint-tormenting pains. That is how my wretched, miserable
body stands, from the tips of the hands to the ends of the feet, clenched with
foul ichor and the bitter juice of bile. The thing squeezes the passages with
a violent blast and draws the pains still tighter.

A fiery evil runs over the very innards. The flesh is consumed in whirls of
burning, like a crater full of Aetna's fire, or that Sicilian sea-cleft of a
strait where a crooked surge, hard to unwind, heaves and twists in the hollows
of the rocks.

O end that no man can guess, how vainly we all cherish you, herded by empty
hope, feeding on folly.

* Chorus
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h2
:END:

On Cybebe's Dindymon the Phrygians raise an inspired cry for tender Attis. To
the tune of the Phrygian horn-pipe, along the mountains of Tmolus, the Lydians
shout a revel. The Corybantes, frenzied about the drums, sound the strain in
Cretan rhythm: euan. The heavy trumpet blares, striking the cry of war for
furious Ares. But we, Gout, in the first hours of spring, as your initiates,
perform our laments, when every meadow blooms with grass that bears green, and
the trees, by Zephyr's breaths, grow leafy with tender leaves, and the
ill-mated swallow cries through the houses of mortals, and in the night wood
the Attic nightingale weeps and groans for Itys with her wails.

* The Gouty Man
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3
:END:

Alas, helper of my pains, O staff that has drawn the lot of a third foot: prop
up my trembling step and set the path straight, that I may plant a firm track
on the ground. Raise, wretched one, your limbs from the bed, and leave the
roofed shelter of the house. Scatter from your eyes the deep night of air. Go
outdoors, and toward the light of the sun draw an unclouded breeze of bright
breath. This is already the tenth day added to the fifth since, shut in gloom
without the sun, I wear out my body on beds without coverlets.

My soul and eagerness are ready. I have set out to change my steps toward the
door, but the sluggish body will not serve my desires. Yet hurry, heart. Know
this: a poor gouty man who would walk and cannot should be counted among the
dead. But come.

Who are these, staffs in their hands, their heads crowned with elder leaves?
For which of the daimons do they lead a reveling chorus? Surely, Phoebus
Paean, they are honoring your worship? But they are not crowned with a leaf of
Delphic laurel. Or is some hymn being revelled for Bacchus? But no ivy seal is
on their hair. Who then are you who have come to us, O strangers? Speak and
send forth a true word. Who is she whom you hymn? Tell us, friends.

The Cyprian Aphrodite fell in drops from the aether, and Nereus reared her as
a cosmic harmony in the waves of the sea. By the springs of Ocean, Tethys
nursed in her wide bosom white-armed Hera, consort of Olympian Zeus. On the
peaks of his immortal head the son of Cronus, far the best of the Olympians,
gave birth to the fearless nature of a daughter, battle-waking Athena. But our
blessed goddess old Ophion first brought forth in his shining arms, when dark
chaos ceased and shining dawn rose and the all-shining light of the sun. Then
too the power of Gout appeared. For when Fate Clotho, having borne you from
her flanks, then washed you, all the light of heaven laughed, and the clear
aether thundered greatly. At milk-rich breasts blessed Pluto reared her.

:PROPERTIES:

We do not pour fierce blood with cuttings of iron. No neck is twisted with
whirls of unbound hair. Backs are not struck with rattling knucklebones. We do
not eat raw torn flesh of bulls. But when in spring the slender blossom of the
elm swells, and the much-voiced blackbird sings on the branches, then a sharp
dart is fixed through the limbs of the initiates, unseen, hidden, sunk in the
recesses of the limbs: foot, knee, hip-socket, ankles, hips, thighs, hands,
shoulder-blades, arms, elbow, wrists. It eats, it grazes, it burns, it
masters, it fires, it softens, until the goddess bids the pain to flee.

* Gout
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h11
:END:

Who of mortals on the earth does not know me, Gout, the unconquered mistress
of pains? Neither the steam of frankincense propitiates me, nor blood poured
beside the altar fires, nor a temple hung round with images of wealth. Paean
has no strength to conquer me with drugs, though he is healer of all the gods
in heaven, nor the much-learned child of Phoebus, Asclepius.

For since the race of humans first arose, all dare to cast out my strength,
always stirring up the devices of drugs. One tries one art against me, another
another. They grind plantain and celery for me, and leaves of lettuces and
pasture purslane. Others horehound, others knotgrass, others grind nettles,
others comfrey, others bring lentils from the marshes. Boiled carrot, some
peach leaves, henbane, poppy, bulbs, pomegranate-rinds, fleawort,
frankincense, root of hellebore, nitre, fenugreek with wine, fine meal,
collamphacon, cypress gall, barley meal, leaves of boiled cabbage, gypsum from
Paros, dung of a mountain goat, human dung, bean-flour, flower of Assian
stone. They boil toads, shrews, lizards, weasels, frogs, hyenas, goat-stags,
foxes.

What metal has not been tried by mortals? What juice? What tear of a tree?
Bones, sinews, skins of all animals, fat, blood, marrow, urine, dung, milk.
Some drink the fourfold remedy, some the eightfold, more the sevenfold.
Another drinking the sacred purge is cleansed. Another is cheated with applied
incantations. A Jew takes another fool and sings him out. Another took a
treatment from Cyrrane the healer.

But I tell all these to go wail. Those who do these things and try me I am
accustomed to meet more angrily. To those who think nothing contrary to me I
have a gentle mind and become gracious. For he who has a share in my mysteries
is taught at once to speak well, delighting all, saying witty words. All see
him with laughter and applause when he is carried to the baths. For I am Ate,
whom Homer named, walking upon the heads of men and upon their feet, with
tender steps. Among the many of mortals I am called Podagra, being a snare of
the feet. But come, all initiates of my rites, honor with hymns the
unconquered goddess.

of your holy mortals. Great is your power, blessed Gout, at whom even the

* Messenger
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h13
:END:

Lady, you have met me with timely foot. Hear, for I bring no idle word. The
deed travels with the words. As you ordered, with quiet foot tracking cities,
I searched all houses, wanting to learn if anyone does not honor your power.
Of the others I saw a quiet mind, conquered, queen, by the force of your two
hands. But these two men, bold in daring, declared to the peoples and swore
that your power is no longer to be revered, and that they will make you an
outcast from the life of mortals. Therefore, having bound my foot with a
strong bond, I come on the fifth day, having completed two stades.

* Messenger
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h15

First I left a ladder of five steps, trembling with joints of wood that come
apart. From it a cobble-strewn floor receives me, meeting my steps with hard
soles. Having finished that with painful tracks I entered a road paved with
pebbles, hard to walk with the sharp points of stones. After that I hit the
slick of a smooth road and hurried forward, but loose mud dragged my weak
ankles behind. As I passed through it a moist sweat flowed from the limbs, my
step rotten and unstrung. From there a broad way receives me, every limb
weary, but not safe. On this side and that vehicles were laid out. They
pressed, forced, urged me to run. I lifted my sluggish foot lightly and went
sideways to the narrow edge of the road until a wheeled wagon ran past. Being
your initiate I had no strength to run fast.

* Gout
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h16
:END:

Not in vain, my good man, has this deed of yours been rightly done. For your
eagerness I will balance the favor with equal honors. Let this gift be
pleasing to you: for three years in a row you shall feel light pains. But you,
filthy and most hateful to the gods, who are you and of whom born, that you
dare to contend against the power of Gout, whose force not even the son of
Cronus knows how to conquer? Speak, you worst of men. I have subdued very many
heroes, as the wise know. Priam, though gouty, was called Swift-foot. Achilles
son of Peleus died gouty. Bellerophon endured while gouty. The ruler of
Thebes, Oedipus, was gouty. Of the Pelopids, Pleisthenes was gouty. The son of
Poeas led a fleet while gouty. Another Swift-foot was leader of the
Thessalians, who, after Protesilaus fell in battle, still led the fleet though
gouty and in pain. The lord of Ithaca, Odysseus son of Laertes, I killed. It
was not the sting-ray's spine. So you shall not rejoice, you ill-fated ones.
Suffer a punishment equal to what you have done.

* The Gouty Man
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h24
:END:

Alas, papai, I am worn out, I perish. Every limb is pierced by an unlooked-for
evil. Zeus does not swing such a bolt of thunder. No surge of the sea rages
so. The whirling force of a hurricane is not so great. Does the jagged bite of
Cerberus ravage me? Does some viper's venom feed around me, or the robe soaked
with the Centaur's ichor? Pity me, queen. Neither my drug nor any other can
check your course, and by every vote you conquer every race of mortals.

* Chorus
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h26
:END:

Neither did the force of Salmoneus contend with the thunders of Zeus. He died,
subdued in mind by the god's smoky bolt. Nor did the satyr Marsyas rejoice
after contending with Phoebus. His skin is lightly swept about a pine. Niobe
the mother had ever-remembered grief through strife, but still mourning she
pours much tear on Sipylus. Maeonian Arachne came into strife with Tritonis,
but having lost her form even now she still weaves threads. For the daring of
mortals is not equal to the angers of the blessed, as of Zeus, as of Leto, as
of Pallas, as of the Pythian. Gentle, O goddess of all the people, may you
bring the pain, Gout, light, slight, not pungent, short-harming, painless,
easy to bear, easy to cease, of little power, easy to walk with.

Many are the forms of the unfortunate. Let practice of pains and habit console
those who have gout. Therefore cheerfully, O partners in this lot, you will
forget pains, if what was expected was not fulfilled, and for the unexpected
god found a way. Let every one of those who suffer endure being mocked and
jeered. For such is the nature of this affair.
