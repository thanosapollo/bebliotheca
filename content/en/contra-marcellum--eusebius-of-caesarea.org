OF EUSEBIUS PAMPHILI OF THE THINGS AGAINST MARCELLUS BISHOP OF ANCYRA

Discourse I

1. From what impulse Marcellus wrote this treatise. 2. That he did not even
accurately examine the divine scriptures. 3. Of Marcellus on the proverbs
among the Greeks. 4. That he slandered both the ecclesiastical teaching and
those who preside over it.

1.1.1 The subject of his writing became hatred of brethren, and the cause of
this was jealousy and envy, which have also dashed countless others down to
the extremes of evils; jealousy and envy, at any rate, from the beginning
worked fratricide. Setting out from this, the man, having written this one and
only treatise, which he ought never to have written, immediately beginning
from the hearth proposed to make war on the holy ministers of God, leaving off
speaking and writing against the great and large company of the heretics who
were ruining his own country, which he ought especially to have done if any
power of the saving word was present to him, in the manner of a good shepherd
driving away as wolves and wild beasts the enemies of the church of God of
1.1.2 the nation of the Galatians. But he, as if overlooking his own country
as a certain dry land full of creeping venomous things, poured out his wrath,
boiling up randomly and in vain, upon the ministers of God. And not upon
ordinary ones among these, but upon those who by the grace of God are
altogether distinguished among all by life and by a philosophical way of
living; and not upon the living only, but also upon those who have fallen
asleep in Christ; and among these, upon those whose fame, because of virtue of
piety and exercise in divine words, is sung, so to speak, throughout the whole
inhabited world. And of these very men he did not make mention badly once, but
often and in different ways, having made the slanders 1.1.3 and false
accusations against them unsparingly. And already he has also used curses
against them, as women in a fight, reviling and calling down imprecations upon
those who had done him no injustice. Wherefore also beyond what was needed his
writing ran out into a long and measureless length, so that the one discourse
was completed at nearly 1.1.4 ten thousand verses in all. Reasonably
therefore, having sharpened a slanderous tongue against the holy ministers of
God and having trained in these things the irreverent and fearless, going on
next he advanced to the very summit of the whole, subjecting the only-begotten
and beloved Son of God to such godless blasphemies as it is better to hear a
little later as he himself says them, but not I. 1.1.5 And having used the
setting-beside of his own words I will stop at this point for the present
toward opposition and overturning of them, not bringing on long discourses,
because neither are the men blasphemed by him some unknown and obscure
persons, but they are known among 1.1.6 all even if one is silent, nor is the
blasphemy against the Christ of God some disputed and unknown thing, as to
need some great and more logical zeal for refutation; so to all who have
partaken of the ecclesiastical grace, small together with great, this
empty-voicing of this strange theologian, even if only spoken, has become
quite evident. Wherefore I will use only brief notations, giving body to the
discourse by sequence and order, and marking 1.1.7 only the irrationality of
the things brought in. But before going on to his utterances I would
necessarily advise those who meet with the man's treatise, if indeed they
should be especially some of the Galatians, not to be unmindful

of the divine voice of Paul the apostle, who writing to the Galatians
themselves exhorted them not to be transferred "from him who called" them "in
the grace of Christ to another gospel"; "for there is not another," as he
himself said, "except there are some who trouble" them 1.1.8 "and wish to
pervert the gospel of Christ," and necessarily to give heed also to that
saying through which even attacking himself the so great apostle, for the
shaming of those trying to distort the men, added saying "but even if we or an
angel from heaven should preach to you a gospel besides what we preached to
you, let him 1.1.9 be anathema." Which taking up again he says a second time
"as we have said before and now I say again, if anyone preaches to you a
gospel besides what you received, let him be anathema." And what was this,
besides which it was not possible to preach another gospel, or surely
altogether that very thing which the Saviour is recorded to have said handing
it over to his own disciples, "going, make disciples of all the nations,
baptizing them into the name of the Father and 1.=1.10= of the Son and of the
Holy Spirit"? For this grace of the knowledge of the holy Trinity he himself
alone has granted us through the mystical regeneration, neither Moses nor any
of the prophets having ministered this to the former people; for it was
fitting for the Son of God alone to announce the paternal grace to all men,
since "the law was given through Moses," but through 1.=1.11= Jesus Christ
alone, as through the "only-begotten Son" Jesus, "grace and truth" came to be.
Hence reasonably the one in the manner of a pedagogue handed over to the
former people while it was still infantile "elements of the beginning of the
oracles of God," restraining from polytheistic error, and proclaiming to know
one God only. But the saving grace, furnishing us a certain supercosmic and
angelic knowledge, unveiled openly the mystery long silent to the former
people, proclaiming that the God himself who is beyond the universe, the one
known to men of old, is God and at the same time Father of the only-begotten
Son, and supplying through the Son to the 1.=1.12= worthy the power of the
Holy Spirit. From this very point, somehow, the church of God, having received
the holy and blessed and mystical Trinity of Father and Son and Holy Spirit
into a saving hope through the regeneration in Christ, guards it. And this was
the gospel, which it is not lawful to be transferred "to another gospel, which
is not another," as the great apostle still even now testifies to the
Galatians, crying through the letter to them "even if we or an angel from
heaven should preach to you a gospel besides what you received, let him be
anathema"; distinguishing from afar, as is likely, that one should give heed
neither to bishops nor to rulers 1.=1.13= nor to teachers, if any of them
should distort the true word of the faith. And what was this but the one
teaching to know God as Father and handing over to recognize a Son of God and
to be ambitious to partake of Holy Spirit? Which things would be marks of
Christians alone, the 1.=1.14= holy church of God being marked off in this way
from the Jewish manner. For as that manner rejected the polytheistic and Greek
error by confession of one God, so also the exceptional knowledge of the
church concerning the Son provided something better and something more,
teaching to know the same God as Father of an only-begotten Son, who is truly
Son and living and existing. "For as the Father has life in himself, so also
he has given to the Son to have life in himself," the only- 1.=1.15= begotten
of God himself taught saying, in order that the Father also may be truly
Father, not being so styled merely as far as a voice, nor having acquired a
false title, but in truth and in deed Father of an only-begotten Son, and the
Son truly Son. But the one who supposes the Son to be a bare word and who
testifies that he is word only and who says this very thing often, that he was
nothing other than word, remaining within in the Father when he is silent but
operating when he creates the creation, similarly to

our word which is silent in those who are silent but operates in those who
utter, would be evident as running together with a certain Jewish and human
thought and denying the true Son of 1.=1.16= God. If at any rate someone
should ask any of the Jews whether God has a word, he will surely say so;
since every one of the Jews would confess that he has both a word and more
words. But that he also has a Son, he would no longer 1.=1.17= confess if
asked. And if not of the Jews but some one of the bishops should introduce
this opinion, giving him as word only united to God and that this one is
eternal and unbegotten and is one and the same with God, being styled by
different names of Father and Son but being one in essence and hypostasis, how
would he not become evident as putting on Sabellius, and as alienated from the
knowledge and grace in Christ? For neither to inquire nor to doubt nor to ask
them whether the only-begotten Son of God was and pre-existed would the
ordinance of the church allow; but with confessed and undoubted faith, with
much courage and frankness, it teaches to confess that God is Father of the
only-begotten Son, and neither to think that the Father is Son or the Son
Father; but to worship the unbegotten and eternal and beginningless and first
and only Father, and to believe that the Son was begotten from the Father and
came to subsist and is only-begotten Son alone, and to recognize 1.=1.18= him
as God, as being truly Son of God. If then someone should appear contradicting
these things, and should define this Word of God similarly to that in men, how
would he not be refuted as neither thinking nor reckoning that one must
attribute nothing mortal nor human to the God who is beyond the universe, nor
at all any of the things that come up into our thought, even if the divine
scriptures, as pedagogues of those who meet them as certain infants, converse
more humanly about him, attaching to him hands and feet and eyes and voices
and words and mouth and face and countless other such things; which it is
fitting to transfer to thoughts worthy of God, imagining nothing mortal nor
human to be in God; for "God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship
in spirit and truth," the 1.=1.19= Saviour himself taught. And if spirit, it
is clear that it is divine, better than every perceptible and composite body,
so as neither to have a perceptible word, sounded out by a tongue and heard
through utterance by mortal ears, nor tongue nor voice nor face nor anything
else compared to a mortal and 1.=1.20= human reasoning. For if the divine
apostle taught that some other things "which eye has not seen nor ear heard,
nor have they gone up upon the heart of man" are such as "God prepared for
those who love him," the giver himself of all these would be more unspeakable
than unspeakable things, so that it would be much more truly said in the
theology concerning him that eye has not seen nor ear heard nor has the
knowledge of the grasp concerning him 1.=1.21= gone up "upon the heart of
men." And the same word would apply also concerning the generation of the Son.
And of these things the Saviour himself would become a sufficient hierophant
for us, saying in this way "all things have been handed over to me by my
Father, and no one knows the Son except the Father, nor 1.=1.22= does anyone
know the Father except the Son." If then someone should speak against these
things and should dare not to say that the Son, to whom the Father handed over
all things, exists, but should define him as word only similar to that in men,
at one time being silent in God similarly to the silent word among us, and at
another time operating similarly to the one among us that utters in speaking,
then should say that this same one at a certain time not even a whole four
hundred years ago took on flesh I know not how and through it completed the
economy according to man, and then became Son of God and was styled Jesus
Christ and was proclaimed king and image of "the invisible God," and

firstborn "of all creation" was shown not having been so at any time before;
then upon such an absurdity, not even from the time he supposed, giving to
Christ the infinite and endless life and kingdom, but should bring upon him an
end neither favorable nor such as for those who will be counted worthy through
him of the promises which the Saviour himself has promised, having promised
eternal life and incorruption and immortality and a kingdom of heavens to
those who love him 1.=1.23=; and then according to the hoped-for hope of these
things should say that for all the others the things of the kingdom will last
into an endless age, but for Christ alone of all these there will come to be a
sudden deprivation, his kingdom being made to cease, and the flesh which he
took being left deserted, and the Word who pre-existed in God being separated
from the body even if this should be immortal and incorruptible, and being
joined to God, so as to become one and the same again with God just as he also
was before; what 1.=1.24= excess of impiety would be left for this man?
Wherefore for these things the testimony of the apostolic distinction is
necessary, which said "even if an angel from heaven should preach a gospel
besides what you received, let him be anathema." And it comes upon me to
wonder as I look upon the apostolic mind. For as if prophesying the future he
accurately set out for the Galatians themselves the theology of the Saviour,
writing from the very beginning of the letter to them more newly than
according to the customary manner thus: "Paul an apostle, not from 1.=1.25=
men nor through a man but through Jesus Christ." For one would not find him
writing thus to others; because there were not, as is likely, others similarly
concerning the faith to those to whom he wrote. For as they supposed Christ to
be a bare man he brought on the correction, teaching at the beginning of the
letter that he is not a man; and going on he said to the same people that "my
gospel, the one preached to you, is not according to man, nor did I receive it
from a man nor was I taught it, but through a revelation of Jesus Christ,"
1.=1.26= through which again he set forth that Jesus Christ was not a man. And
what he was, if not a man, going on again he made clear saying "but when God
who set me apart from my mother's womb was well pleased to reveal his Son in
me." You see how he clearly proclaimed the Saviour Son of God, 1.=1.27= and on
this account God. And that he knew him to be Son of God even before the
incarnate presence, he sets forth most distinctly adding in the same letter to
the Galatians "but when the fullness of the time came, God sent forth his Son,
born of a woman." Therefore not now, but before he was born of a woman, being
Son and pre-existing, the Father sent him; so that the one who was of old Son
of God might also become 1.=1.28= Son of man, born "of a woman." But he also
teaches that he became mediator of the law of Moses, defining the account of
the mediation through what he says: "What then? The law was set because of
transgressions, until the seed should come to whom it has been promised, being
arranged through angels in the hand of a mediator. And the mediator is not of
one, but God is one." You hear how in these things the apostle taught the
Galatians themselves from 1.=1.29= that time to know one God, and one the
mediator of God and of angels. Which he also made clear in other places saying
"for there is one God; and one mediator of God and men, the man Jesus Christ."
But when he named him mediator "of God and men," reasonably he called him man
because of the incarnation; but when he introduced the same one not as
mediator of God and men, but of God and angels, he no longer named him man,
but says mediator only, having said that the law was arranged 1.=1.30=
"through angels in the hand of a mediator," and necessarily making clear the
name of mediator by a distinction in saying "and the mediator is not of one,
but God is one." Neither therefore would God be the mediator; for of whom
would he even become mediator? Nor would the mediator himself himself

be God; 1.=1.31= for "the mediator is not of one." He has therefore stood in
the middle of two. Of whom these are, he makes clear naming angels and God; of
whom being in the middle the Son of God, he says, received the law with his
own hand from the Father, but arranged it "through angels" for the former
people. From that time then the Son was mediator of God and of angels, before
he be- 1.=1.32= came "mediator of God and men." And he was not as a bare word
of God, without hypostasis, being one and the same with God; for thus he would
not be mediator; but he was and pre-existed as "only-begotten Son full of
grace and truth." And he was mediating for the Father who provided the law
"through angels" to men. Which teaching from that time to those ignorant and
unlearned in the theology of the Son of God the apostle sealed saying "and the
mediator is not of one." For it does not have nature to 1.=1.33= define the
mediator upon one. Wherefore this one is not of one, but of necessity in the
middle of two, being neither of those of whom he happens to be in the middle;
so as neither to think him to be the God over all nor one of the angels, but
middle and mediator of these, when he mediates for the Father and angels; as
again, when he becomes "mediator of God and men," being middle of each order,
he is neither of those of whom he is mediator; being neither himself the one
and only God nor similarly to the remaining 1.=1.34= men a man. And what, if
none of these, but only-begotten Son of God, now having become mediator of men
and God, but long ago in the time of Moses existing as mediator of God and
angels? And writing these things to them, the great apostle hands them over
saying in this way "the law having been ar- 1.=1.35= ranged through angels in
the hand of a mediator. And the mediator is not of one, but God is one." "One"
then is "God," and "one mediator of God" and of all generated things, not now
beginning the saving mediation, but even before his theophany toward men, just
as 1.=1.36= the argument has shown. These things having been set beside in
brief for the Galatians themselves from the letter to them alone, and the
saving faith furnishing the mystical regeneration also "into the name of the
Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit," and besides the divine writings
the catholic church of God from the ends of the earth to the ends sealing the
testimonies from the divine scriptures by unwritten tradition, it is time
remaining also to examine the phrases of Marcellus and to furnish the proofs
of the things promised by us, so that no one may think the man to be falsely
accused by us. 1.=1.37= And since my aim is set to bring to light the things
said through brief remarks, I will not collect all the utterances of the man,
but I will use only the connective ones, postponing the more as if superfluous
and going through the same things. But before the proof of the things said I
think it necessary first to set before those who meet with it, that he did not
even accurately examine the ready phrases of the divine readings, so that it
may be known to those who still do not know him of what sort he was when he
was led on to the daring of the things said. 1.2.1 Immediately then, when the
prophet Zechariah, who came to be according to the times of the return from
Babylon, made mention of Jesus the great priest, clearly the son of Josedek,
who together with Zerubbabel led those who returned from Babylon, this noble
and wonderful writer, being ignorant of these things, sets down the phrase of
Zechariah through which he makes mention of Jesus, but having been shot far
from the history 1.2.2 he supposed him to speak about Jesus the successor of
Moses. And again when the apostle wrote in the letter to the Galatians in this
manner "but the Jerusalem above is free, which is our mother," either he
forgot or he did not understand or even willingly he distorts the saying,
writing "but our Jerusalem is above; for she is in slavery with her children."
1.2.3 And again when our Saviour had said to Peter "go behind me, Satan, you
are a stumbling-block to me, because you do not think the things of God

but the things of men," not having understood how the saying was said to the
apostle and for what cause, he says these things were spoken to the devil. And
together he sets so many errors under one passage, writing word for word in
this manner: that no name greater than Jesus of those named upon the earth has
come to be, the gospel testifies, where "the angel" said to Mary "fear not,
for you have found favor with God. And behold you will conceive in the womb
and will bear a son, and you will call his name 1.2.4 Jesus. This one will be
great, and will be called Son of the Highest"; and it is clear also from the
prophecy of Zechariah, which of old prophesied about this name, for "the Lord
showed me," he says, "Jesus the great priest, standing before the face of an
angel of the Lord, and the devil stood at his right to oppose him. And the
Lord said to the devil: the Lord rebuke you who has cho- 1.2.5 sen Jerusalem."
For when did he rebuke him? When he joined the man beloved by him to his own
Word. "Who has chosen," he says, "Jerusalem," clearly this one of ours, about
which the apostle says "but our Jerusalem is above; for she 1.2.6 is in
slavery with her children." For then, having come to be in that great
Jerusalem, that is in our church, he rebuked the devil according to the
prophecy saying "go behind me, Satan, because you are a stumbling-block to
me." This one then is the 1.2.7 great priest, whose type the Jesus of that
time preserved. For it was not possible for that one to be called great
priest, although he had become glorious among all, Moses not having been named
great; for Moses was not yet great, because he both heard "servant" of God and
was named by God himself god of 1.2.8 Pharaoh. And if someone should think
Jesus to have been called great according to this, that he was counted worthy
to lead the people into the holy land and did many other wonders, let him know
also through this, that the greatness spoken of upon Jesus did not differ so
much by the typical deed, but by the one about to lead his own people a little
later into this great Jerusalem. You see how much he was ignorant of, having
wandered from the history, and not having been able to at- 1.2.9 tend to the
Jesus indicated by the prophet Zechariah. For that one was son of Josedek, of
the tribe of Levites from the race of Aaron having taken up the
high-priesthood, wherefore also he was styled great priest, just as the
prophet teaches saying "and the Lord showed me Jesus the great priest."
1.=2.10= But the successor of Moses, the one leading the people into the land
of the promise and having done many other wonders, was son of Nun, of the
tribe of Ephraim, which has nothing in common with priesthood. How then would
the one who has so far missed the ready phrase and been ignorant of the bare
history become sufficient for teaching of the highest theology? And upon Peter
likewise 1.=2.11= it was necessary to attend to how "go behind me" was said to
him, and what is the interpretation of the name Satan, and why he also called
him "stumbling-block," and how almost under one and the same time both
"blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jonah" was said to him and 1.=2.12= "go behind me,
Satan," and what is the mind of "going behind" and of the Saviour which he
himself made clear at his feet, joining next "if anyone wishes to come behind
me, let him deny himself and take up his cross and follow me"; which also
1.=2.13= Peter himself accomplished in deed a little later, being perfected by
martyrdom, but it was also necessary to inquire upon what things he blessed
him, and upon what he rebuked him; but he, having given heed to none of these,
declared these things to have been said 1.=2.14= to the devil, contrary to the
scripture. Was this then the only thing he was ignorant of? Did he not also
going on again, having made mention of the resurrection of our Saviour, then
wishing to show that even before him according to the times

of the prophets someone is reported raised from the dead, again even here he
errs, saying first that Elisha the prophet raised from the dead; having been
ignorant that even before Elisha Elijah the great raised the son of the widow
who had died, as it is possible 1.=2.15= to learn from the third of Kingdoms?
And he appears ignorant also of this, through the things he writes thus: not
only then does the apostle say that he is firstborn of "the new creation," but
also firstborn "from the dead," for no other reason, as it seems to me, but in
order that through the firstborn "of the dead" it may be possible to know how
he has also been called "firstborn of all creation." For our master Jesus
Christ did not rise first from the dead; but the one raised through Elisha the
prophet rose ear- lier, and Lazarus also rose before his resurrection, and at
the time of the passion "many bodies of those who had fallen asleep" rose.
1.=2.16= And this too Marcellus seems to me to have set down without
observation, I mean that "many bodies of the saints who had fallen asleep" had
been raised before him. For he did not give heed to the whole writing of the
gospel teaching that "after 1.=2.17= his rising" the bodies of the saints have
been raised. And you would find him erring also in the setting-out of the
apostolic saying. For the divine apostle writing to the Romans says these
things: "set apart for the gospel of God, which he promised beforehand through
his prophets in holy writings concerning his Son who came to be from the seed
of David according to flesh, 1.=2.18= who was appointed Son of God in power
according to a spirit of holiness." But he, I do not know with what mind, even
here distorts the apostolic phrase, having made "fore-appointed" instead of
"appointed," in order that he may be similar to those 1.=2.19= fore-appointed
"according to foreknowledge." He says then word for word thus: just as then
the almighty God long ago fore-appointed the church, so also the economy of
Christ according to flesh, through whom he fore-appointed to call the race of
the godly "into adoption," having first founded it in his own mind. On this
account the apostle clearly proclaims by the Holy Spirit saying "of the Son of
God who was fore-appointed." And again he says in other places: this is the
one about whom Paul said "of the Son of God who was fore-appointed." 1.=2.20=
And here he has not only corrupted the apostolic phrase alone, but also the
mind itself through the addition of the preposition "fore." And doing things
akin to these, when the psalmody contains "from the womb before the
morning-star I begot you," he even here uses an addition nowhere brought in,
toward blaming the one who does not 1.=2.21= speak similarly to him. Hear then
how he writes saying: on this account then it seems to me to be well still now
to go through things which I have not 1.=2.22= yet gone through before. For
the greater part of the things written by him have become clear from the
things already said by us. 20"from the womb"20 he says 20"before the
morning-star I generated you out"20. For he thought altogether that the
preposition "ex" having been stolen would run together with the opinion of the
heresy. Wherefore having taken out the most proper part of the syllable he
wished to signify his ancient regeneration. And since in these things he
strongly strained about his own error, blaming the one who wrote correctly,
himself having said nothing, he hands over to those who meet with it the same
testimony of the reading. 1.=2.23= And going on again the same man introduces
the Saviour saying "I am the day," saying in this way: for darkness being
formerly because of ignorance of piety, and the day being about to appear, for
"I am" he says "the day," reasonably he names the star morning-star. 1.=2.24=
And in another place he says: since after the taking-up of the flesh he is
proclaimed both Christ and Jesus, and life and way and day. And again going on
he sets down the apostle's "but our Jerusalem is above." 1.=2.25= And why must
I lengthen, it being possible for the one who wishes from the things said to
collect similar things from the whole

treatise and to observe the facility of the man. From which facility you will
hear him calling Solomon a prophet often and naming the Proverbs 1.=2.26=
prophecies; just as in the things in which he says: for this reason the most
holy prophet Solomon said "both to receive turns of words," and again "both
sayings of wise men and riddles," he names the proverb a prophecy, and again:
wherefore it seems to me that this most wise prophet has also said the first
words of the prophecy proverbially. 1.=2.27= And a second and a third time and
often, as I said, he does this, being ignorant that "there are divisions of
gifts" even if "the same Spirit," and to one "a word of wisdom is given"
according to the apostle, "to another a word of knowledge according to the
same Spirit, to another faith in the same Spirit, to another pro- 1.=2.28=
phecy." Wherefore Solomon, having received a gift of wisdom, is testified "for
the Lord gave wisdom to Solomon," as the scripture says, "and he was made wise
above all men"; but having neither had a prophetic life nor the character of
prophecy, that which is recognized from "thus says the Lord" and things of
like manner, he makes this clear through his 1.=2.29= own words. But he,
having seen none of these things, as if granting some favor to the man calls
him a prophet, not understanding that the prophets "went about in sheepskins,
in goatskins, being in want, being afflicted, being maltreated." And he also
says that his Proverbs are a prophecy, although nowhere does the divine
scripture teach this, but the most wise man himself with divine Spirit having
set upon this book the name PROVERBS, and upon the other ECCLESIASTES and upon
the third SONG 1.=2.30= OF SONGS, not without God nor by human reasoning
applying these titles. But he also tries to interpret his God-inspired writing
itself through Greek examples, not having heard Paul the apostle saying "but
we have not received the spirit of the world but the Spirit that is from God,
in order that we may see the things granted us by God; which things we also
speak not in words taught of human wisdom, but in those taught of Holy Spirit,
comparing spiritual things with spiritual. But a soulish man does not receive
the things of the Spirit of God; for it is folly to him, and he is not able to
know, because it is examined spiritually." And being ignorant also of these
things the noble man tries to set beside us the mind of the divine Proverbs
