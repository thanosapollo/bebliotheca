Of Chrysostom on almsgiving.
=60.747=
Again there has been given us by our Lord and God and Savior Jesus Christ a
time to cast down into your hearing the seeds of almsgiving; again Christ has
given us to imitate the sower who sows his seed upon the good earth, and
receives a hundredfold. For behold, the proclamation of the hands has sounded
the trumpet; behold, the theater of almsgiving has been assembled. Let them
therefore be called into the midst, the lovers of God and the lovers of honor
and the lovers of the poor; let them be called, the lovers of the crowns. For
God is present, the dispenser of the prizes, receiving a few coins from the
lovers of the poor, and granting them the kingdom of heaven.
Let none of you then, I beg, be cheated of such grace; let no one overlook
this great and supermundane gift for the sake of a few coins, neither poor nor
rich, neither slave nor free, neither wise nor unlearned, neither man nor
woman; but let us all, I beg, with eagerness purchase the kingdom of heaven.
And let none of the poor say to me: I am a beggar; for it is necessary to take
hold of them first, since they think their apology appears acceptable before
God. For what does the poor man often say? I have no money, I do not have
plenty of daily food, the whole day I toil and am worn out, that I may barely
be able to get through life; I carry loads on my shoulders, I work as a
carpenter, I hammer with the hammer, and I waste away the strength of the
body; and you sit judging me, that I should give away my labors to strangers?
For tell me, do you altogether speak of your own labors, and do you think the
strength of your body is your own possession? and do you not believe that if
you give a little of what you call your labors, you are offering alms to him
who granted you the strength and power of the work? Rather give a little less
to your body, that you may grant the great treasure to the soul. For if,
having ten obols, you give nine to the body and one to the soul, are all the
things of your life overturned by this? If you have plenty of fifty obols, and
you give five to God through the helpless, and the forty-five to yourself, you
have strengthened the soul by faith, you have also empowered the body through
the prayer of others, and you have received grace to work again, and again to
gain many times over, and you have become richer than the rich toward
almsgiving. But you will certainly say to me: And what, he says, shall I gain
from this? That you are no longer called a poor man, but a lender to God. And
whence, he says, will this come to me? Believe the Scripture that says: He who
has mercy on a poor man lends to God; and you have to hear from him: Inasmuch
as you did it to one of these least, you did it to me. Have you not often,
sitting in the marketplace, seen a rich man passing by, and blessed him
saying, Blessed is this rich man, because he has no need of the provision of
others, but rather even lends? And that one lends to men, but you to God; that
one ta =60.748= kes from each the things from labors with toil and with
fighting in sin; but you receive from God without toil, without care, with joy
a hundredfold; that one receives for this short life, but you for the unending
life inherit eternal life.
And if you do not believe me, believe God who has given you full assurance
with an oath, and says: Amen I say to you, you shall receive a hundredfold,
and you shall inherit eternal life. Do not then think that you are diminished
by the giving to the poor; for you do not become poor from the sharing with
the poor. If the poor man has mercy on a poor man, he does not become poorer,
but praiseworthy, and he rather makes his poverty secure.
Have you heard the Prophet saying: I was younger; for I have also grown old;
and I have not seen a righteous man forsaken, nor his seed seeking bread? and
again, All the day the righteous man has mercy and lends, and his seed shall
be for a blessing? For a blessing, why? Because he who sows upon blessings,
also reaps upon blessings; for if you take care of your garments, he will also
take care of your soul. For nothing so clothes the soul as the covering of the
widow and orphan and poor man. For it does not let the soul be seen naked
before God on that fearful and dreadful day; but clothed in a robe of light
through almsgiving it appears to God with all glory. For God says through the
prophet: Break your bread for the hungry, and bring the homeless poor into
your house; and then your light shall be early, and your healings shall
quickly rise, and your righteousness shall go before you. Did you see how
great a reward is laid up for you through a little bread or one obol? did you
learn how much light the fruit of almsgiving brings you? Do you wish to learn
how great a treasure the small gift of the poor has procured for us? do you
want to see what poor people, bringing poor things, inherited the place of
great treasures? Hear. For we do not give you full assurance from the New
Testament only, but also from the Old, that you through love of the poor may
gain the heavenly treasure. A famine once fed upon men throughout the whole
earth, and so great a famine, that each day a river of men and beasts was
given over to death, not in a short time, but in three and a half whole years,
so that the whole earth too was wasted. And the cause of the evil was the
idolatry of the Israelites, and the lawlessness of Ahab and Jezebel, through
which Elijah, being a man, was able to put bolts upon the heavens, saying: As
the Lord lives, there shall be no rain, except through my mouth. And it did
not rain upon the earth for three years and six months. Then thereafter the
lawless king, perceiving that the prophet Elijah had become the minister of
this sentence, went about seeking to kill him. So that for this reason the
prophet, fleeing away into the land of the foreigners, found a widow woman, a
remnant of the famine, having a little flour and a little =60.749= oil, in
which she had hanging all the hope of her own life and of her children. And
the prophet Elijah says to her: Bring me a little bread in your hand. And the
woman said to him: As the Lord your God lives, if I have a cake, but only as
much as a handful of flour in the house, and a little oil in the cruse; and
behold I am gathering two sticks, and I am going in to make it for myself and
my children, and we shall eat and die. Does any of us today drive on into such
poverty? God forbid. But from this remnant of death the widow first made a
cake for the prophet, and brought it out to him, having despised herself and
her children. And what recompense did she receive of this poor almsgiving?
Hear plainly what the prophet says: Thus says the Lord: The jar of flour shall
not fail, and the cruse of oil shall not be diminished. Until when, prophet?
Until the Lord gives rain and dew upon the earth.
For this reason the prophet says: Blessed is the man who has trusted in the
Lord; and his hope shall be in a time of drought, and he shall not be
diminished nor fail. And the word of the prophet became a deed, and neither
did the jar fail, nor was the cruse diminished. Are you poorer than this
woman, O man? does so great a famine lie upon you, that you should not do
almsgiving? It is not so, God forbid; but you both have and are able, whoever
you are, and especially if you are faithful. And toward the body you are
ambitious of honor, but concerning the soul you are a niggard. Almsgiving is
food of the soul; and just as bread and wine are food and gladness of the
body, so also almsgiving and prayer are healing and joy of the soul. And what
does a man give as an exchange for his soul, except almsgiving only? And if
you do not know, learn also from the widow in the New Testament, that through
two obols she gained the whole kingdom of heaven, having received God himself
entire as a praiser of this poverty, and her fruit-offering having gone up as
far as heaven. Did you not see the Savior in the holy Gospels accepting the
widow who was casting the two mites into the treasury, and granting her the
heavenly crown? For he saw the fragrance of the soul, he saw the poor woman a
lover of the poor, he saw her who was lacking money contributing together with
those who were not lacking. There were many who were casting much into the
treasury; and present at the things that were happening was God and man at
once, the same one not judging according to face, and searching the hearts of
men.
And what sentence he brings out concerning these, let us hear: Amen, amen I
say to you, that this widow has cast in more than all. For I do not accept all
the things that are cast in, he says, but I also test the purpose of those who
contribute. Amen, amen I say to you, that this widow has cast in more than
all. Why? Because these brought from what was overflowing to them, but she
brought in her whole living. Therefore since he judged justly, let us also all
say to God, as the Prophet said: You are righteous, Lord, and your judgments
are upright; because you test the purpose of men, and you do not circumscribe
the kingdom by the multitude of the things offered. For you do not attend to
the things offered, but to the disposition of those who offer. Let none of you
then hesitate, I beg, even two mites, even ten obols, to cast down into the
treasury of the widows and orphans; for so also the holy Scripture teaches,
that according as a man has purpose, he is well-accepted before God. Let not
then that table become foreign to your fruit-offering; but have a share with
it, that you may receive a share with those who are receiving, mercy from the
merciful God who said: Have mercy, that you may be shown mercy. For just as it
is believed that there is an angel from heaven who receives the prayers of the
people, and offers them to God, and you are eager with all to render the
prayers to God, that your gift of prayer may be accepted with all; so believe
that in these things also there is an angel who receives the things given by
you to the poor, and offers these to God. And who is this angel, but the one
who offered the prayers and the alms of Cornelius to God, and said to him:
Cornelius, your alms and your pray =60.750= ers have gone up for a memorial
before God? And he was formerly a Greek; but after this, from the time the
angel spoke to him, he became faithful, and so they were accepted. How much
more yours, who are a lover of Christ?
And we ought thereafter to exhort the rich also. Most of all many are lovers
of the poor, and of themselves they do this; for they have well practiced
being watered by such men, those teachers before us, to bear fruit; but
nevertheless since they also demand an account of the poor among us, I will
not hesitate to exhort them, and to lead them by the hand toward almsgiving,
guarding these two things, that they may not be accused before God, nor I be
slandered by the angels; and how, I say. For if I do not cast down the words
of the divine Scripture onto your good table, as silver upon the banker, I am
at once accused by God, and I hear from him: You ought to have cast down my
silver to the bankers, and I having come would have demanded my own with
interest. For this he also accused in the holy Gospels the one entrusted with
the denarius, who hid it. And how the prophet calls it silver, hear: The words
of the Lord are pure words; silver fired, tried for the earth, purified seven
times. So also God says to the prophet: Son of man, bear witness to this
people; and again otherwise, Comfort, you priests, my people; speak into the
ears of Jerusalem. Having therefore received such charges I exhort all of you
to come to almsgiving, that I may not also be slandered by you at that hour,
if, which God forbid, someone should hear from the Judge on that fearful day:
Wicked servant, why did you not have mercy on your fellow-servant? and now how
do you wish to be shown mercy?
And again: Repent now, because there there is no time of repentance; lest
someone being accused at that dreadful hour should say, that I was not taught
by the shepherd, I did not learn what a man shall give as an exchange for his
soul, nor did he tell us that almsgiving atones for sins, he did not interpret
for us the Scripture given him by you that says, Blessed is he who understands
upon a poor and needy man; on an evil day the Lord will deliver him; but he
only separated us from Jewish unbelief, and again forbade the superstition of
the Greeks; from whose lawlessness also having been taught we stood away. For
if he had also trained us toward almsgiving, we would have had the fruits of
this also to show. For this reason also I do not cease exhorting with
boldness, but I admonish and bear witness to rich and poor together, men and
women alike, spare your souls, and ransom them through money from the wrath
that is to come; lighten the burden of sins through almsgiving; have mercy,
that you may be shown mercy. For almsgiving has the power of holy baptism; and
just as the holy washing cleanses away the sins of men, so also almsgiving
cleanses the faults of the soul of those who have mercy. The word is not mine,
but the divine Scripture teaches saying: alms and they are faithful; sins are
cleansed away. The money, even if we do not wish, remains here; but the soul,
even if we do not want, is conducted away from here, undergoing the accounts
of the sins. For this reason also our Lord Jesus Christ said in the Gospels:
What shall a man be profited, if he gain the world, but be damaged in his
soul? or what shall a man give as an exchange for his soul? Hear then, I beg,
this saying, that in peace it is despised, but in necessities it shows the
power of the law of God.
For the hearer often hears: What shall a man be profited, if he gain the whole
world, but be damaged in his soul? and he runs past the power of this saying.
And if he despise the saying in peace, he learns in necessities the power of
the one who spoke. For what does God do, when he sees someone clinging to
money, but turning away from almsgiving, and choosing to suffer all things
rather than betray the money? In necessities he teaches him to know the law of
God; and how, hear: Think for me of some merchant going by sea, and having his
=60.751= mind only about money, choosing to suffer all things rather than lose
one obol, bringing countless oaths against his soul, so as not to betray
anything of his own. But such a one, harsh and niggardly, in an hour of
necessity, when he is fiercely storm-tossed and in danger in the sea; for the
surge stands over him, the sea raises triple waves, the waves are lifted up as
far as heaven; at once then the love of money withers, and he who endured all
toil, that he might gather the money, makes jettison with his own hands of all
that he has, and grants the wealth to the sea. But ask him at once, What are
you doing, O man? why are you mad? why do you throw the money uselessly? the
sea does not keep your deposits; it is not as the earth receiving the seeds,
and giving you many times over; for it does not take seeds, and give sheaves.
Why then do you lend uselessly to an ungrateful element? why do you trust
unfaithful waves? why do you give your cargo as a deposit to the sea that does
not pay back? You were not willing to lend to God, and you throw the money to
the wild sea?
What then does that one answer at once? I wish to save my soul, rather than
gain the whole world. Now you have known, What shall a man give as an exchange
for his own soul? now you have learned the saying of Christ, where exhorting
he said, What shall it profit a man, if he gain the whole world, but be
damaged in his soul? But did the law not train you? Let fear at least train
you. Did the teaching of God not train you? Let the madness of the sea train
you. O the inconsistent deed; O the necessity that trains all things; the sea
taking by force takes all, but God exhorting toward the kingdom of heaven,
does he not even take some part? you trust the sea that promises you nothing
good, and you do not trust God who promises you a hundredfold and grants it?
Do you wish that I also set before you another image of such necessity? for it
is necessary that your soul be benefited from every side both through the
fearful =60.752= and through the good things. Think then also of those who
trade on the land, who do and perform all things for the sake of gaining
something, who dare with an oath, and are full of every perjury for the sake
of taking one obol more, that such men set aside the laws of God. Prophets
proclaim, and those men shut their ears; teachers cry out, and those men sneer
at the admonition. And what thereafter happens in the case of these? for not
in the case of all, but in the case of these. For such a merchant often falls
among robbers; and he who was gaping toward wealth, and had insatiably put on
the passion of love of money, at once strips off such a disease, and becomes
lavish in granting; for he sees the sword which the robber carries, and he
flees the passion; the robber comes upon him, and that man is found
thereafter, like some lover of honor, offering his own things with entreaty.
What then does he say to the robber then? Take all, and only grant me my soul.
Let this man also hear: Now you have known what a man shall give as an
exchange for his soul. To a robber wanting to take all you give with all
entreaty, to him who harms you in all things; but to God wanting to take some
part you do not lend, to him who richly provides you all good things, both
earthly and heavenly; but you do not even fear God as much as the robber? And
you fear a sword being bared, but you despise the fire of Gehenna being
threatened. Do not, I beg, let some of you become such; for you are not,
especially being lovers of Christ; for you know all the things that will
result for men.
Make your souls secure before the departure from here, and recognize the one
who has said in the holy Gospels: Blessed are the merciful, for they shall be
shown mercy. To him then, the God who has mercy and feeds and does not
reproach, let us send up glory, with the Father and the all-holy and
life-giving Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
