* Letter to Eusebius
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

An exact clarification of our father among the saints Epiphanius, bishop of
Constantia of the island of the Cyprians, concerning the day of Pascha; from
the letter to Eusebius and Marcellus, and to Vivianus (Βηβιανόν cod.) and
Carpus.
205 The Jews then ate the Passover, with whom the Lord ate this together, two
days before the appointed true Passover; as the scribes and the Pharisees were
saying, Let us do the Passover in advance, lest in the tumult, he having been
seized, we be unable to eat the Passover; as also the most holy Matthew says
concerning the Savior, that he said to the disciples: "You know that after two
days the Passover takes place and the Son of Man is delivered up to be
crucified." And this was accomplished exactly when Christ was being led into
the mystery on the second of the week, the tenth being a symbol of the name of
our Lord Jesus through the iota. For then Judas received the price of the one
who was valued, whom they valued from the sons of Israel according to what is
written, and he was seeking an opportunity how he might betray him. And they
anticipated, being in tumult, instead of the lamb purchased for them through
the silver given to Judas being kept among them until the fifth of the week,
and counting from the tenth until the fourteenth, which was the fifth of the
week. And they anticipate, being troubled because of the people, and they say:
Let us do the Passover in advance, lest when the people come we be unable to
seize him. And for this reason they no longer ate the Passover on the fifth,
which fell on the 14th of the moon, but on the third of the week, as the
fourth was dawning, they seize Christ, on which they also, anticipating, were
celebrating the Passover.
206 He had therefore eaten the Passover with them toward evening on the
twelfth, two days before the Passover, as the 13th was dawning, the night
being reckoned toward the day, that is from the third to the fourth of the
week; whence the Church keeps the fourth day because of the Lord's arrest,
fasting throughout the whole year except only Pentecost and Epiphany.
And having been seized toward evening as the fourth was dawning, he remains in
the court of Caiaphas the whole night. For do not think that he was seized on
the fifth toward evening; read in the Gospel according to John and examine and
you see that when he is seized from Gethsemane he is brought to Caiaphas. And
he remained in the house of Caiaphas until dawn. And in the morning he is
delivered to Pontius Pilate; and this happened on the fourth of the week. And
he spends that day with Pontius Pilate and the night until morning. And on the
fifth of the week he is sent to Herod, who was then in Jerusalem, because he
too had come with the others to eat the Passover. And as that one, having
asked him many things and not having been deemed worthy of an answer, sends
him back to Pilate, and having remained until the ninth hour shut up in the
praetorium. And a certain tradition has run of those speaking even down to our
own times, that at the ninth hour the disciples entered secretly to him and he
broke plain bread and tasted it with them in the prison. And he remained there
the whole night and very early in the morning he brings him forth from the
praetorium on the day before the Sabbath, called Preparation, and thus at the
third hour he is crucified according to the exact account of Mark and of John,
the divine evangelists; even if in some copies of the Gospel according to
John, because of a scribal error, the letter Gamma has been altered, which
signifies the number three, into the episemon, which represents six, because
the strokes of both letters look from the left toward the right for us; which
also before us Clement and Origen and the Pamphilian Eusebius established
exactly. And the eager-to-learn hearers ought not, because of the removal or
insertion of the letter, to tear apart the harmony of the divine Gospels. For
see how also Matthew the most wise marks the time of the cross exactly by
saying "and coming to a place called Golgotha, which is Place of a Skull",
then also the wine with the myrrh next 207 and the dividing of the garments **
he adds: "and from the sixth hour darkness came over all the earth until the
ninth hour" and what follows. And some think that holy Matthew says he was
crucified at the sixth hour; but what has been treated by the saint is the
darkness from the sixth hour.
He is crucified then, as has been said, on the day before the Sabbath at the
third hour, and one day was reckoned to him from the third hour to . For after
being crucified he was thereafter working the salvation of those under the
earth, and from the sixth to the ninth one night, and from the ninth to
evening one day. ** And there have come to be three days and two nights. And
through the whole Sabbath one day and the night that dawns toward the Lord's
day. And what was said concerning him was fulfilled, that "the Son of Man must
spend three days and three nights in the heart of the earth." At the third
hour he was crucified complete on the sixth of the week, the fourteenth
according to the moon, the twenty-fourth of the month Phamenoth, thirteen days
before the Kalends of April. Wherefore we also fast on the day before the
Sabbath. And he rose on the day of the Lord, from when the cocks crowed, in
the night of the equal-night joined to the equinox, that is on the
twenty-first of the month Phamenoth, which was Nisan among the Hebrews,
interpreted Pharmuthi. And the months of the Hebrews anticipate the months of
the Egyptians, and those of the fortunate Athenians likewise and of the
Lacedaemonians, by eight days, if it so happen. And the night was the one
having the same hours, and the day that had passed the same.
