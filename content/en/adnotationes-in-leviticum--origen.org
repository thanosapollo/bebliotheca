ORIGEN'S SELECTIONS ON THE LEVITICUS.
=17.17= CH. 4. Verse 27. A soul if it sin involuntarily, he says, and not do
from all the commandments of the Lord, which one must do. And if it has
according to nature. There are perhaps some ordinances of the Lord, which one
must not do, according to the: They gave them ordinances not good. A soul
therefore sins involuntarily, when on a pretext of piety it keeps what is not
due. And it needs a sacrifice toward remission, the one doing it on purpose
not needing a sacrifice; as he who became a Jew, that he might gain Jews, and
having circumcised Timothy. Verse 28. It signifies therefore, that sin is
called also the very thing being offered. Thus one must understand the: Sins
of my people they shall eat. If therefore the one sinning seems to sacrifice
the sin itself in repenting, he ought not to fall into the same things, since
the sin lives again. Thus it is possible to call Christ sin, as having been
sacrificed for the sin of all. CH. 12. Verse 2. A woman whoever if she be
sown with seed, and bear a male. The, if she be sown with seed, being set
before the: and bear a male, will seem to be superfluous. But I attend lest
perhaps that prophetically Mary, not from having been so =17.20= wn with seed
bearing a male, might not be thought to be unclean, having generated the
Savior, he said this whole. And it was possible even without the, If she be
sown with seed, being set forth, to be understood that Mary was not unclean;
for she was not simply a woman, but a virgin. CH. 13. Verse 45. And the
leper, in whom is a touch, his gar ments shall be loosened, and his head
uncovered. Why does he command the leper's head to be uncovered, and the
garments loosened? That he may be recognizable, and those approaching may not
share in the uncleanness. Thus the Apostle concerning those sinning said:
Withdraw yourselves from every brother walking disorderly, and not according
to the tradition, which you received from us; and If any brother being named,
either a fornicator, or a covetous, or an idolater, or a drunkard, or a
reviler, or a robber; with such a one not to eat together. CH. 23. Verse 2.
These are the feasts of the Lord. Every cheerful day, on which it has been
commanded, as on a holy one to do these certain things exceptional beside the
rest, is called a feast. But not every is a panegyris; but according to the
name, when they are gathered together to the same from everywhere. So that
the panegyris is also a feast; but a feast is no longer also a panegyris; for
example: Sabbaths and New Moons are feasts; but Unleavened, and days of the
Weeks, and Tabernacles not only feasts, but also panegyreis. Verse 40. And
palm-fronds. It is possible through these to understand, that again to us in
Christ God assigns the delights in paradise. And the branches of the
ever-green plants signify on themselves the continuity of the grace and the
unfading of the hope. CH. 27. Verse 15. And if the one who sanctified it
redeem his house, he shall add upon it the fif th of the silver of the
price, and it shall be his. And if not the price of a house, or of a field
someone, but sanctify the things themselves to God, he shall put down the
price with the fifth redeeming them, and he shall hold the possessions; but
to others he shall not sell. But even if he sell, until the Jubilee the one
who bought shall hold, and after this it shall be of God. Verse 1 0. And if
from the field. And the one having bought a field from purchase, shall not
recover this for himself; but having given back the fruit to God according to
reason, in the Jubilee he shall restore the field to the one who sold.
Origen on Leviticus. And indeed the laws concerning leprosy I suppose
=17.21=.40 to describe certain different forms of the soul; for who would not
be at a loss seeing, that in one place the leprosy makes the man unclean
having come to be in living skin, and in another it releases from all
uncleanness the one who has become wholly leprous? for then someone is
reckoned to be unclean, when living skin after the wholly-leprous is found in
him, the living skin defiling the leprosy from all, which is said to be
undefiled; for I think the leprosy is taken instead of the irrational
passions of the soul; and the living skin, instead of the rational
condi =17.22=.40 tion; the word therefore seems to me to contain a mystery;
for in the age of infants the soul is blameless, the irrationality releasing
it from every blame; but if living skin appear in it, which is if beginnings
of the word sprout up, then a beginning of sin comes to be, until the living
skin having prevailed, that is the word increasing and being perfected, the
word become the living skin, and the leprosy be completely made to vanish.
