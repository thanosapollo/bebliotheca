* Letter to John Tornikes
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Letter of the most wise megas logothetes written to the much-beloved
co-father-in-law of our mighty and holy lord and emperor, the
all-most-fortunate sebastokrator kyr John Tornikes, who wrote to him
moderately and in a friendly way that "you sit falsely and eat the bread of
the emperor within the city of Constantine; for the service which you perform,
I myself am able to come and fulfill as well, that is, to interpret the
Organon for the boys and to carry through the affairs of the sekreton."
Heracles, suddenly the sebastokrator dearest to me is muse-possessed, revealed
as greatly possessed by Hermes; for his tongue has been whetted toward
eloquence, his voice has been polished toward melody, and he is wholly filled
with Attic graces. Has he then been fed on laurel from the Muses and thus
suddenly become most eloquent, as the men of old tell of him from Askra, or
having fallen asleep and woken found bees that had made a hive of his mouth,
as some have related concerning the Theban lyre? I at any rate, by the words,
am struck with amazement, and I have been filled with shuddering and wonder at
such great and marvelous things that have come about quicker than speech. For
I have believed clearly that you both are eloquent and full of philosophy, and
that there is a flood of words in you, and that you are able to teach those
who wish it, as you also made plain in the letter. For how could you fail to
speak the truth, being such as you are? But you have begrudged me the goods
and have sent an envious eye upon the things dear to the friend 68 and have
chosen to exchange lives, wishing in truth to trade bronze things for golden
ones. For you know that speech is a great thing and that its glory will never
perish and that all time will not be able to destroy the fame of the
teachings. You looked then to the greatness of the matter, and you aimed at
the height that stands aether-walking, and for these reasons, setting the
friendly bond at nothing, you have chosen to take these things from the
beloved one, and to put upon yourself the fairest and most precious, and to
give to him your own things, reckoned much inferior to these; and that you
should enjoy the glory from words and the honor, while he should receive from
you some low and earth-bound things. For what, compared to these great and
intellectual contemplations, raised above matter and the compound, are
dark-blue skins and the fortunate coloring on the outside, even if it is
written with intensity? I then, even with a heart that is hard to bear and
bitten by grief, receive with heavy pain the friend's request. For what could
one suffer when a friend uses force? But to exchange with the lives also the
fortunes is among the necessary things; for you have written not that we
change places but lives, and fortune follows life and life is present with
fortune. Already then I myself have fulfilled the things of friendship; for I
know the friend himself as another in the unfolding of nature. But (what
escaped me of the letter) of those things which the friend mentioned as first
and more precious (I shall speak according to him) and sweeter and gentler, I
myself have made no mention at all, I mean the pleasant things of
Constantine's city, those described with elaboration in this letter, even if
the things of the depiction did not come to completion for him where he was
discoursing about luxuries and clear waters and well-tempered airs and all the
other delights. For he has omitted things much better than these, the beauties
and magnitudes of the buildings in the churches and dwellings. What do you
say? Were you not delighted in sight from the surface of the sea, not the
baths that cleanse and furnish much pleasure? Or do you also reckon as nothing
the many-formed tribes of 69 fishes and the various fruits and the
flower-scented wines, in which we who indulge the belly luxuriate even more,
of which Constantine's city has partaken superabundantly? But, my good man,
some things you said, and some you did not say. And that you reckon such
things among us, but that certain other things much better are with you, you
do not consider in any way at all. And that you happen to be almost by the
cosmic sun and are filled immediately with its rays and take of its sparkles
to satiety, so that you both are and are named sun-like among all, while we
have become Cimmerians outright, pressed by cold and gloom, you do not set
down at all. And that you have been stationed by the greatest spring of
paradise, from which the four rivers of the virtues gush forth, and you enjoy
the life-flowing streams and have no suffering of thirst at all, while we are
distressed being deprived of the waters leaping from it, you reckon as
nothing. Exchange your things for mine, for I already agree, and I will come
to Ares as you have said, swelling, even if I fear the fickleness of battle;
but the teacher's seat is prepared for you, and the chair of judgments has
been made ready for you. And teach those who come to you for philosophic
education the true things, and by justice straighten the ordinances. Let then
the exchange of lives come about for us by the will of the one who rules both;
for neither do you happen to be fulfilling the things of the sebastokrator by
your own will, nor I toward those things to which I have been assigned. I
then, as you see, have fulfilled the things of friendship; it would be yours
from this point that your request be put into action.
