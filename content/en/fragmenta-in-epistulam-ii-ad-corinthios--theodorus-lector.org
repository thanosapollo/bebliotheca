196 2 Cor 1:3 It is wise to begin from thanksgiving and not narratively of the
things that happened to him, so as not to seem to think highly of himself on
account of them; and he did not even begin from the affliction but from the
consolation, so that what was set before him appears to be the thanksgiving
for the consolation, while by the sequence of the discourse he seems to have
been carried into the account concerning the afflictions. Blessed be God:
here one must place a slight stop, so that it is by ellipsis; for "our" is
understood, instead of "blessed be our God." 2 Cor 1:4 The word "of our
consolation" is bare, but having already anticipated the work; for having been
tried by the consolation from God we have learned from the things we have
suffered to console. 2 Cor 1:6 Instead of: hoping true things concerning you
we choose to suffer. 197 2 Cor 1:12 He said this, because those of the
circumcision were slandering him, saying that he himself also keeps the law,
even if he discourses otherwise to you as being from the Gentiles. 2 Cor 1:15
Most shaming is the "in this also," as no longer able to be confident in the
same way because they have changed, and are not the same toward him. But from
the things said it is clear that he preserved a great affection toward them.
2 Cor 1, Amen, he says, is a voice of assent upon thanksgiving that comes to
be toward God, as he himself says in the former: how shall he who fills the
place of the unlearned say the Amen upon your thanksgiving? 2 Cor 1:24
According to the construction the particle men is lacking, so that it may be:
for by faith indeed you stand. And he says this: for the sake of the faith I
by no means blame you; for you wear the health of it; but certain other things
are being done amiss by you, which need some correction.
2 Cor 2:13 He calls relief of the spirit the being able for something suitable
to the gift of the Spirit to come to be, and these things were the benefit of
others; for it is not possible to suppose that Paul postponed the teaching
because of a human affliction, having found those able to receive the gospel
preaching.
2 Cor 3:7-11 He did not say the ministry of the law, since the name of the law
had a certain credibility of itself, but that of death, speaking of it rather
from the result so as to diminish the impression concerning it. And well also
"being done away" toward increase of the matter, instead of "even these things
being done away." And well also "shall be," since from the things to come the
greatness of those according to Christ is confirmed. For if that which is
being done away was through glory, much more that which remains in glory. Well
of that one he said "through glory," since besides no other sharing in the
like things, Moses himself also 198 became such for a little; but here "in
glory," inasmuch as both Christ himself and those sharing the glory will
always be in the same things. 2 Cor 3:17 If he wished to call the Lord spirit,
he would have said spirit simply, not the Spirit with the addition of the
article. So that God is spirit, even if this is most naive to say. For it was
not, as in those things when discoursing with the Samaritan woman who thought
the divine to be in a place, that he necessarily said it was spirit,
incorporeal, so also here it lay before the apostle to show that the Lord is
incorporeal; for his aim in these things is clear. But since he did not say
spirit but the Spirit with the article, it is clear that he does not call the
Lord spirit but the Spirit Lord. For one could not show through the whole New
Testament the Father or the Son being signified anywhere by the term of
spirit, since even when he says God is spirit, he does not signify him by the
name of spirit, but he names him God; and he says he is spirit not having set
it for signification according to the naming, as God and Lord and Father and
Son and as many such things lie upon the divine Scripture, but for teaching
that he is not circumscribed in a place.
2 Cor 4:7 The "that" is not said of a cause, but customarily of what follows.
2 Cor 4:14 Well the "knowing," as since the resurrection of Christ is
confessed among them, to which it altogether followed that they believe the
like things also upon the rest. And he joined also "with you," so that it
might not be thought that he says the matter to be his own alone.
2 Cor 5:9 He has set "we make it our ambition," showing much eagerness which
he wishes us to display concerning this. 2 Cor 5:11 From "knowing therefore
the fear of the Lord" he inserted, taking hold of them, and then he has given
back the things following to what was set forth 199; for we are constrained,
he says, by the love concerning Christ to do all things according to what
seems good to him.
2 Cor 6:6 That he may say: being eager to be long-suffering both toward the
unknown and toward the known; for by pureness, not chastity but ignorance he
says.
2 Cor 7:8 I am led, he says, to write concerning all things even those of
which I make no account, drawing you into a greater affection by the weight of
the words.
2 Cor 8:23 And the "whether" and "whether" here he has not set for the
exactness of some division but customarily, showing much indifference upon the
phrasing.
2 Cor 10:3-4 He calls flesh everything temporary and easily dissolved. Since
then the commandments of the conduct according to law can fit mortals only
according to the present life (for in the age to come, when we are all going
to be both incorruptible and immortal, no keeping of these legal things has an
entrance), he says to walk according to flesh is to live according to law. And
again he says: for even if we are still mortal, yet we do not wage a mortal
campaign. 2 Cor 10:5-6 Instead of: upon your obedience being fulfilled; for he
wishes to say that we punish even the disobedient, whether of the strangers or
even of our own, by the energies of the Spirit, upon the obedience owed to
Christ being fulfilled through all of you who believe.
2 Cor 11:29 He calls those who are weak those who sin, as for example the one
who sinned among the Corinthians; and those who are caused to stumble those
who are being turned aside in the very word of piety. 0
2 Cor 12:1-4 Since the things that came to be are shown to be of God, he says:
on behalf of such a one I will boast. But if I should be compelled to boast on
behalf of myself, I will put forward the weaknesses only. And "he heard" he
said as from the revelation, as having received the knowledge of them in the
manner of hearing, such as is that of the prophet: who has believed our
hearing? saying hearing of the revelation through which, as through a kind of
hearing, he was receiving the knowledge of the things belonging. And he says
the words are things; for it is likely that he saw the beauty of paradise, and
the choruses of the saints in it, and the all-harmonious voice of the hymnody.
2 Cor 13:11 These things he says not as counseling but praying for them,
instead of: may it be that you rejoice, advance, and the rest.
