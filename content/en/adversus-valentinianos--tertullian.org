* Chapter 1
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

The Valentinians, plainly the most crowded college among the heretics,
because it is mostly from apostates of the truth and easy toward fables
and is not frightened by discipline, care for nothing more than to hide
what they preach; if indeed they preach who hide. The office of custody
is an office of a guilty conscience. Confusion is preached while
religion is asserted. For those Eleusinia too, themselves a heresy of
Attic superstition, what they keep silent is a shame. Therefore they first
torment the approach, they initiate longer before they seal, when they
train the epopts for five years, so that they may build opinion by a
hanging of knowledge, and so seem to display as great a majesty as the
craving they have previously constructed. Then follows the office of
silence. What is found late is guarded closely. But all the divinity in
the inner shrines, all the sighs of the epopts, the whole seal of the
tongue, an image of the male member is revealed. Yet an allegorical
arrangement, putting forward the venerable name of nature, obscures the
sacrilege under the patronage of a forced figure, and excuses the
reproach of falsehood by images. Accordingly the heretics we now mark
out, shaping the vainest and foulest fictions with holy names and titles
and arguments of the true religion for easy affection from the occasion
of divine plenty, because from many things many can succeed, the
Valentinians have made Eleusinian panderings, holy by a great silence,
heavenly by taciturnity alone. If you ask in good faith, with set face
and raised brow they say, It is deep. If you try them subtly, through
bilingual ambiguities they affirm a common faith. If you half-show that
you know, they deny whatever they acknowledge. If you fight at close
quarters, they scatter your simplicity by their own slaughter. Not even
to their own disciples do they commit it before they have made them
their own. They have a craft by which they first persuade before they
teach. But truth persuades by teaching; it does not teach by persuading.

* Chapter 2
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h2
:END:

And therefore we are marked among them as simple, as this only, not also
wise; as if wisdom were at once forced to fail from simplicity, though
the Lord joins both: Be prudent as serpents, and simple as doves. Or if
we are foolish because we are simple, are they then not simple because
they are wise? But they are most harmful who are not simple, as they are
most stupid who are not wise. And yet I would rather take that part with
the better vice. If perhaps it is better to be less wise than worse, to
err than to deceive. Further, the face of God is awaited in simplicity
of seeking, as Wisdom herself teaches, not indeed Valentinus's, but
Solomon's. Then infants have sealed a testimony of Christ with blood.
Were they boys, that voice that shouts the cross? They were neither boys
nor infants, that is, they were not simple. The apostle too bids us
become children again according to God, so that we may be infants in
malice through simplicity, and only then wise in the senses; at the same
time I have granted an order of wisdom flowing from simplicity. In sum,
a dove has been accustomed to show Christ, but a serpent to tempt. The
one even from the beginning is the herald of divine peace; the other
from the beginning the robber of the divine image. So simplicity alone
will more easily be able both to know God and to show him; prudence
alone rather to shake and betray him.

* Chapter 3
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3
:END:

Let the serpent therefore hide himself as much as he can, and twist all
his prudence in the windings of hiding-places; let him dwell deep, be
thrust into the dark, unroll his series through bends, proceed
tortuously, and not all at once, a light-fleeing beast. Our dove's house
too is simple, always in high and open places and toward the light. The
figure of the Holy Spirit loves the east, the figure of Christ. Truth
blushes at nothing except only to be hidden; because it will not shame
anyone to give ears to it, to recognize that God whom nature has already
committed to him, whom he feels daily in all works, known less by this
alone, that he did not think him unique, that he named him in a number,
that he adored him in others. Otherwise, to urge another crowd from
their throng, to move from a household principate to an unknown one, to
twist from the manifest to the hidden, is to strike faith from the
threshold. Already if he is also initiated into the whole fable, will
not something of this sort be granted, that in infancy, among the
difficulties of sleep, you heard from a nurse the towers of Lamia and
the combs of the Sun? But he who has come to faith from another
conscience, if he at once finds so many names of Aeons, so many
marriages, so many births, so many exits, so many events, felicities,
infelicities of a scattered and chopped divinity, will he not doubt
there to pronounce that these are the fables and endless genealogies
which the apostle's Spirit, with these heretical seeds already then
sprouting, anticipated to condemn? Rightly therefore they are not
simple, rightly only prudent, who neither easily produce such things nor
openly defend them, and do not even thoroughly teach all whom they
teach; shrewdly of course, as if they were shameful, but inhumanly, if
they are honorable. And yet we simple ones know all. In short we have
armed this first wedge of the engagement, the detector and pointer of
their whole conscience, and we inaugurate this first victory, because
what is hidden at so great a cost, even only to show is to destroy.

* Chapter 4
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4
:END:

We know, I say, their origin itself very well, and we know why we call
them Valentinians, though they may not seem to be so. For they have
broken away from the founder, but the origin is by no means deleted, and
if perhaps it is changed, the change itself is a testimony. Valentinus
had hoped for a bishopric, because he was able both in talent and in
speech. But indignant that another had obtained the place from the
prerogative of martyrdom, he broke off from the church of the authentic
rule. As minds stirred for precedence are accustomed to be kindled by
the presumption of revenge, he turned to storming the truth and, having
found the path of a certain old opinion, he traced out a road with
snaky craft. Ptolemy afterward entered it, with the names and numbers
of the Aeons distinguished into personal substances, but determined
outside God, which Valentinus had enclosed in the very summit of
divinity as senses and affections and motions. Heracleon also led off
certain tracks from there, and Secundus, and Marcus the magician.
Theotimus has worked much around the images of the law. So Valentinus
is nowhere now, and yet there are Valentinians, who are through
Valentinus. Only Axionicus at Antioch to this day consoles the memory of
Valentinus by an intact custody of his rules. Otherwise as much has
been allowed this heresy to persuade itself as it is a daily custom for
a she-wolf to fit a woman's form. Why not, when they so recast that
spiritual seed of theirs in each one? If they have added anything new,
they at once call presumption a revelation, and talent a charisma, and
not unity, but diversity. And therefore we observe that, their solemn
dissimulation set aside, most of them are divided, even about to say in
good faith on certain articles, This is not so, and, I take this
otherwise, and, I do not acknowledge this. For by variety the face of
the rules is renewed. It has even the colors of ignorances.

* Chapter 5
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5
:END:

But my bound will be with the archetypes of the principal masters, not
with the affected leaders of promiscuous disciples. Nor shall we be said
from every side to have invented for ourselves the materials which so
many men already, distinguished by holiness and excellence, not only our
predecessors but contemporaries of the heresiarchs themselves, have both
published and beaten back in most instructed volumes, as Justin, the
philosopher and martyr, as Miltiades, the sophist of the churches, as
Irenaeus, the most careful explorer of all doctrines, as our Proculus,
the dignity of a virgin old age and of Christian eloquence, whom in
every work of faith, as in this, I would wish to follow. Or if s(??)in
they are not heresies at all, so that those who drive them out are
believed to have invented them, the apostle, the preacher of them, will
lie. Further, if they are, they will be none other than those which are
being retraced. No one is reported so idle with the pen that, having
material, he invents.

* Chapter 6
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h6
:END:

Therefore in this little book, in which we promise only a demonstration
of that secret, lest a fog pour over anyone from names so foreign and
forced and compacted and ambiguous, I will first charge how we are going
to use them. For the interpretation of some from the Greek does not
occur to a form of the name equally ready, for some not even the genders
do sexu agree, for some the acquaintance is more usual in the Greek. So
we shall put Greek for the most part; significances will be present
along the limits of the pages, nor will Greek be lacking even to the
Latin, but they will be noted in lines above, so that this may be a sign
of personal names because of the ambiguities of those which they share
with another meaning. Although I have deferred the engagement, having
professed only a narrative for the meantime, yet wherever unworthiness
has deserved to be branded, there will altogether be an assault by a
passing taste. Count it, reader, a prelude of the engagement before the
fight. I will show wounds, but I will not press them. If there will also
be laughter somewhere, it will satisfy the materials themselves. Many
things are worthy of being refuted in this way, lest they be adored by
gravity. Festivity properly yields to vanity. It also befits truth to
laugh, because it is glad, to play with its rivals, because it is
secure. Care must plainly be taken lest its laughter be laughed at if it
has been unworthy; but wherever laughter is worthy, it is a duty. In
short I will begin in this way.

* Chapter 7
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h7
:END:

First of all Ennius, the Roman poet, simply pronounced the greatest
dining-rooms of heaven, by the name of an elevated site, or because he
had read in Homer that Jupiter was feasting there. But it is a wonder
how many supernities of supernities, and how many sublimities of
sublimities, the heretics have hung, raised, expanded into a habitation
of each one's god. Even for our Creator Ennian dining-rooms have been
arranged in the form of little buildings, with one balcony after another
built on top, and as many stairs distributed to each god as there have
been heresies. The world has been made a lodging for hire. You would
think Insula Felicula so many stories of heavens, I know not where.
There even the god of the Valentinians lives at the topmost tiles. Him
they call in substance Αἰῶνα Τέλειον, but in person Προαρχήν and Τὴν
Ἀρχήν, also Bythos, which least suited one dwelling in the heights. They
define him unborn, immense, infinite, invisible, and eternal, as if they
at once prove that he is, if they define him such as we know he ought to
be, so that he is thus said to have been before all things. But I demand
that he be, and I note nothing more in this sort than that those who are
said to have been before all things are found after all things, and
indeed not by their own. Let that Bythos then, through infinite ages
behind, be in the greatest and highest quiet, in a very great leisure of
a calm and, so to speak, stupefied divinity, such as Epicurus ordered.
And yet, whom they want to be alone, they give him a second person in
himself and with himself, Ennoea, whom they name besides Charis and
Sige. And perhaps they come in that most commended quiet to advise him
about at last putting forth the beginning of things from himself. This
he places in the manner of seed in the genital places of the womb, as it
were, of his Sige. She receives it at once and is made pregnant and
bears, in silence of course, Sige, and whom she bears is Nus, most like
the father and equal in all things. In short he alone su(??)ffices to
grasp that immense and incomprehensible magnitude of the father. So he
too is called father and beginning of all and properly Monogenes. And
yet not properly, since he is not born alone. For with him there also
came forth a female, whose name is Truth. Monogenes, because first
begotten: how much more fitly would he be called Protogenes? Therefore
Bythos and Sige, Nus and Truth are defended as the first four-horse team
of the Valentinian faction, the matrix and origin of all. For there Nus,
as soon as he has received the office of his own bringing-forth, himself
also emits from himself Word and Life, which if it was not before, was
not in Bythos either. And what sort of thing is it that there was not
life in God? But this offspring too, sent forth for the beginning of the
universe and the formation of the whole Pleroma, makes fruit, begets Man
and Church. You have an Ogdoad, a double Tetrad, from the conjugations
of males and females, the cells, so to speak, of the primordial Aeons,
the fraternal marriages of the Valentinian gods, all the censuses of
heretical holiness and majesty, a crowd I know not whether of crimes or
of divinities, certainly the fountain of the remaining fecundity.

* Chapter 8
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h8
:END:

For look, the second Tetrad, Word and Life, Man and Church, because it
had branched for the father's glory, themselves also eager to offer
something of this sort to the father from their own, bubble forth other
offspring, likewise conjugal, through a coupling of either nature. On
this side Word and Life pour forth a decad of Aeons together, on that
side Man and Church two more, matching their parents, because they
themselves also, two with those ten, make as many as they themselves
have begotten. I now give back the names of those I called a decad:
Bythios and Mixis, Ageratos and Henosis, Autophyes and Hedone, Acinetos
and Syncrasis, Monogenes and Macaria. Against them this will be the
number of twelve: Paracletus and Pistis, Patricos and Elpis, Metricos
and Agape, Ainus and Synesis, Ecclesiasticus and Macariotes, Theletus
and Sophia. I am forced here to bring out what those names want from a
like example. In the schools of Carthage there was a certain most frigid
Latin rhetor, Phosphorus by name. When he was pleading a brave man, I
come, he said, to you, best citizens, from the battle with my victory,
with your felicity, enlarged, glorious, fortunate, greatest,
triumphal. And the schoolboys of Phosphorus's family at once shout Φεῦ.
Have you heard Fortunata and Hedone and Acinetus and Theletus? Shout Φεῦ
to Ptolemy's family. This will be that secret Pleroma, the fullness of a
thirtyfold divinity. Let us see what the privileges of those numbers
are, of the four and the eight and the twelve. Meanwhile in the thirty
the whole fecundity fails; the force and power and genital lust of the
Aeons has been castrated. As if so great clots of numbers were not still
left, and no other names from the schoolroom. Why then are not fifty and
a hundred also begotten? Why are not also companions and messmates
named?

* Chapter 9
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h9
:END:

But this too is an exception of persons, that Nus alone of all enjoys
the notion of the immense father, rejoicing and exulting, while those of
course are mourning. Plainly Nus, as far as was in him, had both wished
and attempted to communicate to the rest also what he knew, how great
and how incomprehensible the father was; but mother Sige intervened,
she of course who prescribes silence even to her own heretics; although
they say it was done by the father's nod, who wished all to be kindled
into desire of himself. And so while they are wasted within themselves,
while they burn with a silent desire of knowing the father, a crime was
almost done. For from those twelve Aeons whom Man and Church had brought
forth, the Aeon last by birth (let the solecism see to itself, for the
name is Sophia) by her own incontinence, without partnership of her
spouse Theletus, bursts forth to inquire into the father, and contracts
a kind of vice which had indeed begun in those others who were around
Nus, but had derived into this one, that is into Sophia, as vices born
in one place in the body are accustomed to blow their ruin into another
member. But under the pretext of love toward the father, rivalry was
prevailing against Nus, who alone rejoiced about the father. But when
Sophia, striving after impossibles, had been frustrated, and is
conquered by the difficulty and stretched by affection, she was a little
short of being swallowed by the force of sweetness and labor and
dissolved into the remaining substance; nor would she have ceased
otherwise than by perishing, had she not by good fortune run upon Horos.
There is a certain force to this one too. He is the foundation of that
universe and the outer guardian, whom they also call Cross and Lytrotes
and Carpistes. So Sophia, exempted from danger and late persuaded,
having declined the investigation of the father, grew quiet, and put
forth her whole animation, Enthymesis, with the passion which had
besides befallen.

* Chapter 10
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h10
:END:

But some have dreamed the exit and restoration of Sophia otherwise.
After vain attempts and a casting down of hope she was deformed by
pallor, I believe, and leanness and neglect of form, as one who grieved
the father no less denied than lost. Then in that sorrow from herself
alone, with no work of marriage, she conceived and begets a female. Do
you wonder at this? Even a hen has been allotted to bear from her own;
but they say vultures too bear only females. And yet a mother without a
male, and at last to fear lest an end also should press, and to stick
over the reason of the mishap, to care about hiding. Remedies nowhere.
For where now are the tragedies and comedies, from which a form might be
borrowed of exposing what had been born aside from shame? While the
matter is in evils, she looks up, she turns to the father. But having
striven in vain, as her strength was deserting, she sank into prayers.
The whole neighborhood also supplicates for her, Nus most of all. Why
not? The cause of so great an evil? Yet no exit of Sophia was empty. All
her distresses work. Since even that struggle then arrived at the origin
of Matter. Ignorance, fear, sorrow flow into substances. There at last
the father, sometime moved, puts forth through Monogenes Nus into these
things Horos, whom we mentioned above, in his own image, female-male,
because they so vary about the father's sex. They add however that Horos
is also called Metagogeus, that is a circumducer, and Horothetes. They
preach his works, and Sophia repressed from the illicit and purged from
evils and thereafter confirmed and restored to marriage; and that she
herself remained in the census of the Pleroma, but that her Enthymesis
and that appendage Passion were relegated by Horos and crucified and
made outside him (Evil, as they say, outside!), yet that spiritual
substance, as a certain natural impulse of an Aeon, but unformed and
unshaped, inasmuch as she had apprehended nothing, and therefore
pronounced a weak fruit and a female.

* Chapter 11
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h11
:END:

Therefore after Enthymesis an exile and her mother Sophia returned to
her spouse, that Monogenes again, that Nus, plainly idle about the
father's care and foresight, for solidifying things and now fortifying
and fixing the Pleroma, lest any shock of this kind should strike again,
hatches out a new coupling, Christ and the Holy Spirit. I would think it
most foul of two males. Or the Holy Spirit will be a female, and the
male is wounded by a female. One divinity is given in these, to procure
the concinnation of the Aeons. And from the partnership of that office
at once two schools, two chairs, a certain inauguration of Valentinus's
doctrine to be divided. It was Christ's to introduce to the Aeons the
nature of the marriages (you see what a thing, plainly) and a conjecture
of the unborn, and to make them fit for generating in themselves the
acknowledgment of the father, that it is not possible to grasp him nor
to comprehend him, not finally to be made partaker of him by sight or by
hearing, except through Monogenes. And yet I will tolerate that they so
discourse of knowing the father, lest that too be ours. I will note more
the perversity of the doctrine, that they were taught that the
incomprehensible of the father is the cause of their own perpetuity, but
that the comprehensible of him is the reason of their generation and
formation. For by this arrangement that, I suppose, is insinuated, that
it is expedient that God not be apprehended, since his inapprehensible
is the cause of perpetuity, but the apprehensible is not of perpetuity,
but of nativity and formation, which need perpetuity. But they
constitute the Son the apprehensible of the father. How however he is
apprehended, Christ, then brought forth, taught. But the proper things
of the Holy Spirit, that, all equalized from zeal of doctrine, they
might know how to follow a thanksgiving, and be led into a true quiet.

* Chapter 12
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h12
:END:

And so all are equalized in form and knowledge, all made what each one
is; no one another thing, because all are to another. They are poured
back into Nus all, into Men, into Theleti, equally the females into
Siges, into Zoas, into Churches, into Fortunatas, so that Ovid would
have deleted his Metamorphoses if he had known a greater today. From
then they have been refreshed, and they have been established, and
composed into rest from the truth they sing the father together with a
great fruit of joy. He himself too was poured out with gladness, of
course to sons and grandsons singing well. Why should he not be poured
out with every jocundity, the Pleroma set free? What shipmaster is not
glad even with disgrace? We see daily the wantonnesses of sailors'
joys. And so as sailors always exult at a contribution, something of
that sort the Aeons too, all now one even in form, not to say in
opinion; those new brothers and masters also agreeing, Christ and the
Holy Spirit, they bring into the midst what each one was flowering as
best and most beautiful. Vainly, I suppose. For if they were all one,
from the equalization aforesaid the reason of a contribution was vacant,
which generally consists of the grace of variety. They were contributing
one good of all, because they were all. Perhaps there will have been a
reason from measure, or from the form of the equalization itself
already. Therefore from a collected bronze, as they say, they compact in
honor and glory of the father the most beautiful star of the Pleroma and
perfect fruit, Jesus. They surname him Soter and Christ and Word from
the paternal things, and now All Things, as constructed from a
deflowering of all, Aesop's jackdaw, Hesiod's Pandora, Accius's stew,
Nestor's mixture, Ptolemy's miscellany. How much nearer it was, from
some Attic cares, to be called Pancarpia by authors of names so idle.
But that they might also adorn so great a figurine from outside, they
bring forth for him satellite angels, a like kind; if among themselves,
it can happen, but if consubstantial with Soter (for I have found it put
ambiguously), what will his eminence be among equal satellites?

* Chapter 13
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h13
:END:

This order then contains the first procession equally of Aeons being
born and marrying and generating: Sophia's most dangerous fall from
desire of the father, Horos's most opportune help, the expiation of
Enthymesis and the joined Passion, the schooling of Christ and the Holy
Spirit, the reformed tutelary of the Aeons, Soter's peacock adornment,
the matched presidency of the Angels. What remains, you say, Farewell
and applaud! Nay, what remains, I say, you hear and profit. But these
things are said to have run through within the assembly of the Pleroma,
the first scene of the tragedy. Another buskin-play however is beyond
the curtain, outside the Pleroma I mean. And yet if such under the
father's bosom within the circuit of Horos the guardian, of what sort
already outside in the free, where God was not?

For Enthymesis, or now Achamoth, written from here by this untranslatable
name alone, when she was exploded with the vice of undivided Passion
into places alien to light, which is a thing of the Pleroma, into that
void and inane of Epicurus, is pitiable even from the place. Certainly
neither form nor any face, an untimely and abortive birth of course.
While she so has of things, Christ is bent from the upper ones, is led
down through Horos, that he may form that abortion from his own powers,
the form of substance alone, not also of knowledge. And yet she is left
with some peculium. The odor of incorruptibility is repeated, by which,
made capable of her mishap, she might be overcome by desire of better
things. Having performed this mercy, not without partnership of the Holy
Spirit, Christ runs back into the Pleroma. It has been the use of things
that names too accede from liberalities. Enthymesis was from the act,
Achamoth whence is still asked, Sophia flows from the father, the Holy
Spirit from an angel. She receives a desire of Christ, by whom she had
at once felt herself abandoned. And so she herself also sprang forth to
seek his light; whom if she had not known at all, as having worked
invisibly, how was she seeking his unknown light with him? And yet she
tried, and perhaps would have apprehended, if the same Horos, who had
come so prosperously to her mother, had not now so inopportunely met the
daughter that he even cried out upon her, Iao! as if, Further, Quirites!
or, The faith of Caesar! From there Iao is found in the scriptures. So
driven off from going on, and not having to fly over the cross, that is
Horos, because she will not have been practiced in any Laureolus of
Catullus, as destitute she began to be afflicted upon that Passion of
hers in a manifold and entangled tangle, with every kind of it, to
mourn, because she had not accomplished the attempt, with fear, lest as
of light so also of life she be bereaved, with consternation, then
ignorance. Nor as her mother. For she was an Aeon. But this one by
condition worse, with still another wave also rising, of conversion of
course toward Christ, by whom she had been vivified, and tempered into
this very conversion.

* Chapter 15
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h14
:END:

Come now, let the Pythagoreans learn, let the Stoics acknowledge, Plato
himself, whence matter, which they want unborn, has drawn both origin
and substance into all this pile of the world; which not even that
Mercury Trismegistus, master of all physicists, thought out. You have
heard conversion, another kind of passion. From this all the soul of
this world is said to have consisted, even that of the Demiurge himself,
that is of our God. You have heard sorrow and fear. From these the rest
were begun. For from her tears the whole nature of waters flowed. From
this it is to be estimated what exit she led, with how many kinds of
tears she inundated. She had salt ones too, she had bitter and sweet and
hot and cold drops and bituminous and iron-staining and sulphurous, and
poisoned of course, so that Nonacris also sweated from there, which
killed Alexander, and the water of the Lyncestae flowed from there,
which makes men drunk, and Salmacis loosed itself from there, which
softens males. Achamoth piped celestial rains, and we take care to keep
even others' griefs and tears in cisterns. Likewise from consternation
and panic the bodily elements were drawn. And yet in so great a
circumstance of solitude, in so great a looking-around of destitution,
she was sometimes laughing, remembering Christ as seen; from that laugh
of joy light flashed out. Of whose providence is this benefit, of what
sort was it that forced her to laugh, for this reason, lest we should
always linger in darkness! Nor should you be stunned who by her
gladness radiated so splendid an element upon the world, when her
sadness also flowed so necessary an instrument upon the age. O
illuminating laugh! o watering weep! And yet she could already deal by
remedy with the horror of that place, for she would have shaken off all
its obscurity as often as she had wished to laugh, or lest she be forced
to supplicate her deserters.

* Chapter 16
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h15
:END:

For she herself also turns to prayers after the mother's manner. But
Christ, whom it already irked to set out outside the Pleroma, sets a
vicar in charge, the Paraclete. He sends Soter to her (this will be
Jesus, the father of all the Aeons having bestowed on him the highest
power, with all to be subjected to them, so that in him according to the
apostle all things might be established) with the office and company of
coeval Angels, you would believe even with twelve fasces. There at last
Achamoth, shaken by his pompous advent, at once drew a veil over herself
from the first office of veneration and modesty; then she contemplates
him and the fruit-bearing display. With the powers from which she had
there conceived she meets him, Κύριε Χαῖρε. Here, I suppose, he
confirms her as received and forms her by acknowledgment already and
cleanses her from all the injuries of passion, not with the same
negligence, the things which had happened in the mother's mishaps being
separated into extermination. But he confused the vices exercised and
vicious by use; and so, solidified in a mass, he fixed them apart into
the corporal preparation of matter, changing from the incorporeal
passion an implanted aptitude and nature, by which it could soon arrive
at rival equalities of corpulences, so that a double condition of
substances might be ordered, from the vices the worst, from conversion
the passional. This will be the matter which has committed us with
Hermogenes and the rest who presume that God has worked all things from
matter, not from nothing.

From here Achamoth, at last expedited from all evils, look, now
advances and grows fruitful into greater works. For by joy of so great a
success from unhappiness, warmed and at the same time by the
contemplation itself of the angelic lights, so to speak, under-fermented

intimate to herself. She bore at last, and from then a trinity of kinds
was made from a trinity of causes, one material, which is from passion,
another animal, which is from conversion, a third spiritual, which is
from imagination.

By this authority of three children of course, made more practiced for
doing things, she resolved to form the several kinds. But the spiritual
she could not so touch, as being herself also spiritual. For the
fellowship of nature denied that likes and consubstantials have force
upon one another. With that mind she turned herself only to the animal,
the disciplines of Soter having been put forth. And first, which is to
be pronounced and read and heard with a great horror of blasphemy, she
fashions this God of ours and of all, except of the heretics, the father
and Demiurge and king of the universes which are after him. For from
him; if yet from him, and not rather from Achamoth herself, by whom,
hidden, sensing nothing of her, and as if by an outward figurine-drawing
he was moved into every operation. In short from this ambiguity of
persons in the works they have mixed for him the name of Metropator,
with the remaining appellations distinguished according to the states
and sites of the works, so that of the animal substances, which they
commend to the right, they may name him Father, but of the materials,
which they delegate to the left, they may name him Demiurge, but King
commonly upon the universe.

But not even the propriety of the names fits the propriety of the works,
from which all the names are, when she ought to be called all these
things, by whom the things were being done, except that already not even
by her. For when they say that Achamoth devised images in honor of the

Archangels, the work of the Demiurge, would express the remaining Aeons.
When I hear so many images of three, I ask, do you not now want me to
laugh at the images of their most perverse painter? a female Achamoth,
the image of the father? and a Demiurge ignorant of the mother, much
more of the father? the image of a Nus ignorant of the father, and
servant Angels, likenesses of lords? This is to paint a mule from an
ass, and to describe Ptolemy from Valentinus.

Therefore the Demiurge, set outside the limits of the Pleroma in the
ignominious waste of an eternal exile, founded a new province, this
world, the confusion having been cleaned and the diversity of that
double disjoined substance distinguished, of animals and of materials.
From incorporeals he builds bodies, heavy, light, sublimating and
verging, heavenly and earthly. Then he bounds the sevenfold scene of the
heavens itself with his throne from above. Whence he is also called
Sabbath from the hebdomad of his seat, and mother Achamoth Ogdoad from
the argument of the first-born Ogdoad. But they count the heavens
noëros, and sometimes they make them angels, as also the Demiurge
himself, as also Paradise a fourth Archangel, since they also fix this
one above the third heaven, from whose virtue Adam took, having lodged
there among little clouds and little trees. Ptolemy had remembered
childish fables enough, that apples are born in the sea and fishes in a
tree; so he presumed nut-groves in the celestials too. The Demiurge
works ignorant, and therefore perhaps he does not know that trees ought
to be set only on earth. Plainly the mother knew. Why should she not
suggest, who was also supplying her own effect? But while raising so
great a summit for her son through those works which testify him both
father and god and king before the talents of the Valentinians, why she
did not want these things to be known to herself as well, I will ask
afterward.

Meanwhile it is to be held that Sophia is also surnamed Earth and

a beard, not to say the rest. Otherwise the Demiurge was so little in

of the made was whoever. How then did he seem to himself alone, if not
certain, at least suspect of some maker?

The infamy among them is more tolerable toward the devil, even because a
sordid origin takes him. For he is counted from the wickedness of that
sorrow, from which they note the births of angels and demons and of all
spiritual malignities. And yet they also affirm the devil a work of the

as animal. He deserves from them the preference for whom all heresies
are procured.

But they place the citadels of the several powers within these bounds.
In the highest summits the thirtyfold Pleroma presides, Horos marking
the extreme line. Below that Achamoth measures a mid-region, treading
her son. For the Demiurge is underneath in his Hebdomad, more the devil,
in this world common with us, coëlemented and concorporified, as was set
out above, from Sophia's most useful mishaps, whereby it would not even
have air, the space of reciprocating spirit, the tender garment of all
bodies, the index of all colors, the organ of times, if Sophia's sadness
had not also strained this, as fear the animals, as her conversion the
Demiurge himself. Upon all these elements and bodies fire has been
fanned. Because they have not yet published the original passion of
Sophia of this, I will meanwhile argue it shaken out by her little
motions. For you would believe that in so great vexations she had even
had a fever.

* Chapter 24
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h23
:END:

When such fictions about God or about gods, of what sort about man? For
having wrought the world the Demiurge puts hands to man, and takes
substance for him not from this, they say, dry earth, which we know as
the only one (as if, even if dry afterward, it had not yet then, the
waters having been first segregated, with surviving mud dried), but from
the invisible body of that philosophical matter, from its fluxile and
fusile, which whence it was I cannot estimate, because it is nowhere.
For if fusile and fluxile is a quality of liquor, but every liquor
flowed from Sophia's weepings, it follows that we believe the mud to
have consisted of Sophia's phlegms and rheums, which are likewise the
dregs of tears, as what settles of waters is mud. So the Demiurge
pots man and animates him from his own breath. Thus he will be both
choic and animal, made after image and likeness; a fourfold thing; so
that the image is counted choic, material of course, even if the
Demiurge is not from matter, but the likeness animal, for this the
Demiurge also is. You have two. Meanwhile they say the carnal surface
was afterward woven over the choic, and that this is the coat of skins
liable to sense.

* Chapter 25

But there was in Achamoth from the substance of mother Sophia a certain
peculium of spiritual seed, as Achamoth herself also had sequestered in
her son the Demiurge, not even this with him aware. Receive the industry
of this clandestine providence. For to this end she had both deposited
and hidden it, so that, when the Demiurge should presently communicate a
soul from his own breath into Adam, that spiritual seed too, as if
through a channel, the soul might derive into the choic, and so, having
been gestated in the material body, as in a womb, and grown there, it
might be found fit for receiving sometime the perfect word. And so when
the Demiurge commits a transplant of his own soul into Adam, the
spiritual man lay hid, inserted by his very breath, and equally led into
the body; the Demiurge had not known the mother's seed more than her
herself. This seed they call Church, a mirror of the supernal Church and
a census of man, counting him from Achamoth in the same way as the
animal from the Demiurge, the choic from the substance of ἀρχῆς, the
flesh from matter. You have a new, that is a fourfold, Geryon.

So they also divide an exit to each. To the material indeed, that is the
carnal, which they also call the left, an undoubted destruction; but to
the animal, which they also call the right, a doubted event, as wavering
between the material and the spiritual and due that way whither it has
most nodded. But the spiritual is sent out into comparison of the
animal, so that it can be educated with it and exercised in
conversations. For the animal had needed even the disciplines of the
sensibles; to this end the preparation of the world also was provided,
to this end Soter also was presented in the world, for the salvation of
course of the animal. By still another composition they want that
monstrous one to have put on slices of those substances whose sum he was
to bring back to salvation, so that he received the spiritual from
Achamoth, but the animal, which he presently put on from the Demiurge,
Christ, but a corporal substance from the animal, yet constructed by a
wonderful and unspeakable talent of reason, that he bore it for the sake
of administration, so that he might unwillingly lie open to encounter
and sight and touch and death. But nothing material was in him, as being
alien to salvation. As if he had been necessary to others than those
needing salvation. And all this, that by alienating the habit of our
flesh from Christ they may also drive it from the hope of salvation.

Now I give back about Christ, him into whom some insert Jesus with as
great license as they stuff the spiritual seed into the animal with the
inbreathing, having invented I know not what stuffings both of men and
of their gods. That there is also for the Demiurge his own Christ, a
natural son. In short an animal brought forth by himself, promulgated to
the prophets, placed in questions of prepositions, that is brought forth
through a virgin, not from a virgin, because, having been brought down
into the virgin, he proceeded by a transmeatory rather than a generative
manner through her, not from her, having suffered her not as mother but
as a way. Over this Christ then Jesus flew down at the sacrament of
baptism through the figure of a dove. But there was also in Christ a
seasoning of spiritual seed from Achamoth, lest of course the remaining
stuffing should wither. For into the figure of the principal Tetrad they
crowd him with four substances, the spiritual Achamothian, the animal
Demiurgic, the corporal unnarratable, and that Soteric, that is the
dovelike. And Soter indeed remained in Christ, impassible, uninjurable,
inapprehensible. In short when it comes to apprehensions, he departed
from him at Pilate's recognition. Likewise the mother's seed did not
admit injuries, equally unsubmissive, and not even discovered to the
Demiurge himself. But the animal and fleshly Christ suffers in a
delineation of the superior Christ, who for forming Achamoth had leaned
on the cross, that is Horos, with a substantial, not a cognitive, form.
So they urge all things into images, plainly themselves too imaginary
Christians.

I will now gather from the scattered to conclude what they discourse
about the disposition of the whole human race. Having professed a
triform nature at the beginning, and yet united in Adam, from there they
already divide it through the several properties of the kinds, having
got an occasion of a distinction of this k(??)ind from the posterity of
Adam himself, tripartite also by moral differences. Cain and Abel and

they reduce to Cain, the animal, deliberated of a middle hope, they
compose to Abel, the spiritual, prejudged to a certain salvation, they
store in Seth. So they also discern the souls themselves by a double
property, good and bad, according to the choic status from Cain and the
animal from Abel. The spiritual from Seth they superinduce from an
accidence, no longer a nature but an indulgence, as those upon whom
Achamoth in the upper things rains it down into good souls, that is
those inscribed to the animal census. For the choic kind, that is bad
souls, never receives the salutary. For they have pronounced the nature
of nature immutable and unreformable. That grain therefore of spiritual
seed, modest and very small by casting, but by educating this faith is
increased and advanced, as we said above, and the souls by this very
thing so outstrip the rest that the Demiurge then, ignorant, made much
of them. From their brick-list therefore he had been accustomed to
choose both into kings and into priests, which now also, if they have
apprehended a full and perfect knowledge of those ditties, already
naturified by the kinship of the spiritual condition, will obtain a
certain salvation, nay one owed in every way.
