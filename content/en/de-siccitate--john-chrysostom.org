Concerning drought.
=61.723=
Who will give water to my head, and a fountain of tears to my eyes? Let the
most compassionate of the prophets lavish upon me the prelude of this
discourse. But he lamented the desolation of one city, Jerusalem; I, seeing
the scourge that has come upon this whole surrounding country, am moved to
dirges and tears. For heaven has been shut because of our sins, and the
clouds, by the solar heat, have been dried up as if they were tinder; the
springs of waters have been dried up, and the streams of ever-flowing rivers
have failed; and the earth, set on fire by the burning heat, refuses to give
her accustomed fruits. Alas for our evils.
For even the irrational animals themselves are in danger because of our
sins. The earth has been dried up and its grass, or rather it did not even
sprout, and it is being destroyed by the plague! And the ox lows piteously,
as if at the point of death; and the flocks bleat with a mournful bleating,
seeing the drinking of waters set before them as a flaming mixing-bowl, and
the standing crops withering before their time; so that the prophecy of Joel
has now been fulfilled among us: The herds of oxen have wept, because there
was no pasture for them; and the flocks of sheep have perished, because the
outlets of the streams were dried up. So greatly have our offenses moved the
gracious and gentle nature of God into just wrath.
And since we do not cease each day kindling the fire of our sins, we are
reasonably punished with drought and burning heat. Perhaps somewhere the
prophet also cries out to us: Walk by the light of your fire, and by the
flame which you have kindled. To the Israelites, then, God promised through
the prophet that if they kept his commandments, I will send upon you the
early rain and the latter rain; and your threshing-floors of wheat shall be
multiplied, and your vats of wine; but for us this promise has been turned
into the opposite; or rather the curse of Jeremiah has overtaken us: Let the
heaven over your head, he says, be bronze; signifying by the threat of the
word the utter dryness and the failing of the waters. Or has this not just
now been fulfilled for us? For the earth, cracked by drought and as it were
turned to stone, has wasted the labor of the farmers, receiving neither the
ploughing nor the digging; and the standing crops, having sprouted a little
and offered fair hopes, before they had conceived the ear, withered away and
were gone. Only the remaining leafage of the vines and plants has been left,
perhaps itself also hinting at the withering that will come shortly.
Such is the fruit of sins, such are the rites of wickedness, because we did
not walk in his ways, that is, in the ways of God, but were enslaved to
wicked deeds, envying one another, lifting ourselves up over those who are
lesser, desiring to be filled with what belongs to others, rejoicing at the
neighbor's misfortune, devouring one another by slander, being led into
slavery by unseemly desires, having become horses gone mad after the female,
as the Scripture says, and ravening wolves, and bearing grudges like camels,
not showing mercy to the poor, not having compassion on those who stumble,
neglecting the gatherings in the Church, despising the saints, not
reverencing their commemorations, fleeing confession, as those who are in a
frenzy flee their attendants.
The priests have become a bad example to the people, insulting, bearing
grudges, being hostile, plotting, looking to faces, not rebuking and
correcting those who stumble, but by their silence sharing in the injustice,
like that ancient Eli; the laypeople, leaving their own affairs, each busies
himself with prying into those of the priests, and is a relentless judge. Do
I not speak the truth? Is not our city full of these evils? And do I not
bear witness of these things each day? Did I not grow weary crying out that
we should abstain from oaths? Did I not foretell that the Lord will not
overlook it, seeing the sacred Gospel so despised, and held shamelessly and
without restraint in greedy hands? Therefore bring in the fruits =61.724= of
the field, those gotten for you from greed, since it will not even yield the
seeds that were sown; nor will you harvest the vineyard which you seized by
greed. For what God of old threatened the Jews through Micah, these things
he has now fulfilled among us: You sow, and you shall not reap; you shall
press the olive, and you shall not anoint yourself with oil; and you shall
not drink wine.
What then, someone might perhaps say, and yet not all are greedy, nor are
all perjurers; how then, when few are sinning, has the wormwood of the wrath
of God been mixed in common for all? For the punishment ought to have been
brought upon the wicked alone. But as in the case of one body, if in a
single part there comes an influx of flux, the whole body needed cleansing;
so also in our case, since we are one body, and all members of Christ, when
one part has fallen sick, we all become partners in the pain. Thus once,
when the child of Saul had sinned, the whole camp was scourged, and when
David numbered the people contrary to what seemed good to God, destruction
came in upon them in common. And this even the education from without says,
that often even a whole city has tasted the wickedness of one man. For this
reason the cup of the wrath of God has been mixed for us unmixed. For a cup,
it says, is in the hand of the Lord, full of a mixture of unmixed wine. You
see how the nature of this cup has been altered. For if it is unmixed, how
is it full of mixture? For what has been mixed is not unmixed. But here both
are said; and unmixed, as unmingled with divine compassions for those who
offend without amendment, because of the justice of the judgment; but mixed,
as composed of various scourges and punishments, with which God is
accustomed to chastise those who have not come into desperate evils. For
just as those who check the diseases of bodies, and who have trained
themselves by experience to be able to heal, cleanse the more unfeeling of
the wounds with hot waters and sharp drugs, and thin out the affection of
the uncleanness present in it by the helps of their art; so also God, who
knows all things, visits those who are without feeling with blows, whose
onset it would be better to decline before the experience. What then shall
we say toward the things that have begun?
Just is the judgment of God, or rather the Master who loves mankind has
greatly lightened the punishment from the just wrath, not so much that we
might have experience of what we deserve, but that we might have a sense of
what we have sinned. Consider, then, the long-suffering of God: we have
sinned, and the elements are punished; we offend, and the earth is
chastised; we live a worthless life, and the lifeless things are scourged;
we insult the Fashioner through our shameful deeds, and those that have
sinned in nothing receive the blow; as if someone, when a child had gone
astray, brought the torture upon the slave. Such a good Master we have, thus
he admonishes us, leading us back toward correction; and he stretches the
bow of the threat, but holds back the arrow of wrath. For he does not will
our death of soul, but awaits our turning and repentance. Let us therefore,
brethren, come into a sense of what is fitting; let us propitiate the
compassionate Lord; let us not appear worse than the Ninevites. They, being
Gentiles and unbelieving, when they learned from the prophet the overthrow
of the city, displayed such repentance that they even compelled the infants
and the cattle to fast, and put on ashes and sackcloth, and so were
delivered from the just wrath of God.
Let us also then weep, let each of us confess his own offenses, let us take
beforehand as mediators toward God the saints, both the others and
especially the all-holy Lady, the swift and unconquerable protection; let us
fall before her, begging with tears: Lady and Mistress, who contained the
Word of God in your womb, who deified our nature by your childbirth, who
mediated by your childbirth between God and men, have mercy on your people
and your inheritance; have compassion on this city of yours, which boasts in
your name; mediate for us =61.725= your unworthy servants; prevail upon your
only-begotten Son to be long-suffering toward our offenses, and to loose
this just wrath against us, and to command the clouds to pour down the rain
as is their custom, and to dissolve the drought, and to refresh the burning
of the earth.
Yes, Lady, Lady, the succor of the world, have mercy, nurse the infants,
worn-out old men, parched poor; do not let us perish =61.726= by our sins;
we have sinned, we have broken the law, we have soiled the clean garment of
baptism, we have set aside the Master's commandments; but even if we slip,
we have not stretched out our hands toward a strange god, but toward your
only-begotten Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, who is worshiped and glorified
together with the Father and the Holy Spirit, now and always, and unto the
ages.
