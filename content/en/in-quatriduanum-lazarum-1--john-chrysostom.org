DISCOURSE ON LAZARUS OF FOUR DAYS
=50.641=
Recently, beloved, weaving for you the many-flowered garland of spring, and
depicting the season of the year as if on a tablet of words, we showed not
only groves in bloom and meadows putting forth growth and breezes hastening
toward revival, but we also declared that our own nature at this season has
received the tokens of the resurrection; and seeking some subject suited to
the time, we drew in Lazarus who had come to life again. But being unable to
last out the promise, we brought the discourse to an end at the first attempt;
and first of all, inquiring why Lazarus, passed over in silence by the rest of
the evangelists, has been written of by John alone, we said that the Holy
Spirit, cutting off in advance the suspicion of fiction, allowed the
evangelists both to have written the wonders of the Savior from concert and
agreement, and also arranged that one should omit one thing and another
another, making this a clear proof that the Gospels were not written by them
wickedly, nor from collusion, nor by a preconcerted signal, nor to curry
favor, and so that in all things, whenever what appears falls short, the
unadorned truth may be made clear.
But since these things were sufficiently prepared for us then, let us consider
what timely need the wonder-working concerning Lazarus had for the
evangelists. The Savior, having often spoken many things to the =50.642=
disciples concerning the Passion, saw them growing numb, and shuddering at
such a hearing, and taking the Passion that was being foretold as a sign of
weakness rather than of economy, and still tossed and trembling with human
reasonings. For this reason, with the Passion already drawing near, and the
Cross about to be planted, He raises Lazarus who had died, that by a deed He
might teach those who were being discipled that the Cross and the death are
not of weakness; that He might persuade those present that He commands death,
and recalls a soul set free from earthly bonds.
And He undergoes the voluntary death with a certain purpose, and perhaps also
prefiguring in Lazarus the resurrection after three days, and by the short
appointed time of the sojourn under the earth comforting the faint-hearted,
and at the very threshold of the Cross necessarily slackening the fear of the
disciples, and showing that what was granted by Him to another will also
easily belong to Himself from Himself, admonishing those who doubt by the very
works, and in such a manner attaching voices to the deeds, and through what He
was doing almost uttering these things: "Nowhere have I left the assumed
humanity unmingled with the divine energy, but now as man, and now as God I
am, by the one showing forth the nature, by the other confirming the economy,
and teaching that the humbler things are to be attached to the =50.643=
humanity, and the higher to be ascribed to the divinity, and through this
unequal mixing of the works interpreting the unequal union of the natures, and
through the authority over the passions making clear that the passions are
voluntary. I bridled nature as God, stretching the fast to forty days; but
after this I hungered, and I grew weary as man. Yet as God I lulled a raging
sea. I was tempted as man by the devil, but as God I drove out demons by a
command. I am about to suffer for men as man; but lest you take what is
happening as weakness, before death comes upon Me I recall the one held by
death, and having first signaled the energy of the divinity, then I pay the
ancient debt of the humanity, and having loosed the bonds, then I am bound,
and through the very deeds [I show] that I have authority to lay down my soul,
and I have authority to take it again." This is the Savior's teaching through
the works. For if it were not so, and if He were not storing up the
wonder-working concerning Lazarus for some purpose, surely when the sickness
of Lazarus was announced to Him along the way (For his sisters sent, it says,
to the Lord, saying, "See, he whom you love is sick"), He would not, having
heard such a thing, have delayed long; but as He did in the case of the
centurion and the Syrophoenician woman, and saved the servant of the one who
was absent and the daughter of the one who was absent, so He would have done
also in the case of Martha making known that her brother was sick.
But now He deliberately waits for the death, and the delay lingers, and He
postpones His presence by providence, that He may display the victory over
death before the wrestling with death. And He postpones three days, for proof
of the exactness of the death. And with Jews present He opens the tomb, that
He may make those who persecute Him heralds of the wonder. And through prayer
and the invocation of the Father He raises Lazarus, lest He seem to set
Himself against the laws of the Creator. And look, I pray you, at the paradox.
He did not say, "Lazarus, live again," but what? "Lazarus, come forth," that
He might teach those present that this is He who calls the things that are not
as though they were, that He might show those present that this is He who is
God of the living and not of the dead, that at the fitting time He might
display the unhindered character of the divine command, and remind those
standing round of Him who said: "Let there be a firmament"; "Let the waters be
gathered into one gathering"; "Let the earth put forth herb of grass"; "Let
the waters bring forth creeping things of living souls"; "Lazarus, come
forth." And this was for a greater and more sure faith of those who had run
together, that the wrapping and the bonds might bear witness to the deadness,
while the unhesitating obedience and the unhindered fear might proclaim the
Master's authority. "Lazarus, come forth." And the one dissolved was set
upright, and the one who had rotted perceived; the dead man obeyed, and the
bound man ran, and the one being lamented leaped. And why does the Savior use
a cry for the present deed? For the evangelist says, "Having said these
things, He cried with a great voice, Lazarus, come forth." And through the
shout perhaps prefiguring the resurrection that is to come: "For the trumpet
shall sound," it says, "and the dead shall be raised." Having still many
things to say, I am drawn to the subject before us, and I am compelled to come
from tomb to tomb.
And so the tomb of Lazarus too is timely, when examined beside tombs; nor is
the death of the women untimely, as it seems, being celebrated at this very
tomb. There a tomb, and here a tomb. But the tomb of Lazarus, being opened,
displays the power of Christ, while the tomb of the women, closed and yet
working, proclaims the grace of the Savior. There a dead man running out of
the tomb beyond nature; here women running toward the tombs contrary to
nature. There a sign of divine power; here a proof of noble choice. There
Lazarus ..... the things of death ..... he knows with good courage. There
after death a coming to life again, and here life. There death is violently
robbed; here death is openly trampled. Death, having lent that one to life,
took him back again through speed. Rather, it says, "Women received their dead
from resurrection," this being by God's command; but these leaped from a life
of a season toward a life without end, a mother and daughters, who have
gathered us together today: a pious mother who bore the pangs of birth,
daughters untried by birth-pangs; a mother who loosed virginity for the birth
of virgins; a mother who labored in birth of chastity; a mother who according
to the law of nature bore virgins.
The tyrant who everywhere makes war upon the pious, who turns the sword from
barbarian slaughters toward kindred blood, a tyrant driving Christ who is not
seen, a tyrant thinking through the flock to ravage the shepherd, a tyrant
attempting to shoot arrows into heaven, a tyrant jealous of the kingdom of
Christ as it is being widened: the persecuting man was a tyrant, and without a
yokefellow; the daughters who shared the contest did not go forth. Goods were
plundered, the fatherland was left behind. Impious soldiers dragged those who
loved temperance and seemliness, house-breakers compelling them to worship the
image of the intelligible Nebuchadnezzar. But what the Babylonian furnace
works in the case of the triad of martyrs, having also loosed the bonds of
both body and soul, it let the souls fly free into heaven.
And the serpent, making a beginning, he who hunted Eve in paradise, who played
the sophist with simple and harmless characters, this one, seeing the river
become a baptismal pool of gore, and the stream mixed with the Holy Spirit,
having pursued the blessed women as far as the river, was beaten back by the
waters blended with spiritual fire; and turning the cleverness upon himself,
he lamented the loss of the ancient device, and he saw those women who before
were easily deceived and easily frightened now playing the young warrior
against death with high spirit; and the heel which he was watching to trip,
this he saw leaping from earth into heaven.
But having sufficiently both kept festival with the trophy-bearing women, and
mixed what is useful everywhere into the subject, and adorned an ancient tomb
with a fresh tomb, and stitched together an unexpected return to life with a
patient death, and set before both men and women patterns toward virtue and
piety, and displayed a death worthy of song and a resurrection to be emulated,
let us give thanks to Christ who has authority over life and death; to whom be
the glory and the power together with the Father, with the Holy and
life-giving Spirit, unto the ages of ages. Amen.
