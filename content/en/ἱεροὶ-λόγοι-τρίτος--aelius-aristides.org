I happened to be staying at Hadriani, the god having sent me. And the
difficulties then were many and continuous, for it was possible neither for me
to take food nor to keep down whatever I tasted, but it immediately set, with
much fire, and it cut the throat and intercepted the breath, and fiery spasms
went up into the head. And

when I tried to vomit it did not come, but there was even a drop instead of a
triple wave, because it was held in and brought choking, and only with
difficulty and with much tumult and much despair would I be delivered, not
without blood, but the whole passage was scraped raw and was established as
another wound. And the exhaustion and the whole weakness of the body and the
purging were in proportion to what happened. And of friends and of those
accustomed none was present, but they were away, each elsewhere, except one
who happened then to be taking the waters, whom I even before seemed in a
dream, when I lay alone on the road, the horse on which I sat having fallen,
to say that this too happened from fate, that I be left alone. This indeed had
happened at Pergamum a little before the departure. Then at Hadriani, when I
was in difficulty, I remember such a dream occurring. I seemed to be carried
on a raft in the Egyptian sea alone, and to be at the end of the raft and the
part toward the land. And when I was at a loss the foster father Zosimus
appeared having a horse on the land, and somehow I got off and took hold of
the horse gladly. And this thus. And I seemed also, going through Alexandria,
to see schools of boys. And they were reading and singing these verses,
echoing as sweetly as possible: "And he drew many from death that looks upon
them, who stood at the very unturning gates of Hades." And these are from our
verses, which we first almost composed for the god. I wondered therefore how
they had already spread to Egypt, and I rejoiced exceedingly, since I happened
to have found my own being sung.

And the main points of the dreams are such. And when day came, for a horse
happened to be present for me, I mount at once, even one whom no one would
have thought would last even to the first going out, and I drive the horse on
courses and become easier while still running, and much of the heaviness from
above went away and some strength, as in such circumstances, was gathered, and
there were hopes again. And there came to me also by night a voice of someone
saying, You have been healed, and this in the very most desperate things. In
what way then to Pergamum

I was saved again and what regimen I followed next it is necessary to omit
because of the multitude of both the speeches and the affairs. And I went out
on another departure to the waters at the height of summer, and it had been
prescribed to return immediately after bathing, having pounded cinnamon and
smeared the neck round about. These stadia, forty and two hundred together, I
accomplished, there being a marvelous stifling heat, and I bore the thirst
more easily than someone walking home from a bath. Again he sends me, having
commanded me to drink cold, and I drank the whole. And these things to this
extent for the present. And the things that happened at Lebedus: I was sent
from an incubation which I had undergone at the Saviors, he himself having
commanded this too, so exhausted that I could not even hold out on the bed at
home. And the physician Satyrus was at Pergamum at this time, a sophist, as it
was said, not of the ignoble. This man, coming to me as I lay and touching
both the chest and the hypochondria, when as the talk went on he heard how
many purgings of blood had happened to me, ordered me to stop the blood and
not to dissolve the body. I will give you, he said, a plaster very light and
simple, which put together on the stomach and the hypochondria, and you will
know how much it will help. These things he advised, and I said that of the
blood I was not master to do either thus or otherwise, but as long as the god
should command to take it away, I would obey both willing and unwilling, or
rather never unwilling. But the gift of Satyrus I did not flee, but I kept it
having taken it. And it was, as it turned out, not the horn of Amalthea. And
when I am brought to Lebedus, having survived quite unexpectedly and
thankfully, this already seemed all gain. But I needed much help, and I used
the waters scarcely and sparingly. It seemed good to me then to put the drug
of Satyrus on the stomach and the chest, as Satyrus had directed. And in any
case it was no great departure from the treatment from the god. Immediately
then the first application did not receive me, but it seemed too cold.

Nevertheless it seemed good to me to endure and to give myself to the drug, if
perhaps with time it might accomplish something. And thus the chest is chilled
with a terrible chill and a cough comes on, frequent and strong, and I was
held in difficulty, and the god signifies that it is consumption. And on the
following day the temples and the whole circle of the face were stretched, and
the jaws were shut and there was tumult if ever there was.

And when I relaxed a little, it comes into my mind to consult the god at
Colophon both about the present things and about the whole illness. And
Colophon is not far from Lebedus, and the sacred night happened to be at hand.
This seeming good, I send Zosimus. And when the night came, to Zosimus this
oracle comes, bearing upon me: "Asclepius will heal you of the disease and
will cure you, honoring the famous city of Telephus, not far from the streams
of the Caicus." And to me on the same night these things happened, to remember
the main points. I seemed to be at the ancestral hearth, and on the wall where
the seats of the gods were there were such inscriptions: So-and-so, saved from
the last things, thank-offerings to all the gods. And traces of the sacrifices
appeared. And this vision, at least, began for me the continuity of the
sacrifices, not so much looking to the dream, but at once such were the things
coming from the gods, and at once the mind was so prepared. From Lebedus then
we withdrew with good cheer, when it seemed good to the god, and I enjoyed a
relief worthy of speech. And what I said about the consumption, the god
indicated later to the temple-warden, as Asclepiacus himself related to me,
having heard nothing beforehand from me. For he said he heard the god saying
that he had removed consumption and catarrh from me, and was healing the
stomach. And also to Neritus, one of my foster fathers, about the same time
especially he showed such wonders. For I think the god seemed to him to say,
being together with Telesphorus, looking at me, that it was necessary to take
out this man's bones and put in sinews, for the existing ones had given out.
And he himself was in every distress and in agony, these

things hearing about me, and the god said, comforting and teaching, that he
would not cut out the bones outright nor cut away the existing sinews, but
that a kind of alteration of the existing things must come about, so great and
strange was the correction needed. And he gives a remedy to Neritus to tell
me, three times a day with oil having no salt. And I did thus from that time,
and when I tried it it helped toward the better. And these other things
happened to me at the ancestral hearth, at the very height of winter and at
the same time of the disease. I was lying at lunch, and strong and terrible
pains of the head came on, and the face was stretched, and the lips were shut,
and there was much difficulty. And coiled up as I was I rushed into the room
where I slept, and I lie down in whatever way, and a fire comes on, much and
violent. And I could not breathe, but there was wailing of both mother and
nurse and the other household, and when indeed Zosimus was thrown into
confusion, and somehow I nodded to most of them an exit and I had arrayed
myself toward what would happen. And after this it was sunset, or even still
further, and a spasm comes on upon the fever, neither speakable nor such as
one could even think, but the body was drawn with every drawing, and the knees
were carried up toward the head and dashed against it, and it was not possible
to hold the hands, but they fell upon the neck and the face. And the chest was
thrust out and the back was drawn backward like a sail swollen from wind. And
nothing of the body was still, nor did even a little remain according to
nature, but the movement was to the utmost and the stretching of the pains
unspeakable, neither allowing silence and meeting the voice with still greater
pain.

And these things happened even into midnight and beyond, not even a little
letting up. Then it took off something of the peak, yet it did not altogether
stop. And there was application and wrapping of warm wool continuously and
every form of fomentation, and thus I barely survive. And before it was day,
someone ran calling a physician. He came either on the following day or even a
day

fathers, and to worship the seat of Zeus by which I was raised. And some
voices, I think, and the manner of the supplication were distinguished. And
there was not a little snow and the going out was altogether difficult, and
the little house was more than a stade from the house. I mounted a horse and
went, and I worshipped, and I had not yet returned when all those things had
settled. And the things about the neck and the stretchings from the back and
the opisthotonos that was being completed he healed at once in this way, when
he had healed the difficulty of breathing. He said it was a royal ointment.
And it was necessary to take it from the woman. And somehow from these things
a servant of the palace appeared by the temple and seat of Telesphorus,
white-clad and girt, and by the doors, where Artemis is, he went out under a
herald, carrying to the king the rest of the ointment. The dream, to remember
it dimly, happened most of all such. And when I come to the temple, going
about by Telesphorus, the temple-warden Asclepiacus comes up, and as he
happened to be standing by the seat, I tell him the vision that had come to
me, and I asked what the ointment might be, or who might use it. And he,
having heard and wondered, as he was accustomed, The search is not long, he
said, nor of much road, but I bring it to you from here. For it lies by the
feet of Hygieia, Tyche here having just put it, as soon as the temple was
opened. And Tyche was a woman of the distinguished. And going in to Hygieia he
brings the ointment. And I anoint myself as I happened to be standing there.
And the ointment was of a marvelous fragrance and the power was immediately
evident. For quicker than I have said the tension was relaxed. And asking

And about this time at once such a thing was for me, whether I was already
vomiting in the evening or the vomitings were not yet. A nut and a dried fig
and a date of the palm and together with these some bread he required me to
eat from bed. And I used these from this time upon the ointment. And for the
stomach and hypochondria and the place about these he gives a plaster-drug,
higher I think than these. And he gives it having presented such a vision: I
seemed to see the physician Asclepiacus coming in to me and examining and
plastering, with the drug called dittany, and at the same time predicting to
use it thirty days. I used it, and the thirtieth night came and I seemed again
to see Asclepiacus coming and taking off the plaster. And later in time,
having composed something from four, he put it on, of which I remember the
two, pitch from wine and wool-grease, and the two, if the dreams should
appear, we shall fill in. And indeed concerning the name of the theriac drug I
remember such. I happened to be eating nothing, and having left much of the
day, and he ordered me both to dine and having risen to drink of this drug.
And it happened for five days in succession, whereas I knew the physicians
commanding the opposite, whenever someone was going to drink, not to dine, and
hitherto it had been customary. And again he ordered me to eat of this same
drug with bread, and I ate it by the sacred tripod, making this a starting
point of safety. And there is a mixture of Philo, I think, another drug. Of
this I was not even able to smell before, but when the god signified both to
use it and at the same time the hour that was needed, I not only drank it
readily, but having drunk I was immediately sweeter and easier. Other things
then countless it would be to tell

things the god having reckoned and seeing the flux borne outright commanded
him to drink water, and to abstain from wine, if he needs to win. Which things
indeed, he said, it is possible for you too, having imitated, to be crowned or

epigram of the speech, Lover of the crown, or Fond of the crown. How long a
time then I bore the water-drinking, this too I am not able to say. But that
easily and readily, though always somehow before I was disgusted at the water
and nauseated. And when this too had been served, he releases me from the
water, and he assigned a measure of wine, and the word was a royal hemina. It
is known, I suppose, that he meant a half-cotyle. I used this and thus it
sufficed as the double did not suffice before, and there were times even when,
being sparing from fear that it might fail, there was a surplus. Yet I did not
make this a reserve for the next day, but from the beginning it was necessary
to be content with the measure. And when he had this trial too, he releases me
already to drink at liberty, jesting somehow thus, that vain are those of men
who, having plenty of what is sufficient, do not dare to use it freely. And
the book itself seemed to be Antisthenes On Use. And it bore upon wine, and
there were some symbols of Dionysus besides. And so I was disposed from the

even when the god allowed it I differed only a little in drinking from the
measure. And I longed in a way for the stewardship of that time. And there was
a time of this sort, when I abstained from living things, except the cock, and
from all vegetables, except wild ones and lettuce, and I abstained from all

some we bore with bathless days and bloodlettings and clysters, and some as
each happened. And from fish even six years I abstained from all. And from
pork I do not know how much. And again when he conceded I used both. Then in

case. And indeed from fish-sauce he barred me once for all. For it was not
safe for the head, and least of all for the teeth. And he gave remedies of the
teeth. First it was to burn a lion's tooth and having pounded it to use it as
a dentifrice, second to rinse with juice, this very ointment. After these
pepper, and he added it for warmth. And upon all, Indian spike, this too a
dentifrice.

And these things are of those that appeared recently. And from beef I was
barred in this way. I seemed to have an oracle come out for Zosimus, that he
would live until the cow in the field lives. And indeed I said to him, Do you
know then what the oracle means? It commands you to abstain from beef. And
Zosimus was said, toward the chill from which he died, even having touched
some beef from a sacrifice, to have been harmed. There was then, as was
likely, much exactness and forethought not to escape notice having touched it
even with the tip of the finger. And later in time the many and frequent
earthquakes happen when Albus was governor of Asia, and this, Mytilene was
thrown down nearly all, and this, in many of the other cities many things were
shaken, and villages were utterly destroyed, and the Ephesians and the
Smyrnaeans ran to one another in tumult, and the continuity of both the
earthquakes and the fears was marvelous. And this, they sent sacred
ambassadors to Clarus and the oracle was fought over, and this, holding
suppliant branches about the altars and the marketplaces and the circuits of
the cities

The things about the earthquakes and how we began to sacrifice an ox to them
have been told. A goose egg then at some time we sought, the god having
signified it at the session in Pergamum, and it was found nowhere in the
marketplace, but there was Meilates from the acropolis, and to this man at
last those at the search come, by chance together and by indication. And
Meilates says to them that he has an egg, and that he keeps it for the sake of
a remedy. For so the god had foretold to him. Then we at least from there,
said those seeking, and he, having worshipped, gives it. And what I did with
it having taken it I do not know, from the multitude of years at least. And
like to this is the thing from Isis, having happened at the beginning still of
the illness about the geese themselves. I was staying at the hot waters and
the goddess commands me to sacrifice to her two geese. I went to the city
having sent ahead those who would look, and having said beforehand to meet at
the temple of Isis having the geese. Other geese then there were not that day,
and there were two only. And when they came to him and were buying, the one
raising the geese said that he was not able to sell. For it had been foretold
to him by Isis to keep them for Aristides. And that he would come in any case
and sacrifice. And when he knew the whole, he was struck and having worshipped
he gives them. And these things I knew at the sacrifice itself. And there was
also light from Isis and other unspeakable things bearing toward safety. And
Sarapis also appeared the same night, both himself and Asclepius together,
marvelous in beauty and size and in a way like to one another. And when the
misfortune about Zosimus happened, for what the god foretold when it was about
to be and how he comforted I omit, but when it happened and I was in a hard
way from the grief, Sarapis seemed to me, having a knife, as he sits in the
