* On Saint Eustathius of Antioch

Encomium on our father among the saints Eustathius, archbishop of great
Antioch.
=50.597=
αʹ. A certain wise man, who knew how to philosophize, having learned with
accuracy the nature of human affairs, and having recognized their rottenness,
and that they have nothing trustworthy nor firm, exhorts in common all men not
to call anyone blessed before death. Therefore, since the blessed Eustathius
has died, we can henceforth praise him with all freedom; for if before death
one must call no one blessed, after death the blessing upon those who are
worthy would be without blame. For he has passed the strait of the affairs of
life, he has been delivered from the turmoil of the waves, he has sailed down
to the calm and untroubled harbor =50.598=.40, he is not subject to the
uncertainty of the future, nor is he liable to a fall, but as one now standing
upon some rock and high cliff he laughs at all the waves. Therefore the
blessing is safe, the encomium is irreproachable; for it does not fear a
change, it does not suspect a reversal.

For we who are still living, like those tossing in the midst of the sea, are
subject to many changes; and just as those are now raised to a height when the
waves are crested, now brought down to the very deep; but neither is the
height safe, nor is the lowliness lasting; for both of these belong to waters
that flow =50.599= and do not stand still; so also in human affairs nothing is
firm and standing, but the changes are frequent and swiftly turning. One has
been lifted to a height by prosperity, another has been brought down into a
great depth by misfortune; but let neither that one be puffed up, nor this one
be downcast; for each will receive a most swift change; but not that one who
has departed to heaven, who has gone away to the longed-for Jesus, who has
come to a place clean of tumults, where pain and grief and sighing have fled
away. There is no image of change there, there is no shadow of turning, but
all things are fixed and unmoved, all things firm and established, all things
incorruptible and immortal, all things unblemished and remaining forever. For
this reason he says, Call no one blessed before death. Why? For the future is
unclear, and nature is weak; the will is slack, sin easily besets, many are
the snares; For know, he says, that you pass through the midst of snares; the
trials come one upon another, great is the crowd of affairs, and the war of
the demons is continuous, and the uprisings of the passions are constant; for
these reasons, Call no one blessed before death, he says. Therefore after
death it is safe to bless the worthy; or rather not simply after death, but
after such a death, when one has ended his life with a crown, when with
confession and unfeigned faith. For if someone blessed those who simply died,
how much more those who died thus? And who, he says, blessed those who simply
died? Solomon, that all-wise Solomon; for do not simply pass by the man, but
consider who he was, and how he had lived, and with how much freedom and ease
he lived the soft and toilless life. For he went through every form of luxury,
and invented all kinds of ways of amusement, and found varied and manifold
kinds of delights, and recounting these he said: I built houses for myself, I
planted vineyards for myself, I made gardens and parks for myself, I made
pools of waters for myself, I acquired male and female slaves, and homeborn
slaves were born to me; possession of herds and flocks came to me; I gathered
silver and gold

equal to sand; I made for myself singing men and singing women, cupbearers
male and female. What then did this same man, after so great an abundance of
money, of possessions, of luxury, of ease? I blessed, he says, the dead more
than the living, and better than these is whoever has not yet come into being.
Truly this is a trustworthy accuser of luxury, who votes such things
concerning it. For if someone of those who had lived in poverty and beggary
had brought this vote against luxury, he would have seemed to accuse these
things not by truth but by inexperience; but when the one who has gone through
all of it and examined each of its ways, when this one dishonors it, the
accusation is no longer suspect. Perhaps you think the word has fallen away
from our present subject; but if we pay attention, we shall find that what has
been said belongs especially to this. For in commemorations of martyrs it is
necessary and following also to stir the words concerning philosophy. And we
say these things =50.600= not accusing the present life, God forbid, but
attacking luxury; for it is not living that is evil, but living at random and
simply.

βʹ. So that if someone lives the present life in achievements and in the hope
of the good things to come, he will be able to say according to Paul, that To
live in the flesh is far better; for this is fruit of work; just as also
happened in the case of the blessed Eustathius, who used both life and death
as was needed. For not in his own land, but in a foreign one he endured this
because of Christ; this was the achievement of the enemies. For they drove him
from his fatherland as if dishonoring him; but he became more splendid rather
and more conspicuous by the removal to exile, as the end of the affairs also
showed. For so great was the conspicuousness that, though his body was buried
in Thrace, his memory blooms among us every day, and though the tomb is in
that barbarian region, the longing is among us, who are cut off by so great a
distance, and after so much time it increases every day; or rather, if one
must speak the truth, his tomb too is among us, not in Thrace only. For the
memorials of saints are not coffins, and chests, and stelae, and letters, but
achievements of works, and zeal of faith, and a sound conscience toward God.
For this Church has been set up for the martyr more splendid than any stele,
having letters not voiceless, but through the very facts crying out more
splendidly than a trumpet his memory and his splendor, and each of you who are
present is that saint's tomb, a living and spiritual tomb.

For if I unfold the conscience of each of you who are present, I find this
saint dwelling within your understanding. Do you see how nothing more came to
the enemies? how they did not quench the glory, but rather raised it and made
it more splendid, having made so many tombs instead of one, living tombs,
tombs that give forth a voice, tombs being prepared toward the same zeal? For
this reason I call the bodies of the saints also springs, and roots, and
spiritual myrrhs. For what reason? Because each of these things mentioned does
not keep its own virtue with itself only, but also sends it forth to a great
length. What I mean is this: springs gush forth many streams, yet they do not
keep these within their own bosoms, but begetting long rivers they join with
the sea, and as by a certain stretching of a hand, by the length of these they
take hold of the waters of the sea. Again the root of plants is hidden in the
bosoms of the earth, but it does not keep all its virtue below, and this is
especially the nature of the climbing vines. For when they stretch out their
branches upon high reeds, the shoots creeping through those canes go forth to
a great

distance, making a long roof by the density of the leaves. Such also is the
nature of myrrhs; for they themselves often lie in a little room, but their
fragrance overflowing through the windows into streets and alleys and
marketplaces teaches even those walking outside the =50.601= virtue of the
spices lying within. And if a spring, and a root, and the nature of plants and
of spices have so great a power, much more the bodies of the saints; and that
what is said is not false, you are witnesses. For the body of the martyr lies
in Thrace, but you, not spending time in Thrace, but standing far from that
country, receive the fragrance from so great a distance, and for this reason
you have come together, and the length of the road did not hinder, the
multitude of time did not quench. For such is the nature of spiritual
achievements; it is cut off by no bodily hindrance, but blooms and increases
every day, and neither does the multitude of time wither it, nor does the
length of a road wall it off.

Do not marvel if, beginning the word and the encomia, I called the saint a
martyr; for he also ended his life with a fitting end; how then is he a
martyr? I have said many times to your love, that it is not death only that
makes a martyr, but also the purpose. For often the crown of martyrdom is
woven not from the outcome only, but also from the mind. And this definition
of martyrdom I do not give, but Paul, speaking thus: I die daily. How do you
die daily? how is it possible for one mortal body to receive ten thousand
deaths? By the purpose, he says, and by having been prepared for death. So
also God declares; for Abraham did not stain the knife with blood, did not
redden the altar, did not sacrifice Isaac, but nevertheless he completed the
sacrifice. Who says these things? He himself who received the sacrifice: For
you did not spare, he says, your beloved son because of me. And yet he
received him living, and brought him down healthy; how then did he not spare?
Because I judge such sacrifices, he says, not from the end of the affairs, but
from the purpose of those who chose. The hand did not slay, but the will slew;
the sword did not dip into the child's throat, it did not cut through the
neck, but there is a sacrifice even without blood. Those who have been
initiated know what is said; and for this reason that sacrifice also came to
be without blood, since it was going to be a type of this. Do you see the
image prefigured in the Old? Do not disbelieve the truth.

γʹ. This martyr then (for the word has shown him a martyr to us) prepared
himself for ten thousand deaths, and endured them all in mind and eagerness;
and of the dangers brought upon him he bore many even through the very
experience. For they both drove him from the fatherland, and removed him to
exile, and then they stirred many other things against that blessed man,
having nothing just to accuse, but that having heard Paul saying, They
worshipped and served the creature rather than the Creator, he fled the
impiety, and feared the lawlessness; but this is worthy of crowns, not of
accusation. But you, consider with me the wickedness of the devil. For since
the Hellenic war had recently been dissolved, and all the Churches had just
now breathed again from the grievous and successive persecutions, and it was
not long since all peoples had been shut out, altars had been quenched, and
all the madness of the demons =50.602= had been dissolved, and these things
grieved the wicked demon, and he was not able to bear mildly the peace of the
Church, what then does he do? He brings in another grievous war. For the
former was foreign, but this was civil; and such as these are harder to guard
against, and easily master those who fall into them.

At that time then this blessed man was general of the Church among us, and the
disease arises, like some grievous plague from the regions of Egypt, then
walking through the cities in between it hastened to throw itself into our
city. But he, being watchful and sober, and seeing all the things to come from
afar, beat back the war as it approached; and like some wise physician, before
the disease threw itself into the city, sitting here, he prepared the
medicines, and he piloted this sacred ship with much safety, running about
everywhere, knitting together sailors, passengers, all who were sailing,
preparing them to be sober, to be watchful, as pirates were attacking and
attempting to plunder the treasure of the faith. And not here only did he use
this providence, but he also sent everywhere those who would teach, exhort,
converse, wall off in advance the assault of the enemies. For he had been well
taught by the grace of the Spirit, that the one who presides over a Church
must care not only for that one entrusted to him by the Spirit, but also for
every Church lying throughout the world; and these things he learned from the
sacred prayers. For if one must make the prayers, he says, for the catholic
Church from the ends to the ends of the world, much more must one also display
the providence for the whole of it, and care likewise for all, and be anxious
for all.

And what happened in the case of Stephen, this also came about in his case.
For just as the Jews, not being able to stand against the wisdom of Stephen,
stoned that saint; so also these, not being able to stand against this man's
wisdom, and seeing the fortifications secured, then drive the herald out of
the city. But the voice was not silent, but the man was driven out, yet the
word of teaching was not driven out. For Paul too was bound, and the word of
God was not bound; and this man is in a foreign land, and his teaching is
among us. So then, having driven him out they came in packed together, like a
violent torrent; but they neither swept away the plants, nor buried the seeds,
nor damaged the farming; so well and with skill had they been rooted,
cultivated by that man's wisdom. But it is worth saying for what reason God
allowed him to be driven away from here. The Church had recently breathed
again; it had no ordinary consolation, his superintendence; he walled it on
every side, and beat back the assaults of the enemies. For what reason then
was he driven out, and did God allow those who were leading him away? For what
reason? And do not think that what is said is a solution of this difficulty
alone; but whether the word concerning such things comes about for you toward
Hellenes, or toward other heretics =50.603=, what will be said is sufficient
to solve the whole difficulty. God allows his true and apostolic faith to be
warred against in many things, but he lets the heresies and Hellenism enjoy
freedom. Why in the world? So that you may learn the weakness of those, not
being troubled, and being dissolved of their own accord; and may recognize the
strength of the faith being warred against, and increasing through those who
hinder it. And that this is not my conjecture, but a divine oracle brought
from above, let us hear what Paul says concerning these things; for he too
suffered something human; for even if he was Paul, yet he shared our nature.
And what is it that he suffered? He was driven, he was warred against, he was
scourged, he was plotted against in ten thousand ways, from without, from
within, by those who seemed to be his own, by those who were foreign. And what
need is there to say how many afflictions he endured? Growing weary then, and
no longer bearing the assaults of the enemies always cutting off his teaching,
and opposing his word, he falls before the Master, and entreats him, and says:
There was given me a thorn in the flesh, an angel of Satan, that he might

buffet me; concerning this I entreated the Lord thrice, and he has said to me:
My grace is sufficient for you; for my power is perfected in weakness.

And I know that some think weakness to be bodily; but this is not so, it is
not; but he calls the opposing men an angel of Satan; for Satan is a Hebrew
word; and Satan means the opposing one. Therefore the vessels of the devil,
and the men who serve him, he calls his angels. Why then, he says, is it
attached to the flesh? Because the flesh was scourged, but the soul was
lightened, being lifted by the hope of the things to come; for it did not even
touch the soul, nor trip up the inner thoughts, but the devices and the war
stood as far as the flesh, not being able to pass within. Since then this was
cut, this was scourged, this was bound (for it was impossible to bind a soul),
for this reason he says, There was given me a thorn in the flesh, an angel of
Satan, that he might buffet me, hinting at the trials, the afflictions, the
persecutions. Then what? Concerning this, he says, I entreated the Lord
thrice; that is, I asked many times, he says, so as to breathe a little from
the trials. And you remember the cause which I mentioned, that for this reason
God allows his own servants to be scourged, driven, to suffer ten thousand
terrible things, that he may show his own power. For see, here too, having
asked that the ten thousand terrible things and the opposing ones depart from
him, he did not obtain the request; and he sets down the cause for which he
did not obtain the request. What then is the cause? for nothing prevents us
from recalling it again: My grace, he says, is sufficient for you; for my
power is perfected in weakness.

δʹ. Do you see that for this reason God allows the angels of Satan to press
upon his servants, and to furnish ten thousand troubles, that his power may be
made manifest? For truly, whether we converse with Hellenes, or with the
wretched Jews, this is sufficient for us as a demonstration of the divine
power, that the faith brought in through ten thousand wars prevailed, and
though the whole world was acting against it, and though all were thrusting
away those twelve men with much =50.604= vehemence, I mean the apostles, they
were able in a short time, being scourged, driven, suffering ten thousand
terrible things, to prevail over those who were doing these things with all
excess.

For these reasons God also allowed the blessed Eustathius to be carried away
to exile, that he might show us more greatly both the power of the truth and
the weakness of the heretics. Therefore, being about to go away to the
departure, he was letting go the city, but he was not letting go your love;
nor, since he was being driven from the Church, did he think himself a
stranger to the presidency and the care on your behalf, but then he cared and
thought more greatly; and having called all he exhorted them not to yield, nor
to give in to the wolves, nor to betray the flock to them, but to remain
within, stopping their mouths and refuting them, and securing the more simple
of the brethren. And that he commanded well, the end showed; for if you had
not remained in the Church then, the greater part of the city would have been
corrupted, the wolves eating the sheep in a desert; but that man's word
prevented them from displaying their own wickedness with freedom. And not the
end only showed it, but also the words of Paul; for this man too, having been
taught by him, was exhorting these things. What then does Paul say? Being
about to be led away sometime to Rome, the last departure, after which he was
no longer going to see the disciples; For I shall no longer see you, he says;
and he said this, not wishing to grieve, but to make them secure; therefore
being about to depart from there, he was securing them somehow saying: I know
that after my departure grievous wolves will come in among you, and from among
you yourselves men will arise speaking perverse things. The war is triple, the
nature of the beasts, the grievousness of the war, that those who wage war are
not foreigners, but even one's own; for

this reason more grievous; reasonably. For if someone strikes and wars against
me from without, I shall easily be able to get the better of him; but if the
sore grows from within from the body, the evil becomes hard to heal; which
indeed also happened then. Therefore he also exhorted saying, Take heed to
yourselves and to all the flock; he did not say, Leaving the sheep flee
outside. These things the blessed Eustathius too, having been taught, was
exhorting his own disciples; which things then this wise and noble teacher
also, having heard, fulfilled the word in deed. When those men were coming in,
at any rate, he did not leave the sheep, although he had not even gone up to
the throne of rule; but this was nothing to that noble and philosophic soul.
For he was letting the honors of rulers go to others, but he himself was
enduring the labors of rulers, turning about within among the wolves; for the
teeth of the beasts harmed him in nothing, so much stronger a faith did he
have than their bites. Turning about within then, and occupying all of them by
the fight that came to be toward him, he prepared much freedom for the sheep.
And he was not doing this only, stopping their mouths, =50.605= beating back
the blasphemies, but also going around the very nurslings he was examining,
lest anyone had received a dart, lest anyone had taken a grievous wound, and
immediately he was applying the medicine. And doing these things he leavened
all into the true faith, and he did not withdraw before God prepared the
blessed Meletius, coming, to take the whole lump; this man sowed, that one
coming harvested. So also it happened in the case of Moses and Aaron. For they
too, turning about like leaven in the midst of the Egyptians, made many
zealots of their own piety. And Moses bears witness to this, saying that a
great mixed people went up together with the Israelites.

This man then, imitating this Moses, even before the rule was doing the things
of the rule; for that man too, not yet having been entrusted with the
presidency of the people, very vigorously and nobly punished those who were
doing injustice, defended those who were being wronged, =50.606= and having
left a royal table, and honors, and presidencies, he ran toward the mud and
the brickmaking, thinking the care of his own more honorable than all luxury,
and ease, and honor; looking toward whom he himself then was exhorting all
rulers by the care concerning the people, and was preferring labors to ease
and receiving continuous hatreds being driven from every side every day; but
all things were light to him; for the cause of the things coming to be
furnished him a sufficient consolation of what was happening.

For all these things then, having given thanks to God, let us emulate the
virtues of these saints, that we may also share the crowns with them, by the
grace and loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom and with whom
to the Father, together with the Holy Spirit, be glory, honor and power unto
the ages of ages. Amen.
