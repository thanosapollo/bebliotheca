FIRST MYSTAGOGY TO THE NEWLY ILLUMINATED. And a reading from the first
catholic epistle of Peter, from: "Be sober, be watchful," to the end of the
epistle; of the same Cyril and of John the bishop. 1.1 I was desiring for you
also long ago, O genuine and longed-for children of the Church, to discourse
concerning these spiritual and heavenly mysteries. But since I knew clearly
that sight is far more trustworthy than hearing, I was waiting for the present
time, that having found you from this evening more readily led concerning the
things said, I might guide you into the brighter and more fragrant meadow of
this paradise. And besides, you have also been made capable of the more divine
mysteries, of the divine and life-giving baptism. Since therefore it is now
necessary to set forth a table of the more complete lessons, come, let us
instruct you in these things accurately, that you may know the meaning of the
things that happened toward you on that evening of the baptism. 1.2 You were
entering first into the vestibule-house of the baptism, and standing toward
the west you were hearing and being commanded to stretch out the hand, and you
were renouncing Satan as present. And you must know this, that this type lies
in an ancient history. For when Pharaoh, the most bitter and most savage
tyrant, was oppressing the free and noble people of the Hebrews, God sent
Moses to lead them out of the wicked slavery of the Egyptians. And the
doorposts were being anointed with blood of a lamb, that the destroyer might
flee the houses that had the sign of the blood, and the people of the Hebrews
was being freed in a marvelous way. But when he pursued even those who had
been freed, and saw the sea being divided for them marvelously, nevertheless
he was going on, matching tracks with tracks, and immediately he was becoming
submerged, being drowned in the Red Sea. 1.3 Pass over then for me from the
old things to the new, from the type to the truth. There Moses being sent from
God into Egypt, here Christ being sent forth from the Father into the world;
there, that he might lead out a people oppressed from Egypt, here Christ, that
he might deliver those in the world being worn down by sin; there blood of a
lamb was a turning-away of a destroyer, here the blood of the unblemished Lamb
Jesus Christ has become a putting-to-flight of demons. That tyrant was
pursuing that ancient people as far as the sea, and this reckless, shameless,
and origin-of-evil demon was following you even as far as the saving waters.
That one becomes submerged in the sea, and this one vanishes in the saving
water. 1.4 But nevertheless you hear, with the hand stretched out, to say as
toward one present: "I renounce you, Satan." I wish also to say for what
reason you stand toward the west; for it is necessary. Since the west is the
place of the appearing darkness, and that one, being darkness, has his power
also in darkness; for this reason looking symbolically toward the west, you
renounce that dark and gloomy ruler. What then was each of you standing
saying? I renounce you, Satan, you the wicked and most savage tyrant; I no
longer fear your strength, saying. For Christ has dissolved this, having
shared blood and flesh with me, that through these sufferings he might abolish
death by death, so that I may not be forever liable to slavery. I renounce
you, the deceitful and most cunning serpent. I renounce you who are a plotter,
and who by a pretence of friendship have worked all lawlessness, and have
produced apostasy in our forefathers. I renounce you, Satan, the creator and
fellow-worker of all wickedness. 1.5 Then in a second saying you learn to say:
"And all your works." And works of Satan are every sin, which it is necessary
also to renounce, just as if someone having fled a tyrant has altogether also
fled his weapons. Every sin then according to its kind has been enrolled among
the works of the devil. But know this, that as many things as you say,
especially at that most fearful hour, are written things, being written in the
invisible books of God. Whenever then you are caught doing anything contrary
to these, you shall be judged as a transgressor. You therefore renounce the
works of Satan, all deeds I mean and thoughts that come about contrary to
reason. 1.6 Then you say: "And all his pomp." And pomp of the devil is mania
for theatres, and horse-races, and hunts, and every such vanity, from which
the holy one praying to be freed says to God: Turn away my eyes from seeing
vanity. Let not the mania for the theatre be eagerly sought by you, where
there are the unchaste sights of the mimes, done with insults and all
indecencies, and the frenzied dances of men made womanish; nor that of those
who in hunts give themselves over to beasts, that they may flatter the
wretched belly; who, that they may serve the stomach with foods, themselves
become food of truly untamed beasts of the belly; and that I may speak justly,
they fight as gladiators over cliffs for the sake of their own god, the belly.
Flee also the horse-races, the frenzied spectacle that throws souls down
headlong; for all these things are pomp of the devil. 1.7 But also the things
hung in idol-temples and at festivals, sometimes meats, or breads, or other
such things defiled by the invocation of the utterly defiled demons, would be
enrolled in the pomp of the devil. For just as the bread and the wine of the
eucharist, before the holy invocation of the adorable Trinity, was plain bread
and wine, but when invocation has come about, the bread becomes body of
Christ, and the wine blood of Christ; in the same way, such foods of the pomp
of Satan, being plain in their own nature, become profane by the invocation of
the demons. 1.8 After these things you say: "And your worship." And worship of
the devil is the prayer in idol-temples; the things coming about toward honor
of lifeless idols; the lighting of lamps, or burning incense by springs or
rivers, as some deceived from dreams or by demons have gone over to this,
thinking to find also the healing of bodily passions; do not pursue such
things. Augury, divination, omens, or amulets, or inscriptions on leaves, by
magics, or by certain other evil arts, and as many such things, are worship of
the devil. Flee these things then; for if you should fall under these after
the renunciation of Satan and the attachment to Christ, you will experience a
more bitter tyrant, perhaps as one who formerly was treating you as his own
and was releasing you from the bitter slavery, but now having been greatly
embittered by you, and you will be deprived of Christ, and will experience
that one. Have you not heard the ancient history narrating to us the things
concerning Lot and his daughters? Was not he himself saved with the daughters,
because he reached the mountain, but his wife became a pillar of salt, set up
as a monument for ever, having the memory of the wicked purpose and turning-
back? Attend then to yourself, and do not turn back again, having put the hand
to the plow, to the salty practice of this life; but flee to the mountain
toward Jesus Christ, the stone cut without hands, and that filled the
inhabited world. 1.9 When therefore you renounce Satan, trampling every
covenant toward him, you dissolve the old contracts toward Hades, the paradise
of God is opened to you, which he planted toward the east, from which our
first father became an exile because of the transgression. And a symbol of
this is your being turned from west toward east, the region of the light. Then
you were being told to say: "I believe in the Father and in the Son and in the
Holy Spirit and in one baptism of repentance." Concerning which things in the
former catecheses, as the grace of God gave, it has been said to you at
length. =1.10= Being secured then by these words, be sober. For our adversary
the devil, as has just now been read, walks as a lion, seeking whom to
swallow. But in the times before this, death swallowed having prevailed; but
upon the holy bath of regeneration, God has taken away every tear from every
face; for you no longer mourn, having put off the old man; but you keep
festival, having put on a garment of salvation, Jesus Christ. =1.11= These
things were coming about in the outer house. And God willing, when in the
following mystagogies we enter into the holy of holies, there we shall know
the symbols of the things performed there. But to God be glory, power,
majesty, with Son and Holy Spirit, unto the ages of ages. Amen.

2.τ MYSTAGOGICAL CATECHESIS 2 CONCERNING BAPTISM. And a reading from the
epistle to the Romans: "Or do you not know that as many as have been baptized
into Christ Jesus have been baptized into his death?" as far as: "for you are
not under law, but under grace". 2.1 Useful to you are the daily mystagogies,
and newer teachings, being proclamations of newer things, and especially to
you who have been renewed from oldness into newness. For this reason I shall
necessarily set before you the things following of yesterday's mystagogy, that
you may learn of what things the things done by you in the inner house were
symbols. 2.2 Immediately then upon entering, you were stripping off the tunic;
and this was an image of putting off the old man with the deeds. For having
been stripped you were naked, imitating also in this Christ who was stripped
upon the cross, and by the nakedness putting off the principalities and the
powers, and with boldness triumphing on the wood. For since the opposing
powers were lurking in our members, it is no longer permitted for you to wear
that old tunic, I do not mean this perceptible one at all, but the old man,
who is being corrupted in the desires of deceit. Which may it not be that the
soul that has once put this off should put it on again, but to say according
to the bride of Christ in the Song of Songs: "I have put off my tunic, how
shall I put it on?" O marvelous thing; you were naked in the sight of all, and
you were not ashamed. For truly you were bearing an imitation of the first-
formed Adam, who in paradise was naked and was not ashamed. 2.3 Then having
been stripped, you were being anointed with exorcised oil from the tips of the
hairs of the head even to the lowest parts, and you were becoming sharers of
the cultivated olive, Jesus Christ. For having been cut out from the wild
olive, you were being grafted into the cultivated olive, and you were becoming
sharers of the fatness of the true olive. The exorcised oil then was a symbol
of the sharing of the fatness of Christ, being a putting-to-flight of every
trace of opposing energy. For just as the breathings of the saints, and the
invocation of the name of God, like a most vehement flame, burn and drive out
demons, so this exorcised oil by invocation of God and prayer receives such
power as not only, burning, to cleanse away the traces of the sins, but also
to drive out the invisible powers of the evil one. 2.4 After these things you
were being led by the hand to the holy pool of the divine baptism, as Christ
from the cross to the tomb lying before him. And each was being asked if he
believes in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit.
And you were confessing the saving confession, and you were sinking down
thrice into the water and again coming up, and here through a symbol hinting
at the three-day burial of Christ. For just as our Saviour has spent three
days and three nights in the heart of the earth, so also you in the first
coming-up were imitating the first day of Christ in the earth and in the
going-down the night; for just as the one in night no longer sees, but the one
in day lives in light, so in the going-down as in night you were seeing
nothing, but in the coming-up again you were happening to be as in day. And in
the same you were dying and being born, and that saving water was becoming
both tomb for you and mother. And what Solomon has said upon other things,
this would fit you; for there he was saying: "A time to give birth, and a time
to die." But in your case the reverse: a time to die, and a time to be born.
And one time was becoming productive of both of these, and your birth was
becoming concurrent with the death. 2.5 O strange and marvelous thing; we did
not truly die, nor were we truly buried, nor truly having been crucified did
we rise, but in image the imitation, and in truth the salvation. Christ was
really crucified and really buried and truly rose; and he has granted all
these things to us, that having shared by imitation of his sufferings, we may
gain the salvation in truth. O surpassing love of man; Christ received nails
upon the undefiled hands and felt pain, and to me without pain and without
toil he grants the salvation through the sharing. 2.6 Let no one then think
that the baptism happens to be of remission of sins only, and of adoption, as
the baptism of John was happening to be provider of remission of sins only;
but we knowing accurately, that as it is a cleanser of sins and a bringer of a
gift of Holy Spirit, so also it is an antitype of the sufferings of Christ.
For this reason also Paul just now crying out was saying: "Or do you not know
that as many as have been baptized into Christ Jesus, have been baptized into
his death? We were therefore buried with him through the baptism." For perhaps
he was saying these things toward some who were disposed as if the baptism
were a bringer of remission of sins and of adoption, but no longer also having
the sharing of the true sufferings of Christ in imitation. 2.7 In order then
that we may learn, that as many things as Christ endured, he has suffered
these in truth and not in seeming, for us and our salvation, and we become
sharers of his sufferings; Paul was crying out with all accuracy: "For if we
have become grown-together with the likeness of his death, we shall also be of
the resurrection." And well also the "grown-together". For since here the true
vine has been planted, we also by sharing of the baptism of the death have
become "grown-together" with him. And apply the mind with much attention to
the words of the apostle. He did not say, for if we have become grown-together
with the death, but, with the likeness of the death. For truly in the case of
Christ death is true, for the soul was being separated from the body, and true
burial; for his holy body was being wrapped in a clean linen cloth, and all
things were truly happening in him. But in our case a likeness of death and of
sufferings; but of salvation not a likeness, but truth. 2.8 Having been taught
these things sufficiently, hold them through the memory, I beseech, that I
also the unworthy may say over you: "And I love you, because you always
remember me, and you hold the traditions which I delivered to you." And God is
able, who presented you as living from the dead, to give you to walk in
newness of life; because to him is the glory and the power now and unto the
ages. Amen.

3.τ MYSTAGOGICAL CATECHESIS 3 CONCERNING CHRISM. And a reading from the first
epistle of John, from: "And you have an anointing from God, and you know all
things", as far as: "and we may not be ashamed from him at his coming". 3.1
Having been baptized into Christ and having put on Christ you have become
conformed to the Son of God. For God having predestined us unto adoption, made
us conformed to the body of the glory of Christ. Having become then partakers
of Christ, you are reasonably called christs, and concerning you God was
saying: "Do not touch my christs." And you have become christs, having
received the antitype of the Holy Spirit, and all things have come about upon
you in image, since you are images of Christ. And he, having been washed in
the river Jordan and having imparted of the flesh of the godhead to the
waters, was coming up from these, and an essential coming-upon of Holy Spirit
was coming about to him, the like resting upon the like. And to you similarly
having come up from the pool of the sacred waters there is chrism, the
antitype with which Christ was anointed. And this is the Holy Spirit,
concerning whom also the blessed Isaiah, in the prophecy according to him, was
saying from the person of the Lord: "The Spirit of the Lord is upon me,
wherefore he has anointed me; he has sent me to preach good news to the poor."
3.2 For Christ was not anointed by a man with oil or bodily myron, but the
Father, having appointed him Saviour of the whole world, anointed him with
Holy Spirit, as Peter says: "Jesus the Nazarene, whom God anointed with Holy
Spirit;" and David the prophet was crying out saying: "Your throne, O God, is
unto the age of the age; a rod of uprightness is the rod of your kingdom. You
have loved righteousness and hated lawlessness; therefore God, your God, has
anointed you with oil of gladness beyond your fellows." And just as Christ was
truly being crucified and being buried and being raised, and you according to
the baptism are deemed worthy in likeness both to be crucified with and to be
buried with and to be raised with him, so also upon the anointing. He was
being anointed with intelligible oil of gladness, that is with Holy Spirit,
called oil of gladness, because it happens to be itself the cause of the
spiritual gladness; but you were anointed with myron, having become sharers
and partakers of Christ. 3.3 But see that you do not suppose that myron to be
bare. For just as the bread of the eucharist, after the invocation of the Holy
Spirit, is no longer plain bread, but body of Christ, so also this holy myron
is no longer bare, nor as one might say common after invocation, but a gift of
Christ, and becoming effective of the presence of the Holy Spirit, of his
godhead. Which is anointed symbolically upon the forehead and your other
sense-organs. And with the appearing myron the body is anointed, but with the
holy and life-giving Spirit the soul is sanctified. 3.4 And first you are
anointed upon the forehead, that you may be delivered from the shame which the
first transgressing man was carrying about everywhere, and that with unveiled
face you may behold as in a mirror the glory of the Lord. Then upon the ears,
that you may receive ears, concerning which Isaiah was saying: "And the Lord
added to me an ear to hear", and the Lord in the Gospels: "He who has ears to
hear, let him hear." Then upon the smell, that perceiving the divine myron you
may say: "We are a fragrance of Christ to God among those who are being
saved." After these things upon the breast, that "having put on the
breastplate of righteousness you may stand against the wiles of the devil."
For just as the Saviour after the baptism and the coming-upon of the Holy
Spirit went out and overcame the opposing one, so also you after the sacred
baptism and the mystical chrism having put on the full armor of the Holy
Spirit, stand against the opposing energy and overcome this, saying: "I can do
all things in Christ who strengthens me." 3.5 Having been deemed worthy of
this holy chrism you are called Christians, making the name also true by the
regeneration. For before you were deemed worthy of the baptism and of the
grace of the Holy Spirit, you were not properly worthy, but going along you
were advancing toward being Christians. 3.6 And it is necessary for you to
know, that the symbol of this anointing lies in the old scripture. For indeed
when Moses was imparting the command of God to his brother, appointing him
high priest, after the washing in water, he anointed, and he was called christ
from the anointing, namely the typical one. So also bringing Solomon forward
to be king, the high priest anointed him after the washing in the Gihon. But
these things were happening to those typically, but to you not typically, but
truly, since from the one who was truly anointed with Holy Spirit is the
beginning of your salvation; for that one is truly first-fruits, and you the
lump; and if the first-fruits is holy, clearly the holiness will pass over
upon the lump. 3.7 Keep this unspotted; for this will be instructive of all,
if it should remain in you, as you have just now heard the blessed John saying
and philosophizing many things concerning the chrism. For this is the holy
spiritual phylactery of the body and salvation of the soul. This from ancient
times the blessed Isaiah prophesying was saying: "And the Lord shall do for
all the nations upon this mountain", and he calls the Church a mountain, and
elsewhere, as when he says: "And in the last days the mountain of the Lord
shall be manifest" "they shall drink wine, they shall drink gladness, they
shall be anointed with myron." And that he may secure you to hear concerning
this myron as mystical, he says: "Deliver these things all to the nations; for
the counsel of the Lord is upon all the nations". Having been anointed then
with this holy myron, keep it unspotted, unblemished in you, advancing through
good works, and becoming well-pleasing to the pioneer of our salvation Christ
Jesus, to whom be the glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.

4.τ MYSTAGOGICAL CATECHESIS 4 CONCERNING BODY AND BLOOD OF CHRIST. And a
reading from the [epistle] to the Corinthians: "For I received from the Lord
[that] which I also delivered to you", and the rest. 4.1 And this teaching of
the blessed Paul has been sufficient to give you full assurance concerning the
divine mysteries of which you have been deemed worthy, having become of one
body and of one blood with Christ. For he himself was just now crying out:
"That in the night in which our Lord Jesus Christ was being delivered, having
taken bread and given thanks he broke and gave to his own disciples, saying:
Take, eat, this is my body. And having taken a cup and given thanks he said:
Take, drink, this is my blood." Since then he has declared and said concerning
the bread: "This is my body", who will dare to doubt any longer? And he having
confirmed and said: "This is my blood", who will ever hesitate saying it is
not his blood? 4.2 He once changed the water into wine by his own nod in Cana
of Galilee, and is he not trustworthy having changed wine into blood? Having
been called to a bodily wedding he worked this marvel of wonder-working, and
to the sons of the bride-chamber shall he not much more be confessed as having
granted the enjoyment of his body and of the blood? 4.3 So that with all full
assurance we partake as of body and blood of Christ. For in a type of bread
the body is given to you, and in a type of wine the blood is given to you,
that you may become, having partaken of body and blood of Christ, of one body
and of one blood with Christ. For so we also become Christ-bearers, his body
and the blood being distributed into our members. So according to the blessed
Peter we become "partakers of divine nature". 4.4 Once Christ speaking with
the Jews was saying: "Unless you eat my flesh and drink my blood, you do not
have life in yourselves." Those, not having heard the things said spiritually,
having been scandalized went away backward, thinking the Saviour was urging
them toward eating of flesh. 4.5 There were also in the old covenant loaves of
proposition; but those, being of the old covenant, have received an end. But
in the new covenant heavenly bread and a cup of salvation, sanctifying soul
and body. For just as the bread is suitable to the body, so also the Word is
fitting to the soul. 4.6 Do not then attend to the bread and the wine as bare;
for they happen to be body and blood according to the Master's declaration.
For even if the sense suggests this to you, yet let the faith confirm you. Do
not judge the matter from the taste, but from the faith be fully assured
without hesitation, having been deemed worthy of body and blood of Christ. 4.7
And the blessed David dictates to you the power, saying: "You have prepared a
table before me over against those who afflict me." And what he says is such:
Before your presence, the demons were preparing a table for men, polluted and
defiled, and filled with diabolic power; but after your presence, O Master,
you have prepared a table before me. When a man says to God: "You have
prepared a table before me", what else does it signify than the mystical and
intelligible table, which God has prepared for us over against, instead of
from the opposite, oppositely to the demons? And very reasonably; for that one
had a sharing of demons, but this a sharing of God. "You have fattened my head
in oil." He has fattened your head in oil upon the forehead because of the
seal which you have of God, that you may become an impress of a seal, a
sanctification of God. "And your cup making me drunk as strongest." You see
here a cup being spoken of, which Jesus having taken in his hands and given
thanks said: "This is my blood, which is being poured out for many unto
remission of sins." 4.8 For this reason also Solomon hinting at this grace in
Ecclesiastes says: "Come, eat in gladness your bread", the spiritual bread.
"Come": he calls the saving and beatifying calling; "and drink in a good heart
your wine", the spiritual wine, "and let oil be poured out upon your head;"
you see him hinting also at the mystical chrism? And "let your garments be
white always, because the Lord has been well pleased with your works;" now the
Lord has been well pleased with your works. For before coming forward to the
grace, "vanity of vanities" were your works. But now having put off the old
garments and having put on the spiritually white, it is necessary to be white-
clad always. We do not say this at all, that you must always have been wrapped
in white garments, but it is necessary to have been wrapped in the truly white
and bright and spiritual, that you may say according to the blessed Isaiah:
"Let my soul rejoice in the Lord; for he has clothed me with a garment of
salvation, and has put around me a tunic of gladness." 4.9 Having learned
these things and been fully assured, that the appearing bread is not bread,
even if it is such to the taste, but body of Christ, and the appearing wine is
not wine, even if the taste wishes this, but blood of Christ, and that
concerning this David of old was saying in psalm: "And bread strengthens a
man's heart, to make a face cheerful in oil;" strengthen the heart, partaking
of it as spiritual, and be made cheerful in the face of the soul. Which may it
be that you, having it unveiled in a pure conscience, beholding as in a mirror
the glory of the Lord, come from glory to glory, in Christ Jesus our Lord, to
whom be the glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.

5.τ MYSTAGOGICAL CATECHESIS 5. And a reading of Peter's catholic epistle:
"Therefore having put away all wickedness and deceit and slander", and the
rest. 5.1 By the love of man of God, in the former assemblies, you have heard
sufficiently concerning baptism and chrism and partaking of body and blood of
Christ. But now it is necessary to pass over to the things following, today
putting the crown upon the spiritual building of your benefit. 5.2 You were
seeing then the deacon giving to wash to the priest, and to the presbyters
surrounding the altar of God. And he was not giving this at all because of the
bodily filth; it is not this. For neither having filth of body did we enter
into the church at the beginning. But the washing is a symbol of our needing
to be clean of all the sins and lawless deeds. For since hands are a symbol of
deed, and we wash these, clearly hinting at the clean and unblemished of the
deeds. Have you not heard the blessed David mystagoguing you in this very
thing and saying: "I will wash my hands among innocents, and I will go around
your altar, O Lord"? Therefore the washing of the hands is the being
unaccountable of sins. 5.3 Then the deacon cries: "Receive one another and let
us kiss one another." Do not suppose that kiss to be customary with those done
in the marketplace by common friends; it is not such as this, but this kiss
mixes the souls with one another, and betroths all unremembering of evil to
them. The kiss then is a sign of the souls being mixed together and of
banishing all remembering of evil. For this reason the Christ was saying: "If
you bring your gift upon the altar, and there remember that your brother has
something against you, leave your gift upon the altar, and go first be
reconciled to your brother, and then having come offer your gift." Therefore
the kiss is a reconciliation, and for this reason holy, as somewhere the
blessed Paul was crying out saying: "Greet one another with a holy kiss", and
Peter: "Greet one another with a kiss of love." 5.4 After this the priest
cries: "Up the hearts." For truly at that most fearful hour it is necessary to
have the heart up toward God, and not down about the earth and the earthly
matters. In power then the priest commands at that hour all to let go cares of
life, worries at home, and to have the heart in heaven toward the man-loving
God. Then you answer: "We have them toward the Lord", agreeing with this, by
the things which you confess. And let no such one be present, as to say with
the mouth: "We have them toward the Lord", but with the thought to have the
mind about the cares of life. Always then God must be remembered; but if this
is impossible because of human weakness, at that hour especially this must be
striven for. 5.5 Then the priest says: "Let us give thanks to the Lord." For
truly we ought to give thanks, because being unworthy he called us into so
great a grace, because being enemies he reconciled, because he deemed us
worthy of a spirit of adoption. Then you say: "It is worthy and just." For we
giving thanks do a worthy thing and just; but he, doing not what is just, but
beyond the just, has benefited us and deemed us worthy of such great good
things. 5.6 After these things we make mention of heaven, and earth, and sea,
sun and moon, stars, all creation both rational and irrational, both visible
and invisible, angels, archangels, powers, lordships, principalities,
authorities, thrones, the many-faced cherubim, saying in power the word of
David: "Magnify the Lord with me." We make mention also of the seraphim, whom
Isaiah saw in Holy Spirit standing around the throne of God, and with two
wings covering the face, and with two the feet, and with two flying, and
saying: "Holy, holy, holy Lord of Sabaoth." For because of this we say this
doxology delivered to us from the seraphim, that we may become sharers of the
hymnody with the supercosmic armies. 5.7 Then having sanctified ourselves
through these spiritual hymns, we beseech the man-loving God to send forth the
Holy Spirit upon the things set forth, that he may make the bread body of

hold, this has been sanctified and changed. 5.8 Then after the spiritual
sacrifice has been completed, the bloodless worship, upon that sacrifice of
the propitiation, we beseech God for common peace of the churches, for the
good order of the world, for kings, for armies and allies, for those in
weaknesses, for those being worn down, and in a word for all who need help,
and all we praying offer this sacrifice. 5.9 Then we make mention also of
those who have fallen asleep, first of patriarchs, prophets, apostles,
martyrs, that God by their prayers and intercessions may receive our petition.
Then also for the holy fathers and bishops who have fallen asleep, and simply
all who have fallen asleep before us, believing there will be a very great
benefit to the souls, for whom the petition is offered up, the holy and most
fearful sacrifice being set forth. =5.10= And I wish to persuade you from an
example. For I know many saying this: What is a soul benefited, departing from
this world with sins, or not with sins, if it is remembered upon the offering?
For if some king should make exiles of those who have offended him, then those
belonging to these, having woven a crown, should offer this to him on behalf
of those in punishments, would he not give them a remission of the
chastisements? In the same way we also, offering the petitions to him on
behalf of those who have fallen asleep, even if they be sinners, do not weave
a crown, but we offer Christ slaughtered on behalf of our sins, propitiating
the man-loving God on behalf of them and of us. =5.11= Then after these things
you say that prayer which the Saviour delivered to his own disciples, with a
pure conscience inscribing God as Father and saying: "Our Father, who are in
the heavens." O the greatest love of man of God; to those who leaped away from
him and came to be in the last evils he has granted such an amnesty of evils
and a sharing of grace, as even to call upon Father. Our Father, who are in
the heavens. And heavens would be also those who bear the image of the
heavenly, in whom God is dwelling and walking about. =5.12= "Hallowed be your
name." Holy by nature is the name of God, whether we say it, or do not say it.
But since among those who sin it is sometimes profaned according to the
"Because of you my name is blasphemed always among the nations", we pray that
the name of God be hallowed in us; not that it comes from not being holy unto
being, but that it becomes holy in us who are being sanctified and doing
things worthy of the sanctification. =5.13= "Let your kingdom come." It
belongs to a pure soul to say with boldness: "Let your kingdom come". For the
one who has heard Paul saying: "Let sin therefore not reign in your mortal
body", but having cleansed himself in deed and thought and word will say to
God: "Let your kingdom come." =5.14= "Let your will be done, as in heaven,
also upon the earth." The divine and blessed angels of God do the will of God,
as David singing was saying: "Bless the Lord, all his angels, mighty in
strength, doing his word." In power then praying you say this: As in angels
your will comes about, so also upon the earth in me let it come about, Master.
=5.15= "Our bread, the epiousios, give us today." This common bread is not
epiousios; but this holy bread is epiousios, instead of being assigned upon
the substance of the soul. This bread does not go into the belly and is cast
out into a drain, but is distributed into all your constitution unto benefit
of both soul and body. And the today he says instead of daily, as also Paul
was saying: "Until the today is called." =5.16= "And forgive us our debts, as
we also forgive our debtors." We have many sins; for we stumble both in word
and in thought, and we do very many things worthy of condemnation. "And if we
say that we do not have sin, we lie", as John says. And we make contracts with
God, beseeching him to forgive us the sins, as we also forgive the debts to
those near. Thinking then in return for what sort of things we receive what
sort, let us not wait nor put off forgiving one another. The faults that come
about toward us are small and cheap and easily dissolved, but the things done
toward God by us are great, and we need his love of man alone. Attend then,
lest because of the small and cheap sins toward you you shut off from yourself
the forgiveness from God of the heaviest sins. =5.17= "And do not bring us
into temptation", Lord. Does the Lord then teach us to pray this, not to be
tempted at all? And how has it been said elsewhere: An untempted man is
unapproved, and again: "Count it all joy, my brothers, when you fall into
various temptations;"? But perhaps the entering into temptation is the being
sunk under the temptation; for the temptation resembles a sort of torrent hard
to cross. Those then who are not sunk in temptations cross over, becoming some
excellent swimmers and not being swept away by it at all. But those who are
not such, entering, are sunk; as, for example, Judas having entered into the
temptation of love of money did not swim through, but having been sunk was
choked both bodily and spiritually. Peter entered into the temptation of
denial, but having entered he was not sunk, but swam through nobly and was
delivered from the temptation. Hear again elsewhere a chorus of complete
saints giving thanks upon being taken out of the temptation: "You tested us, O
God, you fired us as silver is fired; you brought us into the snare; you
placed afflictions upon our back; you set men upon our heads. We went through
fire and water, and you brought us out into refreshment." You see them
speaking boldly upon having gone through and not having been stuck? "And you
brought us out into refreshment"; the coming into refreshment is the same as
to be delivered from temptation. =5.18= "But deliver us from the evil one." If
the "Do not bring us into temptation" were indicative of not being tempted at
all, he would not have said: "But deliver us from the evil one." And evil one
is the opposing demon, from whom we pray to be delivered. Then after the
completion of the prayer you say "Amen", sealing through the Amen, which
signifies "may it be", the things in the God-taught prayer. =5.19= After these
things the priest says: "The holy things to the holy." Holy are the things set
forth, having received a coming-upon of Holy Spirit; holy also are you, having
been deemed worthy of Holy Spirit. The holy things then are suitable to the
holy. Then you say: "One holy, one Lord, Jesus Christ". For truly one is holy,
holy by nature; for we, even if holy, yet not by nature, but by participation
and exercise and prayer. =5.20= After these things you hear the one singing
with a divine melody urging you toward the communion of the holy mysteries and
saying: "Taste and see, that the Lord is good." Do not entrust the judging to
the bodily throat, but to the unhesitating faith; for tasting you do not taste
bread and wine, but an antitype of body and blood of Christ. =5.21= Coming
forward then do not come with the palms of the hands stretched out, nor with
the fingers divided; but having made the left a throne for the right, as being
about to receive a King, and having hollowed the palm receive the body of
Christ, saying upon it "Amen". Having then with security sanctified the eyes
by the touching of the holy body partake, taking care that you do not lose any
of this; for whatever you lose, this you have been damaged as from your own
member. For tell me, if someone gave you grains of gold, would you not hold
them with all security, guarding lest you lose any of them and undergo loss?
Will you not then much more more securely than gold and more precious than
precious stones look about so that a crumb may not fall? =5.22= Then after you
have communicated of the body of Christ, come forward also to the cup of the
blood, not stretching out the hands, but bending, and in a manner of worship
and reverence saying the "Amen", be sanctified also partaking of the blood of
Christ. And while the moisture is still on the lips, touching with hands both
eyes and forehead and the remaining sense-organs, sanctify them. Then having
waited for the prayer, give thanks to God who has deemed you worthy of such
great mysteries. =5.23= Hold these traditions unspotted, and keep yourselves
without stumbling; do not tear yourselves away from the communion, lest
through a defilement of sin you deprive yourselves of these sacred and
spiritual mysteries. And may the God of peace sanctify you wholly, and may
your whole body and the soul and the spirit be kept at the coming of our Lord
Jesus Christ, to whom be the glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
