OF OUR FATHER AMONG THE SAINTS ATHANASIUS ARCHBISHOP
OF ALEXANDRIA TO MAXIMUS THE PHILOSOPHER.
To the beloved and truly most longed-for son Maximus the philosopher,
Athanasius greeting in the Lord. Having come upon the things now written by
you, I approved your piety, but having marveled much at the rashness of those
who understand neither what they say nor concerning what things they make
affirmation, I truly resolved to keep silence. For to answer concerning things
so evident and shining more than light is nothing other than to furnish
pretexts of shamelessness to those who so transgress. And this we have learned
from the Savior. For when Pilate had washed and had perceived the calumny of
the Jews of that time, the Lord no longer answered him, but rather gave an
oracle to his wife, in order that he who was being judged might be believed to
be God not in word but in power. And not answering Caiaphas toward his idle
talk, he himself by the promise brought all into knowledge. Having therefore
long delayed, and seeing the word-fight of those who act shamelessly, I barely
respected your zeal for the truth, and I dictated nothing more than the things
written by you, in order that, being persuaded at last by those things against
which the adversary has spoken, he may cease his tongue from evil, and his
lips from speaking guile. And may it be that such men no longer, together with
the Jews who then passed by, reproach him who was hanging upon the wood, If he
is the Son of God, let him save him; but if they should not even so be put to
shame, then you, remembering the apostolic charge, Reject a heretical man
after a first and second admonition, knowing that such a one is perverted, and
sins, being self-condemned. For if those who dare such things are Greeks or of
those who Judaize, let them, as =26.1088= Jews, consider the cross of Christ a
stumbling-block, and as Greeks, foolishness; but if they pretend as
Christians, let them learn that the crucified Christ is Lord of glory, and
power of God and wisdom of God. But if they doubt that he is also God, let
them reverence Thomas who handled the crucified one and said that he is Lord
and God. And let them fear the Lord himself, who after washing the feet of the
disciples said: You call me the Lord and the Teacher, and you say well; for I
am. And in the body in which he was when he washed the feet, in this he bore
our sins upon the wood. And he was testified to be master of the creation, the
sun drawing in its rays, and the earth trembling, and the rocks splitting, and
the executioners recognizing that truly the crucified one is the Son of God.
For the body that was seen was not of some man, but of God, in which being
even when he was being crucified, he raised the dead. Wherefore that daring of
theirs is not even good which says that the Word of God came to be in some
holy man (for this came to be in each of the prophets and of the other
saints), lest he should appear being born according to each and dying again.
But it is not so; may it not be! But once at the consummation of the ages, for
the putting away of sin, the Word himself became flesh, and from Mary the
Virgin he came forth a man according to our likeness, as he also said to the
Jews: Why do you seek to kill me, a man who has spoken the truth to you? And
not partaking of the body of some man, but receiving the body of the Word
himself, we are deified. I have certainly marveled also at that, how they have
dared even to think at all that he has been born a man by sequence of nature.
For if it were so, the mention of Mary would be superfluous. For nature does
not know a virgin giving birth without a man. Whence by the good pleasure of
the Father, being true God, and Word by nature, and wisdom of the Father, he
became man bodily, for our salvation, in order that, having what he might
offer on our behalf, he might save all of us who through fear of death were
all our lifetime subject to bondage. For it was not some man who gave himself
on our behalf; since every man is liable to death, according to, You are earth
and to earth you shall go, spoken in Adam toward all; nor yet some other of
the creatures; since every creature is liable to change. But the Word himself
offered his own body on our behalf, in order that the faith and the hope might
not be toward a man, but that we might have the faith toward God the Word
himself. Certainly even when he has become man, we behold his glory, glory as
of the Only-begotten from the Father, full of gra =26.1089= ce and truth. For
the things which he endured through the body, these as God he magnified; and
he hungered in flesh, but divinely he filled those who hungered. And if anyone
is scandalized at the bodily things, let him believe in those things which God
works. For he inquires in human fashion where Lazarus lies, but he raises him
divinely. Whence let no one laugh, saying child and naming age, and growth,
and eating, and drinking, and suffering, lest, denying the properties of the
body, he deny completely also his sojourn on our behalf. And just as he has
not been born a man by sequence of nature, so it was fitting that, having
taken a body, he should show the properties of this, lest the phantasy of the
Manichean should prevail. And again it was fitting that, appearing bodily, he
should not hide the things of the Godhead, lest the Samosatene should find a
pretext, calling him a man, as being another besides God the Word. Let the
unbeliever then, considering these things, learn that he was an infant upon a
manger, yet he subjected the magi, being worshipped by them; and as a child he
went down into Egypt, yet he abolished the handmade things of idolatry; and
crucified in flesh, he raised dead who had been rotting from a long time. And
it has been shown to all that he endured all things not for himself, but for
us, in order that we, having put on impassibility and incorruption by his
sufferings, might remain unto eternal life. And these things I dictated to you
briefly, having received them from your own, as I said before, working out
nothing more, but only making mention concerning the divine cross, in order
that in the things at which the careless were scandalized, being re-taught in
these they may worship the crucified one. But you genuinely persuade the
unbelievers; perhaps somehow they may come from ignorance into knowledge, and
believe well. And even if the things written by you are sufficient,
nevertheless it is good for the sake of reminder toward the contentious to add
these also; in order that they may not be ashamed as being convicted from the
things they have dared, but as being reminded may not forget the truth. For
let the things confessed at Nicaea by the Fathers prevail; for they are right
and sufficient to overturn every most impious heresy, and especially the
Arian, which blasphemes against the Word of God, and of necessity is impious
against his Holy Spirit. Greet all who think rightly. All who are with us
greet you.
