* 1
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Cultivate quietness in words, quietness in deeds, and likewise in tongue and
gait. Avoid rash vehemence; for thus the mind will remain steadfast, and will
not, becoming turbulent through vehemence, be weak and short in understanding
and seeing darkly, nor will it be overcome by gluttony, overcome by boiling
anger, overcome by the other passions, lying a ready prey for them. For the
mind must rule the passions, seated high on a quiet throne, looking toward
God. Be in no way filled with irascibility in bursts of anger, nor sluggish in
speech, nor filled with hesitation in your steps, so that a good rhythm may
adorn your quietness and your bearing may appear something godlike and sacred.
Guard also against the signs of pride, a high-necked posture and a lifted head
and a dainty and elevated tread of the feet.

* 2
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h2
:END:

Let your words toward those you meet be gentle, and your greetings sweet. Let
there be modesty toward women and a glance turned to the ground. Speak all
things with forethought, and give the voice what is useful, measuring the
utterance to the need of the hearers, until it is also audible, neither
escaping the hearing of those present through smallness, nor exceeding with a
greater shout. Take care that you never speak anything which you have not
first considered and thought out; nor readily and in the midst of the other's
words insert your own, for one must listen and converse in turn, dividing
speech and silence by time. Learn gladly, and teach ungrudgingly; never hide
wisdom from others through envy, nor stand back from learning through shame.
Yield to elders as to fathers. Honor the servants of God. Take the lead in
wisdom and virtue. Be not contentious toward friends, nor a mocker of them and
a jester. Firmly refuse falsehood and deceit and insolence. With seemly speech
endure even the proud and insolent, as a gentle and great-souled man.

* 3
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3
:END:

Let all things, both deeds and words, be set for you toward God, and refer all
that is yours to Christ, and often turn the soul toward God, and rest the
thought upon the power of Christ, as if in some harbor, in the divine light of
the Savior, resting from all speech and action. And by day often share your
understanding with men, but with God for the most part, by night likewise and
by day; for let not much sleep overpower you away from the prayers and hymns
toward God; for long sleep is a rival of death. Always become a partner of
Christ who sends the divine ray shining down from heaven; for let Christ be
for you a continual and unceasing joy.

* 4
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4
:END:

Do not loosen the tension of the soul in feasting and relaxation in drinks,
but consider what is needful enough for the body. And do not hurry to foods
before the time of dinner has also come. Let bread be your dinner, and let the
herbs of the earth be present, and the ripe fruits from trees. Go to food with
composure, not showing a raving gluttony. Be neither a flesh-eater nor a lover
of wine, unless some disease leads you to this as a cure. But instead of the
pleasures in these things, choose the joys in divine words and hymns, supplied
to you by the wisdom from God, and let a heavenly care always lead you up to
heaven.

* 5
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5
:END:

And let go the many cares about the body, taking courage in hopes toward God,
that He will provide you the necessities in sufficiency, food for life,
covering for the body, and defenses against winter cold. For the whole earth
belongs to your King, and all that grows from it. And as His own members He
tends His servants exceedingly, as His sacred things and temples. For this
reason do not fear excessive diseases, nor the onset of old age expected in
time; for disease too will cease, when with whole-souled purpose we do His
commandments.

* 6
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h6
:END:

Knowing these things, make the soul strong even toward diseases; take courage,
like some man best in the stadiums, to undergo the toils with unturned power.
Do not press the soul too much with grief, whether a disease lying upon you
weighs you down or some other hardship falls, but nobly set the thought
against the toils, offering thanks to God even in the midst of toilsome
affairs, inasmuch as He thinks things wiser than men, and such as it is
neither possible nor easy for men to find. Pity those who are afflicted, and
ask from God help for men; for He will grant the grace to His friend when he
asks, and will provide aid to those afflicted, wishing to make His power known
to men, so that coming to knowledge they may go up to God and enjoy eternal
blessedness, when the Son of God comes restoring good things to His own.
