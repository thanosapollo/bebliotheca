Of Leontius, presbyter of Constantinople, a discourse on Mid-Pentecost and on
the man born blind and on Saint Peter shut up by Herod in the prison.
For those who love the Lord there is no lack of a season of festival; for
already today too, as we keep festival, angels are glad, archangels leap, and
all the heavenly powers keep festival with us. We keep festival not because we
were redeemed from Egyptian slavery as children of the Jews, but because we
have been counted worthy of the spiritual banquet as children of Zion. We keep
festival not because we have been released from brick-making, but because we
enjoy the blessing. We keep festival not because Pharaoh has been sunk, but
because the devil has been made to vanish. We keep festival, to speak briefly,
not because Moses split the sea, but because the Master Christ has broken down
the middle wall of enmity, as one may hear the blessed Paul saying: For he is
our peace, who has made both one and has broken down the middle wall of the
partition. And what middle wall of partition did the Master Christ break down?
What? That which Adam built by disobedience, hiring as workman of the enmity
the earth-eating dragon.
For this reason we keep festival today, because the mediator Christ has broken
down the partition of enmity. For the present feast today is in the middle. In
the middle of what? Of the Master's resurrection and of the coming of the Holy
Spirit. It makes this known, and is not separated from that; it proclaims
this, and greets that; it theologizes that and names this in the proper sense;
for the Lord is the Spirit. Truly for those who love the Lord there is no lack
of a season of festival; for we are persuaded by Paul who says: So let us keep
festival not with old leaven, nor with the leaven of malice and wickedness,
but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth. And the leaven of
wickedness and malice the apostle does not mean that baked from
threshing-floor and wheat and flour, but that put forward from Jewish malice
and wickedness, that which is always turning sour in impiety, the unsalted and
useless as not possessing the salt of knowledge. For which festival of the
Jews has not become a cutting-off? And which of their assemblies has not
become a seizing? And which of their Paschas has not dug a chasm for the
righteous? For you just now heard the two evangelists John and Luke, the
genuine fellow-disciples, the God-bound brothers, the friends of one faith,
the spiritual orators, who have well divided the divine words. For Luke
composed not only the Gospel but also the Acts of the Apostles. Why? That we
might be made bright, and the Jews be put to shame. For even if they shut up
Peter, the chiefest of the apostles, as you just now heard, in a prison,
binding him with two chains and guarding him with four quaternions of
soldiers, that after the Pascha they might sacrifice him like a calf and tear
him in pieces, nevertheless Christ, who raised himself from the dead, who is
today being reproached by them as unlettered, also freed Peter from the prison
in a paradoxical way, that the leopard-beasts might know that, laboring in
vain, they have been found empty of hope.
For neither, having shut the Master Christ in a tomb, and having set the
military watch beside it, and having rolled a great stone upon the monument,
and having carved an artful seal, were they able to hold him, except so far as
he himself, having willed


granted it to them; nor, having shut his servant Peter in the prison, and
having bound him on every side, and having appointed soldiers to keep careful
watch, shuddering at the fisherman as at a barbarian, were they able to
become masters of the one they were eager for, always inheriting shame
instead of glory, whom also the prophet Isaiah, reproaching, said: Lord, your
arm is strong, and they did not know, and having known let them be put to
shame. For truly it is full of shame that Herod the king, and bonds and bars
and guards, and high priests and scribes and Pharisees and the whole
gathering of the Jews, were not able to keep one disciple of the Lord, a
private man, a poor man, a companion of fishes. What will the children of the
Jews say to these things, who just now too with shameless face and mouth
dared to say to the Lord: You have a demon; who seeks to kill you? Do you say
he has a demon, O Jews, whom your father the devil does not even endure to
see? The legion, being invisibly scourged by him and no longer able to bear
it, openly cried out the truth, saying: What have we to do with you, Jesus?
Have you come before the time to torment us? I know who you are, the Son of
God. The demon, being burned, makes his defense, and you, being benefited,
address the Lord Jesus Christ as one possessed? To whom did the legion of
demons say: If you cast us out, permit us to go into the herd of swine, to
Solomon who built Jerusalem, or to the Master Christ who bears all things in
his hand? But at once the demon-loving Jews will say: What then? Did Solomon
not lord it over the demons? Did he not shut them all under one as one? Did
he not bind them with Judaizing characters? Do they not fear him even to this
day? But O Jews, stitchers of leaves, demon-sorcerers, in vain you put these
things forward; for the Master Christ alone bound the strong one mightily and
plundered his vessels. For Solomon not only did not lord it over the demons
in kingly fashion, but was even lorded over by them, being corrupted toward
the end. For having loved the passion of polygamy, being baited by the
devil's pimping, and having neighed like a mare-mad horse into a herd of
foreign women, he defiled the bridal chamber of the knowledge of God. For
having left the God of his fathers, for whom also he built the temple, he was
constructing caves for idols. How then is he master of demons who is a slave
of demons? O the Jewish malice, O the long-suffering of the Master Christ.
Being reproached he exhorts, being reviled he receives, being struck he sets
free, being crucified he prays for those who crucify, he gives eyes to the
man born blind, and he is slandered as a sinner. For do you not know, bearing
the memory as a sanctuary, that the children of the Jews, who are blind in
soul, cried out the day before to the one who had received his sight in body,
saying: Give glory to God; we know that this man is a sinner? O Jewish
doorless lips. Is Christ a sinner? And how is he a sinner who takes away sin?
When does a slave set a slave free? When does one under sentence redeem one
under sentence? When does a debtor tear up a debtor's bond? A free man sets a
slave free; not, surely, a slave a slave? One without liability redeems a
condemned man; not, surely, a condemned man a condemned man? A man of means
tears up a debtor's bond; not, surely, a prisoner looses a prisoner? And what
evidence of sin-making did you behold in the Master Christ? The change of
water into wine? This is a command of divinity, not a belch of sin. Or the
calling of Lazarus from the dead? This too he authored as life-giver, and did
not babble as a sinner. And if we are silent, O Jews, take as guide of your
error the blind man himself. Do not be ashamed to be led by the blind man;
for he does not throw you into a pit, but sets you upon a rock; for he has a
shining, unquenchable lamp of


faith. What then did the blind man say to the Jews? It has not been heard, he
says, since the world began that anyone opened the eyes of one born blind. O
the paradoxical things. The unlettered man becomes a teacher of the lawyers,
saying to them with a tongue of freedom: In vain you rest upon the law. Do
you not call Abraham righteous, and Moses and Elijah and Elisha and all the
prophets righteous, but Christ a sinner? Which of the righteous among you
gave eyes to one born blind? And if none of those was able to do this, but
the one famed as a sinner has outshot all those, do you wish by envy to cover
the wonder done upon me? And the Jews to the blind man: You were wholly born
in sins, and do you teach us? Do you insult the prophets, you spurious blind
man? Did Elijah the prophet not raise a dead man? Did Elisha likewise not
accomplish the like of his? Did Moses, the greatest prophet, not make the
navigable sea dry land? Did he not suckle manna from heaven? Did he not open
the flanks of a barren rock? Did he not prepare the sea to vomit quail? Cease
then, blind man, cease; are these not greater than your imagined receiving of
sight? And the blind man to the Jews: Your rhetorical overthrowing is
contradicted; you boast in vain. You have not to do with an uneducated man;
what the sight lost, the hearing made up. Moses and Elijah and Elisha, on
whom you rest and in whom you take pride because they worked wonders, I too
know, but nevertheless having prayed, having besought, having wept, having
groaned, having needed time, they received the gift of the grace; but the
present Jesus, who by you is thought a sinner, but by me is known as God,
having power running with the will, by will alone made up what was lacking.
What then did the Pharisees say to the blind man? For great was the
contradiction that took place at that time, which I have not the power to set
out in the present because of the narrowness of the time and the desire for
the matters at hand. Therefore, having subscribed myself your debtor in the
present, I will go on to the matter at hand. [What then did the Pharisees say
to the once-blind man? If you name as God the one who gave you eyes, how did
he only smear mud on your eyes and send you to Siloam to wash? So Siloam
healed you, not the one who sent you there whom you call God. And the blind
man to the Jews: Not Siloam, but the one who sent me healed me; for Siloam is
interpreted 20the sent20. And if because I was sent to Siloam and washed
there you ascribe the wonder to Siloam and not to the one who sent me, of
necessity you must also ascribe the wonder done by Elisha, when he sent
Naaman the Syrian to the river Jordan saying: Wash and be clean, to the river
Jordan and not to Elisha who commanded this. And if you wish, he says, O
Jews, to learn more exactly, it was not through any weakness of his own but
for your full assurance that the Master Christ sent me to Siloam.
For just as, having bound up the paralytic, he commanded him to carry the bed
upon his shoulders, so that when the bed was seen the wonder might not be
able to be stolen, so also here, having smeared mud on the eyes, he commanded
me to run to Siloam, so that when more witnesses had run down you might not
be able to slander the truth. It is timely then henceforth to proceed to the
matter at hand.] For you just now heard the evangelist John crying out: In
the middle of the feast Jesus went up into the temple and taught, and all the
Jews marveled, saying: How does this man know letters, not having learned? O
Jewish wonder, filled with condemnation. How does he know letters, not having
learned letters, he says? Does the maker of letters need these? Did he who
turns the tongue on the lathe need to attend a teacher? Does he who drips


wisdom need schooling? Does he who spread out the folds of the heavens need
tablets? Did he who inscribed the tablets with a finger need to hold a pen?
How does this man know letters, not having learned? Leaving the greater
things you come to the least; if you wish to search out the unsearchable, say
first how he gave eyes to the man born blind. Did he bare a whetstone, or
dissolve an eye-salve, or display a probe, or wet a sponge, or shave the head
of the maimed man, that he might check the flux? Did he regulate him with a
light diet? Did he give him an antidote, that he might first purge the bile?
Did he perform a scraping of trachoma? Did he puncture a clouding? Did he
not, by a strange and harmful and contrary method, so far as it lies with
men, give him eyes; for he anointed the eyes with mud only. And mud blinds
even those who see, and does not give eyes to those who do not see. As then,
O Jews, having healed the man born blind without medical art he displayed the
self-authority of his divine power, so also here without rhetorical mythology
and sophistic entanglement and poetic vain labor and grammatical curiosity
and astronomical babbling he taught those present, that also in the case of
the blind man those present might learn that he is the one who took dust from
the earth and molded Adam in clay, and here they might learn clearly after so
great an education that he is the one who said through the prophet: Open your
mouth and I will fill it. What then did the Lord? Answering the Jews he said,
as you just now heard: Why do you seek to kill me? Did Moses not give you the
law? And none of you does the law. And the Jews to the Lord: Do we not do the
law? Is it not for this that we wish to kill you, because you loose the
sabbath, binding up paralytics and giving eyes to the blind? And you say we
do not do the law? And the Lord to them: This is not to do the law, but to
insult the law. For if a man, he says, is circumcised on the sabbath, that
the law of Moses may not be loosed, are you angry with me because I made a
whole man healthy on the sabbath, and opened the blind windows of a rational
being? What is more necessary on the sabbath, O Jews, to circumcise flesh or
to bind up a paralytic? to amputate foreskin or to set a soul free from sins?
to sharpen a sword or to give eyes to a blind man? to assemble an unlawful
court or to multiply a universal grace? If you circumcise a man on the
sabbath, that the law may not be loosed, are you angry with me because I made
a whole man healthy on the sabbath? Do not judge according to appearance, but
judge the righteous judgment. O the paradoxical things. God says to men
concerning himself: Judge for me the righteous judgment. He draws his enemies
as judges of his own wonder-working, saying to them: Do not judge according
to appearance, but judge the righteous judgment. Do not, as in the case of
Susanna, condemn the guiltless; for I there too raised up Daniel, that he
might punish the guilty and set the unliable free. Do not judge according to
appearance, but judge the righteous judgment. Do not, as in the case of
Naboth, stone me unlawfully, lest you undergo as wage of the lawlessness,
like Ahab, a whorish bathing in blood.
Do not judge according to appearance, but judge the righteous judgment. For
you have always judged amiss, and now judge righteously. Did not the prophet
Micah, reproaching you, say: Hear now these things, you who lead the house of
Jacob and the remnant of the house of Israel, who abhor judgment and pervert
all that is right? Do not judge according to appearance, but judge the
righteous judgment; for the bones of men-pleasers shall perish. Do not judge
according to appearance, but judge the righteous judgment. Why do you move
the lips toward slander? Why do you sharpen the tongue toward informing? Why
do you forge the mouth against the unliable? Did I not tell you that even
concerning an idle word you must be made to pay


the penalty? Why do you delight the devil and condemn yourselves? Do not
judge according to appearance, but judge the righteous judgment. Where there
is hymnody, is there flesh-eating there? What did David cry out, saying: My
mouth shall speak wisdom? Not, surely, "My mouth shall speak slander"? And
the meditation of my heart understanding. Not, surely, "calumny"? What is
more honorable to hear from the Holy Spirit, The mouth of the wise shall be
sought in the assembly, or "Whose mouth has multiplied injustice"? What is
better to hear, The mouth of the righteous drips wisdom, or Whose mouth is
full of cursing and bitterness? What is more profitable to hear, A true mouth
shall fill with laughter, or A lying mouth destroys a soul? What is more
honorable for you to say to the Lord, My lips shall praise you, or Our lips
are our own; who is lord over us? Be persuaded then by the one who has said:
Guard against unprofitable murmuring and spare the tongue from backbiting,
because a stealthy utterance shall not pass empty, and a lying mouth destroys
a soul. Do not judge according to appearance, but judge the righteous
judgment, between me and Moses. For Moses through circumcision sets the law
aside, loosing law by law, that the infant may be circumcised; but I both
fulfill the law and honor the grace, for I neither sharpen a craftsman's
sword nor seek a trader's wage. Do not judge according to appearance, but
judge the righteous judgment. Why do you seek to kill me? You are troubled in
vain; I do not yet wish to suffer; when I will I suffer, not when you forge
the nails. I am pursuing an economy; am I, then, undergoing necessity? I
humbled myself; no one took me as one handed over. And you heard also the
evangelist John advocating the same things for me; for what did he add,
saying? They sought to seize him and no one laid hands on him, because his
hour had not yet come. What does he mean to say, his hour had not yet come?
That is, he had not yet fulfilled the things that seemed good to him. Let no
one suppose, hearing the evangelist crying out that his hour had not yet
come, that the hour is the cause, as the children of the Greeks talk vanity,
that for each of men both the manner of death and the day and the hour and
how one must die have been appointed, and that it is not possible for anyone
to die unless the sentence of fate comes. Away with the absurdity. Such a
tale is old-womanish and exceedingly silly and an adversary of the truth. For
if for each the manner of death and the day and the hour and how one must die
have been appointed, and it is not possible to escape, why when we are sick
do we draw medical help, that we may the sooner cut off what distresses? and
why when sailing do we seek harbors and watch a star? and why when traveling
do we turn aside from places of robbers? and why when preparing to war
against enemies do we weave shields and sharpen arrows and raise siege-works,
if it has been appointed for each and it is not possible for anyone to
escape? And otherwise, why do we abhor Cain, if such a manner of death had
been appointed for Abel? And why do we kill the murderers, if it was owed to
those men to die thus? And why do we pray to receive a length of years and
not to fall into temptation? Is it not because the Master Christ is true, who
has said concerning the faithful: I will fill him with length of days and I
will show him my salvation, and again: Call upon me in the day of your
affliction, and I will deliver you, and you shall glorify me? For do we not
know clearly from the things just now read out to us in the Acts of the
Apostles that Peter, the chief of the apostles, having called upon the Lord
in affliction in the prison, obtained help from him? For as soon as the
blessed Peter cried out in affliction, at once the Lord, having sent an angel
from above, the Angel of the great counsel of the Father, illumined Peter's
prison, as he also bedewed the furnace of the three children. For it is one
and the same Lord, who there too showed the coals as roses and here tore the
bonds; who there too smashed the image


and here made impiety vanish; who there too put Nebuchadnezzar to shame and
here struck Herod; who there too burned the Babylonians together and here
took the Jews captive. And what is the beginning of the reading? It is
necessary to set some things out, and to store others for another time. For
you just now heard the writer Luke saying: About that time Herod the king
laid hands to afflict some of those from the church. And he killed James the
brother of John with the sword. And seeing that it was pleasing to the Jews
he proceeded to seize Peter also, whom also having taken he put in prison,
delivering him to four quaternions of soldiers to keep him, wishing after the
Pascha to bring him up to the people.
Truly we keep festival today, because we behold not only the Master's
contests but also those of the martyrs; James is slain and John is glad,
Peter is imprisoned and the church prays, the bonds are torn and the devil is
torn asunder, the Pascha of the Jews has been fulfilled and the one sought is
not found, Herod condemns the guards and Peter announces the wonder, the iron
gate was opened and the Jewish heart was not opened. About that time Herod
the king laid hands. What time? That in which the kingly name of the Savior
granted running to the lame, by handkerchiefs drove out diseases, by shadows
sponged away fevers, wove martyrs' crowns, adorned James with a martyr's
crown, showed John a theologian, kept Peter a noble wrestler. About that time
Herod the king laid hands. What time? When carved images fell from their base
and idols had no strength to mock, when altars were dug up and priests were
cut down, when ventriloquists were muzzled and the sorceries of amulets were
thrown away. About that time Herod the king laid hands. What time? That in
which the synagogue was being dissolved and the church was being bound
together, that in which the lawyers were at a loss and the apostles were well
supplied, that in which the circumcision of the flesh was being diminished
and the seal of the heart was being multiplied, that in which the unleavened
bread was wasting and the heavenly bread was blooming, that in which the
seven-wicked lamp was being quenched and the twelve-rayed cross was being
kindled. About that time Herod the king laid hands to afflict some of those
from the church. O a king's prudence, or rather a tyrant's attempt. For what
sort of king is he who is not in the hand of God, but in the counsel of the
devil? Do you hasten to murder Peter, O Herod? Why? Did he plot rebellion?
Did he fashion a diadem? Did he acquire purple? Did he multiply treasuries of
money? Did he hire hoplites? Did he raise strong cities? Peter is naked and
poor in bodily things; he left even his ancestral nets and the fishing-boat
and despised the catch of fishes. He was persuaded by Christ who became poor
for us, saying: Do not acquire bronze or a wallet or a purse, nor a double
tunic. O Herod, why, leaving the barbarians, do you war against the apostles?
Why, idle from siege-works, do you dig through the church? Why do you stretch
out your hands, not that you may seize a tyrant's village, but that you may
overturn an altar of the King Christ? About that time Herod the king laid
hands to afflict some of those from the church. O hands of Herod not
stretched out to prayer, but armed for the killing of the righteous. For
these hands, as you know, were armed against the pillar of the church. Seeing
these hands Peter cried out and prayed in the prison, speaking the words of
David: Do not destroy my soul with the ungodly and my life with men of blood,
in whose hands are lawlessnesses; their right hand was filled with gifts.
If Herod had not received a wage


from the Jews, he would not have sharpened the sword of lawlessness; he
killed James with the sword, he bound Peter with two chains, wishing after
the Pascha to bring him up to the people. O Jewish Pascha, always mixed among
innocent bloods. They crucified the Master Christ before the Pascha, they
wished to kill his disciple Peter after the Pascha. O unclean Pascha, O
synagogue fond of drinking blood. Well did Solomon from many years ago call
the synagogue of the Jews a leech. Why? As a drawer of others' bloods.
Wishing after the Pascha to bring him up to the people. And why not at the
Pascha? They honor, forsooth, the feast of the Pascha. O honor filled with
insult. If it is not permitted to murder at the Pascha, do not bind at the
Pascha either. Do you think you honor the feast while treasuring up the
murder? Do you hold the victim and think you do not sacrifice? O piety filled
with condemnation, or rather O lawlessness kept even until now among some,
having taken its beginning from there and not seeking to find an end. For do
we not know that often some of those examined in power, having shut some
unliable man in a public prison, as soon as the honorable Pascha is at hand,
at once, forsooth, they release him from the public prison, devising another
harsher prison, either keeping him with themselves and choking him with
famine, or shutting him in the house of the exactor who gapes insatiably, not
considering that this is not to honor the feast, but to insult the Master
Christ. Do you wish to keep festival? Set your fellow-servant free from the
slander; for then one keeps festival in the proper sense, not when he
inaugurates a silken robe, but when he will set a distressed soul free. But
to God be the grace unto the ages of ages. Amen.
