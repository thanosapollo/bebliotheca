ON THE HOLY PENTECOST, And why signs do not happen now, and that the things
done and said among us are written down.
=50.453= Homily 1.
αʹ. Again a feast, and again a festival, and again the Church blooms with the
multitude of her children, this many-childed and child-loving one. But what is
the profit of the love of children, when only at feasts, and not continually,
she sees the longed-for faces of her little ones, as if someone having a
beautiful garment were not allowed to use it continually? For the garment of
the Church is the multitude of those who come, just as the prophet also says
from of old, speaking to the Church: You shall put them all about you as a
bridegroom's ornament, and as a bride's robe.
Just as therefore a temperate and free woman, having her tunic let down as far
as the ankles, appears more seemly and better; so also the Church is shown
more radiant today, wrapping herself in the multitudes of your bodies, and
having her garment let down. For no part of her today is to be seen naked, as
in the former days; but those who have come today only, who do not always
clothe the mother, are the cause of that nakedness. And that it is no small
danger to overlook the mother naked, let us recall an ancient history; let us
recall the one who saw the father naked, and who paid a punishment for that
sight. And yet that one did not make the father naked; but he only saw the
father being naked; and not even so was he freed from vengeance, since he only
saw; but those who have come today, and did not come before this, do not see
the mother naked, but make her naked. And if he who saw the nakedness did not
escape vengeance, of what pardon would those who make the nakedness be worthy?
I say these things not wishing to strike, but that we may flee the vengeance,
that we may flee the curse of Ham, that we may imitate the goodwill of Shem
and of Japheth, and that we too may always clothe our mother.
It is of a Jewish mind to appear to God only three times in the year; to those
it was said, Three times in the year you shall appear before the Lord your
God; but God wishes us to appear to him continually. And for those, the
distances of the places made only so many assemblies take place; for the
worship was then shut up into one place; therefore also the times of their
gathering and presence were few in number; for it was necessary to worship in
Jerusalem, and nowhere else. Therefore he commanded them to appear to God
three times in the year; and the distance of the road made excuse for them;
but for us no manner of excuse will plead. And those were scattered everywhere
upon the =50.454= earth.
For there were, he says, dwelling in Jerusalem Jews, devout men from every
nation under heaven; but we all inhabit one city, we sit under the same walls,
and often we are not even separated from the church by a single alley, and
like those divided by long seas, so rarely do we come to this sacred assembly.
And those he commanded to keep feast at three times only, but you he commanded
always to do this; for it is always a feast for us. And that you may learn
that it is always a feast, I tell the grounds of the feasts, and you will know
that there is a feast each day. Therefore among us the first feast is the
Epiphany. What then is the ground of the feast? Because God appeared upon the
earth, and consorted with men;


because God the only-begotten Child of God was with us; but this is always.
For behold, he says, I am with you all the days until the consummation of the
age; therefore it is possible to keep the Epiphany all the days. What does the
feast of Pascha wish? What is its ground? We proclaim the death of the Lord
then; and this is the Pascha; but not even this do we do at a defined time.
For Paul, wishing to free us from the necessity of times, and showing that it
is possible always to keep Pascha, For as often as you eat this bread, he
says, and drink this cup, you proclaim the death of the Lord. Since therefore
we can always proclaim the death of the Lord, we can always keep Pascha. Do
you wish to learn that this present feast also can be fulfilled each day, or
rather is each day? Let us see what the ground of the present feast is, and
for what cause we keep it. Because the Spirit came to us; for just as the
only-begotten Son of God is with the faithful men, so also is the Spirit of
God. Whence is it clear? He who loves me, he says, will keep my commandments,
and I will ask my Father, and he will give you another Paraclete, that he may
remain with you unto the age, the Spirit of truth. As therefore Christ said
concerning himself, that Behold I am with you all the days until the
consummation of the age, and we can always keep the Epiphany; so also
concerning the Spirit he said that he is with you unto the age, and we can
always keep Pentecost.
βʹ. And that you may learn that it is permitted us always to keep feast, and
there is no defined time, nor are we shut up by a necessity of season, hear
what Paul says: =50.455= So let us keep the feast. And yet it was not then a
feast, when he wrote these things; it was not Pascha, it was not Epiphany, it
was not Pentecost; but showing that it is not time that makes a feast, but a
clean conscience; for a feast is nothing else but joy; and spiritual and
intellectual joy nothing else makes but a conscience of good deeds; and he who
has a good conscience and such deeds can always keep feast. Showing this very
thing then, Paul said: So let us keep the feast not with old leaven, nor with
the leaven of malice and wickedness, but with unleavened bread of sincerity
and truth. Do you see how he did not bind you with a necessity of times, but
exhorted you to have a clean conscience? I wished to spend the whole discourse
upon this; for those who after a time have taken some into their own hands do
not easily let them go; since therefore we too have taken you who come once in
the year inside our nets, we do not wish to let you go today, but that you may
not withdraw empty of the words concerning the feast, it is necessary to lead
the discourse from this exhortation to the ground of the feast. Many good
things then have often come down from heaven to earth for the common race of
men; but such as today, never were brought down before this. Learn then of
what sort the former were, of what sort those of today, that you may see the
difference of both.
God rained manna upon the earth, and gave them bread of heaven; for man ate
the bread of angels; great indeed and worthy of the philanthropy of God. After
that, fire was brought down, and corrected the wandering Jewish people, and
snatched the sacrifice from the altar; rain was brought down again when all
were wasting with famine, and it worked much plenty. These things are great
and marvelous; but the present things are far greater; for not manna and fire
and rain were brought down today, but a shower of spiritual gifts; snowflakes
were brought from above, not raising the earth to fruit-bearing, but
persuading human nature to give back the fruit of virtue to the husbandman of
men. And those who received a drop from there immediately forgot their own
nature, and suddenly all the earth was filled with angels; angels not
heavenly, but in a human body displaying the virtue of the bodiless


powers. For those did not come down below, but what was more marvelous, those
below went up to the virtue of those; for they did not throw off the flesh and
go about with naked souls, but remaining in the nature, they became angels by
choice. And that you may learn that not even the former punishment was a
punishment, when he said that You are earth, and to earth you shall go, for
this he left you to remain in the earth, that the power of the Spirit might be
shown more greatly, working such things through an earthy body. For one could
see a tongue of clay commanding demons; one could see a hand of clay healing
diseases; or rather not only a hand of clay could one see, but what was far
more marvelous, shadows of bodies of clay prevailing over both death and
bodiless powers, I mean the demons. For just as when the sun has appeared the
darkness is driven out, beasts sink down to their own dens, murderers and
robbers and tomb-breakers run up to the peaks of the mountains; =50.456= so
when Peter appeared and sent forth a voice, the darkness of error was driven
out, the devil withdrew, demons ran away, sufferings of bodies were taken
away, diseases of souls were healed, all wickedness was driven out, virtue was
brought back to the earth. And just as from royal treasuries having gold and
precious stones, if someone is able to bring out even a little of what is
stored, gloriously, even one stone only, he works much wealth for the one who
holds it; so also from the apostolic mouths; for those mouths were royal
treasuries having a treasure of healings stored; and each word going out
worked much spiritual wealth. Then it was truly possible to see that the
oracles of the Lord are desirable above gold and much precious stone; for what
gold could not, nor precious stone, this the words of Peter did. For how many
talents of gold were able to set right the man lame from birth? But the word
of Peter was able to take away this maiming of nature. He said, In the name of
Jesus Christ rise,and walk, and the word became a deed.
Did you see how they are desirable above gold and much precious stone? Did you
see how those mouths were royal treasuries? Truly those were physicians of the
inhabited world, and husbandmen, and pilots; physicians, because they healed
diseases; husbandmen, because they sowed the word of piety; pilots, because
they stopped the surge of error. Therefore at one time he says: Going, heal
the sick, as conversing with physicians; and at another, Behold I send you to
reap that for which you have not labored, as conversing with husbandmen; and
again elsewhere: I will make you fishers of men; and to Peter, Fear not, he
says, from now on you will be catching men, as speaking to pilots and fishers;
and one could see wonders upon wonders. For our nature ten days ago went up to
the royal throne, and the Holy Spirit came down today to our nature; the Lord
brought up our firstfruits, and brought down the Holy Spirit. Another Lord
distributing these gifts; for the Spirit also is Lord, and Father and Son and
Holy Spirit divided the economy on our behalf. Christ having gone up had not
yet ten days, and he sent us spiritual gifts, gifts of that reconciliation.
For that no one might doubt and be at a loss what then Christ might have done
having gone up; did he reconcile the Father? did he make him gracious? wishing
to make clear to us that he reconciled him to our nature, he sent us the gifts
of the reconciliation at once. For when enemies are united and reconciled to
one another, after the reconciliation at once there follow the loving-cups and
greetings and the gifts. We then sent faith, and received gifts from there; we
sent obedience, and received righteousness. =50.457= γʹ. And that you may
learn that the giving of the Holy Spirit is a gift of the reconciliation of
God, I will try to persuade you from the Scriptures, first from the contrary


making the word clear, and showing that he withholds the grace of the Spirit,
whenever he is angry with us; that when you have been persuaded that a proof
of his wrath is that there is no Holy Spirit, and you see again Spirit being
sent down, you may learn that if he had not been reconciled, he would not have
sent Holy Spirit. Whence then shall we know this? Eli was an old man, in other
things reasonable and temperate, but not knowing how to set right the
wickedness of children, but loving the offspring beyond measure. Hear, as many
as have children, so as to make the love and the respect measured. For Eli
from this having provoked God, threw him into so great wrath, that he turned
away from the whole nation. Showing then, the one who wrote these things, that
God had utterly turned away from them, said: The word was precious, and there
was no vision distinguishing, calling precious here the rare; for through
these he made clear that the things of prophecy were then scarce.
Again another lamenting and wailing over the wrath of God said: There is no
ruler, nor prophet in this time; and again the evangelist says: For the Holy
Spirit was not yet, because Jesus was not yet glorified. For since he had not
yet been crucified, he says, the Holy Spirit was not yet given among men; for
the, he was crucified, is He was glorified For even if the thing is by nature
a reproach, yet since it took place for those who were loved, Christ calls it
glory. And for what reason, tell me, was the Spirit not given before the
cross? Because the inhabited world was in sins, in offenses, and enmity, and
dishonor; the lamb that takes away the sin of the world not yet having been
offered. Since therefore Christ had not yet been crucified, reconciliation had
not yet taken place; and the reconciliation not yet having taken place,
reasonably neither was the Spirit sent, so that the Spirit having been sent is
a proof of reconciliation. Therefore Christ also says: It is expedient for you
that I go away; for if I do not go away, that one will not come; if I do not
go away, and reconcile the Father, he says, I do not send you the Paraclete.
Did you see through how many things we have shown you that a sign of the wrath
of God is that there is no Holy Spirit among men? The word was precious, and
there was no vision distinguishing; For there is no ruler nor prophet in this
time; For the Holy Spirit was not yet, because Jesus was not yet glorified. It
is expedient for you that I go away; for if I do not go away, that one will
not come. Therefore a sign of the wrath of God is that there is no Holy
Spirit. When you see Holy Spirit being sent down abundantly, doubt no longer
concerning the reconciliation. And where, he says, is Holy Spirit now? For
then it is well said, when signs happened, and dead were raised, and all
lepers were cleansed; but now whence shall we show that the Holy Spirit is
present with us? Do not fear; for I demonstrate that even now there is Holy
Spirit in us. How and in what manner?
For if there is not Holy Spirit in us, how would these who were enlightened on
this sacred night have been freed from sins? for it is not possible to be
freed from sins =50.458= without the operation of the Spirit. Hear then Paul
saying: For we too were once foolish, disobedient, wandering, serving various
desires; but when the kindness and the philanthropy of God our Savior
appeared, not from works of righteousness which we did, but according to his
mercy he saved us through the washing of regeneration and renewal of Holy
Spirit; and again elsewhere: Do not be deceived; neither fornicators, nor
idolaters, nor adulterers, nor the effeminate, nor those who lie with males,
nor thieves, nor the greedy, nor drunkards, nor revilers, nor rapacious shall
inherit the kingdom of God. Did you see all the forms of wickedness? And such
were some of you, but you were washed, but you were sanctified, but you were
justified. How? for this is what is sought, whether through the Holy Spirit we
put off the wickedness. Therefore hear:


But you were sanctified, but you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus,
and in the Spirit of our God. Did you see that the Holy Spirit wiped out all
that wickedness?
δʹ. Where now are those who blaspheme the dignity of the Spirit? For if he
does not remit sins, he is taken into baptism in vain; but if he remits sins,
he is blasphemed in vain by the heretics. If there were not Holy Spirit, we
would not be able to say Lord Jesus; For no one can say Lord Jesus except in
the Holy Spirit. If there were not Holy Spirit, we the faithful would not be
able to call upon God; for we say, Your Father who art in the heavens. As
therefore we would not be able to call him Lord, so neither would we be able
to call God Father. Whence is it clear? From the same apostle saying, And
because you are sons, God sent forth the Spirit of his Son into your hearts
crying, Abba, Father. So that when you call the Father, remember that, the
Spirit moving the soul, you were counted worthy of that address. If there were
not Spirit, there would not be a word of wisdom and of knowledge in the
Church; For to one is given through the Spirit a word of wisdom, and to
another a word of knowledge. If there were not Holy Spirit, there would not be
shepherds and teachers in the Church; for these too come to be through the
Spirit, as Paul also says: In which the Holy Spirit set you as shepherds and
bishops.
Do you see that this too is through the Spirit? If there were not Holy Spirit
in this common father and teacher, he would not, when a little before he went
up upon this sacred bema, and gave peace to you all, and you all cried out to
him in common, And to your spirit; therefore you utter this saying not only
when he goes up, nor when he discourses to you, nor when he prays for you, but
when he stands at this sacred table, when he is about to offer that fearful
sacrifice; for the initiated know what is said; he does not first touch the
things set forth, until he himself has prayed over you the grace from the
Lord, and you have cried out to him, And to your spirit, through that very
answer =50.459= reminding them that he who is present does nothing himself,
nor are the gifts set forth achievements of human nature, but the grace of the
Spirit being present and hovering over all prepares that mystical sacrifice.
For even if he who is present is a man, yet it is God who works through him.
Do not then attend to the nature of what is seen, but consider the invisible
grace. Nothing of the things done upon this sacred bema is human. If Spirit
were not present, the Church would not stand; but if the Church stands, it is
quite clear that the Spirit is present. For what reason then, he says, do
signs not happen now? Here give me attention with accuracy; for I hear this
from many, and it is sought continually and always; why then did all who were
baptized speak in tongues, but now no longer? Let us learn first what speaking
in tongues is, and then we will tell the cause also. What then is speaking in
tongues? The one baptized immediately spoke with the voice of the Indians,
that of the Egyptians, that of the Persians, that of the Scythians, that of
the Thracians, and one man received many tongues, and these who now if they
had been baptized then, you would immediately have heard them speaking in
different voices. For Paul, he says, found some baptized in the baptism of
John, and says to them: Did you receive Holy Spirit having believed? They say
to him: but we have not even heard whether there is Holy Spirit; and
immediately he commanded them to be baptized; And when Paul had laid his hands
on them, the Holy Spirit came upon them, and they all spoke in tongues. For
what reason then has this grace been contracted, and taken away from men now?
Not because God dishonors us, but even greatly honoring us. How? I tell you.
Men were more foolish then, recently


freed from the idols, and their mind was still thicker and more unfeeling, and
they were fluttered and gaping toward all bodily things, and they had not yet
any thought of bodiless gifts, nor did they know what intelligible grace is,
and that which is seen by faith only; therefore signs happened.
For of the spiritual gifts some are invisible, and are grasped by faith only;
others also display a sensible sign toward the full assurance of the
unbelievers. What I mean is this: remission of sins is an intelligible thing,
it is an invisible gift; for how our sins are cleansed, we do not see with the
eyes of the flesh. Why then? Because it is a soul that is cleansed; and a soul
is not seen with eyes of body. The cleansing of sins then is some intelligible
gift not able to become apparent to eyes of body; but speaking in different
tongues is itself also of an intelligible operation of the Spirit; yet it
furnishes the sensible sign, and one easily seen by the unbelievers. For of
the operation taking place within in the soul, I mean the invisible, the outer
tongue being heard is a certain manifestation and proof. Therefore Paul also
says: But to each the manifestation of the Spirit is given toward what is
profitable. I then now have no need of signs. For what reason? Because even
without a giving of a sign I have learned to believe the Master. For the
unbeliever has need of a pledge; but I believing have no need of a pledge nor
of a sign; but even if I do not speak in a tongue, I know that I have been
cleansed from the sins. But those then did not believe, unless they received a
sign; therefore =50.460= signs were given them, as a pledge of the faith which
they believed.
So that the signs were given not as to believers, but as to unbelievers, that
they might become believers; so also Paul says: The signs are not for those
who believe, but for the unbelievers. Do you see that it is not of God
dishonoring us, but honoring us, to have contracted the display of the signs?
For wishing to show our faith, that without pledges and signs we believe him,
he has done this; for those, unless they first received a sign and pledge,
would not have believed him concerning the unseen things; but I even without
this display all faith; this then is the cause of signs not happening now.
εʹ. I wished also to speak concerning the ground of the feast, and to show
what Pentecost is, and why the gift is given in this feast, and why in fiery
tongues, and why after ten days; but I see the teaching stretching out to
length; therefore having added a few things I will stop the word; When the day
of Pentecost was being fulfilled there appeared to them tongues distributing
themselves as of fire; not of fire, but as of fire, that you may suspect
nothing sensible concerning the Spirit. For just as upon the Jordan streams it
was not a dove that came down, but in the form of a dove; so also here not
fire, but in the form of fire. And again above he says: As of a violent wind
being borne; not of a violent wind, but as of a violent wind being borne. And
for what reason does Ezekiel not receive the gift of prophecy through a
likeness of fire, but through a book, but the apostles receive the gifts
through fire? For concerning that one he says that he gave into his mouth a
roll of a book, and there was written upon it lamentation and melody and woe,
and it was written behind and before; and he ate it, and it became in the
mouth as honey sweetening.
But concerning the apostles not so; but There appeared to them tongues as of
fire. For what reason then there a book and letters, but here a tongue and
fire? Because that one was going away to accuse of sins, and to lament Jewish
disasters; but these were going out to consume the sins of the inhabited
world; therefore that one


received a writing-tablet, a reminder of the coming disasters; but these
received fire, so as to burn up the sins of the inhabited world, and to make
all vanish. For just as fire falling into thorns will easily make them all
vanish, so also the grace of the Spirit consumed the sins of men. But the
unfeeling Jews, when these things happened, when they ought to have been
struck and to tremble and to worship the one who gives the gift, again display
their own mindlessness, accusing of drunkenness the apostles filled with the
Spirit; For these, he says, are filled with new wine. Consider the
thanklessness of men, and look at the thankfulness of angels. For the angels
when they saw our firstfruits being led up, rejoiced and said: Lift up gates,
you rulers, and be lifted up, eternal gates, and the king of glory shall come
in; but =50.461= men seeing the grace of the Spirit having come down to us say
that those who received the grace are drunk, and not even the season of the
year put them to shame; for new wine would never be found in the season of
spring; and it was then spring. But let those be left; and let us look at the
exchange of the philanthropic God.
Christ received the firstfruits of our nature, and gave us in return the grace
of the Spirit; and just as it happens in a long war, when battle is dissolved,
and peace comes to be, those who are hostile to one another give pledges and
hostages to one another in return; so also it happened upon God and upon human
nature; it sent him pledges and hostages, the firstfruits which Christ brought
up; he sent us in return pledges and hostages, the Holy Spirit. And that we
have pledges and hostages is clear from this; for the pledges and hostages
must be of royal race; therefore Holy Spirit also was sent down to us, as
being of the most royal essence; and the one brought up from among us was of
royal race; for he was from the seed of David. Therefore I no longer fear,
because our firstfruits sits above; therefore even if someone should speak to
me of an unending worm, even of unquenchable fire, even of other chastisements
and vengeances, I no longer fear; or rather I fear, but I do not despair of my
own salvation. For if God were not planning great goods concerning our race,
he would not have taken our firstfruits above. Before this, looking into
heaven and considering the bodiless powers we saw our cheapness more clearly,
from the comparison of the powers above; but now whenever we wish to see our
nobility, we look up into heaven, to the royal throne itself; for there the
firstfruits from us sits. So also the Son of God will come from heaven judging
us. Let us prepare then, so as not to fall from that glory; for he will come
altogether and will not delay, our common Master; he will come leading armies,
ranks of angels, companies of archangels, clans of martyrs, choirs of the
righteous, peoples of prophets and apostles, and in the midst of those
immaterial armies the king appearing in some unspeakable and ineffable glory.
ʹ. Let us then do all things, so as not to fall from that glory. Do you wish
me to tell the fearful things also? not that I may grieve, but that I may set
right. Then a river of fire is drawn before that bema; then books are opened;
a fearful and shuddering court has stood. Therefore just as in a court the
records of our life are read, and the prophets say many things concerning
these books. And Moses says: If you forgive them the sin, forgive; but if not,
blot me also out from the book which you have written; and Christ again said
to the disciples: In this do not rejoice, that the demons are subject to you;
but rejoice that your names have been written in the heavens; and again the
prophet David: In


your book all shall be written, days shall be formed, and no one in them; and
again: Let them be blotted out from the book of the living, and let them not
be written with the righteous. Do you see that some are blotted out, and some
are enrolled? Do you wish to learn that =50.462= not only the righteous are
enrolled in those books, but our sins also have been written there? It is a
time of feast, let us learn concerning things able to free us from the
punishment; the word is fearful, but also useful and profitable; for it frees
from the experience through the things themselves; let us learn then that the
sins are written, and whatever we utter here, is carried up there at once and
is written. And whence is this clear? for one must not simply declare
concerning such things. Micah says to Jews: Woe to those who provoke the Lord.
And how, he says, have we provoked him? In your saying, Everyone who does evil
is good before the Lord. Words of thankless household servants; and in them he
himself was well pleased; in the corrupted, he says, in those who have not
served him. Behold we have kept his observances, and we count strangers
blessed; for we, he says, serve each day, and others enjoy the goods.
Such things slaves also often say concerning their masters; but for a man to
say these things concerning a man is not so terrible, though it is terrible;
but to utter these things concerning the common Master of the inhabited world,
a Master merciful and philanthropic, would be greater than every condemnation
and worthy of the last vengeance. But that you may learn that such words are
written, hear what the prophet says: Behold these have been written in a book
of the living for a memorial before God. And they are written, not because God
will remember the day, as bringing the book into the midst as some proof and
accusation. Perhaps I have shaken your mind with the fear; not yours, but my
own first. Come then, let me loosen the word, or rather the fear; or rather I
will not loosen it, but I will comfort; for let it remain, cleansing your
mind; but let us take away the unbearable of it. How then shall we be able to
take it away? If we show that sins are not only written, but also blotted out.
For in a court whatever the one being judged utters upon records is altogether
written continually, and it is not possible thereafter to be blotted out; but
in that book even if you utter some wicked things, and you wish, it is blotted
out again. Whence is it clear? From the Scripture; For turn, he says, your
face from my sins, and blot out all my iniquities. And no one will blot out
what has not been written so that since they were written, he asks that they
be blotted out. And another also teaches how they are blotted out, saying: By
alms and faiths sins are thoroughly cleansed. They are not only blotted out,
but also thoroughly cleansed, so that not even a remnant of the stain remains.
And not only the things after baptism are blotted out, but also what had been
written before the washing, all those were wiped out by the water of baptism
and the cross of Christ, as Paul says, that Having wiped out the handwriting
against us, which was contrary to us, and he took it out of the midst, nailing
it to the cross. Did you see how that handwriting was wiped out? and it was
not only wiped out, but also torn apart, the nails of the =50.463= cross
having split it, so that it became useless. But those things by grace and
philanthropy of the strength of the crucified Christ have all been wiped out;
but the things after baptism have need of much zeal, so as to be blotted out
again. Since there is not even a second washing, but it needs our tears,
repentance, confession, almsgiving, prayer, and all the rest of piety; so also
after baptism sins are thoroughly cleansed with much pain and toil. Let us
then display all zeal, so as to wipe them out from here, and to be freed from
shame and from the punishment there. For even if we have sinned countless
times, if we wish


we shall be able to put off all these loads of sins. Let us wish then; for it
is far better having toiled a little here to be freed from inescapable
punishment, than having been slack in this short time to fall into those

Homily 2.
αʹ. Great, beloved, and surpassing every human word are the gifts granted us
today by the philanthropic God; for this very reason let us all rejoice in
common, and leaping let us hymn our Master. For the present day is a feast and
a festival for us. For just as upon the change of the seasons and of the
tropes one succeeds the other, so indeed also upon the Church feast succeeding
feast, thus they send us on to one another. Recently then we kept feast of the
cross, the passion, the resurrection, after these the ascent into heaven of
our Lord Jesus Christ; but today at last we have met the very end of the
goods, we have arrived at the very metropolis of the feasts, we have come to
the very fruit of the Lord's promise. For if I go away, he says, I will send
you another Paraclete, and I will not leave you orphans.


Did you see care? Did you see unspeakable philanthropy? Before these days he
went up into heaven, he took up the royal throne, he received the sitting at
the right of the Father, and today he grants us the sojourn of the Holy
Spirit, and through him he supplies us the countless goods from the heavens.
For what, tell me, of the things that hold our salvation has not been managed
for us through the Spirit? through this we are freed from slavery, we are
called into free50.464 dom, we are led up into adoption, and from above, so to
speak, we are refashioned, we put off the heavy and foul-smelling load of
sins; through the Holy Spirit we see choirs of priests, we have ranks of
teachers; from the spring here also gifts of revelations, and gifts of
healings; and all the rest, as many things as are accustomed to adorn the
Church of God, have the supply from here. And Paul cries saying: All these the
one and the same Spirit works, distributing to each privately as he wills. As
he wills, he says, not as he is commanded; distributing, not being
distributed; having authority, not being subject to authority. For the same
authority which he witnessed to the Father, this Paul also assigns to the Holy
Spirit. And just as upon the Father he says: But God is the one who works all
things in all, so also upon the Holy Spirit: But all these, he says, the one
and the same Spirit works, distributing to each privately as he wills. Did you
see completed authority? For of those whose essence is one, it is clear that
the authority also is one; and of those whose dignity is of equal honor, of
these the power and the authority also is one. Through this we found the
freeing from sins; through this we washed off every stain; through the gift of
this one we who have run to the grace have become angels from men, not having
the nature changed, but what is far more marvelous, remaining in the hu50.465
man nature we display the citizenship of the angels. For such is the power of
the Holy Spirit; and just as this sensible fire, whenever it takes the
dissolved clay, shows it a strong potsherd; so indeed also the fire of the
Spirit, whenever it takes a well-disposed soul, even if it finds it more
dissolved than clay, works it more solid than iron; and the one a little
before defiled with the mire of sins, all at once it shows forth brighter than
the sun. And teaching us this the blessed Paul cried saying: Do not be
deceived; neither fornicators, nor idolaters, nor adulterers, nor the
effeminate, nor those who lie with males, nor the greedy, nor thieves, nor
drunkards, nor revilers, nor rapacious shall inherit the kingdom of God.
And having enumerated, so to speak, all the forms of wickedness, and having
taught that those liable to such sins become alien to the kingdom, he
immediately added: And such were some of you, but you were washed, but you
were sanctified, but you were justified. How and in what manner? tell; for
this is what is sought. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, he says, and in
the Spirit of our God. Did you see the power of the Holy Spirit, beloved? Did
you see that the Holy Spirit made all this wickedness vanish, and those
formerly given over by their own sins, all at once led up into the highest
honor?
βʹ. Who then would worthily wail and lament those who attempt to blaspheme the
dignity of the Spirit, who like some madmen do not even by the greatness of
the benefactions endure to hold off from their own thanklessness, but dare to
do all things against their own salvation, alienating it, so far as it lies
with them, from the master's dignity, and attempting to bring it down into the
rank of the creatures? Whom I wished to ask, for what reason, O these, do you
display so great a war against the dignity of the Spirit? or rather against
your own salvation,


and you do not even wish to take into mind the things said by the Savior to
the disciples? Going, make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them into
the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. Did you see the
dignity of equal honor? Did you see agreement made exact? Did you see the
undividedness of the Trinity? nowhere a difference, or a change, or a
subtraction?
Why do you dare to add orders to the words of the Master? or do you not know
that even upon human affairs, if ever someone should attempt, or should go out
into so great daring, as to add something to the letters from the king, or to
take away, of one of the same race with us and sharing the same nature. he
undergoes the last penalty, and nothing will be able to snatch him from the
vengeance? If then upon a man so great is the danger, what pardon could there
ever be for those who display so great madness, and attempt to corrupt the
things said by our common Savior, and do not even wish to hear Paul who has
Christ speaking in himself, crying with a clear voice and saying: Eye has not
seen, and ear has not heard, and upon a heart of man it has not gone up, the
things which God has prepared for those who love him? If then neither eye has
seen, nor ear has heard, nor a heart =50.466= was able to receive the
knowledge of the things prepared for those who love him, whence, O blessed
Paul, can we have the knowledge of these? Wait a little, and you will hear him
making this also clear; for he added saying: But to us God revealed them
through his Spirit. And he did not even stop as far as this; but that he might
show also the greatness of the power, and that it is of the same essence with
the Father and the Son, he says: For the Spirit searches all things, even the
depths of God. Then wishing from human examples to put into us a more accurate
teaching, he added: For who of men knows the things of the man, except the
spirit of the man which is in him? So also the things of God no one knows,
except the Spirit of God. Did you see a completed teaching? Just as, he says,
the things in the mind of the man it is not possible for some other to know,
but he himself alone knows his own; so also the things of God no one knows,
except the Spirit of God; which is greatest and exceedingly sufficient to show
the dignity of the Spirit.
For he brought forward an example all but saying, it is not possible for any
of men ever to be ignorant of the things in his mind. As therefore this is not
possible, so also the things of God with so much accuracy, he says, the Holy
Spirit knows. But I do not know how not even saying these things this blessed
one reaches those who through their own prejudice display so great a war
against their own salvation against the dignity of the Spirit, and so far as
it lies with them alienating it from the master's dignity, and bringing it
down into the cheapness of the created things. But even if those are disposed
contentiously, standing opposite to the things said by the divine Scripture,
we receiving the divine teachings as oracles brought down from above, let us
send up the fitting doxology, displaying with the right faith also the
accuracy of the truth. Toward those then who attempt to teach things contrary
to what has been said by the Spirit, the things said are sufficient; but it is
necessary to say to your love, for what reason the Lord did not grant us the
cause of so many goods immediately after his ascent; but first he allowed the
disciples to spend a few days, and to come to be by themselves, and then he
sent down the grace of the Spirit. Not simply, nor in vain has this too come
to be. For since he knew the race of men not similarly marveling at the goods
in their hands, nor honoring worthily those things which would appear pleasant
and great, unless also the entrance of the contraries steal in; what I mean is
this; for one must say it more clearly; he who is healthy and in good
condition of body does not perceive, nor can he accurately


know how many goods health granted him, unless he also take experience of
disease, weakness having stolen in; and he who again sees the day does not
similarly marvel at the light, unless also the darkness of night succeed. For
the experience of the contraries always becomes a clear teacher of those
things of which formerly we happened to be in enjoyment.
For this very reason also then, since the disciples had enjoyed countless
goods at his presence, =50.467= and from spending time with him were in much
prosperity; for all who inhabit Palestine looked upon their faces as upon some
luminaries; since they also raised the dead, and cleansed lepers, drove out
demons, and healed diseases, and displayed many other wonders; since therefore
they were so notable and conspicuous, for this he allowed them for a little to
be separated from the power that fought with them, that when they come to be
in desertion, they may learn how great a thing the presence of his goodness
granted them, and having come to a perception of the goods that had passed
they may receive also the gift of the Paraclete with greater eagerness. For he
both exhorted them when they were downcast, and those gloomy through the
separation of the teacher and filled with dejection he lit up through his own
light, and those lying almost he raised, and he scattered the cloud of
dejection, and he loosed the perplexity. For since they had heard the Master's
voice, Going, make disciples of all the nations; and they were thereafter at a
loss and did not know, where each must turn, and toward what climate of the
inhabited world they must preach the word, the Holy Spirit comes in the form
of tongues, distributing to each the climates of the teaching throughout the
inhabited world, and through the tongue given making known to each, as by some
tablet, the limit of the rule and teaching entrusted.
Therefore the Spirit came in the form of tongues; and not for this only, but
that he may also remind us of an ancient matter. For since of old men having
run aground upon mindlessness wished to construct a tower reaching as far as
heaven, and through the division of the tongues he divided their wicked
agreement; therefore now also in the form of fiery tongues the Holy Spirit
hovers upon them, that he may join through this the inhabited world that had
been divided. And a new and paradoxical thing came to be; for just as then of
old tongues cut the inhabited world in pieces, and led the wicked agreement
into division; so also now tongues joined the inhabited world, and brought the
things standing apart into concord. In the form of tongues then for this, but
tongues as of fire because of the thorn of sin that had grown wild in us. For
just as earth, whenever it happens to be rich and fat, but is not farmed,
displays a great bearing of thorns; so indeed also our nature, being good
because of the one who brought it forth, and being fit toward the fruit of
virtue, through not having received the plow of piety, nor the seed of the
knowledge of God, like some thorns and other useless matter generated impiety.
And in the way that the face of the earth often does not appear from the
multitude of the thorns and of the wicked plants; so also the nobility and the
purity of our soul was not shown, until the husbandman of human nature having
come, and having sent upon it the fire of the Spirit, both cleansed it, and
prepared it to become fit to receive the heavenly seed.
γʹ. So many goods and more than these belonged to us because of the
present day. Therefore I exhort toward =50.468= a manner worthy of the goods
granted us, let us also keep feast, not crowning the gate, but beautifying the

adorning the marketplace with hangings, but making the soul radiant with the
wraps of virtue, that thus we may be able both to receive the grace of the
Spirit, and to receive the fruits from here. And what is the fruit of the
Spirit? Let us hear Paul saying: But the fruit of the Spirit, he says, is
love, joy, peace. See accuracy of diction, sequence of teaching; he set love
first, and then he mentioned the things next; he set the root, and then he
showed the fruit; he laid the foundation, and then he brought on the building;
he began from the spring, and then he came upon the rivers. For neither can
the ground of joy enter in first, unless we first consider the prosperity of
others to be our own, and reckon the goods of the neighbor as our own; and
these would never appear from some other thing, unless the tyranny of love
exceed. Love is root and spring and mother of all the goods. For as a root it
sends up countless branches of virtue, and as a spring it begets many streams,
and as a mother it clasps within her own bosom those who take refuge in her.
Which indeed the blessed Paul also having perceived, called it fruit of the
Spirit; and elsewhere he granted it so great a precedence, as even to name it
fulfillment of the law; For fulfillment of law, he says, is love. The Master
of all things however put forward for us, as a sufficient definition and a
trustworthy character, for his own disciples to appear, no other than that
from love, saying: In this all will know that you are my disciples, if you
have love among one another. Therefore, I exhort, let us all take refuge in
it, and let us cling to this, and with it let us receive this feast; for where
love is, the deficiencies of the soul are idle; where love is, the irrational
leapings of the mind have ceased. Love, he says, does not vaunt itself, is not
puffed up, does not behave unseemly. Love works no evil to the neighbor; where
love has citizenship, nowhere is Cain murdering the brother. Take away the
spring of envy, and you have taken away the river of all the evils; cut off
the root, and you have taken away the fruit together.
And I say these things caring for those who envy rather than for those who are
envied; for those especially are the ones who are damaged the most, and who
bring upon themselves much destruction; since indeed for those who are envied,
if they should wish, being envied is even a ground of crowns. And see with me
how Abel the righteous is sung, and is proclaimed each day, and the ground of
the slaughter has become for him a starting-point of good repute. And he even
after the end through the blood speaks with boldness, and with a clear voice
accuses the mur50.469 derer; but the one who supposedly prevailed received the
fruit of the deeds through the recompense of the deeds, and spent his time
upon the earth groaning and trembling; but the one who had been taken away and
was lying upon the earth after the end displayed more boldness. And just as
sin prepared that one even living to spend his time more wretched than the
dead; so virtue made this one, even after the end, shine more. For this very
reason we also, that we may acquire greater boldness both here and there, that
we may harvest more the gladness from the feast, let us take away all the
filthy garments of the soul, above all let us strip off the wrap of envy. For
even if we seem to achieve countless things, we shall fall from all, this
bitter and raw plague troubling us; which may it come to pass that we all
flee, and =50.470= especially those who today through the grace of the
washing, having put off the old garment of sins, and being able to shine back
toward the sun's rays.
You then, I exhort, who today have been enrolled into the adoption, who have
put on this radiant wrap, with all watch keep the radiance in which you now
are, having walled off the entrance to the devil from every side, that


having enjoyed more abundantly the grace of the Spirit, you may be able to
bear fruit one thirty, and one sixty, and one a hundred, and may be counted
worthy to meet with boldness the king of the heavens, when he is about to be
present, and to distribute the unspeakable goods to those who have finished
the present life with virtue, in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom the glory and
the power now and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
