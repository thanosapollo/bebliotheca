* Commentaries on the Epistle to the Romans
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

1 1, 1. He calls himself a slave either because of praiseworthy humility, not
from having the spirit that leads into the fear that belongs to slavery, but
the spirit that leads up into adoption, or also according to the account of
ministry. Therefore he also said slave of Jesus Christ, or also as being
formed according to God: similarly to that one he takes on the form of the
slave, and becomes to the Jews as a Jew, that he may gain the Jews, and he
makes himself a slave to all, being free from all, according to the highest
slavery, that toward our Lord Jesus Christ; and inasmuch as he is unmarried,
as not being a slave of a wife but free, in this he is a slave of Christ, as
not being divided here and there but caring only for the things of the Lord,
and not being drawn away from the slavery toward the Word; and inasmuch as he
serves the one who bought him with his own blood, he is his slave. And it is
the same to say slave of Jesus Christ and slave of Word and of truth and of
righteousness and of wisdom and of peace and of whatever other name of the Son
of God has been written.

2 1, 1. And one would be called "called" in a generic sense as <the one> who
has received a gift, and specifically also the one who has received something
exceptional compared with the others, such as ministry or prophecy or teaching
or something of that sort. As therefore in a generic account many are called,
but few chosen, as Judas became called but not chosen, so Paul, having been
called, became called according to the generic, and immediately obtained also
the specific, that is, the apostleship. And it fits to say from a common
construction: Paul became a slave of Jesus Christ and a called apostle of
Jesus Christ. Being accustomed for the most part to join "apostle" immediately
to his own name, now he has prefixed "slave of Jesus Christ": for what reason?
It seems to me, wishing to lead the Romans into much reverence, so as to make
it a matter of everything to be named at all a slave of Christ, and not to be
puffed up by the rest from seeming to rule over all; for from this they were
then lifted into such conceit that there is a report that they, being
accustomed by a vote of the senate to proclaim as gods whomsoever they wished,
did the opposite in the case of our Savior; for the then ruler of Judea made
known to them through those men that those here, proclaiming that a certain
Jesus, a man who became a wonder-worker, had been raised after death, hand him
down as being God, and that these, being displeased, voted this down in a
common council because it had been dared contrary to their judgment. And this
too, then, was a work of divine grace, which administered what was needed
toward the future, so that this too might not seem to have prevailed by human
zeal, as many things often do. For this reason, then, he has placed "slave of
Christ" before "apostle," and at the same time because the dignity of
apostleship, to whomsoever it may belong, is present for the sake of the need
of others, whereas for someone to be a slave of Christ, this is the proper
gain of the one who possesses it.

3 1, 1. He is set apart, however, even from his mother's womb for the gospel
of God, not because nature had something exceptional beyond the other human
beings, but because of the deeds known beforehand by God earlier, which later
came to be from the preparation and <the> apostolic choice.

4 1, 1. The gospel, then, for which Paul was set apart, is of God, but Mark
says the gospel of Jesus Christ, and Paul himself in the [letter] to the
Galatians.

5 1, 4. And some join "in power" to the Holy Spirit and so read: in power
according to the Spirit, as in "Jesus of Nazareth, how God anointed him with
the Holy Spirit and with power," that is, with the powerful Spirit, and in the
[letter] to the Thessalonians, "our gospel did not come among you in word only
but also in power and in the Holy Spirit." He says, then, that he was set
apart by God to preach the gospel to all concerning his Son Jesus Christ, who
was born of the seed of David according to the flesh, but was appointed to be
Son of God, that is, this was confirmed as what he was, and especially and
more was he manifested by the power of his Holy Spirit, having risen from the
dead unto immortality; for he was put to death, he says, in flesh, but made
alive in spirit, and he was crucified from weakness, but he lives from the
power of God. "Spirit of holiness," however, [means] the sanctifying Spirit,
as also "God of consolation" [means] the God who consoles.

6 1, 9. Since the true worshipers worship God in spirit and not in body, and
in truth and not in type, since therefore God, in distinction from body
according to the custom of Scripture, is spirit and incorporeal, those who
worship him in spirit worship him as they ought, through truth, not through
types; and to serve is properly to be a slave and to do all things from
disposition.

7 1, 10. For the time being, then, he said: "if somehow I shall have a
prosperous journey by the will of God to come to you"; he indicated that it is
possible to come to someone apart from the will of God, as Balaam went off to
Balak to curse Israel for hire. And I think that also every Gentile, or
whoever is in any way alien to piety, not ministering to the saints the things
profitable to them, does not have a prosperous journey in the will of God to
come to those to whom he comes, even if he sometimes has a prosperous journey;
for there is, says Scripture, a prosperous journey of a man in evils.

8 1, 11. As there being a gift that is <not> spiritual, "spiritual" has
necessarily been added; for there are some gifts that are not spiritual, such
as wealth, human glory, and marriage; for the spiritual would never hinder
prayer, but the coming together of man and woman to the same place prevents
it. And that marriage is a gift the apostle makes clear, having said: "I wish
all human beings <to be> as I myself also am, but each has his own gift from
God, one <indeed> thus, another thus." Nevertheless Paul wishes to impart to
them a spiritual gift that establishes and confirms them, so that they may no
longer be infants tossed by every wind.

9 1, 13. Either as providence overtaking his apostolic journeys abroad;
therefore he also prayed to have a prosperous journey, as it being possible
for the hindrance to cease, or he was hindered by Satan, so that he might not
have some fruit, or it was not the will of God, since he knew it was not the
time for persuading; but when the time for persuading came, then he also made
him go. And through the things he said, "I was hindered," and as many things
of the same kind, he apologizes for himself and draws the love of the church
of the Romans toward himself.

10 1, 13-14. And "that I may have some fruit even among you" you should
understand according to hyperbaton, which if it is not observed, fights
against the sequence; for who would say that Paul had been hindered from
coming to the Romans for this reason, that he might have fruit? For the
opposite: if he had not been hindered, he had fruit, but being hindered he was
deprived of fruit. One must therefore read thus: "I often purposed to come to
you, that I might have some fruit even among you as also among the rest of the
nations, to Greeks and barbarians, to wise and foolish I am a debtor, and I
was hindered until now." Why then was he compelled to set the hyperbaton?
Because he was going to join many things to "that I may have fruit," such as
"among you as also among the rest of the nations, to Greeks and barbarians, to
wise and foolish I am a debtor" and what follows. Therefore, so as not to cut
the continuity, inserting "I was hindered until now," he joined to the former,
"I purposed to come to you," the "that I might have some fruit," and thus he
restored the order to the sequence of the discourse.

11 1, 14. And having said for the time being "that I may have some fruit," he
indicates that the fruit is not in choice alone but there is also need of work
in due season, and the one who acquires such fruits becomes rich in good works
and more blessed. And he had fruits not only among the foolish of the world,
whom he now names unintelligent, but also among the wise, speaking wisdom
among the perfect, the [wisdom] hidden in mystery. As therefore being a debtor
to the wise he needed lessons toward the wise that lead them to Christianity,
so also to barbarians he was a debtor to converse with them according to their
dialect. The teacher, then, needs wisdom toward the wise, but long-suffering
and the managerial [gift] and praiseworthy humility toward the unintelligent.
Therefore the debtor, either paying, is not accused, or, withholding what he
may owe, would reasonably be accused.

12 1, 17. And the righteousness of God is revealed to those who come from an
older faith to a new; for one must always believe Moses in order that one may
also believe Christ, and the one who believes Christ while not believing the
prophets does not believe rightly, nor is this one said properly to believe.
And "the righteous shall live by faith" teaches that if someone is righteous
but does not have faith, or believes but is not righteous, he would not be
saved.

13 1, 18. And wrath seems to be called both the minister of the pains upon the
things that have been sinned and those under him, ministers of the same
things, as when it was added that the wrath of God was kindled in Israel, when
David, stirred up by it, commanded Joab to number the people. And the second
is presented from "he sent upon them fury and wrath and affliction, a sending
through evil angels." And when [wrath means] the pains themselves through
sins; for so I hear "according to your hardness you store up for yourself
wrath in a day of wrath," and "but the wrath has come upon them to the end."
Toward this consider whether <the> things that happen to the righteous, when
the evil one tempts them, are wrath, but neither of God nor from heaven, but
from somewhere below, either of the devil or of some of his angels. And
against these someone will not unpersuasively counter that our wrestling is
against the spiritual [powers] of wickedness in the heavenly places; for these
too will seem, being wrathful, to wrestle with the saints and to bring upon
them wrath from heaven. Perhaps then there is a wrath from heaven and another
from below, as we have given account, so also for us there is a wrath of God
from heaven, and another from heaven, but not of God.

14 1, 24-26. When piety, then, which is the first virtue, has gone out of the
soul, temperance also goes out with it, and impiety enters into it, and
incontinence follows this, these things [happening] when the soul has been
handed over; for it was not fitting, piety having been cast out, that God
still guard the impious soul, but that it be handed over to uncleanness so
that their bodies might be dishonored, having become temples of idols and
worthy of every dishonor. For even if some impious idolaters practice
temperance, yet temperance is a virtue which is chosen from a praiseworthy
purpose, and these are of a wicked purpose; therefore also those who seem
virgins of the idols change the natural use into that contrary to nature.

15 2, 7. And wrath is the pain that follows those who sin for punishment, and
they define fury as wrath that is fuming up and swelling exceedingly. And this
fury has as its work to convict, whenever someone suffers something because of
sins, either his conscience striking or some painful things following the sin.
But affliction also follows such people, not the [affliction] that works
endurance, nor that in which one might boast, but that with which distress is
yoked. And no one would have been distressed if he had breadth of heart from
wisdom and had heard the apostle saying: "Be enlarged, you also"; for evils
are accustomed to gather and narrow the soul, so that the devil, as in a hole
according to the serpent, lurks within.
