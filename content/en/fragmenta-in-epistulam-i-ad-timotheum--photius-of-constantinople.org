* Fragments on the First Epistle to Timothy

1 Tim 3:16 Instead of: he was justified spiritually and not legally; for even
fulfilling the legal commandments, he fulfilled them not legally but
spiritually. Or that even if to the fleshly and heavy-hearted he did not seem
to have been justified (for they said: a glutton and a wine-drinker) yet to
those established in the spirit of God he has been justified; for we also
beheld his glory, glory as of an only-begotten, and so on. 1 Tim 4:3 It is not
a scribal fault, as it seemed to some, nor an apostolic oversight, but rightly
phrased also according to Attic custom, as "he hindered him not to do the
unfitting things" does not mean that he urged him toward unfittingness; and
"he turned him away not to offend friends" is not to offend friends but the
opposite; and again "he hindered not to steal" is not that he permitted theft;
and "he hindered to abstain from unspeakable doing" instead of he led away
from such a practice; so also "they hindered to abstain from foods" instead of
they hindered from the eating. And the usage is also much among those outside.
