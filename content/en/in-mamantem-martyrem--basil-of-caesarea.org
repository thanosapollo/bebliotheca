Homily 23.
On the holy martyr Mamas.
I am not unaware of the greatness of the praises that belong to the festival;
but just as I know this, so I also feel my own weakness. For the subject
requires that something worthy be said of those who have gathered, and of the
hope they have in us, and of the subject itself. For since today we keep the
memory of the greatest of the martyrs, every mind is intent and every ear
prepared, expecting something worthy of the martyr to be spoken, and longing
for him draws the Church. For grateful children demand great praises of the
fathers, and they would not accept that the greatness of those being praised
should be endangered by the smallness of the speaker. So then, the greater the
eagerness, the greater the danger. What then shall we do? How shall we both
fulfill your desires and not ourselves go away unprofitable of what is
present? We shall exhort each soul to go away having renewed in thought what
it came bearing in memory, provisioned from home, having gladdened itself with
its own supplies.

Remember the martyr for me, as many of you as have enjoyed him through dreams;
as many as, having come upon this place, have had him as a fellow-worker in
prayer; those for whom, called by name, he stood present at their works; how
many travelers he brought home; how many he raised from sickness; those to
whom he restored children already dead; those for whom he made the appointed
span of life longer. Gathering all this for me, work out a praise from a
common contribution. Give to one another what each knows, to the one who does
not know; what he does not know, let him take from the one who does; and so,
having feasted one another from a shared offering, pardon our weakness. For
these are a martyr's praises, the wealth of the =31.592= spiritual gifts. For
we do not have him to magnify according to the law of outside encomia; we
cannot speak of famous fathers and ancestors. For it is shameful that one who
is illustrious by his own virtue should be adorned with borrowed ornaments.
For they take such things into encomia by the laws of custom. Since the law of
truth demands praises proper to each. For neither does the father's good
fortune in the race make a horse swift, nor is it praise of a dog to have been
born of the swiftest. But just as the excellence of other animals is seen in
each itself, so also a man's own praise is that which is witnessed from the
achievements that belong to him. What is the father's fame to the child? So
the martyr did not receive his fame from elsewhere; but he himself by the life
that followed kindled a lamp of glory. From Mamas the rest come, not Mamas
from others. Let the children taught piety by him take pride in him. For he
himself gushes virtue from his own store. He is not like a torrent that boasts
in an alien confluence, but he is a spring pouring forth beauty from its own
flanks. Let us wonder at the man not adorned with a borrowed ornament, but
magnified by his own. Do you see the splendid horse-breeders? Do you see their
white tombs? How they are stones that men run past? But at a martyr's memory
every land is stirred, and every city is changed over to a feast. Not even the
kinsmen run off to the tombs of the fathers, but all to the place of piety.

They name this man father, the leader of the truth, but not fathers and
leaders of bodies. Do you see how virtue is honored, and not wealth? So the
Church, by the honors she pays those who have gone before, urges on those who
are present. Do not, she says, be eager about wealth; nor about the wisdom of
the world that is being abolished; nor about glory that fades; these vanish
together with this life; but be a worker of piety. For this will both carry
you up into heaven, and will furnish you an immortal memory and a lasting
glory among men. So then, if anyone remembers the shepherd, let him not wonder
at wealth. For we have gathered to praise one who is not rich; do not go away
wondering at a rich man, but at poverty with piety. A shepherd is no great
thing, nor a wise occupation. Would you not, in anger, have said as a reproach
to the one who provoked you: "You are a shepherd?" A shepherd who possesses
nothing more than the day's food, with a wallet hung on him, bearing a staff,
and the day's provisions, having no care about tomorrow. An enemy to wild
beasts, a companion of the tamest animals, fleeing the marketplace, fleeing
the courts, not knowing sycophants, not knowing trade, not knowing wealth,
having no private shelter, dwelling under the common covering of the world,
=31.593= looking up to heaven by night, and reading through the stars the
wonder of the Maker. A shepherd. Let us not be ashamed of the truth. Let us
not imitate the outside myth-makers; let us not dress the truth with
comeliness of words. The truth is naked, without an advocate, showing herself.
The humble thing takes more speech; but you will marvel the more at the
encomium.

Shepherd and poor man: these are the Christian's boasts. If you seek the
founders of the school of piety, they are fishermen and tax-collectors; if the
disciples, poor leather-workers. No one is rich; nowhere is there fame. All
these things have been abolished with the world. See then whose day we
celebrate, at whom we are all bright, at whom life has been changed. Since we
have been reminded of the shepherd, do not despise the name. You have heard
that Abel, the first who was well-pleasing, was a shepherd. Who is his
imitator? Moses the great lawgiver, who fled Pharaoh's attempt, who hated the
plotting of his fellows, this man was shepherding on the mountain of Horeb;
through what he shepherded he conversed with God. He did not see an angel on
the bush while sitting in court, but while shepherding he was counted worthy
of that heavenly converse. Who after Moses? Jacob the patriarch, showing in
shepherding the patience that belongs to the truth, marking his whole life in
a small image. To whom did he hand on the zeal? To David. David passed from
shepherding into kingship. For shepherding and kingship are sisters; inasmuch
as the one was entrusted with the oversight of the irrational, the other of
the rational. So this is the stepping-stone of the greater science. Therefore
the Lord, taking both together, is both shepherd and king, shepherding the
irrational, leading the more rational under the oversight of the kingdom. Do
you want to learn how great a shepherd is? "The Lord shepherds me." And how is
shepherding sister to kingship? "Who is this king of glory?" The one who is a
shepherd here is a king there. And do not think that he is witnessed by
others, yet himself is ashamed of this name. But having put the =31.596=
bastard shepherds to silence, he turns upon himself the true witness of
shepherding: "I am the good shepherd." "I am," and I have not been changed.

How is it that when he says the great things: "I by my hand established the
earth; I stretched out the heaven alone"; as when he says the other things
that are fitting and worthy to be said of God, he likewise says: "The good
shepherd"? He drives away the bastards, and turns the truth upon himself. "I
am the good shepherd." Learn who the shepherd is, and who is the good. He
himself interprets. The true shepherd lays down his life for the sheep; but
the hireling, and one who is not a shepherd, whose own the sheep are not, does
not care when he sees the wolf coming. Here the Church asks, if the Lord is
shepherd, who is a hireling shepherd? Is it the devil? And if the devil is a
hireling shepherd, who is the wolf? But the wolf is the devil, the untamed
beast, the rapacious, the plotting one, the common enemy of all. Let the
hireling shepherd then have his own name. Hireling shepherds the Lord then
called, turning toward those of that time. And there are also those now, as
there ought not to be, who appropriate the name of the hirelings. Then high
priests were shown, and Pharisees, and that whole Jewish faction. Those he
called hireling shepherds, who took the authority of shepherding not for the
truth but for their own need. Those who pray on a vain pretext, that they may
devour the bread of the widows and the orphans, these are hirelings. Those who
serve need, who chase the present, not looking toward the future, are
hirelings, and not shepherds. And now many hirelings, selling their own life
for a wretched little glory, who even now make a schism over the sound words
of the Lord. For when the Lord said these things, a schism arose among them.
Some said, "He has a demon"; others, "A demon cannot make blind eyes see." Do
you see how old the passion of schism is? For at once the winnowing-fan
separates the chaff from the wheat; and what is light and unstable is split
off from the food, while what is fit for spiritual nourishment remains with
those who till. Therefore a schism arose, and some differed in this way,
others in that. To Jews it belongs to be split; but let the Church of God,
having received the seamless tunic, the one woven from above, the unsplit one
kept safe by soldiers, she who has put on Christ, not split the garment. "And
I know my own, and my own know me." The heretic has seized this for his own
construction of blasphemy. See, he says, it has been said that "My own know
me, and I know my own." What then does he mean by "to know"? To grasp the
essence? =31.597= To measure out the greatness? That those things of the
Godhead should be contemplated which you by your own boldness of mouth
promise? Or from what follows do you not understand the measure of knowledge?

What do we know of God? "My sheep hear my voice." See how God is known: from
our hearing his commandments; from hearing and doing. This is knowledge of
God: keeping of God's commandments. How is that meddling about the essence of
God? It is not a search of the things above the world, nor a conceiving of the
invisible. "My own know me, and I know my own." It is enough for you to know
that he is a good shepherd; that he laid down his life for the sheep. This is
the definition of the knowledge of God. But how great God is, and what his
measure is, and of what sort in essence, such things are dangerous to the one
who asks, and impassable to the one asked; and silence is the healing of such
things. "My sheep hear my voice," he said, they do not dispute. That is, they
do not disobey, they do not contend. You have heard Son: do not yourself turn
generation into a technical art, nor bring under a cause the things that are
not to be accounted for by causes, nor by schism divide what is joined. For
this reason the evangelist, going before, made you safe. You heard before, and
indeed you hear: "In the beginning was the Word," lest you think the Son a
human offspring who has come from what is not. He said Word to you because of
the passionless. He said "was" to you because of the timeless. He said
"beginning" to you, that he might join the one begotten to the Father. You
have seen how an obedient sheep hears the Master's voice. "In the beginning,"
and "was," and "Word." Do not even say, "How was he?" and, "If he was, he was
not begotten," and "If he was begotten, he was not." What says such things is
not a sheep. A sheep's skin; but from within it is a wolf that speaks. Let the
plotter be recognized. "My sheep hear my voice." You have heard Son?
Understand the likeness toward the Father. I say likeness because of the
weakness of the stronger bodies. For as to the truth (for I do not fear to
step toward the truth), I am not a ready slanderer. I say identity, guarding
the property of Son and of Father. In the hypostasis of the Son, understand a
paternal form, that you may keep for me the exact account of the image, that
you may understand for me in a godly way: "I am in the Father, and the Father
in me"; not a confusion of essences, but an identity of characters. But it
seems, beloved, to be the most contrary of things, seeing that the obedience
of your hearing has even forced our weakness to say something in the midst and
to speak, that the power =31.600= of God might be most clearly shown in the
weakness of the instrument.

For perhaps for this reason our weakness has superabounded, that he who
strengthens the weak might be glorified the more. And may he who has brought
this our festival round, and given an end to last year's prayers, and given a
head to the coming time (for the same day marks off for us the cycle that has
passed, and becomes again a head for the one coming on), may he then who has
gathered us and granted the working of what is to come, keep us in himself
unharmed, unassailed, unravished by the wolf; may he preserve this Church
unshaken, guarded by the great towers of the martyrs. May he turn away every
plot and assault of heretical wraths; and in quiet grant us to be taught the
divine oracles, and to teach the grace of the Spirit that is supplied; because
to him belongs the glory and the might with the Holy Spirit, now and always,
and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
