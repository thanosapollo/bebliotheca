* On Fasting

On fasting. =62.731= It is a time of propitiations, and let us not overlook
the seasonable moment; it is a time of the recalling of sins, and let us not
forfeit the grace, but let us fast from foods, and first from sins; for the
first good is deliverance from evils. And wickedness is that which is opposed
to virtue, envy and theft, and murder and war, and whatever things are called
evils and are so. And he who is in evils has wickedness as punishment; for
even before Gehenna the presence of wickedness is chastisement. For neither is
a physician's cutting evil; but because of the preceding wickedness the
chastisement is brought on.

The first evil is to be found in wickedness. The impious man is a stranger to
God; even if he is not cast into Gehenna, he has been deprived of the good
things. He who is not temperate is ruined by the desires, the envious man is
wasted by the passion, the thief is ashamed and fears, the murderer does not
trust his own life. The first evil is to be evil. Even if the physician does
not cut, the sick man is sick; even if God does not punish, for he is
long-suffering, the one who sins is in disease. For he who does =62.732= sin,
he says, is a slave of sin. Therefore before the sin the one who sins has been
enslaved, and already has the punishment. Fair is the bridle of fasting to
those who love the benefit from fasting. Do not attend only to the toilsome
thing, but also to the fruit. For if one did not attend to the need, the sower
would not sow, lest he seem to scatter the things stored up; looking toward
hope, he sows what he had, that he may reap what he did not have. The flesh is
in pain in fasts, but the soul is gladdened by the hope of the good things.
What is better in us, soul or body? which, even if it received not to be in
pain, is fed by the beauty of the virtues with a better abundance.

We must be in fasting, which is not a destruction of the body, but a healing
and a cure. Those who fast are delivered also from bodily words; those who
practice melt away the excess of the flesh, and feed what is lacking of the
soul. Always the soul cares for the body; but it also feeds itself and takes
thought for itself. The soul prepares for the body a manner =62.733= and
prepares for itself a word. For if it takes care of the body, much more it is
fitting for it to care for itself. The order from God is that the soul rule
the body; those who set the body before the soul overturn the right order. It
does not itself serve the body, but it came to be right from the beginning,
and always the body was subjected to the soul. And if this does not persuade
you, at least let the feasts which the soul keeps against the body fully
assure you of this, that it too thinks against the body. The body is saved,
when the body rules the soul, and it is destroyed.

Let us not think fasting to be a small gain. If Adam had abstained from one
tree, and had fasted from one, death would have died; or rather he who was not
would not have died. Do you see, if Adam had used this medicine, our race
would not have become mortal? Of how much grace has there been need, that what
Adam invented through incontinence might be dissolved? When you see that the
contrary worked evil, that is, Adam's not abstaining from one tree, recognize
how great a good fasting is and the abstinence from things that are permitted.
For Adam, having touched what was not permitted, destroyed the race; but
fasting, having withdrawn even from things that are permitted, recalls the
transgression. And if Esau had also fasted, he would not have sold the
birthright; but having become weaker than food, he sold the advantage from
nature for lentils. These things I commit to those educated in the Scriptures.
What did Moses bring down to the Jews from the mountain

Moses? a law and a fast that came to be for forty days. But before it was
brought down, the law was seen, the people having acted impiously. The first
commandment, The Lord God is one; and those about to receive such a
commandment say, These are your gods. Great weakness; for before the living
God an imitation of a calf was preferred. What then is the medicine of so
great an evil? Again Moses, who did not sin, fasts, and the root of the old
law is granted to those who sinned. From fasting the preaching of the New
Testament began, from fasting it took its beginning. For the Savior, having
been baptized for us, and having gone into the wilderness, first fasted, and
so began the signs, not himself needing fasting, but teaching us to have the
turning in the power of fasting. If then the Old begins from fasting, and the
New sets its foundation from this, let us not think fasting to be an evil.

Some are distressed, seeing the toilsome law, and they do not see the gain
that comes from this law. Do you not see that even lions in a pit were
persuaded to fast, and did not touch Daniel? And if lions obey the command,
and have put off the nature of wildness, and are as acquaintances, and do not
come on as strangers, let us hasten to fasting, having been commanded. Again
it is written, when Jeroboam the king of Judea had acted impiously, worshiping
a calf according to a paternal inheritance, a prophet stood by, and said
nothing to the king and to the people, knowing that both the ruler and the
ruled are disobedient; and leaving the men who do not hear, he speaks to the
altar, that is, to the altar, that he may show that the stones hear rather
than men; he says, Let Josiah be born, and he will burn the bones of those who
sacrifice upon the altar.

He did not bear the dishonor, and the one being rebuked was angry; with power
he stretched out the hand, that he might turn back the benefactor the prophet;
he stretched it out, but he was not able to bend it; for the sin dried up his
hand. He who stretched out the hand speaks, to seize the prophet, and at the
same time as the cry the prophet heals the hand; and the king says to him:
Come up with me into the house, taste bread and drink water, and so you shall
go. The prophet answers: I have a command of God in these places, in which
impiety is practiced, not to taste water and bread. The prophet withdraws. But
attend to the end of the account. Another prophet, having heard that a prophet
having arrived said such things, pursues and overtakes, and exhorts the
prophet to enter and taste with him, and persuades. And he persuaded by
saying, that To me also the Lord said to do this. He entered and ate. And
while he was eating the Holy Spirit comes upon the prophet who called, and
thereafter he prophesies, not the things from the heart, but the things from
the Spirit, and he says to that one who entered and ate: Since you did not
hear the Lord your God, and you ate where you were commanded not to eat, you
go away from here, and =62.734= a lion eats you.

Do you see how great an evil the overlooking of a command of fasting procures?
The prophet withdraws, and the dignity of prophecy did not stand before him
because of the disobedience. A lion came out, not that it might eat, but that
it might show that it itself hears a command, and it goes after the one who
did not hear, and it laid hold of the body of the one who disobeyed so far as
only to kill; and it killed that it might punish the one who disobeyed. And
having killed, the lion used fasting in place of the luxury of the one who
disobeyed; for the prophet lay dead, but his donkey stood living, not because
it was not easier to kill a donkey than a man, but the irrational lion
distinguished by command, and the donkey that had not sinned it did not kill,
nor did it touch, but the one who disobeyed, no longer a prophet so far as
concerns disobedience, it killed. And one could see about him a triple wonder:
the prophet lay declaring the disobedience; the lion sat by


declaring the obedience; and the donkey stood by, neither fearing the nature
of the lion, nor withdrawing, nor suffering anything from the lion, that it
might appear that the lion did not come on as it came on because of the
thought of the belly, but because the one commanded not to taste did not use
fasts. Again Elijah was taken up as toward heaven.

What then is the sign of the taking up, if not the fast of the forty days? If
Moses fasts, and Elijah fasts upon it, and the Lord seals it, the imitation is
good. And yet we do not recount the restings that are in between; for we do
not fast forty days, but leaving intervals in the middle, we join the number,
but we do not distinguish the fast. Great are the things before us, small the
things from us; and if we fail even of the small things, we have become
plainly strangers to those who fasted. None of us fasts forty days; for over
forty we rest at intervals, as those who journey, lodging toward evening, and
beginning again. And if those men even fasted the forty, and we do not even
with rest travel the same road as they, let us not even dare to mention those
whose imitation we do not have. Fasting is an imitation of angels as far as
possible, a contempt of the present things, leisure for prayer, luxury of the
soul, a bridle of the body and a gentleness of desire. And those who fast know
that it tames the pleasures, and those who have been tried are not ignorant;
it softens wrath, it checks anger, and it stills the natural waves, it raises
up the reasoning, and it makes the soul bright, it lightens the flesh, it
drives away the shameful things of incontinence by night, it delivers from
drunkenness, it rescues from headache; it brings bright both the colors and
the looks. Orderly in fasts are the movements, easy the tongue, not distorted
from wine, right the reasoning; for the desires are not poured out. It dances
in temperance, it holds calm; for the outer man is at leisure, and of what we
suffer in drunkenness we do not remember, but of what we are delivered from in
fasts we have the reminder. For fasting both knows itself and the things
opposed; but drunkenness, being ignorant of itself, is ignorant also of the
good things from fasting.

For what reason, tell me, were the sons of Aaron the priests slain? was it not
because while ministering they tasted wine? What was the law of the Nazirites?
was it not to abstain from wine, and from all things from wine? Those about
Daniel ate seeds, despising the tyrannical table of Nebuchadnezzar. Daniel,
the man of desires, fasted three weeks of days; when he abstained from
desires, he received this last appellation. He fasted not one Jewish day, not
a poor one, but three weeks of days, himself also prefiguring the
ecclesiastical way. Bread of desires, he said, I did not eat; but they hunger
toward desires toward the season; Wine and meats did not enter into my mouth,
from which the Church fasts. See in the Old a Christian way of life. For in
those who are weak are the types, but in the prophets already the traces of
the truth. Fasting recalled a sentence of God. For God said, Nineveh is
overthrown, and it was not overthrown; for they offered fasting, and Jonah
fled, having feared the help from the fasting. For a sea-monster also
swallowed him; and it swallowed while fasting. For the one who swallowed did
not use the prey for food, but laid it away and kept the deposit for the
Master =62.735=, and the one who did not wish to go away of himself, it
carried off where it was commanded.

Nineveh the great city heard one naked prophet, who had come out from a
sea-monster, not even preserving the color of a man because of the fear; one
prophet went there, and the sign, which he had done, the time displayed. But
he also said, that The city is overthrown; having gone before he ought to have
led them away from the faith. But they


heard one prophet, and they proclaimed fasting as a medicine preventing the
overthrow. Jonah contended, not that Nineveh might be overthrown, but that
Israel might be rebuked from the comparison, that the nations readily obey
even one prophet, but Israel disobeyed so many.

Fasting was offered, that the sentence of God might be dissolved. And the
fasting was not simple, but abstinence from foods, and abstinence from sins
also; for he who fasts because of sin, and rolls in sins, what does he still
do? as if someone having washed from mire, rolls again in the mire. One must
first abstain from the sin itself. For what did it profit that sin was brought
in on other days, that we should invent it again after the former proofs? is
the soul not weak upon the preceding blows? do we not, praying, ask, that we
have sinned? If we repent over the former things, why do we gather the second?
It is written again, that they abstained from foods, they abstained also from
sins. God heard; sufficient is fasting to turn the Master. Jonah was vexed,
because he was about to hear prophet; but God, having changed the sentence,
did not shrink from saving. For it was not for God to lie, but he speaks truly
who said: If I suddenly speak against a nation and kingdoms to overthrow and
to root out, and it shall repent of the wickedness, I too will repent of the
things I declared. God did not lie in recalling the sentence; for he did not
send the prophet that Nineveh might be overthrown; for what necessity to
foretell? but since he did not wish to overthrow, he threatened that I
overthrow, that having repented they might not be overthrown. It is not that
Adam ate from the tree, and then God punished him, but he foretold that If you
eat, you shall die, that he might not eat. He did not hear the one who spoke,
he ate, he was eaten. Is there not room to accuse, where there is choice? But
he did not know, someone says, that he was going to eat? Perhaps the one who
says this and runs down the Old is a heretic; to whom it will be said: If then
the Savior knew that Judas was going to betray, how did he choose this man?
But perhaps we shall make a defense to the heretic, setting the things of
Judas against those concerning Adam. Is this then a solution, or a double
bond? We must then make a defense, both why he chose Judas being such, and
why, knowing that Adam sins, he set this man in paradise. And one must move
the great questions in fasts, when the soul is sober.

The Savior knew, for from here one must begin, that Judas betrays. What then,
do you say? Displaying an excess of love for mankind, that as far as concerns
me, you are even an apostle, and I number you with disciples; but if you show
yourself unworthy, mine appears the excess of love for mankind, and yours the
excess of wickedness. For he also entrusted to him the money-box of the
moderate things, not being ignorant that he steals, but wishing through giving
him authority to stop the passion. He foreknew then and foretells; and not
only did he foreknow, but he also often said beforehand, One of you will
betray me. What then will you accuse? That the wicked man by wickedness might
fulfill the good economy; and the one who chose him did not compel him. For
even if Jesus compelled, he did not compel him to become a traitor, but an
apostle; but he does not even compel an apostle. For that which has come to be
with compulsion is itself called what does not come to be; for one temperate
with compulsion is not temperate, because compulsion came to be. For neither
do the virtues come to be by bonds, but the way of life by choice. Why then
did he also leave Adam in paradise? The one who formed was good, he made him
free, as to a free man he set the commandment itself. But he knew that he is
going to eat. But he did not hinder by compulsion, nor did he compel choice;
Adam sinned. And because of the tree the place is honorable, in which the
honorable thing was. For he was not created evil, but the one who commanded
was badly disobeyed. And if the tasting destroyed even the commandment,
fasting with a commandment


recalls and saves. Prove yourself, O man. For what need of readings? look at
yourself, who you were before the fast, and who while fasting. For of things
done some later bear the fruit, and some =62.736= even in the work itself
procure the fruit also. He who goes down into the sea for hunting does not
have the prey in the diving; but the one who fasts immediately has the fruit.
Do you not have calm? have you not been delivered from waves from wine? have
you not been delivered from storm and winter? do you not desire to hear that
the flesh has been bridled, not leaping, not blushing. did you not know
yourself to move a word about temperance now? do you not hear that so-and-so
fasted, and you rejoice? You who fast, do not think the fast for an evil; for
he who thinks the fast an evil, even if he fasts, is in wickedness.

The angels are not in evils, not having need of the luxury among us. We do not
eat according to worth, but we are filled according to want. As many evils as
come from us are quenched in fasts. And I call fasting not only the abstinence
from foods, but that which Isaiah knows: fast, that you may be heard. And if
you say to God according to the Jewish word: We fasted and you did not see; we
humbled our souls, and you did not know; take care, lest you hear. For in your
fasts you find your own wills.

If you fulfill the will of the Lord, you have done the fast. For fasting is
not a business, that we may gain by not eating, but that what you are going to
eat a poor man may eat instead of you; and the good becomes double for you,
both that you fast, and that another does not hunger. We grow weary fasting;
we have not yet been crucified fasting. Why the fast? In place of a cross; we
received greater things, we give back small things. He has been crucified for
you, and he thirsts, and he drinks vinegar with gall. Distasteful is the food,
great is the fast. Not yet as much as the gall, and you stand weary? you have
not yet been hung upon a cross. Do not be ungrateful toward Jesus, do not
lament that you fast, but lament that you do not always fast. Of what have you
been deprived while fasting? of wine? Not of wine, but of inflammation, of
headache, of a wretched shortness of breath. Consider then in what things you
are, in quiet, in gentleness, in calm. A fair color comes to be in fasts upon
the faces, tame, calm; for the color of wine is a burning of fire. He who does
not suffer with Jesus does not know Jesus; he who thrusts away the fast does
not know the cross. Let us not approach the fast as an evil, lest what is
suspected become for you as an evil; nor as a shameful slave, but you shall be
found serving God. No one is burdened in feasts, except the one who is
ignorant of the power of the feasts. When there are feasts, then rather
brighten. But you rather do the contrary; when you were an angel, then you are
harsh. Do you not know the interval? When nature demands marriage, you do not
marry beyond nature. Be reverent in marriage; if you honor the fasts, on the
days of the fasts become an angel. I do not slander marriage, but I prefer the
fast. What we ate before the fast does not remain with us. Why then are we
occupied with the things not present? the whole pleasure of foods is until the
swallowing; and when it has passed the tasting-place, it has become as it was
not. But fasting does not wither, it does not go into a belly, nor through a
belly is it changed into dung; for it is a procurer of immortality, it has its
way of life with angels, because it is an imitation of angels as far as
possible. Are you despondent, because you are sober? because you have not been
weighed down by others' good cheer? because the reasoning is right? because
the flesh is clean? because the mind blooms? because you hear the Scripture
with joy? because you keep silent the secret things, and you are not a man,
but almost an angel?

A feast of the Lord who called us to a spiritual marriage, of the one who
entered to see those reclining; let no one enter having foreign garments.
Garments must be brought to a marriage clean and undefiled and holy toward
holy and undefiled nuptials, bright without, and splendid within. Let marriage
also be quiet, and let each have the charge of the apostle according to
admonition, that from


agreement each may have leisure in fasts. And to the fasts let prayer also be
joined; fasting exhorts, and prayer has a time; fast, because you have not
sinned, and because you have sinned fast, and again that you may not sin fast,
and that what you received may remain. For so many are the kinds of fasting.
Someone sinned, and fasted punishing himself, that he might escape the
punishment? let us then also fast, beloved, that the desires may not rise up
against us. And you see the fasting that follows sin, that is, that after the
sin, becoming a turning in words of punishment.

But fasting went before the sins, not that it might take away the sin that
comes to be, but that it might hinder what is about to be. Another fasting is,
the one seeking to receive a law of a law. For he who asks a law fasts, as
Moses fasted, and received a law. Again another fasts, that he may not fail of
the things of which he was counted worthy. And the Church fasts together, the
grace and the economy. She recognizes Jesus in deed, having been crucified
together with the one crucified, suffering with the Lord, that she may also be
glorified together, not that we may mourn the Lord; for the Son of the living
one lives; but that we may confess the grace of the economy. Even if we mourn
that Jews crucified him, we do not mourn because of him, but because of the
daring of those who dared. For he rose from the dead, he stood in glory, in
which he preexisted with the Father, he was together with the Father from
where he also was. The victory of Jesus is not worthy of mourning. But let us
fast caring for ourselves, and hastening to be partakers of his sufferings; as
it were let us do what is worthy, brethren. Who has been counted worthy to be
a partaker of the sufferings of Christ? Great is the mystery. Let us hasten
toward the good things, and let us not turn aside into evils. Jesus has been
crucified, the Son of the immortal; he hung the body upon the cross, not for
himself, but for you. You ate from the tree, he fasts upon the tree; you took
from things that were not owed you; he set a body in place of the things you
took. You were defeated by a hard desire; he drinks vinegar in place of
pleasure. Do not become a stranger to the kingdom. You knew that he has been
crucified, you knew that he died for you, confess that he lives. Make the
confession for a living one, bear witness to the one who bore witness, die for
the one who died. And if he so demands death, do not leap away; small is the
death of fasting. You do not have great achievements? the death of fasting.
You do not have great achievements? grant him the small things; with gladness
run to him toward feasts, with joy receive the fast. Fasting is a spiritual
luxury. But perhaps I cry in vain; for as the time changed the colors of those
hastening toward a table. And this is not of those who love fasting in which
they have stood. For it was not consistent for you to be burdened at being
drawn away from the table of the Lord. And the sight met beyond expectation,
as the gleam of the lamp met. The hidden conscience was convicted, that we
were being held by force; and so we have not stood. Until when do we play at
the truth; until when do we traffic in the things of the Lord? or did you not
hear the psalm saying, and at the same time obeying it, If you speak truth,
judge =62.738= upright things? If you are true toward the fast, have joy
toward the fast; but if you are in pain, you wrong yourself; if you toil, you
are not gladdened; if you pursue as one deprived of good things, you do not
obtain good things. Jesus has been crucified through you, and he was not
distressed, but rather he even hastened to suffer; and you do not even endure
a small fast for your own salvation? O man, I do not have so great a power,
that I may cry as much as I owe. Jesus has been crucified; look, and strike
the breast, bring down a tear from the eyes. He has been crucified for you; be
crucified together, be glorified together with him, not that you may die, but
that you may be delivered from death. This day of Preparation is a day in
which the cross was set up for you; and you be crucified together, die
together. For the word is for God,


of God who became man for you. Are we not ashamed at the dignity? do we not
blush at the passion?

Consider both, man, what he was, and what he suffered.

The judge has been judged, and the life has been given over to death; and he
who feeds hungers, and he who gives drink thirsts, and he who is in glory in
dishonor, and the king before a governor, and God is condemned by men; he who
speaks truly in the saying. The Father judges no one, but he has given all the
judgment to the Son; he who judges all things, by whom has he been judged?
These things suffice for you, O man, for a reminder of fasting. For one must
first know in what things we are, and then distinguish the things that concern
us. When we have loved the fast and have been well disposed concerning it, as
dancing of a feast, and the banquet in the fasts, we shall set forth the
mysteries of the fast, honoring toward the table of the Lord, to the servants
of the Lord, the glory before the ages, his economy in later times; who he
was, and what he has become, from where he came, and upon what people he
sojourned, and because of what; that both as being gladdened and gladdening,
we may have this fast through all, serving the Lord of the fast, giving thanks
to him from every side, and always glorifying, always loving the one who loved
us, and worshiping the one who took up our sins, and dying for the one who
died, and hoping to be glorified together with the one who was dishonored. And
to the all-holy Father, who counted us worthy of the present fast, and who did
not spare his own Son for our salvation, through the one who came let us send
up glory to the one who sent, together with the life-giving Spirit, now and
always, and unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
