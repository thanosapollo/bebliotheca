* On the Nativity of the Lord
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of Amphilochius, bishop of Iconium, on the nativity of our great God and
Savior Jesus Christ.
This spiritual and bright meadow, adorned with the beauty of heavenly flowers
and fragrant with the apostolic and undefiled scents, seems to me to bear the
type of the divine paradise. For just as that perceptible and unstained place
is made bright with incorruptible trees and immortal fruits and countless
other surpassingly brilliant beauties, so also this most God-like company of
the most sacred Church is made radiant with intelligible and unutterable
mysteries, of which the unbreakable foundation and unshaken base and saving
beginning and all-venerable summit is today's feast of the holy nativity of
Christ our true God; through which also the old things were prophesied in type
and the new things have been proclaimed expressly throughout all the inhabited
world, through which heaven has been opened and earth has been lifted up to
divine height, through which paradise has been restored to human beings and
the dominion of death has been abolished, through which the power of
corruption has been trampled down and the destructive worship of the devil has
ceased, through which human passions have been put to death, the life of
angelic sovereignty has been renewed, through which the error of demons has
been driven away, the wisdom of God and his all-pure presence have been made
known. For "not an angel," it says, "nor a messenger, but the Lord himself
will come and save them." O unspeakable wealth of divine gospels! O
inexpressible knowledge of all-wise mysteries! O indelible treasury of divine
and unutterable gifts! O innumerable grace of providential love of mankind!
For "the Lord himself," it says, "will come and save us."
In what manner do you proclaim, O divine prophet, that the Lord will come to
us? For I will speak freely toward you in this matter, having taken upon
myself the person of the men of old, who did not keep festival at this
all-hymned assembly, nor had they experienced the new and all-pure childbirth
of the undefiled Virgin, nor had they seen the heavenly herald, I mean the
most God-like star, nor had they seen the leapings of the holy angels, nor had
they heard the divine voices which, hymning the Savior who had been born, they
joyfully announced to the sacred shepherds, nor had they understood the gifts
of the magi and the divine worship; having taken on the person of those men, I
would ask you the manner of the coming. For the oracle struck them, as was
reasonable, very greatly, and all but prepared them to be driven out of their
wits by the abundance of dread. For they could not understand that the
immortal God would come to most earthy human beings, and the intangible to
those of sense, the invisible to those who are seen. For how would they have
thought that God would come, and how will he be seen? To some it seemed at
first as he appeared to Abraham through angels, or again as he appeared to
Moses through fire in the bush, or in the manner in which he appeared to
Isaiah through the seraphim, or to Ezekiel through the cherubim. For thus all
have borne witness that they have seen God in these various manners. Which
then of these manners is worthy for the vision of God? Clearly not even one.
And from where do we especially confirm this? From the other voice of the
prophet saying: "Afterward he was seen upon the earth and consorted with human
beings." For those were proofs of a vision and not of dwelling together; these
are proclamations of dwelling together and not of a vision. How then does he
say: "The Lord himself will come and save us"?
You might say to us, O blessed one, how the unformed is given form, how the
unmoved is again transferred from the heavenly thrones onto the earth? But
this divine man would say in reply to them, that through other oracles you
have heard the manner of the coming; why do you inquire excessively into the
oracle here? Or has what was said escaped you, that "The virgin shall be with
child and shall bear a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel, which is
interpreted God with us"? Or have you not heard what is written, that "A child
has been born to us, a son also has been given to us, whose government is upon
his shoulder, and his name is called Messenger of Great Counsel, Wonderful
Counsellor, Mighty God, Ruler, Prince of Peace, Father of the age to come"?
From these words you have come to know the manner of the coming. For an
incorruptible virgin will bring forth bodily the incorruptible lamp, for the
all-venerable Word of God must condescend even as far as flesh toward us, that
he might renew through incarnation those whom he had created by his bodiless
Godhead, who had grown old through sin, and might make them incorruptible
through the likeness toward corruption. These things the prophet having said
to them, we too shall say.
O child older than heaven! O thrice-blessed Son, who comes bearing his own
rule upon his shoulders and does not seek to receive it from another! For it
is innate to the Word to rule all things as Son, and not adventitious as to a
creature; for "the government," it says, "is upon his shoulder." O offspring
of great name! For he is named, it says, Messenger of Great Counsel and Mighty
God. O all-powerful authority! For he is Wonderful Counsellor and Prince of
Peace. How then shall we glorify today's feast? How shall we bless the most
mystical assembly now at hand? For who will trace out the immortal wealth of
this day? With what loud-sounding and most lofty words shall we proclaim this
all-hymned and trophy-bearing mystery of incorruption? O day worthy of
countless hymns, on which the star out of Jacob has risen for us, and the
heavenly man, who appeared out of Israel, and the mighty God has sojourned
among us, and the sun of righteousness has overshadowed us, and the treasury
of divine virtues has been opened, and the plant of eternal life has sprouted
for human beings, and the dayspring from on high has shone forth, and the
Master of things heavenly and earthly has come from virgin loins into a
corruptible world for the world's redemption. For today a Savior has been born
to us, who is Christ the Lord, who is light of the nations and salvation of
the house of Israel. O the wonder! He who is not circumscribed by the heavens
was lodged upon a manger as a child, and he who by a brief word established
all things was warmed in a woman's arms, and he who has granted being to all
the supermundane powers was nursed from the undefiled breasts of the holy
Virgin. For it came to pass, it says, as the Gospel bears witness: "while they
were there the days were fulfilled for her to give birth, and she brought
forth her firstborn son and wrapped him in swaddling clothes and laid him in
the manger, because there was no place for them in the inn." What is this new
and paradoxical initiation? What is this all-powerful goodwill of divine
providence, which works through what is corruptible? What is this dread and
all-wise strategy against the devil? The world has been set free through a
virgin, the world that formerly through her fell under sin. Through a virgin
birth the so many and so great companies of invisible demons have been
consigned to Tartarus. The Master has become conformed to the slaves, that the
slaves might become again conformed to God. O Bethlehem, city sanctified and
allotted to human beings! O manger, O manger, fellow-partaker with the
cherubim and equal in honor with the seraphim! For he who is eternally borne
in divine manner upon those thrones now again lodges in you bodily. O Mary, O
Mary, who has gained as firstborn the Creator of all things! O humanity, who
has given bodily substance to the eternal Word of God, and in this respect has
been preferred above the heavenly and intellectual powers! For Christ did not
deign to be formed according to the forms of the archangels, nor yet according
to the unchanging images of the principalities and powers and authorities, but
according to you, who had been brought down into change and compared with the
irrational animals. For those who are well have no need of a physician, but
she who was held by a continual disease has obtained so great and such a
physician, that she is blessed with a salvation greater than health, once the
disease has fled. But where now is the hostile and unawed one, the accursed
and all-defiled dragon, who promised to lift his own throne on high?
Therefore, brothers, partakers of a blessed heavenly calling, called to the
adoption of God and to brotherhood, we ought to give thanks to him who called
us and to present ourselves worthy of him who grants us the brotherhood, and
as sons to be worthy of him who bestows on us sonship and has received us into
adoption. Willingly then and out of love let us serve him, being ready for
every accomplishment of righteousness, adorned with chastity, pursuing
poverty, persevering in the words of God, devoted to holy prayers and sacred
hymns of God, transforming ourselves from this age, forgetting earthly and
mortal desires, overcoming evil with good, rendering to no one evil for evil,
not reckoning to ourselves that we dwell on the earth, but that we have our
citizenship in heaven, that we consort with angels, that we stand beside the
throne of the heavenly kingdom. These are the teachings of the holy apostles;
in these Christ has appointed for us the blessed and eternal covenant. Let the
world marvel at your virtue; let the Jews be put to shame when they behold the
new and elect people, with how great spiritual beauty it has been adorned,
with how great brightness it illuminates the world. For this reason he left us
mixed among the nations, that we might appear as lights in the world, that we
might be a seed of salvation for conversion, being seen by all who live with
us and meet us. See that none of the Gentiles blaspheme God because of you;
but let him who called and sanctified and saved be well spoken of in us. Let
the violent and insolent marvel at our gentleness and moderation; let the
reviler, being answered with good speech, be put to shame; let him who
contends about money and wishes to go to law find us undisturbed about money,
not thinking ourselves to have an earthly possession, but holding fast the
heavenly; let him who by friendly invitation would draw us toward worldly
pleasures find us grave, unmoved, longing for self-control, not baited by
pleasure, that they may know how great is the power of the Holy Spirit's
dwelling, which has put to death the nature of the flesh. Let him who is ready
for perjury find us not even enduring to swear, but fearing to bring forward
the name of God concerning low and earthly matters. For thus we shall all be
teachers of those who meet us in any matter whatsoever. And living thus we
shall truly become a holy leaven, and through us the world shall be leavened
unto salvation, and much fruit from us shall be found kept for the Lord, and
God shall be glorified in us according to the Lord who says: "In this is the
Father glorified, that you bear much fruit and become my disciples." And the
Lord, having been glorified by us, will glorify us with the eternal glory, in
Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory unto the ages of ages.
