* On Holy Friday
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

A homily of Leontius, presbyter of Constantinople, on Holy Friday and on the
Passion of Christ and on righteous Job.
Great are the gifts of prayer, indelible are its achievements. Wherever one
may turn the eye of the mind, he will find nothing more honorable than prayer.
Prayer steered Noah and kept him unsinkable from the God-sent flood; prayer
dissolved the barrenness of Anna and unexpectedly delivered her from the
accursed curse; prayer made the belly of the whale a house of prayer for Jonah
and kept the city unharmed for the Ninevites; prayer granted king Hezekiah a
space of fifteen years. And that I may not speak at length of how much prayer
can do, let us bring the Lord himself into the midst, him who both did and
taught, who not only prayed but also exhorted to pray, as one may hear him
saying to his own disciples: When you pray, say: Our Father who art in the
heavens, hallowed be thy name, thy kingdom come.
As children, call upon a father; as soldiers, wait upon the king; as those
enrolled as citizens of the kingdom of the heavens, cry out nothing else
besides the Master's name. And the Lord taught the disciples to pray not only
for themselves, but also for those who despitefully use them and who are
hostile and who plot against them, as one may hear the Lord himself saying to
them: Pray for those who despitefully use you, do good to those who hate you.
What the Lord taught he also did: he himself also prayed for the enemies who
rejected him. And when did the Lord pray for the enemies? When? On this
present Friday, when he stood at the cross. For today, as you know, the Judge
of all is crucified. Therefore also, when the Lord saw himself being crucified
by the Jews and the Jews being condemned by the lifeless elements, and that
the sun was darkened while they were not gloomy, and that a universal
earthquake was taking place while they were not turned from their wickedness,
and that the veil of the temple was torn while their hearts were not opened,
and that many tombs were opened while their souls were not enlightened, and
that the dead were rising while they were being numbered with the dead: thus
also the Lord, showing love of mankind toward the race of men, was praying on
the very cross to his own Father on this present Friday, saying: Father,
forgive them this sin, for they know not what they do.
You have seen, O friend, how he both taught and fulfilled, how he prays for
the enemies. Why? The Father of the Lord, as if indignant at the outrage
against the Son, wished to put to death the tribe of the Jews, those who
instead of worship were hewing a cross, and instead of entreaty were forging
nails, and instead of an offering were bringing vinegar, and instead of gifts
were striking with a reed, and instead of treasures were weaving a crown of
thorns, and were crying out boldly: His blood be upon us and upon our
children. For these reasons, when the Father was counseling to wipe out at
once the tribe of the Jews, the only-begotten Son, as having taken the rank of
a mediator, was entreating the Father, saying: Father, forgive them this sin,
for they know not what they do. For many of them will turn back; therefore I
entreat, let not wheat be harvested together with tares, let not lambs perish
together with wolves, let not doves be destroyed together with ravens, let not
roses be burned together with thorns. Not only is Caiaphas here scourging, but
also Paul turning; not only the servant of the high priest striking, but also
Stephen preparing the martyrdom. Father, forgive them this sin. And the Son
was saying these things to the Father not as though the Father were ignorant
of what the Son wills, or the Son were in doubt about what the Father wants
(for there is one will of Father and Son; for the Word knows that which the
Mind wants, [and the Mind knows] that which the Word wants), not then as being
ignorant of the Father's will was he saying these things, but as the
only-begotten Son wishing even on the very cross to make the manifestation of
the Father, that he might not be slandered as a stranger and a bastard, but as
genuine and of the same essence. For these reasons the only-begotten Son
prays, that he may make the manifestation of the Father, that what he received
from the Father he himself may also give back to the Father. And someone will
surely say: What is it that he received from the Father, that he himself may
also give it back to the Father? Hear what you already know: therefore I
remind, I do not teach.
You heard at the Jordan baptism the Father crying from above: This is my
beloved Son in whom I am well pleased; hear him. The Father signifies the Son
and makes him known to men. Wishing then to give this testimony back to the
Father, the Son was crying out on the very cross: Father, forgive them this
sin, for they know not what they do. And that the only-begotten Son uttered
these things for manifestation, hear the Son himself saying to the Father:
Father, I have manifested thy name to men. For this is also the purpose of the
economy: that the Father may make the Son known, that the Son may make the
Father known, and likewise that between Father and Son the Holy Spirit also
may be made known. Hear likewise again the Son praying and saying to the
Father: Father, forgive them this sin. But you, hearer, do not deny the
meaning, do not diminish the incomprehensible. For if you diminish the Son,
you also diminish the Father; for there is the Father, where the Son is. For
this reason he is also called Son from οἷος, that is, such as the Father is,
such also is the Son. Therefore when you hear the only-begotten Son of God
praying, take it according to the account of the incarnation. For he prays
according to the economy, but works wonders in a manner befitting God; it is
not one who suffers and another who works wonders. One and the same is he who
sleeps and who raises the dead, one and the same who hungers and who fills,
one and the same the only-begotten Son who both dwelt unconfined in the
virginal shrine, and was unspeakably fixed upon the wood of the cross, and
slept the three days in the hewn tomb, and in the cloud of the ascension
whirled aloft the firstfruits of humanity, and lodges unspeakably and
timelessly in the Father's bosom, and is nowhere lacking, above or below.
Great then are the gifts of prayer, indelible its bounties. Prayer is absence
of affliction, presence of gladness, consolation of friends, the gift-giving
of the God over all. And someone will surely say: How is prayer the
gift-giving of the God over all? How? You heard just now the writer Moses
saying: The Lord said to Eliphaz the Temanite: You have sinned, you and your
two friends; for you have not spoken anything true before me, as has my
servant Job. And now take seven calves and seven rams and go to my servant
Job, and he will make burnt offerings for you, and you shall live, because if
I had not accepted his face, I would have destroyed you. You have seen prayer
as a benefaction of friends, and how God grants men through prayer a release
from offenses. The Lord said to Eliphaz: You have sinned, you and your two
friends. Go then to my child Job, and my servant Job will pray for you. Well
does the Lord in the same place address Job as both child and servant: child
as the name of service, servant as the achievement of piety.
Go to my child Job; my servant will pray for you and you shall live. O the
goodness of the Lord! He himself shows love of mankind and grants the grace to
Job. Exceedingly well. For just as among present worldly things a king,
wishing both to enrich and to glorify the one nearest him, grants rescripts
through him and grants salvation to the condemned, that he may both benefit
those and enrich the one nearest him, so also God the King of all, wishing to
glorify Job as his own man, through him forgives the sin to the friends, that
both he himself may be glorified and they may be delivered from the sin. Go to
my child Job. If I am not entreated by him, I do not give pardon. Go to my
child Job: entreat him whom you insulted, glorify him whom you rejected,
receive the ulcered priest, behold him who is upon the dunghill as upon the
altar. For I did not abandon him as a sinner, but I glorified him as a
righteous man. Gold is tested in a furnace, and a righteous man is made bright
in trials. Did the dunghill cause him loss? Did he not become more glorious?
Did he trade badly? Did he not give single things and receive double? Go to my
child Job, who has come from toil and beating into rest. Learn the progress of
a faithful friend: he is faithful, but you are unfaithful. From what you have
seen, learn that no one who has believed in the Lord has been abandoned. Go to
my child Job, because if not for him I would have destroyed you. Hearing these
things Eliphaz immediately took the two friends, Bildad and Zophar, and they
went with running foot to the most noble Job, and having gone they fell down,
they begged, they asked, they requested entreaty, saying to Job: We have
erred, most noble one; we did not know what had happened to you. Now we have
known your contests, now we have known that the adversary has been broken,
from the fact that you have been shown forth as a crown-bearer. Be an
ambassador for us; may our king be reconciled to us, who is both your God and
the judge of the contest. He himself sent us to you, having said that without
your entreaty we should not receive pardon of the offenses. Hearing these
things Job, who loved his friends, did not delay, did not hesitate, did not
reproach, did not turn away; he knew how to forgive even the faults of
friends. Therefore immediately having fulfilled the sacrifice of prayer, he
prevailed upon God, and benefited the friends, and presented himself for
thanksgiving. What then does the writer Moses say? For it is good to report
also what follows. And when Job had prayed concerning his friends, he says,
the Lord forgave them their sin. And God added to Job double all that he had
before. And his brothers and sisters, hearing all that had happened to him,
came to him; and eating and drinking with him they comforted him. O the rush
of brothers and kinsmen! In mourning no one, in feasting many. When he sat
upon the dunghill, all fled; when he lodged in the palaces, all ran. And this
happens not only then, but also now. Often someone is poor, and not only is he
not acknowledged by his kinsmen, but on the contrary he receives oaths never
to come near the family. But if by the providence of him who enriched Job a
second time this man also becomes rich and takes hold of rank, immediately all
the kinsmen run down, they entreat, they cherish, they often say: What we
prayed for, we have seen: so that such people are kinsmen of the property and
not of nature. In the same way also with the most noble Job: when he sat upon
the dunghill and scraped the discharges and, suffering, was counted among the
wretched and, groaning, said: Why did I not die in my mother's womb? And why,
having come forth, did I not perish at once? And why did knees meet me? And
why did I suck my mother's breasts? then there was no brother weeping, no
sister mourning, no kinsman groaning.
But as soon as he took hold of the kingdom and became master of a double
estate, immediately the brothers were drawing up, the sisters putting on airs,
the friends leaning upon him. But why do I speak of the brothers and the
kinsmen and not rather transfer the word, because of the lateness of the hour,
to the wife of Job? For she too, while Job was suffering and living in
contest, became harder than all, that is, reproaching, railing, bidding him to
blaspheme, saying to her husband: How long will you endure, saying: Behold, I
wait yet a little time, expecting the hope of my salvation? For behold, your
memorial has been made to vanish from the earth; sons and daughters, the pangs
and labors of my womb, have died, whom I toiled for in vain with hardships.
And you yourself sit in the rottenness of worms, passing the night in the open
air, while I am a wanderer and a servant, going about from place to place and
from house to house. When will the sun set, that I may rest from the hardships
and the pains that now hold me? But say some word toward the Lord and die. You
have seen words of despair? How long will you endure, saying: Behold, I wait
yet a little time? That is: Until when do you deceive your own mind, looking
for an expectation of good things? What hope is left to you? And what did it
profit you to raise your hands often to heaven and to spread out your prayers?
You were warred upon from above, and fire was brought down from heaven. While
the wife was saying these things, Job still living in the trial, then Job
answered nothing; but as soon as he took hold of the kingdom and obtained the
estate and saw brothers and friends and kinsmen and all feasting, and likewise
the wife made bright with the royal robe and becoming mother of the same
children, Job, turning to the woman, said: What do you say, woman? Are you not
the one who rebuked me more harshly than all? Did you not say to me: How long
will you endure, saying: Behold, I wait yet a little time, expecting the hope
of my salvation? The hope of my salvation has come. Does not the farmer wait
for the ear of grain? And shall I not wait for God, who raises the poor from
the earth and lifts the beggar from the dunghill? Has not the harvest of good
things come? Has not the spring of enjoyment arrived? Are you not richer now
than before? Look around at your children: are these more unshapely than the
first? Have you not become a vine that bears twice? You were harvested of
those, but you hold these; and those live and these leap.
For thus I received all things double; if I had listened to you, woman, and
having blasphemed had gone out of life, from where would the resurrection of
good things be? You have seen how great a fruit the dunghill of endurance has
sprouted. Therefore, woman, from now on do not begin to say: I am a wanderer
and a servant. For the deceiver has fallen and the idolater has been
abolished. Truly the deceiver devil has fallen, who always raises pits against
himself. For today both a cross is woven and Job is crowned; today both Adam
is renewed and a thief is praised; today both a tomb is sealed and heaven is
opened; today the Master is struck and humanity receives freedom. And Paul
bears witness to my word, crying: For freedom Christ has redeemed us; stand
firm. To him be the glory and the power unto the ages of ages. Amen.
