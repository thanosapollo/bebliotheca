... the disobedience ... going away. Nor was he baptized for himself, but
for the one soiled by sin. Our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ therefore
fasted after the baptism. But nevertheless let us inquire also into this
in a digression. Why do we fast before baptism? But the Master Christ
fasted after the baptism. Why? Because we, being swollen with sins and
loaded in the belly of the soul, necessarily receive the fast before
baptism as a God-given antidote, so that, "having cleansed ourselves from
every defilement of flesh and spirit," we may come to a desire for
heavenly bread. But the Master Christ, as free from sin, fasts after the
baptism, bestowing a type, not lamenting sin. For even if our Master
Christ was called sin, nevertheless he himself has not been soiled, but
he took upon himself our sins, and the surety is held to account for
anything whatsoever not as principal, but as mediator. Hence, having
taken upon himself the debt of Adam and having paid it, he also wiped out
the handwriting of our sins on the cross with the sponge of
loving-kindness, and did not engrave it. But why did he wipe it out and
not engrave it? Why? Hear: if he had engraved and not wiped it out, it
would have been about to bear even a small remnant of the former debt.
Hence, having seen this, the blessed 47 Paul also cried out saying:
"Christ took the handwriting of our sins out of the midst, nailing it to
the cross." And why "did he nail it to the cross"? so that everyone going
by might learn its cancellation. Our Master Christ therefore fasted
after the baptism. And he suffered this voluntary passion (not
immediately after the baptism but after a three-year time), the one
signified as six days before, the one that abolishes every passion, the
bridal-escort of the church. He therefore suffered this passion after
working many and wondrous signs: after raising the dead, after binding
up the paralyzed, after giving eyes to the blind and cleansing lepers.
But nevertheless the apostles, filled with the Holy Spirit, having
joined these two festivals, the fast and the passion, handed down one
public feast to the race of Christians. Let us therefore rejoice and be
glad, because "six days before the Pascha Jesus came to Bethany." Why?
that he might sting the Jews and save the nations. "Six days before the
Pascha Jesus came to Bethany." Why? that he might fulfill the mystery
and open the mercy-seat; that he might dine with Lazarus and incense the
burial beforehand; that as a slave he might be tried and as a son he
might set free. "Six days before the Pascha Jesus came to Bethany." Why?
that he might be scourged for the condemned and as a lover of mankind
release them from lashes. "Six days before the Pascha Jesus came to
Bethany." Why? that, having his side pierced with a spear, he might heal
the side of Adam. that Judas Iscariot might value the material ointment
at three hundred denarii, but agree upon the Master Christ for thirty
pieces of silver. "Six days before the Pascha Jesus came to Bethany."
Why? that he might root the cross and make the seal blossom. that he
might block up Hades and open paradise. "Six days before the Pascha
Jesus came to Bethany." Why? to wear a crown of thorns and to uproot our
sins. that he might take leave of the faithful and journey to the
Father. "Six days before the 48 Pascha Jesus came to Bethany, where
Lazarus was who had died," who cannot be sent into forgetfulness, the
chief citizen of a city, who died once and was sent back a second time
to life, who was stolen by natural death and raised by the Master's
grace, seized by the tyrant Tartarus and torn away by King Christ,
soiled by disease and renewed by the Word. For you all know clearly, the
friends of Lazarus, or rather the disciples of the Lord, that when
Lazarus had died and been given over to the tomb, after four days "Jesus
came to Bethany," that he might awaken Lazarus. For with him ...
