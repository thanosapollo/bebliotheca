* On the Harlot
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of the same Saint John Chrysostom, a homily on the Pharisee and on the
harlot.
At every season repentance is good and saving for those who make use of it,
but in these days of the fast especially it is profitable for all who run
to its arms. For now our souls, laying aside fleshiness as a heavy burden,
easily fly up toward God the Creator and Master; and they accuse their own
offenses with heat, and ask pardon for their falls from the Judge who loves
mankind. It is therefore timely for us to bring into the midst, as a
teacher of repentance, that harlot who clung about the undefiled footprints
of the Master Christ, who with the streams of her tears wiped out the bond
of a most worthless life, whom the Lord, having had mercy, wiped clean, and
having pitied, washed.
Let us therefore run back to the Gospel history, that we may behold
Christ's love for mankind, and wonder at the woman's persistence, and learn
from her how we too, who are sinners, ought to run to God the Redeemer and
receive from him the forgiveness of what we have done amiss. One of the
Pharisees asked the Lord that he might eat with him; and entering into the
house of the Pharisee he reclined. Do you see the condescension of God? Do
you see the mystery of the divine economy? Do you see =150= the excess of
love for mankind? He who gives food to all flesh, invited to a meal by his
servant, came to him without hesitation, that through the dining he might
show the greatness of his condescension; and he took the Pharisee's
chamber, as a man, he who fills every place by his Godhead, that he might
show both the economy and the love for mankind. And behold, a woman in the
city, who was a sinner, knowing that he was reclining in the house of the
Pharisee, took an alabaster flask of costly myrrh, and standing behind him
began to wet his feet with her tears, and wiped them with the hair of her
head, and kissed his feet and anointed them with the myrrh, saying nothing
openly to him.
For she feared lest some foul word, coming from her sinful lips, should
move the Judge who loves mankind to anger against her. But in silence she
wove her embassy, and making confession with the heart alone, she offered
her entreaty to the Master who alone knows the things of the heart, and
toward him, the lover of mankind, she cried with the mind: I have dared to
come to you, Master, because of your mother and virgin, as you alone know
as Creator and God. Through that unwedded mother of yours and virgin, then,
pity me, the harlot; grant me forgiveness of my offenses; through that
virginity of hers pity my fornication; have mercy, lover of mankind, on her
who weeps, whom you justly hated when she laughed prettily; pity her who
groans in soul, from whom you reasonably turned away when she adorned the
body toward the ruin and deceit of many; pity the locks which you yourself
created for the adornment of my head, but which I used to deck out for the
destruction of my soul; receive the myrrh as heavenly myrrh, and wipe away
the mire of my life; receive this myrrh, and grant me spiritual myrrh, O
fountain of good things; wash me who have been soiled, almighty physician;
heal her who has received many wounds from the devil; scrape from my soul
the leprosy of sins, as you scraped from the believing leper the stain of
the body; dry up the springs of my lawless deeds, =151= as by the hem you
dried the channels of blood of the woman with the issue; have mercy on me,
Lord, Son of David, as you had mercy on that Canaanite woman, of low birth,
yet faithful. While the harlot, with tears, faithfully made this entreaty
in the hidden workshop of the soul, all who were reclining were astonished
at the Savior's long-suffering, seeing him bear with meekness a harlot's
kisses and the grasp of sinful hands. But the Pharisee who had invited him
to the meal stirred blasphemous thoughts against him in the unseen
workshops of the soul, and blamed the Savior's love for mankind as
ignorance, saying in himself: This man, if he were a prophet, would have
known what sort of woman it is who touches him, that she is a sinner. The
Pharisee's mind is altogether inhuman and blind. But what does he say to
him, the only good one and lover of mankind and giver of good things?
Simon, Simon, I have something to say to you. I have heard the words you
did not utter; I have known the counsels of your soul; I know what was
pondered in your mind against me; and therefore I answer you who have said
nothing, that from my answer you may learn the power of my Godhead; that
from my words you may know that I am maker of souls and overseer of hearts;
if then you wish to call me a prophet, or even the creator of the prophets,
me who according to what is seen by you am a man, glorify me as you will.
Simon, Simon, I have something to say to you. There were two debtors to a
certain lender. The one owed five hundred denarii, and the other fifty.
When they had nothing to pay, he forgave them both. Which of them then
ought to love him more? And the Pharisee answered and said: I suppose, the
one to whom he forgave the more. The Savior says to him: You have judged
rightly. You too owe me many accounts of your own life, and I forgave you,
just as I did this woman the charges of her way of life; and when you
invited me I readily obeyed and became one roof with you and one table; not
as one poor or lacking food, but as one rich in mercy, receiving the
repentance of those who come and forgiving their sins. Why then do you
slander my =152= condescension? Why do you find fault with my love for
mankind, of which you first enjoyed the fruit? Why do you demand of others
a great strictness which, if I should show it now, does not profit you? Why
do you call my goodness to account, of which you yourself have stood in
need? And turning toward the woman he said to Simon: Do you see this woman?
O infinite height of the love of God for mankind! O unsurpassed greatness
of the Master's long-suffering! The acknowledged harlot he addressed gently
as woman; he hid the name of the wicked choice, and brought forward the
common name of nature. Do you see this woman, whom I long ago beheld with
the eyes of the Godhead; the one who came in among us uninvited; who first
and alone was shameless with a beautiful shamelessness; who, at the needed
time, showed the life-bearing boldness; who is accused by you of many
charges, for which she came to the Life, and who did not dare, as one
without a face, to stand face to face, but hid herself behind, and became
an accuser of herself through her former life? I entered your house; you
gave no water for my feet, but she has wet my feet with her tears, and
wiped them with the hair of her own head. The feet which the sea did not
wet with its waves, she washed with her tears and entreated with her
kisses. When did you ever learn of such a woman making her own hair a
towel, as she has done upon my feet? You gave me no kiss, though being a
just man, having invited us to the meal, you ought first to have met me and
embraced me and shown the things of courtesy in all; but you did not do
this, while she, from the time I entered your house, has not ceased kissing
my feet. See her insatiable longing and chasten your slander-loving manner;
look at her and learn her weeping, if you too will not love her whom you
hated out of season. You did not anoint my head with oil, but she has
anointed my feet with myrrh; wherefore I say to you, her many sins are
forgiven.
Let us then also, brothers, emulate this faithful and wonderful harlot, and
while we still have a time of repentance in =153= these few days of the
fast, let us be eager through a clean life and an excellent way, through
fasting, through almsgiving, through tears, through confession, to wash
away every mire and stain of our many sins, that we may also hear the
gentle voice of the Master who loves mankind, saying to us: Your sins are
forgiven; that we may also become heirs of the heavenly kingdom, and be
counted worthy of the blessed voice that says: Come, you blessed of my
Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you; of which may it be that we
all attain by the grace and love for mankind of our Lord Jesus Christ, with
whom to the Father together with the Holy Spirit be glory, power, honor,
now and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
