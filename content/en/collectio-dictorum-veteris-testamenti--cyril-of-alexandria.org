COLLECTION OF THE SAYINGS OF THE OLD HOLY SCRIPTURE INTERPRETED ANAGOGICALLY
Gathered in synopsis and clearer statement, from those of the inspired Cyril,
and of the great Maximus, and of the remaining interpreters. BOOK OF GENESIS.
=77.1176= Cursed is the earth in your works. The cursed earth in the works of
Adam is the flesh of the mind that has become earth; whose works are the
passions of dishonor. Therefore it is also cursed. Which flesh yields up to
him, as thorns, cares and anxieties; and as thistles, temptations and dangers,
according to reason and anger and desire. And the saying, In the sweat of your
face you shall eat your bread, may be taken both of practice and of
contemplation; for in the toil of the practical life you shall eat the bread
of the contemplative. Cain is the mind of the flesh, which Adam first begot
through disobedience; Abel is the mind of the spirit, which the same Adam
later begot through repentance. Therefore the mind of the spirit according to
Abel, before the perfect habit, going out together with the mind of the flesh
according to Cain into the field, I mean the breadth of natural contemplation,
is killed by him. For it is good, before the perfect habit, not to touch
natural contemplation, lest while seeking spiritual meanings from the
sensibles ..... gathering passions. For among the imperfect, the apparent
forms of the sensibles dominate more toward sense, to those toward the soul,
the meanings of generated things hidden in the apparent forms. Everyone who
kills Cain will loosen seven vengeances. Cain is the thought of gluttony. And
seven vengeances, that is, things worthy of punishment. The thought of
fornication and of love of money, and that of grief, and that of anger, and
that of acedia, and that of vainglory and that of pride. Therefore the one who
has killed gluttony through self-control has killed together with it also the
passions that follow it, loosening the connections.

=77.1177= Concerning the building of the tower. Those who were building the
tower of Chalanne, having been moved from the country of light of the east, I
mean from the one and true knowledge of God, came into the land of Shinar,
which is interpreted Blasphemous teeth, and having fallen into many opinions
about God, having composed the account of each opinion as if some bricks, they
built as a tower the polytheistic atheism. Just as to Abraham dwelling in his
own land the Lord was not seen; but when he had been called and had gone out,
and had arrived at the land shown to him, He was seen and gave the great
promises, and allowed him to build an altar and to sacrifice; so also to us
remaining in the world, and in the constructive pleasures, no illumination
comes from God; but when we obey the divine commands, and arrive where the
Lord bids, He manifests the knowledge concerning Himself, as far as is
possible; and He furnishes great hopes, and strengthens our mind; so as to
raise an altar to Him, the heart; and to sacrifice good deeds, and spiritual
contemplations. And there was a famine upon the earth, and Abraham went down
into Egypt. Whenever the mind of each falls into a famine of divine thoughts
and of the food that spiritually nourishes the soul,


he is compelled to go down into the alien habit; and if the demons should see
some good understanding journeying with him, they hasten to subject it to the
seedings of their ruler; as the Egyptians did Sarah to Pharaoh. And it belongs
to God alone to deliver both this one and that one, as Abraham and Sarah,
being overpowered by the tyrant's hand and in danger. And Abraham went up out
of Egypt, himself and his wife, and all that was his, and Lot with him, into
the desert. For the one running up from an extreme worldly and sinful life
into a holy way of life must leave none of his own things behind, not thought,
not imagination, lest through that he should be forced to return. And he must
first be recast into a state desert of vice and undisturbed by mixture with
baseness. Just as Abraham, when his kinsman Lot had been taken captive by the
enemies, taking his home-born servants and some who were foreigners but
friends, pursued after him; and fighting with them and putting them to flight
he brought back the kinsman, and all the captivity; so also the apostles,
having spiritually armed the nurslings of the Church, and taking along also
some of those still minded toward worldly things, but friends to piety,
overran the demons who had taken us captive, and having contended against
them, they turned back the captives who were their kinsmen according to the
account of humanity.

=77.1180= And as a reward of such valor they receive nothing worldly, but
praise from those saved, and blessing from Christ, just as Abraham also. For
Melchizedek blessed him, who was a type of Christ, as the great Paul taught;
and taking no spoil, he nourished him with bread and wine. For the laborer, he
says, is worthy of his food. The friends, however, received portions of
spoils, God increasing here also often the substance of those who contend
together with the saints. On Melchizedek. Fatherless and motherless and
without genealogy, as having become according to virtue above nature, and the
things in nature; having neither beginning of days nor end of life, as
according to knowledge flying above the things under time and age, and having
entered into God Himself. And made like to God the Most High, according to the
likeness toward God from deeds and contemplations, and as having been made for
God and transformed into God. And he remains a priest forever, as being
stopped by no death of vice from ministering and standing before God. And he
is said to possess these things alone, as having first acquired them. When the
angels had commanded Lot to be saved toward the mountain, he himself said: I
shall not be able to be saved to the mountain, lest the evils overtake me and
I die. Behold this city is near for me to flee there, which is small; and
there my soul shall be saved. For those just now coming out of vice cannot at
once run up to a height of way of life, but they depart from the wicked
customs for the time, and they run over toward the goods not far off, and the
conduct that sits under the one who has it perfectly. And thus from there they
go up into the life that lies above, being raised from practice to
contemplation. And Lot went up from Zoar. And he sat in the mountain himself,
and his two daughters with him, and he dwelt in the cave with them. The mind
goes up as through steps toward the more elevated, having with itself prudence
and courage as daughters. For love of pleasure fell out and was cast away
after the manner of the wife of Lot. And he dwells as in a cave in the
solidity and stability of the good. For it is written that the righteous shall
dwell in a high cave of a strong rock. And the strong rock would be Christ,
because of His all-might; and His cave the Church, the enclosure and shelter
of the saints. For they put on a rock, he says, which they inhabit, those
fleeing the fire of sin, and


escaping the fire of Gehenna. The heifer taken first signified the first
generation. For just as the heifer is restive toward the yoke, so also the
first crop of the people that went down =77.1181= into Egypt was restive
toward slavery. And the goat taken second hinted at the second generation. For
just as the goat furnishes little profit to the masters, so also the second
generation assigned little slavery to the Egyptians. And the ram taken third
prefigured the third generation. For just as this gives more profit, so also
that furnished more of a slave. The division then of the three generations;
and the facing placement of the divided parts, the communion of the
ill-treatment. For the first generation also was distressed dwelling among the
Egyptians. And God allowed the affliction in Egypt, so that later being
delivered from so many hardships they might know more greatly the one who
delivered them; and that He Himself also might be known in all the earth
through the signs He did. Discordant with the new people is the one firstborn
according to time. For this was hinted by the infants leaping and being
troubled in the womb of Rebecca, indicating the enmity that would be. Jacob,
being born, had taken hold of the heel of Esau. And the thing that happened
showed that the mind that sees God through purity (for this is how Israel is
interpreted) trips up the passions of gluttony, from which the other diseases
of the soul also are born. Jacob, putting on dead skins of kids, typed Christ
putting on dead flesh as regards sin, through which He brought our salvation
as a most pleasant food to the Father, and received the inalienable blessing,
as having become acceptable, and granted it to the Christians. Jacob anointing
the stone. He shows that the hard-hearted soul of the nations will be anointed
with the Holy Spirit, and will be set apart for God. And he hinted also that
the assumed flesh will be anointed with the Godhead; for Christ is the
cornerstone joining through Himself the two peoples. And Pharaoh said to
Joseph: Say to your brothers, Taking your Father and your possessions, come to
me, and I will give you of all the goods of Egypt. The devil, if only he
should take some as subjects, fills them with every love of pleasure. For
Egypt is sin, and its goods are the pleasures according to it. But he also
gives wagons for the transport of what belongs to them, making the journey
toward him easy and without toil. The father then of those captured is the
mind. And the possessions are the thoughts and the deeds. And likewise again
the king of the Assyrians promised those in Jerusalem saying: Come out to me
and each of you shall eat his own vine, and his own fig tree, and you shall
drink water of your cistern. For since the manner of sin is twofold, and we
either, overcome by the innate pleasures, or by those from outside, go wrong,
=77.1184= the vine and the fig tree would be the pleasures from outside, and
the water of our cistern the innate ones, bubbling up from our body. And those
from outside are temporary, while the innate are more long-lasting. Of all of
which the devil promises the enjoyment to those who desert from the
holy-seeming way of life, as from Jerusalem, toward him, and who accept
serving him. Of the Exodus concerning Pharaoh. Pharaoh first afflicted the
Israelite people with wild toils, thinking to hinder its increase into
multitude. But God working against it: For in whatever, he says, the Egyptians
humbled them, by so much they became more and were strong; he comes secretly
upon another device, and commands the midwives of the Hebrews to kill the
males of those born, but to preserve the females. And when God had hindered
this also: For the midwives, he says, feared God, and did not do as Pharaoh
commanded them; he lays hold of a third plot, and openly commands those under
him, to cast every male born into


the river, but to keep every female alive. And the devil also, the
intelligible Pharaoh, against the intelligible Israel, I mean the pious
people, devised a triple manner of plot, trying through wicked schemes to
hinder its advance into virtue. And first he brings upon them toil and
hardship; and missing the aim, he arms their brothers according to faith and
friends against those manly toward piety. For he does not plot against the
womanish as weak, knowing them useful to himself toward sin. And the midwives
are sisters and friends of the Hebrews, because of the kinship from the race.
And failing also of the second plot, the fear of God holding back those armed,
he openly permits his own shield-bearers the savagery and bloodshed against
the saints. Pharaoh of our way of life loves and keeps alive the female and
weak and obedient to him; but he is eager to murder the male and manly and
suspected toward uprising. And the sober and foreseeing thoughts, having
begotten virtue, as the parents did Moses, and having secured him in the ark
of education, do not allow the virtuous one to be sunk in the stream of life.
And when he has been brought to the bank of stability, outside philosophy,
taking him, being a daughter to Pharaoh because of pride and impiety, but
barren because she brings forth no offspring of knowledge of God into light,
adopting him nurses him on the maternal milk. For he does not stand away from
the holy doctrines, even if he pursues Greek education. And having run up to a
height of spiritual stature, he becomes in the midst of two fighting, one of
the same race and a foreigner: that is, of pious speech and impious, or of
virtue =77.1185= and vice, and standing with his own he destroys the alien.
And fearing the danger from Pharaoh, he flees farthest from his authority,
stretching toward a higher way of life, and he drives away the wicked
shepherds who use unjustly the stream of teaching, with the rod of rebukes,
even if he must again dwell with a foreigner, that is, keep company with
outside philosophy, he accepts this because of the need, living in calm and
shepherding the movements of the soul. And to one living thus the mystery
according to the bush will appear shining upon him, and teaching that the fire
of the Godhead, having surrounded human nature according to the incarnation,
fired this and deified it, having kept it whole. And it burned up the thorns
of sin put forth. And that it is a prefiguration of the Church from the
nations. For just as the bush is low-lying, and spread out upon the earth, and
bristling with thorns; so also the nations formerly were dragged about earthly
thoughts, and the passions of sin, able to imagine nothing high; and they were
scattered upon the earth, and had put on the thorns of sin. And the immaterial
fire through divine baptism, flaming them without passion, cleanses them,
burning only the thorns of sin; For He, he says, will baptize you in the Holy
Spirit and fire. And that it sketches the womb of the Mother of God; in which
the fire of the Godhead flashing preserved her unburned. Just as Moses was
slow of tongue, so also the Mosaic law is slow of tongue toward articulating
the account concerning the One who is, and making clear the theology
concerning the Holy Trinity. And thin-voiced also, as able to speak to few.
For the Israelites are few before all the nations. Therefore there is added to
this also Aaron his brother, that is, the evangelical law. For just as Christ
is brother of Moses according to the human; so also the evangelical law is
brother and in accord with the Mosaic according to its spiritual and
intelligible. Which also speaks to all the nations, as Aaron also to all the
people. And the evangelical becomes a mouth to the Mosaic, since Aaron also to
Moses and


interprets what that one speaks spiritually and unclearly, and transfers his
thin voice toward the more well-sounding. For I did not come, he says, to
destroy the law, but to fulfill it through the addition of what was lacking.
And the Mosaic becomes to the evangelical the things toward God; for to the
evangelical the starting-points of piety have come from the Mosaic. And Moses,
he says, was 80 years old when he spoke to Pharaoh. And Aaron was 83 years
old; for according to time Aaron is first. Since also the evangelical way of
life appeared before the legal in Abraham, and those according to him. But
according to the sending and ministry, second, since also the Gos =77.1188=
pel was given later than the law. And Aaron exceeds by a triad of years, since
Christ also according to the Godhead. And otherwise also Christ is older
according to the Godhead; but later-born according to the humanity and the
economy. The rod falling from the hand of Moses, and being reshaped into a
serpent, and again taken in his hand by the tail, and restored into a rod,
signified that the first-formed, being at the beginning in the hand of God,
fell away into the earthly things, transgressing the commandment, and through
vice crept into the death-bearing wickedness of a serpent. And again the
Creator God, laying hold of him through the incarnation, restored him to the
first image, and clothed him with the ancient beauty through the keeping of
the commandments. And the tail, being the last part, indicated the incarnation
in the last days. His hand being brought into the bosom of Moses, and in being
brought out becoming leprous, and again being brought in, and being brought
out clean, signified that the human made according to the image of God, while
he was under the shelter of God, as in a bosom, was clean and most beautiful.
But in being brought out of this through love of pleasure and disobedience, he
became leprous with the ugliness and abomination of sin. And again coming to
be under the shelter of God, through self-control and obedience, and adoption,
he was restored to the original fairness, and appeared clean and bright. The
desert from which Moses is sent into Egypt is this world, human nature, or the
habit and state deprived of the passions, where the mind, having been educated
through contemplation in the knowledge of beings, is commanded by God
according to the unseen of the heart, to lead out from the flesh and from
sense, as from Egypt, like Israelites, the divine thoughts of beings, toiling
about the mud of the passions. And going toward this with also the wedded
wisdom, and the virtuous thought begotten to him from her, and a solemn
manner, when he should halt and stand from the course of virtue, whose
standing is a movement of vice, being occupied about some of the material
things lying on either side of this road; then, the indicated son of his
becoming uncircumcised and profane, he sees as a severe angel the convicting
word of conscience threatening death. Whom his housemate wisdom shames,
circumcising the material imagination that has come to be in the child
thought, and as a flow of blood drying up the movement of passionate life.
Moses, being appointed to a sending, is timid and does not run straight, but
also confers with those related by race about the departure, and takes along
the wife and the children. But Aaron, having done no such thing, sets out at
once. For those of the Mosaic law are both cowardly toward =77.1189=
temptations and shrinking, and divided toward the divine commandments, and
toward the bodily and worldly relations. But those of the Gospel, and of whom
Aaron is a type, are both bold toward dangers and eager, and not divided
toward God and world, but clinging only to the divine commandments. Zipporah
the Midianite was a type of the Church from the nations, who, being subjected
to the seedings of Moses, that is, to the dim theologies of the Mosaic law
concerning the Holy Trinity of the one who educates toward


the Gospel according to Christ, had as a son the multitude of those being
regenerated to her through the Holy Spirit; whom she also circumcised with a
stone knife, taking away all his love of pleasure and sin by the word
according to Christ, who is called a rock because of the firm, and a knife
because of dividing the better from the worse. And the blood of the
circumcision stood, he says, i.e., the indicated spiritual circumcision has
been fulfilled completely. Therefore also the angel who was stretching death
against Moses withdrew. So that it is clear that a teacher is often freed from
danger, purifying the disciple, and receives the benefit of this as his own
salvation. Just as Pharaoh demanded of the Hebrews the brickmaking without
excuse, but did not give straw through which it is done; so also the devil
demands by compulsion of the humans serving him the defilement and the
uncleanness, inflaming them wildly into rabid desires; but he makes the thing
hard to obtain, lest by easiness they should quickly come to satiety and
contempt. Moses said to Pharaoh: We will go a journey of three days into the
desert, that we may sacrifice to the Lord our God; lest death or murder meet
us. For since the Egyptians in sacrificing abominated the dead; Moses makes
this a pretext of withdrawal to a distance. Lest, he says, even of the
foreigners we should encounter a corpse while sacrificing, and dissolve the
sacrifice. Saying death, the one who has died naturally; and murder the one
killed from a plot. And we too must, coming farthest from sin, sacrifice to
God, and turning away the passions that deaden and murder our souls. And Moses
said to Pharaoh: We will go with our young men, and our elders, with our sons
and daughters, and our sheep and oxen; for it is a feast to the Lord our God.

Those who desire freedom from the demons and passions must try to flee
completely, leaving no remnant of their own soul and mind behind, through
which they would be likely to be drawn down again, and to be caught by the
former evils. And the young men would be as a type of courage, and the elders
as a symbol of prudence, and the sons and daughters as an image of infancy and
simplicity in Christ. For with courage and prudence and simplicity according
to God =77.1192= one must depart from sin into sanctification. Be manly, he
says, and let your heart be strengthened; and, Become prudent as the serpents,
and harmless as the doves. And the sheep and the oxen are a type of the bodily
and more irrational movements; which themselves must not be given over to the
devil. But Pharaoh lets the men go even unwillingly; but the women and the
underage children, and the weak old men, and the other animals, he tries to
hold back. For he fears those manly toward virtue, as able to resist; but he
claims the womanish and soft souls, and the weakened and unwarlike thoughts,
and he preserves whatever is childish toward understanding, and whatever is
thick and irrational. And Moses said to Pharaoh: But you also will give us
whole burnt offerings and sacrifices which we shall make to the Lord our God.
One takes from Egypt and Pharaoh, and offers to God, taking from the godless
wisdom and the education of those outside, as victims the fluency and beauty
of speech, and mixing them with the doctrines of piety, and through the
artistic helping piety, and making the weapons of the lie weapons of the
truth; but also the contemplative and examining, and the love of learning and
the endurance of toil and whatever such, transferring from the harmful to the
beneficial, we bring over from Egypt and Pharaoh toward God. And God did not
lead them by the way of the Philistines, because it was near, lest the people
repent, seeing war, and turn back into E gypt. For near


and easy to see is the mystery concerning Christ. But God did not lead the
Jews at once toward this, but through the long law and the many riddles, as
through some long road, He was leading them toward this; lest being warred
upon by the thoughts of the excess, they should turn back again into Egyptian
impiety, but that practicing from afar the shadow of the truth, and being
trained according to the fitting time, they might come easily upon the
perfect. Philistines then is interpreted the marvelous, and a marvelous way is
the short one, of which sort is that of the divine Gospel. And God led them,
by day in a pillar of cloud, to show them the way; and by night in a pillar of
fire. A pillar of the truth is God drawing from earth into heaven. And night
is the time before the incarnation of the Lord; because of the darkness of
error, and the prevailing gloom of the passions; and day that of His
sojourning, in which He Himself the sun of righteousness rose, lighting and
brightening the souls of those who believe. For the rest then He led the Jews
with the punishing law; for fire is a symbol of punishment. But He leads the
Christians with the salvation through holy baptism. For the cloud is water and
a symbol of the divine water of baptism.

For it is =77.1193= not possible to arrive at the city above and holy, unless
Christ leads the way, and shows the road of salvation. And the pillar of cloud
also lifted from their face, and stood from behind them, and went in between
the camp of the Egyptians, and between the camp of the sons of Israel, and
they did not mix with one another the whole night. For it being night, that
is, a time bringing cowardice upon the pious, the power of the Lord stands in
the midst of them and of those pursuing them, visible or invisible enemies;
not allowing them to clash until the fear should pass. Or also the account is
about those just now being catechized in the word of piety, with whom, being
still weak, He prevents the enemies from engaging. But after they should come
as through a sea, through divine baptism, He lets them fight, having put away
the cowardice. For the Israelites, having crossed the Red Sea, became most
warlike and hard to contend against. The thirst that came upon the Israelites
in the desert was a type of the toils in ascesis, and a prelude of training.
For see, that not at the beginning did a war of enemies fall upon them; for
they had not yet been trained toward excellence. For this they are first
tested by the toils of the flesh. And to this also looks the saying:
Temptation has not yet overtaken you, except a human one. With wood the bitter
water of Marah was sweetened. For in Christ the wood of life the bitter things
are sweetened; and the hard to bear become bearable. Just as the Israelites,
having remembered the meats, desired to be in Egypt, and to serve again so as
to be filled with them, so also sharp pleasures of passions often persuade the
more mindless among us to consider the slavery of the devil bearable, for the
sake of enjoying them. And God lets them, being unrestrained. For this the
partaking of the quail images. But just as for those who preferred the meat to
the manna the eating ended in cholera; so also for these who prefer the
earthly luxury to the heavenly, and the fleshly pleasure to the spiritual, the
partaking will end in destruction of the soul. Concerning the manna. The manna
came down in the morning, prefiguring Christ, who is heavenly bread feeding
the souls of those who hunger; who also as a rising of the sun has sojourned
to those sitting in darkness; and the quail toward evening, showing the
animal-sacrifice of the legal worship, which was performed in shadows and dim
traces of the truth, and was going to receive an end. For evening is the end
of the day. Moses, having commanded


Joshua son of Nun to choose powerful men and to go out and draw =77.1196= up
against Amalek, indicated that Jesus Christ will be subject to the
commandments of the Mosaic law. For I did not come, he says, to destroy the
law, but to fulfill. And having chosen from all Israel, and from every nation,
powerful men; I mean the holy apostles and their successors; he will draw up
against the devil the king of vice and sin, and will war him down.

And he appointed the array for the morrow; showing the later time of the
incarnation after the fullness of the legal time. And Moses goes up upon the
peak of the hill, that he may have a view of the battle. For the mystagogy
according to the law also leads up toward a commensurate height of knowledge;
from which Jews might be able to view the generalships of those about Christ;
and when Moses stretched out his hands Israel prevailed; and when he let them
down Amalek prevailed. For hands shown stretched out into air write the figure
of the cross, and every cross-bearer conquers; just as again everyone not
bearing the cross, but letting go the faith according to Christ, is warred
upon. And the hands of Moses are heavy, and barely raised toward the said
type. Because those of the law also barely come to wearing the cross of
Christ. And taking, he says, a stone they put it under him, and he sat upon
it; and Aaron and Hur supported his hands, from here one, and from here one.
For Christ is a precious and chosen stone, upon which those from Israel who
believe rest, leaning. And the same Christ is understood as both Aaron and
Hur, as high priest and judge; for Aaron is high priest, and Hur a judge.
Which Christ, being understood through both, underprops the faithful from
Israel, into the faith through the cross, and victory, and does not let them
be weighted and pulled down. Moses, having been sounded by God, commanded the
people to wash their own garments, and to abstain from a woman, and thus to
stand before God under Mount Sinai. For one must wash off the shame of vice
and put on the beauty of virtue, and be pure of bodily pleasure and
defilement. And the law, of which Moses is a type, commanding this, presents
those who obey to Christ God, educating and ordering, and leading them to
perfection.

And God came down not toward the people, but toward the mountain. For the
people of the Hebrews is earth-loving and earthly, but that of the Christians
high-minded, and elevated in contemplation, and lifted above the earthly
things. To whom also God sojourned bodily and kept company having become
human. And fire about the mountain, for fear of the thick people, who had not
a spirit of adoption, but of slavery unto fear, being led by force and not
piously of their own will; but for cleansing of the new; for there is also a
purifying fire. And the smoke hinted at the hidden of the divine nature. For
He set, he says, darkness His hiding-place. Or also otherwise, the smoke as a
symbol of the mist which has been poured over the mind of the Jews, =77.1197=
not allowing them to gaze into the rays of the truth. And the fire as an image
of the light which shines upon the mind of those who believe in Christ. And
the voices of the trumpet going forward became stronger; for when Christ
sojourned with us, the things declared concerning Him by the law have become
clearer and more known. Hear these things, he says, all you nations. But Moses
spoke, he says, and God answered him with a voice. For the one as a pedagogue
sought the law by which they were going to be improved; and God legislated.
And the voice of the Father is the Son, as Word. For from Him is the law, even
if it has been spoken through angels to Moses. And at God's command, Moses
goes up to the peak of the mountain; for when Christ has called the faithful
people, the one lying above


the people of the Jews, and servant of God, this one goes up upon the highest
knowledge of God; but the people is barred from drawing near, lest they die.
For to the worldly and flesh-loving the higher contemplation is dangerous, but
also to the unclean priests; For let them be sanctified, he says, lest the
Lord make an end of them; that is, destroy them, or also withdraw because of
their uncleanness. And when Moses had come down and testified to the people;
for condescendingly and as it were earthily and bodily the law speaks to Jews;
Aaron also goes up with him, God having commanded. For the Mosaic law draws
near to God first as holy, and later also the evangelical, as most holy, and
they are yoked according to God, that is, according to the spiritual
contemplation; for they sing together at least according to what is
contemplated spiritually. And the remaining priestly race is prevented from
going up to the peak of the mountain. For to those still performing the
worship in shadows it is not given by God to be led up toward a height of

Zerubbabel is the practical mind as more warlike. And Joshua the
contemplative, as more holy. And rulers of the families, the powers of the
soul, under which both the manners of virtue, and the meanings of knowledge.

And the demons of the said passions come to these, wishing to build together
the house of the Lord. For none of these demons hinders the eagerness of those
who contend according to God; but even rather having deceitfully cut around
the lackings of the goods, he suggests the stretchings, as being eager
together; that having lost the equal weight of the mean, we may be carried
unnoticed toward another lodging dear to them. Therefore they also say, that
Likewise to you we hear of the Lord, and we sacrifice to him, that is, we
accept the commandments of the Lord, and we work with those who work them, and
truly they do not turn away from these, until the aim of those who work has
inclined toward them having been turned from the straight. For the other
demons one would easily understand; but those who seem to work with the course
of the life according to God, barely those according to Zerubbabel and Joshua
would discern, and beat off their cooperation, knowing well, that the mixture
of this makes a taking down of the whole building. For it does not look to the
glory of God, but toward the passion, into which the cooperating demons wish
to transfer. The demons then who war according to a lacking of virtue are,
those who suggest the passions of dishonor from anger and from desire; and
those according to excess, those who stand before the four passions.
