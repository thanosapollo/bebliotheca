For the man of God always receives progress in each of the good things, in
humility, in kindness, in longsuffering; and the foundation of all these is
confidence toward God. For those who do not have such a foundation are as
those who build a house without a foundation; for just as those around the
Maccabees, having greatly shown valor, subdued their enemies in the bodily
war, let us take an example for ourselves toward the war "against the
spiritual hosts of wickedness," thus 116 noble and great-souled are the true
warriors of Christ. Just as the visible world is governed by grace and by
justice, by grace in abundance of fruits, and by justice in famine and in
plague, so also the soul that wishes to inherit the upper world of the Godhead
is governed in many ways by grace, and at one time it rejoices in the gladness
of the Spirit, and at another it is handed over to temptations and afflictions
of the evil spirit, so that thus, having been fired, it may be restored as
proven.
C Nr. 32c f. 134r13-25 - R Nr. 30b f. 139v13-140r2 - J Nr.. 19 For just as in
life there is not always fruitfulness, 118 for if there were always ease and
luxury, they would have grown wanton against God; for "Israel ate and grew fat
and the beloved kicked." So also the soul, if it always lived in the ease of
the Spirit, would no longer know itself and would be ignorant of the way of
"righteousness" and would progress only in conceit, as the apostle says,
recounting the afflictions as being for advantage: "There was given me," he
says, "a thorn in the flesh, that I might not be exalted." You see that for
such a holy man the afflictions were for advantage.
C Nr. 32e f. 134v15-25 - R Nr. 30d f. 140r22-140v8 - J Nr. 19 For just as the
form of hemp, the more it is beaten, becomes finer and more useful, so the
God-loving soul, entering into many trials and afflictions and enduring, 120
becomes more useful and purer for the spiritual work, and at last is deemed
worthy to inherit the heavenly choir of the saints. For as the soul endures
and is longsuffering, little by little the portion of evil is diminished. For
just as if someone travels a long road carrying a purse full of sand, and it
has a very fine hole below, the more one walks that sand flows out and it is
lightened, until all the sand is emptied, so little by little the indwelling
sin is lessened for the one who clings to God.
C Nr. 32e f. 134v15-25 - R Nr. 30d f. 140r22-140v8 - J Nr.. 19 For just as an
infant is useless for the works of the world, for it can neither build cities
nor plant nor do any other 122 thing, so souls not proven in various
afflictions are still infants, they are not yet useful to the kingdom, as he
says: "But if you are without discipline," "then you are bastards and not
sons." See how much the afflictions are for the advantage of man, only that
one prepare himself eagerly and nobly to bear them.
C Nr. 32f f. 134v25-135r15 - R Nr. 30e f. 140v8-141r4 - J Nr. 19 For if in
this life one will not be able to pass through to offices and great
advancements, unless he first come into much training and succeed in toils and
in wars, having shown the victory by experience and bearing trophies against
the adversaries, how much more, with the heavenly and true King, 124 no one is
deemed worthy of the heavenly gifts of the Spirit, unless he first be trained
in the practice of the holy commandments, and thus he receives a heavenly
weapon (grace itself) and contends against the spirits "of wickedness." 126
For the more one progresses into the spiritual contest he still receives of
the spiritual mysteries and of the hidden treasures of wisdom, and the more he
increases in grace, so much does he progress in knowing the "thoughts" of
evil.
