Of the same, an Ascetical Discourse, and an exhortation concerning
renunciation of the worldly life, and spiritual perfection.
Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest, says
the divine voice, whether hinting at the rest there, or at the rest from here.
Yet at any rate it calls us, on the one hand urging us to cast off the burden
of much possession through the laying of it down for those in need; on the
other, having thrown off through well-doing and confession the swarm of sins
that comes upon us from this, to run toward the cross-bearing life of the
monks.
He therefore who has chosen to obey Christ, and who is hastening toward the
poor and undistracted life, is truly wonderful and blessed. But I beg, let him
not do this untested, nor subscribe for himself an easy life and an
uncontested salvation; but rather let him train himself beforehand toward
success in endurance of afflictions both bodily =31.628= and spiritual, lest,
having thrown himself into unexpected wrestlings, then being unable toward the
resistance of the trials that come upon him, he be found running back toward
the things from which he came out, returning to the life with shame and
laughter, with condemnation of soul, and becoming a scandal to many,
implanting in all suspicions of weakness of the life in Christ, whose danger
you all know who read the Gospels, in which the divine voice says: It is
expedient for him that a millstone of an ass be hung about his neck, and he be
thrown into the sea, rather than that he should scandalize one of these little
ones.
For he will not only be liable to the condemnation of desertion, but also of
the destruction of those overturned by him, even if he seem with deranged
reasonings to persuade himself that, living in the worldly life, he
conciliates the Divine by good works; which is impossible for him. For he who
was not strong, in the life hard to sin in through its being undistracted, to
bear the wrestlings of the enemy, how in the much-sinning life and ruled by
him will he be able to accomplish anything of the virtuous things? And even if
one should grant that he corrects his own life, he will not escape the charge
of the abandonment of Christ, just as also the disciples shown in the Gospel,
of whom the divine evangelist says: And many of the disciples went back, and
no longer walked with Jesus, saying: His word is hard, who is able to hear it?
For this reason also the man-loving God, caring for our salvation, divided the
conduct of men into two lives, I mean marriage and virginity, so that he who
cannot bear the contest of virginity might come to cohabitation with a wife,
knowing this, that he will be required an account of temperance and
sanctification, and of likeness to the saints in marriages and child-rearings.
Such was Abraham in the Old Testament, who, sacrificing the only-begotten
without pity by preference of God, was exalted, and kept the doors of his tent
spread open, making ready for the reception of those about to be entertained
as guests. For he did not hear: Sell your possessions, and give to the poor.
And Job displayed things greater than these, and very many others, both David
and Samuel. And in the New Testament, such was Peter, and the rest of the
apostles. For every man will be required the fruits of love toward God and the
neighbor, and for the turning aside from these commandments and from all he
will pay the penalty. As also the Lord makes clear in the Gospels saying: He
who loves father or mother above me is not worthy of me; and, Whoever does not
hate his own father, and his mother =31.629=, and his wife, and the children,
and even his own soul, cannot be my disciple.
Does it seem to you that the Gospels have been set also for those under wives?
See, it has been made plain to you that all men will be required the obedience
toward the Gospel, both monks and those in marriages. For to the one who has
come to marriage the pardon of incontinence and of desire toward the female
and of intercourse will suffice; but the rest of the commandments, legislated
likewise for all, are not without danger for those who transgress. For Christ,
evangelizing the charges of the Father, was speaking to those in the world;
and if somewhere it also happened that, being asked privately, he answered his
own disciples, he testified saying: And what I say to you, I say to all. Do
not then lie back, O man, you who have chosen communion with a wife, as if
having authority to put on the world. For you have need of more toils and of
watchfulness toward the attainment of salvation; inasmuch as you have chosen
to dwell in the midst of the snares and of the power of the apostate powers,
and have the provocations of the sins before your eyes, and have all your
senses pried toward desire of them both by night and by day.
Know therefore that you will not escape the wrestling-ground against the
apostate, nor will you lay hold of victory over him without many toils spent
upon the keeping of the evangelical doctrines. For how will you be able to
deny the battle against the enemy, while you are living in the wrestling-pit
of the battle? Which is all the earth under heaven, in which we are taught by
the writing of Job that this one goes about and walks about, as a raging dog,
seeking whom he may swallow. If then you deny the battle against the
antagonist, pass over into another world, in which he himself is not; and
there will belong to you the denial of the wrestling against him, and a
danger-free idleness from the evangelical doctrines. But if this is
impossible, hasten toward the learning of the contest against him, being
taught by the Scriptures the art of wrestlings; lest, defeated by him from
ignorance, you be delivered to the eternal fire. And let these things
nonetheless have been said in passing toward those in marriages who carelessly
neglect the keeping of the commandments of Christ as if without danger. But
you, the lover of the heavenly commonwealths, and the dealer in the angelic
conduct, and who desire to become a fellow-soldier of the holy disciples of
Christ, strengthen yourself toward the endurance of the =31.632= afflictions,
and come manfully to the assembly of the monks; and at the beginning of your
renunciation play the man, not to be dragged down by attachment to kinsmen
according to the flesh, being strengthened by the exchange of the mortal
toward the immortal; and when you make the laying-down of the things belonging
to you, be unbending, being fully assured that you send them on into the
heavens, hiding them in the bosoms of the poor, but finding them with God with
much addition.
And being torn from friends and familiars, do not become over-distressed,
being joined to Christ who was crucified for you; than whom what more friendly
thing is to be reckoned by us? And when by the cooperation of God you conquer
your enemy in this first boxing-bout, do not throw yourself down as some
dishonored vessel. For already by the renunciation of earthly things you have
honored yourself with Christ; but with much care and circumspection make it
your work to find a man, an unerring advance of your manner of life, who knows
well how to guide those going toward God, flourishing in the virtues, having
from his own works the witness of love toward God, having knowledge of the
divine Scriptures; undistracted, unloving of money, unbusied, quiet,
God-loving, poor-loving, without anger, unremembering of evil, abundant toward
the building-up of those who draw near him, without vainglory, without pride,
not a flatterer, not to be turned aside, preferring nothing to God. And if you
find such a one, give yourself over to him, having spat out and thrown outside
all your own will, so that you may be found as a clean vessel, keeping the
goods thrown into you toward your praise and glory. For if you leave behind
for yourself any of the passions that existed in you beforehand, having turned
the goods thrown into you into vinegar, you will be thrown outside, as some
useless vessel. This is a second wrestling against the antagonist of our
salvation. For of good teachers the lessons are good; and surely also of bad,
bad. For when our wicked adversary is not strong enough to persuade us to
remain in the confusion and destruction of the world, he hastens to persuade
us not to give ourselves over to an exact life, or to a man who brings all our
sins under sight and corrects them, but to one of those mad for glory, who
establishes his own passions on the pretext of condescension toward those
living with him, so that, having thus made us again secretly subject to a
myriad passions, he may set us under the proper bonds of sin. But if you give
yourself over to a man of many virtues, you will become an heir of the goods
in him, and most blessed =31.633= with God and with men.
But if from sparing of the body you should seek a teacher condescending to the
passions in you, or rather to say, falling down with you, you have undergone
the contest of renunciation in vain, having given yourself over to a
passionate life, using a blind guide, and being led forward onto a pit. For if
a blind man guide a blind man, both will fall into a pit. For it is enough for
the disciple to become according to his teacher. The voice is of God, and it
will not miss. You must live by an athletic law, and if not, not be crowned,
as the Apostle said: And if anyone contend, he is not crowned, unless he
contend lawfully. If then you find by the grace of God (for surely having
sought you will find) a teacher of good works, keep it with yourself to do
nothing against his judgment. For everything that comes to be outside him is a
kind of theft and sacrilege leading to death, not to benefit, even if it seem
to you to be good. For if it is good, for what cause does it come to be in
secret, and not in the open? Question your reasoning that contrives to rob you
through the right-hand things; for through the supposedly good acts of
disobedience he prepares for you the left-hand deeds. Do not wish to correct
with incantations those bitten by beasts, being inexperienced in the chanting,
lest, having drawn the snakes upon yourself, and being gripped round by them,
then being unable toward the resistance, you be consumed by them without
pardon. Do not lean upon nobility of flesh, and seek honor; For the fleshly
man does not receive the things of the Spirit. Do not be tempted to
counterfeit anything of the custom of the truth, and by your own slackness to
trip up those who are contending, and to heap upon yourself a burden of sins,
not in a softer bed, not in garments, or shoes, or any other form, or in a
change of foods, or a table beyond the time of your renunciation, or in
standing, or in sitting, or in a more restful or cleaner handicraft. For all
these things, not only when they belong to you, but also when they are sought
after, will not have a good outcome. For unless, having recognized quickly
that the method is diabolic, you cut it out of your heart, they prepare for
you a falling-away from the life in Christ.
But setting =31.636= it in yourself to be more dishonored and more sinful than
all men, both a stranger and a wanderer, and taken up by pity by those who
renounced before you, hasten to be last of all, and a slave of all. For these
things will bring you true honor and glory, not those. Having ears opened
toward obedience, and hands ready toward fulfillment of what has been heard,
have a silent mouth, and a circumspect heart. Be idle concerning idle talk,
but intelligent and knowing toward the saving hearings of the divine
Scriptures. Let the hearing of worldly narratives be a bitter taste to you,
but the narratives of holy men honeycombs of honey.
Hasten toward the imitation of those who have trained beforehand in the
manners, and do not wait to be taught each thing. Struggle to stretch out
toward the greater of the virtues, and not to neglect the lesser. Do not
despise a fault, even if what has come to be is smaller than every scandal.
But hasten rather toward the correction through repentance; even if many go
wrong in many things, both small and greater, and remain unrepentant. Do not
become a judge of others' stumblings. For they have a just judge, Who will
render to each according to his works. You, master your own, and make light,
as much as is in your power, your own burden. For he who makes his burden
heavy, he himself will also bear it. Repentance is salvation; but mindlessness
is the death of repentance. Hide yourself from the more idle men; but manifest
yourself, for the most part, to God. Decline every going-out, as much as is in
you, fleeing the dissipations of your heart. For have you gone out of your
cell?
You have left self-control; you have stooped into the world; you have met a
harlot woman, who, having bewitched you, with provocative words the hearings,
and with the surrounding beauty of the face the sights, and with greedy foods
the tastes, as with a hook, will draw you toward herself; then having wrapped
herself about your limbs, and having taken you to her breast, she softens the
steadfastness of your desire toward self-control; and so, having quietly
dragged you away from the =31.637= life according to virtue, she corrupts you
in herself. And if somewhere, being helped by God, you should also be able to
escape her nets, you have returned to the cell, but not the same; but some
palsied and diseased person, and displeased toward every work of the virtues,
and able only after much time to return into your proper habit. For the desire
toward either life will straiten you with reasonings; and with much labor will
you be able to give the prizes of victory to the soul. If then it happen that
you go abroad from the cell by some circumstance, having armored yourself
round with the fear of God, and having fixed in your hand the love of Christ,
having wrestled down by all self-control the assaults of the pleasures, at
once when the need has come to be, leap away, not having lingered, but using a
sharp wing toward the return, as some innocent dove, having gone of yourself
toward the ark that sent you out, bearing in your mouth the mercies of Christ;
and so persuading the reasonings within that the saving rest upon every other
place is impassable. Being young either in the flesh or in the mind, flee the
living-together of those of the same age, and run away from them, as from a
flame. For the enemy, having burned many through them, has delivered them to
the eternal fire, having overturned them by a supposedly spiritual love into
the abominable pit of the people of the five cities. And those who had been
saved on the sea through every wind and wave, being careless inside the
harbors, he delivered to the deep with the boat and its men. In sitting sit
far from him. In the lying-down of sleep let not your garments neighbor his;
rather use an elder as mediator.
And when he converses with you, or psalms face to face, answer him bending
downward, lest, having received by the gazing into the faces a seed of desire
from the enemy who sows over, you bear harvests of corruption and destruction.
In a house or in a place where there is not the one who sees your works, do
not be found with him, on the pretext of study of divine oracles, or of any
other even most necessary need. For nothing is more necessary than a soul, for
which Christ died. Do not trust a craft-working reasoning that suggests to you
that it is without scandal; being fully assured that this very thing is a
scandal, the much experience of those who have fallen having furnished these
things plainly to you. =31.640= Trust my words brought forth from a
brother-loving heart. Run toward elders hard of access, who with words of
proverbs anoint the young toward the virtuous deeds, but harm in no way by the
face.
With all watch keep your heart. For just as gold is watched without ceasing by
the thieves in night and in day, but is snatched unexpectedly, and you do not
know; see that the enemy not deceive you by the sin of the forefather, and
quickly cast you out of the paradise of delight. For he who dragged Adam away
from life by the theft of the eating, and who expected to trip up Jesus also,
will not be ashamed much more to mix for you also the first cause of the
evils, knowing this to be a strong poison. For the passion of gluttony is not
wont to show its own strength in a multitude of foods, but in desire and a
small taste. If then desire of some brief taste be strong enough to subject
you to the passion of gluttony, it will deliver you without toil to death. For
just as a certain nature of water, being divided among many channels, prepares
all the place about the channels to flourish; so also the passion of gluttony,
if it be given up into your heart, watering all your senses, having planted in
you matter of wickedness, will set your soul as a dwelling-place of beasts.
For I have seen many who were held by passions come into health, but one from
all who was a secret-eater, or a glutton, I have not seen, except either
having broken away from the self-controlled life, and having been corrupted by
the world, or trying to hide among the self-controlled, and shielding together
with the devil by love of pleasure. For these are liars, much-swearers,
perjurers, resisters, counter-strikers, shouters, deniers of having eaten,
slavish, luxurious-living, fault-finders, pryers, lovers of darkness, willing
counter-workers of all the virtuous life.
For in order to cover the passion of gluttony they fall in with the swarm of
evils; who in the form are among those being saved, but in the deeds among
those condemned. This delivered Adam to death, and brought a completion upon
the world through the pleasure of the belly. Noah is laughed at, Ham has been
cursed, Esau falls from the birthright, and marries into the Canaanites, Lot
becomes married to a daughter, son-in-law of himself and father-in-law; the
father a husband, and the grandfather a father, outraging on either side the
bounds of nature. This also made Israel a worshiper of an idol, and =31.641=
prepared the limbs of these to fall in the wilderness. This also made one of
the prophets, sent from God for the rebuke of a lawless king, food of a wild
beast; and he whom Jeroboam the king with all the kingly power was not strong
enough to hurt, having been taken by the deceit of the belly, carries away a
pitiable death. But Daniel the man of desires, having mastered the belly,
became lord of the kingdoms of the Chaldeans, having become an overthrower of
idols, and a destroyer of a dragon, a tutor of lions, a foreteller of the
incarnation of God, and an interpreter of hidden mysteries. The three holy
children, having appeared above the pleasure of the belly, despised kingly
anger, and of that fearful furnace that was burning, which Nebuchadnezzar the
king was strong enough to kindle, they dared without fright. They showed idle
a golden image being made a god; and that which had been constructed in much
time by Satan for insult of the glory of God, taking as spoils, they have
offered to their own Master. They prepared the most hateful king himself, and
all the camp against God, with all the creation, to hymn God. And, that I may
say all in short, if you master the belly, you will dwell in paradise; but if
you do not master it, you have become a consumption of death. Become a
faithful treasury of the virtues, and have as a key the tongue of your
spiritual father. Let this open your mouth toward partaking of bread, and let
this shut it. Do not receive the serpent as a counselor, wanting to plunder
you instead of good counsel. Guard against the sin of secret-eating even to a
taste of the tongue. For if he be strong enough to turn you in a small thing,
he has thrown you in the wrestling, and he holds you with the bonds. Do not
hold out your ears to everyone speaking, and do not answer every chatterer, in
conversations not agreeing with the aim of the ascesis. Become a hearer of
good teachings, and by the study of these keep your heart. Guard your ears
from worldly narratives, lest by a sprinkling of mire you stain your soul.
Do not weary yourself in listening in to the things spoken by others, nor
throw your head into the midst of those conversing; lest you yourself also be
sneered at, and make them slanderers. Do not be a busybody, nor wish to see
all things, lest you hold discharges of passions in your mind. See as needed,
=31.644= hear as needed, speak as needed, answer as needed. Do not rashly sit
above one greater than you. And if you are also invited, do not become a
fellow-sitter, but having looked about here and there, hasten to find a lower
seat, so that God may glorify you by the humiliation. Being asked, answer with
a fitting and humble voice; not being asked, keep quiet. When another is being
asked, tighten your mouth, lest your tongue, being shoved from a rash heart
and running out, strike one of those who keep the ascesis exactly, and set you
under the bonds of the charges. And sitting, you will not give your leg over
the other foot; for this is of inattention, and of a soul being lifted in the
air. Conversing with one lesser than you, or also being asked something by
him, you will not make the answers slothfully, thinking yourself above the
brother to the insult of God. For he who dishonors a poor man provokes him who
made him, says the Proverb. Let the word of encouragement leap forth before
your remaining words, confirming your love of the neighbor. Let it be set also
in the middle, and most full of the conversation, with a bright face, so that
you may give gladness to the one conversing with you.
In every success of your neighbor rejoice, and glorify God; for his successes
are yours, as also yours are his. Decline the first couches and the first
seats in the gatherings; but pursue the last place, so that you may rather
hear: Friend, go up higher. At table let not your left hand be disorderly, nor
lord it over the right. Let it rather be idle; and if not, at least let it
serve the right. In every summons of your prayer let your mouth sound along,
and remain with the rule until the last prayer, considering the leaving a
great loss. For if, being fed for the constitution of your flesh, you are hard
to tear away from the table before the completion according to need, and you
would not easily do this without much necessity; how much more must you remain
with the spiritual food, and strengthen your own soul by prayer? For as much
as heaven differs from the earth, and the things in heaven from the things on
earth, so much does soul differ from body. Soul is an imitation of heaven; for
in it the Lord dwells; but flesh is from earth, in which mortal men and
irrational animals dwell. Therefore measure the needs of the body toward the
hours of the prayers, and become ready not to obey a reasoning that tears you
away from the rule. For it is a custom of the demons at the hours of the
prayers, on the pretext of a supposedly reasonable =31.645= cause, to press us
toward withdrawal, so that they may lead us away with a fair-seeming reason
from the saving prayer. Do not say, making excuses, Woe for my head, woe for
my belly, putting forward unseen witnesses of a pain that does not exist, and
giving way, for the sake of rest, from the vigor of the vigil. But rather have
hidden prayers, which God sees in the hidden, and he will repay you in the
open. Have a cargo of excellent conduct, so that you may find hidden wealth in
a day of necessity. In the daily courses of your service, together with the
toil of the body have also an encouraging word toward love of those being
served, so that your service may become acceptable, seasoned with salt. Do not
let another do the works that fall to you, lest the wage also, being taken
from you, be given to another, and another be glorified in your wealth, while
you are humbled.
Do the works of your service well-formed and careful, as serving Christ. For
cursed, he says, is everyone who does the works of the Lord carelessly. Fear
the misuse from excess and contempt, even if the services in your hands seem
cheap, as God is overseeing. Great is the work of service, and a bringer of
the kingdom of heaven. For it is a net of the virtues, carrying all the
commandments of God in itself; and first of all the all-virtuous humility,
bringing with it the swarm of the goods; then the, I hungered, and you gave me
to eat; I thirsted, and you gave me to drink; a stranger, and sick, and in
prison, and you served me. And especially when what is owed is fulfilled in a
humble mind, without lifting-up, and without anger and murmuring. Become a
zealot of those living rightly, and write their deeds in your heart. Pray to
become of the few. For the good is rare; therefore also few are those who
enter into the kingdom of heaven. Do not reckon that all who are in the cell
are being saved, both the worthless and the good. For it is not so. For many
come to the virtuous life, but few accept its yoke. For the kingdom of heaven
is of the violent, and the violent seize it. The saying is evangelical. He
called violence the wearing-down of the body, which the disciples of Christ
endure willingly in the denial of their own wills and of the rest of the body,
and in the keeping of all the commandments of Christ. If then you wish to
seize the kingdom of God, become violent; yoke your neck to the yoke of the
slavery of Christ. =31.648= Tighten the yoke-collar about your neck; let it
press your neck; thin it by the toil of the virtues, in fastings, in vigils,
in subjection, in quiet, in psalmodies, in prayers, in tears, in handwork, in
endurance of every affliction that comes upon you both from demons and from
men. Let not a high-minded reasoning persuade you with time to give way to the
toils, lest somewhere, having been caught naked of virtues toward the doors of
the departure, you be found outside the gates of the kingdom.
Let not a rank of clergy lift you up, but rather let it humble you. For
progress of soul is progress of humiliation; but falling-short and dishonor
are born from high-mindedness. As much as it happen that you draw near to the
greater of the priestly ranks, so much humble yourself, fearing the example of
the sons of Aaron. Knowledge of godliness is knowledge of humiliation and
meekness. Humiliation is imitation of Christ; but lifting-up and bold license
and shamelessness are an imitation of the devil. Become an imitator of Christ,
and not of antichrist; of God, and not of an adversary of God; of a Master,
and not of a runaway; of a merciful one, and not of an unmerciful; of a lover
of men, and not of a hater of men; of those of the bridechamber, and not of
those of the darkness. Do not hasten to command a brotherhood, lest you set
others' burdens of sins upon your neck. Inspecting the work of each day,
compare it with that before it, and hasten toward the bettering. Advance in
the virtues, so that you may become near the angels. Tarry long in the cell
not days nor months, but circuits of many years, hymning your Master in night
and in day, imitating the cherubim. If you begin thus, and also finish thus,
having traveled the afflicted way in your brief time of the ascesis, by the
grace of God you will enter into paradise in the brightness of the lamp of the
soul, rejoicing together with Christ unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
