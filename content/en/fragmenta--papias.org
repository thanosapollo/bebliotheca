2.1 Of Papias five writings in number are in circulation, which are also
entitled Exposition of the Dominical Oracles. Of these Irenaeus also makes
mention as being the only ones written by him, speaking somehow thus: These
things also etc. (cf. Iren. v, 33, 4).

2.2 And Irenaeus [says] these things. But Papias himself, in the preface of
his discourses, in no way shows himself to have become a hearer and eyewitness
of the holy apostles, but teaches that he received the things of the faith
from those known to them, through the words which he says:

2.3 I will not hesitate to set down for you also, along with the
interpretations, as many things as I once learned well from the elders and
remembered well, confirming the truth concerning them. For I did not take
pleasure, as the many do, in those who say many things, but in those who teach
the truth; nor in those who remember the commandments of others, but in those
who remember the ones given by the Lord to the faith and coming from the truth
itself.

2.4 And if anywhere anyone who had followed the elders should come, I inquired
into the words of the elders: what Andrew or what Peter said, or what Philip
or what Thomas or James or what John or Matthew or any other of the Lord's
disciples, and what Aristion and the elder John, the Lord's disciples, say.
For I did not suppose that the things from books would profit me so much as
the things from a living and remaining voice.

2.5 Here it is also worth noting that he enumerates the name of John twice: of
whom he ranks the former with Peter and James and Matthew and the rest of the
apostles, clearly indicating the evangelist; but the other John, having
distinguished the account, he ranks with others apart from the number of the
apostles, having set Aristion before him, and he clearly names him elder.

2.6 So that through these things also the history is shown to be true of those
who have said that two in Asia used the same name, and that two tombs came to
be in Ephesus and that each is still now called John's. To which it is also
necessary to give heed; for it is likely that the second, unless one should
wish the first, saw the Apocalypse that is circulated under the name of John.

2.7 And Papias, who is now being indicated by us, confesses that he received
the words of the apostles from those who had followed them, but says that he
himself became a hearer of Aristion and of the elder John. At any rate, having
mentioned them often by name, he sets down their traditions in his writings.
And let these things also have been said by us not to no purpose.

2.8 And it is worthwhile to attach to the words of Papias already given other
sayings of his, through which he relates certain marvels and other things, as
having come to him from tradition.

2.9 That Philip the apostle spent time at Hierapolis together with his
daughters has been shown through the things before (iii, 31); but that Papias,
having been at the same time, mentions having received a wondrous narrative
from the daughters of Philip, must now be noted. For he relates that a
resurrection of a dead man took place according to him, and again another
marvel that happened concerning Justus who was surnamed Barsabbas, as having
drunk a deadly poison and having suffered nothing unpleasant through the grace
of the Lord.

=2.10= And that this Justus, after the Savior's ascension, the holy apostles
set with Matthias and prayed, in place of the traitor Judas, for the lot of
the filling-up of their number, the writing of Acts relates somehow thus: And
they set two, Joseph called Barsabbas, who was surnamed Justus, and Matthias;
and having prayed they said.

=2.11= And the same man has also set down other things as having come to him
from unwritten tradition, both certain strange parables of the Savior and
teachings of his and some other things more mythical.

=2.12= Among which he also says that there will be a certain thousand years
after the resurrection from the dead, the kingdom of Christ being established
bodily upon this earth; which things I also suppose that he, having taken the
apostolic narratives in a wrong sense, assumed, not having seen the things
said by them mystically in figures.

=2.13= For he appears to have been very small in mind, as one might say having
judged from his own words; except that he also became the cause of the like
opinion to very many of the churchmen after him, who put forward the antiquity
of the man, as indeed Irenaeus and if any other has appeared holding the like
things.

=2.14= And in his own writing he also hands down other accounts of the words
of the Lord by Aristion, previously indicated, and traditions of the elder
John, to which having referred the lovers of learning, we will now of
necessity add to his words already set forth a tradition which he has set out
concerning Mark who wrote the Gospel, through these things:

=2.15= And this the elder said: Mark, having become the interpreter of Peter,
wrote accurately as many things as he remembered, yet not in order, the things
either said or done by the Lord; for he neither heard the Lord nor followed
him, but later, as I said, Peter, who used to make the teachings according to
the needs, but not as making an orderly arrangement of the dominical oracles,
so that Mark did not err at all, writing some things thus as he remembered;
for he made provision for one thing, not to leave out anything of what he
heard or to falsify anything in them. These things then have been related by
Papias concerning Mark.

=2.16= And concerning Matthew these things have been said: Matthew then
compiled the oracles in the Hebrew dialect, and each interpreted them as he
was able.

=2.17= And the same man has used testimonies from the former epistle of John
and likewise from that of Peter, and he has also set forth another story
concerning a woman accused of many sins before the Lord, which the Gospel
according to the Hebrews contains. And let these things also have been
necessarily observed by us in addition to those already set forth.

3.1 Of Apollinarius: Judas did not die by hanging, but survived, having been
taken down before he was choked. And this the Acts of the Apostles make clear,
that having become prone he burst asunder in the middle, and all his bowels
gushed out. And this Papias, the disciple of John, relates more clearly,
saying thus in the fourth of the Exposition of the Dominical Words:

3.2 And Judas walked about in this world as a great example of impiety, having
swollen in the flesh to such a size that not even where a wagon easily passes
through could he pass through, nay, not even the bulk of his head alone. For
they say that his eyelids of the eyes swelled so much that he himself could
not see the light at all, and that his eyes could not even be seen by a
physician through an optical instrument; so great was the depth they had from
the outer surface. And his private part appeared more unsightly than all
shamefulness and larger, and through it there were carried ichors and worms
flowing together from all the body, to outrage, through the necessary parts
alone.

3.3 And after many torments and punishments, they say, having died in his own
place, from the stench the place has become desolate and uninhabited until
now, but not even until today can anyone pass by that place unless he stop his
nostrils with his hands. So great a discharge went through his flesh and upon
the earth.

4.1 And Papias thus verbatim: And to some of them, namely of the old divine
angels, he also gave to rule the ordering concerning the earth, and he charged
them to rule well. And next he says: And it came about that their order ended
in nothing. Concerning the inspiration of the book (of the Apocalypse of John,
scil.) we think it superfluous to prolong the discourse, the blessed Gregory I
mean the Theologian and Cyril, and besides also the more ancient Papias,
Irenaeus, Methodius and Hippolytus bearing witness to this of its credibility.
Having taken the starting-points from Papias the great, the Hierapolitan, who
was a disciple of the bosom-friend [+ ἐν ξοδδ], and Clement and Pantaenus the
priest of the Alexandrians, and Ammonius the most wise, the ancient
interpreters even before the [first ξοδδ] councils, who understood the whole
Hexaemeron with reference to Christ and the church. The more ancient then of
the ecclesiastical [churches ξοδ] interpreters [exegetical ξοδ], I mean Philo
the philosopher and contemporary of the apostles, and Papias the great, the
disciple of John the evangelist, the Hierapolitan ..... and those around them
contemplated the things concerning paradise spiritually, referring them to the
church of Christ. Those who practiced harmlessness according to God they used
to call children, as Papias also makes clear in the first book of the
Dominical Expositions and Clement of Alexandria in the Pedagogue. These things
he says, hinting I think at Papias who was then bishop of Hierapolis in Asia
and flourished together with the divine evangelist John. For this Papias in
his fourth book of the Expositions of the Dominical <Oracles> said the
enjoyments through foods in the resurrection ... and Irenaeus also of Lyons in
the fifth discourse Against Heresies says the same and brings forward as
witness of the things said by him the aforementioned Papias. ..... nor indeed
Papias the bishop of Hierapolis and martyr, nor Irenaeus the holy bishop of
Lyons (scil. does Stephen accept), in the things in which they say that the
kingdom of the heavens is enjoyment of certain sensible foods. Papias bishop
of Hierapolis, having become a hearer of John the Theologian, and companion of
Polycarp, wrote five books of Dominical Oracles. In which making an
enumeration of apostles, after Peter and John, Philip and Thomas and Matthew,
he recorded as disciples of the Lord Aristion and another John, whom he also
called elder. So that some think that of <this> John are the two small and
catholic epistles which are circulated under the name of John, because the
ancients accept only the first. And some, having gone astray, thought that the
Apocalypse also was of this one. And Papias also errs concerning the
millennium, from which Irenaeus also.

11.2 Papias in the second book says that John the Theologian and James his
brother were killed by Jews. The aforementioned Papias related as having
received from the daughters of Philip, that Barsabbas, who is also Justus,
being tested, having drunk the poison of a viper by the unbelievers in the
name of Christ, was kept unharmed. And he also relates other wonders and
especially that concerning the mother of Manaemus who rose from the dead; and
concerning those who rose from the dead by Christ, that they lived until
Hadrian.

12.1 And after Domitian, Nerva reigned one year, who having recalled John from
the island released him to dwell in Ephesus. He alone then remaining in life
of the twelve disciples and having written the Gospel according to him was
counted worthy of martyrdom.

12.2 For Papias the bishop of Hierapolis, having become an eyewitness of this
one, in the second book of the Dominical Oracles says that he was killed by
Jews; having fulfilled, that is, together with his brother the prediction of
Christ concerning them and their own confession and consent concerning this;
for the Lord having said to them: Are you able to drink the cup which I drink?
and they having nodded eagerly and agreed: My cup, he says, you shall drink,
and with the baptism with which I am baptized you shall be baptized. And
reasonably; for it is impossible for God to lie.

12.3 Thus also the very learned Origen in the interpretation according to
Matthew (t. xvi p. 6) affirms that John has borne witness, indicating that he
learned this from the successors of the apostles. And indeed also the very
learned Eusebius in the Ecclesiastical History (iii, 1) says: Thomas was
allotted Parthia, and John Asia, among whom having also spent time he died in
Ephesus.
