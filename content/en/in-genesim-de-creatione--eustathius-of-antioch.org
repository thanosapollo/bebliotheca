85 For indeed it is possible to distinguish, the Father says, by many
ways the difference and the dissimilarity of these animals and birds;
first through the saying "and the Lord God formed yet from the earth";
for we do not find a formation of God to be except man; again also
through the saying of the "yet", the Scripture shows that certain other
animals and birds are here prophesied besides the former ones; for
neither is it written that the first birds came to be from earth, but
from waters; but these came to be from earth, or rather were formed by
God's own hand and not by command. Likewise also through the saying that
all the birds and all the beasts are now formed by God it does not give
us room to say at all that they came to be formerly, but a part now;
whence neither concerning seed-eating food does God command anything
among these; for those that enter into paradise do not eat grass, but
every tree beautiful to the sight and good for food, and before all from
the tree of life of Christ who gives life; and these animals too it is
possible to say that these beasts are nations gathered in the Church by
God, or that a horse-stable and a swine-yard and a camel-station and a
sheep-fold (...) today paradise exists.
86 Not so, O philosopher, but rather the saying of God: "let them rule
over all the earth," and: "let them have dominion over it and over all
things in it," this is the principal thing of making the man according
to the image of God and of his ruling over all the earth. Since, as God
rules over all the earth and all things in it, so also he appointed man
second ruler of all the earth and of the things in it, this I say is the
coming to be of man according to the image of God and according to
likeness. 87 And God said: "Let us make man according to our image and
according to likeness." But to whom did he speak? Let us inquire, O
philosopher. For in the saying: "And God said: Let us make man according
to our image and according to likeness," he cast in a question for us to
understand, to whom he was saying the "Let us make"; for having said:
"And God said: Let us make man," he added saying: "And God made the man,
according to the image of God he made him, male and female he made
them." The meaning then of the "Let us make" signifies that there is a
co-creating person and introduces one equally addressed. For as God the
Father is the one who said: "Let us make man," so also God is the one to
whom he has said the "Let us make," there being one godhead of both
persons, both of the one who said: "Let us make man according to our
image and according to likeness," and of the one who made the man. For
the utterance "Let us make" presents the all-holy and unvarying
[character] of the godhead of the Father and the Son; for the image of
God is simple and uncompounded, being fire by nature.
