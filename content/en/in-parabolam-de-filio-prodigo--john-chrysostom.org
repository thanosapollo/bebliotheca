On the parable concerning the prodigal.
=59.515=
αʹ. Always, brothers, we ought to proclaim the philanthropy of God (for
through it we live and move and are); but especially at this time we owe it to
do this, on account of the common benefit, and the benefaction of the stars
that are about to rise from the font. For these too will shine through it, and
we through it have been saved and are being saved; which was given to us in
place of an inheritance from God the creator and our Father. Let us speak then
concerning it, the things which the Master Christ said, the philanthropic Son
of the philan =59.516= thropic Father, the only interpreter worthy of the
paternal substance; let us unfold the whole parable concerning the prodigal,
that we may learn from it how one must approach the unapproachable, and ask
pardon for faults.

A certain man, it says, had two sons.

The Savior discourses parabolically, not dogmatically. For this reason he
converses concerning his own Father as concerning a certain man, and speaks
concerning the slaves as concerning children, that he may show God's affection
toward men. A certain man, it says, had two sons. Who is this =59.517= man?
The Father of mercies, and God of all consolation. And whom did he have as
these two sons? The righteous, and the sinners; those who abide in his divine
commandments, and those who transgress the Master's commands. And the younger
of them said to the Father. And who is this younger son? The one who has
acquired an unstable mind, and is tossed by the breezes of youth. Nature
recognized as Father the one who formed him, even if choice did not honor the
one who made him. Father, give me the portion of the substance that falls to
me. Well did he ask the things of God from God; but badly did he consume what
he received. And the father divided the living to them. He gave them, as his
own house, the whole world; he gave them, as creator, the whole creation; he
furnished them bodies and rational souls, that being led by reason reasonably
they might do nothing irrational; he set over them his own law, the natural
and the written, as a divine pedagogue, that being trained by this they might
fulfill the will of the lawgiver.

And after not many days the younger son, having gathered all (he acted as a
younger one), journeyed into a far country. He departed from God, and God
departed from him; for he does not force the one who does not wish to serve
him. For all the virtues are of choice, not of necessity. There he scattered
his substance living prodigally. There he lost the whole wealth of the soul;
there delighting and being delighted he was shipwrecked; there playing and
being played he became poor; there buying soul-destroying pleasures, and
purchasing laughter, he received fathers of tears; and the virtues which he
had, he cast away; and the vices which he did not have, he took on. And when
he had spent all (for the wealth of grace is not wont to remain with those who
live shamefully), there came then a mighty famine upon that country.

For where the grain of temperance is not cultivated, there is a mighty famine;
where the vine of self-control is not planted, there is a mighty famine; where
the cluster of purity is not trodden in the vat, there is a mighty famine;
where the heavenly must does not gush, there is a mighty famine; where there
is abundance of evils, there altogether is barrenness of goods; where there is
plenty of wicked deeds, there altogether is scarcity of virtues; where the oil
of philanthropy does not spring, there is a mighty famine. Then therefore he
also began


to be in want. For nothing remained to him any longer, except only the evils
of incontinence, since he had practiced the evils of disorder. And going he
joined himself to one of the citizens of that country. And the citizens of
that country were the demons, in which he happened to be a sojourner. And that
citizen sent him into his field to feed swine. For thus the demons honor those
who honor them; thus they love those who love them; such gifts they grant to
those who obey them. And he desired to fill his belly from the husks which the
swine were eating. And what is, From the husks? The taste of the husks is
sweet, but nevertheless also hard in itself and rough.

For such also is the nature of sin; it gladdens a little, and punishes
greatly; it delights for a time, and scourges eternally. Coming then to
himself, and reckoning the former blessedness and the second misery, and
casting in mind who he was when he was ranked with God and the father, and who
he had become, subjected to the demons. Coming then to himself, he said: How
many hired servants of my father have bread enough and to spare, but I here
perish with famine? How many catechumens now feast on the holy Scriptures, but
I am constrained by the famine of the divine oracles? O how many goods I have
deprived myself of! O with how many evils I have clothed myself! For why did I
stand apart from that blessed way of life? and why did I come within this
death-bearing life? I have learned from what I suffered not to abandon God; I
I have learned to hold fast the one who always guards those who hold him; I
have learned not to trust the unclean demons, who teach every uncleanness and
corru =59.518= ption. What then does he say? Rising I will go to my father. I
will return well, whence I went out badly; let me go to my own father, and
Maker, and Master, and guardian, and provider; let me overtake the one who of
old waits and anticipates those who turn to him. Rising I will go to my
father, and I will say to him: Father, I have sinned against heaven and
before you, and I am no longer worthy to be called your son; make me as one of
your hired servants. These words suffice me for salvation; the name of my
father suffices me for intercession; for my father, being addressed as father
by me, cannot fail to appear a father in deeds; he cannot fail to move his
bowels, being compassionate; he cannot, having heard, I have sinned, fail to
give pardon to my slips; he cannot, having heard my voice, fail to forget his
just wrath. I know how much repentance can do with him; I know how tears
prevail with him; I know how each sinner, looking up to him and weeping
warmly, as Peter receives forgiveness of sins; I know the goodness of my God;
I know the gentleness of my father. He will have mercy on me repenting, whom
he did not punish when I sinned.

βʹ. And rising he came to his father. He added to the good counsel the good
deed. For we must not only counsel the things that profit, but also show the
good impulses by deeds. And while he was still far from the place, but near in
manner, and continually wringing his hands and striking his own breast, as
being a workshop of wicked thoughts, and fixing his face to the earth, and
demanding of his eyes to give as fellow-ambassadors the drops of tears, and
rehearsing the apology, arriving he cried out with a great voice, saying with
tears: Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you. I have sinned, I
know, Christ Master and God; you alone know my sins; I have sinned, have mercy
as God and Master. I am not worthy to look into heaven, and to entreat you my
good Master, being full of greatest and grievous charges; there is no number
of my sins. Have mercy as being always a good God, Because I am not worthy to
be called your son, make me as one of your hired servants.


Thus entreating from the depth of the heart, the one who sees those who
offend, and overlooks those who sin, and waits for their repentance, saw him;
his father saw him, and was moved with compassion. For he was a father in
kindness, even if he was God by nature. And running he fell upon his neck, and
kissed him. He did not wait for the one who had offended to come near, but he
himself met him eagerly. And he did not loathe his neck, as spotted and
defiled with the stains of prodigality; but having embraced him with his
undefiled hands he kissed insatiably the one long since desired. O the
unspeakable and fearful compassion! O paradoxical philanthropy! O strange
reconciliations! He persuaded God at once in a single moment both to
condescend to the tears, and to run past so great a multitude. Have you
marveled, beholding God coaxing a sinner? O the affection of the paternal
bowels! A sinner wept upon earth, and the only sinless one from heaven bent
himself toward philanthropy.

Who ever saw a sinner being coaxed by God; who ever saw the judge tending a
condemned man; who ever saw a convict being coaxed? But nevertheless God
entreats, as once Israel. My people, he says, what have I done to you, or in
what have I wearied you? And now the same things happen, and have happened,
since the Father of mercies and God of all consolation is thus accustomed to
be conquered by himself. Yet this prodigal son was not content with these
things; but being prodigal even in the goods of repentance, he did not even
think that such philanthropy sufficed him, for the multitude of the sins
committed, toward perfect salvation; but what he had practiced to say to the
father, these things also being pre =59.519= sent he was speaking with the
fitting posture: father, if indeed it is at all permitted me to call you
father; if I do not, calling you father, sin this too along with my other
faults; if I do not insult by the address the uninsultable name; if conscience
does not shut my lips; if the manner of the deed does not bind my tongue; if
life does not hinder my speech; receive, holy father, a filthy petition from a
filthy mouth.
Father by grace, and creator by nature, "I have sinned against heaven and
before you, and I am not worthy to be called your son." I have sinned, I
confess my transgressions, I do not hide what you see, I do not deny what you
know; as liable I lie before you, as a lawbreaker I am condemned; as a judge
have mercy on me. I have sinned against heaven and before you. I fear to lift
my eyes to heaven; for I dread the form of the firmament, as an accuser's
voice; I am wary of gazing at the light of divinity, having the eyes of the
mind filthy. I have sinned against heaven and before you, and I am not worthy
to be called your son. Behold I proclaim myself, I condemn myself, I give
sentence against myself.

I do not need a judge for a sentence, I do not need accusers for proof, I do
not need witnesses for written records; within I have conscience sitting in
judgment, I bear in the soul the inexorable judge, the fearful tribunal; in
conscience I carry about the witnesses, bringing on the accusers in my own
eyes; my theaters accuse me, my horse-races cry out against me, the spectacles
of the beast-fighters shout against me; prodigality triumphs over me, my deeds
set me on a pillar of shame, the present nakedness makes me manifest, the very
rags of shame which I have put on put me to shame, and I am not worthy to be
called your son; Make me as one of your hired servants. Do not send me away
from your court, Master, lest the enemy again finding me wandering lead me off
as a captive; nor draw me near to your fearful and mystical table; for I do
not dare with polluted eyes to look upon the holy of holies. Permit me to
stand with the catechumens, within the doors of the church, that beholding the
mysteries celebrated in it, I may desire little by little


to take these up again; that being flooded with the divine streams, I may wipe
away the shame of the shameful songs, the filth that lies in my ears; that
beholding your pearls being snatched by pious men, I too may desire to acquire
hands worthy of their reception. These things of the prodigal who had
offended, and crying with tears, The father said to his servants. To what
servants? Hear: to the priests and ministers of his commands. Bring forth the
first robe, and clothe him; bring forth the robe woven from above, that which
is prepared from the spiritual fire; bring forth the robe that is woven in the
waters of the font. Bring forth the robe that is constructed from the
spiritual fire, and clothe him. Clothe the one who has stripped himself,
clothe the new Adam, whom the devil stripped; clothe the king of creation;
adorn this one, for whom I adorned the world; beautify the dearest members of
my son; for I cannot bear to look upon him unadorned; I cannot bear to leave
my image naked; I consider the reproach of my child my own reproach, I
consider his good fame my own glory.

γʹ. Give also a ring upon his hand; that he may wear the earnest of the
Spirit, and wearing it may be guarded by the Spirit himself; that bearing my
seal he may be fearful to all the enemies and adversaries; that from afar it
may appear of what father this one is a son. Give also sandals for his feet;
lest the serpent again find his heel bare, and strike him through the sting;
but rather that he himself may trample the dragon's head, that he may crush
the stings of the enemy, and that he may run the way according to God. And
bringing the fatted calf, sacrifice it. What calf does he call fatted? What?
The one which the heifer Mary the virgin be =59.520= got.

Bring the untamed calf, the one that did not receive the yoke of sin, the
virgin, and from a virgin, the one who follows those who follow him, not from
necessity, but willingly; the one not using his power, nor the horns, but
readily bending his own neck to those who wish to slaughter. Sacrifice then
the one who is sacrificed willingly, sacrifice the one who makes the
sacrificers live, sacrifice the one who is sacrificed and is not put to death;
sacrifice the one who is divided, and who sanctifies those who divide him;
sacrifice the one who is eaten by those who know him, and is never consumed;
sacrifice the one who makes those who eat blessed. And eating let us all be
glad, Because this my son was dead, and has lived again; he was lost, and has
been found. And they began to be glad. You know the spiritual gladness, you
who have tasted of it and remember the fearful mysteries, of the ministers of
the divine liturgy, who imitate the wings of the angels with the fine linens
that lie upon the left shoulders, and run about in the church and cry: Let
none of the catechumens, let none of those who do not eat, let none of the
spies, let none of those unable to behold the Calf being eaten, let none of
those unable to behold the heavenly blood poured out for the forgiveness of
sins, let none be unworthy of the living sacrifice, let none be uninitiated,
let none be unable with unclean lips to touch the fearful mysteries; then also
of the angels from heaven acclaiming and saying: Holy is the Father, who
willed that the fatted Calf be sacrificed, who knew not sin, as the prophet
Isaiah says; Who did no sin, nor was guile found in his mouth; holy is the
Son, at once also Calf who is always sacrificed willingly, and always lives;
holy is the Paraclete, the Holy Spirit, who completed the sacrifice. While
these things then were being performed within, the


elder son coming from afar heard music and dancing; and calling one of the
servants, he inquired what this might be: A certain sound was ringing around
my ears.

And he says, David the prophet is singing within: Then they shall offer calves
upon your altar. The same again urges those present toward the feast, and
says: Taste, and see, that the Lord is good. Paul the interpreter of the
divine mysteries cries out and says: Christ our passover was sacrificed for
us. The Church keeps festival rejoicing, and dances. And he says to the
servant: Yes, and while I am not present others divide my mysteries in my
absence in my court. Yes, he says; because your brother has come, and your
father has sacrificed the fatted calf, because he has received him back
healthy. And the righteous one was angry at these things, and was not willing
to enter. The righteous one was angry, and became a slave of envy; he who
trampled the delights of life is mastered by envy. And how does Paul say,
saying: I could wish that I myself were anathema from Christ for my brothers,
my kinsmen according to the flesh? Not that he may show the righteous one
envious, but because of the excess the Savior shaped the word thus; that he
might proclaim the surpassing wealth of the kindness of his Father.

And this he makes clear through what follows. For his father, it says, coming
out was entreating him. O unspeakable wisdom, O God-loving providence; both
the sinner he pitied, and the righteous he coaxed; and the one standing he did
not let fall, and the one fallen he raised; and the poor man he showed rich,
and the rich man he did not allow to become poor by envy. And answering he
said to the father: Behold I have so many years serving you, and I never
passed by your commandment; and you never gave me a kid, that I might be glad
with my friends; but I go about in sheepskins, in goatskins, being in want,
being afflicted, being ill-treated. But when this your son came, who despised
you, and ate up your living with harlots, immediately you sacrificed for him
the fatted calf; and neither by word did you reproach him, nor by look did you
turn your face from him; but immediately =59.521= you received him as a guest,
and with all your robe you adorned him, and with the gold ring you made him
flash, and with the sandals you fenced him, and you opened the church, and you
beautified the table, and you filled the mixing-bowls, and you slaughtered the
fatted calf, and you called the faithful to the banquet, and you made the
angels dance, and you assembled a strange symposium of heaven and earth; and
such gifts you furnished to the one who despised your goodness, and who
insulted the nobility. What shall I say toward the depth and the sea of your
mercies? how shall I marvel at the ocean of your calm? You have mercy, Lord,
on all, because you can do all things, and you overlook the sins of men unto
repentance. And his Father said to him: Child, you are always with me. You
were never separated from my bosom; you did not leap away from my Church; you
always attended to psalms and hymns; you converse with the angels continually;
you standing at the altar cry with boldness, Our Father who is in the heavens,
hallowed be your name. But this one approached me condemned, put to shame,
thrusting his face into the earth, and with a crushed and gloomy voice he
cried out: Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you, and I am not
worthy to be called your son; make me as one of your hired servants. What was
I to do toward these words? was I able not to have mercy on my son who
approached me? You judge, you who are angry. But I am not by nature, being a
lover of men, to accomplish anything inhuman; I cannot fail to have mercy on
him whom I made; I cannot fail to pity him whom I begot from my own bowels.
Child, you are always with me, and all that is mine is yours; the heaven


is yours, the firmament yours, the sun your torch-bearer, the moon your
handmaid, the stars your lamps, the air your nourisher, and all things in the
air yours; the earth and the things in it yours, the sea and the things in it
yours, the world yours, the Church yours, the altar yours, the fatted calf
yours, the sacrifice yours, the angels yours, the apostles yours, the martyrs
yours; the present things yours, the things to come yours, the resurrection
yours, immortality yours, incorruption yours, the kingdom of the heavens
yours, all things that appear and that are thought yours. Did I take away your
things, and bring them to that one? did I strip you, and clothe him? did I not
grant the mercy from my own affairs? am I not likewise father both of you and
of him? And I honor you for the virtue, and I pity him for the fairest turning
back; and I desire you for the life, and him for the change; and I love you
for the way of life, and him for the repentance; and you for long-suffering,
and him =59.522= for the turning back in me. But you ought to be glad and
rejoice, because this your brother was dead, and has lived again; he was lost,
and was found. Who seeing a dead man rising, is not glad? and who finding what
he lost, does not exult? Come you also, my son, be glad together with us, and
leap together with the angels, and embrace together with us your brother, and
sing together with David that spiritual strain, the one fitting for the
present festival: Blessed are they whose iniquities have been forgiven, and
whose sins have been covered; blessed is the man to whom the Lord will not
reckon sin. You have heard the divine parable, and you have known its aim, and
you have seen its power; you have learned how philanthropic a Lord we have,
how forbearing.

Let us then take refuge with him with a pure heart. Come, with a common voice
let us cry to him: Master, Lord lover of men, only-begotten Son of God, we
have sinned against heaven and before you, and we are not worthy to be called
your sons; but we take courage in your mercies; we have as hostages of your
philanthropy the precious cross, which you endured for us; we have as sureties
of your compassion the one who was once a harlot, and the one who was once a
robber. For through these both we and all sinners are urged to run to your
philanthropy. As you showed those renowned and blessed, Lord, have mercy also
on us who fall before you; as you raised the dead when you were crucified,
raise us also who have been put to death by sin through your great
philanthropy, that we may enjoy your resurrection with those who have been
redeemed. Saying these things, let us remain, that our Master Christ may
say also to us: According to your faith be it unto you.

And you who are about to enjoy the gift of baptism, having cast away every
alien thought, and having directed your souls toward the heavenly bridegroom,
enjoy the grace of the Holy Spirit. The Lord is near, be anxious for nothing.
At the doors the redeemer stands, the physician is present to those who
believe, the infirmary has been opened, the medicines lie ready, the font
receives all, the grace has been spread out, the spiritual robe is woven by
the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit. Blessed are those who are counted
worthy to wear the robe. Only you light the lamps of faith, and pour on
abundantly the oil of piety, that at night, when there comes a voice saying,
"Behold the bridegroom comes," you may go out to meet him with bright lamps,
dancing and leaping, and crying, Blessed is he who comes in the name of the
Lord; to him be the glory and the power, now and always, and unto the ages.
Amen.
