Martyrdom of those who were perfected at Lyons 1.3 The servants of Christ who
sojourn in Vienne and Lyons of Gaul to the brethren throughout Asia and
Phrygia who have the same faith and hope of redemption as we: peace and grace
and glory from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Lord. 1.4 Then, having
prefaced other things next after these, they make the beginning of the account
in these words. The greatness of the tribulation here, and the so great wrath
of the nations against the saints, and how many things the blessed martyrs
endured, we are not sufficient to tell accurately, nor indeed is it possible
for it to be comprehended in writing. 1.5 For with all his strength the
adversary fell upon them, already making a prelude of his coming that is about
to be without fear, and he went through all things, accustoming his own and
training them beforehand against the servants of God, so that they were not
only shut out from houses and baths and the marketplace, but it was forbidden
that any of us even appear to them at all in any place whatsoever. 1.6 But the
grace of God was campaigning against him and was delivering the weak, and was
setting in array against him firm pillars able through endurance to draw the
whole onset of the evil one upon themselves; who also went to close quarters
with him, bearing every kind of reproach and punishment; who also, counting
the many things few, were hastening toward Christ, truly showing that the
sufferings of the present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory
that is about to be revealed to us. 1.7 And first they endured nobly the
things brought upon them in heaps by the crowd of the whole people together,
shoutings and blows and draggings and plunderings and throwings of stones and
imprisonments and all things that a savage multitude is accustomed to do as
toward enemies and foes. 1.8 And being led up into the marketplace by the
chiliarch and the authorities standing over the city, and having been examined
before the whole multitude and having confessed, they were shut up into the
prison until the arrival of the governor. 1.9 And afterward, when they had
been brought before the governor, and he was using all cruelty against us,
Vettius Epagathus, one of the brethren, who had contained a fullness of love
toward God and toward the neighbor, whose manner of life also had been so
exact that, although he was young, he was equaled with the testimony of the
elder Zacharias. For he had walked in all the commandments and ordinances of
the Lord blameless, and was unwearied in all service toward the neighbor,
having much zeal for God, and boiling in the spirit. Being then such a one, he
did not bear the judgment thus unreasonably coming about against us, but was
exceedingly indignant and demanded that he himself also be heard, making a
defense for the brethren, that there is nothing godless nor impious among us.
=1.10= But those around the tribunal shouted against him, for he was a man of
note, and the governor did not endure the so just demand put forward by him,
but asked only this, whether he too were a Christian; and when he confessed
with a most splendid voice, he also was taken up into the lot of the martyrs,
having been called an advocate of Christians, but having the Advocate in
himself, the Spirit more than Zacharias, which he showed through the fullness
of love, having been well pleased to lay down even his own soul for the
defense of the brethren; for he was and is a genuine disciple of Christ,
following the Lamb wherever he goes. =1.11= From this point the rest were
distinguished, and there became manifest and ready proto-martyrs, who also
with all eagerness were filling up the confession of the martyrdom; but there
appeared also the unready and untrained and still weak, not able to bear the
tension of a

great contest; of whom also about ten in number miscarried; who also caused us
great grief and immeasurable mourning and cut short the eagerness of the rest
who had not been arrested; who, although they were suffering all the terrible
things, nevertheless were present with the martyrs and did not leave them.
=1.12= Then indeed we all were greatly frightened because of the uncertainty
of the confession, not fearing the punishments being brought upon us but
looking to the end and fearing that someone might fall away. =1.13= Yet those
who were worthy were being arrested each day, filling up their number, so that
there were gathered from the two churches all the zealous and those through
whom the things here were especially constituted; =1.14= and some pagan
household servants of ours were also arrested, since the governor publicly
ordered that all of us be sought out; who also, by an ambush of Satan, fearing
the tortures which they saw the saints suffering, the soldiers urging them on
to this, falsely accused us of Thyestean banquets and Oedipodean unions and
things which it is not lawful for us either to speak or to think, nor even to
believe that any such thing ever came about among men. =1.15= And when these
things had been rumored, all were made savage against us, so that even if some
formerly through kinship were moderate, then they were greatly angry and were
being sawn asunder against us; and what was said by our Lord was fulfilled,
that there will come a time in which everyone who kills you will think to
offer a service to God. =1.16= Then at last the holy martyrs were enduring
punishments beyond all telling, Satan being ambitious that through those also
some of the blasphemous things be spoken; =1.17= and all the wrath of crowd
and governor and soldiers fell beyond measure upon Sanctus the deacon from
Vienne and upon Maturus, a newly baptized man but a noble contestant, and upon
Attalus, a Pergamene by race, who had always been a pillar and foundation of
those here, and upon Blandina, through whom Christ showed that the things that
appear cheap and unsightly and contemptible among men are counted worthy of
great glory with God because of the love toward him that is shown in power and
does not boast in appearance. =1.18= For while all of us were fearing, and her
fleshly mistress herself, who was also herself one contestant among the
martyrs, was in agony lest she not even be able to make the confession with
boldness because of the weakness of the body, Blandina was filled with such
power that those who were torturing her in every way in succession from
morning until evening were exhausted and paralyzed, themselves confessing that
they had been defeated, having nothing any longer that they would do to her,
and wondering that she remained still breathing, her whole body having been
broken around and opened, and testifying that one kind of racking was
sufficient to bring out the soul, not to speak of such and so many. =1.19= But
the blessed one, as a noble athlete, was made young again in the confession,
and her recovery and rest and insensibility to the things happening was the
saying that "I am a Christian and among us nothing vile comes about." =1.20=
And Sanctus himself also, enduring nobly beyond measure and beyond every man
all the outrages from men, the lawless hoping that through the persistence and
the greatness of the tortures they would hear from him something of the things
that ought not to be, opposed them with such resolution that he did not even
declare his own name, nor of nation, nor of city from which he was, nor
whether he were slave or free, but to all the things asked he answered in the
Roman voice: "I am a Christian." This he confessed in succession both instead
of name and instead of city and instead of race and instead of everything, and
the nations heard no other voice from him; =1.21= whence also a great rivalry
of both the governor and the torturers came about against him, so that when
they no longer had anything that they would do to him, last of all they
applied red-hot bronze plates to the most tender members of his body. =1.22=
And these things were being burned, but he himself

remained unbent and unyielding, firm toward the confession, being bedewed and
strengthened by the heavenly spring of the water of life that goes out from
the belly of Christ; =1.23= and the little body was a witness of the things
that had happened, all wound and weal and contracted and having cast off the
human form from without, in which Christ suffering was accomplishing great
glories, bringing the adversary to nothing and showing for a pattern to the
rest that nothing is fearful where the Father's love is, nor painful where
Christ's glory is. =1.24= For when the lawless after some days were again
racking the martyr, and thinking that, the bodies being swollen and inflamed,
if they should apply the same instruments of punishment they would get the
better of him, when he could not even bear the touch from the hands, or that
dying in the tortures he would put fear into the rest, not only did nothing of
the sort happen about him, but also contrary to all human expectation the
little body rose up and was set straight in the later tortures and recovered
the former appearance and the use of the members, so that the second racking
became for him not punishment but healing through the grace of Christ. =1.25=
And a certain Biblis, one of those who had already denied, the devil thinking
he had already swallowed her down, and wishing also to condemn her through
blasphemy, was leading her to punishment, compelling her to speak the godless
things about us, as being already fragile and unmanly. =1.26= But she in the
racking came to her senses and, so to speak, woke from a deep sleep, being
reminded through the temporary punishment of the eternal punishment in
Gehenna, and she spoke against the blasphemers, saying: "How could such people
eat children, for whom it is not even permitted to eat the blood of irrational
animals?" And from this she confessed herself a Christian and was added to the
lot of the martyrs. =1.27= And when the tyrannical instruments of punishment
had been brought to nothing by Christ through the endurance of the blessed,
the devil invented other devices, the confinements in the prison in the dark
and in the most harsh place, and the stretchings of the feet in the wood, they
being stretched to the fifth hole, and the remaining outrages which angry
attendants, and these full of the devil, are accustomed to inflict on those
shut in; so that most were suffocated in the prison, as many at least as the
Lord willed thus to go out, showing his own glory. =1.28= For those who had
been tortured bitterly, so as to seem no longer able to live even if they
should obtain all treatment, remained in the prison, destitute of the care
from men, but being strengthened again by the Lord and being empowered both in
body and soul and urging on and comforting the rest. But the young and those
just arrested, whose bodies had not been maltreated beforehand, did not bear
the weight of the confinement but died inside. =1.29= And the blessed
Pothinus, who had been entrusted with the ministry of the episcopate in Lyons,
being over ninety years of age and very weak in body, barely breathing because
of the bodily weakness lying upon him, but being strengthened again by
eagerness of spirit because of the desire of martyrdom lying within, was
himself also dragged to the tribunal, the body having been loosed both by old
age and by the disease, but the soul being kept in him in order that through
it Christ might triumph. =1.30= Who, having been carried to the tribunal by
the soldiers, the civic authorities and the whole multitude escorting him,
making all kinds of shoutings as though he were the Christ, was rendering the
good testimony. =1.31= And being examined by the governor who the God of
Christians was, he said: "If you are worthy, you will know." From this he was
dragged without sparing and was suffering various blows, those nearby
insulting him in every way with hands and feet, not even respecting his age,
and those far off hurling at him whatever each had in his hands,

all thinking they were greatly erring and acting impiously if anyone should be
left behind from the wantonness against him; for they thought they would thus
avenge their own gods. And barely breathing he was thrown in the prison and
after two days he expired. =1.32= Then indeed a great economy of God was
coming about and immeasurable mercy of Jesus was appearing, something that has
come about rarely in the brotherhood, but not falling short of the art of
Christ. =1.33= For those who at the first arrest had become deniers were
themselves also shut up and were sharing in the terrible things; for not even
at this time was the denial of any benefit to them, but those who were
confessing what they also were were shut up as Christians, no other charge
being brought upon them, while these henceforth were held as murderers and
polluted, being punished twice as much as the rest. =1.34= For those the joy
of the martyrdom and the hope of the things promised and the love toward
Christ and the paternal Spirit were lightening, but these the conscience was
greatly punishing, so that even among all the rest their faces were
conspicuous in the passages. =1.35= For the one group were going forward
cheerful, much glory and grace being mixed in their faces, so that even the
bonds were lying about them as a seemly ornament, as a bride adorned in
gold-fringed things embroidered, smelling at the same time the fragrance of
Christ, so that some thought they had even been anointed with worldly myrrh.
But the others were downcast and humble and unsightly and full of all
unseemliness, and besides being reproached by the nations as ignoble and
unmanly, having the charges of murderers, but having lost the all-honorable
and glorious and life-giving name. And the rest, seeing these things, were
made firm, and those being arrested were confessing without hesitation, not
even having a thought of a diabolical reasoning. =1.36= After saying some
things in between these, they add again. After these things then the
martyrdoms of their departure were being divided into every kind. For having
woven one crown from different colors and all kinds of flowers they offered it
to the Father. The noble athletes, then, having endured a varied contest and
having conquered greatly, had to receive the great crown of incorruption.
=1.37= So Maturus and Sanctus and Blandina and Attalus were being led to the
beasts into the public place and into the common spectacle of the nations'
inhumanity, the day of the beast-fights being given on purpose because of our
people. =1.38= And Maturus and Sanctus again went through every punishment in
the amphitheater, as having suffered nothing at all beforehand, or rather as
having already through more lots forced out the adversary and having the
contest about his crown; they were bearing again the courses of the scourges
customary there and the draggings from the beasts, and all things that the
maddened people, some from one place and some from another, were shouting and
urging, and upon all the iron chair, on which the bodies being fried were
filling them with the smell of roasting. =1.39= But they did not even thus
leave off but were still even more raging, wanting to conquer their endurance.
And not even thus did they hear from Sanctus anything other than the voice of
the confession which from the beginning he had been accustomed to say. =1.40=
These then, through a great contest, their soul remaining a long time, were at
last sacrificed, having themselves become through that day, instead of all the
variety in the gladiatorial shows, a spectacle to the world. =1.41= And
Blandina, having been hung upon a wood, was set forth as food for the beasts
being let in, who also, through being seen hanging in the form of a cross,
through her intense prayer was producing much eagerness in those who were
contesting, they seeing in the contest even with the outward eyes through the
sister the one crucified for them, that he might persuade those who believe in
him that everyone who has suffered for the glory of Christ always has
fellowship with the living God. =1.42= And when none of the beasts had then
touched her,

having been taken down from the wood she was taken up again into the prison,
being kept for another contest, in order that, having conquered through more
exercises, she might make the condemnation inescapable for the crooked
serpent, and the small and weak and contemptible one might urge on the
brethren, having put on the great and unconquerable athlete Christ, having
through many lots forced out the adversary and through contest having been
crowned with the crown of incorruption. =1.43= And Attalus himself also,
having been greatly demanded by the crowd (for he was notable), entered as a
ready contestant because of a good conscience, since he had been genuinely
trained in the Christian order and had always been a witness of truth among
us. =1.44= And having been led around the circle of the amphitheater, a tablet
going before him on which had been written in Latin: "This is Attalus the
Christian"; and the people being greatly swollen against him, the governor,
having learned that he is a Roman, ordered him to be taken up with the rest
also who were in the prison, concerning whom he had written to the Caesar, and
he was waiting for the decision from him. =1.45= And the time in between was
not idle for them nor unfruitful, but through their endurance the immeasurable
mercy of Christ was appearing. For through the living the dead were being made
alive, and martyrs were granting grace to those who were not martyrs, and much
joy was coming about for the virgin mother, receiving living those whom she
had miscarried as dead. =1.46= For through those most of those who had denied
were being taken back into the womb and conceived again and rekindled and were
learning to confess, and already living and having been made taut they were
going forward to the tribunal (God who does not will the death of the sinner,
but is kind toward repentance, making it sweet), in order that they might also
again be questioned by the governor. =1.47= For when the Caesar had written
that they should be beaten to death, but if some should deny, these should be
released, as the festival here (and this is a populous one, from all the
nations coming together to it) was beginning to be constituted, he was leading
them up to the tribunal, making a theater of the blessed and parading them
before the crowds. For which reason he also examined them again. And as many
as seemed to have had Roman citizenship, of these he cut off the heads, and
the rest he was sending to beasts. =1.48= And Christ was being greatly
glorified in those who had formerly denied, then confessing contrary to the
suspicion of the nations. For these also were being examined privately as
though about to be released, and confessing they were being added to the lot
of the martyrs. But those remained outside who had never had even a trace of
faith nor a perception of a bridal garment nor a thought of the fear of God,
but were even through their manner of life blaspheming the way, that is, the
sons of destruction. =1.49= And all the rest were added to the church; of whom
also, while they were being examined, a certain Alexander, a Phrygian by race,
but a physician by profession, having spent many years in the Gauls and known
to almost all through the love toward God and the boldness of the word. For he
was also not without a share of apostolic grace. Standing by the tribunal and
by a nod urging them toward the confession, he was manifest to those standing
around the tribunal as one in birth-pangs. =1.50= And the crowds, being
indignant that those who had formerly denied were again confessing, shouted
against Alexander as though he were doing this. And when the governor had
given attention and examined him who he was, and he had said that he was a
Christian, becoming in anger he condemned him to beasts. And on the following
day he entered with Attalus also. For the governor, gratifying the crowd, had
given Attalus also again to beasts. =1.51= Who also, having gone through all
the instruments invented for punishment in the amphitheater and having endured
a very great contest, were at last themselves also sacrificed, Alexander
neither groaning nor muttering anything at all, but conversing in heart with
God. =1.52= And Attalus, when he was placed upon the iron chair and was being
burned around, when

the smell of roasting from the body was going up, said to the multitude in the
Roman voice: "Behold, this is to eat men, which you are doing. But we neither
eat men nor do any other wicked thing." And being asked what name God has, he
answered: "God does not have a name as a man does." =1.53= And after all these
things, on the last day of the gladiatorial shows, Blandina was being brought
in again with Ponticus also, a boy of about fifteen years, who also were being
led in each day to see the punishment of the rest; and they were being
compelled to swear by their idols, and because they remained steadfast and
were treating them as nothing, the multitude was made savage against them so
as neither to pity the age of the child nor to respect the woman. =1.54= And
they were throwing them against all the terrible things and were leading them
through every punishment in a circle, compelling them in succession to swear
but not being able to do this. For Ponticus, being urged on by the sister so
that even the nations were seeing that she was the one urging and making him
firm, having endured every punishment nobly gave up the spirit. =1.55= And the
blessed Blandina, last of all, like a well-born mother having urged on the
children and having sent them forth as victors to the king, measuring out also
herself all the contests of the children, was hastening toward them rejoicing
and exulting at the departure as having been called to a bridal dinner and not
as having been thrown to beasts. =1.56= And after the scourges, after the
beasts, after the frying-pan, at last having been thrown into a net she was
set before a bull, and having been tossed sufficiently by the animal, no
longer even having perception of the things happening because of the hope and
the holding to the things believed and conversation with Christ, she also was
sacrificed, the nations themselves also confessing that never among them had a
woman suffered such and so many things. =1.57= But not even thus was their
madness and the cruelty toward the saints taking its fill. For wild and
barbarous tribes, having been stirred by a wild beast, were hard to stop, and
their outrage was taking another beginning of its own upon the bodies. =1.58=
For having been defeated did not shame them, because they did not have human
reasoning, but rather it was even kindling their wrath as of a beast, both the
governor and the people showing the like unjust hatred toward us, that the
scripture might be fulfilled: "Let the lawless still do lawlessness, and let
the righteous still be justified." =1.59= For they were also throwing those
who had been suffocated in the prison to dogs, carefully guarding night and
day that none be buried by us; and then setting forth both the remains of the
beasts and of the fire, here torn, there charred, they were likewise guarding
unburied the heads of the rest with their cut-off parts with military care for
many days. =1.60= And some were raging and gnashing their teeth at them,
seeking to take some more abundant vengeance from them; and others were
laughing and mocking, magnifying at the same time their idols and attaching to
those the punishment of these; and the more decent and those seeming to
sympathize in some measure were reproaching much, saying: "Where is their God,
and what did the religion profit them which they chose even before their own
soul?" =1.61= And the things from those had such variety, but the things
concerning us had come to stand in great mourning, because of not being able
to hide the bodies in the earth. For neither did night contribute to us toward
this, nor did silver persuade, nor did supplication shame them, but in every
way they were watching, as about to gain some great thing if they should not
obtain burial. =1.62= Next after these, after other things, they say: The
bodies of the martyrs then, having been in every way made a spectacle and
exposed to the open air for six days, afterward burned and reduced to ashes by
the lawless, were swept down into the river Rhone flowing nearby, in order
that not even a remnant of them might still appear upon the earth. =1.63= And
they were doing these things as being able

to conquer God and to take away from them the regeneration, in order that, as
those were saying, "They may not even have a hope of resurrection, in which
having trusted they introduce some strange and new religion to us and despise
the terrible things, coming ready and with joy to the death. Now let us see if
they will rise, and if their God is able to help them and to deliver them from
our hands." 2.1 Such also were the things that happened to the churches of
Christ under the emperor indicated, from which it is possible by reasonable
reckoning to conjecture also the things that were wrought in the remaining
provinces. It is worthwhile to join to these from the same writing other
phrases, through which the gentleness and humaneness of the martyrs
indicated has also been recorded in these very words. 2.2 Who also became so
far zealots and imitators of Christ, who being in the form of God did not
count it robbery to be equal with God, that being in such glory and having
testified not once nor twice but many times and having been taken up again
from beasts and having the brands and the weals and the wounds lying about
them, they themselves were neither proclaiming themselves martyrs, nor indeed
were they permitting us to address them by this name, but if ever one of us
through a letter or through speech called them martyrs, they were rebuking
sharply. 2.3 For they gladly were yielding the appellation of the martyrdom to
Christ the faithful and true martyr and firstborn of the dead and prince of
the life of God, and they were mentioning the martyrs who had already gone out
and were saying: "Those are already martyrs whom in the confession Christ
counted worthy to be taken up, having sealed their martyrdom through the
departure, but we are moderate and humble confessors." And with tears they
were exhorting the brethren, begging that intense prayers come about toward
their being perfected. 2.4 And the power of the martyrdom they were showing in
deed, having much boldness toward all the nations, and they were making the
nobility manifest through the endurance and fearlessness and unshakenness, but
they were declining the appellation of martyrs toward the brethren, being
filled with fear of God. 2.5 And again after a little they say: They were
humbling themselves under the mighty hand, by which they have now been
sufficiently exalted. Then they were making a defense to all, but they were
accusing no one; they were loosing all, but they were binding no one; and they
were praying for those who were inflicting the terrible things, just as
Stephen the perfect martyr: "Lord, do not set this sin to them." And if he was
begging for those who were stoning, how much more for the brethren? 2.6 And
again they say after other things: For this also became the greatest war for
them against him because of the genuineness of the love, in order that the
beast, having been choked, might vomit up living those whom he formerly
thought he had swallowed down. For they did not take a boast against those who
had fallen, but in the things in which they themselves were abounding, this
they were supplying to the more lacking, having maternal bowels and pouring
out many tears concerning them to the Father, 2.7* they asked for life and he
gave it to them; which they also shared with the neighbors, going away to God
as victors against all, having always loved peace and having always enjoined
peace, they went with peace to God, having left neither toil to the mother nor
faction and war to the brethren but joy and peace and concord and love. 2.8
Let these things also concerning the affection of those blessed ones toward
the brethren who had fallen beside them be usefully added, because of the
inhuman and unmerciful disposition of those who afterward have been
unsparingly brought against the members of Christ.
