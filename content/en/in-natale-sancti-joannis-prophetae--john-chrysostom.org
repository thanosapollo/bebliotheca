On the nativity of Saint John the prophet, forerunner and baptist.

=61.757=

A timely day of feast and of public joy, in which I have recalled the ministry
of Gabriel and the priesthood of Zechariah, and I consider him condemned to
speechlessness by unbelief. For you have heard the evangelist Luke, who has
opened for us today the soul-profiting infirmary. For the evangelist Luke was
a physician also by craft; but then a healer of bodies, now a visitor of
affairs; he exchanged craft for craft, and succeeded circuit with circuit.
What then does Luke, the gifted physician? You have heard him just now saying.
According to the custom of the priesthood, he says, it fell to him to burn
incense, and Zechariah entered into the temple of God, and all the multitude
of the people was praying outside at the hour of the incense. And behold an
angel of the Lord appeared to him standing at the right of the altar of
incense. And Zechariah was troubled when he saw, and fear fell upon him. And
the angel said to him: Fear not, Zechariah, for your petition has been heard;
and behold your wife Elizabeth will bear you a son, and you shall call his
name John. O the paradoxical and mystical wonder! Justly did Zechariah
contradict the angel. He was making one petition, and he was receiving
another; he was praying for the people, and he was being named father of a
child; he was seeking pardon of offenses, and he was receiving promises of a
barren womb. For Zechariah wished the souls of those who had sinned to be
healed, rather than the belly of Elizabeth to be swollen. Justly was Zechariah
troubled, seized with fear; for he was a man subject to change, even if he was
clothed with priestly dignity. And Zechariah said at once in soul only, when
he saw the angel: Strange is the appearing of the man, strange the promise of
the words. Who is this, who with bold foot treads the holy temple? Who is
this, who stands at the right of the altar of incense? Who is this, who
flashes round with forms of an alien-shaped vision? Who is this? I have not
seen a winged man, nor again a bird in human form. Who is this, who goes round
my soul with fear? Who is this, the light-bearer from the mouth, speaking
great things with the lips shut? Who is this, the watcher upon my course of
service? Who is this, the hinderer of my embassy? Outside the multitude of the
people waits for me praying, as a secretary of the heavenly king; and this one
holds me within.

Through me they have given petitions to the king, asking pardon as having
offended. These things I pray to be signed, that I may bring the people their
desire, that I may show the work of my course of service. Cease then, whoever
you are, who frighten me. If you were a God-sent angel, you would have joined
with my need, you would have suffered with those weeping outside, you would
have been afflicted with the afflicted. Depart then. Who are you who frighten
me? I have no concern of a child; why do you signal to me a promise of a
child? What gain to the people, if I shall have a child? And what profit, if
Elizabeth should nurse an infant? What gain, if I should leave an heir of
life? Depart then, you who frighten me, whoever you may be; depart then from
me. We are advanced in years; the season of sowing has passed, the desire of
marriage has been quenched; time has cooled the heat, barrenness has denied
the fruit, nature has rebelled against itself; we have both died as to the
part of intercourse; what youth =61.758= did not bloom, how shall old age
sprout? But these things Zechariah reasonably. And the angel says to him, as
you just now heard: Fear not, Zechariah, nor be troubled with reasonings; I am
an angel of light, and not of darkness; I am Gabriel, one of the rulers of the
heavenly king; I bring a command, I do not order by authority. I was sent to
announce to you, not to make an assault upon you. I have been clothed with a
form, and I do not appear to you with naked essence. For the one who sent me
spares your life. Fear not, Zechariah; I was sent to evangelize you, and not
to astound you. You put forward old age, and the barren gray hair, and the
unsown womb. And who begets by willing? The gift is God's; is it man's
discovery? Did you not hear the Lord saying: I am he who makes man, and forms
spirit in man? You disbelieve, Zechariah? How was Adam formed? How was Eve
created? How was Isaac born? Abraham having obeyed did not miss the mark; and
you being a priest disbelieve? Is every word impossible with God, Zechariah?
Do you contradict because of old age, O elder? Do you define bodily deadness
as more effective than God? If you had not seen Abraham, you would have
doubted well. You put forward natural weakness, and with a divine promise
present you do not believe, but you doubt and contend? You ask small things,
and you receive great; and as one suffering a loss you cry out, Zechariah? You
pray only for the Jewish people; but what I evangelize to you brings in
salvation unto all the nations. And Zechariah having heard these things, and
having turned his face a little away, answering the angel said: What do you
say, O angel? Will my wife bear? What then? Did I pray for this, or for this
do I move the incense of the course of service, that I may show fruit-bearing
to the people?

I ask pardon of sins; do I long for a loosing of barrenness? I wish the
synagogue to be lightened; that Elizabeth be swollen? I beseech wounded souls
to be healed; that dried breasts become milk-flowing? What do you say, O
angel? Will my wife bear? And what is the gain of those praying outside? Will
they not, having heard, stone me, saying that I am not appointed high priest
for them and for the common-profit salvation, but for myself alone? and that I
received not a word, but a begetting? I have no concern toward childbearing;
away, I do not want this. What I prayed, I did not receive; and now you make
known to me other things instead of other things? I have no concern about
making children; a disease of barrenness has come to be. Did time harvest the
desire, and the weakness of nature, not of intercourse? I have no concern now
about childbearing. We cannot walk without a staff, and how are we commanded
to begin childbearing? Old age has denied nature; the thing is outside of
faith. Do we look round at a tomb, and shall we betroth a nurse? I have no
concern about childbearing. I seek the salvation of the people, and the
well-being of the nation, the captivity of the enemies, and the prosperity of
the people, and not whimperings, and swaddling-clothes and infantile
nourishments. I have no concern about this. How shall I be found father of a
genuine child? Old age thrusts away the faith, the thing is henceforth outside
nature, the generative parts have been dissolved; holding a staff as a horse I
mount, and you scrape up for me a trial of monogamy =61.759=, when the whole
body has become a tomb? By what shall I know this, tell me, O greatest Gabriel
(for this also is necessary for those who do not know to know, that Gabriel is
interpreted man of God); say, by what shall I know this? Behold my members
trembling; behold the old age of both, looking also to the earth. If a part
were growing young, and a part had grown old, there was some small hope of the
things said; now the body of both has been rotten. We wait for nothing else,
except the sickle of death, as ripe ears toward harvest. And if you altogether
speak truth, master Gabriel, what you say, give then another sign to come
along, entrust to me as an earnest the wonder-working of the promise. By what
shall I know this? Aaron did not believe, unless the rod bloomed; Moses was
not fully assured, unless his hand was whitened; Gideon did not understand,
unless the dew came upon the fleece.

Hezekiah did not recognize, unless the sun went backward. By what shall I know
this? Give you a sign, and I dance youthfully; give a sign, that Elizabeth may
not laugh rashly, as Sarah. By what shall I know this, archangel messenger?
Whence are the things spoken to me from you clear to me? If I appear a second
Abraham, the properties of Abraham will belong to me. Enter under my chamber,
and I will wash your feet who have made a journey, and I will set before you a
table filled with mystery; Elizabeth will knead three measures of flour,
pre-portraying the Church. Seek a calf, that you may gift a begetting. By what
shall I know this, you who bring good gospels from above upon earth to us? Why
do you foretell to me the name of the child, O angel, and number the virtues
of the one not present? Show me the thing above nature, that I may believe the
thing contrary to nature; if the heaven is re-vaulted, I also am renewed; if
the sea is measured, my wife Elizabeth also is midwifed; if the sun is
overtaken, the dry breasts also will flow milk; if the moon is reined back,
the dry womb also is moistened. By what shall I know this? For I am an old
man, and my wife advanced in her days. And the angel to Zechariah: Do you
demand God as a pledge? Do you define weakness as more sure than the Master's
promise? Do you not fear to examine the one bringing gospels to you? By what
shall I know this, you say, O Zechariah? Are you being sent into Egypt, that
you should fear Pharaoh? By what shall I know this, you say, O Zechariah? Does
God work nothing above nature? You seek a sequence of nature, and you have not
seen a paradoxical making of deity?

Tell me, Zechariah, where have the pillars of the earth been fixed? And where
has the vault of heaven been fitted? And where are the hollows of clouds
found? And where are drops of rain stretched as warp? And where are clods of
snow quarried? And who charioteers the course of the sun? And who bounds the
waning of the moon? Who will number the multitude of stars? And how does a
raging sea reverence sand? And how are hot-flowing rivers mixed together? And
how is the darkness of mist woven out? And how is a man formed in a womb? And
how is the female found smoother than the male? And how is a God-like soul
found all at once in the body? By what shall I know this, you say, O
Zechariah? You seek a sequence of nature, where there is a working of deity?
Why do you disbelieve that the barren woman should bear? What then? If you
should hear a virgin bringing forth paradoxically? And may the unbelievable be
trained beforehand unto faith? For hence now also unbelief upon the barren
woman is punished, that you may not doubt the wonder-working upon the virgin.
You shall be, he says, silent, and not able to speak. O the philanthropic
punishment, working rather a correction! For the angel chastens the very voice
that slipped, he bridles the very tongue that dared. For he was, he says,
making signs to them, and he remained dumb. Well did the evangelist also write
together the, He remained.

For the silence was waiting for the voice being born, Zechariah for John, the
elder for the child, the priest for the prophet. In which things the angel
says: Since then as an unbeliever you seek a sign from God, receive in your
own members the scourge-bearing sign. For you shall be, he says, silent, and
not able to speak, until the day on which these things come to be. For where
the instrument of contradiction, there also the command of the punishment;
where the boldness of rashness, there also the bridle of discipline; where the
insolence kicked, there the sentence of the rebuke. God indeed wished,
Zechariah, that you become a herald of such a wonder; for a commander of the
army of the heavenly =61.760= king is being born, and a pre-cleanser of the
worldly redemption of sins; but since you made the weakness of the body more
sure than the promise of the Master, for this You shall be silent, and not
able to speak until the day on which these things come to be, because you did
not believe my words, which will be fulfilled in their season. You see that
without faith none of the things needed is brought to a head. And as soon as
Zechariah heard these words, he immediately went out from the temple, bearing
the muzzling as wages of unbelief. O the wonder! He entered to free others,
and he himself went out condemned; he who was bearing a liturgical censer was
carrying about a title of exile.

The people was waiting to hear some good thing from him, and that one made
signs: Let no one ask me; I bear a shudder of the Master's indignation. O the
paradoxical things! Zechariah is muzzled, and Elizabeth feasts; a tongue is
opened, and a womb is swollen; the well-speaking tongue becomes barren, and
the fruitless barren woman becomes a mother; the passion of the womb is
migrated to the tongue; the voice is bridled, and the seed is set free.
Zechariah is silent, and John leaps. For as soon as the barren woman saw the
virgin, the morning-star perceived the sun, and John leaped greatly in the
maternal womb, blaming the slowness of nature. I am a herald of the Master, he
says; and how am I bound with bonds similarly to fellow-slaves? Let me
anticipate the time of gestation; I do not wait for the birth, lest I lose the
course, lest the Master anticipate the slave writhing about the pangs, lest
the sequence of nature become a transgression of the order.

I have recognized the one present, and I do not endure to be silent; I have
recognized the one who sent me forth beforehand to go before his face, to
prepare his way before him. I shake the bonds of nature; for I am eager to
proclaim. O the paradoxical things and wonders! What angels in the heavens did
not know, John being carried in the womb recognized, and was saying to his
mother. He escaped the notice of Thrones, and God sojourning did not pass by
the one in the womb; he hid it from Lordships, and to the one in the belly he
revealed the mystery of the economy; and what the archangel came bringing as
gospel to the Theotokos, the
embryo leaping from within dictated. For having sojourned, as you just now
heard, the redeemer of our race came at once being carried in the womb to his
own friend John; and it was possible to see the fashioner greeting the clay,
the king lodging in the tent of the soldier, the Master arrived at the chamber
of the slave. Whom John having seen from womb in womb, attempted to run past
the bounds of nature, saying, I do not know a Lord who bounds nature, I do not
wait a time of gestation; a nine-month birth in the belly is not necessary for
me; I do not need this imprisonment, into which I have now been shut.

And why do I not pass through the bonds that hold me? Let me come out, let me
proclaim a concise knowledge of paradoxical things; I am a signal of divine
presence, I am a trumpet of the incarnation of the Word of God. I will
trumpet, and I will make a paternal tongue well-speaking to speak; I will
trumpet, and I will make alive a maternal womb that has been deadened. See the
strangeness of the mystery; he is not yet born, and he utters by leapings; he
does not yet appear, and he sends threats; he is not yet permitted to cry, and
he is heard through works; he does not yet learn life, and he pre-proclaims
God; he does not yet see the light, and he recognizes the sun; he is not yet
brought forth, and he is eager to run before. For he does not bear to be held
back when the Master has come; he does not endure to wait the bounds of birth,
but he contends to burst the prison of the belly, and he is eager to
pre-announce the Savior coming, crying with the leaps: He who looses the bonds
has arrived, and why am I held bound? He has come who by a word constructed
the universe, and why do I wait the bounds of nature? Let me come out, let me
run before, let me proclaim, let me cry to all present: Behold the Lamb of
God, who takes away the sin of the world. And these indeed the leapings of
John, or rather words. And for Elizabeth, he says, the time was fulfilled for
her to bring forth, and she bore a son. And her kinsfolk heard, and the
neighbors, and they were rejoicing with her. And why has it been said, She
brought forth and she bore? Because the one is a work of nature, and the other
above nature. Inasmuch as a woman, she brought forth; inasmuch as =61.761=
grace of the Holy Spirit stood upon, she bore above nature; for one thing is a
matter of nature, and another a work of grace.

For what was coming to be by John was a wonder; and he had not so much of the
paternal race as of the Word of God. And a witness of this is the archangel
Gabriel himself saying to Zechariah: Behold your wife will bear you a son, and
he will be filled with the Holy Spirit still from his mother's belly, and he
will turn many of the sons of Israel to the Lord their God; and he himself
will go before him in the spirit and power of Elijah. O the birth of the child
of another paradoxical nature! It is paradoxical, and not natural; for the one
begetting begets with pleasure, and the one bringing forth brings forth in
pains. Since then Zechariah disbelieved, and Elizabeth did not doubt,
receiving the late-born child beyond hope, for this reason she is named both
to bring forth and to bear, the one through nature, the other through faith.
For the one begetting, as I said before, begets with pleasure; and the one
bringing forth brings forth in pains. And what pain upon Elizabeth, where
there is presence of the Holy Spirit, and not a circuit of an inexperienced
midwife? when the work of grace, not the weight of nature? and how, hear. The
labor was pitiable in comparison with the gladness. For John did not receive
the grace according to being born, but having received the robe of
sanctification from the maternal womb itself, he became a paradoxical general,
as the angel said: He will be filled with the Holy Spirit from his mother's
belly, and he himself will go before him in the spirit and power of Elijah.

Who will go before whom? John of the Master Christ; for this he is named
Forerunner, as it is possible to hear John himself saying about the Savior: A
man comes after me, who has become before me; after, because of the time;
before, because of the throne. And why in spirit and power: of Elijah the
Tishbite did Gabriel say that John would ride before the king Christ? Hear
understandingly: Elijah is interpreted God. Since then John had God in
himself, for he was filled with the Holy Spirit still from his mother's belly,
and the Spirit is God; for about him the Lord was saying through the prophet
from many of the times: Behold I send my angel before your face, who will
prepare your way before you, and will turn many from error to the truth; for
this reason John goes before the Lord in spirit and power of Elijah; since
John also had many things similar of Elijah. At any rate the infant is brought
forth, and the voice is given back to the father. For Zechariah being asked,
he says, what he would wish the child to be called, He asked for a tablet, and
wrote saying: John is his name; He who did not be =61.762= lieve the words of
the angel was now compelled to write the vision, proclaiming in writing; he
who did not then receive the things said with the hearing, now legislates with
the hand writing the things coming to be.

And having given the appellation to the child, he received back the voice;
Whence, he says, all marveled. For immediately his mouth was opened, and the
bond of his tongue was loosed, and he was speaking blessing God. O the
paradoxical and strange wonder! a name of a child is written, and a deaf mouth
of a father is opened; John is called, and the tongue of the one calling is
set in rhythm. Do you see that it did not miss, and we having spoken the
fitting interpretation unimpededly have not missed? For behold the name of the
righteous one himself opens a mouth of silence, and raises a hard-to-move
tongue. For thus the voice of John is shown crying, that even the name itself
becomes an energy of a voice. I, he says, am a voice of one crying in the
wilderness: Prepare the way of the Lord, make his paths straight. O the
wonder! The Word sojourns, and a voice pre-proclaims; a Master comes, and a
slave is sent forth beforehand; a King arrives, and a soldier is prepared
beforehand. Let us therefore rejoice and be glad, that Elizabeth bore, and
Zechariah spoke; that a barren woman brought forth, and the elder cried, that
a wax-poured tablet was asked for, and a priestly tongue was loosed, and the
voice returned; that a forerunner appeared, and the whole world rejoiced. But
here indeed it was necessary to dissolve the discourse; but Zechariah does not
permit, crying: Blessed is the Lord the God of Israel, for he has visited and
made redemption for his people. O the paradoxical things! What does Zechariah
say? The first voice after the resurrection Zechariah cried, Blessed is the
Lord. What then? Did Zechariah die, and rise? He did not die naturally; but he
underwent things similar to death. For from his own, as a tomb, he had the
silence, and he was bound with swaddling-clothes, the tongue-bonds; he went
through life, not breathing the priesthood; and he underwent a lament, the
gloom of the people. For with a priest idle, all who were being mediated were
falling in the mourning. This first voice Zechariah cried after this
figurative resurrection: Blessed is the Lord the God of Israel. O Zechariah,
if you had not seen the spiritual ear of grain, you would not have recognized
the intelligible farmer; if you had not received the gift, you would not have
blessed the giver. But Zechariah prophetically hymned to God a song worthy of
the gift.

And let us also cry: Blessed is the Lord the God of the Christians, for he has
visited and made redemption for his people; to whom be the glory and the
power, now and always, and unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
