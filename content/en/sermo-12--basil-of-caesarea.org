Of the same, Discourse on Asceticism, how the monk ought to be adorned.
The monk must before all things possess a possessionless life; solitude of
body, and seemliness of habit, a measured voice, and orderly speech; food and
drink without tumult; and to eat with quiet; to be silent before elders, to
listen before those wiser; to have love toward equals, toward inferiors
loving counsel, to withdraw from the =31.649= base and fleshly and
busybodies, to think more, but to speak little; not to be bold in speech, nor
to overflow in conversation, not to be ready concerning laughter, to be
adorned with modesty, to have the gaze below, but the soul above; not to
contradict with contradictions, to be obedient; to labor with the hands, to
remember the last things always, to rejoice in hope, to endure in affliction,
to pray without ceasing, to give thanks in everything, to be humble toward
all, to hate pride, to be sober and keep the heart from evil thoughts, to
store up in heaven through commandments, to examine himself concerning the
daily thoughts and deeds, not to be entangled in the affairs of life and in
superfluous talk; not to meddle with the life of the idle, but to emulate
the life of the holy Fathers; to rejoice with those who succeed in virtue,
and not to envy; to suffer with those who suffer, and to weep with them, and
to mourn these greatly, yet not to condemn; not to reproach the one turning
from sin, never to justify himself; before all things to confess himself a
sinner before God and men, to admonish the disorderly; to comfort the
faint-hearted; to serve the sick, to wash the feet of saints; to take care
of hospitality and brotherly love; to be at peace with those of the
household of the faith; to turn away from a heretical man; to read the books
that are received; not to meet with apocrypha at all; not to dispute
concerning Father and Son and Holy Spirit, but to say and think with
boldness an uncreated and consubstantial Trinity, and to say to those who
ask, that one must be baptized, as we have received; and to believe, as we
have been baptized; and to glorify, as we have believed.
To conduct himself in good works and words, not to swear at all, not to give
silver at interest, not wheat and wine and oil for increase; to abstain from
surfeit, and drunkenness, and worldly cares; not to converse with deceit,
nor at all to speak against anyone; not to slander, nor at all to hear
slander gladly; not to believe quickly against anyone; not to be tyrannized
by anger, not to be held by dejection, not to be angry with the neighbor
without cause, not to hold wrath against anyone, not to render evil for
evil; rather to be reviled than to revile, to be struck than to strike, to
be wronged than to wrong, to be deprived than to deprive. =31.652= And
before all things the monk must be continent from meeting with women, and
from wine-drinking, because wine and women will withdraw the prudent; and
while fulfilling the Lord's commandments as far as possible, not to grow
listless, but to await the reward and the praise from him, and to desire the
enjoyment of eternal life, and always to have the saying of David before his
eyes, and to say: I foresaw the Lord before me always, for he is at my right
hand, that I may not be shaken. And as a son from whole heart, and strength,
and mind, and power to love God; and as a slave, to revere and fear, and
obey him, and with fear and trembling to work out his own salvation; to be
fervent in spirit, to be clothed with the panoply of the Holy Spirit, and to
run not as uncertainly and to box as not beating the air, and to wrestle
down the enemy in the weakness of the flesh and in the poverty of the soul,
and to do all that is commanded, and to call himself unprofitable; to give
thanks to the holy and glorious and fearful God, and to do nothing from
rivalry and vainglory, but for God, and toward his own pleasing; Because God
has scattered the bones of men-pleasers.
Not to boast at all, nor to speak his own praises, nor to hear another
praising him gladly; but to serve all things in secret, and not to do them
for display before men; but to seek only the praise from God, and to
consider his fearful and glorious coming; the departure from here, the good
things laid up for the righteous, likewise the fire prepared for the devil
and his angels. And above all these things to remember the apostolic saying:
That the sufferings of the present time are not worthy to be compared with
the glory about to be revealed to us. And with David to say beforehand: For
those who keep his commandments there is much recompense, great reward, and
crowns of righteousness, eternal tabernacles, endless life, unspeakable joy,
an indissoluble dwelling with the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit,
the true God in the heavens; manifestation face to face; dances with angels,
with Fathers, with patriarchs, with prophets, with apostles, and martyrs,
and confessors, and those who from of old have been well-pleasing to God,
with whom let us strive to be found by the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ,
to whom be the glory and the power unto the ages of ages. Amen.
