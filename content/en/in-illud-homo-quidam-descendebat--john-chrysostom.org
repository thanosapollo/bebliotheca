* On that saying, A certain man went down
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

On the parable of the Gospel according to Luke which says, A certain man went
down, and fell among robbers.
=61.755=
Truly, according to the divine apostle, They that run in a stadium all run,
but one receives the prize. The priest ran today, and the Levite ran, and the
Samaritan alone received the prize. For those who came and ran before him he
leaped past, running without toil: for what the learned in the law did not do,
these things the foreigner accomplished. For they, seeing in another their own
nature in danger, shut the bowels of compassion, and closing their eyes, they
stepped over the one lying there as a dead pillar, as once also the rich man
stepped over Lazarus thrown before his doors; but the unbelieving Samaritan,
more faithful even than the priests, forgetting his own affairs, comforted the
stranger, and forgetting the need that pressed him, hurried to the healing of
the wounded man, and though he did not know the law, he fulfilled it, having
done all the things of the law.
For when Gentiles that have not the law do by nature the things of the law,
these, not having the law, are a law unto themselves: who show the work of the
law written in their hearts, their conscience also bearing witness, and their
reasonings accusing one another between themselves. What then does the
evangelist say? A certain man was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho, and
fell among robbers, who also stripped him, and having laid on blows, went
away, leaving him half-dead. And by a coming-together a certain priest was
going down on that road, and seeing him, passed by on the other side. And
likewise also a Levite, coming to the place, having come and seen, passed by
on the other side. But a certain Samaritan journeying came upon him, and
seeing him, was moved with compassion, and coming near bound up his wounds,
pouring on oil and wine.
And having set him upon his own beast, he brought him to an inn, and took care
of him. And on the morrow, going out, he took out two denarii, gave them to
the innkeeper, and said to him: Take care of him, and whatever you spend in
addition, I, when I come back, will repay you. Let us therefore learn what
Jerusalem is, and what Jericho, and who the robbers among whom he fell, and
what the garment they stripped from him, and what the blows they laid on the
man, and why they went away leaving him half-dead, and who the priest is, and
who the Levite, and who the Samaritan, and what the oil, and what the wine,
and what the bandages, and what the beast, and what the inn, and who the
innkeeper, and what the two denarii, and what the extra outlay which he
promised to repay in addition, and what the return of the Samaritan. Who then
is the man going down from Jerusalem to Jericho? Jerusalem =61.756= is
paradise, Jericho this world; and the man going down from Jerusalem to Jericho
is the first-formed Adam: for having fallen from paradise he went down to
Jericho, that is, into this world. And having come, he fell among robbers:
that is, among the wicked demons, the most murderous; who also, having
stripped him of the garment of immortality, which he wore before the sin,
clothed him with fig-leaves.
Why fig-leaves? Because they are rough and bitter.
And since after the sin roughness of affairs and much bitterness came upon
him, for this reason he put on fig-leaves. And having laid on blows, that is,


the pains of this life. The, In the sweat of your face you shall eat your
bread, and, Thorns and thistles shall the earth bring forth for you, and, In
sorrows you shall bear children: these are the blows of the soul.
And they went away leaving him half-dead. For Adam, having transgressed the
commandment of God, was neither among the living, nor was he wholly dead, in
that he did not wholly deny God. And by a coming-together a certain priest was
going down on that road. And he did not say, By chance, but, By a
coming-together: for by chance is unforeseen and unexpected, when someone
arrives of his own accord; but a coming-together is when someone does
something according to the providence of God. Here then according to the
providence of God the priest was going down on that road, And seeing him he
passed by on the other side. And likewise also a Levite. Who is the priest,
and who the Levite? The priest is the Mosaic law, and the Levite the prophetic
word. These then came according to the providence of God for the healing of
the one wounded by the robbers; but not being able to heal him, they passed by
on the other side.
These then not having had strength to raise up the one lying there, a
Samaritan comes, who is the Master Christ, the guardian of our souls: for
Samaritan is interpreted guardian. For this reason then he said: But a certain
Samaritan journeying came. And he did not say that He came to him, but, He
came upon him: that is, having borne flesh like unto him, subject to bodily
passion like unto him without sin. And seeing him, he was moved with
compassion over him, and coming near bound up his wounds, pouring on oil and
wine. What are the bandages, and what is the oil, and what is the wine? The
bandages are the commandments of Christ, the oil the holy chrism, and wine the
mystery of the faith. Through these three our wounds of soul are healed. Those
who have been initiated know the things said. And having set him upon his own
beast, he brought him to an inn. Christ calls his own beast the body which he
took up for us from the undefiled Virgin. He therefore set upon his own beast
the one wounded by the rob =61.757= bers: since, according to the prophet
Isaiah, He took up our infirmities, and bore our diseases. And he brought him
to an inn, that is, into the holy Church, and took care of him, first himself
through himself. And going out on the morrow, that is, after the holy
resurrection, having taken out two denarii, he has given them to the
innkeeper. Who is the innkeeper, and what are the two denarii? The innkeeper
is Paul the apostle, who was set over the Church from the Gentiles; and he was
called innkeeper because he receives all, and draws all to salvation. Christ,
having taken out two denarii after the resurrection, both the Old and the New
Testament, gave them to the innkeeper, saying to him: Take care of him; to
you, he says, I deliver the people from the Gentiles, wounded by the most
murderous robbers; only take care of him; and whatever you spend in addition,
I, when I come back, will repay you.
What then did Paul spend in ad =61.758= dition to the denarii? The fourteen
epistles, which he sent to the Gentiles. What is the, I, when I come back,
will repay you? At my second coming, when I come to judge the living and the
dead, I, when I come back, will repay you what you spent in addition: as Paul
himself also says: I have fought the good fight, I have finished the course, I
have kept the faith; henceforth there is laid up for me the crown of
righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous judge, will repay me in that day:
and not to me only, but to all who have loved his appearing.
Of such a crown then, brethren, may it be granted that we too all obtain it,
by the grace and love of mankind of our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom to the
Father together with the Holy Spirit be glory and power, now and always, and
unto the ages of ages. Amen.
