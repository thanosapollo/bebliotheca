* Preface

The faithful old man, first way of believing,
Abram, late father of the blessed seed,
whose name an added syllable increased,
called Abram by his parent, Abraham by God,
who dedicated the pledge of old age as a victim,
teaching that when anyone would offer at the altar
what is sweet to the heart, what is dear, what is unique
must gladly be offered to the God believed in,
urged us to fight with the profane nations
and as urger gave his own example,
nor to beget a wedded offspring
pleasing to God, brought forth by mother Virtue,
before the warlike spirit with much slaughter
has conquered the monsters of the serving heart.
Fierce kings had by chance taken captive
Lot dwelling in the criminal cities
of Sodom and Gomorrah, which the sojourner cherished,
powerful in the honor of his uncle's glory.
Abram, roused by ill tidings,
hears that his kinsman, taken by the lot of war,
is serving in the hard chains of barbarians:
he arms three hundred and thrice six houseborn slaves,
that they may go cut down the backs of the departing foe,
whom rich treasure and noble triumph
held impeded by the captured spoils.
Nay, he himself draws the sword and, full of God,
drives the proud kings, heavy with the mass of booty,
put to flight, tramples the wounded,
breaks the chains and frees the plunder:
gold, girls, little children, necklaces,
herds 1 of mares, vessels, clothing, heifers.
Lot himself, freed from the burst bonds,
as a free man raises his neck worn by the rings.
Abram, scatterer of the enemy's triumph,
returns renowned with his brother's recovered child,
lest the force of the worst princes should possess
any lineage of faithful blood.
While the man is still fresh from so great a slaughter
the priest gifts him with heavenly dishes,
priest of God, a king and likewise most mighty,
whose origin from an unutterable source
secret, produces no author of itself, 2
Melchisedech, by what stock, by what ancestors
unknown, known to God alone.
Soon too the threefold trinity of angels
revisits the huts of the aged host,
and Sarah, already withered, fertile in the womb,
a bloodless mother marvels at the gift of youth,
rejoicing in an heir, and repenting of her laugh.
This line has been marked out beforehand as a figure,
which our life should recarve with right foot:
there must be watching in the arms of faithful breasts,
and every portion of our body
which, captured, serves foul lust
must be freed by forces gathered at home;
that we are richly wealthy in house-slaves,
if we shall have known by mystic figure what
three hundred with twice nine added can avail.
Soon Christ himself, who is the true priest,
born of an unutterable and single parent, 1
offering food to the blessed victors
will enter the small cottage of a chaste heart,
showing the honor of the Trinity as guest.
Then the Spirit with pious embraces
will make the soul, his bride, long without offspring,
fertile from everlasting seed,
then the late childbearer, possessing the dowry,
will fill the Father's house with a worthy heir.

* Section 1
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h2
:END:

Christ, you who have always pitied the heavy labors of men,
who are famed by the Father's virtue and by your own, but one
(for we worship one God from either name,
yet not also solitary, because you are God from the Father, Christ),
tell, our king, with what soldiery the armed mind
can drive faults from the cave of our breast,
as often as, the senses within disturbed,
sedition rises and the brawl of diseases wearies the soul,
what then is the defence for guarding liberty,
or what battle-line, when furies are mixed among the heart,
may resist with better hand. For not, good leader,
have you exposed Christians, poor in the great virtues
and lacking sinews, to ravaging vices;
you yourself bid the health-bringing squadrons fight it out
in the besieged body, you yourself arm the talent
with excellent arts, by which, powerful to assail
the mockeries of the heart, it may contend for you and conquer for you.
The present means of conquering is if at close quarters we may
mark the very faces of the Virtues and the monsters
wrestling against them with hostile forces.

Faith first seeks the field, under doubtful lot of duel
about to fight, disordered in rustic dress,
shoulders bare, hair unshorn, arms uncovered;
for the sudden heat of praise, burning for new
battles, remembers neither to gird with weapons nor with covering,
but trusting in a strong breast and refreshed limbs
she challenges the dangers of mad war that must be broken.
Lo, with gathered strength the Worship of the Old Gods dares
first to strike Faith who is challenging her.
She, rising higher, makes the hostile head and temples
decked with fillets totter, and applies to the ground
the face sated with the blood of cattle, and with her foot she tramples
the eyes crushed in death, and the broken passages of the
intercepted throat choke the malignant soul,
and long sighs weary a difficult death.
The victorious legion exults, which Queen Faith,
gathered from a thousand martyrs, had animated against the enemy;
now she crowns her brave companions for the praise obtained
with flowers and bids them be clothed in burning purple.

Next, ready to meet on the grassy field,
the virgin Chastity shines in beautiful arms,
whom Lust of Sodom, girt with her native torches,
attacks, and thrusts a pitchy pine with burning sulphur
into her face, and aims at the shamefast eyes with flames,
and tries to cover them with foul smoke.
But the unterrified virgin strikes with a stone the right hand
of the burning fury and the fiery shafts of the dire she-wolf,
and drives the shaken torches from her holy face.
Then with driven sword she transfixes the throat
of the disarmed harlot; she vomits hot vapors
clotted with filthy blood; from there the dirty spirit
breathing out pollutes the neighboring airs.

'She has it,' the victorious queen cries, 'this will be
your last end; you will always lie prostrate,
nor will you any longer dare to scatter death-bringing flames
among the menservants or maidservants of God, in whom the inmost
vein of a chaste soul burns from the lamp of Christ alone.
You, O vexer of men, to have been able, with recovered
strength of an extinguished head, to grow warm again with breath,
after the cut neck of Holofernes washed the Assyrian
bedchamber soaked with the blood of desire,
and harsh Judith scorned the gemmed couch of the
adulterous leader and checked unchaste rages with the sword,
a woman bringing back from the enemy a famous trophy
with untrembling hand, my avenger, heaven-bold!
But perhaps the matron was not strong enough, still fighting
under the shadow of the law, while she figures our times,
in which true virtue has flowed into earthly bodies,
about to cut off a great head through weak ministers.
Does any right remain for you now, after the childbearing
of the untouched virgin? After the childbearing of the virgin, from which
the ancient origin deserted the nature of the human body
and a steep force sowed new flesh,
and an unwed woman conceived God, Christ,
a man from a mortal mother, but godhead with the Father.
Thence all flesh is already divine that conceives him
and takes the nature of God by a covenant of partnership.
For the Word made flesh did not cease to be
what it had been, the Word, while it glues on the use of flesh,
the majesty indeed not degenerating through the use
of flesh, but drawing the wretched to nobler things.
He remains what he always was, beginning to be
what he was not: we are already not what we were, increased
by being born into the better: he conferred it on me and remained for himself,
nor did God lessen his own things from ours, but while he
gave his own to ours he carried us to heavenly gifts.
These gifts are that you lie conquered, filthy Lust,
nor after Mary can you break through my rights.
You are chief toward the road of death, you the door of destruction;
staining bodies you sink souls into Tartarus.
Hide your head, now cold plague, in the grim abyss;
die, brothel; seek the shades, be shut in Avernus,
and be thrust down into the dark depth of night.
Let the flaming shallows roll you beneath, the black shallows,
and a sulphurous whirlpool spin you through the sounding pools,
nor any longer, greatest of furies, tempt the worshippers of Christ,
that bodies purged may be kept for their own king.'
She had said this, and Chastity, glad at the death
of Lust slain, washed in the waves of Jordan the sword
stained, to which gore had clung with reddening dew
and had spotted the bright iron from the wound.
Therefore the victress, skilled, purifies the victorious blade
with a river washing, abolishing by baptism the stain
of the enemy's throat; nor now content to hide
the atoned sword in the scabbard, lest covered rust
occupy with scabrous filth the washed brightness,
she consecrates it in the catholic temple at the altar of the
divine font, where shining it may flash with eternal light.

Lo, modest Patience was standing with grave face
unmoved through the midst of the lines and the various tumults,
and was watching wounds and vitals open to stiff javelins,
her eyes fixed, and she remained slow.
Wrath, swelling from afar, hot with foaming jaws,
twisting bloodshot eyes with gall poured beneath,
challenges her with weapon and voice as one taking no share in the war,
and, impatient of delay, seeks her with a pike, chides with her mouth,
shaking shaggy crests on her helmeted crown.

'See, for you a spectator free of our warfare,' she says,
'receive the death-bringing iron in an untroubled breast,
nor grieve, because it is shameful for you to have groaned at pain.'
So she speaks, and the hissing pine follows the insults,
waved through the tender airs, and sure it is borne
right under the stomach and dashed in with a straight stroke,
but it rebounds, shaken off by the hard repulsion of the corselet.
For the foreseeing virtue had put on her shoulders a threefold
breastplate of adamant joined, and had committed everywhere
scaly weavings of iron through the twisted sinews.
Then Patience remains quiet, strong against all
clouds of weapons and enduring, not to be penetrated.
Nor was she moved by the javelin of the monster raging beyond custom,
waiting for Wrath to perish by her own forces.
When indeed the barbarous warrior had spent her untamed
arms in raging and had wearied her useless right hand
with a cloud of javelins in vain,
when the windy shafts had fallen with light flight,
and the spears lay broken with empty casts,
the wicked hand turns to the hilt and, striving for a blow
with flashing sword, the right hand is raised high from the ear
and, poised, strikes the middle of the brain,
but the bronze helmet formed of cooked metal
when struck returns a ringing and blunts the edge
that rebounds from the hard, and the rebellious vein also breaks
the dashed steel, while 1 not knowing how to yield) it receives
vain assaults from the striker and, safe, resists.
Wrath, when she saw the fragments of the truncated point
and the sword burst far into minute parts,
the hilt now held by the hand without the weight of iron,
out of her mind throws away the luckless ivory and the faithless
tokens of shameful honor, and far she scorns the sad
monuments, and, wild, is kindled to her own death.
One missile from the many she had scattered in vain
she takes from the dust of the field for perverse uses:
she plants the smooth wood in the ground and with point turned
pierces herself and crosses the lung with a hot wound.
Standing over her Patience says, 'We have conquered,
the vice exulting, by accustomed virtue, without any
hazard of blood and life; our law has that
kind of war, to extinguish furies and all the soldiery
of evils and rabid forces by enduring.
Madness is enemy to itself and by raging
destroys itself, and fiery Wrath dies by her own weapons.'
Having spoken this she cuts unharmed through the mid cohorts,
accompanied by an excellent man; for Job next
had clung to the unconquered mistress amid hard wars,
still severe in brow and panting from much death,
but already smiling at the closed sores with grim face,
and through the number of scars recounting the sweated
thousands of fights, his prizes, the enemy's disgrace.
The goddess bids him at last rest from all
the noise of arms, and multiply with captured wealth
whatever had been lost, and bring back things no longer to perish.
She herself breaks the globes of legions and the meeting
columns, walking untouched through wound-bringing showers.
She is associated as one companion to all the virtues,
and strong Patience mixes her own aid.
No Virtue enters doubtful struggle without that
virtue, for she is widowed whom Patience does not confirm.

By chance Pride, puffed up, was flying through the poured-out
squadrons on an unbridled horse, which she had covered with a lion's
skin and had loaded the strong shoulders with shaggy hair,
that, propped on the beast's mane, she might more boastfully
advance, looking down on the lines with swollen disdain.
She had piled her turreted head high
with hair, so that the added mass might increase the built-up
curls and the steep brow bear a lofty peak.
A linen cloak, gathered from the shoulders, came together
at the top of the fold, tying a smooth knot from the breast;
a border flowing from the neck with thin veil
catches the hostile airs with swelling weaves.
No less the steed is proud with unstable fierceness,
impatient to have its mouth reined with wet jagged bits.
Gnashing it turns its back this way and that, denied
the liberty of flight, and swells under the pressed reins.
Showing herself in this dress the windy virago
overtops between either battle-line and bends about
the decorated horse, and with face and voice she threatens,
looking at the opposing wedge, which with rare soldiery
and poor arms Humble Mind, a queen indeed, but needing
another's help and not trusting enough in her own equipment,
had gathered to the wars. She had joined Hope to herself as colleague,
whose wealth is raised and hung from the ground in a rich kingdom.
Therefore when Pride, ill in mind, sees Humble Mind
equipped with cheap weapons, with no display,
she pours herself into voice with bitter words:
'Does it not shame you, O wretched ones, with plebeian soldiery
to attempt famous leaders and to provoke with steel a race
marked by titles, for whom warlike virtue has produced
ancient riches, and has given them to tread with empire
the glad grassy hills? Now a naked newcomer
strives, if it is lawful, to drive out the ancient kings!
See who want our sceptres to yield as prey
to their right hands! See who contend to plough our fallows
and to lay waste with a stranger's plough the fields taken by the hand r;
and to drive out the hardy farmers by war!
Surely, O ridiculous crowd, in the natal hours
we embrace the whole man and the warm limbs from the mother,
and we scatter the force of our powers through the members of the
fresh nursling, and we all rule in the rude bones.
What place in our seat was then given you,
when the kingdoms were growing with inborn jurisdictions
in equal strength? For born under one light
both house and lords we grew with equal years,
from the time when the new clay, going forth from the enclosed
bound of paradise, fled across into the wide world,
and venerable Adam took the skinned garments,
still naked, had he not followed our commands.
Who is this enemy that now rises from unknown shores,
importunate, idle, unhappy, degenerate, mad,
who claims for himself so late a right, an exile until now?
Doubtless the trifles of empty rumor will be believed,
which bids the wretched hope at some time for a perhaps
future good, so that slow comforts may stroke
soft sloth with sluggish musing on things.
Why should not idle hope stroke those whom in this dust
Bellona does not rouse as recruits with grim bronze,
and virtue grown lukewarm unbinds unwarlike spirits?
Is the cold liver of Chastity useful for war?
Or is the tender work of Piety sweated in arms?
How it shames, O Mars and conscious virtue, to stand
against such a line and to provoke trifles with steel,
and to join right hand with maiden dances,
where Justice is always in need and poor Honesty,
dry Sobriety, Fasting with white face,
Modesty scarce suffused with thin blood, open
Simplicity and a wound patent to every blow without covering,
and Humble Mind, prostrate on the ground and not free
in judging herself, whom trembling betrays as degenerate!
I will see to it that that weak band is trodden underfoot
like stubble; for we do not deign to break through with hard
swords, and to stain iron with cold blood
and to foul men with a fragile triumph.'
Crying such things she urges the swift hoofed one with spurs
and flies reckless with loose reins,
desiring to strew the humble enemy by the push of the horse's boss
and to trample the ruin thrown down,
but she falls headlong into a pit, which by chance crafty
Fraud had secretly dug under the cut ground,
Fraud, one from the detestable plagues of the vices,
cunning worker of deceiving, who, foreknowing the war,
had spoiled the plain with treacherous trenches,
hiding on the enemy's side, so that the ditch might catch
the rushing wedges and swallow the drowned columns;
and lest the cautious line might be able to detect the false well,
she had covered the edges overlaid with twigs,
and had simulated a field with turf placed on top.
But the humble queen, though unaware, remained
in a further place and had not yet come to Fraud's hidden work
or trodden the thefts of the malignant pit.
That rider, while she is borne in headlong course,
falls upon this trick, and suddenly laid open the blind gap.
She is rolled on the prone neck of the falling horse, and under
the pressure of the breast is turned among the broken legs.
But the virtue of calm governance, when she sees the levity
of the monster crushed, lying under death,
directs her step moderately, raises her face also sparingly,
and governs her joys with a courteous look.
Faithful Hope, her companion, succors the delaying one and offers
an avenging sword and breathes in love of praise.
She, seizing the hair, drags forth the bloodstained
enemy and, the left hand calling back, lays the face supine,
then snatches the head of the one begging, cut at the bent neck,
and hangs the neck by the wet hair.
Hope chides the extinguished vice with holy mouth:
'Cease to speak great things; God breaks every proud thing.
Great things fall, the inflated crack, the swollen are pressed down.
Learn to lay down the eyebrow, learn to beware
a pit before your feet, whoever you are that threaten the sublime.
The well-known sentence of our Christ is in force:
the humble climb the high, and the fierce return to the lowest.
We have seen dreadful Goliath in limbs and spirits
fall by a weak hand: a boy's right hand
twisted a pebble at him with the sling's whistle
and pierced the forehead transfixed with a hollow wound.
He, threatening, stiff, boastful, truculent, bitter,
while he swells untamed, while he boils formidable,
while he shows himself, while with shield he frightens the airs,
having found what the sports of a small boy can do,
the turbulent warrior succumbed to tender years.
That boy then, following me in the youth of virtue,
stretched flowering spirits upward into my kingdoms,
because a sure house is kept for me of the almighty
under the Lord's feet, and the victors, the stain of faults
cut away, take hold of me calling to the sublime.'
She spoke, and grazing the air with gilded wings
the virgin snatches herself into heaven. The Virtues marvel at her
going and lift their spirits in vows, willing
to go together, did not earthly wars delay the leaders.
They clash with the vices and keep themselves for their own prizes.

Luxury had come from the western bounds of the world,
long since prodigal of extinct fame,
hair anointed, eyes wandering, voice languid,
lost in delights, whose cause of life is pleasure,
to soften a limp spirit, wantonly to drink in
pleasant enticements and to unbind broken senses.
And then, drooping, she was belching a night-long dinner,
because by chance, lying toward dawn at the dishes, she had heard
the hoarse trumpets, and from there, leaving the tepid
cups, with step slipping through wines and balsams,
drunk, she was going to war over trampled flowers.
Yet not on foot, but borne in a lovely chariot
she was capturing the wounded hearts of wondering men.
O new kind of fighting! No winged reed
struck flees the string, nor does the whistling lance flash
from the twisted strap, nor does the right hand threaten a spear;
but wanton she throws violets and fights with leaves of roses
and pours baskets through the hostile columns.
Then, the Virtues having been coaxed, an enticing breath
inspires tender venom through the loosened bones,
and the ill-sweet odor masters mouths and breasts and arms
and soothes the iron-clad muscles, strength crushed.
They cast down their spirits as if conquered and lay down their darts,
shamefully, alas, stupefied with languishing right hands
while they marvel at the chariot glittering with varied light of gems,
while with fixed gazes they gape at the reins rattling
with gold-leaf and the axle of precious weight from solid gold
and the series of spokes white with silver, which the top
curve of the wheels holds in a circle of pale electrum.
And already the whole line, faithless, was crossing of its own will
with standards turned into love of surrender,
willing to serve Luxury and to suffer the laws
of a flowing mistress and to be held by the loose law of stews.
The strongest virtue Sobriety groaned at so sad
a crime, that her companions were leaving the right wing
and a hand once unconquered was perishing without slaughter.
The lofty standard of the cross, which in the first column
the good leader had borne before, she sets with point fixed,
and she restores the light wing with biting words,
goading spirits now with reproaches, now with mixed prayer:
'What fury agitates insane minds with darkness?
Where do you rush? To whom do you give your necks? What bonds at last,
for shame, is it love to bear on weapon-bearing arms:
lilies shining among yellowish wreaths
and crowns blooming with rust-red flower?
Does it please to hand over palms already accustomed to war
to these knots, to have rigid elbows bound with these nodes,
that a gilded mitre holding in manly hair
may drink nard poured with saffron band,
after the marks written with oil on the forehead, through which
the royal unguent was given and the everlasting chrism,
that a tender gait may sweep the steps with a train
and silk cloaks flow on broken limbs,
after the immortal tunic which with skilled thumb
kind Faith wove, giving an impenetrable covering
to washed breasts, to whom she herself had given to be born again,
then to nocturnal feasts, where a huge tankard
spits out the foaming losses of poured Falernian
onto the table from dripping ladles, where the wet
couches are soaked with much wine and the carved work with old dew?
Has the thirst of the desert then fallen from your spirits, has that
fountain given to the fathers from the rock fallen, which the mystic rod
drew forth leaping from the peak of the split stone?
Did angelic food flow into the first tents for your
ancestors, which now late, happier in age,
the evening people eats from the body of Christ?
Steeped in these feasts, already foul drunkenness
of Luxury, importunate, snatches you to a wet brothel,
and men whom raging Wrath, whom idols had not driven
to yield in war, a drunken dancing-girl has bent!
Stand, I pray, mindful of yourselves, mindful also of Christ.
What your tribe is, what glory, what God and king,
what Lord it befits to remember. You, noble stock of Judah
even to the mother of God, by which God himself
might be man, have come by long blood of chiefs.
Let the most celebrated glory of David stir excellent
minds, exercised by continual cares of wars;
let Samuel also stir, who forbids handling spoil
from a rich enemy and does not suffer a conquered king
uncircumcised to live, lest surviving prey
demand the calm victor into recurring battles.
He thinks it a crime already to spare a captured tyrant,
but for you on the contrary the wish is to be conquered and to succumb.
Let it repent, if any reverence of the highest godhead
moves, to have been willing by unspeakable betrayal
to follow this so sweet evil; if it repents, the error does not harm.
Jonathan repented to have violated sober fasts
with sweet honeycomb, the savor of sceptre and honey
alas ill tasted, while the bland pleasure of kingship
delights the young man and looses the sworn rites.
But because he repented, that lot is not lamentable,
nor does a savage sentence stain the father's axes.
See, I Sobriety, if you prepare to conspire,
open the way to all the virtues, that ill-counseling
Luxury, crowded with much following, may pay
penalties with her legion under Christ as judge.'
Having so spoken she offers the Lord's cross, meeting
the fiery four-horse team, aiming the venerable wood
at the very reins; when the fierce ones feared it
flashing with spread horns and on the top of the brow,
they turn headlong flight, poured in blind fear
through the steep; the charioteer is borne supine with reins
drawn back in vain, and her wet hair
is fouled with dust. Then also the spinning of the wheels
entangles the mistress shaken out; for prone she slips
under the axle and delays the chariot with torn drag.
Sobriety adds a lethal wound to her lying,
hurling a millstone of flint from part of the rock,
since chance offered this blow to the standard-bearer
bearing no darts in hand but the badge of war.
Chance drives the stone so that it might break the breathing
of the mid mouth and mix the lips with the hollow palate.
The teeth loosed inward, the tongue, mangled,
fills the cut throat with bits of blood.
The gullet grows raw with unwonted feasts, and swallowing
the liquefied bones it vomits back the morsels it had drawn in.

'Drink now your own gore after many cups,'
the virgin says, chiding; 'let these at last be sad dishes
for you in place of the excessive sweets of the past age.
The taste of bitter death and the last savor
with horrific draught makes harsh the wanton enticements of life.' 1
At the slaughter of the leader the trifling line flees, scattered
with trembling fear. Jest and Wantonness first
throw away the cymbals; for with such arms they were playing
war, practising wounds with the sounding sistrum.
Fugitive Love turns his back; the shafts smeared with venom
and the bow slipped from the shoulders and the falling quiver
he himself, pale with fear, leaves behind his own footsteps.
Pomp, displayer of empty splendor, is stripped
naked of her vain robe; the torn wreaths
of beauty are dragged, and the gold of neck and crown
is loosed, and dissonant Discord disorders the gems.
Pleasure is not irked to go with worn feet through sharp
thickets, since a greater force compels her to bear
bitter flight; fear of danger
hardens the tender soles toward a tormenting road.
Wherever the fleeing column is borne with trembling courses,
losses lie: hairpin, fillets, bands,
clasp, bridal veil, breastband, diadem, necklace.
From these spoils Sobriety and the whole soldiery
of Sobriety abstain, and she tramples with chaste feet
the condemned scandals, nor with severe brow
and winking eyes does she turn to the joys of prey.

Avarice is borne, girt with a capacious lap,
having seized with hooked hand whatever devouring Luxury
had left precious, gaping with vast mouth
at the beautiful mockeries and gathering fragments of falling gold
among the heaps of sand. Nor does it suffice to have filled
the ample folds; it pleases to cram filthy gain
into purses and to stretch moneybags heavy with thefts,
which she covers with the left hiding and veils with the covering
of the left side; for the swift right hand scrapes
rapines and exercises brazen nails on the spoils.
Care, Hunger, Fear, Anxiety, Perjuries, Pallor,
Corruption, Guile, Falsehoods, Sleeplessness, Filth,
various Furies, are driven as the monster's company.
No less meanwhile, after the manner of rabid wolves,
Crimes leap, raging over the whole field,
born from the black milk of mother Avarice.
If a brother-comrade has seen a brother's helmet
radiate with tawny thunderstones, he does not fear to draw
the sword and to strike the head with a partner's point,
about to snatch gems from a kindred crown.
If a son has by chance seen the corpse of a father
extinguished under the lot of war, he rejoices to have seized
the belts shining with bosses and the bloody spoils.
Civil Discord drives kindred prey,
nor does unsated Love of Having spare its own
pledges, and impious Hunger spoils her own children.
Such deaths throughout the peoples was Avarice, victress
of the world, dealing, strewing a hundred thousand men
with various wounds: this one, the light taken away
and the eyes dug out, she allows to wander blind
as in the darkness of night and to go through many stumbling-blocks,
nor to test with a staff the danger set against him.
Another she captures by the look and deceives him seeing,
showing some badge, which while he seeks,
he is caught off guard by a weapon and, wounded under the very
heart, sighs at the iron driven in, hidden.
Many she forces, thrown headlong, into open fires
and does not suffer them to avoid the hearths in which gold seethes,
which the greedy watcher 1, about to burn likewise, seeks.
She snatches every race of men, occupies all mortal things
with destruction, nor is there any vice of the lands more violent
that wraps the age of the worldly people in such disasters
and damns it to Gehenna.

To the worshippers of Christ the tawny image of ruddy coin

Marked by slaughters and proud with mural ruin

Neither a generous tribe nor Judah the parent of ancestral

Whoever the example of the race pleases, let the form

Why do I delay to deceive by some fraud, unequal in the meetings

She had spoken, and she puts off the grim face and the furious arms

she becomes a virtue in look, in face and in severe dress,

Lying Bellona fits herself to this look,
that she may be thought not a greedy plague but a sparing virtue;

rage, and, drawn over dire fury,

With such images mocking she deceives the too-credulous
hearts of men, and they follow the deathly monster
while they believe it the work of virtue; the impious Erinys captures
those easy by consent and binds them with tenacious manacles.

The leaders astonished and the companies disturbed,

portent, not knowing what it should believe friendly in her

last in grade of the soldiery, but alone about to lay

She had thrown every burden from her shoulders, she was going
stripped of all coverings and had lightened herself of much pack,
once oppressed by riches and heavy talents,
now free by pitying the needy, whom generous she had kindly
cherished, pouring out, well prodigal, the paternal census.
Already enriched, she looked with faith 1 on empty coffers,

Avarice shuddered at the thunderbolt of the unconquered Virtue and,
powerless of mind, stuck with stupefied senses,
sure to die: for what way of fraud would remain, that she herself,
treader of the world, should give way, conquered by worldly

The strongest virtue invades the trembling one with hard
knots of the arms, and with throat crushed she breaks
the bloodless dry gullet; the compressed bindings
of the arms are tied under the chin and with narrowed jaws
they wring out the soul, which, snatched with no wound,
palpitates and, the approach of breath intercepted,
suffers death shut in the prison of the veins.
She, pressing on the struggling one with knees and heels,
pierces and breaks the ribs and the panting flanks.
Soon she tears the spoils from the extinguished body; dirty
lumps of gold, crude matter not yet recooked in the furnace,
purses even eaten by frequent moths, and coins
covered with green rust,
the victress, long saved, scatters and for the needy
disperses and gives to the thin as a captive gift.
Then looking with exulting face at the crown poured round
she cries, eager, in the midst among thousands:
'Loose the girding, you just, and depart from arms!
The cause of so great evil lies slain; with the gluttony
of gaining perishing, it is allowed the holy to rest.
The highest rest is to want nothing beyond what due use
asks, that simple nourishment, and one garment,
may cover the weak and refresh the limbs moderately
and not drag the fulfilled measure of nature outside.
When about to enter a journey, do not take a wallet, nor go
foreseeing of the wearing of a second tunic,
nor let tomorrow's matter trouble you, lest food fail
the belly: daily foods return with the sun.
Do you not see how no bird thinks of tomorrow and believes
without anxiety that it is to be fed, God providing?
The cheap birds trust that food will not fail,
and to sparrows sold for a modest as there is
undoubted faith that the powerful Lord cares
lest they perish. You, care of God, face also of Christ,
do you doubt lest your author ever desert you?
Do not tremble, men; he is giver of life and giver of food.
Seek the light-bringing pasture by heavenly doctrine,
which, multiplying hope, may nourish of an inviolable age,
unmindful of the body: he who founded it is mindful
to supply foods and to foster needy members.'
By these words cares were moved out; Fear and Toil and Force
and Crime and Fraud, denier of agreed faith,

kind Peace drives away war; all terror is ungirt
and unbuckles the flanks, the belts torn away.
A garment descending flows down to the lowest feet,
and private modesty tempers the rapid step.
The curved bronzes of the horn-blowers are silent, the appeasable
sword fills the scabbard, and, the dust of the field settled,
the bright face of liquid day without cloud returns.
You may see the purple light of heaven grow clear.

Christ, to you, and we consecrate due honors

You have willed us to know the lurking dangers of the bodily
