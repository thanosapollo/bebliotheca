75 Through the wine and the bread he proclaims the antitypes of the bodily
members of Christ. 76 He calls Christ Wisdom, describing her perceptible
image, signifying the visible prudence and that her commerce is to be embraced
before all things; and no form of wickedness or cunning arrayed against her
prevails; presenting a clear mark to those who love her, he cries out with
praise: she is well-known to all, and every precious thing is not worthy of
her.
77 For the general, having once taken a city, disposed it as he wished, but
the charioteer of the passions subjects these as long as he is in life; for
the civil enemy is altogether harder than the one from outside, as he who has
sacked has put care away, but the other contends toward an unceasing enemy.
Great therefore is the accomplishment of mastering anger and a great
generalship against the spirit of anger.
78 It has been said not only about a harlot, but also about an evil desire;
for when the mind has rushed toward the bad deed, then the heart, which he
calls a mouth, practices crooked things; for then he will be as in the heart
of the sea, choked by the brine of many reasonings and as a helmsman in a
great surge, being pushed by the waves of evil desire toward the wicked deed,
he is in agony.
