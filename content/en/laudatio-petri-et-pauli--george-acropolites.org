* Encomium on Peter and Paul
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Oration of George Acropolites on the holy and all-praised great apostles Peter
and Paul. Bless, father: 1 The present discourse is not simple, even if it is
offered from a simple disposition; for Peter and Paul are set forth for
praise. The matter was even otherwise among the things most difficult and not
easily attaining the attempt, not only for us who lie on the ground and wallow
about matter and roll continually in the mire of the passions, but also for
those exceedingly high in intellect, for whom the covering from birth has been
cut through, and who see in beings the purest light; "for the pure must be
touched by the pure," I heard a certain ancient sage say. But thus a hymn from
holy lips is fitting also for all saints, and most of all among the others for
the greater, and by as much as they excel the rest in rank and order, by so
much must those who praise them also surpass those in like contests, and the
eulogists and those for whom the encomia have been made must be measured with
equal measures both in the power of speech and in the activity of virtue, so
as not to enter a stadium greater than their strength, not to finish, but to
start a race or leap a trench, and the one remain walking about the middle,
the other, carried down into the pit, become a fall for the spectators, both
laughed at and pitied at once. I myself then hold thus concerning such great
things, whether one must call them men or matters (since these are bound and
fastened to one another), and I think every one of sense who also understands
the grandeur of such things does too. But what would one suffer? From that
side fear draws toward silence, from this desire forces toward speech; from
there the hesitating and delaying because of the greatness of the subject,
from here the saying something through reverence toward the praise of the
disciples of Christ. For if their word has sounded through all the earth and
with the net of teaching has enclosed all within and presented a dear catch to
the first Teacher, how is it not necessary that every word be referred to them
in return, whether it were equaled to the measure or fell short or went far
below? Since also to their Teacher and Master Christ what is according to
power is wholly dear, who even preferred a widow's two mites to collections of
many talents, to whom also the disciples' things are referred, for whom and
through whom they contended. Perhaps too the attempt would be fair-faced for
me, not laying hold of the Greater immediately; but just as he himself through
these widened the gospel to the fullness of the earth, through them again as
intermediates he receives words unto praise. For the praise of the disciples
knows how to pass over to the teacher; for one also greets a king with gifts
and honors by greeting one of those loved by him. And another thing worthy of
the subject also comes upon me, persuading me toward the discourse and urging
me toward the encomia of the heralds of God; for the memory of these will be
for me, as I think, a sanctification of mind and tongue and hearing and every
other sense, and what perhaps their teaching word did not effect, the word of
the encomia might accomplish, conveying the speaker there and somehow joining
him to the teachers; and whom the word from them did not have power to bind
together, the word toward them would altogether join; and thus too there might
come about a mystic rite and one loved by the Greater. 2 But why do we walk
about the foothills? Why do we not attempt to mount the mountain of the
encomia? Peter and Paul have been set before us for praise, the greater not
simply than men, but also than as many as from of old have done good service
to

God, the first of the disciples of Christ, who exchanged their callings with
their deeds, who bear the keys of the kingdom of the heavens, who were lifted
above to the third heaven, who were caught up into paradise, the foundations
of the church of Christ, who have encompassed all the earth with the preaching
of Christ, these Peter and Paul. I shudder, as is likely, and fear as I set
myself to the encomia, but I rejoice and exult being sanctified by their
callings, that of weakness, this of hope, that of daring, this of eagerness.
For the weak was not so much contracted as hope pricked; nor did the things of
daring so much embolden as eagerness persuaded. 3 Peter and Paul are those
praised by us. O divine and holy pair, and worthy of the world-wide field and
fitted to the threshing-floor of Christ, O precious yoke, upon which Christ
has rested. For who would speak of the fatherlands and fathers of Peter and
Paul? Or who would set them beside encomia? For as their Master and Teacher,
having taken on what is according to us from fatherlands and fathers, does not
admit comparison, so also those who followed him and imitated him. For the
fatherland of such men is paradise, of which Peter has been entrusted the keys
and into which he has heard. And their father is God, who set Peter as
foundation of his church, and made Paul a vessel of election, and affirmed
that they would sit with him on thrones in the second regeneration at the
judgment of Israel. Such are the fatherlands attested for Peter and such the
fathers for Paul. For what conspicuous thing could one even say of a
fisherman's or tentmaker's fatherland, if indeed things so cheap and low-lying
and trampled by the well-minded are conspicuous, so as even by the many to
have been thrown away for Christ and reckoned as nothing, to which to be glued
is harm of soul and from which to stand off is a divine command, cutting away
from the spurious and alienating from what is foreign and leading back to the
kindred and the equal in honor, whence from the beginning we fell by the
transgression. For the fatherland even for all men simply is that delightful
paradise, the holy place, the sacred garden, the unslandered luxury, the
dwelling of the angels, the haunt of the saints, the truly divine palace,
which has been built beforehand of the King, and all things have been wrought
for him more munificently. Trees had grown upon this, many-flowered, boasting
fruits varied and of many kinds, images of the forms and thoughts above
nature, with which also the whole paradise is gathered together as
corresponding; and in the midst of these a tree of life or of knowledge, of
which even the food is forbidden for a time. In these man was formed, master
of the divine place and king, and has been honored with free will. But by
satanic malice and womanly persuasion he partook of the forbidden and at that
time became an exile, taking corruption in exchange for incorruption and earth
instead of paradise and exchanging the toilsome way of life for living without
toil, and receiving thorns and thistles in return for those divine plants and
fruits, and simply gaining in exchange the world and the world-ruler instead
of the divine things and God. This is the exile of our race, this the chief
point of our calamity; because of these I the honored one am a stranger and
worthy of laments, and the envied one is pitied, and he who by the divine
breath, which is cause of the divine and intellectual soul for us, was lifted
above the things below, has bent down and stooped under the earth. Whence for
me this thickest and heaviest garment and the loving-kind curse to return to
earth from which I was taken, that I may not be gathered immortal with the
evils and be carried about with sin and for these things be scourged, but that
I may be dissolved into the first element, so that from there I may be
compounded better and be changed into the immortal.

4 Many have laid claim to this fatherland and have contended to return to it
before the appointed time, prophets, leaders of the people, judges, kings,
priests, righteous men, but they have not yet obtained the good; for the venom
of the dragon has made its home in the whole race. And it was possible to
throw off much of the venom, but it has not yet vomited out all the poison.
For these reasons law and prophecy have been introduced and as many things as
belong to the old covenant and the veils and the shadow, pre-cleansing and
preparing for the reception of Christ. For these reasons Israel is baptized
with a cloud and is led by a pillar of fire, that the baptism according to
Christ may be prefigured, which both cleanses with the water and also
abolishes with the fire of the Spirit the filths of soul that are into the
depth. Hence the new covenant, and God becomes man, taking up our lump from a
virgin, and the bodiless is embodied and the immaterial is materialized; and
the end of the mystery is not only to ransom man from the condemnation,
cleansing like by like, nor only to lead him back to paradise whence he first
slipped, but also to give a share of deification, in desire of which he was of
old ostracized from the luxury by the deceiver. Hence the teaching of Christ
and the unfolding of the veils and the breaking of the oyster and the
shining-around of the pearl and the contemplation and manifestation of the
mysteries hidden from of old. Hence a following of disciples, and some go
before and others follow, and they do not boast the grandeur by the
going-before but are preferred and chosen before the others by the measure of
love or by the canon of the teaching power and habit; and some boast autopsy
and narrate the things of Christ, and others, even not having seen, have
beheld and are taught the great mystery more greatly than according to sense.
5 Neither then a going-to-meet toward Christ nor sight has anything more to
take, but unmixed faith and a zeal exceedingly unadulterated, of which those
praised have partaken, since before Peter others came to Christ, and Paul has
not seen him at all. But they have not been hindered by these things from both
being and being named among all first and greater than those who went to meet
and who had seen Christ. For who is hotter unto faith than Peter? And who more
earnest toward the gospel than Paul? The one conquers by the zeal of love
toward Christ, the other surpasses all in words and teachings. What purer mark
of Peter than a friendship toward the Son of God burning and over-hot, through
which he is also loved simply alone, and is also appointed by law, and the
things for the teaching of the whole world are entrusted? And what clearer
token of Paul than an unrestrainable movement toward the preaching and the
becoming all things to all, that he may gain all or the more, through which he
has also partaken of the more unutterable things, so that he would not only
have power to transform the taught by familiar things, but also by the more
divine and strange to transpose them toward the better. 6 These we have just
now set forth for praise and we wish to praise them with words (but we
cannot), not that we may also add something to them unto praise (for it is
desirable if we do not even cast down by the inability of words the chiefs of
the disciples), but that we ourselves by the memory of these may mix words of
salvation into the encomia. We already see then waves borne upon waves as upon
a sea fanned by a violent wind. And even for one it is laborious to encompass
these by a measure; and how would I attain to the two? And how could I
distinguish the proper things of each, which alone is a greatest work and
lying above logical art, since their citizenship is not even according to
nature nor on the earth and the things about earth, but in the heavens and the
things about the heavenly. Paul says these things, the one praised, and Peter
even walks on foot upon waves, and his shadow raises the sick. And who would
equal a word to that man

unto encomium? But O reverend dyad, be gracious to the words, condescend to
the attempt and give me speech toward the working of the discourse, having
received the power from Christ and having drawn to yourself the Spirit of
Christ and having granted it to us toward the completion of the praise. 7
Peter then and Paul are disciples of Christ, and this has been established as
a common characterizing mark of both. For they differ not by small things, not
by great, I say even by the familiarities toward Christ, nor by being first or
by excelling everyone, but by certain partial properties, through which we
have had the grasp of the individuals; for after being or with being, identity
and otherness are found in beings. For neither have beings been split off from
one another nor again have they been contracted into the same by community;
for that is of matter, this of God alone. If then in beings simply identity
has been contemplated in community, much more in those of the same genus or in
those of the same species most and most properly; and if they should also
equal the reason or the manner, the same would be most in them. One must then
contemplate also in those now praised by us first the community present in
them, that which joins them into one and gathers them into conjunction,
whether coming upon them from accidents or from judgment and choice; then let
us also examine the otherness. 8 Both these men then, as I had said, are
disciples of Christ; what would be more blessed than this? And both had
followed the gospel of Christ. To this work nothing would be corresponding,
not wealth, not glory, not good fortune, not height of kingship, not the
haughtiness and pride of thrones, not any other of the things that do not
remain and are corrupted. Someone is discipled to Christ, and who else would
appear more happy than this one? Someone teaches the mysteries of Christ, and
who is more elevated and higher than he? For if pride and the desire of
deification cast down the first man and made this one encamp far from God and
not at all become God but not even approach God at all, to go about with God
and meet with God and hear words of God and enjoy his familiarity is as great
unto dignity and better and more honorable than the restoration of the race to
the ancient. For these do not receive back paradise nor a place of luxury nor
do they touch the tree of life in paradise, but they draw near to God and
become gods by the nearness, as the transparent of bodies that draw near to
fire, or the nature of air mixed with the light of the sun and flashing back.
This is the great mystery of the economy; for these reasons God is flesh, that
he may deify the flesh long ago corrupted by the devices of Satan; for this
God is man upon earth, that he may recast that according to the image and wipe
off the matter and lead this back to the likeness. What mind will be able to
attain such a mystery? What sort of word will declare the grandeur of the
matter? In this angels are silent, in this even archangels have feared, in
this every principality and authority and lordship and power and the cherubim
themselves and the seraphim with the thrones have shuddered, not being able to
see together the greatness of the mystery and the Master's innovation; God a
humble man and going about poor and cheap and homeless and not even, as foxes,
having the good fortune of some small covering, insulted, set at naught,
slapped, slandered, suffering all things for all, having very few attendants
and these not of the conspicuous but even of the much cheaper, nor of the most
glorious but even of the wholly most dishonored. For what more obscure than a
fisherman could one say? And what more dishonored than the one stitching the
nets? He does not then make a show by the circuit, that he may teach men
humility and not to pride themselves in the glorious of the world and to be
lifted up.

9 But let us lead the discourse back to its own sequence. Common to Peter and
Paul is the discipleship of Christ and the teaching about Christ and the
throwing away of all their own things and the reckoning as nothing of what is
pleasant in life and able to stand them off from the profession; and both are
apostles and loud heralds of the mystery according to Christ, both teaching
the world, both loosing men from the bonds of Satan and bringing them in
toward the freedom according to Christ, both drawing all from the salt with
the hook of teaching and leading them back to life, ecumenical teachers both
also and having encompassed all the earth with their teachings, both of one
mind and of one manner and having one aim and common will, the salvation of
men and the return of the image to the ancient dignity; both working wonders,
healing the lame, giving eyes to the blind, raising the dead, casting out
demons, abolishing every disease and every infirmity of the people, having
become all things, the things before the law, the things in the law, the
things after the law. As Adam both also receive the first formation, the
higher and more blessed and truly honored by the hands of God, and they do not
fall out of paradise by disobedience, but they are brought into it and bring
others in with them before the common restoration. They become as Abel and
bring in a well-pleasing sacrifice. As Enos they have hoped in the Lord; for
they have both followed and have hung all their own things upon him. As Enoch
they appear being translated and passing from the earthly to the heavenly,
from the temporal to the immortal, from the things according to the world to
the gospel according to Christ. As Noah they are entrusted with an ark that
lifts all the world above the intelligible surge and saves it from the storm
and the drowning. As Abraham they do not hear "Go out from your land and from
your kindred," but they obey Christ commanding "Come then, and I will make you
fishers of men," and they receive the hooks and nets of the word and bring up
those swimming in the depth of destruction. As Lot they flee the burning of
Sodom and go up to the mountain and do not turn back, lest they appear a
pillar of salt even if they were otherwise called salt. For they were taught
that "no one having put his hand to the plow and turned back is fit for the
kingdom of the heavens." As Isaac they are sacrificed, not by the father, but
by the adversary who envies the paternal teachings; yet after the slaughter
they live, with God the better life, and with men by the teachings and the
wonders. They become by the word also Jacob and hunt the blessing before the
others, and they advance to the increase of the generation of the holy nation,
and they go up into Egypt, that they might dissolve the things in Egypt and
about Egypt, and they return with a multitude to the promised land which the
Lord swore to the fathers. As Isaiah and Jeremiah and the rest they do not
prophesy, but they see the one prophesied and teach all and unfold the things
of the prophets. As Samuel they are given to God even before generation; from
the flocks as David they are taken on and they reign over Israel; for they sit
as judges of this in the coming presence, which has also been established as
better than reigning. And they dance also before the divine ark and they sing
the psalms in more divine and higher melodies. They receive wisdom from the
self-wisdom as Solomon, yet they do not corrupt it in later things but they
also advance and increase by the intellectual stretchings-out. 10 But why do I
not speak the greater and more honorable things? Both guide toward the
Jerusalem above the new people, the new Israel, in a pillar of fire and cloud
in the better contemplation. Both tormenting the intelligible

Egypt as Moses and Aaron the own brothers, [the] brothers these too having
become not by nature but by faith; both put Pharaoh to flight and lead out
from there a peculiar people; both divide the sea of life and save the
faithful; water they both sweeten, and that which is not they cause to spring;
they rain manna, they gush birds, a strange and heavenly food; by the type of
the cross they conquer the intelligible Amalek. They become all things, they
accomplish all things, performing all those ancient wonders in mystic issues.
And they go up also to the mountain, the great lookout of piety, and they come
to be within the gloom, and not even here standing a little apart from one
another either in order or in the power of the Spirit, each desiring each; and
they receive tablets not of stone, but spiritual and raised above breaking;
and they legislate not Jews only but all nations and races. And by a
difference of practices they exchanged one only of the Mosaic things as having
a different end toward that one; for not as Moses have they not arrived at
that toward which they were hastening, but they both saw it and knew it and
beheld it and brought in those who followed, leading them by the hand. And not
only is the intelligible end like for them, but also the sensible and
appearing; for in Rome both also complete the stadium of martyrdom, murdered
by Nero, contending firmly and as it was not otherwise necessary on behalf of
piety. These things are common to the two; for common to them is also the
mystery, common is the command of Christ, common also is the preaching of
Christ and the chief point of the salvation of men; in Peter then I understand
Paul and I contemplate Peter together in Paul. 11 I wish then to separate
these and to examine the things that belong to them properly. But how would
one have power to tear apart those united in the Spirit? For he who has said
Peter also immediately adds Paul, and he who has remembered Paul no longer has
Peter not also understood together. I fear then lest this not be dear to the
two most divine men, I fear lest they be jealous of the attempt and reject my
will. But do you, Peter and Paul, forgive me the discourse; for I could not
otherwise present to the hearers the characters belonging to each. 12 But O
most divine Peter (for from you I will begin to speak, since you were also
first taken on by Christ), having called upon God to appear to me as helper of
the discourse, whom you longed for, whom having called you followed at once,
for whom you also have laid down your soul. Let those hear me of the things
said who know how to stoop into depths of words. God is the summit of beings,
not as joined but as exceedingly lifted above them, not by some measure of
interval but by unmeasuredness of both place and nature, unknown wholly and
incomprehensible, having been generated neither by himself nor by another. For
what has been generated by someone is not God; for the being-making power will
appear better than that. But neither, being something not-being formerly, then
receives being from himself; for from where it came forth to this and by what
sorts of powers, from there it has had existence, if by its own, there is
something formerly subsisting, in which the things of the powers will be
seated, and if by another's, there is something pre-existing that one and
naturally preferred. God then neither receives being from himself nor from
another, but is craftsman of all things, whether intellectual and immaterial
or material and seen. For the crafted things do not co-subsist with the
craftsman according to essence unless perhaps according to cause, nor do they
happen to be without beginning and without time, boasting God as cause and
having been substantiated later and having taken time as measure of motion.
God then is as I said the beginning of all, who first crafted the immaterial
essences and brought forth the intelligible order from not-being; for the
things of the first crafting had to be nearer to the nature of the crafted.
And he makes these second lights, ministering spirits for service

sent forth. And because they had existence from not-beings, it was not for
them to remain unfalling in the things in which they have also been stationed;
for only the uncreated is unchangeable. Hence among these someone is lifted
airborne and has wished to be equaled to the Master in dignity and falls a
pitiable fall and one not receiving return. For the demonic tribe is of a
nature to take on the alteration into the depth and through the whole the form
is trans-elemented and changed; it is not then possible to sober off what it
has taken on. And with him other powers also fall out and slip toward vice,
powers of these having of their own will shut the seeing ones. Thus then the
things of the first world and intelligible. 13 Again then another world has
been contemplated by the craftsman. And this is heaven and earth and the
things in these or even those that have taken existence from these, both the
nature of irrational animals, walking and watery and winged, and the race of
plants of many kinds, with which the whole hollow of the earth is adorned, and
all the other things as many as sense has not been ignorant of. A sensible
then and an intelligible world stood apart from one another both by position
and by nature, neither those having sense of the things from below nor these
being able to think anything of the things beyond. For these reasons God
thinks a more wondrous mixture, and he gathers mind toward sense and forms
man, taking the body from matter, and by a divine inbreathing having crafted
the intellectual soul. And he sets him on earth a double living being, mortal
and immortal, high and low-lying, being with God and divine things and yet not
standing off from the sensibles, contemplator of the things above and examiner
of the things below, middle of the irrational and of the pure rational nature.
And he plants a paradise for him toward the east, an exceptional place and
befitting the first man formed by the hands of the Greater, and a naming of
the limits of the earth holding them together is set for him, and all things
are subjected to him, and natures are known to him by the purity of thinking.
And since a helper had also to come to be for him not of another essence, God
casts a sleep upon him and cuts a rib and completes the woman; and the man is
raised from the sleep and wonders at the like and accepts the yoke-fellow and
greets the female unto like-mindedness. 14 But the envy of the one who fell
from pride casts into them a thought of godhead; for by the things by which he
knows himself caught, by these he has also known to hunt the first man. Why
then would I tragically detail the calamity, lamented by many and often and by
us not a few times? Both also touch the forbidden plant, they are cast out at
once both from paradise and from God and they are unseemly naked, and they are
covered with leaves of a fig-tree which even later as fruitless has been
cursed by the Master. And of what sort the condemnation is you all know even
if I should be silent, the most heavily burdened and heaviest endurance
according to this life and at last to return to earth, from which we were
first taken; for since we have not remained in the things in which we have
also been formed, we have been condemned to the crushing as is likely. And the
aim is both divine and beneficial, leading to a second life more honorable and
better. But it was not yet so easy for the curse of the race to be loosed nor
for nature through a small thing to be altered toward the more useful, having
received into depth the coloring of the curse, nor to be led up more readily,
having stooped into a great depth of sin. It was not then from the nature of
the matter; for how did it have power to bring the healing upon itself? But
neither was it possible for angels, for whom it is desirable, if they
themselves should not also be turned, to have an unceasing work of looking up
to the brightness, from which they take the shining and boast an unmoved
station. It remains then that this be an activity of God, which indeed doing
well has also come to be; for neither an angel nor yet a man but the craftsman
himself has saved me the one who sinned.

15 These things every believer knows, and perhaps the hearers would say
blaming: "For what are these oracles to you? Running one way you have walked
another, and having set yourself to praise Peter and Paul the apostles you
have passed over to theology and the generation of the world; and what is the
reason? And what is the aim?" I myself would present it to you, O dear
hearers; for I am not so superfluous of speech and babbling in the words as to
be carried wholly outside the purpose. But come, consider, how the things said
are exceedingly in tune for me and altogether fitted and fastened together. I
know double powers upon God, those of contemplation I mean and of action. For
God contemplates, as it is for God to contemplate, thinking the natures which
he forms; and God acts, conveying this thought of his into work. Reasonably
then also the one generated according to his image will be imaged by such
properties; and man has been crafted according to his image, and the reason
gathers that it is fitting for this one to be solemnized by such things. And
these things are conversely about the divine and the human; for upon God
contemplation is wholly prior to action, but upon men it has happened that
action is prior to contemplation as truly; for it is of God to come down a
little from his own lookout, but of man to go up toward him through action and
to enjoy that divine blessed illumination. As then the first man has come to
be, a commandment is also given to him, that by this best action he might be
exercised and from there come to the tree of life of knowledge and then next
also be filled of the plant of life. But these things have been corrupted for
the first, and action and contemplation have been dissolved together for him.
A second world then for these reasons and some other way of life, and first in
this another man and new Adam, God, and the renewal of our image. As then we
have worn the corrupted image of the first Adam that has come to subsist from
dust and of the mixture, so also of the second and firstborn who has appeared
among many brothers we have put on the intellectual and indestructible. 16 And
this image comes upon us through action and high contemplation, of which
Christ has set Peter and Paul as types unto teaching (for to such a thought
the discourse has arrived us), of whom Peter will be equaled to action from
the words, and Paul will be likened to contemplation, through whom or from
whom every man will be led up to Christ, having appeared Peter and Paul
equally, or rather having become Peter, and less Paul, or conversely, but
wholly the one who has willed to go there not being bent back, as it would not
otherwise be except through these likenesses to draw near to Christ and to be
discipled to the Word and to have become conformed both to his passion and to
the brightness. To such a contemplation the discourse has led us up and has
set Peter as image of practical science, whether as champion or as teacher.
Peter is a fisherman and practiced in the catch of fish and he weaves nets and
directs a rod and casts a hook and lets down a line and applies the bait and
practices with the technical things. And Paul (for even if I said beforehand
that I would separate these or examine their things privately, yet I have not
willed to tear them wholly through a long way but to join them as is possible,
because not even a painter would have dared such a thing in shadows), Paul
then is experienced in letters and is numbered with Pharisees and a zealot of
the law and a contemplator of his paternal traditions, for these reasons also
laying hold of the gnostic. Christ leads Peter out both of boat and of sea and
with the like of the art he takes this one alive and transposes him toward a
higher netting; but Paul a sight stronger than the eyes conquers, and he
throws off the contemplation and thinking present in him and after the change
is exchanged into the better. Do you see how I have not been able to stand
those united in the

grace of the Spirit farther apart, even if I chose to speak their things in
part? And this wholly of the Spirit and this of the joining and peaceful grace
in Christ, which we too would greet, since Christ rejoices in nothing else so
as in brotherly love. 17 But let the discourse again hold to the things
forward. Peter has been called over from Simon into this which he is, as one
would say into the firm foundation of the church; and action has been
established as the under-base of contemplation. And from Saul, Paul has been
renamed by Christ into that at which all practical power and economy ascending
according to God rests by degree. Peter follows God bearing flesh, and Paul
follows Christ who has come to be above the flesh; Peter is discipled to Jesus
before the passion and the burial and the resurrection, and Paul is gathered
to him after the resurrection and the ascension and the seating at the right
of the Father, having received a more God-formed knowledge and one raised
above material appearances. What is more the mark of Peter? Divine signs and
wonders; and what is the characterizing mark of Paul? Theology and teaching
and the declaring and manifestation of the hidden mysteries. Peter has wished
to make his own things manifest in Jerusalem and only; for the things of
action are well-bounded; but Paul has gone through into all the earth with the
word of the preaching; for the things of contemplation are unencompassable and
by nature untraversable. Peter greeted the circumcision and only and wished to
transform it from the letter to the spirit, from the appearing things to the
hidden mystery; for the practical is straitened in the movements; but Paul
receives also the uncircumcised; for the things of contemplation have been
widened. Peter bears a wife and does not refuse the yoke; but Paul greets
virginity, the tent-fellow of the contemplative. Peter contemplates a sheet
let down from heaven, in which all the things of the animals have been
gathered; but Paul goes up into a breadth of heavens and one measured with a
multitude of divisions, and he has both heard and beheld things inexpressible.
Peter is first in time in Christ; for action is prior for those according to
us; and Paul is second, since through action those who are able go up into the
more perfect knowledge. Peter is asked and confesses the sonship of Christ
revealed to him from the Father; but Paul even not being asked nor seeing the
one asking confesses the godhead of Christ. Peter receives the keys of
theology as reward of the kingdom of the heavens, a certain sensible grace or
one named from sensibles; but Paul is caught up into a third heaven and goes
within paradise still living in the body and hears strange things and
contemplates the unseen, which he has not even been able to declare, as this
one says. Peter after the glory and the divine signs and the wonders denies
Christ a third time; for action is easily turned, even if it come to the end
of the habit; Paul remained unshaken, since he touched the greater spectacles,
and unturned. For how would he have had power, or where, to fall over, having
laid hold of the last of the desired things? Peter through the friendship
beyond the others toward the teacher inherits the teaching and the things of
the flock are entrusted; but Paul, not having loved first but having been
loved, made clear the gospel of Christ into all the earth. And that I may
speak briefly: I find Peter showing most exact images of the practical, and
Paul as clear the appearances of contemplation. 18 Not indeed as if Peter does
not also luxuriate in the high contemplations, or Paul does not spend time in
the actions according to Christ; for where will you set of that one the
theology and the teachings, and of this one the God-pleasing activities and
earnest works, when he is even said to stretch out his hands into the service
of saints and to care for the churches and to do the other things, as many as
are of the outer and appearing and needing a bodily attendance? And otherwise
they have not even been distinguished from one another completely, but what
someone among the more ancient in philosophers has said in other places, that
"all things together"

lie in all, and it has still also been contemplated in bodies, that these have
been composed from four, even if the one in some has taken the excess, and the
other in others as we remember, so also upon these habits it is known, that it
is not possible to find some unmixed action among those who practice or a
contemplation; but we say that that one abounds of which the power appears
more evident, just as indeed it has also been recognized upon the two being
praised, as each of the two having made each of the habits abound. And both
are praiseworthy and lead those who have acquired them toward a divine
portion, since the one serves Christ or the things of Christ and helps, and
the other hears the things said from him and stands up from the things here
and goes toward the things above of the mysteries and despises the body and
becomes wholly of the mind.

19 But one and for both the end through all has been established; see also for
as into Rome both the teachers are cut off. For into one end the things of the
virtues have arrived (attend, the intelligent one, both to the place and to
the time and to the person), in Rome that rules the cities and signifying
strength from the calling; and Peter goes first, and Paul comes late to the
end (and this is mystic and hidden); and by Nero the greatest among rulers or
among the harsh of the worse. Yet they have also something different in the
two ends, having been figured other and otherwise in the slaughter; for the
one had to, I mean Peter, travel toward heaven by the figure, and Paul to
incline toward earth and nod down, that they might mix the qualities and fit
them toward symphony as from opposites. 20 Both then these are images as I had
said of the divine appearances and of virtue and of masterly commandments and
of contemplation and of action. What then are we, O friends and brothers? Has
the discourse set such things before us in vain? Has it presented Peter and
Paul as images upon an empty archetype? Or is there also some aim, through
which the craftsman discourse has polished them and has brought us into the
midst so as even to spend so many words and to cut so great a path? We are
truly vain and the things we have written, if these should not also look
toward benefit and the good repute of the many; for neither God nor nature nor
a man of sense have anything of the things unto vanity to work. Was it then
for the sake of Peter or of Paul that the discourse went through these things
for me? That the glorified might be glorified and the honored be honored with
a glory above every corruptible glory and an honor which no word has yet
attained? Was it through these that the many of the words were poured out and
the gospels were introduced and the old and new were thrown in? Not at all;
not for the sake of the saints, not of their honor or benefit, but that we
might enjoy the hearings and be provoked toward virtue and be led to the like
zeal and be imaged toward the archetypes or approach as nearest as possible,
even if we should not be able to receive all the color-work, yet as far as
attainable we would attain the imitation. 21 Let us then become Peter (and
this is not daring to say, since we are also charged to become as Christ, and
this is a word of our salvation and this a manner of the divine economy; for
for this God impoverishes the flesh, that we may be rich in his godhead), let
us then imitate Peter and the things of Peter, let us start of ourselves
toward the truth, let us imitate the zeal, the faith, the firm, the speed, the
fiery. If we happen to be in a boat or are occupied with nets and are
affectionately disposed toward catches of fish, and we hear Christ calling
"Come, and I will make you fishers of men," let us not delay, let us leave all
things and follow the one calling; it is better to take men alive than to fish
fishes. If a stater is demanded of us and we hear "Cast the net

into the sea," let us not doubt, let us let this down at once, and a fish will
be brought up for us in whose inward parts a stater will be found, and it will
be given for Jesus and for us, and the collectors will pass from us. If we see
Jesus having walked toward us, let us refuse his coming-together, putting
forward the fault; if we see this one again walking in the waters, let us not
liken him to phantoms; he is truly himself the one bound with flesh and blood,
which even after the resurrection he bears and is not ashamed to show; and let
us seek to come toward this one, that we may be exercised in the wonders or
make trial of the power of the one calling. If then he call, let us walk
toward this one; and if we are troubled because of the incomplete and unfixed
of the thought, let us not refuse the salvation; near is the steersman, near
is the savior, and he will stretch out the hand and take on those being
surged; and let us be reproached for the little-faith and be accused of the
hesitation, but we will also flee the danger. If you are sent unto the gospel
with one tunic and without bronze, do not neglect at all; if unto healing of
the sick, hasten; if unto abolishings of demons, speed; great indeed are these
both the preaching and the healings and the power against the demons; but
greatest is that our names have been written in the heavens. If we are ordered
to go up the mountain together with Christ, let us go up together with this
one and with those to whom he has entrusted the mysteries; and if you see a
sudden alteration and an exchange toward the better, do not be alarmed nor be
troubled in the ruling part so as not even to know what you speak; if you are
commanded to say nothing about this until the Lord is glorified, greet the
silence. If you say something not according to mind toward the teacher and you
hear "Get behind me, Satan, because you do not think the things of God but the
things of men," do not be grieved, but reverence the word. If you hear "Satan
has asked, Peter, to sift you as the wheat, but I have prayed concerning you
that your faith may not fail," do not disbelieve the things said; the one
speaking is true, even if you yourself happen to be strong-minded. If he
washes the feet of the disciples, then indeed arrives about you, do not take
it ill toward the humility of the teacher; for you will hear "If I do not wash
you, you have no part with me"; and learn through this words of mystery and
stretching together toward the better. If again he will say: "Peter, before a
cock sound you will deny me thrice," do not doubt the sentence; this one knows
better than you the issue of the things to come. If you are confident in the
zeal, yet this one has known the easy-turning of nature; if you have trusted
in the exceeding of love, yet this one knows beyond all the easy-altering of
the judgment; for a little and you will deny Christ thrice, for whom you have
often chosen to die, for whom you said you would set down your soul ten
thousand times, and you will be forgetful even of so great an eagerness for
you on behalf of the teacher and of so much tension and of the zeal and of the
earnestness, as even to strike Malchus at his capture and to cut off his ear.
But even if you have suffered such things and it has so happened to you the
zealot, do not despair; weep having gone outside bitterly, standing out of the
sensibles and corruptible things, and you will heal the wound and you will
return to the first honor, and again the preaching of the teaching will be
entrusted to you and again the things of the flock of Christ will be put in
your hands, and you will learn, being a man, to condescend to the faults of
men and not by the sharpness of justice to bring a penalty upon the flock. If
Christ is crucified and buried, do not hesitate to run to the tomb; run
together with others, but also come alone to the monument, stoop within, know
wonders, learn marvels; for you will not behold the masterly body, but you
will look down upon the linens alone and the napkin that was upon the head
lying apart into one place, and you will know accurately the mystery of the
resurrection, and you will announce to the others unto consolation. What yet?
And you wish to receive also the keys of the kingdom of the heavens and

to receive so great a reward, for which you both left your own things and took
up the cross upon shoulders and followed Christ. If you are asked by Christ
who he is, do not, having followed human reasonings, you too say something
unworthy of the Master; he is man the appearing, but also God the hidden. Do
not stumble as the Jews at the outer things; he is a stone of stumbling, but
he is also known as a cornerstone. From the Father of lights then be revealed
the mystery and say without hesitation: "You are the Christ, the Son of the
living God." 22 How wondrous this sentence of yours will become. From this you
will put Arius to shame and Sabellius and will show them as idle-talkers in
the dogmas, and you will also overturn Nestorius and would abolish a whole
swarm of the heretics. For by the name of the Son the consubstantial and
equal-powered toward the Father will be gathered, and the fusion of Sabellius
will be spat upon by you; for Father and Son have been divided by the
properties of the hypostases; the blasphemous of Nestorius will also be cast
down; for Christ is one, the one from the virgin and Theotokos, perfect God
and perfect man, one the hypostasis, but double the natures. If you should
thus confess, through a small word the whole of the preaching will be
accomplished for you. And the return of the confession how high and wondrous
and lying above every hope and thoughts. You will be renamed in the calling
and you will no longer hear Simon but Peter, blessed, and upon your word as
upon a firm rock Christ will build the church, of which the gates of Hades do
not prevail, and you may receive the keys of the kingdom of the heavens, than
which what would be more happy? And whatever you loose upon the earth will be
loosed in heaven, and bound again in heaven, what you should will to bind upon
the earth. 23 What of these exchanges would become more blessed and more
honorable? For if from a mortal king honoring the man for a time one should
take on a small patronage, it brings pleasure and the soul is gladdened at it;
to receive from the greatest and immortal king who holds the authority of all
earth and all heaven and of what is about these so great and such grandeur,
how great an excess of pleasure it bears! This is unutterable and
uninterpreted and unapproachable to human reasonings. Let us be eager then to
become Peters by the workings of the virtues, that we may also obtain the
equal prizes. But if we have been of a nature rather to be imaged to Paul, let
us become Pauls without envy. First let us throw off the zeal which we have
not according to knowledge; let us renounce the attachment of the legal
ordinances, even if not willingly, yet unwillingly; let us be struck in the
eyes; let us throw off this sensible light, that we may look down upon the
intelligible; let us be renamed from Sauls into Pauls; if we go away to
Ananias, let us receive from this one the illumination and let us gain the
shining according to both; let us see together a light of sun appearing and of
the sun of righteousness the unapproachable light; let us restamp that legal
zeal into gospel; let us be eager to teach into all the limits of earth; let
us go through the bounds of Syria, of Phrygia, of Cappadocia, let us be
stoned, let us be whipped, let us receive forty except one, let us be shut in;
if we are forced let us even bear witness of ourselves as Romans; it is good
to gain the salvation; if the gospel would be benefited, let us run through as
far as Illyricum making the preaching clear; if Peter goes first into Rome and
should go first to the end of the martyrdom, let us walk behind this one; and
through whom we went up into Jerusalem that we might inquire him, and again
after this one let us arrive at the world-wide theater, that we may contend
before both angels and men; let us bear the thorn of the flesh; if we are
distressed, let us entreat thrice on behalf of this; if we hear "My grace is
sufficient for you, for my power in

weakness is perfected," let us be eager to endure the passion in silence. Let
us throw off to the fire the viper that has grown to the hand. Let our hands
minister toward the works; for such a thing has also something of spiritual
benefit. Let the care of the churches consume us; for this is good and
pleasing to Christ. If it should be needed, let us flee through a basket, let
us make a night-and-day in the deep; let us become a theater both to angels
and to men; we shall appear as it were offscourings of the world; for these
things are good since they lead up readily to God. Let us suffer all the
things of Paul. Let us become to the Jews as Jews, to those under law as under
law, to the lawless as lawless, being lawful to Christ. Let us become all
things to all, that we may gain all or the more; let us dare even against
ourselves to speak on behalf of our brothers, and let us will to become
anathema through these. If we are so citizenshipped, the world will be
crucified to us and we will be crucified to the world, and we will live no
longer, but Christ will live in us. Has anyone to say something more dear of
such a life? And let us boast in other things least; but if we should choose
to boast, we will not be foolish; for we will speak the true; a third heaven
has been contemplated by us and paradise has been seen, and not even in
knowledge whether with bodies or outside of bodies; and unutterable words have
been heard by us, which it is not permitted to speak to those who think the
human things.

24 Thus we too would appear even Pauls and thus indeed we would also obtain
the like graces. Thus both Peter and Paul will be glorified in our bettering
and will grant by the imitation toward them; since the people of the saints
rejoices in nothing so as upon a man's repentance and change toward the better
and exults upon our salvation, on behalf of which is every aim for them both
of the teaching and of the preaching and of the end itself, which they
accomplished toward imitation of the masterly. Thus in our things the apostles
rejoice, thus the preferred of the twelve are gladdened. But since I
remembered the number, I have something to say about this. And do not take me
as superfluous of speech, if I babble about numbers; for the discourse has
something of a contemplation not ignoble and not out of tune with the saints,
since the heralds of God did not wholly throw off the appearing wisdom, but
they received what does not harm, and they abhorred the superfluous. I seek
then, for the sake of what Christ accepted the number of the twelve and, it
having been needed to take disciples unto the preaching, he chose not fewer or
greater than the twelve. Arithmetic reasons do not have something strange to
say about twelve; for neither in planes a triangle or square or circle nor in
solids a cube or pyramid or any other of the known conspicuous things has
coincided in these. For it has appeared oblong, the twelve having been
composed from a double hexad as from a perfect in the relation, as has been
contemplated by those skilled about these things, lest the discourse be even
wholly over-curious and not fitted to theological theorems. But neither does
the thing of musical harmony have something contributing for us; for it is
common to others of the numbers. This number then is very dear to God; for he
measures the things of his inheritance by this. And Israel is a measuring-line
of inheritance; he has been numbered with so many tribes. The number twelve
then is composed from the ten and two, of the ten as a second monad and
belonging to those under generation; for as indeed to the first as impartible
and not sprouting out of themselves and hidden in essence the first monad
fits, so also to the second and those according to us as divisible and
measured by the ten the second is wholly fitting. It was needed then that
Christ having come to be in the mystery according to us and having taken on
the man whom he has also willed to deify take on something of the ten, and it
was needed that there be added to the decad neither a monad nor a triad nor
any other of the numbers, but a dyad wholly; for a dyad is a first number and
first motion of a monad and fertile

and nature and beginning of procession and measure of nature and character of
matter and first hypostasis of this, which had to be added to the decad of the
numbers because of the advance into this sensible and material life and the
fertility of the gospel. By this dyad then Peter and Paul have been numbered,
and the others to the decad of the numbers in sequence. Thus I have thought
about the number of the twelve. If they are acceptable also to those who have
attained the oracles, thanks to God, from whom every perfect gift; and if not,
yet these will be led up by others into another theorem more perfect and
higher, since not all things have been granted to all, but the gifts have been
divided, to speak according to Paul, being given according to the measure of
each. 25 But let the discourse be led back for us to its own turning-post. Let
us cleanse ourselves, brothers, through the apostles of Christ, and if not
working our own salvation, yet let us reverence their toils and the teachings.
Let us wipe off every sense, let us renounce the smoothnesses of touch, let us
use this only toward the things for which first the craftsman has implanted
it. Let us not be made womanish in the sense, let us smell the fragrant odors
of the myrrh of that according to Christ alone. Let us not use the tongue unto
the things from sea and earth, but let us learn that man will not live on
bread alone, but in every word that leads away toward salvation. Let us not
pipe the hearing with clear songs, but let us sound around it in the spiritual
odes, by which not only the divine is charmed but also the wicked spirits are
charmed down. Let us be eager to take the divine beauties with sights and not
the things that bewitch and lead toward destruction. Let us use the thymos
against the opposing powers alone; for against such it has also been sown in
us. Let us stretch the desire unto God and the things of God; for for the sake
of this it has been rooted in our inward parts. Thus we will imitate the
saints as possible and we will make their teaching go forward into work, thus
we will honor them, thus we will love them, if we throw off every sense and
trans-element it toward the better and lift the mind from the low-lying toward
the things above us. 26 And before all and above all let us acquire the thing
of love, through which we would not become imitators of Peter and Paul, but we
will also appear conformed to Christ himself; for to speak according to Paul
himself if I speak prophecies, if strange tongues, if interpretations of
tongues, if I am rich in all the gifts, but I do not have love, I am as a
clanging cymbal or bronze sounding in vain. Let us then embrace love, O
friends and brothers, than which nothing is better or dearer to God. This is
both beneficial according to the life and a most sure guide toward God; this
Christ disposes as a new covenant, this he makes a sign of his own teaching,
through this the one taught by Christ is marked with a sign. And how would we
gather one another into love? Let the rich give a share of what they are rich
in to the poor, let the poor bring the thanksgiving in the things they enjoy;
those in power the cheerfulness in the things they rule, the subjects the good
order in the things they are subjected; let the equals receive back measures
from the equals, let each offer to each a word that will benefit, for no one
is without need, even if he is supposed of the very rich; let all share their
own powers and habits. If we should thus be armed and thus be equipped, we
would quickly fly up toward heaven and stand beside God and purely lay hold
of the purest and brightest victim, of which we have now received dim
appearances. 27
But O dyad the chief of the disciples of Christ (for I must join them again),
the eyes of the whole body of the twelve of the disciples, the

all-shining luminaries, not the one set for the shining of day and the other
the torch-bearing of night, but both also of day and chasing the night of
ignorance and persuading the taught to walk becomingly as in day, the thrones
of the calling-up of Christ, upon which the double Lord has rested, the
bright-fruited trees of paradise, of which those who partake receive back
incorruption, the two-streamed rivers of the teachings of Christ, from which
those who have drunk do not die but are trans-elemented toward the immortal,
the twofold swords of the Spirit cutting the better from the worse. O living
tablets, in which the things of the new covenant have been written in; O two
fire-breathing pillars guiding the new people not toward a land trampled and
corrupted and at other times changed for others into mastership and lordship,
but into the divine and indestructible, which feet of the meek trample and
beautiful according to you and announcing good things; O unshaken towers of
the church, having kept this unmoved and unbuffeted unto ages; O Peter and
Paul (for I rejoice in your names, for they have something unto virtue and
consolation); O you who appeared as men and contended beyond man and at the
end of the mystery have become gods by grace, since you were well-pleasing to
him who is so by nature; receive this our discourse even if we have
offered it poorly and not munificently, since it is likely that all suffer
such a thing. For what word would attain your virtue? Who of the contests? Who
of the struggles? Who of the wonders? Who of the radiance from there and
brightness? Who will be able to praise the shadow of Peter alone? O shadow of
Peter, for toward you I will turn the discourse and I will converse with you
as with an ensouled one; for I even need you now that you may overshadow me
and the things of the diseases may be loosed for me; O divine shadow worthy of
its own soul, O having come to subsist beside a body, but raising bodies from
ill conditions; O not having the good fortune of sense and furnishing health
to senses; what sort of tongue would praise? What sort of word would make an
encomium?
What mind would attain your strength? Who has ever heard a shadow of a man
raising sick men and lifting the paralyzed? A wise word has said that man is a
shadow, but I find a shadow a craftsman of extraordinary things; how great for
you, Peter, the grace that has come upon you from heaven, through which even
the things of the shadow thus work wonders for you. 28 But one must go back
again to Peter and Paul; for if we have narrated their things in detail, the
whole time would not suffice. Receive this our discourse kindly, even if it is
not such as to be sufficient to be measured against your grandeur, which we
have made using not judgment rather than chance. For what is common to us and
Capua and to the one presiding over the place archieratically? But the
unstable of chance, but the uneven of affairs and the easy-changing of time
have joined us in these things, and the zeal of the one holding the
high-priesthood, which he nourishes for you the apostles, and his hot longing
have forced me into this discourse. Which as I have often said, O Peter and
Paul, receive gently, since it has been offered to you from a loving heart,
and give in return to those who forced and to us who were forced and simply to
all as many things as are good, as many as are solemn, as many as are truly
profitable and making familiar to Christ through cleansing, to lead through
our remaining life in peace and calm. For until when storm-waves and surges?
Until when the factious and warlike? May you send the devices of Satan farther
away, for we have given up falling into them; take our mind and every inward
power from the sensibles and temporal and work us to spend time in the things
above us through all; at last be gracious to us who have stumbled much in the
life, when you sit for judgment according to the voice of the Lord, and settle
us in the eternal and blessed tents, where there is a pure sound of those
keeping festival, where the dwelling of those being gladdened, where the

brightness and unending sojourn of those who have loved the common Master,
because to him is due honor and worship with the Father and the all-holy
Spirit unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
