OF GREGORY, BISHOP OF NYSSA, ON BENEFICENCE The president of this church and
the teachers of unerring piety and of the way of life according to virtue have
much likeness to the schoolmasters and the instructors of the first letters.
For as those, having received from the fathers the infants and children still
lisping, do not lead them at once to the more perfect lessons, but first,
having traced the alpha in the wax and the rest of the letters, they teach
them both to know their names and they exercise the hand in the written forms,
and after this they bring them on to the syllables and next they teach the
pronunciation of the words: so also the leaders of the church, bringing the
hearer first to the elementary lessons, grant according to progress the
knowledge of the more perfect. Since then on the two preceding days we
disciplined the love of pleasure of the throat and of the belly, do not think
that today too I will say the usual things, that it is good to despise meats
and to abstain from the laughter-loving and bacchic wine, and to restrain the
cooks and =9.94= to stop the laboring hand of the cupbearer. For these things
have been said sufficiently by me, and you have shown the counsel active
through deeds. And to you who have practiced the first lesson it is good, by
advancement, to impart the greater and more manly teachings hereafter. There
is then also a bodiless fasting and an immaterial self-control, the abstinence
from evils that turns about the soul, and for the sake of that this abstinence
from foods also was legislated for us. Fast then from wickedness; be continent
from the desire of what belongs to others; abstain from unjust gain; starve to
death the love of money of mammon; let there be no treasure in the house from
violence and plunder. For what is the profit, if you do not bring meats to the
mouth and you bite the brother with wickedness? Or what benefit, if you do not
eat your own and you take the things of the poor unjustly? And what is this
piety, if drinking water you stitch deceit and thirst for blood from
wickedness? Judas surely fasted with the eleven, but not having tamed the
money-loving manner he was helped not at all toward salvation by the
not-eating. And the devil does not eat (for he is a bodiless spirit), but
through wickedness he fell from the height. Likewise also each of the demons
neither takes in cooked dishes nor receives the charge of much drinking or
drunkenness (for nature has separated them from a share in foods), but
nevertheless both by night and by day wandering in the air they are makers and
servants of wickedness and they work the plots against us eagerly. =9.95= And
they waste away with envy and the evil eye, if we men are about to be akin to
God, they having fallen from kinship with the good. Let a philosophic manner
then school the life of Christians, and let the soul flee the harm of
wickedness. For if, abstaining from wine and meats, we are liable for sins
according to purpose, I foretell and I bear witness beforehand, that water and
vegetables and a bloodless table will profit us nothing, having the inward
disposition unlike the outward appearance. Fasting was legislated for purity
of soul. But if that is defiled by the choice and by the other activities, why
do we vainly spend the water that is drunk on what is unwashable and that
farms much mire? What benefit of bodily fasting, if the mind is not clean? For
there is no profit, if the chariot is strong and the four-horse team well
ordered while the charioteer is mad. And what profit of the well-fitted ship,
if the steersman is drunk? Fasting is the foundation of virtue. And as the
foundation of a house and the keel of a ship useless and unprofitable, even if
they are laid very firmly, unless the things that follow are skillfully built
upon them, so also there is no profit of this self-control, unless it receive
the rest of righteousness as naturally joined and consequent. Let fear of God
teach the tongue to utter what is fitting, not to speak vain things, to know
times and measures, and a necessary word and =9.96= a well-aimed answer; to
speak rhythmically, not pelting those who meet with hail by vehemence. For
this is why that finest membrane that binds it to the lower jaw is also called
a bridle, that it may not utter unordered and unmanaged things. Let it bless
and not revile; let it psalm and not blaspheme; let it speak well and not
slander; let the hasty hand be bound by the memory of God as by a chain. For
this we fast, because revilings and blows insulted our lamb before the nails.
Let us then who are disciples of Christ not emulate the Jewish manner. For if
we are so disposed, Isaiah will say to us: "Why do you fast for judgments and
fights and strike a humble man with fists?" From the same prophet be taught
also the workings of the undeceived and pure fasting: "Loose every bond of
injustice, dissolve the knots of violent contracts, break your bread for the
hungry and bring unsheltered poor into your house." And the present time has
brought us a great abundance of the naked and the unsheltered; for a multitude
of captives is at each man's doors. And the stranger and the migrant does not
fail, and the seeking hand stretched out everywhere is to be seen. For these
the house is the open air, and lodgings are stoas and streets and the more
deserted parts of the marketplace. And like night-ravens and owls they nest in
the holes. Their clothing is the patched rags; their farming is the mind of
those who pity; =9.97= food, whatever may fall in from chance; drink, as for
the unreasoning, the springs; cup, the hollow of the hands; storehouse, the
fold of the garment, and this if it has not gaped open, but holds what is put
in; table, the joining of the knees; bed, the ground; bath, a river or a lake,
which God gave common to all and unfurnished. Their life is wandering and
wild, not being such from the beginning, but having become so from the
misfortune and the necessity. Relieve these, you who fast. Be ambitious about
the unfortunate among the brothers. What you took away from your own belly,
add to the hungry man. Let the fear of God become a just equalizer. Heal by
sober self-control two passions contrary to one another, your satiety and the
brother's famine. For so also the physicians do: they empty some, and they
fill others, that by addition and subtraction the health concerning each may
be managed. Be persuaded by a good exhortation; let the word open the doors of
the well-off; let the counsel bring the poor man in to the one who has; let
not discourse enrich those who groan; let the pre-eternal word of God, through
a household word, give them both house and bed and table; farm what is for
need with your possessions. Besides these, other poor are many, being sick and
lying down. Let each search out the neighbors. Do not leave the one near you
to be healed by another; let not another take the treasure that lies beside
you; embrace the one who is being ill-treated as gold; take in your arms the
one who has been harmed as your own health, as the salvation of wife,
children, household servants, and of the whole =9.98= house. The poor man who
is also ill is a double beggar; for the healthy destitute both change from
door to door and go to those who have and, sitting at the crossroads, they
call upon all who pass by, but those bound by the disease, shut up in the
narrow lodgings and the narrow corners, as Daniel in the pit, await you the
devout and poor-loving as Habakkuk. And become a companion of the prophet
through almsgiving; stand by the one in need as a swift and unsluggish
nourisher. The giving is not a loss; do not fear; the fruit of almsgiving
sprouts in much yield. Sow by giving and you will fill the house with sheaves
of good things. But you will say: I too am poor. Be it so; granted. Give what
you have; for God does not seek the things beyond power. You bread, another a
cup of wine, another a garment, and thus from a joint contribution the
misfortune of the one is loosed. Nor did Moses take the expense of the
tabernacle from one minister, but from the whole people. For the one who was
rich brought gold to it, another silver, the poor man skins, the poorer than
this hairs. Do you see how even the widow's quadrans surpassed the offerings
of the rich? For she emptied all that she had, but of them a little fell out.
Do not despise those who lie as worth nothing. Reckon who they are and you
will find their dignity; they have put on the face of our Savior. For the
lover of men =9.99= lent them his own face, that through it they might put to
shame the unfeeling and the poor-haters, just as those who hold out the royal
images against those who do them violence, that from the form of the ruler
they may put the despiser to shame. These are the stewards of the expected
good things, the doorkeepers of the kingdom, who open the doors to the kind
and shut them to the hard and the man-haters; these are both vehement accusers
and good advocates; and they advocate and they accuse, not speaking, but being
seen by the judge. For the deed that has come about concerning them cries
before the knower of hearts more clearly than any herald. For the sake of
these the fearful judgment-seat of God was also written for us through the
gospels, of which you have often heard. For I saw there the Son of man coming
from the heavens and walking the air as land and escorted by the many myriads
of the angels; then a throne of glory being raised and the king sitting in
front upon it, and every tribe of men, as much as came into birth and was
shone upon by the sun and drew of this air, divided in two parts and the
multitude standing by the court. And those on the right were called lambs; and
I heard those on the other side as kids, having drawn the names upon
themselves from the likeness of their manners. And there were dialogues there
of the judge =9.100= toward those being judged and answers of those being
called to account toward the king. And the fitting things were allotted to
each; to those who had lived the best life, enjoyment of a kingdom, to the
man-haters and the wicked, punishment of fire, and this everlasting. And all
things were written carefully and the judgment-seat was painted for us exact
by the word, for no other reason than that we might learn the kindness of
beneficence. For this is what holds life together, mother of those in poverty,
teacher of the rich, good nurse of the young, caretaker of the aged,
storehouse of those in need, common harbor of the unfortunate, dividing from
itself providence to every age and misfortune. For as the prize-givers of
vanity, signaling their own ambition under a trumpet, promise to all of the
wrestling-school the distribution of the wealth, so beneficence calls all who
are in difficulties and circumstances to herself, dividing to those who
approach not honors of blows, but healings of misfortunes. And this is higher
than every praised deed, assessor of God, friend of the good and having much
intimacy with him. So then before all things God himself appears to us,
himself working the good and man-loving deeds; for the creation of the earth
and the ordering of the heaven and the well-ordered change of the seasons and
the heat of the sun and the cooling genesis of ice and all the things one by
one God himself does not =9.101= for himself (for that one is in need of no
such things), but he works continually for our sake, unseen farmer of the food
of men, timely sower and skilled channeler of water. For this one gives,
according to Isaiah, seed to the sower, and the water from clouds he now
sprinkles quiet upon the earth, and again he pours it vehement upon the
furrows. And when the standing crops have grown and the greening has passed,
scattering the clouds from all the covering of heaven he then sets over them
the sun bare, stretching a warm and fiery ray, that the ears may become ripe
for cutting. He also nourishes the vine by the seasons, preparing the drink
for the thirsty, and he nourishes for us herds of various kinds, that there
may be abundant relish for men; and of some the hides, farming wool, furnish
the covering, and of others they work out the shoes for us. You see that the
first inventor of beneficence is God, thus feeding the hungry and giving drink
to the thirsty and clothing the naked, as has been said before. And if you
wish to hear how he also heals the one who has been harmed, learn: who taught
the bee to work the wax and with it the honey? Who made the pine and the
terebinth and the tree of the mastic drip that rich juice? Who created the
land of the Indians as mother of dry and fragrant fruits? Who planted the
olive =9.102= as helper of bodily toils and pains? Who gave us the discernment
of roots and herbs and the learning of the qualities in them? Who established
the medicine that makes health? Who sent up from the earth springs of hot
waters, some healing our cold and wet, and others loosing the dry or the
compacted? And it is fitting to say in season according to Baruch: "This one
found out every way of knowledge and gave it to Jacob his child." For this
there are fiery crafts and crafts without fire and others watery and countless
findings of pursuits, that the service for the needs of life may be
accomplished without lack; and God is thus, the first inventor of beneficence
and a rich and at once compassionate supplier of the things necessary to us.
But we who are trained by every letter of the scripture to emulate our own
Lord and creator, so far as the imitation of the blessed and immortal is
attainable to the mortal, sweep everything toward our own enjoyment and we
allot some things to our own life, and we store some for heirs; but of those
who fare badly there is no account, nor a good care of those in poverty. O the
unpitying mind. A man sees a man lacking bread and not having the necessary
kindling of food. And he does not readily suffice and stretch out the
salvation, but he overlooks him as some thriving plant pitiably drying for
lack of waters, and this while he has the abundance flooding and is able to
channel to many the consolation from the possessions. For as the flow of one
spring =9.103= fattens many plains spread out, so also the plenty of one house
suffices to save peoples of poor men, only if a sparing and unsharing mind,
falling like a stone upon the outlet, does not beat back the flow. Not all
things for the flesh; let us live something also for God. For luxury, having
come in through enjoyment, heals a little portion of the flesh, the throat;
and having put the materials to rot in the belly it has the privy as its end.
But mercy and beneficence are things dear to God and, in whatever man they
dwell, they deify him and stamp him toward imitation of the good, that he may
be an image of the first and unmixed being that surpasses every mind. And of
what sort do they pledge the end of the zeal? Here a good hope and an
expectation that gladdens, and in the things after, when having put off this
flowing flesh we put on incorruption instead, a blessed life, unceasing and
indestructible, prepared in certain wondrous well-beings not now known to us.
Therefore you who were created rational and have the mind as interpreter and
teacher of the divine things, do not be baited by the temporary. But acquire
the things that never leave the one who has acquired them; set measures for
the use of life. Let not all things be ours, but let there be a part also of
the poor, the beloved of God; for all things are of God, the common father;
and we are brothers as of the same tribe; and it is best and more just for
brothers to share the inheritance in equal portion; and in second rank, even
if one or two appropriate the more, at least let those who remain =9.104= gain
the part. But if someone should wish to be lord of the whole of all things and
to shut the brothers out even from the third or fifth portion, this one is a
bitter tyrant, an implacable barbarian, an insatiable beast, alone pleasantly
gaping around the feast, or rather even wilder than the beasts themselves; if
indeed a wolf receives the wolf into food and many dogs in the same place tear
one body; but he, insatiable, takes none of those of the same tribe into the
sharing of the wealth. The measured table is enough for you. Do not fall out
toward the sea of unbridled all-feasting; for hard is the shipwreck upon that,
not breaking upon submerged rocks, but thrusting out into the deepest
darkness, whence the one who has fallen will never come out. Use, do not
misuse; for this Paul also taught you. Relax yourself with measured enjoyment;
do not revel in the pleasures; do not be destruction of simply all animals, of
large four-foots, of small four-foots, of birds, of fishes, of the easily got,
of the rare, of the cheap, of the costly; do not fill one belly with the sweat
of many hunters, as some deepest well not filled by the many-handedness of
those who heap it. Because of those who live in luxury not even the depth of
the sea remains undisturbed, nor are only the fishes that swim in the water
hunted, but also as many sea animals as are borne in the sediment of the
waters, even these are dragged out toward the mainland and this air. Thus the
kinds of shellfish have not escaped =9.105= and the urchin is hunted and the
cuttlefish creeping is netted and the octopus grown upon the rocks is torn off
and the snails are scraped from the lowest depths and all kinds of animals, as
many as float upon the waves of the surface and as many as have been allotted
to dwell the floor of the sea, are brought out toward this air, the
contrivance of the pleasure-lovers devising various traps against them. And of
what sort also are the things that follow of those who live in luxury? For it
is necessary that wickedness, wherever it may strike as a disease, drag the
consequent materials after it. Therefore those who set the delicate and
Sybaritic table are drawn of necessity into constructions of magnificent
buildings and they spend the great plenty upon size of houses and superfluous
adornment. Upon these they make the couches fine, they take thought for
flowery and embroidered clothing and they forge many-talented tables of
silver, some beaten to smoothness, and some varied with sculpture, so that it
is possible for the sight together with the throat to feast on the stories.
Reckon for me the things that follow: mixing-bowls, tripods, jars, the pouring
vessels, the relish-bearers, countless kinds of drinking-cups, jesters, mimes,
lyre-players, singers, fine-talkers, musicians male, musicians female,
dancing-girls, the whole string of wantonness, boys made womanish in the hair,
shameless girls, sisters of Herodias in disorder, destroying John, the
God-formed and philosophic mind in each. =9.106= While all these things are
being accomplished in the house, countless Lazaruses sit by the gate, some
ulcered badly, others with the eye struck out, others groaning a mutilation of
the feet, and some of them creeping altogether and having endured deprivation
of all the limbs. And crying they are not heard; for the sound of pipes and
the strains of the self-voiced songs and the guffaw of the broad laughter
hinder it. And if somewhere they also trouble the doors still more, a bold
gatekeeper of a pitiless master, leaping up from somewhere, drives them off
with rods, calling them dogs and carding the wounds with whips. And the
friends of Christ go away, the chief of the commandments, having shared
neither a little bread nor relish, but having gained in addition insults and
blows; and within, in the workshop of mammon, some as overloaded ships belch
the food, others sleep upon the table with the cups standing by. And the sin
inhabits the unseemly house twofold, the one upon the satiety of those who are
drunk, and the other upon the famine of the driven-out poor. If God oversees
these things, as indeed he watches over them, what catastrophe of life do you
poor-haters think? Speak: or do you not know that all the shuddering and
fearful examples the holy gospel cries out and testifies for the sake of such
as these? And there has been written one roaring heavily and groaning, the one
reared in the fine linen and held in the abyss of evils =9.107=; and another
again of like manner with this one was condemned to an unexpected death,
planning in the evening the morning luxury and not overtaking the ray of the
dawn. Do not be mortal in the faith and immortal in the enjoyment; for thus we
have of mind, we who wish to grant all things to the flattery of the flesh, as
householders without successor, as continual lords of the things upon earth,
in the summer caring for the sowing and in the sowing hoping the delight of
the summer; planting the plane-tree and hoping the shade of the tall tree;
setting the pit of the palm and awaiting the sweetness of the fruits; and
these things often in gray hair, when the autumn of the age has come on, when
the winter of death is near, and there remain for the life not a cycle of
years, but three or four days. Let us then who are rational reckon that our
life is passing and the time flowing, both unstable and ungraspable, like some
stream of a river, driving everything that happens in it toward the end of
corruption; and would that, being short and perishable, it were without
account. But this is the dangerous thing, that of every hour, and not only but
also of the words which we utter, we owe the defense to the unbribable court.
Therefore also the blessed psalmist, bringing himself to a thought like the
things now said, desires to know the appointed term of his own end. And he
entreats God to be taught the number of the remaining days =9.108=, that he
may prepare himself toward the works of the departure, and may not be troubled
all at once as an unready traveler walking in the middle and seeking round
about the necessary things of the provisions. He says at any rate thus: "Make
known to me, Lord, my end and what is the number of my days, that I may know
what I lack. Behold, you have made my days handbreadths, and my substance is
as nothing before you." See the intelligent care of a good soul, and this in
royal dignity. For he looks as in a mirror at the king of kings and the judge
of judges and he seeks, having received the perfect adornment of the
commandments, to depart complete and lacking nothing, a citizen of the life
there, which may it be that we all obtain by the grace and loving-kindness of
our Lord Jesus Christ, to whom the glory unto the ages. Amen.
