and the ungodly turns aside the ways of righteousness. The ways of one who
takes unjust thoughts in the heart do not prosper; for he named passionate
thoughts gifts brought to us by the demons. Or: an ungodly man will take gifts
in the bosom in order to turn aside the ways of judgment. 167 17, 21 And the
heart of a fool is pain to him who possesses it; a father is not gladdened
over an untrained son; but a prudent son gladdens his mother. The prudent one
gladdens the mother; but the fool pains her who bore him. 168 17, 24 An
intelligent face belongs to a wise man; but the eyes of the fool are at the
ends of the earth. And the heart of a fool is in the extremity of vice. 169
17, 25 A foolish son is wrath to a father and pain to her who bore him. The
prudent son has both her who bore him and a mother; but the foolish son has
only her who bore him; for the mother is also she who bore; but she who bore
is not in every case also a mother. For if the child dies in vice and
ignorance, she is one who bore, but she is not a mother, because the child has
not even partaken of the spirit of adoption. And the same will be understood
also of perceptible mothers and of perceptible death; for we shall call the
mother also her who bore; but her who bore we shall no longer call mother of
the child once it has died. 170 17, 26 It is not good to penalize a just man,
nor holy to plot against just rulers. They do not

seek what is ours, but us. 171 17, 27 He who spares to let go a hard
word is discerning; and a long-suffering man is prudent. He named the thought
that moves anger a hard word. Or he named the speech that moves his neighbor's
anger a hard word. 172 17, 28 Wisdom will be reckoned to a senseless man who
has asked for wisdom; and one who has made himself mute will seem to be
prudent. If this one asks about wisdom who wishes to learn something about
wisdom, this one is silent from wisdom who wishes to know nothing about
wisdom; therefore the former is also said to be more prudent than the second.
173 18, 1 A man wishing to separate from friends seeks pretexts; and at every
time he will be open to reproach. He calls sins pretexts; "for to make
pretexts," he says, "pretexts in sins." And friends, all the saints to whom he
was joined through virtue. 174 18, 2 One lacking in mind has no need of
wisdom; for he is rather led by folly. For he loved the darkness rather than
the light. 175 18, 5 It is not good to admire the face of an ungodly man, nor
holy to turn aside what is just in judgment. He who accepts the vice that is
in the devil and acts according to it, this one admires the face of the
ungodly. 176 18, 6 The lips of a fool lead him into evils; and his bold mouth
calls upon death. If death is begotten from boldness, and death separates the
soul from the life that truly is, boldness separates us from him who said: "I
am the life." And just as death is begotten from boldness, so life from
meekness; for meekness is opposed to boldness. 177 18, 8.1 Fear casts down the
sluggish. Fear casts down the sluggish, either the hostile fear or that of the
Lord, which separates them from sluggishness through good works. 178 18, 8.2
And the souls of the androgynous will hunger. An androgynous one is he who is
neither able to teach another nor willing to learn from another. 179 18, 9 He
who does not heal himself in his works is a brother of him who ruins himself.
And Paul says: "If then anyone cleanses himself, he will be a vessel useful to
the master." 180 18, 10 From greatness of strength is the name of the Lord;
and the just who have run to it are raised high. The name of the Lord
signifies the knowledge of God. And through the right life the just run to it;
and through contemplation they are raised high. 181 18, 12 Before destruction
a man's heart is raised high, and before glory it is humbled. Just as
destruction follows pride, so glory follows humility. 182 18, 13 He who
answers a word before he hears, it is folly to him and a reproach. This saying
is to be used toward those who have not received knowledge from God, yet
attempt to teach others. And David says: "to hear the voice of your praise"
and then "to declare" also to others "your wonders." And again Solomon says:
"What your eyes have seen, speak." 183 18, 14 A prudent servant soothes a
man's anger; but who will bear a faint-hearted man? Just as our Lord is said
to become a leopard at a loss toward those who offend and a stone of stumbling
toward those who disobey, so also faint-hearted over those who sin. And he can
call faint-hearted also the wicked one and every demon that has fallen from
virtue and knowledge. 184 18, 16 A man's gift makes him broad and seats him
beside rulers. He now called the right life a man's gift, which makes him
broad and renders him worthy of the fullness of God, which he names a seat of
the holy powers; for a seat of the intellect is an excellent habit that keeps
the one seated hard to move or unmoved. 185 18, 18 A lot stops contradictions;
and among rulers it defines. Knowledge stops folly; for this is the lot of the
rational nature. 186 18, 21 Death and life are in the hand of the tongue; and
those who hold it will eat its fruits. Here he says that the soul is receptive
of death and

of life, from which we establish that its self-determination has come to be.
187 18, 22.1 He who found a good wife found graces ... 18, 22α.2 But he who
holds an adulteress is a fool and ungodly. "He who found" a "good" wisdom
"found graces." 188 "But he who holds" vice is "a fool and ungodly." 189 19, 4
Wealth adds many friends; but the poor man is left even from the friend he
has. Wealth of knowledge and wisdom adds to us many angels; but the unclean
man is also separated from the angel given him from childhood. For spiritual
friendship is virtue and knowledge of God, through which we are joined in
friendship to the holy powers, if indeed men who repent become a cause of joy
to the angels. So also the Savior calls friends those who were once slaves,
having deemed them worthy of the greater contemplation. So also Abraham,
having grown rich in knowledge, sets that mystical table before the friends
who appeared to him at noon. But Saul is separated even from the friend he has
because of vice; for it is written, "and the spirit of God departed from Saul
and an evil spirit from the Lord choked Saul," calling the angel a spirit of
the Lord; "for he who makes," he says, "his angels spirits and his ministers a
flaming fire." And that angels are also entrusted with men, the Lord teaches
in the Gospels, saying: "See that you do not despise one of these little ones,
for their angels always behold the face of my Father who is in heaven." And
again Jacob: "the angel who delivers me from all the evils." And Zechariah:
"and the angel who speaks in me said." 190 19, 5 A false witness will not go
unpunished; and he who accuses unjustly will not escape. As many as, being
ignorant of the words concerning judgment and providence, blaspheme the
Creator, these accuse unjustly. And again, as many as, being troubled by their
own passions, think that virtue is impossible to achieve, these accuse
unjustly him who gave the law. 191 19, 7.3–4 A good thought will draw near to
those who know it; and a prudent man will find it. He now named the knowledge
of God a thought, and those who know it those who are pure in heart. 192 19,
7.5–6 He who does much evil completes vice; and he who provokes with words
will not be saved. The passionate thoughts provoke the soul toward vice. 193
19, 10.1 Luxury does not profit a fool. Neither intelligible luxury nor
perceptible luxury profits the fool. For the one he will trample with his
feet, being a pleasure-loving swine; and by the perceptible luxury his flesh
will be moved. 194 19, 11 A merciful man is long-suffering; and his boast
comes upon the lawless. If he who boasts rightly boasts in the Lord, and our
Lord is wisdom, then he who boasts rightly boasts in wisdom. The boast, then,
of the long-suffering and merciful man, which is his wisdom and knowledge,
comes upon the lawless, delivering them from vice, now as far as it is
possible, and of necessity in the age to come. 195 19, 12 A king's threat is
like a lion's roar; and as dew on grass, so is his cheer. Our Lord becomes a
consuming fire and an angry lion toward those who sin, burning wood, hay,
stubble and consuming the flesh that wars against the spirit; but light and
dew toward those who succeed, showing them the reasons of the things that have
come to be and quenching the flaming arrows of the wicked one and cooling the
burning heat that comes from the practice. 196 19, 13.2 Prayers from a
harlot's hire are not pure. He named the unclean soul a harlot and called her
hire the state, from which prayers do not become pure. 197 19, 14 Fathers will
divide house and substance to children; but a wife is fitted to a man from the
Lord. To teach the sons concerning virtue and

knowledge of God belongs to the fathers; but to give wisdom to the children
belongs to the Lord. For here he calls the wife wisdom; "for fall in love," he
says, "with wisdom and she will keep you; honor her, that she may embrace
you." 198 19, 16 He who keeps a commandment keeps his own soul; but he who
despises his own ways will perish. The commandments, as toward God who
commanded, are called commandments; but as toward the intellect that walks in
them they are called ways. And again, since they were given upon witnesses of
heaven and earth, the commandments are called testimonies; therefore the
intellect that has received them is also named a witness, who, if he has lied
about them, will not go unpunished. 199 19, 17 He who has mercy on a poor man
lends to God; and according to his gift he will repay him. He now named the
gift the purity of the heart; for in proportion to our impassibility we are
deemed worthy of knowledge. 200 19, 19 A man of evil mind will suffer many
losses; and if he is pestilent, he will add even his soul. I think the many
losses contain deprivation of the contemplation of the things that have come
to be, and the addition of the soul the disappearance of the natural
conceptions concerning God, when it has fallen altogether into irrationality.
And the Savior also says in the Gospels: "What will a man be profited, if he
gains the whole world, but" loses "his soul" and "suffers loss." But here the
destruction indicates ignorance of bodies and of bodiless things; and the loss
presents the last irrationality. 201 19, 20 Hear, son, the discipline of your
father, that you may become wise at your last things. After the right way of
life is knowledge. For "at the last things" here does not signify time, but
the purity after the practical virtues. 202 19, 23 Fear of the Lord is unto
life for a man; but the fearless one will lodge in places where knowledge is
not overseen. If "fear of the Lord is unto life for a man," and "fear of the
Lord is discipline and wisdom," the life of the man is discipline and wisdom.
But Christ says: "I am the life"; therefore Christ is discipline and wisdom.
So "to know discipline and wisdom" is to know Christ. Therefore the fearless
one will be in vice and ignorance, in which Christ is not. 203 19, 24 He who
hides his hands in his bosom unjustly will not even bring them to his mouth.
He who has not lived rightly hides his hands in his soul unjustly, not wishing
to work his own earth nor to be filled with bread; for the practical virtues
hold the place of hands, bringing to our mouth the bread that came down from
the heavens and gives life to the world. 204 19, 26 He who dishonors a father
and thrusts away his mother will be put to shame and will be open to reproach.
Through transgression of the law he dishonors God and also thrusts away the
mother, who is discipline, if indeed "a just man is begotten unto life." And
righteousness is composed of all the virtues. 205 19, 27 A son who fails to
keep a father's discipline will meditate evil sayings. The unclean thoughts
are evil sayings begotten in the soul of him who has not kept the commandments
of the heavenly Father. 206 20, 1.1 Wine is intemperate and drunkenness is
insolent. If "their wine is the wrath of dragons," and wine is intemperate,
then wrath is intemperate, making men intemperate, and anger is insolent; for
this drunkenness is of such a nature as to come from boiling wrath. And if the
Nazirites according to the law abstain from wine, then it has been legislated
that the Nazirites be outside wrath. 207 20, 2 A king's threat does not differ
from a lion's wrath; and he who provokes him sins against his own soul. Here
he has plainly called Christ a king; for he who provokes him through sins sins
against his own soul. 208 20, 4 A sluggish man being reproached is not
ashamed; likewise also he who borrows grain in harvest. If it is possible to
borrow grain in harvest, it is possible also in the age to come to borrow
intelligible grain from those who

have worked their own earth in the six years and who feed widows and orphans
in the seventh year. 209 20, 7 He who walks blameless in righteousness will
leave his children blessed. Those, clearly, whom he has begotten according to
virtue. 210 20, 9α The lamp of one who speaks evil of father or mother will be
quenched; and the pupils of his eyes will see darkness. The same Christ can
according to aspect be both father and mother; father of those who have a
spirit of adoption, and mother of those who need milk and not solid food. For
the Christ speaking in Paul became father of the Ephesians, revealing to them
mysteries of wisdom, and mother of the Corinthians, giving them milk to drink.
211 20, 9β A portion eagerly pursued among the first things will not
be blessed. "A portion which for a time fattens" "your throat; yet afterward
you will find it more bitter than gall." 212 20, 9ξ Do not say: I will repay
the enemy; but wait for the Lord, that he may help you. Do not say, being
moved according to pride: I alone will fight the enemies; but wait for the
Lord, that he may help you. And David says: "I will not hope in my bow and my
sword will not save me." And again: "a horse is false for salvation" and
"unless the Lord builds a house and guards a city." 213 20, 10 A great and a
small weight and double measures are both unclean before the Lord. I think
that the one called by some both rash and cowardly is a great and a small
weight, and in general I think that every excess and deficiency is a great and
a small weight; for both are vices. 214 He who wishes to be treated well by
another, yet does not himself choose likewise to give rest to others, has
double measures, not obeying the commandment that says: "Whatever you wish
that men should do to you, these things you likewise also do to them." 215 20,
12 An ear hears and an eye sees; both are works of the Lord. It is not the eye
that sees badly that is a work of the Lord, but the one that sees; and it is
not the ear that hears badly that is a work of the Lord, but the one that
hears; and of the other members of the body likewise it must be said. And you
will use this saying toward those who speak evil of this body of ours and
insult the Creator. 216 20, 13 Do not love to speak against, lest you be taken
away; but open your eyes and be filled with bread. Through the virtues we open
the eyes of the soul; and through wisdom we are filled with intelligible
bread. 217 20, 23 A double weight is an abomination to the Lord, and a
deceitful balance is not good before him. He calls a deceitful balance the
intellect that is of such a nature as to judge things justly, yet inclines by
the swing of free will. 218 20, 24 A man's steps are directed by the Lord; but
how could a mortal understand his ways? He who is still mortal and has not
died together with Christ cannot understand the ways of the Lord. 219 20, 25
It is a snare for a man to consecrate something of his own hastily; for after
vowing, repenting comes to be. Repentance over good things does not happen to
the just, but to the unjust. And let the unjust promise nothing to God without
consideration; "for after vowing, repenting comes to be." 220α 20, 26 A wise
king is a winnower of the ungodly and he will set a wheel upon them.
Separating the chaff from the wheat. "Make them like a wheel." 221 20, 27 The
light of the Lord is the breath of men, the lamp that searches the storehouses
of the belly. If the light of the Lord is the knowledge of the Lord, and the
light of the Lord is the breath of men, then the knowledge of the Lord is the
breath of men. And he named the devil who is in ignorance, seeming to shine, a
lamp that empties the intellect of good things and transforms itself into an
angel of light. 222 21, 3 To do just things and to speak truth are more
acceptable with God than the blood of sacrifices. This saying casts out the
sacrifice through irrational animals; "for a sacrifice to God is a broken
spirit." 223 21, 8 Toward the crooked, crooked ways

God will send; for his works are pure and straight. If the works of God
are pure and straight, and the intellect also is one of his works, then the
intellect was created by the Lord straight and pure. 224 21, 9 It is better to
dwell on a corner of the open air than in plastered places with injustice and
in a common house. He set the open-air corner against the plastered and common
house. And if the plastered and common house is vice, the open-air corner is
virtue. An open-air corner, then, is practice that lays hold of contemplation
and is lit by the sun of righteousness. And Paul also well called the high
priest of the Jews a plastered wall that would be struck by God. And the
Savior also in the Gospels called the Pharisees plastered tombs. And it is
well too to say "on a corner," where the stone lies that was rejected by the
builders and became the head of the corner. And he called common that which is
not of the one God. 225
21, 14 A secret gift overturns angers; but he who spares gifts raises a strong
wrath. A secret gift is, so that what the right hand does the left may not
know. 226 21, 16 A man wandering from the way of righteousness will rest in a
synagogue of giants. The synagogue of the giants is vice and ignorance. 227
21, 19 It is better to dwell in a desert land than with a fighting and
talkative and angry woman. And David says: "In a desert and trackless and
waterless land, so I appeared to you in the holy place." A desert land, then,
is the virtue that does not have the old men dwelling in it who are being
corrupted according to the desires of deceit. Therefore neither does the devil
find rest in it, if indeed "he goes through waterless places seeking rest and
does not find it"; "for he himself is king of all that are in the waters"; and
again of wisdom: "tight," he says, "are the dwellings of her houses." And if
this is so, then the fighting and talkative and angry woman is vice, which
makes the one living with her fighting and talkative and angry. 228 21, 20 A
desirable treasure will rest on the mouth of a wise on the mouth of a wise
man; but foolish men will swallow it. The wisdom of the Lord will rest in the
heart of a wise man; but foolish men will destroy it. 229 21, 22 A wise man
went up against fortified cities and took down the fortification in which the
ungodly had trusted. Wisdom is a fortified city, in which the wise dwell,
"taking down thoughts and every height raised against the knowledge of God."
230 21, 23 He who guards his mouth and keeps his tongue keeps his soul from
affliction. Mouth and tongue, soul and intellect; and they call soul anger and
desire, which some name the passionate part of the soul. 231 21, 26 An ungodly
man desires evil desires the whole day; but the just man has mercy and pities
unsparingly. It belongs to angels never to desire evil desires; and to men to
desire at one time, and at another not to desire, evil desires; and to demons
always to desire evil desires. For "the whole day" makes clear throughout
life. Such also is "be in the fear of the Lord the whole day," i.e. throughout
life. 232 21, 31 A horse is prepared for a day of war; but help is from the
Lord. He calls the intellect a horse; "for you will mount," he says, "upon
your horses and your horsemanship is salvation"; and of Paul the Lord says "to
bear my name." 233 22, 1 A good name is more to be chosen than much wealth;
and good grace above silver and gold. He called a good name the virtue
signified by its own name; for that name is good which has the good thing
signified. Thus we shall not praise the unjust woman who is called
righteousness, but her who has righteousness, even if she is named injustice.
234 22, 2 Rich and poor met one another; and the Lord made both. The rich man
through alms purifies anger, acquiring love; but the poor man through poverty

is taught to be humble-minded. 235 22, 4 The generation of wisdom is fear of
the Lord and wealth and glory and life. "These are the generations of Noah,"
these are the generations of Abraham. 236 22, 5 Thistles and snares are in
crooked ways; but he who guards his own soul will keep away from them. And the
Lord says to Adam: "Thorns and thistles the earth will grow for you," that is,
the soul. And he calls thorns and thistles the sins, from which the crown of
Christ was also plaited; for he took away the sin of the world. 237 22, 7 The
rich will rule the poor and servants will lend to their own masters. In the
age to come those who have been enriched in everything in all knowledge and
all wisdom will rule the unclean and those deprived of this wealth. But who
the servants are and who the masters, it is not now necessary to publish,
because the account concerning them is mystical and deeper. 238 22, 8α God
blesses a cheerful man and a giver; but he will complete the vanity of his
works. The Lord completes the vanity of the works through virtue and
knowledge. 239 22, 9α He who gives gifts procures victory and honor, yet he
takes away the soul of those who possess them. He names the virtues a man's
gifts, through which, conquering the devil, he renders himself honorable to
God and takes away his own soul from the demons that possessed it. 240 22, 10
Cast a pestilent man out of a council and strife will go out with him; for
when he sits in a council, he dishonors all. He called the worst state a
council, from which the wise Solomon wishes the man to be cast out through
spiritual teaching; and strife signifies contentiousness. And he can call
pestilent also the devil, whom one must drive from the soul. For if he sits in
it, through uncleanness he dishonors all the right thoughts. And the apostle
also shows the soul to be a council, through the thoughts he introduces
accusing and defending; "for," he says, "between one another of the thoughts
accusing or also defending"; and where there is accusation and defense, there
is also a council. 241 22, 11.3 A king shepherds with lips; 22, 12 and the
eyes of the Lord will keep perception; but a lawless man makes words cheap.
The Lord who keeps our souls himself also shepherds us through spiritual
knowledge, which knowledge he who transgresses his law makes cheap. And it is
to be noted that Christ, being king, is said through condescension to shepherd
us; "for I," he says, "am the good shepherd." And if he is king of those who
reign, and shepherd of sheep, he will at some time be king only, when the
sheep have passed over to the royal dignity. 242 22, 13 The sluggish man makes
a pretext and says: a lion in the ways, and in the streets murderers. Our
enemy the devil goes about as a lion seeking whom he may swallow, and the
sluggish man, fearing him, draws back from the working of the virtues. 243 22,
14 The mouth of a lawless man is a deep pit; and he who is hated by the Lord
will fall into it. Job, not being hated by the Lord, but for the sake of
testing, has fallen into it. 244 22, 15 Folly is fastened to a young man's
heart; but a rod and discipline are far from him. Far from the heart of the
fool is the rod from the root of Jesse. 245 22, 16 He who slanders a poor man
makes his own evils many; and he gives to a rich man for less. Just as the
devil slanders us, taking from us the virtues which he has not given us, so we
also slander him, taking from him the vices which we have not given him. And
insofar as we take the vices from him, we take as from one poor in virtues;
and insofar as we give him our virtues to our own lessening, we give as to one
rich in vice. And one of the elders used to say that we are the slanderers who
slander Christ who became poor for us and owed us nothing, taking many things
from him and giving to Satan for the humiliation of our souls. 246 22, 17
Apply your ear to words of the wise and hear my

word; and set your heart, that you may know that they are good. This one hears
the words of God who does the things commanded by them; "for not the hearers
of a law are just with God, but the doers of the law will be justified." 247
22, 20 And you, copy them for yourself threefold for counsel and knowledge
upon the breadth of your heart. He who has made his heart broad through purity
will understand the words of God, the practical and the natural and the
theological. For the whole treatment according to Scripture is divided
threefold into ethical and natural and theological. And Proverbs follows the
first, Ecclesiastes the second, and the Song of Songs the third. 248 22, 26 Do
not give yourself into surety, being ashamed before a face; 22, 27 for if you
have not from where to pay, they will take the bedding under your sides. Let
those who take on the base thoughts and by this honor the wicked one strive
with all their power to pay back the worse ones through the good thoughts. For
if they are not strong enough to do this, they will take the bedding under the
sides of the soul, that is, the virtue. For this is called a garment of the
intellect that stands in righteousness; but of the one who has now fallen
through the base thoughts it is called a mattress. 249 22, 28 Do not move
eternal bounds which your fathers set. He who transposes the bounds of piety
shows it to be superstition or impiety, and he who transposes the bounds of
courage works it into rashness or cowardice. And likewise it must be
understood also of the other virtues and of the doctrines and of the faith
itself. And this is especially to be kept in the case of the holy Trinity; for
he who does not theologize the Holy Spirit dissolves the baptism; and he who
also names certain others gods introduces a people of gods. 250 23, 1 If you
sit to dine at a ruler's table, understand intelligibly the things set before
you ... 23, 3 and if you are more insatiable, do not desire his foods; for
these have hold of a false life. For not all have room for the more mystical
meaning of Scripture. 251 Divine Scripture must be understood intelligibly and
spiritually; for the perceptible knowledge according to the history is not
true. 252 23, 6 Do not dine with an envious man; nor desire his foods; 23, 7
for in the way that if someone should swallow a hair, so he eats and drinks;
23, 8 nor bring him in to yourself and eat your morsel with him; for he will
vomit it and will ruin your good words. If the envious man eats foods of
impiety and is drunk with lawless wine, and one must not dine with this man,
then one must not be ungodly and lawless; for these vices ruin spiritual
knowledge. 253 23, 9 Say nothing into the ears of a fool, 253 lest he sneer at
your intelligent words. Say nothing intelligent, that is, deep and mystical;
for one must not cast the pearls before the swine. 254 23, 10 Do not transpose
eternal bounds which your fathers set; and do not enter into a possession of
orphans. A possession of orphans is vice, through which they were deprived of
the Father in heaven. 255 23, 17 Let not your heart emulate sinners, but be in
the fear of the Lord the whole day. If "by the fear of the Lord everyone turns
from evil," he well exhorts us throughout the whole of life to turn from every
evil. 256 23, 18 For if you keep them, you will have offspring; and your hope
will not depart. He called the right thoughts and the spiritual contemplations
offspring of the intellect. Therefore the soul that does not have these
offspring is also childless, which offspring are of such a nature as to be
born from the spiritual bridegroom. 257 23, 21 For every drunkard and
whoremonger will become poor, and every sleepy one will put on torn and ragged
things. A torn and ragged garment is not a wedding garment. 258 23, 22 Hear,
son, the father who begot you, and do not despise that your mother has grown
old. I heard one of the elders

calling the soul mother of the intellect; for this, he says, through the
practical virtues brings the intellect into light. And he used to call soul

raises the rational nature; but the antichrist digs it down. 360 29, 7 A just
man knows how to judge for the needy; but the ungodly will not understand
knowledge and a poor man will not have a discerning intellect. Therefore a
poor man is he who does not have a discerning intellect. 361 29, 9 A wise man
judges nations; but a base man being angry

is laughed at and does not cower. "And at him," says David, "they will laugh
and will say: behold a man who did not set God as his helper." 362 29, 10 Men
sharing in bloods will seek a holy one; but the straight will seek out his
soul. This one seeks out a soul of a just man who wishes to understand the
things understood by it. 363 29, 11 A fool brings out all his anger; but a
wise man stores it in part. This one stores the anger in part, either he who
is angry over the just matters, or he who through long-suffering consumes the
anger in part. And toward the simpler ones the former must be said, and toward
the serious the second. 364 29, 18 There will not be an interpreter for a
lawless nation; but he who keeps the law is most blessed. For those who have
kept the law there will be an interpreter; but for those who have acted
lawlessly there will not be an interpreter, yet there will be a punisher, if
indeed not a word, but a rod, is given to a lawless nation. 365 29, 19 A hard
servant will not be disciplined by words; for even if he understands, he will
not obey. That no one is hard by nature, "for even if he understands, he will
not obey" presents; for he who is hard by nature would never understand
anything right. And I say this because of Pharaoh hardened against Israel and
disciplined not by words but by scourges. 366 29, 21 He who lives in luxury
from a child will be a servant; and at last he will be pained over himself. If
living in luxury makes one sin, "and everyone who does the sin is a slave of
the sin," then everyone who lives in luxury is a slave of the sin. 367 29, 23
Insolence humbles a man; but the Lord props the humble-minded with glory. Now
he names vice insolence of a rational soul. 368 29, 24.1 He who shares with a
thief hates his own soul. "The thief," he says, "does not come except that he
may steal and sacrifice and destroy." 369 29, 24.2 And if when an oath has
been set forth they hear and do not report, 29, 25.1 having feared and been
ashamed of men they will be tripped. He called the law an oath; for just as
the oath sets God in a soul, so also the law brings God into a soul; and in
the way again that perjury takes God out of a soul, so also lawlessness casts
God out from it. If then, he says, when a law has been set they hear and do
not confess their own sins, "having feared and been ashamed of men they will
be tripped." So also David says: "I swore and set to keep the judgments of
your righteousness." And again Solomon says: "He who covers his own impiety
will not prosper; but he who declares and rebukes will be loved." And David:
"I said," he says, "I will confess against myself my lawlessness to the Lord
and you forgave the impiety of my heart"; and "you say first your
lawlessnesses." 370 29, 26 Many serve faces of leaders; but from the Lord what
is just comes to a man. In succeeding we serve the angels; for they are our
leaders, having taken us from the beginning, "when the Most High divided
nations" and "set bounds of nations according to the number of his angels."
And what is just from the judgment will belong to us from the Lord on that day
when he will judge the inhabited world in righteousness, if indeed the Father
has given all the judgment to the Son. 371 31, 10.1 Who will find a courageous
woman? Courage is an excellent habit of a rational soul, according to which it
has mastered the enemies opposed to it. 372 '31, 11.2 Such a one will not lack
good spoils. Having conquered the opposing power we spoil it, learning the
reasons concerning it. 373 '31, 13 Carding wools and flax she made them useful
for her hands. A soul cards wools and flax exercising the reasons concerning
ensouled and soulless things, or examining the reasons concerning practice and
nature. And someone used to say that a soul cards wools and flax drawing to
itself through the practice the contemplation concerning bodies and bodiless
things. 374 '31, 15.1–2 And she rises from nights and she gave foods to the
house. The soul that rises from night

the sun of righteousness finds watching, and certainly also praying, that she
may not fall into temptation; for she too hastens to say, "I kept watch and I
became as a sparrow alone upon a housetop." 375 '31, 18 And she tasted that it
is good to work and her lamp is not quenched the whole night. A lamp is a pure
intellect filled with spiritual contemplation. 376 '31, 19 She stretches her
hands upon the things that profit; and she props her arms upon a spindle. A
spindle is a pure intellect intertwining virtue with virtue and doctrine with
doctrine, or a spoken word drawing spiritual contemplation from the intellect.
377 '31, 21 Her husband does not take thought for those in the house, whenever
he lingers; for all those from her are clothed. The intellect would not go
forth nor come to be in the contemplation of the bodiless things without
having set right the things within; for the disturbance of its own people is
accustomed to turn it back toward the things from which it has gone out. But
having acquired impassibility it will linger in the contemplation and will not
take thought for those in the house; for anger has been clothed with meekness
and humility, and desire with temperance and self-control. 378 '31, 22 She
made double cloaks for her husband; and from linen and purple garments for
herself. The reasons of earth and sea are garments of a rational soul from
linen and purple. And someone else will say that the contemplation of the
things that have come to be and the contemplation of the holy Trinity is a
garment of a pure intellect from linen and purple. 379 '31, 24 She made linens
and sold them, and girdles for the Canaanites. The linen that appeared upon
the housetop to Peter was a symbol of the perceptible world; for the animals
contained in it made clear the various characters of men purified by the cross
of Christ. If then the one linen signified this world, perhaps the more linens
contain the contemplation concerning various worlds, which the pure soul,
having contemplated, also shared with others. And if the Canaanites are
interpreted humble, she well did not sell the linens to those humble in
understanding, but the linens to the pure, and to the Canaanites girdles,
which are symbols of practice tightening the passionate part of the soul. 380

well are the dwellings of the pure soul said to be tight. 381 '31, 27.2 And
she did not eat sluggish foods. They say that sluggish foods are the vices.
