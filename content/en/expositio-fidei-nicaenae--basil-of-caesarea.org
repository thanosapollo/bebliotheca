* Exposition of the Nicaean Faith
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Following the faith set forth we anathematize the faith of Sabellius that says
the same is Father and Son and Holy Spirit; for they err, as that the Father
himself is the Son, and as that the Son himself is the Father, likewise also the
Holy Spirit, so as to be one person, and three names; for these things happen to
be alien to the faith; for we know a Father as Father, a Son as Son, Holy Spirit
the Spirit itself holy, one kingdom, one essence, one Godhead. Still we
anathematize the faith of Photinus that says the Son is from Mary and here, and
that he is not before this, but is said in the scriptures by predestination, and
that from Mary he is only according to the Godhead; and these things we know as
alien to the faith. For the Son always having been begotten with the Father
existed and was with the Father, as it is written; for there was not sometime,
when there was not a Son, but always a Father having a Son; for it is impossible
for a Father being sometime without a Son later to be spoken a Father according
to a turning, but always Father, always Son, as we said before; not a
co-brother, but having been begotten he was with the Father, as has been written
before. And these things concerning the Father and the Son.
And concerning the Holy Spirit we believe thus: a divine spirit, a holy spirit,
a perfect spirit, a paraclete, uncreated, having spoken in law and prophets and
apostles, and having come down upon the Jordan. And concerning the incarnation
from a virgin of the Son we believe thus: that he took a perfect man from the
Theotokos Mary through the Holy Spirit, both body and soul, truly and not in
seeming; for thus he came to perfect the man whom he took; and that he suffered,
and that he was buried, and that he has been raised on the third day, and that
he was taken up into heaven and sat from the right of the Father, having
deified the man whom he took, coming to judge living and dead according to the
taking-on from God. And if somewhere the divine scriptures say him by
predestination as a creature, we grasp it to be filled according to flesh. And
still we anathematize those who do not confess a resurrection of flesh and all
heresies against this faith. And there was also anathematized in the things
written before the godless faith of the Arians in our believing and saying:
begotten, not made. for they say the Son of God a creature. And toward filling
all parts of the faith we confess and say: that we believe into one church, into
one baptism, into a resurrection of flesh, into a kingdom of heavens, into an
eternal judgment.
