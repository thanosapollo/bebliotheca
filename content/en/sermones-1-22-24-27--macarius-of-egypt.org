2.1
Question. Do then the bodies of Adam being raised stand naked over against the
Godhead, or do they have upon them "coverings" and are they fed with another
food, in the way that from here the body is covered in garments and is fed in
foods (for it is necessary that those who are in this age, both men and women,
be clothed and have the unseemliness covered and be fed with the food that
perishes)? If then still, having been raised together from the dissolution of
the earth and having returned to the ancient compacting, they have need of the
same things or not?
Answer. This appears to me unseemly and unreasonable; for we know that all the
"comeliness" and harmony of the creation is abolished being loosed, and no
longer does the earth bring forth fruits toward the nourishment of the body,
and the heaven also passes away with all its world. From where then will food
still be supplied to men and the coverings be prepared, when the things seen
are loosed according to the sentence of the Lord? Or is it clear that
something else beyond the things seen is what is going to be given? For they
must both be clothed and be fed. And if to the Israelites the manna which was
given in the wilderness was foreign and alien to their nature (for it is
written that "man ate bread of angels"), how much more in the age to come does
God give food and coverings to men foreign to their nature; as many therefore
as from here receive "the earnest of the Spirit in their hearts" and the
heavenly treasure, and as many as now wear glorious and heavenly garments, and
as many as have the earth of the heart sown with a heavenly and spiritual
seed, these without doubt will also enjoy the glory of the body. For the
beauty of God now hidden and sojourning in the soul, having come forth and
been poured out in the resurrection, will cover and glorify the outward body
with the eternal light. And it is necessary that the mind, being honorable, be
evangelized from here and receive the Spirit of God, and thus after these
things the body will be glorified together. For God, having from now clothed
the soul with the glory and having made it to be swallowed up by his fire, in
the expected time will also clothe the body and will show it "conformed to the
body of his glory," giving there henceforth rest of heavenly foods and
garments and an incorruptible work, that of the angels.
2.2 And in the things seen night has its own work and day has its own work.
And the works of the night are evil; for in it the evildoers display
lawlessness, for being helped by the darkness they plot and steal and
fornicate and commit adultery without fear, being convicted by no one. But the
works of the day are light-bearing, works of righteousness and piety, for
everything that comes to be in light "is light," having been worked in God.
Thus also those who work lawlessness, as in night, have now escaped the notice
of many, but afterward "all things being convicted by the light are made
manifest." For those who from now conceive seed of the evil one in their
hearts, the mind being joined to the shameful works of the evil one, hasten to
escape notice and to hide their own evil works, but they cannot escape the
notice of God; for "all things" are to him "naked and laid open," "to whom our
word is." For the darkness that has now covered the soul and the heart, on
that day will also darken and cover the body, for it is swallowed up by the
dark bosoms of the evil one, but the good works of the righteous will shine
forth "as the sun," for neither can they be hidden. And as the fruit-bearing
trees at the time of movement, having conceived the fruits within,


are no longer able to hide what is contained, the season by a certain drawing
power and force bringing forth the fruits for food, and as the body of the
grain being buried into earth first in the unseen in the belly of the earth
takes root and conceives and thus growing and coming forth toward the surface
of the earth is made manifest being fed and being raised into a stalk and
yields a full "ear" and grain, and in the way that the shoot being moved in
the vine first conceives within in the heart and thus brings forth into
manifestation what was conceived being fruit-bearing and yields the wine "in
season" in the same way also the Christians who from now have been sown and
from now have been planted in the heavenly vineyard, having fixed sweetest
roots into the earth of the heart, cannot hide the fruits, but what was
conceived within on the day of the resurrection will shine together also upon
the body, gladdening it and making it joyful too. Thus again also the works of
the sinners are made manifest. And as a chaste virgin having fallen into
fornication and having conceived and hastening to hide her foul incontinence
cannot escape notice, for the embryo having come to perfect compacting comes
forth convicting the transgression of the one who bore it, so also those who
in the heart conceive sin and have borne children of lawlessness cannot on
that day escape the fearful and unspeakable fire, but their souls and bodies
together are condemned.
2.3 This then is what is sought, that from now we be eager in this little time
to be counted worthy to receive and to conceive in our hearts the Holy Spirit
of God, that through him on that day our bodies also may be clothed with glory
and rest, for he restores them into a new and incorruptible age, creating for
them "a new heaven and a new earth" and new luminaries, and he gives them his
own image and all "the promise" "of the inheritance." And such are precious
stones and choice pearls standing in the presence of the Godhead; for the
righteous were called precious stones and eagles and doves. And the
master-builder Christ looses buildings of darkness of the evil reasonings,
concerning which it has been said: "casting down reasonings and every height
lifting itself up against the knowledge of God" (for these, entering as in a
wilderness, roar in our hearts), and having taken down he lays the foundation
and his own rock and rolling the remaining stones (I mean the reasonings of
nature) he brings them up to the top of the building of the heights of heaven,
joining them to the holy body of Christ and attaching them to the building of
the holy apostles. And the divine building is composed of two substances: he
first leads up our inner man into the building of his body and of angels and
of perfected holy spirits, and again he himself comes down from above toward
those on earth and nests, having pitched a tent for himself here and having
chosen a place for himself, he remains.
2.4 And as the eyes of sensible sight see the sensible sun, so it is necessary
with the eyes of the soul to see the noetic light of the sun of
"righteousness," and as the swallows having withdrawn at the time of winter,
when they cannot bear the roughness of the airs and the hardness of the
frosts, returning at the time of spring, when they find the air fair and calm,
the whole earth now being warmed, and having come they enter as into their own
houses into the houses of men and having leapt in without fear and having
built nests for themselves they produce young and all the hours they speak
with the voices of nature thus also the Lord having arrived into the


houses of our souls rests in the nest of our heart and there he dwells, the
waves and the wild winter and the darkness now passing away, and there his
rays shine, full of sweetest light, deep peace having entered into such a
house. In the time then of spring, the warmth of the sun now drawing near and
remaining, the earth blooms being moved together with all the fruits and
plants in it; for vines and all the trees of the wood and fields grow thick,
being shaded with flowering leaves and being adorned with bright flowers. And
further also the irrational animals bear fruit, bringing forth the embryos
from the womb, so that there are now also new bodies of lambs and of the other
animals and innocent and simple hearts of newly born children, and almost all
things that a little before were gloomy and contracted and unmoved and
unfruitful have now been changed, bearing fruit and being moved and being fed.
For both the earth compacted by the frost, having been loosed, is lit up with
the varied herbs of the grasses, and the desert mountains having been clothed
with the greens cast off the wildness, and all the springs having grown rich
with the addition of the water pour forth a sweet and clear stream, and every
kind of animals, of birds and of four-footed things, leaps and rejoices, being
changed together with the sweetest and well tempered season and being fed
abundantly they rejoice.
2.5 And all these things are types of the spiritual and noetic things, for
from the terrible mist and the bitter winds the mind is bound and fettered and
unfruitful, and all the reasonings having been enslaved are in much grief; for
many reasonings foreign to nature come up there and each evil reasoning is
from Satan. But in the "time" of the "visitation" of the Holy Spirit a certain
great change and alteration comes to be. For the mind begins to blossom and to
bear spiritual fruits from the earth of the heart, the thistles and the hedges
and the evil demons are torn apart by the power of the Holy Spirit. And the
heart itself begins to be moved and to conceive the heavenly seed and to be
farmed by the true farmer. Much calm is there, much peace, and blessed are
those who have been visited and farmed by the Holy Spirit. And as the doves
running upon the windows take the nest, so the righteous run upon the glory of
the body of Christ into the rest (for "where the carcass is, there the eagles
will be gathered together"). To him the glory.
3.1 An unarmed wood, a cross without iron, and a dead body conquered and put
to death the devil and his angels; for through his own panoply the "stronger"
one put to death the strong man at arms, having conquered. And now also he
comes toward every soul seeking him in truth and drawing near to the soul he
crushes and destroys the power of the darkness that guarded and fettered the
soul. And the soul ought to have so much longing and so much love toward the
bridegroom Christ who has been betrothed to her, as a chaste and
husband-loving wife, often seeing the husband in prison or in bonds or in
another punishment, because of the love toward him seems to be bound together
and to suffer together and is more tortured being tormented in the inward
parts than the one being held. And in the way that Mary standing by when the
Lord was being crucified wept, crying because of the goad of longing, and
seemed to be crucified together, so also a soul that has loved the Lord and
taken up a zeal of affection and hastening in truth to be joined to her own
bridegroom


Christ she ought to suffer together with his sufferings, always having before
her eyes and remembering his marks that came to be for her, how many things
the impassible one suffered for her, and how he who is above all punishment
was punished for her, and how being "in the form of God" he took "the form of
a slave," and thus in all things to suffer together and to be bound together
with him; for thus she will also be glorified together. And as then by the
power of God the stone was rolled away from the tomb and Mary saw the Lord, so
by power and visitation of the Holy Spirit the stone that lies upon the soul,
"the veil" of sin, is rolled away and is taken out of the midst and the soul
is counted worthy to see the face of Christ and to rest in his Spirit, having
been loosed and set free from the stone of sin that lies upon her. For every
soul that loves the Lord is afflicted by the evil demons warring against her
and not allowing her to come to the life-giving Christ. And this comes to be
by concession and permission of God; for he tests her, whether she truly loves
the master, whether she remains in the purpose even when the toils are
troubling, whether perhaps having grown sluggish she may deny him, declining
the labor of the way and fleeing the war "against the spiritual things of
wickedness," but having remained and having spent many years in the
temptations of evil, she is judged with boldness toward the Lord as having
been abandoned and counted worthy of no help.
3.2 And the Lord, having seen the courage of the soul and the endurance in the
temptations and that being tempted she was found approved, appears by his
kindness, manifesting himself and lighting her with his exceeding bright light
and calling her toward himself he says: "come" in peace "my neighbor," and she
running toward him accuses him and says: why, Lord, did you abandon me for so
long a time, suffering many things and being insulted by the enemies? For "the
watchmen" of the night who go around the city "found me" seeking you and they
troubled me. And the Lord, full of unspeakable light, answers, persuading and
comforting and saying toward her: you speak well, "come" in peace "my
neighbor, my beautiful one, my dove." And he is judged with her, showing her
the marks of the nails and saying: see the marks of the nails, see the
scourges, see the spittings, see the wounds. All these things I have suffered
for you who were wounded with many wounds and being dragged by many enemies in
much slavery; and I by my love of man came upon your seeking and upon your
freedom, since from the beginning I made you my image and I created you into
my bride, and for you I the impassible one suffered, and I the uninsulted bore
many insults for your redemption. And you, for yourself, who have so many
evils and have been sunk into so great a darkness, ought you not to suffer and
be afflicted? And being judged peaceably and conversing with the soul he shows
that even her remaining under affliction he himself gave her and he himself
was strengthening in the temptations and was secretly encouraging.
3.3 And the soul, having heard these things, knows that she had nothing of her
own, but all things of the Lord the good and beautiful bridegroom. And she,
having condemned from the whole heart the purpose and the love and the will
which he gave her, answers and says: see Lord, see a chaste body, see a pure
soul, receive me wholly being covered under your right hand and resting in
your bosoms. The Lord shows himself to her in two faces, both in his marks and
in the glory of his light, and the soul contemplates the passions which he
suffered for her, and she contemplates also the exceeding bright glory of his
divine light, being transformed "the same image" "from glory to glory,


even as from the Lord, the Spirit" and advancing in both faces, both in that
of the passion and in that of the glorious light, she takes in a certain way a
forgetfulness of nature, having been lit through by God and mixed together and
mingled with the heavenly man and with the Holy Spirit, herself also having
become spirit. For as some beggar and one exceedingly poor and going from door
to door for the sake of "the daily food," having suddenly from the unexpected
become king forgets the poverty because of the happiness in his hands, so the
soul having grown rich with heavenly wealth no longer remembers the first
poverty. For if Christ, being by nature "in the form of God," in a certain way
forgot the dignity, "having taken the form of a slave and having become in the
likeness of men," how much more the soul, having taken on substance and power
and nature of God, forgets the former shamefulness.
3.4 Let us therefore beseech the Lord and let us expect him in love to appear
and to ransom us from now from the darkness, that thus in the resurrection the
body of weakness also may shine from the light that has been inlaid and has
shone in the soul, itself also being glorified together with the soul. "The
Lord is near" us, only let us seek him in a true heart. Everyone therefore who
hears the words let him hope to receive the Word who is in hypostasis and to
learn from him all righteousness. And see, O you who hope to inherit God and
for the soul to be mixed together with the Spirit of the Lord, what manner of
way of life and dignity of life you must take up and in what way of life you
must live and turn about; for all these things you ought to do and to display
from yourself as much as is in your power. Amen.
4.1 There is an incomplete world and there is a complete world. The incomplete
and passing one has many kinds similar to the complete and eternal. There is
here a palace and a king clothed in purple and a crown composed of precious
stones and his ministers and his defenders, some spending time in the palace
itself, and some are counts and others of another dignity, themselves also
clothed in a bright and honorable garment. There is in heaven a palace and the
king Christ clothed in the royal purple. Palace-men and counts and dignitaries
serve as soldiers there, themselves also clothed in the equal glory and
wearing crowns of precious stones (for you have set "upon his head a crown of
precious stones"). There are in this earth warriors and generals who have gone
through many contests and have conquered many wars and have raised countless
trophies against the enemies. There are found here also many courageous ones
and warriors who have conquered the devil and his angels and have come into
advancements. There are in the apparent palace of the Romans honorable men,
freed from all tumult and contest, being compelled by no one to carry weapons
or to enter into battle-array, the so-called royal eunuchs, being honored by
every man and being loved by the king himself, carrying and having been
entrusted with the purple, and some the diadem, and others some other
honorable and royal thing, and there are also in the heavens men honored by
the heavenly king, the true eunuchs who have been made eunuchs and have been
cleansed from the war of sin; to these have been entrusted blessed fatherlands
having peace and rest, and they have been set over the royal purple and the
heavenly treasures; these have been altogether freed from the war, living
henceforth in freedom from care. There are there craftsmen and wise men, and
again here there are wise men who have been made wise with the spiritual
wisdom by the


all-wise Christ, who have light-bearing garments and spiritual wealth that
never passes away. And for this reason the work of the Christians is more
honorable than every device and every fleshly occupation. For they work a
divine work which the world does not know.
4.2 Mind differs from mind and man is more honorable than man. And all the
things seen appear to me opposed and foreign to the noetic things. For there
is a mind journeying and running as far as heaven and walking through the way
of its pure reasonings and through it arriving at the paths and the ways
prepared for the saints in the heavenly places. And there is another mind
creeping upon the earth and rolling in the ways of the flesh. There is a
fleshly mind and there is a spiritual mind. And the spiritual mind differs
much from the fleshly. For as the winged animals, being lifted by the light
wing, pass through the air in the midst, being helped by the wings and being
lightened by the air itself (for being winged they take on as it were a
certain wind that underbears and cooperates with the flight), but the wingless
and those lying on the ground, even if they are present with eagerness to be
lifted into air, have no strength thus the mind that has been purified and has
taken on spiritual wings, using a sharpest impulse, goes up into heaven, being
taken up into the air of the Godhead, but the beastlike and wild and material
mind, rolling into earth, creeps pursuing the deserts, not being able to
overtake or to know the mind spoken of before. There are kinds of trees which
neither in summer nor in winter shed leaves, but are always clothed and
adorned with the flowers, as olive and cypress and the like, and again there
are others that are stripped and that cast off the leaves in winter. The
example is like the Christians; for they were named trees by the scripture and
they never cast off the adornment and the comeliness of the leaves, neither
when wild winds are being moved, nor when summer droughts have come upon them,
but they are always clothed with the glory of the Spirit. This is what was
said: "which will give its fruit in its season, and its leaf will not fall
away" and there are others having a lighter mind, who because of the
surrounding weakness, when temptations and winters and winds come upon them,
do not stand against them nor endure.
4.3 Those then who are spiritual according to the mind are enemies of the
fleshly. For in the way that those that live and are fed in waters do not bear
the life of the dry land but are destroyed when separated from the waters, and
again those that feed on the earth flee the sojourn in the waters, hating the
life in the deep, so also the Christians, being occupied and being dragged
into the things of this age, are constrained and in a certain way seem to be
choked. Thus the fleshly, having withdrawn from the material things and being
compelled to attend to the spiritual, grow dizzy, being constrained and being
choked. Wherefore very rare are the courageous ones and those who "until the
end" in endurance complete their work. And these have need of much
shamelessness and sharpness toward the asking of God "in the present time."
For as the sharp-winged of the birds, using the light wing, come to be above
the snares, mocking the plot of those who hunt, so also the one acute and
sharp in reasonings flees the plots and snares of the devil. For many spirits
constrain the soul and all the things that creep upon the earth draw toward
themselves and occupy and make the mind wander. Wherefore the Christian ought
to be as an athlete and a contestant. And as a man foreign and inexperienced
in the things according to the city, having sojourned in a certain city that
is crowded and flourishing in multitude, and not trusting


in his own inexperience and weakness, having run, lays hold of a certain
experienced patron able to cast out the force of the crowds this one, as long
as he runs at the feet of the one going before without being lifted away,
completes a straight way, but being lifted away and being carried about
through sluggishness by the peoples in the city is made distant and stands
off, being deprived of the help. But if he will be eager and will despise
toils, having girded "the loin" in strength, he breaks through the force of
the crowds and overtakes the patron and of the

were named a city of God and they have now in the marketplaces a crowd of
spirits and multitudes of reasonings in the broad places of the mind and of
the heart, and they look ahead to their own master and guardian Jesus Christ

5.1 Those who have withdrawn from the world and have genuinely heard the word
of God and live in a reverent way, being still under the veil of the passions,
which all acquired through the disobedience of Adam (that is the fleshly mind
of the evil reasonings, which the apostle calls death saying: "the mind of the
flesh is death"), these then are like men walking in night; for being lit by
the stars (that is the holy scriptural commandments) they are nevertheless in
night, by the invisible operation of the passions, and they stumble and cannot
see all things accurately because of the operation of the darkness of the
passions. Wherefore it is necessary that they, living in virtues with toil and
much faith, ask much of the heavenly master, that when the day of the sun of
"righteousness" has shone through in their hearts they may be able to see all
things accurately, both the varied and differing harm of the noetic beasts and
the unspeakable variety of the good things of the enjoyments in the
incorruptible world and the extraordinary beauties, which the spiritual and
perfect men, in whom the noetic light has shone through operatively in the
hearts, know in true discernment, both the virtues of the better things and
the terribleness of the worse things according to the differing operation of
each, as the blessed Paul says: "solid food is of the perfect, of those who
because of habit have the senses exercised toward discernment of good and of
evil," and the blessed Peter also says: "and we have the prophetic word, to
which you do well giving heed as to a lamp appearing in a dry place, until the
day shine through and the light-bearer rise in your hearts," and elsewhere:
"and to you who fear" the Lord "the sun of righteousness will rise, and
healing in their wings." But the many who have been mixed into the world are
like men walking in a gloomy night filled with clouds and mist, in which not
even a little gleam of light of a star shines (that is of a divine word
lighting through the soul), so that they are like almost to the blind. Thus
those who have been mixed wholly in the material entanglements and who neither
work commandments or any other good works in the fear of God but have been
wholly deceived by the vanity of the world in the hard works. But as many as
are of the life of the world who are lit by commandments of God as by stars,
turning about in faith and fear of God, are not wholly in a dark and gloomy
night; wherefore they can also have a hope of salvation.


5.2 For as from different crafts and from different occupations men in this
world gather the wealth, and likewise also from different ranks in the ruling
armies they procure the gain of the gold, and from different possessions or
animals or certain other things the gold is gathered to the master of the
house, so from different gifts (as it has been said: "and having gifts
differing according to the grace that has been given to us") and from
different disciplines and justifications and virtues coming to be through God
each procures the heavenly gold (that is the heavenly wealth of the Spirit of
the eternal life), according to the good occupation and eagerness and faith.
For this reason each must not judge or condemn or set at nought the neighbor,
only let each run through God faithfully and contend and procure the spiritual
gain.
5.3 Except those who dig gold appear, those seeking out the very veins of the
gold, that is those who with a highest aim of arriving at "the perfection" run
without pause and without yielding. For those who through longsuffering and
endurance work the crafts, or those who labor in the earth according to hope,
grow rich by parts, who also become glorious in the world, but the hired
Helots who are sluggish, who immediately eat what falls in, not laboring with
longsuffering, always remain naked and poor. Thus as many as in hope labor the
works of virtue with longsuffering and endurance eagerly and faithfully await
the heavenly hope and having the help of the grace before their eyes, these
truly gather the wealth of the Spirit through their endurance and
longsuffering and toil and petition, wherefore they are also found glorious in
that age before God, but the impatient, who are ready only to receive the
grace, but do not endure to toil and to labor and to please God in all things
in all longsuffering, are stripped even of the grace of which they were
counted worthy. For always the weak and sluggish choice, being out of accord
with the grace, being found naked of good works and poor of virtues, is shown
unapproved and inglorious before God in that age.
6.1 Those who go out from the world and love poverty and become foreign to
fleshly communion and to all the things that appear in the world, despising
glory and the things that seem in life to be great and standing in poverty and
attending to the Lord, consider well and have set up a good and virtuous aim.
These then attend to entering into the glorious city of the saints, into a
heavenly fatherland, into buildings and houses not made with hands, and since
their mind attends there, as that they have already entered and have been
glorified and are heirs of it, thus they are in themselves and these things as
they are living in the flesh because of their good purpose and the aim, for
they await, as they are in this age, to enter into "a city, of which God is
craftsman and maker." For the buildings of this age are rotten and do not have
a solid foundation. For "in the consummation of the age" all its works and the
buildings are destroyed; for "the heaven will be rolled up as a book" "and the
earth will pass away." Where then are the glorious and great things of life,
when the time that looses them stands upon them? Gather for me the wealth, the
gold, the silver, the possessions and all the beauty and confidence in the
wealth and begin to build a city through them, the wisdom, the glory, the
dignities, the powers, build these


as a wall of the city and all the adornment of this age and when you have
finished the city, the city does not have a seating nor solidity. For see, the
foundations of the wall are rotten and they fall, and all the things in the
city are destroyed and vanish. Thus is all the variety of this age, being
destroyed and vanishing.
6.2
And you do well building buildings undissolved and never being destroyed; for
you are eager to set your foundation upon the rock and the foundation being
built upon the rock never falls; for the scripture says: "the kingdom of the
heavens is like" "a prudent man" "who dug and deepened and set a foundation
upon the rock; and a flood having come to be" and the winds having struck "it
did not fall; for it had been founded upon the rock." And what is this
building not made with hands? Faithful souls that love the Lord; for they are
built by the wise master-builder Jesus Christ "upon the rock," upon the Lord
himself, upon his life-giving word, upon the divine power, upon the Holy
Spirit. And who are the stones? The pure and good reasonings of the nature of
Adam; for these are like the heavenly stone and according to his measure they
are thus built upon. What does the scripture say? Until we arrive "at a
perfect man, at the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ." But
there is also another building, that "upon the sand," sinful souls, a worldly
mind, those being carried down into the passions of the flesh. These are built
into the evil reasoning, upon the spirit of the devil. And who are the stones?
The evil reasonings that love the pleasures of sin. Such a building then, that
"upon the sand," is corrupted and loosed.
6.3 Those then who in the present time knock "the door" and reckon the things
to come as present are much more intelligent. For reckoning the corruption and
dissolution of this age, they despise fleshly communion, knowing that after
these things there will be a dissolution of the bodies, again reckoning that
the appearing wealth is subject to corruption and nakedness, they despise all
the things seen, and remembering also the condemnations that come to be for
the sinners and the differing punishments brought upon them they stand in care
and fear and they make their souls safe, lest they fall into Gehenna and
torments. And reckoning again also the city of the saints and the great ones,

gladness. And the camp of the angels that is with them also bears witness to
these, when they were contending and when they were remaining in the contest

6.4 Standing then in the poverty, through this poverty draw to yourselves the
heavenly wealth. For also exercising your bodies in the lying on the ground,
in the kneeling, in the prayer you will be more beautiful than those who live
in purple. For those living in the purple do not minister to the Lord, but you
in the hard training and the apparent grass come to worship and to glorify
God. But being in this poverty and the nakedness do not become unwilling,
having as a pattern and aim the Lord who thus journeyed the way. When your
body toils and you grow weary, remember the body of the Lord, how it was
struck by Pilate and grew weary with journeyings. When a house fails you,
remember that the Lord of the creatures having come upon the earth said this:
"the foxes have dens and the birds of the heaven nests, but the Son of man has
not where he may lay the head" and rest. When you walk, remember that the feet
of the Lord made upon the earth, except only because of the they were dusty,

with tears, remember that the Lord was weeping your fall, praying toward the
Father "with a strong cry and" many "tears," that you might be delivered from
the death. When men mock you, give heed to his slaps and to his spittings, and
endure in your humiliation. And likewise also your lying on the ground is not
harder than the thorny crown that was set upon his head. And thus all the
angels and the camps of the saints rejoice over the bridegroom and the bride
with trumpets and harps. As at an apparent marriage of a rich man, in which
the whole city runs together and peoples, and they themselves are mediators
hastening together with the bridegroom and the bride, so also the whole
heavenly church runs together, and a soul is the one sharing with the heavenly
bridegroom. And as in the time of summer each expects to harvest that which he
sowed before the time and to receive back the fruits from the earth, so also

7.1 As the animal that proceeds into food for men must first be sacrificed and

and more difficult dungs of the so-called second belly hidden under in the
little wings of the second belly be washed accurately through the water, and
then the meats being given over to the fire and being boiled little by little
are sweetened, and thus it comes to be for food and enjoyment to the master of
the house, in the same way also the Christian withdrawing from the world and
as being put to death from the former worst life and as a hide with hairs and
blood having stripped off the world, but nevertheless still possesses within
the foul dung of the evil reasonings, the memories of the evil works, both the
obvious and cheap and other thinner and hard-to-find passions, those also hard
to be grasped. It is necessary then that the perfect Christian desiring to
become put himself to death from the evil works of the world, which he
practiced before, and as a hide with the hairs strip off this age from without
through the perfect withdrawal and complete renunciation and then thus through
the cutting open (that is the visitation of the divine grace) wash out from
within the dung of the evil reasonings in the heart in a double way the
thinner and hard-to-find passions, and then to the heavenly manner the obvious

rawness of choice and having received every perfect change, become useful and
sweet in the heavenly table for the enjoyment of the heavenly king and an heir
of the kingdom, as it has been said in the gospel by the Lord: "my food is,
that I may do the will of him who sent me and finish his work."
7.2 But as formerly in the law the sheep was inspected for blemish, and then
proceeded into sacrifice and through the fatness of the animal and the savor
being spread through the fire a whole burnt offering was offered to God "for a
smell of fragrance," so also here now a soul that wishes to offer herself and
her body "a living holy sacrifice well-pleasing to God" is inspected for
blemish by the heavenly high priest Christ, and if she has the fatness of the
Spirit, which she acquired through the good laws of the teachings of the grace
and of the virtues, truly a whole fruit-offering and a living sacrifice in the
spiritual fire through the true high priest Christ is brought to the heavenly
Father into the living kingdom of the heavens, having been counted worthy to
become an heir of the eternal good things. It is necessary then that each in
all things always examine himself and test, whether he lives according to the
word of the truth and follows "the traces" of the Lord (or is tested being
examined by the spiritual men), and always to seek out those who proclaim the
word of the truth and hold Christianity "in work and truth." For as when many
cups are given to men to drink, those mixed with sweetest wine are manifest
and those filled with water only (for those mixed with wine furnish taste and
strength and gladness to the one drinking), so of the many in the world
offering drink to men through varied words and wisdoms those are the


ones gladdening souls with a heavenly gladness and leading into a good change,
those speaking by Holy Spirit and seasoned with grace, those uttering with a
living voice of heart and not watering the many who are being watered out with
noises and boasts of lips, those furnishing power and heavenly gladness to the
hearing soul through the grace speaking in them and changing men into the like
holiness of the grace. So that those must be searched out and sought, as we
said before, who utter with a living heart in Spirit, and to persist with
those from whom they can receive the benefit that is truly according to God,
those truly wise in the Lord and not in the world, concerning whom it has been
said: "if you see" a wise man, "rise early toward him and let your foot wear
out the steps of his doors"; for the obedient can through remaining toward
them in time be made like them.
7.3 For as in this world there are many who possess much gold within and are
not manifest to all, but as toward the many they are moderate, boasting
nothing themselves either in possessions or in houses or in differing vessels
or servants or animals and thus with all safety they have their treasure
hidden, and there are many others who having not possessed gold at all,
through certain many vessels and painting of houses make themselves to be
suspected, as that they possess much gold, deceiving the many with the
appearing boast, and are thought rich being poor and in want thus also among
the Christians there are those who in power possess in themselves the heavenly
treasure of the Spirit and are not manifest to all, but they hide themselves
as being moderate and not wishing what they are to be known, and there are
others who through certain few appearing virtues, fasts or vigils or quiet or
a word of "wisdom" or through certain such things, wish to be thought as
spiritual, not having the operation and power of the Spirit. For as according
to the example, if the one who has his much wealth hidden should wish to bring
forth a part of the gold, with a little money he buys all the abundance of the
one displaying himself as rich and wishing to be thought what he is not; but
if he uncover his wealth and set himself manifest to all, many being amazed
are raised into much envy against him, how the one thought as poor to all
suddenly possesses so much wealth, and they begin to cast this one into
liturgies and many losses, but also the thieves and the robbers night and day
watch this one to destroy him and to take away his wealth, and from every side
he falls into dangers and losses and unexpected afflictions, since he has not
yet received a dignity or a certain authority from the king. Wherefore the
prudent man who has much gold hidden but has not received a dignity hides with
all safety his own wealth, lest having been made known to the many and having
fallen under envy or plotting men he lose with his wealth also the life. But
if he receive a dignity from a royal hand of a certain rule or authority, such
a one, even if he uncover the wealth of his treasure, does not fear, but
rather is also glorified in it, buying together great possessions and founding
glorious houses, and acquiring a multitude of servants and myriads of
differing animals through the treasure, and he is without fear from a robber
assault, having received from the king the authority of the sword against the
plotting and evil-doing men, and thus through the dignity he rather gathers
wealth upon wealth and adds gold to gold through all possessions, until he
become wholly rich, almost no longer even knowing how many things he
possesses.

7.5 And the whole, the understanding of such a soul being occupied concerning
the divine and better things, is found burning with a spiritual love and a
better desire upon the more glorious and more light-bearing beauties of the
Spirit, "wounded" with unsated longing concerning the heavenly bridegroom and,
that I may so speak, living luxuriously henceforth of the lesser things
concerning the better and greater she has the desire of the things that can no
longer either be said by word or be lit in a human thought. And thus it is, as
a king's son or daughter living luxuriously casts off the honorable things of
the parents and seeks the more honorable, suppose instead of silver dishes she
seeks gold ones, instead of bright and honorable all-silk garments she chooses
those set with stones and woven with gold, instead of old and good wine she
desires wine aged of old and more honorable, she cheapens the honorable things
themselves by the desire of the better and as it were makes them common,

been counted worthy to obtain the royal and ruling Spirit and have received in
themselves the unchangeable power of love and have been wounded with perfect
love toward the heavenly bridegroom are no longer occupied concerning the
passions of the evil. For with much toil and strain and time and a perfect
contest through faith they have been ransomed from these by the Spirit and
they have been snatched always into heavenly


mysteries of the Spirit and they have been occupied into the differing
beauties of the Godhead, seeking the better and greater in much desire. For
the Godhead of the Spirit has differing and much-varied beauties unspeakable
and unthought, which are displayed for delight and joy and life and rest to
the worthy souls so that the holy soul, being wounded always with a more
vehement and more fiery love toward the heavenly bridegroom, no longer look
around at the things upon earth, but be occupied wholly from the whole with
the longing toward him.

and be fully assured as having already overtaken something, but looking toward

through faith to obtain the promised perfect love of the Spirit, which "casts
out the fear," as John has said, and it is necessary always to hunger and
thirst "the righteousness" and as beginning always thus to be disposed in
herself because of "the exceeding" "wealth" of the Spirit promised to those
who believe, that the devout soul having thus concerning the much desire of
the better things both not lose the good things which she possesses, but also
take on those which she does not yet have. For the Christian ought not as a
hired man or "slave" to watch the time, shrinking back at the labor, but as
"son and heir" having served the Father in all goodwill and eagerness until
the end, he will thus be heir of the paternal belongings. For all in this age
seem to enjoy life from king as far as beggar and each is fully assured that
he lives, but if you compare the life and the luxury of the rich toward that
of the poor, you will find much difference. For according to the enjoyment of
the luxury of the differing foods and of differing wine and of differing
garments and of gold and of silver and of the remaining possessions, comparing
the poor man to the rich you will find this one dead toward that one; for the
rich man only handles gold with the hands and counts coins but the poor man
even if he find a bronze nummus in dung is not ashamed to stoop and take it
because of the much want. If then you compare the life of the poor toward the
rich, you will find it as not being beside being, and as a dead man beside a
living. Thus also into the spiritual. All think themselves to be Christians
because of the confession of the faith in Christ or also because of certain
few virtues, but the true Christians are few, those rich in Holy Spirit, those
enjoying the differing luxuries of the grace, those being gladdened with the
heavenly longing of the Spirit, those having the soul adorned with the
differing heavenly garments of the gifts, those who have possessed
Christianity not by confession and bare faith but by power and operation of
Spirit, those who handle without ceasing with the mind the heavenly gold (that
is the knowledge and narration of the mysteries of the Spirit) with the hands
of the soul, these are the true Christians. For if you compare the conduct of
their mind and life of the soul toward that of those, you will find much
difference as of living toward dead. For those being in the terrible poverty
of the worldly spirit and having been deprived of the power of the heavenly
wealth and having the mind confused by the passions, scarcely even finding one
good reasoning from many evils in themselves they rest, being administered by
the poverty and with simple garments of small virtues they are fully assured,
not having the differing operations of the Spirit in the divine virtues, as
also the Lord made manifest the much difference of the true worshipers who
were going to be from the many who seem to worship


with a superficial and outer worship, not worshiping "in spirit" and "truth,"
saying: "an hour comes and now is, when the true worshipers will worship the
Father in spirit and truth; for the Father also seeks such who worship him.

distinguishing the heavenly palace-men and those rich in spirit from those in
terrible poverty of sin, who because of the confession of the faith in Christ
seem to live, not however having possessed the rich life of the operation in
power of the Spirit.
7.7 For as all have the image of the body, but some possess it healthy and
unharmed, and others sick or even wounded. But also in the very passions of
the body there is a certain great difference; for some having wounds openly
are in pain, and others not having obvious wounds have a terrible chill in the
body so that they cannot even be moved and according to what is seen the body
seems as healthy, but according to the pains and the movement of the working
there is a much worse and hard-to-heal passion of the one obviously struck.
That one having the wounds manifest, if he come toward the physician, is more
quickly counted worthy of healing than that one. And all the hidden passions
of the body happen to be worse than the obvious and hard to heal. Thus also
the Christians. Many are thought to be healthy (that is spiritual) because of
certain superficial and bodily virtues, but within they have been held by
terrible hidden passions; and others being held by obvious sins and being
before the many as unapproved and rejected (as "the tax-collector" to the
Pharisee), if they turn toward the true physician Christ, drawing near to him
they receive the healing more quickly than those spoken of before (in the way

"justified"); for they are not thus "according to the inner man" as those
struck with hidden passions, such as either conceit or lifting up or puffing
or unbelief or pride or seeming or hypocrisy or vainglory or cowardice or
shameful reasonings and the like. "Man," he says, "will look into a face, but
God will look into a heart." Except what is sought is, that both from without
and from within the man be found healthy through the divine grace and his own
self-determining contest, and then he is counted worthy to become an heir of
the kingdom in Christ Jesus, to whom the glory and the might unto the ages of
the ages. Amen.

ransomed from every temptation and affliction through him. But if she is not
made manly and does not bear nobly enduring every affliction but is grieved
and is in accidie and is burdened and is constrained and faints of the contest
and despairs as no longer being ransomed (which this also is an art of the
evil itself, casting the soul into accidie and faintness) and if she does not
become holding the hope, always awaiting the mercy of the Lord in undoubting
faith, such a soul does not become a child of the life, since she has not
become a follower of all the saints nor has she walked in the traces of the
Lord. For consider and see how from the beginning the fathers patriarchs
prophets apostles and martyrs went through the narrow way of the afflictions
and temptations and thus they were able to please God having endured every
affliction and temptation nobly and in the constraints and ill-treatments they
were pleased because of the expected hope of the recompense, as the scripture
says: "child, if you come to the Lord to serve, prepare your soul for
temptation; deepen your heart and endure," and again the apostle: "but if you
are without discipline, of which all have become partakers, then you are
bastards and not sons," and elsewhere: "all the things brought upon you
receive as good things, knowing that apart from God nothing comes to be." And
the Lord says: "blessed are you when they reproach you and persecute and say
every evil word against you lying because of me. Rejoice and be glad, because
your reward is much in the heavens," and the: "blessed are those


who have been persecuted because of righteousness, because theirs is the
kingdom of the heavens," and persecuted either manifestly by men or hiddenly
by the spirits "of wickedness." For they contend against the soul that seeks
God and they cast her into differing afflictions toward hindering her from
entering into the eternal life, and at the same time also that having been
tested she may be made manifest, whether according to truth she loves God in
enduring every affliction and whether she will hold the hope to the end
awaiting the ransom in undoubting faith or again not having endured but having
shrunk back into the afflictions and being in accidie or fainting and being
lessened of the hope, that she may be shown not loving God in truth. For the
differing afflictions and the temptations show the unworthy and worthy souls,
those having faith and hope and endurance and those not having, that in all
things having been shown approved and faithful the worthy souls that have
endured until the end and have held the hope firmly and thus having received
the redemption through the grace may justly become heirs of the kingdom. The
hope then and the endurance before all things let each soul wishing to please
God hold nobly and thus she will be able to go through every uprising and
affliction of the evil one.
9.2 For God does not concede a soul hoping upon him and enduring him to be
tempted so much, so as even to be at a loss and to be given over into
temptations and afflictions which she cannot bear, as the apostle says: "God
is faithful, who will not allow you to be tempted beyond what you can, but
will make with the temptation also the way out of being able to bear." For
neither does the evil one tempt and afflict the soul as much as he wishes, but
as much as is conceded to him by God, only let the soul hold the hope nobly
and in faith await the help and assistance from him. And it is impossible for
her to be abandoned, but as much as she contends through faith and hope
fleeing for refuge to God and awaiting the help and ransom from him without
doubt, so much more quickly the Lord ransoms her from every surrounding
affliction. For he himself knows how much the soul ought to enter into testing
and discipline and temptations and so much he concedes, only let her become
holding of the endurance and hope unto the end and she will not be put to
shame, as it has been said: "the affliction works endurance, and the endurance
approval, and the approval hope; and the hope does not put to shame," and
again: "as ministers of God, in much endurance, in afflictions, in
necessities, in constraints" and the rest. And the Lord says: "but he who has
endured unto the end, this one will be saved" and: "in your endurance possess
your souls" and elsewhere he says: "who has believed God and been put to
shame? or who has remained in his fear and been abandoned? or who has called
upon him and he overlooked him?" For if men having a share of little
understanding and mind know to test and to discern how much weight and load
each of the animals can bear, such as a mule or an ass or a camel, and they
load on so much according to the power of the animal; and the potter who has
formed the vessels, unless he cast them in the furnace, that having been fired
they be made solid, they do not become suitable for use to men, and he knows
how much it is necessary to put them in the fire, until they become useful,
and neither beyond what is due does he leave them in the furnace, lest having
been burst they be destroyed, nor again deficiently, lest they be unfinished
and useless if then in the things that appear men possess so much knowledge
and discernment, how much more God, being an incomprehensible and unspeakable
certain knowledge and understanding and being wholly wisdom, knows of how many
testings and afflictions and temptations the souls that wish to please him and
that long to obtain the eternal life have need. And


thus enduring nobly and faithfully in hope every affliction until the end then
they are set down approved and suitable for the kingdom of the heavens. For as
the form of the hemp is not useful for finest threads to come to be from it,
unless it is beaten for long, and as much as it is tormented, it is heckled
and so much it becomes cleaner and more suitable, so also the God-loving soul
entering into many testings and temptations and afflictions and enduring nobly
is set down cleaner and more useful for the spiritual working of fineness and
finally is counted worthy to inherit the heavenly place of the kingdom. For as
the newly formed vessel not having been cast in fire is unsuitable for use to
men, or as an infant being a babe happens to be unsuitable toward the works of
the world, for it can neither build cities nor plant nor set down seed nor
complete any other work of the world (for "it is" an infant), so souls even if
they have become partakers of divine grace being borne about in the sweetness
and rest of the Spirit because of their infancy by the kindness of the Lord;
but not yet having been tested and tempted in differing afflictions by the
evil spirits, through which the love toward God is shown, they still happen to
be infants and (that I may so speak) they are not yet useful to the kingdom,
being still unexercised as it has been said: "but if you are without
discipline, of which all have become partakers, then you are bastards and not
sons." So that the afflictions and the temptations are for benefit to the man
and they work the soul approved and firm, if he endure nobly and eagerly in
confidence and hope of God the things brought upon him, awaiting in undoubting
faith the ransom from the Lord and his mercy. And it is impossible for her to
miss the promise "of the Spirit" and to be counted worthy of the redemption of
the passions of the evil, if as approved and faithful she should hold the hope
toward the Lord until the end in much endurance.
9.3
For as the holy martyrs in the appearing endured many torments and having come
"until death" because of the hope toward the Lord they remained in the good
confession and thus having been shown approved they were counted worthy to
obtain the crown "of righteousness," and those who bore more and harder
torments received and acquired more glory and boldness before God. But as many
as shrank back fearing the afflictions and the scourges, not having remained
in the good confession unto the end, are shown without boldness and put to
shame both here and in a day of judgment in the same way also the souls that
are given over into afflictions, that they may be tested, and being tormented
invisibly and in differing ways by the spirits of wickedness, whether hiddenly
from within with pains of afflictions or evil reasonings or also manifestly
through bodily passions, but enduring nobly and holding the hope and awaiting
in faith the recompense of the Lord are counted worthy of the same crown "of
righteousness" and receiving ransom from here they find the same boldness of
the martyrs in a day of judgment before God. For they endured the same
martyrdom of the afflictions, which those also through men, these through the
very spirits of wickedness that also operated in those, and as much as they
endure more afflictions and uprisings of the evil one and hold the hope, so
much more glory before God they procure and from here they are ransomed
according to the expectation of their hope, being counted worthy of the
consolation of the Spirit and from there becoming heirs of the eternal good
things of the kingdom. But as many souls as shrink back with cowardice and
fear not enduring the afflictions, but come into faintness and impatience and
despair and


are turned away from the way "of righteousness" and do not await the mercy of
the Lord unto the end, such souls having been found as unapproved will not be
able to obtain the eternal kingdom. For until death every soul owes through
the Lord who died for us to endure in longsuffering and to hold the hope
toward him and thus to be counted worthy of the eternal salvation; for as many
as wish perfectly to be drawn out of the coming Gehenna that punishes the
sinners and to obtain the heavenly kingdom, they must suffer from here the
afflictions of Gehenna through the temptations brought upon them by the evil
one, and if they endure until the end awaiting the mercy of the Lord through
the hope in faith, they are both ransomed from the temptations and the
afflictions through the grace and they are counted worthy of the communion "of
the Holy Spirit" and from here they are set free from the eternal Gehenna and
they inherit the eternal kingdom of Christ. For the Lord set such a way, the
one carrying away into the life, narrow and afflicted as it is written, for
this reason "also few are" those who journey into it. Such a hope then being
set forth and such promises having been promised by the unlying God let us
endure nobly every uprising and affliction of the evil one because of the hope
laid up for us in the heavens. For however many afflictions we endure through
the Lord, what will come to be of equal worth either toward the coming
promised life or toward the consolation "of the Holy Spirit" that comes to be
from here for the enduring souls or toward the redemption of the darkness of
the passions of the evil or also toward the debts of the multitude of our
sins? As it has been said: "and being judged by the Lord we are disciplined,
that we may not be condemned with the world," and again: for "the sufferings
of the present time are not worthy toward the coming glory to be revealed unto
us."
9.4 Let us become then as noble soldiers dying readily for our king. For why
in the world, when we were turning about in things of life, were we not
suffering these things nor did we have such afflictions, but now, when we have
come to the Lord to please him, so many uprisings and afflictions of the evil
one are raised against us? You see that we suffer these things through the
Lord, the enemy envying us and trying to twist us from the way of the life or
to lead us into slackness or into faintness, that having pleased we may not be
saved. As much then as the evil one is raised against us, if we are found in
endurance and courage being ready coming "until death" to endure because of
the hope toward the Lord, all his devices against us are loosed. For we have
our defender and champion Jesus, who also to us being afflicted and hoping
upon him gives endurance, and that one is put to shame when we receive the
victory-prizes of the toils (that is the kingdom of the Lord). Let us become
then as anvils being struck and not yielding nor receiving in ourselves marks
of slackness or faintness or accidie through the scourges of the temptations,
being beaten let us conquer the adversary through the endurance. For also our
Lord thus journeyed this age being scourged being reproached being persecuted
being spat upon, and afterward he was also punished with a dishonorable death
of a cross by lawless ones. And all those things he bore for our salvation
"leaving us a pattern" of life, that through the way of temptations and
afflictions and death which he himself journeyed, by the same way those who
believe in him in truth and who wish to become "fellow-heirs" of him may
journey, that as he himself suffered many things for us, and afterward having
died upon the cross he conquered and having been crucified he crucified and
having died he put to death having condemned "the sin" "through the


flesh" and having taken down the opposing powers, as it has been said: "having
stripped the rulers and the authorities" upon the cross "he made a show of
them having triumphed over them in it," so also we, if we endure every
uprising and affliction of the evil one nobly and eagerly and having come
"until death," then we will conquer the adversary through the faith and the
endurance and the hope toward the Lord, and thus having been found approved we
are counted worthy of the redemption and we are filled of the sanctification
of the Spirit and we become heirs of the eternal life from there. For into the
spiritual contest the victory against the opposing one comes to be through
passions and death. Suffering and being put to death through the Lord eagerly
then we conquer the opposing one. If then we wish not to reckon every
affliction and temptations toilsome and hard, but easily to endure every
uprising of the evil one, let us have the death for the Lord always before the
eyes in desire and as it has been said by the Lord taking up "the cross daily"
(which is death) let us follow him eagerly behind, and thus let us easily
endure every affliction whether hidden or manifest. For if we expect death for
the Lord in desire and have it always before the eyes longing, how much more
easily with joy will we endure whatever heavy afflictions. For this reason we
reckon the afflictions difficult and heavy and burdensome having no endurance,
since we do not have the death for the Lord before the eyes nor does the
understanding always long in it; for he who desires to inherit the Lord let
him also in sequence desire his sufferings. So that those who love Christ
appear in this, when they endure all afflictions coming upon them nobly and
eagerly because of the hope toward him. Let us beseech then the Lord to give
us understanding for knowing his will and eagerly completing it in all
endurance and longsuffering with joy, which he himself will grant us having
strengthened us into all well-pleasing, that having been found approved and
worthy we may obtain his eternal salvation in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom
the glory unto the ages. Amen.

conscience know both to test the coins and the counterfeits.

the things of the world having left them, nor have you inherited the heavenly
and spiritual things; thereafter you proceed nowhere.
11.2 And in the way that the pupil of the eye being small can see many things,
heaven, earth, sun, but when a little covering falls into the pupil and
overshadows, the eye is blinded and is in darkness, thus if a little smoke or
a misty power darken the mind, it has been blinded and sees nothing, but if
again it shine through, it sees spiritual and heavenly things. For in the way
that the earth having been worked appears fruit-bearing, so also the earth of
the heart being worked by the Holy Spirit no longer lies fallow. And as that
there be a city lying in a high place or mountain and have there rest and much
peace and wealth and treasures, and there be there well-born men and senators,
and thereafter the way, through which one goes through upon the city, is
narrow, having a circuit of a step, a track of one man, and there be from the

the foot turn a little from the narrow path it is sunk either into the fire or
into the water, but if someone journeying safely go through the rough and
arrive at the city, he is in rest and great wealth and much freedom from care.
Thus also into the spiritual. Rough is the way into which the Christians
journey; for around them is a great chaos of fire of the unclean spirits. But
if they are able to prevail over those and to go through the walls of the

things and of deep peace, where the spirits of the just rest with the Lord, to

Question. For the sake of what word, when the man is being renewed, is the
ancient habit not wholly changed? For it happens that someone is speaking more
simply having a sharper movement, and after the grace these things remain.
Answer. It comes to be according to concession and economy of the faith. For
also the apostles, into whichever city they entered, they did great powers,
unto so much that even their shadows drove away passions, and they raised
dead. If then it happened that someone was dying in that city, could not the
grace that was with them that raised the dead raise all the dead? Likewise
also those being ill-treated with various diseases came, some obtained
healing, and to whom the grace did not permit, they did not furnish healing.
They did not then do all things as many as they wished. Likewise Paul, when
the God-bearing one was let down "in a basket," could he not through the power
that was with him break through the wall or say by a word: give place? But
this did not come to be, that both the choice of those who believe and the
natural weakness might appear. Not all then obtained, that those who choose
evil and those who wish to be good might become obvious.
The Pharisees said to the Lord: come down from "the cross," and he did not
come down. For the weakness according to economy tests those who wish to be
firm toward the faith, whether they are "unmoved," whether from the few
successes even into the weaker they do not


stumble. Why? They can "then" "when" they are weak. For Christianity is a
stone "of stumbling" and a rock "of offense." Thus also those who are in
grace, a treasure and rest is with them and nevertheless in a time it shrinks
back so that they be tested and warred against.
12.2 Question. Why in the same vessel of the body, when the grace is
operating, does the sin also operate again? Answer. First he who has tasted of
grace, his soul was refreshed and rested with a heavenly rest foreign to this
age, that he may know the sweetness of the good by experience. Then if the
mind is lifted away a little or converses or does something else, again he is
filled of the sin, that he may be afflicted and by experience learn its
bitterness, and then he may flee sharply seeking that unspeakable consolation
and rest. And again he obtains and having been refreshed and having rested a
little, again if he is careless, the evil finds pasture and afflicts the man
bitterly, the grace conceding, that by experience he may know the sweetness
and rest and consolation of this and the bitterness and pain and affliction of
the sin, that more eagerly he may flee it (if at least he wishes to be saved)
and be glued wholly from the whole to the grace, giving himself over always
and hanging upon the Lord, that by the bitterness and shamefulness and
affliction of that the sweetness and assistance and rest and exultation of the
grace may become more desired and longed for, that by the same experience of
the two natures, the bitterness of the sin and the sweetness of the grace, the
soul having tasted more often may become more perceiving and more sober unto
fleeing the evil wholly and being glued wholly from the whole to the Lord,
until she become with him into "one spirit." For if the sweetness and the rest
and the joy were always to the soul, she would not know the difference, she
would not know what she has possessed and she would become more careless, she
was ignorant of the excess of the good and of the excess of "the kindness" of
the Lord and she did not understand the enjoyment, even if she possessed them
she did not know what she has possessed, and she did not know the excess of
the bitterness and terribleness of the sin and she did not understand its
wickedness. But thus the unspeakable wisdom of God administers the things,
that both its own good may be known by experience by comparison and setting
beside of the bitterness of the evil one, and the soul herself may receive the
benefit according to two manners; in the taking experience of the bitterness
and affliction of the sin having tasted of the kindness of the Lord. And she
wishes to flee the evil with all strength and to be eager always and in strain
into the good and not an infant and inexperienced and unknowing of both the
good and the evil, but she has a perfect knowledge of both by experience and
operation and perception, and thus having been disciplined and having run a
perfect course through the much eagerness and desire upon the good, having
"the senses exercised" perfectly (according to what has been said) "toward
discernment of good and evil" she will also receive "the crown" and having
come "into a measure of stature" spiritual she will be set down an heir of the
kingdom. To some such a manner the thing is like, for for this reason she
tastes little by little of the kingdom, that by a whole will and a whole
choice from the self-determining she may have loved God and that the
self-determining choice may appear (to which part it is glued and what it
respects), that she may also justly inherit the kingdom and she herself having
contended wholly and having run may have great boldness toward the Lord unto
the endless ages of the ages. Amen.

you love and take pleasure together with the willings of the evil, immediately
they work together with you and are glued. For the sin is easily surrounding
and comes with eagerness; for it is a ruler rejoicing upon evil-doing of men.
Likewise again if you love and take pleasure together with the good, again it
also comes and helps. But here it has been commanded with much contest, since
it is a kingdom not having "an end"; for this reason the honorable is rare and
that which is accomplished with difficulty is glorious, whence also eternal,
since without toil all wish to enter and to become of the good, but in the
midst a long course and a contest is set forth and from many few are drawn
out. Those then who have conquered have arrived toward a peaceful and tame
leader and king, rejoicing upon benefaction of men, for such from now inherit
the land of the promise and enter into a harbor of rest and into the city of
the saints and "into the rest." For as there is an appearing heaven which is
called a firmament, so there is above this another light-formed heaven, where
camps of the angels (even if especially God and the angels are not
circumscribed by bodily bounds), and there is a tent not made with hands. All
these things then are divine and unspeakable and light-bearing, since they are
spiritual and hypostatic, not being of this age but of another world, where
there is not darkness or night or war or Gehenna. Since then it is not
possible for all to consider those heavenly things with the outer eye, for
this reason this firmament was created as a curtain, that it may not be
permitted to anyone simply to look down unless to those pure in heart and
having the mind sanctified, the fellow-citizens and fellow-initiates of the
saints. And when this is contracted, then those things that do not appear
receive the elect. For many being of the good part and being operated by the
good Spirit thought they had been perfected and after these things they
suffered an assault by the evil spirits and a plot, as if soldiers having
thought they had conquered the enemies, thereafter having thrown the weapons
and being in freedom from care, the enemies having made an ambush and having
stood upon them suddenly may kill some, and bind some, take others captive.
Thus also the sin having been moved upon used them wrongly and bound some into
its own deep and the nooses of the evil, and took others captive into the
"passions" of "dishonor." All men then whether just or sinners, unless they
become free through the law "of the Spirit" (that is through Jesus Christ),
cannot be set free from the evils and be drawn out from the domination of the
devil. For Paul says describing the fall of the man: "wretched man that I am;
who will deliver me from the death? I thank God through Jesus Christ," because
"the law of the Spirit of the life set me free from the death."
13.2 As of a spring gushing as many as drink nothing is lacking, so those who
have the revenues and the treasures of the Spirit from within in the mind, as
many things as they speak, those who have heard are benefited by faith, and
they themselves remain such as they also were before. For to the spiritual
from now palaces and dining-rooms and exceedingly great houses are created by
the Spirit, and a differing and glorious garment is also prepared for them,
and when they depart toward Christ, their own receive them with much joy, the
spirits of the just and the whole heavenly church. For the Lord says: "well,
good and faithful slave, you were faithful upon few things, I will set you
over many; enter into the joy of your Lord." For many of the brothers
receiving grace and agreeing


daily into increase and advancement and proceed into great measures; and there
are others who receive a gift from God, and these although the grace is with
them are turned aside into disorders and into many evils.
13.3 Question. And when they fall out into sins, does the grace remain or
immediately stand off? Answer. It happens that even when the man himself has
offended, because of the love of man, until he is in flesh it remains, but
when the soul is separated from the body, then the grace also stands off and
the soul goes into its own part being given over to the evil spirits. For the
two laws are in the man, the law of the evil stealing into its own part and
the law of the grace drawing into the heavenly things. For as much as someone
enters into the places of trial, he is made solid and is founded and becomes a
rock that does not fall, and such a mind, standing at the door and not giving
back to the enemy but standing against, in the going out of the body the
right-hand angels take him into the part of the kingdom and he goes into the
eternal life. As in the things that appear that there be a king giving gifts
to his ministers, some receiving spend them into fornications and wantonnesses
and gluttonies, and others receiving keep them and acquire for themselves
precious vessels, these are glorious who have multiplied their own substance
from the gifts furnished to them. For from truth those who have tasted of that
sweetness and pleasantness, these seeing an appearing king clothed in purple
and dignities and seeing treasures, they reckon as a foul smell and they
appear abominable and low to them, since they have entered into royal and
heavenly houses of Christ and heavenly treasures have been opened to them and
they have inherited another wealth, having become of another dignity, sons of
a heavenly light, of an endless age. Comparing then the earthly to the
heavenly they then know the chaos in the midst and the difference. For as that
there be someone being held by a fever, while the ill-treatment is still at
its height if you offer to such a one differing foods or a certain sweetness
or another food, he is nauseated and is disgusted and turns away, being filled
and drunk from the fever and as having it as food. Thus also the spiritual
having the burning of the heavenly love and of the divine fire, as many of the
appearing things of this world as you offer to them, whether dignities or a
kingdom or wealth or glory, they are nauseated and abominate, as someone is
disgusted journeying where there are corpses and much foul smell. In the same
way again also those who have been filled of the opposing power and who are of
the spirit of the world, these when a spiritual word is spoken concerning
light, concerning a kingdom, concerning another age and a divine power, as it
were they are disgusted and are in accidie not being able to contain the word
"of God." For from truth these are wise and courageous, these are warriors who
have their own mind above and who are angry at the desires and pleasures that
are with them and who act against the law of the evil and who become obedient
and take pleasure together with "the law of God." What then is the rhetor or
philosopher? Desire rises against him and he commits adultery †and upon greed
he is snatched and† the mind is driven by the evil spirits, and where is their
much wisdom?
13.4 Question. Why when I go away into the prayer am I constrained by many
reasonings and wanderings?
Answer. Come manfully and eagerly and not sluggishly and as sleeping, but with
a sober understanding, as having a work and not as doing nothing wander with
the reasonings. For as doing a fleshly work you are there wholly from the
whole with the mind and with the reasonings and with the body, in the same way
you ought to the prayer


come as expecting to complete a spiritual work; for God sees a mind and
attends to reasonings. Be eager always to gather yourself together into the
work and into the seeking and into a longing of God, stretch out there and
think and meditate, where the body prays, whether there is need of a cry or of
silence or of whatever sort, only with a sober and attending understanding.
For just as the image-writer who writes the image of the king, if he make all
the members well, but in the eye if he lack a little or destroy and not having
written as it is necessary, the whole work has been destroyed; he destroyed a
royal image and is set down liable to punishments because of his slackness
likewise also let each write the image of the thoughts straightly and rightly
of the heavenly king in the soul and let him adorn it with all good thoughts.
Or as the builder measures the straight of the rule, lest something be turned
aside, or as the farmer attending to the furrow is occupied nowhere, but is
wholly there, that he may make the furrows rightly. If then into the bodily
things thus, how much more the soul ought in the prayer as in a true work to
attend and to straighten with much sobriety the thoughts and the reasonings
toward God always "into the good." For thus having laboriously set right the
straightness of the mind he will be able to be counted worthy of praise and to
obtain spiritual gifts, and thus he can bring his toils into rest, advancing
daily and being made better and becoming "a cause of salvation" of many, and
having been shown as a lover of God and well-pleasing to God he is set down an
heir of the kingdom of the heavens in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom the glory
unto the ages. Amen.
14.1 Question. Why cannot those who do not have the Holy Spirit in themselves
give back a complete and living word? Answer. As the child being born from the
womb comes out having the members sound not having a hurt into the body, so
also those who have been born from the womb of the Spirit, even if they appear
in flesh into this world, yet by the Spirit throughout they are into the world
there with the Lord having rested, from where they were also born, and these
alone can give over a perfect word to the brothers, having the members of the
Spirit perfect in themselves. But if certain have not been born from there,
they cannot give over a spiritual word, since they do not have a living
spirit, which dictates to them the heavenly things. As that there be a child
coming from a womb blemished or blind from birth or lame, as it is a monster
and a sign to the world, so are the wise who wish to be interpreters of the
scriptures not having the heavenly Spirit, neither having been mixed in the
Godhead they are blemished, not having the members healthy, but giving over a
blameworthy and rotten word. As that there be a coin, which into one part
seems to have a stamp, and into another part is damaged and useless, so also
are those who have been damaged by the sin; from their mind or the wisdom they
wish to speak the heavenly things, from where they have not been born. Since
set before me one face, that there is a wise man, a virgin and in the things
that appear blameless, fasting, keeping vigil, having renounced the world,
praying. Thereafter I converse toward this one: are you a virgin? rather I
than you. Into the appearing you fast? rather I than you. Are you poor? more
than you I. That one ought not to remain in these appearing things and the
mind ought not to be without care and to rest upon these as being perfect, but
it is necessary that the mind seek a certain heavenly power to be mixed up
with it and to await according to every hour the heavenly gift, that which is
above your nature, with the


natural fruits be mixed, that all things which you do you may be doing purely.
As if there be a king having the members sound, if he is not clothed with the
purple, he is not worshiped as a king, so also you, even if you have the
natural members sound, if having these you are not clothed with the purple of
the Spirit, which is above your nature, you cannot be crowned nor sit upon a
royal throne.
14.2 But you will answer me, that if into thirty years or forty I do not
receive the divine power and it happen that I go out of the life, what?
And I also answer you toward this, that God does not judge you as a
blasphemer, inasmuch as you displayed a more abundant eagerness into the good.
God has to say to you: since you went out behind me and you wept and you
sought night and day and beyond what you were formerly being without care in
the things that appear and being puffed upon them, now you have been more
eager according to the scriptures seeking the truth and desiring to receive
the divine power in yourself, I do not judge you in this as a blasphemer; may
it not be, but I receive you as having become more beautiful than yourself
beyond what you were formerly. And you yourself also test in yourself and
discern that your present way of life and working is higher. And I say to you
according to the scriptures, that "he who seeks finds, and he who asks
receives, and to him who knocks it will be opened." But you say to me that:
show me, how the divine power is with certain ones in the present age. I also
answer you: how shall I persuade you, that the divine power is with the
faithful, from the old covenant or from the new? Let us come first upon the
shadow. Who strengthened Abraham being an old man and having "the body put to
death" to come into a renewal and to beget the son? But you say to me: he
received his sacrifices, but he was not toward him. And what friend wishing to
meet with his friend first moves into his substance, into land or into a
vineyard, and then sees him and kisses him? And God then received the
sacrifices of Abraham and the belongings, but he was not toward him and did
not dwell within him? And when he joined into war, who was the one conquering?
His nature alone or was God dwelling in him and making the victory? Let us
come upon Noah. Who was the one who subjected before him all the kinds of the
beasts? His nature or a certain heavenly thing with him that is above the
nature, which created the animals themselves and him and subjected the beasts
before him? Could Jacob then from his own power roll away such a stone or was
the divine power with him that rolled away the weight of the stone? And God
was with him so much without ceasing, that not even if he slept did he leave
him. For he opened the doors of the heavens and showed him the heavenly house,
it appeared to him into a type of a ladder. Then he wrestled with him as a man
toward a man.
15.1 The Christians worthy of Christ do not learn and speak from a letter, but
they are taught all things from a Spirit of God, "not in taught" letters (as
the apostle says) "words of human wisdom, but in taught of Holy Spirit." For
their covenant and the law and their book is Spirit, as the Spirit says
through the prophet: and it will be in the last days "I will set" "a new
covenant" with the house of Jacob, "not according to the covenant which I set
with their fathers," but "this is the covenant which I will set with the house
of Israel," "giving my laws into their understanding and upon their heart I
will write them" and the rest. For as those of old from the old covenant were
reading with the bodily eyes the bodily letters, so the Christians with the
inner


eyes of the soul read and learn from the covenant of the Spirit and with the
inner new tongue they speak and with the inner ears they hear. For thus also
the Lord chose the apostles, private and unlettered men, and he filled them of
Holy Spirit, and having been made wise by the Spirit they spoke teaching wise
men and scribes; for even if "these" (he says) "will be silent, the stones
will cry out." And the Lord was saying this beforehand either concerning the
apostles or concerning all souls that are being ransomed from the heaviest
stone of the sin that lies upon them, but when it is taken away, they cry out
praising God.
For as the stones then and "the rocks" seeing the Lord were split and "the
tombs" were opened, so when the Lord Jesus Christ shines upon and appears
"his" good "face" in the soul, the heavy stone of the sin is split giving
place to the master, that having entered he himself may dwell into her. When
then the souls as a stone see the desired face of Christ, then they cry out.
And as if there be a mountain not having dwellings within but being stone, and
a certain craftsman man come knowing to quarry and work out there most
beautiful houses, in the same way also Christ the true and good craftsman
comes toward the souls that love him and he quarries and cuts off from them
the spirit of the sin and he prepares palaces for himself and makes a dwelling
in them. The souls as a stone then cry out, when the Lord appears "his face."
And concerning the apostles that they cried out wishing and not wishing as a
flute when the spirit blows (as the one blowing wishes so the flute speaks),
so also the apostles and those like them having been born "from above" and
having received the Paraclete Spirit, as the Spirit itself wished it spoke in
them. For to Nicodemus the Lord narrating concerning the one being born "from
above" was saying: "the spirit blows where it wishes, and you hear its voice,
but you do not know from where it comes and where it goes." As of the spirit
of the wind no one knows the house, from where it comes, or its fullness,
where it arrives, nor can anyone hinder nor measure nor overtake, likewise as
no one can hold back or overtake the streams of the rivers the same also upon
the soul; no one can overtake the reasonings or again hinder the streams of
the mind or hold them back or know the spring of the reasonings of the
understanding, from where it goes out, or overtake where it goes away. For
where it is not here it sits and elsewhere with the mind and with the
understanding it is into far lands. If then, he says, the earthly things here
in hands are such and so much incomprehensible, how much more he who has been
born "from above" and has received the heavenly Spirit of the Lord and has
been mixed with it "according to the inner man" how much is that soul
incomprehensible? For where it wishes, see it is there, for being here, the
heavenly and divine Spirit having come led it into the heavenly things and
taught and that soul is everywhere incomprehensible. Glory to his greatness
unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
16.1 What the Lord says in the gospel: "how narrow and afflicted is the way
that leads away into the life, and few are those who journey it," is
understood according to the highest virtue because of the afflictive and
because of acquiring it with toil and contest each. Nevertheless when he says
a narrow way to those who do not wish to go through it, it is a narrow way to
its enemies, to those who cannot walk into it, as it is written: "how very
rough it is to the undisciplined, and a heartless one will not remain in it";
for there is need of much understanding and prudence and endurance and faith,
that


someone may arrive firmly and safely upon the blessed and divine "way of
righteousness." As if there be a mountain exceedingly high and precipitous and
it have a track exceedingly narrow as if a circuit of a step of a foot, and
through that four-footed animals or anything else cannot journey and go
through or perhaps only birds that are lightened by their own wings, so also
the highest commandments of the gospel and "the way" of God "narrow and
afflicted" is to the men of this world who do not share a Spirit of God, and
it is impossible for certain ones to go through (that is to be found obedient
and blameless into all the gospel commandments), unless they acquire wings of
the Holy Spirit, and then being lightened by the Holy Spirit they can easily
journey the gospel "way of righteousness," that they may be counted worthy "to
enter into the life," as the Lord has said: that "my yoke is kind and my
burden is light," and then they will know that the commandments of Jesus are
not heavy, when having been winged by the Holy Spirit they journey the way
that is narrow to the remaining men in much lightness and good cheer; for it
is impossible without the holy wings of the Spirit for anyone to go through
the aforesaid way (that is to fulfill all the commandments of the Lord purely
and perfectly and blamelessly), and thus to be counted worthy to enter into
the gate of the kingdom of the heavens.
16.2 And by many and various manners of the grace according to advancement and
increase and by a temporal extension into the perfect measures of the purity
the grace of the Spirit counts worthy souls that are obedient to it according
to all things to come. For in the way that certain of the birds fearing the
beasts upon earth make the nest into a higher and more lofty part either in a
roof or in a certain high tree, and the mother herself going around brings to
the nestlings tender food as they can receive because of their still being
infants, then according to time she offers a more solid food according to the
increase of the stature of the nestlings, and at the same time also teaching
them as the wings grow and increase to be exercised little by little in the
air the flight and to practice first around the nest, then around the branches
of the tree from branch into branch, then a little from afar, until they are
made men and the wings are made solid, and thereafter from hill into hill and
from mountain into mountain, easily and without toil in rest they fly up thus
also the children of God, which the Holy Spirit begets from its own power,
first in the beginnings it gives food of spiritual milk, full of sweetness and
heavenly longing in the heart; for "I gave you milk to drink," says the
apostle, "and not food," and Peter also thus said: "as newly born babes long
for the reasonable and guileless milk." Then according to advancement and
increase of the soul being renewed it gives a more solid food of the Spirit,
and at the same time also the wings of the grace (that is the power of the
Spirit) as the soul advances in good works increase in her. Then the divine
grace (the good and heavenly mother) teaches the understanding to fly first
around the nest of the heart or of the reasonings (that is to pray without
wandering according to God by a power of Spirit). Then as much as it receives
a more solid food of the Spirit of the Godhead, so much also it can fly more
greatly and more highly being led and underborne by the Spirit. But when
thereafter it is made a man and comes "into a measure" of the spiritual
"stature," from hill into hill and from mountain into mountain (that is from
this world into the world above and from this age into the blessed and
incorruptible and boundless age) easily the mind flies in much freedom from
care and rest by the wings of the


Spirit being borne and being led "into visions and revelations" of heavenly
mysteries and into unspeakable spiritual contemplations, which a tongue of
flesh cannot speak. For thereafter the children of God become higher and more
lofty than the evil having been strengthened by the Spirit and having the way
of life into heavens, no longer fearing the wildness of the unclean spirits.
In the way that the nestlings having been made men and the wings having
increased perfectly no longer fear a plot of beasts or of men because of their
way of life being in the air for the most part, and as no man knows the
converse of the winged things, so also no man of those filled of the spirit
"of the world" knows in power the tongue of the Spirit or only the sons of the
Spirit of the grace know the speech of their mother according to what has been
said by the apostle: "comparing spiritual things with spiritual. But a soulish
man does not receive the things of the Spirit of God; for it is foolishness to
him." For as the voice of the swallows and of the remaining birds is barbarian
to men, so also to the fleshly the words of the spiritual are barbarian. And
likewise also to the spiritual the words of the fleshly, that is "the wisdom
of the world is foolishness" and they know them but they turn away as words of
idle talk and of vanity of the world.
16.3 All these things have been related by me through the appearing things
both through birds and animals and all the things seen because it is otherwise
impossible for the spiritual things to be said or interpreted, as also the
Lord spoke the most in parables taking examples from the things seen likening
the kingdom to "a grain of mustard" and "leaven" and "a treasure"; "I will
open" (for he says) "my mouth in parables, I will utter hidden things from the
foundation of a world." For the spiritual things are unspeakable not being
able to be phrased by a human word, unless the Spirit itself by experience and
operation teach the worthy and faithful soul. For the spiritual rebirth and
the increase and the perfecting as it is necessary to say it is impossible to
be said or only to believe, that God thus does these things to the men who
have taken up the way of life well-pleasing to him. Thus then also the
converse of the spiritual is barbarian to the men of the world. For as the
fishes of the sea know all the things in it, the tracks and the depths, and it
is not possible for a man or beasts or the remaining animals to know the
things there or to spend time here, since they perish being choked, so it is
impossible for someone not having been born from the Holy Spirit from that age
to know the things there as they are "and the depths of God" and to know the
tracks of the Spirit of the kingdom, unless those who turn about in the Spirit
itself and live from it and share and walk in it according to what has been
said by the apostle: "but our citizenship exists in heavens," and again: no
one knew "the things of God" "except the Spirit of God." "But to us God
revealed through his Spirit," and again he says: the Spirit "searches even the
depths of God." Thus it is impossible for him who has "the spirit of the
world" and has not been born in Holy Spirit to know anything in an experience
of knowledge of the things of the Spirit as it is, even if they wish to enter
in, they are puffed in vain by the mind of their flesh. For leaning upon
worldly wisdom and thinking they can overtake with their understanding the
spiritual things without the visitation and revelation of the Spirit, they are
at a loss and they perish not having a true foundation, (which is the Lord,
through whom the worthy comprise all spiritual knowledge). For into a certain
unclarity and into a fantasy of conjectures their mind arrives through its own
understanding and strength

all the scriptures from the mouth, they do not know the power of the things
they speak. For as children being taught by a teacher learn by heart the
narrations of the terrible rhetors and with the mouth they announce the words
of the wise, but they do not know the power of the words, nor do they
understand what they read or announce because of the infancy thus also those
who announce and meditate the scriptures unless they receive from spiritual

voice (that is the very divine power that dictated the scriptures), they have
not been benefited as it was necessary to be benefited. For they are still

being born in this world is not, but when he is born then he is into this age,

was well-pleased and declared: "unless someone is reborn from above," he will

Spirit is spirit," as also "that which has been born of the flesh is flesh."

her from the spirit of the sin that is "in" her "members" and to become

and let us clothe him, having given over our very choices wholly given out to
him and following out the will of his Spirit, or rather let us be fed and
given to drink his Spirit and be clothed with the Lord himself, for he himself
is our food and the drink and the garment and the treasure and our inheritance
