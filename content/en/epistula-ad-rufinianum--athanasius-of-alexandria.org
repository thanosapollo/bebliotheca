Of the same, Letter to Rufinianus the bishop.
Γ Concerning those who were drawn aside by necessity, but were not corrupted
in the evil faith.
To the lord, son, and most longed-for fellow-minister Rufinianus, Athanasius
greeting in the Lord. 77 You write the things that befit a beloved son toward
a father; having drawn near, then, through the letter I embraced you,
Rufinianus most longed-for of all to me. And I too, being able to write as to
a son both in the openings and the middle and the endings, held back, so that
the commendation and the testimony might not be known from letters; "for you
are my letter, according to what is written, known and read in the heart."
Being thus disposed, yes, believe it, I greet you and urge you to write; for
not a little, but greatly you gladden me by doing this. And since you asked,
in a manner loving what is good and ecclesiastical (for this again befits your
piety), concerning those who were drawn aside by necessity, but were not
corrupted in the evil faith, and you wished me to write the things that seemed
good concerning them in the synods and everywhere, know, my most longed-for
lord, that at the beginning, when the violence that had taken place had ceased
78, a synod was held with bishops present from the outer parts; and it was
held also among the fellow-ministers dwelling in Greece, and no less also
among those in Spain and Gaul. And it pleased, as here so also everywhere,
that to those who had fallen and who headed the impiety pardon should be
granted if they repent, but that they should not be given a place in the
clergy; but to those who did not originate the impiety, but were swept along
by necessity and violence, it seemed good that pardon be given, and that they
should also have the place of the clergy, especially because they brought a
persuasive defense, and this seemed somehow to have been done by economy; for
they affirmed that they had not changed over into impiety, but that, lest
certain most impious men being appointed should corrupt the churches, they
chose rather to go along with the violence and bear the burden, than that the
peoples should perish. And saying this they seemed even to us 79 to speak
persuasively, because they also put forward Aaron, the brother of Moses, as
having gone along with the people's transgression in the wilderness, but as
having had a defense, lest the people, turning back into Egypt, should remain
in idolatry; for it also appeared reasonable, that remaining in the wilderness
they can cease from the impiety, but entering into Egypt they would be worn
down and increase the impiety among themselves. For this reason, then, pardon
was granted with respect to the clergy. And to those who were deceived and
suffered violence, pardon is given. These things I also make known to your
piety, being confident that your godliness will also receive the things that
seemed good, and will not condemn the forbearance of those who thus assembled.
And deign to read them to the priesthood and to the people under you, so that
they too, knowing, may not blame you for being thus disposed toward such
persons; for it was unseemly for me to write, when your piety is able both to
report 80 our disposition concerning them and to fill up all that is lacking.
Thanks be to the Lord who has filled you in every word and all knowledge. Let
those who repent, then, openly anathematize, naming the evil faith of Eudoxius
and Euzoius; for they themselves, henceforth blaspheming him as a creature,
enrolled themselves as champions of the Arian heresy; and let them confess the
faith confessed at Nicaea by the fathers, and prefer no other synod to this
synod. Greet the brotherhood with you; the one with us greets you in the Lord.
