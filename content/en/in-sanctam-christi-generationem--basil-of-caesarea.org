"God with us." Of old that name too holds the disclosure of the whole mystery,
that God is among men, since Emmanuel, he says, is interpreted "God with us."
And let no one be overturned by the Jewish insults, who say that not a virgin,
but a young woman, was spoken of by the prophet. "For behold," he says, "the
young woman shall conceive." For first of all it is most unreasonable that
what was given by the Lord as a sign should seem to be something common and
confessed by all nature. For what does the prophet say? "And the Lord added to
speak to Ahaz, saying: Ask for yourself a sign from the Lord your God, in the
depth or in the height. And Ahaz said: I will not ask, nor will I tempt the
Lord." Then

after a little he says: "Therefore the Lord himself will give you a sign.
Behold, the Virgin shall conceive." For since Ahaz did not ask for the sign in
the depth or in the height, so that you may learn that he who descended into
the lower parts of the earth is himself also the one who ascended above all
the heavens, the Lord himself gave a sign: a sign paradoxical and wondrous,
and greatly altered from the common nature. The same woman is both =31.1468=
virgin and mother, remaining in the sanctity of virginity, and inheriting the
blessing of childbearing.

But if some of those who interpreted the Hebrew speech have rendered
"young woman" instead of "virgin," it does no damage to the argument. For we
have found in the usage of Scripture that "young woman" is often taken
instead of "virgin," as in Deuteronomy it says: "If anyone finds the maiden
who is a virgin, who is not betrothed, and having forced her he lies with
her, and they are found, the man who lay with her shall give to the father
of the young woman fifty didrachms."

"And rising he took his wife." And regarding her as wife in disposition, and
affection, and all the care that belongs to those who dwell together, he
abstained from the works of marriage. "For he did not know her," it says,
"until she bore her firstborn son." And this already furnishes a suspicion
that after she had purely served the generation of the Lord accomplished
through the Holy Spirit, Mary did not refuse the accustomed works of marriage.

But we, even if nothing damages the account of piety (for virginity is
necessary up to the service according to the economy, and what follows is not
to be meddled with by the account of the mystery), nevertheless, because the
hearing of those who love Christ cannot accept that the Theotokos ever ceased
to be a virgin, we hold those testimonies sufficient.

As to the saying, "He did not know her until she bore her son," that "until"
in many places seems to hint at some limitation of time, but in truth shows
the unlimited. Such is what was said by the Lord: "And behold I am with you
all the days until the consummation of the age." For surely it is not that
after this age the Lord would not be with the saints; but the promise of the
present signifies the perpetual, there is no cutting off of the future. In the
same way we say that "until" has been taken here also.

And since "firstborn" has been said, the firstborn does not altogether have
comparison with those who come after, but the one who first opens the womb is
named firstborn. And the history concerning Zacharias also makes clear that
Mary was a virgin unto the end. For there is a certain account, and this too
has come down to us from tradition, that Zacharias, having ranked Mary in the
place of the virgins after the conception of the Lord, was murdered by the
Jews between the temple and the altar, accused by the people as thereby
constructing that paradoxical and much-hymned =31.1469= sign, a virgin who had
given birth and had not destroyed her virginity.

"And when Jesus was born," it says, "in Bethlehem of Judea, in the days of
Herod the king, behold magi from the east came to Jerusalem, saying: Where is
he who has been born king of the Jews?" The magi are a Persian nation,
attending to divinations and incantations and certain natural antipathies, and
occupied with the observation of the things on high.

And of this divination Balaam also seems to be, who was sent for by Balak, so
as to curse Israel through certain rites; who also in his fourth parable says
such things concerning the Lord: "A man seeing," he says, "hearing the oracles
of God, knowing the knowledge of the Most High, and having seen a vision of
God in sleep; his eyes are uncovered. I will show him, and not now; I bless,
and it does not draw near. A star shall rise out of Jacob, and a man shall
arise out of Israel."

Therefore, seeking out the place of Judea, according to the memory of the
ancient prophecy, they came to learn where is he who has been born king of the
Jews. And perhaps also, the opposing power having become weaker at the
manifestation of the Lord, perceiving their operation being abolished, they
bore witness of great power to the one born. Therefore finding the child they
worshiped him with gifts.

Magi, the nation alienated from God and foreign to the covenants, were first
counted worthy of the worship, because the testimonies from enemies are more
trustworthy. For if Jews had worshiped first, they would have been thought to
be magnifying their own kinship; but now those who have no relation worship as
to God, so that the household may be condemned, having crucified this one whom
the foreigners worshiped.

Since, however, they were attached to the motions of the heavenly things, they
did not idly observe the paradox of the spectacles in heaven, a new and
unaccustomed star having risen at the generation of the Lord. And let no one
drag the construction of astrology into the rising of the star. For those who
introduce nativity from the stars already existing set down such-and-such a
configuration of these as the cause of the incidents of life for each; but
here none of the existing stars signified the royal generation, nor was this
one among the accustomed.

For those that were laid down with the creation from the beginning are either
unmoved throughout, or have unceasing motion; but this one that appeared is
seen to have both, both moving and standing. And among those already existing,
the fixed never move, and the planets never stand; but the one having both in
himself, both motion and rest, is clearly belonging to neither. For he moved
from the east as far as Bethlehem; and he stood over where the =31.1472= child
was.

Therefore the magi, having been moved from the east, following the guidance of
a star, having come upon Jerusalem, threw the whole city into confusion by
their arrival, and put fear into the king of the Jews. Finding then the one
they sought, they honored him with gifts, gold, and frankincense, and myrrh;
perhaps following even here the prophecy of Balaam, who said such things
concerning Christ: "Having lain down he rested as a lion, and as a cub. Who
shall raise him up? Those who bless you are blessed, and those who curse you
are cursed."

Since therefore through the lion the word indicates the royal, and through the
lying-down the passion, and through the power of blessing the divinity, the
magi, following the prophecy, offered gold as to a king, myrrh as to one who
would die, and frankincense as to God.

Nor indeed is it possible for those who take the things concerning the birth
in an overcurious way to say that the star was like the comets, which
especially seem, indicating successions of kings, to stand in heaven. For
these too are for the most part unmoved, the burning taking place in a
circumscribed place. For comets and beams and pits are various shapes, and
names proper to the shapes. But the generation of all is the same. For when
the air around the earth overflows, and is poured over into the ethereal
place, and as it were furnishing to the fire as matter the thick and turbid of
the exhalation from here, it provides a clear appearance of a star.

The one, however, that appeared in the east, having moved the magi toward the
search for the one born, was again invisible until, when they were at a loss
in Judea, it appeared a second time, so that they might hear whose it was, and
whom it serves, and for whose sake it had come to be. For coming it stood over
where the child was. Wherefore also seeing it the magi rejoiced with great
joy. Let us then also receive this great joy in our hearts.

For the angels announce this joy to the shepherds. With magi let us worship,
with shepherds let us glorify, with angels let us dance, "Because today a
Savior has been born to us, who is Christ the Lord. The Lord is God, and he
has appeared to us," not in the form of God, lest he terrify what is weak, but
in the form of a servant, that he might set free what had been enslaved. Who
is so sluggish in soul, who so ungrateful, as not to rejoice and exult and be
gladdened at the things present? A common feast of all creation: this grants
to the world the things above the world; it sends the archangels to Zacharias
and to Mary; and it sets up choirs of angels saying: "Glory to God in the
highest, and on earth peace, among men good will."

Stars run across from heaven; magi =31.1473= are moved from the nations; earth
receives in a cave; no one is without contribution, no one ungrateful. Let us
too utter some voice of exultation; let us name our feast Theophany; let us
celebrate the salvation of the world, the birthday of humanity. Today the
condemnation of Adam has been loosed. No longer, "Earth you are and to earth
you shall go;" but joined to the heavenly one you shall be taken up to heaven.
No longer, "In sorrows you shall bear children;" for blessed is she who
travailed with Emmanuel, and the breasts that nourished. Therefore "A child
has been born to us, and a son has been given to us, whose government is upon
his shoulder."

My heart blooms, and my mind wells up; but the tongue is short and the speech
dim, so as to announce so great a magnitude of joy. Think for me in a manner
befitting God of the incarnation of the Lord. Think for me of the Godhead as
undefiled and unstained, even if it comes to be in material nature. What is
passible is set right; the Godhead itself is not filled with passion. Do you
not see this sun both coming to be in mire and being in no way defiled, and
shining upon filthy things, and not partaking of stench? On the contrary,
whatever it consorts with at length, it dries up the putrefactions.

Why then do you fear concerning the impassible and unmixed nature, lest it
contract some stain from us? For this he was born, that you might be cleansed
through what is akin. For this he grows, that you might be made familiar
through custom. O depth of the goodness and love of God for man! Through
excess of gifts we disbelieve the benefactor; through the great
loving-kindness of the Master we rebel against the servitude. O this absurd
and wicked ingratitude! Magi worship, and Christians dispute, how God is in
flesh, and of what sort of flesh; and whether the assumed is a perfect man or
imperfect.

Let the superfluous things be silent in the Church of God; let the things
believed be glorified; let the things kept silent not be pried into. Mix
yourself with those who with joy received the Lord from heaven. Consider
shepherds being made wise, priests prophesying, women rejoicing, when Mary was
taught to rejoice by Gabriel, when Elizabeth had John leaping in her very
womb. Anna was announcing the good news, Simeon was embracing, worshiping the
great God in a small infant; not despising what was seen, but glorifying the
majesty of his Godhead.

For the divine power appeared, as light through membranes of glass, through
the human body, shining through to those who have the eyes of the heart
purified; with whom may we also be found, with unveiled face mirroring the
glory of the Lord, that we ourselves also may be transformed from glory to
glory by the grace and love for man of =31.1476= our Lord Jesus Christ, to
whom be the glory and the might unto the ages of ages. Amen.
