ORATION 32.

On good order in discussions, and that it is not for every man, nor for every
time, to discourse concerning God.

1. Since you have come together eagerly, and the assembly is full of people,
and for this reason especially it is a time for work, come, let us give you
some merchandise, even if it is not worthy of the common eagerness, yet at
least not falling short of our power. For the one demands the greater things,
the other contributes the moderate; and it is better to bring in according to
power than to fall short of the whole. For the one who was not able is not
liable in such things, but the one who was not willing is blameworthy, alike
in divine and in human affairs. I am a small and poor shepherd, and not yet
pleasing to the other shepherds; for to speak thus is moderate, whether
because of the good pleasure and the right reason, or through smallness of
soul and strife, I do not know this: God knows, =36.176= says the divine
Apostle, and the day of revelation will show it clearly, and the last fire, by
which all that is ours is judged or purified. Yet I will try, as far as I can,
not to hide the gift, nor to put the lamp under the bushel, nor to bury the
talent (things I have often heard from you, when you reproach idleness and are
displeased at silence), but to train with words of truth and to fit you to the
Spirit.

2. From where then shall I begin to set you in order, brothers? And with what
words shall I honor the athletes, whose present assembly this is? What shall I
say first, or what is greatest? What is most profitable to your souls? Or what
is most useful for the present time? We would know it thus: What is the
fairest thing of our discourse? Peace; and I will add that it is also the most
advantageous. And what is the most shameful and the most harmful? Discord. And
since I asked this and answered, I will ask the second also: What most of all
dissolved that? And what brought this in? So that, as in diseases, having cut
away the causes, and having blocked or dried up the springs of the passions,
we may thus cut off together the streams and results from there. For it is not
possible to know anything well concerning the end, without having considered
the beginning rightly. Do you then wish to name the cause and make it known?
Or do you yield to me the healer, both to show it and to set it right? For I
am ready for those who wish to speak, and readier still to hear those who
speak. You do yield, I know well; for perhaps you take us to be not bad
physicians of such things, nor unlearned in the healing of souls; whether you
take this badly or rightly. But do not wonder if I speak a paradoxical word;
for it is paradoxical, yet true, as I at least say, and you will agree, if you
wait to learn it through to the end, and do not suffer what I accuse, and rise
up from the discourse beforehand through heat.

3. Hot and great natures are the cause of this disturbance; not simply fiery
and great (for let us not yet condemn heat, without which it is impossible for
anything great to be accomplished toward piety or any other virtue), but noble
ones together with unreason, and =36.177= ignorance, and, the evil offspring
of this, boldness; for boldness is the offspring of ignorance. For the weak
ones are sluggish and hard to move toward virtue and toward vice alike, and
they do not incline greatly either way, like the movements of those who are
numb. But the noble ones, when reason tutors them and directs them, are a
great thing toward virtue; but when knowledge and reason fail, they are found
equal toward vice.

For a horse too must be high-spirited and noble, if he is to be a winner,
whether for war or for the race; but the same would be nothing of good, if he
is not trained with a bit, and has not learned gentleness by more laborious
exercise.

4. And this is, for the most part, what tore members apart, divided brothers,
disturbed cities, maddened peoples, armed nations, set kings in uprising,
priests against people and one another, people against itself and priests,
parents against children, children against parents, men against women, women
against men. All the names of goodwill, slaves and masters against one
another, teachers and disciples against one another, old and young, and having
dishonored the law of reverence, the greatest help toward virtue, it brought
in the law of self-will; and we have become not tribe, and tribe against
itself, which of old Israel was reproached with; nor Israel and Judah, the two
segments even of one nation and this a small one, but we have been divided by
houses and the necessary pairings, and as it were each against himself, the
whole inhabited world, and every race of humans, as far as the divine word has
run; and many-rule has become anarchy, and our bones have been scattered
beside Hades; and it was necessary, since we had mastered the enemies outside,
that we be destroyed by one another, and, like those who are mad, laying hold
of their own flesh, not even to perceive it, but even to rejoice in the evil
more than others who are at peace, and to make the disaster a gain, and to
think that we offer as worship to God the destruction, and to be divided, and
to be burned, not the praiseworthy division, but the blameworthy, nor the
purifying burning, but the ruinous. For it is not the cutting word that
divides, the sword of Christ, those who believe from the unbelieving, nor is
fire thrown and kindled, the faith that consumes the matter and eats it, and
the boiling of the Spirit; but contrary to before, we are consumed and cut.
=36.180=

5. This is what has made the one Church to be many parts, and has divided it,
not into one Paul, or Cephas, or Apollos, or this or that planter, or this or
that waterer; but it has shown forth many Pauls, and Apolloses, and Cephases,
from whom we are called instead of Christ, the great and new name, and of whom
we are said to be; and would that I had only this much to say; but even many
(which I shudder to speak) Christs instead of one, the one being generated,
the one being created, and the one beginning from Mary, and the one dissolving
back whence he came into being, and the mindless man, and the one who is, and
the one who appears. And as also many Spirits, the uncreated, and the
creature, and the equally honored, and the operation, and the mere name. When
we ought to know one God the Father, without beginning, and unbegotten, and
one Son begotten from the Father, and one Spirit having existence from God,
yielding unbegottenness to the Father, and generation to the Son; but in the
other things connatural, and sharing the throne, and of one glory, and of one
honor; to know these things, to confess these things, to stand as far as
these, and to send away the much nonsense, and the profane empty voices of the
words, to those who have leisure. What has set all these things in motion?
Heat without reason and knowledge, ungovernable, and a voyage of faith without
a steersman.

6. Knowing this then, brothers, let us be neither sluggish toward the good,
but let us boil with the Spirit, lest we fall asleep little by little into
death, or, while we sleep, the enemy sow the evil seeds; for sluggishness is
yoked with sleep; nor fiery together with unreason and self-love, lest we be
carried away, nor fall outside the royal road; failing in one thing at any
rate, either needing goads because of the sluggishness, or being thrown down a
cliff because of the heat. And taking from both as much as is useful, from the
one the gentleness, from the other the zeal; let us flee from both as much as
is harmful, from the one the hesitation, from the other the boldness; so that
we may neither be unfruitful by the falling short, nor be in danger by the
excess. For equally useless are both sluggishness without action, and

untutored heat; the one not approaching the good, the other overshooting, and
making something more right than the right. Which even the divine Solomon,
knowing well, says, Do not turn aside to the right, nor to the left; nor
through the opposites fall into an equal evil, sin. And yet, praising the
right by nature, For the Lord, he says, knows the ways on the right; =36.181=
but those on the left are twisted. How then does he praise the right, and
again lead away from the right? Clearly from this seeming right, and not being
so. Looking toward which elsewhere he says, Do not become just much, nor be
wise beyond measure. For the same passion is both concerning justice and
concerning wisdom, heat concerning action and word, falling outside the good
and virtue through excess. By which both the lacking and the exceeding spoil
alike, as also addition or subtraction to the rule.

7. Let no one then be wise more than is well, nor more lawful than the law,
nor brighter than the light, nor straighter than the rule, nor higher than the
commandment. And how will this be? If we know order, and praise the law of
nature, and follow reason, and do not dishonor good order. Look into the
heaven above, and into the earth below, and consider how the whole was put
together, and from where, and what it was before the ordering, and what name
the whole now has. By order all things were ordered, and the Word that ordered
them; and yet it was possible for all things to subsist as one and all at once
(for he who gave being to the things that were not, and to the things that
came to be the forms and the shapes, was not weak even to show forth and order
the whole together), but for this reason a first thing and a second are
numbered, and a third, and the things next, so that order may enter together
with the creatures at once.

8. Order then constituted the whole. Order holds together both the heavenly
things and the earthly; order in intelligibles; order in sensibles; order in
angels; order in stars, and motion, and size, and relation toward one another,
and brightness. Another glory of sun, and another glory of moon, and another
glory of stars; for star differs from star in glory; order in seasons and
times, advancing in order and receding, and through the mean taming the
severity; order in the measures and intervals of day and night. Order in the
elements, from which the bodies; order led heaven around, spread out air, set
earth below, or even set it above, poured out the wet nature and gathered it,
released winds, and did not let them go entirely, bound water in clouds, and
did not hold it back, but scattered it upon the face of all the =36.184=
earth, in good order and with equal honor; and these things not for a little,
nor unto one season or time, but from beginning to end, being steered and
going around the same road, both fixed and moving, the one by the Word, the
other by the flow: He set them unto the age, and unto the age of the age; he
laid a command, and it will not pass. This belongs to the fixing; and if
anything has come to be, or will be generated, this belongs to the flow. And
when order prevails, the All is a cosmos, and the beauty unmoved. But disorder
and want of order invented in air the thunderbolts, in earth the earthquakes,
in sea the floods, in cities and houses wars, in the bodies the illnesses, in
the souls the sins. All these are names not of order, nor of peace, but of
disturbance and disorder. And the destruction that is talked of and expected,
let us think it, brothers, nothing other than an excess of disorder. For order
binds together, but disorder looses, whenever it comes to the one who bound it
together to loose the whole or to recast it.

9. Order also legislated for all the animals generation, and food, and the
countries fitting to each. And no one has seen a dolphin cutting a furrow, nor
an ox sliding through water; just as neither a sun waning in night, or being
filled, nor a moon lighting torches in day; the high mountains for the deer,

a rock is a refuge for the hares. He made the moon for seasons; the sun knew
its setting; night, and a human is drawn in by sleep, and the wild beasts
speak freely, and each seeks the food given by the one who created; day, and
the wild beasts are gathered, and a human hastens to the work; and we yield to
one another in order, by a law and reason of nature. I will add the greatest
and most proper. Order from a mixture of rational and irrational constituted
the human being, a rational animal; and it bound together mystically and
unspeakably the dust to the mind, and the mind to the spirit. And that it
might work some greater wonder in its own fashioned thing, the same both
preserved and dissolved. For it brought the one in, and withdrew the other, as
in a flow, and for the mortal it procured immortality through dissolution.
This also divided us from the irrational things =36.185= and settled cities,
and set laws, and honored virtue, and punished vice, and found arts, and
fitted pairing, and by the love concerning the things generated tamed life,
and something greater than the desire below and the fleshly, it established
the one concerning God.

10. And why must one speak of each? Order is mother of the things that are,
and safety; and the saying of the Word, it alone could well say, of the one
who fashioned all things, if it should take a voice: When the whole was being
given being by God and was being established, I was with him fitting together;
when he was preparing his own throne upon winds, and was making the upper
clouds strong; when he was founding the earth, and was setting as secure
springs the ones under heaven, and by the Spirit of his mouth granted every
power. But (for whose sake I went through all these things, and toward which
the discourse was long hastening), order even in the Churches marked off the
one thing to be a flock, the other shepherds; and the one to rule, the other
to be ruled; and the one to be as a head, the other feet, the other hands, the
other an eye, the other some other of the members of the body, toward the
harmony of the whole and the profit, either being surpassed, or surpassing;
and, as in the bodies, neither are the members torn from one another, but the
whole is one body composed from different things; nor is the operation of all
the same, even if the needing of one another is the same, unto a necessity of
goodwill and of inclination together; and neither does an eye walk, but it
leads; nor does a foot foresee, but it goes over, and transfers; nor does a
tongue receive voices, for that belongs to hearing; nor does hearing speak,
for that belongs to a tongue; and a nose is a sense-organ of smells; but a
throat tastes foods, says Job; and a hand is an instrument of giving and of
taking; and mind is leader to all, from which comes perceiving, and into which
perception. So also among us, the common body of Christ.

11. For we all are one body in Christ; and individually of Christ, and members
of one another. For the one rules and sits in front, the other is led and
directed, and neither do both operate the same, if indeed to rule and to be
ruled are not the same; and both become one into one Christ, being fitted
together and composed by the same Spirit. And among those ruled again, how
great the interval among those separated by education, and practice, and age;
=36.188= and among those who lead, how great the difference; and spirits of
prophets are subjected to prophets, Paul saying, do not doubt. And some, he
says, God set in the Church, first apostles, second prophets, third shepherds
and teachers; first because of the truth, second because of the shadow, third
because of the measure of the benefit, and of the illumination. And the spirit
is one, the gifts not equal, because neither are the vessels of the spirit.
For to one through the Spirit is given a word of wisdom and contemplation, to
another a word of knowledge or of revelation, to another faith firm and
without doubt, to another operations of powers and of higher wonders, to
another gifts of healings, helps, or rather protections, governings, or rather
trainings of flesh, kinds of tongues,

interpretations of tongues, the greater gifts, and the second, according to
the proportion of the faith.

12. Let us reverence this order, brothers, let us guard this. Let one be an
ear, another a tongue, another a hand, another some other of these. Let one
teach, another learn; another work the good with his own hands unto a sharing
with the one in need and who requires. Let one rule and be set forward;
another be justified through the service; and let the one who teaches do so in
an orderly way. For let two or three prophets speak, and in turn, and let one
interpret. And when another has been made clear, let the first withdraw. And
let the one who learns be in obedience; and the one who supplies, in
cheerfulness; and the one who serves, in eagerness. Let us not all be tongue,
the readiest thing; let us not all be apostles, let us not all be prophets,
let us not all interpret. Is it a great thing to speak concerning God? But
greater to purify oneself for God; since into a soul that works evil wisdom
will not enter. And we were commanded to sow unto justice, and to harvest
fruit of life, so that we may also be lit with the light of knowledge. And
Paul, through our loving the Lord, wants us to be known by the Lord, and
through being known, to be taught, and he knows this as a better road into
knowledge than the conceit that puffs up.

13. Is it a great thing to teach? But to learn is without danger. Why do you
make yourself a shepherd, being a sheep? Why do you become a head, happening
to be a foot? Why do you attempt to general, being ranked among soldiers? Why
do you pursue the great and unsafe gains of the sea, when it is possible to
farm land without danger, even if you would gain =36.189= less? And if you are
a man according to Christ, and your senses have been exercised, and the light
of knowledge is bright for you, speak God's wisdom, the one spoken among the
perfect, and the one hidden in mystery; and this, when you receive a time, and
are entrusted. For what do you have from yourself, which has not been given,
nor have you received? But if you are still an infant, and low-creeping in
thought, and not sufficient to step up to the higher things, become a
Corinthian, be fed with milk. Why do you need firmer food, which the members
do not consume, and which makes nourishment fail through weakness? Speak, if
you have something better than silence (for you have learned that to set a
watch on the lips is praised); but love quiet, where to be silent is better
than a word; and some things to say, some to hear; and some to praise, some
not to send away bitterly.

14. You do not know our contest, brothers, of us who sit solemnly here in
front, and legislate these things for you the many. And perhaps not even most
of us ourselves know (which is even worthy of tears) how every thought, and
word, and deed is weighed before God; and not before God only, but also before
most of humans; who are slow judges of their own things, but swift examiners
of the things of others, and they would more easily grant the greatest things
to others than the least to us; and even if they are more ignorant, they would
more quickly condemn us of impiety than a moderate ignorance of themselves.
You do not know how great a gift from God is silence, and not to have a
necessity of every discourse, so as by authority to choose the one, and to
flee the other, and to store up for oneself both word and silence. For by
nature every discourse is unsound and easily moved, and not having freedom
because of the opposing discourse; but the one concerning God, so much the
more, as the subject is greater, and the zeal more, and the danger harder. And
fearing what, in what shall we take courage? mind, or word, or hearing, the
danger tossing in these three? For it is hard to think, and impossible to
interpret, and more laborious to meet with a purified hearing.

15. God is light, and the highest light, of which a small outflow and radiance
reaching below =36.192= is all light, even if it appear exceedingly bright;
but you see, he treads our gloom, and he made darkness his hiding place,
having set between himself and

us, as Moses of old also the veil of himself and of the hardness of Israel, so
that a dark nature may not easily see the stored-away beauty, worthy of few,
nor, having easily obtained it, easily throw it away too through the ease of
the acquiring; but let light converse with light, always drawing toward the
height through desire, and let mind, having been purified, draw near to the
most pure, and let the one appear now, the other later, a prize of virtue and
of the inclination from here toward it, or rather of assimilation. For we see
now, he says, through a mirror and in a riddle, but then face toward face; now
I know from a part, but then I will know fully even as I was also fully known.
And how great our lowliness! and how great the promise! to know God as much as
we have been known. These things Paul the great herald of the truth, the
teacher of the nations in faith, the one who filled the great circle of the
Gospel, the one living by no other than Christ, the one who reached as far as
a third heaven, the spectator of paradise, the one longing through perfection
for the departure.

16. And Moses scarcely saw the hinder parts of God through the rock (whatever
these are, and whatever the rock is), and this after begging much, and
obtaining a promise; except that he did not see as much as he longed for, but
what escaped was more than what was presented to the imagination; Moses, the
god of Pharaoh, the one leading so great a people, the one who displayed so
great a power of signs. But you, whom have you fed from heaven? What water
from a rock have you given? What sea have you split with a rod? What people
have you led through as through the wet as dry? What enemies have you sunk?
Whom have you guided with a pillar of fire and of cloud? What Amalek have you
warred down by prayer, and stretching of hands, and by the cross being
prefigured from afar mystically, that it should be a disaster for you not to
grasp God perfectly, and because of this everything is shaken, and is carried
up and down? And since I remembered Moses, did you not learn from here a
sequence of gift and a law of order? If you are Moses, even enter inside the
cloud, and speak to God, and hear a voice, and receive a law, and legislate.
=36.193= If you are Aaron, go up together, but stand outside the cloud nearby.
If you are some Ithamar, or Eleazar, and third from Moses, or some one of the
council of elders and of the Seventy, stand still farther, even if in the
standing you have the third place. And if you are one of the people and of the
many, the mountain does not admit you, which if even a beast touches it, it
shall be stoned. Remain below, and hear only the voice, and this, having
consecrated yourself and purified, as you have been ordered.

17. And that I may train you through more, who of the priests filled the
hands? Moses. Who of those being filled was first? Aaron. And still before
these, who was the one for the things toward God? And who instead of a voice
to the people? And into the Holy of Holies who entered, except one? And this
one always? By no means; but once of the year, and there was a time when. And
who bore the tent, except the Levites? And these, as it had been ordered, some
the more honorable things of it, others the second, according to the worth of
those bearing? And since this also had to be guarded, who guarded, and how,
Some this side, others that; and not even undefined, nor unordered, nor of the
least things. But we, if we obtain a little bit of glory, or often not even
this, but as it happens, or we have practiced two or three sayings of
Scripture, and these in a truncated and senseless way (this is that, the
same-day wisdom, and the tower in Chalane, which well divided the tongues):
and we must go mad against Moses, and become Dathan and Abiram the insolent
and godless. Let us flee their self-will, and let us not imitate the madness,
nor receive the end.

18. And do you want me to set beside you another order too, and this one
praiseworthy, and this one worthy of the present memory, and of warning? You
see of the disciples of Christ, all being high, and worthy of the choosing,
one is called Rock, and is entrusted with the foundations of the Church,
another is loved more, and rests upon the breast of Jesus, and the rest bear
the preference. And when it was needed to go up onto the mountain

so that he might shine with the form, and show forth the Godhead, and strip
the one hidden in the flesh, who go up together (for not all are spectators of
the wonder)? Peter, and James, and John, those before the others both being
and being numbered. And to be present to him as he agonizes, and withdraws a
little =36.196= before the passion, and prays, who? The same again. This is
the preference of Christ. And the remaining good order and order, how great?
One thing Peter asks, another Philip, another Judas, another Thomas, another
some other, and not all the same thing, nor one all the things, but each in
part, and according to one. Of that which each needed, would you say? But that
at least, how does it seem to you? Philip wants to say something, and does not
dare alone, but takes Andrew too. Peter needs to ask something, and puts John
forward by a nod. Where is the harshness here? And where the love of rule? How
could they have shown more that they were disciples of Christ, the meek and
humble in heart, and a slave through us his own slaves, and sending up all the
glory to the Father in all things, that he might give us a type of good order
and of moderation? Which we are so far from honoring that I would be glad if
we were not even bolder than all, we who display this concerning the greatest
things even among the greatest.

19. Do you not know that the humble is not judged so much in the small things
(for it could come about even through display and a false shaping of virtue),
as it is tested in the greater? And humble-minded to me is not whoever
discourses small things concerning himself, and this toward few, and few
times, nor whoever addresses the more dishonored in a humble way, but whoever
speaks moderately concerning God; and knows some things to say, some to hold,
and of some to confess the ignorance, and yields the discourse to the one
entrusted, and accepts that there is someone more spiritual, and one who has
gone further in contemplation. For it is shameful, in clothing and diet, not
to choose the higher but the cheaper, and by calluses of knees, and springs of
tears, and still by fasts, and vigils, and lying on the ground, by toil and by
all buffetings to display the humble, and the awareness of one's own weakness;
but to be autocrat and tyrant in the discourses concerning God, and to yield
to no one at all, and to raise the brow above every teacher of the law; where
the humble has, together with good repute, also safety.

20. What then? Shall we be silent concerning God, and do you command this
(some one of the hotter ones interrupted)? And concerning what rather than
this shall we speak? And where shall we put the, His praise is continually in
my mouth; and, I will bless the Lord in every =36.197= time; and, My throat
will study truth; and, Behold, my lips I will not hinder; and will he say such
well-practiced and defined sayings? To whom one must meet with the words
gently and not harshly, teaching good order from here. I do not command to be
silent, O most wise one, but not to stand contentiously; not to hide the
truth, but not to teach contrary to the law. I am first of those who praise
wisdom, even of those occupied in the divine discourses, or at least wishing
to be occupied; and may I never set anything before this occupation, nor hear
wretched from Wisdom herself, as despising wisdom and instruction. Yet I flee
the unmeasured, and I chasten insatiability; and I accept being idle more than
is due, rather than over-curious, if it is not possible to escape both and hit
the moderate; and more timid than is due, rather than more bold. But you do
something similar, as if, when I hinder excess of food, you accused me of
hindering food altogether; or even of praising blindness, when I introduce
seeing soberly.

21. If you have a word of understanding, answer, he says, and no one is the
one who will hinder; and if not, let a bond lie on your lips. How much more
does this fit those unto teaching

ready? If you have a time, teach; and if not, having bound the tongue, loose
the hearing. Study in the divine things, but remaining inside the bounds.
Speak the things of the Spirit, and, if possible, not even anything else; and
speak rather than breathe (for this is good and inspired, to be always goaded
toward God by the memory of the divine things), but thinking the things you
were ordered. Do not be over-curious about the Father's nature, the
coming-to-being of the only-begotten Son, the glory and power of the Spirit,
the one Godhead and brightness in the three, the undivided both nature, and
confession, and glory, and hope of those who have believed. Hold to the words
you were raised with; let the discourse belong to the wiser. It is enough for
you to have the foundation; let the craftsman build upon it. It is enough that
the heart be strengthened with bread; yield the relishes to the rich. No one
of those who have sense judges you for not feeding sumptuously; but if you do
not set out bread, nor give water to drink, whether a disciple of Christ, or
some other, when you are able as far as these. Do not be swift in words,
wisdom commands you; do not stretch yourself alongside a rich man, being poor,
nor seek to be wiser than the wise. =36.200= Wisdom is also to know oneself,
but not to be lifted above, nor to suffer the same as voices, which fail
altogether if they are out-shouted through lack of measure. It is better,
being wise, to yield through gentleness, than, being in ignorance, to stretch
beyond through boldness. Let your speed be as far as the confession, if ever
you are required of this; but beyond this be more timid. For there slowness
has the danger, here speed.

22. What is terrible to you, if you do not lord it over every discourse, and
have the front seat upon every problem or question, but others appear than
you, either wiser, or bolder? Thanks be to God, that he both gives the
exceptional things, and knows how to save through the common. And this wonder
is not concerning discourses only, but also concerning the fashioning itself,
if ever you have considered. Neither among the creatures do the first places
belong to some, but to all, and it is a common grace of the one formation;
nor in the faith are the things that save of the more powerful, but of those
who will. What is fairer than air, fire, water, earth, rains, fruits both
cultivated and wild, shelter, clothing? The sharing of these is common, of
some even altogether, of others moderately; and no one is so tyrant as to
enjoy the common grace alone. He makes the sun rise with equal honor, he rains
on rich and poor; common the exchange of night and day, common gift health,
common bound of life, common measure and grace of body, common power of
senses; and perhaps the poor even has more, the giving more thanks upon these,
and enjoying the common things more sweetly than the more powerful do the
things from surplus. These then are common, and of equal honor, and marks of
God's justice; but the gold, and the transparent and loved stones, and as much
of clothing as is soft and overwrought, and the table that inflames and
maddens, and the excesses of possession, the labor of those who possess them,
are ornaments of few.

23. This I observe also concerning the faith. Common, law, prophets,
Covenants, oracles of Covenants, grace, training, perfection, passions of
Christ, new creation. apostles, Gospels, remaining of the Spirit, =36.201=
faith, hope, love toward God and from God, and =36.201= not according to
measure, as the gift of the manna once to ungrateful and senseless Israel; but
according as each wills, ascent, illumination, little the one from here, but
clearer the one hoped for; the greatest, the knowledge of Father, and Son, and
Holy Spirit, and confession of our first hope. What of these is greater? And
what more common? But the things beyond these, even if they have the more
honorable by rarity, yet by necessity they are second; for those without which
it is not possible to be a Christian are more useful than the things reachable
by few.

24. One is rich in contemplation, and is lifted above the many, and compares
spiritual things with spiritual, and writes down threefold, upon the breadth
of the heart, the word that builds all, and the one that builds many, and the
one that builds some instead of more or all, and he does not endure being
poor, and he enters into the depths. Let him go up, and be led, and be carried
by the mind, and, if he wills, as far as a third heaven, according to Paul;
only with reason and knowledge, and let him not fall through the lifting-up,
nor shed his feathers through the height of the flight. What envy of a
praiseworthy ascent? And what fall is such as to be pierced through by
lifting-up, and not to know the lowliness of human looking-upward, and how
much the highest of all still falls short of the true height?

25. But the other is small in thought, and poor in tongue, and does not know
turns of words, both sayings of the wise and riddles, and the objections of
Pyrrho, or suspensions, or oppositions, and the solutions of the syllogisms of
Chrysippus, or the evil craft of the arts of Aristotle, or the enchantments of
the eloquence of Plato, who have come into our Church badly, like certain
Egyptian plagues. This one too has that from which he may be saved. And
through what words? Nothing is richer than grace. You need nothing, he says,
to go up into heaven, that from there you may draw Christ; nor to go down into
the abyss, that from there you may pull him up from the dead, whether being
busy about the first nature, or the last economy. Near you, he says, is the
word; the mind has this treasure, and the tongue; the one believing, the other
confessing. =36.204= What is more concise than this wealth? what easier than
this gift? Confess Jesus Christ, and believe that he has been raised from the
dead, and you will be saved. For righteousness is even to believe only; but
complete salvation is also to confess, and to add to the knowledge the
boldness of speech. You seek something greater than salvation, the glory from
there and the brightness; for me even to be saved is greatest, and to flee the
torments from there. You walk the unbeaten and inaccessible; I the worn, and
which has saved many.

26. Nothing would be more unjust than our faith, brothers, if it fell only to
the wise, and those superfluous in discourse, and to the rational
demonstrations; and the many would have had to miss this too, as they miss
gold, and silver, and the other things, as many as are honorable below, and
much sought by the many; and the high and reaching few would have been dear to
God and proper; but the nearer, and reachable by the many, spat out and thrown
away. For not even the more moderate of humans would suffer this, so as not to
require the honors according to power, but to rejoice only in the surpassing
ones, much less God; of whom, though there are many things for which he is
wondered at, nothing is so peculiarly his as to benefit all. Do not dishonor
the customary, do not hunt the new, that you may be well thought of among the
more. Better a small portion with safety than a great one with unsoundness,
let Solomon teach you by the counsel; and, Better a poor man walking in his
simplicity (this too is one of the proverbs that has wisdom), the one poor in
discourse and knowledge, and leaning on the simple words, and being saved upon
these as upon a thin raft, than a crooked-handed fool, the one trusting a
demonstration of discourse together with ignorance, and emptying the cross of
Christ, a thing better than discourse, through the power in words, where the
weakness of the proof is a lessening of the truth.

27. Why do you fly toward heaven, being on foot? Why do you build a tower, not
having the things toward completion? Why do you too measure the water with the
hand, and the heaven with a span, and all the earth with a fist, great
elements, and measurable by the maker alone? Know yourself first; consider the
things in your hands; who you are, and how you were fashioned, and how

you were put together, that you might be an image =36.205= of God, and be
bound to the worse, and what it was that moved you, what the wisdom concerning
you and what the mystery of the nature; how you are measured by place, and
mind is not bounded, but remaining in the same goes over all things; how sight
is small, and reaching very far, and whether it is some reception of what is
seen, or a passage toward that; how the same both moves and is moved, being
steered through will; and what is a pause of motion; what the dividing of the
senses, and how through these the mind converses with the things outside, and
receives the things from outside; how it takes up the forms; and what is the
keeping of what was taken up, or memory; and what is the taking up again of
what has gone, or recollection; how word is an offspring of mind, and
generates a word in another mind, and how a thought is passed by a word; how a
body is fed through a soul, and how a soul through a body shares passion; how
fear fixes, and courage looses, and grief contracts, and pleasure diffuses,
and envy melts, and pride lifts, and hope lightens; how anger maddens, and
shame reddens through blood, the one when it has boiled, the other when it has
withdrawn; and how the characters of the passions are in the bodies; what is
the front seat of reasoning, and how it oversees all these and tames the
movements of the passions; how the bodiless is held by blood and breath; and
how the failing of these is a withdrawal of soul. Consider these, or some of
these, O human; for I do not yet say nature, or motion of heaven, and order of
stars, and mixing of elements, and differences of animals, and lowerings and
raisings of heavenly powers, and all the things into which the creative word
is partitioned, and reasons of providence and administration; and then I still
do not say, Take courage; but, Still fear to approach the higher things, and
those more above your power.

28. For especially every contentious and honor-loving discourse is a training
of the love of strife concerning the greater things; and we must, as we shape
children in the first habits, that they may flee the later wickedness: so also
with discourse, not to appear bold and untrained even concerning the small
things, so that we may not misuse the practice concerning the greater. For it
is easier from the beginning not to yield to vice, and to escape it as it
approaches, than when it is advancing to lift the head and appear above it; as
also to prop a rock from the beginning and hold it, or to push it back when it
is being carried. But if you are more insatiable, and cannot hold the =36.208=
disease, study these things, be in these; let your love of honor be emptied in
the things without danger.

29. You do not accept this, nor does your tongue have a bit, nor are you
stronger than the rush, but you must altogether be beside yourself, and not
yield to the first powers, if indeed even for those there is not a measure of
knowledge, and to be great more than is profitable; but do you not condemn the
brother, nor name the timidity impiety, nor go away having rashly condemned,
or despaired, you who promise gentleness. But here show yourself humble to me,
while it is allowed; here prefer the brother, not with your own harm, where to
condemn and dishonor is to cast out from Christ and from the only hope, and to
cut down together with the tares the hidden wheat, and wheat perhaps more
honorable than you. But the one thing, correct him, and this gently and
humanely, not as an enemy, nor as a severe physician, nor as knowing this one
thing only, the burning and the cutting; the other, condemn yourself. and your
own weakness. For what if, being blear-eyed. or suffering something else in
the eyes, you see the sun dimly? And what if the whole seems to you to turn,
being seasick perhaps, or drunk, and you take your own ignorance to be of
others? One must be turned about and suffer many things, before condemning
another of impiety.

30. It is not equal to cut down a plant, or some flower of the season, and a
human being. You are an image of God, and you discourse with an image of God,
and he who judges is himself judged; and you judge another's household-slave,
and one whom the other administers. So test your brother

as one who is yourself judged in the same measures. For this reason do not cut
quickly, nor alienate the member, it being unclear whether you will not
through this also harm something in the healthy part; but admonish, rebuke,
exhort. You have a rule of healing. You are a disciple of Christ, the meek and
humane, and who bore our weaknesses. And if the first time he resists, be
long-suffering; and if the second, do not despair. there is still a time for
healing; and if the third, become a humane farmer; still beg the Master not to
cut down, =36.209= nor to hate the unfruitful and useless fig tree, but to
turn it, and to tend it, and to put dung around it, the correction through
confession, and through shame into the open, and a more dishonored way of
life. Who knows if it will change, and bear fruit, and feed Jesus returning
from Bethany?

31. Bear something of the brother's stench, whether it is or seems, you who
have been anointed with the spiritual myrrh, compounded by the perfumer's art,
that you may give him a share of your fragrance. Vice is not a viper's venom,
so that together with being struck you are wrapped in pains, or even
destroyed, and for this you would have pardon fleeing the beast, or killing
it. But if you are able, heal him too; and if not, you have at least the safe
thing, in taking no share yourself of the wickedness. His passion is some
unpleasant smell, which your fragrance, overcoming, will perhaps drive away.
You would quickly have accepted something of this sort for the fellow-slave
and kinsman, such as Paul the zealot dared both to think and to say, that
Israel be brought in instead of himself, if it were possible, to Christ,
because of the sympathy; you who even from suspicion often alone cut off the
brother. The one whom perhaps you would have gained by kindness, this one you
destroy by boldness, your own member, for whom Christ died. If then you are
also strong, says Paul discoursing about foods, and you take courage in the
word and in the nobility of the faith, yet also build up the brother. Do not
destroy with your food the one honored by Christ with the common passion. For
even if the matter is other, yet the word of the exhortation is similarly
useful.

32. And there ought also to be a law among us, as among the wise of the
Hebrews of old, that some of the sacred books not be let loose to the young,
as not profitable to souls still unstable and more tender; so also that the
discourse concerning faith not be allowed to everyone, nor at every time, but
at some times, and to some, I mean to those not wholly careless and sluggish
in mind, or to those not too insatiable, and honor-loving, and hotter than is
due toward piety; and to station some here. where they will harm neither
themselves =36.212= nor others by being stationed; and that those moderate in
discourse, and truly orderly and sober, have the freedom in words; but to lead
the many away from this road, and from the love of talking and the sickness
that now holds them, and to turn them to some other more unendangered form of
virtue, where both the carelessness is less harmful, and the insatiability
more pious.

33. For if, as there is one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God, the Father
of all, and through all, and in all, so also there were some one road of
salvation, that through discourse and contemplation, and those who fell from
this would have to miss the whole, and be thrown away from God, and from the
hope from there: nothing would be more precarious, neither advising such
things, nor being persuaded. But if, as in human things there are many
differences of lives and choices, both greater and lesser, more splendid and
more obscure, so also in the divine, it is not some one thing that saves, nor
one road of virtue, but more, and of there being many mansions with God, and
of this thing talked of, lying even on everyone's tongues, there is no other
cause than this, that there are many roads carrying there, some more dangerous
and more splendid, others more humble and safer: why, leaving the safer ones

do we turn to this one only, so precarious and slippery, and I do not know
where it carries? Or is food not the same fitting for all, but another for
another according to the difference both of ages and of habits; but is the
same life profitable for all, or discourse? I at least would not say this, nor
would I add myself to those who say it. If then you are persuaded by me at
all, young and old, rulers of peoples and ruled, monks and those mixed in the
world, let the superfluous and useless ambitions farewell; and you yourselves,
drawing near to God through life, and conduct, and the more unendangered
discourses, will obtain the truth and contemplation from there, in Christ
Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory unto ages of ages. Amen.
