Of our father among the saints John, archbishop of Constantinople, Chrysostom,
a letter sent from exile to bishop Cyriacus. Come then, let me drain the sore
of your listlessness and scatter the thoughts that have gathered this cloud.
For what is it that holds your mind and grieves you and makes you distressed?
That the winter which has overtaken the churches is wild and gloomy and has
made all things a moonless night, and that each day it rises to a peak,
travailing with bitter shipwrecks, and that the utter destruction of the
inhabited world increases? I know this too, brother, and no one will deny it,
and if you wish I recast an image of the things that are happening, so as to
make the tragedy clearer to you. We see a sea heaved up from the abyss itself
below, sailors floating dead upon the waters, others going under, the planks
of the ships coming apart, the sails torn, the masts snapped, the oars flown
from the sailors' hands; instead of the tillers sitting on the decks, wrapping
their hands about their knees and wailing at the helplessness of what is
happening, crying sharply, mourning, howling, only lamenting; no heaven, no
sea appearing, but all a deep and lightless and gloomy darkness, not even
allowing them to see those nearby, and a great crash of the waves, and sea
beasts attacking those who sail from every side. Rather, how far shall we
chase the unattainable? For whatever image of the present evils I seek, the
word, being worsted, withdraws. But nevertheless, knowing even these things, I
do not despair of the better hope, considering the steersman of this universe,
who does not overcome the winter by art, but by a nod looses the storm. And if
not from the preludes nor at once, such is his custom, not to dissolve the
terrible things at the beginning, but when they have increased and come toward
the end and been despaired of by the many, then he works wonders and does the
unexpected, both displaying his own power and training the endurance of those
who fall into them. Do not then lie down; for one thing only is fearful, one
temptation, sin only. And I have not ceased continually chanting this word to
you. But all the rest is a fable, whether you speak of plots, or hatreds, or
deceits, or slanders, or revilings, or accusations, or confiscations, or
exiles, or sharpened swords, or the sea, or the war of the whole inhabited
world. For whatever these may be, they are both temporary and perishable and
happening in a mortal body and harming in no way the sober soul. Therefore the
blessed Paul, wanting to show the cheapness of both the good and the grievous
things according to the present life, disclosed the whole in one saying,
saying: for the things that are seen are temporary. Why then do you fear the
temporary things? The things that flow past after the manner of river streams?
For such are the present things, whether good or grievous. And another prophet
compared all human prosperity not to grass but to another cheaper matter,
naming it all together a flower. For he did not set down a part of it, such as
wealth only or luxury only, but taking all the things that seem to be splendid
among men under one name, that of glory, he thus brought on the image of the
grass, saying: all the glory of man is as the flower of grass. But is ill
fortune terrible and heavy? But see this too


again compared to another image, and despise this as well. For likening the
revilings and the insults and the reproaches and the jeers that come from
enemies and the plots to a garment grown old and to wool eaten through, he
said: do not fear the reproach of men, and do not be worsted by their
contempt, because they shall grow old as a garment and as wool they shall thus
be eaten by a moth. Let nothing then of the things that are happening disturb
you. But leaving off calling upon this man and that and running round the
shadows (for this is human alliance), continually call upon God, whom you
serve; he will only nod, and all things are loosed in one turn of a moment.
And if you have called upon him and it was not loosed, such is God's custom,
not from the preludes (for I will take up the word from before) to dissolve
the terrible things. But when it has risen to a peak, when it has increased,
when almost nothing of the wickedness of those who make war has been left
remaining, then all at once to change them toward the calm and to lead the
affairs themselves toward some unexpected states. For he is able to do not
only so many good things as we expect and hope, but also far more and
boundlessly greater. Therefore Paul also said: to him who is able to do beyond
all things, exceedingly beyond what we ask or think. For was he not able from
the preludes to prevent those three children from falling into that
temptation? But he did not wish, gathering much merchandise for them.
Therefore he allowed them both to be delivered into barbarian hands and the
furnace to be kindled to an unspeakable height and the kingly wrath to be
kindled more harshly than the furnace, and hands and feet to be bound with
much vehemence and to be thrown into the fire. And when all who were watching
them despaired of their salvation, then all at once and beyond hope the whole
wonder-working of God the master-craftsman appeared and shone out with much
excess. For the fire was bound, but the prisoners were loosed, and the furnace
became a house of prayer and a spring and dew and more august than royal
halls. And that all-devouring substance, which overcomes iron and stone and
masters every material, was conquered by the nature of hairs. And an
all-harmonious choir stood there of those holy ones, calling either creation
to this wondrous melody, singing, sending up thanksgiving praises, for the
things for which they were bound, at least on the enemies' part, for which
they fell from fatherland, for which they became captives, for which they were
robbed of freedom, for which they became cityless, homeless, and sojourners,
for which they spent their time in a strange land and a land of barbarians;
for this belongs to a grateful soul. And when both the wickedness of those who
made war had been completed (for what could they attempt after death any
longer?) and the athletes' part had been fulfilled and the crown had been
plaited and the prizes gathered for them and nothing any longer was lacking
for their good repute, then indeed the terrible things are loosed, and he who
kindled the furnace and delivered them to so great a punishment becomes a
wondrous praiser of those holy athletes and a herald of God's unexpected
working. And he sends out letters everywhere in the inhabited world, full of
much eulogy, narrating the things that had come to be, and becoming a
trustworthy herald of the wonders of God who works the unexpected. For since
he was an enemy and a foe, the things written were henceforth unsuspected even
among the enemies. Did you see the resourcefulness of God? Did you see the
wisdom? Did you see the unexpected? Did you see the loving-kindness and the
carefulness? Do not then be thrown into tumult nor be disturbed, but remain
continually giving thanks to God for all things, glorifying, calling upon him,
asking, entreating, even if countless tumults, even if countless disturbances
come upon you, even if storms, even if all things lie before your eyes, let
none of these disturb you. For our Master is not forestalled by the difficulty
of affairs, even if to the last


destruction all things have been brought down. For it is possible for him both
to raise those who have fallen and to turn back those who have gone astray and
to set right those who have been scandalized and to free those who have been
filled with countless sins and to make them righteous and to give life to
those who have been made dead and to make the things that have been razed
splendid and to renew the things that have grown old. For if he makes the
things that are not as things that are, and grants being to the things that
appear nowhere and in no way, much more will he set right the things that are
and have come to be. But many are those who go astray? Many those who are
scandalized? Often even already such things have come to be, but afterward all
things received the fitting correction, except if some remained incurably and
even after the change of affairs. Why are you disturbed and why do you wander
in mind, if this man has been cast out and that man has been brought in?
Christ was being crucified and Barabbas the robber was being led out, and the
corrupted people was crying that the man-slayer ought rather to be saved than
the savior and benefactor. How many do you think these things scandalized
then? How many did these things destroy for a time? Rather it is necessary to
lead the word higher. Did not this crucified one, as soon as he was born,
become a sojourner and a fugitive and with the whole household was he not
settled from the very swaddling clothes into a strange land, being led away a
journey of so great a distance into a barbarian country? And streams of blood
came to be from this occasion, and unjust murders and slaughters, and all the
untimely age was cut down as in array and war, and the children, being torn
from the breasts, were delivered to slaughter, and while the milk was still on
the throat the sword was driven through the neck and the throat. What is
harsher than this tragedy? And these things he who sought to kill him was
doing, and the long-suffering one endured such a tragedy being dared, so much
blood flowing. And he endured, being able to prevent it, displaying by some
unspeakable wisdom so great a long-suffering. And when he returned from the
barbarians' country and had grown, war was fanned from every side. And first
the disciples of John were envious and grudged, although that man was serving
his affairs, and they said: he who was with you beyond the Jordan, see, this
man baptizes and all come to him. For these words were already of men being
pricked and struck by envy and melting with the passion. Therefore also with a
certain Jew one of the disciples who had said these things contended and
boxed, moving the word about purifications and comparing baptism with baptism,
that of John with that of the disciples of Christ. For there came to be, he
says, from the disciples of John with a certain Jew a questioning about
purification. And when he had also touched the signs, how many the slanders?
Some called him a Samaritan and possessed by a demon, saying: that you are a
Samaritan and have a demon. Others a deceiver, saying: this man is not from
God, but deceives the crowd. Others a sorcerer, saying: that by the ruler
Beelzebul he casts out the demons. And they continually added these things and
named him an opponent of God and a glutton and a drunkard and a friend of
wicked and corrupted men. For the son of man came, he says, eating and
drinking, and they say: behold a man a glutton and a wine-drinker, a friend of
tax collectors and sinners. And when he was conversing with the harlot, they
called him a false prophet. For if he were a prophet, he says, he would have
known who this woman is who is speaking to him. And each day they sharpened
their teeth against him. And not Jews only warred against him thus, but not
even those who seemed to be brothers were sound toward him. But even from the
house the war was fanned against him. See then how even they were corrupted,
from the things which the Evangelist also, bringing them on, said: for not
even his brothers believed in him. But since you have remembered many being
scandalized and being destroyed, how many


do you think were scandalized at the time of the Cross? Of the disciples one
betrayed, others ran away, one denied, and when all had withdrawn he was led
alone bound. How many do you think, who had first seen him working the signs,
raising dead men, cleansing lepers, driving out demons, making loaves well up,
working the other marvels, were scandalized at that time, seeing him alone
being led away and bound, surrounded by cheap soldiers, and Jewish priests
following and making tumult and disturbance, and him seized having only the
enemies in the midst, and the traitor present and exulting for a time? And
what when he was being scourged? For it is likely that an infinite multitude
was present. For it was a conspicuous feast that gathered all, and a
metropolis that received the drama of the lawlessness, and in mid midday. How
many then do you think were present then and were scandalized, seeing him
bound, scourged, flowing round with blood, being examined by a governor's
court, and none of the disciples present? And what when those varied and
continual and successive comedies were coming to be against him? And sometimes
they crowned him with thorns, sometimes they put a cloak about him, sometimes
they put a reed in his hand, sometimes falling they worshipped him, moving
every kind of mockery and laughter. How many do you think were scandalized?
How many thrown into tumult? How many disturbed, when they struck him on the
head and said: prophesy to us, Christ, who is it that struck you? And they led
him and led him about all the day, jeers and revilings and mockery and
laughter in the midst of a Jewish theater. And what, when the servant of the
high priest struck him? And what, when the soldiers divided his garments? And
when he was led up onto the Cross naked, having the scourges on his back, and
was impaled, not even then were the wild beasts softened, but they became
rather more mad, and the things of the tragedy were stretched, and the things
of the mockery increased. Some said: he who destroys the temple and in three
days raises it. Others said: he saved others, himself he cannot save. Others
said: if you are son of God, come down from the Cross and we will believe you.
And what, when with the sponge they gave him gall and vinegar to drink and
insulted him? And what, when the robbers reviled him? And what, which I also
said before, that fearful and most lawless thing, when they said that that
robber and housebreaker, full of countless murders, was more worthy to be
demanded than he, and taking a choice from the judge they preferred Barabbas
not only not to crucify him, but even wanting to put a wicked glory upon
Christ? For they thought they could construct from these things that he was
worse than the robber and so lawless that he could be saved neither by
loving-kindness nor by the dignity of a feast. For they did all things for
this, so as to slander his reputation. Therefore they also crucified the two
robbers with him. But the truth was not only not overshadowed, but even shone
more greatly. And they also accused him of tyranny, saying: everyone who makes
himself a king is not a friend of Caesar, bringing a charge of tyranny against
him who had nowhere to lay his head. And they also slandered him for
blasphemy. For the high priest tore his own garments, saying: he has
blasphemed. What further need have we of witnesses? And what sort of death?
Not of condemned men? Not of accursed men? Not the most shameful? Not that of
those who have committed the last lawless deeds and are not even worthy to let
the soul go upon the earth? And the manner of the burial, is it not filled in
the part of grace? For someone came and asked for his body. Thus not even he
who buried him was of his own, of those benefited, of the disciples, of those
who had enjoyed so great a salvation, of all


who had become fugitives, of all who had sprung away. And that wicked
reputation which they constructed of the resurrection, saying that his
disciples came and stole him? How many did it scandalize? How many did it trip
up for a time? For the word prevailed then although it was fabricated and had
become bought with money. But nevertheless it prevailed with some, after the
seals, after so great a conspicuousness of the truth. For the multitude did
not even know the word about the resurrection, where even his disciples did
not know, he says, then, that he must rise from the dead. But nevertheless the
long-suffering God endured, managing all things unspeakably by his own wisdom.
Then after those days again the disciples, hiding, escaping notice, being put
to flight, fearing, trembling, continually changing place from place, thus
escaped notice. And after fifty days, beginning to appear and to touch signs,
not even thus did they enjoy freedom from fear, but after those things
countless scandals came to be among the weaker when they were being scourged,
the church being disturbed, they being driven, the enemies prevailing in many
places and making tumult. For when they had acquired much boldness from the
signs, then again the death of Stephen worked a harsh persecution and
scattered all and set the church in tumult, and again the affairs of the
disciples were in fear and again in flight and again in agony. And thus
through everything the affairs of the church increased, when it flourished
through the signs, when it appeared bright from the preludes. And one was let
down through a basket and thus escaped the ruler's hands, and an angel cast
others out and thus released them from the chains, and men of the marketplace
and craftsmen, receiving those driven by those in power, tended them in every
way. For purple-selling women and tentmakers and tanners, dwelling toward the
very outskirts of the cities, by the shore and the sea, and often they did not
even dare to appear in the midst of the cities. And if they themselves dared,
yet the hosts did not dare. And thus the affairs were woven, through
temptations, not through easements. And those who had been scandalized before
were after these things set right, and those who had gone astray were brought
back, and the things that had been razed were built more greatly. Therefore
also when Paul asked that the preaching go only through easement, the all-wise
and resourceful one did not bear it, but even when he called upon him often he
did not nod, but said: my grace is sufficient for you. For my power is
perfected in weakness. If then you wish even now to reckon the good things
with the grievous, you will see many that have come to be, even if not signs
and wonders, yet affairs like signs, unspeakable proofs of the much providence
and help of God. But, so that you may not hear all things from us with ease, I
leave this part to you, so that with accuracy you may set all things beside
the grievous, and occupying yourself with a good occupation you may thus lead
yourself out of the despondency; for from there too you will receive much
consolation.

Of John Chrysostom a letter to Cyriacus, being a bishop and himself also in
exile Come then again, let me drain the sore of your despondency and scatter
the thoughts that gather this cloud. For what is it that grieves you and makes
you distressed? Because the winter is harsh and the shipwreck that has
overtaken the church is bitter? This I know too, and no one will deny it. But
if you wish, I recast for you an image of the things that are happening. I see
a sea raging, heaved up from the abyss itself below, the sailors instead of
the tillers and the oars wrapping their hands about their knees, toward

the helplessness of the winter, at a loss, seeing neither heaven nor sea nor
dry land, but lying on the decks and lamenting and weeping. And these things
thus happen upon the sea. But now upon our sea the tumult is worse and the
waves more. But call upon our Master Christ, who does not overcome the winter
by art, but by a nod looses the storm. But if even often you have called upon
him and were not heard, do not grow faint; for the loving God has such a
custom. For was he not able to redeem those three children, that they might
not fall into the furnace of fire? But when they had been taken captive, when
they had been cast into a barbarians' country and had fallen from the
ancestral inheritance and had been despaired of by all and nothing any longer
was not left remaining, then Christ the true God suddenly worked the
wonder-working and scattered the fire. For the fire, not bearing the virtue of
the righteous, leapt outside and burned those of the Chaldeans whom it found
about the furnace. And henceforth the furnace was a Church for them, and they
called all creation, both the visible and the invisible, angels and powers,
and thus taking all together they said: bless the Lord, all the works of the
Lord. Do you see how the endurance of the saints both changed the fire into
dew and put the tyrant to shame? And he sends letters throughout all the
inhabited world. Great is the God, he says, of Sedrach, Misach, and Abdenago.
And see how much severity he set: if anyone should speak a word against them,
his house is to be confiscated and his goods plundered. Do not then be
despondent, nor grow faint. For I too, when I was being driven from the city,
took thought of none of these things, but said to myself: if the empress
wishes to exile me, she will exile me; the earth is the Lord's and the
fullness of it. If she wishes to saw me, she will saw me; I have Isaiah as a
pattern. If she wishes to hurl me into the sea, I remember Jonah. If she
wishes to throw me into a furnace, I have the three children who have suffered
this. If she wishes to throw me to the beasts, I have Daniel thrown among the
lions. If she wishes to stone me, let her stone me; I have Stephen the first
martyr. If she wishes to take my head, let her take it; I have John. If she
wishes to take my goods, let her take them; naked I came out of my mother's
womb, and naked I shall also depart. The Apostle exhorts me: God does not
accept the face of a man, and if I were still pleasing men, I would not be a
slave of Christ. And David arms me: I spoke before kings and was not ashamed.
They have constructed many things against me and say that I communicated with
some after they had eaten. If I did this, may my name be blotted out from the
book of the bishops and not be written in the book of the orthodox faith,
because I did no such thing, and may Christ cast me out from his kingdom. But
if they say this to me once and contend, let them depose Paul, who after
dining baptized a whole household. Let them depose also the Lord himself, who
after dining gave the communion to the apostles. They say that I slept with a
woman; strip my body and you will find the deadness of my members. But they
did all these things through envy. But it altogether grieves you, brother
Cyriacus, that those who exiled us speak freely in the marketplace and a
multitude of spear-bearers follows. But again remember the rich man and
Lazarus. Which of them was afflicted in the present age? And which enjoyed?
What did poverty harm that man? Was he not carried as an athlete and victor
into the bosom of Abraham? And what did wealth benefit that man who rested in
purple and fine linen? Where are the rod-bearers? Where the spear-bearers?
Where the gold-bridled horses? Where the


parasites and the royal table? Was he not led away bound as a robber into the
tomb, carrying the soul naked out of the world? And he cries with an empty
voice: father Abraham, send Lazarus, that he may wet the tip of his finger and
cool my tongue, because I am terribly fried. Why do you call Abraham father,
whose life you did not imitate? That man hosted every man into his own house,
but you did not take thought of one poor man. Is it not to mourn and weep that
he who had so much wealth did not become worthy of a drop of water? For since
he did not give a share of crumbs to the poor man he does not receive a drop
of water. In the winter he did not sow mercy, the summer came and he did not
reap. And this is the Master's economy, that he made the punishment opposite
for the impious and the rest for the righteous, that they may see one another
and recognize one another. For then each martyr will recognize his own tyrant
and each tyrant the martyr of his own whom he punished. And the words are not
mine. Hear Wisdom saying: then the righteous man will stand in much boldness
against the face of those who afflicted him. For just as a traveler walking in
heat, when he finds a clean spring, burning with thirst and constrained by
much hunger, sits down at a table that has all the foods, but is prevented by
someone more powerful from touching the table or enjoying the foods, has the
pain and the punishment exceedingly, because he sits at the table and cannot
enjoy the meats. Thus on the day of judgment the impious see the saints being
gladdened and cannot enjoy the royal table. For God, wanting to punish Adam,
made him work the earth opposite paradise, that every hour and day, seeing
that longed-for place from which he went out, he may always have the pain in
the soul. And if even here we do not meet one another, yet there no one is the
one who prevents us from living with one another. And we shall see those who
exiled us, as Lazarus the rich man and the martyrs the tyrants. Do not then be
despondent, but remember the prophet saying: do not fear the sneering of men,
and do not be worsted by their contempt. For as wool they shall thus be eaten
by a moth and as a garment they shall grow old. And consider also the Master,
how straightway from the swaddling clothes he was persecuted and was cast into
a barbarians' land, he who holds the world becoming a pattern for us of not
growing faint in the temptations. Remember the passion of the Savior, how many
insults he endured for us. For some called him a Samaritan, others possessed
by a demon and a glutton and a false prophet. For they say: behold a man a
glutton and a wine-drinker and that by the ruler of the demons he casts out
the demons. And what, when they led him to throw him down a cliff, and when
they spat into his face and gave him blows? And what, when they gave him
vinegar and gall to drink, when they struck his head with the reed? And what,
when they put the cloak about him and when they crowned him with thorns and
fell before him mocking and bringing every kind of jeer upon him? And when
those blood-drinking dogs led him about, and when he was led to the passion
naked and all the disciples left him? And one denied him, one betrayed him,
and the others ran away, and he stood alone naked in the midst of those
crowds; for it was a feast that gathered all then. And when they crucified him
as a wicked man in the midst of evildoers and he lay unburied, when they
brought him down from the cross, until someone asked for him, that he might
bury him; thus that he was not counted worthy of burial. And when they
declared a wicked blame against him, that his disciples stole him and he did
not rise. And remember also the apostles, that they were persecuted from every
side and were hidden in the cities


not being able to appear. How Peter was hidden at Simon the tanner's and Paul
at the purple-selling woman's, because they did not have boldness with the
rich. But afterward all things became smooth for them. Thus even now do not be
despondent. For I too heard about that nonsense Arsacius, whom the empress
seated on the throne, when he afflicted the brothers and the virgins who did
not wish to communicate with him. And many of them even died in prison because
of me. For that wolf in sheep's shape, who has the form of a bishop but is an
adulterer (for as the woman is called an adulteress who, while the husband
lives, is joined to another, so also this man is an adulterer), not of flesh
but of spirit; for while I live he seized my throne of the church. And I sent
these things to you from Cucusus, where the empress ordered us to be exiled.
And many afflictions happened to us along the road, but we took thought of
none. And when we came into the country of the Cappadocians and in
Taurocilicia, choirs, choirs of fathers met us, holy men, and not only so but
also multitudes of monks and virgins pouring out the immortal springs of
tears. And they wept seeing us going away in the exile and said to themselves:
it were better that the sun should have drawn in its rays than that the mouth
of John should have been silent. These things threw me into tumult and
afflicted me, since I saw all weeping about me. Over all the other things,
whatever happened to me, I did not take thought. And the bishop of this city
received us very kindly and showed much love toward us, so that, if possible,
he even yielded his own throne to us, if we were not keeping the rule. I beg
and entreat and grasp your knees. Cast off the mourning of your despondency
and have remembrance of us toward God. And be persuaded to write back to us.

Of the same, to Cyriacus a bishop, himself also being in exile Come then, let
me drain the sore of your despondency and scatter your thoughts that compose
this cloud. For what is it that grieves you and makes you distressed? Because
the winter is harsh and the shipwreck that has overtaken the churches is
terrible? This I know too. But if you wish, I recast for you an image of the
things that are happening, so as to make the tragedy clearer to you. I see a
sea heaved up from the abyss itself below, sailors floating dead upon the
waters, others having gone under, the planks of the ships coming apart, the
sails torn, the masts snapped, the oars flown from the sailors' hands; instead
of the tillers sitting on the decks, wrapping their hands about their knees
and wailing at the helplessness of what is happening, mourning, howling,
lamenting; no heaven, no sea appearing, but all a deep and lightless and
gloomy darkness, not even allowing them to see those nearby, and a great crash
of the waves, and sea beasts attacking those who sail from every side. Rather,
how far shall we chase the unattainable? For whatever image of the present
evils you will seek, the word, being worsted, withdraws. But nevertheless,
knowing even these things, I do not despair of the better hope, considering
the steersman of this universe, who does not overcome the winter by art, but
by a nod looses the storm. And if not from the preludes nor at once, such is
his custom, not to dissolve the terrible things at the beginning, but when
they have increased and come toward the end and been despaired of by the many,
then he works wonders and does the unexpected, both displaying his own


power and training the endurance of those who fall into them. Do not then lie
down; for one thing only is fearful, one temptation: sin only. And I have not
ceased continually chanting this word to you. But all the rest is a fable,
whether you speak of plots, or hatreds, or deceits, or slanders, or revilings,
or accusations, or confiscations, or exiles, or sharpened swords, or the sea,
or the war of the whole inhabited world. For whatever all these may be, they
are both temporary and perishable and happening in a mortal body and harming
in no way the sober soul. Therefore the blessed Paul, wanting to show the
cheapness of both the good and the grievous things according to the present
life, disclosed the whole in one saying, saying: for the things that are seen
are temporary. Why then do you fear the temporary things, the things that flow
past after the manner of river streams? For such are the present things,
