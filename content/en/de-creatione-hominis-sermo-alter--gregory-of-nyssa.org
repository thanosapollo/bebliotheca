HOMILY II
ON MAN

Wise Solomon, not in persuasive words of wisdom but having been made wise in
the teachings of the Holy Spirit, in the passages just now read to us,
honoring man, cried out saying, Man is great and a merciful man is precious.
But I was considering in vain by myself both what I had in my own mind and
what I had been taught from Scripture concerning man. For I myself was
reasoning how man is great, the perishable animal subject to countless
passions, from birth to old age drawing out a swarm of countless evils,
concerning whom it has been said, Lord, what is man, that you have been made
known to him? And the psalmist despises the animal as cheap, and the proverb
honors man as something great. But this perplexity of mine was resolved by the
history of the generation of man 42 being read. For we were hearing now that
God took dust from the earth and formed man. I found from this saying both
things, that man is nothing and man is great. If you look only at the nature,
he is nothing and worth nothing; but if you look at the honor with which he
was honored, man is great. What kind?

God said, Let there be light, and there was light. Compare the generation of
man and the generation of light. There he said, Let there be a firmament. The
great heaven is stretched above us, coming into being by the word of God;
stars and sun and moon and all things, as many as we behold with the eye and
as many magnitudes as we grasp with the mind, received being by a word. Sea
and earth and the ordering in them, kinds of animals of every sort, various
kinds of plants, all these came to be by the word. But man, how? It was not
said, Let there be man, as Let there be 43 a firmament, but you see something
more in man: above light, above heaven, above the luminaries, above all things
is the generation of man. The Lord God took: he deigns to form our body with
his own hand. He did not command an angel, the earth did not spontaneously
spit us out like cicadas, he did not say to do this and that to ministering
powers, but with his own hand he crafts artistically. He took earth. When you
look at what was taken, What is man? When you consider the one who formed, Man
is great: so both nothing because of the matter and great because of the
honor.

And God took. But how there in what precedes: And God made man, but here from
the beginning the generation of man? As if we had been taught nothing about
man, the history says, And God took dust from the earth and God formed man.
Some have already said that He formed was said of the body, and He made of the
44 soul. Perhaps the account is not outside the truth: for where it is said,
And God made man, in the image of God he made him, He made is said; but where
thereafter it recounts to us the bodily subsistence, it says He formed. And
the psalmist taught the difference of making and forming, saying, Your hands
made me and formed me. He made the inner man, he formed the outer. For forming
befits clay, and making befits that which is according to the image: so that
the flesh was formed, but the soul was made. Having spoken above, then, about
the subsistence of the soul, now he discourses with us about the forming of
the body. Hold this account also. And what is the other? That some things are
said in summary, and others are handed down to us according to the manner in
which they came to be. From above, then, that he also 45 made, here also how
he made. For if he had simply said that he made, you would have thought that
he made as the cattle, as the beasts, as the plants, as the grass. So that you
may flee the more savage


fellowship, the account handed down the distinctive craftsmanship of God
concerning you.

God took dust from the earth: there he said that he made, here how he made: he
took dust from the earth and formed with his own hands. Consider how you were
formed, reckon the workshop of nature: the hand that tanned you is God's. Let
what was formed by God not be defiled through wickedness, not be altered
through sin; do not fall out of the hand of God. You are a God-formed vessel
coming from God: glorify the one who made you. For you came into being for no
other reason than that you might be a fitting instrument of the glory of God;
and this whole world is for you, like some written book dictating the glory of
God, the 46 hidden and invisible, announcing to you through itself the majesty
of God, to you who have a mind for the contemplation of truth. So then
remember entirely what has been said. And God blessed them and said, Increase
and multiply and fill the earth. A double increase, one of body, one of soul.
But the increase of soul is the addition through lessons toward perfection;
the increase of body is the restoration from small to the fitting measure.

Increase, then, for the irrational animals has been said according to the
perfection of the body, according to the completion of nature, but for us
Increase according to the inner man, according to the progress that is toward
God. Such was Paul, stretching 47 forward to the things ahead, forgetting the
things behind. This is the increase of contemplations, the taking up of piety,
the stretching toward the more, our always reaching after the better things,
always leaving behind what has already come to be, seeking what is still
lacking of piety as something wanting. Such was Isaac, concerning whom that
testimony has been written, that Going forward he was exalted until he became
great. For he did not stand still, nor having grown a little remain, but he
always went forward, with great strides he crossed over to the works of
virtue: he crossed over with a great step to temperance, he stepped upon
justice, from there he went up to courage. Thus crossing over to the peak of
the height of the good, the righteous man is crowned. Increase, then, with the
increase according to God, the perfection according to the inner man.
Multiply: the blessing of the Church. Let theology not be confined 48 in one,
but let the gospel of salvation be proclaimed to all the earth. Multiply. Who?
Those who have come to be according to the gospel. And fill the earth. Fill
the flesh given to you for service with good works: let the eye be full of
seeing what is due; let the hand be full of good works; let the feet be able
to go for the visiting of the sick, going to what is fitting. Let the whole
constitution of our members be full of the workings according to the
commandments: this is Fill the earth. Thus the voices are common even with the
irrational animals, but they become distinctive when we use that according to
the image and that with which we were honored. For they increase bodily, but
we spiritually; and they fill the earth with multitude, but we fill with good
workings the earth yoked to us, that is, the bodily service.

Behold, I have given you every tree that has fruit in itself; it shall be for
you for food. Let the Church pass over nothing; all things are lawful. He did
not say, I have given you the fishes 49 for food, I have given you the cattle,
the reptiles, the four-footed. For it was not for the sake of these that he
created, he says, but the first legislation granted enjoyment of fruits; for
we were still thought worthy of paradise. And what is for you the mystery
lying beside in what is hidden? For you and for the beasts and for the birds
the fruits, he says, and the green herb and the grass, for you for food and
for the birds of heaven and for all the beasts of the earth. And yet we see
many of the beasts not fed on fruit: with what fruit does a leopard consent to
be fed? What fruit can nourish a lion? But nevertheless 50 these, subjected to
the law of nature, were fed on fruits. But when man changed his diet and went
outside the bounds given to him, after the flood the Lord, seeing men
unsparing


granted the enjoyment over all things.

These all you shall eat as green herbs of grass. By this concession the
remaining animals also received license to eat. From that time onward the lion
is raw-flesh-eating, from that time also vultures wait for carrion. For surely
vultures were not, at the same time as animals were born, scanning the earth:
for nothing yet had died of the things indicated or made by God, so that
vultures might be fed. Nature did not separate them, for it was in its prime;
nor had hunters consumed them, for there was not yet human craft; nor had
beasts torn them apart, for they were not yet raw-flesh-eating, and it is
customary for vultures to live from the dead. So there were not yet 51 dead
things, not yet stench, not yet such food of vultures, but all lived the diet
of swans and all grazed the meadows. And such as we see dogs often grazing
grass for the sake of healing (not because the food is connatural to them, but
because by some untaught teaching of nature the irrational animals come to
what is beneficial), such, reckon, were then also the raw-flesh-eating
animals, inventing grass as food for themselves, not plotting against one
another. But such as the restoration is going to be afterward, such was the
first generation. And man returns to his ancient condition, having put away
wickedness, this life of many cares, the slavery of the soul concerning the
things of life; having put all these away he returns to that life in paradise,
the one unenslaved to the passions of the flesh, the free, the one conversing
with God, the one sharing diet with angels.

These things, then, have been said to us, not because we wish to shut out the
use of foods given to us by God, but that we may bless the time that has
passed. What the life was like. How without need. How few things men needed
for the conduct of life. How the variety of diet was brought in for us by the
52 cause of sin. For when we fell from the true luxury that was in paradise,
we invented adulterated luxuries for ourselves; and since we no longer see the
tree of life nor take pride in its beauty, cooks were given to us for
enjoyment thereafter, and bakers and varieties of cakes and of incense and
such things consoling our fall from there. Just as the sick, when they are
thrown down by a violent illness, not being able to partake of the customary
enjoyment, receive from the physicians greetings with things to smell and such
things; for since they have fallen from the stronger things for enjoyment,
those who flatter their senses invent things commensurate with the weak. But
even now, wishing to lead ourselves in imitation of the life in paradise,
fleeing this much-material enjoyment of foods, let us lead ourselves as far as
possible toward that life, using fruits and seeds and the things from
tree-fruits for conduct, and let us dismiss the surplus of these as not
necessary. For they are not 53 abominable because of the one who created, nor
yet are they to be chosen because of the ease of the flesh.

And God rested from all his works on the seventh day. Let the arithmeticians
speak, then, and those who have occupied themselves with such things and have
put forward the wisdom concerning these as something great: how the number six
is kindred to the creation of the world, that it is much-generating,
generating many figures of numbers from itself and perfect in its own parts,
and as many things as arithmeticians recount about the hexad in school
lectures, if anyone has leisure, let him collect them for himself. Let them
speak also about the hebdomad, that there is a certain barrenness in the
seventh; for the number of seven neither generates from itself nor is born
from another. And so that I may not bring the account down into much-material,
running past the discourse toward you, I will proceed to the more reasonable
things. But if anyone also has some experience concerning these, let him know
that the treasure of these also 54 is laid up with us; so that if arithmetic
is great, here are the seeds of arithmetic. But we do not † think with these,
by being of the wisdom of the world, having stolen small things here,
ourselves with the treatises as with great


to exalt ourselves †. So then, in order to show them that the things most
eagerly pursued among them are contemptible to us, let us pass the account
concerning these in silence; and at the same time the contemplation concerning
these is not easily grasped by every people. And the Church is present not
expecting a hearing of paradoxical words, but seeking the solutions of
contemplations for edification.

The seventh day, then, has been honored, the sabbath. The seventh number is
precious among the Jews, in which are the booths, the trumpets, the day of
atonement. A seventh year 55 is precious among them, the one called the year
of release. For they were permitted to work the earth for six years, but in
the seventh year to be content with what grew of itself, when they had the
land of promise. For six years the Hebrew was a slave and in the seventh he
was released from slavery. In the seventieth year the captivity among them was
loosed. And then also our things: Seven times, he says, the righteous will
fall and rise. Thus we appropriate the hebdomad. The seventh from generation
did not see death, a mystery of the Church. The seventh from Abraham, Moses,
received the law: a change of life, a dissolving of lawlessness, an entrance
of righteousness, a sojourning of God, an ordering of the world, a legislation
of things to be done. In the seventy-seventh generation from Adam, Christ
appeared. Peter knows the mystery of the hebdomad: How often shall my brother
sin against me and I forgive him? Up to seven times? He knows little; he has
not yet learned the mystery; for he is still 56 a disciple. Up to seven times?
The teacher did not depart from the seven. The disciple spoke as he knew, the
Master surpassed with wealth. How often shall he sin and I forgive? Why did he
not say, Up to six times? Up to eight times? but, Up to seven times? Why did
the Lord not say, Up to a hundred times a hundred, but multiplied the number
seven? Neither did Peter ask another nor did the Lord go outside the
hebdomadic canon. Peter observed that it is an ancient canon of tradition,
that the hebdomad has a certain emphasis of forgiveness of sins, of perfect
rest, of which the sabbath is a sign, the seventh day from generation. Peter,
Seven times; the Lord, Up to seventy times seven. Sins are avenged seven
times: Not so; everyone who kills Cain will pay sevenfold vengeance. Not eight
there either, but Seven. Why? Wait a little and you will find the mystery. The
first sin is avenged seven times; the second murder of Lamech seventy times
seven: Because from Cain it is avenged seven times, but from Lamech seventy
times seven. And if there is forgiveness from Peter seven times corresponding
to the vengeance 57 of Cain, concession from the Lord seventy times seven as
the condemnation of Lamech seventy times seven. As great as the fall, so great
also the gift; where the sin is little, little also the forgiveness: For to
whom he forgave little, he also loves little; For where sin abounded, grace
superabounded. What then is the mystery? The seventh day is called the age of
judgment, in which the sinner seven times, the one in excess seventy times
seven will be avenged; the righteous seven times will be honored, the
exceedingly righteous seventy times seven. The love of mankind of God now
through riddles hints to us the things to come, but then at the presence the
truth will be clear and made distinct. For he will show who is worthy of what.
Sevenfold, then, the recompense owed to the sinners the Lord will forgive us
here, having been propitiated by us through 58 confession and repentance. So
that, knowing that fearful day, the recompense stored up for those who have
sinned, the measuring back according to the worth of the things transgressed,
taking it in advance through repentance let us dissolve the sins, so that
there we may escape the painful multiplication of the recompense. This seventh
day, then, is a type of that seventh of the consummation of the age.

And God rested from all the works. No longer works of the world on that day,
no longer marriages, no longer traffics of contracts, agricultures no longer,
but all the earth in terror, all creation in contest, sweat without
consolation, and the


righteous in contest, what vote then will be brought forth concerning them.
And Abraham will be in agony then, not that he may be condemned to Gehenna,
but in what rank of the righteous he will be stationed: first or second or
third. The Lord from the heavens and the heavens are torn, power is revealed,
59 all creation trembling. Who is without fear? Not even angels. They too
stand by to give account to God. But nevertheless the glorious appearing casts
trembling upon all. Do you not hear Isaiah saying, If you open the heaven,
trembling will seize mountains from you? He means that day in which the
mountains cower, in which the sea is frozen, in which creation will be at
leisure, nature is put to death, every method of dialogues is idle toward the
sojourning from the heavens. Then the righteous are snatched up, then clouds
are a chariot of the righteous, then angels escorts of the righteous, then the
righteous as stars being taken up from earth to heaven; but the sinners bound,
fettered by the weight of their own sins, will fall below, of evil conscience.
That seventh, then, is figured by this seventh. And God rested from all the
works. No longer the things of this life on that day. Youthful desires have
gone to sleep; no longer a purpose concerning marriage, desire of childbearing
60 no longer, no care at all about gold. You have forgotten, O lover of money,
the purse; you have forgotten the land, O many-propertied; you have forgotten
glory, O glory-mad; all those things have flown from the mind. And the soul is
toward the thing that frightens, toward the expectation of the terrible things
hanging over. For fear drives out all the passionate thinking that dwells in
our souls. Where there is fear of God, all the stains of passion have been
driven out from our mind. This seventh is a type of that seventh.

God took, then, dust from the earth and God formed man. The world-generation
was not completed, the sequence was not cut off, so that our narrative might
be introduced, but it was said, God made man, and, He rested from all the
works; and since he took leisure, he teaches us how he made: God took dust
from the earth. When you hear dust, be trained in fearlessness toward men: Do
not 61 accept a face. Do not think great things of yourself. When thoughts
come to you, a swelling of the heart, producing inflammation, let the reminder
of the creation enter you, how you were created: God took dust from the earth
and God formed man. When can you forget yourself? Then forget yourself, when
you withdraw from the earth. But if you never separate from the earth, but are
grown together with the earth, you walk upon the earth, you rest upon the
earth, you judge upon the earth, everything you do whether great or small upon
the earth, you have near the reminder of your own humiliation. You were
insulted and you are wrathful? Whence is the wrath to you? From the dishonor.
You did not accept to hear of low birth? Immediately the wrath boiled in you?
You contend to say something worse than what you heard? Down with the gaze,
and the wrath has ceased. Look at the earth and consider: he called me of low
birth, me who came from earth? He said less than what I am; for he did not say
from the earth but from a human. But how much more honorable is an ensouled 62
human than earth that is trodden. And I look at the ancient mother, the earth,
so that it is not an insult to have come from a slave, but an honor to have
been born ensouled. And the one thinking to insult me escaped notice having
honored more what he insulted; for I have understanding of my own nature: I
know who I am and whence I am. Thus to remember that we have been generated
from earth never allows wrath to keep watch. Let the earth be an ally to the
reasoning, always present and reminding. When you run upon desires, consider
how you go away. If you consider that you will be dissolved into earth, the
madness of desire has ceased: Earth you are and to earth you shall go.
Remember that after a little these members now pulsing, or this present desire
of the flesh after a little will not be, the members having been dissolved and
resolved into earth. Remember the nature, and every impulse toward the evil is
loosed. Let this reminder be for us a phylactery against every sin.


God formed man, dust from the earth. Good is the humiliation, connatural the
reminder. If he had said that 63 God formed man, taking from heaven, when
would we look there, so as to be reminded of the nature? From things at hand
the reminder of our nothingness is present to us, from the things we tread.
Bend down to the earth and understand that from the kindred of the earth, the
discarded of the things in you, you were shaped. What is more contemptible
than we? What of us is more worthy of making nothing of ourselves? You saw
someone thinking great things, clothed in a flowery garment, his hand flashing
with a ring, wearing a bezel of the precious stones and thinking great things
by this, clothed in silken threads, † having servants with hair let down and
these blond, and swaggering with affectations, wearing golden collars, sitting
on a silver seat, walking greatly, thinking greatly, 64 speaking greatly on
the multitude of servants, of the flatterers whom he drags along, on the
costly table, on the greetings which all bring to him in the marketplace,
those rising from the benches, those meeting him in advance, those escorting,
the rod-bearers. When you see these rulers being led forward under a herald
with a high voice, when you see them frightening so-and-so and scraping
so-and-so, confiscating another and handing that one over to death, do not
fear the things seen, do not be terrified at the things that have come to be †
the same one commanding, let not the appearance astonish you. Consider that
God formed man, dust from the earth. If it is something else, fear something;
but if dust from the earth, despise.

And God formed man. Does not the very voice of He formed hint to us some
craftsmanlike 65 activity of God concerning man? God formed. Did he then form
thus, as those who form clay things, as those who melt bronze? But the forming
of a statue and the forming of plaster has the imitation as far as the
surface. Did you see how the statue stands with a certain character? Either
the statue of the soldier shows wrath, or the bronze a womanly disposition
when it is shaped into a woman, or the other things, as many as art is able to
introduce through imitation by means of the character into the stamp. But the
forming of God is not such, but He formed man, and his creative activity
organized everything, having gone through to the depth within. If I had so
much leisure remaining that I might show you the construction of man, you
would also have learned from yourself the wisdom of God concerning you, that
in truth man is a small cosmos, and they did well who honored him with this
name. How many treatises 66 have been spent on this matter. The physiologies
of the physicians, the observations of the trainers, either about proportion
or about the symmetry of the members toward one another or about abundance of
fleshiness; all these enter into the † forming concerning man. And whence is
so great a discourse to me, so that I might be able to say with accuracy as
many things as are contained in one voice of He formed? But the things at
hand, even if I do not say them, you know. God formed upright. He gave you
this exceptional forming compared with the remaining animals. Why? Because he
was also going to assign you the exceptional activity. For the others are
cattle and toward what they are naturally fitted, toward these they have the
construction. The sheep was generated to graze, it has the head below,
nodding, to look at the belly and the things under the belly, because for
those the end of happiness is filling of the belly and enjoyment of pleasure.
But man no longer looks 67 to the belly, but to the things above. A high head
is his, that he may look at the kinship above, eyes not nodding toward the
earth. Do not then make yourself contrary to nature, do not scan the earthly
things, but the heavenly, Where Christ is; If you were raised with Christ, he
says, seek the things above, where Christ is. Thus you were formed. The very
forming is a school of the end toward which you came to be. You came to be
that you might look at God, not that your life might be dragged upon earth,
not that you might have the cattle-like enjoyment, but that you might
accomplish the heavenly citizenship. For this reason


The eyes of a wise man are in his head, says the wise Ecclesiastes. And whose
eyes are not in the head? But in the head, that is, that they may look at the
high things. But the one not looking at the high things but at the earthly has
eyes dragged to earth. 68 Eyes, then, sit † circular of a head lying upon the
shoulders. Nor is it itself sunken, lest it be low, but it lies upon a notable
support, the neck. Above is the head and the eyes twin casts. Was one not
enough, tell me? But two eyes successors of one another, so that the
dissolution of the one may have the consolation of the other. Then also the
visual coming forth from one is weaker, but the current combining from two
springs becomes more vigorous; for the sight going forth at once both rests
upon the partition of the nose, and at once protruding is united. For like
some channel from each of the sights this visual proceeds from here and that
from there, but from afar it is united by the meeting, and being united it
becomes stronger. What is the proof that it is united? Do you not see the old
men, that they do not see the near things? For since the visual is weak, being
split it does not see 69 the near, but where the meeting of the sight, as upon
some flood happening according to the sight, the taking up of the sensibles
becomes more vigorous.

And how many guards around the eye. A tunic within, and this is not enough.
For one could not be thick. If it were thick, it would be a covering of the
sight. But it needed to be transparent and light. Therefore one is
translucent, one rare; the one crystal-like, the other horn-like; the one
covered in front stronger, the one within rarer, so as not to hinder the
entrance. The third crystal-like, so that it may have in itself the impression
and the translucence. A projection, the eyelid; 70 this is a covering and a
shelter and as it were a dwelling and a phylactery. The hand could shelter.
Until the hand arrived, the eye would already have been darkened. But now the
eyelid lies near, it lies upon from above. At once it perceived the harm and
put forth the covering. For this reason also the pupil sitting under a curtain
is not always easy to attack. The eye wishes this alone of the members in us
to be untouched, being covered under a curtain, the eyelid. Points of the
hairs have been put around. Why? So that the intertwining of the eyelids of
the upper with the lower may become more precise. For the hairs intertwining
with one another are like bonds. Then they also hinder from afar the small
animals and do not allow the downy things to approach the pupil, which can be
darkened from every side. There is a kind of household guard lying around us
through the hairs, at once granting comeliness by the outline, and at once
furnishing the benefit. 71 An eyebrow projected above, a household bulwark, so
that it may direct the visual. What is the proof? When you wish to look from
afar and, hollowing the hand, you stretch it above the eyebrows, why does this
happen? So that the part of the sight being dispersed upward may not be
scattered in vain † being directed from the projection of the hand and being
pushed forward it may make the correction of the visual more precise and more
vigorous, and the passage of the seeing is directed by the projection
according to the hand. † For this reason eyebrows at once directing the eye,
and at once not allowing the sweat from the labors to flow upon and become a
hindrance to the sights, for this reason the eyebrows wall off †. What
vinedresser can so well hang the vine and put a brow around it, so that it is
hard to attack, so that the place is not easy to run upon for the neighboring
torrents, as the Lord made 72 the putting around of the eyebrows? Which he
outlined for us moon-shaped, from one meeting at the nose dividing to each
side, so that the sweat flowing around this way and that may furnish no
occupation to the farmer nor, wiping off the sweat, lead the hand away from
the vine, but at once the sweat may flow through its own channels (the
craftsman of old having channeled it out upon the unused things), and at once
the eye may accomplish its own


activity.

If we should wish to speak † only of the things about us artistically made by
God, the whole day will fail us. But from this one thing take the notion of
all. And send us on our way with prayers, as we hasten upon some necessary
road, so that having been brought back to you quickly we may also pay the debt
of the things remaining, by the grace of the Lord who has ordered all things
concerning us, who created us by his own grace, because to him is the glory
and the power unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
