On the precious Cross.
=59.675=
1 A feast of the Cross is set forth today: let the choir of angels leap; a
spectacle of the Cross is coming: let the people of mortals worship; the
divine power of the Cross is raised up: let the multitude of demons be put to
flight. But, O divine Cross, the unspeakable spectacle of the angels, the
boast of mortals, the royal scepter! For I will converse with you as with one
living, you who bear the joyful things and the victory-tokens of our
salvation. Through you the gates of paradise, once shut, are opened again;
through you the power of death has been shattered; through you Hades has been
despoiled; through you those who were put to death have been counted worthy of
life; through you the thief dances in paradise and rejoices; through you the
heavenly things have been joined to the earthly; through you knowledge of the
truth; through you baptism of regeneration has been granted; through you
forgiveness of sins has been given as a gift to men; through you the gift of
the Holy Spirit has been sealed; through you Churches, being marked with the
sign, are accounted houses of God; through you we partake of the body and
blood of Christ; through you we are counted worthy to become sons of God and
heirs of the kingdom of heaven; through you festivals and feasts are made
bright and shine. And what tongue could have the strength to recount how many
and of what kind are the things Christ our God has bestowed through you upon
the race of men? Since then, O sanctified and feast-loving multitude, we have
come to know these things through the word, let us with white hearts and pure
mouths embrace the venerable and life-giving wood, on which Christ our God was
fastened, and divine palms were pierced together with nails, on which the
slayers of the Lord drove the spear through the side, and they made him drink
the taste of vinegar mixed with gall; and as they passed by and went along
they shook their heads, mocking and saying, Woe! He saved others, himself he
cannot save; if he is the Son of God, let him come down now from the Cross,
and we will believe him.

These things the assembly of the Hebrews, the ungrateful and hardened, who had
enjoyed the benefit of the divine wonders; but let us faithfully worship the
life-giving sufferings of Christ, the crucifixion, the burial, the three-day
rising from the dead; so that we too may rise with him and be glorified with
him, if indeed we suffer with him, as the vessel of election, the divine Paul,
declared beforehand. In these things and besides these the all-holy Cross that
is being celebrated summons us today, and it offers these incorruptible and
ever-living foods alike to all, in houses, in marketplaces, by night, by day,
in cities, in deserts. Let us then receive brightly her who through so many
things grants us her own good, according to the Lord's word, not looking
gloomy like hypocrites, but showing without artifice the cheerfulness of the
soul. And do not think I need so great a struggle in the exhortation
concerning fasting, as I need that we not fall today into the evils of
drunkenness; for fasting, both through custom and through shame before one
another, most people accept; but I fear drunkenness, which the lovers of wine
claim as a kind of ancestral inheritance. For as those who set out on long
journeys, so some of the foolish today drink wine toward the five days of the
fasts. Who is so foolish as, before he has even begun to drink, to rave with
the ravings of the drunken? Do you not know that the belly does not keep a
deposit safe? The belly is a most untrustworthy exchanger, an unguarded
treasury of many =59.676= things laid up, holding on to the harm, yet not
preserving what was deposited. See that tomorrow, when you come from
drunkenness, what has now been read may not be said to you: This is not the
fast that I have chosen, says the Lord. Why do you mix things that cannot be
mixed? What fellowship has fasting with drunkenness? What communion has
wine-bibbing with self-control? What agreement has the temple of God with
idols? For they are a temple of God in whom the Spirit of God dwells; but they
are a temple of idols who through drunkenness receive the refuse, the satiety
of licentiousness.

This day is the porch of the fasts; and surely he who has been defiled in the
vestibules is not worthy to enter into the holy things. No household servant,
wishing to propitiate his own master, uses the master's enemy as advocate and
reconciler. Drunkenness is enmity toward God; but fasting is the beginning of
repentance. If then you wish through confession to return to God, flee
drunkenness, lest it make the alienation harsher for you. Yet abstinence from
foods is not of itself enough for the praiseworthy fast, but let us fast a
fast that is acceptable, well-pleasing to God. True fasting is alienation from
evils, restraint of the tongue, abstinence from anger, separation from
desires, from slander, from falsehood and perjury; the lack of these is true
fasting. In these things, then, fasting is a good.

Let us take our delight in the Lord in the study of the oracles of the Spirit,
and in the taking up of the saving laws, and in all the teachings that set our
souls right. But let us guard ourselves from the hidden famine, concerning
which the prophet also prays saying: The Lord will not starve the soul of the
righteous; and, I have not seen the righteous forsaken, nor his seed seeking
bread. For he would not have spoken of sensible bread, he who knew that the
children of the patriarch Jacob went down to Egypt for bread, but he speaks of
the spiritual food, according to which the inner man is perfected. Let not the
fast threatened against the Jews come upon us also: For behold, days are
coming, says the Lord, and I will bring upon this land a famine, not a famine
of bread, nor a thirst of water, but a famine of hearing the word of the Lord;
which the just Judge brought upon them for this reason, because he saw their
mind being famished in the lack of the teachings of the truth, while their
outer man was being overfattened and made flesh. All the days that follow,
then, the Holy Spirit will feast you with morning and evening gladness. Let no
one willingly be left out of the spiritual banquet; let us all share in the
sober practice of the cakes. Let the house too at last be quiet from the
countless tumults, and from the smoke and the smell of roasting, and from
those running up and down, and serving the belly as if it were an inexorable
mistress. Even tax-collectors at any rate sometimes allow their subjects a
little freedom. Let the belly too give some opportunity to the mouth, let it
give it; let her who is always demanding and never ceasing, who takes today
and tomorrow forgets, make a five-day truce with us. When she is filled, she
philosophizes about self-control; when she has been emptied by digestion, she
forgets the teachings. Fasting does not know the nature of a loan; the table
of the one who fasts does not smell of interest; the ancestral
interest-breeders of the faster do not throttle an orphan, wrapping around
like snakes. And otherwise, fasting is an occasion for gladness. For as
=59.677= thirst makes the drink sweet, and hunger that has gone before
prepares the table as pleasant, so also fasting brightens the enjoyment of
foods.

For inserting itself in the middle and cutting the continuity of luxury, it
will make the partaking appear desirable to you, as if it were a thing that
had been away.

2 So if you wish to make the table desirable for yourself, receive the change
that comes from fasting. You, by being so exceedingly wrapped in luxury, have
without noticing dimmed luxury for yourself, and by love of pleasure you are
destroying pleasure. For nothing is so desirable as not to become contemptible
through the continuity of enjoyment; but of things whose possession is rare,
the enjoyment is much sought after. Thus also he who created us, through the
interchange in life, contrived that the grace of the things given should be
lasting for us. Do you not see that the sun too is brighter after the night,
and waking sweeter after sleep, and health more desirable after the experience
of the opposites? And a table too, then, is more gracious after the fast,
alike for the rich and those with well-spread tables, and alike for those
whose diet is simple and improvised. Fear the example of the rich man;
lifelong luxury delivered him over to the fire. For it was not injustice, but
the luxurious way of life of which he was accused, and he was fried in the
flame of the furnace. In order then that we may quench that fire, there is
need of water. And fasting is not useful only toward the things to come, but
it is more profitable even to the flesh itself; for extreme good condition has
reversals and collapses, nature giving way and being unable to bear the weight
of the good condition. See then that you, now spitting at the water, may not
later desire a drop, like the rich man. No one has rioted from water; no one's
head has ever ached, being weighed down with water; no one has needed
another's feet while living with water-drinking; no one's hands have been made
useless by being watered with water. For the fault in digestion necessarily
follows those who live in luxury; this works the violent diseases in the
bodies. The color of the one who fasts is solemn, not blossoming into a
shameless redness, but adorned with a temperate paleness; a gentle eye, a
composed gait, a thoughtful face, not insulted by unbridled laughter, measure
in words, purity of heart. Remember the saints from of old, Of whom the world
was not worthy, who went about in sheepskins, in goatskins, being in want,
afflicted, ill-treated; imitate their way of life, if indeed you seek their
portion. What gave Lazarus rest in the bosom of Abraham? Was it not fasting?
And the whole life of John was one fast; he had no bed, no table, no arable
land, no ploughing ox, no grain, no baker, nor anything else of the things
that belong to life. For this reason there is none greater among those born of
women than John the Baptist. And Paul, among the other things, fasting also,
which he numbered among his boasts on behalf of affliction, raised him to the
third heaven. And the chief point upon what has been said: our Lord, having
fortified by fasting the flesh which he assumed for our sake, thus in it
received the assaults of the devil, both teaching us to anoint and train
ourselves by fasts for the contests in temptations, and as it were giving the
adversary a hold through the lack. For he was unapproachable to him because of
the height of the Godhead, unless through the lack he stooped toward the
human. Yet when he was returning into the heavens he took food, confirming the
nature of the risen body.

But you do not cease overfattening yourself and making yourself flesh, while
wasting the mind in lack of the saving and life-giving teachings, you make no
account. Or do you not know that, as in a battle-line the alliance of =59.678=
the one works the defeat of the other, so he who has sided with the flesh
overthrows the spirit, and he who has shifted his post toward the spirit
enslaves the flesh? For these things are opposed to one another. So if you
wish to make the mind strong, tame the flesh through fasting. For this is what
the Apostle says, that As much as our outer man is decaying, so much is our
inner being renewed; and, When I am weak, then I am strong. Will you not
despise perishable foods? Will you not take desire for the table in the
kingdom, which the fasting here will altogether prepare in advance? Or do you
not know that by the immoderation of satiety you are preparing for yourself a
fat tormenting worm? For who, in abundant food and continual luxury, has
received any communion of a spiritual gift? Moses, receiving a second
legislation, needed a second fasting; the Ninevites, unless the irrational
creatures too had fasted with them, would not have escaped the threat of
overthrow. Whose limbs fell in the wilderness? Was it not of those who desired
the eating of flesh? Those, as long as they were content with the manna and
the water from the rock, were conquering the Egyptians, they traveled through
the sea, there was none among their tribes who was weak; but when they
remembered the meats, the pots, and turned back to Egypt in their desires,
they did not see the land of the promise. Do you not fear the example? Do you
not shudder at gluttony, lest somehow it shut you out from the hoped-for good
things? But the wise Daniel would not have seen the visions, unless fasting
had made his soul more translucent. For from thick food, as it were sooty
vapors being sent up, after the manner of a dense cloud, cut off the
illuminations that come upon the mind from the Holy Spirit. And if there is
some food of angels, it is bread (as the Prophet says, Man ate the bread of
angels), not meats, not wine, not all the things that are eagerly pursued by
the slaves of the belly.

Fasting is a weapon for the campaign against demons;

For this kind does not come out except by prayer and fasting. And so many are
the good things from fasting. But satiety is the beginning of insolence. For
immediately there rushes in together with luxury and drunkenness and the
various sauces every kind of beast-like licentiousness. Hence men become
stallions mad after females, because of the gadfly that comes upon the soul
from luxury among the drunken; the exchanges of nature, seeking the female in
the male, and the male in the female. But fasting also makes known the measure
of marital works, and chastises the immoderation even of the things conceded
by law. You, of finding a man who owes to go to law with someone else before
the king, and the not finding at all.

For it has destroyed many of those who choose to do injustice. For these
reasons the God who loves the good did not overlook the tears and the
groanings of his priests and holy ones who were entreating him on his behalf,
he who said through the prophet Isaiah, Judge for the orphan, and do justice
for the widow, and come, and let us reason together. But having heard these
prayers as well, he both forgave that one's transgressions beyond what is
human, and assigned to the Church her own adornment, and the seemliness of the
holy and venerable icons, and to us who sincerely worship and kiss them, and
keep his divine commandments, he grants a settled peace and forgiveness of the
things done amiss each day; because to him belongs the glory and the power and
the honor and the worship, unto the ages of ages. Amen.
