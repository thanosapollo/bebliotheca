ORIGEN'S SELECTIONS ON EXODUS.
=12.281= "And I will give this people favor in the sight of the Egyptians. And
when you go out, you shall not go out empty." This promise was handed down
before to Abraham: "After these things, God speaking, they shall come out here
with substance." "And the heart of Pharaoh was hardened, and he did not listen
to them, as the Lord commanded them. And the Lord said to Moses: The heart of
Pharaoh is heavy, so as not to send the people away." The saying, "The heart
of Pharaoh is heavy," spoken by the Lord, after the rod of Aaron, changed into
a dragon, had swallowed the rods of the Magi, it is worth seeing whether it is
other than the "heart of Pharaoh hardened," said often, and the "having
prevailed." For even this is so said: "And the heart of Pharaoh was hardened,
and he did not listen to them, as the Lord spoke." And it seems to me that the
heart that yields toward the divine things is softened, and has not strength
to resist the divinity, and is not weighed down, =12.284= nor dragged down to
earth and matter; but the heart that strains against and opposes the divine
will is hardened and resists like a stone, and repels the word, and
obstinately resists with some falsely named strength. And such a heart, cast
down by wickedness, which is by nature downward-looking, must needs be heavy.
Yet these things do not happen to the heart according to the same meaning,
even though it has befallen one and the same heart, being evil, both to
prevail and to be weighed down. For the one, as it is somewhere said, becomes
stony and is hardened; the other does not yield to the divine nature as to
something better; but standing up to fight against God, it is said to prevail.
"And Moses said to him, When I go out of the city, I will spread out my hands
to the Lord, and the voices shall cease, and the hail shall be no more, and
the rain shall be no more." For the maker of all has nature yielding to his
command. Of the same. It is to be noted that Moses does not work all the signs
with the rod, but also with hands and with word, lest the many should think
that the rod is wielded through magical deceptions. "Let each take a lamb
according to the houses of the fathers;" and what follows. He says that from
the tenth they take a lamb and keep it until the fourteenth; and that they
name it, saying, This lamb is sacrificed for this one and for that one; not
exceeding ten names; and that it is sacrificed on the fourteenth, between the
evenings, the fifteenth beginning; so that from the taking until the sacrifice
there are five days, when the light of the moon is full. "A lamb perfect,
male, a year old, shall be to you." A year-old lamb is Christ. For to fall
short in nothing, and to have nothing deficient, is shown by the year. "And
they shall sacrifice it, the whole multitude of the assembly of the sons of
Israel, toward evening." And we are commanded to sacrifice the lamb toward
evening, since also in the last hour our true lamb, the Savior, came into the
world. "And they shall take of the blood, and shall put it upon the two
doorposts, and upon the lintel, in the houses;" and what follows. And we
sacrifice, and anoint our houses with the blood; I mean the body, if the
anointing is the faith that is toward him, through which I believe the power
of the destroyer to be brought to nothing. And after we have been anointed,
that is, have believed in Christ, then we are also commanded to come to the
eating; neither raw, nor boiled in water, but roasted with fire. "Everyone who
shall eat leaven, that soul shall be destroyed from Israel." A man shall be
destroyed because of leaven; and the God of all, who gives oracles in the
laws, as he destroys the adulterer, as he destroys the fornicator, as he
destroys the corrupter of children, so he destroys him who has leaven in his
house on the day of unleavened bread. Is this according to God? Is this
according to the heavenly law? But I would say that if you have leaven
=12.285= of wickedness, and do not destroy every leaven of malice from your
ruling part, for this is your true house, the God of all legislates that you
be destroyed, not having cast the old leaven away from yourself. For you must,
having come to the season of the new, becoming new, have nothing old. "The old
things have passed away," and what follows. See how it was needful to keep the
feast of unleavened bread in the month of the new. "Dip the bunch of hyssop,
and upon both the doorposts, from the blood which is by the door." The bunch
of hyssop, as one of those before us has rendered, of the rational; and the
two doorposts, of the irascible and the appetitive. Of the same. For we do not
cast out the destroyer by a conception formed within, but make a guard through
the law, lest he should slip in even at the beginning. And the guard is also
the safety, that by the blood of the lamb both the lintel and the doorposts of
the entrance are marked. "And it came to pass about midnight, and the Lord
struck every firstborn in the land of Egypt." For through angels God brings
these deadly blows, as we learned in the Kingdoms how in one night the angel
destroyed of the Assyrians a hundred and eighty-five thousand. "This is the
law of the Passover: Every stranger shall not eat of it. And every servant of
anyone, and one bought with money, you shall circumcise him, and then he shall
eat of it;" and what follows. In sequence with the things set forth, this is
the third law, which the Lord spoke again besides the two laws. And attend to
the precision of the Scripture, that the Lord did not speak this law
concerning the Passover at the beginning. For if he had spoken while they were
yet in Egypt, before removing Israel out of Egypt, the saying, "Every stranger
shall not eat of it. And every servant of anyone, and one bought with money
you shall circumcise, and then he shall eat of it," the much-mingled great
crowd that went out of Egypt would not have gone out. He therefore kept this
law, so that after the going out another law might be given to those willing.
And after a little. If we allegorize these things to our soul, we shall say
that the words grown up with us and cultivated are house-born, beginning from
conceptions innate with us; but those bought with silver are such as become
ours from outside, from the lessons of teaching. He says then to circumcise
and cleanse all, both the house-born and those bought with silver. But no
stranger must be received, as receiving a foreign doctrine; for he does not
accept strangers who wishes to profane the feast of the Lord. And after a
little. For if you eat the words of God in the Church, and eat also in the
synagogue of the Jews, you transgress the commandment that says, "In one house
it shall be eaten." And if you partake of the words of God in one house, the
Church, and then, leaving it, you undertake to partake of God in a heretic
=12.288= synagogue, the commandment saying, "In one house it shall be eaten,"
you do not eat in one house. Understand then one house to be the Church;
therefore eat nowhere of the lamb, having gone outside the Church. "And you
shall not carry forth out of the house the flesh outside." The ecclesiastical
word must not be championed outside the Church, as the flesh must not be
carried forth outside the house; I mean into a synagogue of Jews or of
heretics; for it is like casting pearls before swine. "Every uncircumcised
shall not eat of it." Every offspring of the soul, intelligible, transcending
the material things (and the material things are the female), must be
circumcised and cleansed, that so one may partake of the flesh of the
Passover; and the proselyte becomes as a native of the land; for God does not
prefer the well-born seeds, but receives the purpose. Of the same.
Uncircumcised in flesh, he says, is the one not circumcising the fleshly
things, as also uncircumcised in eyes the one looking at what he ought not. Of
the same. See lest anyone of you be uncircumcised in heart; lest anyone have
not circumcised the circumcisions which the word teaches to be circumcised. Of
what kind then are these? Some are reproached as uncircumcised in ears; and as
many as have not circumcised their hearing are unclean. Uncircumcised in lips
is the one not circumcising the words that are contrary and unclean. So also
one may speak of uncircumcised in flesh, the one not circumcising the fleshly
things; and uncircumcised in eyes, the one seeing what he ought not, and not
cleansing the faculty of sight; and uncircumcised in flesh, the one doing what
he ought not. "And the sons of Israel removed from Socoth, and encamped in
Othom, which is before the wilderness." In Numbers it is set down, "From Butha
to the mouth of Iroth, which is over against Beelsephon, adjoining Borrrha,"
for the sons of Israel to encamp. "And he took six hundred chosen chariots,
and all the cavalry of the Egyptians, and tristates upon all of them." Those
able to fight as three; or those standing in the line of battle after the
first man and the second, stationed in the third rank. But others say that
they made the chariots large for the hands of the enemies, so as to hold even
three, that one might drive, and the two fight; or those mounted upon three
horses. For the ancients in wars rode out as charioteers upon two or three
horses. Or he calls tristate the king in the seat, standing third, or having a
third seat; such was David with Saul, as you will find in the first of the
Kingdoms. "There he set for him justifications and judgments, and there he
tried him." When God tries, he tries for benefit, not to do harm; wherefore
also it was said that "God is untried of evils," as after a little the word
brings in: "If hearing you hear the voice of the Lord your God, every disease
which I brought upon =12.289= the Egyptians, I will not bring upon you." He
then who bears the trials bravely is crowned. And another thing is that of the
devil; for he tries, that he may destroy those obeying him; and God tries not
ignorant of what shall be, but giving man leave to do what he wills, because
of free choice. "This is the word which the Lord spoke; Sabbaths, holy rest to
the Lord tomorrow." I set down the things said concerning the Sabbath; first
according to the letter, that they may see what the letter signifies; second
according to the anagoge, according to which I would say that the Sabbath day
has arrived for the just man who has dissolved the works of the world, and
glorifies God, and keeps holiday, neither departing from the place where he
stands in Christ; nor kindling fire, according to sinning in nothing; nor
lifting burdens. Every sin is a burden heavier than a mountain; wherefore the
Hymnologist said, "As a heavy burden they were heavy upon me." And another
account of the Sabbath would be that according to which "there remains a
sabbatism to the people of God," a rest sacred and holy. And he who has done
all his works is judged worthy of that Sabbath; keeping holiday for nothing
but the contemplation of the truth, and of wisdom. We have set these things
down because Moses here makes mention of sabbath-keeping. "And you shall
strike the rock, and water shall come out of it." Christ is likened to a rock
because of his unshaken and unmoved steadfastness. For the divine and most
high nature is rooted in the good. "For I am the Lord your God, a jealous God,
rendering the sins of the fathers upon the children, to the third and fourth
generation of those hating me, and doing mercy to thousands of those loving
me, and keeping my commandments." Whom did this saying not disturb? If the
father sinned, and the son, and the third and fourth generation receives the
sin, one party is led to accuse the God who gave the laws; and the others, not
indeed to accuse, but truly to hope some such things, and to expect that their
sins are received by the sons. The Jews think these things, not introducing
another god. The Samaritans suppose these things, as themselves also glorying
to have partaken of the one God. Those of the heresies, not pleased that God
can be good or just, rendering the sins of the fathers into the bosom of their
children, say that the God of the law is not just, is not good, rendering the
sins of the fathers upon the sons; but that some greater god is above him. But
the churchly man neither hears the things said bodily, as a Jew, nor
refashions another god, as a heretic, but stands in the middle of both, and
says that these things were well written; but that he knows not the mind of
the things written. Lift up then your minds, all of you, and pray, that he may
reveal to you how good he is, rendering the sins of the fathers upon the
children. Who are the fathers? Bring the Scriptures as witnesses; let the
sacred =12.292= logia be examined. The Savior and Lord Jesus Christ says to
those sinning that "You are of your father the devil, and the desires of your
father you will to do. He was a murderer from the beginning, and stood not in
the truth." If then anyone sins, he is of the father the devil, and therefore
the sin of the devil is rendered upon the son. Only he is the first sinner,
who laid the sin before me; and I am the second sinner, who did not cast out
his will, but received his working; it being needful for me to stand having
armed myself with the whole armor of God, and having the shield of faith, that
all the fiery darts of the evil one may be quenched in the shield of my faith.
If then I sin, I do the things of the devil, who has cast into the heart that
I should do this or that sin. First I receive his dart, and when I have
received it, then I receive him also. For so it is written concerning Judas,
that Satan did not first enter into him, but first the devil, having cast into
the heart that Judas Simon Iscariot should betray him, then, after the morsel,
Satan entered into him. I have then a father: if I am holy, God; but if I am a
sinner, I have the devil for father; but also the Antichrist, as one might
say, working closely at hand, or some angel of the devil, or a demon, whose
working I received. And simply, I have fathers by the myriad, if I become a
sinner; as again, if I become just, my father is God, as the Savior says,
"Little children, yet a little while I am with you." And again, "Daughter,
your faith has saved you." And again, "Child, your sins are forgiven you." So
I know other fathers also, according to the thing written to Abraham; "But you
shall go to your fathers;" not to the fathers according to the flesh; for they
were not faithful, they were not holy, as is clear from the history written
concerning them. For if his father were holy, it would not have been said to
him, "Go forth out of your land, and out of your kindred, and out of the house
of your father." For the word has the bodily sense, and has also the
spiritual. If you have understood the fathers, see then further that you, who
received the working, because you sinned, receive from the devil, or from some
one of the spirits under him. Becoming then their son, you receive from the
good God visiting you. For he does not visit your father, but he visits you,
and says to you, "Hear, daughter, and see, and incline your ear," and what
follows; and he gives to you as good. For he visits you, the son of the evil
father, rendering your sins. "I will visit then with the rod your iniquities,"
yours who are saints; and "with plagues their injustices." Why? That "my mercy
be not scattered from them." For when he abandons =12.293= someone, he no
longer punishes him, nor scourges him. For he scourges not all, but "every son
that he receives, the Lord scourges." And it is said in the prophet concerning
the very sinful: "I will not visit upon your daughters when they play the
harlot, nor upon your brides when they commit adultery." So long a time has
the devil been sinning, from the creation and foundation of the world, and
neither fire, nor scourge; for he is not worthy of the punishments from God.
For the devil cannot say, "Lord, rebuke me not in your wrath," and what
follows. Let each one then, conscious to himself of sin, pray to be punished.
For it is a good thing to have done nothing worthy of punishment. But if we
have done anything worthy of punishment, let us be punished in such a way as
to receive it here, and after these things to rest in the bosoms of Abraham.
"And these my justifications, which you shall set before them. If you acquire
a Hebrew servant," and what follows. It is necessary to inquire what is a law,
and what a command, and what are justifications, and judgments, and
testimonies, and commandments. Justifications then are the thing said here,
"If you acquire a Hebrew servant," and what follows, said concerning it; a
judgment is that, if a priest's daughter commit adultery, she be burned with
fire; commandments are, "There shall not be to you other gods;" a command is,
"Having gone down, solemnly testify to the people, and let them wash their
garments;" and testimonies are, "I solemnly testify to you today the heaven
and the earth." "Six years he shall serve you; and in the seventh year he
shall go out free without payment." The freedom in the seventh year signifies,
and further, the ceasing of works on the Sabbath, that freedom in grace given
to us through Christ. "And his lord shall bring him to the judgment of God."
Aquila and Symmachus said, "to the gods." And they name the judges gods, and
the judgment of God, their verdict. "Three seasons of the year keep feast to
me. Keep the feast of unleavened bread. Seven days you shall eat unleavened
things, as I commanded you, in the season of the month of the new. For in it
you went out of Egypt. You shall not appear before me empty; and you shall
make a feast of harvest of the firstfruits of your works." The feasts then of
God, to whom is spirit, are spiritual; and no earthy man keeps the feasts
spiritually. "For what concord of righteousness and lawlessness?" that I say
not what follows. An earthy man cannot keep spiritual feasts, as neither can a
spiritual man keep an earthy feast. And after a little. What is the month of
the new? You will say, the sensible fruits. The month of the new has not yet
come, the year being, let us say, too late. But if ever a year should come
such that the earth does not even bear the fruits, can we keep feast in the
month of the new, as God commanded those keeping feast to keep feast? We must
then keep the feast year by year. And I inquire again also concerning the
second law, that of the second feast. For he says, "a feast of harvest of the
firstfruits." If someone sows, and the fruits do not come first, but =12.296=
last, this man could not keep the feast of the beginning of the harvest of the
firstfruits. For you must have fruits, that you may be able to keep feast. But
what, if I have no land, how should I be able to keep this feast, the
expression saying, "And you shall make a feast of harvest of the firstfruits
of your works, whatever you sow in your field?" I did not sow; I am a builder;
I did not sow; I am a weaver; I have no field; how can I keep the second
feast? And so too concerning the third, "And a feast of consummation on the
going out of the year." And other things after it. What does the word wish to
say to us, commanding us to keep the feast of unleavened bread in the month of
the new? Cast away the old man with his deeds. And that you may keep the feast
in the month of the new, become new yourself, that the month of the feast of
the new may become yours. For of the new, and not of the old, is this month.
How then will you need to become new? Having understood the feasts, of the
feast of the new the Apostle Paul says, "Having put off the old man with his
deeds, and having put on the new, the one being renewed to full knowledge
according to the image of him who created him." Then, that you may keep the
feast in the month of the new, you must become new, and put away all oldness.
Then what feast do you keep first? That it is the feast of unleavened bread,
learn from the Apostle; "For our Passover," he says, "was sacrificed for us,
Christ; so that let us keep feast not with old leaven, nor with the leaven of
malice and wickedness, but with unleavened bread of sincerity and truth." And
at the same time gather the things legislated concerning the feast of
unleavened bread, and you will see the unreason of those who do not attend to
the letter as spiritual, and who despise the spiritual things as not useful.
"You shall not appear before me empty." Know that every man is either empty or
full. For if he has not the Holy Spirit, he has not the knowledge of him who
created him, he has not received the life, Jesus Christ; if he does not know
the Father in the heavens, if he does not live according to the word,
according to the heavenly law; he is not prudent, he does not act justly; such
a one is empty. But if there has made his dwelling in him the God who said; "I
will dwell in them, and walk about in them, and I will be their God," this one
is not empty, but full. "Three seasons of the year shall all your male appear
before the Lord your God." I would say what the saying is. What we do is
either female or male. If then what we do is female, it is either bodily or
fleshly. For, sowing to the flesh, we make the offspring of the soul female,
not male; but unmanned, and soft and material. If indeed, seeing the eternal
things, and having the mind set upon the better things, we bear the fruits of
the Spirit, then all the offspring of ours are male. The things then brought
before =12.297= God, the things also to be seen by the sight of him who
created, are male, not female. For God does not deign to see the female
things, and the bodily things.
