* On the Resurrection of the Lord
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

A discourse of our father among the saints John Chrysostom, archbishop of
Constantinople, on the resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ.
1 Splendid are the tokens of the Master's resurrection: deceit has ceased
and envy has been banished, strife has been trampled down, peace is honored
and war has been dissolved. No longer do we mourn Adam the first-formed but
we glorify the second Adam, no longer do we reproach Eve who transgressed
but we bless Mary the Theotokos, no longer do we turn away from the tree
but we bear the cross of the Lord, no longer do we fear the serpent but we
entreat the Holy Spirit, no longer do we go down into the earth but we run
up into the heavens, no longer do we come to be outside of paradise but we
lodge in the bosom of Abraham, no longer do we hear in Jewish fashion "I
have likened to night" your day but we chant spiritually: "This is the day
which the Lord has made, let us rejoice and be glad in it." Why? Because no
longer is the sun darkened but all things are illuminated, no longer is the
veil rent but the church is made known, no longer do we bear the branches
of palms but we carry about the newly illumined.
2 "This is the day which the Lord has made; let us rejoice and be glad in
it." This is the day, this and no other; for there is one kingdom and not
many tyrannies. This is the day named after the Lord, the trophy-bearing,
the customary of the resurrection and the beautifier of grace and the
distributor of the rational lamb, the milk-giver of those reborn and the
steward of the poor. "Let us rejoice and be glad in it," not running to
taverns but hastening to martyrs' shrines, not honoring drunkenness but
loving moderation, not leaping in Jewish fashion but feasting in apostolic
fashion, not playing in the marketplaces but chanting in the houses. The
present day is of resurrection, not of insolence. no one dancing goes up
into the heavens, no one being drunk stands before a king. Let none of us
therefore dishonor this day, the one typed of old through the law, the one
promised with a threat, the one proclaimed through the prophetic voice, the
one expected through the promise of the fathers, the one fulfilled through
the apostles' own seeing, the one received through the ecclesiastical
faith.
3 This is the day on which Adam was set free, on which Eve was delivered
from grief, on which untamed death shuddered, on which the strength of the
mighty stones was loosened being split and the bolts of the tombs being
torn asunder were released, on which the bodies of those who died long ago
were given over to the former life, on which the laws of the underworld
strong and firm through all time have been defeated, on which as the Master
Christ was rising the heavens were opened, on which the flourishing and
fruitful thing of the resurrection sprouted as in a garden in the inhabited
world for the race of men, on which the lilies of the newly illumined
sprang up, on which the streams of the sinners were dried up, on which the
sinews of the devil were loosened, on which the ranks of the demons were
scattered, on which the multitudes of the Jews were put to shame, on which
the ranks of the faithful rejoice, on which the diadems of the martyrs
bloom again. "This day therefore the Lord has made, let us rejoice and be
glad in it," by the grace of Christ who through his own resurrection has
illumined the whole inhabited world "sitting in darkness and the shadow of
death," with whom to the Father together with the Holy Spirit be glory and
worship unto the ages of ages. Amen.
