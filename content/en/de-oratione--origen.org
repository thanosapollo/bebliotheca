Of the things that are moved some have the mover from outside, as the soulless
things and those held together by habit alone and the things moved by nature
and soul, not being moved as such at times but similarly to those held
together by habit alone; for stones and woods, those cut out from the mine or
having lost the growing, being held together by habit alone have the mover
from outside, but also the bodies of the animals and the portable things of
the things that have been planted, being transferred by someone, are not
transferred as animals and plants but similarly to stones and woods that have
lost the growing; and even if these too are moved by being fluid, all the
bodies wasting, they have the movement in the wasting as a following one. And
second besides these are things moved that are moved by the nature or soul
existing in them, which are also said to be moved from themselves among those
who use the names more properly. And third is movement that in the animals,
which is named the movement from themselves; and I think that the movement of
the rational ones is movement through themselves. And if we take away from the
animal the movement from itself, it can no longer even be supposed still being
an animal, but it will be similar either to a plant moved by nature alone or
to a stone being carried by someone from outside. And if something follows
along with its own movement, since we named this to be moved through itself,
this must necessarily be rational. 6.2 Those then who wish there to be nothing
in our power will necessarily accept something most foolish; first that we are
not animals, and second that we are not even rational, but as if by a mover
from outside we ourselves not being moved at all would be said to do by that
one the things we are thought to do. And otherwise also let someone having
attended to his own affections see if he will not shamelessly say that he
himself does not wish and he himself does not eat and he himself does not walk
nor he himself assent and accept whatever of the dogmas nor he himself refuse
others as false. As therefore it is impossible for a human being to be
disposed toward certain dogmas, even if ten thousand times he construct them
inventing words and using persuasive words, so it is impossible for someone to
have been disposed concerning the human things, as if that in our power being
in no way preserved. For who is disposed concerning there being nothing
graspable or so lives as holding back concerning every whatsoever thing? And
who does not rebuke, having received a representation of a servant having
sinned, the attendant? And who is there who does not blame a son not rendering
the fitting toward parents or does not find fault and censure as having done a
shameful thing the woman who has been adulterated? For the truth forces and
compels, even if someone invent words ten thousand times, to rush and praise
and censure, as that in our power being kept, and this coming to be
praiseworthy or blameworthy from us. 6.3 If then that in our power is
