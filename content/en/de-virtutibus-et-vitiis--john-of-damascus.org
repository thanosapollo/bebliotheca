OF OUR FATHER AMONG THE SAINTS John of Damascus, Concerning virtues and vices,
of soul and of body.

It must be known then that man, being double, that is from soul and body, has
also double the senses, and the virtues of these; and five are of the soul,
and five of the body. And the senses of the soul, which the wise also call
powers, are these: mind, thought, opinion, fantasy, and sensation. And the
bodily, sight, smell, hearing, taste, and touch; whence indeed the virtues of
these are double, double also the vices; so that it is necessary that every
human being know clearly how many are the passions of the soul, and of what
sort the bodily; and we say the virtues of the soul are first these four most
generic, which are these, courage, prudence, temperance, and justice; and from
these are born virtues of the soul: faith, hope, love, prayer, humility,
meekness, long-suffering, forbearance, kindness, freedom from anger, divine
knowledge, the unwrathful, the simple, the untroubled, the unhypocritical, the
unconceited, the unproud, the unenvious, the guileless, the unloving of money,
the compassionate, the merciful, the sharing, the fearless, the ungrieving,
the compunctive, the reverent, piety, the longing of the good things to come,
the appetite of the kingdom of God, the desire of adoption. And bodily virtues
are these, or rather tools of virtues, coming to be in knowledge and according
to God, and outside of all hypocrisy and man-pleasing, bearing the human being
toward progress of humility, and of dispassion; =95.88= all-night standing,
bending of knees, continence, fasting, thirst, vigil, continual, unwashedness,
single-tunic, dry-eating, slow-eating, little-eating, water-drinking,
ground-lying, poverty, possessionlessness the squalid, the unadorned, the
unself-loving, isolated, the quiet, the unoutgoing, the needy, the
self-sufficient, the silent, to pursue handicraft with one's own hands, and
every hardship, and bodily ascesis; all which things, when the body is
healthy, and is troubled by the fleshly passions, are most necessary and most
beneficial; but when it is weak, and by the help of God has overcome these,
they are not so necessary, as holy humility, and prayer filling all things.

We ought then to speak also concerning those of soul and of body, that is
passions; and passions of the soul are these, forgetfulness, sloth, and
ignorance; by which, namely these passions, the eye of the soul, that is the
mind, being darkened, is mastered by all the passions, which are these,
impiety, evil opinion, that is every heresy, blasphemy, wrath, anger,
bitterness, quick temper, hatred of humans, remembrance of wrongs, slander,
condemnation, irrational grief, fear, cowardice, strife, jealousy, envy,
vainglory, pride, hypocrisy, falsehood, unbelief, greed, love of matter,
attachment, relation of earthly things, acedia, smallness of soul,
unthankfulness, murmuring, conceit, self-opinion, gravity, boastfulness, love
of rule, man-pleasing, craftiness, shamelessness, insensibility, flattery,
underhandedness, irony, double-mindedness, the consents of the sins from the
passionate part, and the continual meditation of these, wandering of thoughts,
self-love the mother of evils, and the root of all evils love of money, and
evil character and wickedness. And bodily passions: gluttony, greediness,
luxury, drunkenness, secret-eating, manifold love of pleasure, fornication,
adultery, wantonness, uncleanness, incest, corruption of children, bestiality,
evil desires, and all the passions contrary to nature and shameful, theft,
sacrilege, robbery, murder, bodily relaxation and enjoyment of the willings of
the flesh, rather when the body is healthy, divinations, magics, sorceries,
omens, chance-word omens, loves of adornment, boastings, stupidities,
beautifications, face-paintings, the condemned idleness, distractions,
dice-playings, the passionate misuse of the pleasant things of the world, the
body-loving life, which fattening the mind, renders this earthly and brutish,
and never allows it to be renewed toward God, and the working of the virtues.
And roots of all the passions, and, as one might say rashnesses, love of
pleasure, love of glory, love of money, from which every evil is born. And the
human being does not sin any sin, unless first these mighty giants, as says
the wisest among ascetics, Mark, prevail and master him; that is,
forgetfulness, sloth, and ignorance; and these pleasure and relaxation beget,
the loving the glory of human beings, and distraction; and first cause of all
these, and as a most evil mother, as has been said before, self-love, that is
irrational love of the body and passionate attachment; for diffusion and
slackening of mind with jesting and shameful speech, procurers of many evils
and falls, as familiarity, and laughter. And besides all these it is necessary
to know that passionate love of pleasure is some varied and many-formed thing,
and many are the pleasures deceiving the soul, whenever not being sober toward
God, it is lifted by the divine fear, and by the love of Christ, taking care
of the working of the virtues. For countless pleasures are borne drawing to
themselves the eyes of the soul: those of bodies, those of money, those of
luxury, those of glory, those of sloth, those of anger, those of power, those
of love of money, those of greed; and they appear in deceit having bright
appearances, and pleasing; able to draw those fluttered about these things,
and not strongly loving virtue, and enduring the hardness of this. For also
every earthly relation, and the attachment toward something of the material
things, makes pleasure and delight for the attached, both useless and harmful.

The desiring of the soul shows itself passionate in this, that for the sake of
this, the one overcome is subjected to wrath and anger, and to grief and
remembrance of wrongs, by the deprivation of the thing longed for. And if
along with the attachment even a small habit will prevail, insensibly so to
speak, and incurably until the end to cling to the irrational attachment,
through the pleasure hidden here it prepares the one caught. For the pleasure
of desire is many-branched, as has been said before; and it is fulfilled not
only by fornication, and by the other bodily enjoyment, but also by the
remaining passions. Since to be temperate is not this only, to abstain from
fornication, and from the pleasures below the belly, but also to be outside
from the remaining pleasures. Hence also he of money-loving and he of love of
money, and he loving greed, is intemperate; for as that one loves a body, so
also this one money. And rather this one is more intemperate, inasmuch as he
does not even have so great a force from =95.92= nature thrusting him. For
indeed that one would be called an unskilled charioteer, as truly and
especially, not he who does not hold the hard and hard-to-rein horse, but he
who the tame and more gentle, not being able to subject. And it is clear from
every side, that the desire of money is superfluous and not according to
nature, inasmuch as it has not the force from nature, but from the wicked
choice. Wherefore also he who is willingly overcome by this sins things
unforgivable; so that we must clearly recognize, love of pleasure is not
defined only toward luxury, and the enjoyment of bodies, but in every manner
and thing loved by choice of soul and by attachment. And in order that the
passions may still more clearly be discerned according to the tripartite of
the soul, we have judged to add also these things in epitome.

Concerning the tripartite of the soul.
The soul is divided into three: the reasoning, the spirited, and the desiring.
From the rational the sins are these: unbelief, heresy, folly, blasphemy,
unthankfulness, and the consents of bodily sins, which come to be from the
passionate part. And the healing and therapy of these evils is the undoubting
faith toward God, the true and unerring and orthodox doctrines of piety, the
continual meditation of the oracles of the spirit, the pure and unceasing
prayer, and the thanksgiving toward God. Concerning the spirited.}1 And of the
spirited the sins are these: unmercifulness, hatred, the uncompassionate, the
remembering of wrongs, envy, and murder, and the continual meditation toward
such things. And the healing and therapy of these, love of humans, love, and
kindness.

Concerning the desiring.
And of the desiring the sins are these: gluttony, greediness, wine-bibbing the
fornication, adultery, uncleanness, wantonness, love of money, the desire of
empty glory, of gold and of wealth, and of the fleshly pleasures. And the
healing and therapy of these, fasting, continence, hardship,
possessionlessness, the scattering of money toward the poor, the longing of
those immortal good things to come, the appetite of the kingdom of God, and
the desire of adoption. It is necessary then that we also set in the
discernment of the passionate thoughts, through which every sin is
accomplished. Eight are all the thoughts encompassing vice: that of gluttony,
that of fornication, that of love of money, that of anger, that of grief, that
of acedia, that of vainglory, and that of pride. =95.93= These eight thoughts
to trouble, and not to trouble, is not in our power; but to remain, or to move
a passion, or not to move, is of the things in our power. And one thing is
assault, and another coupling, and another struggle, and another passion, and
another consent, which approaches and likens to the act, and another energy,
and another captivity. And assault is the reminder simply coming to be from
the enemy; as, do this or this. As in the case of our Lord and God: "Say that
these stones become bread;" this as has been said, is not in our power.
Coupling is the acceptance of the thought being suggested from the enemy, and
as it were meditation with it and pleasurable converse which is from our
choice. And passion, the habit coming to be from the coupling from the enemy
of a passion being suggested, and as it were continual meditation and fantasy.
And struggle, the resistance of the thought, that toward destruction of the
passion in the thought, that is of the passionate thought, or toward consent,
as the Apostle says: "For the flesh desires against the spirit, and the spirit
against the flesh. And these are opposed to one another." And captivity is a
violent and involuntary leading away of the heart, being tyrannized from
prepossession and long habit; and consent, the nodding toward the passion of
the thought. And energy, the act itself of the passionate thought in consent;
he then who thinks the first dispassionately, or from the first sending it
away by contradiction and severity, that is the assault, has cut off all the
last things at once. Gluttony is destroyed by continence; and fornication by
divine longing and longing of the good things to come; and love of money by
compassion toward the poor; and anger by goodness and love toward all; and
worldly grief by spiritual joy; and acedia by patience and endurance, and
thanksgiving toward gods; and vainglory by the hidden working of the virtues,
and by the continual prayer in contrition of heart; and pride by not judging
anyone or despising, as the boastful Pharisee, but reckoning oneself last of
all. Thus then the mind having been freed of the said passions, and having
been lifted toward God, from this point lives the blessed life, receiving the
earnest of the Holy Spirit; and having departed from the things here, with
dispassion and true knowledge, it stands by the light of the holy Trinity,
with the divine angels, being illuminated unto ages without end.

=95.96= The soul then being tripartite, as has been shown before; for three,
as has been said, are the parts of this, reason, spirit and desire; if there
is in the spirited love, and love of humans, and in the desire purity and
temperance, the reason is illuminated. But if in the spirited there be hatred
of humans, and in the desire intemperance, the reason is darkened; the reason
then is healthy and temperate and is illuminated, when it has the passions
subjected, and contemplates the reasons of the creatures purely, and is led up
toward the blessed and holy Trinity. And the spirit again then moves that
according to nature, when it loves all human beings, and toward none of them
has either grief, or remembrance of wrongs. And the desire, when by humility
of mind and continence and possessionlessness it deadens the passions, that is
pleasure of flesh, and longing of money and of passing glory, and is turned
toward the divine and immortal love; for indeed the desire has the movement
toward the three, either toward pleasure of flesh, or toward empty glory, or
also toward deceit of money, and through this irrational longing it despises
God, and his divine commandments, and forgets the divine nobility, and is made
savage toward the neighbor, and darkens the reason, and does not allow it to
look up toward the truth; of which he who has acquired the higher mind, from
this point, as has been said before, receives of the kingdom of the heavens,
and lives a blessed life, in expectation of the blessedness laid up, for those
who love God; of which may we also be deemed worthy through the grace of
Christ. Amen. But these things thus, as we had of ignorance, we have set out
simply, having sketched a readily-surveyed and clear account concerning
virtues and passions, in order that one may easily have to distinguish and
discern both the division and the difference of these, by the detailed
treatment and clarity of these. For this very reason, we have also set out the
varied and many-formed of each, so that no idea of virtue, if possible, and a
vice be unknown; and thus let us draw the ones gladly toward ourselves, the
virtues namely and rather those of the soul, through which we also draw near
to God, and the vices indeed having turned away even strongly let us flee; for
blessed, as truly, is he who seeks virtue, and pursues this; and that he
diligently searches what virtue is, as drawing near through it to God, and
being together with this intellectually. For this is properly, prudence ....
both wisdom and unlying knowledge, and inalienable wealth, to be led up
through practical virtue toward contemplation of the one who made. And it is
called virtue, because of being chosen; for it is choosable and willed,
because of our doing the good choosingly and by free will, not unwillingly and
by compulsion; and prudence is said, the bearing the beneficial things beside
the mind.
=95.97= And if you will, let us add to this simple account, as a golden seal,
also concerning the according to the image, and according to likeness, a
little of the most honorable of all the creatures of God, the intellectual and
rational living being man, alone from all is according to the image, and
according to likeness of God. According to the image indeed every human being
is said, according to the dignity of the mind, and that of the soul, that is
the incomprehensible, the unseen, the immortal, the self-determining, yes
indeed also the ruling, and the child-begetting, and the building; and
according to likeness, according to the reason of virtue, and these god-named
and god-imitating acts, that is according to being disposed lovingly-of-humans
toward the same-kind; both to pity and to have mercy, and to love the
fellow-slave, and to show all compassion and sympathy. "For become, says
Christ the God, compassionate, as also your Father in the heavens is
compassionate." And the according to the image, every human being has
acquired; for the gifts of God are without repentance; but the according to
likeness, few, and only the virtuous and holy, and those imitating the
goodness of God according to what is possible for human beings; thus may we
also be deemed worthy of all-good love of humans, having pleased him through
good-working, and having become imitators of those who from the age have
pleased Christ; because to him is mercy, and to him is due all glory, honor,
and worship, and to the beginningless Father, and to his all-holy and good and
life-giving Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
