Rom 1:11 12 He teaches who enlightens those not yet knowing, but he exhorts who
gladdens those already enlightened with the word of wisdom, as he himself,
dividing, says: if the one who teaches, in the teaching; if the one who
exhorts, in the exhortation. And when the Romans are exhorted he too, he says,
will be exhorted together, sharing in their exhortation because of love, and at
the same time as not himself being above the need of the benefit from the word;
for the word is exhortation for the speaker as well. And through this he trains
the teachers not to think highly if they teach, but to know that they do not
give their own things even when they teach, and to be persuaded that they do
not only benefit but are also benefited; for the progress of the gospel is also
a strengthening of those who preach. Rom 1:13 And the fruit of those entrusted
with the word is the benefit of those who hear; and the Lord says he has set
the apostles to bear much fruit. So a few men bore fruit of the whole church.

Rom 1:16 17 Even if then in the greatest of the cities, he says, one must be
mocked among the foolish while preaching the saving cross, I will not decline;
for if the Son of God bore the shame of the cross in our place, how is it not
absurd for us to be ashamed of the Lord's passion for us? And instead of the
righteousness through works, which is neither sincere nor life-giving, he was
granting the life through faith. And the prophet has said: from faith to faith.
For if you believed Moses, he says, you would believe me. He teaches from how
many evils the gospel and the faith through it set free, and having gone
through the evils both among the nations and among the Jews, after these things
going forward he brings in the account concerning faith, teaching that the
coming of Christ has become 59 necessary. And thus he discourses concerning the
falling away of the Jews, and last he adds the exhortations; and he begins from
the account against the nations and says that they fearlessly transgressed the
law written in nature by the Creator.

Rom 4:1 2 By the type according to Abraham he shows that faith is given for
justification not only to those in law and circumcision but also to those in
uncircumcision; for Abraham too has been justified not from works (for neither
was his boasting splendid, being incomplete and unable to appear before God,
with whom a limping righteousness cannot have a boast), but from faith is the
justification of our father Abraham. And he calls him father according to the
flesh of the Israelites who have become sons according to the Spirit of God; to
whom Christ, having been made like in the generation according to the flesh,
made them like to himself through the birth in the Spirit according to grace.
Rom 4:13 God did not, having defined a law, give the promise to Abraham and to
his seed, but having justified him by faith. And the promise given to Abraham
was, as we have learned from Moses, the one saying: to you I will give and to
your seed all this land. For which reason also the apostle says the promise is
an inheritance of the world, being a promise of land. And in the [letter] to
the Corinthians, referring it to us the successors of Abraham according to the
Spirit, all things are 62 ours, he says, whether world or present things or
things to come. And it is clear to those who read the things of Moses that
Abraham, not having received commandments of law, had such a promise upon
these; but Paul in what lies before also gives the reason for which God did not
give the promise upon law, but upon faith.
Rom 4:24 The Lord raises his own temple; and the Father has raised him from the
dead, having worked this too just as [he works] all things through the Child.

Rom 5:1 6 The Son inseparable from the Father, having drawn us to himself and
having distributed by faith the sanctifying grace, has become cause of our
being drawn to God, having become cause of union for those who stood apart
through his mediation, joined to the Father by the Godhead, and joined to us
according to the humanity; so that as many as he draws according to the human,
these he draws to God according to the divine. And having said that we stand
upon the grace that leads forward to God, he adds to the faith the things
concerning hope, showing that faith, as it is written in the [letter] to the
Hebrews, is the substance of things hoped for. And he says that we boast upon
hope of the glory of God, expecting to be clothed with the divine glory, of
which there is a type in Moses, but truth in Christ, and a forming unto us. And
the cause of such glory that will be rendered to us is the present grace. For
just as some craftsman, the Holy Spirit taking up house in us, now stamps and
forms toward the likeness of Christ through hidden energies, until, having been
given form and completed, whenever it shines forth at the appearing of the
Savior, of which John says: Brothers, now we are children of God, and it has
not yet been manifested what we shall be. We know that if he is manifested, we
shall be like him. And great is the firmness of the hope of those already
boasting as at things present in the things to come; for boasting comes upon a
good that firmly exists. And this was the blessed boasting which the prophet
Jeremiah introduces, saying, let not the rich boast in his wealth, but in this
let the one who boasts boast, in understanding and 63 knowing the Lord, and
what follows. And not because labors, he says, and afflictions are present, for
this reason do we blunt the boast upon the goods that will come, and by this we
boast still more, knowing that endurance of labors is a co-cause of the
enjoyment of the goods; for through affliction endurance is constituted, and
the one who has endured is approved, and the one approved before God hopes to
have

the things from God. And the one who has hoped in God will not be put to shame;
for he is well pleased in those who hope upon his mercy. Affliction then
becomes a co-cause of the hoped-for goods, being set as a guide unto hope. And
he shows the hope to be secure as coming upon the God who has loved us, and as
a token of the love the gift of the Spirit; for he who poured out the Spirit
poured out the love upon us. And he poured it out first upon the hearts, since
he also housed the Spirit first in the hearts, as the Savior himself also says
that he remains with you and will be with you.
Rom 5:11 That old voice of Moses being made true in you: this is your boast and
this is your God. For how would one not exult and boast, even if harassed by a
myriad of enemies, being honored with friendship of God, through which we are
also not least envied by the adversaries and abused.
Rom 7:7 Let no one hearing these things wrangle toward the apostle, saying that
if this is what is in the law, to declare the things that put to death and to
make known the base of deeds and the good, no less also the Lord,
distinguishing
in his own words both the base and the good, would seem to work the same road
for sin against those who do not obey; for the difference is great and much the
space between, the law lying in words only and giving no power unto
righteousness, but leaving the whole upon the practice of those who hear, but
our Lord Jesus Christ working the whole in the giving of the Spirit, the
salvation of those under him, and as it were creating us from the beginning and
reshaping and ordering us in the believers' receiving the divine inbreathing
and being made akin to it, but using words unto exhortation through which he
prepares us as 64 fit for the Spirit that forms and disposes, not having
entrusted the things concerning us to ourselves; for in that case we would
again be condemned, being unable to obey, and the knowledge of sin would come
rather against us than from us, but preparing us as vessels for the Spirit,
relaxing, day by day, the sympathy toward the world and loosing the fleshly
bonds, but stretching the kinship toward the Spirit. Wherefore also the law is
sharp unto punishment, but the Spirit forbearing of evil, the one seeking the
humans' own achievement unto righteousness, the other furnishing that from
itself. For it follows upon this that the law, when what is set forth is not
achieved, brings a punitive [clause] upon the commanding one (for this was
bound to that), but the Spirit, having power unto the correction of those who
have believed, even if at first I should be too weak to receive the creating
from it, neither despairs nor punishes at once, but by the salvation from
itself keeps the human being being trained, as Paul said he would hand over to
Satan for destruction of the flesh, that the spirit may be saved; for the
Spirit of the Lord holds out until the coming of Christ, until the time of the
creating now being accomplished should run out, when judgment stands by, by
which it thrusts away the uncorrected thereafter found unfit for eternal life.
For this reason also elsewhere the apostle says that God is the one working in
you both the willing and the working for his good pleasure, not cutting off our
self-determination, but explaining the power from God unto virtue. And the
Savior breathed Holy Spirit when he rose, thereby making us akin and commanded
us not even to be anxious what we shall speak, saying, for it is not you who
speak, but the Spirit of your Father who speaks in you. And already also the
prophetic word, defining the new covenant in the giving of the Spirit, says,
explaining a divine discourse toward Israel: and this is the covenant from me
for them, he said, and my Spirit is upon you, and the words which I have given
into your mouth shall not fail from your mouth and from your seed; said the
Lord from now and

unto the age. And these things through Isaiah. And through Jeremiah God,
promising the new covenant: this, he says, is the covenant which I will
covenant toward them in those days, giving my laws in their hearts, and upon
their mind I will write them, and they shall not teach each his neighbor
saying, know the Lord, because all will know me from small to great of them,
because I will be gracious to their sins; promising in these the 65 energy of
the Spirit unto hearts, through which also propitiation comes from God, the
reckonings of sin according to the law being relaxed, and the spiritual
forbearance granting the propitiation. And indeed also through Ezekiel he
promises to give a new heart and a new spirit unto the fulfillment of his own
willings in us. So that well also in what lies before the apostle, defining the
life according to Christ as not in letter, also says that the strong [hold] of
sin against us is being loosed, inasmuch as the Spirit sojourns not unto
knowledge of it and the condemnation through this, but unto abolition of sin,
and an energy of righteousness not human but divine, as also at the beginning
he said a righteousness of God is revealed through the gospel.
Rom 7:8 For those who have not heard a law, the knowledge of it comes about
naturally, as he has also said earlier that nations not having law, doing by
nature the things of the law, show the work of the law written in their own
hearts; but for those under law the letter of the law also stirs up the natural
notion, being catechized by the teacher toward being able also to understand
the good and the base from themselves.
Rom 7:10 Think then for me, he says, a law not first laid down, as now it is
not present for those still infants and unable to understand; here there is no
rebuke; for ignorance, being not accountable, is not subject to punishment.
After these things behold the commandment coming in; here sin rises up against
us, knowledge having been added and ignorance no longer standing the wrath off
from the things that happen. And thus sin lives, taking power and strength, but
the human being dies, being condemned by the knowledge, and the life-giving
good, the ordinance of the law, is turned about into a pretext of death; for
that without which sin cannot be sin, nor condemn the human being to death,
through this surely both the charge of wickedness and the penalty of punishment
are constituted. Rom 8: All creation, he says, waits for us and longs for the
time when we shall be seen upon the glory due to us, so that nothing is more
splendid than the good repute that will be about us. And it is consistent for
66 creation to follow sons of God, being a possession of God; for the slave
follows the free, and the possession is subjected to the son because of the
noble birth. And the kinship of the sons toward the Father subjects the slave.
And it is clear that having by grace the being sons of God through the
participation of the Spirit, as he has said before, we have by grace the ruling
[place] in the creation, having received the glory of the true Son; so that it
is not contrary to glory that so great and such a creation has been subjected
to human beings, who appear small in the creation.

Rom 9:1 5 From the hoped-for goods he has been led forward unto both, both unto
the strong assertion concerning love and unto the lament concerning those who
have fallen away; for those who are benefited love, but those who fail of the
benefactions are reasonably mourned. And he says, stretching himself with a
divine witnessing, that he would speak not in word only but also in disposition
and truth, that he has the greatest grief for his kinsmen always present to
him. For let no one disbelieve, he says, if I lament concerning the adversaries
and those who persecute, nor let him think it paradoxical if I even wished to
suffer the greatest things for them, being given over unto death by Christ. For
how is what concerns them not pitiable, and especially among those who are
their own and belong to them as human beings, called sons of God from the
beginning, and glorified in the world with glory from God, and having received
first the covenant upon bodily ordinances, and having in hand the second that
came upon spiritual things, and those chosen upon worship of God and the
promises of the goods to come, those of the holy and God-loving fathers, those
of the same race as the Lord's becoming flesh, that these have been cast out
and overlooked and have now fallen into the greatest 67 shame and lowliness.
But then even if the things that have happened concerning them are worthy of
grief, it is still worthy to bless the God who has so judged, the Master over
all, to whom every blessing upon all is justly sent up. And indeed also upon
the judgments, which are most holy, and [who] will neither through the eminence
of those condemned pass over any of the due justice, nor through their
preexisting kinship toward God honor those alienated from him by

choice. Rom 9:14 21 What then shall we say? Is there unrighteousness with God?
May it not be! For he says to Moses: I will have mercy on whom I have mercy,
and I will have compassion on whom I have compassion. So then it is not of the
one who runs nor of the one who wills, but of God who shows mercy. Let no one
think, he says, that these things, which in no way belong to the account of
justice, but are arranged according to the economy of mercy, have
unrighteousness of God; for God is faithful and there is no unrighteousness in
him, nor is it unjust to have mercy on whom it should seem good to the one who
shows mercy, and not on others, whom he himself would not wish to have mercy
on. For he has said, he says, through Moses and has made clear the manner of
the mercy, not proceeding toward the counter-measuring of human measures, but
according to the wisdom of God, so that being shown mercy is not of our works,
but of God who has the showing of mercy according to authority. For showing
mercy has been divided from judging, and of the things owed as a reply each
becomes cause for himself, reaping this which he sows, according to the
apostle. But of the things given from mercy God is cause, knowing by wisdom and
arranging for whom it is due and not. And this not in vain; for nothing with
God is vain or irrational, but each thing has been arranged beforehand by
wonderful reasons, as indeed he has also made clear concerning Pharaoh; for the
scripture says to Pharaoh, that for this very thing I raised you up, that I may
show in you my power, and that my name may be proclaimed in all the earth. See
the not being shown mercy of Pharaoh. It has been shown why it comes about
through the benefaction of the whole world, so that all, perceiving the
judgment against the impious as powerful, may be held by fear and wonder toward
the one who judged and did these things. So that God, having also foreknown the
impiety of Pharaoh, for this reason both made him and entrusted the kingdom, so
that the just and powerful judgment against him may run together unto common
benefit for the world, being without mercy toward the one judged, since also,
he says, the judgment is without mercy to the one who has not done mercy.

And Pharaoh has shown no mercy but all cruelty against 68 the Israelites. He
has therefore taken away the mercy from the one who has not furnished this to
others, just as from the contrary the Savior says to those who forgive that
they also are forgiven, acting justly according to the equality of the judgment
and the return of righteousness, but in not showing mercy to the sinner no
longer keeping the same which [he kept] upon other sinners who were shown
mercy. Wherefore the apostle adds again: so then he has mercy on whom he wills,
and whom he wills he hardens, and what follows. For the one who does not
obtain, he says, the same things toward the other sinners, but instead of being
shown mercy and being called toward a change from wickedness suffers the
contrary and is given over to be hardened, how would he reasonably still also
bring a complaint from God, namely that he does not obtain the same things as
those shown mercy? Neither is Pharaoh hardened, nor any other of those falling
into the judgment, [such that] it is not possible to contradict, nor as if the
one being hardened is cause of the hardness. For this too is surely clear, but
somewhere he says: O human being, even to seek beyond you the mystical account
with God of this inequality. For that it is outside unrighteousness has been
said before, being of providential wisdom, not of judicial recompense, the not
showing mercy to the sinner. Next upon these the apostle leads the account both
ways, both toward showing that it does not belong to human beings to busy
themselves over the depths of God, and toward a most correct account of the
saving economy being preserved, according to which there are some who are not
shown mercy and those who obtain the mercy; for the thing formed, he says, will
not say to the one who formed it, and what follows. This on the one hand cuts
short the rash busyness of the many who contend to examine finely the willings
of God, unsearchable to human beings toward the finest point. For who are you,
he says, who examine God? and who inquire about the things he has arranged? It
is impossible for you to know the mind of the Creator, just as also for a thing
formed [to know] that of the one who formed it; for you differ in nothing from
a soulless thing toward being able to grasp God's intent. And it is also absurd
to attempt to cut off the authority of God, as if he ought not to have it in
his authority to set each of the things made in whichever rank he should wish
and should know to be beneficial, even if the nature of all is one and all are
capable of mercy. For just as it is authority for the potter, one and the same
matter of clay being set forth, to make one vessel unto honor, another unto
dishonor, so also for the Creator of the wholes, who foreknows the things to
come as present, to bring forward the one who will be shown mercy, and the one
who will be set forth. And having said these things reverently and divinely
toward the rash and those who lift themselves above human nature, he also
unveils the aim of the counsel of God and says: but if God, willing. 69
Rom 9:33 The saying that lies before lies in the prophecies of Isaiah in a
scattered way, not as it has now been said by the apostle, in a gathered way.
For toward the beginning of the book it has been written concerning the Lord
according to the edition of Aquila, with which the apostolic also has agreed:
he himself is your fear and he himself is your terror and he will be unto a
sanctuary and unto a stone of stumbling, a solid [stone] of scandal. And after
many things there is: behold I cast into the foundations of Zion a costly
stone, elect, a chief cornerstone, precious, and the one who believes shall not
be put to shame. And it is possible, looking exactly at each part of the
saying, to understand clearly the stone simply spoken in the one, in the other
the Lord interpreted upon the divine dignity.
Rom 10:4 For Christ is an end of law unto justification. For not a human being
being justified, he says, in law, having believed Christ is justified, as also
in Antioch of Pisidia he has evangelized saying: be it known to you, men
brothers, that through this one forgiveness of sins is proclaimed to you, and
from all things from which you could not be saved in law, in this one everyone
who believes is justified. If then what was being striven for by the law, but
through human inability was not being perfected, this Christ

furnishes to those who believe, imparting the holy righteousness, Christ is an
end of law, and unto Christ the law was preparing, showing this one to be
fullness of itself, but salvation of human beings.

Rom 10:14 15 The salvation through invocation of the Lord, he says, is common
to all human beings, but the aforementioned contentiousness deprived Jews of
the catholic grace, they having had a private unfitness toward the common good,
through which the apostleship and the preaching and the hearing and the faith
and the invocation have turned not unto them but unto the nations. Just as the
light, being common to all by nature, becomes alien to those who are blind, and
the sun neither shines nor rises for the blind, nor does the one who cries out
speak to the deaf, so then neither do those sent and preaching [speak] anything
toward Jews; for it is laid down beforehand that they cannot hear, as having
fallen into deafness toward the divine preachings, whence they have been
deprived 70 of the salvation by not invoking, as also in the prophet it had
been made clear; for there is not, he says, the one who invokes your name and
who remembered to take hold of you, because you turned your face away from us.
And this testimony is sufficient also toward Jews; for it is clear that Christ
whom they do not invoke is the one they do not hold to. Who turned his face
from them, from which indeed he also said: you shall not see my face from now
until you say: blessed is the one who comes, the one who comes in the name of
the Lord. And inasmuch as he has not chosen in the present, as I said, the
refutation of the Jews, but shows the mystery of the economy concerning them as
cause of their not invoking the Lord, that the apostles were sent not to them
but to the nations, as also the Master's saying has: going, make disciples of
all the nations. And the apostles were not transgressing the command when they
first rushed toward Jews, and later went over to the nations, whenever they
should be driven out by those; but the one commanding knew upon whom the
preaching was going to end, the former not being receptive. Nor does the
apostle now say things contrary to what he used to do, discoursing first with
Jews, but saying he had not been sent toward those, but according to the
definition and the foreknowledge of God he says the apostleship is given to the
nations, with the aim of being persecuted rather as the apostles go toward
Israel, as also of Moses toward Pharaoh; for God knew that he would not
persuade and said beforehand: and I know that Pharaoh will not let you go. And
there the end of Pharaoh's contradiction was the display of the signs toward
benefit of the world, but here the end of the Jews' unbelief was becoming the
journey of the apostles toward the nations according to that saying of Paul: it
was necessary that the word of the Lord be spoken first to you, but since you
thrust it away and judge yourselves not worthy of eternal life, behold we turn
to the nations. For so the Lord has commanded us. This then was the command and
the apostleship toward the nations. Hence Israel was turned about into the
former rank of the nations, unto not invoking the Lord. It is not possible then
for them to invoke, he says, since they did not believe. And they were not able
to believe, being unfit to hear, as also the Lord was discoursing with them in
a hidden way, and he showed the one able to hear among them to be rare, saying:
the one who has ears to hear, let him hear. And being present toward them he
had as aim the departure from them, saying: O wicked generation, how long shall
I be toward you; how long shall I bear with you? And that they were unable to
hear him 71 and believe, he said: you do not believe, because you are not of my
sheep. My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me. And he was
