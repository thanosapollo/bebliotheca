Of the same, a letter concerning the synods that took place at Ariminum in
Italy and at Seleucia in Isauria

1.1 Perhaps the report has reached even you concerning the council that is
even now being talked of; for letters of the emperor and of the prefects went
everywhere calling those who were to assemble at it. And since, being lovers
of learning, you also seek to know what was done, I have thought it fitting to
make known to you also the things that I have seen and come to know
accurately, so that you may not, hearing from others, have an uncertain mind,
especially because some are accustomed 1.2 to report the things done otherwise
than as they happened. At Nicaea then, as had been appointed, the assembly no
longer took place, but a second command went out, that the bishops in the
western parts should meet at Ariminum in Italy, and those in the eastern 1.3
parts should be convened at so-called Rugged Seleucia in Isauria. And the
pretext of such a gathering was talked of as concerning the faith in our Lord
Jesus Christ. And those who furnished this pretext are, from Pannonia,
Ursacius and Valens and a certain Germinius, and from Syria Acacius, Eudoxius,
and Patrophilus who 1.4 happens to be from the city named after the Scythians.
For these men, being always of the party of Arius and "understanding neither
how they believe nor of what they affirm," and deceiving each one quietly and
sowing the seeds of their own heresy, snatched away some "who seemed to be
something" and even the emperor Constantius himself, being a heretic, having
put on a show about the faith, so as to cause a council to take place,
thinking they would be able to cover over the council at Nicaea and to
persuade all, so that impiety instead of the truth should prevail everywhere.

2.1 First then I myself marvel (and I think every sensible man whatever also
sees this) that, when a general council had been appointed and all were
expecting it, it was suddenly divided; and some met there, and others convened
themselves here. But this was after all a work of providence, so that in each
the one party's guileless and pure mind of the faith might be shown, and the
other's suspected and doubtful character might be exposed. Next I was also
considering that with the genuine brothers here, and we were distressed by
ourselves seeing the impropriety of 2.2 so great a concourse. For what so
great a thing pressed, that the world should be thrown into confusion and
those called clergy at this time should run up and down and seek how they
might learn to believe in our Lord Jesus Christ? For if they believed, they
would not 2.3 have been seeking as if they did not have it. And this was no
small scandal to the catechumens, and to the Greeks not a chance thing, but
even furnished broad laughter, if Christians, as if now waking from sleep,
seek how one must believe concerning Christ. And those called clergy of these
things, although claiming the privileges from the peoples as teachers, now
convicted themselves 2.4 as unbelievers seeking what they do not have. And
those around Ursacius, having become causes of these things, did not know how
great a wrath they stored up for themselves, the Lord saying through the
saints: "Woe through those by whom my name is blasphemed among the nations,"
and through himself in the gospels: "Whoever then shall scandalize one of
these little ones, it is profitable for him that a millstone of an ass be hung
about his neck and he be sunk in the depth of the sea, or that," as Luke
added, "he scandalize one of these little ones."

3.1 For what was lacking of teaching toward piety to the catholic church, that
they should now seek concerning the faith and prefix the consulate of the
present times to the words set out by them ostensibly concerning the faith?
For Ursacius and Valens and Germinius and those 3.2 with them have done what
has neither happened nor been heard of ever among Christians. For having
written as they themselves wished to believe they prefixed the consulate and
the month and the day of the present time, so that they might show to all the
sensible that not formerly, but now under Constantius, the faith of these men
has its beginning. For they wrote looking all toward their own heresy. Besides
this, pretending to write concerning the Lord, they name another "master" for
themselves, Constantius; for he was the one furnishing them the power of
impiety; and they have called him "eternal emperor," they who 3.3 deny the Son
as everlasting; so they are Christ-fighters toward impiety. But perhaps they
have as a pretext for the consulate the chronology of the holy prophets. But
even if they dare to say this, they proclaim their own ignorance greatly. For
the prophecies of the saints have a mention of times. And Isaiah and Hosea
came to be "in the days of Uzziah and Jotham and Ahaz and Hezekiah," and
Jeremiah in the days of Josiah, and Ezekiel and Daniel under Cyrus and Darius,
and others prophesied at other times, not laying a beginning of godliness. 3.4
For it was even before them and always was, and before the foundation of the
world God prepared this for us in Christ. Nor were they indicating the times
of their own faith; for they themselves were faithful even before these times;
but the 3.5 times were of the announcement through them. And the announcement
was leading concerning the sojourn of the Savior, and as a consequence
concerning the things that would be for Israel and the nations. And the times
were indicated not of a beginning of faith, as I said before, but of the
prophets themselves, according to whom these men, having come to be, 3.6
prophesied such things. But these present wise men, not interpreting histories
nor foretelling the future, but having written "the catholic faith was set
forth," immediately added also the consulate and the month and the day, so
that, just as the saints wrote the times of the histories and of their own
ministry, so these might indicate the time of their own faith. And would that
they wrote concerning their own "for now it has begun" and did not attempt it
as concerning the catholic; for they did not write "thus we believe," but that
"the catholic faith was set forth."

4.1 The boldness of the purpose then convicts their ignorance, and the novel
device of the writing is equal to the Arian heresy. For having written thus
they showed when they themselves began to believe, and from now they wish
their faith to be 4.2 proclaimed. And just as according to the evangelist Luke
"a decree was set" concerning the enrollment, and this decree was not
formerly, but began from those days in which it was also set by the one who
wrote it, so these too, having written "the faith was now set forth," showed
4.3 that the mind of their heresy is newer and was not formerly. And if they
add "of the catholic," they have escaped their own notice falling into the
madness of those from Phrygia, so as themselves also to say according to
those: "to us first it was revealed and from us the faith of the Christians
begins." And just as those men Maximilla and Montanus, so 4.4 these inscribe
Constantius as "master" instead of Christ. And if according to them the faith
has its beginning from the present consulate, what will the fathers and the
blessed martyrs do, and what will they themselves do to those catechized by
them and fallen asleep before this consulate, how will they raise them, so
that they may wipe out what they seemed to have taught these, and sow upon
them what they have now written as having invented it? Thus they are

ignorant, knowing only how to invent pretexts, and these unseemly and
unpersuasive, having the refutation near.

5.1 For the council at Nicaea did not happen simply, but it had an urgent need
and a reasonable cause. For those from Syria and Cilicia and Mesopotamia were
lame concerning the feast and kept the Pascha with the Jews, and the Arian
heresy had risen up against the catholic church and had as champions for zeal
on 5.2 its own behalf and for plotting against the pious those around
Eusebius. And this became a cause for an ecumenical council to be gathered, so
that everywhere one day of the feast might be celebrated and the heresy that
had sprung up might be anathematized. It happened then, and those from Syria
were persuaded, and they declared the Arian heresy a forerunner of the
antichrist and 5.3 having written against it they published it well. And yet
having written, and being so many, they dared nothing such as these three or
four. For they did not prefix consulate and month and day, but concerning the
Pascha "it seemed good, the things set below." For then it seemed good that
all be persuaded; but concerning the faith they wrote not "it seemed good,"
but "thus the catholic church believes," and immediately they confessed how
they believe, so that they might show that their mind is not newer but
apostolic, and that what they wrote was not found from themselves, but these
are the things which the apostles taught.

6.1 And the councils now being moved by them, what reasonable cause do they
have? For if some other newer heresy has arisen after the Arian, let them say
the words of its invention and who those who invented it are, and writing let
them anathematize the heresies before this council, among which is also the
Arian, just as those at Nicaea have done, so that they too may seem to have
some persuasive pretext for saying newer things; but if nothing such has
happened, nor can they show it (and they themselves rather, having the impiety
of Arius, utter these things and being convicted daily they change), what is
the need of the councils, the one that happened at Nicaea sufficing both for
the Arian and 6.2 the other heresies, all of which it condemned through the
sound faith. For even the much-talked-of Aetius who was surnamed Atheos does
not grow bold having invented a madness of his own, but being storm-tossed
into the Arian heterodoxy he too was shipwrecked with those deceived by him.
In vain then running about they pretend that they thought it right that the
councils take place on account of faith. For the divine scripture is more
sufficient than all, and if there is also need of a council concerning this,
there are the things of the fathers. For those who met at Nicaea did not
neglect this either, but they also wrote so well that those who genuinely
encounter their writings can be reminded by them of the piety toward Christ
announced in the divine scriptures.

7.1 Therefore, since there is nothing reasonable with them, and they are at a
loss from every side, although they also invent pretexts, this remains for
them to say: we, contradicting those before us and transgressing the
traditions of the fathers, thought it right that a council take place. But
again fearing that if they met again into one our zeal would become vain, we
thought it right that the council be divided into two, so that, having come
about in part, showing what we wrote we might be able to snatch the greater
number, threatening Constantius who stands at the head of the impiety, and
thus we may cause the things done at Nicaea to be idle, 7.2 having put on a
show of the simplicity of the things written by us. And these things, even if
they have not said them in the words themselves, yet with this mind they have
done all and thrown all into confusion. At any rate having said many things
many times and written in various councils they never yet mentioned the Arian
heresy as bad, but if ever some present were slandering the heresies

they themselves were defending the Arian, which the council at Nicaea 7.3
anathematized, and rather these received genuinely those who say the things of
Arius. This then is a great proof that the device of the present councils was
not for truth but for the destruction of the things done at Nicaea; and what
has been done in the councils themselves by them and their like shows these
things to be no less true. For it is necessary remaining to make clear how
each thing happened.

8.1 When all were expecting those whom the letters from the emperor called to
come together into one, and that the council become one, since they were
divided, some went down to Seleucia called Rugged, and others met at Ariminum.
And the bishops in it were 400 and more; and to this also went up Germinius
and Auxentius and Valens 8.2 and Ursacius and Demophilus and Gaius. But while
all the bishops were discoursing from the divine scriptures these men were
displaying a paper and, reading the consulate, they thought it right that
these things be preferred to every council and that nothing more than these be
sought from the heretics nor their mind be examined, but that they be content
with these alone. And the things written by them are these: 8.3 The catholic
faith was set forth in the presence of our master the most pious and
victorious emperor Constantius Augustus the eternal Augustus, in the consulate
of the Flavii Eusebius and Hypatius the most illustrious, at Sirmium on the
11th before the Kalends of June. 8.4 We believe in one, the only and true God,
Father almighty and creator and maker of all things, and in one only-begotten
Son of God, begotten impassibly from God before all the ages and before every
beginning and before every conceived time and before every comprehensible
essence, through whom both the ages were framed and all things came to be,
begotten only-begotten, only from the only Father, God from God, like to the
Father who begot him according to the scriptures, whose generation no one 8.5
knows except only the Father who begot him. This one we know as the
only-begotten Son of God, coming from the heavens by paternal nod for the
putting away of sin and born from Mary the virgin and having lived with the
disciples and having fulfilled all the economy according to the paternal will,
crucified and having died and having gone down into the underworld and having
administered the things there, whom "the gatekeepers of Hades seeing"
shuddered, and having risen from the dead on the third day and having lived
with the disciples and having fulfilled all the economy and, fifty days being
fulfilled, taken up into the heavens and sitting at the right of the Father
and coming on the last day of the resurrection in the paternal glory,
rendering to each 8.6 according to his works. And in the Holy Spirit, which
the only-begotten himself promised to send to the race of men, the Paraclete,
according to what is written: "I go to my Father and I will ask the Father and
he will send you another Paraclete, the Spirit of truth; that one will take
from what is mine and will teach and remind you 8.7 of all things." And the
name of essence, because it was set by the fathers more simply, and being
unknown to the peoples brings scandal, because the scriptures do not contain
this, it seemed good that this be taken away and that there be altogether no
mention of essence upon God for the rest, because the divine scriptures
nowhere mention essence concerning Father and Son. And we say the Son like to
the Father in all things as also the holy scriptures say and teach.

9.1 When these things were read the knavery of those who wrote them was known
not far off. For when the bishops proposed that the Arian heresy be
anathematized together with the other heresies also, and all agreed upon this,
Ursacius

and Valens and those with them refused. And remaining they were condemned as
having written these things not genuinely but for the destruction of the
things done at Nicaea, so that they might introduce the ill-named heresy
instead. 9.2 Marveling then at their deceitful words and knavish purpose they
said: "We did not meet needing faith (for we have in ourselves the sound
faith), but that we might put to shame those who contradict the truth and
attempt to innovate. If then you wrote these things as now beginning to
believe, you are not yet clergy, having a beginning of being catechized; but
if you also came having this mind with which we met, let there be
like-mindedness of all, and let us anathematize the heresies with an anathema,
and let us keep the things of the fathers, so that pretexts of councils no
longer run about, those who met at Nicaea having once gone before and having
done all things for the catholic 9.3 church." But even thus, when all the
bishops again agreed, the aforementioned refused, and remaining, having
condemned them as ignorant and deceitful and rather heretics, all voted with
the council that took place at Nicaea and all judged to be content with this,
and they declared the aforementioned Ursacius and Valens, Germinius,
Auxentius, Gaius, Demophilus heretics and, having deposed them as not being
truly Christians but Arians, they wrote against them in Latin, and being
translated into Greek according to ability these things:

10.t Copy of a letter from the council to Augustus Constantius

10.1 From both the command of God and the order of your piety we believe that
the things long ago dogmatized have come to be. For from all the cities toward
the west all the bishops met together at Ariminum, so that both the faith of
the catholic 10.2 church might be made known and those who think the contrary
might become evident. For as we have found examining at greatest length, it
seemed acceptable that the faith remaining from of old, which both the
prophets and the gospels and the apostles proclaimed through our Lord Jesus
Christ, who is also the guardian of your empire and protector of your
strength, that holding this we should keep it and keeping it we should
preserve it until the end. 10.3 For it seemed absurd and unlawful to change
anything of the things rightly and justly defined and of the things considered
in common at Nicaea with the most glorious Constantine your father and
emperor, whose teaching and mind both went through and was proclaimed into all
hearings and minds of men, which alone was antagonist and destroyer of the
heresy of Arius, through which not only this but also the remaining heresies
were taken down, in which truly both to add something is slippery and to take
something away is dangerous, so that, if either should happen, there will be
for the enemies license to do what they wish. Whence Ursacius and 10.4 Valens,
since they had long ago been established as partners and of one voice with the
Arian dogma and were declared separated from our communion, of which, that
they might partake, they thought it right to obtain repentance and pardon for
the faults of which they had convicted themselves, as also the documents made
by them bear witness, through all of which sparing came to be and pardon of
the charges (and the time at which these things were done was when the session
of the council was convened at Milan, the presbyters of the church of the
Romans also being present), and knowing at the same time also Constantine of
worthy memory after death to have, with all accuracy and

examination, having set forth the faith that was composed. 10.5 And since he,
as from men, having been baptized, departed to the peace owed, we thought it
absurd after him to innovate anything and to overlook so many holy confessors,
martyrs, who were both writers and finders of this dogma, who have remained
thinking all things according to the ancient ordinance of the church, whose
faith God has also transmitted into the times of your empire through our
master Jesus Christ, through whom it has so belonged to you also to reign 10.6
as even to hold the world among us. Again then the pitiable and wretched in
mind announced themselves as heralds of the impious thinking by an unlawful
daring, and they attempt to overturn every composition of truth. For when
according to your command the session of the council had been convened, those
men also were laying bare the 10.7 plan of their own deceit. For they were
attempting with a certain knavery and disturbance, bringing something to
innovate, having found of such a company those caught together, Germinius,
Auxentius and Gaius, who produce the strife and division, whose teaching,
being one, has surpassed every multitude of blasphemies. And when they
perceived that they were not of such a purpose nor of one mind upon the things
they thought badly, they transferred themselves into our council as if to seem
to write something else; but the time was short which also convicted their
minds. 10.8 So that therefore the things of the church may not always fall
upon the same and disturbance and tumult rolling about confuse all, it seemed
firm to keep the things long ago defined as lawful and unmoved, and that the
aforementioned be separated from our communion, for which cause we have sent
the ambassadors who instruct toward your equity, to indicate through the
letter the mind 10.9 of the session. And we charged the ambassadors this
before all, to confirm the truth, starting from the things long ago ancient
and just; who will also instruct your holiness that it will not be peace, as
Ursacius and Valens said, if anything of the just things should be overturned.
For how is it possible for those who dissolve the peace to keep peace? For
rather strife and disturbance from these will come to be, together with the
remaining cities, also for the church of the Romans. =10.10= Wherefore we
beseech your equity, that with gentle hearings and a calm look you would
behold our ambassadors, and would not permit to change anything new toward
insult of those who have died, but would allow us to remain in the things
defined and legislated by the ancestors, whom we would say to have done all
things with sagacity and prudence and Holy Spirit. =10.11= For the things now
being innovated by those men produce unbelief in those who have believed, and
cruelty in those who do not believe. And we still beseech that you command the
bishops who are spending time in foreign parts, whom both the toil of age and
the lack of poverty wear down, to make the return to their own easy, =10.12=
so that the churches may not remain deserted of the bishops who have been set
apart. And still besides all we also ask this, that nothing either be lacking
of the things that existed before or be in excess, but that all remain
unbroken, being kept from the piety of your father even into the present time,
and that you no longer permit us to toil and to become strangers to our own
sojournings, but that the bishops with their own peoples may have leisure with
peace for prayers and worships, beseeching for your empire and salvation and
peace, which the divinity will grant you unto the continuous. And our
ambassadors bring both the signatures and the names of the bishops, who will
also instruct your holiness from the divine scriptures themselves.

11.t Decree of the council

11.1 As far as it was fitting and possible, most honored brothers, the
catholic council and the holy church has borne not ignobly with its own
patience and long-suffering Ursacius and Valens and Gaius and Germinius and
Auxentius, who thinking other things at other times have thrown all the
churches into confusion, who have now also dared to attempt to join the
heretical reasoning to the faith of the orthodox and to dissolve the council
at Nicaea, which is antagonist to the heresy of the Arians, bringing from
outside a certain faith of their own, most alien to the most holy church,
which we have considered it unlawful 11.2 for us to receive. For long ago
these being heretics and thinking the contrary have now also been shown, whom
we do not permit to come together to our communion, having condemned and
deposing them present by their own voice. Now then declare what seems good,
11.3 so that the knowledge of each one may be firm from the signature.
Unanimously we the bishops say: "Let the aforementioned enemies be deposed, so
that the catholic faith may remain in peace." And the things done at Ariminum
had a swift and such a dissolution. For no one there disagreed, but all in
agreement wrote the things that seemed good and deposed the Arians.

12.1 And the things that happened at Rugged Seleucia are these. The month was
that called according to the Romans September, and according to the Egyptians
Thoth and according to the Macedonians Gorpiaios, and day 10 of the month
according to the Egyptians, on which all those called 12.2 to meet ran
together; and there too they were about 160. And there being many of those
accused among them and of the accusers crying out against them, Acacius and
Patrophilus and Uranios from Tyre and Eudoxius who had leapt upon the church
of the Antiochenes, and Leontius and Theodosius and Evagrius and Theodulus and
George who was driven from the world, 12.3 do something knavish. For fearing
the proofs against them from the accusers they took to themselves the others
of the party of Arius who become hired for this very thing toward impiety and
were appointed by Secundus who was deposed by the great council, from Libya
Stephen and Seras and Polydeuces, being accused upon various charges, and
remaining Pancratius and Ptolemy a certain Melitian; and having put on a show
of seeking ostensibly concerning the faith they did not escape the accusers
12.4 as being afraid. And they were defending the heresy and remaining they
were split. And those with those around Acacius were suspected and altogether
few, and the others were the multitude. Those around Acacius then, using the
daring of madness, were denying completely the things written at Nicaea and
were slandering the council, and the others (and these were the multitude)
accepted all the other things of the council, and only the word homoousion
they were pretexting as suspected from 12.5 unclarity. When then they were
lingering in the place and the accusers were pressing, and the accused were
fleeing and through this being led out more in impieties and blaspheming
against the Lord, the multitude of the bishops was angry and they deposed
Acacius, Patrophilus, Uranios, Eudoxius and George from the collectors and
others from Asia, Leontius and Theodosius, Evagrius and Theodulus, and they
made without communion Asterius, Eusebius, Augarus and Basilicus 12.6 and
Phoebus and Fidelis and Eustathius and Eutychius and Magnus. And they did
this, because, having been called to defend themselves upon the things of
which very many were accusing them, they did not present themselves. And they
defined that they remain thus, until, having defended themselves, they show
12.7 themselves clean from the charges brought against them.

charges. And having written into the parish of each the judgment that had come
about against them they went up to the most impious Augustus Constantius to
announce to him the things done; for they had this command. And this became an

13.1 Who then would not accept the reverence of the bishops who met at the
council at Ariminum? Who endured so great a toil of road and dangers of sea,
that they might depose those who think the things of Arius, and keep the
definitions of the fathers unharmed, having resolved this holily and
canonically. For each of them thought that if they should dissolve the things
of those before them, they would give a pretext to those after these, so as to
dissolve the things now coming about 13.2 from them. And who would not condemn
the easiness of those around Eudoxius and Acacius, who drink away the honor of
their own fathers to the zeal and favor toward the Ariomaniacs? For what faith
is there in the things coming about from them, if the things of the former are
dissolved? Or how do they name "fathers" those whom they succeeded, of whose
mind they themselves become 13.3 accusers? And especially Acacius, what would
he say to Eusebius his own teacher, who not only subscribed at the council at
Nicaea, but also through a letter made clear to the peoples under him that
this is true faith, that confessed at the council at Nicaea? For even if he
defended himself as he wished through the letter, yet at least he did not deny
the words, but also accused the Arians because saying "the Son was not 13.4
before he was begotten, they did not wish him to be even before Mary." What
then will they also teach the peoples who were taught by those men? That the
fathers erred? And how will they themselves be believed among them whom they
teach to disobey the teachers? And with what eyes will they look even upon the
tombs of the fathers whom they now name heretics? And why do they pull down
those from Valentinus and from Phrygia and the Manichees, and name those who
uttered the like, as they themselves suppose, "saints"? Or how can they
themselves still 13.5 be bishops, if they were appointed by heretics, as they
themselves slander? And if they also thought badly and writing they deceived
the world, let even the memory concerning these cease completely. And if their
writings are cast out, you going away from the cemeteries cast out the remains
of these, so that all may know those men as deceivers, and you as
father-killers.

14.1 The blessed apostle then accepts the Corinthians saying: "that you
remember me in all things, and just as I delivered to you the traditions, so
you hold them," but these, thinking such things concerning those before them,
will altogether dare to say also the contrary to the peoples: "we do not
praise you remembering the fathers, but we rather accept 14.2 you, when you do
not hold their traditions." And remaining let them slander their own ill-birth
and say: "we have come to be not from the pious, but from heretics." For such
things it is fitting to say to those who, as I said before, drink away the
honor of the fathers and their own salvation to the Arian heresy and do not
fear to hear what is written in the divine proverb, "a bad offspring curses a
father," and the threat 14.3 lying in the law against such. Those men then
through zeal on behalf of the heresy have had such a contentious mind also,
but you do not on this account be disturbed nor consider their daring to be
truth. For they also stand against themselves and, having stood away from the
fathers, they do not have one mind, but they swim in varied and different
changes. And wrangling toward the council at Nicaea they have themselves also
made many councils, and setting forth a faith at each they have remained in
none; but they will not even cease always doing this, because seeking badly
they will not 14.4 find the wisdom which they hated. I have therefore
necessarily subjoined in part both the things written

by Arius and as many as I was able to collect of the things these men set
forth in various councils, so that you may know and marvel why they, wrangling
toward an ecumenical council and toward their own fathers, are not ashamed.

15.1 Arius and those who thought with him and say "God has made the Son from
things that are not and has called him a son for himself, and the Word of God
is one of the creatures, and there was once when he was not, and he is
changeable, being able, when he wishes, to change" were cast out 15.2 of the
church by the blessed Alexander. But having been cast out and being worn by
those around Eusebius, Arius composed his own heresy on paper and, as in a

"another child to him," and concerning the son of the widow also, whom the
Lord raised from the dead, that he also did not have a brother, but was
only-begotten, then was each of them not consubstantial with the one who
begot? And yet they were; for they were children; and this is proper of

52.1 And otherwise also, if confessing Father and Son we say two certain
beginnings or two gods just as Marcion and Valentinus or that the Son has some
other manner of divinity and the Son not to be image and character of the
Father because of having grown from him, let him be unlike; for such essences
are wholly foreign to one another; but if we know one and only divinity that
of the Father and the Son to be Word and wisdom of this one and believing thus
we do not say two gods and we think the unity of the Son toward the Father not
by likeness of teaching, but according to the essence and in truth, so that
through this we do not say two 52.2 gods, but one God of one form of divinity
being as is the light and the radiance. For this was seen by the patriarch
Jacob, and the scripture says: "and the sun rose upon him, when the form of
God passed." And the holy prophets seeing this and thinking, of whom he is son
and image, were saying: "the Word of the Lord came to me" knowing the Father
seen and revealed in him they took heart to say: "the God of our fathers
appeared to me, the God of Abraham and Isaac and 52.3 Jacob." And this being
thus, why do we, the one being toward the Father and appearing as he is
Father, according to the likeness and one divinity, be cautious to say
consubstantial? For if, as has been said many times, he does not have the
property of the paternal essence nor the resemblance as a son, we are well
cautious, but if this is the illuminating and creative the most proper of the
Father, without whom he neither creates nor is known ("for all things through
him and in him consist"), why thinking the thing do we decline to say the
phrase that signifies this? For what is that 52.4 thus of the same nature with
the Father if not consubstantial of him? For God did not take on the Son as
foreign needing a minister nor are the things made equal in honor to the one
who created, so as for these to be honored as that one or to think that these
and the Father are one. Or let someone dare to divide and say the sun and the
radiance to be two lights or some other essence to be or that the radiance has
supervened and this not to be a simple and pure offspring from the sun, so
that they are two, sun and radiance, and the light is one because this is an
offspring from the sun. 52.5 And such being and rather undivided the nature of
the Son toward the Father and the divinity not having supervened to the Son,
but the paternal divinity being in the Son, so that he who has seen the Son
sees in him the Father, why would such a one not be said consubstantial?

53.1 These things then also are sufficient to persuade you not to accuse those
who have said the Son to be consubstantial with the Father, and yet let us
also examine the phrase itself of the homoousion by itself so that we may
know, if one must say consubstantial at all and if it is a proper phrase and
this is fittingly 53.2 said upon a son. For you also know and no one would
doubt that the like is said not upon essences, but upon shapes and qualities
as like; for upon 53.3 essences not likeness, but identity would be said. A
man then is said like to a man not according to the essence, but according to
the shape and the character; for by the essence they are of the same nature.
And

again a man is not said unlike to a dog, but of another nature; therefore the
of the same nature is also consubstantial, and the of another nature is also
of another essence. Wherefore also he who says like according to essence says
this like from participation. For the like is a quality, which would be added
to the essence. And this would be proper of the things made; for these from
partaking are likened to God. "For when," he says, "he is manifested, we will
be like him" like clearly not in 53.4 the essence, but in the sonship, of
which we partake from him. If then you also say the Son from participation,
let him be said by you homoiousion. He is not however being said thus in truth
nor wholly light nor by nature God. For the things from participation are said
like not in truth but by likeness of the truth, so as even to be able to fall
away and to be taken away the partaking from those who 53.5 partake. And this
again is proper of the creatures and things made. Therefore if this is absurd,
he would not be from participation, but by nature and in truth Son, light,
wisdom, God. And being by nature and not by partaking he would not properly be
said homoiousion, but homoousion. And what one would not say even upon others;
for it was shown that the like is not said upon essences; how is it not
mindless or perhaps even hard to bring this forward upon a son and not rather
the homoousion?

knowing that those who wrote these things not as misinterpreting the truth,
but defending the truth and the piety toward Christ, and taking down 54.2 the
