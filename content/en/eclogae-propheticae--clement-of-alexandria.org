EXTRACTS FROM THE PROPHETIC SCRIPTURES 1.1 Those around Sedrach, Misak,
Abdenago, hymning God in the furnace of fire, say: "Bless, O heavens, the
Lord; hymn and exalt him unto the ages." Then: "Bless, O angels, the Lord."
Then: "Bless, O waters and all things above the heaven, the Lord." Thus the
scriptures rank the heavens and the waters among the powers of the pure, as is
also made clear at the creation. 1.3 Reasonably then, the power being named in
various ways, Daniel adds: "Let every power bless the Lord." Then next:
"Bless, O sun 1.4 and moon, the Lord." And: "Bless, O stars of heaven, the
Lord; bless, all who worship the Lord, the God of gods; hymn and confess, for
unto all the ages is his mercy 1.5." [It is written in Daniel of the three
children hymning in the furnace.] 2.1 "Blessed are you who look upon the
abysses, seated upon Cherubim," Daniel says, being of the same mind as Enoch
who said: "And I saw all 2.2 the materials." For abyss is the unbounded
according to its own subsistence, 2.3 but bounded by the power of God. The
material substances, then, from which the particular genera and their species
come to be, have been called abysses; since he would not have called water
alone an abyss. And yet water too is allegorized as abyss or matter. 3.1 "In
the beginning God made the heaven and the earth," the earthly 3.2 and the
heavenly. That this is true, the Lord said to Hosea: "Go, take for yourself a
wife of fornication and children of fornication, because 3.3 committing
fornication the land will fornicate away from behind the Lord." For he does
not mean the element, but those upon the element, those who have an earth-born
mind. 4.1 And that the Son is beginning, Hosea teaches clearly: "And it shall
be in the place where it was said to them 'you are not my people', they too
shall be called sons of the living God. And the sons of Judah and the sons of
Israel shall be gathered together, and they shall set for themselves one
beginning, and they shall go up 4.2 from the land, for great is the day of
Israel." For whom one believes, this one he chooses. And one believes the Son,
who is beginning. Wherefore also he said [beforehand]: "But the sons of Judah
I will have mercy on and I will save in the Lord their God," and Savior is the
Son of God who saves. This one then is the beginning. 5.1 Through Hosea the
Spirit says "But I am your instructor": "Sound 5.2 a trumpet upon the hills of
the Lord, sound upon the high places." And is baptism itself, being a sign of
regeneration, perhaps a going-out from matter through the teaching of the
Savior, of a great and violent 5.3 stream always being carried and carrying us
aside. Leading us out then from disorder the Lord illuminates, bringing us
into the light that is shadowless and no longer material. 6.1 This river of
matter and the sea two prophets cut through and divided by the power of the
Lord, matter being bounded according to 6.2 each interval of water by the will
of God; both served as pure generals, through whom the signs were entrusted,
in order that the righteous man might 6.3 come to be from matter, having
traveled through it first. And to one of the two generals the name of our
Savior had even been applied.


7.1 Immediately regeneration is through "water and spirit," just as also 7.2
all generation. "For the Spirit of God was borne upon the abyss." And for this
reason the Savior was baptized, not needing it himself, in order that he might
sanctify all the water for those being regenerated. In this way we are
purified not only in the body but also in the 7.3 soul. A sign, at any rate,
that our invisible things also are being sanctified is that unclean spirits
entangled with the soul are filtered out from the new and spiritual
generation. 8.1 "Water above the heaven": since baptism comes about through
"water and spirit," being a defense against the double fire, both that which
touches the invisible and that of the visible, it is necessary that of water
too one part be intelligible, the other perceptible, a defense against the
doubleness of 8.2 the fire; and the earthly water washes off the body, but the
heavenly water, through being intelligible and invisible spirit, is
allegorized as holy, purifying of the invisible things, as it were water of
the spirit just as that other is of the body. 9.1 God mixed fear also with
goodness through goodness. For he already provides this, what is beneficial
for each, as a physician to one who is ill, 9.2 as a father to a child who is
disorderly. "For he who spares his rod hates his son." And the Lord too and
his apostles 9.3 conducted themselves in fear and toils. When then a
believer's body is sick, either because of some sin previously committed, the
Lord rebuking, or because of things about to be, having guarded in advance, or
also according to an activity * * an assault coming from outside, not having
prevented it on account of something useful both for him and for those nearby,
for the sake of an example. 10.1 Already those dwelling in a rotten body, as
if sailing in an old ship, are not supine, but always pray stretched out
toward God. The elders were exceedingly vexed if they did not suffer something
in the body each time; for they feared lest somehow, not receiving here the
wages of the sins, which follow in many ways those in the flesh through
ignorance, they might receive the penalty all at once there, so that here they
thought it right to be 11.2 healed. One must fear then not the sickness from
outside, but the sins, on account of which the sickness, and sickness of soul,
not of body, because "all flesh is grass," and the bodily and external goods
are "temporary, but the things not seen are eternal." 12.1 Of the things of
knowledge some we already share, and others we firmly hope for through those
we have; for we have neither received all nor do we lack all, but as it were
we have received an "earnest" of the eternal goods and of the paternal wealth;
and the provisions of the Lord's way are the 12.2 beatitudes of the Lord. For
he said, "Seek, and be anxious for the kingdom 12.2 of God, and all these
things shall be added to you; for 12.3 the Father knows of what you have
need." Thus he cuts off not only the occupations but also the cares. "For you
cannot," he says, "by being anxious 12.4 add something to your stature." For
God knows clearly which things it is beneficial for us to have, and of which
to be in want. Having emptied ourselves then 12.5 of worldly cares he thinks
it right that we be filled with that toward God. "For we groan, longing to put
on" the incorruptible things, before putting off 12.6 the corruption. For when
faith is poured on, unbelief flows away. Likewise also in the case of
knowledge and righteousness. One must then not only empty the 12.7 soul, but
also fill it with God. For it is no longer evil, since it has ceased, nor yet
good, since it has not yet received; and what is neither good nor 12.8 evil is
nothing. "For it returns into the house that has been cleaned and is empty,"
if none of the saving things


has been put in, the unclean 12.9 spirit that previously dwelt, taking along
with it seven other unclean spirits. Wherefore having emptied of evils one
must fill the soul with the good God, which is a chosen dwelling-place. For
when the empty things have been filled, then the seal follows, in order that
the holy thing may be kept for God. 13.1 "Every word stands upon two and three
witnesses," upon Father and Son and Holy Spirit, upon which witnesses and
helpers the commandments that are spoken ought to be kept. Fasting is
abstinence from food according to the signification, and food makes us neither
more righteous nor more unrighteous, but according to the mystic sense it
shows that just as for individuals life is from food, and want of food is a
symbol of death, so also we must fast from the worldly things, in order that
we may die to the world and after this, having partaken 14.2 of divine food,
may live to God. And otherwise fasting empties the soul of matter and presents
it pure and light together with the body to the 14.3 divine words. Worldly
food then is the former life and the sins, but divine food is faith, hope,
love, endurance, knowledge, 14.4 peace, self-control. "For blessed are those
who hunger and thirst for the righteousness of God; for these shall also be
filled." And a soul, but not a body, takes this appetite. 15.1 The Savior
showed prayer stronger than faith to the faithful apostles, in the case of a
certain demoniac whom they were not able to 15.2 cleanse, saying: "Such things
are accomplished by prayer." The one who has believed received forgiveness of
sins from the Lord, but the one who has come to be in knowledge, inasmuch as
he no longer sins, receives from himself the forgiveness of the rest. 16.1
Just as the healings and the prophecies and the signs, so also the gnostic
teaching is accomplished through men, God working. 16.2 For God displays the
power through men. And rightly the prophecy says: "And I will send upon them a
man who will save them." He himself then sends out sometimes prophets,
sometimes apostles, saviors of men. Thus God does good through men; 16.3 for
it is not that God can some things and cannot others, nor is he ever weak in
anything, nor do some things come about with him willing and others unwilling,
and some by him and others by another, but he also generated us through men
and educated us through men. 17.1 God made us not previously existing. For we
would have needed also to know where we were, if we previously existed, and
how and why we have come here; but if we 17.2 did not previously exist, God
alone is cause of the generation. As then he made us when we were not, so also
having come to be he saves us by his own grace, if we appear worthy and fit,
but if not, he will let us go toward our own end; "for he is Lord of the
living and of the dead." 18.1 See the power of God, not only in the case of
men bringing those who are not into being and increasing those who have come
to be according to the progress of ages, but also saving those who believe
according to what is proper to each; 18.2 and already he also changes seasons
and times and fruits and elements. For this one is one God who has also
measured the beginning and the end of the things that have come to be in a
manner proper to each. 19.1 A man who has progressed from faith and fear into
knowledge knows to say "Lord, Lord," but not as the slave; he has learned to
say "our Father," having freed "the spirit of slavery unto fear" and having
progressed through love into "adoption,"


already reverencing through love him whom he 19.2 formerly feared; for he no
longer abstains from the things to be abstained from through fear, but through
love he holds to the commandments. "The Spirit itself," he says, "bears
witness," when we say: "Abba, Father." 20.1 And the Lord buys us with
"precious blood," releasing us from the bitter masters of old, from the sins,
through which "the spiritual things of 20.2 wickedness" ruled over us. He
leads then into the freedom of the Father, 20.3 sons and friends,
fellow-heirs. "For my brothers," says the Lord, "and fellow-heirs are those
who do the will of my Father." "Do not then call for yourselves a father upon
the earth"; for there are masters upon the earth, but in the heavens the
Father, "from whom every family in heaven and 20.4 upon earth." For love rules
the willing, but fear the unwilling, this "base" one; but that which trains
toward the good leads to Christ and is saving. 21.1 If then someone conceives
God (in no way according to worth, for what conception is worthy of God? but
as it is possible), let him conceive a great and unthinkable and most
beautiful "inaccessible light," having as his lot every good power, every
refined virtue, caring for all, pitiful, impassible, good, knowing all things,
foreknowing all things, unmixed, sweet, bright, undefiled. Since the soul
moves from itself, the grace of God demands from the soul what it has, the
eagerness, as a contribution toward salvation; for he wishes the good to be
the soul's own, which the 22.2 Lord gives it, for it is not without
perception, that it may be carried as a body. Having then belongs to the one
who has received, but receiving to the one who has willed and reached out, and
22.3 holding what he received to the one who has practiced holding and is
able. For this reason God has given the choice [to] the soul, in order that he
himself may indicate what is needed, and it, having chosen, may receive and
hold fast. 23.1 Just as through the body the Savior spoke and healed, so also
formerly through the prophets, and now through the apostles and 23.2 the
teachers; for the church serves the activity of the Lord, whence also then he
took up a man, in order that through him he might serve the 23.3 will of the
Father. And always the man-loving God puts on a man for the salvation of men,
formerly the prophets, and now the church. For like serving like is fitted
toward the like salvation. 24.1 When we were of dust, we were Caesar's. And
Caesar is the temporary ruler, whose image also is the dusty old man, into
whom he ran 24.2 back. To this one then the dusty things must be rendered,
which "we have borne in the image of the dusty," and "the things of God to
God"; for each of the 24.3 passions is as it were a letter and stamp and sign
for us. Another stamp now the Lord imprints on us and other names and letters,
faith instead of unbelief, and the rest. Thus we are transferred from the
material things to the spiritual, "having borne the image of the heavenly."
25.1 John says that "I baptize you with water, but he comes after me who
baptizes you in spirit and fire." But he baptized no one with fire; "but
some," as Heracleon says, "marked with fire the ears of those being sealed,"
having heard the apostolic saying thus. "For the winnowing-fan is in his hand
to cleanse the threshing-floor, and he will gather the wheat into the
storehouse, but the chaff he will burn up 25.2 with unquenchable fire." There
is added then to "through fire" the "through spirit," since <as> the wheat is
separated from the chaff (that is, from the material garment) through spirit
and the chaff is separated through the spirit being winnowed, so the spirit


has a separating power 25.3 of material activities. And since some things have
come to be from the ungenerated and incorruptible, the seminal things of life,
<are gathered as> the wheat and stored away, but the material, as long as it
is with the better, remains; but when it is separated from that, it perishes;
for it had its being in another. This then is separating in power, the spirit,
and consuming, the fire, and fire 25.4 must be understood as the material. But
since what is saved is like wheat, and what has grown around the soul like
chaff, and the one is incorporeal, and what is separated is material, he set
opposite to the incorporeal the spirit, fine and pure almost beyond mind, and
to the material the fire, being neither wicked nor evil, but strong and
purifying of evil; for fire is conceived as a good power and strong,
destructive of the worse and saving of the better, wherefore also this fire is
called intelligent among the prophets. 26.1 Thus at any rate also when God is
said to be "a consuming fire," not 26.2 of wickedness, but a name and symbol
of power must be taken; for as fire is the strongest of the elements and
masters all, so also God is all-powerful and almighty, the one able to master,
create, make, nourish, increase, save, having authority of body and soul. As
then fire surpasses the elements, so the Almighty surpasses gods and 26.3
powers and rulers. And the power of fire is double, one fitted toward
fashioning and ripening of fruits and generation and nourishment of animals,
of which the sun is an image, and the other toward consuming and 26.4
destruction, as the earthly fire. When then God is said to be "a consuming
fire," it is a strong and unapproachable power, to which nothing is
impossible, 26.5 but even destroying is possible. Concerning such a power the
Savior also says: "I came to cast fire upon the earth," clearly a power
purifying of the holy, but of the material things, as those men say,
annihilating, but as we would say, educative. And fire also has fear, and
light has spreading. 27.1 And the elders did not write, neither wishing to
occupy the teaching care of the tradition with the other care concerning
writing, nor yet consuming the time of considering the things to be said 27.2
upon writing. And perhaps being persuaded that the compositional and the
teaching form are not even an achievement of the same nature, they 27.3
conceded it to those naturally fitted for this. For the one is carried
unhindered and with rush, a stream of the speaker, and perhaps somewhere even
able to snatch along, but the other, being tested each time by those who
encounter it, obtaining an exact examination, is thought worthy also of the
highest care, and is, so to speak, a written confirmation of teaching, and
thus the voice is sent on through the composition also to those born 27.4
later. For the deposit of the elders, speaking through the writing, uses the
writer as a servant toward the tradition <unto salvation> of those who will
27.5 encounter it. As then the magnet, sending past the other matter, draws
only iron through fitness, so also the books, there being many who encounter
them, draw 27.6 only those able to understand. For the word of truth is found
"foolishness" to some, "a stumbling-block" to others, and to few "wisdom" thus
and "power 27.7 of God"; and let envy be far from the gnostic. For this reason
he also seeks whether it is worse to give to the unworthy or not to hand over
to the worthy, and he risks through much love that he will share not only with
every fitting person, but sometimes even with an unworthy one who asks
persistently, not through the asking (for he is not glory-loving), but through
the persistence of the one asking, who is practicing toward faith through much
asking. 28.1 And there are some who, saying they are gnostics, envy their own
more than those outside. And as the sea is let go for all, but one swims,
another trades, another


hunts the fishes, and as the earth is common, but one travels, another plows,
and another hunts, and someone else searches mines, and another builds, so
also when the scripture is read one is benefited unto faith, another unto
character, and another takes away superstition through the knowledge 28.2 of
the things. And the athlete, having recognized the Olympic stadium, strips for
the teaching and contends and becomes victory-bearing, having knocked aside
and contended down those who oppose and run against the gnostic 28.3 way. For
knowledge is necessary both toward exercise of soul and toward dignity of
character, making the faithful more worthy of reverence and exact spectators
of the things. For as it is not possible to believe without catechesis, so
neither to grasp without knowledge. 29.1 For there being things useful and
necessary unto salvation, such as Father and Son and Holy Spirit, but also our
soul, the account concerning them, which is gnostic, must altogether be both
useful 29.2 and necessary. And for those who have taken up also the presidency
of benefiting, much experience is useful toward nothing escaping of the things
that seem among others to have been recognized as necessary and with much
learning. 29.3 And otherwise the setting-out of heterodox teaching also brings
exercise of a searching soul, and keeps the disciple of the true undeceived,
already having practiced in advance to be trumpeted around from every side by
the warlike instruments. 30.1 Of the gnostic alone, just as they say Crete is
barren of destructive beasts, the life is pure from every wicked deed and
thought and word, having no enemy at all, but being also outside of envy and
hatred and all blasphemy and slander. In a long life he is blessed not through
having come to live long, for whom it has also been possible through living
well to become worthy of living always. 31.2 He has pained no one, except by
the word educating those wounded in the heart, as with saving honey, being
both sweet and biting. So that above all the gnostic would keep what is
fitting together with what is according to reason. 31.3 For all that is
passionate having been circumcised and stripped away from the whole soul by
the strongest part, having become pure and having been freed unto adoption,
for the rest he both is together with it and lives. 32.1 Pythagoras thought it
right to consider not only most eloquent but also oldest 32.2 of the wise the
one who set the names to the things. One must then, searching the scriptures
exactly, since they have been agreed to have been spoken in parables, hunt
from the names the opinions which the Holy Spirit, having about the things,
having stamped as it were its own mind into the expressions, teaches, in order
that, being examined exactly by us, the names spoken in many senses may be
unfolded, and what is hidden in the many coverings, being felt out and 32.3
learned, may be brought to light and shine forth. [For thus] also the lead
flowers out white from black for those who rub, white lead, so also knowledge,
pouring brightness and radiance upon the things, would be the truly divine
wisdom, the unmixed light, which illuminates the pure of men as the pupil of
an eye unto vision and firm grasp of the truth. 33.1 Having kindled then from
the beginning of that light from the longing concerning it, trying as much as
possible to be likened <to it>, 33.2 full of light, we become in truth lights,
Israelites. For he called friends and brothers those who hold to the
assimilation toward the divine according to desire and pursuit.


34.1 * * which pure places and meadows received, <having> voices and certain
visions of holy appearances, and the man who has been exactly purified wholly
will be thought worthy of teaching and divine power. And I know that the
mysteries of knowledge bring mockery to the many, and especially when not
dressed with sophistic tropology, but the few, as a light brought in suddenly
upon some densely-covered symposium, it astounds at first, then having grown
accustomed and been nourished together and exercised in reasoning, as being
gladdened by pleasure and 35.2 rejoicing * * the Lord. For as pleasure has as
its substance the release from pain, so knowledge has 35.3 the removal of
ignorance. For as those who sleep most think they are most awake, falling
under clearer and undistracted dream-phantasies, so those who are most
ignorant think they know most. 35.4 And blessed are those who are being
awakened from this sleep and madness and have looked up at the light and the
truth. 36.1 One must then have equally with the willing the persuaded in the
disciple, and having mixed faith with the longing, exercise and practice,
examining each time toward himself the truth of the theorems; 36.2 and
whenever it seems to him to be well, then indeed also to go down into the
inquiries of those nearby; since even the nestlings try flying in advance upon
the nest, having exercised the wings. 37.1 For the gnostic virtue is
everywhere beautiful and gentle and harmless and painless and blessed and
prepared best to converse with every divine thing, and best with men,
contemplative together with practical, fashioning the man as a divine image
and making him a lover of beauty 37.2 through love; for the beautiful, as
there it is contemplative and intelligible by wisdom, here is practicable by
self-control and righteousness through faith, when one has practiced in flesh
an angelic service, having consecrated the mind as in a transparent and
undefiled place, the body. 38.1 And toward Tatian saying that "let there be
light" is optative, one must say: if then praying he knew the God lying above,
how does he say "I am God and besides me there is no other"? 38.2 He said that
there are punishments of blasphemies, of idle talk, of unrestrained 39.1
words, being punished and educated by a word; and he used to say that women
are also punished on account of the hair and the adornment by a power
stationed over these things, which also furnished Samson power in the hair,
which punishes those who through adornment of hair rush toward fornication.
Just as they are made good by an outflow of the good, so also they are made
evil by <evil>. Beautiful is the judgment of God, both the separation of the
faithful from the unfaithful and the pre-judgment lest one fall into a greater
judgment, and the judgment being education. The scripture says that the
exposed infants are handed over to a caretaking angel, by whom they are both
educated and increased, "and they shall be," it says, "as the faithful of a
hundred years here." 41.2 Wherefore also Peter in the Apocalypse says: "And a
lightning of fire leaping from those infants and striking the 41.3 eyes of the
women"; since the righteous "shines as a spark through stubble and judges
nations." 42.1 "With a holy one you will be hallowed." According to the praise
of holy ones your name has been glorified, God being glorified according to
our knowledge [both according to the knowledge and according to the
inheritance]. Thus also "the Lord lives" and "the Lord has risen." "A people
whom I did not know,


served me"; I did not know according to a covenant. 43.2 "Alien sons": those
who have been jealous of the things of the other. 44.1 "Magnifying the
salvations of his king." All the faithful are called kings, <having been
called> into a kingdom both according <to the knowledge> and according to the
inheritance. "Long-suffering is sweetness beyond honey," not because it is
long-suffering, but according to the fruit of long-suffering, since neither is
the self-controlled man impassible, mastering the passions not without toil;
but when it becomes a habit, he is no longer self-controlled, the man having
come to be in one habit with the Holy Spirit. "Spirits": the passions in the
soul are called not spirits from substance, since a passionate man will be a
legion of demons, but 46.2 according to the urging. For the same soul,
receiving according to changes other and other qualities of vice, is said to
have taken up spirits. 47.1 The word does not command to stand away from
possession, but to administer the possession without attachment, and if
something happens not to be vexed nor to grieve nor to desire to acquire; for
it commands to stand away from possession in passion and from all attachment.
The divine providence does not come to an end upon those in the flesh alone.
Immediately Peter in the Apocalypse says "the infants that have been aborted,
being about to be of the better lot, these are handed over to a caretaking
angel, in order that having partaken of knowledge they may obtain the better
48.2 abode, having suffered what they would have suffered also having come to
be in a body." But the others will obtain salvation alone, as having been
wronged, having been pitied, and will remain without punishment, having
received this as a gift. 49.1 "And the milk of the women, flowing from the
breasts and being curdled," says Peter in the Apocalypse, "will generate small
flesh-eating beasts and running back upon them it eats them up," teaching that
the 49.2 punishments come about through the sins. He says they are generated
from the sins, as through the sins the people was sold, and "through unbelief
toward Christ," as the apostle says, "they were bitten by the serpents." 50.1
An elder said that what is in the womb is a living being. For the soul,
entering into the womb from the purification, prepared for conception and
being introduced by one of the angels set over generation, who knows in
advance the time of conception, moves the woman toward intercourse, and the
seed having been cast down, so to speak, the spirit in the seed is made at
home and thus it is conceived 50.2 with the formation. † he named a testimony
for all. And whenever the angels evangelize the barren, they as it were
introduce the souls in advance of the conception; and in <the gospel> "the
infant leaped" as ensouled. 50.3 * * and the barren are barren for this
reason, as the soul not being introduced, gathering the casting of the seed
into a holding of conception and generation. 51.1 "The heavens tell the glory
of God." Heavens are spoken in many ways, both those according to interval and
circuit and the proximate activity according to covenant of the first-created
angels; for by a more proper presence of angels the covenants were enacted,
that upon Adam, 51.2 that upon Noah, that upon Abraham, that upon Moses. For
having been moved through the Lord the first-created angels worked upon the
angels proximate to the prophets, telling "the glory of God," the covenants.
But also the works that have come to be upon the earth by angels 52.1 came to
be through the first-created angels unto "the glory of God." And heavens are
called properly the Lord, and then also the


first-created, after whom also the holy men before the law, as the patriarchs,
and Moses and the prophets, then also the apostles. 52.2 "And the firmament
announces the making of his hands." Firmament he calls God who is impassible
and unchangeable, as also elsewhere the same David: "I will love you, Lord, my
strength, the Lord my firmament and 52.3 my refuge." The making then of his
hands the firmament itself announces, that is, it shows and makes appear the
making of his angels; for it announces and shows those whom he made. 53.1 "Day
unto day utters speech." As the heavens in many ways, so also the day. And the
Word is the Lord, and he himself also has been called day in many places. 53.2
"And night unto night announces knowledge." The devil knew that the Lord would
come, but whether <Jesus> himself it was, he did not understand; wherefore
also he was tempting him, in order that he might know; † if he is able, he
says to let be; "and he departed from him for a time," that is, he postponed
the discovery until the resurrection. For he knew that this one is the Lord
who would 53.3 rise. Likewise also the demons, since they also suspected
Solomon 53.4 to be the Lord, but knew he was not, when he sinned. "Night unto
night": all the demons knew that it was the Lord who rose after the passion.
And already also Enoch says that the angels who transgressed taught men
astronomy and divination and the other arts. 54.1 "There are no speeches nor
words, of which their voices are not heard," neither of the days nor of the
nights. "Into all the earth went out 54.2 their sound." He transferred the
word upon the holy ones alone, whom he has also called heavens and days. 55.1
The stars, spiritual bodies, having communion with angels set over them, being
administered, are not causes of generation, but significant of the things
coming to be and that will be and that have come to be, both upon changes of
airs and upon fruitfulness and unfruitfulness and upon plagues and burnings
and upon 55.2 men. Not even in a dream do the stars make the activities, but
they signify "the things that are, the things that will be, and the things
that were before." 56.1 "And in the sun he set his tabernacle." Here there is
a hyperbaton; for the word is concerning the second presence. Thus then the
hyperbaton must be read according to sequence. "And he as a bridegroom going
forth from his chamber will rejoice as a giant to run his way. From the end of
heaven is his going-forth; there is none who will be hidden from his heat."
And then: "in the 56.2 sun he set his tabernacle." Some then say that he
deposits the body of the Lord in the sun, as Hermogenes, and some say body
means his tent, others the church of the faithful, but Pantaenus * of us used
to say "that prophecy for the most part brings out the expressions
indefinitely and uses the present tense instead of the future and again the
present instead of the past," which 56.3 also now appears. For "he set" is
arranged both upon the past and upon what will be; upon what will be, because
when this period according to the present constitution has been fulfilled the
Lord will come to the righteous the faithful, upon whom he rests as upon a
tent; for all are one body, from the same race, having chosen the same faith
and righteousness, about to be restored into the same unity; 56.4 but some as
head, some as eyes, some as ears, some as hands, some as breasts, some as feet
will be set in the sun, luminous, "having shone as the sun" or in the sun,
since a ruling angel is 56.5 in the sun; for he has been appointed unto the
rule of days, just as the moon unto 56.6 ruling night; and days have been
called angels. With the angels with the sun, he says, they will be arranged
(one as a head of a body, being one, the sun), being about to be at some
period also themselves rulers of days, as


that one upon the sun <will come> unto the greater, unto which <the one>
before him passed over in the same. And again going up 56.7 they will arrive
according to progress upon the first abode. <But> according to the past "he
set" the first-created angels so as no longer to serve according to providence
the defined, but to be in rest and toward the contemplation of God alone; and
those more proximate to these will progress into the order which those have
left, and thus those below analogously. 57.1 There are then according to the
apostle those in the highest restoration, the first-created; and they would be
"thrones," although being powers, the first-created, through God resting in
them, as also in those 57.2 who believe. For each according to his own
progress has a knowledge concerning God proper to him, upon which knowledge
God rests, those who have known having become 57.3 everlasting through the
knowledge. And perhaps "in the sun he set his tabernacle" is heard thus: in
the sun he set, that is, in [God,] the El, that is God, as in the gospel "eli
57.4 eli" i.e. "my God, my God." And "above every rule and authority and power
and every name that is named" are those who have been perfected from men,
angels, archangels into the 57.5 first-created nature of the angels. For those
who have been transferred from men into angels are discipled a thousand years
by the angels, being restored unto perfection; then those who taught are
transferred into archangelic authority, and those who learned disciple those
being transferred again from men into angels, then thus in stated periods they
are restored to the proper angel-placing of the body. 58.1 "The law of God is
blameless, converting souls." "Law and word" the Savior himself is called, as
Peter in the Preaching and the prophet: "For from Zion will go forth a law,
and a word of the Lord from Jerusalem." "The testimony of the Lord is
faithful, making wise infants," the covenant of the Lord is true, making wise
infants, the innocent, both the apostles, then 59.2 also us. But also the
testimony of the Lord himself, according to which having suffered he rose,
having become true according to the work itself, led the church unto
confirmation of faith. 60.1 "The fear of the Lord is pure, remaining unto age
of age." Those who have been turned from fear unto faith and righteousness he
says 60.2 remain unto an age. "The judgments of the Lord are true," firm and
unmoved, distributing according to worth and restoring the righteous 60.3
"unto the unity of the faith." For this is what "justified together"
signifies. Such things are "desirable above gold and precious stone." 61.1
"For your servant also keeps them": not because a servant such as David, but
every people that is being saved, having been called a servant of God through
obedience of commandment. "Cleanse me from my hidden things": from the
thoughts contrary to right reason, the remnants. For these he calls "alien" to
a righteous man. "If they do not lord it over me, then I shall be blameless."
Equal to: if those who persecute me do not lord it over me as they did the
Lord, I shall not be blameless; for no one becomes a martyr if he is not
persecuted, nor does he appear righteous unless, having been wronged, he does
not wrong in return, nor yet forbearing, * * * * "Lord, save the king," that
