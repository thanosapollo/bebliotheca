1 Mt 1:16 The promises came to David and to Abraham, that from them the Christ
would appear, not because Christ was going to gain some benefit from this, but
because God was honoring those men. The evangelists therefore wished to show
this, that it came to pass according to the prophetic voices; but lest they be
thought to be so eager about this as if Christ were going to be shown great
from this, they passed over Mary, who truly happened to become mother of God,
and genealogized Joseph, wishing to show Christ from there by implication, or
rather by some clear proof, making this clear to the readers, that it makes no
difference to state the lineage of Christ through Mary or through Joseph; for
how [could it], she who bore him not according to the law of women? And if
also something of Mary was taken, from which Christ was formed according to
the flesh, not naturally but paradoxically, the Holy Spirit taking a little
from her, and from that forming the temple for the God Word. So that he
appears from the kinship of David, shown at least according to the prophetic
voices, but he appears not priding himself on their race, but, if one must
speak the truth, honoring those whom he wished to be inscribed as fathers. 2
Mt 1:17 For this reason he mentioned the three divisions, in order to show the
times of the judges before the kings as far as David; for he does not mention
Saul because he had become rejected and was given in anger of God. Therefore
because of the different conditions of those who ruled among the Jews, of the
judges, kings, of the priests, he made the division. And even if the kings
from David to Jechoniah are seventeen, still the evangelist, having omitted
the three, did not err; for he did not propose to state successions of kings,
but generations. And so that the number of the fourteen generations might
meet, he omitted the three; for he did not <introduce> a generation of a time
of a man according to which one begets children, this being unclear, but a
certain number of years. Therefore those of them he knew to suffice for the
number, these he took, not caring about the persons. Thus also in the third,
<comprising> fourteen, he numbers only twelve, as these too sufficing to fill
up the things from the fourteen generations. Or rather in the third genealogy
fourteen are found when Christ himself also is counted in and the second
Jechoniah, who was also called Joachim, being a descendant of Josiah. Having
therefore named all the ancestors and having finished that through her and
this one he genealogized. 3 Mt 1:23 The angel confirms the word by the
antiquity of the prophecy; and this word was spoken to Ahaz, when the king of
Samaria and of Syria campaigned against him, since he was anxious lest they
take the city and set up as king the son of Tabeel, God spoke this oracle to
him, making clear that until the Christ should be born of the virgin, those
from the race of David would not cease ruling Israel. And he remained fifteen
years in the kingship and died. And his son became king being twenty-four
years old, so that Hezekiah was nine years old when his father began to reign.
How then do the Jews say the oracle came to pass upon Hezekiah, who was born
nine years before his father's kingship, even if we grant that in the first
year of his kingship this oracle was given. And let no one say the birth from
a woman is shameful; for first the incorporeal nature of God is not naturally
defiled, being light, since not even the perceptible light of the luminaries
is defiled when it approaches unclean places. And altogether he who was not
defiled through the forming when creating the woman was not defiled even in
being incarnate from her. These things [are said] toward the

objections of the shameful; for among the pure there is no difference of the
generative parts and of the rest; for the same God created the whole woman
very good, as also all creation. 4 col1 Mt 1:24 She conceived after betrothal
for three reasons: that she might seem to have conceived from him and not die,
and that he might be genealogized from him, and that she might have a guardian
sharing with her against the plot from Herod. For what reason did she conceive
after the betrothal? I think the virgin is encompassed by these three causes:
both that the conception seemed to all to have come from Joseph and thereby
she escaped the danger, and that Christ was genealogized from this very man,
and further that she had him as guardian and minister in the straits, when
they were persecuted by Herod. 5 Mt 1:24–25 And Mary dwelt with her own
betrothed so that the birth of Christ might take place in a veiled way, in
order that both the Jews and the devil might miss him while the Word of God
was being carried and born, who took hold of the seed of Abraham having become
man, and did not liken himself to angels, who can both change form and appear
as another and make those who see them imagine. And the virgin remained even
after giving birth uninitiated in bodily intercourse and did not marry; for
the evangelist explained what happened, which by contrast indicates the
implication of what did not happen. 6 Mt 2:1 For what reason does he tell us
both the time and the place, having said in Bethlehem and in the days of Herod
the king? He mentions the days of Herod, so that you may find the number of
the years stopped according to the prophecy of Daniel numbering the seventy
weeks of years as far as the Christ.

7 Mt 2:1–2 In accordance with the prophecy Jesus was born in Bethlehem. And
the evangelist also mentions the times of Herod, so that counting from the
prophecy of Daniel as far as the Christ the seventy weeks of years we may find
the completion of the years. And since through their own unbelief the Jews did
not know the time of the birth, he puts them to shame from the magi as
careless. And the star for this reason was hidden for a time, so that the magi
might be forced to ask where is the one born king of the Jews, and this might
become evident to all. 8 Mt 2:2 And as comets and such things around the
aether signify something new for life, so far more paradoxical appeared the
star of the great king Jesus; and they speak neither to Herod nor to priests
nor to scribes, but simply in the city, thinking the star and the king to be
evident to those who know the prophecy, which is a reproof to Jews. 9 Mt 2:3–4
Herod is outlandish, at one time believing the prophetic things, that there
will be a Christ the king of all, at another thinking he can kill him and
overturn the prophecies; and it is a token of madness; for if he believed the
things said, he ought to have considered that the things said and that had
come to pass were above man, and he was not able to overturn the things that
seemed good to God. Or if he did not believe, why did he fear and wish to plot
against the infant? This Herod was Idumaean by race; for when a ruler failed
from Judah according to the prophecy of Jacob and thereafter the Romans ruled,
from then on Herod also ruled the nation of the Jews. 10 Mt 2:9–10 10 For this
reason the star is hidden, when the magi set foot on Jerusalem, so that having
lost the one leading them by the hand they might be forced to learn from Jews,
or rather in learning to teach those; for they seek only the place, but that
he was born they already knew beforehand. And for this they undertook a long
journey abroad and threw themselves into danger; for coming into a city that
had a king they asked where the king was born, not suspecting the wrath of the
one then reigning, not the plot of the city. And when they

asked and had teachers now to appear and having appeared to stand. For having
led them by the hand to the manger, it is not shown above them, but having
come down it stood where the child was, over his head, so to speak. 12 col1 Mt
2:13–15 The magi having withdrawn made Herod's wickedness of no effect. And
similar to "out of Egypt I called my son" there lies in Numbers: God called
him out of Egypt; glory as of a unicorn is his. "Out of Egypt I called my son"
is written in the book of Numbers: God called him out of Egypt; glory as of a
unicorn is his.

13 Mt 3:5–6 The crowds, having heard the man's exceeding manner of life,
desired to see him. And for this reason, when they heard that he had arrived,
all ran together to him as to a man sent from God. And "they were confessing
upon him," so that as a priest he might offer the sacrifices on their behalf.
14 Mt 3:9 Inflexibly having cast out the kinship of the flesh he introduces
that from faith; for imitation of virtue makes the true kinship; for he knew
that boasting in these they neglected the virtue according to the soul. 15 Mt
3:9 If children are given to Abraham from the stones, not by the habit of
flesh and spirit, but by the habit of virtue; therefore the people of God have
Abraham styled as father. And how or why, seek. 16 Mt 3:9 The stones are taken
for a barren woman and for the idolatry of the nations. In addition to these
consider also the heart of the dragon, fixed as a stone. 18 Mt 3:11 "The one
behind me," i.e. the one after me; and "coming," i.e. the one who will be
manifested. The souls of the saints are said to be baptized in fire sincerely
as receiving the mystery of piety, because the Spirit first came down upon the
disciples in tongues of fire, through which they were baptized, being
perfected in their souls. Or that in the age to come all are going to be
baptized with fire; for every one shall be salted with fire, so that of each
the work, of what sort it is, the fire may test the *** beyond mind, but to
the material he <set opposite> the fire, being neither wicked nor evil, but
strong and purifying of evil; for fire is understood as a good power and
strong, destructive of the worse and preservative of the better. Therefore it
is also called prudent among the prophets. Thus then also whenever God is
called a consuming fire, it must be taken not as a name of wickedness, but of
power and a symbol; for as fire is the strongest of the elements and mastering
all, so also God is almighty and all-ruling, able to master, create, make,
nourish, increase, save, the one having authority over body and soul. As
therefore fire surpasses the elements, so the Almighty [surpasses] gods and
powers and principalities. And the power of fire is double, one suited to
creation and ripening of fruits and generation and nourishment of animals, of
which the sun is an image, and the other toward consumption and destruction,
as the earthly fire. When therefore God is called a consuming fire, [it is] a
strong and unapproachable power, to which nothing is impossible, but even to
destroy is possible. Concerning such a power the Savior also says: I came to
cast fire upon the earth, clearly a power purifying of the saints, but of the
material, as some say, annihilating, as we would say, disciplinary. And fire
also has fear, and light has spreading-out. 19 Mt 3:12 As judge, Christ
separates those pure in life from the unjust. Mt 3:15 The Savior is baptized
by John not as a sharer in sin and needing to be cleansed from it, but so that
he might not be thought opposed to God by not fulfilling the things of the
law, and in order to show John sent from God he accepts to be baptized by him.
21 Mt 3:15 The one perfect according to the law, being baptized with the
baptism of John, arrived at the end of the law. Therefore Christ also, as
perfect in the law, was baptized with the baptism of John; whence he said: for
thus it is fitting for us to fulfill all righteousness.

22 Mt 4:4 And "not on bread": the first Adam sinned through eating, Christ
prevails through self-control and teaches that one must not stand away from
God, even if we starve. This is also an earnest of the coming condition, which
began in Christ, that human beings are also going to live even without food.

23 Mt 5:13 Salt of the earth is the seasoning of the soul. 25 Mt 5:16 And he
leads us away from our own love of glory, setting the glory of God as the end
of the works. 26 Mt 5:19 Kingdom of the heavens someone says is not only the
dwellings of the saints, but also the condition of the age to come. 27 Mt 5:22
He has said two things, "whoever says raca" and "whoever says fool," taking
the folly upon the soul, and the contemptible upon the bodily things, wishing
to say this: but whoever through arrogance lifts himself against those of the
same faith, both hating them and turning away, or even loathing, or often
<overlooking> them as worthy of no account, taking on the arrogance either
from bodily advantages or from those according to the soul, and through this
overlooking those of the same faith as being inferior to himself, this one I
have judged not free of condemnation; for he too is near the murderer, hating
those whom he ought especially to love because of the kinship of the faith,
the kinship according to nature also being present, sufficient to join them
toward friendship. 28 Mt 5:22 The council and Gehenna are naturally the same;
for both punish the one who said it. And some are at a loss how the Lord,
having called sinners fools, punishes the one who calls his brother a fool.
And he does this, not because the one toward whom the word is is perhaps not a
fool, but because such things are of anger. And the Savior does not so much
take the part of the one wronged as of the one who did the wrong. For this
reason, he says, such a one shall be liable to the council and to the judgment
and to Gehenna. 30 Mt 5:22 The one calling the fellow-believer a fool seems
also to mock the mystery of the faith, through which he calls the one who
believes rightly and is prudent according to this mystery a fool. 31 Mt 5:25
This he wishes to say: we must, he says, do good to the adversary and not harm
[him] while we live, lest we be required to give account of what we do both in
the present age and in the one to come; for he indicates both in the word. 34
Mt 5:31–32 He teaches clearly through these things that it is not the
irrational bill of divorce that dissolves the marriage before God, but the
out-of-place deed even if it is not dissolved by law; for the whole teaching
of Christ judges the things according to disposition. 35 Mt 5:36 We no longer
remain in the shadowy things, and just as we are not circumcised though
circumcision is in the law, so we must not swear either, since it is written
shadow-wise in the law; for when the truth came the shadows passed. 36 Mt 5:37
Let the testimony of your life be more firm than an oath. But if some
shameless person, not put to shame by your life, dares to bring an oath upon
you, let your yes yes and no no be instead of every oath. 37 Mt 5:38–40 He did
not set the legal things upon the bare expression, granting us to examine
their aim accurately; for he does not altogether command to give to the one
asking, even if one does not have; for it is impossible; nor indeed if one
should have, but someone should ask badly; for the giving is worse, when the
one asks toward wantonness and lack of restraint and not toward need, and the
other provides, giving material of such lack of restraint. Since how is it set
concerning the apostles: it was distributed to each as anyone had need? Which
is of those no longer simply giving to those who ask, but supplying toward the
need; and how also "according to what one has he is acceptable, not according
to what he does not have," and not so that there may be relief for others, but
afflictions for you, and as many other things as he says, showing that toward
the things at hand it is fitting to give? 38 col1 Mt 5:40–42 Whether he is a
friend, he says, or an enemy, whether faithful or unfaithful, do good
indiscriminately to the one in need; for the Jews wished to requite friends
only. Therefore they also said to the Lord, urging

him to heal the son of the royal official: for he is worthy for whom you will
grant this, because he himself also built our synagogue. Give, he says, always
and do not refuse, whether friend or enemy, whether faithful or unfaithful the
one asking happens to be, and continually do good to the one needing your
help. 39 Mt 5:44 Pray for the enemies, warding them off in this, in not being
with them while they persist in wickedness. 40 Mt 5:44 The law of the Lord
overpasses both nature and the divine law, I mean that of Moses; for the
things impossible with men are possible with God. Yet Christ does not
legislate impossibles, as Stephen showed at the time of his passion, bending
the knees and praying for those stoning him, and likewise Paul who suffered so
many things from Jews and praying for them. The rare then shows that they are
not impossible, even if they are hard to accomplish for the many because they
do not work virtue to the utmost. 42 Mt 5:45 The imitator of God and of Christ
makes the word in himself a sun and the righteousness shining in himself to
rise upon evil and good, and rains the word through his mouth as rain
economically upon righteous and sinners. 43 Mt 5:45 All who wish to be deemed
worthy of adoption must as far as possible imitate God in doing good
indiscriminately. 44 Mt 5:46 God does not wish [us] to repay anyone of those
who sin against us by him, but rather to love and care in every way, because
they were harmed while hating us who were benefited.

45 Mt 6:16–17 Disfigurement of face he now calls appearing pale as an ascetic
through vainglory; and to be anointed and washed one must not understand
toward the letter, but having the face bright, through which one tries to
escape notice that he is fasting; for this is a charge of love of glory, which
as being filth of the soul one must wash off. Yet one must also brighten the
ruling part of the soul through virtue and thereby wash the intelligible eyes
of the face and make them sharp toward comprehension of the contemplation of
the better things. Therefore through the more ruling parts of the body he
signifies the purity of the soul; for in the face are the many of the senses,
which the mind uses toward service of good or base things, which the Lord says
to wash by abstinence from evils, and to brighten by the working of good
things. 46 Mt 6:22 The lamp of the body is the mind, which when it is clean as
an eye, the bodily passions also are guided and driven toward what is seemly
and pleasing to God. 47 col1 Mt 6:22–23 The perceptible eye sees all things
that fall in the way, but the simple mind discerningly sees the good things
and turns away from the evil, in a way shutting its eyes so as not to see or
handle them; for the base is darkened with darkness of wickedness and
intelligible light is illuminated with light of truth. The perceptible eye
apprehends the visible things and distinguishes each, which of these is white,
which black, and which in the middle of these; but the mind is both
apprehensive and distinguishing of the things thought, and knowing of the good
and the base and of those in the middle of these. Whence when the eye of the
body is darkened the whole body will be dark, but when the mind has suffered
not only the soul will be darkened, but with it also the body.

48 Mt 7:14–15 He prepared [us] to be sober not only by this, that it is narrow
and afflicted, but also by adding that it has many who trip [people] up, and
what is more grievous, that they do not even attack openly, but hiding
themselves; for such is the race of the false prophets. 49 Mt 7:15–16 He makes
the hearer secure, lest somehow by desire of hearing a solemn word he run to
one of those trying to distort the doctrines of the church, who promising to
be sheep are wolves putting to death the rational sheep of Christ. 50 Mt
7:15–20 Many are those

practicing virtue and according to appearance seeming to be good through
temperance and gentleness, which is a sheep's clothing, but being made
manifest from their fruits, that is from the mind and the doctrines concerning
the faith. 51 Mt 7:15–20 These are those who pretend virtue, but live
unseemly, or those who according to appearance practice virtue, but do not
think rightly, such as the heretics are, who through fabricated words and
disguises deceive some, and when they persuade then they put under them to do
even what is not lawful, who will also be known before long, since as is the
word, such is not also the life; for from the fruits the tree is known, not
from the flowers, and the knowledge of the teacher from the fruit and the
manner of life, not from the flower and the word. 52 Mt 7:16–20 As the thing
of the parable is impossible, so [is] the base conceiving some useful work of
the heart in a way compelling each toward what it wishes; for it is impossible
that from a wretched disposition something pious or beneficial be said.
Therefore the base are not unpunished; for they are good or bad by choice, not
by nature. And the word especially fits the doctrines; for also the one
speaking something impious is punished, and likewise also the one speaking
good things but doing base things, and the one pretending piety and not truly
doing it. 53 Mt 7:28 Reasonably they were astonished; for with unspeakable
power he persuaded those hearing, and they were not astonished at bulk of
phrasing; for there was not in them anything hard or riddling or dark.

speaking, but the Spirit of your Father. 68 col1 Mt 10:23 He calls his own
arrival not the manifest one that will be at the end of life, but the
encouragement and help that comes from him at times to those afflicted for
God. He does not speak of his second arrival, but of the encouragement that
will be at the time through him for the saints. 69 Mt 10:26 Both your virtue
and the wickedness of the Jews will become evident to all. 70 Mt 10:34 Having
taught the disciples to endure insults and to be steadfast toward the labors
and not to be impatient, he now also arms them toward certain other things,
which seem violently to compel a person not to stand away from the evils. And
these are the separation from father and mother and from the other friends
together with kinsmen. For this reason he says: I did not come to cast peace,
but a sword. And he means this [sword] that cuts and separates us from the
unbelievers, even if it be a father, even if a mother, even if some other of
those very friendly toward us. 71 Mt 10:37–38 The one not bidding farewell to
the present life is not worthy of me. 72 Mt 10:40 The one receiving those sent
by someone gladly honors the one who sent, and the contrary. Or also thus: the
one receiving the apostles who preach the Trinity receives the very fullness
of the Godhead. And we seem even now to receive them intelligibly, if with an
opened soul we receive their counsels. 73 Mt 10:40–42 God defines the
recompenses not toward the things given, but toward the dispositions of those
providing.

is that of the resurrection, through which we are born again and through the

And the very least there overpasses John, even if from the womb he obtained
the Holy Spirit. 76 Mt 11:11 And others say: John is less than those already
spiritual who have been renewed according to the new man, but greater than the
earthy because of seeing the things which he hinted; but those did not see, as
the Lord testified, and he himself also says his joy has been fulfilled,

being the living and subsistent wisdom was judged righteous by those who
believe in him, having managed all things, or that being insulted by the
unbelieving Jews he did not cease speaking kindly to them and naming them
children, so that he might be justified in his words and prevail when he is
judged. The subsistent wisdom was judged righteous by those who believe in
him, or that calling those who insult him children he did not cease speaking
kindly to them. 78 col1 Mt 11:20–24 And since Christ did many signs

in Capernaum, it seemed to be exalted as far as heaven, which through
unbelief, having fallen an extraordinary fall, reached as far as Hades. And if
someone should say: and why did the signs not happen in Sodom, we shall say

miracles he says this [city] is exalted as far as heaven, but through the sin
and unbelief of those in it an extraordinary fall came to be as far as Hades.
80 Mt 11:25 Here he calls the Jews wise, either because they had been
entrusted with the oracles of God, or because they were malicious and wise to
do evil, and infants the apostles. Or wise the scribes and Pharisees, not
having the true wisdom, but the seeming from cleverness of words, and infants
the fishermen simple in wickedness. In that then he is made known to the
simple, the grace of God is shown. Yet Christ does not say this as rejoicing,
but that it is an excellent way that the wicked not know the power of the
divine words, lest after the knowledge they spit them out and fall into
greater punishment. Even if especially Christ himself also did this, yet
through the things for which he gives thanks for what the Father did, he shows
that of both there is one will and the love concerning us, giving thanks for
the things by which we are benefited. 81 Mt 11:28–29 He calls those laboring
those who have the burden of sins in the soul, and yoke he calls the virtuous
life, through which one imitates Christ in meekness and humility. 82 Mt 11:30
The yoke of Christ is light and easy, even if it seems heavy to those who
through greed bear the weight of sins as most light. 83 Mt 11:30 The yoke is
kind, since it is not punitive as the law; and the burden light, since the
pattern of virtue is also in simple and easy undertakings.

84 col1 Mt 12:1–8 You will also understand thus that to know God is more
necessary than the rest of the Sabbath, which was given for this to the Jews
who had turned to idolatries in Egypt, so as not to say the world is
ungenerated and without providence, but that there is also a God who provides,
and he is the one who in six days made the world and on the 7th rested, which
reminding them as God he commanded to rest on the Sabbath, which is thereafter
superfluous after it became known to all that God is maker of the wholes; for
if the word had something useful, he would not have brought this to be done
for humans only, but also for sun and moon, and he himself would not have
worked on the Sabbath the benefactions toward us, but also from the foundation
of the world this commandment was given to humans. It is better to know God
and more necessary than the rest of the Sabbath; for if it were necessary or
