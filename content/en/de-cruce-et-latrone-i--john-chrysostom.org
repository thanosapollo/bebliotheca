On the cross and on the thief, and concerning the second coming of Christ, and
concerning praying continually for enemies.
=49.399= Homily 1.
αʹ. Today our Lord Jesus Christ is on the cross, and we keep a feast, that you
may learn that the cross is a feast and a spiritual festival. For formerly the
cross was a name of condemnation, but now it has become a matter of honor;
formerly a symbol of condemnation, but now a ground of salvation. For this has
become the cause of countless goods for us, this has delivered us from error,
this has enlightened those sitting in darkness, this has reconciled us who had
been made enemies with God, has made those who were alienated his own, has
made those who were far off to be near; this is the destruction of enmity,
this the security of peace, it has become a treasure of countless goods for
us. Through this we no longer wander in deserts; for we have come to know the
true way; we no longer live outside the palace; for we have found the door; we
do not fear the fiery darts of the devil; for we have seen the fountain.
Through this we are no longer in widowhood; for we have received the
bridegroom; we do not fear the wolf; for we have the good shepherd; For I am,
he says, the good shepherd. Through this we do not shudder at the tyrant; for
we sit beside the king; and for these reasons we keep a feast, celebrating the
memorial of the cross. Thus also Paul commanded to keep a feast because of the
cross; For let us keep the feast, he says, not with old leaven, but with the
unleavened bread of sincerity and truth. Then adding the cause, he went on:
For our Passover has been sacrificed for us, Christ. Do you see how he
commands to feast because of the cross? for on the cross Christ was
sacrificed. And where there is a sacrifice, there is the taking away of sins,
there reconciliation with the Master, there feast and joy. Our Passover has
been sacrificed for us, Christ. And where was he sacrificed, tell me? Upon the
high scaffold. New is the altar of this sacrifice, since the sacrifice also is
new and marvelous. =49.400= For he himself was both sacrifice and priest;
sacrifice according to the flesh, priest according to the spirit; the same
both offered and was offered according to the flesh. Hear then how Paul made
both of these clear: Every high priest, he says, taken from among men, is
appointed by men; whence it is necessary that this one also have something to
offer. Behold he offers himself. But elsewhere he says that Christ, having
been offered once to bear the sins of many, shall appear to those who wait for
him unto salvation. Behold here he was offered, but there he offered himself.
Did you see how he became both sacrifice and priest, and the altar was the
cross? And for what reason is the sacrifice not offered in the temple, he
says, but outside the city and the walls? That that might be fulfilled, that
He was numbered with the lawless.
And for what reason is he slain upon the high scaffold, and not under a roof?
That he might cleanse the nature of the air, for this reason upon high, no
roof lying over him, but heaven lying over him. For the air was cleansed when
the sheep was sacrificed on high; and the earth too was cleansed; for the
blood dripped upon it from the side. For this reason not under a roof, for
this reason not in the Jewish temple, lest the Jews should monopolize the
sacrifice, and lest you should think that this is offered for that nation
alone; for this reason outside city and walls, that you may learn that the
sacrifice is catholic, that the offering is for the whole earth, that you may
learn that the cleansing is common, not partial, as it was among the Jews. For
to the Jews for this reason


God commanded them to leave all the earth, and to offer the sacrifices and to
pray in one place, since all the earth was unclean, smoke and savor and all
the other pollutions from the Greek sacrifices being carried upon it; but for
us, since Christ having come has now cleansed the whole inhabited world, every
place has become a house of prayer. For this reason Paul also, taking courage,
ex =49.401= horted thereafter to pray everywhere without fear, saying thus: I
will that men pray in every place, lifting up holy hands. Did you see how the
inhabited world was cleansed? For through the place we can lift up holy hands
everywhere, since all the earth has become holy, holier than the holy things
within. For there an irrational sheep was offered, but here a spiritual one;
and as much as the sacrifice is greater, so much more also is the
sanctification. For this reason the cross is a feast.
βʹ. Do you wish to learn another of its achievements as well? Paradise, closed
for five thousand years and more, today he opened for us. For on this day, at
this hour, God brought in the thief, accomplishing two achievements: one, that
he opened paradise, and another, that he brought in a thief. Today he restored
to us the ancient fatherland, today he brought us back to the ancestral city,
and he granted the house to the common nature of men; For today, he says, you
shall be with me in paradise. What do you say? You are crucified and nailed,
and you promise paradise? Yes, he says, that on the cross you may thoroughly
learn my power. For since the thing was gloomy, that you may not attend to the
nature of the cross, but may learn the power of the one crucified, on the
cross he works this miracle, which especially shows his strength.
For not by raising a dead man, not by rebuking sea and winds, not by driving
out demons, but being crucified, nailed, insulted, spat upon, reviled, mocked,
he had strength to change the wicked mind of the thief, that from both sides
you may see his power. For he both shook all creation, and split the rocks,
and drew and honored the soul of the thief, more insensible than the rock; For
today you shall be with me in paradise, he says. And yet the cherubim were
keeping paradise; but this one is Master even of the cherubim; a flaming sword
is turned there; but he himself has authority over flame and gehenna and life
and death. And yet no king would ever endure to seat a thief, a man, or some
other of his fellow-servants with himself, and so bring him into a city. But
Christ did this, and entering into the holy fatherland, he brings in the thief
with himself, not trampling paradise, nor shaming it with the feet of the
thief, but rather honoring paradise; for it is an honor to paradise to have
such a master as to make even a thief worthy of the delight that is in
paradise.
For when he brought tax-collectors and harlots into the kingdom of the
heavens, he was not dishonoring it by this, but rather honoring it, showing
that such is the Lord of the kingdom of the heavens, as to make even harlots
and tax-collectors so approved as to appear worthy of the honor and gift
there. For just as we especially marvel at a physician when we see him
delivering men who have incurable sufferings from the illness and restoring
them to health; so it is right also to marvel at Christ, when he heals
incurable wounds, when he restores a tax-collector and a harlot to such health
as to declare them worthy of the heavens. And what such thing, he says, did
the thief display, that he should obtain paradise after a cross? Do you wish
me to tell his courage briefly? When Peter denied below, then that one
confessed above. And I say these things not accusing Peter, =49.402= God
forbid, but wishing to show the


magnanimity of the thief. The disciple did not bear the threat of a cheap
girl; but the thief, seeing a whole people standing around, shouting, raging,
hurling blasphemies and jeers, did not attend to those, did not consider the
apparent cheapness of the one crucified, but running past all these things
with the eyes of faith, and leaving aside the lowly hindrances, he recognized
the Master of the heavens, and falling before him he said: Remember me, Lord,
when you come in your kingdom. Let us not then simply run past this thief, nor
be ashamed to take as teacher him whom our Master was not ashamed first to
bring into paradise; let us not be ashamed to take as teacher a man who before
all nature appeared worthy of the citizenship in the heavens, but let us
examine each thing accurately, that we may learn the power of the cross.
He did not say to him, as to Peter: Come after me, and I will make you a
fisher of men; nor did he say to him, as to the twelve, that You shall sit
upon twelve thrones judging the twelve tribes of Israel; rather he did not
even think him worthy of a word at all; he showed no miracle, he did not see a
dead man being raised, nor demons being driven out; he did not see the sea
obeying, he said nothing to him about a kingdom, nothing about gehenna; and
before all he confessed him, and this, while the other was reviling; for the
other thief was reviling him. For there was also another thief crucified with
him, that that might be fulfilled, that He was numbered with the lawless. For
the Jews wished also to slander his glory, and from every side they insulted
the things that were happening; but from every side the truth shone forth, and
grew through their hindrances. The other thief then was reviling him. Did you
see a thief and a thief? both on a cross, both from a robber's life, both from
wickedness; but not both in the same things, but the one inherited a kingdom,
and the other was being sent into gehenna. So also yesterday: disciples and a
disciple, Judas and the eleven; but those said: Where do you will that we
prepare for you to eat the Passover? but this one was preparing a betrayal and
said: What will you give me, and I will deliver him to you? and those were
making ready for service and the divine mystagogy, but this one was hastening
to betray. So also here a thief and a thief; but the one reviles, and the
other worships; and the one blasphemes, and the other speaks well, and stops
the mouth of the one blaspheming, saying: Do you not even fear God? for we
receive things worthy of what we have done.
γʹ. Did you see the boldness of a thief? did you see boldness on a cross? did
you see philosophy in punishment, and piety in chastisement? For that he was
in himself, that he had his wits, with the nails driven into him, who would
not be amazed? And he was not only in himself, but even leaving aside his own
affairs, he was concerned with those of others, becoming a teacher on the
cross, and rebuking and saying: Do you not even fear God? Do not attend, he
says, to the court below; there is another invisible judge, there is an
unbribable court. Do not then look at this, that he was condemned below; for
the things above are not such; for here in the court below even the righteous
are condemned, and the unjust escape, and the guilty are released, and the
guiltless are pun =49.403= ished. For those who judge sin much both willingly
and unwillingly; for either not knowing the just and being deceived, or
knowing it, but being corrupted by money, they have often betrayed the
verdict. But above there is nothing of this kind; for God is a righteous
judge, and his judgment shall go forth as light, having neither darkness nor
ignorance.
That he may not say that he was sentenced below and condemned, he raised him
to the court above; he reminded him of that fearful tribunal, all but


saying: Look there, and you will not bear the condemning verdict, nor will
you stand with the corrupted judges below, but you will accept the judgment
that takes place above. Did you see the philosophy of a thief? did you see
understanding and teaching? All at once from the cross he leaped up into
heaven. Then also stopping his mouth from abundance, You have not feared, he
says, that we are in the same judgment? What is, That we are in the same
judgment? In the same punishment. For are you not also on the cross?
Reproaching that one then, you put yourself forward before him. For just as
the one who is in sins, accusing another, accuses himself before that one, so
also the one who is in misfortune and reproaching another with misfortune,
reproaches himself before that one. That we are in the same judgment. He reads
to him an apostolic law, speaking gospel words: Judge not, that you be not
judged. That we are in the same judgment. What are you doing, O thief?
attempting to make a defense for him, have you made yourself a partner of the
thief? No, he says; I correct this suspicion by what follows. For lest through
the partnership of the punishment you should think that he made him a partner
of the sin, he added the correction, saying: And we indeed justly; for we have
suffered things worthy of what we have done. Did you see a confession brought
to completion? did you see how on the cross he stripped off the sins? For say,
he says, you your sins first, that you may be justified. No one compelled, no
one forced, but he himself made a spectacle of himself, saying: And we indeed
justly; for we have suffered things worthy of what we did; but this one has
done nothing wicked. And after these things he says: Remember me, Lord, in
your kingdom. He did not dare first to say, Remember me in your kingdom, until
through confession he had laid aside the burden of the sins. Do you see how
great confession is? He confessed, and he opened paradise; he confessed, and
he received such boldness as to ask a kingdom from robbery. Do you see of how
many goods the cross is the cause for us? You mention a kingdom? For what such
thing do you see, tell me? Nails and a cross are the things seen; but this
very cross, he says, is a symbol of the kingdom. And for this reason I call
him king, because I see him crucified; for it belongs to a king to die for
those who are ruled. He himself said of himself: The good shepherd lays down
his life for the sheep. Therefore also the good king lays down his life for
those who are ruled. Since then he laid down his life, for this reason I call
him king. Remember me, Lord, in your kingdom.
δʹ. Did you see how the cross is also a symbol of a kingdom? Do you wish to
learn this from another place as well? He did not allow it to remain upon the
earth, but drew it up, and brought it into heaven. From where is this clear?
Because he is going to come with it at his second and glorious coming, that
you may learn how august a thing the cross is, wherefore he also called it
glory. But let us s =49.404= ee how he comes with the cross; for it is
necessary to bring the proof back. If they say, he says, behold Christ is in
the inner rooms, behold he is in the wilderness, do not go forth; speaking of
his second glorious coming because of the false christs, because of the false
prophets, because of the Antichrist, lest anyone being deceived should fall in
with that one. For since before Christ the Antichrist comes, lest anyone
seeking the shepherd should fall in with the wolf, for this reason I tell you
a mark of the shepherd's coming. For since his first coming took place in
secret, lest you should think that the second also takes place such, he has
given this sign. For this one reasonably took place in secret; for he came
seeking the lost; but that one is not so; but how, tell me? For as the
lightning comes out from the east, and appears as far as the west, so also
shall be the coming of the Son of man. All at once he shall appear to all, and
no one shall need to ask whether Christ is here or there. For as when
lightning appears we do not need to examine


whether it has happened; so when the coming of Christ takes place, we do not
need to examine whether Christ has arrived.
But the thing sought is whether he comes with a cross; let us not forget the
promise. Hear then what follows. Then, he says; then, when? When the Son of
man comes, the sun shall be darkened, and the moon shall not give her light.
For so great then shall be the excess of the light, that even the brightest
stars shall be hidden. Then also the stars shall fall, then the sign of the
Son of man shall appear in heaven. Did you see how great is the virtue of the
sign of the cross? The sun shall be darkened, and the moon shall not appear;
but that shines and appears, that you may learn that it is brighter even than
sun and moon. And just as when a king is entering a city, the soldiers going
before carry upon their shoulders the so-called signs, announcing his entrance
beforehand; so also when the Lord is descending from the heavens the armies of
angels and archangels go before, carrying that sign upon their shoulders, and
announcing to us his kingly entrance. Then the powers of the heavens shall be
shaken, he says concerning the angels; trembling shall take them then and much
fear. And for what reason, tell me? That court shall be fearful; for all our
nature is going to be judged and to stand before the fearful judge.
For what reason then do the angels fear and shudder? for they are not going to
be judged. For just as when a ruler is judging, not only those who are liable,
but also those of the order, who are conscious of nothing in themselves, fear
and shudder because of the fear of the judge; so also then, our nature being
judged, even the angels who are conscious of nothing in themselves fear
because of the excess of the fear of the judge. But why does the cross appear
then, and why does he come having it? That those who crucified him may learn
their own ingratitude, for this reason he shows the very symbol of their
shamelessness. And that he carries it for this reason, hear the prophet
saying: Then the tribes of the earth shall mourn, seeing the accuser, and
recognizing the sin. And why do you marvel if he comes carrying the cross,
when he even shows the very wounds then; For they shall look, he says, on him
whom they pierced? For just as he did in the case of Thomas, wishing to
correct the unbe =49.405= lief of a disciple, and having risen he showed him
the print of the nails, and the wounds, saying, Put your hand, and see, that a
spirit has not flesh and bones; so also then he will show the wounds and the
cross, that he may show that this was that one who was crucified.
εʹ. And not from the cross alone, but also from the very words upon the cross
it is possible to see his unspeakable loving-kindness. For still nailed and
being made a comedy and being mocked then and spat upon, he said: Father,
forgive them the sin; for they do not know what they are doing. And being
crucified he prays for those who crucify; and yet those were saying the
opposite: If you are the Son of God, come down from the cross, and we will
believe you, he says. And yet for this reason he does not come down from the
cross, because he is the Son of God, and for this reason he came, that he
might be crucified for us.
Come down from the cross, he says, and we will believe you. These are words,
and a pretext of unbelief. For far greater than coming down from the cross was
the rising, with the stone lying upon the tomb; far greater than coming down
from the cross was leading Lazarus out of the memorial, dead, bound, and four
days, with the grave-clothes. Those then said: If you are the Son of God, save
yourself; but he was doing all things, that he might save those who were
reproaching


him, saying: Forgive them the sin; for they do not know what they are doing.
What then? did he forgive them the sin? He forgave, if they were willing to
repent. For if he had not forgiven them the sin, Paul would not have become an
apostle; if he had not forgiven them the sin, three thousand would not have
believed at once, and five thousand, and many myriads. For that many myriads
of Jews believed, hear what the apostles say to Paul: You see, brother, how
many myriads there are of Jews who have believed? Let us then imitate the
Master, and let us pray for enemies. For again I take hold of this same
exhortation; I have today a fifth day discoursing to you on this subject, not
condemning you for disobedience, God forbid, but especially hoping that you
will be persuaded. And if some are hard and wrathful and hard to lead, so as
to disregard what has been said about prayer, even if only out of respect for
the number of the days let them at last put away the enmity and the smallness
of soul. Imitate your Master; he was being crucified, and he was speaking with
the Father for those who were crucifying. And how can I, he says, imitate the
Master? If you will, you can.
For if you were not able to imitate, how did he say, Learn from me, that I am
meek and humble in heart? If it were not possible to imitate, Paul would not
have said: Become imitators of me, as I also of Christ. But you do not wish to
imitate the Master? imitate the fellow-servant, I mean Stephen the apostle;
for that one also imitated the Master. And just as Christ in the midst of
those crucifying, leaving the cross, leaving his own affairs, entreats the
Father for those who crucify; so also the servant in the midst of those
stoning, being struck by all, receiving the blows of the stones, and leaving
aside the pains that come from these, said: Lord, do not stand this sin
against them. Did you see how the Son speaks? did you see how the servant
entreats? That one says: Father, forgive them this =49.406= sin; for they do
not know what they are doing; but this one says: Lord, do not stand this sin
against them. And that you may learn that he prays with eagerness, he does not
simply pray standing and being stoned, but having set his knees he spoke with
contrition, with much compassion. Do you wish me to show you also another
fellow-servant who suffered much greater than that one? Paul says: From Jews
three times I was beaten with rods, once I was stoned, a night and a day I
have spent in the deep. What then after these things? I was praying, he says,
to be myself anathema for my brothers, my kinsmen according to the flesh. Do
you wish to see another also, not from the New, but from the Old? For this is
especially the marvelous thing, that where they were not commanded to love the
enemies, but to knock out an eye for an eye, and a tooth for a tooth, and to
repay with equal evils, they arrived at apostolic philosophy. Hear what Moses
says, who was often stoned by the Jews, and despised by them: If you forgive
them the sin, forgive; and if not, blot me also out of the book which you have
written.
Do you see each one setting the safety of the others before his own salvation?
You have not sinned; and why do you wish to share the punishment with them?
Because I do not strongly feel the well-being, he says, when others are
suffering badly. These examples then would have been enough for us; but that
from abundance we may correct ourselves, I will try another also who
philosophizes the same things. For David also, that blessed and meek one, when
the whole army had risen up against him, and had armed his son Absalom, and he
had set upon the tyranny, and wished to slay him, then God being provoked for
this (for what if he also laid another pretext upon the slaughter?), and
having sent the angel with a drawn sword, bringing the blow from above, seeing
all being destroyed, what does he say? I the shepherd have sinned, and I the
shepherd have done evil. Let your hand be upon me, and upon the house of my
father. Do you see


again kindred achievements? Do you wish me to show you another also? for we
shall not be at a loss for another who philosophizes the same things. Samuel
that prophet was insulted by the Jews, was voted out, was so dishonored that
God wished to console him and say: They have not set you at naught, but me.
What then did that one who was set at naught, who was insulted? But far be it
from me, he says, to sin thus, as to cease praying for you to the Lord. He
thought it a sin, not to pray for the enemies. For far be it from me to sin
thus, as not to pray for you. Christ says: Father, forgive them the sin; for
they do not know what they are doing. Stephen says: Lord, do not stand this
sin against them. Paul says: I was praying to be anathema for my brothers, the
kinsmen according to the flesh. Moses says: If you forgive them the sin,
forgive; and if not, blot me also out of the book which you have written.
David says: Let your hand be upon me and upon the house of my father. Samuel
says: But far be it from me to sin by ceasing to pray for you to the Lord. Of
what forgiveness then shall we obtain, tell me, when the Master, the servants,
those from the New, those from the Old, all are pushing us to the =49.407=
prayer for the enemies, while we ourselves do the opposite, and pray against
the enemies? Do not, I entreat, do not, brothers. For as much as the examples
are more, so much, if we do not imitate, the punishment is greater. Greater is
praying for the enemies than for the friends; for that does not benefit you so
much as this.
For if you love those who love you, you do nothing great, he says; for even
the tax-collectors do the same. So that if we pray for the friends, we have
not yet become better than the gentiles or tax-collectors; but when =49.408=
we love the enemies, we have become like God according to human power, because
He makes his sun rise upon evil and good, and rains upon righteous and
unrighteous. Let us then become like the Father. For become, he says, like
your Father who is in the heavens, that we also may be counted worthy of the
kingdom of the heavens, by the grace and loving-kindness of our Lord and God
and Savior Jesus Christ, to whom be the glory and the power unto the ages of
the ages. Amen.
