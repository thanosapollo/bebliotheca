* Fragments of a Homily That Christ Is One
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

From the same discourse that Christ is one. Those who divide the one Christ
into two sons have come as it were into forgetfulness of the voices of the
blessed Paul; for he writes clearly, at one time that "for us there is one God
the Father, from whom are all things and we from him, and one Lord Jesus
Christ, through whom are all things and we through him," and again "One Lord,
one faith, one baptism"; but if there were two sons according to some, he
would have said Two lords, two faiths, two baptisms; but saying one and one
faith and one baptism, he condemns their want of instruction. For if according
to them there are two, which lord shall we name, to whom do we confess the
faith, into whose name are we baptized? From the same discourse.
Let us see first what the blessed Daniel says, and come, let us of necessity
inquire into his most dreadful vision, first that he had seen the Ancient of
Days sitting upon a throne and thousands serving him, and myriads standing
about his throne; then he says "I was watching in a vision of the night and
behold with the clouds of heaven one as a son of man coming, and he came as
far as the Ancient of Days, and he was brought before him and to him was given
the honor and the kingdom and all tribes tongues serve him." See then clearly
one as a son of man comes with the clouds and he came as far as the Ancient of
Days. And let us examine exactly what this is. Is then the God of all, of whom
all things are full, comprehended by place? Do we not 456 believe him who says
"I fill heaven and earth, says the Lord?" Where then did the son of man come
as far as the Ancient of Days? According to the glory fitting to God, clearly,
and according to the preeminence over all; for he sits with the Father and is
of the same throne with him; not only when the Word was still naked and
fleshless, but also when he became man. See then the Son being God even in
humanity; for the honor of the Godhead and the kingdom over all would not have
been given to him, nor would tribes and tongues have served him, unless he is
truly God and from God according to nature, being one and only Son even when
he appeared as man. And receive full assurance also upon this; for God and
Father said through the voice of the prophet "I will not give my glory to
another," then he says "For whoever is ashamed of me and my words, of this one
also the son of man will be ashamed when he comes in the glory of the Father
with the holy angels." Is it not then a great and wonderful thing that is
promised, if a son of man is in the glory of Godhead, he who is from a woman
according to flesh is in the highest preeminences, and he who has come to be
in likeness toward us has the angels as his own? The whole heaven,
principalities, powers, authorities, thrones, lordships escort a man as a
bodyguard; they have stood by as to the Judge of all, serving him. And the
all-wise Paul is a witness, having written to the Hebrews concerning him "For
when he brings the firstborn 457 into the world he says And let all the angels
of God worship him." When then did the Only-begotten become firstborn? When he
came to be among many brothers and was named with us a son of man. But if the
powers above knew that he who is from the seed of David according to flesh was
not God nor Son by nature and truly, how would they have endured to worship
him? Therefore him whom angels worship, do not yourself dishonor, saying that
there is another son separately and properly; but believe rather that one and
only is Jesus Christ, the same from the Father in a divine manner according to
the unspeakable generation, and from a woman according to flesh because of the
likeness toward us.
Of the same from the homily that there is one Lord Jesus Christ. Toward such
things perhaps those who wish to contend would say Therefore the Son of God by
nature and truly came to be under law? Then tell me what is the thing that
grieves, even if this should be said to have been suffered? For if we believe
that he became flesh, that is man, who deigned to come to be according to us,
why should he not be said to have come to be with us also under law? For just
as even if he came to be according to us, he did not depart from being above
us; and if he has been named a part of creation because of the human, he has
even so the being above all creation. And just as having taken the form of a
slave he is no less Master, and it is confessed that he is also Lord of all;
so even if he should happen to be said to have come to be under law as man, he
did not depart from being above law as God. For what, tell me, is more
burdensome than this, that it has been written concerning him that he became
"a curse for us," and again "Him who knew not sin he made sin for us," but
neither is he a curse, he who has shown the 458 curse upon us to be of no
effect, nor indeed is he sin according to the truth, he who has wiped away our
sins and is freeing all from the ancient charges. What then is more
burdensome, to be named a curse and sin, or to come to be with us also under
law according to the human? Therefore he appropriates all things that befit
humanity because of having come to be with us according to flesh. When then
did he come to be under law, the blessed Luke the evangelist will explain
saying "When the days of their purification were fulfilled according to the
law of Moses, they brought him up to Jerusalem to present him to the Lord, as
it is written in the law of the Lord that every male opening the womb shall be
called holy to the Lord, and to give a sacrifice according to what is said in
the law of the Lord, a pair of turtledoves or two young pigeons." Do you hear
how he is brought into the sight of the Father as a man according to us, the
God Word who sits with him, who is also written among the firstborn through
the shadow of the law? For holy and sacred to God are also the firstborn
before the incarnation. They were sacrificing then for him according to the
law; and he also paid the didrachm to those who collect tribute, though he
himself was not owing to pay, because of being Son according to truth. And at
any rate he said to the blessed Peter "The kings of the earth from whom do
they take customs or census? from their sons or from the strangers?" And when
Peter said, and very rightly, "From the strangers" he said again himself "Then
the sons are free."
