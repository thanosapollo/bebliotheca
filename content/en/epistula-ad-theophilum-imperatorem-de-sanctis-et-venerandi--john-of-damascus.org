OF JOHN OF DAMASCUS, A LETTER TO THE EMPEROR THEOPHILUS CONCERNING THE HOLY
AND VENERABLE ICONS.

αʹ. Since therefore, according to the God-spoken voice that says, "That if two
of you shall agree on earth concerning any matter whatsoever they shall ask,
it shall be done for them by my Father who is in heaven. For where two or
three are gathered in my name, there am I in the midst of them." And again:
"Receive the Holy Spirit; if you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven
them; if you retain the sins of any, they are retained." And again: "You shall
sit upon twelve thrones judging the twelve tribes of Israel." And again:
"Whatever you bind on earth shall be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose
on earth shall be loosed in heaven." And elsewhere: "I do not ask concerning
these only, says the Lord, but also concerning those who believe in me through
their word; that they all may be one, as you, Father, are in me, and I in you.
And I have given them the glory which you have given me, that they may be
perfected into one, and that the world may know that you have loved them as
you have loved me; and that where I am, they also may be with me, that they
may behold my glory which you have given me." And the divine apostle Peter
says: "Giving the Holy Spirit to them, as also to us," and God has given the
equal gift to the nations, even as they also spoke with tongues and
prophesied. And again the great Paul, herald and teacher of the inhabited
world, says that "God has set in the Church first apostles, second prophets,
third teachers." Therefore following the definition and canon of the divine
apostles and of the holy and blessed Fathers, in the great Church of God,
where with one mind and one judgment =95.348= the definition and the sound of
those who keep festival is made in a voice of exultation, holding the same
symbols of the orthodox faith, the keys of the pious and orthodox dogmas, we
subscribed such things as these.

βʹ. Believing in the holy and consubstantial and life-giving Trinity, we
confess the incarnate economy of God the Word, and the holy ecumenical synods;
likewise we also kiss the precious icons equally with the type of the
life-giving cross, and of the God-spoken Gospels. Let those who do not so
think be anathema; let those who do not so believe be driven far from the
Church. This faith has enlightened the inhabited world. Those therefore who
dare to think otherwise, or to overturn any of the ecclesiastical statutes or
traditions, these we anathematize and cast out. For there is nothing crooked
or twisted in the catholic Church. "For all things are straight to those who
understand, and right to those who find knowledge. For she is altogether fair,
as it has been said, and there is no blemish in her."

γʹ. And first, he who laid the foundation of the most Christian empire, the
apostle of Christ among emperors, Constantine the Great and just, who awarded
to the Church of God the countless praiseworthy achievements, and who dashed
to the ground that labyrinthine and hard-to-exit Arian tower of fighting
against God, shone forth to the ends, as a fire-appearing pillar, the
knowledge of the superessential and life-originating consubstantial Trinity.
As the very first and exceptional offering of piety toward Christ our true
God, he engraves as a mark upon the imperial coin of the state both the
heaven-appearing sign of the saving Cross and the venerable and theandric

character of Christ upon it together with his own, showing the authority of
the heavenly king that has come to pass toward the earthly one, and peaceful
libations and covenants of deep peace, one flock coming to be, and one
authority, of angels and of men. Likewise also that the venerable and august
character of his incarnate way of life should be set up and erected, and the
characteristic properties of his undefiled form, according as he was seen on
earth as perfect man and consorted with men, from the holy, undefiled, and
Theotokos Mary; displaying the marks, as the blessed apostles handed down to
the catholic Church; adorning her with painted histories and mosaic tesserae,
and from the writing of the God-delivered Gospels establishing the theandric
character of Christ =95.349=. For the Gospel according to Matthew was written
eight years after the ascension of Christ; that according to Mark after ten,
that according to Luke after 15, that according to John after 32, under
Domitian. Wherefore he also first legislated that these things be depicted in
the churches, and tracing these in painted colors: the Bethlehem nativity, the
shepherds' seeing with their own eyes, the Magi's bringing of gifts, the
course of the star, the reception by the righteous Simeon, the baptism by
John, the manifestation of the paradoxical and divine miracles; the voluntary
sufferings, the supernatural and life-bearing resurrection, the divine
ascension into heaven, and the consequent wonder-workings of the apostles. For
this definition and account has been handed down to the Church, that the
Only-begotten and Word of God and our God, the beginningless, the eternal, the
immaterial, the without quantity, the without quality, the infinitely great,
the intangible. who brought all things from non-being into being, who
constituted the angelic and heavenly bodiless Powers by the word; who measured
the heaven with a span, holding the earth in a handful, and measuring the
water with the hand; who formed man with undefiled hands, having become man
from the holy Virgin and Theotokos Mary without change and without alteration,
has shared in flesh and blood, inasmuch as he became man, a rational living
being receptive of mind and knowledge, of perhaps three-cubit circumscription,
and circumscribed by the thickness of flesh in that which is seen according to
us, characterizing the properties of the maternal likeness, and displaying the
formation of Adam. For which reason he is characterized, as the ancient
historians describe his impression: meeting of eyebrows, fair-eyed,
well-nosed, curly-haired, somewhat stooped, well-colored, having a black
beard, wheat-colored in appearance according to the maternal manifestation;
long-fingered, well-voiced, sweet of speech, most meek, quiet, long-suffering,
forbearing, and carrying about the similar advantages of virtue, in which
properties his theandric Word is characterized, lest a shadow of change or an
alteration of variation be detected in the divine incarnation of the Word,
according to the ravings of the Manichaeans.

δʹ. For the divine apostle and evangelist Luke, while the all-holy Mother of
God Mary was still living in the flesh in Jerusalem and making her sojourns in
holy Sion, traced with painted mixtures the monument of the all-pure one upon
panels, [and] leaving it as in a mirror to the generation thereafter. And when
he had shown this to her, "My grace shall be with it." And another thing more
paradoxical: the God-seeing chief apostles Peter and John, making their
sojourns in Lydda called Diospolis, eighteen miles before Jerusalem, having
built a house of prayer =95.352= in the name of the mother of the Lord and
Mother of God, and her to be present at the enthronement of the temple


earnestly entreating, she said to them: "I am there also with you." And by
some divine and invisible energy her character was established upon one
pillar; at which the Mother of God herself, having come and seen her own
impression, filled with joy and amazement, marveled and was struck at the
maternal glory and good lot of him who became incarnate from her, who assigned
the reverence. Which character, preserved until Julian the Apostate, he sent
certain Hebrew painters, and because, seeing the impression established upon
one of the pillars of the temple, her whole stature impressed; both the purple
and the array, seeing her as already looking and speaking, trying to bury her
with marble devices, they found her still more brilliant and unaltered in the
depth of the pillar. And many other signs and wondrous portents are displayed
in the icon of the all-holy Mother of God by the same divine energy; through
which also demons are driven out, diseases are healed, lepers are cleansed,
powers are worked, every malady and weakness is strengthened εʹ. And the
Savior and Lord of all himself, while still living on earth, having wiped the
impression of his holy form upon a sudarium, sending it to a certain Abgar,
toparch of the great city of the Edessenes, through Thaddaeus the divine
apostle, also wipes off the divine sweat of the face, preserving his
characteristic properties in this. Which impression even until today the most
brilliant and renowned city of the Edessenes, holding as a royal scepter,
boasts and vaunts; Christ our true God who granted this grace to her showing
signs among the people; in which also once, when Chosroes the king of the
Persians had kindled a fire of olive woods around her wall in a circle all
about, intending to reduce these to ash upon the earth, the most holy
metropolitan of the time, seeing the people already about to die from the
excessive fire, having lifted this venerable character of the God-imprinted
sudarium and gone about the walls, a certain divine power going forth in a
wind and a violent blast turned the fire back upon the adversaries, and passed
through and burned those around, as the Chaldeans the Assyrians.

ςϛʹ. For once when someone contemptuously lifted a stone against the icon of
the Savior, immediately a dove going forth from his mouth, a raven is
introduced instead; the darkened devil plainly taking up dwelling in place of
the Holy Spirit, and instead of light putting on black-dyed =95.353= darkness.
And another, a prefect by rank in the city of Alexandria, going about the holy
icon of the Mother of God in the forecourt of the Great Church, often mocking
and jeering, on one occasion she appeared to him while he was still awake,
having with her two eunuchs, and stretching out his hands and feet, and when
she had traced the limbs with her holy finger, the joints of the hands from
the elbows and of the feet from the ankles, being shattered, fell off as
leaves torn from a fig tree. And another in the same city who had dared such
things and was being pursued by soldiers, having become a fugitive to the
august icon of the Mother of God, she turned away from him in the eyes of all
and delivered him ready to slaughter as a plotter. And many who have often
struck against her boldly, no ordinary justice has punished.

ζʹ. Time will fail me as I recount the narratives which have been legislated
from of old and from above by holy prophets and apostles, and Fathers and
teachers; for nothing has escaped the Spirit-bearing and God-seeing apostles;
but whatever the Holy Spirit spoke in law and prophets and gospels, these they
also taught, these


they also handed down; these they also adorned the holy Church from the ends
of the inhabited world, from the glorious coming of Christ, until the ends of
the age. But the hostile and ancient plotter of the race, the devil, wasting
away with envy and malice against the good things, has not ceased stirring up
his much-troubled storm, as a wild hurricane, of the heresies. And
Constantius, who succeeded to the scepters of the empire of the great
Constantine, the rotten shoot of the great root, having introduced no moderate
shaking and turmoil of the paternal foundation, a war against the Church worse
than the idolatrous superstition, being deceived by the fraud of the Arian
minds, shook and dashed down the bulwarks and pillars of the Churches.
Liberius Pope of Rome, Athanasius the greatest of Alexandria, Paul another of
Constantinople the confessor, Eustathius the renowned of Antioch, and the
chosen and holy Fathers with them he condemned to ostracism; he entrusted the
holy things of the saints to impious and accursed men. Again followers and
fellow-workers: Julian the firstborn son of Satan, the apostate and
transgressor, likewise also Valens, even if not an apostate, yet a persecutor
of Christ and a fighter against Christ; but immediately there rose the
spiritual star of the western lamp-bearing, the great Theodosius, who,
Macedonius the fighter against the Spirit having been ostracized from the
patriarchal throne, by synods and gatherings of the holy Fathers, having fixed
the greatest holy symbol of the piety of Christ in the Church unassailable,
established it.

ηʹ. Successors of his empire, Arcadius and =95.356= Honorius, and of these
again Theodosius the scion of Arcadius; then again the gangrene taking
pasture, of Nestorius the man-worshiper, of Eutyches and Dioscorus, those who
hold the truth in unrighteousness; but nevertheless unrighteousness lied to
itself. For Marcian, the greatest law-guardian of imperial piety, with the
gathering of the holy Fathers dashed the impious to the ground. Then again
certain others shattered the briny heresy of theopaschism, Severus, James,
Peter the Fuller, and certain other spat-out things of unholy work, against
whom the imperial, most tyrannical hand of Zeno campaigned. Whom the God-sped
synod of the pious emperors, of Justinian the Great, and of the holy Fathers,
drove out like starving dogs from the ecclesiastical assembly. Then again the
rams against the ecclesiastical tower-fortresses, Honorius of Rome, Sergius of
the queen city, Pyrrhus, Paul, Cyrus of Alexandria, Macarius of Antioch,
Theodore of Pharan, were dashed to the ground of the heresy by the
much-contriving originator of evils, receiving anathema of condemnation by the
pious great Justin with the God-gathered synod of the holy Fathers.

θʹ. Then when these and more heresies had been tossed up like chaff from a
summer threshing-floor, and dissolved like wild waves of the sea, what now the
creeping crooked serpent, the adversary of our race the devil, the sower of
tares, who some other hurricane of impiety and of heresy ... and of the
unchangeable and unalterable incarnation of God the Word already for about 130
years distributing this incurable and unhealable pasture by such a pretext:
Theodosius of the Adramyttian race having been entrusted with the imperial
scepters; and Jezas holding the rule of the Arabs, two certain God-fighting
sons of Hebrews, who always and at all times stiffen the neck against the Lord
and snort against his Christ, occupying themselves with wonders and
symbol-fights, and sorceries and demonic omens, as if pursuing some
astrological science, being from kinship of the Isaurian Jews, wandering


they arrive at the royal court of the Arabs; and they are reported to the
aforementioned Jezas; and they initiate him with oracles of long life and
long-lasting continuance, if indeed he should be able to dash down the
ordering of the Christians, to wipe out the theandric character of our Savior
Jesus Christ, and of the Mother of God who bore him, from the ecclesiastical
precincts. And that life-loving and swine-living man yielded to the counsel of
the deceivers, and shook all the Eastern =95.357= Churches; but the wretch was
deceived. For no small time passed, and divine justice mastered him. And his
son, succeeding to the rule, wishing to kill these as false-diviners, they
went off as fugitives, returning again to the Isaurian borders.

ιʹ. Then cooling at a certain spring in the inflammation of the heat, behold a
young man named Leo, fair in appearance, graceful in face, tall in body, a
craftsman by trade, furnishing his life from it. And having taken the load
from the beast of burden, he too sat at the spring cooling himself. It was
about the sixth hour. Then the initiates of the ventriloquists initiate him
that he should begin the scepters of the Roman empire; and Leo putting it off
and being at a loss, looking to the uttermost of his own cheapness, is assured
by oaths by the Christ-haters that these things must so come to be. And they
ask to be assured from him by words of oath-taking, that if indeed this should
be brought to an end, whatever at all should be asked for them, it should be
given them by him without delay. And there was nearby a temple of the holy
martyr Theodore; and immediately the craftsman Leo entering most readily, and
becoming master of the holy chancel-screens of the sanctuary, gave the martyr
of Christ as surety, the Jews standing in the gateways of the temple. And they
receive the oath from him, and immediately each went off to his own. ιαʹ. And
there was then a certain patrician holding the leadership of the Eastern
generalship, named Masissinios, going about the country with enlistments;
among whom Leo the craftsman also having been enlisted, in a short time is
raised to the greatest advancement, being appointed to the spatharic rank by
the emperor Theodosius; and having been sent by him into the western parts,
Campania, and Amalfi, and Naples, then having gone barbarian, and having
reached the places there in sailing dromons to the number of 120, and having
awarded a trophy-bearing victory, peace, he reaches the palace; and
immediately he is proclaimed by the soldiers Caesar and emperor autocrat. And
Theodosius the most meek emperor meets him gladly; and taking the crown from
his own head, he places it upon Leo, and thus unshakenly and without
disturbance Leo is entrusted with the palace. And Leo having become famous
with acclamations, not "behold Magi from the East came to worship with gifts
=95.360= Christ," but behold certain Magudaioi, sorcerers and enchanter
Gazarenes, come running into the palace, receiving as the end of their omens
and oracles joy. Then having been taken by the right hand by the new lord they
exact the debt unforgiven. And he most readily promising to give: What then,
he said, are these things? And they say: This we ask of you, most mighty
emperor, that you wipe out the character of the Nazarene Christ, and of his
mother Mary, from every ecclesiastical history; that doing this, you are going
to rule and reign up to a hundred years in your generation; but if you put
this off, you are going to be destroyed more quickly, as Artemius, Apsimar,
Anastasius. And the simple and unsteadfast in the faith, as if pressed by a
serpent at the two nostrils, no longer even able to breathe, most readily


promises to accomplish what was commanded.

ιβʹ. And this man having been shown a manifest heretic, having taken on a
fox's color and having gone through the first things somewhat underhandedly,
he was arranging to be pious. But after the tenth year, he breathes heresy
into the Church; and having assembled the people under him, in the midst of
all the untamed beast named for a lion roared in lion-fashion and said: Since
the representation of the icons is of idolatrous craftsmanship, one must not
do reverence to these. Truly an opened tomb is his throat, and a whitened wall
is this man's heart. And not much in between, and Leo dies as a man, and has
fallen as one of the rulers. And this man having broken off from living, and
having become fuel of the eternal fire, he raised up in his place that man's
defiled offspring, Constantine I mean. This man then, the tree of that wicked
root, having stored up for his own soul all manner of deadly poisons,
plunders, twists together, overthrows, burns with fire every sight of holy
icons. And Germanus the herald of piety, knowing this, makes it known to him:
Emperor, since the Master Christ and our Savior God was incarnate from the
undefiled bloods of the holy Theotokos, and was seen perfect according to us
and circumscribed, all idolatrous worship has been put out of the way; and
from his consorting with us, and from the world-saving teaching of the
apostles until now, 736 years, and the holy Fathers who have come around in
the times between these have thought no such thing concerning the holy icons;
but also the holy and ecumenical synods, choosing these, have arranged that
they be reverenced, and not overthrown. But you, emperor, know that if you
should prevail in this, I am ready to die for the august icons. For the icon
of Christ bears his name, inasmuch as he was seen in flesh and consorted with
men.

ιγʹ. The emperor hearing these things, and roaring as a lion, sending
sword-bearing satraps into the patriarchal house, ordered them to bring down
the saint of those there with fists and insults. Who having come down, and
having become a sharer of the monastic life, he puts forward the impious
Anastasius for the high-priesthood militarily, and not by a vote of God's
piety; who delivered all the things of the church to the palace. Having taken
away every character of his divine incarnation, likewise also of the all-holy
Mother of God, denying her maternal intercession and freedom of speech toward
God the Word incarnate from her, the champion of the proud devil, and shaking
off the intercessions of the saints; and throwing the God-inspired books of
the holy Fathers into a fire; and the Christ-hater abhorring the God-loving
order of those who live as monks, and yoking the catalogue of sacred virgins
with marital contracts, burning the garments of piety with fire, burning with
Bromian fire the venerable beard of the sacred monks, and the honorable gray
hairs, and the divine pupils; having called this a habit of darkness, he who
is darkened in soul, called those enrolled under that habit unmentionable and
idolaters, because of the reverence toward the august icons; and having
assembled all the people under him, he made all swear, the life-giving body
and blood of Christ being set forth, and the undefiled woods on which Christ
stretched out hands for us, and the holy Gospels, through which Christ
commands us not to swear at all, not to reverence holy icons, but to call this
an idol, nor to communicate from an abba, or to give him "hail" at all; O the
idle talk! having found an instrument of like manner and like sound, and
indeed also of like name, and a certain like-minded one of the priestly
catalogue, having raised this man upon the Church, not by a vote of God and of
priests, but by his own knavery, both


having gone up on the ambo, by the hands of the emperor Constantine,
Constantine the unholy is clothed with the sacred double-cloak and the
omophorion. O the unworthiness! the shield-bearer, a setter of priests; and he
who has occupied himself with wars and murders, a liturgist; and he who has
been unlawfully yoked to three women, was established as ordainer of the
sacred. ιδʹ. From this then the defiled pair having been woven together, as an
unlawful chain, programs were sent throughout every province to the chief
satraps throughout the land together with bishops, to reach the reigning city,
for a synod to come to be against the august icons; and the tyrant having
buried the venerable character of the all-undefiled Theotokos, the one in
Blachernae, a synod of false-priests who set it at naught in that all-august
temple the =95.364= accursed man presiding together with the like-named
betrayer of the flock of Christ, the abominable and hostile synod performing,
having called the august icons statue-idols. Then having anathematized
Germanus the divine and right-dividing one of the Church, they called him a
wood-worshiper. O the hardening of the enemy! O the blasphemy! O the turning
aside! and they having blasphemed very many things, last and worse than all
those defiled unholy men cried out that pitiable voice, and said: "Today is
salvation to the world, because you, emperor, have redeemed us from the
idols." O painful voice! which having been buzzed forth darkened heaven, and
shook earth, and defiled the aether, and made all who had cried it out aliens
of God. Truly "the fool will speak foolish things, and his heart will think
vain things." For because of such blasphemies of the godless tyrant and
unworthy of the most Christian purple, heavenly thunderbolts struck upon the
earth, the stars not bearing the Master's insult fell to earth, elements were
shaken, earth was tossed being troubled, corresponding earthquakes, terrors
and signs from heaven; the wretch himself fearing the awaited wrath, a
fugitive and migrant, went off to the city of the Nicomedians. For what
Diocletian or Maximian, or Trajan, did such slanders against Christians, as he
who is unworthy of the Christian name? A day of judgment then was at hand, and
divine justice anticipated this, and he himself is a trustworthy witness
concerning himself. For still breathing around his last, wailing he cried out
with a great shout, saying these things: "The forecourts of Gehenna." What
then is more trustworthy than his own voice, in return for which he did not
know the time of his visitation? for he blasphemed oversized things of vanity
against the Church of Christ, calling the Christians idolaters. ιεʹ. But his
scion, the second Leo, succeeding to the beginning of the empire, transferred
the much-troubled surge of the Church with a most calm and peaceful manner of
life, as having recovered her from squall and triple-wave. Whose both life and
burial has been completed in Irene the aptly named spouse. Again a new Helen
and a new Constantine, son of Irene, having sprung as rose and lily in the
midst of the thorns, as from Hellenism and Judaism they recall the Roman
state; with whom also the new Silvester, the great and celebrated patriarch
Tarasius, having assembled a holy ecumenical synod, scattered every heretical
mind-harm, because the Holy Spirit of God was upon him. As luminaries shining
in the world, as the divine and blessed apostles who from the beginning became
eyewitnesses and ministers of the word handed down to the Church, and the
God-bearing holy Fathers, and the God-inspired and ecumenical orthodox synods,
they ordained to hold and to reverence all saving and life-giving symbols of
Christ; as the divine oracle says: "Show me your face and make me hear your
voice, because your face is fair, and your voice is sweet." There is not among
Christians a recent God, nor


has an alien God been worshiped among them; because, who is God besides our
Lord Jesus Christ? or who is God except him? Where are the accusers of
Christians, the accusers of the sacred, who have alienated themselves from the
Christ-named calling? who have despaired themselves of the coming hope of the
Christians, who have been led astray from the womb of the divine font, sinful
men, the deceitful lips that speak lawlessness against the catholic Church, in
pride and contempt, who twist every God-inspired and profitable Scripture
according to their own desires, who have trampled the son of God, and have
counted the blood of the covenant a common thing, in which they were
sanctified, and have insulted the Spirit of grace in which they were ordained,
the false-Christians, who have called the holy and pure Church of Christ a
polluted table, who do not know a difference of holy and profane, of clean and
unclean? I ought to have written many things, but we are not able through ink
and paper; but let us return to the matter at hand.

ιςϛʹ. Again the aptly named Nicephorus, most pious among emperors and orthodox
servant of Christ; likewise also the sons of piety and successors of the
empire, having kept the faith firm and unshaken for Christ, also entrusted the
Church to the great high priest patriarch Nicephorus, pure and undefiled, not
having spot or wrinkle. But again an aptly named beast of impiety, as a lion
seizing and roaring, struck upon us; and the blight of such a soul-destroying
heresy struck by such a pretext. Michael and Theophylact, the most God-loving
and most pious men, steering the palace, and the Church of God being in
deepest calm, and all the hierarchs living a quiet and still life with the
priests and devout monks in all piety and gravity, I know not how to say, a
strange-appearing portent slipped into the all-blessed city; a certain woman
of the locals having borne an infant, the upper members of the body as far as
the navel had all of a child, but those below, of wild beasts, of lions and
leopards and wolves. And of these the forms were moving, so as to know the
shaping of each; but the ventriloquist by =95.368= the Python spirit of the
air addressing in her, wonders the swift succession of the emperors; and the
Christ-loving emperor delivers this abominated woman into secure guard to one
of his own and secret servants. And a certain one of the obscure and cheap of
the city named John, occupying himself in the reader's rank at the sacred
monastery of the holy Theotokos of those called Hodegon, having spoken freely
with the ventriloquist, is initiated into the foreknowledge of the things to
come, both the exchange of the emperor, and the restoration of the successor
of the empire, and that he would live a great and supernatural thirty years in
prosperity and trophy-bearing victory, if indeed having made the demolition of
the sacred icons complete, he should utterly wipe out their memorial. And Leo
shall be his name, descended from kinship of Armenians; and, he says, he
oracles, and that the fellow-initiate John shall be deemed worthy of greatest
honor by this Leo, and shall be lifted up, and shall be raised exceedingly
exceedingly above every rule, as no other of those who have gone before
according to him.

ιζʹ. And Leo being patrician and general of the Eastern prefecture, being
within in the blessed, a certain Nicephorus surnamed Cinnarius initiates Leo
into the oracles of the ventriloquist; and another certain one of the
itinerant false-hermits, mocking the inexperienced people with false-speakings
and oracles, is introduced through the same Nicephorus to Leo the patrician;
and this man initiates him into the


similar and matching wonders of the ventriloquist. And the world-loving man
with a fiery heart of disposition confirmed with an unchangeable oath to do
these things, having hidden in his heart the spark of impiety.

ιηʹ. And Leo having seized the imperial scepters, becomes a more abundant
zealot and imitator of the impious lords who had gone before, Leo and
Constantine who had come to be of the kinship of the Isaurians, and had lasted
many years in the empire, and had thrown down the boldness of enemy barbarians
