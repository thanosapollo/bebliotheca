* Against Heresies, Book 4

1 As Isaiah says: "The children of Jacob shall sprout, and Israel shall
blossom, and the inhabited world shall be filled with his fruit." Since then
his fruit has been sown throughout the whole inhabited world, those things
that once bore fruit well have reasonably been abandoned and taken out of the
midst (for from them Christ according to the flesh was brought forth as fruit,
and the apostles), but now they are no longer fit for fruit-bearing. 2 God
does all things with measure and order, and nothing is without measure with
him, because nothing is unnumbered. 3 "And Abraham believed God, and it was
reckoned to him for righteousness," first because he himself is the Maker of
heaven and earth, the only God, and then because he will make his seed as the
stars of heaven, that is, what was said by Paul: "As lights in the world."
Rightly then, leaving all earthly kinship, he followed his Word, sojourning as
a stranger that he might live as a citizen with the Word. Rightly also the
apostles, having their race from Abraham, leaving the boat and the father,
followed the Word. Rightly also we, having the same faith as Abraham, taking
up the cross as Isaac also the wood, follow him. And he, readily yielding his
own only-begotten and beloved as a sacrifice to God, that God also might be
well pleased, on behalf of all his seed, to furnish his own only-begotten and
beloved Son as a sacrifice for our redemption. 4 And well does Justin say in
the treatise against Marcion that I would not have believed even the Lord
himself if he had announced another God besides the Creator. 5 The Lord taught
us that no one is able to know God unless God himself has taught him, that is,
that God is not known without God; and that his being known is the will of the
Father; for they shall know him to whom the Son may reveal him. 6 Every just
king has a priestly rank. 7 How do they say that the flesh goes into
corruption and does not partake of life, the flesh that is nourished from the
body of the Lord and from his blood? Let them either change their opinion, or
refuse to offer the things spoken of. But for us the Eucharist is in agreement
with the opinion, and the Eucharist confirms the opinion. We offer to him his
own things, fittingly announcing the communion and union of flesh and Spirit.
For as the bread from the earth, receiving the invocation of God, is no longer
common bread, but Eucharist consisting of two things, an earthly and a
heavenly, so also our bodies, partaking of the Eucharist, are no longer
corruptible, having the hope of the resurrection unto eternity. 8 Well then is
the Scripture that says: "First of all believe that there is one God who
created and fitted all things." 9 And this is his Word, our Lord Jesus Christ,
who in the last times became God among men, that he might join the end to the
beginning, that is, man to God. And for this reason the prophets, having
received the prophecy concerning this Word, prophesied his incarnate presence.
10 Just as those who see the light are within the light and partake of its
brightness, so those who see God are within God, partaking of his brightness;
therefore those who behold God shall partake of life. And for this reason the
uncontained and incomprehensible and invisible granted himself to the faithful
as seen and comprehended and contained, that he might give life to those who
contain and see him through faith. For as his greatness is untraceable, so
also his goodness is unutterable, through which, being seen, he imparts life
to those who behold him. Since it was not possible to live without life, and
the existence of life comes about from the participation of God, and
participation of God is to know God and to enjoy his kindness. 11 Through the
marriage of Moses the intelligible marriage of Christ was shown, and through
the Ethiopian bride the Church from the nations

was made manifest, whom those who speak against and slander and mock shall not
be clean; for they shall become leprous and shall be excluded from the camp of
the just. 12 Christ is the treasure hidden in the field, that is, in the
Scriptures that are in the world (for the field is the world), but hidden,
because he was signified through types and parables that could not be
understood before the outcome of the things prophesied should come, which is
the presence of the Lord. For every prophecy before the outcome is a riddle
and a contradiction to men; but when the time comes and the thing prophesied
comes to pass, then it has obtained the most exact interpretation. And for
this reason the law, being read by Jews in the present time, is like a myth;
for they do not have the interpretation of all things, which is the presence
of the Son of God according to man; but being read by Christians, it is a
treasure, hidden indeed in the field, but revealed to them 13 Thus Paul was
making his defense to the Corinthians: "For we are not as the many, peddling
the word of God." "We have wronged no one." Such presbyters the Church rears;
concerning whom the prophet also says: "I will give your rulers in peace and
your bishops in righteousness." 14 This came about with Lot not knowing, nor
having been enslaved to pleasure, but an economy was altogether being
accomplished, through which the two synagogues, having borne children from one
and the same Father, were made known, without pleasure of flesh. For there was
no other able to give them living seed and the fruit of children, as it has
been written. 15 And he also examines the Ebionites. How can they be saved, if
God was not the one who was to work their salvation upon the earth? Or how
shall man pass into God, if God has not passed into man? 16 And the divine
examines those who work schisms, being empty of the love of God and looking to
their own advantage, but not to the unity of the Church, and for small and
chance causes cutting and dividing the great and glorious body of Christ and,
so far as in them lies, destroying it, speaking peace and working war, truly
"straining out the gnat, but swallowing the camel"; for no such achievement
can come about from them as is the harm of the schism. 17 All things consist:
a complete faith in one God Almighty from whom are all things; a firm
persuasion in the Son of God, Jesus Christ our Lord, through whom are all
things, and his economies through which the Son of God became man; a true
knowledge in the Spirit of God, who exhibits the economies of the Father and
of the Son in each generation unto men as the Father wills; the teaching of
the apostles and the ancient constitution of the Church throughout the whole
world. 18 Those who proclaim Emmanuel from the Virgin were making known the
union of the Word of God with his own fashioning. 19 Through the parable of
the laborers sent at different times into the vineyard, one and the same
householder is shown, having called some at the beginning of the world's
framing, some after this, and others after the mid-time, others as the times
were advancing, and at the end again others, so that the laborers are many
according to their own generations, but one the householder who calls them
together. For there is also one vineyard, because there is also one
righteousness; and one steward, for one is the Spirit of God that governs all
things; and likewise also one wage, for all "received a denarius apiece," the
image and inscription of the king, the knowledge of the Son of God, which is
incorruption. And for this reason he began to give the wage "from the last,"
because in the last of the times the Lord, having been manifested, restored
himself to all. 20 Force is not present with God, but a good will is always
present with him. 21 If by nature some have become base and others good,
neither are these praiseworthy being good, for they were made such, nor are
those blameworthy,

having come to be so. But since all are of the same nature, being able both to
hold and to do the good, and being able again to cast it away and not to do
it, justly both among men who are well ordered, and much more with God, some
are praised and obtain a worthy testimony of the complete choice of the
beautiful and of persistence in it, while others are accused and worthily
obtain a penalty for the casting away of the beautiful and the good. And for
this reason the prophets were exhorting men to do justly and to work out the
good, as such a thing is in our power, and because through much neglect they
had fallen into forgetfulness and were in need of a good mind, which the good
God was furnishing as counsel through the prophets. For all these things
exhibit the self-determination of man and the advisory character of God, who
turns him from disobeying him, but does not force him. For even the gospel
itself, if someone should not wish to follow it, it is permitted to him, but
it is not expedient; for disobedience toward God and the casting away of the
good is in man, but it brings no ordinary harm and penalty. And for this
reason Paul says: "All things are lawful for me, but not all things are
expedient." Interpreting the freedom of man, wherefore all things are lawful,
God not compelling him, he also shows what is expedient, that we may not abuse
freedom as a cloak of wickedness, for this at least is inexpedient. 22 Things
that come of themselves are not loved in the same way as those found with
zeal. 23 But if someone should say: Could God not have made man perfect from
the beginning? let him know that for God, who is always the same and
unbegotten, all things are possible as regards himself; but the things that
have come to be, inasmuch as they afterwards had their own beginning of
generation, must in this respect also fall short of the one who made them. For
the things newly generated could not be unbegotten; and inasmuch as they are
not unbegotten, in this respect they also fall short of the perfect; for
inasmuch as they are younger, in this respect they are also infants, and in
this respect unaccustomed and unexercised toward the perfect training. As
therefore the mother is able to furnish perfect food to the infant, but it is
still unable to receive the nourishment of one older than itself, so also God
himself was able to furnish the perfect to man from the beginning, but man was
unable to receive it; for he was an infant. And for this reason our Lord also,
in the last of the times, recapitulating all things into himself, came to us,
not as he himself was able, but as we were able to see him; for he himself was
able to come to us in his unspeakable glory, but we were not yet ever able to
bear the greatness of his glory. And for this reason, as to infants, the
perfect bread of the Father furnished himself to us as milk, which was his
presence according to man, that, having been nourished as from the breast of
his flesh and through such milk-feeding having been accustomed to eat and
drink the Word of God, we might be able to contain in ourselves the bread of
immortality, which is the Spirit of the Father. And for this reason Paul says
to the Corinthians: "I gave you milk to drink, not meat, for you were not yet
able to bear it," that is, you were discipled in the presence of the Lord
according to man, but the Spirit of the Father does not yet rest upon you
because of your weakness. "For where there is jealousy and strife," he says,
"among you and divisions, are you not fleshly and do you walk according to
man?" that is, that the Spirit of the Father was not yet with them because of
the unfinished and weak character of their manner of life. As therefore the
apostle was able to give the meat (for those on whom the apostles laid hands
received the Holy Spirit, which is the meat of life), but they were unable to
receive it because they still had weak and unexercised senses of the exercise
toward God, so also at the beginning God was able to give the perfect to man,
but he, having just come to be, was unable to receive it, or even receiving to
contain it, or even containing to hold it. And for this reason the Word of
God, being perfect, became an infant together with man, not for himself but
because of the

infancy of man, being contained thus as man was able to contain him. The
inability and lack, then, is not about God, but about man newly come to be,
because he was not unbegotten. 24 About God, power together with wisdom and
goodness is shown: power and goodness in willingly creating and making the
things that were not yet, and wisdom in having made the things that have come
to be well proportioned and well fitted and well constructed, which, through
his surpassing goodness, having received increase and remaining still more,
shall bear away the glory of the unbegotten, God granting the beautiful
without envy. For inasmuch as they have been generated, they are not
unbegotten; but inasmuch as they remain through long ages, they shall take on
a power of the unbegotten, God freely granting them continuance forever. And
thus God has the first place in all things, who is also alone unbegotten and
first of all and cause of being to all, while all the rest remain in
subjection to God, and subjection to God is incorruption, and continuance of
incorruption is the glory of the unbegotten. 25 Through this order and such
rhythms and such training, the generated and fashioned man becomes according
to the image and likeness of the unbegotten God, the Father well pleased and
commanding, the Son ministering and doing, the Spirit nourishing and
increasing, and man quietly advancing and going up toward the perfect, that
is, becoming near the unbegotten; for the end is the unbegotten, and this is
God. And man had first to come to be, and having come to be to grow, and
having grown to become a man, and having become a man to multiply, and having
multiplied to grow strong, and having grown strong to be glorified, and having
been glorified to see his own Master; for God is the one who is to be seen,
and the vision of God is productive of incorruption, "and incorruption makes
one to be near God." 26 Nor does the light grow weak because of those who make
themselves blind, but while it remains such as it is, those who have been
blinded are set in unseeing by their own cause, neither the light leading
anyone into slavery with necessity, nor God forcing, if someone should not
wish to hold his art. Those then who have stood away from the paternal light
and transgressed the law of freedom stood away by their own cause, having
become free and self-determining in their mind. 27 One and the same Father,
who has prepared the good things with him for those who long for his communion
and remain in his subjection, but has prepared eternal fire for the devil, the
leader of the apostasy, and for those who stood away with him, into which the
Lord said those separated to the left would be sent. And this is what was said
by the prophet: "I am a jealous God, making peace and creating evils," making
peace toward those who repent and turn to him and composing friendship and
union, but having prepared eternal fire and outer darkness for those who do
not repent and flee his light, which things are evils to those who fall into
them. 28 The Lord sowed good seed in his own field ("and the field is the
world"); "but while men were sleeping, the enemy came and sowed tares in the
midst of the wheat and went away." For from then this angel is apostate and
enemy, from the time when he envied the fashioning of God and attempted to
make him an enemy toward God. Wherefore God also separated from his own
participation the one who secretly sowed the tares from himself, that is, who
brought in the transgression; but he pitied the man who received the
disobedience carelessly but without malice, and he turned back the enmity
which he had wished to make toward him onto the one who was making the enmity,
thrusting away from himself the enmity toward him, but bending it back and
sending it against the serpent, as the Scripture says God said to the serpent:
"And I will put enmity between you and the woman," And this enmity the Lord
recapitulated into himself, "becoming from a woman" a man and trampling his
head.
