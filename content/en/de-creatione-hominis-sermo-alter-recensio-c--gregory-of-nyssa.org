41a Wise Solomon, not in persuasive words of wisdom but having been made wise
in the teachings of the Holy Spirit, in the passages just now read to us,
honoring man, cried out saying: Man is great, and a merciful man is precious.
But I for my part was considering in vain by myself both what I had in my own
mind and what I had been taught from Scripture concerning man. For I was
reasoning how man is great, the perishable animal subject to countless
passions, from birth to old age drawing out a swarm of countless evils,
concerning whom it has also been said in Scripture: Lord, what is man, that
you have been made known to him? And the psalmist despises him as a cheap
animal, but the proverb honors man as something great. But this perplexity of
mine was resolved by the history of the generation of 42a man being read. For
we were hearing now that God took dust from the earth and formed man. I found,
then, from this saying both things, that man is nothing and that man is great.
For if you look only at the nature from which he has come to be, he will
appear to you as nothing and worth nothing; but if you look at the honor with
which you were honored, man is great. What honor is this? Compare the
generation of man and of all the others: God said, Let there be light, and
there was light; Let there be a firmament, and the great heaven is stretched
above us, brought forth by a word alone. Stars and sun and moon and all
things, as many as we behold with the eye and grasp with the mind, received
being by a saying. Sea and earth and the ordering in them, kinds of animals of
every sort, various kinds of plants, all came to be by a command. But man is
not so. For it was not said: Let there be man, 43a but something more is seen
in this: above light, above heaven, above the luminaries, above all things is
the generation of man.

He took, it says, God took dust from the earth and formed man. With his own
hand he deigns to form our body. No angel served at the forming, the earth did
not spontaneously spit us out like cicadas, he did not use some ministers and
servants for the work, but with his own hand he crafts artistically, having
taken dust from the earth. When, then, you look at what was taken, you will
reasonably say: What is man? But when you consider the one who formed and his
own working, turning again you will declare from within that Man is great: the
one on account of the matter from which he has been constructed, reckoned as
nothing according to his own nature, the other on account of the honor with
which he has been honored, judged as great in truth because of According to
the image and According to likeness. And God took. But how there in what
precedes: And God made man, but here from the beginning again the generation
of man is recounted, as if perhaps we had been taught nothing yet about man?
For it says: And God took dust from the earth and formed man. Some have
already said that He formed was said of the body, and He made of the 44a soul,
and perhaps the account is not outside the truth. For where the making of the
perfect man has been spoken and that In the image of God he made him, He made
has been written; but where thereafter it recounts our bodily subsistence and
the forming, He formed has been said. And the psalmist also taught the
difference of making and forming, saying: Your hands made me and formed me.
They made the inner man, they formed the outer. For forming befits clay, and
making that which is according to the image: so that the flesh was formed, but
the soul was made. Having spoken first about the subsistence of the soul, now
he discourses with us about the forming of the body. Hold this account also.
And what is the other? That some things are said in summary, and others are
handed down to us according to the manner in which they came to be. From
above, then, that 45a he made, here also how he made. For if he had simply
said that he made, and had not lingered over the manner of the making, <man>
would have reckoned himself as the cattle,


as the beasts, as the plants, as the grass. So that you may flee fellowship
with the more irrational and more insensible, the account handed down the
distinctive craftsmanship of God concerning you. God took dust from the earth
and formed with his own hands. Consider how you were formed: a hand of God is
what constructed you. Let what was formed by God not be defiled through
wickedness, not be altered through evil; do not fall out by force from the
hand of God that holds you together. You are a God-formed vessel come from
God: glorify the one who made you. For you came into being for no other reason
than that you might be a fitting instrument of the glory of God. And this
whole world is like some written book dictating the hidden 46a and invisible
glory of God and announcing his majesty to you who have a mind for the
contemplation of truth. For you remember entirely what has been said. And God
blessed them and said, Increase and multiply and fill the earth. For the
increase is double, one of body, one of soul. For the increase of body is the
restoration from small to the fitting measure, and the increase of soul is the
addition through lessons toward perfection. Increase, then, being said of all,
has been said for the irrational animals according to the perfection of the
body and the completion of nature, but for us who are rational Increase
according to the inner man, according to the progress that leads up to God.
Such was Paul, stretching 47a forward to the things ahead, forgetting the
things behind. This is the increase, the taking up of the contemplations of
piety, the stretching toward the more, our always reaching after the things
above us and leaving behind what has already come to be, but seeking as far as
power allows the things still lacking of piety. Such was Isaac, concerning
whom that testimony has been written, that Going forward he was exalted, until
he became great. For he did not stand still, nor having grown a little remain,
but he always went forward, going up with great strides upon virtue: he
crossed over with a great step to temperance, he stepped upon justice, from
there he went up to courage. Thus crossing over to the peak of the height of
the good, the righteous man is crowned. Increase, then, with the increase
according to God, the perfection according to the inner man. Multiply: the
blessing of the Church. Let theology not be confined 48a in one and two, he
says, but let the gospel of salvation be proclaimed to all the earth.
Multiply. Who? Those who are being born according to the gospel. Fill the
earth. Fill the flesh given to you for service with good works: let the eye be
full of seeing what is due; let the hand be full of good works; let the feet
walk for the visiting of the sick, being able to go to what is fitting. Let
the whole constitution of our members be full of the workings according to the
commandments. For this is Fill the earth. For such voices are perhaps common
even with the irrational animals, but they become distinctive toward man,
whenever we use without ceasing that according to the image and that with
which we were honored. For those increase bodily, but we spiritually; and they
fill the earth with multitude, but we fill with good workings the earth yoked
to us, that is, the bodily forming. Behold, I have given you every tree that
has fruit in itself; it shall be for you for food. Let the Church pass over
nothing; all things are lawful. He did not say: I have given you the fishes
49a for food, I have given you the cattle, the birds, the four-footed. For it
was not for the sake of this that they were created, but the first legislation
granted enjoyment of fruits. For we were still thought worthy of paradise and
of the diet in it. And what a kind of mystery also lies beside in what is
hidden, that not only for us but also for all the irrational animals the same
food from the fruits of the earth and from the seeds was then appointed.
Behold, it says, I have given you every seed-bearing herb sowing seed, which
is upon the earth, and every tree that has in itself fruit of seed-bearing
seed; it shall be for you for


food and for all the beasts of the earth and for all the birds of heaven and
for every creeping thing that creeps upon the earth. And yet someone <might>
say: we see just now that most of the beasts are not fed on fruits. For with
what fruit does a leopard consent to be fed? And what fruit can nourish a
lion? But let him know that 50a these too, subjected to the law of nature,
were formerly fed on fruits; but when man changed his diet and went outside
the bounds given to him, after the flood the Lord, knowing men to be
unsparing, granted the enjoyment of all things; for you shall eat these as
green herbs of grass. By this concession the remaining animals also at once
received license to eat flesh. From that time onward the lion is blood-eating,
from that time also vultures † at the same time as animals were born were
scanning the earth: for nothing yet had died of the things indicated or made
by God, so that vultures might be fed; nor did nature separate them, for it
was in its prime; nor had hunters consumed them, for there was not yet human
craft; nor had beasts torn them apart, for they were not yet blood-eating.
(And it is customary for vultures to live from the dead.) So there were not
yet 51a dead things, not yet stench, not yet such food of vultures, but all
lived the diet of swans and all grazed the meadows. And such as we see dogs
often eating grass for the sake of healing (not because the food is connatural
to them, but because by some untaught teaching of nature the irrational
animals come to what is beneficial), such, reckon, were then also the now
raw-flesh-eating animals, inventing grass as food for themselves, not plotting
against one another. For such as the restoration is going to be afterward,
such entirely was also the first generation. And man returns to his ancient
and first condition, having put away wickedness, the misery concerning this
earthy and painful life, the cares and anxieties about these things of life
that trouble the soul. Having put all these away, then, he returns to that
life in paradise, the one unenslaved to the passions of the flesh, the free,
the one sharing diet with angels, the one belonging to God. These things have
been said to us, not because we wish to shut man out from the use of foods
conceded by God, but that we may bless the former time. What the life then was
like, how without need, how content with little. How very few and easily
obtained were the things contributing to men for the conduct of life, and how
the variety of diet was brought in for us later by the 52a cause of sin. For
when we fell from the true luxury that was in paradise, we invented
adulterated luxuries for ourselves; and since we no longer see the tree of
life nor take pride in its beauty, cooks were given to us thereafter for
enjoyment, and bakers and varieties of cakes and of incense and such things
consoling our fall from there. Just as the sick, when they are thrown down by
a violent illness, not being able to partake of the customary enjoyment of
foods, receive consolations from the physicians with things to smell and such
greetings; for since they have fallen from the stronger things, those who
flatter their senses invent for enjoyment a treatment commensurate with their
weakness. But even now, those who wish to lead themselves in imitation of the
life in paradise, let us flee entirely this much-material enjoyment of foods,
and let us be led up, as far as is possible, toward that former life, using
fruits and seeds and tree-fruits for sufficient nourishment, and dismissing
whatever is surplus of these as not necessary. Not because they are 53a
abominable; for all things have come to be from God; but because they are
harmful on account of fleshly well-feeling. And God rested from all his works
on the seventh day. Let the arithmeticians speak, and those who have occupied
themselves with such things and have put forward such wisdom as some great
achievement, some that the number six is kindred to the creation of the world,
others that it is much-generating and generating many figures of numbers from


itself and that it is perfect in its own parts and as many other things as
arithmeticians recount about the hexad in school lectures. Let them speak also
about the hebdomad, that there is barrenness in the seventh; for the number
seven neither generates another from itself nor is born from another. But we,
so that we may not, being carried over into a much-material account, come to
be outside the discourse toward you, will proceed to the more reasonable and
more necessary things. But if anyone also has some experience concerning
those, let him know that the treasure of these also 54a is laid up with us.

We do not think these great because they are from the wisdom of the world,
things which we have been commanded rather to despise as small and
unprofitable toward virtue, but not to display. So then, in order to show them
that the things most eagerly pursued among them are contemptible to us, let us
pass such accounts by in silence, and let us dwell in the things at hand and
more beneficial. And at the same time the contemplation of those technical and
sophistic accounts is not easily grasped by the common and unlearned people.
And the Church is present not expecting a hearing of paradoxical words, but
seeking the solutions of contemplations for its own edification. The seventh
day, then, has been honored, the sabbath, and from there the seventh number is
precious among the Jews; in which are the booths, the trumpets, the day of
atonement. And a seventh year 55a is precious among them, the one called the
year of release. For they were permitted to work the earth for six years, but
in the seventh year to be content with what grew of itself, when they had the
land of promise. For six years the Hebrew was a slave and in the seventh he
was released from slavery. In the seventieth year the captivity among them was
loosed. Likewise it is possible to see also our things. Seven times, he says,
the righteous will fall and rise. Thus we too appropriate the hebdomad. The
seventh from generation did not see death: this is a mystery of the Church.
The seventh from Abraham, Moses, received the law. This is a change of life, a
dissolving of lawlessness, an entrance of righteousness, a sojourning of God,
an ordering into the world, a legislation of things to be done. In the
seventieth and seventh generation from Adam, Christ appeared. Peter knows the
mystery of the hebdomad: How often shall my brother sin against me and I
forgive him? Up to seven times? 56a The teacher answered and he himself also
did not depart from the seven, but he spoke as the disciple knew, and the
master surpassed by wealth: How often shall he sin and I forgive? He did not
say: up to six times or up to eight times. Then he added, Up to seventy times
seven. He did not fall over in the increase to another number. He did not say:
a hundred times a hundred, but he multiplied the seven. Neither did Peter ask
another number nor did the Lord go outside the hebdomadic canon. Peter
observed that it is an ancient canon of tradition, that the hebdomad has some
indication of a remission of sins, of a perfect rest, of which the sabbath is
a sign, the seventh day from generation. Peter seven times, the Lord seventy
times seven. But also sins of old are avenged according to this number. Not
so, says God, everyone who kills Cain will pay seven vengeances. Nor there
eight but seven. Why? Wait a little and you will find the mystery. The first
sin is avenged seven times, the second murder of Lamech seventy times seven.
Likewise then also here remission with Peter is seventh, corresponding to the
57a vengeance of Cain, and forgiveness from the Lord seventy times seven as
the condemnation of Lamech. As great as the transgression, so great also the
gift; where the sin is little, little also the remission; For to whom he
forgave little, he also loves little; Where sin abounded, grace superabounded.
What then is the mystery? The eighth day is called the age of judgment, in
which the sinner will be avenged seven times, the one in excess seventy times
seven; and conversely the righteous will be honored seven times, the one
righteous in excess seventy times seven. For the philanthropy of God now
through riddles shows us in part the things to come, but then


at his second coming it presents the truth clear and made distinct. For he
will show at that time who is worthy of what. Sevenfold, then, the recompense
owed to sinners the Lord will forgive us, if here through repentance and
confession 58a he should be propitiated by us. So that, knowing that fearful
day, in which has been stored up for those who have sinned here the measuring
back and counterbalancing according to the worth of the sins, let us, going
before through repentance in life, dissolve the sins, so that there we may be
able to escape the multiplication of the toilsome and painful recompense. Let
us reckon this seventh day, then, as an antitype of that eighth of the
consummation of the age. And God rested from his works. For no longer are
there works of the world on that day, no longer marriages, no longer
contracts, tradings, farmings no longer, but all creation stands startled and
trembling, and sweat from all is unrestrainable, and all alike are in contest,
even those righteous among us, what vote then will be brought out concerning
them. The Lord from the heavens and the heavens are torn, power is revealed,
59a all creation in trembling and contraction. And the angels themselves
perhaps stand in great fear, not because they fear concerning their own sins,
but because the glorious and mighty appearing of Christ casts from itself fear
alike upon all generated things. Do you not hear Isaiah saying: If you open
the heaven, trembling will take mountains from you; hinting at that day, in
which the sea is frozen, in which creation is at leisure, nature is deadened,
every method through words is idle toward the sojourning of God from the
heavens? Then the righteous are snatched up to the things above toward heaven;
for clouds are vehicles for them and the intellectual spirits escorts; but
<the> sinners will fall below, bound by the weight of their own faults and
self-condemned by the wicked conscience. This seventh day, then, figures that
eighth day: For God, it says, rested from all his works. No longer the things
of this life on that day, nor the things that follow here upon the fleshly and
irrational desires; youthful desires have fallen asleep, practices of
marriages, hopes of childbearing, 60a no care of affairs or of possessions.
The lover of money has forgotten the purse, the greedy man snatching and doing
violence. Empty and contemptible glory has been quenched for those whose mind
has been lifted from wherever of these worldly preeminences. Anger does not
trouble toward defense against the one who grieved. All those things have then
failed from the reasoning. But the soul is toward the one who frightens,
toward the expectation of the terrible things that hang over. Which fear of
God, if even here someone should be able to settle in his own soul
unfailingly, he will sin either nothing or perhaps little. And God formed man,
having taken dust from the earth. This account concerning the construction of
man is not to be thought a repetition, even if it appears so to some, but
rather an unbroken sequence of the former attempt concerning this and [the]
completion of the narrative joined as it were to the beginning. For having
said that God made man and the things that follow concerning this very one,
and having made clear that in the making of man God completed all the works of
the world, he again holds to the same narrative concerning man, working as it
were continuously and without interval and speaking in detail the manner of
his making. For first he said simply: God made man; but now he adds also how
he made: having taken dust from the earth. When, then, you hear dust, be
instructed 61a ]62a being, but you both have come to be and exist from the
soulless and properly insensible earth. So that what has been said against you
is not an insult, but rather an honor. Again whenever some fleshly desire
masters your mind and persuades you to practice the fulfillment of the
appetite, look quickly to the earth. Remember that just as from


this you came forth, so also into this before long you will be dissolved, and
the members now pulsing and the irrational desire and the tickling of the
flesh will tomorrow not be, the members having been deadened and the appetite
having flown away. Thus the continual inclination toward the earth our mother
and recognition becomes a cause of abstinence and freedom from all the hard
and hard-to-heal passions that trouble us each time. Dust, he said, having
taken from the earth. If 63a we had been formed from heaven, as not being able
always to look at that we would not have been continually reminded of our own
nature; but now from what is at hand there is present to us also an unceasing
reminder of our cheapness and weakness from the things we tread, from the
things we touch, from the things we behold, from the things we are fed, from
the things with which we are continually mixed. What is more abominable and
more to be rejected than earth and clay, from which you have been shaped? What
greater cause of our thinking moderately and of reckoning ourselves worth
nothing? Whenever, then, you see someone thinking great things of himself,
clothed in a blooming garment, his hand shining with a ring, wearing a bezel
of precious stones, drawing after him a surplus of household servants, clothed
in great and loosened hair, and this both yellow and curled, and swaggering
with studied airs, wearing golden collars, seated on a golden chair, putting
on a solemn face, speaking solemnly, thinking highly, 64a swaggering at the
multitude of escorts, of followers, of flatterers whom he drags along, of
parasites about the costly tables, at the acclamations and greetings which all
offer him about the marketplace, some rising from the benches, others coming
to meet him, others going before, each displaying something else of the things
toward his honor and glory; when you see these rulers led forth under a
herald, with a high voice swaggering against this one, scraping that one,
confiscating another and handing that one over to death, do not think the
things seen great, do not let your mind fall nor let the appearance astonish
you. Consider that God formed man, having taken dust from the earth. And if
what is seen is something other than man, fear and wonder at the things being
done, but if the one displaying these things is dust from the earth, despise
him. And God formed man. The voice He formed at once intimates some technical
energy of God concerning man. 65a Did he then also craft our forming thus, as
those who form things of clay or arrange things of plaster or forge statues or
complete some other of the works of art, for whom the things of imitation
stand only as far as the surface, as we often see a man formed and showing
outwardly a character perhaps of anger and courage or of fear and cowardice,
and again a woman either in love or in shame, or some other passion attending
her nature which the molder, recognizing, has worked out? But the forming of
God is not such, but those very original characters of man the creative
energy, having gone to the depth, organized each within, completing in an
instant a mixture and blending of many and various and differing passions and
judgments into the same. Would that I had so much leisure as to show you in
detail and with accuracy the construction of man. For you would have learned
from yourself the wisdom and economy of God concerning you.

For in truth man is a small cosmos, and those who have honored him with this
name have aimed rightly. How many treatises, then, and by how many from of
old, have been spent upon this wonderful formed thing 66a. The physiologies of
the physicians, the observations of the trainers, that concerning proportion,
that concerning the symmetry of the members toward one another, that
concerning the multitude of much flesh; all these enter in together concerning
the forming of man. And from where is so great an account for me, so as to be
able to say with accuracy as many things as are contained in one voice, He
formed? The


things at hand, however, even if I do not say them, you know. For God formed
you, as you see, upright, and he has given you this forming as exceptional
beside all the remaining animals. Why? Because he was going to give you in
addition also the exceptional activity owed and necessary. All the irrational
animals are only cattle and toward what they have been born, toward this they
also have the construction. Such also is the sheep; for since it was born to
graze, it has the head also inclined downward, so as to look at the belly and
the things under the belly; for the end of well-being for them is filling of
belly and enjoyment of pleasure. But for man 67a the head from the forming
itself has run up toward the things above and has been set higher than all the
other parts of the body, so that it may look at the kinship above. Do not then
remake yourself into what is contrary to nature, nor having stooped scan the
earthy things, but continually look upon and mirror the heavenly things, where
also your citizenship has been prepared beforehand. Thus also your body has
been formed and the formed thing is a teaching of the end toward which you
have come to be. For you were not constructed upright so that your life might
be dragged upon the earth as of the creeping things, but so that you might
look at heaven and the God in it, nor so that you might pursue the brutish and
earthy enjoyment, but so that you might accomplish the heavenly citizenship in
the mind given to you. On this account: The eyes of the wise man are in his
head, says the wise Ecclesiastes. And for what reason are the eyes not about
the lower parts of the body but in the head? So that they may scan the things
in height. But the one who does not look at the high things but stoops toward
the earthy has brought down the eyes to the nature of a creeping thing, being
dragged upon the earth as those are. 68a The head, then, standing above the
whole body, lies upon the shoulders, not having sunk into them, so that it may
not be hidden, but resting upon a notable support and as it were some
platform, the upright of the neck. And in this the twin casts of the eyes have
been fixed. For the one was not sufficient, but two came to be helpers of one
another, so that the chance dissolution of the one might have the consolation
of the other unharmed. And otherwise the visual coming forth from one place is
altogether weaker, but the current contributed from two sources becomes more
vigorous. For the sight going forth from each side of the nose both rests upon
the middle partition of this and, coming forward to the front, the one is
united to the other, as from two certain channels going out from each sight as
from a spring and after a little being mixed one river is completed whenever
indeed it also becomes stronger or more active. And a proof of this is the old
men among humans, who see the things very near least of all, but see more
clearly the things standing farther apart from the eyes, since while the
visual is going forth dividedly from the beginning the sight of each is weak,
69a but after the meeting of both and union, as upon some flood being
multiplied, it becomes more vigorous and more effective concerning the taking
up of the visible things. And the guards about the eye are not few. A tunic
within, and this is not sufficient. For it was not possible for it to be
constructed thick; for it would have stood as an obstacle of the sight. And
the covering had to be transparent and light. Therefore one is translucent,
one is rare, the one crystal-like, the other horn-like, the one covered over
more solid, the one within rarer, so that it may not hinder the transmission,
the third again crystal-like, so that in it each may have the impression and
the translucence. A projection, the eyelid; 70a for this is a covering of the
eye and a shelter and as it were a dwelling. For the hand also could have
sheltered, but until that came up, the eye often was already dimmed. But now
the guard lies beside from there; for at the same time it perceived the harm
and put forward the covering. For on this account also the pupil of the eye is
for the most part unassailable by the harms coming from without, as sitting
under a curtain, the eyelid, and being kept almost alone of all the members in
us untouched. And there have been projected


before the eyelids also points of hairs. Why? So that the intertwining of
these may become more exact, the upper being fitted to the lower through such
hairs. For the hairs, being intertwined with one another, stand as some bonds;
then also they keep far off the small animals and do not allow the downy
things to approach the pupil, which is sensitive and able to be dimmed by
whatever happens. And there is again another guard lying above upon each of
the eyes through the hairs, at once granting seemliness by the outline, and at
once providing safety, 71a which is the brow projected from above, so that it
may direct the visual. And a proof of this: whenever you wish to spy something
from afar and, hollowing the hand, you stretch it over the brows, for what do
you do this? So that the part of the sight being carried off toward the above
may not be scattered in vain, being poured into the breadth of the air, but
being directed both from the support of the hand from below and from the
projection of the brows from above and being pushed together toward the front
it may have a more exact taking of the visible. On this account brows at once
direct the optic of the eyes, and at once hold back the sweat flowing down
from above and do not allow it to be poured upon the sense-organs and to
hinder the sights, as some partition sufficient toward the turning away of the
harm coming from without. What vinedresser is able so securely to enclose his
own vine and to throw around it such a fence that the place may stand entirely
unapproachable to the neighboring torrents, as God worked 72a the surrounding
of the brows for the guard of the eyes, outlining them in us crescent-wise,
stretched from one meeting at the nose toward each of the parts, so that the
sweat flowing around this way and that may provide no occupation to the
laboring man concerning the work at hand and to his hand going up continually
upon the eyes and wiping off the moisture being carried down, but at once the
sweat may flow off of itself being transferred to the sides on each part
through its own channels from above the brows, and at once the eyes may
accomplish their own activity unhindered.

If then we should wish to speak in detail also concerning the remaining
members of the body and to make distinct and hymn the wisdom of God upon each,
the day will entirely fail us. But you from one part alone have altogether
understood the infinitely powerful character of the divine providence
concerning man and of the technical economy. And send us, who are hastening
upon some necessary road, forward with prayers, so that having been brought
safely through quickly we may also render the debt of the things remaining by
the grace of the Lord who has ordered all things concerning us, who created us
by his grace; because to him is the glory unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
