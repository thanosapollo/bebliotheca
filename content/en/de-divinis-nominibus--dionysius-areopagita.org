<15> Love, whether we call it divine or angelic or intellective or psychic or

<16> Since we have arranged the many loves from the one, saying in sequence of
what sort are the knowledges and powers of the encosmic and hypercosmic loves,
which the orders and arrays of the intellective and intelligible loves surpass
according to the assigned aim of the discourse, after which the
self-intellective and divine loves of the things truly beautiful there stand
over, and have been hymned by us in a manner proper to us. Now again taking
them all up into the one and folded-together love and father of them all, let
us both convolute and gather them from the many first into two, contracting it
into erotic powers in general, of which the uncontained cause of every love
from the beyond of all altogether prevails and pre-begins, and toward which
the total love from all beings stretches up connaturally for each of the
beings.

<17> Come then, gathering these again into one, let us say that there is one
simple power, the self-moving toward a certain unifying mixture, from the Good
as far as the last of beings and from that again in sequence through all into
the Good, from itself and through itself and upon itself circling itself and
always unfolding itself into itself in the same way.

<18> And yet someone might say: If the beautiful and good is beloved and
desired and lovable to all, for even non-being longs for it, as has been said,
and contends somehow to be in it, and it is the form-making even of the
formless, and upon it even non-being is said superessentially and is, how does
the demonic multitude not long for the beautiful and good, but being
matter-bound and having fallen away from the angelic identity concerning the
desire of the Good, is cause of all evils both to itself and to the others, as
many as are said to be made evil? And how altogether was the demonic tribe,
led forth from the Good, not good-formed, or how was a good that came to be
from the Good altered? And what was it that made it evil, and what altogether
is the evil, and from what beginning did it subsist, and in which of the
beings is it? And how did the Good will to lead it forth, and how, having
willed, was it able? And if the evil is from another cause, what other cause
is there for beings besides the Good? And how, providence existing, is there
the evil, either coming to be at all or not being taken away, and how does
something of the beings long for it besides the Good?

<19> These things then perhaps such a perplexed discourse will say, but we
will think it worthy to look toward the truth of the things, and first we will
be bold to say this: The evil is not from the Good, and if it is from the
Good, it is not evil, for neither is it of fire to cool nor of good to lead
forth things that are not good. And if all beings are from the Good, for it is
nature for the good to lead forth and save, but for the evil to destroy and
ruin, nothing of the beings is from the evil. And not even the evil itself
will be, if indeed it should be evil even to itself. And if not this, the evil
is not altogether evil, but has some portion of the Good, according to which
it altogether is. And if the beings long for the beautiful and good and all
things, as many as they do, they do through the seeming good, and every aim of
the beings has beginning and end the Good, for nothing looking toward the
nature of the evil does the things it does, how will the evil be in the
beings, or altogether being, having been deprived of such a good appetite? And
if all beings are from the Good and the Good is beyond the beings, even
non-being is being in the Good, but the evil is neither being, and if not, it
is not altogether evil, nor non-being, for there will be nothing that is
universally non-being, unless it should be said in the Good according to the
superessential. The Good then will be seated far prior both to simple being
and to non-being. But the evil is neither in the things that are nor in the
things that are not, but is more alien even than non-being itself, standing
away from the Good and more unsubstantial.


Whence then is the evil? someone might say. For if the evil is not, virtue and
vice are the same, and the whole to the whole and that in part to the
corresponding, or not even that which fights against virtue will be evil. And
yet temperance and intemperance are contrary, and justice and injustice. And I
do not mean, to be sure, according to the just man and the unjust and the
temperate and the intemperate, but even before the outwardly appearing
divergence of the virtuous toward the opposed, in the soul itself far prior
the vices have altogether stood apart from the virtues, and the passions
revolt toward reason, and from these it is necessary to give some evil
contrary to the good. For the Good is not contrary to itself, but as from one
beginning and offspring of one cause it rejoices in communion and unity and
friendship. And not even the lesser good is contrary to the greater, for
neither is the less hot or cold contrary to the more. There is then in the
beings, and it is being, and has been set against and made contrary to the
Good, the evil. And if there is destruction of beings, this does not cast the
evil out of being, but it too will be being and generative of beings. Or is
not the destruction of this often becoming of that? And the evil will be
contributing to the completion of the all and providing through itself that
the whole not be incomplete.

<> But the true discourse will say toward these things that the evil, as evil,
makes no substance or generation, but only makes evil and destroys, as far as
is in it, the subsistence of the beings. And if someone should say it is
generative and by its destruction gives generation to the other, one must
answer truly: Not as it destroys does it give generation, but as destruction
and evil it only destroys and makes evil, but generation and substance come to
be through the good, and the evil will be destruction through itself, but
generative through the good, and as evil neither being nor productive of
beings, but through the good both being and being good and productive of
goods. Or rather not even will the same according to the same be both good and
evil, nor is the same power according to the same destruction and generation
of the same, nor self-power or self-destruction.
The self-evil then is neither being nor good nor generative nor productive of
beings and goods, but the good, in whichever things it comes to be perfectly,
makes perfect and unmixed and complete goods, but the things that participate
it less are also incomplete goods and mixed through the lack of the good. And
the evil is not universally either good or good-making, but that which
approaches the good more or less will analogously be good, since the
all-complete goodness that goes through all does not proceed only as far as
the all-good substances around it, but stretches even as far as the last,
being present to some wholly, to others subordinately, to others lastly, as
each of the beings is able to participate it.
And some participate the good altogether, others have been deprived more and
less, others have a dimmer communion of the good, and to others the Good is
present according to a last echo. For if the Good were not present analogously
to each, the most divine and eldest would have the rank of the last. And how
also was it possible for all to participate the good uniformly, not all being
similarly fit for its total participation?
Now this is "the exceeding greatness" of the power of the good, that it even
empowers the things deprived, and its own deprivation, according to
participating it at all. And if one must speak the truths boldly: Even the
things that fight against it both are and are able to fight by its power.
Or rather, that I may say it in a gathering, all beings, insofar as they are,
are also goods and from the Good, but insofar as they have been deprived of
the good, they are neither goods nor beings. For upon the other habits, as of
heat or cold, there are the things heated or cooled even when the heat and the
cold have left them, and many of the beings are without share of life and
intellect. And God is transcended from substance and is superessentially. And
simply, upon all the other things, even when the habit has altogether gone
away or has not even come to be, the beings are and are able to subsist, but
that which has been deprived of the good in every way is nowhere in no way; it
neither was nor is nor will be nor is able to be. As the intemperate, even if
he has been deprived of the Good according to the


irrational desire, in this he neither is nor desires beings, but he
nevertheless participates the Good according to the dim echo itself of union
and friendship.
And spirit participates the Good according to the being-moved itself and
longing to set upright and turn back the seeming evils toward the seeming
beautiful. And even he who longs for the worst life, as longing for life at
all and for that which seems best to him, according to the longing itself and
longing for life and looking toward a best life, participates the Good.
And if you take away the Good altogether, there will be neither substance nor
life nor desire nor movement nor anything else. So that even becoming
generation from destruction is not a power of evil, but presence of a lesser
good, insofar as also disease is a lack of order, not of all. For if this
should come to be, not even the disease itself will subsist. But the disease
remains and is, having as substance the least order and being parasitically in
it. For that which is altogether without share of the good is neither being
nor in the beings, but the mixed, through the good, is in the beings and
according to this in the beings and being, insofar as it participates the
good. Or rather all beings will be more and less by so much, insofar as they
participate the good, for even upon being itself that which is nowhere in no
way being will not even be. But that which is being in a way and not being in
a way, insofar as it has fallen from the always being, is not, but insofar as
it has taken share of being, by so much it is, and even its not-being is held
together and saved.
And the evil that has altogether fallen from the good will be neither in the
more nor in the less goods. But that which is good in a way and not good in a
way fights against some good, but not against the Good as a whole. And it
itself also is mastered by the communion of the good, and the good even
substantiates its own deprivation by the participation of it at all. For if
the good has gone away altogether, there will be neither anything good
universally nor mixed nor self-evil.
For if the evil is incomplete good, by total absence of the good both the
incomplete and the perfect good will be away. And then only will the evil be
and be seen, when it is evil to some, to whom it has been made contrary, and
depends on others as goods. For it is impossible for the same things according
to the same to fight against one another in all. The evil then is not being.

<21> But neither is the evil in the beings. For if all the beings are from the
Good, and the Good is in all the beings and contains all, either the evil will
not be in the beings or it will be in the Good. And indeed it will not be in
the Good, for neither is the cold in fire, nor being-made-evil in that which
also makes the evil good. And if it will be, how will the evil be in the Good?
If from it, this is absurd and impossible. For "a good tree cannot," as the
truth of the oracles says, "make evil fruits," nor indeed the reverse. And if
not from it, then clearly from another beginning and cause. For either the
evil will be from the good or the good from the evil or, if this is not
possible, both the good and the evil will be from another beginning and cause.
For every dyad is not a beginning, but a monad will be beginning of every
dyad.
And yet it is absurd that two things altogether contrary should proceed from
one and the same and be, and that the beginning itself not be simple and
unified, but divisible and two-formed and contrary to itself and altered. And
indeed it is not possible that there be two contrary beginnings of the beings
and these in one another and in the all and fighting. For if this should be
granted, God also will not be unharmed nor outside of difficulty. If indeed
there should be something even for him that troubles, then all things will be
unordered and always fighting. And yet the Good imparts friendship to all the
beings and is hymned as self-peace and peace-giver by the sacred theologians.
Wherefore also the goods are friends and all things harmonious and offspring
of one life and ordered together toward one good and gentle and like and
kindly to one another. So that the evil is not in God, and the evil is not
in-God.
But neither is the evil from God. For either he is not good, or he makes good
and leads forth goods, and not sometimes and some, and sometimes not and not
all, for in this he will suffer change and alteration even concerning the
cause itself, the most divine of all. And if the Good is existence in God, the
God who changes from the Good will be sometimes being, sometimes not being.
And if he has the good by participation, he will have it also from another and
will sometimes have it, sometimes not have it. The evil then is not from God
nor in God nor


simply nor according to time.

<22> But neither is the evil in angels. For if the good-formed angel announces
the divine goodness, being that by participation secondarily which the thing
announced is by cause primarily, the angel is "image" of God, a manifestation
of the unmanifest light, a pure "mirror," most translucent, unharmed,
undefiled, "spotless," receiving the whole, if it is lawful to say, beauty of
the good-typed God-form and shining back unmixedly in himself, as far as is
possible, the goodness of the silence in the inmost shrines. The evil then is
not even in angels.
But they are evil by punishing those who sin. By this account then even the
chastisers of those who go wrong are evil, and of the priests those who keep
the profane away from the divine mysteries. And yet not even to be punished is
evil, but to have become worthy of punishment, nor to be kept away from the
sacred things according to desert, but to have become accursed and unholy and
unfit for the undefiled.

<23> But neither are the demons evil by nature. For if they are evil by
nature, they are neither from the Good nor in the beings, nor indeed did they
change from goods, being evil by nature and always.
Then are they evil to themselves or to others? If to themselves, they also
destroy themselves; but if to others, how destroying or what destroying:
substance or power or activity? If substance, first it is not contrary to
nature, for they do not destroy the things incorruptible by nature, but the
things receptive of destruction. Then not even this is evil to every and
altogether. But neither is anything of the beings destroyed, according as it
is substance and nature, but by the lack of the order according to nature the
account of harmony and symmetry is too weak to remain holding in the same way.
But the weakness is not total, for if total, it would have taken away both the
destruction and the underlying, and such a destruction will be destruction
even of itself. So that such a thing is not evil, but lacking good. For that
which is altogether without share of the good will not even be in the beings,
and concerning destruction into power and activity the same account.
Then how are the demons that came to be from God evil? For the good leads
forth and substantiates goods. And yet they are called evil, someone might
say, but not according as they are, for they are from the Good and obtained a
good substance, but according as they are not, having been too weak, as the
oracles say, to keep "their own beginning." For in what, tell me, do we say
the demons are made evil, if not in the ceasing of the habit and activity of
the divine goods?
Otherwise, if the demons are evil by nature, they are always evil. And yet the
evil is unstable. Therefore, if they always hold in the same way, they are not
evil, for the always the same is proper to the good. And if they are not
always evil, they are not evil by nature, but by lack of the angelic goods.
And they are not altogether without share of the good, according as they also
are and live and intellect and there is altogether some movement of desire in
them.
And they are said to be evil through being weak concerning the activity
according to nature. The evil then is for them a turning-aside and an exit
from the things proper to them and a missing and incompleteness and inability
and weakness of the power that saves the completeness in them and a
fleeing-away and a falling-away.
Otherwise what is the evil in demons? Irrational spirit, mindless desire,
reckless imagination. But these, even if they are in demons, are not
altogether nor upon all nor themselves by themselves evil. For even upon other
animals it is not the relation of these, but the taking-away and destruction
that is evil for the animal. But the relation saves and makes to be the nature
of the animal that has these.
The demonic tribe then is not evil as it is according to nature, but as it is
not. And the whole good given to them has not been altered, but they
themselves have fallen away from the whole good given. And the angelic gifts
given to them, we never say that they have been altered, but they are both
complete and all-shining, even if they themselves do not see, having closed
their own good-seeing powers.
So that what they are, they both are from the Good and are good and long for
the beautiful and good, longing for the being and living and intellecting of
the beings. And by the deprivation and fleeing-away and falling-away from the
goods proper to them they are called evil. And they are evil, according as
they are not. And longing for non-being they long for the evil.


<24> But would someone say that souls are evil? If because they keep company
with evils providentially and savingly, this is not evil, but good and from
the Good that also makes the evil good. But if we say that souls are made
evil, in what are they made evil, if not in the lack of the good habits and
activities and by their own weakness in missing and slipping? For we also say
that the air around us has been darkened by lack and absence of light. But the
light itself is always light, that which also lights the darkness. Neither
then
in demons nor in us is the evil as being evil, but as lack and desolation of
the completeness of the proper goods.

<25> But neither is the evil in irrational animals. For if you take away
spirit and desire and the other things, as many as are said and are not simply
by their own nature evil, the lion, having lost the stout and proud, will not
even be a lion, and the dog, having become gentle to all, will not be a dog,
if indeed it is of a dog to be watchful and to admit the proper and drive away
the alien.
So that for the nature not to be destroyed is not evil, but destruction of
nature is weakness and lack of the natural habits and activities and powers.
And if all things through generation have the complete in time, not even the
incomplete is altogether contrary to every nature.

<26> But neither is the evil in the whole nature. For if all the natural
accounts are from the universal nature, there is nothing contrary to it. But
to each according to each there will be the according to nature, and the not
according to nature. For another thing is contrary to nature for another, and
what is according to nature here is contrary to nature there. And vice of
nature is the contrary to nature, the deprivation of the things of the nature.
So that nature is not evil, but this is evil for the nature, to be unable to
complete the things of its own nature.

<27> But neither is the evil in bodies. For ugliness and disease are lack of
form and deprivation of order. And this is not altogether evil, but less
beautiful. For if there should come to be a total dissolution of beauty and
form and order, the body itself also will be gone. And that the body is not
even a cause of vice for the soul is clear from its being possible for vice to
subsist parasitically even without body, as in demons. For this is evil for
intellects and souls and bodies, the
weakness and falling-away of the habit of the proper goods.

<28> But neither is the much-spoken: The evil is in matter, as they say,
according as it is matter. For even this has communion of cosmos and beauty
and form. And if matter, being outside of these, is by itself qualityless and
formless, how does matter make anything, which does not even have the being
able to suffer by itself? Otherwise how is matter evil? For if it is nowhere
in no way, it is neither good nor evil. And if it is being somehow, and all
the beings are from the Good, it too would be from the good, and either the
good is productive of the evil or the evil as being from the good is good or
the evil is productive of the good or even the good as from the evil is evil
or again two beginnings, and these
hanging from another one summit.
And if they say matter is necessary toward completion of the whole cosmos, how
is matter evil? For the evil is one thing and the necessary another. And how
does the Good lead forth some things from the evil toward generation? Or how
is that which needs the good evil? For the evil flees the nature of the good.
And how does matter, being evil, generate and nourish nature? For the evil, as
evil, is generative or nutritive or altogether productive or saving of
nothing.
And if they should say that it does not make vice in souls, but draws them,
how will this be true? For many of them look toward the good. And yet how did
this come to be if matter was altogether drawing them toward the evil? So that
the evil in souls is not from matter, but from unordered and erring movement.
And if they say that this also altogether follows matter, and the unstable
matter is necessary for those unable to be seated upon themselves, how is the
evil necessary or the necessary evil?

<29> But neither is this, which we say: The deprivation fights against the
good according to its own power. For the total deprivation is universally
powerless, and the partial has the power not according as it is deprivation,
but according as it is not total deprivation. For while there is a partial
deprivation of the good it is not yet evil, and when it has come to be even
the nature of the evil has gone away.


<30> And to speak in a gathering: The good is from one and the whole cause,
but the evil from many and partial lacks. God knows the evil as good, and with
him the causes of the evils are good-making powers.
If the evil is everlasting and crafts and is able and is and acts, whence are
these for it? Either from the good or for the good from the evil or for both
from another cause?
Everything according to nature is generated from a defined cause. If the evil
is uncaused and undefined, it is not according to nature, for neither is the
contrary to nature in the nature, nor is there an account of unskill in the
art. Is then the soul cause of the evils just as fire of heating, and does it
fill with vice all things to which it may neighbor?
Or is the nature of soul good, but in the activities it sometimes holds thus,
sometimes thus? If by nature even its being is evil, even whence is being for
it? Either from the good cause that crafts all beings? But if from this, how
is it evil according to substance? For all things are offspring of this as
goods.
And if in the activities, not even this is unchangeable. And if not, whence
are the virtues if it is not also becoming good-formed? It remains then that
the evil is weakness and lack of the good.

<31> The cause of the goods is one. If the evil is contrary to the good, the
causes of the evil are many. Not indeed are the productive things of the evils
accounts and powers, but inability and weakness and incommensurate mixture of
the unlike.
Neither are the evils unmoved and always holding in the same way, but infinite
and undefined and carried in others and these infinite. Of all things even of
the evils beginning and end will be the good, for all things are for the sake
of the good, both as many as are goods and as many as are contrary, for even
these we do longing for the good, for no one looking toward the evil does the
things he does. Wherefore neither does the evil have hypostasis, but a
parasitic subsistence coming to be for the sake of the good and not of itself.

<32> Being is to be set down for the evil according to accident and through
another and not from a proper beginning. So that what comes to be seems indeed
to be right, because it comes to be for the sake of the good, but in being is
not right, because we think the not-good good.
It has been shown that the desired is one thing and the thing coming to be
another. Therefore the evil is beside the way and beside the aim and beside
the nature and beside the cause and beside the beginning and beside the end
and beside the limit and beside the will and beside the hypostasis. The evil
then is deprivation and lack and weakness and incommensurability and missing
and aimless and unbeautiful and lifeless and mindless and irrational and
incomplete and unsettled and uncaused and undefined and barren and idle and
inactive and unordered and unlike and infinite and dark and unsubstantial and
itself nowhere in no way nothing being.
How is the evil able to do anything at all by mixture toward the good? For
that which is altogether without share of the good neither is anything nor is
able. For if the good is both being and willed and in-power and active, how
will that contrary to the Good be able to do anything, that which has been
deprived of substance and will and power and activity? Not all things are the
same evils to all and altogether according to the same. For a demon, evil is
to be beside the good-formed intellect; for a soul, beside reason; for a body,
beside nature.

<33> How altogether are there evils, providence existing? The evil is not, as
evil, either being or in the beings. And nothing of the beings is
unprovided-for, for neither is the evil being existing unmixed of the good.
And if nothing of the beings is without share of the good, and evil is the
lack of the good, and nothing of the beings has been deprived universally of
the good, the divine providence is in all the beings, and nothing of the
beings is unprovided-for. But even the evils that come to be providence uses
in a manner befitting the good toward their or others' either particular or
common benefit and provides properly for each of the beings.
Wherefore we will not accept the random account of the many, who say that
providence ought to lead even us unwilling toward virtue, for to destroy
nature is not of providence. Whence as providence saving of the nature of each
it provides for the self-moved as self-moved and of the wholes and of each
properly for whole and each, insofar as the nature of the things provided for
admits the providential


goodnesses of the whole and manifold providence given out analogously to each.

<34> The evil then is not being, nor is the evil in the beings. For nowhere is
the evil, as evil. And the coming-to-be of the evil is not according to power,
but through weakness. And for the demons, what they are is both from the Good
and good. But the evil for them is from the falling-away of the proper goods,
and alteration is the weakness around identity and habit of the
angel-befitting completeness proper to them. And they long for the good,
according as they long for being and living and intellecting. And if they do
not long for the good, they long for non-being. And this is not desire, but a
missing of the truly desire.

<35> And the oracles call those who sin in knowledge those who are too weak
concerning the unforgettable knowledge of the good or the doing, and those who
have known "the will" and do not do it, those who have heard but are too weak
concerning the faith or the activity of the good. And for some "to understand
to do good" is unwilled according to the turning-aside or the weakness of the
will.
And altogether the evil, as we have often said, is weakness and inability and
lack either of the knowledge or of the unforgettable knowledge or of the faith
or of the desire or of the activity of the good. And yet someone might say:
Weakness is not punishable, but on the contrary pardonable. If it were not
possible to be able, the account would hold well. But if the being able is
from the Good that gives according to the oracles the things proper simply to
all, the missing and turning-aside and fleeing-away and falling-away of the
habit of the proper goods from the Good is not praiseworthy. But let these
things have been said by us sufficiently according to power in the On just and
divine judgment, according to which sacred treatise the truth of the oracles
struck the sophistic and those speaking injustice and falsehood against God as
deranged accounts.
But now as sufficient according to us the Good has been hymned as truly
admirable, as beginning and limit of all, as encompassing of the beings, as
form-making of the non-beings, as cause of all goods, as uncaused of the
evils, as providence and goodness all-complete and exceeding the beings and
non-beings and making good the evils and its own deprivation, desired and
beloved and lovable to all and as many other things as in the things before
the true discourse showed, as I think.

<1> And one must now pass over to the truly being substance-name of the truly
being, the divine-name. But let us remind of this much, that the aim for the
discourse is not to manifest the superessential substance as superessential,
for this is unspeakable and unknown and altogether unmanifest and lifting even
union itself above, but to hymn the substance-making procession into all the
beings of the thearchic substance-rule.
For the divine-name of the Good, manifesting the whole processions of the
cause of all, stretches both into the beings and into the non-beings and is
above the beings and above the non-beings. But that of being stretches into
all the beings and is above the beings. And that of life stretches into all
the living and is above the living. And that of wisdom stretches into all the
intellective and rational and sensitive and is above all these.

of the providence. For it does not profess to express the self-superessential
goodness and substance and life and wisdom of the self-superessential Godhead
seated above in hidden things, as the oracles say, above every goodness and
Godhead and substance and life and wisdom, but it hymns the manifested
good-making providence, as goodness by transcendence and cause of all goods,
and being and life and wisdom, the substance-making and life-making and
wisdom-giving cause of those that have taken share of substance and life and
intellect and reason and sense.
And it does not say that the Good is another thing and being another and life
or wisdom another, nor that the causes are many and productive Godheads of
others, overlying and subordinate, but the whole good processions of one God
and the divine-names hymned by us, and that the one is


manifestative of the all-complete providence of the one God, and the others of
the more total and more partial of the same.

<3> And yet someone might say: Instead of what, life stretching beyond being
and wisdom beyond life, do the living of the beings, and of as many as live
the sensitive, and of these the rational, and of the rational the intellects,
overlie and are about God and approach him more? And yet those participating
the greater gifts from God ought also to be stronger and to overlie the
remaining.
But if someone were hypothesizing the intellective as unsubstantial and
lifeless, the account would hold well. But if the divine intellects both are
above the remaining beings and live above the other living and intellect and
know above sense and reason and long for and participate the beautiful and
good beyond all the beings, they themselves are more about the Good who
participate it excessively and have received more and greater gifts from it,
just as also the rational overlie the sensitive, having advantage by the
excess of reason, and these by sense and others by life. And this is, as I
think, true, that the things that participate more the one and
infinitely-giving God are more near to him and more divine than those left
behind.

<4> Since we have spoken also concerning these, come, let us hymn the Good as
truly being and substance-making of all the beings. The One Who Is is
according to power superessential substantiating cause of the whole of being

and measure of ages and of times, beingness and age of the beings, time of the
things coming to be, the being for those that are in any way, generation for
those that come to be in any way.
From the Being are age and substance and being and time and generation and
thing coming to be, the beings in the beings and the things existing and
subsisting in any way. For God is not being somehow, but simply and
unlimitedly having gathered and pre-taken in himself the whole of being.
Wherefore he is also called king of the ages as in him and about him of every
being and thing that is and has subsisted, and he neither was nor will be nor
came to be nor comes to be nor will come to be, or rather he not even is. But
he himself is the being for the beings, and not only the beings, but even
being itself of the beings is from the one who is before the ages, for he
himself is the age of ages, "he who exists before the ages."

is from the pre-being. And every age and time is from him, and of every age
and time and of every thing that is in any way the pre-being is beginning and
cause, and all things participate him, and he stands away from none of the
beings "and he is before all, and all things have been put together in him,"
and simply, if anything is in any way, it both is and is conceived and is
saved in the pre-being, and before the other participations of him being has
been put forth, and being itself by itself is elder than being self-life and
being self-wisdom and being divine self-likeness, and the others, of as many

being of which the self-being is not substance and age.
Of all the others then fittingly more beginning-like as Being God is hymned
from the elder of his other gifts. For having and exceeding pre-being and
super-being, he pre-substantiated being all, I mean being itself by itself,
and by being itself he substantiated every thing that is in any way. And
indeed all the beginnings of the beings, participating being, both are and are
beginnings and first are, then are beginnings.
And if you wish to say the beginning of the living as living is self-life and

participation of being, much more

more. And simply, insofar as they are nearer to the center, by so much they
have been unified both to it and to one another, and insofar as from it, by so
much they have also stood apart from one another.

<7> But also in the whole nature of the wholes all the accounts of the nature
according to each have been gathered according to one unconfused union, and in
the soul in a monad-formed way the providential powers of all the things in

Nothing then is absurd, having gone up from dim images to the cause of all, to
contemplate with hypercosmic eyes all things in the cause of all, even the

beginning of the beings, from which is even being itself and all things that

<VIII.>

<1> But since the theologians hymn the divine truth and super-wise wisdom also
as "power" and as "justice" and call it salvation and redemption, come, let us
also unfold, as attainable for us, these divine-names. And that the thearchy
has been transcended from every and exceeds the power that is and is conceived
in whatever way, I do not think anyone of those reared in the divine oracles
is ignorant. For in many places theology has handed down assigning to it even
the lordship of the superheavenly powers themselves. How then do the
theologians hymn it also as power, that which has been transcended from every
power? Or how upon it are we to take the power-name?

<2> Let us say then that God is power as pre-having and exceeding every power
in himself and as cause of every power and leading forth all according to
unswerving and unlimited power and as being cause of even the being power
either the whole or that according to each and as infinitely-powered not only
by leading forth every power, but also by being above every even the


self-power and by being super-able and leading forth infinitely times infinite
other powers of the powers that are, and by the infinite and unto infinity
led-forth powers never being able to blunt his super-infinite making of the
power-making power, and by the unspeakable and unknown and unconceivable of
his power that exceeds all, or through abundance of the able he even empowers
the weakness and holds together and holds fast the last of its echoes, just as
also upon the able according to sense we see that the super-shining lights
reach even as far as the dull seeings, and they say the great of the noises
enter even into the hearings that do not take hold of the sounds very easily.
For that which is altogether unhearing is not even hearing, and that which
altogether does not see is not even sight.

<3> This then infinitely-powered distribution of God proceeds into all the
beings, and there is nothing of the beings which has been altogether taken
away from having some power, but it has either intellective or rational or
sensitive or vital or substantial power, and even being itself, if it is
lawful to say, has power into being from the superessential power.

<4> From it are the God-formed powers of the angelic arrays, from it they also
have the being unfallenly and all their intellective and immortal
ever-movements and the unswerving itself and the undiminished desire of the
good they have received from the infinitely-good power, it sending upon them
the being able both to be these and to long always to be and the being able
itself to long for the always being able.

<5> And the things of the unfailing power proceed also into men and animals
and plants and the whole nature of the all and empower the unified toward the
friendship and communion of one another and the distinguished toward being
according to the proper account and limit each unconfused and unmingled and
save the orders and good-arrangements of the all into the proper good and keep
unharmed the immortal lives of the angelic henads and make unalterable the
heavenly and light-bearing and starry substances and orders and make the age
able to be and distinguish the convolutions of time by the processions, but
gather them by the restorations, and make the powers of fire unquenchable and
the inflows of water unfailing and define the aerial pouring and seat the
earth upon nothing and keep undestroyed its life-generating birth-pangs and
save unconfused and undivided the harmony and mixture of the elements in one
another and hold together the binding of soul and body and stir up the
nutritive and increasing powers of the plants and hold fast the substantial
powers of the wholes and make safe the undissolved remaining of the all and
grant even deification itself, providing power into this for those being
deified. And altogether there is nothing universally of the beings that has
been taken away from the all-ruling safety and encompassing of the divine
power. For that which universally has no power neither is nor is anything nor
is there any position of it altogether.

<6> And yet Elymas the magician says: If God is all-powerful, how is he said
not to be able something by the theologian according to you? And he reviles
the divine Paul who said that God is not able "to deny himself." And putting
this forth I exceedingly fear lest I even incur a laugh of folly, attempting
to dissolve buildings of playing children both upon sand and weak, and as it
were hastening to aim at some unattainable mark of the theological meaning
concerning this.
For the denial of himself is a falling-out of truth, and the truth is being
and the falling-out of truth is falling-out of being. If then the truth is
being, and the denial of truth is falling-out of being, God is not able to
fall out of being, and the not-being is not, as someone might say, the not
being able is not able and the not knowing according to deprivation he does
not know. Which the wise not conceiving imitates the infinitely-victorious of
the athletes, who often hypothesizing the antagonists to be weak according to
what seems to themselves and fighting the shadow toward them bravely while
they are absent and striking the air confidently with empty blows think they

And if we say these things rightly, as many as revile the divine justice

other-moved necessity in the self-moved and identity in the alterable and the

divine justice being in this truly true justice, that it assigns to all the
proper things according to the desert of each of the beings and saves the
nature of each upon its proper order and power.

<8> But someone might say: It is not of justice to let holy men be unaided,
worn down by the worthless. Toward whom one must say: That if those whom you
say are holy love the things upon earth zealously sought by the matter-bound,
they have altogether fallen out of the divine love, and I do not know how they
would be called holy, wronging the truly beloved and divine by the unzealous
and unbeloved things not piously preferred by them. But if they love the truly
beings, those who long for certain things must rejoice whenever they hit the
desired. Or do they not then approach more the angelic virtues, when, as
possible, by desire of the divine they withdraw from the passion for the
material, being exercised toward this very manfully in the circumstances over
the beautiful? So that it is true to say that this is more proper of the
divine justice, not to charm and destroy the manliness of the best by
distributions of the material, nor, if someone should attempt to do this, to
let them be unaided, but to seat them in the beautiful and unsoftened standing
and to assign to them, being such, the things according to desert.

<9> This then divine justice is also hymned salvation of the wholes, saving
and keeping the proper and pure from the others substance and order of each
and being purely cause of the proper-acting in the wholes. And if someone
should hymn the salvation also as snatching the wholes savingly from the
worse, we will altogether I suppose accept even this hymn-singer of the
manifold salvation and will think it worthy that he define this also as first
salvation of the wholes, that which saves all unchangeable upon themselves and
unrevolted and unswerving toward the worse and guards all unfightable and
unwarred, each arrayed by their own accounts, and drives out every inequality
and other-acting from the wholes and sets up the analogies of each unfallen
into the contraries and unmoved-away. Since even this salvation one would hymn
not away from the aim of the sacred theology as redeeming all the beings by
the saving goodness of all from the falling-away of the proper goods, insofar
as the nature of each of the saved admits.
Wherefore the theologians also name it "redemption," both insofar as it does
not let the truly beings fall through toward non-being and insofar as, even if
something should slip toward the erring and unordered and suffer some

the suffering and the inactivity and the deprivation, filling the lacking and
fatherly under-propping the slackness and raising from the evil, or rather
standing in the beautiful and filling up the good that has slipped under and
ordering and adorning its disorder and unadornment and making it complete and
releasing it from all the things that have been maimed.

pre-conceived of each one the unified are said to have been unified, and the
one is elemental of all. And if you take away the one, there will be neither
wholeness nor part nor anything else of the beings. For the one has pre-taken
and encompassed all in itself in a one-formed way. In this way then theology
hymns the whole thearchy as cause of all by the naming of the one, and "one
God the Father" and "one Lord Jesus Christ" and "one and the same Spirit"
through the exceeding undividedness of the whole divine unity, in which all
things have been gathered unitarily and super-unified and are present
superessentially.
Wherefore also all things are justly sent up and assigned upon it, by which
and from which and through which and in which and into which all things are
and have been ordered together and remain and are held together and are filled
up and are turned back. And you would not find anything of the beings which is
not by the one, according to which all the Godhead is named superessentially,
even this which it is, and is completed and saved.
And we also must, turning back from the many upon the one by the power of the
divine unity, hymn unifiedly the whole and one Godhead, the one cause of all,
that before every one and multitude and part and whole and limit and
undefinedness and bound and infinity, that which defines all the beings and
being itself and is of all and of wholes all and at once and before all and
above all both unitarily cause and being above the one itself and defining the
one itself being, since the being one that is among the beings is numbered,
and number participates substance. But the superessential one defines both the
being one and every number and it itself is beginning and cause and number and
order of one and of number and of every being.
Wherefore also being hymned monad and triad the Godhead above all is not even
a monad, nor a triad that has been known toward us or some other of the
beings, but in order that we may truly hymn both its super-unified and its
God-generating, we name the super-named by the triadic and unitary
divine-name, the superessential by the beings. But no monad or triad, nor
number nor unity or fertility nor anything else of the beings or of things
known together with some of the beings
leads out the hiddenness above all both reason and intellect of the
super-Godhead that superessentially super-is above all, nor is there a name of
it nor reason, but it has been transcended in untrodden things.


And not even that of goodness itself do we bring forward as fitting it, but by
longing to intellect something and to say concerning that unspeakable nature
we first dedicate to it the most august of the names. And we would agree even
in this with the theologians, but we will be left behind the truth of the
things. Wherefore they themselves also have preferred the going-up through the
denials as standing the soul out of the things connatural to itself and going
through all the divine intellections, from which "that above every name" and
every reason and knowledge has been transcended, and at the last of the wholes
joining it to it, insofar as even for us to be joined to that is possible.

<4> These intelligible divine-names we, having gathered, have unfolded as
attainable, not only being left behind their exactness, for this even angels
would truly say, nor of their hymn according to angels, for even of the last
the strongest of the theologians among us fall short, nor indeed of the
theologians themselves nor of their practitioners or companions, but even of
those of the same rank with us lastly and subordinately. So that if the things
said should hold rightly and as according to us we have truly touched with
thought the unfolding of the divine-names, the thing is to be assigned upon
the cause of all goods who grants first the saying itself, then the saying
well.
And if something of the equal-powered has been left aside, that also we will
need to understand additionally according to the same methods. And if these
things either do not hold rightly or incompletely, and we have wandered from
the truth either wholly or partially, it would be of your love of man to set
right him who is unwillingly ignorant and to impart account to him who needs
to learn and to aid him who does not have self-sufficient power and to heal
him who does not wish to be ill and finding some things from himself, some
from others, but taking all from the Good, to pass them through to us. And do
not grow weary doing good to a friend man. For you see that we also have not
drawn together into ourselves any of the hierarchical accounts handed down to
us, but we have both imparted them unadulterated to you and to other sacred
men and will impart, as we are able to say and those to whom it is said to
hear, wronging the tradition according to nothing, unless perhaps we shall be
too weak toward the intellection or the expression of them. But these things,
in whatever way it is dear to God, in that way let them both hold and be said,
and let this then be the limit according to us for the intelligible
divine-names. And upon the Symbolic Theology, God leading, I will pass over.
