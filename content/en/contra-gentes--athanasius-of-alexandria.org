* Against the Gentiles

Of our father among the saints Athanasius, archbishop of Alexandria Against
the Greeks
1 The knowledge concerning piety and concerning the truth of the all does not
so much need the teaching from men as it has what is knowable from itself; for
it all but cries out every day by the works, and it displays itself brighter
than the sun through the teaching of Christ. Yet since you long to hear the
things concerning it, come, O blessed one, as far as we are able, let us set
forth a few things of the faith according to Christ, you being able to find
this also from the divine oracles, yet loving the beautiful and hearing it
also from others. For the holy and God-inspired scriptures are sufficient for
the declaration of the truth; and there are also many discourses of our
blessed teachers composed upon these things; if anyone should meet with them,
he will know in some way the interpretation of the scriptures, and will be
able to attain the knowledge for which he longs. But since we do not now have
the compositions of the teachers in our hands, it is necessary that what we
learned from them, this also we declare and write to you; I mean the faith
according to the Savior Christ; so that no one may think the teaching of our
discourse cheap, nor suppose the faith toward Christ to be irrational; such
things as the Greeks, slandering, mock, and laugh broadly at us, bringing
forward nothing else than the cross of Christ; at which especially one might
pity their insensibility, because, slandering the cross, they do not see that
its power has filled all the inhabited world, and that through it the works of
the knowledge of God have been made manifest to all. For if they themselves
had genuinely applied their mind to his Godhead, they would not have mocked so
great a thing; but rather they themselves would have recognized this one as
Savior of the all, and that the cross has come to be not a harm but a healing
of the creation. For if, when the cross came to be, all idolatry was
overthrown, and all fantasy of demons is driven away by this sign, and Christ
alone is worshipped, and through him the Father is known, and those who
contradict are put to shame, and he of those who contradict daily persuades
their souls unseen; how (for one might reasonably say to them) is it still
possible to invent the matter as human, and not rather confess that the one
who went up upon the cross is Word of God and Savior of the all? And these too
seem to me to suffer something like as if someone should slander the sun when
it is covered by clouds, yet marvel at its light, seeing that all creation is
illuminated by it. For as the light is beautiful, and more beautiful is the
sun, the leader of the light; so, it being a divine matter that all the
inhabited world has been filled with his knowledge, of necessity the leader
and guide of such an achievement is God and Word of God. We speak then as far
as is reachable for us, first having refuted the ignorance of the unbelievers;
so that, the false things having been refuted, the truth may thereafter shine
through itself, and you too may take courage, O man, that you have believed in
truth, and knowing Christ you have not been deceived. And I think it fitting
for you, being a lover of Christ, that the things concerning Christ be
discussed, since I have also believed that you hold the knowledge and faith
concerning him more honorable than all things.
2 From the beginning evil was not; for neither even now is it among the
saints, nor does it exist at all according to them; but men later began to
invent it, and to form it against themselves; whence also they fashioned for
themselves the invention of the idols, reckoning the things that are not as
things that are. For the Fashioner of the all


and all-king God, who exists beyond all essence and human invention, being
good and more than beautiful, through his own Word, our Savior Jesus Christ,
made the human race according to his own image; and he constructed him as a
contemplator and knower of the things that are through the likeness toward
himself, giving him also thought and knowledge of his own eternity, so that,
keeping the identity, he might never stand off from the imagination concerning
God, nor leap away from the company of the saints, but, having the grace of
the one who gave, having also his own power from the paternal Word, he might
exult and converse with the Divine, living the unharmed and truly blessed
immortal life. For having no hindrance toward the knowledge concerning the
Divine, he always contemplates through his own purity the image of the Father,
the God Word, according to whose image he also came to be; and he is struck
with wonder as he considers the providence through him toward the all,
becoming above the sensibles and all bodily imagination, and being joined by
the power of the mind to the divine intelligibles in the heavens. For when the
mind of men does not converse with bodies, nor has mixed from outside any
desire from them, but is wholly above, being with itself as it came to be from
the beginning; then indeed, having passed the sensibles and all human things,
it becomes aloft above, and having seen the Word, it sees in him also the
Father of the Word, taking pleasure in the contemplation of this one, and
being renewed upon the longing toward him. Just as therefore they say that the
first of men who came to be, who also according to the tongue of the Hebrews
was named Adam, the holy scriptures say that according to the beginning he had
the mind toward God with unashamed boldness, and that he lived with the saints
in the contemplation of the intelligibles, which he had in that place which
the holy Moses also named tropically paradise. And the purity of the soul is
sufficient even to mirror God through itself, just as the Lord also says:
Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God.
3 Thus then the Fashioner, as has been said, constructed the race of men, and
willed that it remain; but men, having neglected the better things, and having
been sluggish concerning the grasp of them, sought rather the things nearer
themselves, and nearer to them was the body and its senses. Whence they stood
their mind off from the intelligibles, and began to consider themselves. And
considering themselves, and laying hold of the body and the other sensibles,
and being deceived as in their own, they fell into desire of themselves,
having preferred their own things to the contemplation toward the divine
things; and having lingered in these, and not wishing to stand off from the
nearer things, they shut their soul up with the pleasures of the body,
disturbed and mixed with all desires; and they wholly forgot the power from
God that was theirs from the beginning. And one might see this true also from
the first-formed man, as the holy scriptures say concerning him. For he too,
as long as he had the mind toward God and the contemplation of him, turned
away the contemplation toward the body; but when by the counsel of the serpent
he stood off from the thought toward God, and began to consider himself, then
they also fell into desire of the body, and they knew that they were naked,
and knowing they were ashamed. And they knew themselves naked not so much from
clothing, but because they had become naked of the contemplation of the divine
things, and they transferred the thought toward the opposite things. For
having stood off from the consideration toward the one and the one who is, I
mean God, and from the longing toward him, they thereafter entered upon
various and particular desires of the body. Then, as it is wont to happen,
having taken desire of each and of many things, they began also to have the
relation toward them; so as even to fear to leave these. Whence also
cowardices, and fears, and pleasures, and thinking mortal things have come to
be for the soul. For not wishing to stand off from the desires, it fears death
and the separation of the body.


And desiring again, and not obtaining the like things, it learned to murder
and to do injustice. And how it also does these things, it is reasonable to
indicate according to power.
4 Having stood off from the contemplation of the intelligibles, and misusing
the particular activities of the body, and having taken pleasure in the
contemplation of the body and having seen pleasure to be a beautiful thing for
itself, having been led astray it misused the name of the beautiful, and
thought pleasure to be the really beautiful itself; just as if someone, being
stricken in thought, and demanding a sword against those he meets, should
think this to be being temperate. And having fallen in love with pleasure, it
began to work it in various ways. For being by nature easily moved, even if it
turned away from the beautiful things, yet it does not cease from being moved.
It is moved then no longer according to virtue, nor so as to see God; but
reckoning the things that are not, it alters its own power, misusing this
toward the desires it invented, since it also came to be self-determined. For
it is able, just as to incline toward the beautiful things, so also to turn
away from the beautiful things; and turning away from the beautiful, it
altogether reckons the opposite things; for it cannot wholly cease from being
moved, being by nature, as I said before, easily moved. And knowing its own
self-determination, it sees itself able according to both to use the members
of the body toward the things that are and the things that are not; and the
things that are are the beautiful things, and the things that are not are the
base things. And I say the beautiful things are things that are, inasmuch as
they have the patterns from the God who is; and I say the evils are things
that are not, inasmuch as by inventions of men they have been fashioned, not
being. For the body having eyes for seeing the creation, and through this
all-harmonious composition knowing the Fashioner; and having also hearing for
listening to the divine oracles and the laws of God; and having also hands,
both for the activity of the necessary things and for the stretching out of
prayer toward God; the soul, having stood off from the contemplation toward
the beautiful things, and from the movement in them, thereafter being led
astray is moved toward the opposite things. Then seeing its own power, as I
said before, and misusing this, it thought that it can also move the members
of the body toward the opposite things; and for this reason, instead of seeing
the creation, it turns the eye toward desires, showing that it can do this
also and thinking that, once it is moved, it keeps its own dignity, and does
not sin in doing what it can; not knowing that it came to be not simply to be
moved, but to be moved toward the things that one ought; for this reason also
an apostolic voice charges: All things are lawful, but not all things are
expedient.
5 But the daring of men, having looked not toward the expedient and fitting,
but toward the possible, began to do the opposite things; whence, moving the
hands also toward the opposite, it made them murder, and it led the hearing
into disobedience, and the other members into committing adultery instead of
lawful childbearing; and the tongue instead of well-speaking into blasphemies
and insults and perjuries, and the hands again into stealing and striking like
men; and the smell into varieties of erotic odors; and the feet into swiftness
to pour out blood; and the belly into drunkenness and unfilled satiety; all of
which is evil and sin of soul. And there is no cause of these, but the turning
away from the better things. For as if a charioteer, having mounted horses in
a stadium, should despise the mark toward which it befits him to drive, and
having turned away from this, should simply drive the horse as he can; and he
can as he wishes; and often he rushes upon those he meets, and often he also
drives down cliffs, being carried wherever the swiftness of the horses might
carry him, thinking that running thus he has not missed the mark; for he looks
only toward the running, and does not see that he has come to be outside the
mark; so also the soul, having turned away from the way toward God, and
driving the members of the body beyond what is fitting, or rather itself also
with them being driven by itself, sins and fashions the evil for itself, not
seeing that it has wandered from the way, and has come to be outside the mark
of the truth, toward which the


Christ-bearing man, the blessed Paul, looking, said: I press according to a
mark, toward the prize of the upward calling of Jesus Christ; the holy one,
then, looking at the beautiful, never did the evil.
6 Some of the Greeks then, having wandered from the way, and not having known
Christ, declared that evil is in hypostasis and by itself, sinning according
to these two things: either stripping the Fashioner of being maker of the
things that are; for he would not be Lord of the things that are, if according
to them evil has hypostasis by itself and essence; or again, wishing him to be
maker of the all, they will of necessity give him to be of the evil also; for
among the things that are the evil also according to them is. But this would
appear absurd and impossible; for the evil is not from the beautiful, nor is
it in him, nor through him; since he would no longer be beautiful, having the
nature mixed, or becoming cause of evil. And those from the heresies, having
fallen out of the ecclesiastical teaching, and having been shipwrecked
concerning the faith, these also are beside themselves that there is a
hypostasis of the evil; and they fashion for themselves besides the true
Father of Christ another god, and this one unbegotten, maker of the evil and
leader of wickedness, who is also fashioner of the creation. And one might
easily refute these both from the scriptures and from the very thought in men,
from which also having fashioned these things they rave. Our Lord and Savior
Jesus Christ then in his own gospels says, confirming the words of Moses, that
the Lord God is one; and, I confess to you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth.
And if God is one, and this one is Lord of heaven and earth, how would there
be another god besides this one? And where also will the god according to them
be, the one and true God filling all things according to the encompassment of
heaven and earth? And how would there be another maker, of things of which God
himself and Father of Christ is Lord according to the voice of the Savior?
Unless perhaps they should say, as in equal standing, that the base one can
also become lord of the things of the good God. But if they say this, see into
how great impiety they fall; for among those able to do equal things the
superior and better would not be found. For if, the one not wishing it, the
other is; equal is the power of both and the weakness; equal, because they
conquer one another will in being; and weakness of both it is, because against
their will things turn out contrary to their judgment; for the good one is
against the judgment of the base, and the base one is against the will of the
good.
7 And otherwise; for one might also say this to them: if the things that
appear are works of the base one, what is the work of the good? For nothing
appears except only the creation of the fashioner. And what also is a mark of
the good one being, there not being works of his through which he might be
known? For from the works the fashioner is known. And how wholly would there
even be two opposite to one another, or what is that which divides them, so
that they may come to be apart from one another? For it is impossible for them
to be at once, because they are destructive of one another. But neither could
the one be in the other because of the unmixed and unlike character of their
nature. Therefore from a third the dividing one will appear, and this itself a
God. But of what nature would the third also be? Whether of the beautiful, or
of the base? It will appear unclear. For it to be of both is impossible. This
thought of theirs then appearing rotten, of necessity the truth of the
ecclesiastical knowledge must shine forth; that the evil is not from God nor
in God, nor did it come to be from the beginning, nor is there some essence of
it. But men, according to deprivation of the imagination of the beautiful,
began to invent for themselves and to fashion the things that are not, and
whatever they wish. For as if someone, the sun shining, and all the earth
being illuminated by this light, shutting the eyes, should invent darkness for
himself, there being no darkness, and thereafter as in darkness wandering


should walk, often falling and going down cliffs, thinking there is not light,
but darkness; for thinking he sees, he does not see at all; so also the soul
of men, having shut the eye through which it can see God, invented the evils
for itself, in which being moved, it does not know that thinking it does
something, it does nothing; for it fashions the things that are not. And it
did not remain such as it came to be; but such as it mixed itself, such it
also appears. For it came to be to see God and to be illuminated by him; but
it instead of God sought the corruptible things and the darkness, as somewhere
also the Spirit says in writing: God made man upright; but they sought many
reasonings. The finding and invention of evil then for men from the beginning
thus came to be and was fashioned. And how they also descended into the
madness of the idols, it is now necessary to say, so that you may know that
wholly the finding of the idols came to be not from a good, but from evil. And
that which has an evil beginning would never in anything be judged beautiful,
being wholly base.
8 The soul of men, not being satisfied with the invention of evil, began
little by little to lead itself out also into the worse things. For having
learned differences of pleasures and having girt on forgetfulness of the
divine things, and taking pleasure also toward the passions of the body and
looking only toward the present things and the opinions of these, it thought
there is no longer anything more than the things seen, but that only the
temporary and bodily things are the beautiful things. And having turned away
and forgotten that it itself is according to image of the good God, it no
longer through the power in it sees the God Word, according to whom it also
came to be; but having come to be outside itself, it reckons and forms the
things that are not. For having hidden by the entanglements of the bodily
desires the mirror as in itself, through which alone it was able to see the
image of the Father, it no longer sees what a soul ought to think; but it is
carried about everywhere, and sees only those things that fall upon the sense.
Whence then, being full of all fleshly desire, and being disturbed in the
opinions of these, thereafter, the God whom it forgot in thought, this one it
fashions in bodily and sensible things, assigning the appellation of God to
the things that appear, and glorifying only those things which it itself
wishes, and as it sees them pleasant. The leading cause of idolatry then is
evil. For men, having learned to invent for themselves the evil that is not,
thus also fashioned for themselves the gods that are not. And as if someone,
having sunk into a depth, should no longer see the light, nor the things that
appear in the light, because of the inclination of his eyes toward what is
below, and the overlaying flood of the water upon him; and perceiving only the
things in the depth, should think there is nothing more than those, but that
the things appearing to him are the principal of the things that are; so also
the men of old, being beside themselves, having sunk into the desires and
imaginations of the fleshes, and having forgotten the thought and glory
concerning God, using dim reasoning, or rather unreason, formed the things
that appear as gods, glorifying the creation besides the one who created, and
deifying the works rather than the Master God who is cause and fashioner of
them. And as according to the example spoken before, those who sink into the
depth, by how much more they go down, by so much they rush into the darker and
deeper things; so also the race of men has suffered. For they did not have
idolatry simple, nor did they remain in those things from which they began;
but by how much they lingered in the first things, by so much they invented
for themselves newer superstitions; and not taking satiety of the first
things, they were filled again with other evils, advancing in the most
shameful things, and stretching out their impiety more than themselves. And
this also the divine scripture testifies, saying: When an impious one comes
into a depth of evils, he despises. 9 For just now the thought of men leaped
away from God, and men, descending in thoughts and reasonings, first assigned
the honor of God to heaven and sun and moon and the stars, those not only


thinking them to be gods, but also to happen to be causes of the other things
after them; then, descending further in the dark reasonings, they named ether
and the air and the things in the air gods. And advancing in the evils, they
already also hymned as gods the elements, and the principles of the
composition of bodies, the hot and the cold and the dry and the wet essence.
And as those who have fallen completely crawl about the earth in the manner of
the snails on dry land; so the most impious of men, having fallen and fallen
down from the imagination concerning God, thereafter assigned also men and
forms of men, some still living, and some also after death, into gods. And
still counseling and reckoning even worse things, they already also
transferred the divine and supercosmic appellation of God onto stones, and
woods and reptiles, both water and land ones, and onto the untamed animals of
the irrational ones, assigning all honor of God to them, and turning away from
the true and really being God, the Father of Christ. And would that the daring
of the mindless ones had stood even as far as these, and that going further
they had not mixed themselves in the impieties. For some have fallen so far in
thought and have been darkened in mind, that they have invented for themselves
and deified even the things that do not exist at all in any way, nor appear
among the things that have come to be. For mixing rational things with
irrational, and stitching unlike things in nature, they worship them as gods;
such as are among the Egyptians the dog-headed and snake-headed and
ass-headed, and among the Libyans the ram-headed Ammon. And others, having
divided the parts of the bodies, head and shoulder and hand and foot by
themselves, assigned each into gods and deified them, as if not being
satisfied to have the worship from the whole body complete. And others,
stretching the impiety, having deified the pretext of the finding of these and
of their own evil, pleasure and desire, worship them; such as is among them
Eros, and Aphrodite in Paphos. And some of them, as if competing in the worse
things, dared to assign their own rulers or even the children of these into
gods, either through honor of those who ruled or through fear of their
tyranny; as the Zeus famous among them in Crete, and Hermes in Arcadia, and
among the Indians Dionysus, and among the Egyptians Isis, and Osiris, and
Horus, and now the darling of Hadrian the king of the Romans, Antinous, whom
although they know to be a man, and a man not solemn, but full of wantonness,
they worship through fear of the one who commanded. For Hadrian, having
sojourned in the country of the Egyptians, when Antinous the servant of his
pleasure died, commanded him to be worshipped, himself even after death loving
the boy, yet providing a refutation against himself, and a mark against all
idolatry, that this was not otherwise invented among men than through desire
of those who fashioned it, just as also the wisdom of God testifies
beforehand, saying: The beginning of fornication is the invention of idols.
And do not marvel nor think the thing said to be far from belief, since not
much earlier, or perhaps even until now, the senate of the Romans decrees
those who ever from the beginning ruled them as kings, either all, or whom
they themselves wish and judge, to be among gods, and they write them to be
worshipped as gods. For those with whom they are at enmity, these they confess
as enemies by nature, and they name them men; but those whom they have to
their mind, these they command to be worshipped through manly virtue, as if
having it in their authority to deify, themselves happening to be men, and not
denying that they are mortal. But those who deify ought rather themselves to
be gods; for that which makes must be better than that which is made, and the
one who judges of necessity rules the one judged, and the one who gives
altogether grants what he has; just as indeed also every king grants what he
has, and is better and greater than those who receive. If then they decree
those whom they themselves wish to be gods, they themselves also ought first
to be


gods. But this is the marvelous thing, that they themselves dying as men
refute their own vote concerning those deified by them to be false.
10 And this custom is not new, nor did it begin from the counsel of the
Romans, but it was happening from above and being practiced beforehand toward
the thought of the idols. For also the gods famous of old among the Greeks,
Zeus and Poseidon and Apollo and Hephaestus and Hermes, and among females Hera
and Demeter and Athena and Artemis, were judged to be called gods by the
ordinances of Theseus who is related among the Greeks; and those who ordained,
dying as men, are lamented; but those whom they ordained, these are worshipped
as gods. O much contrariety and madness. Knowing the one who ordained, they
prefer those whom he ordained. And would that the idol-madness of these had
stood as far as males, and they had not carried down the divine appellation
onto females. For even women, whom it is not even safe to take into common
counsel concerning affairs, these they worship and revere with the honor of
God, as those ordained by Theseus, as we have said before, and among the
Egyptians Isis and Kore and the Younger, and among others Aphrodite. For the
names of the others I think it not even holy to say, being full of all
mockery. For many not only in the old times, but also in the times according
to us, having lost dearest ones and brothers and kinsmen and wives, and many
women also having lost husbands, all of whom nature convicted to be mortal
men, these men and these women through the great mourning concerning them they
painted up, and having fashioned sacrifices, they assigned them, whom those
after these through the fashioning, and the ambition of the craftsman,
worshipped as gods, suffering a thing not according to nature. For those whom
the parents lamented as not being gods (for they would not, if they knew them
to be gods, have beaten themselves as for those who had perished; for this
reason, not only not thinking them to be gods, but not even to exist at all,
they formed them in an image, so that, seeing the seeming through the image of
their no longer being, they might be comforted), to these nonetheless the
mindless ones pray as to gods, and they put around them the honor of the true
God. In Egypt at any rate even until now the lament concerning Osiris and
Horus and Typhon and the others of the destruction is performed; and the
bronze things in Dodona, and the Corybantes in Crete, refute that Zeus is not
a god, but a man, and this one come to be from a father who eats raw flesh.
And the marvelous thing, that even the one very wise among the Greeks and
having boasted much as having thought concerning God, Plato, goes down to the
Piraeus with Socrates, to worship Artemis fashioned by art of a man.
11 And these and such findings of the idol-madness from above and long before
the scripture was teaching beforehand, saying: The beginning of fornication is
the invention of idols; and the finding of them is corruption of life. For
neither was it from the beginning, nor will it be unto the age. For by empty
glory of men it came into the world, and for this reason a short end of them
was invented. For a father, being worn by untimely mourning, having made an
image of the child quickly taken away, honored the then dead man now as
living, and handed over mysteries and rites to those under him. Then in time
the impious custom having been strengthened, it was guarded as law. And by
commands of tyrants the carved things were worshipped, whom men, not being
able to honor in sight, because they dwell far, having formed the far-off
sight, made a manifest image of the honored king, so that they might flatter
the absent one as present through the zeal. And toward intensification of
worship the ambition of the craftsman also urged on those who did not know;
for the one, perhaps wishing to please the one in power, forced by the art the
likeness toward the more beautiful; and the multitude, being drawn by the
grace of the work, reckoned the man honored a little before now a thing of
reverence; and this had come to be for life into a snare; because, either


having been enslaved to disaster or to tyranny, men put the incommunicable
name around stones and woods. Such then being the finding of the idols, with
the scripture as witness, having begun among men and having been fashioned, it
is time thereafter for you also to show the refutation against it, taking the
proofs not so much from outside, as from what these think concerning them. For
if someone should take the deeds of those called gods among them, so that I
may begin first from these lower ones, he will find them not only not to be
gods, but also to have been the most shameful of men. For what a thing, what a
thing it is to see the loves of Zeus among the poets, and the wantonnesses.
What a thing it is to hear him seizing Ganymede, and working the stolen
adulteries, and fearing and being cowardly lest against his judgment the walls
of the Trojans perish. What a thing it is to see him being pained at the death
of his son Sarpedon, and wishing to help him and not being able; and being
plotted against by the other so-called gods, I mean Athena and Hera and
Poseidon, and being helped by Thetis a woman and the hundred-handed Aegaeon;
and being conquered by pleasures, and being a slave to women, and through them
running risks in the imaginations among irrational animals both four-footed
and winged; and again himself being hidden because of the plot of the father,
and Cronus being bound by him, and that one cutting off the father. Is it then
worthy to suspect this one a god, who has done so many things, and been
slandered, which not even the common laws of the Romans permit even simply men
to do?
12 For, that I may recall a few from many because of the multitude, who,
having seen his lawlessness and corruption toward Semele and Leda and Alcmene
and Artemis and Leto and Maia and Europa and Danae and Antiope; or who, having
seen the attempt and daring toward his own sister, that he had the same sister
also as wife, would not mock, and punish with death? Because he not only
committed adultery, but also having deified the children come to be for him
from the adultery he assigned them, constructing the imagination of
deification as a covering of his lawlessness; of whom are Dionysus and
Heracles and the Dioscuri and Hermes and Perseus and Soteira. Who, having seen
the irreconcilable strife of the so-called gods toward themselves in Ilium for
the sake of the Greeks and the Trojans, will not condemn their weakness,
because through the rivalry toward one another they also provoked the men?
Who, having seen Ares and Aphrodite being wounded by Diomedes, and Hera and
the Subterranean one whom they call the god Aidoneus by Heracles, and Dionysus
by Perseus, and Athena by Arcas, and Hephaestus being thrown and limping, will
not condemn the nature, and will turn away from saying them still to be gods,
and hearing them corruptible and passible, will recognize them nothing else
than men, and weak men, and would rather marvel at those who wounded than
those wounded? Or who, having seen the adultery of Ares toward Aphrodite, and
the snare constructed by Hephaestus against both, and the other so-called gods
being called by Hephaestus to a viewing of the adultery, and themselves
coming, and seeing their wantonness, would not laugh and condemn their
baseness? Or who would not laugh seeing the madness from drunkenness of
Heracles toward Omphale and the profligacy? For their deeds according to
pleasure, and their unreasonable loves, and the god-fashionings in gold and
silver, and bronze and iron, and stones and woods, one must not refute with
zeal, the things having the defilement also from themselves, and through
themselves displaying the mark of the error; at which


especially one might also pity those deceived in them. For hating the
adulterer who attacks their own wife, they are not ashamed, deifying the
teachers of adultery; and themselves not mixing with sisters they worship
those who did this; and confessing that corruption of children is an evil,
they worship those slandered upon this; and what the laws do not even permit
to be among men, these things they are not ashamed to put around those named
by them to be gods.
13 Then worshipping stones and woods, they do not see that they tread the like
things with the feet and burn them, and they address parts of these as gods;
and what a little before they had for use, these through madness having carved
they worship; not seeing, nor reckoning at all, that they worship not gods,
but the art of the one who carved. For as long as the stone is unscraped, and
the matter idle, so far they tread these, and they use them for their own
services often even the more dishonorable ones; but whenever the craftsman
applies to them the proportions of his own knowledge, and stamps a form of a
man or of a woman into the matter, then indeed, having confessed thanks to the
craftsman, thereafter they worship as gods, having bought them for a wage from
the one who carved them. And often even the statue-maker himself, as if having
forgotten what he himself worked, prays to his own works; and what a little
before he scraped and cut, these after the art he addresses as gods. But he
ought, if these were to be marveled at, to accept the art of the knowledgeable
one, and not to prefer the things fashioned by him to the one who made. For it
was not the matter that adorned the art, but the art that adorned the matter
and deified it. Much more justly then was it for them to worship the
craftsman, rather than the things made by him, both because he also existed
before the gods from art, and because as he wished, so also they came to be.
But now, having elbowed aside the just, and having dishonored the knowledge
and the art, they worship the things come to be with knowledge and art; and
the making man dying, they honor the things come to be by him as immortal;
which, if they should not obtain the daily care, altogether in time through
the nature vanish. And how would one not pity them also according to this,
that seeing they themselves worship those who do not see, and hearing they
themselves pray to those who do not hear; and men being by nature ensouled and
rational, they address as gods those who are not moved at all, but are
soulless; and the marvelous thing, that those whom they themselves guard
having under authority, to these they slave as to masters? And do not think
that I say these things simply, or that I lie against them; for the faith of
these also meets the eyes, and it is present for those who wish to see such
things.
14 And a better witness concerning these is also from the divine scripture
teaching beforehand from above and saying: The idols of the nations are silver
and gold, works of hands of men. They have eyes, and they will not see; they
have a mouth, and they will not speak; they have ears, and they will not hear;
they have noses, and they will not smell; they have hands, and they will not
feel; they have feet, and they will not walk; they will not sound in their
throat. Like them may all those who make them become. Nor is the prophetic
blame of these absent, but there is also in these the refutation against them,
the Spirit saying: They shall be ashamed who fashion God, and all who carve
vain things; and all whence they came to be, they were dried up; and let all
the deaf from men be gathered, and let them stand together, and let them be
turned and ashamed together; because a craftsman sharpened iron, and worked it
with an adze, and shaped it with a drill, and stood it by the arm of his
strength; and he will hunger and be weak, and he will not drink water. For a
craftsman having chosen wood, stood it in measure, and shaped it with glue,
and made it as a form of a man and as beauty of a man, he stood it in


a house, which he cut wood from the thicket, which the Lord planted, and rain
lengthened, so that it might be for men for burning, and having taken from it
he may be warmed. And having burned they baked breads upon it, and the
remainder they worked into gods, and they worshipped them, of which he burned
the half of it with fire. And upon the half of it having roasted meat, he ate
and was filled; and having been warmed he said: It is sweet to me, because I
was warmed, and I saw fire. And the remainder he was worshipping, saying:
Deliver me, because you are my God. They did not know to be wise, because they
were darkened from seeing with their eyes, and from thinking with the heart;
and he did not reckon in his heart, nor did he reckon with his soul, nor did
he know with the prudence, that he burned the half of it in fire and baked
upon its coals breads; and having roasted meat he ate, and the remainder of it
he made into an abomination, and they worship it. Know, that their heart is
ash, and they wander, and no one can deliver their soul. See, and you will not
say, that there is a lie in my right hand. How then would they not be judged
godless among all, who are also accused of impiety by the divine scripture? Or
how would they not be ill-starred who thus are manifestly refuted worshipping
the soulless instead of the truth? And what hope for these, or what pardon
might come to be for them, who have trusted upon the irrational and unmoved
things, which they worship instead of the true God?
15 For would that, would that the craftsman had fashioned the gods for them
even without form, so that they might not have the manifest refutation of the
insensibility. For they would have stolen the suspicion of the simple, as of
the idols perceiving, if they did not have the symbols of the senses, such as
eyes and noses and ears and hands and mouth lying unmoved toward the use of
sense and the laying hold of the sensibles. But now having they do not have
and standing they do not stand, and sitting they do not sit. For they do not
have the activity of these, but as the one who fashioned wished, so also they
remain lying, providing no mark of God, but appearing wholly soulless only,
having been set by art of a man. And would that also the heralds and seers of
such false gods, I mean poets and writers, had written them simply to be gods;
but had not also written up their deeds toward refutation of godlessness and
of a shame-making way of life. For they were able even by the name of Godhead
alone to snatch the truth, or rather to lead the many astray from the truth.
But now recounting loves and wantonnesses of Zeus, and corruptions of children
of the others, and jealousies toward pleasure of the females, and fears and
cowardices, and the other evils, they refute nothing else than themselves,
that they not only do not recount concerning gods, but not even concerning
solemn men, but they mythologize concerning shameful ones and those far from
the beautiful.
16 But perhaps concerning these the impious will take refuge upon the
peculiarity of the poets, saying that it is a peculiar character of the poets
to fashion the things that are not and to lie concerning myths for the
pleasure of those who hear, for which reason they will also say that they have
made the things concerning gods. But this pretext of theirs more than all will
be shown stale from what they themselves have as opinion concerning these and
set forth. For if the things among the poets are fictions and false, false
also would be the very naming concerning Zeus and Cronus and Hera and Ares and
the others. For perhaps, as they themselves say, even the names have been
fashioned, and there is not wholly a Zeus, nor a Cronus, nor an Ares; but the
poets fashion these as being toward deceit of those who hear. And the poets
fashioning the things that are not, how do they worship them as being? Or
perhaps they will say again, they do not fashion the names, but they lie the
deeds against them? But this also no less is not safe toward their apology.
For if


they lied the deeds, they altogether also lied the names, of which they also
recounted the deeds to be. Or if they speak truth concerning the names, they
speak truth also concerning the deeds of necessity. And otherwise those who
mythologized these to be gods know truly also what gods ought to do, and they
would never attach thoughts of men to gods; just as neither will anyone assign
the work of fire to the water; for the one burns, and the other has the
essence cold in reverse. If then the deeds are worthy of gods, gods also would
be the workers of these; but if to commit adultery and the aforementioned
works is of men and of men not beautiful, men would be those who did these
things, and not gods. For the deeds must be according to one another with the
essences, so that also from the activity the one who acted may be witnessed,
and from the essence the deed may be able to be known. Just as therefore if
someone, discoursing concerning water and fire, and declaring the activities
of these, would not say that the water burns, and the fire cools; nor if
someone were recounting concerning sun and earth, would he say that the earth
illuminates, and the sun is sown with herbs and fruits, but even saying it he
surpassed all madness; so the writers among them, and especially the most
eminent poet of all, would not, if they knew Zeus and the others to be gods,
have put around them such deeds, which refute them not to be gods, but rather
to be men, and men not temperate. Or if they lied as poets, and you also lie
against these, why did they not also lie upon the courage of the heroes, and
fashion weakness instead of courage, and courage instead of weakness? For they
ought, just as upon Zeus and Hera, so also to have lied cowardice of Achilles,
and to have marveled at power of Thersites; and to have slandered
unintelligence of Odysseus, and to have fashioned madness of Nestor; and to
have mythologized womanly deeds of Diomedes and Hector, and courage of Hecuba.
For upon all, as they themselves say, the poets ought to fashion and lie. But
now they guarded the truth for the men, and they did not fear to lie against
the so-called gods. For one might also say this of them, in their deeds
concerning wantonness they lie; but in the praises, when they say Zeus father
of gods and highest and Olympian and reigning in heaven, they do not fashion,
but speaking truly they say. But this not I alone, but also everyone whoever
would refute to be the argument against them. For again by the first proofs
the truth against them will appear. For the deeds refute them to be men, and
the encomia are above the nature of men; and each of these is unsuited toward
itself; for it is not peculiar of those in the heavens to do such things, nor
can anyone suspect those who do such things to be gods.
17 What then remains to think, than that the encomia happen to be false and
granted as favor, and the deeds are spoken truly against them? And one will
recognize this true from custom. For no one praising someone also accuses his
way of life; but rather those whose deeds are shameful, these because of the
blame from these they lift with encomia, so that having deceived those who
hear by the excess of these they may hide their lawlessness. Just as therefore
if someone, having proposed to praise someone, should not find from way of
life nor from virtue of soul the pretext of the encomia because of the shame
in these, and should otherwise lift them, granting them the things above them;
so also the marvelous poets among them, being put to shame upon the shameful
deeds of those called gods among them, attached to them the name above man,
not knowing that they will not overshadow their human things by the suspicions
above man, but rather by their human lessenings they will refute the thoughts
of God not to fit them. And I at least think that even against their judgment
the passions of these and the deeds of these have been said. For since the
incommunicable, as the scripture said, appellation and honor of God


they were eager to assign to those who are not gods, but mortal men, and great
and impious was that which was dared by them, for this reason even unwilling
they were compelled by the truth to set out the passions of these, so that for
those after these the passions of these toward refutation of these not being
gods, lying in the writings concerning them, might appear to all.
18 What apology then, what proof concerning these being gods might come to be
for those who are superstitious in these? For from the things said a little
before, the argument showed them men, and men not being solemn; but perhaps
they will turn to that, and will think great upon the things useful for life
found by them, saying that for these they also hold them gods, because they
have become useful to men. For Zeus is said to have had the plastic art, and
Poseidon that of the steersman; and Hephaestus the smith art, and Athena the
weaving; and Apollo the music, and Artemis the hunting, and Hera clothing,
Demeter agriculture, and the others other things, as those who relate
concerning them explained. But these and such knowledges men ought not to have
assigned to them alone, but to the common nature of men, looking toward which
men invent the arts. For art even the many say is itself an imitation of
nature. If then they have become knowledgeable concerning the arts about which
they were eager, it is not necessary for this reason also to think them gods,
but rather men. For the arts are not from them, but in these they themselves
also imitated nature. For being men by nature receptive of knowledge according
to the definition set concerning them, it is nothing marvelous if by the human
thought they themselves also, looking toward their own nature, and having
taken knowledge of this, invented the arts. Or if they say they are worthy to
be proclaimed gods through the findings of the arts, it is time also to
proclaim the finders of the other arts gods, according to the argument by
which those also were thought worthy of such a naming. For the Phoenicians
invented letters, and Homer heroic poetry; and Zeno the Eleatic dialectic, and
Corax the Syracusan the rhetorical art; and Aristaeus the fruit of bees, and
Triptolemus the sowing of grain; and Lycurgus the Spartan and Solon the
Athenian laws; and Palamedes invented the composition of letters and numbers
and measures and weights; and others reported other and various things useful
to the life of men, according to the witness of those who related. If then the
knowledges deify, and through these there are carved gods, of necessity also
those later than those who became finders of the others are according to them
gods; or if they do not think these worthy of the honor of God, but recognize
them as men; it follows that Zeus also and Hera and the others are not even
named gods, but that they themselves also have come to be men, and moreover
that they have not even been solemn, as also from the very carving of the
statues they refute them nothing else than men.
19 For what other form do they apply carving them than that of males and of
women, and of those still lower than these and being irrational by nature, of
all kinds of birds, of four-footed tame and wild, and of reptiles, as many as
earth and sea and all the nature of the waters bears? For men having fallen
into the unreason of the passions and pleasures, and seeing nothing more than
pleasures and desires of flesh, having the thought as in these irrational
things, they fashioned the Divine also in irrational things according to the
variety of their own passions, and having carved so many gods. For there are
images of four-footed things and of reptiles, and of birds among them, just as
also the interpreter of the divine and true piety says: They became vain in
their reasonings, and their unintelligent heart was darkened; saying they are
wise, they became foolish, and they exchanged the glory of the incorruptible
God in a likeness


of an image of a corruptible man, and of birds and of four-footed things and
of reptiles; wherefore also God handed them over into passions of dishonor.
For having first suffered the soul in the unreasons of the pleasures, as I
said before, they fell down upon such god-fashioning; and having fallen,
thereafter as having been handed over in God turning away from them they thus
roll in them, and they liken the God Father of the Word in irrational things.
Concerning whom those called philosophers and knowledgeable among the Greeks,
being refuted, do not deny that their apparent gods are forms and types of men
and of irrational things; but apologizing they say they have them for this
reason, so that through these the Divine may answer and appear to them; for
they cannot otherwise know him the invisible one than through such statues and
rites. And those still more philosophical than these and thinking they say
deeper things say that for this reason these have been constructed and typed
toward invocation and epiphany of divine angels and powers, so that appearing
through these they may make known to them concerning the knowledge of God; and
that these are as letters for men, meeting with which they can know concerning
the grasp of God, from the epiphany of the divine angels that comes to be
through them. These things they thus mythologize; for they do not theologize;
may it not be. But if someone examines the argument with care, he will find
the opinion of these no less than of those shown before to be false.
20 For someone coming forward might say to them with truth judging: how is God
answered or made known through these? Whether through the matter lying around
them, or through the form in them? For if through the matter, what is the need
of the form, and not, before these were fashioned, through all matter simply
for God to appear? And in vain also these walled around the temples, shutting
in one stone or wood or a part of gold, all the earth being filled with the
essence of these. But if the form lying upon becomes cause of the divine
epiphany, what is the need of the matter of the gold and of the others, and
not rather through the animals themselves by nature, of which the carvings are
forms, for God to appear? For better would the opinion concerning God
according to the same argument have come to be, if he appeared through
ensouled animals both rational and irrational, and were not expected in
soulless and unmoved things; upon which especially they work impiety against
themselves. For abominating and turning away from the animals by nature
four-footed and birds and reptiles either through the wildness or through the
filth, nonetheless having carved the types of these in stones and woods and
gold they deify them. But they ought rather to worship the living things
themselves than for the types of these in these to be worshipped. Or perhaps
none of these, neither the form nor the matter is cause of the presence of
God; but only the art with knowledge calls out the Divine, as being itself an
imitation of nature. But if through the knowledge the Divine visits the
carvings, what again is the need of the matter, the knowledge being in the
men? For if wholly through the art God appears, and for this reason the
carvings are worshipped as gods, the men being leaders of the art ought to be
worshipped and to be worshipped, by how much they are also rational and have
the knowledge in themselves.
21 And concerning their second and supposedly deeper apology, one might also
say these things consistently: if not through the epiphany of God himself
these have been made for you, O Greeks, but through the presence of angels
there, why do you make the statues, through which you invoke the powers,
better and above the invoked powers themselves? For carving the forms for the
sake of the grasp concerning God, as you say, you put around the carvings
themselves the honor and appellation of God himself, suffering a thing not
holy. For confessing that the power of God surpasses the smallness of the
statues, and for this reason not daring God through them,


but to invoke the lesser powers, yourselves having gone beyond these, of him
whose presence you feared, you assigned the appellation to the stones and
woods, and instead of stones and art of men you name and worship gods. For if
these are as letters for you, as you lie, of the contemplation toward God, it
is not just to prefer the signifying things to the thing signified. For
neither if someone should write the name of the king, would he have it without
danger preferring the letter to the king; but such a one has death as the
penalty, and the letter has been typed by the knowledge of the one who wrote.
So also you, if you had the reasoning strong, would not have carried down so
great a mark of the Godhead into matter; but you would not even have preferred
the carving to the man who carved. For if even wholly as letters they signify
the epiphany of God, and for this reason as signifying God they are worthy of
deification; yet at least the one who carved and engraved these, I mean again
the craftsman, ought much more to have been deified, as being more powerful
than those and more divine, by how much those also according to his will have
been scraped and typed. If then the letters are worthy of wonder, much more
the one who wrote surpasses in the wonder through the art and the knowledge of
the soul. Therefore if it is not worthy to think them gods through this, one
might ask them again concerning the madness of the idols, thinking it right to
learn from them the cause of such a form of them.
22 For if because the Divine is human-formed, for this reason it has been
typed thus, why do they also put around it types of irrational things? And if
the type of this is of irrational animals, why do they also put around it
carvings of rational ones? And if it is both together, and they have grasped
God from both, because he has the types of irrational and of rational, why do
they divide the things joined, and separate the carving of irrational things
and of men, and do not always carve him from both, such as are the fictions
among the myths, Scylla and Charybdis and the Hippocentaur and among the
Egyptians the dog-headed Anubis? For they ought either only thus to be drawn
two-natured, or having one form of them, not also to fashion the other against
them. And again if the forms of these are male, why do they also put around
them types of females? And if they are of female, why do they also lie against
them the forms of males? And if again they are both together, they ought not
to be divided, but both to be joined, and to come to be according to the
so-called hermaphrodites, so that the superstition may provide not only
impiety and slander, but also laughters of them to those who see; and wholly
if they suppose the Divine body-formed, so as also to invent and fashion for
it belly and hands and feet, and again neck and breasts and the other members
toward generation of men, see into how great impiety and godlessness the mind
of these has fallen, so as to suspect such things concerning the Divine. For
it follows for it also to suffer the other things of the body altogether, so
as also to be cut, and to be divided, and again from the whole to be
corrupted; and these and such things are not peculiar of God, but rather of
the bodies upon earth. For God is bodiless and incorruptible and immortal,
needing nothing toward anything whatever; but these are both corruptible and
types of bodies, and needing the use from them, just as also has been said
before; often at any rate we see those that have grown old being renewed, and
those whom time or rain or some other of the animals upon earth vanished,
these being fashioned again. Upon which one might condemn their madness;
because those of whom they themselves happen to be makers, these they proclaim
gods; and from those whom they themselves adorn by the arts for the sake of
not being corrupted, from these they themselves ask salvation; and from those
whom they are not ignorant need their care, from these they themselves think
it right that their own needs be filled; and those whom in small


little houses they shut, these they are not ashamed to call masters of heaven
and of all earth.
23 And not only from these might one learn their godlessness, but also from
what in the idols themselves of them the opinion is unharmonious. For if they
themselves are gods, as they say and philosophize concerning them, to whom of
these should someone add himself, and which of them might he judge more
lordly, so that he may either take courage worshipping God, or, as they say,
in them may not doubt knowing the Divine? For not the same are named gods
among all; but as many as there are nations for the most part, so many also
gods are fashioned. And there is where even one country, and one city, are at
faction toward themselves concerning the superstition of the idols. The
Phoenicians at any rate do not know the so-called gods among the Egyptians,
nor do the Egyptians worship the same idols as those among the Phoenicians.
And the Scythians do not accept the gods of the Persians, and the Persians
those of the Syrians. But also the Pelasgians slander the gods in Thrace; and
the Thracians do not know those among the Thebans. And Indians differ against
Arabs, and Arabs against Ethiopians, and Ethiopians against them in the idols.
And Syrians do not revere the things of the Cilicians; and the race of the
Cappadocians name other gods besides these. And Bithynians fashioned others,
and Armenians others for themselves. And what need of many for me? Mainlanders
worship other gods besides those in the islands; and islanders worship besides
those in the mainlands. And wholly each city and village, not knowing the gods
from neighbors, prefers its own, and thinks these alone to be gods. For
concerning the abominable things in Egypt it is not even possible to speak, it
being before the eyes of all that the cities have the worships opposite and
fighting one another, and those from neighbors always are eager to revere the
opposite things against those nearby. The crocodile at any rate worshipped
among others as a god, this among those nearby is thought an abomination; and
the lion worshipped among others as a god, this the town-neighbors not only do
not worship, but even having found they kill as a beast; and the fish assigned
among others, this of others is ground as food. Whence wars and factions and
every pretext of murders and every pleasure of the passions is among them. And
the marvelous thing, that as those who related explain, the Pelasgians having
learned from Egyptians the names of the gods, these do not know the gods among
the Egyptians, but they worship others besides those. And wholly of all the
nations that have gone mad in idols the opinion and the worship is different,
and the same things are not found among the same. And reasonably they suffer
this. For having fallen out from the consideration toward the one God, they
have fallen down into many and various things; and having turned away from the
truly Word of the Father, the Savior of all Christ, reasonably they have the
thought wandering into many things. And just as those who have turned away
from the sun and have come to be in dark places, circle many ways that are no
ways, and they do not see those present, and they imagine those that are not
as present, and seeing they do not see; in the same manner those who have
turned away from God and have been darkened have the mind wandering, and they
imagine the things that are not as being drunk and not seeing.
24 And these things are not a small refutation of their truly godlessness. For
the gods being different and many according to city and country, and the one
destroying the god of the other, all are destroyed by all. For also the gods
thought among others become sacrifices and libations of the so-called gods
among others; and the sacrifices of others, in reverse, are gods of others.
And Egyptians revere the ox and Apis being a calf; and others sacrifice these
to Zeus. For even if they do not sacrifice those themselves whom they have
assigned, yet sacrificing the like things, they seem to bring the same.


Libyans have a sheep, which they call Ammon, as a god; and this by many among
others is slaughtered into a sacrifice. Indians worship Dionysus, naming him
symbolically wine; and others pour this as a libation to the others. Others
have preferred rivers and springs, and most of all Egyptians the water, and
they proclaim them gods; and nonetheless others, and the Egyptians themselves
who worship these, wash off the filths of the others, and their own, with the
waters, and they cast out the remainder with dishonor. And almost all the
idol-making of the Egyptians is a sacrifice of the gods among others; so that
they would even be mocked by those themselves, because they deify not gods,
but the things of the others that are still also among themselves things to be
averted and sacrifices.
25 And already some have been carried out into so great impiety and madness,
as even to slaughter the men themselves, of whom there are types and forms, to
the false gods among them and to bring sacrifices. And the ill-starred do not
see that the victims being slaughtered are archetypes of the gods fashioned
and worshipped by them, and to whom they bring the men. For they almost bring
the like to the like or rather the better to the lesser. For they sacrifice
ensouled to soulless, and they bring rational to the unmoved. For Scythians
the so-called Taurians bring up to the one called Virgin among them those from
shipwrecks and as many of the Greeks as they take into sacrifices, being so
impious against men of the same race, and thus refuting the savagery of their
gods; because those whom Providence saved from the sea from dangers, these
they themselves slaughter, all but becoming against Providence; because they
hide her benefaction by their own beastly soul. And others to Ares, whenever
they return from wars and bring victories, then dividing those taken into
hundreds, and taking one from each, they slaughter as many as they may choose
according to one hundred. And not only Scythians through the wildness inborn
in them among barbarians do such abominable things, but this drama is peculiar
of the evil of the idols and demons. For also Egyptians of old sacrificed such
victims to Hera; and Phoenicians and Cretans propitiated Cronus in their
child-sacrifices. And the Romans of old worshipped the so-called Latian Zeus
with human sacrifices; and others otherwise, and all simply defiled and were
defiled. They were defiled themselves doing the murderous things; and they
defiled their own temples smoking them with such sacrifices. From these then
the evils reached a multitude for men; for seeing the demons among them taking
pleasure in these, immediately they themselves also imitated their own gods in
such transgressions, holding the imitation toward the better, as they
themselves think, a peculiar achievement. Hence men were overcome by
man-murders and child-killings and all wantonnesses. For almost every city is
full of all wantonness, coming to be through likeness of manners of the gods
among them; and there is not a temperate one among the idols, unless only the
one witnessed among them upon wantonness.
26 Women at any rate in idols of Phoenicia of old sat forth, offering as
first-fruits to the gods there the hire of their own body, thinking by the
fornication to propitiate their goddess, and to bring her into goodwill
through these. And men, denying the nature and no longer wishing to be males,
fashion the nature of women, as from these making things to the mind and honor
to the mother of the so-called gods among them. And all together live with the
most shameful things, and compete with themselves in the worse; and as the
holy deacon of Christ Paul said: For their females also exchanged the natural
use into that against nature. And likewise also the males, having left the
natural use of the female,


were burned in their appetite toward one another, males in males working the
unseemliness. And doing these and such things, they confess and refute that
their so-called gods also have had such a life. For from Zeus they have
learned the corruption of children and the adultery, and from Aphrodite the
fornication, and from Rhea the wantonness, and from Ares the murders, and from
others other such things, which the laws punish, and every temperate man turns
away. Is it then still worthy to think those who do such things to be gods,
and not rather to hold these more irrational than the irrational because of
the wantonness of the manners? Is it worthy to think those who worship them
men, and not rather as more irrational than the irrational, and more soulless
than the soulless, to pity them? For if they had reckoned the mind of their
own soul, they would not have fallen in these wholly prone, and denied the
true God Father of Christ.
27 But perhaps those who have gone up beyond these and are excited concerning
the creation, being put to shame by the refutations concerning the
abominations, will not deny even themselves that these are easily condemned
and easily refuted among all; but they will think that opinion safe for them
and undeniable, the worship toward the world and the parts of the world; for
they will boast not as simply worshipping and revering stones and woods and
forms of men and of irrational birds and reptiles and four-footed things, but
sun and moon and all the world according to heaven, and earth again and all
the nature of the wet; and they will say that no one can show these also not
to be gods by nature, it being manifest to all that they are neither soulless
nor irrational, but even surpass the nature of men, by some dwelling in the
heavens, and some upon the earth. It is worthy then also concerning these to
see and to search through. For altogether also in these the argument will find
the refutation true against them. And before we see and begin the proof, it is
enough that the creation itself all but cry out against them, and show its own
maker and fashioner God, who also reigns over this and the all, the Father of
our Lord Jesus Christ; whom the seeming-wise turn away from, but they worship
and deify the creation come to be from him, although it itself also worships
and confesses the Lord whom those through it deny. For thus, men gaping toward
the parts of this and thinking these gods, the need of the parts toward one
another would well put them to shame; and it also makes known and signifies
him who is also Lord and maker of them, the Father of the Word, by the
undeniable command of the obedience toward him, as also the divine legislation
says: The heavens declare glory of God; and the firmament announces a making
of his hands. And faith of these is not unclear, but even very evident to
those who do not have the eye of the thought wholly maimed. For if someone
should take the parts of the creation by themselves and think each privately,
such as sun by itself alone, and moon apart, and earth again and air, and
having divided the hot and cold and dry and wet essence from the joining
toward one another, should take each by itself and contemplate it privately;
he will find altogether nothing sufficing itself, but all needing the need of
one another, and being constituted by the helps from one another. For the sun
is carried around with the whole heaven and is encompassed, and outside of the
circling of that it would never come to be; and moon and the other stars
testify the help that comes to be for them from the sun; and earth again
appears not giving back the fruits without rains; and the rains would not come
down upon earth without the need of the clouds; but neither would clouds
without the air appear by themselves and be constituted ever. And the air is
not by itself, but is burned through by the ether, and being illuminated by
the sun is made bright. And springs and


rivers will never be constituted without the earth; and earth is not supported
from itself, but it has been constituted upon the essence of the waters, and
it itself also is encompassed, having been bound according to the middle of
the all. And the sea and the great ocean flowing around the whole earth from
outside is moved by winds and is carried wherever the force of the winds may
dash it. And the winds themselves are not in themselves, but, according to
those who have spoken concerning these, from the burning-through of the ether
toward the air and heat they are constituted in the air itself, and through it
they blow everywhere. For concerning the four elements, from which also the
nature of bodies has been constituted, I mean the hot and the cold, dry and
wet essence, who has so turned away the thought, as not to know that these
being joined together are constituted, but being divided and coming to be by
themselves, thereafter these are also destructive of one another according to
the dominance of that which abounds in them? For the hot is destroyed by cold
having abounded; and cold again is vanished by the hot power; and the dry
again is made wet by the wet, and this is dried by the other.
28 How then would these be gods, needing the help from others? Or how was it
fitting to ask something from these, they themselves also demanding from one
another the need toward themselves? For if the argument concerning God is that
he is in need of nothing, but self-sufficient and full of himself, and in him
all things consist, and rather he gives to all; how is it worthy to proclaim
sun and moon, and the other parts of the creation not being such, but even
lacking the need of one another, gods? But perhaps being divided and taken by
themselves, they themselves also agree that they are in need, the proof being
before the eyes; but joining all together, and completing as one great body,
they will say the whole is God. For the whole having been constituted, there
will no longer be need for them from outside; and the whole will become
sufficient to itself and self-sufficient toward all things, the seeming-wise
will say, so that from here also they may be refuted; and this argument even
more will show their impiety with great want of education no less than the
former. For if the each having been joined fills the whole, and the whole is
constituted from the according to one; the whole then has been constituted
from parts, and each happens to be a part of the whole. And this stands far
away from the thoughts concerning God. For God is a whole and not parts, and
he has not been constituted from different things, but he himself is maker of
the constitution of all. For see how great impiety they explain saying these
things against the Divine. For if he has been constituted from parts,
altogether he himself will appear unlike himself, and having the completion
from unlikes. For if he is sun, he is not moon; and if he is moon, he is not
earth; and if he happens to be earth, he would not be sea; and thus taking
upon each one might find the absurdity of such an argument of theirs. And one
might also seeing this from the human body according to us condemn these. For
as the eye is not hearing, nor the hearing a hand, nor the belly is chest, nor
again the neck is a foot; but each of these has its own activity, and from
these being different one body is constituted, having the parts joined
according to the need, but divided according to the presence of time, whenever
the nature that gathered these divides, as the God who commanded wishes; so
(and let the argument have pardon from the better itself), if joining the
parts of the creation into one body they proclaim God, of necessity he himself
by himself is unlike himself, as was shown, and is divided again according to
the nature of the parts that came to be toward being partitioned.
29 And otherwise one might also refute their godlessness according to the
contemplation of the truth. For if God is bodiless, and invisible, and
untouchable by nature, how


do they invent God a body, and worship the things that appear to the eyes and
of which we touch with the hand with the honor of God? And again, if the
argument concerning God prevails, that he is able according to all things, and
nothing rules him, but he himself rules and is master of all things; how do
those who deify the creation not see it being outside of such a definition
concerning God? For the sun having come to be under earth, the earth shadows
the light so as not to be seen; and the sun hides the moon after day by the
brightness of the light. And hail often harms the fruits of earth; and the
fire, if some flood of waters should come to be, is quenched. And spring
elbows winter aside, and summer does not permit spring to go beyond the
bounds, itself again being hindered by autumn from going out of its own hours.
If then they were gods, these ought not to be defeated by one another and
hidden, but always to be with one another, and to complete common activities
together; sun together with moon after day and after night and the other
chorus of the stars ought to have the light equal, and this to shine for all,
and all to be illuminated from them; summer together with winter and spring
and autumn ought to be constituted unvaryingly and according to the same; the
sea ought to be mixed with the springs and to provide the drink common to men;
calms and the breathings of the winds ought to come to be in the same; fire
together with water ought to provide the use common and one to men. For
neither would anyone even have endured harm from them, they being gods
according to them, and doing all things not upon harm, but rather upon
benefit. But if these things are impossible to come to be because of the
opposition toward one another, how is it possible still to name these opposite
to one another and fighting, and not able to be constituted with one another,
gods, or to be worshipped with honors of God? And those having the nature
unharmonious toward themselves, how would they provide peace to others
praying, and become presidents of concord for them? Neither sun then
reasonably, nor moon, nor any other part of the creation, and much more not
even the statues in stones and gold and the other matters, nor those
mythologized among poets, Zeus and Apollo and the others, would be truly gods,
as the argument showed; but some of them are parts of the creation, and some
of them happen to be soulless, and some have come to be only mortal men.
Wherefore also the worship and deification concerning these is not of piety,
but is an introduction of godlessness and of all impiety, and a refutation of
great error from the knowledge toward the one and only true God, I mean the
Father of Christ. When then these things are thus refuted and the idolatry
among the Greeks has been shown being full of all godlessness, and having been
introduced not upon benefit, but upon destruction for the life of men; come
thereafter, as from the beginning the argument promised, the error having been
refuted, let us travel the way of the truth, and let us contemplate the leader
and fashioner of the all, the Word of the Father, so that through him we may
also consider his Father God, and Greeks may know how far they have fenced
themselves off from the truth.
30 The things said before have been refuted as nothing else than error for
life; and the way of the truth will have the mark toward the really being God.
And toward the knowledge of this and unerring grasp we have no need of others,
but of ourselves; nor, as God himself is above all, so also is the way toward
this one far or from outside us; but it is in us, and from us it is possible
to find the beginning, just as also Moses was teaching, saying: The word of
the faith is within your heart. Which also the Savior signifying and
confirming said: The kingdom of God is within you. For having the faith and
the kingdom of God within in ourselves, we can quickly contemplate and think
the king of the all, the saving Word of the Father. And let not Greeks who
worship the idols make pretext; nor


anyone else simply deceive himself, as not having such a way, and through this
finding a pretext of his own godlessness. For we have all stepped upon it and
have it, even if not all wish to travel it, but to pass by going out because
of the pleasures of life that drag them from outside. And if someone should
ask, what this would be; I say that it is the soul of each, and the mind in
it. For through this alone can God be contemplated and thought; unless, just
as they denied God, so also the impious will decline to have a soul,
reasonably saying this before the others. For it is not of those who have a
mind to deny the maker and fashioner of this, God. That then each of men has a
soul and this rational, this also it is necessary to show through a few things
because of the simple, since especially some from the heresies deny this also,
thinking man to be nothing more than the apparent form of the body; so that
this having been shown, they may be able more manifestly through themselves to
have the refutation against the idols.
31 First then it is not a small mark of the soul of men being rational from
its differing toward the irrational things; for this reason nature has been
accustomed to call those irrational, since the race of men is rational; then
also this would not be a chance thing toward proof, from man alone reckoning
the things outside himself, and considering the things not present, and again
calculating and by judgment choosing the better of the reasonings; for the
irrational things see only the present things, and rush toward only the things
in the eyes, even if after these they have the harm. But man does not rush
toward the things seen, but by the reasoning he judges the things seen through
the eyes; often at any rate having rushed he has been held by the reasoning;
and having reasoned, he calculated again, and each perceives, if he should
become a friend of the truth, that other besides the bodily senses is the mind
of men. For this reason at any rate, being other, he becomes judge of the
senses themselves; and of what those lay hold, these he distinguishes, and
reminds, and shows them the better. For it is of eye only to see, and of ears
to hear, and of mouth to taste, and of nose to lay hold of odors, and of hands
to touch; but what one must see and hear, and of what one must touch and taste
and smell, is no longer of the senses, but of the soul and of its mind to
distinguish. Indeed the hand can also take a sword, and the mouth taste a
deadly thing; but it does not know that these harm, unless the mind
distinguish. And such a thing is like, so that we may contemplate it upon an
image, a lyre well constructed, and the musician holding this with knowledge.
For as the strings in the lyre each has its own sound, one heavy, and one
sharp, and one middle, and one high-toned, and one another; but the harmony of
them is undistinguished and the composition undiagnosed without the
knowledgeable one; for then both their harmony is shown and the composition
right, whenever the one holding the lyre strikes the strings, and touches each
fittingly; in this manner also of the senses having been fitted in the body as
of a lyre, whenever the knowledgeable mind is leader of them; then also the
soul distinguishes, and knows what it does and acts. And this is peculiar of
men only, and this is the rational of the soul of men, using which it differs
from the irrational things, and shows that truly there is another besides the
things that appear in body. Often at any rate the body lying upon earth, man
imagines and contemplates the things in the heavens; and often the body being
at rest and being quiet and sleeping, the man is moved within, and
contemplates the things outside himself, going abroad to countries and
walking, and meeting those known, and often through these divining and knowing
beforehand his own deeds after day. And this what else would it be than a
rational soul, in which man reckons and thinks the things above himself?


32 And this also would be toward accurate proof for those still turned toward
shamelessness of unreason; how, the body being mortal by nature, man reckons
the things concerning immortality, and often calls forth death for himself
over virtue? Or how, the body being temporary, man imagines the eternal
things, so as to despise the things in the way, and to have the longing toward
those? The body then would not reckon such things concerning itself, and would
not reckon the things outside itself; for it is mortal and temporary; and of
necessity other is that which reckons the opposite things and besides the
nature of the body. What then would this be again, than a rational and
immortal soul? For not from outside, but from within this sounds the better
things to the body, as the musician to the lyre. And how again the eye being
by nature toward seeing, and the hearing toward hearing, they turn away from
some things, and choose others? For who is the one turning the eye away from
seeing? Or who shuts the hearing being by nature hearing from hearing? Or who
often hinders the taste, being by nature tasting, from the natural impulse?
And who holds the hand, being by nature toward acting, from touching
something? And who turns the smell, it also having come to be toward smelling,
away from not laying hold? Who is the one working these against the natural
things of the body? Or how the body, having turned away from the nature, is
turned toward the counsels of another, and is driven as by a charioteer toward
the nod of that one? For these show nothing else than a rational soul leading
the body. For neither has the body been born to drive itself, but it is led
and carried by another, just as neither does a horse yoke itself, but it is
driven by the one who holds. For this reason at any rate also there are laws
for men to do the beautiful things, and to turn away from the evil; but for
the irrational things the evils remain unreasoned and unjudged, as happening
to be outside of rationality and of the thought according to reason. That then
there is a rational soul in men from the things said before, I think has been
shown.
33 And that the soul also has come to be immortal, this also it is necessary
to know in the ecclesiastical teaching toward refutation of the taking away of
the idols. The knowledge concerning these then might come to be nearer rather
from the knowledge concerning the body, and from its differing toward the
body. For if the argument showed it other besides the body, and the body is by
nature mortal; of necessity the soul is immortal, by not being according to
the body. And again if the soul moves the body, as has been shown, and is not
itself moved by others, it is consistent that the soul being moved by itself,
even after the putting away of the body into earth, is moved again from
itself. For it is not the soul that dies; but through the withdrawal of this
the body dies. If then this also was moved by the body, it was consistent, the
moving one withdrawing, for it to die; but if the soul also moves the body, of
necessity rather it moves itself. And being moved by itself, of necessity also
after the death of the body it lives. For the movement of the soul is nothing
else than its life; just as indeed also we say the body then to live when it
is moved, and then death of it to be when it ceases from the movement. And
this also from its activity once for all in body one might see more manifest.
For if even when it has mounted the body and has been bound to this, it is not
contracted and measured according to the smallness of the body, but often,
this lying upon a bed and not being moved, but sleeping as in death, this
according to its own power is awake, and goes out beyond the nature of the
body; and as going abroad from this, remaining in the body, it imagines and
contemplates the things above earth, and often also meets the saints and
angels outside of the earthly bodies, and arrives toward them daring by the
purity of the mind; how not


rather and much more, having been released from the body when the God who
bound it wishes, will it have the knowledge of the immortality more manifest?
For if even having been bound to a body it lived the life outside of the body,
much more also after death of the body it will live, and will not cease from
living because of the God who thus made it through his own Word, our Lord
Jesus Christ. For this reason also it reckons and thinks immortal and eternal
things, since it also is immortal. And just as, the body happening to be
mortal, mortal also are the things its senses contemplate, so the soul
contemplating and reckoning immortal things, of necessity it itself also is
immortal and always lives. For the thoughts and contemplations concerning
immortality never leave it remaining in it, and becoming as a kindling in it
toward security of the immortality. For this reason at any rate it also has
the thought of the contemplation concerning God, and it itself becomes a way
of itself, not from outside, but from itself taking the knowledge and grasp of
the God Word.
34 We say then, just as has been said before, that just as they denied God and
worship soulless things, so also thinking they do not have a rational soul,
they have from the spot the penalty of the madness being numbered among
irrational things; and for this reason as soulless having the superstition in
soulless things, they are worthy of pity and of being led by the hand. But if
they think it right to have a soul, and think great upon the rational,
reasonably doing this; why as not having a soul do they dare beside reason,
and do they not think what one must think, but make themselves better even
than the Divine? For having an immortal soul and not seen by them, they liken
God in the things seen and mortal. Or why, just as they stood off from God, so
do they not take refuge again toward him? For they can, just as they turned
away God by the thought and fashioned the things that are not into gods, so
also go up by the mind of the soul, and turn again toward God. And they can
turn, if they put away the filth of all desire which they put on, and wash off
so far, until they put away all the accidental alien to the soul, and show it
alone as it came to be, so that thus they may be able to contemplate in it the
Word of the Father, according to whom they also came to be from the beginning.
For it has been made according to image of God and has come to be according to
likeness, as also the divine scripture signifies saying from the person of
God: Let us make man according to image and according to our likeness. Whence
also when it puts away from itself all the poured-on filth of the sin, and
guards only the according to image pure, reasonably, this having been made
bright, as in a mirror it contemplates the image of the Father the Word, and
in him it reckons the Father, of whom also the Savior is image. Or if the
teaching from the soul is not self-sufficient because of the things that muddy
its mind from outside, and it does not see the better; yet it is possible
again also from the things that appear to grasp the knowledge concerning God,
the creation as by letters through the order and harmony signifying and crying
its own master and maker. 35 For God being good and a lover of men, and caring
for the 35 souls come to be by him, since he is invisible and incomprehensible
in nature, existing beyond all generated essence, and for this reason the
human race was going to miss the knowledge concerning him, by the things being
from things that are not, and him ungenerated; for this reason God thus
arranged the creation by his own Word, so that, since he is invisible in
nature, he might be able to be known even from the works to men. For from the
works often the craftsman even not being seen is known; and as they say
something concerning the statue-maker Phidias, that the works of this one from
the proportion and the analogy of the parts toward one another show even
Phidias not being present to those who see; so one must think from the order
of the world the maker and fashioner of this God, even if with the eyes of the
body


he is not contemplated. For God did not misuse his invisible nature; let no
one make this a pretext; and leave himself wholly unknown to men; but as I
said before, he thus arranged the creation, so that even not being seen by
nature, nonetheless he is known from the works. And this I do not say from
myself, but from what I learned from the theologian men, of whom one is Paul,
writing to Romans thus: For his invisible things from creation of world are
seen, being thought by the things made. And to Lycaonians speaking boldly and
saying: And we are men of like passions with you, evangelizing you to turn
from the vain things upon a living God, who made the heaven and the earth and
the sea and all the things in them, who in the generations that have passed
allowed all the nations to go in their ways. And yet he did not leave himself
without witness doing good, giving us rains from heaven and fruit-bearing
seasons, filling our hearts with food and gladness. For who, seeing the circle
of heaven and the course of sun and moon, and the positions and the circuits
of the other stars, coming to be opposite and different, but in the difference
the like order being kept by all together, does not consider that not they
themselves, but other is the fashioner who arranges them? And who, seeing sun
rising after day, and moon appearing according to night, and waning, and being
filled unvaryingly according to the altogether equal number of the days, and
of the stars some running through and variously exchanging the courses, and
some being moved without wandering, would not take a thought that altogether
there is the fashioner who steers them?
36 Who, seeing the things opposite by nature joined, and having the harmony
concordant, such as, who, having seen fire mixed with cold, and dry with wet,
and these not standing against one another, but completing one as from one the
body, would not consider that outside of these is the one who joined these?
Who, having seen winter yielding to spring, and spring to summer, and summer
to autumn, and that these being opposite by nature; for the one cools, and the
one burns, and the one nourishes, and the one wanes; nonetheless all
completing the use equal and unharmed for men; would not think that there is
someone better than these, who provides the equality to all and steers all
things, even if he does not see this one? Who, seeing the clouds being
supported in air, and in clouds the heaviness of the waters bound, does not
take a thought of the one who bound these and commanded them to come to be? Or
who, seeing the earth itself being most heavy by nature, having been settled
upon the water and remaining unmoved upon that which is by nature moved, will
not think there is some God who ordained these and made them? Who, having seen
the fruit-bearing of the earth according to season, and rains from heaven, and
inflows of rivers, and bubblings of springs, and generations of animals from
unlikes, and these not always but coming to be according to defined seasons;
and wholly who, having considered in unlikes and opposites the equal and like
order completed from them, would not consider that there is one power that
arranged these and manages, as it may seem good to it, remaining well? For
they themselves by themselves would not be constituted and be able ever to
appear because of the opposition of the nature toward one another. For the
water is by nature heavy and flowing down, and the clouds are light and happen
to be of the light and upward-borne things; and nonetheless we see the heavier
water being supported in the clouds. And again the earth is most heavy, and
the water again is lighter than this; and nonetheless the heavier is supported
by the lighter things, and is not carried down, but the earth stands unmoved.
And the male is not the same as the female, and nonetheless it is gathered
into one, and one generation of the like animal is completed from both. And to
speak in sum, the cold is opposite to the hot, and the wet fights the dry; and
nonetheless


having come together they do not faction toward themselves, but from concord
they complete one body and the generation of all.
37 Therefore things fighting and being opposite by nature would not have
gathered themselves, unless better and lord was the one who bound them, to
whom also the elements themselves, as slaves obeying a master, yield and are
persuaded; and not, each looking toward its own nature, does it fight against
the other; but knowing the Lord who bound, they have concord toward
themselves, being by nature opposite, but being made friends by the will of
the one who steers. Since if one mixture of these had not come to be by a
better command, how would the heavy have been mixed and come together with the
light, or the dry with the wet, or the circular with the straight, or the fire
with the cold, or wholly the sea with the earth, or the sun with the moon, or
the stars with the heaven, and the air with the clouds, the nature of each
toward the other being unlike? For there was going to come to be also a great
faction toward them, the one burning, and the one cooling, and the heavy down,
and the light from the opposites drawing up, and the sun illuminating, and the
air darkening; for also the stars would have factioned toward themselves,
because some have the position higher, and some lower; and the night would not
have yielded to the day, but it would remain altogether fighting toward it and
factioning. And these coming to be, thereafter one was to see no longer cosmos
but uncosmos, no longer order but disorder, no longer constitution but the
whole unconstituted, no longer measures but unmeasure. For by the faction and
fight of each either all would have been destroyed, or only that which
prevailed would have appeared. And this again would have shown the uncosmos of
the all. For having come to be alone and lacking the need of the others, it
would have made the whole unfitted; just as if also only a foot, and only a
hand remaining, would not have saved the body complete. For what cosmos would
there have been, if only sun appeared, or only moon circled, or only night
was, or day always happened? And what again was harmony, if only the heaven
was without the stars, or the stars without the heaven? And what also useful,
if only sea was, and if only earth without waters and the other parts of the
creation lay? And how would man also have appeared or wholly an animal upon
earth, the elements factioning toward themselves, and one being that which
prevailed and not being able to suffice toward the constitution of the bodies?
For neither from only hot, or only cold, or only wet, or dry would any of the
wholes have been constituted; but all would have been unordered and wholly
uncomposed. But not even that which seemed to prevail would have been able to
be constituted without the help of the others; for thus even now it has been
constituted.
38 Since then there is not disorder but order in the all, and not unmeasure
but proportion, and not uncosmos but cosmos and all-harmonious composition of
cosmos, of necessity one must reckon and take a thought of the Master who
gathered these and bound them together, and works symphony toward them. For
even if he is not seen with the eyes, yet from the order and symphony of the
opposites, it is possible to think the ruler and arranger and king of these.
For just as a city constituted from many and different men, small and great,
and rich again and poor, and again old and younger, and males and females, if
we should contemplate it being inhabited in good order, and those in it being
different, but being of one mind toward themselves, and neither the rich
coming to be against the poor, nor the great against the small, nor the young
against the old, but all being at peace according to the equal share; if we
should see these, we altogether think, that presence of a ruler presides over
the concord, even if we do not see him. For disorder is a mark of anarchy; and
the order shows the one who leads. For also seeing the symphony of the members
in the body toward themselves, that the eye does not fight the hearing, nor
the hand factions toward the foot, but each completes its own need without

we think from this altogether there is a soul in the body that leads these,

necessity think the leader of the all God, and this one one and not many. And

lyre composed from many and different strings, and would marvel at the harmony
of the symphony of these, that not only the heavy completes the sound, nor
only the sharp, nor only the middle, but all according to the equal


resistance sound together with one another; and altogether from these he

harmonic symphony by the knowledge, even if he does not see this one; so the
order in the whole world being all-harmonious, and neither the things above
factioning toward the things below, nor the things below toward the things
above, but one order of all being completed, it is consistent to think one and
not many the ruler and king of all the creation, who illuminates and moves all
things by his own light.
39 For neither must one think the rulers and makers of the creation to be
many, but toward accurate piety and truth it is fitting to believe one the
fashioner of this; and this the creation itself showing manifestly. For a
secure mark of the maker of the all being one is this, that not many but one
is the world. For it was necessary, if there were many gods, that there be
also many and different worlds. For neither was it fitting for the many to
construct one world, nor for the one to be made by many because of the
absurdities shown from these. First that, if the one world had come to be by
many, there was weakness of those who made; because from many one work was
completed; and from this there was not a chance mark of the incomplete
knowledge of each toward making. For if one sufficed, the many would not have
filled the lack of one another. And to say there is something lacking in God
is not only impious, but also beyond the unlawful things. For also a craftsman
among men one would not say perfect, but weak, if not alone, but with many he
should complete the one work. And if each was able to complete the whole, and
all worked because of the communion of that which comes to be; such a thing
would be laughable, if each worked through glory, so that he might not be
suspected as unable. And again to say empty glory among gods is of the most
absurd things. Then, if each was sufficient toward the fashioning of the
whole, what is the need of the many, one becoming self-sufficient toward the
all? And otherwise it would appear impious and absurd, if the thing made
happens to be one, and those who made different and many, there being a
natural argument that the one and perfect is better than the different. And
this also one must know, that if the world had come to be by many, it had also
the movements different and unlike itself. For looking toward each of those
who made, it had also the movements different. And in the difference, just as
has been said before, again there was uncosmos and disorder of the all; since
neither a ship steered by many will sail according to the right, unless one
steersman hold the tillers of this; nor a lyre by


many being struck will complete the sound concordant, unless one be the
craftsman who strikes this. Therefore the creation being one, and there being
one world, and one the order of this, one must think there is also one the
king and fashioner Lord of this. For this reason also the fashioner himself
has made the whole world one, so that by the constitution of the many many
also the fashioners might not be thought, but the thing made being one, one
also the maker of this may be believed. And not because the fashioner is one,
for this reason also the world is one; for God was able also to make other
worlds. But because the world that came to be is one, of necessity one must
also believe the fashioner of this to be one.
40 Who would this be? For this also it is especially necessary to make clear
and to say, so that, having been led astray by ignorance concerning this one,
someone may not suppose another, and fall again into the same godlessness as
those before. And I think no one has the thought doubtful concerning this. For
if indeed the argument showed those called gods among poets not to be gods,
and refuted those who deify the creation as wandering, and wholly showed the
idolatry of the nations to be godlessness and impiety; of all necessity these
being taken away, thereafter the pious worship is with us, and the one
worshipped and proclaimed among us, this one only is true God, who is also
Lord of the creation and fashioner of all hypostasis. Who then is this, but
the all-holy and beyond all generated essence, the Father of Christ, who, just
as a best steersman, by his own wisdom and his own Word our Lord and Savior
Christ, steers the things everywhere savingly and arranges, and makes as it
may seem to him to be well? And it is well, as it came to be and we see coming
to be, since he also wishes this; and this no one would disbelieve. For if the
movement of the creation was irrational, and the all was simply carried; well
might someone also have disbelieved the things said; but if it has been
constituted by word and wisdom and knowledge, and has been arranged with all
cosmos, of necessity the one set over and who arranged this is no other than
Word of God. And I say Word not the one interwoven and grown together in each
of the things that came to be, which some are also accustomed to call
spermatic, being soulless and reckoning nothing nor thinking, but working only
by the art from outside according to the knowledge of the one who applies it;
nor such as the rational race has a word composed from syllables, and
signified in air; but the living and active God Word himself of the good and
God of the all I say, who is other than the generated things and all the
creation, but is own and only Word of the good Father, who arranged this all
and illuminates by his own providence. For being good Word of a good father,
he himself arranged the order of all, joining the opposite things to the
opposite, and from these arranging one harmony. This one, being power of God
and wisdom of God, turns heaven, and having hung earth, and it lying upon
nothing he settled it by his own nod. Illuminated by this the sun shines upon
the inhabited world, and moon has the light measured. Through this one also
the water is hung upon clouds, and rains flood the earth, and the sea is
bounded, and the earth is leafy with all kinds of plants and bears green. And
if some unbeliever should seek concerning the things said, if there is wholly
a Word of God, such a one would rave doubting concerning Word of God; yet he
has nonetheless the proof from the things seen, that all things have been
constituted by Word of God and Wisdom, and none of the things that came to be
would have been settled, unless it had come to be by Word and by the divine
Word, just as was said.
41 And being Word, he is not according to the likeness of men, as I said,
composed from syllables; but he is an unvarying image of his own Father.


For men being composed from parts, and having come to be from that which is
not, have their own word composed and dissolved; but God is, and is not
composite; wherefore also his Word is, and is not composite; but one and
only-begotten God, who having come forth good from the Father as from a good
fountain, arranges all things and holds them together. And the cause for which
wholly the Word of God has mounted the things that came to be is truly
marvelous and making known that it was not otherwise fitting than thus to come
to be, just as also it is. For the nature of the generated things, as having
stood from things that are not, happens to be some flowing and weak and mortal
by itself when compared; but the God of the all is good and more than
beautiful in nature. Wherefore he is also a lover of men. For envy would not
come to be for a good one concerning anything; whence he does not even envy
being to anyone, but wishes all to be, so that he may also be able to love
men. Seeing then all generated nature, as far as according to its own reasons,
being flowing and being dissolved; so that it may not suffer this, and the
whole again be dissolved into not being, for this reason having made all
things by his own and eternal Word, and having given essence to the creation,
he did not leave it to be carried by its own nature and to be storm-tossed, so
that it might not also be in danger again into not being; but as good he
steers and sets the whole by his own Word, himself also being God, so that the
creation being illuminated by the leadership and providence and arrangement of
the Word may be able to remain firmly, as partaking of the really being Word
from the Father and being helped through him toward being; lest it suffer what
it would have suffered, if the Word were not keeping it, I mean the not being,
who is image of the invisible God, firstborn of all creation; because through
him and in him all things have been constituted, both the visible and the
invisible, and he himself is the head of the Church, as the ministers of the
truth teach in holy letters.
42 Himself then the all-powerful and all-perfect holy one, the Word of the
Father, having mounted all things and having spread his own powers everywhere,
and having illuminated all the things that appear and the invisible, holds
together and binds into himself, having left nothing deserted of his own
power, but all and through all, and each privately, and all the wholes at once
together making alive and guarding; and mixing the principles of all sensible
essence, which are hot and cold and wet and dry, into one, he makes them not
to stand against, but to complete one and concordant harmony. Through him and
his power neither does the fire fight the cold, nor the wet the dry; but as
friends and brothers the things being by themselves opposite, having come
together, both generate the things that appear, and become principles of being
for the bodies. Obeying this God Word the things upon earth are made alive,
and the things in the heavens are constituted. And through this one all sea
and the great ocean have their own movement in their own bounds; and all the
dry bears green and is leafy with all kinds and different plants, as I said
before. And so that I may not linger naming the each upon manifest things,
there is nothing of the things that are and come to be which has not come to
be and stood in him and through him, as also the theologian man says: In the
beginning was the Word, and the Word was toward God, and the Word was God. All
things came to be through him, and without him came to be not even one. For as
if some musician having fitted a lyre and having gathered by the art the heavy
to the sharp, and the middle to the others, should complete one signified
melody; so also the wisdom of God, holding the whole as a lyre, and having
gathered the things in air to those upon earth, and the things in heaven to
those in air, and joining the wholes to the according to part and turning them
by his own nod and will, completes one the world and one the order of this
well and fittingly, himself remaining unmoved with the Father, but moving all
things by his own constitution, as each may seem good to his own Father. For
this is the paradoxical of his


Godhead, that by one and the same nod he turns and arranges all things
together and not from intervals, but all at once, both the straight and the
circular, the things above, the middle, the things below, the wet, the cold,
the hot, the things that appear and the invisible, according to the nature of
each. For together by the same nod of his, the straight as straight, and the
circular is turned; and the middle, as it is, is moved; the hot is heated, and

constituted by him; and a marvelous and truly divine harmony is completed
through him.

true God, may begin to deify for himself the things that are not, such as are
those named among poets and writers and shown not being gods. And the very
wording shows them not to be gods, through which he says: There shall not be
for you other gods, which is signified upon a future. And that which comes to
be upon future things is not then when these things are said.
