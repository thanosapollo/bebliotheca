ENCOMIUM On the holy martyr Romanus 1.

=50.605=

αʹ. Again a memorial of martyrs, and again a feast, and a spiritual festival.
They toiled, and we rejoice; they wrestled, and we exult; theirs is the crown,
and the glory is common, or rather it is the glory of the whole Church. And
how might this come to pass? he says. Because the martyrs are our parts and
members. And if one member suffers, all =50.606= the members suffer with it;
if one member is glorified, all the members rejoice with it. The head is
crowned, and the rest of the body exults; one man becomes an Olympic victor,
and a whole people is glad, and receives him with much acclamation. And if in
the Olympic contests those who contributed nothing to those sweatings reap so
great a pleasure, much more would this come to pass in the case of the
athletes of piety. We are feet, the martyrs a head; but the head cannot say to
the feet, I have no need of you. =50.607= The members are glorious, but the
excess of glory does not produce an alienation from union with the remaining
parts; for by this they become especially glorious, when they do not thrust
away the union with us; since even an eye, though it is more splendid than all
the rest of the body, then preserves its own glory, when it is not split off
from the rest of the body. And why do I speak of martyrs? For if their Master
was not ashamed to become our head, much more are they not ashamed to be our
members; for they have love rooted in them, and love is accustomed to join and
bind together things that stand apart, and does not make exact reckoning of
rank. As therefore they suffer together with our sins, so we rejoice together
with their achievements. Thus Paul also commanded us to do, saying, Rejoice
with those who rejoice, and weep with those who weep. But to weep with those
who weep is easy, while to rejoice with those who rejoice is not very easy;
for we more readily share the pain of those in calamities than we share the
delight of those who are in good repute. For there the very nature of calamity
is able to bend even a stone toward sympathy; but here, in the case of
prosperity, envy and jealousy do not allow one who does not philosophize
greatly to become a partner of the pleasure. For just as love gathers and
binds together things that have been divided, so jealousy divides things that
have been united. Therefore I exhort, let us practice rejoicing with those in
good repute, that we may also cleanse the soul of envy and of jealousy; for
nothing so drives away this grievous and hard-to-heal disease as sharing the
delight of those who live in virtue. Hear then how Paul is supreme in both of
these: Who, he says, is weak, and I am not weak? who is made to stumble, and I
do not burn? He did not say, and I am not grieved, but, I do not burn, wishing
by the name of burning to set before us the intensity of the pain.

And again writing to others, You have reigned without us, he says; and I would
that you had reigned, that we also might reign with you; and again: Now we
live, if you stand in the Lord. See how eagerly sought by him was the good
repute of the brethren, he who did not even think he was living, if those were
not being saved. A man caught up to the third heaven, and carried away into
paradise, and made partaker of unspeakable mysteries, and enjoying so great a
freedom of speech toward God, did not take a very great perception of those
goods, unless he also saw the brethren being saved together with him. For he
knows, he knows clearly, that nothing is either greater than love or equal to
it, not even martyrdom itself, which is the sum of all goods; and how, hear.
For love even without martyrdom makes disciples of Christ, but martyrdom
without love would not have the strength to accomplish this. From where is
this clear? From the very words of Christ; for he was saying to the disciples:
By this all will know that you are my disciples, if you love one another.
Behold, love without martyrdom makes disciples. =50.608= And that martyrdom
without love not only does not make disciples, but does not even profit the
one who endures it at all, hear Paul saying: If I give my body to be burned,
and have not love, I am profited nothing.

βʹ. For this reason especially I love this saint who has gathered us together
today, the blessed Romanus, because together with martyrdom he displayed much
love, for which reason also his holy tongue was cut out. For this too is
worthy of examination, why ever the devil did not lead him to tortures and
punishments and retributions, but cut off the tongue; for he did not do this
at random, but with much wickedness; for the beast is a worker of evil, and he
contrives and sets in motion everything against our salvation. Come then, let
us examine why ever he proceeded to the cutting out of the tongue, bringing
the account a little higher at least; for thus we shall know both the
loving-kindness of God, and the endurance of the martyr, and the evil-working
of the devil; and having learned the loving-kindness of God we shall give
thanks to the Master, and having come to know the endurance of the martyr we
shall imitate our fellow-servant, and having thoroughly learned the
evil-working of the devil, we shall turn away from the enemy. For this
reason God gave us to know his devices, that having hated him more greatly we
might more easily get the better of him. For that it is possible to know his
thoughts, hear what Paul says concerning the one who had committed
fornication; for writing to the Corinthians he says somehow thus: Confirm your
love toward him, lest we be overreached by Satan; for we are not ignorant of
his thoughts. What then is the cause for which he cut out the tongue? Bear
with me a little as I narrate from above. A grievous war was once fanned up
against the Churches, not of barbarians overrunning the cities, nor of some of
another race, but of those who seemed to preside over our inhabited world, and
who treated their subjects more harshly and more cruelly than barbarians, and
enemies, and all tyrants. For the danger then was not for freedom, and
fatherland, and money, and the present life, but for the kingdom of heaven,
and the goods laid up, for immortal life, for the confession of Christ.

And a new kind of captivity was being invented; for they were not casting them
out of the city here, but they were attempting to deprive them of the
Jerusalem above, the free, and they were compelling each one to sacrifice his
soul upon the altars, and to deny his own Master, and to bow under the tyranny
of the demons, and to serve the demons who are the destroyers and enemies of
our salvation; which was more grievous and more unbearable than ten thousand
deaths and all Gehenna to the Christ-loving souls. Then indeed, when many were
going under, and the storm was rising, and many were being shipwrecked, this
blessed Romanus coming forward into the midst did not immediately consider how
he might give himself over to the dangers, but first he was gathering those
who had been frightened, those who had fallen back, those who had betrayed
their own salvation, he was encouraging them, he was preparing them to fight
back, raising up those who had fallen, and making those who were standing
secure with prayers, and exhortations, and counsels, philosophizing much
concerning the things to come, =50.609= concerning the present affairs,
showing the temporary character of these, the perpetual character of those,
setting the prizes against the toils, the crowns against the tortures, the
awards against the pains, teaching what the present life is, and what the
future, and how great the interval of each, and that it is altogether
necessary to die; for even if we do not dissolve this life in this manner,
being altogether compelled by the law of nature we shall a little later at
least depart from these bodies. These things then and things like them he
was exhorting, and he raised up the hands that were hanging down, he made firm
the knees that had been paralyzed, he brought back the fugitives, he cast out
cowardice, he drove away the agony, he put in courage, he made them eager
instead of cowardly, he wrought them into lions breathing much strength
instead of gazelles and deer, he assembled the camp of Christ, he transferred
the shame that was among us onto the heads of the enemies. The devil then,
seeing a sudden change that had come about, and those who yesterday and the
day before were fearing him and trembling, now laughing at him and daring
against him, stripping for the dangers, leaping upon the places of punishment,
and having come to know the cause, leaving all, thereafter set his whole
blast against that one, moving all his power and his madness against this
blessed head. And what does he do? Consider the evil-working. He did not lead
him to tortures, he did not cut off his head; for the time that had passed
taught him that all these things were coming to pass at random and in vain.
For not only did he
not cut short the eagerness of those who had believed, but he even stretched
it further, and made it greater and hotter.

I strewed coals, he says, and they were running as upon roses; I kindled fire,
and they threw themselves as into springs of cold waters; I tore the sides,
and I cut deep furrows, and I brought down streams of blood, and they were
adorning themselves as if gold were flowing around them on every side; I threw
them from the cliffs, and I sank them in the sea, and they were not as those
going down into the deep, but as those going up into heaven itself, so they
were disposed, leaping, and exulting, and as if dancing in a sacred
procession, and playing in a green meadow, so each was snatching the
punishments, not as punishments, but taking them as spring flowers, and being
crowned, and by the excess of their own eagerness they were outrunning my
punishments. What then would there be to do, he says? Shall I cut off this
one's head? But what he prays for, this comes to pass, and the disciples
receive a greater exhortation through the deeds; for he also exhorted that the
death of martyrs is not death, but a life that has no end, and that especially
for this one must endure all things, and that one must despise the end. If
then I cut off the head, and he bears what has happened nobly, he will
instruct them more clearly through the deeds that one must thus despise death,
and he will raise up their minds still more, and having ended he will breathe
into them a greater eagerness. For this reason he cut off the tongue, that
being deprived of the voice which the disciples of the martyr were enjoying,
and falling away from the counsel, and being left desolate of the =50.610=
exhortation, they might become more cowardly, and return to the former agony,
not having the one who anoints them for the fight, nor the one who rouses and
arms them.

γʹ. And consider for me the evil-working. Herod cut off the head of John; but
this one not the head, but the tongue only. Why ever? Because of much
evil-working and perversity. If I cut off the head, he says, and he dies, he
will go away not seeing the destruction of his own brethren; but I wish to set
him as a witness of the falls and of the calamity of his own soldiers, that he
may be choked by the pain, seeing them falling, but not being able to stretch
out a hand, and not having power to counsel those things which he had also
before, the voice having been cut out together with the tongue. But he who
catches the wise in their craftiness turned this sophism onto that one's head,
and not only did he not deprive them of counsel, but he even made them enjoy a
greater exhortation, and partake of a more spiritual teaching. For when this
prevailed, the physician is thereafter called to the cutting, and he becomes
an executioner instead of a physician, not correcting the member that has
fallen sick, but destroying the one that is healthy; and having taken out the
tongue, he did not have the strength to take out together with it also the
voice; but the tongue of the flesh was being cut out, while the tongue of
grace was flying upon the mouth of the blessed one; and nature was letting go
its own member, the iron compelling this, but grace did not allow the voice to
fall out together with it; whence thereafter the disciples were enjoying a
more spiritual teaching, not hearing with a human voice as before, but with a
certain divine one, and spiritual, and greater than our nature; and all were
running together, angels from above, men from below, each desiring to see a
mouth without a tongue, and to hear one speaking thus.

For it was truly a wondrous and unexpected thing, a mouth speaking without a
tongue, bringing much shame to the devil, and much glory to the martyr, and
great consolation to the disciples and a ground of endurance. For such is the
custom of God from above and from the beginning, whatever the devil may stitch
together against us, to turn these onto that one's head, and to arrange them
for our salvation. Consider: that one cast man out of paradise, and God opened
heaven to him; that one drove him from the rule upon the earth, and God gave
him the kingdom of the heavens, and settled our nature upon the royal throne.
Thus he always gives greater goods than those from which the devil attempts to
deprive. And he does this, both making that one more hesitant in the plots
against us, and teaching us never to fear his devices; which indeed also
happened here in the case of the martyr. For of the voice of which he expected
to deprive him, God granted him a much greater and more august one. For it was
not equal to speak with a tongue, and without a tongue; for the one was
according to sequence, and common to all, but the other was beyond nature, and
became peculiar to him alone. And yet even if the martyr had stood
voiceless after the cutting out of the tongue, even so the things of the
contests would have been fulfilled for him, and the crown would have been
completed; for the cutting of the tongue was the greatest defeat and a clear
proof. For if you did not =50.611= fear the tongue, O abominable and
all-abominable one, for what reason did you cut it off? for what reason did
you not leave the matter of the wrestlings, but shut the stadium? Just as if
someone about to contend in the pancration, then having received unspeakable
blows, and no longer being able to stand against, should command the hands of
the antagonist to be cut out, and thus should strike him, there is no longer
need of another proof for voting the victory to the one whose hands were cut
out; so indeed also in the case of the martyr the cutting out of the tongue
became a clearer proof than all of the victory against the devil. For even if
the tongue was mortal, yet since it had given immortal wounds to the devil,
for this reason he set his whole blast against it, setting himself into a
greater shame, and making the crown more splendid for the martyr. For just as
it is wondrous to see a tree without a root, and a river without a spring; so
also a voice without a tongue.

δʹ. Where now are those who distrust the resurrection of bodies? Behold, the
voice both died and rose, and in one turn of a moment both of these came to
pass. And yet this is much greater than the resurrection of bodies; for there
the nature of the bodies lies underneath, and the composition only has been
dissolved; but here the very underlying matter of the voice had been taken
away, yet nevertheless it was becoming more splendid again. And yet if you
take away the reeds of a flute, the instrument thereafter lies useless; but
not so the spiritual flute, but even having been deprived of the tongue it was
not only not voiceless, but it was also sending forth a more tuneful and more
mystical strain, and with a greater astonishment. And again in the case of a
lyre, if someone snatches away only the plectrum, the craftsman is idle, the
art useless, the instrument becomes vain; but here nothing of the kind, but
quite the contrary.

For the mouth was a lyre, the tongue was a plectrum, and the soul a craftsman,
and the confession an art; yet nevertheless when the plectrum had been taken
away, I mean the tongue, neither the craftsman, nor the art, nor the
instrument became useless, but all displayed their own power. Who did these
things? who displayed these wondrous and unexpected things? God who alone does
wonders, concerning whom David says, O Lord our Lord, how wondrous is your
name in all the earth; because your magnificence has been lifted up above the
heavens; out of the mouth of babes and sucklings you have perfected praise.
Then therefore out of the mouth of babes and sucklings, but now out of the
mouth of the =50.612= tongueless; then an unripe nature; but now a deserted
mouth; then the root in the children was tender, but the fruit was completed;
but here even the root itself had been taken away, and the generation of the
fruit was not hindered; for the fruit of a tongue is a voice. The later things
are more wondrous than the former. For in order that these things might not be
disbelieved, for this reason those ran ahead, that we might not be thrown into
confusion in these, our mind having been trained beforehand in those. For this
reason these met us, that those things that are unseen and ancient might be
believed from the things that are manifest and recently come to pass. Thus
once also the rod of Aaron blossomed, just as the mouth of the martyr
blossomed now. But for what reason did the rod of Aaron blossom then? Because
the priest was being dishonored. And for what reason did the mouth of the
martyr blossom now? Because the great high priest was being blasphemed, Jesus
the Christ. See how great both the kinship and the superiority of the wonder.
For just as that rod, not united to a root, nor drawing the moistures from the
earth, but being deserted of the supply from there, and having lost the
fruit-bearing power, suddenly showed fruit; so indeed also here the voice,
having been deprived of the root, and not having the power from the
instrument, suddenly blossomed in a dry and barren mouth. In this the kinship,
and in another the superiority; for great is the interval of each fruit. For
the one was sensible, but the other spiritual, and opening the very heavens to
the one who then brought forth this voice.

For all these things then let us rejoice with the martyr, let us glorify God
who works these wonders, let us imitate the endurance of our fellow-servant,
let us give thanks for the grace to the Master, let us take from the things
that have been said a sufficient consolation in temptations, and having been
struck with awe at the power of God who made us and at his care, let us
contribute all that is from ourselves, and the things from him will altogether
follow. Even if men, even if demons, even if the devil himself box against us,
there will be nothing more for those who war against us; only if we display
our own eagerness, and contribute all that must be contributed from us. For
thus we shall draw to ourselves the inclination from God here, and in the life
to come we shall enjoy much glory and salvation; of which may it come to pass
that we all attain, by the grace and loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus Christ,
with whom to the Father, together with the Holy Spirit, be glory, honor and
power, unto the ages of ages. Amen.
