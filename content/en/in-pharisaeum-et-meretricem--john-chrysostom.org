On the Pharisee, and on the harlot. Spoken on Holy and Great Wednesday.
=61.727=
Every season is suitable for repentance to anyone who wishes. For it is
possible for each who wills both in spring to make the good life sprout, and
in summer to gather the sheaves of salvation, and in autumn to behold the
damage, the corruption of the soul, and in winter to flee the winter of
lawless deeds, and in every turning to turn the turning toward what is better,
and in night to shun the night of sin, and in day to run toward the light of a
pure conscience, and to run back to the One who formed him; but this present
season, fenced about with self-control and fasting, is more suitable for
repentance, yet not for all. For the voice all but says, If anyone comes to
me, I too, taking him, will bring him to the common Creator. Let us therefore
wing ourselves with the wings of faith and of repentance, and let us will to
fly up to our Maker, both we, as firstborn sons of the Church, and you, who as
embryos are being brought to birth in the flanks of the Church, and who long
to embrace the blessed and painless birth-pangs, and to become children of him
who waged war for us (for the grace of faith is necessary also for you who are
about to enjoy the same goods); we, that we may wash with tears the royal
tunic which, having put it on, we soiled; but you, that you may be able to put
this on, and keep it better than every stain. For without sincere faith it is
not possible for anyone to obtain the loving-kindness of God. That harlot,
among countless others, speaks in support of my word, who, taking faith and
repentance as two lamps, ran to the heavenly bridegroom, and became the bride
of Christ in one day. But since I have mentioned the blessed harlot; for I
still call her by the reproachful name (that we may know what she was
formerly, what she became later), I wish to bring her into the holy Church,
that we too may learn from her how we ought to approach Christ, and receive
the release of sins.
For the things done by her are a clear teaching of what ought always to be
done by us; and the Savior's converse with her will be a consolation of
sinners. For who, hearing that a woman marked with countless stains, merely
that she fled to him, and poured hot drops of tears upon his feet, received
the ordination of virginity? Show the same manner, that you may harvest the
same fruit. For who, considering her change, and the recompense, will not
exercise the same mind, that he may find the equal repayment? For what is a
harlot, but corruption, or a manifest outrage of nature, or a public triumph
of the female sex, a ruin of bodies and of souls, a plague that uses delight,
a whitened tomb, an unrestrainable and insatiable mouth of Hades, a beautified
forecourt of death, a pleasant poison, shamelessness speaking freely, a
shameless shame, a fair-faced destruction, a bait of licentious devices,
birdlime of the young, a trafficker of her own members, a depth of perdition,
a beloved shipwreck, a common disaster of every sin, a bold workshop, a
teacher of every lawlessness, a creator of enmity toward God, the matter of
the Gehenna of eternal fire? But nevertheless she who was the servant of such
and so great evils, when contrary to habit she fell in love with temperance,
has been written in the books of the Gospels. Having unrolled these

=61.728= ll of Christ. One of the Pharisees asked Jesus that he might eat with
him. And having entered into the house of the Pharisee, he reclined. Who has
seen a king summoned by a soldier? who has seen a master fed by a slave? who
has seen so great a height descending into so great a lowliness? O the
exceeding astonishment! o the divine condescension! He did not say to the
Pharisee who invited him: You invite me to breakfast, me who invite all men to
the heavenly breakfast? you invite me to breakfast, me who with five loaves
satisfied a whole people? you invite me to breakfast, me who give food to all
flesh?
The Savior said none of these things; but sitting in silence he reclined in
the house of the Pharisee. For he knew what breakfast he was about to
breakfast; he knew how the harlot was about to run to him, and be saved; and
he sent his glance everywhere, surveying the hunt, and all but crying to those
reclining. I have food to eat, which you will see after a little; I have food
to eat, the salvation of the woman who is about to come to me. For my food is
the turning of men; my food is the life of my slaves; my feast is the
repentance of sinners; my delight is the release of the condemned; my banquet
is the ransom of captives. While the Savior was saying these things on the
couch with his glance and his bearing, Behold, he says, a woman in the city,
who was a sinner. The catch ran together with the aim of the heavenly king;
the hunter had reclined ready, and the prey was hastening toward the hunter.
And behold a woman in the city, who was a sinner. Two grievous marks of
wickedness. Tell us, evangelist, her appellation, write the name of the woman,
let us learn how she was called.
She had sin as her appellation, and wickedness as her name. This woman, seeing
that Jesus was reclining in the house of the Pharisee, said in herself: Within
is Christ, who abounds in loving-kindness, and gushes with compassions; within
is Christ, who speaks and gives healings, and utters cures; within is Christ,
who is hymned by the angels, and dwells among men, as one of them; within is
Christ who shares the throne with the Father, and the same roof with the
Pharisee; within is Christ, who feeds all by his divinity, and is fed by his
humanity; within is Christ, and he endures a Pharisaic table, he who fills
every table with every good; and he wholly deigned to enter into the
Pharisee's tent; the sinless one wholly endured to feast together with the
sinner. I have good hopes concerning my evils. For he who has shown himself
such toward the Pharisee will surely be good and loving also toward me. I have
found, as I wished, the common mercy-seat, I have found him who finds those
who seek him, I have found him who seeks those who long for him, I have found
him who runs to those who run and meets those who come to him, I have found
both the time and the place striving together with me. Let me enter toward the
pure spring, I who am mired in pleasure; let me not remain filthy, such a
spring having appeared; I will wash myself freely; for so the spring wishes.
Let me enter toward the eternal light, I who have used even days as nights;
let me see the heavenly light in light; let me enter toward the sun of
righteousness, I who bring on the gloom of injustice.
For who conversing with light does not partake of light? Let me enter toward
the king of =61.729= the heavens, who for my sake has come to me, and is
occupying this house, and as it seems has long been waiting for my coming
forth; let me enter toward the heavenly physician, I who have been given up by
all; let me enter toward the heavenly physician, I who am sick in health; for
so seeing me, he is able to apply to my passions a medicine able to put every
pain to sleep. I will show to him who knows them


the wounds, and I will receive the medicine. Let me enter toward the only
life, I who through the vilest life have cut myself off from life; let me
enter toward the loving judge, I the condemned. Before the resurrection from
the dead I will dissolve the accounts, before the fearful judgment I will
escape the judgment, before the dread tribunal I will steal away the scourges;
before I see Christ declaring judicially, let me see him conversing lovingly;
before I fear him punishing, I will court him present. I am not the first to
dare converse with him; already another also, as I have heard, a Samaritan
woman, lawless in the law, met at the well with the Well, and went away having
drawn knowledge of God. And again another woman of the Canaanite race, a free
shoot of the accursed and slavish seed, having come in an encounter against a
demon tyrannizing her only-begotten, returned having received the authority
against the demon, and the mother became a physician to the daughter, and that
cruel and barbarous demon no longer dared to approach the girl, having feared
the command of the voice of Christ.
Let me therefore imitate this woman also, and let me become beside him who
grants all things and is able and has chosen; let me use the habit of
shamelessness toward what is profitable for me; let me present myself to the
Savior as he feasts, self-invited, having as it were the form of a city
feasted by many; I will loose my hair, and I will lament my own wretchedness;
I will slacken the many-plaited nets, that I may hunt the mercy that supplies
unsparingly; I will weep well once, I who have laughed badly many times; I
will weep now before the loving judge, that I may not weep at the last
unprofitably; I will lament now a little, that I may not lament then much; I
will flood the feet of Christ with tears, that he may wash away my lawless
deeds with his words; I will wipe the tears with my locks, that some
sanctification may come from this also to these; I will kiss the undefiled
tracks, which even the sea kissed, that with chaste kisses I may spit out the
venom from the licentious kisses; I will embrace the divine members, the
virginal, those from a virgin, that to my condemned members I may channel from
those holy members some drop of righteousness.
The compassionate one will be bent toward these things; he will have mercy on
her who falls before him, whom he did not punish when she was sinning; he will
have mercy on her who kneels, whom he continued to benefit while she danced
disorderly; he will have mercy on her who wishes to be saved, whom he bore
gently when he found her perishing. He does not bear to overlook genuine
tears, he cannot fail to appear toward me also what he is by nature,
compassionate; great power with him has a groan coming up from the depth of
the soul; repentance has great freedom of speech toward him; he will surely
speak also to me something gentle and calm, and fitting to his divinity. These
things the wise woman meditated, these the faithful woman did. And having
arrayed herself in the woman's wrap, virginally, and not harlot-wise, and so
having hidden all her form which before this was set forth to all, as in a
theater, and having looked around safely from every side, that no harlot
figure might enter in with her toward the Lord accusing her, she ran into the
Pharisee's chamber, and standing at the Master's feet, she fulfilled the
purpose premeditated. For having brought, he says, an alabaster flask of
myrrh, and standing behind at his feet, weeping she began to wet his feet with
the tears, and with the hairs of her head she wiped them, she kissed his feet,
and anointed them with the myrrh, speaking by silence, and crying by quiet,
and uttering with the soul toward him who knows how to hear souls in pain, and
mouths that do not speak, writing with the tears, as is likely, such a
supplication: I was cast away as a lost sheep; but I recognized you as Master,
my shepherd and Lord; I became a prey of beasts because I wished, but I
received a sense to flee the bitter bites of the wolf; I was shipwrecked many
times willingly, but I was saved from the shipwreck, having seized your
presence as an anchor. But I have no freedom of speech;


=61.730= for every sinner is without freedom of speech; I have no
justification whatever; for every sinner and lawless one is without
justification; I have no voice; for every condemned person is voiceless; the
wicked conscience stops my mouth, which my corrupted life has corrupted; the
accusing wealth throttles the tongue, always crying from within me, Shut your
word within your lips; the mouth of sinners is without freedom of speech
toward intercession, and a condemned tongue is unfitted for supplication, and
a counterfeit word is not fair-faced toward asking. Being silent I teach you
who know all things the things concerning me; I speak with the face, I cry
with the form, with the eyes I proclaim the lawless deeds, with the tears I
confess the faults, with the groans I declare you who alone cannot be hidden.
As a captive with all my weapons I fall before you, the king of the heavens;
and all my members, through which I made war against you, or rather against
myself, I offer to you, O loving one. Have mercy on this head, Because my
iniquities have gone over my head; have mercy on these hairs, which you
formed; have mercy on these eyes, which you created; have mercy on this clay,
which you yourself shaped into beauty, but I used toward the world unto
formless unseemliness; have mercy on your image, on which the enemy has
sported as he wished; have mercy on your statue, which your enemy dragged
about as ownerless; receive as a ransom even tears, and as good loose the
charges; only will it, and I am loosed; only nod, and I am ransomed; command,
and the longed-for thing is accomplished. For what you will is a deed; and
your word is a work. Let me receive freedom from your undefiled feet, as the
woman with the issue of blood stole the healing from your hems, you having
willed this to be stolen; let me too become a narrative of your
loving-kindness to those after me. Send me on, and the things concerning me,
as a fair memorial of your compassions to later generations; let all say that
a harlot once lawless and condemned, having approached Jesus, was justified by
him; and seeing, let all come with me, and seize your gifts. Just and loving
judge, who through much loving-kindness also associate with the condemned, I
have approached you here as judge, here dictate the pardon to me; do not
triumph my sins over all creation, do not publish my lawless deeds in the
coming tribunal; do not send me to the eternal fire, which has as its matter
the life of the careless. Jesus Christ, Son of the Virgin Mary, and creator of
the Virgin, for the sake of the Virgin, both your mother, and again a virgin,
recover through that holy one me the unholy, and make me holy. From a woman
you too were born, as you alone know, and you know the weakness of men. While
the faithful woman was saying such things in herself and entreating, the
Savior being near granted to her who longed to take her fill of the longing,
and to put those feet into her hands for unsated enjoyment, being on the
couch, and taking his fill of her weeping, and feasting on the tears of
repentance, and hungering the thirst of her who thirsted for mercy.
From this the Pharisees quietly whispered to one another saying: What is this
strange and paradoxical spectacle? who taught this harlot to be temperate? who
persuaded her who was poured out toward shameful pleasures to think such
things? is this not the notable disgrace of our city? is this not the woman of
those who always wish? is this not the accuser of each man's sin? is this not
the enemy of temperance, and the housebreaker of lawful marriages? is this not
she who with flattery plunders men? How then from such will she become such?
how has she who is fond of laughter and fond of delight appeared fond of tears
and fond of mourning? how has she who is unwise, and untimely, both in form
and in glance, and in bearing and in curious walkings, and with all her
members betrothing all to death, fallen over to this temperate figure and


deed? O the unexpected change! o the strange exchange. The household servants
used such words; but the Pharisee who invited Jesus, begetting blasphemous
reasonings in his own soul, said: This man, if he were a prophet, would have
known who and what sort of woman this is who touches him, that she is a
sinner. Simply, he says, and as it chan =61.731= ced, this man is called a
prophet; I too was deceived with the others, having brought this man into my
house as an examiner of things unseen. For if he were a prophet, he would also
have clearly known the things to come. Not knowing what is manifest to all and
quite clear, how then does he understand the things secret from all? how does
he endure harlot hands? how does he not drive far off her who has no part of
the body unused? how does he not even abhor the abominable one, full of every
shamefulness? The Pharisee faults the great loving-kindness as ignorance, and
subjects the condescension to the name of ignorance, himself being sick with
ignorance of what is happening. And answering Jesus said to him: I have
something to say to you. I know what you say though saying nothing, I know
what you utter being silent, I know what you cry being quiet, I know what she
too converses with me according to her mind, I too know how many reasonings
you forge in your soul against me. I will narrate to you the voices of the
thoughts, I will confess the things blasphemed by you, I will publish the
sentiments of your notions, as one seated in the reasonings of your soul. Thus
you say, If he were a prophet, he would have known who and what sort of woman
this is who touches him, that she is a sinner. These are the words of your
mind. How then did I discern the storm of the reasonings moved in the depth of
your mind, if I were not God, creator of all? As you yourself suspect from the
answer, know who I am answering you who speak nothing; I uncover the words of
your soul. If I were a prophet, would I know who and what sort of woman? and
where is there need of prophetic illumination upon a recognized face? do I not
know her who has stood forth in the marketplace? do I not know her who is
quite clear to all? do I not understand this woman, for whose sake I came to
you? I know who and what sort of woman this is; for nothing escapes the eye of
the divinity, not a greedy man, not a wickedness; I know who and what sort of
woman this is, and that she is a sinner; and for this reason I rejoice being
held by her, that I may save god-fittingly her who holds me piously. For I am
not defiled by her hands, but I work her clean. For mire does not darken the
sun, clay does not change a pearl, sin does not touch the sinless one. Did I
not touch a leper the other day, and the passion fled? did I become a leper,
having drawn near to a leper? So also now working the accustomed wonders,
changing her toward holiness, I am not changed toward her quality, enduring
the touch of the intervening hand. For I am a physician; and a physician not
of wounds and putrefactions and ichors, but one who works salvation for those
sick in soul; I am a maker, and I spare my own formation; I am a master, and I
do not endure that a master-loving slave-woman be lost; I am a shepherd, and
having found my sheep wandering I naturally rejoice; I am a harbor, and I
receive all who put in. Simon, I have something to say to you. There were two
debtors to a certain lender. For the present you have been ranked with this
woman; why do you campaign against her, as if you alone had gone up to the
acropolis of the virtues? for the present you stand liable to me; why then do
you condemn this woman, as if you alone were not liable? two debtors, you and
she happen to be; but the lender is I; I lent to you and to the woman the
present life. The one owed five hundred denarii, and the other fifty. This
woman owes five hundred denarii; for she has sinned much; you owe fifty; for
you have stumbled a little; but nevertheless you are subject to an accounting
of life. And they themselves not having anything to repay, he granted freely
to both. I have granted you the charges, I grant also to this woman the
faults; you first enjoyed my presence, let her too enjoy my unremembering of
evil.


Did you wish me to appear loving toward you, but inhuman toward this woman?
But I do not endure to take you as administrator of my goodness. If you had
such a place, and she yours, would you have wished her to say concerning you
such things as you think against her? Did you not hear that, What you do not
wish to suffer, do not will to do; for this is the common law of nature. Who
then, tell me, will love him more? And answering Simon said, I suppose, that
he to whom he granted the more. And the Savior to him: You have judged
rightly. Therefore remain in what you yourself first judged. And having turned
to the woman, he says to Simon, Do you see this woman? I no longer call her a
harlot, her who has thrown away with the deed also the =61.732= name. Do you
see this woman who self-invited came in upon us, who displays a praised
shamelessness, who practices a beloved lack of shame, the guide of the road
that leads to me, the leader of piety, the teacher of repentance, her who
taught the matters by deed, her who weeps unceasingly, the freedom of speech
of sinners, the herald of my compassion, her who made your house a church, her
who prays alone to the Alone, her who beholds me as a man and supplicates me
as God, with no bodyguards, no judicial tribunal, and kneeling to me as judge,
worshiping him who lies down? Do you see this woman, the unwearied, the worker
of good things, her who through her locks and her tears weaves for herself the
robe of immortality, her who intercedes through all her members concerning
herself, her who was unsated in things in which it was not right to look, more
unsated in things in which the unmeasured is blessed? Consider the things done
by you, and behold the things done by her, and you will crown with me her who
has crowned herself. If you too do not marvel at her piety and her work, blame
my compassions. I entered into your house, I the Christ, who am above and
below and everywhere and here thus as you see, and by my presence I have
honored you, and your house; and you did not give water under my feet. You did
not pour upon my feet the easy thing, the ready thing, the easily gotten; but
this woman, who is reviled by you, wet my feet with tears, which all the
waters of the sea and the waves did not dip. You see a strange watering
flowing upon my feet; you see a paradoxical rain, not brought from the clouds,
but born from the woman's eyes. What spring is so well supplied with streams,
as this woman with tears? what fountain gushing so many drops is not put to
shame? This woman wet my feet with tears, and wiped them with her hairs, and
made her hairs a strange towel, and made her locks a paradoxical wiping-cloth
of my feet. You did not give me a kiss; you did not deign to fit your lips to
my lips; but this woman, from the time she entered, has not ceased kissing my
feet. O the even attendance! o divine kisses! o spiritual kisses that have
made defense for the former entwinings. You did not anoint my head with oil;
you did not moisten the more precious part of the body with plain oil; but
this woman anointed my feet with oil of myrrh. What then do you think? shall I
turn away from her who brings me such guest-gifts? shall I shake off the gifts
dear to me? shall I not be put to shame by such and so great a disposition?
shall I not be put to shame by a faith higher than heaven? shall I overlook
such and so great goods; shall I overlook pitiable groans? But I am not such
by nature.
Shall I overlook the streams of tears? But I cannot. Shall I overlook the
tear-stained form? For loving-kindness alone I took the form of a slave. Shall
I deny myself because of you? shall I hate her who thus genuinely longs for
me? shall I drive off her who thus entwines my feet? shall I chase away the
suppliant who has not obtained what she hoped? shall I fulfill the will of the
devil? shall I grant the prey to the enemy? shall I send my dove again toward
the hawk? shall I not pity her who nothing else


says, except only, have mercy on me? shall I not have mercy on her who has
thus fled the devil, and fled to me? Why then did I come down from the
heavens? and why have I put on the appearing body? and why did I become within
your house? if I were going to drive off her who appears and wishes to be
hunted, why did I occupy the place of the hunt? But, I did not come to call
the righteous, but sinners to repentance. For which reason I say to you, Her
many sins have been forgiven, because she loved much. But he who loves little,
will be loved little. Having conversed these things with the Pharisee, Christ
shared with her who was poured around him a glance and utterance gentle and
kind, and having bent himself a little toward her who was hidden and
entreating, he shared with her the longed-for sentence, having forgiveness of
the sins. Your sins have been forgiven. Receive then, O woman, the petition of
the tears subscribed; receive the repayment of faith, receive the recompenses
of obedience, receive the gifts of piety, receive the crown of repentance,
receive the sweetest fruit of the bitter groans, =61.733= receive the sweetest
harvest of the gloomy seed.
You sowed in tears, harvest in gladness. Your sins have been forgiven. The
cords of the evil life have been torn, the chains of wickedness have been
loosed; you have been released from the charges and the accounts, you have
fled the unquenchable furnace of fire, with few tears you have quenched so
great a flame, you have trampled the head of the wicked enemy, you have
conquered the adversary conspicuously. Take courage, daughter, God is he who
justifies, who is he who condemns? I the judge become surety of the
forgiveness; who else will overturn my sentence? I the judge release; who else
will condemn? Take courage, daughter; consider suddenly how you have been
raised, how you came as condemned, and were counted worthy to be freed from
condemnation, and to become a daughter of me the king of the heavens. You came
as wounded to receive a medicine, and as a queen you put on a royal crown. You
were born again without a womb, you were reshaped without clay, you were
recast without fire, you were baptized without a font. You yourself mixed a
font from your tears; my word has become your baptism; my word is the
illumination of your face. Take courage, daughter, your faith has saved you.
As you believed, you drew; as you longed, you received; as you willed, you
reigned. You stooped through the body, and you have become wholly full of
light. Your faith has saved you. For faith is a beginning of salvation, faith
a prelude of immortality, faith a pure eye of knowledge of God, faith sees the
things unseen. Your faith has saved you. Would that Jews also had imitated
your faith! Would that Greeks also had emulated your mind! Would that all men
might learn to believe as you! Go in peace.
Since if you should will, you will have me always with you and together with
you; if you remain thus chaste, I will never be separated from your mind if
you keep this purity undefiled, I will always remain with you; if you remain a
virgin bride, I will be to you a virgin bridegroom, a bridegroom guardian of
incorruption; if you do not set aside the dowries accomplished between us
today, I will not withdraw from your cohabitation; if you do not fornicate
against me, I will not cast you out of my intimacy; if you do not bring upon
me an adulterous thought, as a heavenly suitor I will also gift you the
heavenly things; if you keep unwounded the conscience toward me, you will
enjoy my goodwill always. Go in peace. Remember the present day, on which you
enjoyed the freedom; remember these good tears, through which you were
established in so great a brightness; remember this repentance, through which
you found the daughter's =61.734= dignity. Go in peace. I have been reconciled
to you, having for a long time before this been grieved toward you because of
you. Do not loose the good peace by wicked deeds; do not again make war on me
through your members; do not again plait, as Satan, the hair; do not
whitewash, as a wall,


your face; do not contrive a curious and destructive adornment; do not set
forth, as in a marketplace, your face to the merchants of shameful pleasures;
do not send the eyes, as a hunter, against those who are willingly hunted; do
not use the lips, as arrows, against those who are wounded with pleasure; do
not make your mouth death; do not condemn the hands with unseemly entwinings;
do not sentence the feet to shameful courses; do not overturn your good course
with evil courses; do not scatter the pearls of chastity, which you chose
without toil; do not become again a workshop of corruption; do not run back to
your ancient deceit; do not take up again the former tent; do not build badly
what you dissolved well; do not receive the serpent as counselor, that you may
not be stripped as Eve; do not leave the spring of faith for pleasure, that
the many-formed dragon may not cast his venom against you; do not sleep the
sleep of pleasure, that you may not move my just wrath against yourself; do
not after so great myrrh pour mire upon yourself; do not become again what you
were; once you were cleansed, once you were recast; remain a newly-wrought
statue of temperance, that you may not be recast by the fire. Go in peace;
announce to those who meet you, how good I am and loving; display to those who
do not know him who wiped your stain; become henceforth a net of life. Until
the present day you hunted the young unto perdition; from the present day fish
all men unto salvation; cry before Paul, as having been netted before Paul:
Faithful is the word, and worthy of all acceptance, that Christ Jesus came
into the world to save sinners, of whom I am first.
Let us all emulate this woman's repentance, that we may acquire the equal
freedom of speech; let us loose the injustices, as she the hairs; let us
sprinkle such tears, that we may receive such medicines; let us thus kiss the
Master, that we may thus be kissed by him; let us too hold with hands of faith
him who is gladly held, and let us never leave him, until he say even to us by
the very deeds: Your sins have been forgiven. Take courage, children; your
faith has saved you. And let us inscribe that woman's faith in our thoughts,
sending up glory to the Father and to the Son and to the Holy Spirit, now and
always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
