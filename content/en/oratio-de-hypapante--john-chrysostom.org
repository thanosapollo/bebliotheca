* Oration on the Meeting
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of our father among the saints John, archbishop of Constantinople, Chrysostom,
a discourse on the Meeting of our Lord Jesus Christ, delivered in Antioch,
containing at the end a most beneficial moral. Father, bless.

Bright is the theatre for us today, and the assembly more splendid than the
usual; what then is the cause? whenever we interpret the evangelical sayings,
the church becomes full with the multitude of hearers; for indeed, beloved,
whenever a virgin is evangelized, then the salvation of the inhabited world
comes to be. But since some have babbled concerning what was just now read,
asking why we have now addressed what does not bear on the present festival, I
exhort your love to be led by the ordinances of the priesthood, and to doubt
nothing of the things ordained by the fathers. And since the sheep resist the
shepherds, it is just that we set forth the time when Simeon received the
Lord.

But attend to me with all exactness; for I wish to make the teaching clearer,
that the things said may be easy to take in for the hearers. The blessed
virgin then, Mary the Theotokos, bore the Lord in the month of December, as we
persuaded in Antioch all who contradicted when we were explaining to you the
conception of the Forerunner and the silence of Zechariah, as those who labor
at the scriptures remember, when we also brought forward ancient testimonies
agreeing with the evangelical words. Count then from the birth of Christ until
now, how many days there are; that is, from the twenty-fifth =58= of the month
of December until this present; complete seven days of December, measuring
also the very day when the virgin bore, and thirty-one of January, and two of
this present month, and they become forty days.

Since then the Lord came, having been incarnate of the holy virgin, he was
eager to take up the law in his own flesh, that he might give it fullness and
thus begin to give the gifts of grace. For this truly belongs to the all-wise
economy of the Lord, to take up the commands of the law in his own flesh, that
he might show the law to be a type and shadow of the Spirit and of grace. And
that the law proclaimed all things prophetically, hear Paul saying: "the law
is spiritual", and again: "the law has become our pedagogue unto Christ".
Since then Christ, as has been said before, was eager to do all the things of
the law, that he might give an end to the law and begin to give the gifts of
grace, and having shown the law to be a type and shadow of the true things of
grace, let us hear the law itself crying: "For the Lord spoke," he says, "to
Moses saying: If a woman is sown with a bed of seed and bears a son, she shall
be unclean seven days; and on the eighth day she shall circumcise the child,
the flesh of his foreskin, and she shall sit in blood and in her clean
thirty-three days." The day then on which it is permitted for the infant to be
circumcised is one of the thirty-three days. Count then seven days of
uncleanness, and thirty-three of purification, and they become forty days;
behold then until today from the twenty-fifth of the month of December there
are forty days. Since then we have persuaded your love concerning the number
of the days and the nature of the law, let us come to Simeon who walks upon
earth and dances with angels. But if you still doubt that the virgin did not
altogether act according to the law, hear what the evangelist says: "When the
days of their purification were fulfilled according to the law of Moses, they
brought the =60= child up to Jerusalem to present him to the Lord, as it is
written in the law of the Lord: that every male opening the womb shall be
called holy to the Lord." See then, the evangelist himself persuades us; she
completed the forty days according to the law of Moses. And then "they brought
the child up to Jerusalem to present him to the Lord", that is, to the Father,
him who had not yet been separated from the Father; "they brought him up to
present to the Lord" the Lord of all the ages; "they brought him up to
present" the one enthroned with the Father, to whom the Cherubim stand by with
fear; "they brought him up," says the evangelist, "to present him to the Lord
as it is written in the law of the Lord, that every male opening the womb
shall be called holy to the Lord." Why then did the Lord, having come, do the
thing of the law? that he might lead the law aside, for this is proper to the
wisdom and foreknowledge of God. For he was shutting out their evil thoughts
both through the writings and through the words, saying "I came not to destroy
the law but to fulfill it." For neither did the Jews hesitate to invent
accusations against the Lord, wherefore even while doing the commandments of
the law, he was thus dishonored by them. And if he did not do the things of
the law, who then was shutting their shameless mouths when they were about to
say such things, that from some feebleness, not being able to do the commands
of the law, he invents some other light law to preach, himself not being able
to do or to practice the things of the law? Nevertheless he does not follow
their words, since he was doing many mysteries for the sake of our salvation
and economy; yet he was eager both to draw their salvation and to fulfill
toward us the mystery of the economy. For his doing the things of the law is
nothing else than that he may give fullness to the law, and begin to give the
gifts of grace. †Not this only† to be a type and shadow of the true things of
grace. And God the Word took up in his own flesh =62= the things of the law
and, having shut the law, opened through his own compassion the charisms of
the gift. Let us then marvel at Paul crying: "O the depth of the wealth and
wisdom and knowledge of God, how unsearchable are his judgments and
untraceable his ways." And Christ cries: "I came not to destroy the law or the
prophets, but to fulfill." For truly the Son of God and Father came to take up
in his own flesh the things of the law, not having brought the flesh down from
somewhere above, but having entered into the womb of the virgin and been
incarnate from her; no infant having pre-existed, but after entering into her
belly then being incarnate from the members of the virgin without seed. And
the all-holy virgin, having conceived him from the Holy Spirit, whom the
universe could not contain, bore him incarnate. O the wonder of the fearful
and dread mystery; who having heard is not struck? who having thought would
not marvel? that he whom the universe did not contain, a virginal womb
contained. Since then the law has it that the woman who bears should bring in
with her a yearling lamb for a whole-burnt-offering, and two turtledoves or
two young pigeons, she did not bring in the lamb, because she did not have it
at hand. For indeed the law also says: "And if their hand is not well
supplied, they shall bring two turtledoves or the two young of the pigeons."

Did you see where God dwelt, he who alone is rich? do you see into what
poverty and beggary he came down? and such poverty that the virgin was not
well supplied even as far as a lamb? know then where God dwells, not in a very
wealthy woman sunk in money, but in the wealth of a virtuous soul. And the
parents among us, whenever they are about to yoke their own children to a
wife, search where there is some rich girl having masses of money, then if she
is fair of face =64= and very beautiful, and they do not search out whether
she is chaste or anything else of such things, but wealth that always wings
the souls of the young, and the fair look that easily slips, which after a
little procures lamentations for the husband. Let us then imitate the Lord and
Master who became poor for us, and did all things for a type and for our
salvation. The blessed Mary the Theotokos then kept chastity above every human
nature, and through this she conceived the Lord in the womb. And if there were
anywhere some other virgin adorned with chastity above this virgin, the Lord
would altogether have passed her by and dwelt in that one. Since then he
himself was the lamb sacrificed for the life of the world, the unblemished
lamb as John also cried him a lamb, there was no need of another lamb. For
true are the words of Paul: "for it is impossible," he says, "that the blood
of goats and bulls take away sins." And concerning the slaughter of the lamb
the fellow-chief of Paul, Peter, cries thus saying: "By whose bruise we were
healed." Since then he himself was the lamb whom the law prefigured through
the writings, what need of another lamb when the true one himself is found.
What then should one take the two † covenants †? For the law commands the one
to be offered as a whole-burnt-offering, and the other for sins. And if you
wish, let us transfer it to the mystery of the economy. For truly the Word of
God was crucified, receiving in his own flesh the piercings of the nails and
being sacrificed, and according to the Godhead granting the remission of
faults to all. And if anyone should doubt that I said: God has been crucified
in flesh, I know him who on the cross darkened the sun, and raised the dead
from =66= the tombs, and while he was being put to death the veil being torn
and the side being pierced, and when he bowed the head, death being destroyed,
him who received the piercings of the nails, and the centurion crying: "Truly
this is the Son of God." Where are the heretics who say that the Son of God
was not crucified? The unbeliever believes, and those who seem to believe
disbelieve the economy concerning them, or rather they set aside that which
came to be on behalf of the whole world. Let us come then to Simeon who was
held by so great a longing toward the love of the Lord, whose warmth of faith
the grace of the Spirit, having seen, confirmed him "not to see death before
he should see the Christ of the Lord." For indeed it seems to me that I behold
him sitting in Jerusalem and awaiting the Lord Jesus who was expected by him.
And †going up† to Jerusalem of both the all-holy virgin and her son and our
God, Simeon, pricked by the grace of the Spirit that the one whom he seeks is
in the temple, rose and was running to the temple. And see him for me,
beloved, sending his hands forward and giving over his whole self to the
course. For one could see an old man straining and his feet being forced to
run and not able to follow the eagerness of the soul; and the saying that
speaks was fulfilled in him. "Your youth shall be renewed as an eagle's", "And
he came," it says, "in the Spirit into the temple; and when the parents
brought the child into the temple to do †for them† according to the custom of
the law, he also" having run drew him to himself, and having "received him
into his arms" he cried out saying "Now you dismiss your servant, Master,
according to your word, in peace; for my eyes have seen your salvation, which
you prepared before the face of all the peoples, a light for revelation of the
nations, and glory of your people Israel." For from now I go to announce good
tidings to those in Hades, that he who looses death has been born.

And how did you dare, O Simeon, to bear God, whom the Cherubim tremble at?
reasonably =68= he says: "For this he became man, not only that he might be
borne by me, but that he might also be crucified for me. And I go to show
those in Hades my arms bearing witness to my words through the deeds and
confirming the gospels as sure. I no longer fear death, for now I go with joy,
seeing the resurrection with my eyes; for there I await him to come and from
there, having raised me with all, he will grant unending life." And see this
man's greatness of mind, announcing beforehand the words of Paul; for that one
says: "who made both one and broke down the middle wall of the fence, the
enmity, in his flesh, having abolished the law of the commandments in decrees,
that he might create the two in himself into one new man, making peace, and
might reconcile both in one body to God." That is, having taken up the middle
wall of the fence, which is law; for the law is called a fence of the Jews
because it does not permit the Jew to be found with a Gentile. Since then the
Lord, having come, took it away, having broken down the middle wall of the
fence, and made both one, both the races of the Jews and the multitudes of the
nations, having made peace through his blood, and granted one and the same
grace to all. For this Simeon also says "A light for revelation of the nations
and glory of your people Israel." O gray hair twined with prudence, O old age
made bright by an unstained life; O best of prophets, having become a vessel
of the Holy Spirit. Imitate this man, as many of you as have advanced in life;
for this man received the Lord; and nothing hinders us also from being thrones
of the Holy Spirit, if we imitate this man's life. And how then could this
come to be, when we spend our days in drunkenness and in theatres and in dice,
in which "the pouring out of profligacy" comes to be. Simeon did not do these
things, =70= but the contrary. For you run in drunkenness and dice, but he in
the temple; you look around at the theatres, but this man was at leisure for
spiritual contemplations; you take the mind captive toward shameful desire,
but this man as a noble athlete wrestled against such things. Do not be
ashamed when rebuked but rather be pricked as you hear. For if you wish,
having received the certificate of the remission of sins, thus to go out from
here, do not put forward sloth to me, but give yourself with a warm soul,
having groaned bitterly over the things you did, and having confessed to the
Master that you are no longer found in the same. From then on hold it as sure,
that you too have received the gift of the remission of sins, and do not yet
despair of yourself. Sin is not as hard as despair is hard. But many times, he
says, having stumbled I repented, and again I fell into the same, and I do not
know what to do; and I reckon this, that God has turned away from me. Hear,
beloved, concerning this. This comes from nowhere else than from your not
hearing the scripture nor being at leisure in the church which says: "God is
untried of evils; for each is tempted, being drawn out and baited by his own
desire." For having just now wept a little, and after going out of the church
no longer remembering the exhortations which you have heard, but giving
yourself over to wanderings of mind, how then would you not fall into a
transgression, tell me? But hold my words, and be at leisure in prayer, using
well-doing abundantly, and living with holy men, no longer give yourself over
to gluttony; let your eye not roam untimely things; let your heart not reckon
empty and vain things; possess humility; increase toward love; "to him who
strikes you on the right cheek, offer him also the other"; when impressed, add
more; "not rendering evil for evil, or reviling for reviling"; let an oath no
longer pass through your mouth, but cry with Paul: "I have been crucified to
the world and the world to me." Have then as sure the traditions of the holy
=72= apostles and of the fathers, be at leisure in the readings of the divine
scriptures; keep the divine sayings sure and firm and unmoved in your heart.

Be persuaded then at that time that you have also received the forgiveness of
faults. Since then until now you have not known the Master, and you have
gladdened the opposing powers by your destruction, hasten even from now that
the Master who has been long-suffering toward you become known to you toward
him through repentance, and gladden the angels who have never been made glad
over you, now through the return toward the Master; make gladness in heaven,
and secure yourself, being no longer found in the same; since you will be
reckoned as "a dog returning upon his own vomit", lest you too hear the
apostle Peter crying: "For it was better for them not to have known the way of
righteousness than, having known it, to turn back again from the holy
commandment delivered to them." Having then received the remission of faults
and the bright garment of chastity, reap the delights of paradise, marveling
at the love of mankind of God, that in what sort of stench of works and
dimness of soul you entered here, and how you have been made fragrant and
bright through repentance and go out having received the remission of sins.
Wherefore I exhort and I beg and I entreat not to soil the bridal robe, but
much more to make it bright through repentance. But let us return to the
matter at hand. For what does the righteous Simeon say to the undefiled
virgin? "And a sword shall pass through your own soul also." What then does he
say the sword is, if not altogether the assaults of the enemy? And where, he
says, did the virgin receive a sword? when she stood by at the cross. And how
then did she receive this? how? I say; the =74= blessed virgin then, having
thought on her own incorrupt offspring and the healings of his signs, and the
unspeakable mysteries of the teaching, knew him truly to be God; but having
seen him later hanging upon the wood of the cross, then she received the sword
in the soul, as Simeon says: "And a sword shall pass through your own soul
also." He did not say "it shall remain" but "it shall pass through." Since
then a little later she saw the veil being torn, the rocks being split, the
dead being raised, having cast off the dart at once she knew the mystery of
the economy. See then, it passed through, and did not remain. Let us all then
run to the holy virgin and Theotokos, and we shall take the gain; as many as
are virgins now, be at leisure with the mother of the Lord. For she is the
procurer for you of this good and incorrupt possession. For great indeed is
the wonder of the virgin; for what greater among the things that are will ever
be found? for she alone appeared broader than the earth and the heaven; for
who has become holier than this one? not forefathers, not prophets, not
apostles, not martyrs, not patriarchs, not fathers, not angels, not thrones,
not dominions, not the Seraphim, not the Cherubim, not anything else among
both the visible and the invisible in created things is found greater than
this one. She is a servant and Theotokos, she is a virgin and a mother. And
let no one doubt saying: how a servant and Theotokos? or how a virgin and a
mother? receive it by faith, O man, and do not doubt the things reckoned, do
not doubt the things approved by the fathers, but fear, and believe without
examination. Rather then believe, and do not be curious. If you will believe
as you yourself understand, look to your danger. But if you will believe the
preaching of the word, you no longer give account, but the one who presides.
Believe then the things said by us concerning the virgin, and do not doubt to
confess her both servant and Theotokos and virgin and mother; for she is a
servant, as a creature of him who was born from her; and she is Theotokos
inasmuch as she bore God incarnate from her; and a virgin, because she did not
know a bed of seed from a man; a mother, inasmuch as =76= she generated and
became mother of him who was generated from the Father before the beginning.
She then is the mother of the Master of the angels and of men; from her the
Son of God was incarnate, who accepted to be crucified in flesh. And do you
wish to know how great the virgin. is better than the heavenly powers? attend;
those, flying about with fear and trembling, cover the face, but she offers
the race of men to him and through her we receive the remission of sins; she
bore this one whom the angels, having come at the birth, were glorifying with
fear, saying "Glory to God in the highest and on earth peace, among men good
pleasure." Rejoice then, the mother and heaven; the maiden and cloud; the
virgin and throne; the boast and foundation of our church; make intense
supplication on our behalf that we may receive through you mercy on the day of
judgment, and may obtain the good things laid up for those who love God, by
the grace and love of mankind of our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom to the
Father together with the Holy Spirit be glory, power, honor, now and always
and unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
