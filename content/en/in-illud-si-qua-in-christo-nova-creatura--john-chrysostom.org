8. Of the same, on the saying, "If anyone is in Christ, a new creation; the
old things have passed away, behold all things have become new." =64.25= Great
is the eagerness of the husbandman to cast down the seeds, whenever he sees
the field cleared, and neither a multitude of thorns injuring the sprout of
the things growing, nor a stone striking against the plow; but greater is the
ease for the one about to cast down the word, whenever he beholds a hearer
prepared. I say these things, not because the rich are not present, displaying
the philosophy of the poor in the word of the hearing. For there is need not
only of the speaker, but also of the one who hears with understanding; for
Scripture seeks both, a wise teacher and an understanding hearer, lest our
labor become superfluous by the listlessness of those who hear. For care does
not let the things said fall into the mind, but it strips away the judgment of
those who hear.

Since then I see all prepared; for the secrets of the mind I do not know, but
I examine the eyes of each, and I see the eyelids standing as horns, and the
steadiness of the eye showing us the unshaken state of the mind, and each mind
freed from worldly cares, each one aiming at this alone, reckoning this, how
he may receive the things said; stretch your mind still more; spread out the
bosom of the soul, so that the seeds may be cast down with ease. For after a
long time I wish to let my tongue go upon the table of Paul; for I will not
cease calling him thus, the unlearned, the obscure, the ignoble, the
tentmaker. These are my advantages, these my praises, that I have such
teachers, shining not by worldly advantages, but by spiritual achievements. If
the Greek says to me, Whom do you have as teacher? I say the tentmaker; and
whom else? the fish-seller; and whom else? the tax-collector; and whom else?
the magi and the harlot; and whom else? the robber; and I am not ashamed, but
I even glory. Do you glory in having a teacher from the marketplace? And very
much. Why? For if the cheapness of the disciple shows the power of the
teacher, the cheapness of the teacher shows that the preaching is divine. For
even if men announced it, yet men did not find it; even if men sowed it, yet
men did not beget it. Men are instruments, God the craftsman; these are a
lyre, that mighty hand moved the string. And for this reason that Prophet too,
though he was a king and wrapped in purple and wearing a diadem, and
addressing the inhabited world, leaving the worldly display, so that [δὲ] no
one might think that he was speaking kingly words: My heart has belched forth
a good word; I tell my works to the King. My tongue is a pen of a scribe. Why
do you call it a pen? That you may learn that the letters are not mine, but of
the right hand that holds it. For the pen does not write from itself, but
whatever the holding hand may wish. And my tongue is a pen; for the Holy
Spirit holds it. When then the Greek says to you, Who is your teacher? do not
be ashamed nor blush, but say: The man of the marketplace, the tentmaker, the
one standing in the marketplace, the one stitching hides. And who is your
teacher? Plato, Pythagoras, not a man of the marketplace, but a philosopher.
But if I say, Who is your teacher? a philosopher; your teacher well-born, your
teacher rich, your teacher having a tongue sharpened, flowing beyond the
rivers,


enjoying honor, having splendor of ancestors, honored everywhere; mine the
contraries; so the victory becomes plain to us. How? Because a man of the
marketplace conquered the philosopher, the poor man the rich, the tongueless
the one flowing beyond the rivers, the one being scourged the one suffering
nothing. Uneven is the array, that =64.26= the trophy may become more
splendid. Mine solecizes, yours Atticizes; the one who solecizes conquered
the one who Atticizes; he solecizes in tongue, but he philosophizes in mind;
I have no need of words, but I need thoughts. Mine conquered yours; how? the
unlearned the wise, the rustic the orator. And from where, he says, is it
clear that he conquered? Shameless one, do you not see the facts crying out?
Where is Plato? nowhere; where is Paul? in the mouths of all. Where is Plato?
he has been silenced, and given over to forgetfulness; and where is Paul? he
has taken the queen city, Rome, which he not only ruled while living, but
even when he had died he prevailed. The body died that he might live in
spirit; the paper was torn, and the letters remain; for it was not written in
ink, but fixed by spirit. It is easier to pass by the sun than the words of
Paul.

For what does the one who granted him the words say? Heaven and earth will
pass away, but my words will not pass away. If they are words, refute them;
but if they are facts, worship. Heaven and earth will pass away, but my words
will not pass away. It is easier for the sun to be quenched, and for heaven to
vanish, than for the words of my Master to be dissolved. And the experience of
the facts bears witness to these things, and the length of time, and the
assaults of those who war against them; for the wonder is this, that it was
not only not dissolved, but that even being warred against it prevails. Attend
with accuracy to what I say: Paul spoke, John spoke, the fisherman and
evangelist, the one who threw away the net and took the Gospel, the one who
left the fishing rod and took in hand the word of teaching. What did he speak,
and what did the fisherman say? Let us see whether I am fishing the words even
of a fisherman, and not of a fisherman; of a fisherman, since that mouth
spoke; yet not of a fisherman, since the words were of the Spirit. For what
does he say? In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the
Word was God. What does the Jew say? He thinks great things of Moses, that he
split the sea, and divided and sewed up the deep, and changed the air, and cut
the rock, and brought down manna, and overthrew cities with their men, and won
wars without weapons.

Is it for this that you think great things? Hear what Moses said, and what the
fisherman said, that you may see what is the poverty of the Old Testament, and
what the abundance of grace. And what did Moses say? In the beginning God made
the heaven and the earth. And what did the fisherman? Nowhere does he set down
the word, He made, but what? In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was
with God. That one begins from created things, and this one from the Creator.
Do you see how he mounts higher than Moses? He heard Moses saying that God
made the heaven; he left the heaven and went up higher. Angels met him, and he
ran past; archangels, and he went by; the Cherubim, and he did not stand
there. Where are you running? I am seeking something else; and if the Cherubim
shine, yet they are works; and if the Seraphim sing, yet they are my
fellow-servants. What do you seek? And if Principalities and Dominions, these
too are slaves, these too are created things; and if Authorities, these too
are made things. Where are you going? I seek the Creator himself, I seek him
who is consubstantial with the Father; I do not stand away until I find him as
it is possible for a man to find. Moses spoke about heaven, and stopped; for
he did not have the spirit which I have; for he did not receive the spirit of
bondage unto fear, but he received the spirit of adoption unto the obedience
of faith.

Greater things have been entrusted to me. For this my Master came down from
above below, that I from below might go up above. What do you seek? The
Creator himself; when I find him, then I stand. He found him, and what does he
say? In the beginning was the Word. Nowhere the word, he made, nowhere the
word,


he came to be; for the teaching is not about created things. Behold Word: In
the beginning was the Word. How many tyrants since the fisherman spoke this
word wished to wipe it out, and they were not able? but wherever you go, you
hear that In the beginning was the Word; whether in country or in city, it
has gone before, In the beginning was =64.27= the Word; and in Persia and in
India and in the land of the Mauritanians this word shines more manifest than
the sun. For the sun shines by day, but is hidden by night; but the word even
by night sends forth its own brightness, it has been written in the mind of
each. The one who wrote has died, and the word shines; the body of the one
who spoke has been dissolved, and the word flourishes beyond the sun.
Wherever you go, the word has gone before, In the beginning was the Word. And
each, both woman and man, both slave and free, both king and private person,
both rulers and ruled, meditate it in the heart, and no one can wipe out this
fairest inheritance, but it remains keeping the wealth. Heaven and earth will
pass away, but my words will not pass away.

A word the devil was not able to wipe out; why? Because it is not a bare word,
but a word of God. Do not then be ashamed when you hear: These things the
fisherman spoke, these things the one stitching nets. Did you see how he sat
down into the very bottom of poverty and went up to the peak of virtue? But
lest, having begun of other things, we lead the word onto other things by the
rush of what is said, letting the thoughts roll out, come then, using a bridle
for the tongue, let us bring the subject back to the former promise. What then
does the tentmaker say, the man of the marketplace? for I will not cease
sounding these words continually, because of the madness of the many and their
unphilosophical mind. For since many in the marketplace, whenever they insult,
say, Marketplace fellow? what is Marketplace fellow? what is a man of the
marketplace? tell me; is it a charge to be of the marketplace? is it a charge
to stand in the marketplace and work? it is a charge to remain within and
seize the things of others. Learn what is insult, and what is praise, and do
not mock; to be of the marketplace is not a charge, but to be a robber, to be
a greedy man. How do you benefit me, that you sit in a chamber, and you are a
wolf? and how does the poor man standing in the marketplace and imitating ox
and plowman harm me? You are of the marketplace, you are a craftsman? these
are not charges; these are praises, that from just labors you gather your
food, not meddling in another's house. It is not then a charge to be of the
marketplace, nor to be a craftsman; but what is a charge? to live idly; would
that it were idle, and not working evil. Paul was of the marketplace, and he
stood at a workshop; and he was not such before the Gospel, after the Gospel
he was changed. But he both preached and stitched hides; he preached and
stayed with Priscilla and Aquila, Because of the same craft; for they were
tentmakers by trade. O philosophy of Paul! O high mind, and unconquered
judgment! O thought that has touched the vault of heaven! O soul overlooking
all things seen! O judgment! he thought the displays of life a spider's web;
he did not go to kings, nor did he go to the rich, he did not go to the
well-off, but he went to tentmakers because of the same craft, and he was not
ashamed uncovering his own trade, but he made this plain to all. He stood in
the marketplace preaching the Gospel, raising the dead, cleansing lepers, and
he was not ashamed of the place, but he gloried in the manner, and he stood in
the marketplace; and he was giving out hides, and they were saying, Raise the
dead; and two women were standing, the one saying, Make the hide a covering
for me, and the other saying, Raise my son, and he answered both; and this did
not become a hindrance of that, but the hands were working, and the grace was
operating, and the tentmaker answered both, furnishing to the one the thing
from the craft, to the other the thing from grace. What do you want? I make
the hide a covering; for I stitch hides.


What do you want? I make your dead to be raised; for I call upon God. Let not
a craft be a reproach. He did not go into a rich and well-off house; for he
was fulfilling a Masterly law saying: Into whatever city or house you enter,
inquire who in it is worthy, and remain there. For is the tentmaker worthy of
Paul? And very much. Why not? since he was a tentmaker according to the love
of poverty. Shall I tell you his praise better than a diadem, more splendid
than purple? Greet Priscilla and Aquila; and he sets the woman before the
man, that you may learn that the virtue was more with the woman. Greet
Priscilla and Aquila, writing to the Romans, who =64.28= laid down their own
neck for my soul. Did you see praise? did you see living martyrs? Who laid
down their own neck for my soul. A martyrdom completed, not by the pouring
out of blood, but by the choice of the mind. Do not tell me, They were not
slain; for even if they were not slain in the event, yet they were slain in
resolve; and God does not seek the end, but the preparation; since Abraham
too slew his son, though he did not slay him; for the knife was clean; and he
says: You did not spare your beloved son for my sake; you did not spare in
resolve, but I spared in the event.

Who laid down their own neck for my soul. They received the Apostle with
blood. Did you understand what I said? For they did not sacrifice swine and
birds to him, but they poured out their own blood. O excess of hospitality!
others, if they give ten obols, do not think it worthy to be troubled; those
poured out their own blood, and despised the present life. The lover of
strangers appears thus, not as those now, with a grim face, and raising the
eyebrows, if they give a poor man ten obols, and these through the household
slave. But these not so, but they themselves even became household slaves. So
too Abraham and Sarah; for she too mixed the flour, not according to the
softness of the women now, which by golds and silken garments makes the nature
softer. But that woman fulfills the works of a miller; and the husband says to
her: Hasten and mix three measures of fine flour. And she did not say: Are
these things bearable? did you take me, your kinswoman, as a baker? did I not
bring a dowry? am I not free? you order me the things of servants; on these
hopes did I come into your house? She said none of these things, but she
hastened and mixed; for she was Abraham's wife, not by the law of life only,
but also by the virtue of achievements. Let the hospitality become common, he
says, and let us harvest the fruit in common.

It is a treasure, he says; let us share together; I the calf, you the flour.
He sacrificed a calf, and received Isaac; she mixed the flour, And her seed
became as the stars of heaven and as the sand of the sea. I say these things
humbling human conceit, and making cheap the madness of all who are rich, and
comforting the poor, that no one may either be drowned in poverty, or be
lifted up in wealth; and both these are hindrances of virtue. But reason holds
the rank of a steersman, neither drowned by the storm, nor made slack by the
calm, but in a time of unevenness keeping the same philosophy for him, not yet
shaken by the difference, and becoming this or that; just as Job neither
became a blasphemer in poverty, nor lifted up in wealth, but when he was rich,
he opened the door, and when he became poor, he opened his own sides, being
then a lover of the poor, and later becoming a philosopher, and trampling the
difference of worldly things.

But since so much about the tentmaker, come let us take in hand also some of
the things said, bringing a few words into the midst, yet opening for us a
vast sea of thoughts.


For such is the treasure of the Scriptures; they show you the abundance not
by the multitude of words, but by the power of the thoughts. What then does
the tentmaker say, the mouth filled with holiness, the tongue fearful to
death? what does that right hand write, which is adorned with a chain and
wrapped in bonds, and raises the dead? what does it write; what does the
tongue say? what does the right hand write? But we, even if we have known
Christ according to the flesh, yet now we know him no longer. So that if
anyone is in Christ, a new creation. Attend, we are going down into depth. So
that if anyone is in Christ, a new creation; the old things have passed away,
behold all things have become new, and all things are of God. A treasure then
hidden, but let us dig and the depths toward the depth; for it is not a depth
having gloom, but a depth gushing light. Even if the word is unclear, yet I
will try to make it easy even for the unlearned and the poor and the
household slave and the cook and the sailor and the woman and the free and
the slave. For the things of God are set forth in common to all, overshadowed
with unclarity, but becoming easy to grasp by interpretation. And if there is
any slowness, by the ease of the teaching I will make the table clearer; only
gra =64.29= nt me a stretched mind, and do not grow soft =64.29= for I have
taken experience of your obedience according to the mind. Therefore I have
often attempted difficult thoughts, not expecting them to be easy to take,
but from the zeal according to the hearing seeing the difficult things become
easy. Let the mind be sober, and nothing is difficult, a stretched judgment,
and all things clear.

So that if anyone is in Christ, a new creation; the old things have passed
away. Reading these things Greeks laugh and mock us; such things, he says,
Paul says: If anyone is in Christ, a new creation. You boast, O Paul (I speak
their things), puffing us with hopes; you have lifted our mind, you fill our
ears with a new notion: If anyone is in Christ, a new creation. Learn first
not to be simply blinded; you throw yourself into a pit. Do you not know what
you have heard? For the natural man does not receive the things of the spirit;
for they are folly to him. Do you not know the treasure? the treasure has
thorns and stones above, but when it is dug through, pearls are found below.
Do not give me the words simply, but the thoughts; do not give me the words;
learn with security what New creation is, learn what In Christ is; learn and
go down into light; since a child too often sees a king passing and laughs,
and the king sees and it does not concern him; for it is not the blame of the
king, but the defect of unfinished age. Come, let me lead you into the inmost
places; do not simply view from the forecourts; come, let me uncover for you
all the secret of the mysteries. And what have you, he says, to tell me? did
he not say that there is a new creation among Christians? Very much.

Show me then, he says, a new creation; show me that a new heaven came to be,
that before Christ was present there was another, and after these things
another came to be; that another sea, that other seeds, that another nature,
that other fruit, that other rivers, that other winds; for all these are old,
and you say that it is a new creation? Bear with it, do not rave; the more you
contradict, so much more the excess of the truth gushes. I am not raving? he
says, I am not contradicting? see, I am quiet. Be quiet; I will ask you. Ask.
Does he say, before Christ was present, the heaven was another, and after he
was present another came to be? and before he was present according to the
flesh the same heaven was, and after he was present the same heaven remained,
and earth and sea and the air and the winds. What then, he says, does Paul
boast saying: If anyone is in Christ, a new creation; the old things have
passed away, behold all things have become new? I, he says, see nothing new.
For you do not have eyes that you may see; come, I will make you eyes and you
see. And you make eyes? Very much. And are you a craftsman? Very much. Have
you gone mad? No.


Then make eyes of the body. But the greater, of the mind. And do you make me
eyes? Very much. Do I not have them? No. Since you have eyes, since I do not
have; how? tell me. When you go into a temple, and see there a naked statue,
a voiceless stone, and you say: This is the god, do you have eyes? when you
call the stone god, how do you have eyes? is there any use of the outer eyes,
when the inner are maimed? Do you see that you have need that I make you
eyes, that you may see the stone as stone, that you may not mix the
unmixable, that you may not gather the things standing apart? Do you have
eyes, when you see wood and worship, and I taking it will burn it, do you not
have eyes naming the thing being burned god, when you deify the passions? A
man gets drunk and is carried about, and from the drunkenness becomes an
irrational instead of a man; and you say drunkenness is a god, and you call
him Dionysus; fornication is a god, and you call him Hermes. When then you
deify the passions and woods, how do you have eyes? you do not see the things
seen, and shall I trust you for the things not seen? Do you see that you have
need of eyes? Dionysus is a rotten stone, and Aphrodite is wood.

You do not believe my words? believe the axe; I push it, and I throw it down;
give me fire, and I burn. This is a passion of drunkenness; the law punishes
these things, and you deify them? do you not have eyes? Do you see that there
is need that I make you eyes through the word? Since the Jews too were such;
they had eyes, and they did not see; the blind man did not have them, and he
saw. Christ says to the blind man, Do you believe in the Son of God? and he
says: I believe, and he worshipped; but the Jews, see =64.30= ing, crucified.
The blind man worships, and the one who sees crucifies; for this he says: For
judgment I came into this world, that those who do not see may see, and those
who see may become blind. Do you have eyes at all? Do you wish then that I
fashion you eyes through the word, eyes of understanding, eyes that see unseen
things? For such are the eyes of us who see by faith; they do not see the
things seen, but the things not seen. And if you wish to follow, follow; but
if you do not wish, I do my own, I teach.

For the word is varied, converting opponents, correcting those of the
household, both many-faced and completed from every side. So that if anyone is
in Christ, a new creation; the old things have passed away, behold all things
have become new. O mouth! O brightness! for I see the treasure, before I
uncover it; I see the whole vessel of the Creator. O eyes, what things they
see! Stretch yourself toward me, rise up. Creation, in Scripture, is called
not only the bringing from not being into being, but also the change of what
is toward the better. It is said briefly, but it must be said more clearly
too. God is said to create, not only when he works what is not, but also when
he corrects and cleanses what is, that has been twisted or soiled. What I
mean: God created man, this is to work what is not; and when he works the
wicked man good, here is a change of things toward the better; there a
bringing of what is not, here a change of what is, but perishing, toward the
better. And that you may learn that creation is called not only the bringing
from not being into being, but when he corrects and cleanses something soiled
and twisted, hear David saying. For since he himself was, and his heart
created, but it had been soiled by the adultery and the murder; he therefore
calls upon God and says: Create a clean heart in me, O God. Before this then
was he without a heart? how then did he bring down Goliath? and how was he
well-pleasing to God? He is not speaking about the substance of the heart, but
about the best way of life. See then creation is called, not the bringing of
the substance from not being into being, but also the change of what is, but
has been corrupted, into the better.


When you see some unchaste man become temperate, say, His mind was created;
when you see a harlot being temperate, say, Her mind was created; when you
see a robber become merciful, say, His heart was created; for the wolf became
a sheep, the hawk became a dove. For this, creation. For this the bath too is
called a created thing, that of regeneration. For putting off, he says, the
old man, who is being corrupted, according to the desire of his flesh, and
putting on the new man, who has come to be according to the image of the one
who created him. So that his creation is the work; for we were created in
Christ Jesus for good works; first then for corruptible works. Then the
Father took clay, and made the man; the Son takes water, and makes it wine.
And there he has an element, and here an element; an exchange of element, and
the crafting is better. So that, if anyone is in Christ, a new creation. Do
you see then that there is a new creation? for what use to me if a heaven
come to be? Attend with accuracy: A new heaven did not come to be, nor a new
sun, nor earth. For what use to me, if a new heaven came to be, but I was
going to be in sins? how does the heaven remaining benefit me or harm me,
when I do not remain the same? The heaven is a house, but I am sick. If then
you see a physician coming into the house, and taking no thought for the sick
man, but whitening the walls, gilding the roof, do you not say, Man, raise
the one who is sick? why do you beautify the house?

And Christ entered into this world, in which he found my nature lying sick.
Attend again to what I say: A physician enters into a great house, he finds a
sick man, fevered, having sores; does he leave the sick man and rise? does he
whiten the walls? does he gild the roof? does he beautify the floor? No; but
he makes no account of the house, and uses all haste toward the sick man. If
the master of the house is healthy, the other things follow too. So also here.
Christ entered into the world; he took on flesh, and he is present everywhere
always; but Scripture calls his entrance the economy =64.31= .

He entered into this great world, which he himself constructed; he found the
nature of men sick, sick, not lying on a bed, but cast down upon wickedness,
the common member corrupted, altars being kindled, the savor of fat polluting
the air, children being slaughtered by parents, nature being trampled, the
laws of substance being shaken, sympathy being dishonored; They sacrificed
their sons and their daughters to the demons; men being driven in a frenzy
unchastely, one a fornicator, another an adulterer, another a murderer, all
the earth perishing, men lying on the bed of wickedness, the devil standing
by, kindling the furnace, filling the sores, the physicians growing weak, I
mean the prophets, accusing, not correcting, the physicians sitting beside,
preparing drugs, yet not helping, but accusing all. There is none righteous,
not even one, there is none who seeks God. The physician these things? What
hope then, when a physician accuses the one who is ill? One said: They became
horses mad for females, each was neighing after the wife of his neighbor.
Another sat in the wilderness, as the crow: Woe is me, that the devout has
perished from the earth, and there is none who does right among men; another:
Curse and falsehood and theft and adultery have been poured out upon the
earth; each oppresses his neighbor with oppression; another: Woe, nation of
sinners.

The physician goes out weeping; and what hope does the sick man have, when he
sees the physician weeping? what hope do you have, when you see the one who
has despaired? what good thing do you have to expect concerning the sick man?
And yet physicians often, even knowing the last,


so as not to grieve the relatives, conquer the passion by philosophy, and
show a bright face of their own opinion, lest they cast down the minds of the
sick. For when the sick man sees the physician twisting his look, what good
thing does he have to hope? But here the disease so conquered, as to make the
physician himself weep; what have I to do? sores, rottenness, worms,
countless evils. Woe, sinful nation! And the other even wrote whole laments,
Jeremiah; one mourns, another laments, another exhorts; God from above: I
have left my house. What hope then, when God says, I have left all things, I
have forsaken my inheritance, I have given my beloved soul into the hands of
my enemies? Elijah from below: Lord, they have dug down your altars, and
killed your prophets, and I have been left most alone, and they seek my soul.
And if these things were in Judea, where law, where temple, where ark, where
Jewish sacrifice; what was it where idols, where savor of fat and smoke,
lawless rites, impieties; where the passions were deified, where they
imitated the voice of dogs, who in the orgies of Dionysus used goat-skins and
a voice of dogs, that from every side they might lose the nobility? Dogs,
swine the devil made, he made vipers, he wrought serpents, he fashioned white
asps. The law grows weak, the prophets accuse. What then, wretched one?
having entered and found the inhabited world so suffering, was he going to
beautify the heaven, and leave the sick man? But he did not do this, but he
ran to the sick man, for whom also heaven, for whom also earth and sea and
air and all the other things. For this he did not take on the form of heaven;
for he was not going to heal heaven, but man. For this he recovered the
kindred of the sick man, and he comes as that sick man himself, not being
sick himself; For he did no sin, nor was deceit found in his mouth; but he
came having a vessel kindred of that one's nature.

He did not come with naked godhead, lest the sick man flee, lest he scare the
prey; but as a physician often, being well-born and distinguished, having
splendor of ancestors, and wrapped in silken garments, whenever he enters
toward a poor sick man needing to be operated, or something else, puts off the
garments and leaves the splendor and takes a towel, and girds himself, being
about to burn, being about to cut, and he does not seek his own dignity, but
the care of the one who is ill; so also the Master of the angels did, whose
glory is uninterpreted; he came down as man, he girded himself with a towel,
he girded himself, our nature, =64.32= the rich one became poor, not having
lost the wealth, but having hidden the wealth; For you know, he says, the
grace of the Lord, that for us he became poor, being rich, that we by his
poverty might become rich. Did you see poverty a mother of wealth? did you see
poverty a ground of abundance? the rich one became poor, that I the poor might
become rich. As a physician he girded himself with a towel. Where did he come
down? He girded himself with a towel; the wrapping of the nature was not
enough for him, but he also took on the form of a slave, not the nature of a
slave only.

He came then, he found our nature sick, he became this which I am, yet without
sin; he came into the hut in which I was lying; he saw worms, he saw
rottenness, he saw much stench, the law not helping, the prophets accusing,
sin lying on, the desire of the one who is ill disordered, not wishing to be
continent, nor enduring the physicians. You shall not murder, this one
murdered. You shall not commit adultery. Each was neighing after the wife of
his neighbor; You shall not steal. Curse and falsehood and adultery and theft
have been poured out upon the earth. The physician was saying other things,
and the sick man was doing other things. What is to happen? the physician was
ordering, the sick man was not obeying; the disease was increasing, the
enslaver devil was standing by like a runaway slave. What do you want? I give
a cold drink; what do you want? I give meats, I give the things that destroy
your stomach, the things contrary to the physician. The physician was
ordering,


and was going out; the runaway was coming in secretly and was furnishing all
things. Do you want fornication? do you want falsehood? That one was saying:
these I want, not those which the physician says; these I want which you say.
The wretched bodies were perishing. God accuses, prophets accuse, that
executioner was standing by, and the desire of the one who is ill toward the
one who harms, not toward the one who helps. The race was perishing, all
things had been corrupted. The physician came, the well-resourced among the
resourceless, having found a way easy and convenient and saving. If I say, he
says, Do this and that, they do not do it.

How? they did not receive the things of the law, and do they have to receive
the things of grace? they did not receive the things below and do they have to
receive the things above? If I say fasting, if I say wrapping of sackcloth, if
I say possessionlessness, if I say virginity, they do not know an honorable
marriage, and shall I speak to them about virginity? they seize the things of
others, and shall I speak to them about possessionlessness? they fornicate,
they commit adultery, and shall I speak of wrapping of sackcloth? What then? I
become man for the time in my flesh, and I speak with the one who has been
cast away; I abolish sin for the time, from the race I show a healthy man; I
show the same nature, and not the same fall; I show a man who has not done
sin, nor deceit in his mouth, that these too being spectators may have hope of
salvation, and those who plot may flee, and they may see a man not having sin.
Be ashamed, devil. This is flesh, this is a body, which my Master has. For
this he was going about always saying, if this man is Son of God, how does he
not sin? all the others are under sin. For sin is not of nature, but of
choice. See, I give a healthy man, a vessel cleaned out and unstained.

And standing in the midst of the people he was saying: Who of you convicts me
concerning sin? and no one found anything to say. What then now? he enters
toward the sick for the time, exhorting them with words, as a physician
through the drugs; you have nothing wicked, if you are temperate; the disease
is hard, but it can be loosed. He exhorts them, Come to me all who labor and
are heavy laden, and I will give you rest; the sick were heavy laden; for
burdens are disease, disorder, fornication theft, falsehood, false witness;
for a wicked conscience is not so heavy a burden. But since the sick man fears
the physician, especially when the disease is hard, lest somewhere he sharpen
iron, lest somewhere fire burn, and many of the sick chose to die in the
diseases, rather than to endure the pain from the healing, and to harvest the
health from the pain, that no one may fear this: this one has come, he says;
He demands of us accounts of the sins; he demands of us the offenses; he has
to punish us, and then he corrects us; that no one having thought this may
flee; Come, he says, to me. I did not come that I may judge the world =64.33=
but that I may save the world. Nothing heavy; I have no iron, I have no
burning fire, I do not burn, I do not cut; the healing is without pain, the
cure without toil. If you do not believe, look at the harlot, look at the
robber, look at the Magi, the tax-collector, the Canaanite woman; I have given
you an example from the beginnings; did I burn? did I cut? did the harlot only
weep, and become more august than virgins? did the Magi not worship, and
become evangelists? did the Canaanite woman not come, and receive the healing
of her little daughter? I did not come, that I may judge the world, but that I
may save.

Come all who labor and are heavy laden. I say these words; I am Master, I have
no need, I am not a slave subject to law; the law accuses you, but I loose the
accusation; I stop the accuser himself. How? For grace stopped him; the
prophets accuse, but I give the greater freedom. Having come then and found
the nature in a bad way, and some in fornication, others in adultery, savor of
fat and smoke polluting the air, all the other things, what does he do?


Seeing our race fevered, he washes; first he says, Wash, and be delivered
from the disease; he washes with a bath of regeneration and anoints; and with
the chrism of the myrrh I anoint you, and I wash and I change you. And is the
strength of the bath so great? So great. Go down below. He goes down below,
and the old man does not remain. Is there need of iron? of cutting and pain?
the benefit is in pleasure; is the healing toilsome? And what shall we do
that we may be saved? we have worked countless evils, we have a wicked
conscience, we have perished. He says to be baptized and to be saved; for
taking he anoints, he washes, then he feeds solid food. For since the sick
man has become guiltless, he feeds him and he feeds his own body, and he
gives his own blood to drink; Of me, he says, the sinless. For since you
became sinless from the bath, taking such a body, he anoints, he washes, he
feeds.

This is a new creation. When a fornicator enters, and becomes righteous, is it
not a new creation? when a robber enters, and is delivered from the sins, is
it not a new creation? Did you see the crea =64.34= tion in the grace? look
also at the creation in the way of life. How many harlots have left the
theaters, have left silken garments, have sprinkled themselves with ashes,
have wrapped themselves in sackcloth, and now live as citizens in Christ? is
it not a new creation? When then you see the harlot no longer a harlot, but
more august than a virgin, say: Her judgment was created, her heart was
changed, the mind has been moved from the manner; she no longer into the
theater, she no longer snares others, but she herself is also saved; she no
longer becomes a scandal to others, but salvation; for the fornicators see,
and become temperate.

Do you see the new creation? When you see the robber, the Greek, the one
licking altars, the one raving against Christians, stripping countless
orphans, stripping countless widows, having become a Christian, and giving his
own things to the poor, and coming to the all-night watches, say, The same one
was created; so that, If anyone is in Christ, a new creation. See everywhere
in the inhabited world the new creation. When you see monks who have taken the
mountains, crucified, trampling the necessities of nature, imitating angels in
passionlessness; so that, If anyone is in Christ, a new creation. Go to
Persia, if not with the body, yet with the mind; they used to marry mothers,
but now they practice virginity. So that, If anyone is in Christ, a new
creation. Look as far as kings at the creation of those who have the diadems.
Before this kings filled all things with impiety, but now kings come to
church; and yet kings are fearful to all; but now the Church is fearful to
them.

Who dares to say to a king, Set down your diadem? but the Church says it to
him, and he obeys, and he comes with a bare head, no one saying it, but reason
advising; with a bare and unadorned head; he throws the diadem, and takes a
cross. The poor man and the king enter alike to the table; not otherwise the
poor man, and otherwise the king; nor does the king say, Give me more, and to
the poor man less, but having the same humility, the same reverence, as the
poor man and I; so that, If anyone is in Christ, a new creation. And for all
these things let us give thanks to God, because to him is the glory unto the
ages. Amen.
