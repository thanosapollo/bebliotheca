On the interpretation of the Canaanite woman. And it was spoken after his
return from exile.

=52.449=

αʹ. The winter is severe, but it did not hinder the eagerness of those who
have come; the trials are many, but they have not dissolved your longing. The
Church does not cease being warred upon and conquering, plotted against and
prevailing. As much as others plot, so much does she increase; and the waves
are dissolved, but the rock stands unmoved. By day teaching, by night
all-night vigils; day contends with night; there assemblies, and here
assemblies. Night makes the marketplace a church; and the eagerness is more
vehement than fire. You do not need exhortation, and you display the zeal. Who
would not be struck? Who would not wonder? Not only have those who were
present not been left behind, but even those who were not have been added.
Such is the gain of trials; for just as rain coming down upon the earth wakes
the seeds, so also trial entering the soul wakes eagerness. It is the word of
God; the Church is unmoved; The gates of Hades shall not prevail against her.
He who wars destroys himself, and shows the Church stronger; he who wars casts
down his own strength, and makes our trophy more splendid. Job was fair even
before this; but after these things he appeared better. He was not so fair
when his body was unharmed, as he was fair when he was crowned with the ichor
of his wounds. Never fear a trial, if you have a soul prepared. Affliction
does not harm, but works endurance. For just as the furnace does not harm the
gold, so neither does affliction destroy the noble man. What does the furnace
do to the gold? It makes it pure. What does affliction work for the one who
bears it? Endurance. It makes him higher, it cuts away sloth, it gathers the
soul, it makes the reasoning more sober. They brought on a trial, that they
might drive away the sheep, and the opposite came out; for it brought in a
shepherd. In what are our things? In good repute. In what are =52.450= theirs?
In shame. Where are theirs? They do not even appear. I go about the
marketplace, and I see no one. They were leaves, and when a wind blew they
fell; they were chaff, and were winnowed, and the ripe wheat appeared; they
were lead, and melted, and the gold remained pure. Who drives them? No one;
but they have conscience as an enemy dwelling with them after the sin. They
know what they did. For Cain too wished to slay his brother; but when he
wished to slay, the desire was in bloom; but when he had worked the sin,
groaning and trembling he went about everywhere in the inhabited world. These
men, even if they did not slay in deed, yet they slew in intent. The slaughter
advanced as far as their wickedness; but life was granted through the
philanthropy of God. I say these things, anointing your eagerness, that you
may never fear a trial. Are you a rock? Do not fear the waves; For upon this
rock I will build my Church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against
her, he says. Sometimes the wars are from without, sometimes from within; but
no one sinks the ship.

βʹ. But lest we spend all the time on their accusations, having given them
over to the fear of conscience, and having left the executioner to make
wretched their mind and the reasoning of untimely desires, having left them to
be fugitives, with no one driving them, having left them to be dishonored,
with no one warring against them;


let us set forth the accustomed table. For it is not just to spend the time on
the accusations of enemies, and to overlook the children wasting with hunger.
Yesterday then Paul set the table for us, today Matthew; yesterday the
tentmaker, today the tax-collector; yesterday the blasphemer, today the
extortioner; yesterday the persecutor, today the greedy man. But the
blasphemer did not remain a blasphemer, but became an apostle; and the
extortioner did not remain an extortioner, but became an evangelist. I speak
both of the former wickedness and of the virtue after these things, that you
may learn how great is the benefit of repentance, that you may never despair
of your salvation. Our teachers shone from sin before this, but later they
shone from righteousness, tax-collector and blasphemer, the first-fruits of
wickedness. For what is a tax-office? Lawful robbery, violence made bold,
injustice having law as advocate; the tax-collector is harsher than brigands.
What is a tax-office? Violence putting forward law =52.451= as advocate,
having the physician as executioner. Did you understand what I said? The laws
are physicians, then they also become executioners; for they do not dissolve
the wound, but increase it. What is a tax-office? Shameless sin, robbery that
has no pretext, harsher than brigandage. The brigand is ashamed even when he
steals; but this man is bold when he seizes. But this tax-collector suddenly
became an evangelist. How and in what manner? As Jesus was passing by, he
says, he saw Matthew sitting at the tax-office, and he says to him: Follow me.
O power of a word; the hook entered, and made the captive a soldier, worked
the clay into gold; the hook entered, And immediately rising he followed him.
He was in the depth of wickedness, and he went up to the vault of virtue. Let
no one then, beloved, despair of his own salvation. The things of wickedness
are not of nature; we have been honored with choice and with freedom. Are you
a tax-collector? You can become an evangelist. Are you a blasphemer? You can
become an apostle. Are you a brigand? You can plunder paradise. Are you a
magus? You can worship the Master. There is no wickedness that is not
dissolved by repentance. For this reason Christ chose the first-fruits of
wickedness, that no one toward the end might have a way to flee.

γʹ. Do not say to me, I have perished, and what now? do not say to me, I have
sinned, and what shall I do? You have a physician superior to the sickness,
you have a physician who conquers the nature of the disease, you have a
physician who heals by a nod, you have a physician who sets right by will,
both able and willing. He brought you forth when you were not; being, and
having been twisted, he will be able all the more to set you right. Did you
not hear how at first he took dust from the earth, and fashioned man? how he
made the earth flesh? how sinews? how bones? how skin? how veins? how a nose?
how eyes? how eyelids? how a brow? how a tongue? how a chest? how hands? how
feet? how all the rest? Earth was the substrate, one substance; and art
entered, and worked the creation into variety. Can you not tell the manner in
which you were created? So neither can you tell the manner in which sins are
taken down. For if fire falling upon the thorns consumes them, much more does
the will of God spend our offenses and pull them up by the roots, and make the
one who has sinned like the one who has not sinned. Do not seek the manner, do
not be curious about what is happening, but believe the wonder. I have sinned,
you say, many and great sins. And who is without sin? But I, he says, have
sinned things harsh and great and beyond every man. This is enough for you as
a sacrifice; Speak your lawless deeds first, that you may be justified.
Acknowledge that you have sinned, and this will become for you a beginning of
correction. Be gloomy, become downcast, pour out tears. For did


the harlot pour out anything else? Nothing else, but tears and repentance; she
took repentance as a guide, and came to the fountain.

δʹ. But what does the tax-collector and evangelist say? Let us hear. And Jesus
going out from there came into the parts of Tyre and Sidon; and behold, a
woman. The evangelist wonders; Behold, a woman, the ancient weapon of the
devil, she who cast me out of paradise. =52.452= the mother of sin, the leader
of transgression, this very woman comes, this nature; a new and strange
wonder; Jews flee, and the woman pursues. And behold, a woman coming out from
those borders was begging him, saying: Lord, son of David, have mercy on me.
The woman becomes an evangelist, and confesses both the godhead and the
economy; Lord, the mastery; Son of David, the taking-up of the flesh. Have
mercy on me. See a philosophic soul. Have mercy on me; I have no achievements
of life, I have no boldness of conduct; I flee to mercy, to the common harbor
of those who have sinned; I flee to mercy, where there is no court, where
salvation is unexamined; and yet being so wicked and lawless, she dared to
approach. And see the philosophy of a woman; she does not beg James, she does
not entreat John, nor does she come to Peter, nor did she divide the choir. I
have no need of a mediator, but taking repentance as advocate, I come to the
fountain itself. For this he came down, for this he took on flesh, that I too
may speak with him. Above the Cherubim tremble at him, and below a harlot
converses with him. Have mercy on me. The word is bare, and it finds a vast
sea of salvation. Have mercy on me; for this you came, for this you took on
flesh, for this you became what I am. Above, trembling, and below, boldness.
Have mercy on me; I have no need of a mediator, Have mercy on me. What have
you? I seek mercy. What do you suffer? My daughter is badly demon-possessed;
nature is tortured, fellow-feeling is put to the test. She has come out as
advocate of her little daughter; she does not bring the sick one, but she
brings the faith; He is God, and he sees all things. My daughter is badly
demon-possessed. A harsh mourning; the sting of nature has split the womb, the
surge in the bowels. What shall I do? I am perishing. And why do you not say,
Have mercy on my daughter, but, Have mercy on me? She has the passion in
insensibility, she does not know what she suffers, she does not feel the pain,
having as a curtain of the disaster the painlessness, or rather the
insensibility. But have mercy on me, the spectator of the daily evils; I have
a theater of disaster in the house. Where shall I go? Into the desert? But I
do not dare to leave her alone. But into the house? But I find the enemy
within, the waves in the harbor, a theater of disaster. What shall I call her?
Dead? but she moves. But living? But she does not know what she does. I do not
know how to find a name that interprets the passion. Have mercy on me. If the
little daughter had died, I would not have suffered such things; I would have
given the body to the bosom of the earth, and with time I would have brought
in forgetfulness, and I would have worn away the wound; but now I have a dead
thing working a continual spectacle for me, weaving the wound for me,
multiplying the passion for me. How shall I see eyes twisting? hands writhing?
locks loosed? foam coming forth? the executioner being within and not
appearing? the one who scourges not seen, but the scourges appearing? I stand
a spectator of another's evils, I stand with nature stinging me; Have mercy on
me. Harsh is the surge, passion and fear; passion of nature, and fear of a
demon. I cannot approach, nor hold her. The passion pushes me, and the fear
beats me back. Have mercy on me.

εʹ. Consider the philosophy of a woman. She did not go to magi, she did not
call seers, she did not make amulets, =52.453= she did not hire sorceress
women,


those who enchant demons and increase the wound; but she left the workshop of
the devil, and comes to the Savior of our souls. Have mercy on me; my daughter
is badly demon-possessed. You know the passion, as many of you as have become
fathers; help my word, as many of you as have become mothers. I cannot
interpret the storm which the woman endured. Have mercy on me; my daughter is
badly demon-possessed. Did you see the philosophy of the woman? did you see
the endurance? the courage? the patience? But he answered her not a word. New
things. She begs, she entreats, she weeps the disaster, she increases the
tragedy, she narrates the passion; and the philanthropic one does not answer;
the Word is silent, the fountain is shut, the physician draws in the
medicines. What is the new thing? what is the strange thing? You run to
others, and this woman who runs to you you drive away? But consider the
philosophy of the physician. But he answered her not a word. For what reason?
Because he was not examining the words, but was learning the secrets of the
mind. But he answered her not a word. But what of the disciples? Since the
woman did not obtain an answer, they came to him saying: Send her away, for
she cries after us. But you hear the outer cry, and I the inner; great is the
voice of the mouth, but greater that of the mind. Send her away, for she cries
after us; but another evangelist says, Before us. The things said are
contrary, but not false; for she was doing both. For first she was crying
behind; when he did not answer, she came in front, like a dog licking the feet
of its master. Send her away; she has set a theater around, she has gathered a
people; those were beholding the human pain, but the Master the philanthropy,
and the salvation of the woman. Send her away, for she cries after us. What
then does Christ? I was not sent except to the lost sheep of the house of
Israel. When he answered, he made her wound worse; for he was a physician
cutting, not that he might divide, but that he might join.

ʹ. Here attend to me with accuracy, and stretch your mind for me. For I wish
to search out a deep question. I was not sent except to the lost sheep of the
house of Israel. Is this the whole? For this you became man, you took on
flesh, you worked so many economies, that you might save one corner, and this
one perishing? and the whole inhabited world is a desert, Scythians,
Thracians, Indians, Moors, Cilicians, Cappadocians, Syrians, Phoenicians, as
much earth as the sun looks upon? You came for Jews alone, and you overlook
the nations in a desert? and you overlook the steam of fat? you overlook
smoke? you overlook your Father being insulted? idols being worshipped? demons
being served? And yet the prophets do not say these things, but what does your
ancestor according to the flesh say? Ask of me, and I will give you nations as
your inheritance, and the ends of the earth as your possession. And Isaiah,
the beholder of the Seraphim: And there shall be the root of Jesse, and he who
rises to rule nations; upon him nations shall hope. =52.454= And Jacob: A
ruler shall not fail from Judah, nor a leader from his thighs, until he come
for whom it is laid up, and he is the expectation of nations. And Malachi:
Therefore among you bronze gates shall be shut, and they shall not change what
is set forth, because from the risings even to the settings of the sun your
name is glorified among the nations, and in every place incense is offered to
the Lord, and a pure sacrifice. And David again: All nations, clap your hands,
shout to God with a voice of gladness; for the Lord is most high, terrible, a
great king over all the earth. God has gone up in a shout, the Lord in a voice
of a trumpet. And another: Rejoice, nations, with his people. And you
yourself, when you came, did you not immediately call magi, the citadel of the
nations? the tyranny of the devil? the power of the demons? did you not,
having come down, make them prophets? You call magi; the prophets


speak about nations. Rising from Hades you say to the disciples: Go, make
disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of
the Son, and of the Holy Spirit; and when the wretched, the miserable one
came, begging for a daughter, entreating that a disaster be loosed, then you
say: I was not sent except to the lost sheep of the house of Israel? And when
a centurion approached, you say: I will come and heal him; when the brigand,
Today you shall be with me in paradise; when the paralytic, Rise, take up your
bed, and go; when Lazarus, Lazarus, come out, and he came out four days dead.
You cleanse lepers, you raise the dead, you make a paralytic firm, you heal
the blind, you save brigands, you make a harlot more chaste than a virgin, and
to this woman you answer nothing? What is the new thing? what is the strange
thing? what is the paradoxical thing?

ζʹ. Attend accurately, that you may learn a woman's courage and the Master's
wisdom and care, that you may learn a delay that has gain, that you may learn
a refusal that furnishes wealth; that even if you too pray, and do not
receive, you may never stand away. Attend and stretch yourself. When the Jews
were delivered from the Egyptian tyranny, and having fled Pharaoh's hands were
going into the desert, and were about to enter the land of the Canaanites, of
the idol-mad and impious men, who worshipped stones, served woods, displaying
much impiety, God gave them this law, saying: You shall not take from their
sons as sons-in-law, nor give your daughter as a bride to them. You shall not
share gold with them, nor communion of table, nor intercourse, nor anything
else of such things, because the nations are lawless, into which I lead you to
inherit them. This then the law was all but commanding them: You shall not
buy, you shall not sell, you shall not accomplish a marriage, nor contracts,
but be, even if near in place, yet separated in manner. Let nothing be common
to you toward them, not dealings, not sales, not purchases, not
intermarriages, not connections by marriage, lest the necessity of kinship
prepare you to slip into impiety, lest the giving and the taking make you a
friend of theirs; but always be an enemy to them. Nothing to you and
Canaanites; you shall not take their gold, nor sil =52.455= ver, not clothing,
not a daughter, not a son, not anything else of such things; but be yourself
by yourself. You have a tongue that separates you, and I have given you a law;
for this reason the law also is called a fence. For just as a fence is laid
around a vineyard, so also the law around the Jews, that they may not overstep
and mix with the Canaanites. For among them there were lawless mixings, the
laws of nature corrupted, idols worshipped, woods served, God insulted,
children slaughtered, fathers spat upon, mothers dishonored, all things were
altered, all things overturned, they were living a life of demons. For this
reason they never made dealings, not contracts, not sales with those; but the
law, laid down on the greatest things, forbade marriages with one another, the
contracts, the connections by marriage; they had nothing in common toward
those. It was forbidden then by the law to deal with Canaanites, to share
gold, or anything else, lest the occasion of friendship become a
starting-point of impiety; the law was laid around instead of a fence; A
vineyard, he says, I planted, and I set a fence around it, that is a law, not
of thorns, but of commandments, walling and shutting them off. The Canaanites
then were forbidden, abominable, impious, accursed, polluted, unclean;
therefore the Jews did not even endure to hear them, fulfilling the law
thereafter. Since then this woman was from the Canaanites; For behold a woman,
he says, coming out from those borders; since then this woman was from the
Canaanites, and she approached Christ,


for this he was saying, Which of you convicts me concerning sin, if I have
transgressed the law? For since he was man, he was also displaying the human
things.

ηʹ. Now attend with accuracy to the word. Since the woman was a Canaanite, and
of those borders, where there are also rages, and madness, and impiety, where
tyranny of the devil, and bacchanals of demons, and nature trampled, and they
have been brought down into the unreason of unreasoning things, into madnesses
of demons, and the law was also commanding, Nothing to you and Canaanites, do
not give, do not take from those, do not take a wife, do not take a son, do
not make contracts, do not make dealings; and for this I set a fence around;
but Christ coming, and taking up the weapons of men, was immediately
circumcised, offered sacrifices, offerings, all the rest, and he was about to
dissolve the law; lest they say, that having failed to fulfill it, for this
reason he dissolved it, first he fulfills it, and then he dissolves, lest you
think that he fails, but he fulfills all things according to the custom. For
this he cries and says, Which of you convicts me concerning sin? Since then
this too was lawful, to have nothing in common with Canaanites, lest the Jews
begin to accuse him and say, For this we did not believe you; for you were
lawless, you dissolved the law; you went away into the country of the
Canaanites, you mixed with Canaanites, the law saying: Do not mix; for this he
speaks nothing to her for the time. Attend how he fulfills the law, and does
not betray the salvation; and he stops the mouths of Jews, and wins her back.
But he answered her not a word, he says. Do not give me pretexts. See, I do
not speak; see, I do not converse; see a disaster, and I do not display what
is my own; see a shipwreck, and I the helmsman do not dissolve the storm
because of your ingratitude, =52.456= lest you have a pretext. See, a woman
has set a theater around me, and she does not yet receive an answer, lest you
say, that You gave yourself to Canaanites, and you transgressed the law, and
we have this pretext for not believing you. Consider how for this he did not
answer the woman, that he might answer Jews; the silence toward the woman
became a voice of ingratitude for the Jews.

θʹ. And he was doing these things not toward his own dignity, but
condescending to their weakness. For even when he cleansed the leper, he says:
Go, offer the gift which Moses commanded. You cleansed, and you send him to
the law of Moses? Yes. Why? Because of the Jews, lest they begin to accuse,
that I have transgressed the law. For this even when he healed the leper, he
healed him in a strange way; and hear how: And behold a leper coming begged
him saying, Lord, if you will, you can cleanse me; and stretching out his
hand, he touched him saying: I will, be cleansed. In the law one ought not to
touch a leper. When then Naaman the general came to Elisha the prophet held by
leprosy, the disciple says to him, Behold a leper general outside. And he
sends the disciple to go out and say to him, Go, wash in the Jordan. And he
did not dare himself to go out and see the leper, and touch him. Since then
Elisha cleansed a leper, lest the Jews say, that he cleansed likewise as
Elisha, for this that one does not dare to touch, but this one touches, and
says, I will, be cleansed; and stretching out his hand, he touched the leper.
Why did he touch? That he might teach you that he is not a slave subject to
law, but a Master set over the law. How then did he keep the law? By saying, I
will, be cleansed, and not immediately touching him. The word went first, the
disease fled, then he touched the unclean, and said, I will, be cleansed. How?
Immediately he was cleansed. The evangelist does not find how to say it; for
even the immediately is slow, he does not find a word equal in weight to the
speed of the energy.


Immediately; how? At the same time the word went out, and the disease fled,
the leprosy was banished, thereafter the leper was clean. For this he says:
Go, show yourself to the priest, and offer the gift which Moses commanded, for
a testimony to them. To whom? To the Jews, lest they say that I transgress the
law. I healed, and I say, Offer the gift of the law, that on that day the
leper may accuse them saying, He commanded me to offer a gift according to
law. And just as Christ was doing many things for the sake of Jews, making
them without defense in all things, so also here. Have mercy on me, because my
daughter is badly demon-possessed. But he was not answering her a word. And
his disciples coming say, Send her away, for she cries after us. What then he?
I was not sent except to the lost sheep of the house of Israel; lest Jews say,
that You left us, and went away outside, and for this we did not believe you.
Behold, he says, even from nations they come, and I do not receive them; but
you even as you flee =52.457= I call, Come to me, all who labor, and you do
not come; this woman I throw off, and she remains. A people, whom I did not
know, served me, he says; at the hearing of the ear they obeyed me; and
elsewhere: I became manifest to those who did not seek me, and I was found by
those who did not ask for me. Send her away, for she cries after us. Let us
see then what Christ says. I was not sent except to the lost sheep of the
house of Israel. Were the words not turning her away? For he all but says,
Stand away, because you have nothing in common toward me; I did not come for
you, but I came for Jews. I was not sent except to the lost sheep of the house
of Israel. And she, having heard, said: Yes, Lord, help me, and she was
worshipping, saying; but he did not answer her. But see an answer: It is not
good to take the bread of the children, and throw it to the little dogs. O
care of a physician; in a refusal he draws her. It is not good to take the
bread of the children; of whom? Of the Jews; and give it to the little dogs,
that is, to you.

ιʹ. Truly the Lord has said these things unto the shame of Jews; for being
called children, they became dogs. For this Paul also says: Look at the dogs,
look at the evil workers, look at the mutilation; for we are the circumcision.
The Gentiles were called dogs, and they became children. My children, whom I
again travail, until Christ be formed in you. This encomium is an accusation
of a Jew. It is not good to take the bread of the children, and give it to the
little dogs. What then the woman? Yes, Lord. O force of a woman, o contention
of a soul. The physician says, No, and she says, Yes; the Master says, No; she
says, Yes; not accusing nor being shameless, but awaiting salvation. It is not
good to take the bread of the children, and give it to the little dogs. Yes,
Lord. You call me a dog, but I call you Lord; you insult me, but I hymn you.
Yes, Lord; for even the little dogs eat from the crumbs that fall from the
table of their masters. O wisdom of a woman; from the example she found a
fitting word. You call me a dog, as a dog I am fed. I do not refuse the
reproach; I do not flee the calling; let me then receive the food of the dog;
and she says a thing that happens in custom. You set your own things; you
called me a dog, give me a crumb; you have become advocate for me in the
request; in the refusal display the consent. Yes, Lord; for even the little
dogs eat from the crumbs that fall from the table of their masters. What then
the one who refuses, the one who pursues, the one who drives, the one who
says, It is not good to take the bread of the children, and throw it to the
little dogs? the one who says, I was not sent except to the lost sheep of the
house of Israel? O woman, great is your faith. Have you suddenly become a
praiser? do you proclaim the woman? were you not driving, were you not
pursuing? Take courage; for this I delayed. For if


from the beginning I had sent her away, you would not have learned her faith.
If from the prologue she had received, she would have withdrawn quickly, and
no one would have known her treasure. For this I delayed the giving, that I
might show the faith to all. O woman. God says, O woman. Let those hear who
pray =52.458= ing with coarseness. When I say to someone, Beseech God, entreat
him, supplicate him, he says: I have beseeched once, twice, a third time, a
tenth, a twentieth; and I have not yet received. Do not stand away, brother,
until you receive; the end of asking is the giving of what is asked. Then
stand away, when you receive, or rather not even then, but even then remain.
Even if you do not receive, ask that you may receive; and when you receive,
give thanks, because you have received. Many enter the church, they complete
ten thousand verses of prayer, and they go out, and they do not know what they
said; the lips are moved, but the hearing does not hear. You do not hear your
prayer, and you want God to listen to your prayer? I bent, you say, the knees;
but your mind was flying outside; your body inside the church, and your intent
outside; the mouth was saying the prayer, and the mind was counting interest,
contracts, dealings, fields, possessions, companies of friends. For the devil,
being wicked, and knowing that in the time of prayer we accomplish great
things, then comes upon us. Often we lie on our back in bed, and we reason
nothing; we have come to pray, and ten thousand reasonings, that he may cast
us out empty.

ιαʹ. Knowing then that these things happen in the prayers, beloved, imitate
the Canaanite woman, the man the woman, the foreigner, the weak, the one
thrown off and easily despised. But you do not have a daughter
demon-possessed? But you have a soul that sins. What did the Canaanite woman
say? Have mercy on me; my daughter is badly demon-possessed; say you also,
Have mercy on me; my soul is badly demon-possessed. For sin is a great demon.
The one possessed by a demon is pitied, the one who sins is hated; that one
has pardon, this one is deprived of defense. Have mercy on me; the word is
short, and it found a sea of philanthropy; for where there is mercy, all the
good things. Even if you are outside, cry and say, Have mercy on me, not
moving the lips, but shouting with the mind; for God hears even those who are
silent. A place is not sought, but a beginning of manner. Jeremiah was in
mire, and he drew God; Daniel in a den of lions, and he propitiated God; the
three children were in the furnace, and hymning God they prevailed upon him;
the brigand was crucified, and the cross did not hinder, but opened paradise;
Job was on a dunghill, and he made God gracious; Jonah in the belly of the
whale, and he had God as one who hears. Even if you are in a bath, pray, even
if on a road, even if on a bed; wherever you are, pray. You are a temple of
God, do not seek a place; there is need of intent only. Even if you stand
before a judge, pray; when the judge is angry, pray. There was a sea in front,
Egyptians behind, Moses in the middle; much distress in the prayer, but great
was the breadth of the prayer. Behind the Egyptians were pursuing, in front
the sea, in the middle the prayer; and Moses was saying nothing; and God says
to him: Why do you cry to me? The mouth does not speak, but the mind shouts.
You too then, beloved, when you stand before a judge raging, tyrannizing,
threatening the greatest things, and other executioners doing the same, pray
you to God, and as you pray the waves are stilled. Is the judge upon you? You
flee to God. Is the ruler near you? You =52.459= call the Master. For is he a
man, that you should go away to a place? God is always near. If you wish to
beseech a man, you ask what he is doing, he sleeps, he is occupied; serving he
does not answer you. But in the case of God none of these; wherever you go and
call, he hears; no occupation, no mediator, no servant walls you off. Say,
Have mercy on me, and immediately God is present; For still, he says, while
you are speaking, I will say, Behold I am present. O word full of gentleness.


He does not even wait for you to finish the prayer; you do not yet finish the
prayer, and you receive the giving. Have mercy on me. Let us imitate this
Canaanite woman, I beg; Have mercy on me; my daughter is badly
demon-possessed. And the Lord says to her: O woman, great is your faith; let
it be to you as you will. Where is the heretic? Did he say, I will beseech my
Father? did he say, I will supplicate him who begot me? is there need of
prayer here? By no means. Why? Because the faith was great, the vessel was
great, and the grace too was poured out great. Where there is need of prayer,
the vessel is weak. O woman, great is your faith. You did not see a dead man
being raised, you did not see a leper being cleansed, you did not hear
prophets, you did not study law, you did not see a sea being split, you did
not see any other sign done by me; you were rather insulted by me, and you
were at a loss; I forbade your passion, and you did not withdraw, but you
remained; thereafter receive you also from me a worthy and fitting praise; O
woman, great is your faith. The woman has died, and her encomium remains,
being more splendid than a diadem. Wherever you go, you hear Christ saying, O
woman, great is your faith. Enter the church of the Persians, and you will
hear =52.460= Christ saying, O woman, great is your faith; into that of the
Goths, into that of the barbarians, into that of the Indians, into that of the
Moors, as much earth as the sun looks upon; Christ uttered one word, and the
word is not silent, but with a great voice it proclaims her faith, saying, O
woman, great is your faith; let it be to you as you will. He did not say, Let
your little daughter be healed; but, As you will. You heal her; you become a
physician; I put the medicine into your hands; go, apply it, Let it be to you
as you will. Let your will heal her. The Canaanite woman healed by will, and
the Son of God does not heal from himself? Let it be to you as you will. The
woman did not even command, nor did she order the demon, but she only willed,
and the will of the woman healed, and cast out demons. Where are those who
dare to say, that the Son accomplished it through prayer? Let it be to you as
you will. See also the nobility of the diction; he imitates his own Father.
For when God was making the heaven, he said, Let the heaven be, and the heaven
was; let the sun be, and the sun was; let the earth be, and the earth was; by
a command he brought the substance. So also he himself, Let it be to you as
you will. The kinship of the diction showed the communion of the nature. And
her daughter was healed. When? From that hour; not from when her mother came
into the house, but before she came. She came to find her demon-possessed, and
she found her healthy, healed by her will. And for all these things let us
give thanks to God, because to him belongs glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
