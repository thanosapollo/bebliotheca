* On the Saying, I Came to Cast Fire upon the Earth
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

I came to cast fire upon the earth; and, I came not to cast peace, but a
sword.
=62.739=
The child-loving mothers of infants, until the infants grow to a greater and
stronger age, holding forth the milk-nourishing springs of their breasts to
their dearest ones, coax them to draw the unwearied food of milk; but when
they come to a greater age and perception, so as to be able with teeth
thereafter to grind the tender foods, the mothers first crush the hardness of
the bread, and having prepared the food soft and tender, like marrow, they
lodge it in their mouth. Such are the teachers of the Church. For to those who
are still infants toward the habit of understanding, they do not set before
them thoughts hard to understand and hard to discern, but, in the manner of
mothers, having let down to them the teat of the mind, they invite them with a
gentle nod to draw the unadulterated milk of the word. Then, whenever the
infants have grown in understanding toward a greater grasp of thoughts, they
are accustomed to uncover for them the thoughts of greater mysteries. This
Paul also used to do; for writing to those who were infants he said: I gave
you milk to drink, not meat. In the same manner our Lord still even now does;
for having measured the writing of the evangelical thoughts according to the
measure of the hearers' habit, in the days that went before, as to infants, he
supplied us the food of the word tender and easy to digest; but now, having
observed the mind of our understanding grown, he grants us to enjoy greater
teachings. For what does he say? I came to cast fire upon the earth; and, I
came not to cast peace, but a sword. But those who are infants in their minds
think that the Lord has come to consume all things with fire, and to slaughter
all with a sword. But come, and let us, having thought the same things as the
infants, converse with the Lord about the Lord's words, that we may ferry the
Lord's words across to the infants. I came to cast fire upon the earth. Did
you come to cast fire upon the earth, Lord, and did you not come to cast
peace, but a sword? Sin has overthrown us, death has lorded it over us, a
gehenna of fire has been threatened against us, the army of the accursed
demons has over =62.740= thrown us, and hearing of you as a helper from the
prophets we waited; and having come you say, I came to cast fire upon the
earth; and, I came not to cast peace, but a sword? There was no trophy of
faith among us, we had no strength against the devil's array; for we had no
weapon of faith; the devil, having stripped Adam while he was still in
paradise of our breastplate of righteousness, left him naked. Therefore each
of us, being naked of help, cries to you saying: Judge, Lord, those who wrong
me, fight those who fight me; take hold of weapon and shield, and arise to my
help; pour out the sword, and shut it against those who pursue me; say to my
soul, I am your salvation; and having come to our help you say, I came not to
cast peace, but a sword? And where is the supplication of the saints that was
made to you concerning peace, the, Lord our God, give us peace. For you have
rendered all things to us. We begged for peace, we asked for calm, we did not
want war, we refused a sword; and having come you say, I came not to cast
peace, but a sword? He armed a terrible army of the accursed demons against
us; having slaughtered us all with the two-edged sword of sin, he hurled us
down into hades. For this reason we ask pity from you; the whole choir of
saints cried out to you saying: You who shepherd Israel, give heed, you who
lead Joseph like a sheep, you who sit upon the Cherubim, appear, stir up your
power, and come to save us. You say, I came not to cast peace, but a sword?
and where is your promise that says: Behold, I turn aside to them as a river
of peace and as a torrent flooding the glory of the nations? or where is the
prophecy of the holy Fathers that says, Behold, the Lord goes forth, and he
will crush war? And when the war had been crushed, we waited for peace and
help; and having come you say, I came not to cast peace, but a sword? You sent
prophets beforehand, you sent patriarchs ahead, you armed them with weapons of
righteousness to stand against =62.741= the battle of the devil, and having
crushed the war to award peace to the world, as one of them giving thanks
said: Blessed is the Lord who teaches my hands for array, my fingers for war;
and these, having been defeated, have turned their backs, crying to you,
calling you as helper, saying: Lord, bow the heavens, and come down; and
having bowed the heavens, and come down to save the race of men in peace, you
say, I came not to cast peace, but a sword? And why do you bless the
peacemakers, that they shall be called sons of God? Why then do you who teach
others to make peace say concerning yourself, I came not to cast peace, but a
sword? Because of our sins the Lord has become our enemy; we needed a mediator
who would dissolve the enmity between us, and award sweet peace. And our
father Job prayed concerning the mediator saying: Would that there were our
mediator also to reprove; and having come to mediate and to dissolve the
enmity, you say the contrary, I came not to cast peace, but a sword? And in
what then shall we wait for the hope of peace? But yes, I came to cast fire
upon the earth, the earth that is trodden under your feet. Since the devil has
sown in the hearts of men thorns and thistles of sins, I came to cast fire
upon the earth, the consuming of the thorns. For this reason I came to cast
fire upon the earth, and I wish, if it is already kindled, to cleanse my
earth. I must consume with fire the devil's bitter-bearing and harm-working
sprouts, that I may sow the heavenly seed in a clean soul. For this reason I
came to cast fire upon the earth. Having formed man from the beginning from
earth, I settled a divine spark of fire in the midst of his heart, that
through fire he might cling to the love toward God. Since then it was
impossible to put forth this divine spark of fire and this warmth, that they
might have the fruitful kindling of the Holy Spirit (for the devil has
deadened the souls of men with the frost of impiety), I must cast fire upon
the earth; that having dissolved and loosened the winter of unbelief that has
come upon the souls from the devil, and having set the mind fair-weathered and
calm, I may make it bloom again and bear green. He therefore enters into his
fold, covering the shepherd in the lamb of the body, carrying no staff, no rod
threatening the former wandering, lest the sheep, having grown timid and
frightened, should again flee from its own shepherd. But he enters into the
house of the Pharisee, not as God, not as king, not as sun of righteousness,
but as man toward man, as lamb toward lamb, he dwells with him, feeds with
him, grazes with him, enters, reclines with him who has already fallen under
sin, that in reclining together he may raise up with himself the one already
fallen. And having entered he was not washed according to the custom of the
Pharisees. And this he did in two ways, that he might give the sheep an
occasion to speak to the shepherd, and further also to dissolve a Jewish
dogma, and to confirm a spiritual one. For the Pharisee, it says, saw, and
wondered that he was not first washed. He wondered, not praising the teacher,
but being at a loss at what was happening; not praising, but judging. And it
was possible to see, beloved, the fashioner of life being judged by the thing
fashioned, and the potter being corrected by the clay. And the Pharisee said
this, not seeing with the hidden eyes of the heart the one within, the
commander of the angels and of the =62.742= Seraphim and Cherubim. For if he
had beheld this one, he would have fallen at his knees, and with his tears
washed his feet, and said: Be merciful to me the sinner; for he would have
said: You are the one who brought all things from not-being into being; by
your word the heavens were established, and by the spirit of your mouth all
their host; gathering as in a wineskin the waters of the sea; you set the
deeps in the treasuries; of you all the earth is afraid; of you the whole
inhabited world is shaken. But having neither thought nor said any of these
things, he judged the teacher. But the Lord, having given an occasion, just as
also to that Samaritan woman in asking water from her, he gave her a time to
ask from him the eternal water; the Lord therefore begins gently to turn the
sheep, and to lead it by the hand from error to the truth. For now, you
Pharisees, you wash the outside of the cup and of the platter, but the inside
is full of plunder and deceit. That is, You now beautify the body, but you
make the souls evil. And how will even a golden cup delight, if it should be
so, when it has within a death-bearing drink? Are not the harlots such?
painting their faces with colors, they destroy their souls. For do they not,
reddening their faces with crimson, work with their own fingers an image of
the flame that is about to receive them, when they ought to sprinkle their
hearts with the blood of Jesus, and whiten their souls with the spirit, that
the flower of the soul might flash, as a rose mixed in a lily? But now they
sponge their faces, that they may draw the hearts of the young into madness.
Now you Pharisees wash the outside of the cup and of the platter, but your
inside is full of plunder and wickedness. John said: Why do you wash the body
with water, but do not wash the faults with the spirit? For I dare to say,
beloved, a man will be benefited nothing, even if he wash the body and the
spirit, if he does not have almsgiving as fellow-worker and companion. And as
a cup washed outside and inside, but not having drink, would not heal the
desire of the thirsty, and likewise a platter washed within and without will
not feed the hungry, if it does not have foods within; so also a man, even if
he be washed within and without, will not feed the Lord, if he does not feed
the fellow-servant. Therefore the Lord adds and says: But give as alms the
things that are within, and behold, all things are clean to you. But the
things that are within, he says. For he is not so delighted by the bringing of
those rich in gold, as by the unfeigned offering of the unadulterated. Do not
then cry out, O poor man, that you do not have so much gold and silver; cast
into the treasury what you have; even two mites justify; remember that widow,
that she was justified above those rich in gold. God does not know how to
weigh money, but he tests conscience. Almsgiving wipes away the stains of
adulterers, and treasures up good wages; almsgiving whitens the souls, and
weaves the tender ones. O almsgiving, life-giving of souls, supply of the
soul, good generalship. Cast gold, and receive Christ; give silver, and
receive the Holy Spirit; scatter bronze, and with it the rust of sin will go
out; dew forth mercy, and quench the furnace; and give garments, that you may
receive healings, in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory and the power
unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
