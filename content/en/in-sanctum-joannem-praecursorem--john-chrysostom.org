On Saint John the Forerunner.
=50.801=
1. Let us keep the festival of John the Baptist; with longing let us crown
him, than whom none greater has arisen among those born of women, according to
the voice of the Master. For nothing so honored the prophet as the baptizing
of the Lord. Let us go then to the Master's baptism, which most of all made
bright the forerunner of the King of the heavens; and let us in longing pass
within the places of Judea, from which we have harvested the yield of good
things; or rather let us write the matters in the word, and from the things
read here let us harvest the fruit. Let us suppose that Jordan, which received
its own Maker when he came in flesh, to be flowing through our midst; and let
us think John the Baptist standing at the lips of the river, and let us behold
the Master Christ coming to his own servant, and being baptized by him, and
being pointed out by him from heaven at the descent of the Spirit; and let us,
worshipers of the consubstantial Trinity, worship the Father who bore witness,
and the Son who was witnessed, and the Holy Spirit who bore witness.
Let us follow the book of the Gospels as it leads us toward the things longed
for, and setting our feet upon the divine words as upon tracks, let us run to
the mystery of the inhabited world that is being kept as festival. Then, it
says, Jesus comes from Galilee to the Jordan to John, to be baptized by him.
The Master came to the Jordan; for the treasury of wisdom did nothing out of
season or without reason, but each of the things he ordered for our sake he
accomplished at a well-timed season. For being God and Maker of every time and
every interval, he accomplished the time, and by the condescensions there
then, as with flowers, he laid down the beginnings of the things that have
come to pass from him. Then Jesus comes. Then: when? When he had run up all
the steps of age; when he had climbed the whole ladder of nature; when the
perfection of his body and of his soul shone forth; when, having become a
perfect man in thirty circuits of years, then in very truth he reached the
Jordan, and sinless he came with the sinners to the baptism, and having bowed
his own head to this man's hand, he was eager to receive the baptism.
But John the Baptist, having learned accurately him whom he had learned from
the Father from his own mother's womb, then with fear and much trembling
having drawn his right hand back from the head, What is this, he says, O
Master? what does this strange wonder-working mean? what is this humble and
gentle spectacle of this coming forward? Do you bow your head to me, O head of
the Church? do you bow your head to me, you who bowed the heavens and came
down upon earth? do you bow your head to me, you who have God as head? do you
bow your head to me, which the Cherubim worship? do you bow your head to me,
which the Seraphim revere and tremble at? do you bow your head to me, you who
in paradise, as in a bridal chamber, decked Adam and Eve as bride and
bridegroom, and by your voice set the crown upon their heads, and added the
blessing of fair offspring, and said to them: Increase, and multiply, and fill
the earth? you who from that time by a word manage nature, the constitution of
nature; you who marshaled birth against death, that this might plant more, as
that one prunes; you who roll the wheel of life as you will; you who fill with
every sanctification the heads that are bowed to you; you who are contained
nowhere; you who gush rivers of compassions, do you wish to sink down together
into this river? and you, the maker of the streams, have you chosen to
converse with these? and do you ask the baptism which you yourself gave me to
give to those in need? And where have you need of a baptism of repentance, you
who yourself have mercy on those who repent? where have you need =50.802= of
such a medicine, you who by a command alone heal the wounds of others? where
do you need repentance, you who do things worthy of praise? did you not come
down with men, as the only lover of mankind? You wear the form of a slave,
which you fashioned for yourself; and through this you have sanctified your
image, which the devil shattered, renewing it as you willed; you have become
the begetter of us all. Has not the exiled Adam been called back in you? does
not the race once condemned reign in you? did you not through Mary raise the
fallen Eve? do you not go about, wishing to make men equal to angels? do you
not drive lawlessness from the earth as a plotter against the race? do you not
bring in good order to life as a nurse of humanity? do you not scare away the
gloomy night of deceit? do you not cause the day of the knowledge of God to
rise for those who wish to see its beauty? do you not spread the light of
truth over the souls of all? do you not dissolve the clouds of impiety as
smoke? do you not, as sun of righteousness clothed with the white cloud of the
body, sow everywhere the rays of the Godhead? are you not a virgin in look,
and in step, and in form, and in reasoning, and in mind, and in soul? did you
not from a virgin fashion for yourself a virgin body? Do these things not need
understanding and contrition? For what cause then shall I baptize you now,
Maker of the river, and giver of baptism, and man through love of mankind?
There is no trace of lawlessness in you, nor has a stain of sin defiled your
undefiled flesh, nor has deceit ever sat beside your mouth. The poison of asps
is hidden in Jewish lips; for they are offspring of vipers, and their tongues
are deceitful; but in your mouth springs of everlasting life gush forth; for
being life, you speak life and you bestow it. How then do you, the bestower of
the present and of the coming life, step upon poor streams? All the others who
are baptized by me hear these things from me: Repent; for the kingdom of the
heavens has drawn near; and believe in the Lord Jesus Christ the Son of God,
the Only-begotten. But when you are being baptized, what shall I say? what
shall I utter? shall I say, Repent? that who may receive your repentance? for
what other God is greater than you? Shall I say, Repent; for the kingdom of
the heavens has drawn near? and shall I announce the good news of the kingdom
to the kingdom? and shall I converse with you concerning you? for are you
ignorant of what you are by nature? do you not know the kingdom of the
heavens, which you have come bearing from heaven to men? Shall I say to you:
Believe in the Lord Jesus Christ? shall I charge you to believe in yourself?
and who has ever been admonished to believe in himself? For the one who comes
must believe that God is; but you are God, and Son of God, even if you are man
in what appears, and you are justly believed in by all. But shall I say to
you: Believe in your Father? For when were you seen unbelieving and an enemy
to the Father? and when were you separated from the Godhead of God who has
begotten you?
What then shall I say to you? Believe in the Holy Spirit? But this is
established in you, and with you. I have need to be baptized by you, and do
you come to me? the Only-begotten Son of God to the son of Zacharias? the one
from the virgin to the one from the barren woman? the one who did not loose
the virginity of his mother, to the one who loosed the barrenness of her who
bore him? the one worshiped from the womb to the one who from the belly
recognized your lordship? the one who became man to the voice of the
wilderness? the one so high to the one so lowly? the Lord of all creation to
the servant unworthy of your goodness? the one rich by nature to the one poor
by nature, and well-supplied through your grace alone? the one heavenly and
earthly to the one earthly only? the one who has the Godhead from above, and
the manhood from below, to the one =50.803= who sprouted from below only? so
great a Master to me the little household-servant? But a slave does not set a
master free, a lamp does not light a sun, clay does not set a potter right, a
sheep does not shepherd a shepherd, a forerunner does not honor a king. A
river does not bear your Godhead; a river does not measure him whom heaven
does not contain; the Jordan does not wash its own Maker; the water recognizes
him who fashioned it; these streams do not know the light, and shall I dare
the ministry? My soul grows dizzy, my trembling hand fears to draw near to
your all-holy body. What shall I do? what shall I accomplish? Both I tremble
at you, Master, and I long for you; and I do not dare to flee, and I have not
the courage to come near; and I shudder to give the baptism, and I long to
receive sanctification. I have need to be baptized by you; I have been taught
to worship you, not to baptize you; I rather bow my own neck to your right
hand.
Bless me, you who bless the years of the righteous; make me dance today in the
Jordan streams, as you formerly made me leap among the embryos in the womb.
Yes, says the Master, O Baptist, I marvel at your reverence, but I seek your
obedience. You have become an advocate of my kingdom, but you ought also to
become a minister of my economy. I have come to you, because this profits both
you and those after you; I have come to you, that all men may come to me; I
came down from the heavens, and I am wholly in the bosom of the Father, that I
may join the creature to the Fashioner; I have come, I who am present, that I
may catch those who flee; I have come as man, where I was present as God, that
I may make men gods; I took from you a poor mother, that you might be able
with boldness to call my Father; I was fed as an infant with the food of milk,
that I might set myself as food before the faithful; I was wrapped in a
child's swaddling-clothes, that I might tear apart the bonds of lawlessness; I
was laid not on a royal bed, but on a humble manger, that after my lying in
the manger no longer might any of the rational suffer unreason as if
irrational; I received in my flesh circumcision, which I myself formerly
legislated, that I might bring in the circumcision not made with hands; I
offered the lawful sacrifice as firstborn, I the Only-begotten, that I might
recast such a sacrifice, and make myself a strange sacrifice. I fled into
Egypt, but I did not cease from being a refuge of all; I became a new infant,
a perfect man, as you see, that I might grace the imperfect with perfection; I
came through every age, that I might wall every age with the advance little by
little. I have stepped also upon a river, that I may make the earth-born step
upon heaven; and I receive this baptism, that I may grant to my disciples the
baptism of regeneration. I endure a sinking, as I endured the birth according
to the flesh. As lawgiver I honor the just things of the law, that I may make
new the symbols of grace; as a physician in health I partake of the medicine
of the sick, not needing it, that I may invent a newer help, that of the font,
for those who are ill. Do I accept a cross for sinners, and shall I refuse a
bath without danger? am I about to endure a three-days' death, and shall I not
make a sinking without pain? am I eager to go down into the very depth of
Hades, and shall I not embrace a river's stream? Baptize me then, me who am
about to baptize and save those who will; baptize me, me who am about to raise
to heavenly height the nature baptized in the sins of men; baptize me as man
below, me who above am hymned by the angels as God. Become a swift servant of
my mysteries; turn the refusal into a good deed; stop the tongue, and move the
right hand to the work; set your hand with courage upon my crown, which was
taken from your lump for your sake; gain also yourself from my baptism the
being named Baptist.
Suffer it now; for the present baptize, and then theologize; for the present
baptize, and then worship. For thus it is fitting for us to fulfill all
righteousness. =50.804= Nothing so honors the lofty one as voluntary
condescension; for true height does not honor mild deeds. Then John suffers
him, crying to him: Behold, I minister to your command, Master; for what I
learned while not knowing, these things I accomplish with joy. Behold, I bring
my right hand to your head, since you commanded this; and I am your slave, and
son of your handmaid; and my hand is your workmanship, and this river is an
outflow of your lordship, and these streams are your works, and this baptism
is your gift; and you alone know what mystery you are working. Be baptized, be
baptized, I beg; and save, as you will, those whom you fashioned. Blessed am
I, who through you was strangely born, and strangely nourished in the
wilderness, and now have been counted worthy of this ministry; blessed am I,
having been approved by you to serve so great a mystery. O right hand, how
high you have been raised! O clay instrument, how you have been honored! Lover
of mankind and great-giving Lord, after mixing the grace with the waters, and
giving them the sinking as a kind of leaven of sanctification, having gone up
from the waters (I fall on my knees, Master), run up and overtake humanity
gasping out its soul and toiling, visit her who is fevered of her own will,
raise her who is stretched out upon the earth as upon a bed, and is sick with
a various passion; as a physician drive the diseases away.
The lame wait for you, that when you have spoken they may run; the blind wait
for you, toward the recovering of sight; all the dead look for your voice,
toward resurrection; those burning with the heat of misfortunes thirst for
your presence; stand over all as a dew of healing. And Jesus, having been
baptized, went up from the water; and behold, the heavens were opened to him,
and he saw the Spirit of God descending, as a dove, and coming upon him. O
strange and wondrous mysteries! heaven from above worships him whom the river
a little before washed over; and the Holy Spirit theologizes him whom the
slave baptized. And behold a voice from the heavens saying: This is my beloved
Son, in whom I am well pleased. This is the one who was baptized, not through
his own need, but through love of mankind alone, the one witnessed by the Holy
Spirit, the one proclaimed by me who have clearly seen him; the one begotten
from me before the ages, and now born in flesh from Mary, the one
consubstantial with me and with you; the one co-eternal with me, and conformed
to your form; the maker of Adam, and descendant of David; the fashioner of the
Virgin, and son of the Virgin; the one thought and the one appearing; the one
surveyed by faith and by sight; the one perfect in Godhead, and perfect in
manhood. This is my beloved Son. This one, both working wonders as God, and
suffering as man, is my Son; for a cross does not separate from me him who is
by nature inseparable. This one even when pierced with the nails is my Son;
for nails do not harm his divine essence. This one even when suffering is my
Son; for passion has no power to seize his Godhead. This one even when being
put to death willingly, according to that which is by nature subject to being
put to death, is my Son; for to die does not belong to Godhead by nature, nor
does it will it, nor can it; for death does not conquer life, and death does
not get the better of deathlessness. This one even when rising from the dead
is my Son; for as Maker of the whole he will raise the temple of his body,
which he allowed to be loosed by his enemies. This one going up with this
flesh into the heavens, from which he did not depart, is my Son.
He who receives this one receives me with him; he who worships this one
worships me; he who believes in this one believes in me; he who makes this one
small makes me small; for outrage of a child is a father's loss; he who
receives this one, and does not busy himself, but reveres without curiosity,
is blessed. This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. You sinners,
run to him who takes away the sin of the world; you condemned, overtake the
judge who loves mankind; you who have been put to death by sins, hasten to the
fountain of life. These are the fairest gifts of the Baptist's festival; all
these the Master Christ our God, who for our sake has =50.805= appeared, has
granted us; to him be the glory and the power together with the Father and his
all-holy and =50.806= life-giving Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of
ages. Amen.
