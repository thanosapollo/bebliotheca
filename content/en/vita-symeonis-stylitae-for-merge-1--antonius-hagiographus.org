* Life and Conduct of the Blessed Symeon the Stylite
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

1 A strange and marvelous mystery has come to pass in our days. It seemed good
to me, the sinner and least Antony, to write according to what I grasp. For
the composition is filled with benefit and compunction. Therefore I entreat
you, incline your ear and hear the things that I grasp. 2 The one among the
saints and most blessed Symeon, being a child in age, used to shepherd the
sheep of his father in the same manner as the prophet David; and on the holy
Lord's day he would go into the church toward the oracles of God and would
gladly listen to the holy scriptures, not knowing what he heard. And advancing
in age and being pricked by the word of God, he goes one of the days into the
holy church, and hearing the apostle being read he asks a certain old man:
Tell me, father, what is it that he reads. The elder says to him: Concerning
continence of soul. Saint Symeon says to him: What is continence of soul? The
elder says to him: Child, why do you ask me? For I see you young in age, but
possessing an aged understanding. Saint Symeon says to him: I am not tempting
you, father; but I am astonished at the name of the word. The elder says to
him: Continence is salvation of soul, guiding into light, leading into the
kingdom of the heavens. Saint Symeon says to him: Teach me, honored father,
the things you say, because I am unlettered. 3 The elder says to him: Child,
if one fasts to God unceasingly and renders all the prayers to him with
gentleness, that is, at the third hour one prayer, likewise also the sixth,
the ninth and the twelfth and the things that follow these; just as they are
also performed in the monasteries. If then you know, child, what you have
heard, reckon these things in your heart; for you must both hunger and thirst
and be insulted and be buffeted and be reproached, groan and weep and be
afflicted and be unwell, be healthy and desire to renounce and be humbled and
suffer many things from men, and thus be comforted by angels; see, you have
heard all these things, may the Lord of glory give you a good mind toward his
will. 4 And having heard these things Saint Symeon went out of the church and
goes to a desert place and sets himself on his face seven days, weeping and
praying to God, partaking of neither food nor drink. And after the seven days,
rising he comes running to a monastery. And he falls at the feet of the
archimandrite crying out and saying: Have mercy on me, father, a humble and
wretched man, and save a soul that is perishing and that longs to serve God.
The archimandrite says to him: Who are you and from where? And what is your
name? And from where have you come? The blessed Symeon says: By birth I am
free, and the name is Symeon, but how I have come here or of what parents I
am, do not ask me, master, I beg you, but redeem for yourself one soul that is
perishing. Having heard these things the archimandrite raised him from the
ground and the archimandrite says to him: If you are from God, the Lord will
guard you from every wicked and deceitful thing and you shall do the service
for all, that you may be loved by all. 5 And his parents did not cease weeping
and seeking him; but the saint was spending his time in the monastery serving
all and being loved by all and fulfilling the rule of the monastery. So one of
the days, going out of the monastery, he finds above the well a bucket, from
which they used to draw the water, having


a rope; and loosing the rope he goes to a private place and binds himself
about his whole body; and he puts on over the rope a hair tunic and went into
the monastery and says to the brothers: I went out to draw water and I did not
find the rope on the bucket. The brothers say to him: Be silent, lest anyone
learn of it to the archimandrite. And no one knew that he was bound with the
rope within. So he remained up to one year and more having the rope wound in
his flesh, and it ate up his flesh, so that the rope was covered by the
putrefied flesh of the righteous one, and from his stench no one was able to
stand near him, and no one knew the mystery. And his bed was filled with
worms, and no one knew what had happened. 6 And he would take his food and
give it to the poor, no one knowing. So one of the days one of the monks goes
out and finds him giving his bread and the pulse which he received to the
poor. And all used to fast until evening, but Saint Symeon used to taste from
Lord's day to Lord's day. And one of the monks, going in, slandered him to the
archimandrite saying: I beg your holiness; this man wants to dissolve the
monastery, that is the rule which you handed down to us. The archimandrite
says to him: How does he want to dissolve the rule? The monk says to him: We
have received to fast until evening, but this one eats from Lord's day to
Lord's day, and his bread, which he receives, and the pulse he secretly gives
to the poor each day; and not only this, but also from his body an unbearable
stench rises, so that no one is able to stand near him; and his bed is filled
with worms, and we are not able to bear it. But if you wish, keep this one
here and we withdraw, or dismiss him to go away, whence he came. 7 Having
heard these things the archimandrite became astonished, and he examines his
bed, and finds it filled with worms, and from the stench he was not able to
stand there. And the archimandrite says: Behold even the young Job. And
seizing him he says to him: Why have you done these things, O man? Whence is
this stench of yours, why do you lead the brothers astray, why do you dissolve
the rule of the monastery? Or are you a phantom? Go away elsewhere and die
apart from us; was it perhaps through you that I the wretched one came to be
tried? For if you were a true man from prudent parents, you would have
announced to us who your father was and your mother and your race, and whence
you came here. Having heard these things the saint, looking down toward the
earth, was silent saying nothing, and from his tears the place in which he
stood was filled; and the archimandrite, having become furious for a long
time, says to the monks: Strip him, that we may see whence this stench is. 8
So they wished to strip him and were not able; for his garment was stuck from
the rotten flesh. So for three days they did not cease soaking him with warm
water with oil, and thus with much pain they were able to strip him, so that
with the garment his putrefied flesh was torn off, and they find the rope
wound about his body, so that nothing of it appeared, except only the ends of
the rope; and of the worms that were in him there was no conjecture; then all
the monks became astonished at him seeing that incurable wound, and they were
reasoning among themselves, how and by what device they might take the rope
from him; but Saint Symeon was crying out saying: Forgive me, my lords
brothers, let me die thus, the stinking dog, for according to my deeds I ought
also to be judged; for all injustice and greed has come to be with me, for I
am the


sea of sins. And the monks and the archimandrite were weeping seeing that
incurable wound; and the archimandrite says to him: You are not yet eighteen
years and what sins do you have? Saint Symeon says to him: The prophet David
says: Behold I was conceived in iniquities and in sins my mother bore me; and
I have been clothed with the like of all these. And the archimandrite was
amazed at his prudent answer, that being a rustic how he was pricked unto the
fear of God. And the archimandrite, having called two physicians, with many
toils and pains so that he was thought to have died, they tore the rope from
him † from the flesh stuck to it, and having tended him fifty days they
benefited him in part. And the archimandrite says to him: Child, behold you
have become healthy; go where you wish. 9 Then Saint Symeon goes out from the
monastery; and behold there was a well lying near the monastery, in which
there was no water, but a multitude of unclean wicked spirits dwelt in it; and
not only of unclean spirits, but also of asps and vipers and snakes and
scorpions an incomparable multitude; whence all were afraid to pass through
that place. And Saint Symeon went there, no one knowing, and throws himself
into that well, having made the seal in Christ, and hides himself in the side
of the well. 10 So after seven days of his departing from the monastery the
archimandrite sees in a dream an incomparable multitude of men in white
clothing circling the monastery, holding lamps and saying: Now we burn you
here, unless you hand over to us the servant of God Symeon. And for what cause
did you drive him out, and what did he do, that you cast him out of the
monastery, and what is his fault? Tell us, before we burn you; do you not know
what you had in your monastery? For this one will be found greater than you on
that fearful and terrible day. And the archimandrite, having been awakened,
becoming trembling, says to the monks: As I see, that man is a genuine servant
of God; for behold I suffered many evils in a dream this night because of him;
but I entreat you, brothers, run about for me and find him for me, since
otherwise let none of you come here. 11 And going out they sought him in every
place and not finding him they came saying to the archimandrite: Truly,
master, we did not leave a place where we did not seek, except the place in
which no one dares to pass because of the multitude of the wild beasts. The
archimandrite says to them: Children, having made a prayer and taken lamps, go
and go down and search for him. And having made a prayer above the well for
three hours they let down into the well five monks with ropes holding lamps;
and the reptiles seeing them fled into the corners. And Saint Symeon seeing
them cried out saying: I beg you, brothers and servants of God, grant me a
little, that I may give over my spirit, for it is enough for me, that what I
set myself to I did not fulfill. But the monks, forcing him with much force,
brought him up from the well, dragging him as one who had done evil; and they
led him to the archimandrite. And he, seeing him, fell at his feet saying:
Forgive me, servant of God, become my teacher yourself and teach me the things
which endurance has and grants. 12 But Saint Symeon did not cease weeping and
entreating God, and having spent three years in that monastery he goes out, no
one knowing, and goes to a desert place, in which there lay a good number of
villages, and near it was a


village called Gelasois, and he builds there for himself a small place of dry
stones, and he stands in the midst four years being snowed on, rained on,
burned by heat, and many were going to him; and his food was soaked lentils
and his drink water. And after these things he made for himself a pillar of
four cubits and stands on it seven years, and his fame was running everywhere;
and after these things the crowds build for him two enclosures of dry stone,
and they set a door on the inner enclosure and they make for him a pillar of
thirty cubits, and he stands on it fifteen years performing many healings; for
many who were demon-possessed used to go there and were healed. 13 And Saint
Symeon used to imitate his teacher Christ, whom also calling upon he made the
lame walk, cleansing lepers, making the mute speak, the paralyzed run,
granting healing to the long-sick, admonishing and exhorting each: If someone
says to you, who healed you, say you, God healed me; let it not seem good to
you to say that Symeon healed me, since you would find yourself again in the
same sufferings. And this I say to you: never lie and swear by God; but if
also a necessity comes upon you of swearing, swear by me the humble one,
whether with truth or with falsehood; for it is a great sin and a fearful
thing to swear by God. 14 Hear a fearful and marvelous wonder: his mother
after twenty years learns where Saint Symeon is and having come running she
wished to see him after so many years and weeping much to see him and she was
not permitted to see him; and as she wished for a long time to be sanctified
by his holy hands, she was forced to climb upon the wall; and climbing upon
the wall of the enclosure she was dashed to the ground, not being able to see
him. And Saint Symeon sends word to her: Forgive me, mother, for now, and if
we are worthy in that age we shall see one another. And when she heard these
things, she was the more inflamed to see him. And Saint Symeon sends word to
her: Cease, my lady mother, since you have come from afar and have toiled for
me the humble one, but set yourself down a little and resting take strength
and after a little I see you. And she, having heard, sets herself at his
doorway and immediately she gave over her spirit to God. And the attendants
came to wake her; and they found her dead and they announced to the saint
concerning her. And he, having heard, orders her to be brought in and he sets
her before his pillar, and seeing her with tears he began to say: Lord, the
God of the powers, the guide of the light and charioteer of the Cherubim, who
guided Joseph, who empowered your prophet David against Goliath, who raised
Lazarus four days from the dead, raise your right hand and receive in peace
the soul of your handmaid. And as he was praying her holy relic moved and she
smiled in the face, and all who were watching were amazed and glorified God;
and having buried her they buried her before his pillar, so that, as he prays,
he remembers her. 15 Hear another strange and marvelous mystery: some were
coming to prayer from a long way off, and a grazing deer meets them, having in
the womb, and one of them says to the deer: I adjure you by the power of the
holy Symeon, stand, that I may seize you. And immediately the deer stood, and
having seized it he slaughtered it and they ate its flesh; but the hide
remained; and immediately their speech was hindered, and they began to bleat
like the irrational animals, and they come running and fall before his pillar
begging to obtain healing, and they filled the hide of the deer with chaff and
for the recognition of many people the hide was set for a sufficient


time, and the men, having spent a sufficient time in repentance and having
been healed, withdrew to their own. 16 Hear another strange and wondrous
thing: a certain woman, being thirsty through the night, wished to drink
water; and taking hold of the pitcher of water she drinks down together with
the water a small snake, and being nourished in her belly it became large, and
her face became as the appearance of green grass, and many physicians came,
that they might heal her and they were not able; and those in her house,
having known the wonders and the healings which Symeon the saint of God used
to do, taking her they led her to the saint and they announce to him all the
things concerning her and he orders them saying: Put in her mouth from this
water and from this spring. And having done thus according to what was
commanded, the beast was moved before all, and she dashes herself to the
ground, and it comes out and having set down its head in the midst of the
railings it made a noise, and all glorified God. 17 And they changed his
pillar making it of forty cubits, and he was noised abroad into the whole
inhabited world. Whence the multitude of the Saracens came to him burning with
the faith, and he pricked them unto the fear of God. So the man-hating devil,
having the custom of tempting the saints and of being trampled by them,
brought a plague upon his thigh in the manner of the blessed Job, a pain of
the so-called plague, and his thigh was putrefied and thereafter he stood on
one foot for a two-year time. And incomparable worms were falling from his
thigh upon the earth, whence those near him had no other work except only to
collect them and bring them up to the place whence they fell, the saint
saying: Eat, whence the Lord has given to you. 18 And according to the will of
God it comes to pass that the king of the Saracens comes to prayer to him, and
as soon as he went near the pillar to be blessed by Saint Symeon himself, the
saint of God seeing him began to admonish him. And as they were speaking a
worm falls from his thigh, and the king sets his mind, not indeed knowing what
it was that had fallen, and running he snatches it. And he sets it upon his
eyes and upon his heart and goes out outside holding it in his hand. And the
saint sends word to him saying: Come in, put down what you took up, you bring
a burden to me the sinner. It is a stinking worm from stinking flesh. Why do
you destroy your hands, being an honored man? And when the righteous one had
said these things the Saracen comes in and says to him: This shall be for me
unto a blessing and unto forgiveness of sins. And having opened his hand there
was a precious pearl in his hand. And seeing it he began to glorify God and
says to the righteous one: Behold what you said is a worm, it is a pearl such
as has no price, through which the Lord has enlightened me. And after these
things the saint, having heard, says to him: As you have believed, be it unto
you all the days of your life, and not only to you, but also to your children.
And having been blessed the king of the Saracens went away to his own
rejoicing in peace. 19 Hear another mystery. On the mountain on which he
stands, toward the east a great dragon remained. Whence not even herbage used
to come up in that place. And it happened to that dragon, as he was going out
to cool himself, that a stick entered into his eye, and for a sufficient time
from the pain no one was able to bear his hissing. So one of the days the
dragon comes out from his den dragging himself and comes, all seeing, and sets
himself at the doorway of the enclosure. And suddenly his eye was opened and
the stick comes out from his eye, and he spent three days there, until he was
restored healthy. And thus he went away to his place, all seeing,


having wronged no one, but as a sheep thus he lay at the doorway of the
righteous one, and all used to go in and go out being wronged in nothing by
him. 20 Hear another marvelous wonder. There came to be a chief robber in
Syria, his name Antiochus, and his surname was called Gonatas, of whom the
things concerning him were spoken in the whole world. And from here and from
there soldiers were sent to hunt him and bring him to Antioch, and no one was
able to hunt him because of the great power of his strength. So bears and
other beasts were prepared in Antioch, as he was due to fight with beasts, and
the whole city of Antioch was moved because of him. And they go out to hunt
him and find him drinking, in a certain village in an inn, and the soldiers
surrounded the inn. And he, having learned, began to make a tragic show, and a
river lay by the village, and that chief robber had a mare and he used to
command her as a man, and rising he throws his garments upon the mare and says
to her: Go to the river and wait for me there, and the mare goes out from the
inn biting and kicking, and having gone to the river she was waiting for him.
And the chief robber also goes out from the inn having bared his sword, crying
out and saying to the multitude of the soldiers: Flee lest anyone die, and
none of the soldiers mastered him. And having escaped the notice of all who
were guarding him and having crossed the river together with the mare, and
having mounted her, he reached the enclosure of Saint Symeon. And going in he
threw himself before his pillar, and when the soldiers had gathered in the
enclosure of the saint the saint says to them: With our master Jesus Christ
two robbers were crucified, the one according to his works received, but the
other inherited the kingdom of the heavens. And if anyone is able to withstand
the one who sent him, let him come and himself snatch him away, for I neither
brought him nor am I able to dismiss him. For the one who sent him here
himself also takes his part, so let no one any longer revile me the humble
one, who has toiled much because of my many sins. And having said these things
to them he dismissed them. And as these were going away the chief robber says:
My lord, I am going away. But the saint says to him: Do you go away again into
the same evils? And the chief robber says: No, master, the Lord is calling me.
And having stretched out his hands to heaven, he said nothing more, except
only: Son of God, receive my spirit in peace. And he wept for two hours, so as
to make even the righteous one weep together with those standing by. So
setting himself before the pillar of the righteous one, immediately he gave
over the spirit, and the crowds wrapped him and buried him near the enclosure
of the righteous one. So on the next day there come from Antioch more than a
hundred men with swords to snatch him and they began to shout against the
righteous one: Release to us the one whom you have. But the holy one said to
them: Brothers, the one who sent him here is stronger than you, and having
need of him as being useful, having sent to him two fearful soldiers in arms,
who were able to thunderbolt your city with those dwelling in it, he took him,
and I the sinner seeing their fearful form, having feared I did not dare to
speak against them, lest they kill me the humble one also as opposing God. And
those men, having heard these things from the saint and having learned with
what glory the chief robber gave over the spirit, glorifying God and trembling
went away again to Antioch. 21 Hear another fearful and glorious wonder. In
the place in which the saint was, water did not appear. And the multitude of
the animals and of the people was perishing of those coming to the place of
Saint Symeon. And the saint, having made a prayer for seven days, did not
speak to anyone, but was having bent his knees and praying, so that all
thought


that he had already died. And about the fifth hour of the seventh day suddenly
water gushed forth from the east of his enclosure, and having dug they found
the form of a cave filled with water. And they built in it seven mouths. And
all glorified the God of heaven and of earth. 22 [And a certain queen of the
Saracens, being barren, went away entreating him that she might bear. For she
was reproached by her husband each day and falling down much and entreating
for a sufficient number of days she was persevering at the pillar of Saint
Symeon. So the saint sent word to her: Go away to your house and the Lord has
to grant you the things that are beneficial. And having gone away to her house
she came together with her husband and immediately she conceived. And she bore
a daughter. And for five years she neither spoke nor walked. And she comes
with her husband and her child weeping, and Saint Symeon answering said to
them: Remain and God has to help. And when they had spent seven days they were
benefited in nothing and being grieved they were withdrawing saying that
Certainly the Lord did not wish to heal her. And as they were journeying and
had gone not a little they turn to see the column of Saint Symeon. And behold
their daughter suddenly cried out saying: Glory to you, Saint Symeon. And the
parents of the child, seeing, glorified God through Saint Symeon. 23 And hear
another mystery. One of the days as soldiers were journeying, a certain woman
from much desire having come to see the face of Saint Symeon by what pretext
to see him; so she was devising to take on the form of a man and to see him.
So one of the days she saw a sufficient number of soldiers going away to the
saint to pray. And having taken on the military form she also was journeying
with them. And having arrived at the place where the pillar of Saint Symeon
was, she says to the soldiers with him: Brothers, if you wish, leaving me your
animals go in and be blessed by the righteous one, and when you come out I
also go in again, that I may be deemed worthy to be blessed by the righteous
one. And when they had gone in and had done obeisance the Saint Symeon says to
them: One soldier from you was left outside. And they said to him: Yes,
master; he guards our animals. And the righteous one answering said to them:
Going out tell him: Do not trouble yourself, for you have been heard and you
are blessed to the Lord. And they, going out, were inquiring, entreating him,
What good has been done for you before God? for behold the righteous one said
to us, Tell the soldier with you who remained outside: do not trouble
yourself, for your petition has been heard and you are blessed to the Lord.
And answering she said to them: I confess to you, brothers, I am a woman and
from much desire I had to see the righteous one, since I am a sinner. And the
soldiers, having heard, were amazed blessing God through Saint Symeon and they
went in peace. 24 And another mystery if you hear. When a sufficient multitude
had come † according to the custom with incense crying to him: Servant of God,
the people waits to receive your blessing, command, dismiss them, for behold
they have been waiting a long hour, and they were receiving no answer. For his
spirit was not present. And as I was crying to him persistently and receiving
no answer the deacon began to lament above beside the column, having thought
that he had died. And the multitude of the people, seeing, began to groan and
to beat a great beating, mourning their sins. And when after a sufficient hour
his holy spirit returned again, and answering he said to the people: Brothers,
a great ship was being storm-tossed violently in the sea at this hour,
having[ta] about three hundred souls; and they cried out


all the souls entreating the master God. And crying to me with terrible oaths
and having seen what was happening to them I entreated the God who is always
long-suffering upon our sins to calm the sea, and having given them a hand I
saved them all. And the multitudes of the people, having heard the mystery,
glorified God through Saint Symeon. And the righteous one, having blessed
them, dismissed them in peace. 25 From a female snake a great swelling came
out and from her pains she was going a mile. And the male, sharing the pains
of her sufferings, taking his female they were coming to lord Symeon and
having arrived at the pillar they were separated from one another. For the
female, not daring to be seen by the righteous one, went away to the women's
part, but the male going in among that multitude was doing obeisance to the
pillar making his head up and down and entreating the righteous one. And the
crowds, seeing the enormous size of the snake, fled from him. And Saint Symeon
seeing said to the crowds: Do not flee, brothers, for truly having a prayer he
has come here. For his female is very sick and she went away to the women's
part. And he says to the snake: Take up mud from the earth and bring it up to
your wife. And having put it on, blow on her and he has to heal her. And the
snake, having taken from the mud, was going to his female. And the crowds,
seeing, followed him to see what he was about to do. And they see his female
standing upright outside the railings having a great swelling and the snake,
having taken the mud, puts it on her and having blown on her in the presence
of all he healed her. And taking her he withdrew. And the crowds, seeing the
mystery, glorified God. 26 And another wonder came to pass in his days. 26 For
when the great threat of the fears had come upon the whole inhabited world all
the East and Antioch were being brought down to him entreating him to prevail
upon God. For many falls of dwellings were coming to pass and deaths of a
sufficient number of people, so that even the righteous one with the pillar
was being shaken as a reed by wind. And weeping with them he was saying to the
crowds: Brothers, all have turned aside, together they have become useless,
there is not even one. You do not hear me, but as to who will defraud whom
your fornications and your injustices have overflowed. Truly I say to you,
brothers: it is easier for me to speak with the master than with you the
unbelieving. And having commanded the litany to cease, he made a prayer, and
again the threat was remaining in the same things shaking all things. And he
permitted them to cry Lord have mercy. And as they were continuing for
sufficient hours and crying: Give us peace, he made a great prayer. And after
he had completed the prayer answering he said to the people: Brothers, truly I
say to you, that from the whole multitude except one soul another was not
heard; and that I may persuade you, behold in the midst I make him come. And
having commanded him to come in the presence of all he questions him and says
to him: Brother, believe me saying: from all these you alone were heard; but
inform all and tell us, what good has been done for you. But that one did not
wish, saying: I am a sinner, forgive me. And as he was being compelled for a
long time a voice came to him from heaven saying: Your petition has been
heard. And as the rustic was standing among all the people he began to narrate
to them that I am a man, a farmer, and it is my custom, my labor of the day,
whatever I work, I divide them into three parts. And first I set aside the
part of the poor, after the end of the public tax, and thus that of my
expense. Doing this until now I have not ceased. And all greeting were doing
obeisance to him. And they were pressing the man, each one being mad to see
him.


27 And a certain Julian, being a man of Ardaburius, did not cease troubling
Ardaburius saying: Permit me and going up I will bring him down from the
column and I fill him fehlt eine Zeile and having rushed toward him and having
set a ladder to the column Julian went up, the one expecting to bring him
down. And having gone up as far as the third step the ladder stood away from
the column and was hung from the earth as from four cubits, and he was hanging
in the air. And all were marveling at the spectacle that was coming to pass;
and Ardaburius, being distressed, stretched his bow wishing to shoot him and
immediately his hand grew numb and he was not able to release the arrow. And
not only was he seized in the hand but he also suffered in the feet until his
end.] 28 So the blessed one stood on various columns forty-seven years and
after all these things he sought him; and it was Friday; and he was shut up in
prayer and as he had custom, he spent the whole Friday and on the Sabbath and
on the Lord's day and he did not nod, as he had custom, to bless those who had
bent the knees. And when I saw this, I went up to him and I saw his face and
it was flashing as the sun. And as he had custom of speaking to me, he
answered me nothing. And I was saying in myself, that he has been rested; and
again I was unbelieving and I was fearing to draw near to him and having dared

he had died; and setting my face in my hands I wept bitterly and bending I
kissed his mouth and his eyes and his beard and lifting aside his garment I
kissed his feet and taking his hand I set it upon my eyes. And in his whole
body and in his garments there was a scent of myrrh, so as to be gladdened
from that fragrance. And standing about a half-hour I attended to his precious
relic and behold his body was shaken together with the pillar and I heard a
voice saying: Amen amen amen, and from the fear I said: Bless me, lord, and
remember me in your good rest. 29 And I went down and I did not reveal the
mystery to anyone, lest a tumult come to pass, but through a faithful man I
signaled to the bishop of Antioch Martyrius and to the general Ardaburius. And
on the next day the bishop of Antioch comes with also six other bishops; and
Ardaburius also comes with six hundred names, lest when the villages had
gathered they snatch his precious relic; for thus they were also planning. And
curtains came to be around his pillar. And three bishops go up and kiss his
garments having said three psalms and they brought up a lead coffin and they
placed his holy relic and they bring it down by machines and then all knew
that Saint Symeon had died, so that all the Saracens also were gathered in
arms with camels wishing themselves also to snatch the relic. And a confusion
of the crowd comes to pass so that the mountain was not appearing from the
multitude and from the smoke of the incenses and of the candles and lamps that
were burning, of which there was no number. And the voices of the weeping of
the men and women and children were going far and the whole mountain was
shaken from the cry of the birds that had gathered and circled the enclosure
of the saint. So when they had brought him down, they set him upon the marble
altar, which he had before his pillar, having already the fourth day died. And
thus his holy relic was laid out as having been rested before one hour. And
all the bishops gave him the love; and his face was very bright as the


light; and the hairs of his head and of his beard were as snow. And the bishop
of Antioch wished to take a hair of his beard for a blessing and his hand was
dried, and all the bishops made a prayer for him weeping and saying to the
holy relic that Nothing is lacking to you of the members or of the garments
nor again shall anyone take from your holy and precious relic. And as they
were saying these things with weeping the hand of the bishop was restored
healthy. And then with psalms and hymns they put him in the coffin. 30 And I
the sinner was watching before five and six days of his rest a man in a
fearful robe, which I am not able to narrate, and his length being as of two
men and speaking to him three times in the day and giving him the love. And I
wished to narrate to some the things concerning him and my mind was being
snatched and I was hindered from speaking until he was rested. So as the man
whom I said was coming to him, I was watching, he says, them eating; but what
they were eating, I did not know and they were psalming; but what they were
psalming, I did not know, except only the amen. And fear was holding me toward
the sight of the man. 31 So they set the coffin of the saint upon the carriage
and thus with candles and incenses and psalmody they carry him away to their
city Antioch. And as they were coming about five miles from the city in a
place called Merope the mules stood and were not moving; for a marvelous
mystery was there; for there was on the right of the road a tomb. And a
certain man remained at it; and the matter of the man was of this sort. For he
was loving a married woman for twenty years and he was not able to master her.
And the woman died, and they place her in that tomb. And that somehow the
hater of the good might gain the soul of the man, having gone to the tomb he
opens aside the slab of the tomb and comes together with the dead body. And
immediately he was made mute and was held in the tomb and he was no longer
able to withdraw from that place. And as the wayfarers were passing they were
attending to him sitting on the little steps of the tomb, and each for God's
sake was bringing to him, one water, another food. And according to the will
of God as the precious relic was passing on that day and (λιες ὁ) neither
speaking nor hearing, the carriage standing and the crowd, he came out from
the tomb crying out and saying: Have mercy on me, saint of God Symeon, and
having taken hold of the carriage, immediately his bridles were opened and his
wits came to understanding. And all seeing what had come to pass glorified
God, and from the cry of the people that place was shaken. And the man was
crying out: I today through you have been saved, servant of God, for I was
lost in the sin. 32 And the whole city went out to the meeting of the precious
relic wearing white with candles and lamps. And they bring him into the church
called of Cassian. And after thirty days the general Ardaburius commands and
they place him in the Great Church, and from there again according to a
revelation of God a prayer-house temple of the holy and righteous Symeon was
built and thus with much glory and hymns they transferred his holy relic into
that prayer-house. 33 And many entreated, having given gold and silver, that
they might receive a blessing from his holy relics. And the bishop granted to
no one because of the oaths which he swore. And many healings are performed in
the place where his precious relics are laid through the grace given to him by
our master Christ. And the saint of God Symeon was perfected in the month of
September αʹ, reigning
