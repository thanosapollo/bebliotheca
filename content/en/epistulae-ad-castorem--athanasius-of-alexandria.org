Of the same great Athanasius to Castor the most blessed concerning the
canonical ordinances of the coenobia.
Since you commanded me, most blessed father Castor, to make known to you the
canonical ordinances of the coenobia of the Easterners, and especially of the
Egyptians, of which I have become an eyewitness: yielding I to your command,
sent this discourse to you as in brief; in order that those gathered with God
in your new monastery may have a provision toward virtue, the canons of the
blessed men and others also. It is necessary therefore before all things to
relate concerning prayer, the cause of all good things, how also, and with all
the constitution, they perform this in the coenobia throughout Egypt
especially and the Thebaid. =28.852= For when they are gathered to the same
place at the time of the synaxis, when the psalm has been finished, they do
not rush at once to kneeling, but before they bend the knees, standing for a
little, and having the hands stretched out, they pray; and after these things
falling to the ground, and praying again for a little in the kneeling, they
all rise at the same moment; and again spreading out the hands, they fulfill
the supplications more intently and strainedly, no one bending a knee, or
rising from the kneeling, until the one making the prayer for them first
bends, or first rises. As therefore it has been said, when they come together
to perform the aforementioned synaxes, so great a silence is furnished by all,
as for it to be supposed that no human being is present in so great an
innumerable multitude of the brethren, especially at the time of the prayers,
when neither a spit comes forward, nor a cough sounds, no acedia, no
drowsiness, no yawn, nor groans with sound are sent up. For they say that the
one performing the prayers in slackness of mind with a cry sins doubly: first
as praying slothfully, second as striking the hearing of the neighbor with an
uneducated voice, and scattering the mind, the wicked spirits also attacking
together, who, whenever they see us standing unto prayer, weigh down the soul
with unseemly thoughts and acedia. And for this reason they do not hasten to
complete the psalms with noise and disturbance, nor do they rejoice in the
multitude of the verses, but in the understanding of the mind, following that
thought which says: "I will psalm with the spirit, I will psalm also with the
mind;" for they hold it more profitable to psalm ten verses with
understanding, than the whole psalter with confusion of mind. And when the
psalms and the canon have been finished, thus as we mentioned above, none of
the brethren dares even for a little to stand, or to converse with another;
but not even to come forth from the cell, or to leave the work which they have
in hand, fulfilling the apostolic command, unless perhaps a need arise for all
to come in together toward some necessary work that is due, which they thus
fulfill with zeal even going out, so that no conversation arises among them,
but each attending to the meditation of the mind and to the psalm, fulfilling
what has been set forth. And they keep with great zeal, lest anyone of the
brethren be left remaining with another, and especially with a novice,
privately somewhere, or stretching out his own hands dare to hold or to
embrace another. And if anyone is found transgressing this rule, he is
subjected to a most heavy penance. Concerning then the canonical manner of
both the prayers and the psalms, which ought in the

daily assemblies to be kept, enough has been said by us. And we ought
hereafter to relate concerning those who renounce the world =28.853= in what
manner they enter into these coenobia, and remain being humble-minded and
submitting even unto deepest gray hair. For so great among them is the
obedience and humility even unto old age, as would not be found even in the
first year among those who renounce in the monasteries among us. Therefore he
who wishes to live as a monk will not first be received inside in the
monastery, before he furnishes some proof and trial of the longing toward God
and of the humility and long-suffering through obedience for the most part.
Having been tested then in these things, he becomes acceptable among them, and
is taught not to have anything of his own substance stored up; for they know
that the one holding back anything of his own things is not able to remain
long-timed in the submission of the monastery, and to endure hardship unto the
end. For whenever any pretext or affliction whatsoever will move his mind, at
once the hope of what has been laid away, as a sling a stone, shakes him out
of the monastery. For this reason each of them is stripped of the ancient
substance, so that not even the very garment in which he came wrapped is
allowed to be worn any longer. But, all the brethren having been gathered,
being led forward into the midst, he is stripped of his own clothes, and
through the hands of the abba he is changed into the garments of the
monastery; so that through this type he may recognize that, having been
stripped of all worldly things and of boastfulness, and of conceit, he has put
on the poverty of Christ, and that he numbers himself without shame, and
equals himself to the body of the brotherhood. And the clothes which he puts
off the steward of the monastery takes and keeps, having set them apart until
he furnishes proof of his progress and of his manner of life, and of endurance
in various trials. And if they find him able to endure in these things, and
remaining in all zeal, and in the fiery heat with which he began, they number
him with the remaining brethren; but if they detect [φ. catch] in him a fault
of murmuring, or a sin of any disobedience whatsoever, stripping him of the
things which they put on him from the monastery, and wrapping him again in the
worldly garments, they thrust him out of the coenobium. So that from this
exactness hereafter it is not easy for the one wishing to enter as he wishes
or to withdraw from the monastery, but the one not remaining in every manner
in his own confession is reclothed out of the monastery. When therefore the
one received has been tested with this exactness which we have said, and has
been found blameless, he is not at once permitted to be mixed together with
the company of the brethren, but he is handed over to the one appointed with
the care of the strangers, and he is commanded to do all attendance and
service to these; and when he has served the strangers blamelessly for a whole
year, having been formed through this toward humility and long-suffering, he
is numbered hereafter with the company of the brethren, and he is handed over
to one of the elders, who =28.856= has this concern, how he may teach the one
being discipled, by what road beginning he may be able to arrive at the
perfection of the virtues. First then he teaches him to be eager to conquer
his own desires and pleasures, through commanding him the opposites of his
willings. For the fathers say that one is not otherwise able to bridle the
pleasures, or to get the better of anger, or of grief, or to acquire true
humility, or simply to be perfected well with a brotherhood in a coenobium,
unless first through obedience he put to death his own willings. And having
advanced the one being discipled with these ordinances and teachings as with
certain elements, and syllables, they teach in turn again not to hide his own
thoughts; but at once whenever they come upon him, to reveal these to his own
Father, and to trust nothing concerning the discernment of these to his own
heart; but to know that to be good or bad? which by his own

discernments having tested, the Father will show. For thus the one being
formed toward virtue will be able in nothing to be deceived by the enemy, as
inexperienced, and not yet having acquired knowledge of methods; they say it
is a clear thing of the art of the devil, to persuade those being discipled to
hide their own thoughts from the Fathers by their own disdain, as being
sufficient, forsooth, to counsel for themselves the things that profit, or by
their own vainglory being ashamed to make public their own inward thoughts.
And so great a submission is kept among them, that no one dares apart from the
abba even to go forward from the cell where he sits; and they are eager to
complete the things commanded by him thus, as receiving these from God. And
sitting in their own cells, being devoted to the work and to the meditation,
and to the prayer, and attending with zeal, whenever they hear the one
striking the door of the cell either unto the common prayer, or calling them
toward some work, at once leaving all things together, they rush toward what
has been commanded, with all zeal emulating the virtue of obedience; which
they prefer not only to the work of the hands, and to reading and to the quiet
in the cell, but also to all virtues, and they set all things second to this.
And that is even superfluous to say among the other things, that none of them
has acquired anything else except a kolobion, and a hemiphorion, and sandals,
and a sheepskin, and a mat; judging it a shame that there should be said among
them, my book, my writing-tablet, or any other thing of mine. And so much does
each of them offer every day from work and from his own sweat as provision to
the monastery, as not only to suffice for their own needs, but also to
minister to the service of strangers and of the poor. And laboring thus they
are not puffed up, they are not arrogant, nor does any of them from his own
work and sweat demand more than the need given by the abba; he does not
procure for himself a possession of any matter whatsoever =28.857= having
himself as a sojourner and a stranger of the whole world, and reckoning
himself rather a slave and a servant of the brethren, than a master of any
earthly thing whatsoever. And if anyone break a vessel of any sort whatsoever,
or lose it, he confesses to the abba the fault of his own sloth, and thus
repenting with humility he receives forgiveness. And whoever having been
summoned toward some work, or toward synaxis, having despised it meet more
slowly, or answer more harshly, or more rashly, or more carelessly, or do what
has been commanded while murmuring under, or preferring the reading to the
work and the obedience, complete the works commanded to him more slowly, or
having been dismissed from the synaxis not run back quickly to his work, or
converse with someone apart from necessary need, or hold the hand of another
with boldness, or meet with kinsfolk without the permission and blessing of
his abba, or receive a letter from someone, or attempt to write back to
someone without the abba, or commit any of the things like these, is subjected
to such a penance: The brethren having been gathered in the synaxis, having
thrown himself upon the ground, and confessing, he asks forgiveness of his own
fall. But the thicker faults than these if anyone is found doing, which are
contempt, contradiction with pride, or a going forth from the monastery
without permission of the abba, or a meeting with a woman, or anger, or a
fight, or enmity, or remembrance of wrongs, or love of money, which is leprosy
of soul, or acquisition of any thing whatsoever apart from the things given by
the abba, or of food coming about in a thievish manner without blessing, and
the things nearly like these, he is not subjected to the aforementioned
penance, but to a more vehement, and greater. And if he is not corrected, he
is cast out of the monastery. And that a reading is performed in some coenobia
at the time of the meal, is not from the canon of the Egyptians, but from the
Cappadocians, who, not so much for the sake of spiritual exercise, as for the
sake of quiet from the conversations, so great a thing

they ordained. But among the Egyptians, and especially among the
Tabennesiotes, so great a silence is furnished by all, when into so great a
much-numbered multitude of the brethren at the time of the synaxis of their
table, as is almost not found in others, so as that no one dare to cry out
anything apart from the leader of the table, who, if a need of something
arise, signifies this rather by a knock than by a voice. And these wear the
cucullia down to the eyelids, in order that, not having wandering eyes, they
may not look at one another more curiously; but they gaze only at the table,
and at the thanksgiving of God who nourishes. And there is a great guard among
them, that none of them take of food outside the table. And in order that we
may not seem to have left out anything of the coenobitic canons, one must
mention also the ordinances handed down in other =28.860= climates. Throughout
all Mesopotamia then and Palestine, and Cappadocia, and all the East at the
time of the synaxis, of the third, and sixth, and ninth hour, if anyone,
before the first psalm has been finished, does not meet, he does not dare
thereafter to enter into the oratory, nor to mix himself with those psalming;
but he awaits the dismissal of the brethren standing before the doors. And
when all are going out, having put a repentance upon the ground, he asks
forgiveness of his own sloth. But in the nocturnal synaxes of a second psalm,
pardon is furnished to those who delay among these; and the brethren make
weeks in succession both in the kitchen and in the other due tasks, at once
both resting themselves, and filling up a law of love, through serving one
another. And so great a humility and zeal is performed among them in the
service, as would not be furnished to masters by slaves. And when the week has
been completed, on the resurrection day after the morning hymns they hand over
again to others who are entering to serve the vessels marked for the use;
which receiving, they show so great a concern and care unto the guarding of
these, lest anything of them be broken or lost, as it is likely for the one
entrusted with sacred vessels and things dedicated to God. For they prepare to
render an account not only in the present to the steward, but also in the
future to God, if it happen that anything of them be lost from negligence or
sloth. And toward a greater faith of the things said, I will add to the
narrative what I beheld with my own eyes. In the week of a certain brother the
steward passing by beheld a few grains of lentil thrown on the ground, and he
did not despise it as a pitiable and not costly thing; but having accused much
the brother entrusted with the week, he gave him a penance, as neglecting the
things dedicated to God, and trampling his own conscience. For they take care
of all things with so great faith and zeal, and they administer all things, as
even those very things, which appear to us cheap and worthy of contempt, to
keep with much attention, such as to shift a vessel lying badly, to gather a
vessel thrown, believing that even for these they have a reward from the
Master God himself. And these canons of the weeks we have found taking place
throughout all the East. But among the Egyptians the exchange of the weeks is
not thus performed, but to one most tested of the brethren the care of the
kitchen is entrusted, for as long as the virtue of this one advances, and the
strength of the body ministers in the service, and neither old age nor
weakness hinders him. And since our discourse has been spoken concerning
submission and humility, through which we are able to go up to the highest
point of virtue, I take it as necessary to add to the narrative for the sake
of example a few from many achievements of the Fathers who have shone in the
coenobia in contests [saints]. =28.861= desiring to renounce the world, having
arrived in a coenobium, he endured for a long time before the doors of the
monastery,

until when by his much endurance with a small son he was received contrary to
the customary canon of the coenobium. And when after many toils he entered,
they were not only handed over to various works, but also they were separated
according to the dwelling-together of the cell, in order that by the unyoking
from one another they might little by little be released from the natural
affection toward one another; from which by the grace of Christ through the
much exercise the father was freed. And once toward a torture and a test of
his virtue the abba pretended to be vexed with anger toward the child, and he
commands the aforementioned father of his to take him and throw him into the
river. Then that one, as if having been commanded by God to carry his child,
arrives at a run upon the bank of the river, and hastening to fulfill the
command with fiery faith and heat of obedience, he was hindered by the
brethren, who came upon him and snatched away the child. So greatly then was
the faith of this one and the obedience accepted before God, that it was
revealed to the abba from God, that this one did the work of Abraham. And
after a little time the aforementioned abba of the coenobium dying, left this
one his successor. We knew another brother who had renounced, who was also
great according to the world (for his Father was serving as a count and
happened to be rich), and he had been exercised not moderately in zealous
readings. This one then having left the worldly distinction, rushed toward the
solitary life, and indeed toward a test of his faith and humility he is
commanded by the abba to carry ten baskets, and to sell them in his own city,
and not all together but to sell them one by one; which that one fulfilled
with all endurance and humility, having set the baskets on his shoulders, and
having sold them one by one, not having reckoned the cheapness of the matter,
and the brilliance of the family, and the seeming dishonor of the sale. For he
was eager to establish himself an imitator of the humility of Christ. And we
also beheld another of the Fathers, abba Pinophas, a man adorned with every
virtue, who was first-living and abba of a great coenobium, which is in Egypt
near the city Panepho; this one, seeing himself even through the way of life
present to him, and through old age, and through the leadership, being
glorified and honored by all, and not being able to exercise himself in the
practices of humility, with which from the beginning, through the submission
dear to him in which he was called, having fled secretly from his own
coenobium, he withdrew alone unto the extremities of the Thebaid; and having
taken up the schema of the monk and having put on worldly clothes he went away
unto the coenobium of the Tabennesiotes, thinking in this to escape notice who
he might be through the multitude of the brethren, and through being far from
the climate, in which this one's coenobium was. =28.864= And for many days
entreating he was received by the archimandrite, who hands over to him as to
an old man, and one useful for nothing else, the care and attendance of the
garden, together also with another brother, being submitted to whom he
completed the humility and obedience dear to him. And he was not satisfied
with this service only, but also as many of the works as seemed difficult to
others, these he did readily. Having continued thus escaping notice for a
three-year time, his disciples seeking him throughout all Egypt, he was
scarcely known by a certain brother, who had arrived there; who having beheld,
and having recognized the character of the face, and still doubting, attempted
also to hear his voice; for he did not have the sure from the sight alone,
seeing such a man having grown old, and holding a mattock, and working earth,
and carrying dung on his own shoulders, and casting it on the roots of the
vegetables. When therefore, tracking out the sound of the voice carefully, he
was able to grasp who he is, having thrown himself before his feet, he
furnished a very great astonishment to those seeing, that thus

he had done to a man, who among them as a novice, and not long ago having left
the world. was reckoned last. And still they were astonished most of all, when
they learned the name of this one, who before this was heard among them as
great; and they asked pardon, because having been ignorant of this one they
ranked him with the last novices. And having entreated him much, they sent him
out to his own coenobium unwilling and lamenting, inasmuch as not being
allowed to complete the humility and submission dear to him. Having spent then
a little time in his monastery, having been fired again with the longing of
humility and submission, having watched a time of night, he fled no longer a
foreign country; for having gone up into a boat he came unto the parts of
Palestine, hoping there to escape notice unto the end. Having arrived then in
Palestine he rushed unto the monastery near the cave where the Master Christ
was born; in which monastery he happened at that time to be sojourning. And
having arrived even there, he was not able to hide according to the master's
voice, which says: "A city is not able to be hid set upon a mountain." For
brethren having come from the parts of Egypt unto veneration of the holy
places, and having recognized him, led him away again unto his own coenobium,
having persuaded him with many entreaties and lamentations. With this holy man
then I dwelt a little time in Egypt; and having related what I heard of him
admonishing one brother, whom he received in my presence, I will finish the
discourse. You knew, he says, brother, how many days having continued before
the doors of the monastery you were received today; if therefore you learn the
cause of the difficulty, it will be able to contribute to you in this road, on
which you desire to step, and to work you a faithful slave of Christ. For as
to those serving the Master faithfully glory, honor toward the future has been
promised, so most heavy punishments =28.865= to those coming forward
lukewarmly and slothfully to this promise. For it is better according to the
Scripture not to vow, than to vow, and not to pay. And again it has been said:
"Cursed is the one doing the work of the Lord carelessly;" for this reason we
both turned away and delayed to receive, not that we do not desire the
salvation of you and of all who believe unto Christ, but in order that we may
not, receiving rashly and as it chanced, ourselves pay a penalty before God of
lightness, and praying, and make you liable to a heavier punishment, if toward
the present easily, and not having learned the weight and the exactness of the
profession, you be shown hereafter slack, and slothful. For this reason, as
has been said, you ought to know the cause of the renunciation, what you must
do. The renunciation then has been established nothing else unless a promise
of a cross and of death. Know therefore from the present day that you have
died, and that you are crucified to the world, and the world to you, according
to the Apostle; and learn thoroughly what is the power of the crucifixion;
since no longer hereafter do you live, but that one lives in you who was
crucified for you. In this type then and in this schema, in which the Master
was hung for us, it is necessary also for us to live in the coenobium, as also
the blessed David prays from the fear of God calling for his flesh to be
nailed. For as the one having the body nailed to the wood is unmoved toward
every activity which he wishes, so the one having the thought nailed by the
divine fear, is unmoved toward every fleshly willing. And as the one nailed to
a cross no longer reckons the present things, nor is disposed together with
his own willings, is not troubled by desire, is not stretched by care of
acquiring, is not puffed up by pride, is not kindled by contention, nor by
envy, does not grieve concerning present dishonors, does not remember past
insults; for a little later he awaits the exit from life through the cross; so
the one who has genuinely renounced the world, and nailed by the divine fear
as on a cross, awaiting each day the exit from this life,

has all the desires and the fleshly dispositions unmoved and inactive. Guard
therefore lest you ever desire to take up again these things, which renouncing
you have thrown off. For according to the sentence of the Master, "The one who
has put the hand upon a plow, and has turned unto the things behind, is not
fit unto the kingdom of the heavens;" and the one coming down again from the
high way of life toward the low and earthly things of this world acts contrary
to a command of Christ, coming down from the housetop, and attempting to take
up something of the things of the house. Guard therefore lest, having
remembered the ancient disposition of the parents according to flesh, and
having turned unto the cares of this age, you be found unfit according to the
voice of the Lord in the kingdom =28.868= of the heavens. Guard lest you ever
raise up against yourself the pride, which beginning you trampled with the
heat of humility, when you learn the psalter, or something of the Scripture,
or even according to the voice of the Apostle, building again the things which
you pulled down, you show yourself a transgressor; but rather as you began,
and confessed the humility before God and angels, keep it through unto the
end. Likewise the endurance, which you showed in the monastery, enduring for
many days before the doors, and entreating with tears, be eager to increase.
For it is truly wretched that you, who ought each day to add to the first
heat, and going up unto the perfect to progress, should subtract from it, and
withdraw toward the things below. For not the one beginning the good is
blessed, but whoever remain in this unto the end. For the serpent being
dragged upon earth always watches our heel, that is he plots against our exit,
and unto the end of our life he attempts to trip us. And for this reason to
have begun well will profit nothing, nor the beginning of the heat in
submission, unless also the end come about likewise. And the humility of
Christ also, which you now promised before him, will not otherwise be
confirmed, unless you show this unto the end. Wherefore, in order that you may
be able to acquire this perfectly, and to crush the head of the dragon, be
eager, when thoughts come upon you, immediately from the beginning to report
these. For thus making public the beginnings of the destructive thoughts of
the dragon, and not being ashamed to bring them out to your Father, you will
crush the head of this one. Wherefore indeed according to the Scripture, if
you come forward to serve God, prepare yourself not toward freedom from care,
not toward relaxations, but toward trials, toward afflictions. For "through
many afflictions we must enter into the kingdom of the heavens;" and, "Narrow
is the gate, and afflicted is the road that leads away unto the life, and few
are those who find it." Attend therefore to the few good, and from their
example regulate your own life; do not attend to the slothful and the
despisers, even if they be many; "For many," he says, "are called, but few
chosen," and small is the flock to which the Father was well pleased to give
the kingdom. For do not think it a small sin to profess perfection, and to
follow the more careless and slothful. In order therefore that you may be able
to step upon perfection, use this order, and go up by these steps. The first
step of virtue and beginning of our salvation is the fear of God; through this
both a cleansing of the sins, and a guard of the virtues, and a road upon the
perfection comes about. For when the fear of God enters into the soul, it
persuades to despise all the things of the world, and it generates a
forgetfulness of the kinsfolk according to flesh, and of this whole =28.869=
world. And from these things humility, the head of the virtues, and fullness
of all good things is achieved; for by contempt and deprivation of all things
humility is added. And humility is tested by these marks. First if one had his
own willings put to death for himself; and second if not only of his own
actions, but also of the inward thoughts he hides nothing from his own

Father; third, if not by his own understanding, but he trusts all things to
the discernment of the Father, thirsting always and longing, and hearing
sweetly his admonition; fourth, if he will serve all the commands of the
Father without shame; fifth, if not only he himself does not dare to insult
anyone, but will also be receiving with joy the insults brought upon him by
others; sixth, if he will attempt to do nothing newer, which the common canon
no longer urges, nor the examples of the Fathers; seventh, if being content
with all cheapness, and giving thanks even upon the things given, he also
judges himself unworthy of these; eighth, if himself always lower than all,
and is not rash in words; tenth, if he is not easy in words, and ready in
tongue. For by these and the signs like these humility is known. These things,
when they are achieved in truth, quickly lead up toward the height of love, in
which there is not a fear of punishment, and through which all things are no
longer kept with necessity, and toil, but with fiery longing and desire of the
good. Toward which virtue in order that you may have strength to arrive more
easily, let the words of the brethren who meet you, and who have acquired
reverence, be examples to you toward imitation; not the more, and those living
more carelessly and more slothfully. For also from imitating and emulating the
saints a very great benefit is added to those wishing to arrive toward
perfection. If therefore you wish to be able to follow out all these things,
and through these to endure through unto the end in the spiritual contest of
the coenobium, let these things which David said be necessary to you toward a
guard in the company of the brethren: "And I as a deaf man did not hear, and
as a man not hearing, and not having reproofs in his mouth." As many things
therefore as you see alien to building and benefit, become as blind, and as
many as you hear, as deaf and speechless, and not having reproofs in the
mouth. And do not imitate these, as many as are unfaithful, and disobedient,
and rash, and rabble, and insulters, but as deaf and not hearing, send these
by. If anyone send upon you reproofs, or revilings, or insults, be unmoved as
muzzled and speechless, always saying over this verse of the psalm: "I said, I
will guard my ways, that I may not sin in my tongue. I set a guard to my
mouth, when the sinner stood against me, I was made deaf, and I was humbled."
Still indeed toward these things also this fourth before all things meditate,
and guard, which also =28.872= is able to adorn these three things said above.
to make himself foolish and senseless, according to the command of the
Apostle, in this age, in order that you may become wise, that is discerning
nothing or doubting in whatever things you are commanded, doing the obedience
of simplicity and of faith. Reckoning that only holy of holies, and believing
it wise, and profitable, which has been commanded to you by your Father. For
having secured your heart with such a disposition, you will be able unto the
end to endure the yoke of Christ of the submission, and none of the trials and
devices of the devil will be able to shake you from the science of the life.
And do not expect your endurance, and the humility, and your long-suffering,
to be achieved from the virtue of the others; such as if no one provoke, or
will not set at nought, or will not dishonor, which is neither a proof of your
virtue, nor lies in the authority; but when being reviled, or being
dishonored, or being set at nought you bear it gently; for this lies in your

Of the same great Athanasius to the same Castor concerning the eight thoughts
of wickedness.
Having composed formerly the discourse concerning the ordinance of the
coenobia, again upon the present, having taken courage by your prayers, we
attempted to write concerning the eight thoughts of wickedness; I mean
gluttony and fornication, love of money and anger, grief and acedia, vainglory
and pride. First then it will be said by us concerning continence of belly,
which is opposed to gluttony; and the manner of the fasts, and the quality and
quantity of the foods. And these things we will not say from ourselves, but
the things which we have received from our holy Fathers. Those then do not
give over one canon of fasting, nor one manner of the partaking of foods, nor
the same measure to all, through not all having the same strength, either
through age, or through weakness, or through a habit of a more well-formed
body. But they have handed down one aim to all, to flee fullness, =28.873= and
to turn away from the satiety of the belly; and they approved the daily fast
to be more profitable, and to be contributing toward purity, more than the
fast dragged in three or four, or even unto a week. For they also say that the
one stretching himself immoderately in the fast often uses food beyond
measure, so that from this sometimes the body is weakened by the excess of
not-eating, and becomes more sluggish toward the spiritual liturgies; and
sometimes, being weighed down by the multitude of the foods, it produces
acedia and slackness in the soul. And again the partaking of vegetables is not
suitable to all, nor that of pulses to all, nor are all able to use the food
of dry bread; and again he says that one eating two pounds of bread is still
hungry, and another eating a pound, or six ounces is satiated. To all then, as
has been said, they have handed down one aim of continence, not to be deceived
by satiety of belly, nor to be drawn out by the pleasure of the throat. For
not only the difference of the quality, but also the quantity of the multitude
of the foods is accustomed to kindle the fired arrows of sin; for a belly
being filled with food of any sort whatsoever, bears seeds of profligacy. And
again not only a surfeit of wine, but also a fullness of water, and an excess
of all foods, make this stupefied and drowsy. For the Sodomites not a surfeit
of wine and of various foods became a cause of overthrow, but a fullness of
bread, according to the prophet. The weakness of the body is not opposed
toward the purity of the heart. These things then having furnished to the body
which the weakness demands, not what the pleasure wishes. The use of foods is
taken up as much as to minister toward the living, not as much as to be a
slave to the impulses of the desire. The commensurate partaking of the foods
and with reason takes thought of the health of the body, it does not take away
the holiness. A definition of continence and a canon handed down by the holy
Fathers exact is this, that while the appetite is still lying upon him, the
one eating abstain from the food. And the Apostle also having said: "Of the

flesh do not make provision unto desire," he did not hinder the necessary
steering of the life, but he forbade the pleasure-loving attendance. And
otherwise also toward a perfect purity of the soul the abstinence from the
foods alone does not have strength, unless also the remaining virtues run
together. Therefore humility through the obedience of the work and the wearing
down of the body, contributes great things to us. The abstinence from love of
money is not only not to have money, but not even to desire to have acquired.
The abstinence from anger, from grief, from vainglory, from pride, leads the
soul toward purity. All these things work the catholic purity of the soul. And
the partial purity of the soul through chastity, continence =28.876= and
fasting especially achieve. For it is impossible for the one having the belly
filled to war according to mind against the spirit of fornication. Therefore
let the first contest for us be to rule the belly, and to bring the body into
slavery, not only through fasting, but also through vigil, and toil, and
reading, and gathering the heart into the fear of gehenna, and into the
longing of the kingdom of the heavens. Second is a contest for us against the
spirit of fornication and of the desire of the flesh, which desire from first
age begins to trouble the human. Great is this contest and hard, and having
the wrestling double; because this has been established double, being
constituted in the soul and in the body. And for this reason it is necessary
to take up also the wrestling against it double. For neither is the bodily
fasting alone sufficient toward acquisition of the perfect chastity, and of
true purity, unless also an intent prayer toward God, and a common meditation
of the divine Scriptures, and labor and work of hands, which are able to
contract the unstable impulses of the soul, and to call it back from the
shameful fantasies; and before all things humility of soul contributes,
without which one will not be able to get the better even of fornication, nor
of the other passions. First then it is necessary with all guard to keep the
heart from filthy thoughts. For from this they come forth, according to the
voice of the Lord, wicked reasonings, murders, adulteries, fornications, and
the rest. For also the fasting has been commanded to us not only toward
hardship of the body, but also toward sobriety of the mind, in order that,
having been darkened by the multitude of the foods, it may not be unable
toward the guarding of the thoughts. It is necessary therefore not only in the
bodily fasting to show all the zeal, but also in the attention of the
thoughts, and in the spiritual meditation, without which it is impossible to
go up toward the height of true purity and cleanliness. It is fitting
therefore that we cleanse first, according to the voice of the Lord, the
inside of the cup and of the platter, in order that the outside also may
become clean. For this reason, if there is zeal for us, according to the
Apostle, to contend lawfully, and to be crowned, having conquered the unclean
spirit of fornication, let us not take courage in our own power or exercise,
but in the help of the master God. For the human does not cease being warred
against by this daemon, until he believe in truth, that not by his own zeal or
labor, but by the tipping and help of God he is released from this disease,
and goes up unto the height of purity. For also the matter is beyond nature,
and in a certain manner it comes about outside the body, the one who has
trampled the irritations of the flesh and the pleasures of this. And for this
reason it is impossible for a human by the wings, that I may speak thus, to
fly up toward this height and heavenly prize of holiness, and to become an
imitator of angels; unless grace of God lead this one up from the earth and
from the mire. For by no virtue =28.877= more do humans bound together with
flesh become like the heavenly angels, as by chastity; for through this, still
living on the earth, they have, according to the Apostle, the citizenship in
heavens. And a proof of our having acquired this virtue, that the soul does
not even in the sleeps run toward

an image of one shameful fantasy. For even if such a movement is not reckoned
a sin, nevertheless it is a token of the soul being diseased, and of not yet
having been released from the passion. And for this reason we ought to believe
that the shameful fantasies happening to us in the sleeps are a conviction of
the preceding sloth and of the weakness in us, the flux that comes about in
the relaxation of the sleep making manifest a disease hidden in the hidden
places of the soul. Wherefore also the physician of the souls deposited the
medicine in the hidden places of the soul, where he also knew the causes of
the disease to exist, saying: "The one who has looked at a woman toward
desiring her has already committed adultery with her in his heart;" not so
much correcting the fornicating and curious eyes, as the soul seated within,
which has used badly the eyes given by God unto good; for this reason also the
wise proverb does not say, With all guard keep the eyes, but, "With all guard
keep your heart;" having set the therapy of the guard especially on that,
which has used the eyes unto what it wishes. Therefore let this be the first
guard of our cleansing, in order that, when a memory of a woman come about in
our mind offered up through the diabolic activity, if it happen of a mother,
or of a sister, or of other reverent women, we may quickly cast it out from
our heart; in order that we may not, while we linger for a long time in that
very judgment, the deceiver of the evils from these persons roll down and
throw down the mind unto shameful and harmful inward thoughts, as also the
command given by God toward the first orders to keep the head of the snake,
that is the beginning of the harmful thought, through which it attempts to
creep into our soul; in order that we may not by the reception of the head,
which is the assault of the thought, receive the remaining body of the snake,
that is the consent of the pleasure, and from this hereafter it may bring down
the mind upon the unlawful deed; but it is fitting, according to what has been
written, in the mornings to kill all the sinners of the earth, that is by the
light of knowledge to discern, and to destroy the sinful thoughts from the
earth, which is the heart, according to the voice of the Lord; and while they
are still infants, the sons of Babylon, that is the wicked thoughts, to dash
these to the ground, and to crush them toward the rock, which is the Christ.
For if through our consent they be made men, they will not be moved without
great groaning and labor. And toward the things said from the divine Scripture
it is good also to mention a word of holy Fathers =28.880= indeed. It has been
said then by the holy Basil the bishop of Caesarea of Cappadocia: I both do
not know a woman, and I am not a virgin; so much he knew that the gift of
virginity is not achieved in the bodily abstinence from the woman alone, as
much as in the holiness and purity of the soul which is accustomed to be
achieved by the fear of God. And the Fathers also say this, that we are not
able to acquire the virtue of purity perfectly, unless first we acquire true
humility in the heart; nor to be deemed worthy of true knowledge for as long
as in the hidden places of the soul the passion of fornication is seated. And
in order that we may show this great achievement also from the Apostle, having
mentioned one saying of his, we will stop the discourse. "Peace, he says,
pursue with all, and the sanctification, without which no one will see the
Lord." For that he speaks concerning this, it is clear from what he added,
saying: "Let no one be a fornicator or profane as Esau." As heavenly then, and
angelic the achievement of holiness is, so much is it warred against by
heavier plots from the adversaries. And for this reason we ought to be eager
not only to practice continence of body, but also a crushing of heart, and
frequent prayers with groanings; so that the furnace of our

toward these things a very great weapon for us toward this enemy has been

been grieved or disturbed we despise saying, that these have not been grieved

the anger from the root from heart, not only if we happen to have been grieved

heal this one through the apology, and then to offer the gift. But why do we

again: "Ways of those who remember wrongs unto death." For here also he
hinders and punishes not only the anger according to activity, but also that
according to mind. Wherefore it is fitting, following the divine laws, to
contend with all power against the spirit of anger, and against the disease
stored within in us; and not, moving the wrath against the humans, to pursue a
desert and a solitude =28.893= as there forsooth, and there being no one who
moves us unto anger, the virtue of long-suffering will easily be achieved in
the solitude; for from the pride and from not wishing to blame ourselves and
to ascribe to our own sloth the causes of the anger, we long for the
