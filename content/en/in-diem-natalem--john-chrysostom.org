to read ancient laws, so that from every side the word may become clearer to
you. There was an ancient law for the Jews; or rather let us take the word
further back: When God delivered the people of the Hebrews from the Egyptian
tumults and from the tyranny of the barbarian, seeing them still having
remnants of impiety and being struck with awe at sensible things, and marvel
=49.355= ing at the sizes and beauties of temples, he commanded a temple to be
built for them, surpassing all the temples on earth not only by the costliness
of the material, nor by the variety of the craft, but also by the form of the
building. And just as an affectionate father, taking his own son who has
associated for a long time with filthy men and corrupters and prodigals and
has enjoyed much luxury, sets him with security and gravity in still greater
abundance, so that neither being cramped he may take memory of the former
things, nor come into desire of them; so also God, seeing the Jews struck with
awe at sensible things, makes the excess in these things also great, so that
they may never come into desire of the Egyptians or of the things among the
Egyptians. And he makes the temple after the image of the whole world, both
the sensible and the intelligible. For just as there is earth and heaven, and
in the middle this firmament as a partition; so he commanded that one also to
come to be. And cutting this temple into two, and letting down a veil in the
middle, he granted the part outside the veil to be accessible to all, but the
part within inaccessible and unseen to all, except the high priest alone. And
that this is not our conjecture, but that the temple was constructed in a type
of the whole world, hear what Paul says, speaking concerning Christ who went
up into heaven: For Christ did not enter into handmade holies, antitypes of
the true; showing that the things here were antitypes of the true. And that
the veil also separated the Holy of Holies from the holies outside, just as
this heaven walls off the things above it from all these things among us, hear
how he hinted this too, calling heaven a veil. For speaking concerning the
hope, that we have it as an anchor of the soul both sure and firm, he added:
And entering into the inner side of the veil, where Jesus entered as
forerunner for us, above the heaven above. Do you see how he called heaven a
veil? Outside the veil then was the lampstand, and the table, and a bronze
altar, receiving the sacrifices and the whole-burnt-offerings; but within the
veil the ark, covered round on all sides with gold, having the tablets of the
covenant, and the golden jar, and the rod of Aaron that budded, and a golden
altar, not of sacrifices nor of whole-burnt-offerings, but of incense only.
And the things outside were permitted to all to tread, but the things within
to the high priest alone. And of these very things I will furnish testimony
again, Paul speaking somehow thus: The first tabernacle then had ordinances of
service, and the worldly holy place. And he calls the outer tabernacle the
worldly holy place, since it was lawful for the whole world to enter; In which
were both the lampstand and the table and the setting forth of the loaves. And
after the second veil a tabernacle that is called the Holy of Holies, having a
golden censer, and the ark of the covenant covered round on all sides with
gold; in which =49.356= a golden jar having the manna, and the rod of Aaron
that budded, and the tablets of the covenant; and above it Cherubim of glory,
overshadowing the mercy-seat. And these things having been thus constructed,
into the first tabernacle the priests enter continually, accomplishing the
services; but into the second once in the year the high priest alone, not
without blood, which he offers for himself and for the ignorances of the
people. Do you see that the high priest both enters alone, and once in the
whole year?

you the reason for which I stirred up all these things. What then is the

conceive. If then we learn which that sixth month was, we shall also know when
Mary began to conceive; then having learned when she began to conceive, we
shall also know when she gave birth, having numbered nine months from the
conception. From where then shall we know which was the sixth month of
Elizabeth's pregnancy? If we learn which was the month in which she began to
conceive. And from where shall we know which was the month in which she
conceived? If we learn at what season Zechariah her husband was given the good
news. And this very thing, from where will it be known to us? From the divine
Scriptures; as the holy Gospel says, that the angel gave the good news to
Zechariah being within in the Holy of Holies, and spoke to him concerning the
birth of John. If then it be shown clearly from the Scriptures that the high
priest both entered once into the Holy of Holies, and alone, and when, and in
which month of the year he enters this once, the season at which he was given
the good news will be quite clear; and this having become clear, the beginning
of the conception also will be known to all. That he entered once in the year,
both Paul made clear, and Moses too makes this very thing clear, saying thus:
And the Lord spoke to Moses saying: Speak to Aaron your brother, and let him
not enter at every hour into the holy place within the veil before the face of
the mercy-seat, which is upon the ark of the testimony, and he shall not die.
And again: And no man shall be in the tent of the testimony, when he goes in
to make atonement in the holies, until he come out, and he shall make
atonement for himself, and for his house, and for all the congregation of the
sons of Israel; and he shall make atonement upon the altar that is before the
Lord. That he did not go in at all times into the Holy of Holies, nor was it
lawful for anyone to touch while he was within, but to stand outside by the
veil, is clear from these things. But hold these things then with accuracy.
For it remains to show which was the season at which he went in into the Holy
of =49.357= Holies, and that once in the year he alone did this. From where
then is this clear? From this very book. For he says somehow thus: In the
seventh month, on the tenth of the month, you shall humble your souls, and you
shall do no work in it, the native, and the sojourner who sojourns among you.
For on this day he shall make atonement for you, to cleanse you from all your
sins; before the Lord you shall be cleansed. A sabbath of sabbaths, this rest
shall be for you, and you shall humble your souls; it shall be an eternal
ordinance for you. And the priest shall make atonement, whom they shall
anoint, and whom they shall fill his hands to serve as priest after his
father; and he shall put on his holy robe, and he shall make atonement for the
holy of the holy, and the tent of the testimony, and he shall make atonement
for the altar, and he shall make atonement for the sins of the priests, and
for all the congregation he shall make atonement. And this shall be for you an
eternal ordinance, to make atonement for the sons of Israel from all their
sins. Once in the year it shall be done, as the Lord commanded Moses. Here he
discourses concerning the Tabernacles. For then once in the year the high
priest went in; which he himself also made clear, saying, that Once in the
year this shall be done.

These months then are Hyperberetaios, Dios, Apellaios, Audonaios, Peritios,

among them being laughed at remain upon their own gravity, being in nothing
harmed from that laughter into their own glory; and their things, being
wrapped up from every side, display their own unseemliness. For how is it not
of extreme madness, that they themselves, being prone to slip, introducing
their own gods into stones and woods and cheap images, and shutting them up as
in some prison, think it shameful neither to do nor to say anything; but
charge us, saying that God, having constructed for himself a living temple
from the Holy Spirit, benefited the world through it? And what charge is this?
For if it is shameful for God to dwell in a human body, much more in stone and
wood, and by so much as stone and wood are more dishonorable than man, unless
perhaps our race also seems to them cheaper than these senseless materials.
For they even dare to bring down the essence of God into cats and dogs, and
many of the heretics even into things still more dishonorable than these. But
we neither say any of these things, nor would we ever endure to hear them; but
we say this, that Christ took a pure and holy and blameless flesh, become
inaccessible to all sin, from a virginal womb, and corrected his own
fashioning. And those men, and the Manichaeans who are impious in the same
things with them, bringing the essence of God into dogs and apes and beasts of
every kind (for they say the soul in all these is from that essence), neither
shudder nor sink down, but they say we speak things unworthy of God, because
we do not endure even to take any of these into mind; but what was fitting and
proper to him, we say, that having come he corrected his own work by the
manner of this birth. What do you say, tell me, O man? Saying that the soul of
murderers and that of sorcerers is of the essence of God, you dare to charge
us, that we endure none of these things, nor bear to hear it said, but even
judge those who say it partakers of impiety; but we say this, that God having
constructed a holy temple for himself, through that introduced the citizenship
of the heavens into our life. And how would you not be worthy of ten thousand
deaths, both for the charges with which you charge us, and for the impieties
which being impious you do not cease? If =49.360= it is unseemly for God to
dwell in a pure and blameless body, as you say; much more unseemly is that of
the sorcerer, that of the grave-robber, that of the robber, that of the ape,
that of the dog, not that holy and blameless thing now sitting at the right


of the Father. For what harm could come to God from this economy, or what
stain? Do you not see this sun here, whose body is sensible and corruptible
and perishable, even if Greeks and Manichaeans choke ten thousand times
hearing these things? And not this only, but also earth, and sea, and simply
all the visible creation, has been subjected to vanity. And hear Paul making
this clear, in the words in which he says: For the creation was subjected to
vanity, not willingly, but because of him who subjected it, in hope. Then
making clear what it is to be subjected to vanity, he added, saying: That the
creation itself also will be freed from the bondage of corruption into the
freedom of the glory of the children of God. So that now it is perishable, and
it is corruptible; for to be enslaved to corruption is nothing other than to
be corruptible. If then the sun, being a corruptible body, sends forth its
rays everywhere, and associating with mire and pollutions and many other such
things, is in nothing harmed from the association of the bodies into its own
purity, but even draws back the rays again pure, imparting its own excellence
to many of the bodies that received it, but itself taking on not even a chance
thing of the stench and the pollution; much more the sun of righteousness, the
Master of the bodiless powers, having come into pure flesh, not only was not
defiled, but even worked this very flesh more pure and more holy. Thinking all
these things then, and remembering the voice that says: I will dwell in them,
and walk among them; and again, You are a temple of God, and the Spirit of God
dwells in you, let us also say toward those men, and let us stop up the
shameless mouths of the impious, and let us rejoice upon our good things, and
let us glorify the God who became flesh for so great a condescension, and
according to our power let us render to him both the honor and the recompense
that is worthy; but no recompense could come to God from us, except only our
salvation and that of our souls, and the care concerning virtue. ζʹ. Let us
not then become ungrateful toward the benefactor, but let us all bring in all
things according to our power, faith, hope, love, temperance, almsgiving,
hospitality. And what I recently entreated you, this both now and always I
will not cease entreating. And what is this? When you are about to approach
this fearful and divine table and holy mystagogy, do this with fear and
trembling, with a pure conscience, with fasting and prayer, not making a
tumult, nor kicking, nor pushing those near; for this is of extreme madness,
and of no ordinary contempt. Therefore he also brings upon those who =49.361=
do these things much chastisement and punishment. Consider, O man, of what
sacrifice you are about to take hold, to what table you approach; consider
also that being earth and ash, you partake of the blood and body of Christ.
And when a king calls you to a banquet, you recline with fear, and you partake
of the foods set before you with reverence and quiet; but when God calls you
to his own table, and sets forth his own Son, angelic powers standing by with
fear and trembling, and the Cherubim covering their faces, the Seraphim crying
with trembling, Holy, holy, holy Lord; do you cry out, tell me, and make a
tumult toward this spiritual feast? Do you not know that the soul must be full
of calm at that season? There is need of much peace and quiet, not of tumult
and anger and disturbance; for these things make the approaching soul unclean.
What pardon then could there be for us, if after so many sins we are not even
at the season of the approach clean of those irrational passions? And what at
all is more necessary than the things set before us? or what troubles us, that
we should be eager, leaving this, to


run there? Do not, I beg and entreat, do not move the anger of God against
ourselves. What is set before us is a saving medicine of our wounds, it is
unfailing wealth, a procurer of the kingdom of heavens. Let us shudder then as
we approach, let us give thanks, let us fall down, confessing our faults, let
us weep mourning our own evils, let us render intense prayers to God; and thus
having cleaned ourselves quietly, and with the fitting good order, as
approaching the king of the heavens, so let us approach; and having received
the blameless and holy sacrifice let us kis =49.362= s it, let us embrace it
with the eyes, let us warm our own mind, so that we may not come together unto
judgment or unto condemnation, but unto temperance of soul, unto love, unto
virtue, and reconciliation toward God, unto firm peace, and a ground of ten
thousand good things, so that we may both sanctify ourselves, and build up
those near. These things I say continually, and saying I will not cease. For
what is the profit, to run here simply and in vain, learning nothing of the
useful things? and what gain, always to converse the things toward favor?
Short is the present season, beloved; let us be sober, let us watch, let us
compose ourselves, let us display all genuine zeal concerning all, let us
become reverent concerning all things; whether it be necessary to hear the
divine oracles, or to pray, or to approach, or to do some other thing, let
this be done with fear and trembling, lest we draw a curse upon ourselves
through sloth. For cursed, he says, is everyone who does the work of the Lord
carelessly. The tumult and the anger become an outrage toward the sacrifice
set before us. It is extreme contempt to offer oneself polluted to God. Hear
what the Apostle says concerning such men: If anyone ruins the temple of God,
God will ruin this man. Let us not then instead of reconciliation provoke God,
but displaying all care and all adornment, and undisturbedness of soul, let us
approach with prayer and a contrite heart, so that having propitiated by this
very thing our Master Jesus Christ, we may be able to obtain the good things
promised to us, by the grace and philanthropy of our Lord Jesus Christ
himself, with whom to the Father, together with the Holy Spirit, be glory,
power, honor, now and always, and unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
