A Sermon on the Turtledove, That is, on the Church.
=55.599=
αʹ. The turtledove that loves the wilderness, when she has settled in the
woods and calls her yoke-fellow, shaking the valleys with mournful coaxings,
and charming her own male as he feeds beside the waters, and setting him to
flight, urges him to make a swift passage; and with one mind they fix their
nest upon the solid trunks, and they brood without fear, and from there they
send their young to fly. Some say that the turtledove is devoted to her
husband and to chastity; so that even if it should happen that her male is
consumed by an eagle, or hunted by a fowler, she does not mix with another
husband, but longs for him, waits for him, and dies with the memory of him.
Hear, women, what kind of chastity is found among birds. As many of you, then,
as bear the form of the turtledove in your countenance, imitate her chastity.
Such is the all-holy Church. For when her husband Christ was crucified and
went up into the heavens, she does not mix with another husband; but she longs
for him, waits for him, and dies with the memory of him. But lest we should
seem, beloved, to be outside the Scriptures when we have mentioned a
turtledove, come, let us mention the Scriptures themselves. Blessed David
bears witness, and sings saying: For the sparrow has found a house for itself,
and the turtledove a nest for herself, where she will lay her young. And what
sparrow does he mean? Surely man: For our soul, as a sparrow, was delivered
from the snare of those who hunt. And the Lord, wishing to show his own
disciples how much they differ from others in holiness, says to them: Fear
not; you are of more value than many sparrows. For this reason Solomon also
cried out saying: That you may be saved, as a gazelle from snares, and as a
bird from a trap. And again the divine voice: The kingdom of the heavens is
like a grain of mustard, which a man took and sowed in his own field; which is
smaller than all the seeds; but when it has grown, it becomes greater than all
the herbs, so that all the birds of heaven come and nest in its branches. For
here too he likened the countless races of men to birds. For the sparrow has
found a house for itself, and the turtledove a nest for herself, where she
will lay her young. He likened the all-holy Church to a turtledove because of
the varied gifts of wisdom. And that the Church has been embroidered with
variety, one may hear David saying: All the glory of the king's daughter is
within, clothed in golden fringes, embroidered. For the sparrow has found a
house for itself, and the turtledove a nest for herself, where she will lay
her young; that is, under the solid branches of the cross, rearing the
newly-illumined children.
Jeremiah also mentions this turtledove, saying: The turtledove and the swallow
of the field, sparrows and cicadas have known the time of their coming in,
calling the turtledove the all-holy Church, the swallow John the lover of
mankind, the cicada the eloquent Paul, the flute of the Church. Concerning
this turtledove the Lord commands Abraham: Take for me a heifer of three
years, and a ram of three years, and a she-goat of three years, and a dove,
and a turtledove. Scripture mentions this turtledove also in the Song of
Songs, saying: The voice of the turtledove has been heard in our land. The
voice of this turtledove signifies to us that the heavenly spring, Christ,
comes from heaven. The voice of the turtledove has been heard in our land
today. But let us hear the voice of this turtledove, what melodies she sings.
I am black and beautiful, daughters of Jerusalem. Do not look upon me, because
I am darkened, because the sun has looked askance upon me. I am black and
beautiful: black, because of the former transgression, beautiful, because of
the swift repentance; black, because of the mourning in the fall, beautiful,
because of the brightness in baptism. I am black =55.600= and beautiful,
daughters of Jerusalem. He calls daughters of Jerusalem the holy souls of the
fathers. I am black and beautiful, the Church from the nations. Do not look,
you holy souls, do not look upon me, because I am darkened, because the sun of
righteousness looked askance upon me, because of my former error. What sun
looked askance upon her? Not at all the one of whom he says in Ecclesiastes,
The sun rises, and the sun sets, and draws toward its place. For the sun of
righteousness rises for us, Christ from Mary, according to the flesh;
Dayspring is his name; he set again, when upon the cross he also went down
into the underworld. The sun rises, and the sun sets, and draws toward its
place.
When he went up into the heavens, then he drew us. For when I am lifted up,
then I will draw all to myself. Concerning this sun of righteousness Isaiah
also speaks, saying: In those days the sun of righteousness shall rise, and
healing in his wings. For when the sun of righteousness, Christ, had come, the
woman with the issue of blood, having touched the hem of his garment, dried up
the fountains of her blood. I am black and beautiful, daughters of Jerusalem.
If there is any soul standing here darkened by sin, let her not fear; only let
her repent, and say: I am black and beautiful. Even if she is at her last
breath, even if it is toward evening hour, let her not fear; let her repent
with weeping. For in the evening weeping shall lodge, and in the morning
gladness. Imitate Hezekiah the king, who, having fallen into illness, lay upon
a bed; and having heard Isaiah saying to him: Set your house in order; for you
are about to die, and you shall not live; turning toward the wall, he bedewed
the bed with tears of repentance, he held back the sun, and at once received a
fifteen-year span of life.
βʹ. If there is any catechumen here, let him repent, let him come to God, let
him be baptized with the water, let him be enlightened in his sight. If there
is any faithful man here who has sinned, let him wash the body with the water
of tears, and let him make the soul bright with the oil of well-doing. If you
adorn yourself thus, if you beautify it with right deeds, the divine Word
loves you, he touches your understanding, he draws you out toward longing for
God; you too say, Woe is me, I am wounded with love; for as with an arrow the
souls of the saints are wounded by love. Do you wish to hear the arrow itself
speaking? Read Isaiah, and in him you will find how the arrow speaks: And he
has set me as a chosen arrow, God the Word says, and he has hidden me in his
quiver. Jeremiah, wounded by this arrow, said to him: But I have not grown
weary following after you, and I have not desired the day of man. David,
wounded in his understanding by this arrow, said to him: My soul has cleaved
after you, and your right hand has upheld me. Peter, wounded by this arrow,
said to Christ: Lord, you know that I love you. Paul, wounded by this arrow
and bound together with Christ, said: Who shall separate us from the love of
Christ? The all-holy Church, wounded by this arrow, said: Woe is me, I am
wounded with love. O groan gushing from the midst of the heart with
Christ-loving streams; O groan, drawing the heavenly bridegroom Christ to
herself.
For this reason she herself says: Let him kiss me with the kisses of his
mouth. I have been betrothed to the heavenly bridegroom through the law and
the prophets. I, the Church from the nations, being betrothed, no longer wish
through Moses, or through Isaiah, or through Jeremiah and the rest of the
prophets, but let him himself come; I wish to speak with him mouth to mouth;
let him himself come. Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth. I hear
Jeremiah saying concerning him: =55.601= His heart is deep beyond all, and he
is a man, and who shall know him? Him I long for, him I beseech. Let him kiss
me with the kisses of his mouth. I hear Amos saying: Behold a man standing
upon a wall of adamant, and in his hand an adamant. Him I seek, let him come,
let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth. And just as a woman who loves
her husband, when the husband is absent, leaning out through windows, looks
about for him, searches for him, both by land and by sea; and if she sees a
ship coming, there she imagines her own husband; and if she sees travelers
coming from afar, there she reckons her own husband to be; for often having
met them she asks about her own husband: Tell me, where did you leave him, in
what city or village, saying what, or doing what, or when coming?
γʹ. In the same way the all-holy Church also, held by a holy kiss and by love,
asks all the prophets, saying: Have you seen him? Is he coming? Is he not
coming? Is he in heaven? Does he pasture the ninety-nine sheep that have not
gone astray in heaven, and not come to the sheep that has gone astray? Tell
me, where does he pasture, where does he lie down, him whom my soul has loved?
And the prophets, being asked, speak through one, Isaiah, and say: We have
seen him; but if it is that one you await, we have seen him, and he had
neither his form nor his beauty, but his form was without honor and failing
beside the sons of men. The all-holy bride, the Church, answers: That is he,
him I long for, him I seek, I beseech, Let him kiss me with the kisses of his
mouth. Isaiah answers her: If it is he, he is coming, and he is coming with
much strength. The Lord, the Lord is coming, and his arm with dominion. Behold
his reward, and his work before him; and in his hand he shall gather lambs,
and he shall comfort those with young.
She hears that Isaiah recounts the things concerning him accurately and calls
him a shepherd: As a shepherd he shall shepherd his flock, and in his hand he
shall gather lambs, and he shall comfort those with young; and she says to
him: Where does he pasture, where does he lie down, him whom my soul has
loved? Isaiah sends her to John, and John says to her: A voice of one crying
in the wilderness: Prepare the way of the Lord, make his paths straight. She
goes away into the wilderness to John, takes hold of him, asks: Tell me, are
you the Christ? John answers: I am not the Christ. And she says again to him:
If you are not the Christ, why do you baptize? He answered again: I am not the
Christ; He who comes after me is stronger than I; whose sandal strap I am not
worthy to loose. While he still delayed, she said: Woe is me, I am wounded
with love. Again John to her: Do not groan, cease, only wait a little. John
sees the Lord approaching, and says to her: Cease, groan no longer. From afar
he points, stretching out his finger, and says: See, him whom you seek;
Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world. Having heard
from John a lamb, and from Isaiah a shepherd, she is in doubt, saying in
herself: Whom then shall I believe? Shall I believe this one, or Isaiah? The
Master sees that she was in doubt; he cries out and says: I am the good
shepherd; he dispels her doubt by the saying.
She approaches the bridegroom, embraces him, and kisses him. And what is
written in David is fulfilled: Mercy and tru =55.602= th have met,
righteousness and peace have kissed. And that we may learn more completely
that a bride was joined to the bridegroom, and a spiritual marriage took
place, and a flute of the bridal chamber; John sings of him, and says: He who
has the bride is the bridegroom; but the friend of the bridegroom, standing
and hearing his voice, rejoices with joy because of the voice of the
bridegroom. He must increase, and I must decrease. There was need, as at a
marriage, that gifts also be written for the bride; there was need to seek
paper, and a scribe, and a pen. The paper is found from Isaiah. The Holy
Spirit commands the scribe, and says: Take for me a new, great roll, and write
in it with the pen of a man. The pen was sought, it was found with David. For
he says concerning it: My tongue is the pen of a swift-writing scribe, fair in
beauty beyond the sons of men. David arrays this Church as a bride; he himself
adorns her, he himself admonishes her, and teaches her as a bridesman, or, as
it is customary for men to say, as groomsman. He charges the bride: See,
child; Hear, daughter, and see, and incline your ear, and forget your people;
and the king shall desire your beauty. Do not think that you are equal to him.
Hear, daughter, and see, and incline your ear, and forget your people; and the
king shall desire your beauty; because he is your Lord; and the daughters of
Tyre shall worship him with gifts. If you hear me, daughter, The rich of the
people of the earth shall entreat your face.
It remained then that there be written also the things given by each of them,
menservants and maidservants; from Christ, menservants; from the bride,
maidservants; Christ gives the menservants; Paul and Silvanus and Timothy
come, servants of Jesus Christ; they also demand the maidservants from the
bride. David dictates: Virgins shall be brought to the king after her, her
companions shall be brought to you. The marriage takes place; the Lord comes
mystically in Cana of Galilee. And Cana is interpreted creation of God; and
the creation of God is the whole world. Here he accomplishes the spiritual
nuptials, he comes in Cana of Galilee, and there the whole choir of the saints
arrives; they offer gifts to the bridegroom, each according to his own power,
as at a marriage: Abraham the calf, his wife Sarah the cakes baked in ashes,
Isaac the wood, Jacob the kids, Joseph supplies grain, Moses bears the torch,
Isaiah sings, and David dances. And since holy women also had to be present at
such a marriage, and those too to sing, Esther comes, Judith is arrayed, is
adorned; and Miriam the sister of Moses takes the tambourine, and says: Let us
sing to the Lord; for he has been gloriously glorified. And since, as at such
a marriage, praises also had to be written, Paul, praising the marriage, says:
Marriage is honorable in all, and the bed undefiled.
And that he speaks of Christ and the Church, let him interpret himself: For
what, he says, is written? For this cause a man shall leave his father and
mother, and shall be joined to his wife, and the two shall be one flesh. This
mystery is great; but I speak concerning Christ and the Church. What he
therefore interprets, let us not doubt, in Christ Jesus our Lord; to whom be
the glory and the power, with the unoriginate Father, and his all-holy and
life-giving Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
