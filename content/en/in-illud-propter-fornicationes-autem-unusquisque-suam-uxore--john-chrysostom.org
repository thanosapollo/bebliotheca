On the apostolic saying: But because of fornications let each man have his own
wife.

=51.207=

αʹ. I wish to lead you today also to the springs of honey, honey that never
has satiety. For such is the nature of Paul's words, and all who fill their
own hearts from these springs speak through the Holy Spirit; or rather, the
pleasure of the divine oracles hides all the excellence of honey. And making
this clear the Prophet said, How sweet are your oracles to my throat, above
honey to my mouth. And the pleasure of the divine oracles is not only sweeter
than honey, but also more precious than gold and every stone, and purer than
all silver.

For the oracles of the Lord, he says, are pure oracles, silver tried in the
fire, proved for the earth, purified seven times. Therefore also a certain
wise man said: To eat much honey =51.208=.20 is not good, but one must honor
glorious words. For from that even a disease that was not is often born, but
from these we can put off even the sickness that is present; and honey is
destroyed in digestion, but the divine oracles, when they have been digested,
then become both sweeter and more useful, both to those who have them and to
many others. And one who enjoys a sensible table with abundance, then belching
from it, becomes unpleasant to the one who shares it; but one who has belched
from spiritual teaching imparts much fragrance to his neighbor. David then,
continually enjoying such a feast, said, My heart has belched forth a good
word. For it is possible also to belch forth an evil word. And just as at a
sensible table, according to the nature of the foods, the quality of the belch
is also given forth: so also with the power of words, such things as they feed
on, such things =51.209= many men also belch forth.

For example, if you go up into a theatre and hear fornicating songs, such
words you will certainly also belch forth toward your neighbor; but if you
come into the church and take part in spiritual hearings, you will have such
belches also. Therefore the Prophet also said, My heart has belched forth a
good word, showing us the food of the table of which he always partook. Paul
also, being persuaded by this, exhorted saying: Let no rotten word go forth
from your mouth, but if any is good. And what is the rotten, he says? If you
learn the good, then you will know also the rotten; for he has set this one
over against that one. What then the good is, you will need to learn nothing
from me; for he himself has interpreted its nature for us. For having said, If
any is good, he added, For the building up of the Church, showing that this is
good which builds up the neighbor. As therefore he who builds is good, so he
who tears down is rotten and worthless. You too then, beloved, if you have
something of this kind to say, which can make the hearer better, do not hinder
a word in the season of salvation; but if you have nothing of this kind, but
wicked and corrupted words, be silent, do not accuse your neighbor.

For this word is rotten, not building up the hearer, but even overthrowing
him. For if he is attending to virtue, he is often lifted up to arrogance; and
if he has been neglected, he becomes more careless. If you are about to utter
a shameful word and one full of laughter, be silent. For this word too is
rotten, making both the speaker and the hearer more wanton, and kindling the
wicked desires in each.


Just as then wood becomes food and matter for fire, so words for wicked
counsels. Therefore one must not utter everything that we have in mind; but
strive both to banish from the mind itself the wicked desires and every
shameful thought. But if ever, without noticing, we admit filthy reasonings,
let us never bring them out through the tongue, but let us smother them
through silence. For both wild beasts and reptiles, when they fall into a pit,
if they find some way out above, come up and become fiercer; but if they
remain below continually shut in, they perish easily from every side and
disappear; so also wicked thoughts, if they find some exit through our mouth
and words, kindle the flame within; but if you shut them out through silence,
they become weaker, and as if wasting away by famine through the silence, they
quickly die in the mind. So that even if you desire some shameful desire, but
do not utter a shameful word, you have quenched the desire also.

You do not have a pure mind? At least have a pure mouth, and do not empty the
mire outside, lest you harm both another and yourself. For not only to those
who speak, but also to those who hear, when others utter shameful things, much
stain is added. Therefore I exhort and =51.210= advise, not only to abstain
from saying such things, but also from hearing them when others say them, and
to be fastened continually to the divine law. For such a man the Prophet also
blesses, saying: Blessed is the man who has not walked in the counsel of the
ungodly, and has not stood in the way of sinners, and has not sat upon the
seat of pestilent men; but his will is in the law of the Lord, and in his law
he will meditate day and night.

βʹ. In the gatherings outside, then, even if something useful should ever be
said, still among many worthless things the many scarcely utter one sound
thing; but in the divine Scriptures it is altogether the opposite: you will
never hear any wicked word, but all full of salvation and much philosophy;
such as also those read to us today. And what are these? Now concerning the
things of which you wrote to me, he says, it is good for a man not to touch a
woman; but because of fornications let each man have his own wife, and each
woman her own husband. Paul legislates concerning marriage, and is not
ashamed, nor does he blush; and very reasonably. For if his Master honored
marriage, and was not ashamed, but even adorned the matter both by his
presence and by the gift (for he also brought gifts to the marriage greater
than all, changing the nature of water into wine), how would the servant have
blushed when legislating concerning marriage? For marriage is not a wicked
thing, but wicked is adultery, wicked is fornication; and marriage is a
medicine that destroys fornication.

Let us not then dishonor it with the devilish processions; but, what those in
Cana of Galilee did, let those also now taking wives do: let them have Christ
in the midst. And how is this possible, he says? Through the priests
themselves; For he who receives you, he says, receives me. If then you drive
out the devil, if you drive out the fornicating songs, and the broken
melodies, and the disorderly dances, and the shameful words, and the devilish
procession, and the tumult, and the poured-out laughter and the rest of the
indecency, and bring in the holy servants of Christ, Christ too will certainly
be present through them with his mother and his brothers; For whoever does, he
says, the will of my Father, this one is my brother and sister and mother.

And I know that I seem to some to be heavy and burdensome, advising these
things and cutting out an ancient custom. But this is no concern of mine; for
I do not need the favor from you, but your benefit; not the applause and the


praises, but the gain and the philosophy. Let no one tell me that it is
custom; where sin is dared, do not mention custom; but if the things done are
wicked, even if it is an ancient custom, destroy it; and if they are not
wicked, even if there is no habit, introduce it and plant it. And that it was
not an ancient custom to behave so indecently, but that the things done are a
certain innovation, remember how Isaac married Rebecca, how Jacob married
Rachel. For Scripture also mentions their marriages, and tells how these
brides were brought into the houses of the bridegrooms, and mentions nothing
of this kind; but they made a banquet and dinner more cheerful than the
customary, and called those who belonged =51.211= to the marriages; but
flutes, pipes, and cymbals, and the wine-soaked leapings, and all the rest of
the present indecency were out of the way. But those in our time even sing
hymns to Aphrodite while dancing, and they sing many adulteries, and
corruptions of marriages, and unlawful loves, and lawless unions, and many
other songs full of impiety and shame on that day, and after drunkenness and
so much indecency they lead the bride in procession publicly with shameful
words.

How then do you demand chastity of her, tell me, training her from the first
day into so much shamelessness, and arranging that before her eyes these
things both happen and are said, which it is not even lawful for serious
slaves to hear? So long a time the father labored with the mother, guarding
the virgin, so that she might neither speak, nor hear another speaking, any of
such words, and chambers, and women's quarters, and guards, and doors, and
bars, and the evening outings, and not appearing even to any of those
belonging to the household, and many other things more than these they were
busy about, and you, having come, poured all those things out in one day,
preparing her to become shameless through that dishonorable procession, and
introducing corrupted words into the soul of the bride? Are not the later
evils from this? are not adulteries and jealousies from this? are not
childlessness and widowhoods and untimely orphanhoods from this? For when you
call the demons through the songs, when you fulfill their desire through the
shameful words, when you bring mimes and the effeminate into the house and the
whole theatre, when you fill the house with harlots, and prepare the whole
chorus of the demons to revel there, what sound thing do you expect
thereafter, tell me? And for what reason do you even bring in priests, when
you are about to celebrate such things the next day? Do you wish to display an
ambition that has gain? Call choruses of the poor. But you are altogether
ashamed, and you blush? And what is worse than this irrationality, when
dragging the devil into the house you think you do nothing shameful, but when
about to bring in Christ you blush? For just as when the poor enter Christ is
present, so when the effeminate and mimes dance there the devil revels in the
midst. And from that expense there is no gain, but even much harm would come;
but from these outlays you will quickly receive a great reward.

But no one in the city has done this? But you strive to make a beginning and
become founder of this good custom, that those after these things also may
refer it to you.

And if someone is zealous, and if he imitates this custom, the grandchildren
and those from grandchildren will have it to say to those who inquire, that
So-and-so first introduced this good law. For if in the contests outside, at
the banquets, those who carry these useless liturgies to a more ambitious
pitch are sung by many; much more in the spiritual liturgy all will praise,
and will give thanks to the first who introduced this wondrous beginning, and
the same thing will be both ambition and gain. For even when this is
accomplished by others, it will bring to you, who first planted it, the
recompense of those fruits; this will also make you


quickly a father, this will also protect those who are born, and will prepare
the bridegroom to grow old together with the bride =51.212=. For just as God
continually threatens those who sin, saying that Your sons shall be orphans,
and your wives widows; so also to those who in all things obey him he promises
both a sleek old age and to give all the good things with these.

γʹ. And it is possible to hear Paul saying this, that a multitude of sins has
often caused untimely deaths. For this reason, he says, many among you are
weak and sick, and a number sleep. And that the poor when fed allow nothing of
this kind to fall out, but, even if some unexpected thing happens, they bring
the swiftest correction of it, learn from the girl in Joppa. For this one
once, lying dead, the poor who were fed, standing around and weeping, raised
up, and brought back to life. So much more useful is the prayer of widows and
of the poor than all laughter and dancing. Here the delight is for one day,
there the gain is continual. Consider how great it is for the bride, having
received so many blessings upon her head, to enter into the house of the
bridegroom.

How much more august are these than crowns? how much more useful than wealth?
since the things now done are of extreme madness and frenzy. For even if no
punishment, nor any vengeance, were laid upon those who behave so indecently,
consider of how much punishment it is, to endure being washed with so many
insults, publicly, with all hearing, by men drunk and corrupted in mind. For
the poor when they receive bless, and join in praying for countless good
things; but those after the drunkenness, after the gluttony, pour every mire
of mockeries upon the head of those marrying, having a certain devilish
rivalry toward one another; and as if those coming together were enemies, so
those belonging to them compete with one another, in saying speakable and
unspeakable reproaches about those who have married, to imitate those set
against them; and this rivalry of theirs toward one another prepares the
bridegroom with the bride to be put to shame with all excess. Shall we then
seek another proof, tell me, that demons are moving their souls that these
things both happen and are said by them? Who then will still dispute, that
with demons moving their souls all these things both are said and happen by
them? There is no one; for such are the devil's recompenses, insults, and
drunkennesses, and frenzy of soul. And if someone should take it as an omen
that the poor are brought in instead of these, and should say that these are
symbols of misfortune, let him learn this also, that it is not the feeding of
the poor and widows, but the effeminate and harlots, that is a symbol of every
unpleasantness and of countless evils. For often from that very day the
harlot, having taken the bridegroom captive from among his friends, has gone
off, and has quenched the love toward the bride, and has undermined the
goodwill, and has destroyed the affection before it was kindled, and has sown
seeds of adultery.

These things the fathers ought to have feared, even if nothing else, and to
hinder the presence of the mimes and of the dancers at the marriages. For
marriage has been introduced not that we may be wanton =51.213=, nor that we
may fornicate, but that we may be chaste. Hear then Paul saying: But because
of fornications let each man have his own wife, and let each woman have her
own husband. For these are two things, for which marriage has been introduced,
both that we may be chaste, and that we may become fathers; and of these two
the cause of chastity is the leading one. For since desire entered, marriage
also entered, cutting off the excess, and persuading one to use one woman. For
childbearing is not produced by marriage in every case, but by that word of
God which says, Increase, and multiply, and fill the earth; and they bear
witness, as many as have used marriage, but have not become fathers. So that


this is the leading cause, that of chastity, and especially now, when the
whole inhabited world has been filled with our race.

For at the beginning the matter of children was desired, because of leaving a
memorial and remnants of each one's own life. For since there were not yet
hopes of resurrection, but death held sway, and those who died thought they
were destroyed after the life here, God gave the consolation from children, so
that living images of those who have departed might remain, and our race be
preserved, and that for those about to die, and for those belonging to them,
their offspring might be a very great comfort. And that you may learn that for
this reason especially children were desired, hear what the wife, after the
many blows, laments to Job. Behold, she says, your memorial has perished from
the earth, your sons and your daughters. And again Saul to David: Swear to me,
that you may not make my seed disappear, and my name after me. But since now
the resurrection is at the doors, and there is no account of death, but we are
traveling toward another life better than the one that is, the zeal about
these things is superfluous. For if you desire children, you will be able to
acquire far better and more useful ones now, when certain spiritual
birth-pangs have been introduced, and a better birth, and more useful tenders
of old age. So that there is one cause of marriage, not to fornicate, and for
this reason this medicine has been introduced.

But if you are going to use fornications even after marriage, you have come to
marriage superfluously, and in vain and to no purpose; or rather not only in
vain and to no purpose, but even to harm. For it is not the same to fornicate
when not having a wife, and after marriage to do this same thing again. For
such a thing is no longer fornication, but adultery. For even if what has been
said is paradoxical, yet it is true.

δʹ. For we are not ignorant that many think adultery is only when someone
corrupts a woman who has a husband; but I, even if it is a public harlot, even
if a maidservant, even if he approaches badly and wantonly any other woman who
does not have a husband, while having a wife, I say that such a thing is
adultery. For the charge of adultery is constituted not only from those who
are outraged, but also from those who outrage. Do not now tell me the laws
outside, which drag wives who commit adultery into court =51.214= and demand
accounts, but do not demand accounts of men who have wives and who corrupt
themselves with maidservants; but I will read you the law of God, which is
equally indignant both in the case of the woman and in the case of the man,
and which says that the matter is adultery. For having said, And let each
woman have her own husband, he added: Let the husband render to the wife the
goodwill that is due. What in the world did he wish to make clear when he said
this? was it that he should keep for her the revenues of the money? that he
should keep the dowry intact? that he should furnish costly garments? that he
should furnish a more abundant table, that he should furnish splendid outings?
that he should furnish much attendance of household slaves? What do you say?
what kind of goodwill do you seek? for all these things too are of goodwill. I
say nothing of this kind, he says, but chastity and dignity. The body of the
husband is no longer the husband's, but the wife's. Let him therefore keep the
possession intact for her, and not diminish it, nor corrupt it; for that
household slave is said to be well-disposed, who, having received the master's
money, destroys nothing from it. Since then the husband's body is the wife's
possession, let the husband be well-disposed concerning the deposit. For that
he says this, having said, Let him render the goodwill, he added: The wife
does not have authority over her own body, but the husband; likewise also the
husband does not have authority over his own body, but the wife. When then you
see a harlot baiting, plotting, loving the body, say to her: The body is not
mine, it is my wife's; I do not dare to misuse it, nor to give it over to
another woman.


Let the wife also do this. For here there is much equality of honor; and yet
in the other things Paul gives much superiority, saying thus: Nevertheless you
also, each one, that each may love his own wife as himself; and the wife, that
she fear the husband; and, The husband is the head of the wife, and, The wife
ought to be subject to the husband. And again in the Old, Your turning shall
be toward your husband, and he shall rule over you. How then has he here
introduced an equal exchange of slavery and mastery? For to say, The wife does
not have authority over her own body, but the husband; likewise also the
husband does not have authority over his own body, but the wife, is the part
of one introducing a certain great equality. And just as that one is master of
her body, so also she is mistress of his body. For what reason then did he
introduce so much equality of honor? Because here the superiority is
necessary; but where it is a time of chastity and dignity, the husband has
nothing more than the wife, but is punished similarly to her, if he has
corrupted the laws of marriage; and very reasonably. For the wife did not come
to you for this, and leave father and mother and the whole house, that she
might be outraged, that you might bring in a cheap little maidservant upon
her, that you might make countless wars; you took a partner, and a companion
of life, and a free woman, and one equal in honor.

For how is it not absurd, when receiving her dowry, to display all goodwill,
and to diminish nothing of it; but what is more precious than the whole dowry,
the chastity and the dignity =51.215= and his own body, which is her
possession, to corrupt and defile? If you diminish the dowry, you give account
to the father-in-law; if you diminish the chastity, you will undergo the
accounts before God, who introduced the marriage and entrusted the wife. And
that this is true, hear what Paul says concerning those who commit adultery:
For he who rejects, rejects not a man, but God who has given his Holy Spirit
into you. Do you see through how many things the word has shown that it is
adultery, not only to corrupt a woman who has a husband, but also any harlot
whatsoever, when one has a wife? For just as we say a woman commits adultery,
even if she sins with a household slave, even if with anyone whatsoever, while
having a husband; so we would also say a man commits adultery, even if he is
wanton with a maidservant, even if with any common woman whatsoever, while
himself having a wife. Let us not then neglect our own salvation, nor stretch
forth our soul to the devil through this sin. For from this come the countless
overturnings of houses, the countless wars; from this the things of love drain
away, and the things of goodwill are undermined. For just as it is impossible
for a chaste man ever to overlook a wife and despise her; so it is impossible
for a wanton and unbridled man to love his own wife, even if she be more
beautiful than all. For from chastity love is born, and from love the
countless good things. Think then the other women to be of stone, knowing that
after marriage, even if you look with unbridled eyes at another woman, even if
a public one, even if one who has a husband, you have become liable to the
charges of adulterers. Chant these words to yourself each day; and if you see
desire of another woman rising in you, then from this your own wife appearing
unpleasant, go into the chamber, and having opened this book, and having taken
Paul as mediator, and continually chanting these words, quench the flame. And
thus the wife again will be more desired by you, no desire undermining the
goodwill toward her; and not only will the wife be more desired, but you also
will seem much more dignified and more free. For there is not, there is not
anything more shameful than a man who fornicates after marriage. For he
blushes not only before the guardian and the friends and those who meet him,
but even before the household slaves themselves. And this is not the only
terrible thing, but that he will also see the house itself more


unpleasant than any prison, looking toward the beloved, and continually
imagining the harlot.

εʹ. Do you wish to learn accurately how great the terrible thing is? Consider
what kind of life those who suspect their own wives live, how unpleasant the
foods, unpleasant the drinks. The table seems to be full of deadly drugs; and
as destruction, full of countless evils, so they flee the house. There is no
sleep for them, no night is gentle, no company of friends, not even the rays
of the sun themselves; but they think they are troubled even by the light
itself, not only when they see the wife being adulterated, but even if they
simply suspect. Think that the wife also suffers these things, when she hears
from anyone, or even =51.216= suspects, that you have given yourself over to a
harlot woman. Reckoning these things, do not flee only the adulteries, but
also the suspicions; and even if she suspects unjustly, tend her and persuade
her. For she does this not from enmity or arrogance, but from care, and from
fearing greatly concerning her own possession. For your body is her
possession, as I said before, and a possession more precious than all that
exist.

Do not then wrong her in the greatest things, nor give the mortal blow. For
even if you despise her, yet fear God, the avenger of such things, who has
threatened unbearable punishments for such sins. For of those who dare such
things, he says, Their worm shall not die, and the fire shall not be quenched.
And if the things to come do not sting you greatly, let at least the present
things frighten you. Many at any rate of those who attend to harlots have even
here perished badly as bad men, having undergone meddling from the women with
whom they fornicate. For those women, rivaling to make him stand off from the
wife who dwells with him and has been pledged, and to bind him completely to
their own love, have set in motion enchantments, and have prepared
love-charms, and have stitched together many sorceries; then thus having
thrown him into a grievous sickness, and having given him over to corruption,
and to a long wasting, and having surrounded him with countless evils, they
have carried him off from the present life. If you do not fear Gehenna, O man,
fear their sorceries. For when through this wantonness you make yourself
desolate of God's alliance, and strip yourself of the help from above, the
harlot, having taken you with license, and having called her demons, and
having stitched the leaves, and having worked the plots, with much ease gets
the better of your salvation, having made you a reproach and a laughing-stock
of all who dwell in the city, so that you are not even pitied when you suffer
evils. For who will pity, he says, a charmer bitten by a serpent, and all who
go near to beasts? I pass over the loss of money, the daily suspicions, the
conceit, the arrogance, the insolence that comes from the harlots upon the
foolish, things which are more bitter than countless deaths. And the wife,
often not even having spoken a heavy word, you did not bear, but the harlot
even when she strikes you, you worship. And are you not ashamed, nor do you
blush, nor do you pray that the earth split apart for you? How will you be
able to enter into church, and stretch out your hands to heaven? with what
mouth will you call upon God, with which you kissed the harlot? And do you not
fear, nor shudder, tell me, lest some thunderbolt be brought from above and
burn up your shameless head? For even if you escape the notice of the wife who
has been wronged, yet you will never escape the sleepless eye; since also to
that adulterer who said, Darkness is around me and walls, what do I revere?
that wise man answered, saying thus: That the eyes of the Lord are more
luminous than the sun ten thousand times, looking upon the works of men.
Therefore Paul said all things: Let each man have his own wife, and let each
woman have her own husband; let the husband render to the wife the goodwill
that is due, likewise also the wife to the husband. Honey drips from the lips
of a harlot woman, which for a


time anoints your throat, but later you will find it more bitter than gall,
=51.217= and sharper than a two-edged sword. The kiss of the harlot has venom,
venom that lies hidden and is concealed.

Why then do you pursue a pleasure that has condemnation, that begets
destruction, that brings an incurable blow, when it is possible to rejoice and
suffer nothing terrible? For in the case of the free wife there is together
both pleasure and safety and ease and honor and adornment and a good
conscience; but there much is the bitterness, much the harm, and continual the
accusation. For even if no man sees, the conscience will never cease accusing
you; but wherever you go, this accuser will follow accusing and crying out
greatly. So that if anyone pursues pleasure, let this one especially flee the
company with harlots. For nothing is more bitter than that custom, nothing
more unpleasant than the intercourse, nothing more defiled than the manners.
Let a deer of friendship, and a colt of your graces, keep company with you;
let the fountain of your water be a fountain for you. Having a pure fountain
of water, why do you run to a pit full of mire, smelling of Gehenna, and of
unspeakable punishment? what defense will you have? what pardon? For if those
who before marriage attend to fornication are punished and give account, just
as that one who was clothed in the filthy garments, much more those after
marriage. For here the charge becomes double =51.218= and triple, both that
while enjoying consolation they have leaped off into that wantonness, and that
the matter is reckoned not fornication only, but also adultery, which is more
grievous than every sin.

Chanting these things then both to ourselves and to the wives, let us continue
thus; wherefore I myself also will close with these words: But because of
fornications let each man have his own wife, and let each woman have her own
husband; let the husband render to the wife the goodwill that is due, likewise
also the wife to the husband. The wife does not have authority over her own
body, but the husband; likewise also the husband does not have authority over
his own body, but the wife.

Having kept these words with accuracy, both in the marketplace, and in the
house, and in the day, and in the evening, and at the table, and on the bed,
and everywhere both let us ourselves meditate, and let us teach the wives,
both to say them to us, and to hear them from us, that having lived the
present life chastely, we may also attain the kingdom of the heavens, by the
grace and loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom and with whom
to the Father, together with the Holy Spirit, be the glory unto the ages of
the ages. Amen.
