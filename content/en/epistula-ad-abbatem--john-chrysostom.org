* Letter to an Abbot
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

A letter of our father among the saints John Chrysostom to a certain abbot who
asked that a rule of spiritual teaching be sent to him from him, for the
benefit of himself and of the brothers with him.

Since you wrote to me, my most longed-for brother, that I should hand over to
you an ascetic rule, what I should write to you I do not know. But the Lord of
glory, Christ Jesus, is able to become a guide for you, if you offer yourself
genuinely with the brothers to the Lord your God, if you wish to strip off
fatherland, kindred, property, and the whole world together as an athlete (for
he who does not renounce all his possessions and die to the whole world cannot
become a disciple of the Lord), if you love the Lord your God from your whole
soul and your neighbor as yourself, if you keep your virginity blamelessly in
body and soul until the end, if each of you acquires mercy and compassion in
the vessels of the heart and prepares himself with bright lamps for the
meeting of the heavenly bridegroom, so that in that hour the lamp of each may
not be quenched, not having oil. For what will it profit a man if he keep the
body chaste, but the inside of the dish, that is the heart, is full of
wickedness, hatred, remembrance of wrongs, wrath, jealousy, envy, pride? For
he who is angry with his brother still walks in the darkness and is a
murderer, for the Scripture says. But if anyone thinks that I write out of
season, let him look to the parable of the ten virgins. The divine Scripture
named five wise and five foolish, not fornicators, but the outward virginity
profited them nothing. For instead of mercy and brotherly love, their heart
was filled with poison and wickedness, and because of this they were cast out
from that heavenly and unspeakable bridechamber, crying: Lord, Lord, open to
us. And they were no longer profited at all by the Lord of glory, but only
heard, I do not know you; for after death there is no confession of
repentance, but inescapable punishment. For here is petition, here is release
from evils; and whatever burden each binds, this he will also bear.

I beseech you therefore, my children and brothers, be subject to the Lord and
to one another in the fear of Christ. And let each of you prefer the brother;
for he who glorifies the brother glorifies Jesus, and he who dishonors the
brother sets aside the Lord who said: inasmuch as you did not do it to one of
these least, you did not do it to me.

I beseech you therefore, children, be subject to the abbot, as to Christ
himself, and to one another, so that none of you may be found as Judas, but as
the apostles, being subject to the Lord; for those who have resisted will
receive judgment for themselves. I do not say these things condemning, but
admonishing I beseech. I beseech you therefore, children, let none of you be
contradictory or insolent or a babbler or an idle-talker or a slanderer or a
murmurer. For all these things and what is like them are fruits of the devil,
and from the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks. For neither did
virginity benefit the sister of Moses and Aaron, but she became leprous, since
she sinned against her greater brother, as the Scripture of Moses teaches. I
beseech you therefore, children, acquire obedience without contradiction. For
obedience is mother of eternal life, and disobedience is mother of
destruction. I beseech you therefore, children, love one another because of
the Lord who said: if you love me, keep my commandments; and in this, he says,
all men will know that you are my disciples, if you love one another. For
where love is, there Christ dwells; for Christ is love. But where hatred is
and such things as we said before, there Christ is not, but an evil dragon
lodges there. It is good, then, to take care that contention or a fight may
not arise among you. But if one of you is also overcome, let him immediately
fall down with tears before the brother whom he grieved, and be reconciled to
him from the whole heart, as the Lord sees; for man looks to the face, but God
to the heart. For if you do not forgive men their trespasses, the Lord said,
neither will your heavenly Father forgive your trespasses; and forgive, and it
will be forgiven you; and let not the sun go down upon your wrath; for we do
not know what the coming day will bring forth, and we do not know at what hour
the thief comes. For it is impossible for a soul that remembers wrongs to
enter into the door of life. If it does all righteousness, it is an
abomination before the Lord, and all the work of such a man is vain. And
henceforth let each, whether he is rebuked justly or unjustly, or reproached
or insulted, hold himself the cause and not the brother. And he who does not
endure now is afflicted then, because he did not endure, but utterly destroyed
his reward. For what grace is it, says the Lord, if you love those who love
you? But love your enemies and pray for those who despitefully use you, bless
those who persecute you, bless and do not curse. And from him who takes your
things do not demand them back. And if anyone strikes you on the right cheek,
offer him the other also. And blessed are you when they reproach you and
persecute you and say every evil word against you, lying, for my sake. Rejoice
and be glad, for your reward is great in the heavens. He did not say that you
should be bitter, but rejoice. Therefore one must attend to the commandment of
the Lord and be subject to it with force and much long-suffering. For if there
is not force and long-suffering, what reward? For those who wrong you and
slander you, these procure a heavenly reward for you and benefit your souls.

For a choice that is not good cooperates with the hearing of good things; for
as much as the devil strives through the more careless brothers or by other
methods to put the soul to death, so much he prepares the zealous one to run
toward and cling to God. And henceforth, being helped and strengthened little
by little by the Lord, it becomes superior to the passions, and thus receives
the reward from the Lord, and the devil is found, as he does not wish,
becoming a benefactor of it, the heart contending until death. Henceforth he
who has ears to hear, let him hear. I beseech you therefore, my child and
brother, since the word is again toward you: if your brother sins against you
or against your neighbor, or some other fault is found, and you wish to
correct him, first secure your inner man, that is your heart, and keep it
untroubled with the memory of the Lord Jesus Christ, so that, wishing to
correct another, you may not destroy your own soul in the anger (for evil does
not cast out evil, but overcome the evil in the good), and bring the
correction to the fault, not through insults or blows, but through fasting, if
need be, as you judge with the Lord, knowing this, that without him you can do
nothing. Beseech him therefore at every time, that he himself may become an
eye for you and grant you understanding of good and of evil. But if he falls
down repenting, receive him even so; for condescension is good. And thus, I
think, the Lord does not know how to be angry at compassion, but is angry at
severity. Which we also find in the case of the debtor of the ten thousand
talents, the forgiveness of so many evils, and he himself, being entreated by
his fellow-servant, did not forgive the debt of a hundred denarii, and because
of this was condemned into the outer darkness. But if someone remains in the
same things, concerning this I neither dare nor am able to give an opinion.
Henceforth, whatever you judge in the Lord, this do; for it is good to gain a
soul; for he who turns a sinner from the error of his way will save a soul
from death and will cover a multitude of his sins.

I beseech you therefore, children, through the Lord, love humility, which
Christ especially spoke, who humbled himself for us unto death, and death of a
Cross, so that henceforth we too may become imitators of him and thus conquer
the pride of the devil through humility; and the Lord said because of the
humility that he who wishes to be great among you, let him be last of all and
servant of all and minister of all; for he who exalts himself will be humbled,
and the humble man can fall from nowhere, lying beneath all, but he who exalts
himself is carried down into the abyss of fire. I beseech you therefore,
children, have mercy on yourselves. I beseech you, weep, while you have time,
so that we may not weep there, where there is the gnashing of teeth, where the
sleepless worm never dies, where the unquenchable fire travels like a river,
where there are pitiless and unmerciful angels. They have no authority to have
mercy on anyone, or to reverence an old man, or to spare anyone, or to honor a
king, but to punish each unsparingly according to his deeds. I beseech you
therefore, force the back until death.

This work has need of much force, because narrow and afflicted is the way that
leads into the door of life, and those who force their way enter in it; for
the kingdom of the heavens is of those who force, says the Lord. Flee the
spirit of vainglory and the demon of man-pleasing, which especially also
separates from God; for God scattered the bones of men-pleasers, they were put
to shame because God set them at nought. Either here we are to be glorified
and there we receive the debt in full into the gehenna of fire unto unending
ages, or here we are to be dishonored and there we are to receive the reward
unto age of age. I beseech you therefore, let none of you be a babbler or an
idle-talker or a slanderer or an inventor or a murmurer (for these passions do
not let the soul see the beam in its own eye, but the speck in the brother's
eye), pleasing themselves, not God, whose end is destruction with the Pharisee
and the very mindset. But love the affliction according to God above the joy
of this age, and weeping above laughter; for blessed are those who mourn, the
Lord said, because they will be comforted. And woe to you who laugh, because
you will weep. Flee those who wish to occupy you, certain ones playing the
sophist with nothing useful from many readings, unto the twisting of those who
hear. And do not suck the poison of an asp through the hearing, and do not
wish to read many things but few and well, so that it may give grace to those
who hear. Learn rather to pray much and to weep much; for this strengthens the
mind and feeds the soul, but reading many things makes the mind wander. Hate
many meetings and the kinsmen according to the flesh, and do not turn back
toward the affection of parents or children or any others, but see them as
spiritual, as also the rest. And never wish to reveal a mystery of the
monastery to a fleshly kinsman, so that you may not be condemned in the day of
judgment; for you died to the world and have no affair with the world. For the
Lord says through the Gospel, when his mother and his brothers came to see
him; and stretching out his hand upon his disciples, saying: these are my
mother and my brothers, those who do the will of my Father in the heavens. And
again: go out from your land and from your kindred and come into a land which
I will show you, and the rest, as many things as the Lord says. But these
things were written for our admonition, and he who wishes to please God and to
do the will of the Lord does not serve two lords, nor loves anyone else above
the Lord. I beseech you therefore, prefer nothing to the Lord nor to your
soul. For what will it profit a man if he loses his soul, says the Lord, or
what will he give in exchange for his soul? For we brought nothing into the
world, it is clear that we also cannot carry anything out, but we have to rise
naked, clothed only with the acts and the works, whether good or base, as a
garment. And not only of works, but also of words and of thoughts, and thus we
will stand at that fearful judgment seat. And each henceforth has to enjoy his
own labors according to the just sentence of the Lord, whether unto rest or
unto punishment unto age of age; and blessed are those who weep in painful
tears from the heart, because they will then rejoice; blessed are those who
love the Lord and the neighbor, because they will be shown mercy; for he who
loves the brother loves himself, and he who hates the brother hates himself;
for in the brother it lies to inherit the eternal goods. Let us therefore
become imitators of the saints. As they passed through in hunger and thirst,
in cold and nakedness and through fire and sword, who also passed through
every affliction readily, so let us too imitate until death. But if you wish
to live shamelessly, this is not the will of God.

Pray without ceasing, without wrath and reasonings; for every thought that
separates the mind from God, even if it seems to be good, is wholly a devil,
that I may not say it is of the devil. Only that it may lead the mind astray
from God, it both speaks commandments and suggests good deeds within in the
heart, and certain other reasonable and unreasonable fantasies, to which it is
not at all proper even to attend. For the whole contest of the devil is to
separate and lead the mind away from God and to drag it into worldly
pleasures. And the whole contest of the soul is not to separate the mind from
God, nor to pair and agree with the unclean thoughts, nor to attend to what
the all-imitating and ancient painter, the devil, depicts in the heart,
sometimes types, sometimes manners, sometimes faces and shapes. And after all
these things the devil is transformed, and the wretched man, standing in one
place, thinks he is somewhere else, being deceived, and he seems to see
certain people and to speak to faces and to arrange affairs, all of which is
of diabolic deceit. One must therefore take care and hold the reins of the
mind and bridle it, and chastise every thought and every working through the
name of our Lord Jesus Christ who takes away the sin of the world. Where the
body stands, there let the mind also be, so that between God and between the
heart nothing else may be found, either as a middle wall or as a fence
darkening the heart, except only: speak to the Lord. But if at some time it
also snatches the mind away, one must not linger in the thoughts, so that the
consent of the thoughts may not be reckoned to him as a deed before the Lord
in the day of judgment, when God will judge the hidden things of men and when
the thought of man will confess to you. For it is impossible to attain the
kingdom, unless first he denies his own will and does the things commanded him
by the abbot without murmuring, with fear of God, as the Lord says: I did not
come to do my own will, but the will of the Father who sent me; what therefore
someone does not wish, if he suffers and endures, it is reckoned to him as a
crucifixion, and he becomes a child of the resurrection and of eternal life.
Blessed is the man who endures temptation, because, having become proven, he
will receive the unfading crown and will become a temple of the great king
Christ; and I will dwell and walk about, says the Lord, and we will make an
abode with him. Having therefore such promises, give yourselves wholly always
and remain with the Lord our God, until he has pity on you, and seek nothing
else, except only mercy from the Lord of glory, and it is enough for you. And
seeking mercy, seek in a humble and pitiable heart. Henceforth cry from
morning until evening, if possible, and the whole night, saying without pause:
Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy; Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have
mercy; Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on us. Amen. I beseech you
therefore, force, again I say force your mind until death. For this work has
need of much force, because narrow and afflicted is the way that leads into
the door of life, and those who force their way enter in it; for the kingdom
of the heavens is of those who force. I beseech you therefore, do not be
separated from God, nor from your heart, but remain and keep it with the
memory of the Lord Jesus Christ; this same thing always, until the name of the
Lord is planted within in the heart, and nothing else, until Christ is
magnified in you.

I beseech you therefore, never let the rule of this prayer cease. For I once
heard the holy fathers saying, what sort of monk is that man, if he lets a
rule cease? but he ought, whether he eats or drinks or travels or serves, to
cry without ceasing: Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on us, so that
this memory of the name of the Lord Jesus Christ may stir up the war of the
enemy. For through the memory of the Lord the soul that forces its way has to
find all things, whether evil or good; but first it has to see the evils
within in the heart, and then the goods. For the memory has to move the
dragon, and the memory has to humble him. The memory has to convict the sin
that dwells in you, and the memory has to consume it. The memory has to move
all the power of the devil in the heart, and the memory has to conquer it and
uproot it little by little, so that the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, coming
down into the depth of the heart, may humble the dragon who holds the
pastures, and may save and make alive the soul, the name of the Lord Jesus
Christ remaining without ceasing, crying, so that the heart may swallow the
Lord and the Lord the heart, and the two will become one. But this work is not
of one day or two, but of much time and much season; for there is need of much
contest and time, until the enemy is cast out and Christ dwells; for our
wrestling is not against blood and flesh, but against the spiritual things of
wickedness, says the apostle. Those therefore who renounce and are eager to
please the Lord ought to humble and afflict themselves in all things, so that
they may only attain the coming goods and the unending rest. I beseech you
therefore, take heed how you partake of the food. For the prophet says: serve
the Lord in fear; this he said in the time of prayer. And rejoice in him with
trembling; and this he said in the time of food. He did not say in laughter
and indifference, but in fear and trembling. For who will ever dare, when a
king is present, to laugh and bring upon himself indignation and punishment?
And when you psalm, psalm soberly in perception of heart, so that the heart
may taste and know the things said in discernment unto the glory of Christ,
not slackly, but fervently, so that both the mind may be aroused and the body
toward the vigil, so that from every side the soul may be fed and advance. And
never wish to say complex troparia, so that you may not undergo deceit, taking
pleasure in the voice. And concerning the service toward the brothers, take
heed what cellarer you appoint: one able to keep a conscience toward God and
the community, lest he ever fall into judgment and a snare of the devil, or a
secret-eater or one appropriating something, and destroy his soul. Likewise
also commit the services to each according to the conscience; for those who
have served well will receive the reward from the Lord. Do all these things
with the memory of the Lord, and beseech the Lord God that he himself may
become a guide for you, in whatever you do; for where Christ is called and
where Christ will dwell, all things are holy there, all things blessed. There
Christ is glad, and blessed are those who hunger for him and those who thirst
for him and those who eat him and those who drink him and those who love him.
For who will separate us from the love of Christ? Affliction or distress or
persecution or danger or sword or death? For the sweetness of Christ and his
divine affection prevails over all things together with the memory of Christ.

As also the holy martyrs despised death because of the love and the sweetness
of Christ, so that they might gain him alone, as also the holy Paul proclaims:
if anyone does not love the Lord Jesus, let him be anathema, maranatha; and
maranatha is in Hebrew: I saw the Lord. And immediately the Lord appeared to
him and says to him: since you love me, Paul, see where I am. From joy
therefore Paul says maranatha, I saw the Lord, see, the Lord has come, see
where he is. I beseech you therefore, hate the adornment of garments and love
humility and poverty and every affliction instead of joy, so that Christ may
love you. Flee meetings with women, whether worldly or canonical; for it is
hard even for the women who live as monks to meet with one another. The Lord
of glory will deliver us from such love. I beseech you therefore, children, in
this my last and final chapter, that each of you have before his eyes the
departure of his soul each day and night and hour; for this part knows how to
train the soul toward safety and to lead it toward salvation, so that it may
not be anxious about the things of tomorrow and imagine them (for sufficient
for the day is its evil), so that when the Lord comes upon our soul at an hour
which we do not know, he may find us ready for that eternal and endless way. I
wrote these things, my child and brother, in much pain of my heart, not from
reflection or study (Christ bears witness to me). But whatever he himself
dictated through my wicked heart, this I also wrote; I have no composition.
For he is the one who also in irrational animals at a fitting time granted a
human voice, as also in Rome to the dog in the case of Peter the apostle, not
because of the holiness of the dog, but because of the faith of those hearing.
And the Lord of glory who teaches man knowledge will guide you into his will.
The Lord of glory will establish your souls in his faith until the end. The
Lord of glory will grant you his peace and his love always. The Lord of glory
will keep your souls. The Lord of glory will be with you. He who reigns before
the ages, both now and always and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
