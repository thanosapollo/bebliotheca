On the righteous and blessed Job, Discourse 1. 1. There has come to us today
the yearly athlete of the inhabited world; there has come to us the stranger
and the contestant equal in honor with the angels, the one much struck by the
devil, and much testified to by God; the one much smitten by the devil, and
receiving many prizes from the spectator angels; the one much scourged by the
Enemy, and shown forth a great crowned one by God unto the inhabited world.
For thus He himself conversing with him said: Do you think that I have dealt
with you otherwise than that you might appear righteous? Who then is able to
praise such a champion according to his worth? who will dare to weave prizes
equal to his deeds of valor? who will attempt to go up toward an infinite sea
of encomia? For as none of those who have sense would ever dare to cross the
expanse of the sea with human footsteps, so neither will any of the wise and
exceedingly teachers ever attempt to recount to the hearers the praises
according to worth of that noble and thrice-blessed soul. For his achievements
surpass all human wisdom; his wrestlings surpass all crown-bearing. Therefore
we need an angelic tongue, that touching, so to speak, with the tips of the
fingers that casket of myrrh, we may fill the whole church at once. For if
only we attempt to touch the vessel of Job, and shake it a little, at once the
church is filled with the fragrance of the myrrh. Let us hear then the history
saying about him: In all these things that had befallen him Job sinned nothing
with his lips against the Lord. Did you see a testimony that stirs up the
praise of the angels? did you see a testimony that puts the devil to shame
together with his attempts? In all these things that had befallen him he
sinned nothing. This testimony of God throughout every church, as myrrh of
great price, gladdens the hearers. O vessel of great price stored in one place
according to Arabia, and sending forth the fragrance of the myrrh throughout
the whole inhabited world! O vessel struck by the devil with countless stones
and blows and arrows, and not pouring out the myrrh! Great toil came to the
de56.564 vil, that he might crush the vessel. and the myrrh =56.564=.20 of
piety he might make vanish; and he was eager through the stench of the wounds
to dim the fragrance of the myrrh. For indeed his body also stank from the
ulceration of the wounds; but both the vessel remained being wounded, and the
myrrh not being lost, that we too may say to him fittingly: Your name is myrrh
poured out. It was needful then to set up the athlete naked, as he wrestled,
that looking to that wondrous image we may be fully assured through the nature
of the facts, that God, wishing to show the devil that on the earth he has
righteous men, no longer deceived through flattery according to Adam, but
crowned through countless tortures and scourges, permitted this dramaturgy to
come about. Apostate, he says, you are high-minded, because having deceived
Adam, and having flattered the woman, you cast them out of paradise; behold a
man who lived without commands of Scripture, and who set right by
understanding the unwritten law of nature. Bring countless blandishments,
countless flatteries, countless tortures, if you can shake this excellent
soul. Behold, you cast the first man out of paradise. See now, attempt, if you
can, to trip the athlete even from a dunghill. But, if it seems good, let us
run back a little from above to the history itself: There was a certain man in
the land of


Ausitis, whose name was Job. And that man was blameless, righteous, godly,
true, abstaining from every evil deed. Such was Adam also before he was
persuaded by the woman. Therefore this one is not persuaded by the woman, that
he may not become according to that one. That one was clothed in incorruption,
as the one who fashioned him knows, and that he might be persuaded by the
woman, he became naked, and was condemned. This one was naked, and was not
persuaded by the woman, and that he might not be persuaded by the woman, he
put on the crown-bearing of the angels. That one, that he might be persuaded
by the woman, both fell from the luxury of paradise, and made the earth a
mother of thorns, and inherited death; this one, that he might not be
persuaded by the woman, both received the freedom of the dunghill and of the
blows, and inherited the kingdom of the heavens. That one apologizing =56.565=
to God said: The woman, whom you gave me, she gave to me, and I ate. O apology
that has no pardon! She said, Eat; I said, Do not eat. Why then did you hear
the woman, and disobey God? But not so was the blessed Job. But how? With
rebuke he said: You have spoken as one of the foolish women. A man was in the
land of Ausitis, whose name was Job. And why did he name the land? For you,
that you may learn that it is not the company of sinners that can make the
righteous man sin, but the choice of those who are pious ought to have a
standing and firm mind. For this land of Arabia is a dwelling of wicked and
corrupted men. All were lawless, all God-hated, all profane, all proud, and
descendants of Esau, and flourishing in sins; but so great a sea of impiety
was not able to quench the lamp of piety; so great a country of evils was not
able to choke the beauty of endurance. Whence the devil attempted to approach
this man. Why? Because he saw him rich rather in alms than in money. For hear
him saying unlyingly: I was an eye of the blind, and a foot of the lame; and
again, I wept over the powerless, and I groaned seeing a man in necessities.
And not only through words, but also through works he was exceedingly rich,
being found a whole treasure of well-doing for those in need. For what does he
say elsewhere? There went not out from my house one in need with empty bosom;
the mouth of the widow blessed me; and from the shearing of my sheep the
shoulders of the poor were warmed. So that it is clear that through all things
he was rich, a whole treasure, both through words, and through works, and
through almsgiving, and through the protection of the poor, and through the
help of those in need. But see what the devil does: He snatches away all his
possessions from him first, and last he takes the children. He took first the
herds of camels, he took the flocks of sheep, the great number of the asses,
the greatest herd of the oxen; he took all away, and last the children. Why?
Attend then with all accuracy: First he took all his possessions, then the
children; he took the hereditary things, then he made the heirs vanish, that
in every way he might strike him also through the children. First he began to
make the substance vanish, first he began to snatch the hereditary things
while the heirs were living, that while the sons and the daughters were
standing, through the loss of the possessions the righteous man might receive
a mortal blow of despondency. For the wicked demon was considering, lest
perhaps, the children being destroyed first, he might not even come to a sense
of the loss of the possessions. But the truly adamant and noble athlete, the
one much struck, and through all things crowned, the one receiving many
arrows, and through the arrows themselves shooting down the devil, the one
armed through the weapons of righteousness of the right and of the left, did
not suffer, did not weep, was not shaken, but neither was he bent toward the
loss of the possessions, nor toward the death of the children was his piety
plundered, but he remained as an unshaken tower, from below on the earth


rooted and founded, and secured even unto the heavenly vaults themselves.
There came a first messenger, and he says to Job: The yokes of the oxen were
plowing, and the captors came and took them captive, and they killed the
servants with swords. While this one was still speaking, there came another
messenger, and he says to Job: Fire fell from heaven and burned up your sheep,
and likewise it consumed the shepherds. 2. See what the devil does. For since
he saw him standing nobly at the first and second blow, and not at all shaken
from his tortures, being warred against, and not being wounded, he devises to
do some such thing: He casts fire from air, that the fire may seem to be sent
down from heaven, and that, thinking that God from the heavens is warring
against him, he may blaspheme God, who thus without any sin is unjustly
warring against him; Fire, he says, fell from heaven, and consumed your sheep
and the shepherds. While this one was still speaking, there came another
messenger, and he said to Job: =56.566= The horsemen made three heads against
us, and they surrounded the camels, and took them captive, and they killed the
servants with swords. But also in all these things the blessed Job remained,
as an unshaken pillar, as an unshaken tower, as an unconquered contestant,
unbending, unyielding, striking rather than being struck. For when someone
toils without profit, warring much, and benefiting nothing, fighting often,
and not at all prevailing, he himself rather is the one receiving the scourges
according to his vain toils. While this one was still speaking, there came
another messenger. Continuous is the war, but vain for the one toiling; denser
the arrows, but brighter the prizes. He does not let him even for an hour take
breath, that he may not according to the inborn piety of the wrestler reason
something wise. He sent all the arrows, and did not conquer the athlete; he
emptied his own quiver, and was not emptied of the pain. Let those who act
lawlessly in vain be ashamed. Since then that one also was turned back
humbled, put to shame, the one who wrestled much, and through all things was
defeated. For he sent against shepherds, against herds, against the sheep,
against the camels, against the sons, against the daughters, against the
house, against the body, through the friends, through the household servants,
through the worms, through the wounds, through the ichors. He emptied all, and
he did not loose the piety. He dug through the wall, and he did not empty the
treasure. As many swords as the tyrant of the inhabited world had, he moved,
through fire, through swords, through a mound, through captivity, through
blood. He stretched all, and he made the athlete rather more manly. Wherefore
also the prophet cried out saying: The swords of the Enemy have failed unto
the end. And perhaps he says also toward him: It is hard for you to kick
against the goads. He took all the money, the possessions, the substance. And
what of the last messenger? Your sons and your daughters were eating and
drinking in the house of their elder brother, suddenly a wind came from the
desert, and it touched the four corners of the house, and the house fell upon
your children, and they died. And that you may learn, beloved, that it was not
a man who was declaring these things to Job, but it was the devil himself
schemed in the form of a man, learn with all accuracy: Your sons and your
daughters were eating and drinking at their elder brother, suddenly a wind
came from the desert, and it touched the four corners of the house, and the
house fell upon your children, and they died. From where do you know, tell me,
that it came from the desert, and touched the four corners of the house? You
are a man, and how did you see the wind? with what eyes did you behold it? and
how did the wind also touch the four corners? If you are a man, and you were
inside in the house, surely the house was taking you also in; but if you were
outside, how were you seeing the things happening within? But if you are a
wind, show who you are, do not scheme into the form of a man.


Your sons, he says, and your daughters were eating and drinking at their elder
brother. See how filled with all understanding and wisdom the blessed Job was,
and how he was securing the friendship of the children. For he reasoned that
Cain and Abel were two brothers, being alone according to the inhabited world,
having no occasion of war; for the whole earth was set forth in common under
the power of both; and thus through the honor of Abel Cain having envied,
rising up killed the brother. Therefore the blessed Job on the one hand for
the unclear things was offering a sacrifice each day to God, rising early, and
saying: Lest perhaps my sons have thought evil things in their heart toward

contention, he was making them each day eat with one another, considering that
even if they have some small-spiritedness toward one another, the table and
the disposition of the loaves makes them be transformed into friendship, and
to banish the wickedness that has fallen upon them, knowing that through the
continual table all the wicked things that have fallen upon them are loosed.
But the devil who hates the good was watching in such a time to destroy the
children who had sinned nothing; in the time of midday, =56.567= when all were
assembled, when they were being joined toward peace, then he was sending forth
the implacable war against them. They friendship, this one was sharpening a
sword; they were building love, and this one was overthrowing the house. And
it came about in that very hour that the house was a tomb, and the table a
hull of disaster, and a shepherd's fold a shipwreck, and all things taken by
beasts. What then did that noble one? He was not confused, he did not fall.
What of him shall I praise? what word will suffice? what toil will suffice

no excess! Truly, beloved, I am confused in soul, I am thoroughly troubled in
conscience, even seeing Job being crowned. But I am confused in soul now
rather, I who see the crowned one, than that one then seeing the disaster of
the children before his eyes; I am confused in soul; and I think you also are
so disposed. How then I may tell out so great a tragedy, I do not know, even
bearing on my tongue the crown of the encomia. Parents according to the flesh,
when they have a son held by the last breaths, sit around, hear last words,
embrace his hands, join agreements about the unexpected things, kiss the
mouth, the last kisses of the fathers. Then the child has given back the soul,
as the one who gave commanded, the parents arrange him, they stretch out
hands, they close eyes, they set the head right, they stretch out the feet,
they wash him, they clothe him with the worthy burial garments, and they
console their own disaster. But what toward this noble athlete? He went away
to the house that at the same time and one and the same hour had become house
and tomb, banquet and mound, feast and mourning. He was digging around and
seeking the limbs of the children, and he was finding wine and blood, bread
and a hand, an eye and dust. And he was taking now a hand, now a foot, at
another time he was drawing up a head together with the mound, and the stones,
and the woods; and now a part of the belly, at another time the inward parts
of the entrails mixed up with the mounds. And that heaven-high contestant was
sitting, seeing the limbs of the children torn apart. He was sitting putting
together limbs toward limbs, a hand toward an arm; molding a head toward a
breast, knees toward the thighs. For the disaster of the things that had
happened was not one, but from diabolic energy and tyranny the overthrow that
surpasses every word. Job then was sitting, the truly enduring adamant, the
blessed, distinguishing the limbs of his children, lest somehow he should mold
the limbs of the females according to the male limbs, lest somehow the male
form should be fitted together into the limbs of the female. O noble soul,
surpassing the praise of the angels! Why? Because in all these things that had
befallen


him Job sinned nothing against the Lord, but instead of all these he brought
whole thanksgiving as a sacrifice to God, saying: Let the name of the Lord be
blessed, now and always, and unto the ages of the ages. Amen. On the righteous
and blessed Job, Discourse 2. 1. Great things need great narrations, and the
bright histories of the righteous need both a bright tongue and mind; a mind
on the one hand sincerely contemplating the divine Scriptures, and a tongue
clearly interpreting the divine things, and especially upon the present
subject, I mean the things toiled by the blessed Job, which going about
through all under heaven flash forth the word of endurance. Yet even if the
word of the subject conquers our weakness, still I know well that the grace of
God furnishes a word, not through the worth of the speaker, but through the
intercession of the Fathers. But let us come upon the narration itself: There
was a certain man in the land of Ausitis. It is necessary first to consider
what power the preface has, and for the sake of what from this expression the
historian has made the preface, There was a certain man. Since he was about to
narrate a virtuous life, and one much conquering our condition, that no one
looking to the excess of the contests may think the nature to be foreign, and
not human, taking the lead he showed the nature, that you may wonder at the
purpose, that being a man he conducted himself above a man, and sharing the
common nature with all, but leading the soul up higher than the world. There
was a certain man in the land of Ausitis. He proclaims him also from the
=56.568= nature; he proclaims him also from the land; that, just as having
heard him a man, you wonder that he conducted himself above a man, so if you
learn also the land, from which so great a fruit blossomed, you may wonder
truly what sort of rose blossomed in the midst of the thorns. Attend
accurately. The land then of Ausitis was a land of Esau; for from Esau is
Ausitis. And he names the land of the profane and wicked Esau, as the Apostle
says: Lest there be any fornicator or profane person, as Esau; that you may
wonder from what schools of wickedness what sort of fruit of truth blossomed.
But not only did the land of Esau bear him, but also the very root of Esau;
for he was a descendant of the one spoken against. For Scripture says that Job
is fifth from Abraham. And how fifth, hear: Abraham begot Isaac, Isaac Esau,
Esau Raguel, Raguel Zara, Zarade Job. He had then the succession coming down
from Abraham through Esau unto him. He shows then the root, that you may
wonder at the fruit. And who was Esau? A fornicator and profane. And who also
was Job? The one from the fornicator, this one himself the solemn and wondrous
man, the one now set before us for narration; the one saying: If my eye
followed after a strange woman, let my wife also please a strange man. Esau
had mixing toward many women, being insatiable unto licentiousness; but Job
had the manner as a law of life, and so far abstained, as not to be curious
about strange beauties; and from fornication and adultery he so far abstained,
as not even to attend to women not yet pledged, that is, to the unbetrothed
virgins; for one must use the clearer expression; So far he abstained from
plotting against strange marriages, that not even toward the unbetrothed
virgin, upon whom there was no danger, did he stretch the eye toward
incontinence. And who is the one testifying these things? Job himself: I made,
he says, a covenant with my eyes, and I did not understand upon a virgin. See
until what training the things of piety flourished for him. He trains the eye
unto piety, he trains also the members unto righteousness, and bearing the
succession from the fornicator, he shone with greatest chastity. Esau was also
a brother-killer, as far as unto purpose; for even if the deed did not come
out, still the purpose is accused. For Esau said, he says, in his heart: Let
the days of the mourning of my father draw near, and I will take away Jacob my
brother. The root then, a brother-killer; but the one


who blossomed from the root, an umpire of righteousness. That one warred
against nature, and held the brother as a stranger; this one even held
strangers as brothers, the one saying: I became to orphans as a father, and
instead of a husband to their mother. A cause which I did not know, I tracked
out, and I made unjust men cease. And he makes all the strange things his own,
the one bearing the succession from Esau, who was setting the strange things
as his own. Esau in his mind was reckoning to take away Jacob; but Job was
offering sacrifices for the sons, considering, Lest they have sinned something
according to mind. For since that one according to mind practiced the murder,
this great and noble athlete fearing, lest these also in the mind receive the
poison of sin, was offering a sacrifice and one calf concerning sins. For Job
was saying: Lest perhaps my sons have thought evil things toward God? Consider
unto how much accuracy the training of the manner reached for him. And what
does he do? Since Esau and Jacob the ancestors were set at war toward one
another, not of Jacob agreeing unto wickedness, but of Esau beginning unto
wickedness, he dissolves the disagreement of the ancestors by the feasting,
and he makes his sons feast with one another each day, that the accustomed
table may dissolve the poison of wickedness. The sons of Job were going about
to one another, being trained in concord from young age, practicing peace,
being trained in agreement, and they were eating and drinking with one
another. And they were taking, he says, also the three sisters to eat and
drink with them. And what is this unto benefit for the hearer? and why did he
add the joint-feasting of the sisters? That it may be shown unto how much
chastity their symposium was brought, and that there was not disorderly and
poured-out laughter, and incontinence insulting the table, therefore he also
brings in the virgins being feasted, that he may show the character of the
symposium not insulted by drunkenness, but crowned with agreement. A56.569 man
was in the land of Ausitis; a dove in the midst of hawks, a sheep in the midst
of wolves, a star in the midst of the clouds, a lily in the midst of the
thorns, the shoot of righteousness in the village of injustice. And that man
was blameless. 2. Attend accurately: Those outside set a definition of a man.
What is a man? A man is, he says, a rational mortal animal. These the
definitions of the philosophers wish; but Scripture does not give such
definitions; but what? A man blameless, righteous, true. If someone asks you,
and demands a definition, What is a man? say toward him, Blameless, righteous,
true, abstaining from every evil deed; as the one not testified to by such
achievements is not even a man. For if he has the character of nature, but
insults it by the wickedness of choice, he hears from the prophet: Man being
in honor did not understand; he was compared with the senseless beasts, and
was made like them. And that man was blameless. Not small is the encomium.
Test the expression, that you may wonder at the purpose. One thing is an
accusation, and another is blame; for accusation is brought upon the heaviest
of sins; but blame comes to be upon the lightest faults. The writer then
wished to show that the righteous man was far not only from accusations, but
also from blames. No one accused him as having done great things, where indeed
neither did he blame him as having done small things. And this is a great
proof, of whom the life is in the middle, unaccused. For the one against whom
you do not have to accuse concerning adultery, or concerning murders, such a
one is unaccused; but the one to whom you bring blames of insult, slander, or
reviling, or drunkenness, such a one is outside of accusations, but runs under
blame. He wished then to show that he was far also from the small and least
faults, and he says: He was blameless, righteous, true, godly, abstaining from
every evil deed. Whence also God wishing to show Abraham that the first
according to virtue is the blameless, says to him: And you, be well-pleasing
before me, and become blameless. The, Blameless, is taken unto an extremity of
manners. But the apostle Paul, since toward the nations he had those from


polytheism torn away, those from wickedness gathered, those from fornication
and adultery assembled, did not add the extreme of virtue, but as he came into
the inhabited world filled with fornication and adultery, he wished also to
set shepherds of the inhabited world, since then the good things of virtue
were scarce, setting bishops, he says to Titus: Set bishops, as I directed
you, if anyone is unaccused. The, Blameless, did not then have room, since, as
I said before, the things of piety were still scarce. Great is the blameless,
and in the middle is the unaccused; and in evils even the small good is great.
Therefore he said: A man of one wife. Not because this is now kept in the
Church; for the priest must be crowned with complete purity and holiness; but
because then toward those in fornication it was great, he says: Set bishops,
as I directed you, if anyone is unaccused, a man of one wife; not because he
set this as a law, but because he conceded to the error. For he knew that when
the word of piety is flourishing, nature itself will have the good as easily
chosen, and the election of the better will come about. Blameless, righteous.
It is not possible then first to become righteous, not having become
blameless. True. What does the true wish? The one not schemed in the face, and
having another condition; the one not scheming chastity, but clothed in
incontinence; the one not showing righteousness in the figure, but bearing
injustice in the heart. This one is true, and immediately godly. For when
there is the blameless, when the righteous, when the true, then the true piety
is completed. Abstaining from every evil deed. Since many abstain from many
sins, but do not abstain from all, wishing again to set down the extreme of
virtue, he says: Abstaining from every evil deed. Whence also David says: You
who love the Lord, hate evil things. For it is not possible both to be godly
and to do wickedness. And he also sets forth his substance, that he had seven
thousand sheep, three thousand camels, five hundred yokes of oxen, five
hundred she-asses of pasture. Why does he set these forth? Not wondering at
the wealth of Job, =56.570= but leaving to you the hearer the multitude of the
substance, that, when you see him after these things stripped of the
possessions, and not groaning, you may wonder, that being deprived of so many
good things, he does not set aside the benefactor. he does not insult the
Creator. These things then were written, not that you may wonder at the
wealth, but that you may praise the endurance and the patience of the
righteous man, and may have always before your eyes the fear of God. But the
sequence will store the contemplation for another time. For the devil,
brothers, since he saw him being rich with this temporary wealth, and from
this temporary wealth storing up the eternal wealth, considered, that just as
when he was rich righteousness grew rich together with him, so also when he is
poor virtue also will be poor together. He attempts then to strip him of the
money, that he may strip him also of the advantages. And here let those who
say attend accurately: Was not Job rich? was not Abraham rich? why then do you
set down to us possessionlessness? I also will say toward you: Become rich
according to Job, and I pray for you to acquire more; become according to
Abraham, and the whole earth will pray together with you for the wealth. Say
also you, as Job, My door has been opened to every one afflicted; say, I
warmed the shoulders of the poor; say, From the shearing of my sheep the
shoulders of the poor were warmed; say, And the mouth of a widow blessed me;
and be rich with wealth which you wish. For you will be found not being rich
for yourself, but managing for the poor. For my door, he says, has been opened
to every one afflicted. Imitate the extremity of this love of the poor; put on
the zeal of endurance; be manly against the adversaries; think the equal
things. But these things unto so much. And may it come about that we, having
the zeal and the endurance ringing in the ear, have also the sequence of the
things left, and that you demand from us the things left, and that we pay you


the sequence of the contemplations. And may it come about that we both
genuinely accomplish the feast, and having shared in the sufferings of the
saints, share also in the passion of Christ. For truly, brothers, justly the
sufferings of the saints shone forth before the passion of Christ. For they
shone forth before, as lightnings, the sufferings of the righteous, that after
these Christ might appear, as Paul says, the one suffering with all, and being
glorified with all. By prayers then and intercessions of the holy Fathers,
both agreeing with the things said, and expecting the things left, let us send
up glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, unto the ages
of the ages. Amen. On the righteous and blessed Job, Discourse 3. 1. Come, let
us also touch the subject, and you the hearing; and let us all become of the
evening sequence of Job, and with faith let us present ourselves to the word
of endurance. For it is out of place that Job sitting upon a dunghill not grow
numb toward piety, but we dancing in church hesitate toward the truth; and
that he being surrounded with countless lamentations of his own disaster not
be broken in soul; but we being sung around with hymns and spiritual odes and
divine Scriptures betray the eagerness by slackness. Job, his body being eaten
through by worms, was keeping the soul unwounded toward piety; worms did not
conquer that one, and will sweats wrestle us down? Let us also become men, men
according to the definition given before. For the blameless, the righteous,
the true, the godly characterize the man. And just as in the common custom
whenever someone sends his own household servant or his son upon some deed, he
exhorts, saying: As a man touch this deed, and he makes the common name a
private wonder, so even if all are called men, that one alone is a true man,
the one saving the image, and not making the original beauty vanish. He called
him a man, that from the common nature you may praise the purpose. He called
him from the land of Ausitis, that from the land flourishing in impiety you
may hymn the fruit. He spoke of his possessions, that you may wonder at the
advantages. He spoke of his wealth, not that you may praise the abundance, but
that you may wonder at him stripped. For he was stripped of all, and he was

fleshes; surely he will bless you unto your face. The, He will bless, instead
of, He will curse. For Scripture covered the ill-speaking with the
well-speaking. 2. And why have these things been written? That you the
faithful may learn, when you narrate the shameful things of the evils of
others, to cover them with the solemnity of the expressions. Do not be solemn
doing shameful things, but be solemn when you flee the converse of the
shameful things. Therefore the Apostle says: Let every shameful word not go
out from your mouth. Then God delivers the contestant unto a second trial, not
himself learning from the trial of what sort the contestant was, but putting
the devil to shame from the trial. For God before all things knows all things.
And the devil went out from the face of the Lord, and he struck


Job with a wicked sore from feet unto head. He made his whole body one wound,
one =56.578= weal. For it was needful that he be crowned a contestant wholly
through whole. From feet unto head the saving wound. the victorious ulcer, an
ulcer according to the appearing flowing ichors, but according to the thought
full of praises. The ichors of the ulcer came about in few times, but the
praises, which the contest begot, unto all the age are not spent. He struck
him with a wicked sore from feet unto head, that wholly through whole of the

burned outside of the camp. Therefore also Jesus, that he might sanctify the
people, suffered outside of the gate. Christ outside of the gate; Job outside
of the city sat upon a dunghill; the seed of piety, the seed of endurance,
being dunged upon the afflictions, sat upon a dunghill, waiting for the one
who raises a poor man from earth, and who raises up the needy from a dunghill.
He was seeing, brothers, the body flowing through, but the soul established;
he was seeing the body bearing worms, but the soul begetting piety; he was
seeing the earthen vessel being corrupted, and he was remembering before Paul
the sayings of Paul; for it was fitting for him to say: As much as our outer
man is being corrupted, so much the inner is being renewed day by day. He took
a potsherd, that he might scrape the ichor, scraping off the ichor with the
potsherd, loosening the antagonist with the endurance. It was fitting for him
also to say holding the potsherd: And we have this treasure in an earthen
vessel. He was sitting upon a dunghill, the one who had raised many from a
dunghill; he took a potsherd, that he might scrape the ichor; with the dead
clay he was scraping the living clay. The three friends came; for disasters
call the friends unto consolation; Eliphaz came, Sophar, and Baldad, three
kings toward one king. For he was a king before this, the one saying: And I
was among them as a king among the girded. And they sat, he says, opposite Job
seven days and seven nights, and no one spoke. For the moderate disasters
receive consolation; but the greatnesses of the disasters are honored by the
silence. For there is, brothers, when the suffering conquers consolation; and
just as the diseases in a crisis send away all therapy, so also the sufferings
in the crisis of the disasters cast out all admonition. They were silent,
sharing the suffering by the silence. And what does he do? Since the disaster
conquered those, and no one dared to break a voice of consolation, first Job
begins the words: the one suffering opens a door to those not suffering. For
we know, brothers, that in the disasters, or in the mournings, or in some
other matter of dejection, each is ashamed first to break a voice, that he may
not seem to be some untimely introducer of virtue or of mind; but if he sees
another having begun, he follows without danger. This, brothers, both
experience teaches, and the facts set down. As then he saw the suffering
having shut the consolation of the friends, the one suffering opens a door,
and he begins immediately to make a tragedy of the disaster, everywhere
fleeing the suspicion of blasphemy: First voice of Job: Cursed be the day, in
which I was born, and that night let it be darkness. He does not say these
things being impious, but suffering. And see what he does: Just as whenever
someone being held by a terrible abscess is cut with iron by the physician,
not having how he may stand against the one cutting, he touches those standing
around, and he bites those present, having nothing to accuse those, but not
being strong to stretch the hand toward the physician; so also Job fearing of
blasphemy


the greatness, insults the soulless things, and he fills the full assurance of
the suffering, not daring against God, but accusing himself and the day; for
he curses, not the creation, but his own day. For I have authority, he says,
of my day; I do not curse the fashioning, I curse my own day. Cursed be the
day, in which I was born56.579, and that night let it be darkness. These
things Jeremiah also says: Woe is me, O mother, as what sort of man did you
bear me, a man being judged and being tried? Cursed be the day in which I was
born. And all these things the saints make a tragedy of, that you after these
things may learn, that they did not go through the life living in luxury, but
having endured countless evils, they went through the contests. Jeremiah then
also wept saying: Woe is me, O mother, as what sort did you bear me? and he
suffered on the one hand as a man, and he endured as a lover of God. Moses
likewise, the great and divine lawgiver of God, the one who received the holy
tablets, the first introducer of the things beneficial to the life, he himself
also says to God being weighed down by the multitude of the disasters: Lord,
you said, that You have found grace with me, and I know you beyond all; and if
I have found grace before you, take away my soul from me; for I am not able to
bear the weight of this people. A little, and they will stone me. Did I
travail them? did I bear them; that you say to me: Bear them in the manner
which the nurse bears the child? I am not able to bear the shortcomings of the
people. Nevertheless both lamenting and mourning as a man, he did not
blaspheme as a godless one, but he was showing the sufferings of nature, being
convicted by the sufferings themselves. And thus likewise each of the prophets
laments, and Habakkuk says: Why did you show me toils and labors, to look upon
miseries of men? For the prophetic choir was seeing the injustice, and was not
bearing it, but Habakkuk was crying saying: Why did you show me toils and
labors? Before me judgment has come about, and the judge receives, and
therefore the judgment goes out distorted. The ruler asks, and the judge
receives, and the law is set at nothing. 3. Thus all the righteous were
suffering, and they were at a loss toward the difficulty of the world. But do
not taking these things interpret them unto agreement of your choice; do not
say: Am I better than Jeremiah who cried out against the world? let not the
words of the healing become wounds for you. And these things have been
written, that you may learn how great a contest they contested through, how
full of dangers the life they went through. Do you wish to learn of what sort
you ought to be? Learn the things after the grace: Prophets suffered, and the
apostles also suffered; but the ones suffering were in pain, and the ones
suffering were boasting. Paul came into the midst, suffering on the one hand,
but not weeping; being struck, but not lamenting; being tortured, but
boasting. And what does he say? And not only so, but we also boast in the
afflictions, knowing that the affliction works endurance, and the endurance
proof, and the proof hope, and the hope is not put to shame. And again, the
apostles having been struck, They were going rejoicing from the face of the
council, that they were deemed worthy to be dishonored for the name of Christ.
Do not then interpret the things before the grace unto a sequence of the
grace. For these things have been said to us, that you may learn the hope from
the toils. Thus also Job curses the day, and suffers the soul, and he does not
torture the soul with impiety, but he holds up under the wounds, and he does
not wound the soul with blasphemy. Then, since one must cut short, we will
briefly recapitulate the subject for you: In the contest according to Job,
except God, all were ignorant of the aim of the contest. Job did not know upon
what he was contesting, he did not know that the wrestlings have been set
forth for him unto exercise. The friends were ignorant of the aim of God, the
devil was ignorant of the coming thing. For if he knew that he was being
conquered, he would not have attempted. Job did not know the thing that would
result from the contest. That on the one hand God by concession had given
over, Job knew, not knowing beforehand, but having learned from God. But the
devil did not know unto what sort of end the wrestlings will meet. For if he
knew, he would not have attempted, that he might not draw upon himself a worse
shame; but what he suffered upon the martyrs, this


he suffered also upon Job. He took away martyrs, that he might quench the
Church, not knowing, that after the martyrs the Church will blossom. All then
were ignorant, except God. The friends being ignorant of the aim of God, being
ignorant also of the testing of Job, chose as in a comparison of ignorance
rather to condemn Job as suffering worthy things, than to condemn God as
having judged unworthily. Attend accurately: They chose to condemn a man as
suffering things worthy of his own sin, than to say =56.580= against God, that
he judged unworthily. And in one part they were being borne according to the
mark, saying God outside of injustice; and in one part they were missing,
condemning the innocent. But the ignorance apologizes for those who are
ignorant, and they are acceptable, that in ignorance they philosophize, and
they do not vote against, seeing the righteousness of God. The righteousness
of Job on the one hand they set aside, but that of God they hymn. Then Job
begins to be convicted by the friends; and those say toward him: When have
true men been destroyed from the root? when did you see righteous men being
destroyed wholly from the root? If you did not sin, he says, and do things
worthy of sin, the righteous one would not have unjustly wronged you. Many are
your sins. And if you did not sin, from where is this, unless your sons
sinned? And often you wronged a widow, you afflicted orphans. And if these
things have come about, God also justly brought this vote. Job was conscious
on the one hand to himself of having done no such thing, and he was conscious
also to God of not judging unjustly. Since then Job saw, that among men on the
one hand he has been condemned, but the conscience does not condemn him, and
God does not condemn him, since he was not able to persuade men, he transfers
the word upon the judge, and he turns the eye of the soul toward the things
above not blaspheming, but putting to shame, and he says toward him: The one
who knows the mind of men, why have you set me as an accuser of you? You have
made me to plead against your righteousness; you know my heart. Then Job
utters some great thing, not from wickedness, but from guilelessness; and,
that I may cut the many things into few (for much is the matter of the
defense), he says: Who will give a judge in the midst of me and you, that he
may know how many my sins are, that you have thus judged me? Fearful is the
saying, but from guilelessness. For God taking the lead said, that There is
not according to him a guileless one; and to the guileless one no one reckons
the saying unto villainy. For always, brothers, all we men, not to say God, do
not judge the deeds, but the purpose of those who do. A son insults a father;
and the deed is unbearable, both to the one suffering, and to the one hearing;
and such a one is called a father-striker, and a mother-striker, and unholy
and lawless, because he threw an opposite voice to a father, because he dared
to speak ill of his own root. And these things the one being upon complete
reasonings suffers and is condemned; but a guileless little child, even if he
strikes the father, or the mother, or even insults, is more longed for than
all gladness. Often mothers stir the children bringing them unto insult, not
at the insult, but rejoicing at the guilelessness and the unmixed and simple
of the character. Thus also God knowing, that he speaks not from wickedness,
but from guilelessness (for he testifies to him saying, And still he holds to
guilelessness), receives the things from Job, being called into judgment by
him. Fearful is the saying, a household servant to call a Master into
judgment; a man, God; the fashioning, the one who fashioned; the clay, the one
who constructed; but nevertheless, as I said before, the saying is of
guilelessness, not of villainy. Yet nevertheless, brothers, the guilelessness
was not outside of understanding, but within all wisdom. For do you wish to
learn the more accurate? Job asked nothing of the things which he did not do.
He knew himself a household servant of God, he knew God his own Master. But
since he himself also often being judged toward his own slaves, gave them a
worth of equal speech, and if ever a slave was being tested toward him, he was
not silencing him as a slave, but he was receiving the defense; what he did,
he asked, being persuaded by the word, that With what measure you measure, it
will be measured back to you. And he says: Who will give a judge? And when did
you keep equal speech for a slave? Hear him when he enumerates the virtues,

this not from ambition, but from guilelessness, saying: I did not set aside a
judgment of my servant, and of my maidservant, when they were being judged

Considering these things, he was granting to the common of nature the common
