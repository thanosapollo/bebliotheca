ugly men, and then addressing those they met as if acquaintances, and
afterward narrating to others the things conversed toward the road; and others

swords. And indeed thus the greater things were coming out. Nor were those
being saved able to give the bodies of those who had departed to burial and to
perform the due rite upon them, very few carrying out very many. For which
reason they were constructing engines from a certain device, laying boards
upon packed burden-bearing animals and widening the engine upon a great space,
and thus they were placing the greater part of the corpses unseemly and as it
might happen. And already also wagons being borne were serving toward the
carrying-out of the multitude of the dead, the beasts of burden no longer
being able to suffice for them. And the tombs also have failed once for all,
so that even the dry cisterns were filled with bodies of the dead. And fields
were being cut open and vineyards were being dug through and gardens were
being dug up, being newly 64 opened toward the burial of the boundless bodies.
From which it was happening that the greater number of the houses were
becoming entirely shut up. And the things of the breaking lasted as far as a
year, and from that point the greater things were giving way, and little by
little, just as it increased at the beginning, so also the destruction abated.
These things were judged by those who know to think rightly to strike from
divine wrath, when the one then ruling godlessly and impiously, and as many as
were agreeing with him in the lawless thought, dared to set their hands upon
the sacred images to the outrage


of the church of Christ. From this point then, this city having already become
almost uninhabited, he settles it, transferring multitudes of peoples from the
countries and the islands of the authority under the Romans. And under this

then commanding the so-called army of the Kibyrrhaiots was leading. And the
fleet comes to anchor at the island of Cyprus. Where immediately a naval force
of the Saracens also put in from the city of Alexander. Which the strategos
perceiving beforehand suddenly falls upon it and having burned the ships with
fire appropriates all their things, and having taken the enemies by force was
returning to Constantine. After these things a son is born to the emperor,
whom he named Leo. Then also in Syria a great earthquake comes about and a
very great destruction arrives from there. For of the cities there some the
earth gaped to a great extent about them, and some had suffered this in part.
Others having been moved from the high places were dragged down safe to the
underlying plains together with their walls and dwellings, having changed from
their own foundation as far as six [and] stades, or a little more. And already
some 65 said they had seen the earth that is toward the Syria of Mesopotamia,
having been split for two stades, bubble up from below another deep earth as
if sandy and most white, and that with this a female mule was given up
together, proclaiming beforehand in a human voice the dissolution of the
Arabs. For not much was between, and a nation from the desert beyond, having
grown upon them, handled many multitudes of these without a fight. After these
things then Constantine crowns his son Leo as emperor and immediately
campaigns against the Saracens. And having come to the city of the Melitenians
he took this by siege, carrying away from there a great people captive and
very much spoil. And in this Maria the Augusta his wife also was dying. And
what was happening after a little, worthy of memory, it is just not to pass
by. For it happened then that a fearful spectacle and a strange wonder came
about from an aerial coincidence, which beginning about lamp-lighting was
appearing through the whole night, producing amazement and great fear in all
those seeing it. For it seemed to them that all the stars, being moved aside
from the heavenly place assigned to them, were being borne toward the earth.
And those having become near the earth were suddenly being dissolved, having
in no way ever made any harm whatsoever. And many say that through the whole
inhabited world such an extraordinary spectacle was being shown. And the
things of the stars were seen in these; and when some time had elapsed
Anastasios the hierarch of Byzantium was dying. And Constantine, having looked
once for all toward the outrage of the church and already fighting off against
piety, as being moved by the opposing spirit that was leading him, gathers a
synod of priests being eight and thirty and three hundred in number, (of this
Theodosios the 66 archbishop of the city of the Ephesians was leading) and
proclaims as hierarch of the city a certain Constantine who had put on the
habit of the monks, and who had been bishop of the city of Syllaion. And they
set forth a definition of faith, in which they all signed, having agreed badly
and impiously, having proclaimed the destruction of the sacred images; and
like infants they were anathematizing these things in the marketplace.
