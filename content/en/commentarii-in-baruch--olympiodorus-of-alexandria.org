* Commentaries on Baruch

=93.761= Chapter 1. "Son of Chelkias." Perhaps he mentions Baruch's ancestors
as being noteworthy. "In the time." He did not say in the year, but in the
time, so that he might say, in those years; for the city was not taken in the
fifth, nor in the ninth year of the reign of Sedekias, but in the eleventh.
"In the ears of Jechonias." Nabuchodonosor had previously deported Jechonias
to Babylon. And this same Jechonias is son of Joakim, and he himself was also
called Eliakim, being double-named. "And in the ears of all." i.e. of those
who came together to the hearing. "And offer upon the altar." Even if the
temple had been razed, still they sacrificed in the place; for it was not
permitted to perform the sacrifices elsewhere, except in Jerusalem. "And pray
concerning the life of Nabuchodonosor." God had commanded them to pray
concerning the peace of the Babylonians, so that they themselves also might
have peace. "That their days may be." That they may be long-lived, or, that
they may live according to piety. "In days of a feast." For they also had
certain seasons of differences of feasts. "And you shall say to the Lord our
God." According to the word of the =93.764= just, both God has punished, and
we are in shame today, being in captivity justly. "And we devised." We made
excuses, inventing causes. Chapter 2. "And you made a name for yourself."
According to your continual wonders; for God does not cease working wonders
for the prophet and all the saints, but is always wonderful among them. If he
does not do signs, he is not marveled at. "But the soul." Those who carry
great burdens, being weak, stoop. A soul then, he says, that is afflicted
because of sins, and has greatness of grief, stooping and being weak, is
confessed. "And the eyes that fail." Either perceptibly because of the
calamities, or also being dimmed from intelligible contemplation. "That the
bones of our kings be brought out." As a token of the coming judgment, after
death their bones were punished. Chapter 3. "For you sit unto the age." You,
he says, are eternal and sovereign, but we are mortal and perishable. "Lord
Almighty, hear now the prayer of the dead." Of the dead, he says, of those who
have come under the sentence because of sin. The word has clearly shown the
immortality of the soul. "But remember." Use, he says, the customary
wonder-working, and as you led out from Egypt, so free from Babylon. Because
we are named your people. "Behold we today in our exile." We have judged
ourselves, he says, and have blamed ourselves, that it came upon us worthily
because of the works of our fathers. "Hear, O Israel." From here Baruch begins
to prophesy; for all the preceding words belong to the letter of the captives
who sent from Babylon, being put forward in the form of confession. "You have
grown old." Commandment of life is prudence. "You were counted." To those who
have ignorance of God. Of those still held by death. "You forsook." Our Lord
Jesus Christ. You became, he says, the procurer for yourself of the present
evils. For when you ought to have used as a guide the law, which the fountain
of wisdom has brought forth, you followed vain reasonings. "The way of God."
The Lord says: I am the way. Therefore he who walks in imitation of him walks
in his way. "Learn where prudence is." Since you have clung to the letter,
learn that prudence is in the Holy Spirit. "Where is strength." Now at least,
since not =93.765= before, seek and you will know who is the supplier of these
goods. "Where is understanding." That against sin. "Where is long life and
life." To distinguish the good from the base. "Where is light." In the Lord,
clearly. For he who believes in me, he says, shall not die unto the age. For
the Lord says: I am the light of the world, and the Apostle says: He himself
is our peace. "Who has found her place?" Because the judgments of God are
unsearchable, and his ways inscrutable. "And who has entered into her
treasures?" Who has grasped the judgments of God? "Where are the rulers of the
nations?" The wise of this age, who also ruled over beast-like men. "Sporting
with the birds of heaven." Auguring by birds. And since their wisdom is
earthly, soulish, demonic. "And the silver." For all their wisdom is in greed.
"Because those who work the silver." Who harvest the silver through various
persons. "And there is no finding of their works." Because they partake of
darkness. "And others arose." Those to whom the law was entrusted, and they
themselves did not keep it, these are those who arose in place of the wise in
idolatry. "Younger ones saw light." Those not dragged down by fleshly deeds
saw light, that is, the knowledge of God. "And they dwelt upon the earth."
Upon the teachings of the legislation. "The way of knowledge they did not
know." As the Lord says, If you believed Moses, you would believe me. "Nor did
they understand." The varied teaching. "Nor did they take hold of her." As the
evangelist John says: He came to his own, and his own did not receive him. And
the sons, clearly of the lawgivers. "Far from their way they became." For
pursuing a law of righteousness, they did not attain to a law. "Nor was it
heard." They who followed only the letter of the law were judged equal to
these Gentiles who have not heard the law. For seeing they do not see, and
hearing they do not hear. "Nor sons of Hagar." The offspring of the old
covenant, concerning whom the Apostle says in agreement: =93.768= For being
ignorant of the righteousness of God, and seeking to establish their own, they
did not submit to the righteousness of God. "The merchants of Merran." He
calls them merchants because they have no experience of farming. For having
been appointed vine-dressers, instead of grapes they harvested thorns. And
Merran and Thaiman are barbarian tribes, among whom they trade, which are the
beastly manners, those dragged down together with the vanity of this life.
"And the myth-tellers." Who instead of commandment and judgments of God
mythologized washings of cups and pots. And further, thrusting aside judgment
and mercy and faith, they tithed rue and mint. "And the seekers." Those who
seek, but do not know how to make the search with understanding. "But the way
of wisdom they did not know." The Lord, clearly. "Nor did they remember her
paths." The manners of the teaching. "O Israel, how great is the house of
God." The Church, and the word of the mystery. Since now we know in part, and
not yet the perfection of knowledge. And he calls piety a place; and lengthy
because of the always increasing toward the better. "The place of the
possession." Because it contains mysteries of a heavenly king. "There the
giants were born." He says concerning the demons, that having been ranked from
the beginning with the angels, from folly they have been cast out of the
heavenly vaults. And renowned, because it was announced beforehand concerning
them; and of great size, as monstrous. "Knowing war." For being unworthy of
the peace of Christ, both the demons and the heretics practiced war. "Who has
gone up into heaven?" As it is written: Not that we loved God, but that he
loved us, and having loved he bowed the heavens and came down. "Who has
crossed." The Lord saying, Take courage, I have overcome the world. For he
alone crossed over sin. And the phrase, He found her, i.e. He showed us how to
find her. "And he will bring her." Because wisdom is beyond price. And toward
the sense, chosen gold is the faith, through which we know the Son. "There is
none who knows her way." For if anyone thinks he has known something, he has
not yet known as he ought to know. "Her path." The teachings, the prophecies.
"But he who knows all things." For the Father alone knows the Son. "He found
her out." And he manifested him also to us through the wise mystery of the
incarnation. "He who prepared." Toward the letter, that through =93.769= the
incarnate Word all things were prepared. And toward the sense, earth is the
knowledge of piety. "He filled." And the Lord called good and evil to the
wedding. And he says cattle, those previously compared to unintelligent
cattle. "And the stars." The apostles, to whom he said, You are the light of
the world. "He called them." Having shone in the gifts given to them they
rejoiced. "This is God." Those who readily obeyed the apostolic calling, and
have a desire to depart and be with Christ, and prefer nothing to doing what
is commanded. He who became man for us, being the Word of the Father, and was
seen upon earth, and consorted with men. "And he gave her." Because every
spiritual knowledge that leads toward heaven is worked of him [perhaps: by
him]. "After this she was seen upon the earth." For also in order the one and
the same mystery shone, in the manner of the law, of the prophets, of the
gospel. Chapter 4. "This is the book." The varied knowledge of the mystery
through the Gospels, which also lasts unto the ages. "Pass through." For he
himself calls saying: Come to me all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will
give you rest. "Blessed are we." He urges those from Israel to snatch the
grace beforehand. Since it was necessary for them also first to have the word
of God announced. The spiritual Israel says these things; to whom the Savior
says: But blessed are your eyes, because they see, and your ears, because they
hear. "Take courage, my people." O Israel remembered by me. "You were sold."
For if because of sin the devil held the handwriting against us, yet Christ
redeemed us with his own blood. "But you forgot." Because having been fed by
divine word as rational, we have fallen into irrationality. "Hear, you
sojourners of Zion." She calls the sojourning women to the spectacle of the
mourning, that by the example she may make them more sober. As toward the
subject cities the word. "Let no one rejoice over me the widow." And she calls
her a widow, as deserted of the divine care, the one who alone is at rest
[having been made desolate], the one now alone, clearly; or, if not this, as
having alone possessed the divine temple; or as alone left unconquered by the
Assyrians [perhaps: childless]. For even if Chastening, the Lord chastened me,
yet he did not give me over to death. And the Synagogue was properly called
widow then, when having insulted her own bridegroom Christ she was abandoned.
=93.772= "Let the sojourners of Zion come." The vain opinions of the
sojourning of this age. "For he brought a nation upon them." God removed our
lawlessness from us, but we drew them upon ourselves, and through them we are
punished. And he is said to bring it on, because without God's permission
nothing happens. "A shameless nation." First the Babylonian, later also that
of the Romans. And this can also be understood concerning those who teach the
falsely named knowledge through sophistic deceit. "But I, how am I able to
help you." That without the compassions of God it is impossible for a soul to
be saved. "He will deliver you." He urges them toward the training through the
scourge. "But I was left behind." By absence of God. And if the saints say
these things, they say them appropriating the things of the sinners; but if
the Synagogue of the Jews, when because of the insult against Christ she
heard: Behold your house is left to you desolate. And if the city, because of
the desolation of the inhabitants. "I put off the robe of peace." The
brightness which she had while at peace with God. "Sackcloth of my
supplication." Mourning over our sins, and beseeching God. "And it came to
me." She foresees the redemption through the Savior Christ, and she foretells
this. "Which will come upon." For the redemption through Christ showed our
race more splendid than according to the original fashioning. "And you will
see his destruction." Which came to pass for the devil through the precious
cross. "And upon necks." For behold he says: I have given you authority to
tread upon serpents and scorpions, and upon all the power of the enemy. "My
delicate ones." Those who according to the original creation became delicate
and well-formed, walked the uneven and rough ways. "Take courage, Jerusalem."
The God Word, who became incarnate for us, who deigned to call the Church his
bride. "Wretched are those who afflicted you." Demons, heretics, those who at
times persecuted the Church. "Wretched are the cities." The strongholds of
evils, the constructions of the demons. "Thus she will be grieved." Idolatry
and the Synagogue of the Jews. "And I will take away from her." Of the
Synagogue that persecuted the Church, and of the varied assault of evils. "For
fire will come upon." i.e. until he turned back; wherefore he also said the
greater time. =93.773= "Look around." Toward the sun of righteousness, our
Lord Jesus Christ. Chapter 5. "Put off, Jerusalem." The old man with his
desires. "And put on." As many as were baptized into Christ have put on
Christ. "Wrap yourself." These are the teachings of the old and new covenant.
Otherwise: the practical and contemplative virtue. "Set on the mitre." The
cross of Christ, which is marked on the forehead of the faithful. Or also the
Lord himself; for the head of the Church is Christ. "For your name shall be
called." The marks of the new Jerusalem. And he added of righteousness. For
since there is a peace of sinners according to David, wherefore also the
Savior: Peace, he says, my peace I give to you. As the assault of the enemies
is no longer able to tear apart the peace of righteousness, nor to dissolve
the glory of piety. Otherwise: Since our peace is Christ, and he himself is
our righteousness and the glory, and he himself is the pattern of our way of
life according to piety, from him we also are named. "Arise, Jerusalem." Upon
a high way of life, upon the evangelical preaching. "For they went out." The
form is mournful, as David makes clear, fleeing Absalom. And those serving
demons, and being led by their various desires, were doing things worthy of
mourning. "As sons of the kingdom." Being taken up by holy angels, and being
escorted through faith in Christ into the kingdom of the heavens. "He
commanded ... every high mountain to be made low." Every height raising itself
against the knowledge of God. "And everlasting dunes." The demons who from the
beginning from of old have been warring against us. "And valleys to be
filled." The cuttings of the false christs to be brought into concord.
Otherwise: the souls formerly hollow and earthly, to be filled through good
works, the scandals having been taken away.
