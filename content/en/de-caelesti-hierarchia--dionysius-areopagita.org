TO TIMOTHY THE FELLOW-PRESBYTER, DIONYSIUS THE PRESBYTER
ON THE CELESTIAL HIERARCHY

<I.>

<1> "Every good giving and every perfect gift is from above, coming down from
the Father of lights." But also every procession of father-moved
light-manifestation, coming to us as a gift of the good, again as a unifying
power unfolds us upward and turns us back toward the unity of the gathering
Father and the deifying simplicity. For "from him are all things and to him,"
as the sacred word said.

<2> Therefore, having invoked Jesus, the paternal light, the one that is "the
true, which lights every man coming into the world," through whom we have had
the approach to the Father who is source of light, let us lift ourselves, as
far as attainable, toward the father-traditioned illuminations of the most
sacred oracles, and let us contemplate, as we are able, the hierarchies of the
heavenly minds manifested to us by them symbolically and anagogically; and
having received with immaterial and unwavering eyes of mind the originating
and superprincipal light-giving of the thearchic Father, which manifests to us
the most blessed hierarchies of the angels in typical symbols, let us be
stretched up from it again to its simple ray. For not even it ever leaves its
own unified unity, but being multiplied and going forth in a manner befitting
the good toward an anagogic and unifying blending of those provided for, it
remains within itself firmly, fixed lastingly in unmoved identity, and
analogously stretches up those who lift themselves toward it as is lawful, and
unifies them according to its simplifying union. For it is not possible
otherwise for the thearchic ray to shine upon us, unless it is anagogically
veiled by the variety of the sacred curtains and arranged connaturally and
fittingly to us by paternal providence.

<3> Wherefore also the initiating sacred-institution, having deemed our most
holy hierarchy worthy of a supermundane imitation of the heavenly hierarchies,
and having diversified the said immaterial hierarchies with material figures
and formative compositions, has handed them down, in order that we may be led
up analogously to ourselves from the most sacred shapings to the simple and
untypical leadings-up and assimilations, since it is not possible for our mind
to be stretched up to that immaterial imitation and contemplation of the
heavenly hierarchies, unless it use the material guidance according to itself,
reckoning the appearing beauties as copies of the unseen comeliness, and the
sensible fragrances as impressions of the intelligible distribution, and the
material lights as an image of the immaterial light-giving, and the discursive
sacred instructions as of the contemplative filling according to mind, and the
orders of the arrangements here as of the harmonious and ordered habit toward
the divine things, and the partaking of the most divine Eucharist as of the
participation of Jesus, and whatever else has been handed down supermundanely
to the heavenly beings, but to us symbolically. For the sake then of this
analogous deification of ours, the philanthropic origin of initiation, both
revealing the heavenly hierarchies to us and making our hierarchy a
fellow-liturgist of them by our assimilation, according to our power, of their
godlike consecration, has described the superheavenly


minds in sensible images in the sacred-graphic compositions of the oracles, in
order that it might lead us up through the sensibles to the intelligibles and
from the sacredly shaped symbols to the simple summits of the heavenly
hierarchies.

<II.>

<1> It is necessary therefore, as I think, first to set out what we hold the
aim of every hierarchy to be, and what each of them benefits its own devotees;
next to hymn the heavenly hierarchies according to their manifestation in the
oracles; and following upon these to say with what sacred formations the
sacred writings of the oracles shape the heavenly orders, and toward what
simplicity one must be led up through the shapings, in order that we too may
not, like the many, unhallowedly suppose the heavenly and godlike minds to be
some many-footed and many-faced beings, and stamped toward the cattle-form of
oxen or toward the beast-shape of lions, and molded toward the hooked-beak
form of eagles or toward the hairy wing-growth of birds, and that we may not
imagine certain fiery wheels above the heaven, and material thrones
serviceable for the thearchy to recline upon, and certain many-colored horses
and spear-bearing commander-chiefs, and whatever else has been handed down to
us by the oracles in a sacredly shaped way in a variety of manifesting
symbols. For theology has used poetic sacred-shapings quite plainly upon the
unfigured minds, having considered our mind, as has been said, and having
provided for the leading-up that is proper and connatural to it, and having
recast toward it the anagogic sacred writings.

<2> And if it seems to anyone to accept the sacred compositions, as the simple
things in themselves are unknown and uncontemplated by us, but he thinks the
image-drawings of the holy minds in the oracles to be incongruous, and, so to
speak, this whole abrupt scenery of the angelic names, and says that the
theologians, having come at all to an embodiment of the bodiless, ought to
recast and manifest them with figures proper to them and as far as possible
akin, from the things most honored among us and in some measure immaterial and
superior beings, and not to put around the heavenly and godlike simplicities
the last many-formednesses upon earth (for the one would have been more
anagogic for us and would not have dragged the supermundane manifestations
down into the incongruous unlikenesses, while the other is able even to
outrage the divine powers unlawfully and perhaps to lead our mind astray,
seating it in the unhallowed compositions, and perhaps it will suppose the
superheavenly things to be filled with certain leonine and equine swarms and a
bellowing hymnology and a bird-flock leadership and other animals and more
dishonored materials, as many as the likenesses, unlike in every way, of the
supposedly manifesting oracles describe, having declined toward the absurd and
the bastard and the passionate); but the search of the truth, as I think,
demonstrates that the most sacred wisdom of the oracles, in the formations of
the heavenly minds, has provided altogether for both, so as neither to outrage
the divine powers, as one might say, nor yet to have us passionately stuck in
the lowly lownesses of the images. For that the types of the untypical, and
the figures of the unfigured, have reasonably been set in front, one would say
is not only because of our analogy, which is unable to stretch itself up
immediately to the intelligible contemplations and needs proper and connatural


anagogies, which hold out to us the attainable formations of the unformed and
supernatural spectacles, but also because this is most fitting to the mystical
oracles, that the sacred and hidden truth of the supermundane minds be
concealed through unutterable and sacred riddles and be made inaccessible to
the many. For not everyone is sacred, nor is the knowledge of all, as the
oracles say.

And if someone should blame the incongruous image-drawings, saying he is
ashamed to assign forms so ugly to the godlike and most holy orders, it is
enough to say to him that the manner of the sacred manifestation is twofold:
<3> the one, as is likely, proceeding through similar sacred-typical images,
the other being molded through unlike form-makings into what is altogether
unlike and incongruous. Indeed the mystic traditions of the manifesting
oracles hymn the reverend blessedness of the superessential thearchy sometimes
as Word and Mind and Essence, making clear its God-befitting reasonableness
and wisdom and truly existing subsistence and the true cause of the
subsistence of the beings, and they recast it as light and call it life, such
sacred recastings being more august and seeming somehow to lie above the
formations that are near matter, yet even so falling short of the thearchic
likeness toward truth (for it is above every essence and life, no light
characterizing it, and every word and mind being incomparably left behind its
likeness); and sometimes it is hymned supermundanely by the same oracles in
the negative manifestations, they calling it invisible and infinite and
uncontained and the <rest>, from which is signified not what it is, but what
it is not. For this, as I think, is also more proper concerning it, since, as
the hidden and priestly tradition has guided, we speak truly that it is not
according to any of the beings, but we are ignorant of its superessential and
unthinkable and unspeakable indefiniteness. If then the negations upon the
divine things are true, and the affirmations are unfitted to the hiddenness of
the unutterable things, the manifestation through unlike recastings is more
proper upon the invisible things.

The sacred writings of the oracles therefore honor, they do not fill with
shame, the heavenly orders, manifesting them by unlike form-makings and
through these showing them to have gone out supermundanely beyond all material
things. And that the incongruous of the likenesses lead our mind up more, I do
not think any of those of sound mind will contradict. For into the more
honored sacred-shapings it is likely even to be led astray, thinking the
heavenly essences to be certain gold-formed and light-formed men and flashing,
comely, clothed in bright garment and harmlessly gleaming with the fiery, and
with as many other similarly typed beauties as theology has figured the
heavenly minds. Which, in order that those who have conceived nothing higher
than the appearing beautiful things may not suffer, the uplifting wisdom of
the holy theologians is sacredly brought down even to the incongruous
unlikenesses, not permitting our near-matter part to remain at rest in the
ugly images, but raising up the upward-borne of the soul and pricking it by
the misshapenness of the tokens, so that it may not seem lawful or true, even
to those very near matter, that the superheavenly and divine spectacles are in
truth like to things so ugly. Besides, this too must be thought, that not even
one of the beings


is altogether deprived of the participation of the beautiful, if as the truth
of the oracles says, "All things are very beautiful."

<4> It is possible then from all of them to conceive beautiful contemplations
and to recast for the intelligible and intellectual things, from the material,
the so-called unlike likenesses, the intellectual things having in another
manner what has been assigned otherwise to the sensibles. For anger arises in
the irrational from a passionate impulse, and their spirited movement is full
of all irrationality; but upon the intellectual the spirited must be thought
in another manner, making clear, as I think, their manly reasonableness and
the implacable habit in the godlike and unchangeable establishments. Likewise
we say desire upon the irrational to be a certain unconsidered and near-matter
attachment arising intemperately from innate movement or custom in the
alterable things, and the irrational dominance of bodily appetite pushing the
whole animal toward the desirable according to sense; but when, putting unlike
likenesses around the intellectual, we mold desire around them, one must think
it a divine love of the immateriality above word and mind, and the unswerving
and unyielding longing of the superessentially pure and passionless
contemplation and of the eternal truly and intelligible communion with that
pure and highest transparency and the unseen and beauty-making comeliness. And
the intemperate we would take as of the intense and unturned, and of being
able to be cut short by nothing, because of the unmixed and unalterable love
of the divine beauty and the total inclination toward the truly desirable. But
also irrationality itself and insensibility we fittingly call, upon the
irrational animals or the soulless materials, a deprivation of reason and
sense, but upon the immaterial and intellectual essences we confess in a holy
manner their superiority, as supermundane, to our transitive and bodily reason
and to the material sense foreign to the bodiless minds. It is possible
therefore to recast forms not unfitting to the heavenly even from the most
dishonored parts of matter, since even it, having had its subsistence from the
truly beautiful, has throughout all its material arrangement certain echoes of
the intellectual comeliness, and it is possible through them to be led up to
the immaterial archetypes, the likenesses being taken unlike, as has been
said, and the same things being defined not in the same way, but harmoniously
and properly, upon the intellectual and the sensible properties.

<5> These things we shall find the mystic theologians sacredly molding not
only around the manifestations of the heavenly orders, but sometimes even
around the thearchic manifestations themselves. And sometimes they hymn it
from the appearing honored things as sun of righteousness, as the morning star
rising sacredly into mind, and as light shining unveilably and intelligibly;
sometimes from the middle things as fire lighting harmlessly, as water
furnishing a filling of vital fullness and, to speak symbolically, entering
into a belly and bubbling up rivers flowing unrestrainedly; sometimes from the
last things as fragrant ointment, as a cornerstone. But they also put
beast-shape around it and attach to it the property of lion and panther, and
they say it will be a leopard and a bear deprived. And I will add also what
seems more dishonored than all and more incongruous, that those skilled in the
divine things have handed down even a form of worm as it molds around itself.
Thus all the theosophists and interpreters of the hidden inspiration
unstainedly set


apart the "Holy of Holies" from the incomplete and the unhallowed, and they
prefer the unlike sacred-shaping, in order that the divine things may not be
easy to the profane, nor those who love to behold the divine images remain in
the types as true, and in order that the divine things may be honored by the
true negations and by the otherwise assimilations toward the last of their own
echoes. Nothing then is out of place if they recast even the heavenly essences
from the incongruous unlike likenesses, according to the said causes. For
perhaps we would not even have come into search from perplexity, and into
leading-up through the accurate inquiry of the sacred things, if the
ill-formedness of the manifesting recasting of the angels had not disturbed
us, not permitting our mind to remain in the unfitting form-makings, but
stirring it to refuse the material attachments and accustoming it to stretch
itself up sacredly through the appearing things toward the supermundane
leadings-up.

Let this much be said by us because of the material and incongruous
angel-formed image-drawings of the sacred oracles; and next it is necessary to
define what we hold hierarchy itself to be, and what those allotted to
hierarchy are benefited by hierarchy itself. And let Christ lead the word, if
it is lawful for me to say, my Christ, the inspiration of every hierarchical
manifestation. But you, O child, according to the holy legislation of our
hierarchical tradition, yourself hear in a sacred manner the things said
sacredly, becoming inspired among the inspired in initiation, and having
wrapped the holy things in the hiddenness according to mind, guard them as
uniform from the unhallowed multitude. For it is not lawful, as the oracles
say, to cast to swine the unmixed and light-formed and beauty-making
good-order of the intelligible pearls.

<III.>

<1> Hierarchy is, according to me, a sacred order and science and activity,
assimilated as far as attainable to the godlike, and raised analogously toward
the God-imitating in respect of the illuminations given over to it from God;
and the God-befitting beauty, as simple, as good, as originating initiation,
is unmixed entirely of every unlikeness, and is transmissive according to
worth to each of its own light, and perfecting in a most divine initiation
according to the unvarying formation of those initiated, harmoniously toward
itself.

<2> The aim then of hierarchy is the assimilation to God as far as attainable
and union, having him as leader of every sacred science and activity, and
looking unswervingly toward his most divine comeliness, and being stamped as
far as possible, and making its own devotees divine images, mirrors most
transparent and unstained, receptive of the source-light and thearchic ray,
and sacredly filled with the given-over radiance, and again flashing this
ungrudgingly to those next, according to the thearchic ordinances. For it is
not lawful for those of the sacred initiations, or for those being sacredly
initiated, to work anything at all beside the sacred arrangements of their own
origin of initiation, nor even to exist otherwise, if they long for its
deifying splendor and look toward it in a sacred manner and are stamped
according to the analogy of each of the sacred minds. Therefore he who says
hierarchy makes clear some sacred arrangement entirely, an image of the
thearchic beauty, celebrating in hierarchical orders and sciences the
mysteries of its own illumination and being assimilated as is lawful to its
own principle; for to each of those allotted to hierarchy the perfection is to
be led up according to its own


analogy toward the God-imitating, and, what is more divine than all, as the
oracles say, to become a "fellow-worker of God" and to show the divine
activity appearing in himself according to what is possible. As, since the
order of hierarchy is that some be purified, and others purify, and some be
illuminated, and others illuminate, and some be perfected, and others perfect,
the God-imitating will fit each in this manner: the divine blessedness, to
speak as among men, is unmixed of all unlikeness, and full of everlasting
light, perfect and in need of no perfection, purifying and illuminating and
perfecting, or rather itself purification and illumination and perfection,
above purification, above light, preperfect, self-origin of initiation, and
cause of every hierarchy, and by superiority excepted from every sacred thing.

<3> It is necessary therefore, as I think, that those being purified be made
unmixed entirely and be freed from all unlike commingling; that those being
illuminated be filled with the divine light, being led up toward a
contemplative habit and power in all-pure eyes of mind; that those being
perfected, being transposed from the incomplete, become partakers of the
perfecting science of the sacred things contemplated; that those who purify,
by surplus of purification, give over to others of their own purity; that
those who illuminate, as more transparent minds and properly having toward
participation of light and giving-over, and being blessedly filled with the
sacred radiance, channel the light overflowing according to all of them into
those worthy of light; that those who perfect, as scientific of the perfecting
giving-over, perfect those being perfected by the all-sacred initiation of the
science of the sacred things contemplated. Therefore each order of the
hierarchical arrangement is led up according to its own analogy toward the
divine fellow-working, accomplishing by grace and God-given power those things
that are in the thearchy naturally and supernaturally, and that are done by it
superessentially, and that are hierarchically manifested toward the attainable
imitation of the God-loving minds.

<IV.>

<1> The hierarchy itself then having been well defined by us, as I think,
whatever it is, the angelic hierarchy must next be hymned, and its sacred
form-makings in the oracles must be contemplated with supermundane eyes, in
order that we may be led up to their most godlike simplicity through the
mystic recastings, and that we may hymn the principle of every hierarchical
science in God-befitting reverence and initiating thanksgivings.

First of all it is true to say this, that the superessential thearchy, having
substantiated all the essences of the beings by goodness, has brought them
forward to being. For this is proper to the cause of all and the goodness
above all, to call the beings toward communion of itself, as has been defined
for each of the beings according to its own analogy. All the beings then
partake of providence welling out from the superessential and all-causing
deity; for they would not be, if they had not partaken of the essence and
principle of the beings. All the lifeless then partake of it by being (for the
being of all is the deity above being), and the living of its life-making
power above every life, and the rational and intellectual of its self-perfect
and preperfect wisdom above every both word and mind. And it is clear that
those of the essences are about it who have partaken of it in many ways.

<2> The holy orders of the heavenly essences then have come to be in
participation of the thearchic giving-over above those that merely


are and those that live without reason and our rational ones. For, stamping
themselves intelligibly toward the God-imitating, and looking supermundanely
toward the thearchic likeness, and longing to form their intellectual form,
they reasonably have the more ungrudging communions toward it, being near and
always stretching themselves up toward the upward as is lawful in intensity of
the divine and unswerving love, and receiving the principal illuminations
immaterially and unmixedly, and being ordered toward them, and having all
their life intellectual. These then are they who first and in many ways come
to be in participation of the divine, and first and in many ways are
manifesting of the thearchic hiddenness; wherefore also they have been deemed
worthy, beyond all, of the angelic name by distinction, because the thearchic
illumination comes to be in them first, and through them the manifestations
above us are ferried across to us.

Thus then the law, as theology says, was given to us through angels, and
angels led up our renowned fathers before the law and after the law toward the
divine, either introducing what must be done and transferring them from error
and unhallowed life to the straight way of truth, or revealing sacred orders
or hidden visions of supermundane mysteries or certain divine
pre-announcements in an interpretive way.

<3> And if someone should say that theophanies have come to be for some of the
holy even from the thing itself immediately, let him learn this too clearly
from the most sacred oracles, that the hidden of God itself, whatever it is,
"no one has seen" nor will see, but theophanies have come to be for the holy
according to manifestations of visions befitting God through certain sacred
things and analogous to those seeing. And all-wise theology reasonably calls
that vision, which revealed in itself, drawn, the divine likeness as in a
formation of the unformed, a theophany, from the leading-up of those seeing
toward the divine, as through it a divine illumination comes to be for those
seeing and they are sacredly initiated in something of the divine things
themselves. And these divine visions our renowned fathers were initiated in
through the middles of the heavenly powers. Or does not the tradition of the
oracles say that the sacred legislation of the law was given from the thing
itself from God to Moses, in order that it might truly initiate us that it is
a typing of things divine and sacred, yet theology also teaches this clearly,
that it came forward to us through angels, as the God-ruling order legislates
that, that the second things be led up toward the divine through the first?
For not only upon the overlying and the underlying minds, but even among those
of the same rank this ordinance has been defined by the superessential marshal
of all, that in each hierarchy there are first and middle and last orders and
powers, and that the more divine are initiators of the lesser and guides
toward the divine approach and illumination and communion.

<4> And I see that even the divine mystery of the philanthropy of Jesus angels
were first initiated in, then through them the grace of the knowledge passed
over to us. Thus then the most divine Gabriel was initiating Zechariah the
hierarch that the child to be born from him beyond hope by divine grace would
be a prophet of the manly theurgy of Jesus about to appear to the world in a
manner befitting the good and savingly; and Mary, how in her would come to be
the thearchic mystery of the unutterable God-forming. And another of the
angels was teaching Joseph how truly


have been fulfilled the things divinely promised to the forefather David, and
another was announcing good news to the shepherds, as purified by withdrawal
from the many and by quiet, and with him a "multitude of heavenly host" was
handing over to those upon earth that much-hymned doxology. And I will lift
myself also toward the highest light-manifestations of the oracles. For I see
that even Jesus himself, the superessential cause of the superheavenly
essences, having come toward what is according to us unchangeably, does not
leap away from the human-befitting good-order appointed and chosen by him, but
is submitted obediently to the arrangements of the Father and God through
angels, and through their middles is announced to Joseph the withdrawal of the
Son to Egypt administered by the Father, and again the leading-across from
Egypt toward Judea, and through angels we see him being ordered under the
paternal legislations. For I forbear to say, as to one who knows the things
manifested in our priestly traditions, both concerning the angel who
strengthened Jesus, and that even Jesus himself, having come into a
manifesting order according to the saving good-working for us, has been
proclaimed "Angel of great counsel." For as he himself says in an
angel-befitting way, as many things as he heard from the Father he announced
to us.

<V.>

This then is, as according to us, the cause of the angelic name in the
oracles; but it is necessary, as I think, to search out through what cause the
theologians call all the heavenly essences together angels, but when they come
to the manifestation of their supermundane orders they name as an angelic
order properly the one that completively terminates the divine and heavenly
ranks; and before this they order overlying the archangelic orders, and the
principalities and the authorities and the powers, and as many essences as
have gone beyond these, which the manifesting traditions of the oracles know.
And we say that according to every sacred arrangement the orders that have
gone beyond have also the illuminations and powers of the underlying orders,
but the last are unpartaking of those overlying them. The most holy ranks then
of the highest essences the theologians also call angels. For they too are
manifesting of the thearchic illumination. But it has no reason to name the
last order of the heavenly minds principalities or thrones or Seraphim. For it
is not in participation of the highest powers, but just as this leads up our
inspired hierarchs toward the rays of the thearchy known to it, so also the
all-sacred powers of the essences before it are anagogic toward the divine of
the order that completes the angelic hierarchies, unless perhaps someone
should say even this, that all the angelic names are common according to the
underlying and preceding communion of all the heavenly powers toward the
godlike and the light-giving from God. But in order that the word may be more
accurately distinguished for us, let us look sacredly at the holy-befitting
properties of each heavenly order manifested in the oracles.

<VI.>

<1> How many and of what sort the orders of the superheavenly essences are,
and how the hierarchies according to them are accomplished, I say that their
deifying origin of initiation alone knows accurately, and besides that they
themselves have known


their own powers and illuminations and their own sacred and supermundane
good-order. For it is impossible for us to know the mysteries of the
superheavenly minds and their most holy perfections, unless perhaps someone
should say as many things as the thearchy has initiated us in through them, as
knowing well their own. Therefore we will say nothing self-moved; but as many
of the angelic spectacles as were contemplated by the sacred theologians,
these, having been initiated, we will set out as we are able.

<2> Theology has called all the heavenly essences by nine manifesting names;
these our divine initiator of sacred rites distinguishes into three triadic
orders. And he says the first is the one that is always about God and has been
handed down as united to him nearly and immediately before the others. For the
most holy thrones and the many-eyed and many-winged ranks named Cherubim in
the speech of Hebrews and Seraphim, he says the manifestation of the sacred
oracles hands down as established immediately about God according to the
nearness overlying all. This triadic order then, as one and of the same rank
and truly first hierarchy, our renowned leader said, than which there is no
other more godlike and more immediately near to the first-working
illuminations of the thearchy; and he says the second is the one completed by
the authorities and lordships and powers, and the third at the last of the
heavenly hierarchies the order of the angels and archangels and
principalities.

<VII.>

<1> Accepting this order of the holy hierarchies we say that every name of the
heavenly minds has a making-clear of the godlike property of each. And the
holy naming of the Seraphim those who know the things of the Hebrews say
manifests either "those who kindle" or "those who heat," and that of the
Cherubim a fullness of knowledge or a pouring of wisdom. Reasonably then the
first of the heavenly hierarchies is celebrated by the highest essences,
having the order higher than all by being established immediately about God,
and by the first-working theophanies and perfections being ferried across into
it as nearest more principally. They are named then "those who heat" and
thrones and pouring of wisdom, by a manifesting name of their godlike habits.
For their ever-movedness about the divine things and unceasingness, and the
hot and sharp and overboiling of the near and unyielding and unswerving
ever-movement, and the anagogically and actively assimilative of those below,
as boiling them up and rekindling them toward the like heat, and the
lightning-like and whole-burnt purifying, and the unveiled and unquenchable
always light-formed and illuminating property, being a driver-out and
annihilator of every unshining darkness-making, the name of the Seraphim
teaches manifestingly; and that of the Cherubim their cognitive and God-seeing
and receptive of the highest light-giving and contemplative in first-working
power of the thearchic comeliness and filled with the wisdom-making
giving-over and communicative ungrudgingly toward the second by the pouring of
the given wisdom; and that of the highest and lifted-up thrones the being
unmixedly excepted from all around-the-feet underlying, and the supermundanely
upward-borne toward the upward, and unswervingly settled away from every last
place, and about the truly Most High with whole powers unshakenly


and stably established, and receptive of the thearchic visitation in all
passionlessness and immateriality, and the God-bearing and being spread open
therapeutically toward the divine receptions.

<2> This then is the manifestation of their names as according to us; and it
must be said what we hold their hierarchy to be. For that the aim of every
hierarchy is unswervingly dependent on the God-imitating godlikeness, and that
every hierarchical business is divided into sacred participation and
giving-over of unmixed purification and divine light and perfecting science, I

worthily of the highest minds, how the hierarchy according to them is

the essence-making thearchy of them and ordered as in its forecourts, have
gone beyond every invisible and visible created power, one must think their
hierarchy proper and in every way of like form. One must hold them pure then
not as freed from unhallowed stains and pollutions, nor as unreceptive of
near-matter imaginings, but as unmixedly higher than every underlying and

purity with all the most godlike powers, and holding unswervingly to their own
ever-moved and same-moved order according to the God-loving unchangeable, and
not knowing at all the lessening toward the worse according to any, but always
having unfallen and unmoved the most unmixed establishment of their own
godlike property; and contemplative again not as contemplators of sensible
symbols or of intellectual things, nor as being led up to the divine by the
variety of the sacred-graphic contemplation, but as being filled with a light
higher than every immaterial knowledge and being filled as is lawful with the
superessential and triple-appearing contemplation of the beauty-making and
principal beauty, and having been deemed worthy of the communion of Jesus

knowledge of his theurgic lights, and moreover that the God-imitating has been
given to them in the highest way and they commune according to what is
attainable to them in first-working power with his theurgic and philanthropic
virtues; and perfected likewise not as being illuminated with an analytic
science of sacred variety, but as being filled with a first and superior
deification according to the highest science, as among angels, of the
theurgies. For not through other holy essences, but being hierarchized by the
thearchy itself, by being stretched up to it immediately with the power and
order superior to all, they are established toward the all-pure and in every
way unswerving, and they are brought toward the immaterial and intelligible
comeliness as is lawful into contemplation, and they are initiated in the
scientific reasons of the theurgies as being first and about God, being
hierarchized in the highest way by the origin of initiation itself.

<3> This then the theologians make clear, that the underlying orders of the
heavenly essences are well-orderedly taught the theurgic sciences by those
that have gone beyond, but those higher than all are illuminated as is lawful
with the initiations by the thearchy itself. For they introduce some of them
being sacredly initiated by the former that "the Lord of the heavenly powers"
and "King of glory" is he who was taken up into heavens in a human-befitting
way, and some questioning toward Jesus himself and seeking to learn the
science of his theurgy for us, and


these Jesus himself immediately initiating and first-givingly manifesting to

and judgment of salvation." And I admire that even of the superheavenly
essences the first, and so much overlying all, long for the thearchic
illuminations as middle-flying, reverently. For they do not ask from the thing
itself, "Why are your garments red?", but they are first in perplexity toward
themselves, showing that they seek to learn and long for the theurgic
knowledge, yet not leaping before the illumination given over according to
divine procession. Therefore the first hierarchy of the heavenly minds, being
hierarchized by the origin of initiation itself by being stretched up to it
immediately, being filled analogously to itself with the all-holiest
purification, of the unmeasured light, of the preperfect perfecting, is
purified and illuminated and perfected, being made unmixed of every
underlying, and full of first light, and partaker of first-given knowledge and
science. And summing up I would say this too not unfittingly, that the
partaking of the thearchic science is even purification and illumination and
perfection, purifying from ignorance, as it were, by the knowledge given over
according to order of the more perfect initiations, and illuminating by the
divine knowledge itself through which it also purifies her who had not
formerly contemplated as many things as are now manifested through the higher
illumination, and perfecting again by the light itself in the habitual science
of the most shining initiations.

<4> This then is, as according to my science, the first order of the heavenly
essences, which, "around God" and having stood immediately about God, simply
and unceasingly dances around his eternal knowledge according to the highest
ever-moved establishment as among angels, seeing many and blessed
contemplations purely, being illuminated with simple and immediate flashes,
and being filled with divine food, much by the first-given pouring, but one by
the unvariegated and unifying unity of the thearchic feast, and having been
deemed worthy of much communion of God and fellow-working by the assimilation
to him as far as attainable of the beautiful habits and activities, and
knowing many of the divine things in an overlying way and coming to be in
participation of thearchic science and knowledge as is lawful. Wherefore also
theology has handed down its hymns to those upon earth, in which the
superiority of its highest illumination is sacredly manifested. For some of
it, to speak sensibly, cry out "as a voice of waters," "Blessed is the glory
of the Lord from his place," and others shout that much-hymned and most
reverend theology, "Holy, holy, holy, Lord of Sabaoth, the whole earth is full
of his glory." And these highest hymnologies of the superheavenly minds we
have already unfolded as far as attainable in the things concerning the divine
hymns, and it has been said concerning these in those as sufficiently
according to us, from which it is enough to say this much for reminder
according to the present time, that the first order, having been illuminated
as is lawful with the theological science by the thearchic goodness, as a
good-formed hierarchy has given this over also to those next after it,
guiding, to say it by epitome, that the reverend and over-praised and
all-praised thearchy itself is lawfully to be known as far as attainable and
hymned by the God-receiving minds (for these, as godlike, are the divine
"places" of the thearchic "resting," as the oracles say), and moreover that it
is a monad and a triad-hypostatic henad, sending through from the
superheavenly essences as far as the last


of the earth its most good providence upon all the beings, as superprincipal
principle and cause of every essence, and having grasped all superessentially
with unrestrained holding-together.

<VIII.>

<1> We must now pass over to the middle order of the heavenly minds,
contemplating those lordships with supermundane eyes as far as attainable, and
the truly powerful spectacles of the divine authorities and powers. For the
name of each of the essences above us manifests their God-imitating properties
of the godlike. The manifesting naming of the holy lordships I think makes
clear a certain unenslaved leading-up, free of every around-the-feet
underlying, in no way at all declining toward any of the tyrannical
unlikenesses, itself in a free manner, as implacable lordship, overlying every
lessening slave-making, unyielding to every underlying and excepted from all
unlikeness, longing unceasingly for the true lordship and lord-rule, and
molding itself and those after it in a good-formed way toward its lordly
likeness as far as attainable, turned wholly toward none of the things that
seem at random, but toward that which is in the lordly sense, and coming to be
in participation of lord-ruling always godlikeness according to what is
possible to it; and that of the holy powers a certain manly and unshaken
courage into all the godlike activities according to it, not weakly failing
toward any reception of the thearchic illuminations given over to it, being
led up powerfully toward the God-imitating, not leaving its godlike movement
by unmanliness, but looking unswervingly toward the superessential and
power-making power, and becoming a power-formed image of this as far as
attainable, and turned powerfully toward it as source-powerful, and going
forth toward the second in a power-giving and godlike way; and that of the
holy authorities, the same-rank of the divine lordships and powers, the
well-ordered and uncommingled good-order about the divine receptions, and the
ordered of the supermundane and intellectual authoritativeness, not using the
authoritative powers tyrannically toward the worse, but being led up
unrestrainedly toward the divine things with good-order and leading up those
after it in a good-formed way, and being assimilated as is lawful to the
authority-making authority-rule, and flashing this as possible to angels in
the well-ordered ranks according to it of the authoritative power. Having
these godlike properties, the middle order of the heavenly minds is purified
and illuminated and perfected in the manner that has been said by the
thearchic illuminations given over to it secondarily through the first
hierarchical order and being ferried across second-appearingly through the
middle of that.

<2> Indeed we will make the hearing said to go to another angel through
another a symbol of the completion accomplished from afar and, because of the
procession, dimmed into a secondariness. For as those skilled concerning our
sacred initiations say that the self-appearing fillings of the divine things
are more perfect than the God-seeing participations through others, so I think
also among the angelic orders the immediate participation of those first
stretched up toward God is more distinct than those completed through a
middle. Wherefore also by our priestly tradition the first minds are named
perfecting and light-working and purifying powers of the underlying, as
through


them they are led up toward the superessential principle of all and come to be
in participation of the initiating purifications and illuminations and
perfections as is lawful to them. For this has been entirely legislated in a
God-befitting way by the divine marshal, that the second partake of the
thearchic illuminations through the first. And you will find this manifested
also in many places by the theologians. For when the divine and paternal
philanthropy, having trained Israel convertively for the sake of its sacred
salvation and having given it over to punishing and untamed nations for
correction, by the manifold leading-across of those provided for toward the
better both released it from the captivity and was leading it back reasonably
toward the former well-being, one of the theologians, Zechariah, sees one of
the first, as I think, and of the angels about God (for common, as I said, is
the angelic name to all), having learned from God himself the "comforting
words" concerning this, as has been said, and another of the underlying angels
going forward toward a meeting of the first as toward reception and partaking
of illumination, then being initiated by him in the divine counsel as by a
hierarch, and being entrusted to initiate the theologian that "Jerusalem shall
be inhabited fruitfully from a multitude of men." And another of the
theologians, Ezekiel, says this too has been all-sacredly legislated by the
over-glorious deity itself that has mounted upon the Cherubim. For, leading
Israel, as has been said, by paternal philanthropy through training toward the
better, it judged in God-befitting justice that the unresponsible be separated
from the responsible; this is first initiated, after the Cherubim, by the one
girt about the loins with sapphire, who had put on the full-length garment
according to a hierarchical symbol, and the remaining angels, who had the
axes, the divine marshal commands to be initiated by the former in the
God-judgment concerning this. For to the one he said to go through the middle
of Jerusalem and to give a sign upon the foreheads of the unresponsible men,
and to the others, "Go into the city behind him and strike and spare not with
your eyes," "but upon all upon whom is the sign do not draw near." What would
one say concerning the angel who said to Daniel, "The word has gone out," or
concerning the first himself who had taken up the fire from the midst of the
Cherubim, or, more than this for a showing of angelic good-order, that the
Cherubim cast the fire into the hands of the one who had put on the holy robe,
or concerning the one who called the most divine Gabriel and said to him,
"Make that one understand the vision," or as many other things as have been
said by the sacred theologians concerning the godlike good-order of the
heavenly hierarchies, toward which the good-order of the hierarchy among us,
being assimilated as far as possible, will have the angelic comeliness as in
images, being stamped through it and being led up toward the superessential
marshal of every hierarchy?

<1> Remaining for us into sacred contemplation is the order that shuts
together the angelic hierarchies, the one ordered by the godlike
principalities and archangels and angels. And first it is necessary to say, I
think, according to what is possible to me, the manifestations of their holy
names. For that of the heavenly principalities manifests the godlike princely
and hegemonic with sacred order and with powers most befitting the principal,
and that they themselves have been turned wholly toward the superprincipal
principle and of others


to lead in a princely way, and to be stamped toward that origin-making
principle as far as possible, and to reveal its superessential marshalship by
the good-order of the principal powers.

<2> And that of the holy archangels is of the same rank with the heavenly
principalities; for there is of them and of the angels, as I said, one
hierarchy and arrangement. Yet since there is no hierarchy not having also
first and middle and last powers, the holy order of the archangels communally
by the hierarchical middle takes hold of the extremes. For it communes with
the most holy principalities and with the holy angels, with the former because
it has been turned in a princely way toward the superessential principle and
is stamped toward it as far as attainable and unifies the angels according to
its well-ordered and ranked and invisible hegemonies, with the latter because
it is also of the interpretive order, receiving the thearchic illuminations
hierarchically through the first powers and announcing them to the angels in a
good-formed way and revealing them through angels to us according to the
sacred analogy of each of those divinely illuminated. For the angels, as we
have already said before, completively terminate all the orders of the
heavenly minds, having the angelic property according to the last as among
heavenly essences, and being named angels more properly by us than the former,
inasmuch as the hierarchy is about the more apparent to them and more
around-the-world. For the highest order, as has been said, as drawing near
first-rankedly to the hidden, must be thought to hierarchize the second in a
hidden-formed way; and the second, which is completed by the holy lordships
and powers and authorities, to lead the hierarchy of the principalities and
archangels and angels, more apparently than the first hierarchy, but more
hidden-formedly than that after it; and the manifesting order of the
principalities and archangels and angels to oversee the human hierarchies
through one another, in order that there may be according to order the
leading-up and turning-back and communion and union toward God, and moreover
also the procession given over from God to all the hierarchies in a manner
befitting the good and visiting communally with most sacred good-order. Hence
theology has assigned our hierarchy to angels, naming Michael ruler of the
people of the Jews and others of other nations. For the Most High "set the
bounds of nations according to the number of angels of God."

<3> And if someone should say, And how was the people of the Hebrews alone led
up toward the thearchic illuminations? it must be answered that one must not
blame the straight overseerships of the angels for the wandering of the other
nations toward the gods that are not, but those themselves, having fallen by
their own inclinations from the straight leading-up toward the divine, by
self-love and stubbornness and by the analogous reverence of the things
seeming to them God-befitting. This even the people of the Hebrews itself is
witnessed to have suffered. For "knowledge of God," it says, "you have thrust
away," and you went behind your heart. For we do not have a compelled life,
nor because of the self-authority of those provided for are the divine lights
of the providential illumination blunted, but the unlikeness of the
intellectual sights makes the overfull light-giving of the paternal goodness
either entirely unpartaken and undistributed toward their resistance, or makes
the participations different, small or great, dim or shining, of the one and
simple and always likewise having and over-simplified source ray. Since that
even of the other nations,


from which we also have lifted ourselves toward the infinite and ungrudging
sea of the thearchic light, spread open readily to all for giving-over, it was
not some foreign-tribe gods that were overseeing, but one principle of all,
and toward this the angels hierarchizing according to each nation were leading
those following, one must think Melchizedek as being a most God-loving
hierarch not of those that are not, but of the truly being Most High God. For
not simply have the theosophists called Melchizedek not only God-loving but
also priest, or in order that they may manifest clearly to those of
understanding that not only had he himself been turned toward the truly being
God, but besides he was also leading others as hierarch of the leading-up
toward the true and only thearchy.

<4> And this too we will remind your hierarchical understanding, that even to
Pharaoh, by the angel overseeing the Egyptians, and to the ruler <of the>
Babylonians, by his own, [and] the caring and authoritative of the providence
and lordship of all was ferried across according to the visions, and to those
nations the servants of the truly <being> God were appointed leaders, the
manifestation of the visions typed by the angels having been revealed from God
through angels to the holy men near the angels, to Daniel and to Joseph. For
one is the principle and providence of all, and it is in no way to be thought
that the thearchy leads the Jews by allotment, but that angels properly or
with equal honor or oppositely, or certain other gods, oversee the other
nations; but even that oracle must be taken according to this sacred thought,
not as God having divided with other gods or angels the hegemony according to
us and having been allotted to Israel as ethnarch and nation-leader, but as
the one highest providence of all itself having distributed all men savingly
to the uplifting guidances of their own angels, and almost alone beside all
Israel having been turned toward the light-giving and knowledge of the truly
Lord. Whence theology, manifesting that Israel allotted itself toward the
service of the truly <being> God, says "He became the portion of the Lord,"
and showing that even he equally with the remaining nations was assigned to
one of the holy angels in order to know through him the one principle of all,
said that Michael leads the people of the Jews, teaching us clearly that there
is one providence of the wholes, established superessentially above all the
invisible and visible powers, and that all the angels overseeing according to
each nation stretch those following up toward it as their own principle,
willingly, as much as power.

<1> It has been gathered then by us that the most senior order of the minds
about God, being hierarchized by the initiating illumination by being
stretched up to it immediately, is purified and illuminated and perfected by a
more hidden and more shining light-giving of the thearchy, more hidden as more
intelligible and more simplifying and unifying, more shining as first-given
and first-appearing and more total and more poured into it as transparent; and
toward this again analogously the second, and toward the second the third, and
toward the third our hierarchy, according to the same ordinance of the
well-ordered marshalship, in divine harmony and analogy toward the
superprincipal principle and termination of every good-order, hierarchically


is led up.

<2> And all are manifesting and angels of those before them, the most senior
of God who moves, and analogously the rest of those moved from God. For so
much has the superessential harmony of all provided for the sacred good-order
and ranked leading-up of each of the rational and intellectual, that even in
each of the hierarchies themselves it has set sacred-befitting orders; and we
see every hierarchy divided into first and middle and last powers, but also
each order itself, to speak particularly, it has distinguished by the same
inspired harmonies. Wherefore also the theologians say that the most divine
Seraphim themselves have cried one toward the other, making clear in this, as
I think, that the first give over the theological knowledges to the second.

<3> And I would add this too not unfittingly, that even each heavenly and
human mind by itself has particular both first and middle and last orders and
powers, manifested analogously toward the said proper leadings-up of the
hierarchical illuminations according to each, according to which each comes to
be in participation, as is lawful and attainable to it, of the over-purest
purification, of the overfull light, of the preperfect perfection. For there
is nothing self-perfect or entirely without need of perfection except that
which is truly self-perfect and preperfect.

<ΧI.>

<1> These having been distinguished, that is worthy to think, through what
cause we are accustomed to call all the angelic essences together heavenly
powers. For it is not possible to say, as upon the angels, that the last order
of all is that of the holy powers, and that the orders of the overlying
essences partake of the holy-befitting illumination of the last, but the last
in no way of the first, and for this sake all the divine minds are named
heavenly powers, but Seraphim and thrones and lordships in no way. For the
last are unpartaking of the highest total properties. For the angels and,
before the angels, archangels and principalities and authorities, being
ordered by theology after the powers, are often called by us in common
together with the other holy essences heavenly powers.

<2> And we say that, having used in common upon all the name of heavenly
powers, we do not introduce some confusion of the properties of each order,
but since all the divine minds have been divided into three by the
supermundane reason according to them, into essence and power and activity,
whenever we call all or some of them, without observation, heavenly essences
or heavenly powers, one must think us to manifest periphrastically those
concerning whom the word is, from the essence or power according to each of
them; for it is not even to attach the overlying property of the holy powers
already well distinguished by us wholly to the underlying essences, upon an
overturning of the unconfused marshalship of the angelic orders. For according
to the word often rightly given over by us, the orders that have gone beyond
have abundantly also the sacred properties of the underlying, but the last do
not have the overlying totalities of the more senior, the first-appearing
illuminations being ferried across partially into them through the first
analogously to them.




<ΧII.>

<1> This too is sought by those who love to behold the intelligible oracles:
for if the last are unpartaking of the higher totalities, through what cause
has our hierarch been named by the oracles "Angel of the Lord Almighty."

<2> And the word is not contrary, as I think, to the things distinguished
before. For we say that the last fall short of the total and overlying power
of the more senior orders; for they partake of the partial and analogous
according to the one harmonious and binding communion of all. As the order of
the holy Cherubim partakes of a higher wisdom and knowledge, and the orders of
the essences under them partake themselves also of wisdom and knowledge, yet
partial as toward those and underlying, and to be wholly in participation of
wisdom and knowledge is common to all the godlike of the intellectual, but
nearly and first or secondarily and underlyingly is no longer common, but as
has been defined for each according to its own analogy. And this one would
define not erringly even concerning all the divine minds; for just as the
first have abundantly the holy-befitting properties of the underlying, so the
last have those of the former, not indeed similarly, but underlyingly. Nothing
then, as I think, is out of place, if theology also calls our hierarch an
angel, as partaking according to his own power of the interpretive property of
the angels and stretching himself up toward their manifesting likeness as far
as attainable to men.

<3> And you will find that theology also calls gods both the heavenly essences
above us and the most God-loving and sacred men among us, although the
thearchic hiddenness is superessentially excepted from all and established
above, and nothing of the beings can be named like to it properly and totally.
Yet as many of the intellectual and rational as have been turned wholly toward
the union of it as much as power, and are stretched up unceasingly toward its
divine illuminations as far as attainable, have been deemed worthy, by the
God-imitation according to power, if it is lawful to say, even of the divine
homonym.

<ΧIII.>

<1> Come then, let us also look according to power at this, for whose sake the
Seraphim is said to be sent toward one of the theologians. For someone might
be in perplexity that not some one of the underlying angels, but he himself
who is numbered with the most senior essences, purifies the interpreter.

<2> Some then say that, according to the bound already given over of the
communion of all the minds, the oracle does not name one of the very first
minds about God as having come to the purification of the theologian, but some
one of the angels set over us, as celebrant of the purification of the
prophet, to have been called by the homonym of the Seraphim because of the
lightning-like taking-away of the said sins and the rekindling of the purified
toward the divine obedience, and they say the oracle has said simply one of
the Seraphim, not of those established about God, but of the purifying powers
set over us.

<3> And another has furnished me a not very out-of-place apology for the
present objection. For he said that that great one (whoever the angel was who
recast the vision in order to initiate the theologian in the divine things)
referred his own purifying celebration to God and after God upon


the first-working hierarchy. And perhaps then this word is true? For he who
said this said that the thearchic power, going to all, proceeds and passes
through all unrestrainedly and again is unseen to all, not only as
superessentially excepted from all, but also as sending through its
providential activities hiddenly upon all. But it also appears analogously to
all the intellectual, and putting its own light-giving into the hands of the
most senior essences, through them as first it well-orderedly distributes
itself into the underlying, according to the God-seeing due-measure of each
order. Or, that I may speak more clearly and through proper examples, even if
falling short of God who is excepted from all, yet more apparent to us: the
distribution of the solar ray proceeds distributively into a first matter, the
more transparent of all, and through it more apparently flashes its own
gleams, but striking upon the thicker materials it has a dimmer distributive
appearing from the unfitness of the materials illuminated toward a habit
ferrying-across of light-giving, and little by little from this it is
contracted toward the almost entirely undistributed. Again the heat of fire
distributes itself more into the more receptive and easily yielding and easily
led toward assimilation of it, but toward the resistant or contrary essences
either nothing or some dim trace of the enflaming activity appears. And more
than this, that it strikes upon those not akin through those having properly
toward it, first, if it happen, making fiery the things easily alterable
toward enflaming, and through them analogously heating either water or some
other of the things not easily enflamed. According to the same reason then of
the natural good-order, supernaturally the marshal of every visible and
invisible good-order reveals the brightness of its own light-giving
first-appearingly in all-blessed pourings to the highest essences, and through
these the essences after them partake of the divine ray. For these, having
first known God and longing in an overlying way for divine virtue, have also
been deemed worthy to become first-working of the as far as attainable
God-imitating power and activity, and they stretch the essences after them
toward the rival as much as power in a good-formed way, giving over
ungrudgingly to them of the radiance that has visited into them, and again
those to the underlying, and according to each the first gives over to that
after it of the divine light given and going through by providence analogously
into all. There is then for all those being illuminated a principle of being
illuminated, God by nature and truly properly as essence of light and cause of
the very being and seeing, and by thesis and God-imitatingly the
according-to-part overlying to each after it, by the divine lights being
channeled through it into that. The highest order then of the heavenly minds
the essences of all the remaining angels reasonably hold after God a principle
of every sacred God-knowledge and God-imitation, as through those the
thearchic illumination is distributed into all and us. Wherefore also they
refer every sacred and God-imitating activity to God as cause, but to the
first godlike minds as first-working of the divine things and teachers.
Therefore the first order of the holy angels has more than all the fiery
property and the poured giving-over of the thearchic wisdom and the cognitive
of the highest science of the divine illuminations and the throne-property
manifesting the spread-open God-reception, and the orders of the underlying
essences


partake of the fiery, the wise, the cognitive, the God-receiving power, yet
underlyingly, and looking toward the first and through them, as having been
deemed worthy first-workingly of the God-imitating, being led up toward the
attainable of the godlike. The said holy properties then, of which the
essences after them have come to be in participation through the first, they
assign to those themselves after God as hierarchs.

<4> He who said these things said then that that vision was shown to the
theologian through one of the holy and blessed angels set over us, and that by
his illuminating guidance he was stretched up toward that sacred
contemplation, according to which he saw the highest essences, to speak as in
symbols, established under God and after God and about God, and the
superprincipal summit unspeakably excepted from all and from them established
above in the midst of the powers that have gone beyond. The theologian was
learning then by the things seen that according to every superessential
superiority the divine was incomparably established above every visible and
invisible power, and moreover that it is excepted from all as entirely not
like even to the first of the essences of the beings, and besides that it
itself is principle and essence-making cause of all and unalterable
establishment of the undissolved remaining of the beings, by which both the
being and the well-being is even to the powers that have gone beyond. Then he
was initiated in the godlike powers of the most holy Seraphim themselves,
their sacred name signifying the fiery, concerning which a little later we
will speak, to guide as attainable to us the leadings-up of the fiery power
toward the godlike, and the sixfold sacred-shaping of the wings the unloosed
and highest stretching-up toward the divine in first, in middle, in last
intellections. But seeing also their infinite-footed and many-faced, and the
being set apart by the wings of the contemplation under the feet and that
above the faces, and the ever-movement in the middle wings, the sacred
theologian was led up toward the intelligible knowledge of the things seen,
the many-wayed and many-beholding power of the highest minds being manifested
to him, and their sacred reverence, which they have supermundanely toward the
self-willed and rash and unattainable search of the higher and deeper, and the

initiated in, the angel typing the vision giving over according to power to
the theologian of his own sacred-knowledge. He was teaching him then this too,
that purification is, to those in any way pure, the as far as attainable
participation of the thearchic transparency [of purity]. And this, being
accomplished by excepted causes from the thearchy itself into all the sacred
minds in superessential hiddenness, is somehow more apparent to the powers
about it as highest and more manifests and distributes itself, but upon the
second or the last or our intellectual powers, as each has stood apart from it
according to the godlike, so it gathers its shining illumination toward the
unified unknown of its own hiddenness. And it illuminates those second
according to each through the first, and if one must speak briefly, it is
first brought from the hidden toward the apparent through the first powers.
This then the theologian was being taught by the angel light-leading him, that
the purification and all the thearchic activities, flashing through the first
essences, are distributed into all the remaining according to the analogy of
each toward the theurgic participations. Wherefore also he has reasonably
assigned the fiery purifying


property to the Seraphim after God. Nothing then is out of place if the
Seraphim is said to purify the theologian. For as God purifies all by being
cause of every purification, or rather (for I will use a nearby example) just
as our hierarch, purifying or illuminating through his ministers or priests,
is himself said to purify and illuminate, the orders consecrated through him
assigning their own sacred activities to him, so also the angel accomplishing
the purification of the theologian assigns his own purifying science and power
to God as cause, but to the Seraphim as first-working hierarch, as one might
say with angelic reverence, teaching the one being purified by him that "of
the purification being accomplished toward you by me the excepted principle
and essence and crafter and cause is he who both brought the first essences
even toward being and holds them together by the establishment about him and
keeps them unturned and unfallen and moves even them toward the first
participations of his own providential activities (for this, said he who was
teaching me these things, the sending of the Seraphim manifests), and hierarch
and after God leader is the order of the very first essences, from whom I was
initiated in a godlike way in the purifying. This then is he who through me
purifies you, through whom the cause and crafter of every purification has
brought its own providential activities from the hidden even unto us."

These things then that one was teaching me, and I give them over to you. And
it would be of your intellectual and distinguishing science either to be
released from the perplexity by one of the said causes and to honor this
before the other as having the likely and reasonable and perhaps the true, or
from yourself to find out something more akin of the truly true, or to learn
from another, God of course giving a word and angels introducing, and to
uncover to us who love the angels a more transparent, if it were possible, and
to me more beloved contemplation.

<ΧIV.>

And this too is worthy, as I think, of intellectual attention, that the
tradition of the oracles concerning the angels says there are "thousand
thousands" and "ten thousand ten thousands," rolling up upon themselves and
multiplying the highest of the numbers among us, and through these clearly
manifesting the arrangements of the heavenly essences unnumberable to us. For
many are the blessed armies of the supermundane minds, having gone beyond the
weak and contracted due-measure of our material numbers, and being defined
cognitively by the supermundane and heavenly intellection and science
according to them alone, which is all-blessedly given to them by the thearchic
infinite-knowing wisdom-making of the one who is superessentially principle
and essence-making cause of all the beings together and holding-together power
and encompassing termination.

<1> Come then remaining, if it seems good, resting our intellectual eye from
the angel-befitting intensity about the unified and high contemplations, and
having gone down upon the divided and many-parted breadth of the many-formed
variety of the angelic form-makings, let us again from them as from images
analytically bend back toward the simplicity of the heavenly minds. And let it
be for you


already known that the cleansings of the sacred-typical images sometimes
manifest the same orders of the heavenly essences as hierarchizing and again
as being hierarchized, and the last as hierarchizing and the first as being
hierarchized, and the same, as has been said, having first and middle and last
powers, no out-of-place word being brought in according to such a manner of
the unfoldings; for if we said some are hierarchized by the former, then that
the same hierarchize and that the former again hierarchizing of the last are
hierarchized by those themselves who are being hierarchized, truly the thing
would be an out-of-placeness and full of much confusion; but if we say the
same both hierarchize and are hierarchized, no longer of the same or by the
same, but each itself is hierarchized by the former, and hierarchizes the
last, one would say not unfittingly that the sacredly shaped formations in the
oracles can sometimes be put properly and truly around first and middle and
last powers, and the being stretched up convertively toward the upward, and
the being rolled unswervingly about themselves as being guardian powers of
their own, and the being in participation of the providential power by the
communicative procession about the second, will fit unfalsely to all the
heavenly essences, even if to some overlying and totally, as has often been
said, and to others partially and underlyingly.

<2> And the word must be begun, and it must be sought in a first cleansing of
the types through what cause theology is found honoring almost above all the
fiery sacred-drawing. You will find it then not only molding fiery wheels but
also animals set on fire and men flashing as fire, and putting around the
heavenly essences themselves heaps of coals of fire and rivers flaming with
unrestrained rushing. But it also says the thrones are fiery, and even the
highest Seraphim themselves it manifests from the name as being those who
kindle, and it assigns to them the property and activity of fire, and wholly
above and below it honors by distinction the fiery type-shaping. The fiery
then I think manifests the most godlike of the heavenly minds. For the sacred
theologians describe in many places the superessential and unformed essence in
fire, as having many images, if it is lawful to say, of the thearchic property
as in visibles. For the sensible fire is, so to speak, in all and goes
unmixedly through all and has been excepted from all, and being all-shining
and at once as hidden, unknown itself according to itself if matter is not set
forth in which it may manifest its own activity, unrestrained and
uncontemplated, self-ruling of all and altering toward its own activity the
things in which it may come to be, transmissive of itself to all who in any
way draw near, renewing by the kindling heat, illuminating with unveiled
illuminations, unrestrainable, unmixed, distinguishing, unalterable,
upward-borne, sharp-passing, high, enduring no around-the-feet underlying,
ever-moved, same-moved, motive of others, encompassing, unencompassed, without
need of another, increasing itself latently and revealing its own greatness
toward the receiving materials, active, powerful, present to all invisibly,
seeming not to be when neglected, but by rubbing, as by a certain search,
connaturally and properly appearing suddenly and again flying away
ungraspably, undiminished in all its all-blessed givings-over. And many
properties of fire one would find proper as in sensibles images of thearchic


activity. This then knowing, the theosophists mold the heavenly essences from
fire, manifesting their godlike and as far as attainable God-imitating.

<3> But they also describe them as human-formed because of the intellectual
and the having the seeing powers toward the upward, and the straight and
upright of the figure, and the according to nature princely and hegemonic, and
the according to sense least as toward the remaining powers of the irrational
animals, but ruling all by the power of mind according to surplus and by the
dominance according to rational science and according to the by nature
unenslaved and unrestrainable of the soul. And it is possible also according
to each, as I think, of our bodily many-partedness to find harmonious images
of the heavenly powers, saying that the seeing powers manifest the most
transparent lifting-up toward the divine lights and again the tender and moist
and not resistant, but sharp-moved and pure and spread-open passionless
reception of the thearchic illuminations; and the distinguishing powers of the
smellable the as far as attainable receptive of the distribution of the
fragrance above mind and distinguishing in science of those not such and
wholly fleeing; and the powers of the ears the participating and cognitively
receptive of the thearchic inspiration; and the tasting the filling of the
intelligible foods and the receptive of the divine and nourishing channels;
and the touching the diagnostic in science of the connatural or of the
harming; and the eyelids and the brows the guarding of the God-seeing
intellections; and the youthful and young age the of the always peaking vital
power; and the teeth the dividing of the given-over nourishing perfection (for
each intellectual essence divides the unified intellection given to it by the
more divine with providential power and multiplies it toward the anagogic
analogy of the more lacking); and the shoulders and the arms and again the
hands the making and active and efficacious; and the heart again to be a
symbol of the godlike life that scatters its own vital power in a good-formed
way into those provided for; and the breast again to manifest the untamed and
the guarding, as upon the underlying heart, of the life-making distribution;
and the back the holding-together of all the life-generating powers; and the
feet the motive and sharp and nimble of the ever-movement that goes toward the
divine things. Wherefore also theology has figured the feet of the holy minds
as under-winged. For the wing manifests the anagogic sharpness and the
heavenly and the way-making toward the upward and the excepted from every
lowly-to-earth through the upward-borne; and the lightness of the wings the
according to nothing near-earth, but wholly being led up unmixedly and without
weight toward the high; and the naked and unshod the unfettered and easily
loosed and unrestrained and purified of the addition of the outsides and the
assimilative as far as attainable toward the divine simplicity.

taking-up again of former well-being or an addition of other gifts, small or

<6> And their being named also winds manifests their sharp flight passing
through almost all timelessly and the movement ferrying-across from above upon
the below and again from the below toward the upward, which stretches the
second up toward the higher height, and moves the first toward a communicative
and providential procession of the underlying. And one would say that the
windy name of the aerial spirit manifests also the godlike of the heavenly
minds. For this too has an image and type of thearchic activity (as has been
shown by us through more in the Symbolic Theology according to the
four-element cleansing) according to the motive and life-generating of the

not know," it says, "whence it comes and where it goes."

But theology also molds around them a form of cloud, signifying through this
that the sacred minds are supermundanely filled with the hidden light, and
receive the first-appearing light-manifestation unparade-like, and ferry this
ungrudgingly into the second second-appearingly and analogously, and moreover
that the generative and life-making and increasing and perfecting is in them
according to the intelligible rain-bearing that calls out the receiving bosom
with fat rains toward vital birth-pangs.

<7> And if theology also puts around the heavenly essences a form of bronze
and electrum and many-colored stones, the electrum, as gold-formed and at once
silver-formed, manifests the undecaying as in gold and unspendable and
undiminished and unstained transparency, and the shining as in silver and

white the light-formed or as red the fiery or as yellow the gold-formed or as
green the youthful and peaking, and according to each form you will find an
anagogic cleansing of the typical images.

<9> And one must also look at rivers having been said and wheels and chariots
joined to the heavenly essences.

For the fiery rivers signify the thearchic channels furnishing to them an
ungrudging and unfailing inflow and nourishing of life-making generativeness;
and the chariots the yoking communion of those of the same rank; and the
wheels, being winged, and going unturned and unswervingly upon the things in

way, all their intellectual wheeling being supermundanely directed upon the
same unswerving and straight-cutting path.

And it is possible also according to another leading-up to cleanse the
image-drawing of the intellectual wheels. For it was called upon them, as the
theologian says, Gelgel; and this manifests according to Hebrew speech
rollings-up and uncoverings. For the fiery and godlike wheels have the
rollings-up by the ever-whirled movement about the same good, and the
uncoverings by the manifestation of the hidden things and by the leading-up of
the around-the-feet and by the down-leading ferrying-across of the high
illuminations into the underlying.

Remaining for us into making-clear is the word concerning the joy of the
heavenly orders. For they are entirely unreceptive of the passionate pleasure
according to us, but they are said to rejoice with God at the finding of the
lost according to the godlike gladness and the good-formed and ungrudging
good-cheer upon the providence and salvation of those turning back toward God,
and that unspeakable well-being of which even sacred men have often come to be
in participation according to the theurgic visitations of the divine
illuminations.
