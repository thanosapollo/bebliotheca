2 Cor 1:15-16 Through this wording he shows that he had joy when the
Corinthians were being benefited in his making them better. Wherefore
remaining in this intention he wished to sojourn among them again as he went
into Macedonia, and returning from there to see them again, that he might
again be gladdened at their being benefited; for it appears from the wording
that before the writing of both the epistles to them he had been in Corinth.
But also through the 18, sparing you I came no longer to Corinth, the same
thing is shown. 2 Cor 1:23 Knowing what was said by the Savior in promise
about not needing to swear, but having a word not open to blame about the yes
as being yes, and the no as being so, confirming here he seems to swear. And
very justly; for since he announces his own disposition which he had toward
the Corinthians, and this has its being in mind and heart, and it was likely
that those who were loved would doubt about so genuine a love toward them, he
calls God as witness of the things said upon his own soul, who knows also the
things lying in its secret. For he says: I sojourn toward you, longing for
you, and when I do not do this, again I hold back by forbearance toward you;
for you know that formerly, having come to your city, I stayed a whole year
and six months, rejoicing in your advancing day by day, being benefited
according to Christ. At any rate, besides the common teaching I also set
before you mystical traditions; for this I made clear in the epistle before
this one, stirring you to hold fast what you have received. But since I am now
precisely persuaded that certain false teachers are deceiving, setting aside
the resurrection from the dead and the continuance of the soul, and moreover
twisting the knowledge concerning things indifferent, for this reason I have
now held back from sojourning among you before a healing that can be begun
through letters. And I write these things not proclaiming myself lord of your
faith (for we work together toward your believing through teaching), and since
from our catechesis you have come to the faith, as we taught, so also we
remind, that you may remain in the faith you have accomplished; for even if
some were shaken by the deceivers, still I am confident that you stand in the
faith you received, being firm in it. 2 Cor 2:3-4 My joy, he says, is of you
all, through the well-spoken word, also making clear what is before it, I mean
that my grief is of you all; for it was necessary that the Corinthians grieve
with the apostle when he grieved, that they might also rejoice with him when
he rejoiced. And that I write these things from a genuine disposition, he
says, you know from the letter having been dictated out of much affliction and
anguish of heart through many tears; for I did not wish you to need such
rebukes, but demonstrations of the doctrines concerning the truth and
reminder, or rather exhortation toward perfect virtue. 19 2 Cor 2:10-11 This,
in the person of Christ, has a double sense; for it is shown either in the
person, that is, in the presence and while Christ looks on, but also: bearing
the person of Christ I have granted, knowing that he too, having judged to
grant all things toward blessedness, sojourned in this life. Bearing his
person then (for I pray to imitate him and to be conformed to him) I forgave
the sin to the one who repented; whence I also ... exhort to be of this mind.
For one must not be ignorant of the

wiles of Satan, how of old, when the fornication was taking place, wishing to
increase the harm he prepared the lack of sympathy, so that the one caught in
the vice would not be mourned; but now he has persuaded them that there is a
want of compassion toward the one who has ceased from the evil, in order that
through this he may both destroy that man and show you boastful and arrogant
against him. But see that no one, taking up the apostle's meaning, may suppose
it a poor thing to have knowledge of the satanic thoughts (for such a thing is
least of all harmful, but rather beneficial); for to know the thinking and the
choice does not make evil, but the choosing. At any rate, those who choose to
refute the deceivers, knowing the things of those men's deceit, we do not
blame, but even entirely accept; for it is not the knowing but the choosing of
the base things that makes men evil. We know therefore the thoughts of Satan,
not in order to work according to them, but that we may not, being deceived,
be trapped. 2 Cor 2:12 The passage from Troas to Macedonia came about for him
from a vision; for in the writing of the Acts of the Apostles it is said that
while he was in Troas a Macedonian man appeared, commanding in the manner of a
revelation. For he said: Cross over into Macedonia and help us. But he was
silent about this when writing the epistle, having judged by moderation of
mind that it was not timely to say such things about himself, as he will show
in what follows. At any rate he gives thanks to God for the passage, as he
will say adding next. And attend to what he means: a great door having been
opened to me in the Lord; for perhaps a door of the word is opened by the Lord
to those who receive speaking from a divine conception, and not as having
preparation from human art. God says then: Open your mouth wide and I will
fill it. And the wise man has cried: Open your mouth with the word of God,
that is, prepared within by longing and eagerness to receive teaching from
God. And perhaps a door of the word is opened also when the hearers are ready
for reception of the instruction. 2 Cor 2:14-16 For this reason, he says, God
makes manifest through us the fragrance of the knowledge of Christ in every
place, since we are a sweet savor of Christ; for as, he being light, the
partakers are light of the world, so also, he being myrrh, those united to him

And he calls many the deceivers because of the vulgar crowd. For this word
too, I mean "the many", being homonymous, signifies several things; for it is

vulgar, as in the present text and in do not become many teachers, brothers;
and it also signifies those who are more in whatever way, as in many rise up
against me, and many say to my soul. In the word set forth, we are not as some
who peddle the word of God, that is, watering it down through frigid talk and
foolish interpretation, but as from sincerity we offer it without deceit and
purely. 2 Cor 3:1-3 He rebukes the Corinthians gently as still ignorant of the
apostolic greatness, saying: as from sincerity, from God, speaking before God,
we seem to have been made manifest even to you as being of this mind, unless
perhaps you live so as to need a letter that commends us to you. And the
things concerning us are so manifest that neither do you commend [you] to
others, nor others to you; for you yourselves are an epistle of Christ
ministered by us, written in deed by the Spirit of the living God and not with
ink. For words are inscribed with ink, but a life of deed and contemplation is
characterized by the Spirit of the living God, so that the one so written
lives according to God by the Spirit; for the foolish in soul receive the
impressions from ink as stone tablets, but those easily yielding toward assent
to the divine things receive in hearts of flesh, that is, perceptive, a
writing from the Spirit of the living God; for they copy the instruction
threefold upon the breadth of the heart, inasmuch as God gives laws in the
mind, in writing them upon the breadth of the heart. 2 Cor 3:4-6 Let no one
suppose that we are being slack in ourselves when we say: as from sincerity we
speak before God in Christ; for we are so disposed concerning ourselves as
God, having called us sufficient for the ministry of the word, made us, we not
having from ourselves the ability to reckon anything as from ourselves
according to divine knowledge; for he himself who gave us to minister the new
covenant also extended the confidence toward speaking. And the ministry we
were entrusted with is of spirit and not of letter (for the letter kills, but
the Spirit makes alive); the covenant of the life-giving Spirit is new, not
growing old; for no other teaching comes after 22 it in this life; for it is
without a successor. And the heterodox also use this wording toward slander of
the old covenant, saying that that writing puts to death, but the new makes
alive as being of the life-giving Spirit; wherefore one must refute them as
being far distant from the apostolic mind. For even if the letter kills, still
it brings a praiseworthy death upon those who are put away; for living in
ignorance and vice before reception of the legal teaching, after receiving it
they are put to death, casting off the wicked life they had. For so they will
be ready to receive the making-alive of the Spirit, as: the one who is in evil
lives according to it; the one who has turned aside from it casts off the life
he had according to it, coming through the doing of the good unto living
blessedly; for as evil and good cannot coexist, so also the two lives cannot
be at the same time. The letter therefore is not blamed for having set an end
to the base life; for it will lead at once to the making-alive according to
the Spirit. 2 Cor 3:7-16 He compares both covenants, the new and the old,
bearing witness to each that it has been given in glory from God. Yet even if
both are glorified, the one in the Spirit has more glory. And if from
comparison the glory of the Spirit is greater, that with which it is compared
is not blameworthy; for a good is more good than a good. For a ministry of
death, impressed in letters on stones, is the one through Moses, ministering
the death which we said the letter brings on. As therefore that one is
productive of the death spoken of, so of the life after that death

the ministry of the Spirit is the provider. And he tries to bring on a
demonstration that the ministry of the Spirit is in glory, making the attempt
from the lesser; for he says: if the ministry of condemnation had so much
glory that not all the sons of Israel were able to look unto the perfection
(for they scarcely bore it veiled), how much more will the spiritual ministry
be in glory? Of the same contemplation he makes also another attempt, saying:
the former writing, condemning those worthy of the life they had according to
sin so as to bring on the death spoken of, has been given until a certain
time; for the evangelical teaching succeeds it, this being without a
successor. If then that which is being abolished has its end in glory, much
more that which remains has more glory; the wording "is being abolished" does
not lie abusively. At any rate even the knowledge in part of the holy ones and
prophecy will be abolished, not once for all but by the presence of the
knowledge of the truth. And just as if we should say that the introductory
teaching is abolished from knowledge of the perfect contemplations, we do not
speak 23 insultingly; for we propose to show through the wording the need set
forth toward Paul as one being introduced. Therefore having such confidence,
as having been appointed ministers of a new covenant, bringing forth the
teaching unveiled we use much frankness, being teachers of perfect hearers,
since the instruction we bring forth does not need glory from outside, having
it inborn. For Moses wore a veil upon his face when speaking with the many of
the people; for they did not bear the magnificence of the teaching. At any
rate to the elders and to Joshua the son of Nun and if anyone was of their
kind, conversing, he displayed the glory bare. And this too came about, as
also many other things, symbolically under Moses; for this man bears the
person of the law, and since the law has been spoken toward meaning and toward
the literal, to those able to approach only according to letter and wording,
he comes veiled with shadow and histories

to those who have gone beyond, handing on the things according to spiritual
allegory without a veil. If then Moses has been veiled to some, the charge is
not his but of those unable to see his glory without a veil, since one must
also charge Jesus with not making the mysteries manifest to all; for to those
outside he spoke them in parables. So that the evangelical word too speaks in
a veiled way to those outside, but unveiledly to those who have come inside
unto the mystical meaning, making the clarification of the parables, saying to
those who enter into his house: to you it has been given to know the mysteries
of the kingdom, but to those outside all things come in parables. At any rate
he shows the matter at hand, that Moses therefore veils the glory he has upon
his face, since the thoughts of those not able to see him bare have been
hardened. He appears through the sequence of all the things at hand to call
the law Moses. 2 Cor 3:17 From these things the myth of those who cut apart
the Scripture of God is overturned; for it is one, at one time veiled with
types and shadow, at another appearing without veils, since we receive the
unveiling of it from the Lord's Spirit, having been persuaded that the Spirit
of the Lord is freedom. 2 Cor 4:3-4 He says that the devil is god of this age,
as also the demons are gods of the nations, and the belly of the gluttons. And
some punctuate at "in whom God", reading next from a separate beginning: of
this age has blinded the thoughts of the unbelievers. At any rate they also
say things easily refuted; for if some 24 are unbelievers of this age, others
would be found unbelievers who are not of it. For every unbeliever is of this
age; for no one who has gone beyond it and been counted worthy of the age to
come has been blinded in thoughts, but he has enlightened eyes. I have known

certain people explaining the present wording, saying that the gospel is
veiled not to those who are unbelievers in every way; for they have no
experience of it either veiled or bare. And these said that those in whom the
gospel has been veiled are "perishing", those who have received it, yet have
not approached it without a veil. Such were those who heard the words of Jesus
in parables, the "perishing" lying upon them toward showing change by
alteration; for those who are advancing are of this sort, always becoming
other from other. Whence they said that "perishing" does not lie for blame,
but likewise with the heavens will perish, and we perishing the age; for these
wordings set "to perish" of the one who in progress passes from quality to
quality. 2 Cor 4:5-6 The present wording has a reference to "not that we lord
it over your faith"; for consequently to that it would be said: we do not
preach ourselves, wherefore we do not lord it over your faith; for we preach
Jesus Christ God the Lord, and ourselves your slaves through Christ. So that
not we but he himself lords it over our faith, accepting or judging you
according to it. Besides this, also "that God who said light shall shine out
of darkness" must be joined to "and we all with unveiled face behold as in a
mirror the glory of the Lord". And light is shone out of darkness for the one
who comes from ignorance unto full knowledge of truth; we say then that from
one who is ignorant even of the true he becomes knowing and educated. The
ignorance and vice that had previously overtaken human beings having been
named darkness, God shone in the hearts an illumination of practical and
contemplative virtue, that they might have contemplation of the glory of
Christ in his person. And glory either according to which they think
concerning him, or glory according to which we know him glorified. 2 Cor 4:7
He calls earthen vessels the cheap wordings and the unadorned character of the
expression. For if the heralds of the gospel had been ambassadors of the truth
according to human 25 philosophy, it would have been suspected that human
beings had been held by the saving instruction from human art and not from the
power of God; but the ministers being unlettered and unskilled, the excess of
the power of the treasure according to knowledge will be supposed from God and
not from mortals. With this agrees "even if unskilled in speech, yet not in
knowledge", and "my word and my preaching were not in persuasive words of
wisdom"; for he had the treasure of knowledge, not being unskilled according
to it, in earthen vessels being unskilled in speech. And there are those who
say earthen vessels are the bodies we wear while we are among perceptible
things, with this mind: since still wearing flesh that hinders the
contemplation of the truth, we have knowledge concerning the Trinity, the
excess of the greatness of the knowledge is made known to us as not from us,
but from the power of God existing for us. 2 Cor 4:8-9 As far as concerns the
bitterness of the circumstances, we would have given out and perished; but as
far as concerns God who comforts, all things are easy for us toward salvation.
If then we are afflicted in every matter, acting nobly we are not crushed; and
if some suppose us to be at a loss, persecuting and overturning, still we in
all things being well supplied are not utterly at a loss, we do not perish, we
are not cast down; for when the wicked overturn we stand unshaken, being
persecuted we act nobly, all things being resources for us toward salvation.
One must know that "to be crushed" is at one time the same as "to be
afflicted", at another different. For when both are said, "to be afflicted"
shows the outward circumstance, "to be crushed" the giving way in choice and
reasoning. But when both are not taken, they show the same in parallel. 26 2
Cor 4:10-11 The "always" and "at all times" lying in the present wording do
not show the endless and without beginning, as we say the everlasting, but the
unceasing and without interval; for he who does not carry about the dying of
Jesus in his body at one time yes and at another no, will say he does this
always.

But also he who has life and piety and zeal to contend without interval on
behalf of the truth will say: so then we who live in the manner spoken of,
that is without interruptions, are delivered unto death. Unto this you will
also take what was said by Stephen to the Jews: for always you resist the Holy
Spirit; for he coupled with it "which of the prophets did our fathers not
persecute?" Of this mind is also "I said: they always go astray in heart",
that is not at one time yes and at another no, but without interval. But "I
was silent; shall I also always be silent?" shows the endless and unceasing;
for even if now, he says, I am long-suffering while you sin, still the
postponement of punishment will not be unto the unceasing; for after much
long-suffering those who remain in vice will be punished. 2 Cor 4:13 He calls,
he says, spirit of faith that which partakes of the faith which someone has. 2
Cor 4:16 For in everything, we being afflicted, the outer man is wasting away.
2 Cor 5:1 The present writing signifies two different houses: one earthly and
made with hands, the world that appears, brought into being by the hands of
God (for showing all perceptible things he says: your hand made all these, and
also: the heavens are the works of your hands, it is of the same meaning), and
a house not made with hands, being in the heavens, the world other than this
one. Since then, the perceptible world being dissolved, he does not do away
with the form of heaven and earth, they passing away not into non-being, human
beings come to an end; the one who formerly dwelt in the earthly house that is
being dissolved, the coming and 27 eternal will succeed. And he said: we have
a house not made with hands, eternal, in the heavens, showing that from here,
or rather from long ago, it has been made ready for those worthy to obtain it.
At any rate the promises in it which eye has not seen, nor has another sense
or thinking grasped, have been said to have been prepared beforehand by God
for those who love him. And the Savior says: inherit the kingdom prepared for
you before the foundation of the world. And this perceptible dwelling spoken
of has been called a house not once for all ours, but that of our tent; for
the heavenly is our dwelling in the primary sense. For even if from
resurrection the tent of the soul is given back so as to be itself also in the
heavenly house, still this sojourn is primarily of the soul, and by accident
also of the tent it wears; as again the earthly is in the first account a
house of the tent, and secondarily also of the soul being in the body. I have
read somewhere that an earthly house of a tent is said to be the body composed
of bones and fleshes, and a tent being in this house the form of the inner
man, which is a luminous and instrumental body. Those who interpret thus say
then: this house of the thick body being dissolved, the soul is moved over
unto the heavenly place, called a house not made with hands, eternal. And it
will also receive back the body it put off, having become heavenly, which is
named a dwelling from heaven. 2 Cor 5:2-4 Even if, the aforementioned house of
the tent having been dissolved, we wear the tent, passing in it into the
heavenly house; still being in it we groan, longing to put on over it the
dwelling from heaven, and this is the body we put off, then being named from
heaven when it puts on incorruption and immortality. But even if we long for
this dwelling, consider whether the one who has put it on is in every case
covered by it; for this is shown from "if indeed also having put it on we
shall not be found naked". And the one who has put it on is not found naked,
the faithful and holy one, who before this very thing put on the Lord Jesus
and weapons of light and bowels of compassion; for the unbelieving and base,
even if he obtains the spiritual body which is called a dwelling from heaven,
will be found naked, not having been eager

to have the garments of the inner man. These things being so, we who are in
the tent groan being burdened, since the earthly tent weighs down a mind of
many cares. Yet even if we are burdened from 28 having it, we do not long to
cast it off, but to put on the 28 dwelling from heaven, that the mortal may be
held, swallowed by life; for life does not make our tent vanish when we put on
immortality over it, but swallows it, the quality from it becoming more
dominant than that according to which we are mortal. 2 Cor 5:12-13 Let those
men be convicted by you on these points (and he speaks concerning the false
apostles), but we do these things for your sake and through you; for even if,
having come to be outside human things, we are beside ourselves to God, still
we are sober toward you, the divine ecstasy having not madness but sobriety.
For one must not attend to those from the Phrygians who say that the apostles
and prophets are beside themselves so as to be out of their wits; for they
followed the things they said and did, if anyone else did. And the foolish
were deceived from the homonymy; for the word "he was beside himself"
signifies not only being out of one's wits and deranged, but also being struck
with astonishment. For so Isaac was astonished with a great astonishment, and
some will be astonished at the Lord and at his good things, according to the
prophet. And if some other things also are shown by the word, let the one who
meets the Scriptures attend. 2 Cor 5:14-15 He interprets the form of the
ecstasy he undergoes, having said: we are constrained by the great love of God
that has come upon us, with wondering proving that if one, Christ, died on
behalf of all, then all died; for he would not have died on behalf of all, if
we were not all liable to death. If then he died on our behalf and through us
and rose, having risen with him we live, no longer through ourselves, that is
humanly, but to him who suffered on our behalf and rose. And living to him, we
also according to him do and say and think all that he wills, so that each has
dared to say: my soul lives to him, and: I live, yet no longer I, but Christ
lives in me. 2 Cor 5:16 Since we live to him who died and was raised on our
behalf from the time we began to be so, we know no one any longer according to
flesh, having gone beyond the flesh and its passions, and moreover also the
shadowy teaching of the law. And if we also knew Christ according to 29 flesh,
still wishing to judaize and to follow the fleshly narrative of the law, yet
now we no longer know him so but as God Word; for the Word having become flesh
did not dwell but tabernacled among us, that after this we might behold his
glory, glory as of an only-begotten from a father, full of grace and truth.
For to dwell differs from to tabernacle as also the house from a tent; for the
house always remains, founded immovably, but the tent is a portable house. At
any rate those who are advancing tabernacle, those who have been perfected
dwell; for I will pass through in a place of a wonderful tent as far as the
house of God. 2 Cor 5:17-19 The one who has become in Christ by partaking of
him is a new creation. The holy one, praying that such a thing be begun in his
own heart, said: create a clean heart in me, O God. According to this
signification the Savior creates in himself the two into one new man. That
this then might be begun, in this manner he submitted himself to be created,
saying: the Lord created me the beginning of his ways unto works. The verb to
be created does not show coming-to-be in substance, as has been shown in other
places through more things. And a new creation of the believers having come
about in Christ, the ancient things have passed, having grown old, the new
having been brought into the midst. And if he calls the legal and prophetic
things ancient, these have passed, the gospels having succeeded, these old and
new having the difference not according to the underlying subject but in
conception; for the same teaching of the two covenants has come about at one
time in a veiled way, at another bare. Ancient things then are the veiled
things. And if someone should say that the customs before faith and the
ancient condition are ancient, these have passed

having ceased, the teaching according to the gospel having come to be present.
To the former interpretation "all things from God" will fit, for the law and
the prophets and the gospels are from God; according to the later account, all
the new things being from God, yet not also the things that have passed. And
all things are from God who reconciled us to himself through Christ. For we
were enemies, held by much deceit and sins, but we were reconciled to him,
having received forgiveness from him and through him; and he reconciled us to
himself through the Lord who died and rose on our behalf. And that we may not
be without sense of the great benefaction of God, he has given the teaching
concerning the reconciliation to the apostles, that being instructed by 30
them we may remain in being in Christ; for through him the friendship toward
God that came about after the reconciliation will be lasting. 2 Cor 5:21 "Him
who knew no" he says i.e. him who has not been tried by sin; for he does not
say not being ignorant of its nature (for the Savior knew it more than all),
but not having been tried by it. For so also he who keeps a commandment will
not know an evil word. 2 Cor 6:2-6 Since at an acceptable time and a day of
salvation God, having heard, helped, sending his own Son and giving him over
on our behalf, let no occasion of unbelief come about in any way, that the
ministry of the evangelical teaching may not have a blemish. Whence one must
be eager to commend ourselves in good works, even if they are toilsome. And
this comes about if, having endurance, we bear afflictions and necessities and
distresses and the like nobly, bearing bonds and experience of prison
cheerfully; for so it will follow that the soul is pure, thinking and doing
right things; for having purified our souls, he says, in the obedience of the
truth. But also the fear of the Lord, being pure, procures all purity for the
one who has taken it up. 2 Cor 6:7-9 He says there are weapons of
