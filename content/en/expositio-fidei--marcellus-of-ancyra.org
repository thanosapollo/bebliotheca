Of Athanasius, Archbishop of Alexandria, an exposition of the faith.
1.1 We believe in one unbegotten God, Father Almighty, maker of all things
visible and invisible, who has his being from himself. 1.2 And in one
only-begotten Word, Wisdom, Son, begotten of the Father without beginning and
eternally; Word not uttered, not immanent, not an efflux of the Perfect, not a
cutting of the impassible nature nor a projection, but a Son complete in
himself, living and active, the true image of the Father, equal in honor and
equal in glory. 1.3 "For this," he says, "is the will of the Father, that as
they honor the Father, so they honor the Son also." True God from true God, as
John says in the catholic epistles: "and we are in the true one, in his Son
Jesus Christ. This is the true God and eternal life." 1.4 Almighty from
Almighty; for of all things of which the Father rules and holds sway, he also
rules and holds sway. Whole from whole. 1.5 Being like the Father, as the Lord
says: "He who has seen me has seen the Father." And he was begotten
inexpressibly and incomprehensibly; "for who shall declare his generation?"
that is, no one. 1.6 Who at the consummation of the ages, coming down from the
bosom of the Father, took up our man from the undefiled Virgin Mary, Christ
Jesus, whom he delivered of his own choice to suffer for us, as the Lord says:
"No one takes my soul from me. I have authority to lay it down, and I have
authority to take it again." 1.7 In which man, crucified and having died for
us, he rose from the dead, was taken up into the heavens, having been created
for us as a beginning of ways; being on earth he showed us light out of
darkness, salvation out of error, life from the dead, an entrance into
paradise, from which Adam had been cast out, into which he entered again
through the thief, as the Lord said: "Today you shall be with me in paradise,"
into which Paul also entered; and an ascent into the heavens, "where as
forerunner he entered for us," the Lord's man, in whom he is to judge the
living and the dead.
2.1 We believe likewise in the Holy Spirit, who searches all things, "even the
depths of God," anathematizing the doctrines that think contrary to this. 2.2
For we do not hold a Son-Father, as the Sabellians do, saying of one essence
and not of the same essence, and thereby destroying the being of the Son. 2.3
Nor do we ascribe to the Father the passible body which he wore for the
salvation of the whole world. 2.4 Nor three hypostases divided each by
themselves, as one may reckon of men according to bodily nature, lest we hold
polytheism as the nations do; but as a river generated from a spring is not
divided, though they are two forms and two names. 2.5 For neither is the
Father the Son, nor is the Son the Father. For the Father is Father of the
Son, and the Son is Son of the Father. 2.6 For as the spring is not a river,
nor the river a spring, yet both are one and the same water conveyed from the
spring into the river, so the Godhead from the Father into the Son is without
flowing away and without division. 2.7 For the Lord says: "I came forth from
the Father and I have come." Yet he is always with the Father, he who is in
the bosom of the Father. 2.8 And the bosom of the Father was never emptied of
the Godhead of the Son. For he says: "I was beside him, arranging." 2.9 And we
do not hold the God who created all things, the Son of God, to be a creature
or a made thing or from things that are not; he who is from him who is, the
only one from the Only One, inasmuch as the like glory and power was begotten
together from the Father eternally. For "he who has seen the Son has seen the
Father also." 2.10 All things, clearly, were created through the Son, but he
himself is not a creature, as Paul says concerning the Lord: "that in him all
things were created" and "he is before all things." 2.11 And he does not say
that he was created before all things, but that he is before all things. "To
have been created," then, is laid upon all things; but "he is before all
things" belongs to the Son alone.
3.1 He is therefore by nature an offspring, perfect from the Perfect, begotten
"before all the hills," that is, before every rational and intelligent
essence. As Paul also says of him in another place, "firstborn of all
creation." 3.2 But by calling him firstborn he shows that he is not a creature
but an offspring of the Father. For it is alien to his Godhead to be called a
creature. 3.3 For all things were created by the Father through the Son, but
the Son alone was begotten of the Father eternally. 3.4 Therefore "he is
firstborn of all creation," God the Word; unchangeable from the unchangeable.
The body, then, which he wore for our sake is a creature. 3.5 Concerning which
Jeremiah says, according to the edition of the Seventy interpreters: "The Lord
created for us a new salvation for a planting, in which salvation men shall go
about." 3.6 And according to Aquila the same saying stands: "The Lord created
a new thing in the female." And the new salvation created for us for a
planting, and not old, for us and not before us, is Jesus who became man
according to the Savior. 3.7 Who is interpreted in one way as salvation, in
another as Savior. And salvation is from the Savior, just as illumination is
from the light. 3.8 The new salvation created from the Savior, then, as
Jeremiah says, "He created for us a new salvation," and as Aquila says, "The
Lord created a new thing in the female," that is, in Mary. 3.9 For nothing new
was created in the female except the Lord's body born from Mary the Virgin
without intercourse. 3.10 As also in Proverbs he says in the person of Jesus:
"The Lord created me a beginning of his ways for his works." And he does not
say, "He created me before the works," lest anyone take the saying of the
Godhead of the Word.
4.1 Both sayings, then, that concern the creature have been written of Jesus
in a bodily sense. For the Lord's man was created as a beginning of ways,
which he manifested to us for salvation. 4.2 For through this we have access
to the Father. For he himself is the way, as he says: "I am the way" that
leads you up to the Father. 4.3 And a way is a bodily sight, which is the
Lord's man. The Word of God, then, created all things, not being a creature
but an offspring. 4.4 For none of the creatures created anything equal or like
itself. To a father it belongs to beget, to an artisan to create. 4.5 The
body, then, which the Lord wore for our sake is a made thing and a creature,
"which was begotten for us," as Paul says, "wisdom from God and sanctification
and righteousness and redemption." And yet before us and before all creation
the Word was, and is, the Wisdom of the Father. 4.6 And the Holy Spirit, being
a procession from the Father, is always in the hands of the Father who sends
and of the Son who bears him, through whom he filled all things. 4.7 The
Father, holding his being from himself, begot the Son, as we said, and did not
create him, as a river from a spring and as a shoot from a root and as
radiance from light, things which nature knows to be indivisible; through whom
to the Father be glory, might, and majesty before all the ages and unto all
the ages of ages. Amen.
