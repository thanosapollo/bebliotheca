* On Repentance and on the Reading concerning David and the Wife of Uriah

Of our father among the saints John, Archbishop of Constantinople, Chrysostom,
On repentance, and on the reading of David concerning the wife of Uriah.
=64.11=
Behold, today the blessed David has been read to us, the musician of
temperance and teacher. For when he moves his spiritual lyre through the
Psalms, he charms the hearing, but he makes the mind temperate. For this
reason the grace of the Spirit has arranged that he be sung every day by
nearly every Christian soul, so that we may both gladden the hearing and
benefit the soul. And it often comes upon me to wonder, and often I have been
beside myself in thought, reasoning and saying, Why, beyond all the scriptures
in the Old and in the New Testament, do all so love the book of David, and
have all practiced carrying this one alone upon their lips? Moses was a great
lawgiver, who beheld God face to face; who, making known to us all creation
from the beginning from above, and proclaiming the Creator, said: In the
beginning God made the heaven and the earth. And of this man there is a book,
very great and filled with all wisdom, and scarcely once or twice a week do we
unfold it in each church. But you say, It belongs to the Old Testament.
And what of the reading of the holy Gospels? where the wonders of Christ's
coming are proclaimed; where God mixes with men, and death is dissolved by a
word; where demons are put to flight by command, and lepers are cleansed by a
word; where the blind have what nature lacked healed with clay, and five
thousand are fed in the desert from five loaves; where a robber is housed in
paradise, and a harlot is found purer than the stars; where the streams of the
Jordan are sanctified for the sanctification of our souls, and the
only-begotten Son is proclaimed from the Father from heaven; where Christ
renews our life, and in renewing it, proclaiming it for the salvation of the
race of men, says: Blessed are the =64.12= poor in spirit, for theirs is the
kingdom of the heavens. Blessed are they who hunger and thirst for
righteousness, for they shall be filled? But see, even these laws we read once
or twice in the week. But you will say that royal crowns ought not to be
published every day. And what of the blessed apostle Paul, the orator of
Christ, the fisherman of the inhabited world, who through fourteen epistles,
as through spiritual nets, netted the whole inhabited world unto salvation;
who was caught up as far as the third heaven, and again into paradise; who was
entrusted with the mysteries of the kingdom of God, which it is not lawful for
a man to speak? Since even this man we read twice in the week, and we carry
his epistle upon our lips; but having stretched our mind only toward the
reading, we follow this alone at that time. What then can I say concerning the
blessed David, how the grace of the Spirit has arranged that he be proclaimed
every day and night? For all of us carry him upon our lips in place of myrrh.
In church there are all-night vigils, and first and middle and last is David;
in the dawn hymnodies, first and middle and last is David; in the tents of the
dead, funeral processions, and first and last is David; in the houses of
virgins, weaving at the loom, and first and middle and last is David. O the
paradoxical things! Many who have not even had the first experience of
letters, having learned it, recite the whole of David by heart. But not only
in the cities and the churches does he so shine at every time and at every
age, but even in fields and in deserts and in the uninhabited land, with
greater zeal, he raises up sacred choirs to God. In monasteries a holy choir
of angelic ranks, and first and middle and last is David; in the houses of
convents of virgins, flocks of those who imitate Mary =64.13=, and first and
middle and last is David; in deserts, crucified men conversing with God, and
first and middle and last is David. And all men, tyrannized by the natural
sleep of night, are dragged down into the deep; and David alone stands over
them, and rouses the servants of God to angelic all-night vigils, making the
earth heaven, and making men equal to angels, ordering our life in every way,
and becoming all things to all, and through all making our life angelic;
growing with children, calling the young to understanding, granting temperance
to virgins, giving security to the aged, calling sinners to repentance and
saying: Confess to God, for he is good; securing those who have succeeded from
repentance and saying: Remember not the sins of my youth and my ignorance;
rousing those who have received benefactions to thanksgiving and saying: What
shall I render to the Lord for all that he has rendered to me? recalling those
who sin often to confession and saying: If you should mark iniquities, Lord,
Lord, who shall stand? teaching those who seek mercy from God and saying: Have
mercy on me, O God, according to your great mercy; securing those called to
priesthood and saying: Cast me not away from your face; teaching those dragged
into court and saying: Redeem me from the calumny of men; admonishing those
who fear an uprising of enemies and saying: Deliver me from my enemies, O God;
rousing those who endure and are benefited to thanksgiving and saying:
Waiting, I waited for the Lord, and he attended to me, and he heard my
petition.
O great kithara, striking the souls of the men of the inhabited world, as if
they were strings, into one confession! And how was such a one and so great,
who orders the manners of men, and who conquers the words of encomia, who
established the polity of the angels upon earth, and is crowned with the
praise of the angels in heaven; how was he permitted to slip into the two
chief heads of sins? For the heads of sins are, You shall not murder, you
shall not commit adultery; and he was permitted to be brought down to these
two heads of sins. For this reason, often being by myself, and having reasoned
these things, I have been beside myself in thought; and just as a ship on the
open sea without a helmsman, carried here and there, is tossed by the waves,
so often has my reasoning been shaken by the surges, as I thought and said:
How did the man adorned with so many goods, the king and prophet, the servant
and father of Christ; servant by nature, father according to the flesh; for
The book of the generation of Jesus Christ, son of David; who is adorned more
by virtues than by the diadem; who was clothed in the purple, and arrayed in
humility and saying: Lord, my heart was not lifted up, nor were my eyes raised
high, nor did I walk in great things, nor in wonders beyond me; if I were not
humble-minded, but I lifted up my soul as the weaned child; who crowned the
bounds of forbearance and saying: Lord my God, if I have done this, if there
is injustice in my hands, if I have repaid those who repay me with evils; who
not only shakes off pride, but also turns away from the proud and saying: He
who does pride shall not dwell in the midst of my house; he who speaks unjust
things has not directed himself before my eyes; who says with boldness to God:
You have tried me with fire, and injustice was not found in me. And what am I
saying? Why have I used his own testimonies? And often some say: One who
testifies for himself is not trustworthy; let another testify to this man's
achievements, let another proclaim his crowns, let another bear witness to
this man's life, so that the testimony may become more trustworthy. And whom
do we have more trustworthy than God, who says: I have found David the son of
Jesse, a man after =64.14= my heart. What is more secure than this testimony?
And how was he who is crowned with so many achievements, who is testified to
be after the heart of God, how was he permitted to be brought down into the
heads of sins? You shall not murder, you shall not commit adultery; and is the
Prophet of God a murderer and an adulterer? For this passage was read to you
today. I do not slander the Prophet, God forbid, but I secure your life, so
that when you have achieved many things, you may secure yourself, lest you
fall. For so also the Apostle said: Let him who thinks he stands, look lest he
fall. For this reason David himself also said more frequently: Unto the end,
destroy not; for this reason he himself also praying said: And unto old age
and advanced years, O God, do not abandon me. For what reason then was he
permitted to be brought down into so great a sin? For three things; of what
sort, I will say; only you attend with all accuracy. First, that the righteous
may be secured concerning their own life, whether ascetics or desert-dwellers,
and may not say, I have achieved many things, I have been adorned with many
virtues; I fast, I keep vigil, I pray, I weep, I have put on sackcloth, I have
withered my body with ascetic labors; I no longer fear the uprisings of the
devil,
the things of the crowns have now been achieved for me. Do not be deceived, O
man, do not be lifted up in mind; you have not done more than David; hear him
saying: My knees have grown weak from fasting, and my flesh has been changed
through oil; and again, When they troubled me I put on sackcloth, and I
humbled my soul with fasting. Hear him saying concerning vigil: At midnight I
rose to confess to you; and again, Seven times a day I praised you; hear him
saying concerning tears: I have grown weary in my groaning; I will wash my bed
every night; I will wet my couch with my tears; hear him saying concerning
ascesis: For I ate ashes as bread, and I mixed my drink with weeping.
And why do I tell you the things of the prophet David? Hear again the
testimony of God that says: I have found David the son of Jesse, a man after
my heart. And yet after so many achievements he was brought down into such
falls. Do not then be confident, but every day secure yourself, receiving the
exhortation of the Apostle that says: Let him who thinks he stands look lest
he fall. Behold the first cause; take also the second: that sinners may not
despair of themselves, but if someone also sins every day, that he may confess
every day. Even if you sin ten thousand times, confess ten thousand times; for
nothing is worse than despair. If someone, having sinned, despairs over sin,
he no longer runs toward healing. Do you not see the physicians? if they
despair of someone, they no longer apply treatment, but they say to the sick
man: If you wish to give us anything, we do not take it; for this disease no
longer admits of treatment; the wickedness of the disease has conquered the
treatment of the art. These things they say when they give up; but if they
expect health, they do all things, and they apply medicines for the salvation
of the one who is sick.
So if someone despairs over sin, thinking that there is no salvation for the
one who repents, he thrusts himself into greater evils. For this reason, if
you consider that even of the extreme sins, murder and adultery, there has
been treatment and healing toward the prophet, even if you sin ten thousand
times, you may give yourself over to confession. And I say this, not urging
toward sinning, but calling those who sin to confession; since God, being a
lover of men, even wipes out the great sins. For what is greater than murder
and adultery? and yet hear the Prophet saying, the very one who sinned: I
said, I will confess against myself my lawlessness to the Lord, and you
forgave the impiety of my heart.
For this reason Christ also, having come, forgave the great sins; he brought
the robber into paradise, he made the tax-collector an evangelist, he made the
insolent and =64.15= blasphemous Paul an apostle of the inhabited world, so
that you too, sinning, may not despair, but confessing may receive the
healing. Confess to the Lord, for he is good, for his mercy is forever. Behold
the second cause; take also the third. Our Saviour was going to come according
to his incarnate presence, and to take up the lump of our flesh, and to live
among men; but since he was God and alone sinless, he permitted the righteous,
according to their own choice, to fall into human slips; not himself calling
them into the sin itself, but by choice permitting them to go according to
their own aim. So then Abraham also sinned by disbelieving, and for this he
received punishment from God: And your seed shall serve four hundred years.
And again Moses, not glorifying God, when he granted the water from the rock
to the ungrateful; and for this God said to him: And you shall see the land of
the promise, and you shall not enter into it. So also he permitted this man to
fall into sin, so that he himself alone might be found sinless in a human
=64.16= body. For this reason the prophet David also, when at last he came to
the confession of the sin, said this to God: Against you alone have I sinned,
and I have done the evil before you. Why? That you may be justified in your
words, and may conquer when you are judged.
When that day comes, he says; there our lump is to appear, seated at the right
hand of the majesty of God; for Sit at my right hand, until I make your
enemies a footstool of your feet. On that day then the body also will appear,
which he took up from our nature, as men are being called to account. A body
above and a body below; For they shall look, he says, on him whom they
pierced. But the one weighed down with sins, that is ours; but the one that
has not done sin, but that exacts the penalties of the sins, is divine. For
this reason: That you may be justified in your words, and may conquer when you
are judged. How? Because he did not commit sin, nor was deceit found in his
mouth. To him be the glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
