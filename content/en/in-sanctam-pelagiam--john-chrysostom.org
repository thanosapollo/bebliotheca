* On Saint Pelagia
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of the same, an encomium on the same holy martyr Pelagia.
Pelagia needed a greater theatre; for great are the girl's contests and
worthy of a many-peopled spectacle. Yet Christ suffices instead of the
whole world, present at the virgin's festival; for where Christ is present,
there is the choir of all the angels. Every martyr then, showing the body
more contentious of swords and tortures, prepares a great spectacle for the
devil, conquering the bodiless through a body and contending against iron
through flesh. But when I see even girls travailing the death on behalf of
the Crucified, I laugh more broadly at the devil's audacity, who fabricating
very many oracles, as if through them he were announcing the future, did not
today divine his own mockery, which is worth much laughter. For what more
ridiculous thing could one find than the mockery which the devil endured
today? For having a virgin in a net, he had fallen out of the hunt, and
having taken hold of the girl he did not take her, as of a shadow, not
having grasped her herself. For the girl, mixed with both the simplicity of
the dove and the prudence of the serpent, ** as a simple dove she was
caught, but as a prudent serpent she slipped aside. For having been caught
she did not despair of the victory, nor was her understanding held fast
together with the flesh; but she was looking for a device that would strike
back at the audacity of those who had seized her. What then? She pretends
repentance, and, shipwrecking in so great a storm, she orders brightness
upon her face. And they, baited by the fiction and stolen by the girl's
cheerfulness, felt something even humane toward her and, when she asked to
put on a bridal appearance, they grant her leave, as if they would not only
gratify the virgin but also appear better to the judge, if they should lead
away a girl who had been made beautiful. And she, having obtained the
artifice, immediately put on the true adornment; for having girt herself
with daring and having put on the hopes of the resurrection, running to the
wing of the roof, from there she lets herself down, and she conquers a form
of wrestling which as a great thing <ὁ> the devil had once dared to propose
to the Lord, saying, If you are the Son of God, throw yourself down. ***
Before the faith I am struck by the girl's daring; for who would not have
stirred in herself: I give myself over to a forced fall, fearing to be
prostituted; the purpose is good, if the fall should be confirmed by death;
for if they outrage a dead woman, I shall not know it; but if, dashed
against the earth, I should shatter the vital parts of the body, yet the
soul remain in the members, mourning the body's mutilation, I shall suffer
what I fear, being brought to the judge. For having shattered the body and
given it over to fornication, they will release me dishonored, so that my
misfortune would be double, both the body's use having been harmed by the
fall and virginity having been plundered from me. These things would have
troubled another girl; but she, having taken courage in the faith as in a
surety of the end, rushed toward the daring of the fall. You have been
caught by a girl, devil; you have been conquered by a virgin's daring; for
the things you proposed to your own Master, a girl, a slave of his,
challenges you to these, and running to the wing of the roof, from there she
hurls herself. She does not obey you when called through a judge; she does
not accept the interpolated fight; for she recognizes the intent of your
cunning; often you call virgins to judges as to scourges, and without a
fight you throw them out more bitterly than those held from a fight. But if
you truly call to battle-array, or you send for the girl to the
wrestling-grounds, wrestle with her as she throws herself from the roof;
wait for her as she falls from there. Stand and try the wrestling; whet
whatever cunning you wish; you have the earth instead of a wrestling-ground;
plant in it the sword of death; strew a man-killing hardness underneath;
prepare crushing for her as she comes down; for she will somersault over all
your dens, as if they were softer than a baptismal pool. And what is
greater, she does not even demand of God what is written, that he command
the angels concerning her, lest her body dash against a stone; but that he
command the soul to emigrate after the fall of the flesh. O you who were
created into the female kind, but shown male in mind. O you fought over by
flocks of virgins and choirs of martyrs. O you who did not even grant
enjoyment as far as sight to a wanton judge. Let us then ourselves also
pursue the girl's chastity, let us take up the trophies against pleasures,
let us break incoming licentiousness, let us grip piety before the soul, let
us mine a judge out of temptations, let us display timely darings, let us
mortify our members that are upon the earth, that the Lord also may recast
the body of our humiliation so that it become conformed to his body, to whom
be the glory and the power unto the ages. Amen.
