ORIGEN'S SELECTIONS ON THE DEUTERONOMY.
=17.24= CH. 1.
Verse 31. As a man would nurse. Aquila: As if a nurse would lift, he bore the
son, he interpreted. But Origen in the Homilies on Jeremiah says: "He was
borne along with you and came down with you, making our weakness his own; as a
teacher lisping with little children, as a father nursing his own children,
and slipping into their ways, and little by little leading them on to the more
perfect and higher things." CH. 3. Verse 18. And I commanded you. These things
he says to the tribe of Reuben, and to the tribe of Gad, and to the half-tribe
of Manasseh. Verse 27, 28. He gives more to Moses than to Aaron, the seeing of
the land as an eyewitness. But the entrance he does not give, having laid this
penalty for the deficiency of faith, according to which he removes both from
life outside the promised land, even though they were able to arrive, since
the end is given through faith. Which is also a symbol for the law, which
cannot perfect nor restore the promise, because the just shall live by faith.
And this is an honor, to know the judgment of God, and to know beforehand
one's own end. Of the same. He leads Joshua forward, already sanctified from
the following, and from remaining with Moses, but about to receive the ruling
spirit through Moses toward also appointing his own in his place. Just as
Elisha also was present in the spirit of Elijah to do the work of Elijah; for
in the same spirit one must work the same things. Take to yourself, as he says
in the Numbers, Joshua son of Nave, a man who has the spirit of God in
himself, and you shall lay your hands upon him. So also the apostles, having a
life-giving Spirit =17.25= from the breathing of the Lord, receive a spirit,
the ruling one sent down from heaven; not a spirit in a spirit, another upon
another; but an energy in the same spirit, as Paul teaches. CH. 4. Verse 12.
And the Lord spoke to you from the midst of the fire a voice of words, which
you heard. Of the same on the same. Another says: For it is clear that with
the eyes of the mind one sees and hears the thoughts. A voice of words. Here
he has spoken the things according to nature, that hearing receives the voice.
For as far as the precision of the letter, the Decalogue alone was written on
the tablets, but the rest through the command of God Moses taught. CH. 14.
Verse 19. All the creeping things of the birds shall be unclean. Of the birds,
I think, as many as have spurs below the knees above the feet, he wants to be
clean; and as many as do not, unclean. The law therefore denies the eagle, and
the griffin, and the sea-eagle, and indeed also the vulture. For such fly
high, and love to pride themselves in the very highest things, and hate the
things below; For the young, he says, of a vulture fly the high places. And in
some such way is he who is very supercilious, and has the mind lifted up in
himself, and is accustomed to look down on the reasonable and moderate mind,
and least loves to be carried along with the humble. And together with these
he also condemns the kite, and the things like it; through which is very well
signified the race of men that knows how to seize and is terrible in villainy.
And of these the prototype is the kite, not indeed of the other the blackest
and deep. And somehow dark and not evident to all are those swift into
wickedness and unprofitable to the many, furnishing wickedness in themselves
at least. There is somehow always attached to those who are sick with accursed
boastfulness the willing to overreach; whom the ordinary cannot overreach even
with many-formed inventions of villainies; and so they snatch this too
insatiably. In this way then he joins saying immediately: And a sparrow, and a
gull. For these I think are insatiable, always collecting what falls in. Then
he says, a hawk and the things like it, and a night-raven, and a cormorant,
and an ibis, and a porphyrion, and a pelican, and a swan. And these too one
would register against themselves, both the raw and those who plot against
others wildly, and who attach themselves unrestrainedly to whomsoever they
might happen to be able; =17.28= so that he himself adduces that he does not
decline any danger and fear for life. For the hawk leaps down terribly upon
every weaker bird, and rushes against sparrows, and having taken them destroys
them. The cormorant indeed and ibis and porphyrion, and I would add also
pelican, set upon the fishes, and make their destruction a luxury. And such
are all, as far as savagery of manners is concerned, who count it as nothing
to destroy some for what is sweet to themselves, who have thrust aside the law
of mutual love, and have struck against the ordinances of love. CH. 16. Verse
19. And he takes away words of the just. Verse 20. Justly you shall pursue the
just. Of the same from the letter to Photius and Andrew the presbyters. That
is, after knowing and being educated in the words of righteousness, you shall
pursue and seek the just, being clean of every partiality; and in this way,
being just, you shall seek the just, and fenced round from every side, and
standing within the bounds of righteousness. For many, the very willing to be
just, having intoxicated the mind through their precise righteousness, have
done injustice to righteousness by righteousness. Whence also the intelligent
Ecclesiast, or rather the educator of intelligence (for Christ is the true
Ecclesiast, the head of the Church, and of God our Father the highest and
hypostatic wisdom and word), choking what is beyond measure, even if it is
carried to the right, exhorts: Do not become just much, and do not be wise in
extras. CH. 19. Verse 21. Soul for soul, eye for eye. These things the law, as
the Israelites being weak; but the Lord who legislated these things says to
his own disciples: You have heard that it was said: An eye for an eye, and the
rest. But I say to you, not to resist the evil one. Of the same on the same.
(These things upon the sensible and the things according to the world; but
upon the things we have sinned God needs nothing. For he himself is overseer
of all and judge.) It is to be noted that also of those invited to the wedding
there was a refusal: I have bought a field, and I have married wives. CH. 20.
Verse 5. Who is the man who has built a new house, and has not dedicated it?
On the same. A type then of him who does not endure to consider the present
life a sojourning is he who builds a house, and is earnestly given to the
labors about these things; and of the lover of possessions and lover of gain,
he who plants a vineyard. And the woman-mad and =17.29= broken, and who has
given the mind to the desires of the flesh, betrothals of a woman would mark.
For when the voice of the army-herald approaches, they withdraw from the
sacred and most warlike multitude. And what such men caught would likely think
and say, these the herald, having anticipated, proclaims. Or is it not true to
think such things of these? They are accustomed, loving in persecutions to
love freedom from toil, and to follow the zeals for the love toward God,
thinking that, if they should happen to suffer while struggling, they fall
away from houses and possessions, and from the desires honored among them.
Verse 8. Who is the man who is fearing and cowardly in heart? Of the same. He
who is given to earthly things, and building houses, and planting vineyards,
overcome by the pleasures of flesh-love, will be altogether cowardly and
without strength. Verse 10. And if you approach a city to war against it. Like
the: And into whatever city you enter, inquire who of them is worthy. And the
"with peace" is like the apostolic: that a servant of the Lord must not fight.
Verse 13. And you shall strike every male of it with the slaughter of the
sword. Of the same. And a sword of the spirit would be the word of God. And
the sword of the spirit, which is the word of God, with which the divine
disciples killed every male of the opposing power, as Simon and Elymas the
magicians, and they also dissolved their strong doctrines; but they preserved
the women, and souls able to say: Because of your fear, Lord, we have
conceived in the womb until upon the earth; and the infants likewise, to whom
also Paul writes: Little children, do not become infants in the minds, but in
malice be infants. And cattle too, he says, preserve. For a just man pities
the souls of his cattle; hinting through the cattle at the more irrational.
Cornelius too was a beast, according to the vision about the four-footed that
appeared to Peter. CH. 22. Verse 6. You shall not take the mother with the
children. Since they cannot feed themselves, when the mother has been taken.
CH. 23. Verse 3. An Ammonite shall not enter. Why then does he forbid the
mixing with Moabites and Ammonites not according to some stated time, but unto
the age? First because of their lawless root; for they have been born from a
lawless intercourse; then because of the impiety, of which they also prepared
the Israelites =17.32= to partake, having beautified the women, and through
these hunting them into the worship of the idols. The Idumean however and the
Egyptian he commands to be admitted after the third generation, the one as a
brother, the other as a benefactor who came to be in a time of need. For when
famine fell, their fathers having entered into Egypt were fed. The law
therefore teaches us not even to be unmindful of an old benefaction. Verse 14.
What is it, That the Lord your God walks about in your camp? He knows that
often the air, being filled with foul smell, produces a plague. He commanded
them therefore when encamped to bury the dung that is excreted. And so that
they may not, having disobeyed, receive harm, he necessarily added: That the
Lord God walks about in your camp. And he teaches the cause: To deliver you,
and to give over your enemy before your face. And through this he trains them
to do nothing lawless. For this he adds: And your camp shall be holy, and
there shall not be found in you a matter of unseemliness. Verse 12. And let
the place be for you, he says, outside the camp, and you shall go out there.
Verse 13. And a peg shall be for you upon your belt. And it shall be when you
sit outside, you shall dig with it, and bringing it back you shall cover your
own unseemliness. Verse 14. That the Lord your God walks about in the camp to
deliver you, and to give over your enemy into your hands. And your camp shall
be holy, and unseemliness of a matter shall not be seen in you. For it is
fitting that those who desire to be men against the intelligible enemies
decline every form of uncleanness. And since we all stumble much, and the
nature of man is sick with the slipperiness into the faulty; and no one will
understand trespasses, according to the voice of the Psalmist; Let it be, he
says, that by one of the inborn uncleannesses even in you one is all but
shattered, and is caught having become sick with something of the things
outside law and a life befitting holiness. For in the members of the flesh the
law of sin dwells. First then let the faults done from the weakness not be
done clearly and unhidden, nor indeed in the sight of many; but as it were
outside, having escaped notice, and as far as possible from the camp; then let
it be covered by the corrections, he says; For the wise will cover their own
shames, according to what is written. For ceasing from the faults, and by the
inclinations toward the better re-elementing the mind in a way, toward the
need to choose what benefits, we put the stumblings out of the midst; and
having conquered the unseemly sin by the fragrances of what follows, we shall
enter again into the camp of the holy, that is into the church of the
firstborn. And this is fulfilled in Christ, if we should take his cross, using
a keen and most well-girt mind. For our having a peg upon the belt, that is
the =17.33= wood, this hints obliquely. Making then spiritually the bodily
things a type of the intelligible; for we are not in uncleannesses, going into
latrines in the eyes of the God who made, to help. We must therefore bury
uncleannesses, Because the Lord walks in the camp. For Christ dwells in us and
walks about. CH. 24. Verse 6. You shall not take in pledge a mill, nor an
upper millstone, because thus he takes a soul in pledge. On the same
otherwise. A mill is not, nor an upper millstone, a soul, but it contributes
toward the working of bread, through which especially men live. It is to be
noted him through the blood. For also concerning it it was said: That the
blood of the body is soul. These things then are to be understood
catachrestically. For neither the blood, nor the mill is a soul, but things
contributing toward living. CH. 27. Verse 15. And he shall set it in secret.
Since it was likely that some, in fear of the punishment, would be eager to
escape notice, and in a hiding-place and secretly to practice the impiety;
toward a greater fear and threat of those doing these things, he also subjects
to curses those who work the sin without witness. Such are those who play-act
a feigned piety, but bury the passions within, and in the movement of the mind
are driven frantic toward every form of absurdity. These are those who set the
idols in secret. Verse 17. Cursed is he who moves the boundaries of the
neighbor. He also moves the boundaries of the neighbor who from the true
knowledge of God, and the godly way of life, unsettles the one found within.
And the things that follow make it clear. Verse 18. Cursed is he who leads a
blind man astray on a road. Of the same. Such also are those who from idolatry
even draw those of the circumcision toward abominable heresies. For being
blind and not yet having had the light of the truth and knowledge of God, they
deceive and lead astray, showing another road instead of the apostolic. Verse
19. Cursed is whoever turns aside the judgment of a sojourner and an orphan
and a widow. Under the same curse he sets those who likewise turn aside the
judgment of a sojourner and an orphan and a widow, having said the same thing
again in another way. For if the letter has the beneficial, yet also the
contemplation has the higher. For sojourners are those who enter from the
nations into the holy Church of God. And orphans properly are those who have
indeed renounced their own father Satan, but have not yet put on Jesus, but
are still numbered among the catechumens. And likewise these too are widows,
having turned away from him who sows in them the seeds of destruction; but not
yet betrothed to Christ, as a pure virgin, according to the divine Apostle.
Those therefore who turn aside the judgment of these, and do not hand over to
them the unadulterated mystery of piety, are under a curse. And the other
things too someone going through laboriously will be able to contemplate
spiritually, the interpretation being preserved. Verse 26. Cursed is every man
who does not remain in all the words of the law. The "in all," lying in the
Seventy, is confirmed from the Samaritan copy, in which the chol, which is to
all or all things, we found being carried. With whom the Apostle also agrees
saying: For as many as are of works of law are under a curse. Whence those of
the circumcision are convicted of being under a curse, the not doing all
things. CH. 33. Verse 6. Let Reuben live, and not die. Since Jacob dying
cursed Reuben saying: You shall not boil over as water. Moses blessing says:
Let Reuben live, and not die. Both well. For the father cursed him as having
come together with the concubine, training and frightening those after; and
Moses justly blesses after these things; since he alone spared Joseph, when
his brothers wished to kill him. And it was absurd that a curse should go
forward against one who had sinned, but a blessing not be given to one who had
done right.
