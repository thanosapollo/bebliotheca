Of Eusebius of Pamphilus the bishop. Of Caesarea of Palestine concerning the
local names in the divine Scripture. Just as in a preface to the subject set
before you, holy man of God Paulinus, in the work before this rendering the
promised subjects, and first transferring into the Greek tongue the
appellations lying in the divine Scripture with Hebrew names of the nations
throughout the inhabited world, then having made a description of ancient
Judaea from every book, and dividing the allotments of the twelve tribes in
it, and upon these, as in the manner of a drawing, having delineated the image
of their ancient famous metropolis, I mean Jerusalem, and of the temple in it,
with the addition of the notes upon the places, next in this work and in
sequence to those things, as already prepared for the usefulness of the whole
subject, I will attach your purpose, setting out the meanings of the cities
and villages borne in the divine Scripture in the ancestral tongue, of what
country they would be, and how those of our time name them, whether similarly
to the ancients or also changing them otherwise. From all the God-inspired
Scripture, then, I will gather the things sought, and I will set each out
according to the letter, for ready grasp of those that fall scattered in the
readings. Letter A. Of Genesis. Ararat ̣Gen 8, 4̣. Armenia; on the mountains
of Ararat the ark is said to have settled, and they say that until now
remnants of the ark are shown on the mountains of Ar 4 menia. Jeremiah also
mentions Ararat in the vision against Babylon. And Josephus too, in the first
book of the Jewish Antiquities, relating the things about the place, adduces
witnesses also from those outside, writing in these very words: "And Noah,
learning that the earth was freed from the flood, remaining another seven
days, sends out the animals of the ark, and he himself coming forth with his
family and sacrificing to God feasted with his household. The Armenians,
however, call this place Apobaterion. For there, the ark having been saved,
the people of the region still now show its remnants. And of this flood and of
the ark all who have written the barbarian histories make mention, among whom
is also Berosus the Chaldaean. For relating the things about the flood he goes
through it somewhere thus: And it is said that of the vessel in Armenia toward
the mountain of the Cordyaeans some part still exists, and that some bring
away from the asphalt. And people use what is brought especially for
apotropaic rites. Hieronymus the Egyptian also mentions these things, who
wrote the Phoenician archaeology, and Mnaseas too and many others. And
Nicolaus of Damascus also in the ninety-sixth book relates about them saying
thus: There is above Minyas a great mountain in Armenia, called Baris, on
which a report has it that many who fled together at the time of the flood
were saved, and that someone riding upon an ark ran aground upon the peak, and
that the remnants of the timbers were preserved for a long time. And this
would be the one whom Moses the lawgiver of the Jews also recorded." Achad
