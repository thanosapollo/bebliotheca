72 1 Cor 1:1-2 Both are understood together, both the persons of those writing
and of the Corinthians to whom the letter was being written, instead of: I and
Sosthenes write with all those among us who greet this calling, taking all
such persons among us as partners of the authority of writing, and not
receiving into this fellowship the one who is alien to this address. But to
you Corinthians we send these things, to those sanctified in Christ and called
through him together with all simply those among you who greet this name. 173
1 Cor 1:7-8 For the rest, he says, being filled with the good things it is
fitting to look toward the things to come, awaiting the second coming of
Christ, when it is possible to receive the enjoyment of all the good things
together; for he hinted through this that those established in this life
cannot be without care over the things already given, thinking highly, if
indeed we wait for his second coming for the judgment of the things we have
lived. 1 Cor 1:12-13 That is, according to a portion some have been allotted
to receive Christ, others another; some Cephas, others Apollos who is said to
have been the first bishop among the Corinthians. 1 Cor 1:17-18 The cross of
the Savior has not been emptied, because it does not have the venerable and
admirable in bare words but in the deed itself. Yet it has been reckoned
foolishness among those who do not know the truth, because there is not in it
the brilliance as in diction, just as indeed also among their own select
speakers. But those, coloring falsehood with fine speech, fall short in
nothing from those who mix poisons with honey; whereas those who have become
ministers of the divine proclamations set forth the truth simple, as it were,
and free of outward adornments. 1 Cor 1:22-25 Instead of: against all the
opponents of piety we put forward the cross of Christ. And explaining the
things concerning the cross he says that the Jews think it a dishonor, but
Greeks foolishness, while we believe it to be both power and wisdom of God,
knowing accurately 174 the magnitude of the things accomplished through it;
but power of God now and wisdom not the divinity of the Only-begotten, but the
preaching concerning the cross and, as it were, the very nature of the matter
we suppose to have been said. And he also refutes, they say, those of Arius
and Eunomius who try badly from here to show that the God Word is named
wisdom. For from here they also construct the blasphemy from the Proverbial
voice. And the divine apostle, having shown the feebleness of natural
intelligence and the silliness of those who think highly of it, and this from
the joint examination of the facts, toward pulling down the pride of the one
who for this reason wishes to rule and take first place among the rest. And
having shown the silliness of those who think highly of it, as if it were set
in the portion of a good even to be called from it, he moves to a third
demonstration, very shaming, the one from themselves. 1 Cor 1:26 That he may
say: among you according to this life. 1 Cor 1:23 In Romans the things that
are and are not were understood otherwise according to the sequence of what
lies before; but here the things that are not he calls the cheap and among the
many contemptible, and the things that are the brilliant and powerful and
well-reputed. 1 Cor 1:29 All flesh, every fleshly man having his mind on
fleshly things, such as rich, wise, if anyone is such, that he may not boast
as accomplishing the preaching through his own glory, but through the grace of
God; for boasting, even if it comes to be upon good works, harms the soul of
the one who boasts. Therefore the one who boasts in worldly advantages and
does not humble himself is altogether fleshly. 1 Cor 2:6-7 He calls wisdom the
word concerning the cross and the whole economy, which was hidden, which we
also preach. And rulers of this age he calls the wicked powers. And if these
were ignorant, much more the men through whom the demons crucified the Lord.
But others say rulers of this age are Herod and Pilate.
175 1 Cor 2:12 For he names world the whole creation, as elsewhere he says
that we have become a spectacle to the world and to angels and to men. 1 Cor
2:12-13 Those who have the spirit of the world cannot know the mysteries of
God, but we who have received the spirit from God. Which things we also speak,
the mysteries revealed to us and granted for the salvation of the economy,
making clear the spiritual teachings to spiritual men. 1 Cor 2:13 Instead of:
through the wonders of the Spirit we confirm the teaching of the Spirit; for
"comparing" he does not say instead of "examining side by side," but instead
of "demonstrating," having called demonstrating "comparing" for the present
from the metaphor of those who by comparison make the demonstration of the
more approved greater.
1 Cor 3:9 Fellow-workers, instead of servants and ministers. 1 Cor 3:14-15 The
finest of the teachings are not destroyed together with the present life but
remain more brilliant, being shown then upon the works. Whoever therefore
teaches these things, he says, which can also then remain more greatly when
the Master has appeared as Lord, will altogether receive the reward for these;
but whoever teaches the contrary, he says, can somehow prevail until the
present life, but is altogether made to vanish at the time of the
consummation. Not only is he deprived of the reward of the labors here which
he could have reaped choosing to labor for the finest things, but even if he
is saved through some other cause able to save him, not having escaped the
examination through fire, which he must undergo for the worst teachings; for
this he says: "but so as through fire." 176 The sequence of the thought wants
it thus: but thus he shall be burned as through fire. Or rather since he said:
but he himself shall be saved, he says this, that the disobedience of those
taught does not condemn the teacher; for even if he will not receive a reward
but will be condemned, yet he will be saved, unless the fire detains him as he
passes for his own faults. Thus therefore, he says, he shall be saved, not as
altogether escaping the judgment, but as being tested through fire upon his
own works. 1 Cor 3:16 The one who believes in Christ through the regeneration
of the washing receives the Holy Spirit as indwelling and is spiritual. But if
they serve worldly passions, according to this they are fleshly. He says
therefore that having become spiritual according to the confession, you live
fleshly, insulting the Holy Spirit that has dwelt in you. Thus therefore even
having obtained the Holy Spirit they are a temple of God, and being held by
worldly things they are called fleshly, and the apostle does not fight against
himself in calling the same both of God and naming them fleshly. 1 Cor 3:18-19
Just as he said that the foolishness of God is wiser than men, so conversely
he says that the wisdom of the world is foolishness with God, since through
cleverness of words they expected to find the nature of the things that are.
Therefore if anyone thinks he is wise, let him rather choose to be a fool
according to God, because the foolishness of God is wiser than men. 1 Cor 3:21
All things <yours> instead of: through you. Or he said all things are yours
according to having authority to choose what we wish, either the better or the
worse.
1 Cor 4:6 And having transferred it above also to Cephas, but here he did not
mention him, respecting the man. 177 1 Cor 4:7 Distinguishes: he searches out
and compares against another. 1 Cor 4:8 That is, we having not yet become
kings, you have anticipated becoming kings, when you have also been set as
judges of the others; for in the time of the kingdom of the heavens the saints
will judge the world. 1 Cor 4:10 We choose to be fools for Christ, that you
may become prudent in Christ. 1 Cor 4:12-13 For having upon so many things no
consolation from outside but providing the daily food by toil made the
experience of the evils unbearable. Being reviled we bless, being persecuted
we endure, being slandered we exhort. He calls reviling the insults against
him, but blasphemy the things said by the opponents for the slander of life;
whence upon the first he has set "we bless" instead of we pray for those who
insult, and upon the second "we exhort" instead of we converse gently with
those who slander, exhorting them not to lie against us. "Offscouring" has
been said from the metaphor of those who wipe the tables after the time of
eating and throw away the scrapings as superfluous. Humbling ourselves for
Christ, we are the offscouring and wiping of all, cheapness and object of
insult. 1 Cor 4:14 Shaming, insulting. 1 Cor 4:18-19 Since there were some
puffed up upon worldly wisdom, from the proem he slanders these and more
openly mocks them as trampling those wise according to God. Since therefore,
he says, they were so disposed 178 as though I were not coming and convicting
them, they also pride themselves, he said, in this weak and inoperative
wisdom; for I shall come quickly, he says, demanding of them not word but
power of wisdom.
1 Cor 5:5 He shamed them by the memory of Christ so as not to overlook what is
due by the grace toward him; to deliver such a one to Satan for the
destruction of the flesh, that the spirit may be saved in the day of our Lord
Jesus Christ. The delivering to Satan he did not say as himself defining this
to come to be, but as the one expelled from the church through alienation from
God coming under the authority of Satan; for the punishment from him was the
expelling him from the church, and from this that altogether followed. The
apostle however said the result instead of the punishment so as to frighten
more. But I expel him, he says, not wishing to alienate him entirely, but
<that> perceiving his own transgression he may by a certain repentance crush
himself according to the present life and be able to make himself worthy of
the coming salvation. He called destruction of flesh the crushing according to
the present life through repentance. And since our resurrection is going to
come to be by the power of the Spirit, often upon the resurrection and
immortality he takes the signification of the spirit; thus in Romans he says:
but you are not in flesh but in spirit, instead of having come to be outside
the sentence of death, you have been established in the hope of immortality.
Homonymously with the spirit the spiritual gift also is called which one
obtains in the washing of regeneration, which being despised is quenched.
Therefore he says: there is need of repentance, that even thus the spiritual
gift may run back to him, and he himself may be saved in the day of judgment.
1 Cor 5:7 He calls old the sin with which the old man lives together; and
leaven because from a little it assimilates those nearby. 1 Cor 5:8
Correspondingly he has now also used the inference after the sentence; for as
being present and having expelled him, thus he discourses to those present 179
henceforth and says: the one joined to evil according to a necessary
disposition receives in time the fellowship with it. And following the
example, from the facts he makes the exhortation toward them.
1 Cor 6:1-3 Someone might inquire for what reason in Romans, saying all
authority is from God and even greatly exalting it, here he called them unjust
altogether. Because there, discoursing about the need to fulfill the things
owed even to those, he says those things too have reasonably been ordered by
God, judging them in themselves from the purpose according to which they have
been ordered; but here toward those †† because of wishing by time and surplus
and power to crush them; for the poor, being able to suffice for none of
these, cannot easily obtain justice. He called them unjust from the judgment
of these, instead of taking refuge with those among whom you can cover the
injustice against the poor through many things; whence also he well said "do
you dare." Do you not know that the saints will judge the world? Decline
these, he says, you whose virtue judges the whole world, not by examination
but by comparison. By those by whom the whole world is judged, can a dispute
of money not be resolved by these? And most shaming especially is the "if
among you," including also those who decline, as if they were declining
themselves in declining those of the same faith. And by increase he says: do
you not know that we shall judge angels? For he added this as greater. Upon
the world therefore he called the judgments concerning money least, as being
exceedingly small by comparison with that, but upon the angels "matters of
this life," inasmuch as those are not according to this life. He did not wish
them to be judged before those outside, lest the faults of those being taught
gravity and righteousness become a scandal to those outside. 1 Cor 6:4-5 If
therefore you have judgments of this life, those who are despised in the
church, these set as judges. And lest he seem to command them truly to seek
the cheaper of all 180 whoever they are, and to choose these as judges upon
such things, I say this, he says, to shame you. And who ought to judge, he
adds. Accusing these things toward shaming you and bringing you to correction.
1 Cor 6:11 For these things, he says, even if you formerly practiced them, yet
now having obtained sanctification by participation of the Spirit through
faith in Christ, it is fitting to flee them with all eagerness. 1 Cor 6:12 But
this you say, he says, that all things are lawful for me. Then he himself
adds: but not all things are beneficial, not taking away the being lawful for
the time being but that one must not use the authority; for since not all
things are beneficial, it is clear that one must not use all but only those
that benefit. Then again having set the objection he treated it otherwise: all
things are lawful for me, but I will not be mastered by anything. For I see,
he says, that not even the authority is preserved for me intact, if indeed I
simply submit to all things, not making the use of them toward the beneficial.
Thus by concession of all things being lawful he has taken away the also doing
all things. Thus he shows that not even all things are lawful, but as many as
we are not forbidden by some law to do, showing by a certain comparison some
things in us, some not in us. All things are lawful for me, but it is not
beneficial to use the authority to excess, nor to be mastered by the pleasure
of foods, but himself to master the belly. Yet some have badly received "all
things are lawful for me" upon the sins lying before, which not only the
divine word but also the laws of the Romans forbid. 1 Cor 6:13-14 Nor do we
think our body is destroyed together with the present life; for the one who
raised Christ will altogether raise us also. For just as upon eating he gave
the authority both from having come to be toward this and from altogether
ceasing, so upon fornicating he established the not being lawful both from not
having come to be toward this and from the body being going to be again after
the resurrection. 181 1 Cor 6:15 You are all members of Christ, who through
the regeneration of the Spirit have received the conjunction toward him, in
hope of also being raised similarly to him. 1 Cor 6:16-17 If the one living
with a wife is reckoned one with the same, it is clear that also the one
living with the harlot will be reckoned into one body with her and the one
living with the Lord will be reckoned into one spirit with him. 1 Cor 6:18 The
remaining sins, he says, seem to harm another, but this before all the rest
the one who commits it; for this one defiles his own body. The one who wrongs
others does not sin against his own body, but the one who fornicates sins
against his own body; and it is absurd not even to spare himself. And perhaps
the word has also been said as a question. Therefore he says: know that you do
not well declare, you who say: every sin is outside the body, for it comes to
be toward another, but the one who fornicates sins against his own body and
has authority? For no longer is the body of the one who has believed your own
but God's. 1 Cor 6, By his blood the Lord purchased us from the slavery of sin
and led us into the freedom of righteousness. Therefore it is fitting to honor
with chastity the Lord who purchased.
1 Cor 7:5 He does not call incontinence the wantonness, but the one not able

chooses to achieve things contrary to nature is not of our power but of the

1 Cor 7:14 According to our will, clearly being led, it can be holy. "Has been

7:17 For it is superfluous to think these things become a hindrance to him

The flesh that has been taken away is not surely possible to draw on. But the

he also answers those who have written to him concerning this, they having

he speaks concerning virginity, uttering the like also upon this concerning
both the men and the women. 1 Cor 7:26 Having inserted the general exhortation
he runs back to his own sequence, interpreting "because of the present
necessity," and "they will have affliction in the flesh." 183 1 Cor 7:28-29
Because of the circumstances in this world and the cares concerning the wife
and the household. But also pleasure, he says, but see how he also checked
this by the shortness of the time, saying: the time has been shortened, that

1 Cor 8:5 Most excellent is the addition of "so-called"; for through this the
being called falsely-named is shown, not the truly being. Just as therefore
there also are 184 so-called gods among those who wander, but among us who
have the knowledge there is one God, and one Lord. 1 Cor 8:10 Those who are
not perfect, seeing you eating indifferently, eat also themselves; but being
unsound they eat as idol-sacrifice and are harmed. 1 Cor 8:13 Therefore
despise food even if it is not temple-sacrifice; for neither not having eaten
shall we have anything less with God, nor having eaten shall we enjoy anything

make this defense: that I may not scandalize others. For even having authority
both from the gospel to eat and drink as also the Lord ordained, and to lead
about a wife, I do none of these things, everywhere breathing together with
the gospel.
1 Cor 9:14 Where sending them he said: take no bag nor bread nor silver, but

says: if I came willingly to evangelize, I had a reward. But since I was
commanded this unwillingly, not only is there not a reward for me through
this, but also punishment; I am liable, if I do not do what was commanded.
Therefore I have not used my authority to excess, if the Lord has given living
from the gospel, that through not taking a reward I may procure it for myself.

1 Cor 10:2-4 The sea is a type of the baptism in water, and the cloud of that
in the Spirit. And Moses of the Lord himself as leader of the typical baptism
as Christ of the true. They were therefore baptized in the cloud and in the
sea into Moses. And since he said of a following rock, he does not say this,
that a rock truly followed them, but that the power from Christ followed them
granting them to drink from a rock. And he called the manna and the drink
spiritual, because it was given to them according to the energy of the Holy
Spirit, not because they made those who partook spiritual. From the disputed
things of the old the old things were a type of the new; and the law of Moses
is shadow, but the grace body. Since therefore the Egyptians were pursuing the
Hebrews, and the Hebrews having crossed the Red Sea were delivered from the
bitter mastery of the Egyptians, the sea has the type of the font, the cloud
of the Spirit, and Moses of the Savior Christ, and the rod of the cross,
Pharaoh of the devil, the Egyptians of the demons, and the manna of the divine
food, and the water of the rock of the saving blood; for just as those after
crossing the Red Sea enjoyed both strange food and the marvelous stream, so we
after the saving 186 baptism partake of the divine mysteries. And for this
reason the rock was named Christ as having become a type of the Master Christ,
thus also the sea was called baptism, although no one was baptized in it; for
Israel crossed it rather dusted than soaked. 1 Cor 10:3-4 He calls both the
food and the drink spiritual, inasmuch as the Spirit granted both through
Moses according to his ineffable power. Thus he also called the rock
spiritual, inasmuch as by the power of the Spirit it gave forth the waters;
and following, since the water that flowed once followed them through the
desert, so that they nowhere needed drink, the places being waterless. And
"the rock was Christ," that he may say: the rock was to those what Christ is
to us, whose blood we the faithful drink being spiritually transformed upon
the mysteries. 1 Cor 10:6 And these things became types of us, not because
those were punished for this; for how is it possible? but because the things
that came to be among them can educate us in the rank of types. 1 Cor 10:7
That is, do not destroy the true baptism by idolatry. 1 Cor 10:12 "Our" is
lacking according to the sense. 1 Cor 10:13 For behold, he says, there not
having been brought upon you, not persecutions, not some necessity from these
human things, and all men must have as much harm as you have endured; feasting
in an idol-temple through friendship, and being so puffed by the advantages at
hand. 1 Cor 10:22 "To provoke to jealousy" is said of from certain two
sometimes admiring this one, sometimes that one, toward bringing both into
goodwill toward himself. 187 And this from a lesser toward greaters would
never come to be; for when would the superior be provoked at the honor coming
to be from the lesser toward another? But it comes to be from those who are
superior in whatever way; for the lesser both desire the honor from the
greater and being disappointed are stung. 1 Cor 10:25 Buying, he says, do not
be over-curious, lest you strike the conscience; for what comes to be in
ignorance does not strike, and what does not strike does not condemn.
1 Cor 11:3 This he wishes to say, that from Christ we go to God from whom he
is, and from the man to Christ; for from that one we are according to the
second existence, according to which resurrection we shall all likewise be
incorruptible by participation of the grace of the Spirit in him. For being
passible we consider Adam head from whom we have received being, but having
become impassible we consider Christ head from whom we have come to have being
impassible. And likewise, he says, also from the woman to the man, since from
that one she has received being. 1 Cor 11:4-5 Not only men but also women were
prophesying. And more openly the spiritual gifts were given then to those
being baptized, and there was also much difference, the prophecy according to
the needs, and especially they convicted those entering in hypocrisy who were
trying the church. If a man covers himself, he insults his own head, himself
being head as Christ; but if the woman prays uncovered, she insults the man,
her own head, stealing away his honor and wishing herself to be an originating
head. And in many copies it is carried instead of "to have something upon the
head": which shows being subjected to the man. 1 Cor 11:7 He shows it absurd
from piety and from nature and from the creation. 188 1 Cor 11:8 He called the
woman glory, but not also image, he added because of the unclarity, since
glory looks to obedience, but image to the originating. 1 Cor 11:18 For before
all of you I blame that, he says; for "first" he says instead of before all,
as also in Romans: first that they were entrusted with the oracles of God. 1
Cor 11, He calls it the Lord's both from the time and from the type, inasmuch
as on that day in the place of the gathering they eat at the same time of what
is customary. 1 Cor 11:-22 Or he also accuses this, that they approached the
mystical supper as the customary. Therefore also taking as to satiety they
were drunk. They were eating therefore not as a mystical supper but as their
own; for the things they themselves brought by themselves, they consumed as
their own supper. Hence it happened that the poor who did not have means for
the offerings of the mysteries did not even share. Therefore, he says, you
shame the poor not waiting for them, but taking beforehand and eating no
longer as the Lord's supper set forth ungrudgingly in common to all for
salvation, but as your own and as it were human, although the Lord set forth
as his saving body and blood a common reward for all of them; but you as not
following the teaching of the Lord have indeed undergone a sufficient
punishment. 1 Cor 11:23 "I received from the Lord" he says instead of I
received as the Lord himself having handed over to do thus; for he was not
present when these things were being handed over by him to the disciples. 1
Cor 11:28 Let each, he says, examine his own mind, whatever conception he has
acquired concerning the mysteries, and then let him partake of them; for 189
"himself" he says instead of his own mind, speaking in these things altogether
concerning the conception of the mysteries. 1 Cor 11:33-34 Some not having
attended to the sense of the things set think they fulfill the apostle's aim,
if they partake of the mysteries somewhere most rarely. They ought to
understand that if he wishes us to communicate being entirely clean of sin, he
never wishes us to communicate; first it is impossible for one who is a man to
be entirely clean of sin; and second, even if this were possible, yet it would
not be just for us to communicate having supposed this about ourselves. And
where will "as often as you eat" stand? For what was said shows continuity of
communion, with which also the ordinance upon the church runs together,
wishing the mysteries always to be accomplished. Of the mysteries therefore it
is holy for the one who commits the greatest and exceedingly forbidden things
to abstain, concerning which also the apostle declared clearly that the one
who commits them cannot obtain the kingdom; for such a one then it is good to
partake of them, whenever he has first abstained from the sins by fear of the
things legislated. But of the remaining things which must happen to men, many
both from the daily mishaps, and most also from the weakness of nature, it is
fitting to be eager as much as power to abstain; for care of virtue and the
daily practice of life can altogether lessen such things. But falling into
such things it is not good to deprive ourselves of the mysteries, but to
approach with more fear, reckoning their magnitude, and making the partaking
upon good hopes, inasmuch as forgiveness also comes to us from there, whenever
we abstain from <such things> according to power and appear not neglecting the
rest, and indeed also spiritual cooperation toward an easier accomplishment of
life; for all things that have come to us through the death of Christ, these
it is just also to be accomplished from the symbols of the death. So that I
would say with confidence, that even if someone happens to have sinned the
greatest things, yet having tested himself to abstain from every absurd
practice henceforth and to look toward virtue, living correspondingly to the
laws of Christ, should make the <partaking> of the mysteries having believed
fully that he will receive the remission of all, he will in no way fail of the
things believed.

1 Cor 13:1 Every gift deprived of love is vain, being energized not through
the one who has it but through those being built up, so that neither the
cymbal nor any other sounding instrument delights itself but those who enjoy
it. 1 13:2 And that neither faith without love nor love without faith perfects
is obvious; for the works also need faith and faith needs works which are
fulfilled through love. Yet since the apostle saw the Corinthians having
neglected love, he prefers it here as an excellent physician treating the
urgent of the passions. Here he does not speak of this common one; for having
given the unbelief prevailing at that time, they were working many such
wonders for astonishment, guiding them through these toward the truth. 1 Cor
13:4 Does not vaunt itself, which is boasts, and love is puffed up, which is
of unseemliness for one who is human to think highly and to lift himself
against the neighbor as not being subject to passions like theirs. 1 Cor
13:8-13 After the faith has been spread upon all the earth as superfluous both
the prophecies and the tongues and the knowledge in part 192 cease, and
henceforth these three remain as necessary. And reasonably he says love is
greater, since the Corinthians were in faction toward themselves and had zeal
and strifes, and there was danger the church be split.
1 Cor 14:1 Instead of: I wish you especially to take care of love; but if
indeed you also desire the spiritual gifts, at least know which are better and
that to prophesy is better than to speak in a tongue. 1 Cor 14:2-4 He prefers
prophecy after the apostleship; therefore the apostle is also a prophet, but
the prophet is not altogether also an apostle. The one speaking in a tongue
himself is built up, being persuaded that he utters by the Holy Spirit, yet he
does not build up the church which does not know what he utters. But the one
prophesying benefits the hearers and from the foreknowledge shows the
indwelling of the Holy Spirit in him. 1 Cor 14:5 I wish you to speak in
tongues; for this also is a spiritual gift. But I wish rather that you
prophesy; for this gift is greater. 1 Cor 14:14 I will pray instead of I will
give thanks and I will bless. And he said mind the understood word. 1 Cor
14:16 He calls unlearned the layman in distinction from the priest. 1 Cor
14:19 He said this, lest you think because of not having been counted worthy
of a gift of tongues **. 1 Cor 14:26 As a great gift he ranked that of the
psalms before the others. Let all things therefore come to be according to
order: let the one counted worthy of a psalm sing, let the one well-provided
with teaching 193 teach, let the one who has obtained a revelation utter the
revelation. 1 Cor 14:27-28 Do not throw the church into confusion, he says,
with the tongues when there are not those who interpret them. And if they
should be, let all things come to be in order. 1 Cor 14:29 Lest a spirit of
error speak, let them distinguish; instead of let them make clear. 1 Cor
14:34-36 Since later he said: the women to prophesy, perhaps they were

And prophecy differs from teaching. Follow, he says, the other churches and
innovate nothing as if you alone had been counted worthy of the preaching; for
the word did not begin from you, nor was it shut up into you. Let the women be
silent among you also as also everywhere in the churches. 1 Cor 14:37-38 If
anyone is ignorant, he says, that the things I write are of the Lord and not
mine, he is not a prophet nor has he the Spirit of God, but he is unknown by
the Holy Spirit.
1 Cor 15:1-2 For if he evangelized and they received and indeed also still
hold, for this is "in which you also stand," it is superfluous to write
concerning this, unless perhaps those, holding the Christian doctrines in
seeming, appear in the facts to greet the contrary; whence he well added
"through which you also are saved," instead of thus hold as hoping also to
obtain salvation from it. And "in what word I evangelized you," instead of
what is the aim of the gospel, what the cause, that the gospel may be the
resurrection of Christ, and the aim of this to believe that we also shall all
be raised correspondingly to that one; 194 if indeed you hold and have not
thrown away all things as having believed in vain the things said by us. 1 Cor
15:10 The grace has not become empty and inactive in me, but I labored more
than the others in the preaching. 1 Cor 15:14 For through his resurrection is
the forgiveness. 1 Cor 15:23 The divine Paul, revealing to us the mystery
concerning the resurrection that will be, says that Christ will be raised as
firstfruits, for indeed he has also been raised first from the dead, but then,
he says, those of Christ at his coming; for before all the others those who
have been made his own through faith must be raised, he says, showing the
resurrection as owed to them especially properly and particularly, even if it
extends to the whole nature from goodness clearly and loving-kindness of God
who willed to abolish death completely. 1 Cor 15:24 For after his will be the
resurrection of all, when also the end, the drowning of death and there will
no longer be death, but the common enemy of men is abolished, whom the
invisible death introduced. 1 Cor 15:25 He calls enemies the opposing powers.
1 Cor 15:32 He was destroyed by beast-like and murderous men. 1 Cor 15:36-38
He says this: if what you sow is not corrupted, it is not made alive, and not
even itself grows but becomes a genesis for another, and the power of God does
these things. Which things someone inferring ought not to disbelieve the
resurrection. 195 Just as also glass is from sand, yet no longer sand but
something other besides that from which it has come to be, and the ear
likewise is no longer a grain but an ear, something other besides the grain,
so also in the resurrection the body is refashioned better so as no longer to
be flesh and blood but some immortal and incorruptible living thing. 1 Cor
15:45-47 "The man became a living soul" has been written; but "the first" and
"Adam" he himself added, that he may say "the last Adam." For he did not say
into a life-giving soul but living, distinguishing toward the soul of the
irrational animals which is destroyed together with the bodies. Since
therefore the first man also became a living soul, he died and was not able to
rise, but the second, because he had a life-giving spirit and a soul, raised
through himself. Since therefore the soulish came first, and second the
spiritual, correspondingly first death and second the resurrection; for the
first follows the second. Therefore we also as having become soulish die, and
again having become spiritual we are raised. And he ought not first to have
become spiritual, death, since from regeneration it was not possible for him
to put off the sin; but God does all things in foreknowledge. 1 Cor 15:48-49
He said this because of the God Word being from heaven who became man
unchangeably. And he calls heavenly not as like in holiness, but as going to
be raised in incorruption. And image now he says we have borne of the earthy
according to being going to die, and image of the heavenly according to being
going to be raised and remaining incorruptible. And it is clear that he calls
the same both earthy and heavenly; for we are earthy before resurrection, and
heavenly after the resurrection, according to the account of the corruptible
and incorruptible, not according to that of the heavenly kingdom. 1 Cor
15:50-51 Clearly also through these he teaches, that flesh and blood cannot
inherit the kingdom of God he set upon those who will be changed, and "nor
does corruption the incorruption" upon all. 196 1 Cor 15:52 He called last
trumpet here that after which all are raised; for this is the abolition of
death. And it is clear that many voices of trumpets come to be, and of each
thing being accomplished it is altogether a symbol. Since therefore each comes
to be in a certain order, reasonably he says this will be in the last trumpet.
That of the change and incorruption indicates the transformation toward the
better. 1 Cor 15:56 He says law here simply whether that lying in nature or
the positive; for he wishes to say that then together with death sin also is
dissolved, and every law is idle, since having become incorruptible we are
governed by the grace of the Spirit.
