[LETTER OF PETER TO JAMES.]
Peter to James, the lord and bishop of the holy church. Under the Father of
all through Jesus Christ, in peace always.
Knowing you, my brother, as one eagerly hastening toward what is commonly
advantageous for us all, I think it right and I beg that the books of my
preachings which I sent you be given to no one of those from the nations, nor
to one of the same race before a trial, but if someone having been tested is
found worthy, then to hand them down to him according to the initiation,
according to which Moses also handed down to the seventy who had received his
chair. For this reason the fruit of the security appears even until now. For
those of the same nation everywhere keep the same canon of the monarchy and of
the polity, having been able in no way to be led out to think otherwise by
the scriptures that have many sinews.
For according to the canon handed down to them they try to recast the
discordant things of the scriptures, if indeed someone, perhaps not knowing
the traditions, is numb toward the many-signifying voices of the prophets.
For which reason they permit no one to teach, unless he first learn how one
must use the scriptures. For this reason among them there is one God, one
law, one hope. Therefore that the like may come to be among us also, give my
books of the preachings to our seventy brothers with the like mystery of the
initiation, that they may also furnish those who wish to take up the part of
the teaching; since if it does not come to be thus, the word of our truth
will be divided into many opinions. And this I know not as being a prophet,
but already seeing the beginning of the evil itself. For some of those from
the nations have rejected the lawful preaching through me, having accepted a
certain lawless and babbling teaching of the enemy man. And these things
while I am still remaining some have attempted by certain various
interpretations to reshape my words into the dissolution of the law, as if I
myself also so think, but not preaching from boldness. Which may it be far.
For such a thing is to act against the law of God spoken through Moses and
witnessed by our Lord concerning its eternal remaining. Since he said thus:
"The heaven and the earth will pass away, one iota or one tittle will not
pass away from the law".
And this he has said, "that all things may come to be". And they, I do not
know how professing my mind, attempt to interpret the words they heard from
me more prudently than I who said them, saying to those catechized by them
that this is my thought, which I did not even consider. And if while I am
still remaining they dare to lie such things, how much more after me will
those after me dare to do? Therefore that no such thing come to be, for this
sake I thought it right and begged that the books of my preachings which I
sent you be given to no one neither of the same race nor of another race
before a trial, but if someone having been tested is found worthy, then to
hand them down to him according to the initiation of Moses, according to
which he handed down to the seventy who had received his chair, that thus
they may keep the faiths and everywhere hand down the canon of the truth,
interpreting all things toward our tradition, and not themselves being
dragged down by ignorance, being drawn into error by the conjectures
according to the soul, bring others into the like pit of destruction.
And the things that seemed to me I have well signified to you, and what seems
to you, my lord, accomplish fittingly. Be well.
