PROLEGOMENA OF JOHN THE DEACON ARGYROPOULOS TO THE PROGYMNASMATA
OF RHETORIC
Since it lies before us to speak concerning the progymnasmata, it is
necessary first to treat who is the father of these and from where he was
led on toward the composition of them. The father, then, of the present
book of the Progymnasmata is Aphthonius of Antioch the Syrian, the pupil
of Libanius son of Phasganius. And the cause which pricked him on toward
the composition of the Progymnasmata is this. To the emperor Marcus,
being both practiced in law and most devoted to letters, Hermogenes the
sophist was a friend to the highest degree; and granting him a favor,
when he had asked earlier, having encompassed the whole art and power of
rhetoric in four books he handed it over, which are the so-called Issues,
the Inventions, the Ideas, and the Methods; which he also named Rhetoric,
being profitable both to all and especially to those who are eager about
laws. But since this four-book composition, put together for the sake of
exercise toward the art of the rhetors, was formidable toward those of
the young just now meeting with it and as if needing a certain intro
=14.157= duction, this same indicated Hermogenes composed again other
progymnasmata being twelve in number stretching toward this, which also
are these: fable, narrative, chreia, maxim, refutation and confirmation,
commonplace, encomium, comparison, ethopoeia, ekphrasis, thesis, and
introduction of the law. And since these seemed somehow unclear and hard
to grasp inasmuch as they were without examples, others instead of these
were set forth both by many others of the rhetors and indeed also by the
present sophist Aphthonius, which also were rather preferred to be read
as clearer. And being twelve, as we said, those which Hermogenes
composed, this man divided these into fourteen; for while that man was
teaching both the encomium and the invective in one and the same
teaching, this man assigned a teaching of its own to each, one of its own
to encomium and again one of its own to invective, and concerning
refutation and confirmation likewise. But since we have given both the
father of the book and the cause, come let us also go through concerning
the remaining things and first say both why they are called
progymnasmata and what is the definition of a progymnasma and of a
gymnasma. Gymnasmata, then, are called in the proper sense the exercises
