OF THE SAME, ON FAITH.
By the grace of God the Good, having learned the charge of your piety, worthy
of the love in Christ toward God, in which you sought from us a written
confession of the pious faith, at first, being conscious of my own lowliness
and weakness, I hesitated toward the answer; but when I remembered the Apostle
saying, Bearing with one another in love; and again, For with the heart it is
believed unto righteousness, and with the mouth it is confessed unto
salvation; I did not think it without danger to contradict you, and to keep
silent the saving confession, having confidence through Christ toward God, as
it is written: Not that we are sufficient of =31.677= ourselves to reckon
anything as from ourselves; but our sufficiency is from God, who made those
then, and now also us, and this on your account, sufficient to become
ministers of a New Covenant, not of letter, but of spirit. And you yourselves
know well that it is proper to a faithful minister that whatever he has been
entrusted by the good Master to administer to the fellow-servants, these
things he should preserve for them unadulterated and without trafficking.
So that I also am a debtor to set before you, according to what is pleasing to
God, toward the common profit, the things I learned from the God-inspired
Scripture. For if the Lord himself, in whom the Father was well pleased, In
whom are all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge hidden, who received all
the authority and all the judgment from the Father, A commandment he gave me,
he says, what I should say, and what I should speak; and again: The things
therefore that I speak, as the Father has said to me, so I speak; and the Holy
Spirit does not speak from himself, but whatever he hears from him, these
things he speaks; how much more is it both pious and safe for us to think and
to do this in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ? So long then as it was
necessary to contend against the heresies that rise up from time to time,
following those who have gone before, I thought it consistent, according to
the difference of the impiety sown by the devil, to hinder, or even to
overturn, the blasphemies brought in, with opposing utterances, and at
different times with different ones, as the need of those who were sick
compelled, and these often unwritten, yet nevertheless not alien to the pious
meaning according to the Scripture; the Apostle having often not declined even
to use Hellenic phrases toward his own aim. But now, toward the common aim of
both us and you, I judged it fitting to fulfill the charge of your love in
Christ in the simplicity of the healthy faith, saying the things I was taught
from the God-inspired Scripture; sparing both those names and those words
which are not contained in the divine Scripture in the very expressions, yet
which preserve that meaning which is laid in the Scripture; but as many as,
besides the foreignness of the expression, still also introduce to us a
foreign mind, and which cannot be found being proclaimed by the saints, these
I altogether decline as foreign and alien to the pious faith.
Faith then is an unwavering assent of the things heard =31.680= in full
assurance of the truth of the things proclaimed by the grace of God, which
Abraham showed, being witnessed that He did not waver in unbelief, but was
strengthened in faith, giving glory to God, and being fully assured that what
he has promised, he is able also to do. And if The Lord is faithful in all his
words, and All his commandments are faithful, established unto the age of the
age, made in truth and uprightness, it is a manifest falling away from faith
and an accusation of pride, either to set aside something of the things
written, or to introduce of the things not written, our Lord Jesus Christ
saying: My sheep hear my voice; and having said before this: But a stranger
they will by no means follow, but will flee from him; because they do not know
the voice of strangers; and the Apostle, in a human example, more vehemently
forbidding the adding or taking away of anything in the God-inspired
Scriptures, through the things he says: Nevertheless a covenant ratified by a
man no one sets aside, or adds to.
Every voice then and thought alien to the teaching of the Lord we have thus
always and now resolved to flee, and the aim also, as I said before, now set
before both us and you, differing greatly from those subjects by which we were
led at different times in different ways to write or say something. Because
then the refutation of heresy and the overturning of the devil's craft was
being pursued, but now a confession and simple manifestation of the healthy
faith is set forth. Therefore neither does the same character of speech fit us
even now. For as a man would not take in hand the same implements when warring
and when farming (for other are the implements of those who in security work
out for themselves the things toward living, and other the full armors of
those drawn up in war), so the one who exhorts in the healthy teaching would
not say the same things as the one who refutes those who contradict. For there
is one form of refuting speech, and another form of exhorting speech. Other is
the simplicity of those who in peace confess piety, and other the sweats of
those who stand against the oppositions of knowledge falsely so called. So
that if in this manner we also, administering our words in judgment, should
use everywhere the things that follow toward the guarding or building of the
faith, at one time more combatively resisting those who try by the craft of
the devil to destroy it, at another time more simply and more familiarly
expounding it to those who wish to be built up in it, and doing nothing else
than that which was said by the Apostle: To know how you ought to answer each
one. But before coming to the confession of =31.681= faith itself, it is also
worth marking this: that the majesty and glory of God, being both
uncircumscribable by speech and incomprehensible to mind, could neither be
made clear nor be thought by one word or thought; but through more of the
things that turn about in our use the God-inspired Scripture scarcely hinted,
as through a mirror, to those who are pure in heart.
For face to face, and the perfect knowledge, has been promised to be rendered
to the worthy in the age to come; but now, whether it be a Paul or a Peter, he
sees truly the things he sees, and is not deceived, nor imagines, yet he sees
through a mirror, and in a riddle, and receiving now with thanksgiving the
thing in part, he awaits with great joy the perfect for the future. Which the
apostle Paul confirms, constructing the argument in some such manner, that
Just as, When I was a child, still learning thoroughly the elements of the
beginning of the oracles of God, I spoke as a child, I thought as a child, I
reckoned as a child; but when I became a man, and I press to arrive at the
measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ, I have put away the things
of the child. And I had such progress and improvement in the comprehension of
the divine things, that the knowledge in the Jewish worship resembled the
motions of a childish mind, but the knowledge through the Gospel already
belonged to a man perfect in all things. Thus in comparison with the knowledge
that will be revealed to the worthy in the age to come, even what now seems
perfect in knowledge is something small and so much dimmer, as to fall short
of the clarity in the age to come more than seeing through a mirror and in a
riddle falls short of face to face.
And the disciples of the Lord about blessed Peter and John also establish
this, themselves no less confirming, by the always greater advance and
progress according to the present life, the exceeding of the knowledge
reserved in the age to come. Who, after they appeared worthy of the election
from the Lord, of the sojourn with him, of the sending from him, of the
distribution of the spiritual gifts, after hearing: To you it has been given
to know the mysteries of the kingdom of the heavens, after such knowledge,
after the revelation of the things unspeakable to the rest, nevertheless
later, already about the Lord's =31.684= passion itself, they hear: I still
have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now. From these and
such things we learn that so great does the God-inspired Scripture know the
boundlessness of knowledge, and so great the unattainableness of the divine
mysteries for human nature in the present, the more always being added to each
according to progress, yet that which is according to worth always falling
short for all, until the perfect comes, when that which is in part will be
done away; therefore neither is one name sufficient to make clear all the
glories of God together, nor is each received wholly without danger. For if
someone says God, he has not made clear Father; and to Father there is lacking
creator.
And to these again there is need of goodness, wisdom, power, and the rest of
the things borne in the holy Scripture. And again, if we take Father wholly
according to our use upon God, we are impious; for it predicates passion and
efflux, and ignorance, and weakness, and as many such things. And likewise
also creator. For in our case there is need of time, matter, tools,
assistance; from all of which the pious opinion concerning God must be clean,
as far as is possible for a human being. For of that which is according to
worth, as I said, even if all minds were joined toward the search, and all
tongues ran together toward the declaration, no one would ever attain it. And
this thought the most wise Solomon also clearly presents to us, saying: I
said, I will become wise; and it receded far from me, beyond what it was;
not by fleeing, but by its incomprehensibility being made especially manifest
to those to whom knowledge has been added more abundantly by the grace of God.
The God-inspired Scripture then necessarily uses more names and words for a
partial, and this riddling, presentation of the divine glory. But for us,
neither power nor leisure is there in the present to collect all the things
said everywhere by the God-inspired Scripture concerning Father and Son and
Holy Spirit, because of your urgency; but having set forth a few from all, we
think even these sufficient for your conscience, both toward the manifestation
of our mind from the Scriptures, and the full assurance of you yourselves and
of those who wish it concerning us. For as the many announce to us one pious
thought, so also from the few the well-disposed, I think, recognizes the piety
in all. =31.685=
We believe then and confess one only true and good God, and Father almighty,
from whom are all things; the God and Father of our Lord and God Jesus Christ.
And one his only-begotten Son, our Lord and God Jesus Christ, only true,
through whom all things came to be, both the visible and the invisible; and in
whom all things hold together; who was in the beginning with God, and was God;
and after these things, according to the Scripture, he was seen upon the
earth, and consorted with men; who, being in the form of God, did not count
being equal to God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, and through the
birth from a Virgin taking the form of a servant, and being found in fashion
as a man, fulfilled all the things written unto him and concerning him
according to the commandment of the Father, becoming obedient unto death, and
death of a cross; and on the third day having been raised from the dead,
according to the Scriptures, he appeared to his holy disciples, and to the
rest, as it is written; and he went up into the heavens and sits at the right
of the Father; from where he comes at the consummation of this age to raise
all, and to render to each according to his doing; when the righteous will be
received into eternal life and a kingdom of heavens, and the sinners will be
condemned into eternal punishment, where their worm does not end, and the fire
is not quenched.
And one only Holy Spirit, the Paraclete, in whom we were sealed unto a day of
redemption; the Spirit of truth, the Spirit of adoption, in whom we cry: Abba,
the Father; who divides and works the gifts from God to each toward the
profit, as he wills; who teaches and reminds of all things, whatever he hears
from the Son; the good, who leads into all the truth, and establishes all who
believe toward both a sure knowledge, and an exact confession, and a pious
worship, and a spiritual and true adoration of God the Father and of his
only-begotten Son our Lord and God Jesus Christ, and of himself; each name
clearly distinguishing for us the property of the one named, and concerning
each of those named certain exceptional properties being piously contemplated
=31.688=, of the Father in the property of the Father, and of the Son in the
property of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit in his own property; neither the
Holy Spirit speaking from himself, nor the Son doing anything from himself;
and the Father sending the Son, and the Son sending the Holy Spirit. Thus we
think, and thus we baptize into a consubstantial Trinity, according to the
commandment of our Lord Jesus Christ himself saying: Going, make disciples of
all the nations, baptizing them into the name of the Father and of the Son and
of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to keep all things whatever I commanded you.
Which things, when we keep them, we show the love toward him, and are counted
worthy to remain in it, as it is written; but when we do not keep them, we are
proven to be in the contrary. For he who does not love me, says the Lord, does
not keep my words; and again: He who has my commandments, and keeps them, he
is the one who loves me.
And I wonder more exceedingly, that, our Lord Jesus Christ himself saying: Do
not rejoice that the demons are subject to you; but rejoice that your names
have been written in the heavens; and again: In this all will know that you
are my disciples, if you have love with one another; whence the Apostle,
showing in all things the necessity of love, testifies saying: If I speak with
the tongues of men and of angels, but do not have love, I have become sounding
brass, or a clanging cymbal; and if I have prophecy, and know all the
mysteries, and all the knowledge, and if I have all the faith, so as to remove
mountains, but do not have love, I am nothing; and after a little: And whether
prophecies will be done away, or tongues will cease, or knowledge will be done
away, and the things following, to which he adds: But now remains faith, hope,
love, these three; and greater of these is love; these and such things having
been thus defined both by the Lord and by the Apostle, wondering I say, how
concerning the things that are done away and cease men have so great zeal and
flutter, but concerning the things that remain, and especially the love
greater than all, which characterizes the Christian, not only do they
themselves take no care, but they also oppose those who have been zealous, and
fighting they fulfill what was said: They themselves do not enter, and they
hinder those who are entering from entering.
Wherefore I exhort =31.689= and beg, having ceased from curious inquiry and
unseemly word-fighting, to be content with the things said by the saints and
by the Lord himself, and to think things worthy of the heavenly calling; and
to live worthily of the Gospel of Christ, upon hope of the eternal life, and
heavenly kingdom, which has been prepared for all who keep the commandments of
God and Father, those according to the Gospel of the blessed God Jesus Christ
our Lord, in Holy Spirit and truth. These things, having been reminded by your
piety to dictate in the last parts, and to make our own mind clear both to you
and through you to the brethren in Christ, for the full assurance of you and
of them in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, we thought necessary and
incumbent upon us also, toward the mind of some being in no way carried about
in the difference of the things set forth by us at different times in
different ways, we always being compelled to stand against the subject
introduced by those who set themselves against the truth, nor indeed some
being shaken in the opposition of those who wish to predicate alien things of
us, or even of those who often, of their own passions, toward snatching the
simpler, speak falsely of our mind; whom it is necessary for you also to guard
against, as alien to the evangelical and apostolic faith and love; and to
remember the Apostle saying: But even if we, or an angel from heaven, should
preach a gospel to you contrary to what we preached as gospel to you, let him
be anathema; that keeping also that, Beware of the false prophets; and that,
That you withdraw from every brother walking disorderly, and not according to
the tradition which they received from us, we may walk by the rule of the
saints, as Having been built upon the foundation of the apostles and
prophets, the cornerstone being our Lord Jesus Christ himself, in whom all
the building, being fitted together, grows into a holy temple in the Lord.
And may the God of peace sanctify you wholly, and may your spirit and the
soul and the body be kept entire, blamelessly in the coming of our Lord
Jesus Christ. Faithful is God who calls you, who will also do it, if we keep
his commandments, by the grace of Christ in Holy Spirit.
Reckoning the things concerning the healthy faith in the =31.692= things
before these to have been said sufficiently for the present, from here let us
be eager to fulfill the promise concerning the ethical things in the name of
our Lord Jesus Christ. As many things then as we find according to the New
Covenant for now, scattered, forbidden or approved, these, according to our
power, we have been eager to gather into summary rules toward easy grasp for
those who wish; having set beside each rule also the number of the scriptural
chapters comprised in it, whether from the Gospel, or from the Apostle, or
from the Acts; so that the one who has read the rule, and has seen lying
beside it the first, if it happen, or the second number, then having taken up
the Scripture itself, and having sought out the chapter of the aforesaid
number, may thus find the testimony toward which the rule has been made. I
wished then at first also to set beside the rules from the Old Covenant the
things said in agreement toward each of those in the New; but since the need
pressed, the brethren in Christ now especially more earnestly demanding of us
the things long promised, I remembered the one who said: Give occasion to a
wise man, and he will be wiser. So that it is possible for the one who wishes,
having taken a sufficient occasion from the things lying beside, to take up
the Old Covenant, and to recognize from himself the agreement in all the
God-inspired Scriptures, especially also since one voice is sufficient for
those who are faithful and fully assured of the truth of the words of the
Lord. Wherefore even of the things in the New Covenant, not all, but a few
from all, I judged it sufficient to set forth.
