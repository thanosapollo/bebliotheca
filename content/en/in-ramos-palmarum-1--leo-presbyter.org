A homily of John Chrysostom, archbishop of Constantinople, on the Palms.
Already the gifts of the Master's festival shine forth beforehand, already the
tidings of the all-praised feast run ahead, already Lazarus, having received
the earnest of the universal resurrection, reclines with the Lord, already the
mystery of the gift makes fragrant those who run to baptism, already the
believing people, having run forth to meet the Lord, cry out saying, as you
just now heard: Hosanna in the highest, blessed is he who comes in the name of
the Lord, King of Israel. Blessed is he who comes, who is always coming and is
with us and is in no way outside those who believe.
Truly that crowd recognized a kingly entrance. For just as in present times,
when a king has marched against a tyrant and is returning with victory, all
the citizens of his city meet him before the city, repaying the victory with
praises, and what leads them to this is not a love of glory but a desire for
the triumph, in the same way also in the case of the Master Christ, as in the
case of a trophy-bearing king, the whole multitude both by their words and by
the palms made known that the one who had arrived was a victor, or rather that
the one being recognized was God. For to God alone are those voices fitting
which were then spoken, and now have been read out, and which uncover divine
mysteries to those who understand.
Hosanna in the highest. What is Hosanna in the highest? That is: Save now, you
who are in the highest. For Hosanna is interpreted Save now in the Hebrew
tongue. Hosanna in the highest. That is, as salvation above and love of
mankind below. O knowledge of God in a crowd that has shared in angelic
ministry, treading earth and pacing heaven, wearing a body and pursuing the
things of the bodiless, Jews in name and Christians in deed, worldly in manner
of life and apostolic in confession.
These too received the revelation from above, as Peter did. For just as Peter,
the chiefest of the apostles, not from his own study but from divine
revelation said to the Lord: You are the Christ, the Son of the living God,
and the Lord, showing that he received the revelation not from his own
reasonings but from the Father, blessing him said: Blessed are you, Simon
Bar-Jonah, for flesh and blood has not revealed it to you, but my Father who
is in the heavens: in the same way also the crowd, having received the
revelation from above, cried out fitting voices to the Lord, saying: Hosanna
in the highest, blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord, King of
Israel.
And it is likely that some will say: And from where will you prove to us that
this Jewish crowd received the revelation from above? From where shall I prove
it to you? From the Lord's own words. For the chief priests and the scribes,
he says, seeing the children crying in the temple and saying, Hosanna to the
son of David, were indignant and said to Jesus: Do you hear what these are
saying? And Jesus says to them: Yes. Have you never read: Out of the mouths of
infants and sucklings you have prepared praise?
And not from the Lord's words alone, but also from the unexpected gathering
and meeting of the crowds and from the works they were doing, not only crying
out, but also cutting the branches of the olives and the palm-fronds of the
palms and bearing them in their hands, and spreading their garments
underneath, and addressing him as Savior and King and Lord. Whence it is
surely clear to everyone that they received the revelation from above.
From where did the crowd recognize the Lord as king? He was not wearing the
diadem of a worldly king, he was not clothed in purple, he was not bringing a
host of soldiers, horses and chariots and gold-studded shields were not
running before him, he was not riding a royal chariot spread with purple. He
was sitting on a borrowed colt, a small one, bringing only twelve disciples.
From where then did the crowd know that he was a king, unless they received
the revelation from above?
Therefore they did not seek a worldly diadem upon the Lord: for they knew him
as reigning without beginning. They did not seek purple: for they beheld him
wrapping himself in light as in a garment.
They did not look about for a host of soldiers, being persuaded by the prophet
who said: Ten thousand times ten thousand angels ministered to him, and a
thousand thousands of archangels stood beside him. They did not seek horses
and chariots: for they recognized Christ as driving the chariot and the
evangelists following as horses, being persuaded by the prophet who said (and
this is Zechariah), that Behind him are horses white and red and dappled and
ashen. White horse: Matthew, as having set forth more clearly the things of
the Lord's becoming man. Ashen horse: Mark, as having written the Gospel more
floridly. Dappled horse: Luke, as having set forth more eloquently and in
varied colors the things concerning the Lord. Red horse: John, as having
flashed forth the divinity of Christ like a lightning of fire by his own
words, saying In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was God.
Therefore that faithful Jerusalem crowd, having received the revelation from
God, was not scandalized by the cheap colt, but was strengthened by the
Master's presence. Therefore as soon as they saw the Lord mounted on the colt,
immediately pricked by prophetic predictions, taking up the palm-fronds of the
palms the crowds said to one another: Why do we wait and not go out to meet
the intelligible palm, holding the palm-fronds of the palms? His fruit is full
of sweetness and immortality; his leaves are healings of the inhabited world;
his stem, that is the wood of the cross, is the salvation of the whole world
and the trophy of the victory against the devil.
We have heard the Church from the nations crying in the Song of Songs: I will
go up upon the palm, I will take hold of its branches. Let us go before the
Church from the nations and greet him both as an intelligible palm and as a
mighty king.
While the crowds were saying and doing these things and acclaiming the Lord,
the chief priests and the Pharisees, stung very sharply, were rebuking the
crowds, saying: What are you doing, what empty talk are you talking, you most
rash ones? Do you come as to God to the carpenter's son, holding the palms? Do
you name as Lord him who has nowhere to lay his head? Do you cast kingly
voices at him who sits on a colt of an ass? Are not his father and his mother
among us?
Cease, you most rash ones; if not, you will be punished. For this is why we
are here, that we may mark the most rash among you, that they may pay the
penalties of their daring.
Hearing these things that the chief priests were saying, the crowds are not
moved toward sedition, since peace is present, but they answer them, grace
working with them: Do you blame us, Pharisees, and threaten punishment as if
we were sending forth blasphemy? Who are worthy of punishment, you who read
the Scriptures and do not understand, or we who hear and hold fast? Are we in
error, offering palm-fronds of the palms to the Lord? Is he not himself the
palm of righteousness? Has not the prophet cried concerning him: The righteous
shall flourish as a palm? Did he not himself blossom forth the recovery of
sight of the blind? Did he not grant to the lame an unhindered course? Did he
not make the paralytic carry the bed that had carried him? Did he not raise
Lazarus of the fourth day from death? Is it not for this that you wish to kill
Lazarus again?
Did you not roll away the stone, and by unbelief are you hardened as stones?
Did he not bid you to unbind the grave-bands, lest as in the case of the man
born blind you should lie that someone else had leapt from another tomb? You
always envy the good things, O Pharisees, and you oppose the multitude that
believes.
For this reason you again planned to murder Lazarus, that you might cut off
the crowd's faith. Grant that you kill Lazarus: can he not raise him again?
Let us see who will grow weary, you killing or he raising. You kill by deed
and toil; he raises by a word. Who is more wearied? Is not the word lighter
than the deed?
Are we in error, O Pharisees, because we came to the Lord as to a king? Seeing
the colt we were rather strengthened. Therefore we also recognize a mighty
king and we have the Scripture at our feet, that you may learn and understand
that it is not we who are in error, but you who are deceived. You have the
prophet Zechariah: unfold his words, fear his sickle of vengeance, for this
prophet, evangelizing Judea from many years ago, has cried: Rejoice greatly,
daughter of Zion; behold, your king comes to you, meek and quiet, sitting upon
a colt of an ass, the son of a beast of burden.
Are we in error, reading the Scripture and recognizing the king and doing good
to ourselves and turning those who doubt? You hold the prophet and you do not
wish to understand the one prophesied? Depart then, O Pharisees. The more you
press upon us, so much the more you awaken our zeal. Therefore we do not cease
crying: Hosanna in the highest, blessed is he who comes in the name of the
Lord.
We know the name, O Pharisees. This name freed us from Egypt and made the sea
passable and peopled the desert and settled us in a land flowing with milk and
honey; it prepared the prophet to cry: Let them know that your name is Lord,
you alone are most high over all the earth.
And that the crowd was obedient, but the chief priests disobedient, you just
now heard the evangelist saying: And the chief priests took counsel that they
might kill Lazarus also, because many of the Jews on account of him were going
away and believing in Jesus.
Did you see the madness of the chief priests? In order to cut off the
multitude from the faith, they sought to kill Lazarus, saying to themselves:
If we kill Jesus, but leave Lazarus alive, we gain nothing; for seeing this
one they believe in that one. Therefore, lest even after death he become a
stumbling-block to us, so that some again believe in Jesus, let us kill
Lazarus, that we may wipe out his memory altogether.
But the chief priests were saying these things as vain-minded men, not
understanding that the Lord, about to accept the voluntary death for the
immortality of the world, was not going to leave to the world the memory of
Lazarus alone and of his raising, but also the unconquerable cross, which they
will be able neither to sack nor to hide nor to make vain, showing through the
whole inhabited world the power of its working. For what house is there that
does not have the sign of the cross, being a trophy against demons and a
safeguard of souls?
What then does the evangelist say? For it is good also to take in hand the
beginning of the whole pericope. You just now heard the evangelist saying: Six
days before the Pascha Jesus came to Bethany, where Lazarus was, whom he
raised from the dead. And what day is this Six days before? The present day,
which characterizes the forerunner John. For just as John ran before the Lord,
so also this day ran before the resurrection; and just as John, pointing with
the finger to the Lord, said: Behold the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of
the world, so also this day, pointing with the finger to the coming Lord's
day, has cried saying: Behold the mother of those who are about to be born
again.
Six days before the Pascha. Hear the day: for Six days before the Pascha is
not set down simply, but that you may learn to cleanse yourself beforehand
from every stain, that you may dissolve enmity, that you may cease from wrath,
that you may quench slander, that you may bind love tight and widen love of
the poor, that not you alone may keep the feast but the one in need may keep
it with you. For if you do not share with the one who has need, you keep the
feast, but he is gloomy. Therefore, lest the story of the rich man and Lazarus
come upon you, share with the one who has need, do good to the one who is
lacking, that you too may keep feast through all and he may feast together
with you.
What then does the evangelist say? Six days before the Pascha Jesus came to
Bethany, where Lazarus was, whom he raised from the dead. O the wisdom of the
Lord. He raised Lazarus, that he might provoke the devil. Hear in brief.
When the devil saw the Lord crying, Lazarus, come forth, and all the things
under the earth dissolved, and the powers below not bearing to hold him, as a
kingly command having been sent down, or rather as a divine will working, and
that at once Lazarus's hairs were taking root from above, and the dissolutions
of the sinews were being tightened together, and the diminishings of the flesh
were being filled up, and the separation of the bones was being fitted
together, and the streams of the ichors were being drawn in, and the vital
spirit was entering in, and the mistress soul was receiving again her ancient
former dwelling: when he saw these things and things like them taking place,
being thrown into tumult and becoming anxious, he was commanding the powers
under him, saying: Array yourselves, be strong, do not betray my kingdom.
O what have I suffered? The power of death is being dissolved, which I
acquired through sin. Him whom I have been holding four days I now give back
by force. I have fallen into a great disaster. I have begun to vomit those
whom I swallowed. I do not weep for Lazarus only; I fear that I may lose also
the others whom I hold from Adam until now. I have need of haste and of much
strength. If I do not forestall the one who is stripping me by the word, he
will show me empty of all my substance.
I have found what I will do, I have known what I will accomplish. I have the
Jews as fellow-runners; I hire Judas; this one always serves me well. For
being a lover of money, when a woman came forward he priced the myrrh at three
hundred denarii, giving her trouble; and he priced the myrrh at three hundred
denarii, but he will sell the Master of the myrrh for thirty denarii.
Therefore I have need of much haste to prepare a cross. He took Lazarus from
me; I will drag him down here whole and number him with the dead, that he may
learn not to campaign against a greater one.
The raising of Lazarus from the dead provoked the devil to counsel and say
these things and things like them. Therefore he also stirred the Jews to
greater envy so as to serve his counsel and prepare a cross for the Lord, that
the curse might become a blessing and the wood of the cross salvation for all.
For through the resurrection of Lazarus the devil was troubled, the Pharisees
were gathered, Martha was fully assured, Mary was glorified, the resurrection
was shown beforehand, the disciples became stronger, and many of the Jews,
having believed in the Lord Jesus Christ, met him with branches and palms,
crying and saying: Hosanna in the highest, blessed is he who comes in the name
of the Lord, King of Israel. To him be the glory and the power unto the ages
of ages. Amen.
