In most things, then, the god thus led us, both signifying what must be done
and having us obedient if any other of men ever obeyed a god. And in the
second year after the withdrawal from Aesepus, and the twelfth from when I
first fell ill, many and wondrous visions kept coming, leading to Epidaurus,
the holy [place] of the god. And the thing that happened among the first was
such: someone, urging me to take heart, went through a saying of Musonius; he,
he said, wishing to raise up someone who was ill and had given up, spoke
somehow thus, rebuking: why do you wait? where do you look? or until the god
himself, standing beside you, utters a voice? Cut out the dead of the soul and
you will know the god. Such things, he said, Musonius spoke. And besides these
there were voices, save him for the city of the Athenians; and that was as for
the Greeks; and concerning those in Italy there were great predictions.
