* On Job
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

It is timely in the present for the unconquered soldier of God, Job, to speak
the words of the prophet David. I said: I will keep my ways, that I may not
sin with my tongue; I set a guard on my mouth when the sinner stood against
me. I was struck dumb and I was humbled and I was silent from good things, and
my pain was renewed. Truly Job, if anyone did, kept his mouth from blasphemy,
bridled his tongue from railing, palisaded his lips with fear of God. He was
humbled, but he was not left destitute; he was struck dumb, that he might not
hear the dragon; he was silent from good things, but he did not depart from
the good things; his pain was renewed, but his achievement was not destroyed.
He made vain the devil who stood opposite him, and showed him empty of his
hope. What he wished, the devil did; what he expected, he did not hear. He
emptied the quiver of arrows, but he did not bend the soldier; he wrecked the
vessel, but he did not sink the passenger; he cut off the ten branches of the
children, but he did not shake the root of endurance; he brought housebreaking
against the body, but he did not take captive the ruling soul; he made the
friends into enemies, but he did not estrange the friend of God from
friendship toward him. Therefore also the most wise Job, when he saw the three
friends, as you heard just now, sitting with him for seven days and seven
nights, marveled at the sympathy, but did not acknowledge the counsel. Why?
Because the friends reproached him, declaring that he suffered justly; they
made clear that it had become a repayment of sins. Therefore Job also, when he
saw them in these things, after many replies answered them saying, as the
scripture of Job contains: But you too have come upon me without mercy,
physicians of evils, all of you. A friend ought to have comforted: not to
wound, surely? to exhort: not to rail, surely? You become temptations of
temptations to me; do you not see how harsh are the streams of the ichors? Do
you not understand how bitter are the scrapings of the potsherds? Do you not
perceive how hard are the beddings of the dunghill? Have you not heard how
soul-destroying are the wife's counsels? What you ought to lighten, you load
on; you obey the devil. Why do you scrape my wounds further? Why do you shoot
me with the tongue? Why do you make war with the mouth? The one who was
defeated was not satisfied; the one lying down still boxes, and he followed
even onto the dunghill. He cut off all my substance, the ten children; he kept
my wife alone for me, and her not as consolation but as a plot. And when he
was made vain through her also, he is eager to drag me down again through you.
Stand away then, O friends; I have known the crafty one's attempts: first he
showed you too as sympathetic, and prepared you to sit on the dunghill, and
showed you for seven days without food and without speech, so that I, taking
heart at the sympathy, might more quickly receive the plot. Stand away,
friends; I know when he also prepares you to speak the things of friends, I
know when he also sows the things of enemies: first the sympathetic, and then
the treacherous. Stand away then, O friends; you shall not persuade me to
blaspheme; I know to speak beforehand the words of David: My mouth shall speak
wisdom, and the meditation of my heart understanding. But someone may
certainly say: What then? Did Job not sin, nor blaspheme, nor speak contrary?
He cursed all creation and cried out with pain: Let the day perish in which I
was born, and the night in which they said: Behold, a male. He insulted the
mother's nobility, he expected the mother's bosom as a tomb, he slandered the
nobility of the breasts, he insulted the common nursing with milk. Did he not
go after these things and things like them? How then did Job not sin? Because
the scripture has cried out: Neither did Job sin with his lips. Listen with
understanding. In every matter seek the aim of the matter, and you will in no
way fall away from the truth. Listen with understanding. As regards the words,
Job stumbled; as regards the words, not as regards the meanings, he railed at
the creation of God; but as regards the devil's proposal, Job did not sin at
all according to the divine scripture. But someone may certainly say: Did Job
not sin at all? Listen with understanding. The devil, having come to the Lord
according to thought, not according to manifestation: for this too was said
yesterday, that the devil answered the Lord according to thought, and not
before the eyes. For Moses the historian wrote down the scripture of Job. But
he was not in the times of Job, but far after Job; Job was fifth after
Abraham. And Moses after many generations, after the exodus from Egypt,
nevertheless wrote down the things concerning Job, not by his own will but by
the dictation of the Holy Spirit. The Holy Spirit then, as knowing all things
concerning the aim and the thought of the devil, dictated to Moses to set all
these things forth as history. For the Holy Spirit knew both the devil's
counter-speech and God's own counter-speech toward him. For the Holy Spirit
knows also the things of God, according to the blessed Paul who says: No one
knows the things of God except the Spirit of God that is in him, and again:
For the Spirit searches even the depths of God. The Holy Spirit then, having
beheld the devil's thought, dictated a history, so that we who hear might
apply ourselves more clearly to what is said. If then you hear anything of the
devil speaking, hear it according to thought; but if anything of God,
according to declaration: for this the Holy Spirit published. Coming then
according to thought to the Lord, the devil asked for Job, saying: Give me all
his authority, and I show how more shamelessly than all he will blaspheme you.
That is: give me all his governance, and I prepare Job to blaspheme you to
your face, that is, to do the things contrary to you, to proclaim you without
providence, to worship idols and leave you, to sacrifice to statues and not
offer to you, to raise temple-altars and to overthrow the sacrificial altars.
This aim the devil was bringing to pass, and to these things he expected to
set Job. If then Job went after any of these, he blasphemed, since he did the
devil's things; but if he went after none of these, he was righteous and
blameless and God-fearing according to the Lord's teaching: for whom God
justifies, who condemns? So Job did not sin at all in speaking human words; as
a man he spoke, being struck by the pains. For if Job had not spoken these
things and cursed the day, some would have been able to say that Job had
become insensible, or that he had exceeded human measures. From this it is
shown from Job's curse that he was wrapped in great tortures, from which, not
bearing the pain, he cried out humanly, saying: Why did I not die from my
mother's womb, and come forth and not perish at once? Why did knees meet me?
Why did I suck my mother's breasts? These things he said to himself, not as
one blaspheming the Creator. That is: Why did the maternal bosom not become a
tomb, that I might not wrestle and not know with whom I wrestle? If I had
someone of like form, I would have known how I hold and am held. For this
reason Job added, saying: For before my food a groan has come to me, and I
weep, being held by fear; for the fear which I was wary of has come to me, and
that which I dreaded has met me. For before my food a groan has come to me.
The words are proclaimed as human because of the suffering. For if Job had not
set these things forth, some were going to say, the very things they say even
now, that Job received great blows, yet he did not perceive the blows, because
God lightened them. Some say these things also in the present, that he did not
perceive the blows. What do you say, O friend? Did Job perceive the blows, or
did he not perceive? Did he receive a wage, or did he not receive? It is
surely clear to everyone that he perceived: for the one who contends is
temperate in all things. God makes the one who contends for him and receives
martyric blows perceive the blow also; he does not take the blow away. He
supplies endurance and furnishes strength toward the defeat of the
adversaries, but he makes him perceive, that the contest may be with wage. And
of this the blessed Paul is a witness, crying: There was given me a thorn in
the flesh, an angel of Satan, that he might buffet me, that I might not be
exalted. For this I besought the Lord three times, that it might depart from
me. And he has said to me: My grace is sufficient for you; for my power is
perfected in weakness. Truly in weakness the power of God is perfected. Job
was weakened, as you heard just now, with a weakness not disordering by nature
but dominating devilishly. He was ulcerated from head to nails; there was no
drug to apply, no physician to comfort, no oil to pour on, no other help to
bring, nor even one of the bodily things to apply, except only the God-given
plaster of endurance; for Job, using this, treated himself, and wounded the
devil. You heard just now the writer saying: And the devil went out from the
face of the Lord and struck Job with a wicked sore from feet to head. And Job
took a potsherd that he might scrape his ichor. Did you see a treatment
harsher than the suffering? He took a potsherd and scraped off the ichors; who
has seen a potsherd sponge? With a potsherd he scraped off the ichors; a
potsherd even when brought to a whole body raises wounds, and does not comfort
one already ulcerated. And he took a potsherd and scraped the ichors; with a
potsherd he comforted the potsherd, and he did not defile the treasure of
faith, being persuaded by Paul who says: And we have this treasure in earthen
vessels. He took a potsherd and scraped off his ichors. O fearful treatment,
or rather O most noble endurance. With a potsherd he scraped off the ichors.
Job only saw that he was ulcerated from every side and that he was defiled
from head to nails; beholding himself and reasoning that he is a king, a
rational statue, he quickly took the potsherd, as at a public birthday
festival, and scrapes the statue, that he might show it clean by endurance.
Therefore the devil also, when he saw Job going after this work, was
distressed, collapsed, cut himself, saying in himself: What shall I do? I have
been defeated by Job; justly the Lord bore witness to him; I failed in coming
to the wrestling; the temptations showed him more noble; I took all his
substance, and not only was he not bent down in grief, but on the contrary he
even threw at me the tunic he wore as a rag, saying with a loud voice: The
Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it seemed good to the Lord, so also it
has come to be; may the name of the Lord be blessed unto the ages. What shall
I do? Through all things he acts nobly; he stands beside me as an athlete; he
sheared his hair, he sprinkled dust, falling he worshipped the contest-master,
already he is winged as a victor. He holds nakedness more splendid than the
purple; I shattered his royal throne and he sits more splendid on the
dunghill. If he himself, having seen no one before him, so rises up against
me, what will those after him who see this accomplish? Will they not count me
a plaything? I both labor in bringing on the sufferings, and this man is not
distressed; from every side I painted him with the suffering, and this man
intensifies the pain, scraping me with a potsherd; for he is not bitten so
much as I am eaten up. What shall I do? I foresee defeat; I brought every
device, and I did not weaken him at all. One hope alone is left me, through
which I deceived Adam; I have a second Eve. In her I take heart; I know that
she will show me a victor; a man is more quickly tripped by a woman. For this
reason I also destroyed her existing children, hoping through her to lay hold
of the hunt. And that the devil kept her last and, having stored her up, sent
her, you heard just now Moses the historian saying: And when much time had
gone by, that you might learn Job's endurance, that he did not wrestle five or
ten days, but for a long time. And when much time had gone by his wife said to
him: How long will you endure, saying: Behold, I wait yet a little time? Turn
to my counsel, recognize your wife, see who I am. I counsel just things to
you: say some word toward the Lord and die. But Job to her: Shall I turn
toward you? You said well: "Turn and see if you recognize me"; I do not
recognize who you are. You do not have the face from before the temptations;
you now bring me another form, which dwells in the serpent and in you. I am
not persuaded by you, woman; I do not lose paradise through you. Go away from
me, woman; I do not sew fig leaves, I do not hear through you the Lord saying
reproachfully: "Job, where are you?" Go away, woman; it is not permitted for a
woman to sit beside in a wrestling-pit. I box, and you announce misfortunes to
me? These things are examined at home; for it is not at the Olympics that one
trains, is it? Go away, woman. And to God be glory unto the ages of ages.
Amen.
