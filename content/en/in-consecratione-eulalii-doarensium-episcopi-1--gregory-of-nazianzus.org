ORATION XIII.
Homily on the ordination of Doara, issued to Eulalius the bishop.
A. Receive our word, brethren, even if it be least, even if it fall far short
of the dignity; yet the Master God knows how to weigh mercy by just judgment.
For he receives both Paul's planting, as Paul's, and Apollos' =35.853=
watering, and the two mites of the widow, and the publican's humility, and
Manasseh's confession. Receive a newly built word upon a newly built shepherd;
receive a voice of thanksgiving upon the wonders that are seen. We are small
and least among the sons of Israel; yet nothing hinders even the small from
giving thanks richly. The more perfect praise, then, the more perfect will
altogether render to God; but we will bring in today that which is according
to our strength. Therefore sing to the Lord a new song, for he has done
wondrous things. We were tossed, we were warred upon, we were thrust out; some
of the terrors were already present, and some were about to be. But who set
the storm into a breeze? Who crushed weapon, and sword, and war? Who received
us who had been thrust out and cast away? Was it not you, O God, who worked
these things? The Lord strong and mighty, the Lord mighty in war.
B. This one cut the sea; this one turned a fugitive people in the wilderness,
granting a strange rain; this one made a rock spring forth; this one warred
down Amalek, by an ineffable and mystical figure of hands. For this is what a
priest's hands availed, lifted upon the mountain and shaped into prayer, which
many myriads did not avail. This one shook down walls without battle and
without war; and, to speak of the last things, this one brought down Goliath
who dared against the great David, the lifted-up, and boastful, and descendant
of the giants. For these things then let us say with one mind: Blessed be the
Lord, who has not given us as a prey to their teeth; and, Our soul passed
through a torrent; as a sparrow it was delivered from the snare of the
hunters; and whatever else a soul overflowing with joy utters over God's great
gift. We did not come to cast a sword, but peace. We did not come for the
dishonor of the great shepherd, who sits in front of the illustrious city; we
know him as honorable, we recognize him as head, we call him holy, even when
we are wronged; only let him be a lover of his children, and providing for
every Church. We have procured an addition of priests, not a subtraction; an
overturning of heretics, not a lessening of the orthodox.
C. What do you say, O child of Dathan and Abiram, and =35.856= incorrigible
general, who dared against Moses, and laid hands upon us, as they laid their
tongues upon the great servant? Did you not shudder? Were you not put to
shame? Did not your flesh flow down to the earth as you thought these things?
Then do you stretch these hands to God? Then will you bring gifts? Then will
you pray for a people? I fear lest the sword of God should rust so far, and be
at rest. You have granted nothing great to your shepherd, and you have
penalized yourself with the greatest things, alienating yourself from the
grace of God.
D. But now, O best and most perfect of shepherds, come to me and receive your
people both with us and before us, whom the Holy Spirit entrusted to you, whom
angels bring forward, whom life and manner have given in trust. And if you
inherit the throne through trials and hindrances, do not wonder; nothing of
the great things is untested, nothing unexamined. For by nature easiness
follows the low things, and the hard-won follows the high. You have heard the
one who said, That we must through many afflictions enter into the kingdom of
the heavens; say also yourself: We went through fire and water, and you
brought us out into refreshment. O the wonder! At evening weeping lodged, and
in the morning gladness. Let those who make war babble and gape about, like
dogs barking in vain; but let us not be warred upon. Teach to worship God the
Father, God the Son, God the Holy Spirit, in three hypostases, in one glory
and brightness. Seek the lost; strengthen the weak; guard the strong. We
demand of you such good repute in the spiritual things as we have known in the
worldly. May you then receive the more perfect arming from the greater
generals, by which you will be able to quench the fiery darts of the evil one,
and to present to the Lord a peculiar people, a holy nation, a royal
priesthood, in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory unto the ages.
Amen.
