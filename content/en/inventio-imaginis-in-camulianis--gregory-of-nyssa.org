* The Finding of the Image at Camuliana
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

On the 9th of the same month, The finding of the not made by hands and divine
image of the Camulians, written by our father among the saints Gregory, bishop
of Nyssa.
1 Our Lord Jesus Christ, the righteous and only true God of ours, invisible in
the essence of the Godhead, invincible in might, unutterable in power, by
nature loving toward man and good, unregretting in gift; the unchanging image
of the Father, who before the ages was begotten invisibly of the Father and in
the last of the days was born of a virgin mother, here again deigned to appear
in a holy image through his own goodness and immeasurable compassion. And
there, leading magi toward a journey by a star, but here leading those
infantile in mind back to perfect knowledge toward himself. O unspeakable love
of man, O immeasurable care, O ineffable forbearance, O inexpressible gift, O
incomprehensible mysteries; truly a paradoxical and fearful wonder, that in us
who are of clay the Creator is beheld with an immaterial impress. There from a
virgin he appeared as a youth through birth; here the same one deigned today
to be worshipped in a bodily image. O wonder even more paradoxical than
wonders; for again a condescension of a man-loving Master toward his own
servants has come to pass.
2 Come then, brothers and fathers, hear, and I will recount to you, to all who
fear the Lord, as many things as have come to pass in the new Bethlehem of the
Camulians, and the things concerning the precious image and the blessed Bassa
who was renamed Aquilina, I the humble Gregory will set forth in the midst, as
revealed to me the unworthy by the Holy Spirit.
3 This blessed woman, being a Hellene and having a husband named Camulus,
himself also a Hellene and a toparch, and being furiously disposed against the
Christians according to the edict of Diocletian the emperor, having been
illumined by divine grace sought to withdraw both from her husband and from
his religion, hastening to have hope and knowledge toward the Master and King
of life alone, in secret because of the fear she has of her lawless husband,
and she was always entreating the Lord to receive holy baptism, occupying
herself with all-night prayers and with the daily fasts, carrying about in
herself every purity of soul and body, and persisting in chastity. Wherefore
she was also deemed worthy of the revelation of the Holy Spirit.
4 For as she was praying to our Lord Jesus Christ with tears and a contrite
heart, God who does the will of those who fear him heard her petition, having
measured her faith, and he says: "Since for the salvation of men I came down
of the Holy Spirit and of Mary the virgin, I now also come to you, having
pitied your tears. Therefore prepare a clean table and set upon it a white
kidaris and an untouched translucent vessel with water and make them ready in
an adorned inner chamber and cast yourself upon the floor, that of the outer
bedchamber, and my right hand will cover you and I will appear to you as I
will."
5 And the blessed Aquilina fell thus, as the divine voice commanded her. And
the Master Christ came down to her, O strange and fearful mystery, he who is
always and everywhere present and never overlooks those who hope and cry out
to him and long for him. Ah the condescension and ineffable love of man, that
he deemed his servants worthy of so great gifts; and there came together


with him also all the powers of the heavens about the fifth watch of the
night, singing and saying, "Holy, holy, holy, Lord Sabaoth." And he who
formerly washed the feet of his disciples and wiped them with the towel with
which he was girded, himself even now washed with his hands in the water his
own face, O the wonder, and having washed he wiped with the clean kidaris his
undefiled and unsearchable face; and immediately there was shown forth as a
type of his divine-human form the all-holy and most true impress, as is shown
to all today. And just as formerly by his own love of man and condescension he
displayed his own incarnation, so also now he displays this to the precious
and most devout Aquilina.
6 Wherefore when these things had been thus arranged, the woman gave thanks to
the Lord and did not become sated with the thanksgiving. Having laid away such
a holy impress in one pinsos of her own house, fearing her husband, she
honored it exceedingly. Foreknowing then her own departure, having made the
best provision for this holy image not made by hands and uncolored, and having
written the things that followed upon it and laid them away with it, she
secured it.
7 And after her death it was revealed to me, the unworthy Gregory, in the
Camulians, in her dwelling-place, through the Holy Spirit, and having gone
according to the place shown to me, having dug out the wall, I found a casket,
in which was laid the holy impress of the paternal radiance, and the lamp that
had been hung by the blessed woman, burning from very many and unremembered
years, O the wonder, and a small censer providing the fragrance of the
incense.
8 This first and greatest wonder I, the least bishop, have seen with my own
eyes, and thus they made it manifest to all, and I chose that it be placed in
the metropolis of the Caesareans. Through which also many healings came to
pass, as many as also at his incarnation, of the blind and the lame and the
demon-possessed and the paralytics, healed at once, that the fullness of the
grace and of the gift of the Holy Spirit might become apparent, that he
himself, the Word of the beginningless Father who became man, both lives and
has been made strong and remains and reigns unto the ages of ages.
9 These things came to pass among the Camulians, the undefiled and not made by
hands image having come into being and been hidden in the reign of Diocletian;
but under Theodosius the Great having been made manifest and working wonders,
to the glory of Father and Son and Holy Spirit, of the one Godhead and
kingdom, now and unto the endless ages.
