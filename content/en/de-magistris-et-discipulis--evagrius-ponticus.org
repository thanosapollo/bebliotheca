43 An unskilled helmsman is ready shipwreck, and an unlearned superior
destruction of disciples. An inexperienced shepherd has twisted the
ankle-bones of sheep, and a superior the ways of disciples. An unlearned
shepherd will not distinguish in a mist in the midst of sheep, nor in a
time of temptations will an inexperienced teacher say anything
profitable. A skilled superior is a general of war and a physician of
passions; for either he will not allow one to be wounded, or he will
quickly heal the wounded. A disobedient disciple is a warped timber and
will not be set straight even when continually rebuked. A discordant
brother is a string of a jarring lyre and loosed from the communion of
the common member. A drone eats the labors of bees, and a slothful
brother lessens the virtuous practice of the company. A cowardly soldier
unstrings the hands of warriors, and a careless monk slackens the
eagerness of brothers. A swarm of bees works a honeycomb, and a company
of brothers the kingdom of heavens. A trumpet stirs the eagerness of
warriors, and teaching of practical virtue the zeal of disciples. Arm
your brothers, O superior, with words of †fingers, and breastplate them
with promises of the coming goods; fence them with a shield of faith for
contests and greave them with the hope toward God; †κήνησον the feet in
evangelical teachings and gird the loin with words of chastity; give a
sword into the hand against the passions, most sharp anger, and array a
phalanx of passions against pleasure. Train in thoughts the hoplites who
are experienced toward the fight; and show the defeat as fearful by the
threat of the eternal punishment. Proclaim the victor with splendid
praises, for good report also knows how to make men more zealous; and
tend the one who has been abused with sympathy, for pardon turns the
intelligent no less than a threat. Let him not be moved with anger
against a disciple who has sinned, for it is not holy to wound oneself
before the healing of another; but correct without malice toward the
good; for a physician heals the passion, but is not indignant toward the
one who has fallen sick involuntarily. A physician cutting does this
without wrath; and a teacher rebuking, let him not mix anger with the
rebuke. He leads those fleeing the land Egypt through a desert way; for
scarcity knows how to train toward continence even those beginning
virtue. Pitch camp at Elim (and they themselves are interpreted gates),
a symbol of the entrance that leads toward virtue, and show the seventy
palms. And pitch camp by the twelve springs (for you must also proclaim
the things of victory, palms a symbol, and soothe the flame of the
passions, furnishing streams abundantly) until, having overthrown all
the alien nations, they arrive at the land of the promise, in which they
will live the blessed life of impassibility, receiving as wage of the
labors the true gladness.
