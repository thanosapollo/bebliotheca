A HOMILY OF THE SAME
Against those who slander us by saying that we teach three gods.
It is a great consolation to souls wounded by hatred, to be hated
together with the Lord. "For if the world hates you, know," he says,
"that it has hated me before you." So if the Beloved is hated, what
wonder is it if we too are thought worthy of hatred among men who have
hatred to spare? The memory of griefs checks my speech, creeping over
my heart; and the falling tear cuts off the voice, when
I remember that love and peace were left to us by the Lord, and we do
not seek what was left. The gift has vanished, found in no one. Love
was left behind, and strife has taken up residence. Union was given,
and hatred has been kindled. We have lit a great bonfire of hatred
against one another; each of us wails by himself, yet we are not
reconciled to one another. Who will give me the whole inhabited world
as a theater, and a voice stronger than a trumpet, and the laments of
Jeremiah, and an abundant tear =31.1489= to break a heart crushed with
pains, to pour out the dust now heaped on us from our dishonor, and to
mourn the common disaster, that love, the root of the commandments, has
failed? For this reason I embrace those present with an unwearied
spiritual embrace, because they have shown a strange sight: one joined
fast to another. For we have become, each by himself, like sand, not
joined to one another, but each divided on his own. For neither can a
building stand when the bonds are taken away, nor can the Church grow
to a height unless it is bound together by the bond of peace and of
love. Lend us your hearing a little, with patience; we are not speaking
for display, but hurrying to take you into a share of our griefs. For
the sharing of groans brings consolation to those who lament. Perhaps
our grief, joined and offered to the Lord, might also reach him with
more force. Or will each of us be heard by himself, given the feebleness
that comes from our works? This very sight is a remnant of the old love
of the fathers. For this reason they devised these festivals, that the
estrangement time produces might be repaired by meeting together at
these seasons; and that those who live abroad, gathering in this one
place, might offer the meeting as a beginning of friendship and love
for themselves. This is a spiritual festival, renewing the ancient
things, offering a beginning to those who are to come. For we have not
come to exchange merchandise, but to give one another an exchange of
love, to give it full and to receive love full. These things our
fathers established. What we who received them have come to, the things
you see will show. Most of those present are spies of what is said
rather than disciples of the teachings. And a word is sought not for
the building up of those present, but for the abuse of those lying in
wait. And if something is said that matches the desires of those who
drive our teaching into exile, he goes away having accepted it, as
having found his own among us. Let these things be written in your
hearts. Bear
witness to the truth and to us before those who whisper such things,
that we have in fact renewed the ancient gift. For these are the words
of those who do not fear the Lord, who open their mouth against us, as
though we were proclaiming three gods. He who preaches three gods
=31.1492= what does he seek in the Church of the Lord? Polytheism
belongs in one place, piety toward God in another. Let him who says
three dare
four as well; let him stretch the number to

twelve. What then is the madness of those who have a tongue whetted
against the truth? I do not lose heart at the accusation, but I wait
for the judgment seat of Christ. There those who stitch this abuse will
stand against me. "The Lord knows those who are his"; and, "He who
slanders a poor man provokes the one who made him." If I hand on the
faith of the Fathers, why, leaving the Fathers aside, do you make war
on me? If I believe in the Father, if I confess the Son, if I do not
set aside the Spirit. If the one who confesses the Trinity names three
gods, he sets aside baptism and makes war on the faith. Why, putting my
person forward, do you make war on the Lord? Who left the command to
baptize in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy
Spirit? I, or the Master? Whose are the words? A herald's, or the one
who sent him? Why, finding my person an easy mark for slanders, do you
make war on the truth through me? Through me do you shake the fortress
of the faith? I am easy prey for you; the faith is unshaken. "One
Lord," learn from Paul saying, not two, or three. Even if I name the
Son Lord, I have not divided the lordship into two lords, nor into many
gods. The Father is Lord, the Son is Lord; "one faith," since there is
one Lord. One follows one: to one Lord, one faith; to one faith, "one
baptism." Thus one is confirmed from one through one. But if I do not
set aside the Spirit, nor place it in the rank of a slave, it is for
this that I receive that slander. Do not say what I do not say, and let
us see the plausibility of your slander. Why do you hide your own
abuse? You lie, accursed one, saying that three gods are proclaimed
among us, and you do not say openly that we are the ones who
anathematize those who call the Holy Spirit a creature. This charge I
accept. For this I endure fire and a whetted sword. Even if the wheel
crash down, even if tortures be moved against me, I receive the
tortures in the same full assurance in which the martyrs who lie here
were thought worthy of the crowns. This, then, charge against me: that
I do not number the Holy Spirit among the creatures. And if you say
anything beyond that, you will give account to the Judge. "When the
Paraclete comes" was being read just now; but you have made the slanders
=31.1493= an occasion for abuse. Paraclete, he says, means the one who
pleads concerning you. Through the very things by which he does you
good, through these you are thankless. You do not, then, accept the
Paraclete's benefaction. Or did the Lord not himself lay down that this
title was fitting for himself? "I will send you another Paraclete
also." He who said "another also," did he not show himself before the
other? If I speak from myself, do not listen to me; if I read the
things written, bow to the truth. The perfect is neither diminished nor
increased. One unbegotten God; one Only-begotten of his, the Son and
God. Just as there is no other co-unbegotten God, so neither is there
another co-begotten Son? Just as the Father did not come to be by a
word, so neither did the Son. The Father is God, the Son is God; the
Father is perfect God; the Son also is perfect God. The Father is
bodiless; the Son is bodiless, the impress of the bodiless, and a
bodiless image. Do you believe that he has been begotten? Do not ask,
How? For if it is permitted to ask how the unbegotten is unbegotten, it
is also permitted to ask how the begotten has been begotten; but if the
unbegotten leaves no room to inquire how he is unbegotten, so neither
does the begotten leave room to inquire how he has been begotten. Do
not seek the things that cannot be found; for you will not find them.
For if you seek, from whom will you learn? From earth? It did not yet
subsist. From the sea? It was not yet wet. From heaven? It had not yet
been raised up. From sun and moon and stars? They had not been created.
From the ages, then? The Only-begotten is before the ages. Do not
examine the things that are not always, concerning the one who always
is. But if you will not, and you contend, I laugh at your folly, or
rather I weep at your daring. "In the beginning was the Word, and the
Word was with God, and the Word was God." The "was" shuts out the "was
not"; and the "God" shuts out the "not God." Believe the things
written; do not seek the things not written. This Word, the Son of God,
became man because of the fallen man

Adam. Because of Adam the bodiless one was in a body. Because of the
body the Word took the body as a cloud, lest he burn up the things that
are seen. He came down with flesh, so that the flesh also might be
raised up with him. The invisible in the visible, that he might put on
the things that are seen. As man, under time; as God, before times. The
"as" is not by resemblance, but in truth he is both God and man in both
respects. He was God; he became man according to the economy. The
"became" was not first, and then the "was"; but first the "was," and
=31.1496= then the "became." Let not, then, what belongs to the
economy do away with the Godhead of the Only-begotten. For these are
the deviations of heretics, and the mad ravings of Manichees. "For no
one can lay another foundation besides the one that is laid, which is
Jesus Christ." He was born through a woman, that he might regenerate
those who had been born. He was crucified willingly, that he might take
down those who had not been crucified of their own will. He died
willingly, that he might raise those who had died unwillingly. He
accepted a death he did not have to accept, that he might give life to
those under death. Death swallowed, not knowing; having swallowed, it
knew whom it had swallowed. It swallowed life, and was swallowed by
life. It swallowed the one with all, and through the one it lost all.
It snatched like a lion, and had its teeth crushed. For this reason it
is even despised as weak. For we no longer fear it as a lion, but we
trample it as a hide. Let the begetting of the Only-begotten from the
Father be honored with silence. For he who begot knows, and he who was
begotten. For we ought to know what must be spoken and what must be
kept silent. Not all things are speakable to the tongue, lest the mind,
like an eye wanting to look at the whole sun, lose even the light it
had. For in this you know, if you know, that you have not grasped it.
That begetting, then, being unspeakable, let us honor with silence. And
if we also grow weak concerning this one, let us not be grieved. For
that one is fearful because of the nature, but this one is hard to
interpret because of its strangeness. John was called a voice of one
crying in the wilderness, but he was a man by nature. Do not, then, do
away with the hypostasis of the Only-begotten because of the name
"Word." But perhaps you say: Why then, sitting at the right, is he more
honored? But if he had the honor from himself, he would be more
honored; but if he received it from the one who begot him, the grace is
of the giver. There was no other Son, that he might sit on the left.
One unbegotten God, and one Only-begotten of his, Son and God. It was
fitting, then, not that the Son stretch out the right hand to the
Father, but that the Father stretch it to the Son. That is: our nature
having been set apart, set apart the thoughts as well. For a father
among us is from another father, and needs time, and rearing, and
growth, and other care, and he does not beget whatever he wishes; but
God, having power concurrent with his will, begot one
worthy of himself; he begot as he himself knows; to whom be the glory
and the might unto the ages of ages. Amen.
