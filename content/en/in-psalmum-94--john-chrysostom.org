Of the same, on Come, let us rejoice in the Lord.
=55.615=
The cry of the trumpet rouses the hoplite to war, and stations him ready for
battle against the enemies; and the passing of winter has compelled sailors to
go from harbor onto a ship. And the melodist David, sounding a festal strain
through the lyre, has led us today to this spiritual feast. For what does he
say? Come, let us rejoice in the Lord, let us shout unto God our Saviour. Let
us come before his face =55.616= with confession. O Davidic voice; O good
calling; O spiritual food: Come, let us rejoice in the Lord, let us shout unto
God our Saviour. Let us come before his face with confession. O Master's
compassion; O prophetic exhortation calling unto repentance.

Have you seen, beloved, the goodness of the Master's compassion, and the
gladness of the prophetic preaching; how it urges us all unto repentance and
calls us toward piety and salvation, saying: Come, let us rejoice in the Lord.
The Lord commanded, the prophet called, let us obey. For he has not called us
to a perishable meal, wishing to satisfy =55.617= the desire of the belly.
Thus he calls us through the sweet-voiced lyre: Come, let us rejoice in the
Lord. All of us therefore who have bound ourselves with the cords of his love,
and who bear the yoke of obedience, let us draw near to the Master, having
made a cleansing of the evils done in the day. For the present life belongs to
repentance, but the coming advent belongs to threat and judgment. Hear the
prophet himself saying: But in Hades who shall confess to you? Therefore let
not our mind be bound with many-twisted cords, but let us entreat him from the
whole heart; for he is ready to hear the petition of those who ask; only do
you not neglect to ask him. God is present, more ready than you. You have no
need of a long journey, nor to cut through seas; but hear him saying: The
kingdom of heaven is within you. You have no need of many expenses and
outlays, but turn and groan, and at once you have made the Master gracious.
Hezekiah once turned to the wall, not the one composed of stones, but the one
built through prophetic and apostolic teaching. I mean that wall of which Paul
boasts, saying: As a wise master-builder I have laid a foundation; I mean that
wall upon which the cornerstone was set, that is, Christ, of whom Scripture
said that Holy stones shall be rolled; I mean that wall through which the
temple of God was built.

What are you doing, O Hezekiah? Why do you turn toward a wall? He says, I seek
him who is giver of life and deliverer from death; I seek him, and I flee to
him. Yet having turned and repented he reaped life, and he escaped a sentence
of God brought out against him, and God added to him fifteen years of life;
and when enemies had walled it round about and encircled the city, an angel
became a champion, and he laid low those so great multitudes of the Assyrians.
So much did repentance prevail. For an angel of the Lord went out, he says,
and in the array of the Assyrians he slew a hundred and eighty-five thousand;
but their leader Sennacherib he let go alive, for no other reason, as I at
least think, than that God was calling him to repentance. At any rate, when
God saw him not persuaded, nor turning from his wickedness, but being
implacable and inexorable, he put him to death by his two sons. For when he
expected to escape the all-destroying and God-sent death, and to be in his own
land, and that the things of peace were secure for him, then Adramelech and
Sarasar his two sons slay him, and flee to the mountains of Ararat. Do you
see, beloved, how much confession availed Hezekiah? And how much harm
persistence in sins worked for Sennacherib? Do you wish me to show you another
way of repentance, and a door of confession?

Imitate the repentance of those barbarians, I mean the Ninevites. For they,
having become hearers of a prophet, not having received gospel words, not
having heard a prophet of God, not having received the preaching of the
kingdom of heaven, see how they approach God, how they prevail upon God, how
they reconcile the Lord who is angry with them; how they loosed a sentence of
God; how they thrust away the wrath of God, and called upon loving-kindness;
how, their city being a pit and a ruin, they made it stronger than a wall, and
firmer than any adamant. It was not the dignity of purple that prevailed with
God, but sackcloth; nor a royal diadem and power, but ashes with sackcloth;
not a gold-inlaid =55.618= throne composed of stones, but confession. O power
of sackcloth, outshining and conquering royal purple; O power of ashes,
stronger than diadem and royal throne. For while the king was in the diadem,
and in the purple and on the throne that held his power, that is, the royal
scepter, with the armies standing round him, and a great bodyguard encircling
him, the prophet comes into the midst naked, with dusty feet, sweating in the
face, and says: Yet three days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown. What are you
doing, O prophet? If the Spirit of God is in you at all, why do you not know
the will of God, that not wishing to kill, but to call back to repentance, he
therefore gave the sentence? God said nothing else to me, he says, but this
only he said, that Yet three days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown.

What are you doing, O Jonah? Why do you not also add other teaching? For what
else did Isaiah hear from God to say to Israel? Nothing else but one word. And
what was this? Say, he says, to this people: Hearing you shall hear, and shall
not understand, and seeing you shall see, and shall not perceive. And do you
see how much other teaching and instruction he sounded into them? How much
urging and exhortation and rebuke? Rebuke, when he says: The ox knows his
owner, and the ass his master's crib. But Israel has not known me, and the
people has not understood me. These things were the speech of one rebuking.
Hear him also urging: Wash yourselves, become clean, take away the
wickednesses from your souls. And how again he exhorts: If you are willing, he
says, and listen to me, you shall eat the good of the land. And he discourses
with them of still more things than these, having written a whole book for a
testimony. And he did not stop at this, but he also brings in witnesses at the
beginning of the teaching, saying: Hear, O heaven, and give ear, O earth, for
the Lord has spoken. What has he spoken? Say it clearly, O Isaiah. Sons, he
says, I have begotten and raised up, but they have rejected me. But you did
none of these things, O Jonah, but you proclaimed the overthrow, saying: Yet
three days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown. But concerning God you said
nothing to them, nor did you say, For the Lord has spoken, as Isaiah did; but
you bring forth a savage and inhuman sentence.

But nevertheless the Ninevites, even if they did not receive instruction of
teaching, even if they did not become hearers of prophets, did not receive
promises of gospel grace, not a desire of the kingdom, not a fear of
punishment, but having a brutish manner, and a barbarian and inhuman
character, when they heard the sentence, straightway they turn to repentance,
confessing the things they had done, and this not knowing whether it would be
forgiven them. For what were they saying? Who knows if God will turn and
repent, and save us? For they do not know how to look to others who have
repented and been saved, not to other Ninevites, but they still doubt
concerning their salvation; yet they repent. And straightway the king, casting
off diadem and purple, puts on sackcloth and ashes, and so does the whole city
from small to great among them, and not only the men, but also the cattle and
the oxen and the sheep. What are you doing, O king? Because men have sinned,
why have you also condemned the cattle to fast? Why do you also clothe them in
sackcloth and ashes?

We, he says, have been pierced through with many sins, and we are not worthy
to entreat God. For with what face shall we come and petition God? Therefore
we have made those who have not sinned to intercede for us. For one may see
this happening also among men. For when someone has offended one of those in
high place, those who have not offended petition for him, that his fault may
be forgiven. This we also do. We have sinned inexcusably, we are not worthy to
entreat for ourselves and to prevail upon God; and therefore we have made the
cattle and the oxen and the sheep to bow down and supplicate God. O strength
of repentance; O power of confession. Repentance established the city, having
loosed the sentence of God. But nevertheless besides this the prophet
disbelieves the salvation of the Ninevites; and having gone he sat over
against the city, watching what would happen to the city; =55.619= whether the
sentence would prevail, or repentance. And both lay as on a balance and as in
the swing of a scale; on this side the sentence, and on that repentance; and
repentance, having conquered death, raised up the city. Then, to cut the
account short, Jonah was grieved as being about to appear a liar. Then what
does the all-wise God do to him? He commands the sun to cast fire-flaming rays
upon his head, so that he could not bear the heat of the sun. And when he had
been burning for a long time and was utterly distressed, through the night God
commands a morning gourd to come up and shade Jonah's head. Here I would
gladly ask the Jew, saying to him: Tell me, O Jew, you who say, How did the
virgin give birth without a man, and how, having given birth, she remained a
virgin still? Tell me first, how the earth in one hour of the morning put
forth the gourd from itself, and how in one instant it was leafy with foliage,
and shaded the prophet; and I will tell you how the virgin bore, and after the
birth remained a virgin. But let us not cast the holy things to the dogs. Let
the unbelieving Jews be cast out of our roll. Then, while the gourd was
shading him, God commanded again a morning worm, and it struck the gourd, and
it withered at once. See, O Jew, and do not disbelieve, when God works some
very great thing. It came into being overnight, and overnight it perished.
Then when the gourd had withered, Jonah was utterly distressed and lamented,
and his spirit failed, grieving over the gourd, and he said: It is better for
me to die than to live. To whom the all-wise God came and consoles him,
saying: Are you exceedingly grieved over the gourd? And Jonah said: I am
exceedingly grieved, even unto death. And again God to him: You were grieved
over the gourd, for which you did not labor, nor did you nourish it, which
came into being overnight, and overnight perished; and shall I not be grieved
over Nineveh the great city, in which dwell more than a hundred and twenty
thousand men, who have not known, from the multitude of those who dwell there,
their right hand or their left, and much cattle? And what he says is of this
sort: I am a lover of mankind, he says, and not wishing to destroy the works
of my hands, which I made in love.

For this reason I threatened overthrow and death, not as wishing to kill, but
to urge unto repentance. Having waited so many circuits of years for =55.620=
their turning, when they had turned, ought I to have overlooked them? This is
not my way, O Jonah. For you as a man wish to condemn, but I as God have
loving-kindness as my part. Glory to you, Lord, glory to you. How great a
wealth of loving-kindness and mercy is with you. Those who formerly were
subjected to a death sentence have now been set as teachers of the inhabited
world. For all, looking to them, are led by the hand, and taking their
repentance we remain unshipwrecked. Do you see, beloved, how much repentance
prevails? Let us not therefore put off day after day. For you do not know what
the morrow will bring forth. For the day of the Lord comes as a thief in the
night.

Come then, let us rejoice in the Lord, let us shout unto God our Saviour. Let
us come before his face with confession. For the issue of our life is unclear,
not waiting for a season, not appointed as a time over us. But whenever he who
has given breath to all commands, then the soul, having flown away from the
body, leaves it desolate. Each one therefore, knowing the uncertainty of the
departure, let us wait for the bridegroom, and when he knocks at the door, let
us kindle for him our lamps, always kept burning, having oil with us also, so
that whenever a sound comes of those saying, The bridegroom is coming, we may
enter in with him, lest we too be found with the five foolish virgins outside
the bridechamber. Let us not nod into slumber, beloved, but let us always have
oil in our lamps. For he will not permit us in that hour to go and buy oil
from those who sell; for here it is sold, and there it is spent.

And who are the sellers, but the poor? Give bread, and you receive What eye
has not seen, and ear has not heard, and what has not entered into the heart
of man, the things God has prepared for those who love him. Come then, let us
rejoice in the Lord, let us shout unto God our Saviour.

Let us come before his face with confession, for The night is far spent, and
the day of this uncertain life has drawn near. And may it be that we all,
being light from light, walk in the light of the glory of Christ our Saviour,
in him our Lord, to whom be glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
