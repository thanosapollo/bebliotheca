On the nativity of our Lord Jesus Christ.
=61.763=
Whenever a warm spring shines forth from a freezing winter, then the earth
also puts forth green grass, and the trees are made fair with flowering
trunks, and the air, lit by the sun, is made bright, and every choir of birds,
flying through the aether, cries out with songs. Then also those who dwell in
the mountains, both cattle-herds and shepherds, coming out from their huts,
recast their looks by the beauty of the air, and answering the cries of the
sweet-voiced birds with many-toned reeds as on a lyre, sweetening the senses
of the unreasoning sheep, they lead the lambs to the grass with a gentle step.
Then the farmer also, having sharpened the sickle on the rock in his shelter,
goes to the vineyard to cut the barren branch of the vine. Then also the
sailors of the sea, seeing the deep without storm, leaving the dry land,
having entrusted their souls to wood and water and wind, sporting upon the
swelling backs of the sea, fly upon it without fear. And in a word, that I may
not lengthen the discourse in the prologue, every craft, and every occupation
made for men toward the need of life, when the calm air has shone forth, has
unhindered motion toward the impulse of work. Come then, since for us too the
heavenly spring, Christ, has risen from virgin loins, and has scattered the
cold and stormy mists of the devil, and by his divine rays has awakened the
sleeping hearts of men from the smoke of ignorance, let us spur our mind
toward the heavenly and blessed glory.
But the nativity of Christ, flashing forth divinity, stops the course of my
tongue, and draws it to itself. For when I bring before my sight the infant
born of the Virgin, and the virgin without a man, and after childbirth
remaining a virgin =61.764=, I inquire into the work, and not finding the
manner, I seal the matter with faith. For how is it not truly wonderful and
surpassing every thought, that a virgin should give birth, and be found a
virgin after the birth? And that she gave birth, the infant conceived proved;
and that after the conception she was a virgin, the very nature of her body
taught. See then a mystery visible and invisible, held and not held, handled
and not found. For who did not see and hold both the infant and the mother?
But who, seeing the mother a virgin, and the infant a virgin's child, by
inquiring finds out the mystery? O mystery truly heavenly and earthly, held
and not held, seen and not appearing! For such also was Christ who was born
today, heavenly and earthly, held and not held, seen and invisible: heavenly
according to the nature of the divinity, earthly according to the relation of
the humanity, seen according to the flesh, invisible according to the spirit,
held according to the flesh, ungraspable according to the Word.
But Christ, being God almighty, as he willed, being born appeared to his own
world; yet more does the travail of the Virgin strike my mind, that a Virgin,
not having come down from heaven, but seen from the seed of David, not being
of spirit, but constituted of body and soul, was receiving in the womb? And
the bulk of the womb attested the pregnancy, and the sower of the infant
within did not appear. For he dwells in heaven, and mystically brought the
infant to completion in the virgin womb. He was born through nature, and the
midwives handled both the nature that was giving birth and the infant, and
they did not find corruption, as in other women; for it was God who was coming
forth through her. O bush, =61.765= which Moses saw on the mountain burning,
and not consumed! Virgin Mary, giving birth and not corrupted; O stone, which
Daniel saw cut from a mountain without a human hand! O virgin mother of a
virgin infant, and holy child of an unwedded mother! Where are the busybodies?
Let them tell us how the Virgin without a man bore an infant, and having given
birth was not corrupted.
And if the manner is unsearchable, since the offspring is of the Spirit, much
more is the ineffable generation of the Word from the Father untraceable. But
you believe, and you observe that having given birth she was not corrupted;
yet you accuse God, saying: But if the incorruptible Father, having begotten
his Son, according to your folly was corrupted, much more would the Virgin,
being of a corruptible nature, having given birth have been corrupted. But if
you yourself bear witness that the Virgin, having given birth, was not
corrupted, much more believe that the incorruptible Father, having begotten
the incorruptible Son, was not corrupted. But leaving the vain-talkers, let us
run back to the theological heralds. Now when Jesus Christ was born in
Bethlehem of Judea, in the days of Herod the king.
The evangelist necessarily notes both the place and the time, that the
unbeliever who doubts the Savior's birth according to the flesh, having sought
the place and the time, may find the fact of the truth.
For he will find the date from Herod by looking into the chroniclers, and the
place from Bethlehem by seeking its designation. For if in worldly contracts
both consuls are written, and months are numbered, and days are shown, and
cities are marked out for the accuracy of what is said: how much more, when
the salvation of the whole world has been born, must the accuracy of the place
and the season be made known also to those afterward! And Matthew thus
narrates the birth of Christ according to the flesh; but Luke, for the
accuracy of the times, adds also the census that then first took place. For he
says thus: In the forty-first year under Caesar Augustus, There went out a
decree from Augustus, that all the inhabited world should be enrolled. This
was the first enrollment. And Joseph also went up from Galilee into Judea into
the city of David, which is called Bethlehem, because he was of the house and
lineage of David, to be enrolled with Mary who was betrothed to him, being a
wife with child. And Luke notes the first enrollment that took place, at once
both making the time exact, and from what is apparent secretly showing a very
great mystery that is not apparent. For he says a first and compulsory
enrollment, the coming-to-be of man. For when Christ was born, and flesh
thereafter being deified, and the devil being cast out, and death being
dissolved, it was necessary that souls of the righteous, and names of the
faithful, be enrolled in the books of the heavens, Caesar commanding below,
but the Father ordering the angels above. Therefore the Lord also, proclaiming
this good news to his disciples, said: Do not rejoice that the demons are
subject to you, but rejoice that your names have been written in the heavens.
Now when Jesus was born in Bethlehem of Judea, in the days of Herod, behold,
magi from the east arrived saying, Where is he who has been born king of the
Jews? For we have seen his star in the east, and have come to worship him.
Woe is me! I am overcome by the wonders, I am constrained by the
contemplations, and my tongue does not find what first to say, or what to
omit.
If I consider his birth, I am struck, that before the child comes forth from
nature, he accomplishes signs. For is it not a very great sign, that a virgin
after giving birth remain a virgin, and that heaven, being silent, speak
through a star?
And to those thirsting to see the one born, he sends a star as companion on
the way and shows the longed-for infant. And Matthew mentions magi who came
from the east; but Luke an angel; for he says: And there were shepherds
abiding in the field, and keeping watch by night over their flock. And an
angel of the Lord appeared to them, and the glory of the Lord shone round
them, and they feared a great fear. And the angel said to them, Fear not; for
behold, I bring you good tidings of great joy, which shall be to all the
people, that there has been born to us today Christ the Lord in the city of
David. And suddenly there was with the angel a multitude of the heavenly host,
=61.766= praising God and saying: Glory to God in the highest, and on earth
peace, goodwill among men. Christ was born, the earthly things are joined to
the heavenly, angels dance with men, shepherds keep the field, and magi
worship, heaven is lit with lamps, and the earth bears its bright fruit upon a
manger. And then was fulfilled what is written concerning the earth, Truth has
sprung from the earth; for Truth is Christ who says: I am the way and the
truth. From the earth, that is, from the virgin he sprang according to the
flesh. But I seek from where the magi, having been taught concerning the star,
used it as a guide toward the finding of the child they sought.
For it is not, as some have imagined, saying that the star is a deceiver, and
that the magi were not seeking a true child. For how, if the star were a
deceiver, did it point out Christ the truth? For having reached him, it
stopped from its course. And how also did the magi, if they were not going in
truth, bring him such offerings from their own labors, declaring his divinity
and humanity, and fulfilling the prophetic word, He shall live, and there
shall be given him of the gold of Arabia? Nor again, as the myth-tellers say,
that there is a star rising for each of the men who are born; who do not even
know the astrology fabled among the Greeks. Yet such men even suppose
themselves to be Christians. To whom we will say: O philosophers, if this were
what you say, tell us, how, when Adam and Eve were only two human beings, was
the heaven filled with the same stars? and when a flood had come, and all men
had died by water, the same stars were there, not having fallen, and while the
same dance of the stars continued, was Noah alone with his three sons and the
women? But one must attend to the Scriptures, and understand the mysteries.
For there was a certain prophet Balaam, whom the king of the Moabites summoned
to curse Israel going out from the land of Egypt; since those whom he blessed
were blessed, and those whom he cursed were cursed; having come near the camp
of Israel, and having gone up to the ridge of the mountain, having seen Israel
encamped, taking up his parable, and prophesying he said: A star shall rise
out of Jacob, and a man shall arise out of Israel; and he shall crush the
princes of Moab, and plunder the sons of Ammon. These magi then, springing
from the race of Balaam the prophet, having followed his books and prophecies,
and having understood that, when a star shall rise, then also the man shall
arise, namely the King of the Jews, who will not only crush the princes of
Moab, but also grind fine and winnow all the kingdoms of the devil according
to Daniel, they watched the time; and having seen the star rising strange
beside the others, they knew it to be the one long ago prophesied, and having
followed, and having found the one born, they rejoiced, having found the
outcome of the things that were prophesied.
But you, hearer, understand for me that the magi are types of the nations who
have turned from the impiety of error, not being led by a perfect light, but,
so far as by a radiance of light through John, as through a star, being led
toward Christ born in the flesh. For see for me also the star coming as far as
the child, and stopping from its course. For John also, after the manner of a
star, having shone with the preaching, and having pointed out Christ saying,
Behold the lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world, ceased from his
course; for he has said, He must increase, but I must decrease. And having
come, he says, the magi found the child and the mother; and falling down they
worshipped him. For those who come to Christ from a Gentile life must first
fall from the height of impiety, and thus, as if rising from the earth,
confess the birth of Christ according to the flesh, and then in turn look up
toward his heavenly divinity. And opening their treasures, they offered him
gifts, gold and frankincense and myrrh: gold as to a king, frankincense as to
God, myrrh as to one passible for us.
And Christ received the gifts, not himself needing, but benefiting those who
brought them. For receiving gold, he turned them =61.767= away from love of
money; receiving frankincense, he freed them from idolatry; and receiving
myrrh, he wiped the deadness from their soul. And if we must also contemplate
another mystery, let us learn it exactly. They offer gold and myrrh to the one
born. Since it was written concerning him, To him every knee shall bow of
things in heaven and on earth and under the earth, in the offerings they
prefigured the future, declaring in the gold the doxology of the heavenly
angels, in the frankincense the kneeling of earthly men, in the myrrh the
supplication of the souls held in Hades.
Since the one who has come to Christ must possess three virtues, which the
Apostle says, faith, hope, love, =61.768= opening the treasures of the heart,
they brought faith, flashing like gold; hope, giving fragrance like
frankincense; love, like myrrh binding together the members of the Church. And
being warned in a dream not to return to Herod, they departed by another way
into their own country. For those who have once come to Christ must not go
back by the same tracks of impiety; but having practiced the pure faith and
deed, escaping the notice of the man-killing serpent, they must withdraw into
the inheritance of Christ. For to him belongs the glory unto the ages of the
ages. Amen.
