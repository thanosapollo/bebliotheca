Encomium on the Holy Martyr Barlaam
=50.675=
αʹ. The blessed Barlaam has called us together to this sacred feast and
festival, not that we may praise him, but that we may emulate him; not that we
may become hearers of the encomia, but that we may become imitators of his
achievements. Now in the case of worldly affairs, those who ascend to great
offices would never choose to see others as partners of the same presidency;
for there envy and jealousy cut love apart; but in spiritual things it is not
so, but altogether the opposite; for then especially the martyrs receive a
sensation of their own honor, when they see their own fellow-servants having
arrived at the communion of their own goods. So if anyone wishes to praise
martyrs, let him imitate martyrs; if anyone wishes to praise the athletes
=50.676=.30 of piety, let him emulate their labor; this will bring the martyrs
a pleasure not less than that of their own achievements.

And that you may learn that then especially they perceive their own goods,
when they see us being in safety, and they count the thing a very great honor,
hear Paul saying: Now we live, if you stand in the Lord. And Moses too before
this said to God: If you forgive them the sin, forgive; but if not, blot me
also out of the book which you have written; I do not perceive the honor above
because of their misfortune, he says; for the fullness of the faithful is the
connection of a body; what then is the profit of the head being crowned, when
the feet are being punished? =50.677= And how is it possible, he says, to
imitate martyrs now? For it is not a time of persecution. I know this too: it
is not a time of persecution, but it is a time of martyrdom; it is not a time
of such wrestlings, but it is a time of crowns; men do not persecute, but
demons persecute; a tyrant does not drive, but the devil drives, more grievous
than all tyrants; you do not see coals set out, but you see a flame of desire
being kindled. Those trampled the coals; you trample the pyre of nature; those
boxed against beasts; you bridle anger, the wild and untamed beast; those
stood against unbearable pains; you overcome the absurd and wicked reasonings
that teem in your heart; thus you will imitate martyrs. For now our wrestling
is not against blood and flesh, but against the principalities, against the
powers, against the world-rulers of this darkness, against the spiritual
things of wickedness.

The desire of nature is a fire, a fire unquenchable and continuous; it is a
dog rabid and raging, and even if you beat it off ten thousand times, ten
thousand times it leaps on, and does not stand off; the flame of the coals is
harsh, but this of desire is more terrible; we never have a truce of this war,
we never have a cessation in the present life, but the contest is continuous,
that the crown also may become brilliant. Therefore Paul always arms us, since
it is always a time of war, since the enemy is always watchful. Do you want to
learn that desire burns no less than fire? Hear Solomon saying, Shall anyone
walk upon coals of fire, and not burn his feet? so is he who goes in to his
neighbor's wife, and everyone who touches her shall not be held guiltless. Do
you see that the nature of desire contends with the nature of fire? For just
as it is impossible for one touching fire not to receive a burning, so the
viewing of beautiful faces, sharper than fire, lays hold of the soul that
looks licentiously; and just as some easily kindled matter, so the splendors
of bodies lie under the eyes of the wanton. Therefore one must not furnish the
fire of desire with the food of the outward viewing, but hem it in on every
side and quench it with pious reasonings, bridling the burning as it is
brought on, and not allowing it to overturn the firmness of our mind. And
every pleasure, in the time of the mastery of the passions, is accustomed to
burn the mind more violently than fire, unless someone nobly with endurance
and faith should contend against each of the passions; which is the manner in
which the blessed and noble athlete of Christ, Barlaam, did upon his own hand,
bearing a whole pyre in the right hand, and not yielding to the pain, but
being disposed more passionlessly than statues, or rather he did feel pain,
and he did suffer; for it was a body that was subjected, and not iron; but
feeling pain and suffering he displayed in a mortal body the philosophy of the
bodiless powers.

βʹ. But come, I will narrate his martyrdom from the beginning, so that the
history may become clearer; and you, observe with me the devil's villainy. Of
the saints he led some to frying-pans, and others he threw into cauldrons
=50.678= boiling more violently than fire; and of some he scraped the sides,
and others he drowned in the sea, he delivered some to beasts, he led others
into a furnace, and of some he wrenched out the joints, of others still living
he stripped off the skins, under the bloodied bodies of others he set coals,
and the sparks leapt upon the sores, biting the wounds more fiercely than any
beast; for others he invented other harsher punishments. When then he saw all
these things mocked, and those who had suffered them prevailing with much
surplus, and becoming for those who came after them to the same wrestlings a
very great ground of confidence, what does he do? He invents a new manner of
plot, that the unrehearsed and unaccustomed character of the punishment may
cast down the martyr's spirit. For what has been heard and understood, even if
it is unbearable, having been practiced in expectation becomes easy to
despise; but the unrehearsed, even if it is light, is more unbearable than
all.

Let the contest then be new, let the device be strange, that the new and
unexpected, having thrown the athlete into confusion, may trip him easily.
What then does he do? He leads the saint out from the prison bound. For this
too was of that one's villainy, not to bring on the harsh devices immediately
from the outset, nor to inflict the dreadful punishments, but to skirmish from
the lesser ones; why ever? That if the contestants are defeated, their defeat
may become shameful, because they did not even stand against the small things;
but if they prevail and conquer, their strength having been previously spent
in the lesser, they may become easy to handle for the greater. Therefore he
brought on the lesser first, that whether he take them or not take them, he
may not miss; for if I take them, he says, I have mocked them; but if I do not
take them, I have made them weaker for what is coming. He leads him out then
from the prison; and he went out like a noble athlete who has trained a long
time in a wrestling-school; for the prison was a wrestling-school to the
martyr, and there conversing privately with God he learned from him all the
wrestlings; for where such bonds are, there Christ also is present. He went
out then having become stronger by the longer sojourn in the prison; and when
he had come out, the devil, bringing him into the midst through those who
served his lawlessness, did not bind him to the wood, did not station
executioners around him; for he saw him desiring these things, and having long
practiced them beforehand; but a strange and new device, never hoped for, he
brings against the tower, able easily to produce the downfall; for this is
what he is eager for in all cases, rather to trip the saints than to pain
them.

What then is the device? Having commanded him to stretch out the hand supine
above the altar, they laid coals and frankincense on the hand, so that if he
should feel pain and turn the hand over, they might reckon the thing a
sacrifice to it and a transgression. Did you see how villainous the devil is?
But see how he who catches the wise in their craftiness made his devices void,
=50.679= and the intensity of the plot and the variety of the villainy
prepared an addition and increase of greater glory for the martyr. For when
the antagonist, having practiced ten thousand villainies, then goes away
defeated, then the athlete of piety becomes more brilliant; which is what
happened here also. For the blessed Barlaam remained keeping the hand unbent
and unturned, as if he had it composed of iron; and yet if the hand had been
turned aside, not even so would there have been a charge against the martyr.

γʹ. Here all of you attend to me with exactness, that you may learn that even
if the right hand had been turned over, what happened would not have been a
defeat; why ever? Because just as we judge in the case of those whose sides
are being scraped or who are otherwise somehow being tortured, so also here
one must give judgment. For if they yield and sacrifice, the charge of their
weakness comes to be, because they sacrificed not having borne the pains; but
if having endured the tortures they feel pain at what they suffer, yet do not
betray piety, no one accuses them for these pains, but for this we receive
them more greatly, and we marvel, that even having been pained they endured,
and did not deny. So here too, if the blessed Barlaam, not having borne the
burning, promised to sacrifice, he was defeated; but if, while he himself was
not yielding, the hand was turned over, this is not a charge against the
martyr's mind; for this happened not because the purpose was weak, but because
of the nature of the sinews according to their own tension, and the saint's
hand being bent against his will, this was brought about by the fire. For just
as we do not blame those whose sides are being scraped, because their flesh is
split; or rather, that I may bring the example nearer: just as no one would
blame those with fever and convulsions when their hands are bent; for what
happens is not of their slackness, but of the flame of the disease consuming
the moisture and contracting contrary to nature the sequence of the sinews; so
neither would anyone have blamed this saint if the hand had been turned over.
For if fever is naturally able to contract and distort the members even
against the sick man's will, much more would coals laid upon a right hand have
done this even if the martyr did not yield. But nevertheless they did not do
it, that from surplus you may learn that it was God's grace that was present
and anointing the athlete, and correcting the defect of nature; nor did it
itself suffer its own things in this, but as if that hand had been fixed of
adamant, so it remained unturned. Who then seeing would not have marveled? who
would not have shuddered? Angels were looking down from above, archangels were
watching; the theatre was brilliant, and truly surpassing human nature. For
who would not have desired to see a man contending, and not suffering the
things of men; the same man becoming altar, and victim, and priest? Therefore
a double smoke also went up, one of the frankincense being kindled, the other
of the flesh melting; and this smoke was more pleasant than that, and this
fragrance bet =50.680= ter than that.

And the same thing happened as also upon the bush; for just as that bush was
burning and was not consumed, so here too the right hand was burning, but the
soul was not being burned up; the body was being spent, and the faith was not
being consumed; the flesh was giving out, and the eagerness was not giving
out; and the coals, having bored through the middle of the hand, fell out
below, but the courage of the soul did not fall through; but the hand was
consumed and flowed away; for it was flesh, and not adamant; but the soul was
seeking another hand again, so as to display endurance in that one too. And
just as a noble soldier, having gone in among enemies, and having cut down the
phalanx of those contending against him, having broken the sword by the
density of successive blows, then turning seeks another, because he has not
yet taken his fill of the slaughter of the enemies; so also the soul of the
blessed Barlaam, having consumed the hand in cutting down the phalanxes of the
demons, was seeking another right hand again, so as to display eagerness in
that one too. For do not tell me this, that he gave over one hand only; but
consider this before that, that he who betrayed the hand, this man would also
have given over the head, and he offered the sides, and to fire, and to
beasts, and to sea, and to a precipice, and to a cross, and to a wheel, and he
arrayed himself against all the punishments ever heard of, and he suffered all
things, if not in experience, yet in purpose. For the martyrs do not go out to
meet defined punishments, but they strip for undefined torments; for they are
not masters of the tyrants' mind, nor do they set limits and measures of
punishments for them; but as many evils as that inhuman and beast-like mind of
theirs may desire to inflict on them, so many they enter in to suffer; unless
in the meantime the body, having given out, leave the tyrants' desire
unfulfilled in the midst. The flesh then was wasting, and the purpose was
becoming more eager, surpassing the very coals in brilliance, and gleaming
more than they; for a spiritual fire was being kindled within, far more
flaming than this fire.

Therefore he did not perceive the outward flame, because the inward fire was
burning him, the fiery and burning fire of the love of Christ. δʹ. Let us not
only hear these things, beloved, but also imitate them. For what I said from
the beginning, I say now also: let the martyr not be marveled at only as far
as the present hour, but as each goes home let him also bring the saint with
him, and lead him into his own house, or rather into his own heart through the
memory of what has been said. Receive him, as has been said before, and set
him upon your heart having the hand stretched out; receive the crowned one,
and never let him go out of your mind. For this reason we have also led you to
the tombs of the holy martyrs, that from the sight too you may receive some
encouragement of virtue, and may strip for the same zeal. For even hearing of
a champion raises a soldier; but much more sight and spectacle, and especially
when having entered into the very tent of the champion he sees the sword
bloodied, the enemy's head lying, =50.681= the spoils hanging above, the fresh
blood dripping from the hands of the one who set up the trophy, spears and
shields and bows everywhere and all the rest of the full armor lying. For this
reason we too have come together here. For the tomb of the martyrs is a
military tent; and if you open the eyes of faith, you will see the breastplate
of righteousness lying here, the shield of faith, the helmet of salvation, the
greave of the Gospel, the sword of the Spirit, the very head of the devil
thrown down on the ground. For when you see a demon-possessed man lying supine
by the martyr's tomb, and tearing himself often, you see nothing other than
the head of the Evil One cut off. For even now these arms still lie beside the
soldiers of Christ, and just as kings bury champions with their arms, so
Christ also did, and he buried them with their arms, that even before the
resurrection he might show all the glory and the power of the saints. Learn
then their spiritual full armor, and you will withdraw from here having gained
very great things.

There is a great war for you too against the devil, beloved, great, and
mighty, and continuous. Learn then the wrestlings, that you may imitate the
victories; mock wealth, and money, and all the rest of the show of life; do
not count blessed those who are rich, but count blessed those who are
martyred, not those in luxury, but those in frying-pans; not those at a costly
table, but those in a boiling cauldron; not those in daily baths, but those in
harsh furnaces; not those who smell of myrrhs, but those who send forth smoke
and the savor from flesh being roasted. This fragrance is better and more
profitable than that; for that leads those who have used it to punishment, but
this leads them to prizes and crowns above. And that you may learn that luxury
is a wicked thing, and anointing with myrrhs, and drunkenness, and wine that
has no measure, and a costly table, hear what the prophet says: Woe to those
who sleep on ivory beds, and live in luxury upon their couches; who eat kids
from the flocks, and sucking calves from the stalls; who drink the strained
wine, and anoint themselves with the first myrrhs.

And if these things were forbidden under the old covenant, much more under
grace, where the philosophy is greater. These things have been said by me both
to men and to women; for the stadium is common; the camp of Christ is not
divided by nature, but the assembly is one; women too are able to put on a
breastplate, and to put forward a shield, and to send a dart, both in a time
of martyrdom, and in another such time that demands much boldness. And just as
an excellent archer, sending the dart from the string with good aim, throws
the whole array of the enemies into confusion, so =50.682= also the holy
martyrs, and all the contestants of the truth, who contend against the methods
of the devil, send the words from the tongue as from some string with good
aim; and these, like darts flying through the air, falling into the invisible
phalanxes of the demons, throw their whole battle-array into confusion; which
is what happened also in the case of this blessed Barlaam; for from bare
words, as from flying darts, he threw the devil's camp into confusion. Let us
too imitate this good aim. Do you not see those coming down from the theatres
becoming softer? and the cause is that they attend with eagerness to what
happens there; for having deposited in their souls the types of the turnings
of the eyes, and the whirling of the hands, and the circles of the feet, and
of all the images that appeared in the distortion of the twisted body, they go
away thus. How then is it not absurd that those, for the ruin of their own
soul, should display so much care, and have an inward memory of what happens
there; but we, who from the imitation here are about to be made equal to the
angels, should not even contribute the same eagerness as they toward the
guarding of what has been said? Do not, I beg and entreat, do not thus neglect
our own salvation, but let us all deposit the martyrs in our own minds, with
the frying-pans, with the cauldrons, with the other punishments; and just as
painters wipe clean the image that has often become dimmer by smoke and soot
and long time; so do you also, beloved, the memory of the holy martyrs;
whenever worldly cares entering dim your mind, wipe it clean through the
memory of the martyrs. For if you have this memory in your soul, you will not
marvel at wealth, you will not weep at poverty, you will not praise glory and
power, nothing at all of human things, you will not suppose of the bright ones
that anything is great, nor of the painful ones unbearable; but having become
superior to all those, you will have as a continuous teaching of virtue the
spectacle of this image. For he who sees soldiers every day showing themselves
men in wars and battles will never desire luxury, will not marvel at the moist
and dissolved life, but the hard, and tense, and full of contest. For what
communion is there of drunkenness and battle? of stuffing the belly and
showing oneself a man? of myrrhs and arms? of war and revels? You are a
soldier of Christ, beloved, arm yourself, do not prettify yourself; you are a
noble athlete, show yourself a man, do not make yourself fair. Thus let us
imitate these saints, thus let us honor the champions, the crowned ones, the
friends of God, and having walked the same road as they we shall attain the
same crowns as they; which may it be that we all attain, by the grace and
loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom to the Father be glory
together with the Holy Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of ages.
Amen.
