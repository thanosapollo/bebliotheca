HOMILY V. To those who think the present things great, and who are
vainly excited about the splendors of this life.
=64.453=
Heaven is a place of gladness for angels, but the Church is for the
faithful one lawful dwelling in the place of the heavens, granting to
souls the pleasure of hearing the sacred hymns. Once the Giver of
blessedness blessed Mary as she hung upon this: Mary, he says, has
chosen the good portion, which shall not be taken away from her. And
only the gain of pious teaching is both a good safeguard for us while
we live, and a companion for the dead as they go to the Judge. But the
possession of all other things is easily overthrown, not only because
it has the tomb as its successor, but because even before the tomb
there are countless shipwrecks of life. For we sail the present life
as a sea that uses collisions more violent than waves. Wealth does not
enjoy a steady calm, but often, sailing with a fair wind for those who
possess it, it has been plunged by a sudden hurricane of trials. A
throne is not the unshaken height of =64.456= power; for sudden gales
of dangers, blowing all at once, have torn prosperity up by the roots.
Health is not a gift without a successor, but even upon this an
unexpected harm of diseases lies in wait. The possession of prosperity
sheds its feathers in many ways, and man resembles some tree, now
wearing the bloom of plenty, now stripped of that leaf as a tree is
stripped. You will find nothing among the blooms of worldly things
that, if you examine it, does not have withering as its neighbor. For
so the Creator composed life for us, loosening the appetite of those
who gape toward the earth, and he yoked the conspicuousness of life to
withering, so that whenever the pleasantness of earthly things charms
you, the transience, once expected, may raise you up. For you were
appointed a traveler of the present age, not a possessor, and we pass
through the use of creation alone as of an inn. For just as travelers,
having enjoyed a brief stay in an inn, go out and complete the journey
that follows, so also we, traveling the age of this life, having
remained a little while in life as in a lodging, depart from the
dwelling here.
For God has prepared another life for us beforehand, and he has made
the present life a road for the course toward that one, ranking the
needs from creation as provision for the way only. But when we have
gone out from this road, the wealth of all that was acquired does not
travel with us; for nothing of what we have had have we had as our
own. The garment we wear first as our own, we have stripped from the
nature of sheep; the shoes we put on our feet, we have received from
the hide of cattle; the supply of foods and drinks we gather together
from the bosom of the earth; of the earth too is gold, which we all
clutch as ours. Even kings collect pearls from oysters. We live and
grow rich from another's contribution. Give back the wool to the
sheep, and you will have no covering for the flesh; give back their
hides to the cattle, and you will not find a privately owned shelter
for the feet; give back the silken threads to the worms, and you will
not put on a silk garment of your own. Nor will you even abound in
crumbs of your own, unless you flatter the earth that feeds you with
what she loves. This too Christ was showing when he said: If you have
not been faithful in that which is another's, who will give you what
is yours? Do not put forward to me wealth from cattle and from the
earth, and adorn yourself as with what is your own in things that are
not yours. You are private owner of one possession only: piety. Death,
when it comes upon you, will not plunder this from you; but of all the
other things



it will cast you out even against your will; for we have all received
worldly possession for succession, and only the use of it; each one
goes away after enjoying it, taking from life a brief memorial. For
this is the end of all prosperity, this the conclusion of worldly
goods. After a possession of many acres and gold of many talents, we
inherit a narrow and foul-smelling coffin.
Truly All flesh is grass, and all the glory of man as the flower
=64.457= of grass. A small violence of fevers, falling upon us, has
scattered all the plenty of life; for art was applying the medicines
from without, but death, boxing invisibly with the medicines,
advancing little by little, masters the sick man; and he, seeing the
inescapable executioner pressing in and violently thrusting the soul
toward exit, turns his head groaning this way and that, and carries
his gaze about with tears. From nowhere is there help against the
violence; servants standing around the bed do not save him as he is
dragged away; the tears of friends do not flatter death; gold does not
hinder the appearance before the Judge. For death is an unbribable
executioner, having no right hand that yields to gold, nor redeeming
the corpses of the rich for silver, but imprisoning all in tombs as in
jails. Bend over the remnants of the bones, and you will weep more
bitterly the vanity of life. The face has been turned to ash,
blackened beyond soot; eyes are pierced through, carved by decay; the
mouth gapes and often receives a passage for creeping things. Why do I
dwell on each member? Worms, by corruption, divide the whole body
among themselves like some enemies, searching out exactly flesh and
sinews and veins. Rightly then does the infant, peering out of the
womb, begin at once with dirges, not with laughter; for it weeps as if
crying out against life, from which from the very prologue it tastes
gifts of the dead. For once born it is immediately swaddled in hands
and feet, and wrapped in bonds it enjoys the breast, as a prologue of
life that is a forerunner of death. The infant has only just come into
life, and at once a dead men's array has been brought to it. For
nature reminds those who are born of the end; therefore the child,
once born, wails as if crying to its mother through the dirge: Why, O
mother, did you bring me forth into this life, in which the addition
of living is an advance toward death? Why did you lead me into a
much-troubled age, rather than leave me unborn? Why are my first
things swaddling-bands? Why did you lead me into such a life, in which
even youth is pitiable, harvested before old age, and old age is to be
fled as leading on to death? Terrible, O mother, is the stadium of
life, having death as the turning-post of those who run. Bitter is the
worldly road we travel, having the tomb as the lodging of those who
journey. Hard is the sea of life we sail; for it has Hades standing
over it as a pirate. Do I denounce life as evil? That is the disease
of those named for madness; but I bear witness that we should not hang
fiercely upon it as upon what is temporary. Paul too, proclaiming this
wretchedness of life, brought in the nature of creation groaning: The
whole creation, he says, groans and travails together until now. For
the whole of creation is filled with gloom; and if you make an exact
search of the things that are, you will spy out in each its own groan;
first a groan is sent up from men, serving the expenditure of
corruption. =64.460= The earth too groans with men, having received
the curse because of us; the sea wails, fair indeed as it was made by
God from the beginning, but after sin found to be a tomb for many. The
sun too is downcast, lighting those who do not see its Creator; stars
with the moon groan, because by their peaceful dance with one another
they show the Peacemaker and do not persuade. Nor is the race of
cattle without a groan; not only as they are worn down in inexorable
slavery to us, but also as they are slaughtered in vain in corners for
demons.
There is a common dirge of creation from every side, weeping the
condition of the present life, which the prophet has aptly called an
image: For all things



he says, are vanity, every man living; yet a man walks about in an
image. And see the truth of the saying, having searched human life
exactly. For just as in a picture a king sits upon thrones sprinkled
with gold; and cities in the colors offer gifts; and the king's hand
receives what is given; yet all that appears is shadow and a scene,
and when the canvas is torn the figure is stripped bare: in the same
way also the nature of men, sitting as some king in life as in a
picture, receives the tributes given from sea and earth as from
certain cities; but when life is torn for it as a canvas, all the
beauty of life has flowed away. And one greatest of the goods among
men is the humility of a crushed heart, always also meditating the day
of the end, on which, going out naked from this worldly sea, we shall
look upon the pillars of our deeds as they are paraded before
creation. Do you want me to bring you an example of this? On a certain
merchant ship some men were sailing together, and navigating the same
sea with one another, they displayed various thefts against each
other. The helmsman, watching these things exactly, did not yet parade
the proof of the thefts; but when he saw the vessel drawing near the
harbor, having commanded all to go out of the ship naked, he found
each man's theft lying in the boat. Look at this example of life for
me; for life is set before us in place of a sea; and creation has been
fitted together as a vessel for the voyage, using God as helmsman, and
receiving all the cargoes of works. If someone trades in a corrupted
life, he will store it in the ship of creation; if he transacts a
well-governed life, what was done will be kept at the judgment.
Then, when the harbors of the end have drawn near, the helmsman casts
us naked out of the earthly vessel, and the deed of all remains to be
paraded at the judgment. Let us therefore sail the sea of life
blameless; let us acquire creation as a witness of good works; let us
pass beyond the shadowy pomp of present things; let us withdraw the
heart from mortal pleasantness; let us flee the zeal that ends in
corruption; let us not bind the soul with earthly nooses; let us think
scorn of the flowing =64.461= glory of mortal things; let us spit upon
the flower of temporary things as a dye easily washed out; let us run
through life with a heaven-faring, winged prayer, we who travel the
worldly road, and let us pursue the prize of the upward calling in
Christ, to whom be glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
