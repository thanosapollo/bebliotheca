wood of the cross sprouted the fair shoots of the martyrs; for these are the
achievements of that death. See then the proof of the things said then having

Blessed be God, a woman dares death, a woman who brought death into our life,
the ancient weapon of the devil, this brought down the devil's strength; the
weak and easily insulted vessel has become an unconquerable weapon; women dare
death; who would not be astonished? Let Greeks be ashamed, let Jews hide their
faces, who disbelieve the resurrection of Christ. For what greater sign of
that resurrection do you seek, tell me, when you see so great a change of
things having come to pass? Women dare death, a thing that even to holy men
before this was fearful and dreadful. Learn then its former fear, that seeing
its present contempt, you may marvel at God who caused the change; learn its
former strength, that having learned its present weakness, you may give thanks
to Christ who has completely unnerved it. Nothing was stronger than that
formerly, beloved, and nothing weaker than we; but now nothing is weaker than
that, and nothing stronger than we. Did you see how most excellent the change
has become? how God made the strong things weak =50.630= and furnished the
weak things strong, showing us his power from both sides? But that what is
said may not be a mere assertion, I will also bring on the proof. And, if it
seems good, first let us show that, how they feared it before, not sinners
only, but even holy men having much boldness toward God, flourishing from
achievements, and having gone through all virtue. And I show these things, not
that we may condemn the saints, but that we may marvel at the power of God.

Whence then is it clear that the mask of death was fearful before this, and
all shuddered at it and trembled? From the patriarch first. For Abraham the
patriarch, the just man, the friend of God, who left fatherland and house and
kinsmen and friends, and looked down on all present things because of the
command of God, so dreaded and feared death that, being about to enter into
Egypt, he said these things to his own wife: "I know that you are a fair-faced
woman; it will be then, when the Egyptians see you, they will keep you, but me
they will kill. Say then that I am her brother, that it may be well with me
because of you, and


my soul shall live for your sake." What is this, O holy man and patriarch? do
you look on while the little woman is insulted, and the bed wronged, and the
marriage broken into? do you so dread death, tell me? and you not only look
on, but you even weave a plot with the woman, and you join in acting the drama
of the insult, and you do everything so that the king of the Egyptians
attempting adultery may not become evident, and having stripped her of the
name of wife, you put on her the mask of sister? But I fear lest, being eager
to dissolve the power of death, we appear to be accusing the just man;
therefore I will try to do both, both to show the weakness of death, and to
snatch this man from this accusation. But first it is necessary to show him
dreading death, and then to free him from the charges. Let us see then how
great a thing he endured, unbearable and terrible; for to see a wife insulted,
and to behold her being adulterated, is more unbearable than ten thousand
=50.631= deaths. And why do I speak of being adulterated? even if he has some
bare thought of suspicion about her in his soul, all life becomes unlivable
for him. For the passion of jealousy is fire to him and an unconquerable
flame; and someone making clear its tyrannical and inexorable character said:
"For the wrath of her husband is full of jealousy; he will not exchange enmity
for any ransom, nor will he spare in the day of judgment, nor will it be
dissolved by many gifts." And again elsewhere: "Jealousy is hard as Hades";
for just as it is not possible, he says, to persuade Hades with money, so
neither to console and reconcile the jealous man.

Many at least have often even given up their own soul, so as to find the
adulterer; and they would gladly even taste the blood of the man who insulted
the wife, and they would choose both to do and to suffer all things for this;
yet this unbearable passion, this tyrannical and inexorable thing, the just
man endured with still greater excess, and he looks on while the wife is
insulted because of the fear of death and of the end.

2. That he dreaded death is clear from this; it is time then henceforth to
free him from the charges and from the accusation on this matter, having first
stated the accusation itself. What then is the accusation? He ought, someone
says, rather to have died, than to look on while the wife was insulted; and
this is what some accuse: that he chose rather to save his own life, than the
chastity of the wife. What do you say? he ought rather to have died, than to
look on while the wife was insulted? And what more would have come of it? for
if by dying he was going to snatch the wife from the insult, you say these
things well; but if by dying he does not help the wife toward riddance of the
insult, for what reason does he betray his own salvation vainly and simply?
For that you may learn that not even by dying was he going to snatch her from
the adultery, hear what he says: "And it will be, when the Egyptians see you,
they will keep you, but me they will kill." Two absurd things then were going
to happen, adultery and murder; and it was not of ordinary good sense to gain
at least one out of these two. For if he was going (for I will say the same
thing again) by giving over his own soul to free her from the insult, and
those men, having killed the just man, would not touch Sarah, you would accuse
well; but if even when he had died the wife was going to be insulted in the
same way, for what reason do you accuse the just man, who, when two terrible
things were going to happen, adultery and murder, prevented the one by his
wisdom, I mean that of man-slaying? For he ought even to be praised for this,
that he kept the adulterer's hand clean of blood. For you could not even say
that, by saying that she is his sister, by this he drew the Egyptian toward
the insult; for even if he had said that she is his wife, he was not even so
going to


stand off; and he himself made this clear saying: "When they see you, they
will say, that this is his wife, and they will kill me, but they will keep
you." So that if she said that she was his wife, both adultery and murder
would happen; but if she said that she was a sister, that of =50.632= the
murder was prevented. Do you see how, when two terrible things were going to
happen, he destroyed the one by his wisdom? Do you want to learn how he also
cuts under the charge of adultery again according to his own power, so as not
even to leave him to become a completed adulterer? Hear again the words
themselves exactly: "Say," he says, "that I am his sister." What does he mean?
He who takes a sister is not an adulterer; for the adulterer is judged from
the intention; since even Judah, going in to his own daughter-in-law Tamar,
was not judged an adulterer; for he did not go in as to a daughter-in-law, but
as to a harlot woman. So also here the Egyptian, being about to take her not
as Abraham's wife but as a sister, was not going to be judged an adulterer.
What then is this to Abraham, someone says, who knew that he was giving over
his own wife, and not a sister? But not even this is a charge against him.

For if, having heard that she is his wife, he was going to abstain from the
insult, you would accuse the just man well; but if the name of wife was going
to be no defense for Sarah toward beating off the insult, as he himself also
says, that "They will say that this is his wife, and they will keep you," one
must much more marvel at the just man, who in so great a difficulty of the
matter was able both to keep the Egyptian clean of bloods, and according to
his own power to console the charge of the insult. Come then, let us lead the
word also to his descendant Jacob; and you will see that one also dreading and
trembling at the end, a man who from first age displayed apostolic philosophy.
For what Paul legislated for the disciples saying: "Having foods and coverings
we shall be content with these," this man also asked from God saying: "If the
Lord should give me bread to eat and a garment to put on, it is enough for
us." Yet even this man who sought nothing beyond need, who looked down on the
house, who received the blessings, who obeyed the mother, who was a friend to
God, who by wisdom forced nature (for being second by nature, he became first
by the blessings), who was able to do so many things, who philosophized so
many things, who displayed so great piety, after the ten thousand contests,
after the ten thousand wrestling-pits, after the ten thousand wrestlings and
those many crowns, returning to the fatherland, and being about to meet the
brother, as being about to see a wild beast, and fearing his remembering of
wrongs, he falls before God and entreats: "Deliver me from the hand of Esau my
brother," saying, "that I fear him, lest coming he strike me, and the mother
upon the children." Did you see how this man also dreaded death? how he
trembles, and entreats God about this?

Do you want me to show you also another great man who has suffered this same
thing again? Think of Elijah, the heaven-high and divine soul; for that man
who shut the heaven and opened it again, who brought down the fire from above,
who offered the wonderful sacrifice, who was jealous for God, that man who in
a human body displayed an angelic life, who had nothing more than the
sheepskin, who =50.633= became higher than all human things, so trembles at
death and dreads it, that after all those things, after the heaven and the
sacrifice, after the sheepskin and the desert, and the philosophy, and so
great a boldness, he feared a cheap little woman, and fled because of this.

For when Jezebel said: "So may the gods do to me, and more also, if I do not
set your soul tomorrow as the soul of one of those who have died"; Elijah was
afraid, it says, and fled a way of forty days. 3. Did you see how fearful
death was? Let us then marvel at the Master, that he who was fearful to
prophets,


he made easy to despise for women. Elijah fled death, women fled to death;
that man leapt away from death, and these pursued death. Did you see how great
the change became all at once? Those about Abraham and Elijah are in agony
over the end, but women trampled death under their own feet like mud. But let
us not even accuse those saints; the charge is not theirs; it was the weakness
of nature, not an accusation of choice. God then wished death to be fearful,
that after these things the greatness of grace might be shown; he wished it to
be fearful; for it was a condemnation; for this reason he did not wish the
threat of the condemnation to be loosed, lest men become more listless after
these things. Let the sentence remain, he says, frightening them and making
them temperate; for there will be, there will be a time, at which they will
also be delivered from this agony; which indeed also happened. For that he
delivered us from this agony, the martyrs show, and before the martyrs Paul
shows. You heard in the Old Testament Abraham saying, that "They will keep
you, but me they will kill"; you heard Jacob saying: "Deliver me, Lord, from
the hand of Esau my brother, because I fear him"; you saw Elijah fleeing a
woman's threat because of death. Hear how Paul is disposed about this matter,
whether he thinks it fearful as those did, whether he is pained when it comes
on, and dreads. On the contrary rather, he even thinks the thing desirable;
for this reason he says: "To depart and to be with Christ is far better." To
those fearful, to this man better; to those unpleasant, to this man sweet; and
very reasonably; for formerly death led down to Hades; but now death sends on
to Christ. For this reason Jacob says: "You will bring down my old age with
grief to Hades"; but Paul said: "To depart and to be with Christ is far
better."

And he said these things not condemning the present life, far be it; for let
us be on guard lest we give room to the heretics' holds, nor fleeing it as
evil, but desiring the coming one as better. For he did not say that to depart
and to be with Christ is simply good, but better; and the better will be
better than some good. For just as when he says that "He who gives in marriage
does well, but he who does not give in marriage does better," he shows
marriage to be good, but virginity better; so also here; good =50.634= is the
present life, he says, but much better the coming one. Again elsewhere
philosophizing these same things he said: "If I am even poured out upon the
sacrifice and liturgy of your faith, I rejoice and I rejoice with you all; and
you also rejoice in the same and rejoice with me." What do you say? you are
dying, Paul, and you call men partners of the pleasure? what has happened,
tell me? I am not dying, he says, but I am going up to the greater life. Just
as then men receiving office call many who will share the gladness with them,
so also Paul going to death called those who would rejoice with him. For death
is rest, and riddance of toils, and a repayment of sweats, and a return of
wrestlings and a crown. For this reason at the beginning there were some
beatings of the breast and dirges over the dead, but now psalms and hymnodies.
They wept then for Jacob forty days, and the Jews wept for Moses another so
many, and beat their breasts, since death then was death; but now not so, but
hymnodies and prayers and psalms, all making clear that the thing has
pleasure; for psalms are a symbol of good cheer; "Is anyone of good cheer
among you?" he says; "let him sing psalms." Since then we are filled with good
cheer, for this reason we sing psalms over the dead, psalms bidding us to be
confident about the end. "Return," he says, "my soul, to your rest, because
the Lord has done good to you." Do you see death being a benefaction


and rest? for he who has entered into that rest has rested from his works, as
God from his own.

4. And so much about death; come then henceforth let us go to the encomium of
the martyrs, if you have not grown weary toward the hearing; for these things
also took their subject from the encomium of the martyrs; but it is necessary
to bring the word a little higher. A war was once kindled against the Church,
a harsh one, heaviest of all wars; for this war was double; one from within,
one from without; one from those of the household, one from the enemies; one
from aliens, one from acquaintances. And yet even if the war were simple, the
evil was unbearable, and if it struck only from without, the magnitude of the
terrible things was great; but now this was double, and that from those of the
household more harsh than that from those without. For the confessed enemy it
is possible to guard against easily; but he who has a friend's mask, but does
the things of enemies, is hard to catch for those plotted against. Double then
was the war then, one civil, one coming on from those without; or rather, if
one must speak the truth, each war was civil.

For even the judges and rulers and soldiers striking from without were not
some of another tribe, and barbarians, and of another rule and kingdom, but
governed by the same laws, inhabiting the same fatherland, sharing the same
polity. So the one from the judges was civil, but the harsher one was from the
kinsmen, the new and unreasonable one and full of much cruelty. For brothers
were handing over brothers, and fathers =50.635= children, and husbands wives,
and all the rights of kinship were being trampled, and the whole inhabited
world was overthrown, and no one then recognized anyone; for the devil was
prevailing with much excess. In that tumult then and war these women, if
indeed one must call them women; for even in a woman's body they displayed a
men's mind, or rather they not only displayed a men's mind, but even went
beyond nature itself, and set the contest toward the bodiless powers; these
women then, having left city and house and kinsmen, transferred themselves to
the foreign land. "For when," she says, "Christ is dishonored, let nothing be
honorable or necessary to us"; for this reason having left all they went out.
And just as when a house is being burned in the middle of the night, those
sleeping inside, when they perceive the tumult, quickly leaping from the bed
jump out of the doorways of the house, taking nothing of the things inside;
for they are eager for one thing only, how they may snatch their own body out
of the flame beforehand, and outrun the course of the fire coming on with much
speed; so indeed these also did. For seeing the whole inhabited world being
set on fire, they immediately jumped out, and leapt from the doorways of the
city, seeking one thing only, to keep the salvation of their own soul in every
way. For a harsh burning was then, and deep darkness prevailed, a darkness
more gloomy than this night gloom; and for this reason as in darkness friends
did not know friends, and husbands were handing over wives; for this reason
they were running past the enemies, but were dashing against the friends and
those of the household, and there was a harsh night-battle, and the affairs
were full of much tumult.

Then then these went out having left the fatherland, and having emulated the
patriarch Abraham, to whom it was said: "Go out from your land, and from your
kindred." For the time of the persecution was advising these things to these
women also, to go out from the land and from the kindred, so as to inherit
heaven. The woman then was going out from the house having two daughters with
her. But do not simply run past


hearing that women went out who had been brought up freely, never having taken
experience of these terrible things, but reckon in your mind how great the
evil was, how much difficulty the matter has. For if men setting out on a
moderate journey abroad, having abundance of beasts of burden, having
servants, traveling with fearlessness, being masters of the return, perceive
many of the wretched things, endure much hardship; where there is a woman, and
virgins, and a desert of servants, and betrayal of friends, and tumult, and
confusion, and unbearable fear, and varied danger in the very women
themselves, and the race is about the soul, and the enemies from every side,
what word will be able to set forth the contests of the women, the courage,
the greatness of soul, the faith? For if the mother had gone out alone, not
even so was the contest unbearable; but now she was also leading the
daughters, and these virgins; so that the agony was double, and much the
addition of the care; for by how much the possession is great, by so much the
guarding is more difficult. She was going out then having virgins, and not
having where to hide them in =50.636= chambers; and yet you know that even
chambers and women's quarters, and doors, and bars, and guards, and
fore-guards, and maidservants, and nurses, and a mother's sitting by, and a
father's forethought, and much care from the parents comes to be, so that the
flower of virginity may be kept, and even so it is scarcely saved; but that
woman was deserted of all this guard; how then was she able to save them? From
the guard of the divine laws. She did not have a roof surrounding, but she had
the mighty hand covering from above; she did not have a door nor a bar, but
she had the true door walling off the plot. And just as in the midst of Sodom
Lot's house was being besieged, but suffered nothing terrible; for it had
angels inside; so also these martyrs being between Sodom and all the enemies,
and being besieged from every side, suffered nothing terrible; for they had
the Master of the angels dwelling in their souls; and having come onto the
desert road they suffered nothing; for they had the true road leading them
toward heaven. For this reason, being caught in so great a war, and tumult,
and waves, they were walking with safety; and the wonderful thing, that
through the wolves the sheep were being led, through the lions the ewe-lambs
were going, and no one saw them with unchaste eyes, but just as God did not
allow the Sodomites standing near the door to see the entrance, so also then
he blinded the sights of all, so that the virginal bodies might not be
betrayed.

5. They travel then to a city called Edessa, a city more rustic than many, but
more pious; for what did they think equal to that city, to find a refuge in so
great a surge, and to have a harbor in such a winter? And the city receives
the strangers, strangers of the earth, but citizens of the heavens; and having
received the deposit it was keeping it. But let no one condemn weakness of the
women, that they fled; for they were fulfilling a master's command that says:
"For when they persecute you," he says, "from this city, flee to the other";
which these also having heard fled, and for the time one crown was being woven
for them; what then is this? That of looking down on all present things. "For
whoever has left brothers and sisters, or fatherland, or house, or friends, or
kinsmen, will receive a hundredfold," he says, "and will inherit eternal
life." And they were there having Christ as a housemate. For if where two or
three are gathered, he is in their midst, where they were not only gathered,
but even fleeing because of his name, how were they not much more drawing his
help? While the women then were spending time there, suddenly wicked commands
were being sent down everywhere, full of much tyranny and barbarian cruelty;
"let those of the household hand over those of the household," it says,
"husbands the wives, fathers the children, children the fathers, brothers the
brothers, friends the friends. =50.637="


But you, remind yourself here of the words of Christ, and marvel at his
foretelling; for he was saying all these things beforehand from above; for he
says: "Brother will hand over brother, and father child; and children will
rise up against parents." And he was saying them beforehand then for these
three reasons; for one, that we may learn his power, and that he is true God
seeing from far off the things that have not yet happened. For that he was
saying beforehand the things that would be for this reason, hear him saying:
"For this I said to you before it comes to be, that when it comes to be you
may believe that I am." And second, that none of the enemies may say that
these things come to be while he is ignorant or weak; for he who foresaw from
above was able also to prevent; but he did not prevent, that the crowns might
become more brilliant. For this reason he was saying these things beforehand,
and he also foretold for another third reason. What then is this? That he
might make the contest lighter for those in the wrestling-pit; for unexpected
terrible things, of whatever sort they may be, appear harsh and unbearable,
but things hoped for and practiced beforehand become light and easy. Then then
the enemies, commanding these things, both displayed their own cruelty, and
unwillingly were bearing witness to the prophecy of Christ, and brothers were
handing over brothers, and fathers children, and nature itself was warring
against itself, and kinship was being split against itself, and all the laws
were being overturned from the foundations, and everything was full of some
tumult and confusion, and the demons then filled the houses with kindred
bloods. For the father who handed over the son altogether also slaughtered;
for even if he does not push the sword, nor work the slaughter with the right
hand, yet by the judgment he completed the whole; for he who has handed over
to the murderer the one about to be murdered, himself worked the murder.

"Let us then make them child-killers," the demons were saying, "let us make
the children parricides through the betrayal; for such sacrifices were being
sacrificed to them of old, and fathers were slaughtering children." And the
prophet was crying these things saying: "They sacrificed their sons and their
daughters to the demons," and they were thirsting for such bloods. Since then
Christ stopped these unholy and abominable sacrifices, they were contentious
to renew them again, but they did not dare to say shamelessly and openly,
"Slaughter your sons"; for no one was going to obey; but they methodize the
command otherwise and play the sophist with the law, having commanded through
the judges that fathers hand over children; "for it makes no difference to
us," it says, "whether someone slaughters, or hands over the son toward
slaughter; for this man and that man is a child-killer." One could see then
parricides, child-killers, brother-killers. All things filled with tumult and
confusion; but the women were enjoying a deep calm. For the hope of the things
to come was walling them from every side, since even being in a foreign land
they were not in a foreign land; for they had a true fatherland, the faith,
they had as their own city the confession, and being reared with good hopes
they perceived nothing of the present things; for they were seeing the things
to come only. The affairs being in these things, the father presents himself
in that city having soldiers toward the catching of the hunt; the father and
husband presents himself, father of the daughters, but husband of the wife, if
indeed one must call him who is serving such things a father or =50.638= a
husband. Or rather let us spare him as far as possible; for he also became a
father of martyrs, and a husband of a martyr; let us not then make his wound
harsher by accusations from ourselves.

6. But you, consider for me the good sense of the women; for both when it was
necessary to flee, they fled, and when it was necessary to enter into the
contests, they stood, and they were following bound by the longing for Christ;
for just as one must not draw temptations upon oneself, so, when they present
themselves, one must contend, that we may display both from there the
gentleness, and from here the courage; which indeed those women also were then
doing; and they were going back, and


they were contending. For the stadium had been opened, and the time was
calling toward the wrestlings; and the manner of the contests became such.
They came to a city called Hierapolis, and from there they truly ran up to the
holy city through such a device. A river was flowing beside that road, along
which they were going back, and having escaped the notice of the soldiers
breakfasting and being drunk; and some say that they even took the father as a
fellow-worker toward deceiving the soldiers, and I am persuaded; for perhaps
he did these things for this reason, that he might be able to lay up
beforehand for himself at least some small defense through this betrayal
toward the day of judgment, the having worked with them, and helped, and made
easier the course toward martyrdom; having taken him then as a fellow-worker,
and having been able through him to cheat the soldiers, they went into the
midst of the river, and they let themselves go down the streams. A mother went
in with two daughters: let both mothers and virgins hear, and let these so
obey mothers, and let those so train daughters, so love the children; the
mother then went in in the middle, holding the daughters on either side, she
who had the husband, in the middle of those untried in marriage, and marriage
was in the middle of the virginity, and Christ in the middle of them. Just as
then a root of a tree having two shoots standing on either side, so also that
blessed woman then, having the virgins on either side of her, was going in,
and she was letting them go down the waters, and they were being drowned thus;
or rather they were not being drowned, but they were being baptized a new and
paradoxical baptism. And if you want to learn that what happened then was a
clear baptism, hear how Christ calls his own death a baptism.

For conversing with the sons of Zebedee, "You will drink my cup," he says,
"and you will be baptized with the baptism with which I am baptized." And what
baptism was Christ baptized with after the baptism of John, except death and
the cross? Just as then James, not having been crucified, but having laid down
his head by a sword, was baptized with the baptism of Christ, so also these,
even if they were not crucified, yet having been perfected through water were
baptized with the baptism of Christ; and the mother baptized them. What do you
say? a woman baptizes? =50.639= Yes, such baptisms even women baptize, just as
that woman also then both baptized, and became a priestess; for she also
offered rational victims, and the choice became an ordination for her; and the
wonderful thing, that she did not need an altar sacrificing, nor wood, nor
fire, nor a knife; for the river became all things, both altar, and wood, and
knife, and fire, and sacrifice, and baptism, a baptism much clearer than this
baptism. For about this one Paul says: "We have become grown-together with the
likeness of his death"; but about the baptism of the martyrs he no longer says
with the likeness of his death, but that we are conformed to his death. The
mother then was leading in the daughters, not as being about to lead them into
a river, but as if guiding them by the hand into the holy bridal chambers; she
was leading them in holding them on either side and saying: "Behold I and the
children whom God has given me; you gave them to me, to you I deposit them, my
own and myself." And thus the woman's martyrdom became double, or rather
triple; for through herself once, and through the two daughters twice she was
martyred; and just as when she was about to let herself go, she needed much
endurance, so also when she was dragging the children with herself she needed
as much other endurance, or rather much more; for women are not accustomed to
be in pain so, when they themselves are about to die, as when their daughters
suffer this. So that she was martyred more greatly through the daughters, and

two daughters at once, not seeing them dying, but dragging them toward death
through her own hand, consider how many martyrdoms she displayed, she who
endured through the facts that which is not even bearable for others to hear.

The soldiers then, knowing none of these things, were remaining as about to
take them again, but those women were already with the soldiers of Christ, the
heavenly angels; but those keeping watch did not see; for they did not have
the eyes of the faith. Paul then says about the mother, that "She will be
saved through the childbearing"; but here the daughters were saved through the
mother. Thus must mothers be in birth-pangs. For these birth-pangs are better
than the former ones, having a more bitter pain, but a greater gain. Those
know those birth-pangs, as many as have become mothers, of what sort the
birth-pangs are to see daughters dying; but to become herself the own-handed
worker of the slaughter has an unspeakable excess. 7. But for what reason did
the woman not come into the lawcourt? She wished to take the trophy before the
battle, to snatch the crown before the contests, to receive the prizes before
the wrestlings, not dreading tortures, but the unchaste eyes of the
unbelievers; she did not fear lest someone dig through her sides, but she
dreaded lest someone destroy the virginity of the daughters. And that she
dreaded this fear, not that one, and for this reason did not come into the
lawcourt, is clear from there. She endured much greater tortures =50.640= in
the river; for than to see flesh being carded it is much harsher and more
painful, as I said before, to sink one's own inward parts, I mean the
daughters, through one's own hand, and to see them being drowned, and than to
bear the tortures she needed much greater philosophy, so as to be able to hold
the right hands of the children, and to drag them along with herself toward
the streams of the river.

For it is not equal in the account of pain to see them suffering badly from
others, and herself to serve the death, herself to become a minister of the
end, herself to be set down as an executioner for the daughters; but this is
much harsher than that and more unbearable. And all of you as many as have
become mothers will bear witness to my word, and have taken experience of
birth-pangs, and have had daughters. How did she take hold of the right hand
of the children? how did the hand not grow numb? how were the sinews not
loosed? how did the wits not stand out? how did the reasoning have strength to
serve the things coming to be? For the things coming to be were more bitter
than ten thousand tortures, the soul being carded for her instead of the body.
But until when do we contend in chasing uncatchable things? for no word will
be able to set forth the greatness of the suffering, but only the woman who
took experience and contended knows of what sort these wrestlings are. Let
mothers hear these things, let virgins hear, mothers that they may so train
the daughters, virgins that they may so obey the mothers. For one must not
praise only the mother who commanded such things, but also marvel at the
daughters who obeyed such things; for neither did the mother need bonds toward
the victims and the sacrifices, nor did the heifers skip, but drawing the yoke
of martyrdom with equal eagerness and soul, they so went into the river having
left the sandals outside the bank; and they did this, sparing the guards; so
great was the forethought of those saints. For they were eager to leave a
defense for them toward the lawcourt, lest that cruel and savage judge have to
charge betrayal, and that having taken silver they let the women go; for this
reason they left the sandals bearing witness to the judgment of the soldiers,
that not while they knew, but while they were ignorant, they deserted to the
river. Perhaps a great longing for those saints has come to be for you; with
this fire then let us fall before their relics; let us twine ourselves about
their coffins; for even coffins of martyrs can have much power, just as then


also the bones of the martyrs have much strength. And not only on the day of
this feast, but also on other days let us sit by them, let us entreat them,
let us ask that they become protectresses of us; for they have much boldness
not only living, but also having died; and much more having died. For now they
bear the brands of Christ; and displaying these brands, they can persuade the
king of all things.

Since then so great is their power and the friendship toward God, having made
ourselves their own by the continual sitting-by and the unceasing coming to
them, let us draw through them the love for mankind from God; of which may it
come to be that we all obtain, by the grace and love for mankind of our Lord
