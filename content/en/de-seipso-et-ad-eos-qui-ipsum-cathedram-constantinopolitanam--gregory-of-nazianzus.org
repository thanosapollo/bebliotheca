ORATION XXXVI.
On himself, and to those who say that he desires the see of Constantinople,
and on the eagerness of the people which they showed toward him.
A. I marvel what it is that you have suffered toward my words, and from where
you have been so overcome by our voice, which is from beyond the borders and
small perhaps, and has nothing lovable, so that you seem to me to have
suffered toward us the same thing that iron filings suffer toward the magnet
stone. For you hang upon me, and upon one another, one holding to one, and all
upon God, from whom are all things, and unto whom are all things. O the
wondrous chain, which the Holy Spirit forges, fitted together with unbreakable
bonds! And the cause: I know nothing wiser than the other things, as many as I
am conscious of in myself; unless someone takes this itself as my wisdom, the
knowing that I am not wise, nor near the true and first wisdom; which the wise
of now fall far short of supposing about themselves; since it is easiest to
deceive oneself, and to think one is something, being nothing, puffed up by
empty glory. Nor was I first to preach to you the word of orthodoxy, to which
you especially cling, but I followed in another's footsteps, and these your
own; for the truth shall be said; if indeed you are disciples of Alexander the
great, the great champion and herald of the Trinity, who by word and deed
banished impiety. For you remember that Apostolic prayer, which destroyed the
leader of impiety in places worthy of the filth of the tongue, that outrage
might avenge outrage, and an unjust death of souls might be publicly branded
by a just death.
B. We did not then break open a strange spring for you, as Moses showed in a
waterless place to those pursued from Egypt, but we opened one hidden and
buried, after the children of Isaac the great, who not only dug wells of
living water, but also cleaned out those that were blocked by the Philistines.
Nor am I one of the elegant and pleasant, =36.268= and such as to steal
goodwill by flattery; the sort I see many of those who now promise to serve as
priests, who have made our simple and uncrafted piety into a craft, and a new
kind of politics transferred from the marketplace into the holy things, and
from the theaters onto the mystagogy unseen by the many; so that there are two
stages, if one must dare to say this, differing from each other so much as
that the one is open to all, and the other to some; and the one is laughed at,
and the other honored; and the one is called theatrical, and the other
spiritual. You are witnesses, and God, says the divine Apostle, that we are
not of this party, but such as would sooner receive the charge of awkwardness
and rusticity than of flattery and slavishness; we who even toward those who
cling to us too much sometimes and in some places appear harsh, if they are
thought to do something not according to reason toward us. And this was shown
by the recent innovation that happened concerning us, when you the people,
boiling with zeal and anger, set us, both shouting and lamenting, upon this
throne, which I do not know whether one must call tyrannical or high-priestly;
you set us, then, transgressing through longing. Where we were so displeased
with some of the hotter ones, that they leapt away from us, turning affection
into enmity. For we look not so much to what we shall please by, as to what we
shall benefit by.
C. What then is the cause of so great a longing concerning us, and our words:
do you yourselves wish to make this clear and to set it forth, and to make
known your own passion yourselves, or to publish this through us, as you also
use us eagerly as interpreters of the rest? I know that you make the word
ours, as far as I judge from the silence. Hear then, and consider whether I am
a poor guesser of such things. It seems to me that you, most of all, as those
who yourselves called us, help your own judgment, and for this reason tend
your own catch. And this is nature, to be kindly toward everything that is
one's own, whether a possession, or an offspring, or a word, and by willing
goodwill to be mastered by one's own proposals. And second, you respect
nothing so much as our not being bold, nor violent, nor theatrical and
festival-like, but retiring and moderate, and as it were unshared in the
common, and of one manner, and, to speak still more briefly, philosophical;
nor even this invented by us artfully and politically, but honored simply
and spiritually. For we do not hide in order to be sought, nor that we may
seem worthy of more honor, as those who show their bloom a little, then draw
them back; =36.269= but that by quietness we may show the fleeing of the
first seats, and not laying claim to such honors. Third, seeing what we suffer
both from those who war from without, and from those who plot from within;
since, according to Daniel, lawlessness went forth from elders out of Babylon,
who seemed to judge Israel; you are indignant and displeased, and have no way
to help one being tyrannized, and only instead of all things you contribute
pity. Pity then mixed with respect became affection. And this is the mystery
of the honor concerning me.
D. And since the words are what is warred against, and this superfluous and
envied tongue, which, trained in the outer learning, we ennobled by the
divine, and we sweetened bitter and undrinkable Marah with the wood of life,
you have suffered a passion sufficiently free; you love this, for which we are
warred against. Why did we not embrace the mute education? Why not the dry and
downward-going? Why, seeing the many rejoicing in this, did we philosophize
things strange and foreign, and stand against the opposing tongues? When we
ought by boldness to flee reasonings, and to name unreason faith, which I
myself would have loved, you well know, being a fisherman; since this is ready
to the many as a defense of ignorance; if I had the power of the signs as my
word. Would that envy perish from men, the consumption of those who have it,
the poison of those who suffer it, the only one of the passions both most
unjust and most just at once; the one, because it harasses all the good; the
other, because it melts those who have it. For I will not curse those who
first praised us; for they did not know the end of the praises; so that
perhaps they would have added even some abuse, that they might check the envy.
E. This darkened even the Morning Star, fallen through pride; for being
divine, he could not bear not to be thought God also; and it cast Adam out of
paradise, stealing him through pleasure and a woman. For he was persuaded that
he was begrudged being God, being still barred from the tree of knowledge.
This made Cain a brother-slayer, =36.272= not bearing a holier sacrifice.
This covered with water a world that was unordered, and flooded the Sodomites
with fire. This buried Dathan and Abiram, who raged against Moses, and made
Miriam leprous for only murmuring against her brother. This stained the earth
with the blood of prophets, and shook Solomon the wisest with women. This
showed Judas a traitor, stolen by a little silver, the one worthy of hanging;
and it made Herod a child-murderer, and Pilate a Christ-killer. This winnowed
Israel and scattered him, from which sin they have not even now lifted their
heads. This also raised up against us the apostate tyrant, whose coals even
now still vex us, even if we have escaped the flame. This also cut up the fair
body of the Church, dividing it into different and opposing factions. This
also raised up against us Jeroboam the slave of sin, and puts a bond upon the
tongue; for it cannot bear the Trinity being made bright, and shining with
whole Godhead, and making honorable for you her genuine heralds. Do we seem to
you to talk nonsense, guessing thus? Or has the writing word painted the
causes of the longing even very rightly? Concerning these things, then, I know
thus.
F. And since I see some also displeased at the insults, and making our
dishonor their own misfortune, come, let us philosophize briefly about this
too. For I, if thinking something human and small, either laying claim to this
see, I either at the first stood over you, with this gray hair, and these
limbs shriveled by both time and disease, or now I bear these dishonors, I
would be ashamed before heaven and earth, which it is also the custom of the
ancients to call to witness; I would be ashamed before this see, and this holy
council, and this holy and newly-founded people, against whom so great is the
array of the wicked powers, that it may be dissolved before it is constituted,
and deadened before it is born, already being formed according to Christ. I
would be ashamed of my labors and toils, and this ragged garment, and the
desert and the withdrawal with which we lived, and the unequipped life, and
the cheap table, falling little short of the birds. And let some also speak
truth about us, that we desired another man's wife, =36.273= we who did not
even wish to have our own. And let even Gibeonites have more than us, whom the
Holy Spirit, I know, does not even admit as woodcutters and water-carriers, so
long as they approach the holy things with such brands of life and of word.
But if we came to advocate for the word, and to contribute what we could to
the Church that was until now a widow and husbandless, as some stewards and
guardians, to lead the bride to another, whoever may appear worthy of the
beauty, and may bring more gifts from virtue to the queen: are we praiseworthy
for the eagerness, or blameworthy for the suspicion? Because we are judged by
others' passions. As you then, my good man, even if we stretched a hand to a
ship in storm, or to a city under siege, or to a house being consumed by fire,
helping either with boats, or with battle-lines, or with extinguishing
engines, you would altogether have named us either drowners, or lovers of the
city, or of the house, but not protectors and guardians.
G. But it does not seem so, he says, to the many. And what difference is it to
me, to whom the word is more than being, or rather is all? For this either
condemned, or justified; either made wretched, or blessed. But seeming is
nothing to us, as neither is another's dream. You do not seem so, he says, to
the many, O man. And does the earth seem to those who are dizzy to stand? And
to the drunken, do the sober seem to be sober, and not to walk on their head,
or to be turned about? And is honey not bitter, when and to whom it is thought
so, to the sick and those in a bad state? But things are not for this reason
as they seem to those who suffer. Show then that those who have supposed thus
are healthy, and then advise us to change; or condemn us, if we are not
persuaded, but stand upon the same judgment. I do not seem so to the many, but
to God I do; and I do not seem, but I have been made manifest to him who knows
all things before their generation, who fashions our hearts alone, who
understands into all our works, the motions, and the thoughts, with which the
things done, whom nothing of the things that are escapes, nor can escape, who
sees our things otherwise than as men see. For man looks to the face, but God
=36.276= to the heart. You have heard Scripture saying it, and believe. Of
this one must take thought more than of all the others together, at least for
those who have mind. Or, using two men as counselors about the same matter, a
wiser and a more ignorant, you would not have seemed to think well, if leaving
the wiser you followed the more ignorant. For neither was Rehoboam praised,
because having dishonored the counsel of the elders, he set up that of the
younger. And when God and men are being examined, will you prefer the things
of men? Not you, if you are persuaded by me, and think better.
H. But we are ashamed, he says, at your insults. And I at you, that you are
ashamed. For if we suffer these things rightly, we must be ashamed rather than
you on our behalf; not that we are dishonored, more than that we are worthy of
dishonor; but if unjustly, the charge is of those who insult, and one must be
indignant for them rather than for us; for they are the ones who suffer badly.
And if being bad, you supposed me best, what ought I to do? Be rather bad,
that I may please you more? I would not have advised this to myself. Thus
neither, if you suppose me to stumble when I am succeeding, will I change from
succeeding because of you. For I live, not more for you than for myself; and I
have as counselor about all things the word, and the ordinances of God, by
which I am often caught, with no one accusing; and I am let go, when many
condemn. And this alone it is not possible to flee, the court within and in
ourselves, looking to which alone one must go the straight road. But seeming,
if it is present, we will receive, that I may say something human also; but if
it opposes, we will bid it farewell, and we will take away nothing of being
because of seeming.
I. For it is thus: he who practices the good for the sake of something is not
firm toward virtue. For as soon as that has passed, he will also stop from the
good; as he who sails for gain stops from sailing, if the gaining is not
present. But he who honors and tends it for itself, since he loves what
stands, has also a standing eagerness concerning it; so that suffering
something divine, he can also say the thing of God: But I am the same, and I
have not been altered. He will not then be recast, nor be transferred, nor
fall together with the times and the things, becoming another at another time,
and taking on many colors, as the octopuses do those of the rocks with which
they associate; but he will remain always the same, =36.277= firm in things
not made firm, and unturned in things that turn; a rock, I think, against the
assaults of winds and waves, neither shaken, and consuming about itself the
things that fall upon it. These things to this point; for I have no leisure to
fight with tongues, and these things perhaps more than is needed.
J. But already my word is toward you, my flock. Become to me, says Paul,
glory, and joy, and a crown of boasting; you are a defense to those who
examine me; that, as for those of carpentry or painting who are required to
give an account, it is enough, when they have shown what was carpentered or
painted, to be freed from troubles (for a work, he says, is stronger than a
word), so I also, having displayed you, will rise above the abuses. And how
will I rise above? First, if you keep the confession toward Father, and Son,
and Holy Spirit unbending and firm, adding nothing, nor taking away, nor
diminishing of the one Godhead (for what is lessened is a lessening of the
whole); and those who think or say something else, or dissolve the one by
measures of natures, or wall it apart, you would send away as a ruin of the
Church, and a poison of the truth, not hating, but pitying them of the fall.
And second, if you should provide the way of life following the right word,
that you may be ambidextrous in virtue, and not lacking in the other.
K. You kings, reverence the purple; for the word will legislate even for
legislators. Know how great is what has been entrusted to you, and what is the
great mystery concerning you. A whole world under your hand, held by a small
diadem and a short rag. The things above are of God alone; the things below,
of you also. Become gods to those under you, that I may say something even
bolder. The heart of a king is in the hand of God, both it has been said, and
it is believed. Let your power be here, but not in the gold and the
battle-lines. You around the palaces and the thrones, do not be greatly lifted
up by the authorities, nor think immortal things about things that are not
immortal. Remain faithful to the kings, but to God first, through whom also to
these to whom you were entrusted, and to whom you have been delivered. You who
boast of race, ennoble your manner; or I will utter something unpleasant, but
well-born. For then something of yours would truly have been most well-born,
if tablets were not also writing the low-born among you. =36.280=
L. You wise, and philosophers, and solemn in the beard and the cloak, you
sophists, and schoolmasters, and hunters of public applauses, I do not know
how you could be called wise, not having the first word. You around wealth,
hear him who says: If wealth should flow, do not set your heart upon it; know
that you are confident in an unstable thing. Unload something of the ship,
that you may sail lighter. You will perhaps take something away for an enemy,
to whom your things will come round. You around luxury, take something away
from the belly, give to the spirit. The poor man is near; help the disease;
vomit out upon him something of the excess. Why do you also suffer from
indigestion, and this one from hunger? and you from drunkenness, and this one
from dropsy? and you weighing satiety upon satiety, and this one being
overturned by disease? Do not overlook your Lazarus here, lest he make you the
rich man from there. You the great city, the first immediately after the
first, or not even yielding this, appear to me first, not in vice, but in
virtue; not in slackness, but in good order. For it is shameful to rule the
cities, and to be defeated by the pleasures; or to be temperate in the other
things, but about the horse-races, and the theaters, and the stadia, and the
hunts to be so mad, as to make these a life, and for the first among cities to
be a city of players, which it is more fitting also to be a pattern of every
good to the others. May you spit these things out, may you be a city of God.
May you be painted upon the hands of the Lord, and may you stand with us
later, bright brightly, before the great city-founder. This I announce as good
news to you in Christ himself our Lord; to whom be the glory, honor, power
unto the ages. Amen.
