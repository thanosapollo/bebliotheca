* Book 1

I have long and much hesitated, Stella, best of young men and most eminent in
our studies, in the part you wished, whether I should myself send out gathered
together these little books, which flowed from me in sudden heat and a certain
pleasure of hurrying, when they had come forth one by one from my breast. For
why should I be burdened with the authority of this edition also, I who still
fear for my Thebaid, though it has left me? But we read the Culex too and even
acknowledge the Batrachomachia, and there is none of the famous poets who has
not played a prelude to his works in a more relaxed style. What? That it was
too late to hold these back, when you at least, to whose honor they were
given, already had them? But among the rest much of their indulgence must
perish, when they have lost the only grace they had

of speed. For none of them was worked longer than two days, some even poured
out in single days; how I fear that the verses themselves may prove that true
of themselves! The first little book has a sacrosanct witness: for one had to
take /the beginning from Jove./ These hundred verses, which I made on the
great horse, I was ordered to deliver to the most indulgent emperor the day
after he had dedicated the work. /You could have seen it even before,/ someone
will say. You will answer him, dearest Stella, you who know that your
epithalamium, which you had enjoined on me, was written in two days. Boldly,
by Hercules, but it has three hundred hexameters all the same, and perhaps you
mentions it for a colleague. Manilius Vopiscus at least, a most learned man
and one who especially claims letters now almost fleeing from neglect, is wont
of his own accord also to glory in my name, that his Tiburtine villa was
described by us in one day. There follows the little book dedicated to
Rutilius Gallicus recovering, of which I say nothing, lest I seem to lie by
the opportunity of a dead witness. For the testimony of Claudius Etruscus is a
document, who received his little bath from me within the delay of a dinner.
At the end are the Kalends of December, which are certainly believed: for that
most happy night, untried in public pleasures [lost]

** The great horse of the Emperor Domitian
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1-h1
:END:

What mass, doubled by a colossus set upon it, stands embracing the Latin
forum? Did the work flow down, finished in heaven? Or, shaped in Sicilian
forges, did the image leave Steropes and Brontes weary? Or did Palladian hands
fashion you such for us, Germanicus, as the Rhine and the high house of the
astonished Dacian lately saw you holding the reins? Come now, let earlier Fame
marvel at the name, known through the ages, of the Dardan horse, for whom
Dindymon on its sacred peak and Ida, with its foliage cut away, declined: this
one Pergama would not have received even with walls torn open, nor boys and
unwed girls in mixed flock, nor would Aeneas himself nor great Hector lead it!
Add that that one was guilty and embraced the savage Achaeans; this one a
gentle rider commends: it is a joy to look on a face mixed with the marks of
war and wearing peaceful calm, and do not think it greater than the truth:
equal the form and grace, equal the honor. The Bistonian steed does not carry
Mars higher when arms are spent, and it is proud of the great weight, and, not
slow, snatched in its course near the rivers, it smokes and drives the Strymon
with huge breath.

Equal to the work is its site. From this side he opens facing thresholds who,
weary of wars, by the gift of an adopted child first showed our gods the way
into the aether; and from the face he learns how much milder you are than
arms, you who, not ready to rage even against foreign furies, grant the same
to the Chatti and the Dacians. With you bearing the standards, both a lesser
son-in-law and Cato would have gone under Caesar's laws. But the passages of
the flanks are guarded on this side by the Julian halls, on that by the lofty
palace of warlike Paulus; at the back the Father, and Concord looks on with a
winning face. You yourself, your high head enclosed in pure air, shine above
the temples and seem to look out, whether the new Palatine rises more
beautifully, the flames scorned, or whether the Trojan fire keeps watch with
silent torch and Vesta already praises her proven ministers. The right hand
forbids fights; the Tritonian maiden does not burden the left, holding out the
neck of severed Medusa: as if she kindles the horse with goads; nor was a
sweeter seat ever chosen for the goddess, not even if you yourself, Father,
were holding it. A breast that could unroll the cares of the world,

and to which Temese, drained of all her metals, has given herself; a cloak
hangs down the back: the side is secure with a quiet sword, with as great a
point as Orion threatens on winter nights and terrifies the stars. But the
steed, imitating the rider's bearing and spirit, lifts its airy face and
threatens a course; its neck stands with stiff mane, and a living impulse
through the shoulders, and the flanks lie open, wide enough for such great
spurs; instead of turf of empty earth, a bronze hoof covers the hair of
captive Rhine. Adrastean Arion would have feared at the sight of this, and
Leda's Cyllarus is afraid as he looks from the neighboring temple. This one
will never change its master's reins: perpetual in the bit, it will serve one
star! Scarcely do they suffice alone, and, sat upon by so great a weight,

the ground pants beneath; they labor not with iron or bronze: they labor under
a genius, though an eternal pedestal hold it, which would have borne the peaks
of a mountain heaped above and would have grown hard, worn by the knee of
sky-bearing Atlas. No long delays dragged on. The present form of the god
itself delights the labors, and the youth intent on the work marvel that their
hands can do more. The high machine roars with the beat: a continuous crash
goes through the seven peaks of Mars and conquers the wandering murmurs of
great Rome.

The very guardian of the place, whose memorable name the consecrated chasm and
famous pool keep, when he felt the countless sounds of bronze and the forum
bellow with raw blows, moves his face rough with holy neglect and his head
venerable with deserved oak. And first he feared the huge bearing and flashing
light of the greater horse, and three times in alarm plunged his high neck in
the pool; soon glad at the sight of his protector: 'Hail, offspring and father
of great gods, a power long heard of by me! Now my marsh is happy, now to be
revered, when it is granted to know you near and to look on your immortal
radiance from a neighboring seat. Once I was the author and inventor of
Romulean safety: you tame the wars of Jove, you the battles of the Rhine, you
civil crime, you the mountain slow to treaties, in long Mars. But if our ages
had borne you, you would have tried to go into the deep pool, though I did not
dare. But Rome would have held your reins.' Let the horse yield that stands
opposite the temples of Latin Dione in the seat of Caesar's forum, whom you,
Lysippus, are said to have dared for the Pellaean leader; soon it bore
Caesar's face on a neck that marvelled. With weary eye you would scarcely
explore how long the downward look is from that one to this.

Who is so raw that, when he has seen both, he would not say the horses differ
as much as their rulers? This work does not fear rain-bearing winters or
Jove's triple fire, nor the troops of Aeolus's prison, nor the delays of
years: it will stand while earth and pole, while Roman day. Hither even in the
silent night, when earthly things please those above, your crowd will slip
from the sky they have left and mix kisses close by; son and brother and
father and sister will go into embraces: one neck will give place to all the
stars. Use forever the gift of the people and the great senate. Apelles's
waxes would wish to paint you, and the old Athenian of Elean Jove would have
wished to set you like him in a new temple, and mild Tarentum would prefer
your face, harsh Rhodes, scorning Phoebus, your eyes that imitate starry
flames. Be sure to love the earth and the temples we dedicate to you; inhabit
them yourself; let not the hall of heaven please you, and may you gladly see
your descendants give incense to this gift.
** Epithalamium for Stella and Violentilla
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1-h2
:END:

Whence have the Latin mountains sounded with sacred song?

For whom, Paean, do you move new plectra and hang eloquent ivory from your
flowing shoulder? See, far off the goddesses migrate from tuneful Helicon and
shake with a ninefold torch the solemn fire for joining chambers, and vocal
water from Pierian springs; among them Elegy draws near, wanton in face,
taller than her wont, and urges the goddesses and courts them, about to
support an alternating foot, and she wishes to be seen as a tenth and, mixed
in, she deceives the sisters in their midst. The Aenean mother herself has led
the bride with her hand, eyes cast down and blushing with sweet modesty; she
herself prepares the couches and the rites and, her goddesshood disguised in
Latin dress, she tempers hair and face and cheeks and longs to go lesser as a
new wife. I know the day and the causes of the rite: you that chorus sings
(open the doors!), you, Stella; for you Phoebus and Euhan and the winged
Tegean from Maenalian shade bring garlands, nor does winning Love nor Grace
cease to sprinkle with countless flowers and a scented cloud the snowy limbs
of the longed-for wife in your embrace. You now receive roses on your brow,
now lilies mixed with violets, and you stand in the way of the lady's snowy
face. So the day had come, stored in the white fleece of the Fates, on which
the professed hymen of Stella and Violentilla should be proclaimed. Let cares
and fears yield, let the lying tricks of sidelong song cease,

Fame, be silent: that love, once loosed, has submitted to laws and bitten the
bit; the talk of the crowd is spent, and the citizens have seen the kisses
long narrated. Yet you, astonished, though the plenty of so great a night is
given, still wish, and you fear the vows permitted by a favoring power. Put
away, O sweet poet, put away your sighs: she is yours. You may go and return
with open step across the exposed threshold: now nowhere a doorkeeper or law
or shame. At last sate yourself with the sought embrace (it has befallen!) and
remember equally the hard nights. A worthy wage indeed, even if Juno gave you
Herculean labors, and if the Fates granted you to meet Stygian monsters, if
you were snatched through Cyanean surges. For her sake it would have been
worth racing, trembling under Pisaean law, and hearing the roar of Oenomaus
pursuing, nor if you had sat as a rash shepherd on Dardan Ida would these have
been the gifts, nor if the kindly Tithonian pair had rather carried you off,
seized, through the airs. But what cause brought couches and unexpected joys
to the poet? Here with me, while the doorposts seethe with a crowd and the
halls and many thresholds are struck with the rod, here, merry Erato, teach.
There is leisure to move fit talk, and the learned household gods know how to
listen. By chance, where the milky tract of the cleared sky stands, kindly
Venus was lying in her chamber, night just driven off, released from the hard
embrace of her Getic husband.

A tender troop of Loves presses the goddess's couch-frames and beds; they seek
signs, where she bids them carry torches, which breasts to be pierced; whether
she prefers to rage on lands or on waves, to mix the gods or still to vex the
Thunderer. Her own mind is not yet, nor is will fixed in her heart; weary she
lies on the coverlets, where once the Lemnian chains, conscious of guilt,
crept on the caught bed. Here a boy from the crowd of the winged, whose fire
is greatest, and whose light hand never has a frustrated arrow, from the
middle of the troop speaks thus sweetly with tender voice (the quivered
brothers pressed silence). 'You know how, mother,' he says, 'no right hand of
mine is sluggish in service; whoever of men and gods you have given, he burns.
But grant at last that I be moved by tears and a suppliant right hand and the
vows and prayers of a man, O mother: for we are not created from hard adamant,
but we are your crowd. There is a famous youth of the Latin race, whom
nobility, glad, bore sprung from patrician ancestors, and, prophetic of his
beauty, at once set a surname from our sky. This man I myself once (it was
sweet to you) with a whole quiver, wanton, pierced as he trembled with a dense
point. Though much sought as a son-in-law by Ausonian mothers, I tamed him
conquered and bade him bear the yoke of a powerful mistress and hope through
long years. But her we grazed lightly (for so you ordered) with a sparing
torch and an idle bow. From that time what great fires the anxious youth
presses, I am the astonished witness, how he bears me urging night and day.

Never have I leaned more vehemently on any, mother, and I have dug repeated
wounds. I have seen Hippomenes too, eager, run down the cruel field, and he
was not so pale at the very turning-post; I have seen also the arms of the
Abydene youth contending with oars, and I praised his hands and often shone
before him as he swam: lesser that heat by which the savage waters grew warm.
You, youth, have passed old loves. I myself was stunned that you endured
through such great heats, and I strengthened your spirits and wiped with
winning feathers the dripping eyes. How often Apollo complained to me that his
poet so grieved! Now, mother, grant the loved chambers. He is our companion
and loyal standard-bearer; he could have recounted armed labors and the famous
deeds of men and fields streaming with blood, but he gave you the plectra and
preferred to go as a gentle poet and to weave the laurel under our myrtle. He
unrolls the slips of youths and his own or others' wounds; ah, how great is
the reverence of the Paphian power, mother: he has wept the fate of our dove.'
He had ended 1; he hung bland on his mother's tender neck and warmed her
breast with feathers brought near. She answers, not scorning to be asked, with
her face: 'A great and rare wish indeed among the men I myself have approved.
The Pierian youth desires it. I, marvelling at the outstanding grace of this
one's form, with whom the glory of fathers and the honor of race contended,
caught her falling from the earth and cherished her in my bosom, nor did my
hand, son, cease to wear down neck and cheeks

or to draw down her hair with rich amomum. For me a sweet image sprang forth.
Look from afar at the honors of her high brow and the piled hair. Measure how
far she stands out beyond Latin mothers: as much as the Latonian maiden
presses the nymphs, and as much as I myself stand out above the Nereids. She
was worthy to rise with me from the dark-blue waves and could have sat in our
shell; and if she could have climbed the flame-bearing seats and entered these
houses, you yourselves, Loves, would wander. Though I have given her, lavish,
blessed wealth, she conquers riches with her mind. Already we complain that
the greedy Seres strip a narrow grove, that Clymene's seeds fail and the green
sisters do not weep enough, that few fleeces now redden with Sidonian gore and
rare crystals freeze with long-lived snows. For her I have bidden Hermus and
Tagus run with yellow mud (nor is it enough for her dress), for her Indian
necklaces, Glaucus and Proteus and every Nereid to seek. If you had seen her,
Phoebus, wandering through Thessalian fields, Daphne would have wandered safe;
if on the shore of Naxos this one had been seen next to Theseus's bed, Euhan
too, a fugitive, would have left the deserted Gnossian. But unless Juno had
appeased me with long complaints, the false ruler of the aether would have
taken wings and horns for her; on her truly Jupiter would have fallen in gold.

But she shall be given to the youth for whom you, my highest power, son,
desire it, though she often, grieving, refuse to bear the yoke of a second
chamber. I have already felt her herself yielding and in turn growing warm for
the man.' So speaking she lifted her starry limbs and, going out over the
proud threshold of the chamber, she summoned Amyclaean swans to the reins.
Love yokes them and, carrying his glad mother through the clouds, sits at the
doubled pole. Already the Ilian citadels of Tiber: the high house opens its
shining household gods, and the happy swans have beaten the bright thresholds.
A seat worthy of the goddess, nor is it mean from the shining stars. Here
Libyan and Phrygian flint, here the hard rocks of the Laconians are green.
Here curved onyx and a vein the color of the deep sea, and the rocks shine, at
which Oebalian purple often envies, and the controller of the Tyrian cauldron.
Roofs hang, resting on countless columns; oakwoods shine, sated with Dalmatian
metal. Cool let down from old woods shuts out the rays; clear springs live in
the marble. Nor does nature keep her turns: here Sirius is cold, midwinter is
warm, and the house tempers a turned year for itself. Kindly Venus exults at
the sight and the halls of her powerful foster-child no otherwise than if from
the deep sea she were entering Paphos

and the Idalian houses and the Erycinian temples. Then she addressed her
reclining on the couch alone: 'How long this sleep and the modesty of an empty
bed, O loved among the Laurentine girls for me? What measure of manners and
faith? Will you never submit to a husband's yoke? A sadder age will come
already. Exercise your beauty and use the fleeing gifts. Not for this did I
give you such grace and a proud face and myself, that you should pass through
widowed years as if not dear to me. Enough, O too much, to have despised
former suitors. For this one, devoted to you with all his blood, marvels at
and loves you alone among all, lacking neither beauty nor stock. For what
youths, what girls have not learned his learned songs through the city? Him
you will see raise the twice-six fasces before his day (so may the indulgence
of the Ausonian protector go on); surely already he has moved Cybele's
thresholds and read the song of the Euboean Sibyl, and already the Latin
parent, whose mind it is right for me to know in advance, will grant purple
dress and curule ivory to the youth and will give Dacian spoils (this glory
greater) and fresh laurels to celebrate. Come then, join couches and take
idleness from youth. What peoples have I not, what hearts have I not yoked
with the torch 2? The flocks of birds and cattle and of hard beasts

have not refused me. I myself loose the aether into marriage with earth when
the clouds thin with rains. So the series of things and the age of the world
returns. Whence the new glory of Troy and the ravisher of burning gods, if I
were not joined to a Phrygian husband? Whence would Lydian Tiber have repeated
my Iuli? Who would have set the walls of sevenfold Rome, the Latian head of
empire, unless a Dardan priestess had taken Mars by stealth, and I not
forbidding?' With these words she soothes and breathes into the silent one the
honor of marriage. Already the gifts and prayers return to her mind, and the
tears and the man's wakeful complaints near the thresholds, Asteris and the
poet sung through the whole city, Asteris before the feast, Asteris at night,
Asteris at rising, as much as Hylas was not cried. And already she begins,
willing, to bend her harsh heart and already to seem hard to herself.
Blessings on your couches, most gentle among Latin poets, that, having
measured a hard road and the labors of the undertaking, you have grasped the
harbor. So the deserter of shining Pisa, a river inflamed far off in foreign
loves, draws undefiled streams in a sunken channel, until at last, brought
forth, with panting

mouth he may drink the Sicanian springs; the Naiad marvels at the sweet kisses
and does not believe that a husband has come from the sea. What a day then for
you, Stella, eager in the bright gift of the heaven-dwellers, with how great a
vow your breast leaped, when the lady's favoring face nodded to the sweet
marriage! You seemed to go in the pole and wander through the shining axes.
Less did the shepherd exult on Amyclaean sands when Helen came to the Idaean
keels; nor did Thessalian Tempe see such a Peleus, when Chiron on his raised
horse looked out on Thetis approaching Haemonian lands. How long the stars
delay! How slow is Aurora to a husband's vows! But far off, when the Letoan
father of poets and Semeleian Euhan sensed Stella's chambers being prepared,
this one moves Ortygia, this one the swift troops of Nysa. For this one the
Lycian mountains and the shades of cold Thymbra and you, Parnassus, sound 1;
for that one Pangaea re-echoes and Ismara and the shores of once genial Naxos.
Then they entered the dear doors and to their tuneful companion this one
brings a lyre, this one a tawny fawnskin with spotted back, this one thyrsi,
this one plectra; this one presses temples inspired with laurel, that one hair
with a Minoan crown. Scarcely yet was day sent out, and already the social
omens are at hand, already each house seethes with festive pomp. The doorposts
are green with foliage, the crossroads flash with flames, and the most crowded
part of immense Rome rejoices.

Every honor, all the fasces come to the thresholds; every bordered robe is
worn by a plebeian tumult: here the knight, here the stola mixed with a
gathering of youths is hard pressed. They call both happy, but in the crowd
more have envied the man. Long since, leaning on the post, Hymen seeks to
speak an untouched song for the chambers, with which he may soothe the poet.
Juno gives the reverend bonds, and Concord marks them with a twin torch. This
was that day: let the husband himself sing the night! As far as it is
permitted to know, so, overcome by deceitful sleep, Martian Ilia laid her side
on the river bank; not such did Lavinia dye her snowy face, when she blushes
with Turnus watching; not such did Claudia look back at the peoples, a maiden
already, when the keel moved. Now is the work, companions of the Aonides and
ministers of the tripods, to contend in diverse measures: let the cohort
inspired with fillets and wreathed with ivy go, as each prevails with a
triumphant lyre. But foremost you who cheat the noble work with a last step.
Give songs worthy of the festive couches. Philetas himself, with Cos
applauding, and old Callimachus and Propertius in the Umbrian cave would have
gone about to praise this day, nor sad Naso in Tomis itself, and Tibullus rich
with a shining hearth. Not one love and a simple cause of singing draws me at
least:

with you, Stella, like and joined Camenae are mine, and much we revel equal at
the altars and drink a shared wave from learned rivers; but my Parthenope
first received you at birth in her bosom, and you, sweet glory, crawled on our
soil. Let the Euboean land rise shining to the aether, and let Sebethos swell
with a fair foster-child; nor let the Lucrine Naiads please themselves more
with sulphur caves, nor the leisure of Pompeian Sarnus. Come now, hasten
famous descendants for Latium, who may rule laws, who camps, who play songs.
Let good Cynthia hurry the tenth month with birth, but let Lucina spare, I
pray; and you yourself, boy, spare your parent, lest you hurt the soft womb,
lest the standing breasts; and when nature in silent recess has formed your
face, may you bear much of the father's grace, more of the mother's. But you,
fairest form of Italian women, at last possessed by a deserving husband,
cherish the bonds long sought: so may no losses of grace be yours; so may your
face last green in the flower of long youth, and this beauty grow old slowly.
** The Tiburtine villa of Manilius Vopiscus
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1-h3
:END:

If anyone has been able to see the icy Tibur of eloquent Vopiscus and the twin
household gods with Anio inserted, or to know the commerce of the allied bank
and the villas contending to defend their master for themselves, him Sirius
has not barked at with hot star, nor has the heavy foster-child of leafy Nemea
looked on: such is the winter in the halls, so do the wanton chills break the
sun, and the house does not seethe with the Pisaean year. Pleasure herself
with tender hand is said to have written with you; then Venus anointed the
roofs with Idalian juices and stroked them with her hair and left a winning
honor on the seats, and forbade her winged sons to depart. O day long to be
remembered! What joys I bring back in mind, how tired my sight through so many
miracles! How mild the genius of the soil! What form before the hands and art
in the blessed places! Nowhere has Nature indulged herself more largely. High
groves have leaned on the swift shallows: a deceitful image answers the
foliage, and the same shadow flees through the long waves. Anio himself (a
faith to marvel at), below and above

rocky here, lays down his swollen rage and foamy murmurs, as if fearing to
disturb the Pierian days of peaceful Vopiscus and the sleeps that have songs.
Each shore is at home, nor does the mildest river divide you; the palaces keep
alternate banks, they do not complain that the stream is foreign to them or
stands in the way. Now let Fame boast the Sestian bays and the sea that was
swum and dolphins beaten by a bold youth! Here is eternal quiet, here no
rights for storms, never heat for the waters. Here it is given to send across
looks and voices and almost hands. So the returning waves drive Chalcis out;
so the Bruttian shore, parted by the deep, looks round Sicanian Pelorus. What
shall I sing first or in the middle, at what end shall I rest? Shall I marvel
at gilded beams or Moorish doorposts on every side or marbles shining with
pictured vein, or nymphs sent out through all the chambers? Hither I am drawn
with eyes, hither with mind. Shall I speak the venerable age of the groves?
You, hall, who see the river's shallows below, or you who look back at the
silent woods, where all quiet is yours and night, offended by no whirlwind, is
silent, and murmurs invite tender sleeps? Or the baths that smoke, taken up on
a grassy ledge, and fire set on chilling banks? And the river, joined to
vapor-bearing furnaces, laughs at the nymphs panting at the neighboring
stream? I have seen arts and the hands of the ancients and metals living

in various ways. It is labor to recount the figures of gold or ivory or gems
worthy to touch fingers, and whatever a hand first played in silver, or in
lesser bronze, and tried huge colossi. When I wander in looking and lead my
sight through all, I was treading unsuspected wealth. For splendor flowing
down from on high and tiles reflecting shining air showed the floor, where the
painted ground rejoices through various arts and surpasses the unswept with
new figures: my steps started. Why now should I marvel at joining things, or
what halls standing apart with divided three-rooms? Why you, tree kept in the
midst of the household gods, who emerge through roofs and doorposts into
liquid airs, under which master would you not suffer savage axes? And now
perhaps a slippery Naiad, or a Hamadryad, owes you years not broken off, you
unaware. Why should I tell of alternate tables on a twin mound and whitening
pools and fountains deep in the gulf, and you, Marcia, who glide deep through
the slanting river and run across the streams with bold lead? Or does only a
sweet path lead the river of Elis under Ionian waves to Aetnaean harbors?
There Anio himself, the caves and spring left,

under secret night, having put off his grey-green cloak, here and there lays
his breast on fragile moss, or falls huge into the pools and beats the glassy
waters with swimming. There Tiburnus reclines in the shade, there Albula
wishes to dip her sulphur hair. This house could unjoin woodland Phoebe from
Egeria and widow chilling Taygetus of Dryad choruses and summon Pan from
Lycaean woods. But unless the Tirynthian temples gave other lots, the
Praenestine sisters too could migrate. Why should I praise the wild orchards
of Alcinous and you, branches who never go empty into the aether? Let the
acres of Telegonus yield, let Turnus's Laurentine yield, and the Lucrine
houses and the shore of bloody Antiphates; let the treacherous ridges of
glassy Circe yield, howled at by Dulichian wolves, and the citadel of proud
Anxur and the seats which the old woman owes to her Phrygian foster-child; let
them yield, the Antian shores which will call you back already, with suns
narrowed, in cloudy midwinter. Here, of course, those manners ponder weights,
here fruitful quiet is pressed, and virtue grave with a calm brow, and a sane
brightness, and delights lacking luxury, which the old Gargettian himself,
having left his Athens, would prefer, his garden deserted.

These things are worth seeking through Aegean winters and the snowy star of
the Hyades and under Olenian stars, if the raft must be trusted to Malea and
the way is through Sicilian surges: why is neighboring pleasure cheap to the
eyes? Here your lyre pleases the Tiburtine Fauns and Alcides himself and
Catillus, sung on a greater lyre, whether your mind contends with Pindaric
plectra, or you raise the lyre to heroic strength, or you trouble livid satire
with black rust, or your letter shines with no other care. Worthy of the goods
of Midas and Croesus and Persian treasure, blessings on the goods of your

hurried steps, while she wanders through the seven ridges and gathers the

her faithful companions: 'Shall I never keep off this wanton foul herd from
greedy plunder, and will the crowd of my modest chorus always decrease for
me?' So then having spoken she draws from the quiver a short weapon, which she
does not twist with bent horns or with the usual hiss, but sent it content
with one hand, and is said to have touched the left of the sleeping Naiad with
an averted arrow. She, rising, saw equally the day and the shameless enemy,
and into the spring, lest she spread snowy limbs,

so she rushed with all her dress. And under the deep pools, believing Pan
follows, she wraps her side in lowest seaweed. What should the robber,
suddenly deceived, do? He does not dare to trust his body to the deep waters,
conscious of his shaggy hide and raw at swimming from tenderness: complaining
of all, cruel Bromius, envious pools and envious weapons, he set next a young
plane in look, whose long offspring and countless hands and a peak about to go

To you at least, dearest Pollius, and most worthy of this quiet to which you
so faithfully cling, I do not have long to prove the rashness of these little
books, since you know that many of them were born suddenly in your bosom and
you have often feared this boldness of our style, whenever, withdrawn into the
inner shrine of that eloquence of yours, I enter letters more deeply and am
led by you into every bay of studies. Secure therefore this third book of our
Silvae is sent to you. The second had indeed had you as witness, but this has
you as author. For its first threshold Hercules of Surrentum opens, whom,
consecrated on your shore, as soon as I had seen him, I adored with these
verses. There follows a little book with which I so escorted the most splendid
youth and to me most pleasant, Maecius Celer, sent by the most sacred emperor
to a Syrian legion, because I could not follow. The piety of my Claudius
Etruscus also deserved some consolation from our studies, when he mourned with
true tears (which is already most rare) an old father. Earinus besides,
freedman of our Germanicus: you know how long I delayed his desire, when he
had asked that I dedicate with verses his hair, which he was sending with a
double box and a mirror to Pergamene Asclepius. The last is an eclogue, in
which I exhort my Claudia to withdraw with me to Naples. This, if we speak the
truth, is talk and indeed secure, as with a wife, and such as would rather
persuade than please. You will especially favor this little book, since you
know that this destination of my quiet aims at you most, and that I withdraw
not so much to my fatherland as to you. Farewell.

hither with filleted hair and marked with snowy cloak, such as, still present

beaten, or that the face of a son just growing is snatched by flames? It is a
father who is wept. Be present, gods and men, at the rites. Far from here, go

wondrous faith!) and that the black sisters have hurried. Let peaceful shades

Why, wife, do you say sad things to me, why in allied nights, anxious,
sleepless, do you lead sighs of care? I do not fear lest faith be hurt or
another love in that breast; it is given to no arrows to go into you (though
Rhamnusia hear this with hostile face), it is not given, even if I myself,
snatched from ancestral shore, were wandering through wars, through waters,
four lustra earned, you, untouched, would put to flight a thousand suitors,
not having invented to reweave uncut webs, but without fraud, openly, and
armed you would have denied chambers. Yet say, whence a high brow for me and a
cloudy face? Is it that, weary, I guess I return to Euboean household gods and
compose old age in my fatherland?

Why is this sad to you? Surely there is no wantonness in your heart, nor do
the battles of the swift Circus soothe you, or does the crowd of the noisy
theatre enter the senses; but honesty and shady quiet and joys never mean. But
over what waves do I snatch you as companion? Though even if I were going,
about to remain, to the icy Bears, or above the darkening shallows of western
Thule, or the head, hardly penetrable, of sevenfold Nile, you would urge the
ways. For yours (surely kind Venus keeps her joined to me by lot in flowering
years and into old age), yours, who fixed me at the first wound, untouched by
chambers and still wandering youthful, I received your reins willing and
teachable, and, not about to change, I press to the end the inserted reins.
You, when I was bearing Alba's gifts on shining hair and clad in Caesar's holy
gold, embracing me with your vitals, gave gasping kisses to my garlands; you,
when the Capitol had denied our lyre, were grieving with me, beaten, savage
and ungrateful Jove; you snatch with a wakeful ear our songs running forward
with first sounds and whole nights in a murmur; you alone are conscious of the
long labor, and with your years my Thebaid grew. Such as I lately saw you,
snatched near to Stygian shades, when already I was hearing Lethean rivers
close at hand, and I held eyes already falling in death: of course Lachesis,
pitying you only, gave me times of exhausted fate,

and the powerful gods above feared your envy. After those things do you delay
to go as companion on a near journey and bays to be wished? Alas, where is the
known faith, tried through so many uses, by which you equal old Latin and
Greek heroines? Penelope would have gone glad to Ilian houses (for what would
she bring down lovers?), if Ulysses had suffered it: Aegiale complained,
Meliboea complained to be left, and she whom (how savage!) beatings made a
maenad. Nor less than these do you know faith and to give life to husbands. So
surely you still seek the ashes and former shade, so, having embraced the
funeral of a tuneful husband, you repeated huge beatings in your breast,
already mine. Nor is piety other for you, and care of a daughter; so as a
mother you love, so never does a daughter recede from your heart, and you hold
her fixed in the inmost recesses of the mind night and day. Not so does

a father's voice

Vesuvius's peak and the flaming winter of the dire mountain exhausted
trembling cities of citizens: they stand and thrive with peoples. Hence houses
founded with Phoebus as omen, Dicarchus's harbor and shores hospitable to the
world: but hence tracts imitating great Rome, which Capys filled with walls,
Teucrians brought in; ours too, both slight with her own and not rare in
colonists, Parthenope, to whom mild Apollo himself with a Dionean dove showed
the soil, when she was carried across the waters. These seats I labor to
transfer you to (for neither barbarian Thrace nor Libya is native soil for
me), which both a soft winter and a cold summer temper, which an unwarlike
strait washes with numb waves. Secure peace for the places and leisure of an
idle life and quiet never troubled and sleeps carried through. No rage in the
forum or laws drawn for wrangles: only the rights of manners for men, and
justice without fasces. Why now should I praise the magnificent appearances
and culture of the places, and temples and spaces marked among countless
columns, and the twin mass of a naked and a covered theatre,

and the five-year next to Capitoline lustra? Why should I praise the shore and
the liberty of Menander, which Roman honor and Greek license mix? Nor do
various delights of life lack round about: whether it be sweet to visit most
winning shores, steam-bearing Baiae, or the inspired halls of the prophetic
Sibyl and the ridge memorable for the Ilian oar, or the wet vineyards of
Bacchic Gaurus and the houses of the Teleboae, where for trembling sailors the
Pharos, rival of the moon, lifts sweet lights of the night-wandering, and the
dear Surrentine ridges with Lyaeus not soft, which my Pollius, inhabitant
before others, increases, and the healing lakes of Aenaria and Stabiae born
again: shall I tell you a thousand loves of our land? But enough, wife, it is
enough to have said this: she created me for you, she bound me as partner into
long years. Does she not deserve to be seen as mother and nurse of both? But I
am ungrateful who add more and doubt your manners: you will come, dearest
wife, and you will even come first; without me Tiber, leader of waters, and
the halls of arms-bearing Quirinus will be cheap to you.

** The seventeenth consulship of the Emperor Augustus Germanicus

Glad purple accedes to twice eight deeds of Caesar, and Germanicus opens a
marked year and rises with the new sun, himself shining brighter than the
great stars and greater than first Eous. Let Latin laws exult, rejoice,
curules, and let Rome more boastful beat the aether with sevenfold ridge, and
more before others let Evander's citadel-hill triumph: new fasces have gone

received, rejoices to have conquered Caesarean modesty. The very greatest
restorer of immense age lifts his face, and from both thresholds Janus gives
thanks, whom you, bound with neighboring Peace,

have bidden to compose all wars and swear to the laws of a new forum. See, he
lifts supine hands from this side and that and speaks these things with twin
voice: 'Hail, great parent of the world, who prepare with me to renew the

the hands of your Minerva. Do you see how another gleam is for the temples, a
higher fire for the altars, and my own stars of midwinter grow warm for you?

does every honor draw light from the consul? What such, I pray, did the prior
year have? Come tell, powerful Rome, and with me, long Antiquity, number the
fasti, and do not review small examples, but those alone which my Caesar may
think worthy to conquer. Three times and ten Augustus bore fasces for Latium

great leader, and has promised his own years.

** The Domitian Way
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4-h4
:END:

What huge sound of hard flint and heavy iron has filled the rocky side of
Appia, near the sea? Surely Libyan troops do not sound, nor does a foreign
leader, restless with perjured war, shake Campanian fields, nor does Nero
break fords and, mountains cut, lead in filthy marshes, but he who crowns the
warlike thresholds of Janus with just laws and a forum, who restores to chaste
Ceres acres long denied

and sober lands, who forbids the strong sex to perish and as censor prohibits
adult males from fearing the punishment of fair form, who restores the
Thunderer to the Capitol and sets Peace back in her own house, who sanctions
lights always future for the ancestral race and a Flavian heaven 2: this one,
burdened by the sluggish ways of the people and fields that delay every
journey, takes away long circuits and with a new throw solidifies the heavy
sands, glad to move the house of the Euboean Sibyl and Gauran bays and
seething Baiae nearer to the seven mountains. Here once a sluggish traveller,
carried on one axle, was nodding with hanging cross, and malignant earth was
swallowing the wheels, and the Latin people in mid-fields shuddered at the
evils of a sailing; nor swift courses, but impeded silent ruts were delaying
the journey, while a languid quadruped, complaining of too much weight, creeps
under a high balance.

But now, what was wearing a solid day has been made a way of scarce two hours.
Not stretched feathers of birds through the stars, nor will you go more
swiftly, keels. Here the first labor is to begin furrows and cut limits and
with deep digging to hollow the lands thoroughly; next to fill otherwise the
drained ditches and prepare a bosom for the top back, lest the soils nod, lest
a malignant seat give a doubtful couch to pressed stones; then with bosses
driven from this side and that, and with frequent pins, to bind the way. O how
many hands labor equally! These cut the grove and strip mountains, these
lighten rocks and beams with iron; those bind stones and weave the work with
cooked dust and filthy tufa; these dry thirsty hollows with the hand and drive
lesser rivers far. These right hands could also hollow Athos and shut the sad
sea of groaning Helle with a bridge not swimming. For these little Ino's
Isthmos would have mixed the straits, unless the gods forbade the way. The
shores seethe and the mobile woods, a long crash goes through mid-cities,

and Massicus, grape-bearing, sends back from Gaurus an echo broken at once
from here and from there. Quiet Cyme marvels at the sound, and the Liternian
marsh and sluggish Savo. But Vulturnus, his yellow head and hair wet far,
impeded with soft sedge, lifts his face and, reclined on the greatest arch of
Caesar's bridge, overflows such things from hoarse jaws: 'Good founder of my
fields, who have bound me, poured back in pathless valleys and not knowing how
to inhabit banks, with the laws of a straight channel, and now I, that turbid
and threatening one, who before scarce suffered doubtful keels, already bear a
bridge and, passable, am warmed; I who was used to snatch lands and whirl
woods (it shames!), have begun to be a river. But I give thanks, and the
service is worth so much, that under you as leader, at your command, I have
yielded, that you will be read the greatest arbiter and perpetual victor of my
bank, and now you cultivate me with a blessed limit nor suffer me to be
filthy, and far you wipe the evil shame of a sterile soil, lest the bay of the
Tyrrhene deep wash me dusty and heavy for heaven, such as Cinyphian Bagrada
creeps with silent bank among Punic fields, but such may I be borne that with
shining course I can challenge the tranquil sea and neighboring Liris with a
pure gulf.'

These the river, and equally a marble-laid stretch had lifted itself with a
huge back. His door and prosperous threshold is an arch, shining with the
leader's warlike trophies and all the metals of the Ligurians, as great as he
who crowns clouds with rain. There the excited traveller is bent, there Appia
grieves to be left. Then a swifter and sharper course, then the onset itself
pleases the yoked: as when, with the arms of rowers weary, you first breezes
fan the canvas. Therefore come, all you nations who under the first axis
cultivate the faith of the Roman parent, go along the prone limit; eastern
laurels, come more swiftly. Nothing stands against the eager, nothing delays:
he who leaves Tiber at first rising may sail the Lucrine at first evening. But
whom do I discern at the lowest end of the fresh way, where Apollo shows old
Cumae, white with hair and fillets! Are we deceived by sight? Or from the
sacred caves does the Sibyl bring forth Chalcidic laurels? Let us yield; lyre,
now put back songs: a holier poet begins, one must be silent. See! She also
whirls her neck and revels far in new spaces and fills the way. Then thus she
speaks from a maiden mouth: 'I was saying, he will come (wait, fields and
river), he will come with heaven favoring,

who will lift the foul grove and rotting sands with high bridges and a way.
See! This is the god, him Jupiter bids command the blessed lands for himself;
than whom none worthier has gone under these reins, from the time when with me
as leader Aeneas, eagerly seeking things future, both entered and left the
foreknowing groves of Avernus. This one good for peace, this one to be feared
in arms, better and more powerful than Nature. If this one held the
flame-bearing axes, India, you would be wet with large clouds, Libya would
flood, Haemus would grow warm. Hail, leader of men and parent of gods, a power
foreseen by me and founded. Nor now, unrolled from rotting papers, with the
solemn prayer of the fifteen men, survey my sayings, but hear me singing close
at hand, as you deserve. I have seen what series of deserving age the white
sisters twine for you: a great order of ages awaits you, longer than sons and
great-grandsons you will bear years in perpetual youth, which Nestor is said
to have gone to, peaceful, which Tithonian old age computes, and as many as I
asked of the Delian. Already snowy Arctos has sworn to you, now the East will
give great triumphs. You will go where wandering Hercules and Euhan beyond the
stars and the flaming sun and the head of Nile and the snows of Atlas,

and, blessed with every heap of praises, you will climb, warlike, and refuse
chariots; until the Trojan fire and the Tarpeian father thunders in a reborn
hall, until this way, with you ruling the lands, grows older than aged Appia.'

** Funeral poem for Priscilla
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5-h2
:END:

If my hand were teachable to fashion similar waxes, or to animate ivory or
gold with impressed figures, hence, Priscilla, I would conceive welcome
consolations for your husband. For by outstanding piety he deserves that you
be given back to the grieving one, marked in face even with Apellean color, or
born by a Phidian hand. So he tries to take the shade from the pyre and wages
a huge contest with Death and wearies the cares of artists and seeks to love
you in every metal. But mortal is the honor which a nimble right hand labors.
We for you, rarest wife of a praised youth, try to give due things with a
perpetual lyre, about to bear a long and not obscure end. Only let favoring
Apollo and Caesar, who always comes joined with Apollo for me, nod: by no
other tomb better to be laid. Late indeed is medicine built for so great a
pain, when another wheel of winged Phoebus twists the year; but when the blow
was fresh and still in the first wound the house black, what then was access
to the wretched ear of a bereft husband? Then to weep and tear clothes and
weary flocks of servants and conquer beatings and strike the Fates and unjust
heaven-dwellers with rabid complaints was a consolation. Though Orpheus
himself, accompanied by woods and rivers, were present to groans to be
lightened, and every aunt equally covered the poet, every priest of Apollo and
of Bacchus: no songs, no strings heard by the gods of paling Avernus and by
the hair of the Eumenides would have power to soothe: so great grief was
reigning in an astonished breast! Even now the already flat scar flees back to
beatings while we sing, and a husbandly storm presses heavy eyes. Do these
lights even now have pious weepings? Wondrous faith! Sooner is the Sipyleian
mother said to have drained her cheeks, sooner will sad dews fail the
Tithonian, or the mother, sated, grow weary of breaking winters on Achilles's
tombs. Blessings on your mind! That god notes these things, who bends the
reins of the world and, nearer than Jove, arranges human acts, and sees you
mourning: secrets of a chosen minister! Hence he also takes documents, that
you love a shade and cultivate funeral rites. This is the most chaste ardor,
this love deserving to be approved by a master as censor. Nor is it a wonder
if Concord, long, joined you mixed with breasts brought together in an
unbroken chain. She indeed had suffered a husband before and torches for a
spouse elsewhere, but you, as if yoked in virginity, she was cherishing,
having embraced with all vitals and soul; as an elm loves a vine allied with
equal-aged shoot and mixes the grove and prays for a rich autumn and rejoices,
wreathed with dear clusters.

They are praised by ancestors or by the gift of fair form who have lacked
goods of manners, and, falsely powerful, they need true praise: for you,
though both origin shone and a lucky appearance, much to be wished by
husbands, a greater honor is from yourself, to have known one bed, to drive
one fire under secret bones. That love neither a Phrygian ravisher would have
vitiated, nor Dulichian suitors, nor the fraternal adulterer who polluted
chaste marriages with Mycenaean gold. If you gave the wealth of Babylon, if
the weights of Lydian treasure and the powerful riches of Indians and Seres
and Arabs, she would prefer to die unviolated with modest poverty and to weigh
life against fame. Nor a brow stiffly sad and too much horror in manners, but
simple and cheerful faith and grace mixed with modesty. But if doubtful fear
had called to greater things, she, glad, would have received even arms-bearing
troops for a husband and thunderbolt fires and the dangers of mid-sea. Better
that adversities did not prove what care of the couch was yours, how great a
pallor for a husband! But by a better way your vows have deserved favoring
gods for the husband, while night and day you weary the powers, while a
suppliant you roll at all altars and adore the mild genius of the present
master. You have been heard, and Fortune has come with a kind step. For she
saw the pious youth's busy quiet and untouched faith and breasts girt with
cares and wakeful senses and sober hearts worthy to unroll so great

turns. He saw, who knows all things of his own and goes round every side with
ministers inspected. Nor is it a wonder: he sees risings and settings, what
Auster, what wintry Boreas drives, and the counsels of iron and of the toga,
and he proves the mind itself. He has set on subdued shoulders an immense mass
and a weight scarce tractable (for no other care is more numerous in the
sacred house): to send far into the great world the commands of the Romulean
leader and to handle with the hand the strengths and modes of empire; what
laurel from the north, what wandering Euphrates, what the bank of double-named
Hister, what the banners of the Rhine bear, how much the last world has
yielded, and Thule sounding round with a receding gulf (for all the javelins
lifting glad foliage, and no lance is marked with a famous feather); besides,
if the master divide faithful swords, to open who avails to bridle a hundred,

outstanding order of a famous tribune befits, who indeed is worthier to give
the sign to a bridle-bearing wing; also to know in advance a thousand turns,
whether Nile has sunk the fields,

or Libya has sweated with a rain-bearing south. And if I numbered all, the
winged Tegean with interpreting rod does not announce more from the high
stars, nor Juno's maiden who falls through liquid airs and binds rain with a
pictured circle in the air, nor Fame who with winged course carries your

prone, you eagerly roll before the sacred feet of a master deserving things so
great. Not so on the Aonian peak does he rejoice whom the Delian father has
set over the gaps of a secret cave, or he to whom Bacchus has given the
venerable right of the first thyrsus and the banners of an astonished troop.
Yet not hence has quiet changed, nor has honesty swollen with successes: the
same tenor remains for the mind, and modest manners with fortune growing.
Anxious she cherishes a husband's cares and equally urges and turns the
labors. She herself hands modest feasts and sober cups, and warns by the
master's example; as an Apulian wife of a spare farmer, or a Sabine stained by
the sun, who sees, with stars already looking out, that the time of an earned
husband is at hand, hurriedly furnishes tables and couches and waits the sound
of the returning plough. I speak small things. With you that companion through
icy Bears and Sarmatian winters and Hister and the pale chills of the Rhine,

with you hardened in mind through all heats, and, if camps gave it, she would
have wished to wear quivers, would have wished to shut her side with an
Amazonian shield, while she saw you in the dusty cloud of wars, near the
thunderbolt of Caesar's horse, brandishing divine weapons and sprinkled with
the sweats of a great spear. Thus far a kind lyre. Now it is time to put down
your foliage, Phoebus, and doom the hair with sad cypress. What god has bound
Fortune and Envy with unpacified kinship? Who has bidden the unjust goddesses
war eternal? Will that one mark no house which this one does not at once drive
with grim eye and thrust joys out with a savage right hand? The cheerful
household gods were flourishing, unshaken: nothing sad. For what, though
unfaithful and light, could Fortune be feared, with Caesar so favoring? Livid
Fates found a way, and a savage force entered the pious household. So full
vineyards are blown on by a malignant south. So a high crop grows old with too
much rain, so a breeze opposite a swift stern envies and clouds favoring
sails. Eximious Priscilla is plucked of her grace by Fate; as a high pine,
glory of woods in hair, fails either by Jove's evil fire or already with root
loosed, and, spoiled, murmurs back to no breeze. What do honesty or chaste
faith profit, what powers of gods cultivated? Dark nets of death have walled
the wretched one round on every side, the hard threads of the sisters are
stretched, and the last part of the exacted thread remains.

yet companions on every side simulate hope with a feigned face. She notes the
weeping husband. He now in vain asks the uncorrupted streams of infernal

thresholds with his breast; now he calls the exorable power of great Caesar.

like-minded lover: 'Part of my soul about to live, to whom I might leave, O

pray, tears, do not shake the breast with savage beating, nor torture the
fleeing shade of a wife. I leave the chambers indeed, yet with the order of
death safe, because prior: I have exacted times more powerful than long old
age; I have long seen you shining in every flower, I have seen you approach
nearer and nearer the high right hand. Not in you is there judgment for the
fates, not now for any heaven-dwellers:

I bear those things with me. Do you, willing, stretch on the begun limit and,
unresting, love the sacred side and the powerful genius. Now, what you
yourself wish to be ordered, give eternal gold to Capitoline seats, by which
the face of sacred Caesar may shine with a hundredfold weight and mark the

My Crispinus seeks Tyrrhene fields and the glades of Tages; nor is the delay
long or the land pathless, but my breast is plucked with a secret bite, and
wet lights drive swelling drops, as if I were following the sails of a
departing friend over Aegean winters and watching the raft, already weary,
from high rocks, and complaining that eyes are conquered by long air. What if
already the famous first rudiments of service and the sweet omen of camps were
calling you, outstanding boy? With how great weeping would joys run, or what
embraces would I give! Are even sad things to be wished by those near? And
already life has gone round twice eight orbs for you. But a mind stronger than
narrow years, and it succumbs to the burden, and its age does not hold the
mind. Nor is it a wonder: not an unhonored series of parents bore you, obscure
in great-grandfathers and needing ancient light, from a plebeian stock; not
sprung from troop blood and recent in the robe and with a poor stripe

did you, a stranger, strike the august seat and inner shrine of the Latin
senate, but with a troop of yours preceding. Such as through the acres of
Romulean circus, when a horse, fair to see, noble with ancestral titles, is
awaited, whose lucky admission from a long pedigree has earned parents, him
all applauses sharpen, him the flying dust itself and the curved turning-posts
rejoice to recognize: so the senate sensed you, famous boy, born for itself,
and first shut the tracks with a patrician moon. Soon the shoulders recognized
Tyrian folds according to custom and the powerful tunic, but indeed a father
was preparing great examples for you toward titles. For he, entering youth at
once, warlike, invaded quivered Araxes and Armenia, unteachable to serve
fierce Nero. Corbulo was driving the height of stiff Mars, but he too, much
marvelling at Bolanus in outstanding arms as a companion of war and ally of
labors; and to him he was used to trust the harshest of cares and share fears,
what time was friendly to frauds, what good times with war put out, what faith
suspected or what flight true of the fierce Armenian. Bolanus to know in
advance a way to be feared, Bolanus to seek ridges fit for safe camps, Bolanus
to measure fields, to open the malignant delays of torrents and groves, and to
fill so great a mind of a reverend leader, and alone to suffice for huge
commands.

The barbarian land itself already knew the man. He was the second peak of wars
and the nearest helmet. So the Phrygians, astonished, though they saw Nemean
arms and a Cleonaean bow was driving the lines, yet with Alcides fighting they
feared Telamon too. Learn, boy (for not by an external adviser is the fair

As a wood ignorant of the sickle lifts hair and breathes out fruit into

were allowed to prevail on the savage

Eumenides and turn Cerberus from a timid shade and more quickly give a

** Funeral poem for his own boy
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5-h6
:END:

Wretched me! For I shall not now begin with any solemn words, hated by the
vocal Castalian waves and heavy to Phoebus. What orgies of yours, Pierian
sisters, what altars have we polluted? Say; after the punishment let it be
allowed to confess things committed. Did I set tracks in an unaccessed grove?
Did I drink from a forbidden spring? What fault, what error so great do we
pay? See, holding our vitals with dying arms, an infant is torn away, the
soul. Not indeed of stock, nor who would bear my names and face; I had not
been a father, but discern the weepings and livid cheeks and believe the
beatings of the bereft: I am bereft. Hither let fathers and mothers with open
breast gather; and bring ash to the eyes and crimes, if any under full breasts
has herself with slipping step borne sons to funerals and fallen, a wet
breast, and quenched burning embers with milk. Whoever still has sunk in ash a
youth marked with the flower of tender youth, and has seen cruel flames creep
on the first down of one lying, let him be present and grow weary with me in
alternate cry: he will be conquered by tears, and you, Nature, will be
ashamed. So great is my wildness, so great the insanity of grief, this too
when I strive, thrice ten lights carried through, leaning on the mound, and
turn beatings into songs

and labor discordant measures and sobbing words: the beginnings of the lyre is
force, and anger impatient of being silent. But neither accustomed laurels on
the peak for me, nor filleted honor for the brow. See, a yew wood withers in
the hair, and a wept cypress shuts out cheerful ivies with branches; nor with
an ivory thumb do I strike the chords, but, mad, with wandering fingers I tear
an uncertain lyre. It helps, alas, it helps to pour an unpraiseworthy song and
to bare wretched pain uncomposed. Have I so deserved? So may those above look
on me unspeakable in song and bearing? Should Thebes and the new Aeacid be
ashamed? Will nothing peaceful now flow from the mouth? I that one who (how
often!) could soothe blandly the wounds of mothers and fathers, who widowed
pains, I that mild consoler of mourners, heard at bitter mounds and by shades
going down, I fail and seek healing hands and foments for wounds, but the
highest, my own. Now is the time, friends, whose running eyes and wounded
breasts I wiped: give back help. Pay savage thanks. No wonder, when I, chiding
your funerals with sad measures: 'You who grieve others' losses, put back,
unlucky, tears and keep sad songs.' It was true: strengths are spent and there
is no plenty of speaking for me, and the mind has found nothing worthy of so
great a thunderbolt: every voice is inferior and all

words are cheap. Forgive, boy: you overwhelm me with sad fog. Ah, hard, if at
the sight of a dear wife's wound Thracian Orpheus found what he might sing
sweet for himself, if Apollo, having embraced Linus's tombs, was not silent.
Am I perhaps said too much and greedy of pain, and to have exceeded just shame
in tears? But who rebukes our groans and laments 2? O too lucky, too cruel and
without your empire, Fortune, who dares to speak a law for weepings or to
judge bounds of grieving! He urges beatings, alas! Sooner would you hold
rivers fleeing banks or stand against swift fires, than forbid the wretched to
mourn. Yet let that severe one, whoever he is, know the wounds of our cause. I
did not love talkative delights bought from a Pharian stern, and an infant
learned in the wranglings of his Nile, too much in tongue and shameless with
wits: mine that one, mine. I looked on him falling from earth and cherished
him, anointed with a natal song, and, demanding new airs with trembling howls,
I inserted him into life. What more have parents given? Nay, other origins and
liberty under the very breasts I gave you, small one, when you were still
laughing ungrateful at our gifts. He had hurried, but love deservedly was
hurrying, lest liberty so small lose any day. Shall I not thence, bristling,
strike those above with envy and unjust Tartarus?

Shall I not groan you, dear boy? With whom safe I did not desire sons, whom
begotten I at once from first rising wrapped and fixed to myself, to whom I
showed words and sounds and loosed complaints and blind wounds, and, let down,
raised him crawling on the ground to our kisses, and on a winning bosom
already, already falling, to put cheeks to sleep and summon sweet sleeps. For
whom my name was the first voice, and I will hold play, laughter, and joys
were coming from our face.
