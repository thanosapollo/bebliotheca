On Rachel and the Infants.
=61.697=
Those who take the sweetest fruits of the orchard trees give their praises at
once to the branches and to the trees. In the same way, beloved, we who have
received the fruits of the words from the teacher who spoke before give the
honor both to him and to the fathers. For just as a sweet richness is handed
on to the fruits from the richness of the trees, so also to the man just named
the sweetness of speech is sent up from the grace of the Spirit. Let us then
harvest the sweetest fruits of the words, like sweet dates that hold their
honey from the righteous root of the fathers; and let us send the sweetness of
the word into our understanding, and assign the sweetness of thanksgiving to
our fathers.
For the honor of the fathers is constituted from the good order of the sons;
or rather, the crowns of the sons are made beautiful from the good repute of
the fathers. But since from the reading of Rachel's groaning I am pricked as
by a goad and turn toward her: Tell me, O Rachel, tell the pain hidden in your
groaning. For so the vessel of your mind, weighed down by grief, will be
lightened; and when the words that signify the pain come forth from your
heart, the heaviness of grief comes out with the speech. Tell me then, Rachel,
why do you weep? For I see your tears flowing like a river about your cheeks,
and the breath coiled bitterly about the heart and carried in disorder through
the nostrils. Why do you loosen your hair and bring forward what you ought to
hide? Why do you parade the solemn mystery of modest women, =61.698= overcome
by too great a frenzy? Woe, woe, Rachel! I will speak at last, held fast in
bitterness of soul; and what shall I say? I wish to bring the word forth from
the heart, and again I draw back, not finding the cause of the evils and of
the greatest disaster. O king Herod, who teaches me not to murder, and in deed
undoes his own law! The one I thought a king of good order I now see as a
leader of disorder, working in disorder and lawlessly. O greatest folly! He
who has done one murder becomes liable to death, and Herod, having done so
many murders, is named king. And the farmers, bringing the seed from their own
stores, scatter the clean grain in the split flanks of the earth; and when the
ears, having sprouted, signal the bright season of the sowing, then stretching
out their hands they cut the fields with a sharp sickle. But Herod, with a
sharp sword, withered the green shoot of my flanks, the offspring of my womb,
before they had yet blossomed with the flowers of age, before the spring down
of grace had yet risen about the cheeks. Woe, woe! I myself ache in the womb,
and the senses of my heart are in tumult; my soul is torn with the heart; I
will not be silent. For I endure these things because of the unwedded bride
Mary, because of the Word hidden in a manger. I see my clusters already
destroyed, the berries of the senses not yet even turning ripe. Woe, woe!
Those whom nature, having borne with labor through nine months' time, brought
forth ripe, I see slaughtered untimely by a single stroke of the sword. Woe! O
virgin Mary, mother as well, you who did not know a man =61.699= and yet bore
a son, you do not know the pain of mothers; for if you had known, you would
have wept. Without labor you carried, without labor you gave birth, without
labor you hosted the heavenly


Word in the house of your womb, without labor you made the heavenly flower
spring forth for the world.
But come now, and lament no longer, but cease from mourning, Rachel. Herod
killed your infants on earth, but God is bringing them up in heaven; Herod
robbed your children of the passing light, but in heaven he will fill them
with his own light. You do not see them leaping on earth, but then you will
see, and you will rejoice seeing them like lambs skipping in paradise. Now you
grieve, not seeing them stretching out their hands at the table, but then you
will see, and you will rejoice seeing them feasting with angels on the
heavenly bread; now you grieve, not seeing them warbling, but then you will
see and rejoice at the choir of these infants, like spring swallows, hymning
Christ in paradise. For since the prophet said, Out of the mouth of infants
and sucklings you have perfected praise, the choir of infants went before,
preparing victory hymns for Christ, fulfilling the prophet's word, Out of the
mouth of infants and sucklings you have perfected praise. But these things,
beloved, we have said for the delight of the soul; for now let us turn onto
the path of the Gospel.
For having heard, it says, Herod was troubled. Having heard what? That the
king of the Jews had been born. See, beloved, the devil troubled in Herod, and
troubled exceedingly. The wolf sees the shepherd born, and sees him hidden in
a manger, holding in his hands the strong staff of the Spirit, and seeing
these things he is troubled exceedingly; the dragon sees the bait of the body,
and the infant crawling like a worm; For I =61.700= am a worm, and not a man;
and he comes on with gaping mouth, and beholds within the heavenly Word hidden
in the flesh, like a hook, and seeing this he is troubled exceedingly. He sees
the mustard seed come upon the earth, sending out branches of hope, and about
to shelter the race of men under the leaves of salvation, and seeing these
things the demon who hates the good is troubled exceedingly; he sees wisdom
come upon the earth, taking the little leaven of the body and mixing it into
the three parts of the flour, into soul and body and spirit, and about to
leaven all humanity into one salvation of faith, and having beheld these
things he is troubled exceedingly. He sees the legion of the demon being cast
out from humanity and sent into the abyss, and seeing these things he is
troubled exceedingly; he sees the bars of Hades being shattered, and Adam
being launched again into paradise, and he is troubled exceedingly; he sees
Eve again being saved through Mary, and the serpent, in whom he most of all
took courage, being slain, and seeing these things he is troubled exceedingly;
he sees the tree of life being planted, and seeing these things he is troubled
exceedingly.
And time will fail me, beloved, as I recount the devil's trouble. So, able to
do nothing against the Lord, the devil runs down upon the infants and the
unfinished children among the babes. And whom does he slaughter? From two
years old and under; but those who have lived the triad of years he does not
slaughter. For infants are those who confess the dyad and have not believed in
the Trinity. Hence this Rachel, the Church from the nations, mourns these, so
that her voice may be heard in Ramah, that is, on high; for so Ramah is
interpreted, high, the Jerusalem above with the most high God; to whom belongs
all glory, might, with the all-holy and life-giving Spirit, unto the ages of
ages. Amen.
