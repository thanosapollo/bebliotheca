9 And taking from me the money and the other things which I brought, and
selling both the garments and making the greater part of the silver into
precious vessels, the rest within a short time he gave to those in need, not
only in the holy city, but also in other cities and villages and monasteries,
especially those that are in Egypt; for the monasteries there were very poor.
But also to the strangers sojourning he became a second Abraham. Whence in a
very short time all his substance


he distributed, so that he himself came to need the daily food. He therefore
gave himself to the craft of the shoemaker, washing hides and stitching,
imitating in all things that divine Apostle who was not willing to eat bread
as a gift, although he was able from another handiwork to provide the food,
but in all things he wished to become an imitator of the said celebrated man,
both in hardships and labors and persecutions and dangers of the sea and
uprisings of nations. And I also was urging him to live a common life together
with me (for I had also a surplus from my occupation), but he did not consent,
saying: "We brought nothing into the world, nor can we carry anything out."
And again he said: "If anyone does not work, let him not eat." And I, having
become bold, said to him: Why then when you were well provided did you not
work, nor allow me to work? And he answered me: The former work which I was
working was greater and many times the present work; for the present feeds one
or a second, but the former fed myriads, and not only those crowds, but it
also furnished spiritual food to my soul.

10 These things being so, Praylius who held the priesthood of the holy places,
having heard the name and the way of life of the holy Porphyry, sending for
him ordained him with much compulsion into the rank of presbyter, and moreover
entrusted to him also the keeping of the precious wood of the cross. Then we
knew that the things in the ecstasy had come to pass for him, when he had seen
the Lord on the cross and the robber with him, and the Master saying: Take,
keep this wood for me. And the blessed Porphyry was about forty-five years old
when he received the ordination. Having therefore been counted worthy of this
honor he did not change the former custom, but remained in the ascetic
hardship, in fasts and vigils. And his food was coarse bread and vegetables,
and these after the setting of the sun, but on the other holy days he ate at
the sixth hour, taking also oil and cheese and soaked pulses; and he also
partook of one cup of mixed wine, and this because of the bad condition of the
bowels. This manner of life and this rule he remained keeping the whole time
of his own life.

11 And after a three-year time from his being ordained, it happens that the
bishop of the aforesaid city of Gaza departed from life; this was Aeneas,
having been bishop a very small time. And before Aeneas was Irenion, who
himself also became a fellow-citizen of the angelic powers; whose praises it
is impossible to write in a digression, but there was need of a particular
treatise for the composing of the life of the said celebrated Irenion, and we
yield to other writers who know that man's life accurately. When therefore the
aforesaid holy man had fallen asleep, the Christians who were then, being few
and easily numbered, having gathered together with those from the clergy and
having deliberated for open days to whom to commit the bishopric, accomplished
nothing, contention having prevailed among them, some wishing certain of the
clergy, others of the laity; for according to the true word there were some
both among the clerics and among the laity adorned with life and gravity. When
therefore much confusion was coming about and nothing was going forward into
deed, at last it seemed good that five of the clerics and as many of the
notable laity should go out to the metropolitan high priest and ask of him a
bishop, whomsoever the Holy Spirit should reveal to him.

12 And the one who at that time had been entrusted with the high-priesthood
was John, a man himself also approved and adorned with every virtue. When
therefore the Gazaeans came to him, they were urging him to give them a priest
able in deed and word to set himself against the idolaters. And having heard
he immediately proclaimed a fast, and after three days the Lord revealed to
him concerning the celebrated Porphyry, and he writes


a letter to the mentioned holy Praylius the bishop of Jerusalem to send the
blessed Porphyry to him for the sake of a certain question of the scripture
that ought to be solved by him; for the blessed man was sufficient to solve
every thing of the divine scripture that was thought to be difficult, and this
he had from a gift of the Holy Spirit. And the God-loving Praylius, having
trusted the letters of the blessed John, sent him away, charging him not to
linger beyond seven days.

13 And the blessed Porphyry, having heard the force of the things written by
the most holy John, was at first troubled, and after these things he said: The
will of God be done. And having called me on that evening he says to me:
Brother Mark, let us go and worship the holy places and the precious cross,
for much time will have passed until we worship again. And I said: Why do you
speak thus, father? And he answering said: In the past night I saw the Saviour
saying to me: Restore the deposit which I deposited with you. For I wish to
yoke you to a wife, humble indeed but well-mannered, and you taking her, adorn
her, that she may forget her former poverty. For even if she happens to be
humble, yet she is not alien to me, but my genuine sister. And this keep watch
over, that you may not, as having a wife and caring for a house, gather from
injustice or violence or lawlessness, since having angered me you also grieve
her; for she too is displeased at such things. But you only have good
eagerness, and all things will be furnished to you from where you do not
expect. These things the Master Christ made plain to me in the past night, and
I fear lest somehow, wishing to propitiate my own sins, I may propitiate also
those of many others. But nevertheless it is not possible to speak against the
counsel of God.

14 Having said these things he went, and I also with him, and having
worshipped the holy places and the precious cross and having prayed much and
wept, he placed it in the golden casket, and having made it secure he went
out, and having gone to the blessed Praylius the bishop, he delivered the keys
to him, and having received a prayer with a commendation he went out. And
having come to the lodging we were making ready. And having hired three beasts
and taken the travelling-provisions we went out; and the travelling-provisions
were all the things in the house. And we were five on the road, both the
blessed man himself and I and two donkey-drivers and another younger servant
named Barochas, whom the blessed man had found a little time before thrown in
the street, being in last danger, and having taken him he brought him in, and
having spent much on him by the help of Christ he made him healthy. From that
time therefore he remained beside him serving him together with me. And the
things concerning the God-fearing Barochas, as the account proceeds, I will
tell. And having journeyed that day, on the next we came to Caesarea. And the
presence of our entrance was spread through the whole city; for the blessed
man was also named because of his blameless life, much more because he was a
lover of the poor. And we set out for the hospice there; for it was evening.

15 And the blessed John the archbishop, having heard, came to us at a run, and
having greeted one another and prayed they sat a little. And the archbishop
said to him: Rise, brother, for the Lord's sake and taste with me, that we may
rise quickly for the vigil of the holy Lord's day. For we entered late on the
Sabbath. And the blessed Porphyry was urging him to allow him the evening
because of the toil of the road; and he was saying that after the first sleep
he would rise for the vigil. And when the archbishop was not persuaded, the
blessed man having risen went with him taking me also (for we left brother
Barochas in the hospice near the garments), and having been invited by him we
dined. And having conversed much in spiritual things and slept a little, we
rose for the vigil.


16 And in that night the blessed John sends for the Gazaeans and says to them:
Be ready for the going-out; for today you receive your priest, a man whom the
Lord has shown, blameless, God-loving, boiling in the faith. And when morning
had come, having seized the blessed man, they ordained him bishop of Gaza. And
he wept much and there was no season for his tears; for he was saying that he
himself was unworthy of such a priesthood. And having been scarcely entreated
both by the Gazaeans and by the Christians found there he was quiet. And
having performed the holy liturgy of the Lord's day, we were again invited to
taste with the archbishop.

17 And he enjoined us to go out with all speed, and having spent another one
day we went out. And we slept at Diospolis, and from there having passed the
night we entered Gaza late in the evening, having been much wearied and
afflicted. And the cause of the affliction was this. Near Gaza there are
villages beside the road which belong to the idol-madness. By agreement
therefore the inhabitants of these strewed the whole road with thorns and
stakes, so that some were not able to pass, and they also poured out filth and
were making smoke of other foul-smelling things, so that we were choked from
the stench and were in danger concerning the sight. Having therefore been
scarcely saved, about the third hour of the night we entered the city. And
this hardship met the blessed man from a demonic assault. But he was not
distressed. For he perceived the ambush of the devil, that he wished from his
entrance to turn back the righteous man.

18 And we set out for the bishop's house, which the aforementioned Irenion
among the saints the bishop built together also with the holy church called
Peace. And they say that it has the name in two ways. For those from Gaza say
that when the city was taken by Alexander the Macedonian, according to a
certain token the war ceased there, and from that it was called the place
Peace. The blessed Irenion, having found this place honored by the Gazaeans,
built the church in it. Whether therefore because of the things aforesaid or
because of the name of the founder it remained so called until now. There
therefore we set out for the very small bishop's house built by him.

19 And in that year it happens that a drought came about, and all those from
the city were ascribing the matter to the entrance of the blessed man, saying
that It was declared to us by Marnas, that Porphyry is ill-footed for the
city. And when God continued not raining upon their first month called Dios,
and still also the second Apellaios, all were afflicted. And those of the
idol-madness having gathered into the Marneion, were making many sacrifices
and prayers for this; for they were saying that Marnas is lord of the rains,
and they say that Marnas is Zeus. And when they continued seven days saying
hymns and going out outside the city to a place called of prayer, having lost
heart they withdrew to their own works having accomplished nothing. These
things having thus come about, the Christians having gathered with men and
women and children, the number two hundred eighty, were urging the holy
Porphyry to go out together with them to prayer and to make a litany
concerning the sending down of the rains (for already a famine had come
about), and much more because they were ascribing the drought to the entrance
of the blessed man.

20 And the holy man having been persuaded and having proclaimed a fast
commanded all to gather from evening into the holy church, that we might
accomplish the vigil there. And we made through the whole night thirty prayers
and as many kneelings besides the choirs and the readings. And when morning
had come, having taken the sign of the precious cross going before us, we went
out with hymns to the ancient church to the west of the city, which they say
the most holy and most blessed Asclepas the bishop built, who endured many
persecutions for


the orthodox faith, whose life and works have been written in the paradise of
delight. Having therefore come to be in the said church we made as many
prayers there also, and having gone out from there we went to the holy
martyrium of the glorious martyr Timothy, in which are laid also other
precious relics of Maiouros the martyr and of Thea the confessor, and having
made as many prayers and kneelings there we returned into the city, having
made on the road three prayers and three kneelings. And having come about the
gate we found it shut (and it was the ninth hour); for those of the
idol-madness wishing to scatter the people had done this, that we might not
complete the litany. And when we remained two hours before the gate and there
was no one opening, God seeing the endurance of the people and the wailings
and the unspeakable tears, especially of the holy man, having been moved with
compassion as upon the great Elijah the prophet moves a south wind, and the
heaven becomes cloudy and lightnings and thunders begin to come about together
with the sun setting and there is brought down much rain, so that it was
thought that they were not drops, but hail being brought down from heaven. And
we from joy almost did not perceive; for we had embraced one another.

21 And certain of the Hellenes, having seen how many wonders God did for us,
having believed opened the gate and mixed with us crying: Christ alone is God,
he alone has conquered. And they came together with us into the holy church,
and from there the blessed man dismissed them with peace having sealed them
with the seal of the cross. And they were in number 127, men about 78 and
women 35 and children 14, of whom there were five girls. And we having made
the perfect thanksgiving withdrew each with joy and peace to our own. And so
great a rain was brought down in that night and on the other day, that all
feared lest there should be collapses of houses; for the greater part happened
to be of unbaked brick. And our Lord Jesus Christ made it rain unceasingly
from the eighth of Audynaios until the tenth. And Audynaios among them is the
month January according to the Romans, and their months precede the Roman by
five days. And on the eleventh we accomplished the day of the Theophany of the
Master Jesus Christ with joy hymning and giving thanks for all things which
his love of man did for us. And there were added to the flock of Christ in
that same year besides the 127 also others 35. And those of the idol-madness
did not cease laying ambush for both the blessed man and the remaining
Christians. For when they laid hold of a Hellene magistrate, they would steal
in upon him, whether through money or through their godless religion, to
maltreat the Christians, and from this no ordinary affliction was coming about
for the most blessed man. Continuously therefore both by night and by day he
was begging the man-loving God, that he might turn them from the error into
his truth.

22 And since above I made mention of the blessed Barochas, I will narrate the
rest concerning him. This man had a divine zeal if ever anyone did; for he
endured many harsh things from the idolaters. For once when he had gone for
the sake of an ecclesiastical due to a village not far from the city; and the
one who owed the due was an idolater, and being demanded and wishing to drag
it out into delay, the God-fearing Barochas not putting up with it, from this
a wrangling having come about between them the unholy man calls certain
farmers fellow-villagers like himself, and they begin to beat the blessed
Barochas with clubs and having taken him up half-dead they threw him outside
the village into a desert place; and he lay there voiceless and without
perception. And on the other day according to God's love of man Cornelius the
deacon passes through that place with two other Christians, and having found
the God-loving Barochas and recognized him, having taken him up they brought
him into the city.


23 And when those of the idol-madness saw him being carried, and having
thought him to be dead, they turned to madness because they thought it a
pollution to bring a dead man into the city, and having torn him from the
shoulders of those carrying him they begin to beat the God-loving Cornelius
the deacon and the two Christians, and having bound the foot of the blessed
Barochas with a rope they were dragging him. And in the meantime certain of
the brothers report to the blessed bishop, and having been troubled he calls
me and three other brothers found beside him and says to us: Taking courage,
brothers, run; for it is a time of witness. And when we reached the place
where they bound the blessed man, those from the crowd ran together, and some
were insulting the most holy bishop, but others seeing his endurance, how
being insulted he was not angry, but on the contrary was urging each saying
not thus to defile and insult a body of like passions, become of our portion
and they turn against one another even to blows. And we having seen the much
confusion, having taken up the God-loving Barochas withdrew into the holy
church.

24 And when we saw him still breathing, we made treatments for him. And there
was intense prayer for him through the evening and the night; for all the
brothers were gathered. And the holy bishop did not cease weeping and begging
God concerning him; for he knew what sort of divine zeal he had. And when God
saw the tears of the holy priest and the petitions of the people (for they had
him as a second Phinehas against the idolaters), he hastened his mercy to him,
and in that night he opens his eyes, and he begins to speak and to ask that
drink be given him. And I sitting beside him immediately having run reported
to the blessed bishop; for from the much joy I forgot to give him the drink, I
also having suffered the thing of the maidservant in the case of the blessed
Peter the apostle, when having heard the voice of saint Peter from joy she did
not open the door, but having left him first she announced to those in the
house. Some such thing I also underwent. And the blessed bishop having heard
was not conquered by the passion, but remained holding to the prayers, and we
having perceived his firmness and unswervingness, having left him went away,
both I and the God-loving Cornelius the deacon; for he was sitting with me
beside the blessed Barochas. And when the most holy bishop had completed the
prayers and the whole sequence, and he himself having sat together with us, he
was asking the God-fearing Barochas how he had suffered from the beginning,
and he narrated all things to us.

25 And while we were hearing, dawn comes about and behold the ekdikos of the
people with the irenarchs and the two leading men Timothy and Epiphanius and
many others having come begin to cry out and make tumult saying: Why did you
bring a dead man into the city, the ancestral laws forbidding this? And at the
same time they were also insulting the blessed bishop. And we having heard the
tumult went out, and when they saw us, they begin to beat me and the
God-loving Cornelius the deacon. And when we were calling the public officers
to witness, the most holy bishop was putting us to silence, urging each and
admonishing not thus to be angry unreasonably. And the godless men, as much as
they were being urged, so much they were raging and insulting the holy man.
When therefore the tumult was continuing, the God-loving Barochas is
strengthened and is filled with divine zeal and rises and seizes a piece of
wood and begins to beat those who happened to be there, and fear falls upon
all and they begin to fall upon one another fleeing, and he continued chasing
them as far as the then Marneion, and our new Samson returns with a great
victory, having himself also laid low a thousand of the aliens. From that time
therefore those of the idol-madness feared him, not being able to hear his
name. And after a little time we were counted worthy of


the ordination of deacons, both I and the God-loving Barochas, I indeed being
much unworthy, but that man worthily and justly receiving this gift.

26 And Porphyry among the saints, seeing the unlawful things coming about each
day by the idolaters, resolves to send me to Byzantium to ask the emperors
that the temples of the idols be taken away; for they were still in function
in Gaza, especially that called the Marneion. And having made letters to the
most holy and most holy bishop John, who was at that time bishop of
Constantinople, whose fame and praise is remembered among all, he put me to
sea and through 20 days we arrived, and having given the letters to the
blessed John, I taught him from the mouth all things. And immediately having
heard he sent word to Eutropius the cubicularius who then had great power with
the emperor Arcadius, and having read aloud to him the letters of the blessed
bishop and having asked him to run together with the writing, having received
commissions from him he went out and says to me: Attend upon me, child; for I
hope in the Master Christ that he has it to do his mercy as usual. And I was
not ceasing reminding him each day, and that man was sending and annoying
Eutropius. And after seven days a divine writing is uttered so that the
idol-houses of the city of the Gazaeans be closed and no longer function, and
this command is entrusted to a certain Hilarius, a man of the subadiuva of the
magister].

27 And I after three days procured a way out from Byzantium and through ten
days I reach the city of the Gazaeans, having anticipated Hilarius by seven
days. And I found the most holy Porphyry being ill. And when I gave him the
copies of the most blessed John the bishop of Constantinople and he read them,
having become overjoyed he was made strong, being released from the fever; and
he was saying that from the much affliction of the idolaters he had had the
illness. And after seven days the said Hilarius arrives, having two
commentarienses of the consular and many helpers both from Azotus and Ascalon
and every public appearance. And immediately he held the three leading men and
having taken sureties from them, he displayed to them the divine writing that
commanded the idol-houses of the city of Gaza to be closed at the risk of the
head of the first men of the same city, and having overthrown all the idols in
them, he closed them; but the sanctuary of Marnas he allowed to function
secretly, having taken very much money for this. And again those of the
idol-madness were doing the unlawful things from habit.

28 And it happens that another wonder came about calling many to come into
knowledge of truth; for God as compassionate through occasions turns the race
of men into his intelligible light. And the matter that happened was in these.
A woman of the notable of the city named Aelia, being about to give birth,
fell into a great danger; and the cause of the danger was this. Her infant was
no longer coming out according to nature, but was turned aside into the
against-nature, and having let down the one hand the remaining body was not
able to be brought down; for it was sideways in the belly and the midwives
were not able to bring it over into the according-to-nature. And the pain
coming upon the woman was unspeakable, the pains according to the hour
thrusting the infant, and still more the increase of the pangs was coming
about as the second day succeeded the first, and likewise also the third being
more painful than the second; and the pangs were stretched out as far as seven
days, the evil always receiving addition. But the physicians also wished to
cut the embryo out of her, and having seen her powers fallen away, they
despaired of her. Her parents therefore and the husband as being superstitious
were making sacrifice each day for her, and they were also bringing enchanters
and diviners, thinking to benefit her from these, and they accomplished
nothing.


29 And there was for her a faithful nurse, who being in exceeding pain was
making petitions in the houses of prayer for her. On one day therefore as she
was praying in the church with tears, Porphyry among the saints entered about
the ninth hour, and I also together with him, and he sees the old woman in
grief begging God with tears, and having stood he was asking her the cause.
And she having seen him fell at his feet urging him to petition Christ for
her. And when the saint knew the cause of the woman, he also wept; for he was
exceedingly compassionate. And he says to the nurse: I hear concerning that
house that it happens to be full of idols and they can be saved with
difficulty, but nevertheless all things are possible to God; for also through
an occasion he saves those about to perish. Go therefore and gather all the
kinsmen and parents and the husband and say to them: Since there is here an
excellent physician able to treat her, if he should make her escape such a
danger, what do you grant him? And they have it altogether to commission many
things to you. And say this also to them: If he should treat her, give me a
word before this that you will not transgress it nor go away to another. Make
all of them stretch the hands to heaven and give a word that they do all
things which they promised. And when they do these things, say to the woman in
childbed before all: Jesus Christ, the son of the living God, heals you;
believe in him and you shall live.

30 And the old woman having heard the word of the blessed bishop, having taken
a commendation from him ran to the house, and having found all weeping and the
woman in last danger she was urging her parents and the husband not to be
disheartened; and she was saying that An excellent physician has sent me to
you that you may furnish me a word, that when this woman is treated you do not
deny him. And the parents and the husband having heard said: If he should wish
to take all our substance, we will not hesitate, only let us see our daughter
living. And the nurse said: Stretch your hands to heaven and give me a word
that you do not deny the physician. And they eagerly and with tears stretched
the hands saying that Even all our things to him all the time of our life; for
what consolation are we about to have if this one dies? For she was an only
child to them and graceful in manners if ever another woman. And the nurse
having heard said with a great voice before all: The great priest Porphyry
says: Jesus Christ, the son of the living God, heals you; believe in him and
you shall live. And immediately the woman having cried aloud a great cry
brought down the infant living.

31 And all those found there having been struck cried: Great is the God of the
Christians, great is the priest Porphyry. And on the next the parents of the
woman and the husband and all the kinsmen and the genuine ones having gone to
the blessed Porphyry fell at his feet asking the seal in Christ. And the
blessed man having sealed them and having made them catechumens dismissed them
in peace, charging them to attend upon the holy church, and after a little
time having catechized them he baptized them together with the woman and the
infant; and they called his name Porphyry. And those enlightened through the
occasion of the woman were in number sixty-four.

32 And as much as those of the idol-madness were seeing the Christians being
multiplied, they were growing savage and were not allowing them to go after
civic offices, but were using them as bad household servants. And the blessed
Porphyry again seeing the much injustice of the Christians and not bearing it,
and seeing them being despitefully used, goes out to Caesarea to the blessed
John the archbishop and urges him with tears to furnish him relief; for he was
saying that he was no longer able to bear the unseemly things coming about by
the Gazaeans. And the blessed John having heard was urging him to be
long-suffering and to hold to the bishopric.


33 And the most holy Porphyry answering said to him: I call you to witness
before the invisible God and our hope Jesus Christ the Lord of all creation
and the worshipped and life-giving Holy Spirit not to overlook my request,
that destruction of unnumbered souls may not be sought from us. But I ask you,
father, to sail together with me to the queen city, that we may petition the
emperors, the heavenly King nodding, to overthrow the temples of the idols.
And the blessed John answering said to him: Child, the request is just, but
the season is not suitable; for the winter tropic now takes hold. And the
blessed Porphyry answered: If God wishes us to be and to turn the crowds of
Gaza, he is able to save even in winter. But you, father, taking courage in
his compassion, be willing, and we have it to be given a good journey. The
blessed John says to him: The will of Christ be done.

34 And when the blessed Porphyry had received commissions, he writes to me to
reach Caesarea with all speed and to bring with me three books and forty-three
nomismata, which happened to have been left over from the revenue of the holy
church. And I having received the letters and having taken both the books and
the nomismata, immediately went out, and having reached Caesarea, I found the
most holy bishops making ready for the voyage, and after two days having put
out we sailed on the twenty-eighth day of Gorpiaios, and according to the
Romans on September the twenty-third, and by the compassion of Christ having
had a fair voyage, through ten days we put in to the island Rhodes. And there
was then in the island in its retired parts a certain monk named Procopius,
who now is numbered together with angels; for he fell asleep five years ago,
having lived a blameless life both in fasts and vigils and in the extreme lack
of possessions, and he had also a prophetic gift and the driving-out against
demons. And we having put in at Rhodes, as has been said, and having heard
concerning the way of life of the holy man, we thought it necessary not to use
a mere passing, but to enjoy his angelic company, and having asked where he
had his dwelling, we went to him having sailed beside in a boat, and having
arrived we knocked the door. And immediately having come out he opened to us
by himself, although he had another disciple beside him.

35 And when he saw the most holy bishops, having fallen on his face he
worshipped them, then having risen he also kissed me and the God-fearing

they should use it. For some were saying, that it be dug down, others that it
be burned, others that the place be cleansed and sanctified into a church of
God, and there was much consideration concerning this. And at last the bishop
among the saints proclaims a fast to the people and a petition, that the Lord
might reveal to them how it is necessary to use it, and having fasted on that
day and having petitioned God concerning this, in the evening they
accomplished the holy synaxis. And as the synaxis was being accomplished, a
child of about seven years, standing with his own mother, suddenly cried out
saying: Burn the temple the inward one to the ground; for many terrible things
have come about in it, especially the sacrifices of men. And in such a manner
burn it. Bring liquid pitch and sulphur and hog's fat and mix the three and
anoint the bronze doors and upon them throw the fire, and thus the whole
temple is burned; for otherwise it is not possible. And the


outer one leave together with the precinct. And after the burning, having
cleansed the place, build a holy church there. And he was saying this also: I
call you to witness before God, let it not come about otherwise; for I am not
the one speaking, but Christ who speaks in me; and these things he was saying
in the voice of the Syrians. And when all heard they were wondering and
glorifying God.

67 And this wonder came also to the hearings of the holy bishop, and having
stretched his hands to heaven he glorified God and said: Glory to you, holy
Father, that you hid these things from wise and understanding and revealed
them to babes. And he enjoined the child and his mother after the dismissal of
the church to be found in the bishop's house, and having separated the child
he said to the woman: I adjure you by the son of the living God to say if by
your suggestion or of some other, you knowing him, your child uttered those
things which he said concerning the Marneion. And the woman having heard said:
I give myself over to the fearful and dreadful tribunal of Christ, if I knew
beforehand anything of the things which my son uttered on this day. But if it
seems good to you, behold taking the child examine him with a threat, and if
he has spoken these things by suggestion of someone, by fear he confesses, and
if he will speak nothing else, it is plain that he was inspired by the Holy
Spirit. And the bishop having heard the word of the woman and having praised,
said that she be separated for a short time and the child be brought in, and
when the child had stood he said to him: Who suggested to you to utter in the
church those things which you spoke for the sake of the Marneion? And the
child was silent. And the most holy bishop enjoined a whip to be brought and
the child to be stretched up that he might be feared. And he who was holding
the whip cried out with a voice saying: Who told you to speak? Speak, that you
may not be struck with the whip. And the boy stood speechless uttering
nothing. Then we who were about him were saying the same things to him with a
threat; and he was unmoved.

68 At last after all had ceased having opened his mouth, the boy said in the
Hellenic dialect: Burn the temple the inward one to the ground; for many
terrible things have come about in it, especially the sacrifices of men. And
in such a manner burn it. Bring liquid pitch and sulphur and hog's fat and mix
the three and anoint the bronze doors and upon them throw the fire, and thus
the whole temple is burned; for otherwise it is not possible to come about.
And the outer one leave together with the precinct. And after the burning,
having cleansed the place, build a holy church there. I call you to witness
again before God, let it not come about otherwise. For I am not the one
speaking, but Christ who is in me. And the most holy Porphyry wondered and all
those with him, having heard the boldness of the child and how he spoke in
well-divided speech. And summoning his son the Hellenic tongue. And she was
affirming with oaths that neither she herself nor her child knew Greek. Having
heard again the most holy Porphyry glorified God, and having brought up three
nomismata he gave them to the woman. And the boy having seen the nomismata in
the hand of his own mother, cried out saying in the Syrian tongue: Do not
take, mother, lest you also sell the gift of God for gold. And having heard
again we wondered exceedingly. And the woman gave back the three nomismata
saying to the bishop: Pray for me and my child and commend us to God. And the
holy bishop dismissed them with peace.

69 And at dawn having gathered the pious clerics and the Christ-loving people,
and still also the wonderful Cynegius and the rulers, he said to them how the
child uttered for the sake of the Marneion. And having heard they wondered,
and having been of one mind they said that according to the word of the child
it be thus burned. Having brought therefore the liquid pitch and the sulphur
and the hog's fat and having mixed the three, they anointed the


inner doors, and having made a prayer they applied the fire, and immediately
the whole temple caught and was burned. And as many of the soldiers and of the
strangers as were able, were snatching from the fire what they found, whether
gold or silver or iron or lead.

70 And there was a man of those there commanding the soldiers, whom they call
tribune, standing over the burning of the temple; and he was a Christian
according to the appearing, and according to that not manifest to the many, he
was an idolater. This man therefore standing by and seeing both the burning
and the snatching by the soldiers was being sawn, and on a pretext of their
good order he was whipping unsparingly whom he found carrying any of the
spoils. These things coming about and the walls having been destroyed from the
fire, suddenly a burning beam falls upon the tribune and brings a double death
upon him; for having split his head, it burned the remaining body. And
immediately both the faithful soldiers and those of the Christ-loving people
having known the things concerning him, that he was inclined toward the idols,
glorified God and said that psalm that says: "Why do you boast in evil, O
mighty one? Your tongue devised iniquity the whole day. You worked deceit as a
sharpened razor. You loved evil more than goodness, injustice more than to
speak righteousness. You loved all words of drowning, a deceitful tongue.
Therefore God shall pull you down to the end, pluck you out, and remove you
from your dwelling and your root from the land of the living" and the rest of
the psalm. And the sanctuary continued being burned for very many days.

71 And after these things there came about also a search of the houses. For
there were many idols in very many courts, and those found some were given
over to fire, and some were thrown into filth. And there were found also books
filled with sorcery, which they themselves said were sacred, from which those
of the idol-madness were doing the rites and the other unlawful things, and
these likewise suffered equal things to their gods.

72 And many were running to the holy faith, and some by fear and some
condemning their former way of life, and the holy church was opening the doors
to all; for it was remembering the holy scripture that says: "To him who
knocks it shall be opened and he who seeks finds" and again: "Whether in
pretence or in truth Christ is proclaimed." And certain of the faithful also
were saying to the holy bishop that it was not necessary to receive those
approaching through fear, but those with a good purpose.

73 And the holy bishop was saying to those saying these things: There are also
circumstantial virtues happening to men; for just as someone having acquired
an ungrateful household servant first admonishes him through all to be
well-minded and to serve with a simple heart, and when he finds him in no way
persuaded by the admonition, then at last by necessity he brings upon this one
the fear and blows and bonds and other such things, not wishing to destroy him
but to save him and to recognize what is due, such also suppose God to be
long-suffering upon our ungratefulness, and often exhorting us to the things
that are beneficial both through scriptures and other holy men, and we not
being persuaded, wishing in all things as a good and man-loving master to
acquire us and not to thrust us away, he brings his fear and his discipline
upon us, calling us with necessity to recognize what is due. Wherefore the
divine scripture says: "When he was killing them, then they were seeking him
and they were turning and they were rising early toward God." And again he
says through those who skip away and are stiff-necked from God: "With muzzle
and bridle you will draw their jaws of those who do not come near to you."
There is need therefore, my children, that humanity be reminded both through
fears and threats and discipline. For this again he says: "It is good for me
that you humbled me, that I might learn your justifications." And these things
have been said by me because of those wishing to come to our holy faith. For
even if they come doubting, time also is able to soften them, Christ nodding.

And that I may announce also the other thing to you,


that if they should not appear worthy of the faith, already having become in a
habit of the evil, those born from them are able to be saved being brought up
together with the good.

74 The holy Porphyry having said these things and having persuaded the
brothers, received all those wishing to be enlightened, having catechized them
for many days not only before the baptism, but also after these things; for he
was continually teaching the word, not conversing with pompous speech wishing
to display himself, but teaching in simple phrasing, and solving all things
from the scripture. There were added therefore to the flock of Christ in that
year about three hundred names, and from that each year the things of the
Christians were receiving increase.

75 And when the Marneion had been burned to the end and the city had been
settled, the blessed bishop deliberated with those from the holy clergy and
the Christ-loving people to build a holy church in the burned place, just as
it was revealed to him when he happened to be in Constantinople, through which
he also received the money from the most God-loving Eudoxia the empress.
Having therefore dismissed both the rulers and the Christ-loving people, he
held a part of the help because of there not coming about some innovation
after their going-out, and not only because of this, but also because of
helping together for the sake of gathering the materials of the building of
the said holy church. Certain therefore were advising that it be built
according to the position of the burned idol-house; for it was round-formed,
surrounded with two stoas one within another, and the middle of it was a
blown-up ciborium and stretched up into height, and it had also certain other
things which were fitting to the idols, suitable toward the abominable and
unlawful things coming about by the idol-mad. According to this position
therefore certain were saying that the holy church be built, but others were
speaking against saying that even the memory of the position ought to be taken
away; and those saying this were persuading all, as having spoken well; and
the holy bishop was saying: Let us leave this also to the counsel of God. And
while the place is being cleansed, a magistrianus arrives carrying imperial
letters of the ever-memorable Eudoxia, and the letters contained a greeting
and a request of prayers for both herself and the emperors, her husband and
the child. And there was in another paper, inside the letters, the sketch of
the holy church cross-formed just as now with God it is seen, and the letters
contained that the holy church be built according to the sketch. And he among
the saints having read and having seen the sketch rejoiced; for he knew that
this also came about according to a divine revelation, and he remembered the
scripture saying: "The heart of a king is in the hand of God." And the letters
still also contained that costly columns and marbles were about to be sent.

76 The ash therefore having been dug out and all the abominations taken away,
the leftover refuse of the marbling of the Marneion, which they said to be
sacred and to happen to be in an untrodden place, especially for women, these
therefore the holy bishop commanded to be paved before the temple outside into
the square, that they might be trampled not only by men, but also by women and
dogs and swine and beasts. And this grieved the idolaters more than the
burning of the temple. Whence the greater part of them, especially the women,
do not step upon those marbles until now. And after a little time he proclaims
a fast on one day, and when the dismissal of the morning prayers had come
about, the God-loving bishop commanded every Christ-loving man to carry
two-pronged mattocks and shovels and other such tools by each. And this he had
proclaimed beforehand from the evening, that in the morning all might be found
in readiness, which indeed also came about.

77 And when the people had been gathered with the said tools in the holy
church named Peace, he enjoined all psalming together to go


to the once Marneion, and he himself was following carrying the holy gospel
and having about him the holy clergy, truly imitating Christ with the
disciples. And the ever-memorable Barochas was going before the people
carrying the impression of the sign of the cross, and from both parts of the
people were the soldiers who had been left for the sake of the good order of
the city. And going they were psalming, and in the position of the cutting of
the psalm they were saying the alleluia; and the psalm which they were saying
was: "Come let us rejoice to the Lord, let us shout to God our saviour. Let us
come before his face in confession, and in psalms let us shout to him that the
Lord is a great God and a great king over all the earth, that in his hand are
the ends of the earth, and the heights of the mountains are his, that the sea
is his and he made it, and the dry land his hands formed. Come let us worship
and fall before him and let us weep before the Lord who made us, that he
himself is our God and we are a people of his pasture and sheep of his hand."
And they were saying also other psalms until they entered into the Marneion.

78 And the bishop among the saints had invited Rufinus the architect from that
of Antiochus, a faithful man and skilled, through whom also the whole of the
building was completed. This man having taken gypsum marked the position of
the holy church according to the form of the sketch sent by the most
God-loving Eudoxia. And the most holy bishop having made a prayer and a
kneeling, enjoined the people to dig. And immediately all with one soul and
the same eagerness were digging crying: Christ has conquered. And it was not
to see a difference of man and woman or old man or child, but the eagerness
was furnishing the same power to all, and some were digging and others were
carrying out the earth, so that through a few days all the places of the
foundations were dug and the earth carried out.

79 And the material having been prepared beforehand, both stones of great size
from a hill called Aldioma from the east of the city and other material, he
among the saints again having gathered the Christ-loving people and having
made many prayers and psalmodies in the place, having girded himself first
began to carry stones and throw them into the foundations, then also the
God-loving clerics and all the lay people, rejoicing and psalming with a great
voice, so that they were heard from three miles of the city.

80 And there came about a great wonder on that day. There happen to be wells
within the precinct of the sanctuary, of which there is one from the western
part of the now holy church of God, having no small depth. Three boys
therefore having thirsted went away to drink, and having drawn near to the
mouth of the well, they were stooping leaning on the wood upon the mouth, such
as it happens that boys do, and when the wood had been broken the three fell
into the well. And certain found there went away and announced to the people
the things that had come about. And there came about no small tumult, all
running together upon the well. And the most holy Porphyry having known what
had come about and himself having run to the place, commanded silence to come
about, and when it had come about he began to pray and to petition Christ with
many tears that he might keep the children living and unharmed, and especially
because of the idol-mad, that they might not say: Where is their God in whom
they hoped? And having spent one hour lying on the ground and having risen, he
enjoined someone to go down through the ropes of the buckets, to make the
search of the children. For those from the crowd were crying, calling the
children, and there was no one answering from below out of the well.

81 And when the man had gone down the three boys were found sitting upon a
great stone unharmed and cheerful conversing with one another. And when the
man saw them, he wondered exceedingly and glorified God, and having cried from
below he said: Glorify


the Lord, for the three boys live. And the holy bishop among the saints having
heard and those of the people rejoiced, and having sent a great basket he
enjoined the three to be brought up together. For they were small of about six
or seven years. And he below having received the basket and having bound it
securely sat the three, charging them to close their eyes until they reach the
above and to say: Jesus Christ, save. And having done this, he cried with
composure to draw the rope, and drawing they were saying the hymn of the three
children: "Blessed are you, Lord the God of our fathers." And when they
arrived and the bishop among the saints saw them (for this man happened to be
standing at the mouth of the well and holding the rope), and having been
filled with joy and tears he cried out saying: "Bless all the works of the
Lord the Lord, hymn." And when they took them up from the little basket, they
searched lest anything of their body had been struck, and nothing bad was
found in them, but we saw a great wonder.

82 For the three were found having cross-formed signs as from a scraping of a
needle, one in the middle of the forehead, and one above the right hand about
the fingers, and the other on the right shoulder. And the little crosses were
well stamped, neither slanting nor crooked, but of one measure, so that they
were plain to be God-signs; for they were making neither pain for them, nor
even blood, but they were stamped as from cinnabar. And they remained on them
a sufficient time toward all seeing and wondering; for many also of the
other-nations having seen believed.

83 And the man also who went down for the sake of the children into the well
having been brought up, was affirming with oaths saying that When I threw them
in the basket and they were being brought up, I was seeing as it were a
lightning around them until they reached the mouth of the well. And there came
about joy to the Christians on that day, and to the idolaters grief and
scandal. And the building was advancing each day, all working eagerly and
zealously; for neither was there one being deprived of his wage, but he was
also furnishing extra being ambitious toward those working; for he was saying:
It is just that the whole work of the building may not receive a curse but a
blessing.

84 And in the next year the empress Eudoxia sends the pillars which she
promised, being wonderful and great, in number thirty-two (and they are called
Carystian), which are in the holy church shining in the manner of emeralds.
And when they had been put to shore, again the eagerness and the readiness of
the Christ-loving people was shown; for all having heard immediately ran to
the shore, and not only men, but also women and children and old men (for the
longing of the faith was strengthening all), and having brought wagons, having
placed each pillar they were dragging and were setting them down in the open
of the sanctuary, and again they were returning and conveying another until
they conveyed all. And these things concerning these.

85 And at that time there sojourned in the city a woman 85 a certain
Antiochene called Julia, who was of the abominable heresy of those called
Manichaeans, and having known certain to be newly enlightened and not yet
established in the holy faith, having stolen in she was corrupting them
through her sorcerous teaching, and much more through giving of money. For he
who invented the said godless heresy was not otherwise able to bait certain
unless through the furnishing of the money. For also their lesson, to those at
least who have mind, has been filled with all blasphemy and condemnation and
old-wives' myths drawing little women and childish men having both the
reasoning and the thought light. For from various heresies and Hellenic
doctrines they composed this their bad opinion, wishing cunningly and
deceitfully to take all to themselves. For they say many gods, that they may
please Hellenes, and still also genesis and fate and

astrology they assert, that they may sin without fear, as the sinning not
being in us,


but from necessity of fate.

86 And they confess also Christ, for they say that he became man in seeming;
for they themselves also are called Christians in seeming. For I pass over the
things worthy of laughter and of ill-speaking, that I may not fill the
hearings of those who meet with a very heavy sound and marvel-talk. For having
mixed the things of Philistion the stage-player and of Hesiod and of other
so-called philosophers with those of the Christians, they composed their own
heresy. For just as a painter, making a mix from various colors, completes in
seeming a man or a beast or some other thing toward deceit of those looking,
that it may seem to the foolish and unintelligent to happen to be true, but to
those who have mind a shadow and deceit and a human device, so also the
Manichaeans, having drawn from various doctrines, completed their bad opinion,
or rather having gathered the poison from various creeping things and having
mixed it, they prepared a death-bearing drug toward destruction of human
souls. And as has been said before, when the plague-bearing woman had
sojourned, certain were led away together with her deceitful teaching.

87 And after certain days Porphyry among the saints having known from certain
faithful, having sent for her was asking, who and from where she was and what
opinion she carries. And she confessed both the fatherland and that she
happened to be a Manichaean. And those about him having been moved with anger
(for there were certain God-fearing men beside him) the blessed man was urging
them not to be wrathful but with endurance to exhort both once and twice,
keeping the saying of the holy Apostle. Then he says to the woman: Abstain,
sister, from this bad opinion; for it happens to be satanic. And she answered:
Speak and hear, and either you persuade or you are persuaded. And the blessed
man said: Make ready for tomorrow and come to be here. And she having taken
leave went out. And the blessed man having fasted and having petitioned Christ
much that he might put the devil to shame, was made ready for the next, and he
called certain of the God-fearing both clerics and lay people to the dialogue
of himself and of the woman.

88 And on the morrow the woman comes to be present, having with her two men
and as many women; and they were younger and good-looking, and all pale, and
Julia was advanced. And all were setting out from words of the worldly
education, and much more Julia. And their dignity was humble and the manner
mild, and as the saying is: outside sheep, but inside ravening wolves and
venom-shooting beasts; for they both speak and do all things with hypocrisy.
Then having been enjoined to sit, they were making the inquiry. And he among
the saints carrying the holy gospels and having made the seal of the cross in
his mouth, began to ask her to tell out her opinion. And she began to speak.
And brother Cornelius the deacon who was named a short time before, knowing
the signs of Ennomos, having been enjoined by the most blessed bishop was
noting all the things said and opposed, I and brother Barochas reminding. And
I did not write the dialogue in this book because of its being great, wishing
to make the present writing in epitome, and in another book I set it forth for
those wishing to know both the wisdom given by God to the most holy Porphyry
and the old-wives' myths which the marvel-talking and sorceress Julia babbled,
whom the divine justice went after sharply.

89 For after she had mythologized many things and for very many hours and had
spoken the customary blasphemies into the Lord and God of all, Porphyry among
the saints having been moved by the divine zeal, seeing him who contains all
things both the visible and the invisible being blasphemed by a woman
energized by the devil and condescending to that one's will, declared against
her saying: God who made all things, the only everlasting, having neither
beginning nor end, he who in trinity


is glorified, will strike your tongue and will muzzle your mouth, that it may
not speak ill-speaking things.

90 And immediately together with the declaration the punishment also followed;
for Julia began to tremble and the face to be altered, and having remained in
ecstasy for a sufficient hour she was not speaking, but she was voiceless and
unmoved, having the eyes opened and attending to the most holy bishop. And
those with her having seen what she underwent, feared exceedingly; and they
were restoring her soul and they were singing over into her ear, and there was
no voice and there was no hearing. And having spent a sufficient hour
voiceless she delivered the soul, having gone away into the darkness which she
honored, having thought it light, according to the scripture that says: "Woe
to those who make the sweet bitter and the bitter sweet, to those who set the
darkness as light and the light as darkness." And Porphyry among the saints
enjoined her body to be wrapped and given over to burial, having had mercy on
the human nature; for he was exceedingly compassionate.

91 And as many as heard what had come about wondered exceedingly, not only
those of our faith, but also the other-nations. And the two men and women with
her and as many as were corrupted by her, having run fell at the feet of the
most blessed bishop saying: We have been led astray, and they were asking
repentance. And the blessed man made all anathematize Manes the leader of
their heresy, from whom they were also called Manichaeans, and having
catechized them duly for very many days he brought them to the holy catholic
church. And on their occasion also others of the other-nations having repented
were enlightened.

92 And after a five-year time the work of the holy great church was completed,
and it was called Eudoxiana from the name of the most God-loving Eudoxia the
empress. And the most holy Porphyry accomplished the dedication on the
resurrection day of the holy Pascha sumptuously, not sparing expense, but
having gathered all the monks of the surrounding country, about a thousand
names, with other God-fearing clerics and lay people and bishops, he made
gladness all the days of the holy Pascha. And it was to see angelic choirs not
only in the ecclesiastical sequence, but also in the hours, when they were
tasting. For not only was the table perceptible, but also spiritual. For after
the relish a psalm was being said and after the drink a hymn. And those of the
idol-madness seeing the things coming about were melting in the heart. For
also from everywhere strangers were coming to see the beauty and the greatness
of the said holy church; for it was said to be greater than all the churches
of that time.

93 For this reason when he laid the foundations, at the beginning he was
accused by certain faithful that he marked it large the Christians in the city
being few, and the most holy Porphyry answering said: Let your faith not be
little; for I am of good hope in our Lord Jesus Christ the son of God, that he
will multiply his flock and will magnify his house still more from the crowds
of the Christians not being able to have room; for neither is the doctrine of
the Christians human, that it may appear toward a season and be dissolved, but
divine and receiving increase. These things and such the blessed man was
always conversing with the faithful, not only in the church but also in each
place lighting and benefiting the Christ-loving people. And after the days of
the feast he dismissed the crowds with peace each to their own.

94 And after building and sanctifying the said holy church, he appointed that
there be given to each stranger sojourning in the city the expense of one day,
and he was also furnishing to each poor man both stranger and citizen each day
six obols each, besides those things which he himself was furnishing through
himself to those coming to him both in garments and in silver and in gold,
furnishing to each toward his worth, and no one was without share of those


in need of his grants. And in the days of the fasts of the holy Paschal season
he was furnishing to each poor man 10 obols each for forty days. Having
commanded in his pious testament that the said ten obols be altogether given
the forty days, and wishing to set apart a revenue from which they be given,
in the said testament he set in that if these things should not be furnished
each year, the said revenue come to the holy church of Caesarea. And these
things came about later.

95 And those of the idol-madness, as much as they were seeing Christianity
advancing, so much they were raging and were being eager to maltreat the
Christians, and before all, their holy shepherd Porphyry. For once a dispute
having come about for the sake of lands between the steward of the holy church
and Sampsychus the leading man, the God-loving Barochas, seeing the steward
being insulted, took his part and began to insult the said Sampsychus. And the
rest of the council having heard, having gathered they went against both the
steward and the God-loving Barochas. And there went up together with the
councillors also many of the citizens, having found a pretext of maltreating
those from the faith, and as one might say, from a small spark so great a fire
was kindled and burned that all the Christians were in danger and being
destroyed. For the idolaters raged so much as themselves also to take up both
swords and clubs and to murder seven names and to strike many others.

96 Then not having been satisfied with these, they set out upon the shepherd
himself. And certain running before rejoicing in the good, announced to the
most holy bishop the running-down of the crowd. And the blessed man having
heard, having called me said: Let us flee, brother, and hide a little until
the wrath of the Lord pass. And having walked the walls we fled through the
housetops. And the idol-mad, having broken the doors of the bishop's house,
entered in and not having found Porphyry among the saints, snatched all the
things found there.

97 And I and the blessed Porphyry having fled through the housetops, found a
girl of about fourteen years, who having recognized the holy bishop fell at
his feet. And the blessed man asked her who she happened to be and of what
parents. And the girl answering said that she happens to be an orphan both
from father and mother, and she was saying she has a grandmother an old woman
weak in the body, and that she herself works and feeds herself and her
grandmother. And he was asking her if she was a Christian, and she again said
she was not, but that she desired it from long; If indeed I am worthy. And the
compassionate Porphyry having heard the word of the girl and having been
pricked wept saying: How ready toward the good is the race of the Gazaeans.
But the adversary is eager to hinder such a purpose, whom the Lord will strike
with the word of his mouth. And he said to the maiden: Bring us here a mat in
this rooftop-room, that we may remain here until the tumult of the city is
settled, and do not announce to anyone that we are here. And she was affirming
with oaths not to reveal even to her grandmother.

98 And having gone down through a certain little hatch into her house, she
brought the mat and a sack of chaff; having spread the mat she put the sack
under, and having fallen at the feet of the blessed man, she was urging him to
taste of her moderate foods and not to take it ill at her poverty; for it was
also toward evening. And the holy man wishing to become an imitator of the
great prophet Elijah said to the maiden: Make haste, daughter, and bring, that
the Lord may give back to you through me spiritual food and fleshly. And she
having made haste went down, and having gone away she bought bread and olives
and cheese and soaked pulse and wine, and she brought all and set them before
us saying: Take, my lords, and bless my poverty.

And the


blessed man again having been pricked wept, having foreseen the faith which
she was about to have into Christ. And having risen and having made the
customary prayers and having sat we partook; I indeed partook of bread and
cheese and wine, and the holy man of bread and soaked pulse and water. And
having dismissed the maiden to her grandmother, we slept in the rooftop-room;
for it was also the season of summer. And we asked also the name of the
maiden, and she said: Salaptha, which is interpreted in Greek Irene. And we
spent also the next in the rooftop-room, the good Irene making every answer
for us with much eagerness.

99 And when we knew that the tumult of the city had been settled, we went
through the night into the holy church, and having gone up into the bishop's
house, we found nothing in it except the God-loving Barochas lying and being
in last condition from the blows brought upon him by the godless and impious
idolaters. And after a few days the consular having known the things that had
come about in the city (and he was called Clarus), sends a commentariensis
with much help and secures those whom the public officers indicated and
presents them at Caesarea, and some he punished, and some having beaten with
ox-sinews he dismissed, and having made no small fear he thus settled the
city.

100 And after a few days Porphyry among the saints having remembered that good
maiden who received us, sent for her through me; and she came to be present at
a run, having also another woman whom she was saying to be an aunt. And having
entered to the blessed bishop, they fell at his feet; and he received these
kindly as an affectionate father. And he said to the maiden: Truly, daughter,
do you desire to become a Christian? And she answering said: And already I
said to you, my lord, that from long I have this desire and now I have brought
as witness my aunt, who herself also is of the same desire. And he having
become overjoyed said to her: Play the man and be strong, child. And having
called the steward of the holy church, he enjoined him to furnish to her and
to her grandmother four miliaresia of silver each day, and to her aunt he gave
one nomisma, and having sealed them with the sign of the cross he dismissed
them, charging them to attend upon the prayers and the catechesis of the
catechumens. And he also sent into her house the God-fearing Timothy the
presbyter and catechist, and he enjoined him to seal the grandmother of the
maiden; for as I said before she had the body pierced through. And the three
having been catechized a little time, were counted worthy of the precious
baptism.

101 And after putting off the holy garment, the holy man having called the
said maiden said to her: Do you wish that we yoke you to a man toward lawful
marriage? For also there is for you a season of living together; for neither
is the solemn marriage forbidden by our scripture. And the maiden having heard
the word of the holy man, began to weep and to say: Good father, after you
yoked me to a great husband, do you wish to unyoke me from that one and give
me out to a humble and worthless? By no means, my lord, may you do this. And
he among the saints having been seized said: And who is this whom I yoked to
you? And she answered: Jesus Christ, the saviour of our souls, my true
bridegroom, from whom I am not unyoked into the age. And he among the saints
having heard and having been pricked wept, so that from the much compunction
he embraced the maiden and kissed her head; for he was truly perfectly
passionless and from much compassion having the tear near at hand. And we also
who were about him having seen the grace of the Holy Spirit given to the
God-loving maiden glorified God who grants wisdom and grace to his elect. And
he dismissed the maiden on that day.

102 And it happened in those same days that the old woman was rested and went
away to the Lord. Then having called the maiden, he sent for the God-fearing
Manaris the deacon, who herself also is interpreted according to the Hellenic

tongue Photine; to this woman he commended Salaptha, having given her the
canonical garment,


and having commended them to God he dismissed them with peace. And she took up
such a way of life as no other had at that time, fasting each day and after
the fast partaking of a little bread with salt and soaked pulse or fine
vegetables and water only; for of wine she was not partaking at all. And on
the feasts she was also partaking of oil and was eating olives, and she was
tasting nothing else of the things coming about through fire; and in the forty
days of the fasts all the days she was eating through two soaked pulses or
fine vegetables without bread; and the whole holy Paschal week she was
drawing, partaking of nothing, except on the holy Thursday, after the holy
communion, of hot water. And she so wasted her body that those seeing her
thought to behold a shadow. And she became an example also to many other
women; for they were zealous for the life and the way of life of the holy
maiden Salaptha, who until now seems to live; for she has been made dead to
the world, but she lives to Christ and is with him through all; of whose holy
prayers may we have a share. And let these things have been said concerning
the holy maiden Salaptha.

103 And the most blessed bishop Porphyry, having established both the
ecclesiastical rule and the whole sequence, having lived other few years after
sanctifying the great church, having fallen into an illness drafted a pious
testament, having legated many; and having commended all those of the
Christ-loving people to God, he fell asleep in peace with the saints, on the
second of the month Dystros of the year according to the Gazaeans the
eightieth four-hundredth, having been bishop 24 years and 11 months and 8
days, having completed the good contest toward the idol-mad until the day of
his falling-asleep. And now he is in the paradise of delight, interceding with
all the saints for us, by whose prayers may the Father and God have mercy on
us with Son and Holy Spirit, to whom the glory and the power into the ages of
the ages. Amen.
