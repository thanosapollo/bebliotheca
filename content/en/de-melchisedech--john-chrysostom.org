OF THE SAME, On Melchizedek.
=56.257=
1. Today I wish to set before you an apostolic table, and I am preparing to
stretch the discourse toward the sea of Paul's voice. But what am I to do? I
hesitate and I fear lest, having gone out from the harbor and having come down
to the depth of the apostolic thoughts, we grow dizzy, which is what those
unused to seafaring suffer. For they, when they have left the land and see sea
on either side of the ship, and nothing else but sea and sky, are seized with
vertigo, and they think the ship is being whirled round with the sea.

But the dizziness comes not from the nature of the sea, but from the
inexperience of those who sail. Other sailors, then, with naked bodies, dive
down upon the waves, and they suffer nothing of this sort; but having gone
down to the very depth, they pass their time more securely than those sitting
on the deck, and receiving the brine with mouth and eyes and the whole body,
they are not distressed. So great and of such a kind is inexperience as an
evil. Thus experience persuades even to despise fearful things; inexperience
prepares even to suspect and to fear the things that are safe. For those
sitting upon the high benches of the ship even grow dizzy at the sight, but
those in the midst of the waves are not thrown into tumult.

This also happens in our mind. For waves of passions often overtake it too,
wilder than those of the sea: a storm, as it were, anger turning the heart
from below, winds of evil desire, making a great confusion of the mind. But
the inexperienced and untrained, when the storm of anger begins, is at once
thrown into tumult, is troubled, is shaken; he sees the soul becoming
submerged under the passions and suffering shipwreck. But the experienced and
practiced bears such things nobly; like a helmsman upon the tillers, having
seated reason above the passions, he does not leave off doing all things until
he has steered the vessel toward the calm harbor of philosophy.

What happens then upon the sea, and what occurs in the mind, this also of
necessity befalls in the interpretation of the Scriptures: to be thrown into
tumult, to be troubled, whenever we go out into the open sea; not because the
sea is fearful, but because we who sail are inexperienced. That a discourse
easy by nature =56.258= can become difficult because of the inexperience of
the hearers, I will present Paul to you as a witness. For having said that
Christ became high priest according to the order of Melchizedek, and seeking
who this Melchizedek is, he added: Concerning whom we have much discourse, and
it is hard to interpret. What are you saying, O Paul? Hard to interpret for
you who have the spiritual wisdom? for you who heard the unspeakable things?
for you who were caught up to the third heaven? If it is hard to interpret for
you, to whom is it comprehensible? Hard to interpret for me, he says, not
because of my own weakness, but because of the difficulty of the hearers.
Having said, then, Hard to interpret, he added: Since you have become dull of
hearing. Do you see that it is not the nature of the discourse, but the
inexperience of the hearers, that has made what is not difficult difficult?
And not only difficult, but the same cause makes a short discourse long.
Therefore he said it was not only hard to interpret, but also much,
attributing the cause both of the length and of the difficulty to the dullness
of hearing.

For just as with those who are sick it is not enough to set a short and
hastily prepared table, but one must prepare various foods, so that if the one
who is ill does not want this, he may take the other, and if he accepts that,
he may receive the next, and if he rejects this, he may take hold of the
other, and by variety we may overcome the difficulty, and by the many kinds of
the table we may heal the fastidiousness of the mind: so often one must do
also in hearing, when we are weak, and one must prepare a long discourse,
having varied parables and examples, arguments and periods, and many other
such things, so that from all of them the choice of what is profitable may
become easy for us. Nevertheless, even if the discourse is much and hard to
interpret, he did not deprive them of the teaching concerning Melchizedek; by
saying, Much and hard to interpret, he aroused their zeal, so that they might
not become more slothful about the hearing; and by providing the table, he
granted their desire.

2. Let us then do this also. Even if we are unable for the sea and depth of
the thoughts, let us dare the sea, not according to our own power, but
according to the grace given us from above; not because of our own boldness,
but because of your benefit let us dare the sea, imitating Paul in this also.
For that he did not deprive them of the discourse concerning Melchizedek, hear
what follows. Having said, then, Concerning whom the discourse is much and
hard to interpret, he added: For this Melchizedek, king of righteousness, and
then also king of Salem, which is king =56.259= of peace, without father,
without mother, without genealogy, having neither beginning of days nor end of
life, but made like to the Son of God, remains a priest continually. Did he
not grate upon your hearing, speaking about a man and saying, Without father,
without mother, without genealogy? And why do I say about a man? For if this
were said about the Son, would it not even so furnish much inquiry? For if
without father, how is he Son? And if without mother, how Only-begotten? For
the son must have a father, since otherwise he would not be a son. But the Son
of God is both without father and without mother: without father as to the
generation below, without mother as to that above. For he had neither a father
on earth, nor a mother in the heavens. Without genealogy. Let those who busy
themselves over his essence hear this.

And yet some think that this Without genealogy has been said about the
generation above. For the heretics do not even want this; for they busy
themselves over that one too and pry into it; but those more moderate than
these yield that one, and think that Without genealogy has been said about
this one below. Let us show, then, that Paul said this about both generations,
both about the one above and about the one below. For that one too is fearful,
and this one is most mystical. For this reason Isaiah also says: Who shall
declare his generation? But he said it, someone says, about that one above.
What then shall we say to Paul, who spoke of both generations, and then added,
Without genealogy? So that you may believe him to be without genealogy not
only according to that generation in which he is without mother, but also
according to this one in which he is without father, I mean the one below: for
this reason, having set down both, he then said, Without genealogy. For this
lower one too is incomprehensible, so that we may not dare even to peep toward
that other. For if the forecourts of the temple are so fearful and
unapproachable, who will attempt to enter the inner sanctuary?

That he was begotten from the Father I know, but the how I do not know. That
he was born of the Virgin I understand, but the manner I do not grasp even
here. For each generation is confessed, and the manner of each has been kept
silent. And just as here, in the case of the Virgin, not knowing how he was
born of the virgin, I confess that he was born, and I do not abolish the fact
because of the ignorance: so you also do in the case of the Father; even if
you do not know how he was begotten, confess it. And if the heretic says to
you: How was the Son begotten from the Father? drag down his pride to the
earth, and say to him: Come down from the heavens, and show how he was born of
the Virgin, and then look at those things.

Hold him and surround him, do not let him leap away, nor withdraw into the
labyrinth of reasonings, but hold him and choke him, not with the hand, but
with the word. Do not give him room, lest he escape as he wishes. For from
that quarter they throw those who converse into tumult, because we follow
them, and do not lead them away under the laws of the divine Scriptures.
Therefore wall him in on every side with the testimonies from the Scriptures,
and he will not even be able to gape. Say, How was he born of the Virgin? I do
not leave you, nor do I withdraw. But he would not be able to tell us the
manner, even if he contend ten thousand times. For when God has shut, who will
open thereafter? Such things are acceptable by faith alone. But if you would
not be able, and yet you seek reasonings, I will say to you what Christ says
to Nicodemus: If I told you earthly things, and you do not believe, how,
=56.260= if I tell you heavenly things, will you believe? I spoke of the birth
from the Virgin, and you do not know, nor do you dare to gape, and you pry
into the heavenly one? And would that it were the heaven only, but you also
busy yourself over the Master of the heavens.

If I told you earthly things, and you do not believe. He did not say, you are
not persuaded, but You do not believe; showing us that if even earthly things
need faith, much more the heavenly. And yet then he was conversing with
Nicodemus about a far lesser birth; for the discourse was about baptism, and
the spiritual regeneration; but it is clear that these too are grasped by
faith. And he called them earthly, not because they are earthly, but because
they are accomplished on earth, and by comparison with the generation above,
which is unspeakable and surpasses every mind, these are earthly. If then it
is not possible to know how we are regenerated from the waters, but one must
receive what happens by faith alone, and not pry into the manner: of how great
madness would it be, in the case of the generation above of the only-begotten
Son, to set human reasonings in motion, and to demand an account of the manner
of generation? How the Son of God is without father and without mother has
been sufficiently shown, and how without genealogy.

3. But since many, not understanding the things written about him, have said
that he is greater than Christ, and have established a heresy for themselves,
and are called Melchizedekians, and contend with us, wishing to show that he
is greater than Christ, bringing forward, You are a priest forever according
to the order of Melchizedek: we must also meet them. For they say: How would
he not be greater than Christ, according to whose image and order Christ
serves as priest? We then say that he is a man of like passions with us, and
neither greater than Christ, nor even than John the Baptist; for Greater than
he, says Christ, has not arisen among those born of women. And others again,
having gone astray, say that he is the Holy Spirit; but we do not say this
either. For what need was there for the Word of God to become man, if the
Spirit had already long ago become a man? And that he is not greater than
Christ, nor again the Holy Spirit, let those men tell us of what region they
say he is: of heaven? of earth? or of the underworld? If then they say that
Melchizedek is of the heavenly beings, or of some other region, let them hear
that he too bends the knee to Christ who was incarnate of the Theotokos Mary.
For the Apostle says that To him every knee shall bow, and what follows. If
then every knee bows, Melchizedek would be less than Christ; for he worships
the Christ who is worshiped.

But if the wretched and miserable would also consider what is said next; for
he adds, saying, Made like to the Son of God: one must understand it thus,
that just as we too have been made according to the image and likeness of God,
so also he. For the Jews say that he was born of fornication, and for this
reason became without genealogy; to whom we shall say, You speak badly. For
Solomon too, born of the adulterous wife of Uriah, is given a genealogy. But
since Melchizedek was a type of the Lord, and bore the image of Christ, just
as Jonah also did, for this reason Scripture omitted his father, so that in
him, as in an image, we might behold the Christ who is truly without father
and without genealogy. And the Melchizedekians also contradict us in this,
saying: What then is it that the Father says to him: You are a priest forever
according to the =56.261= order of Melchizedek? To whom we say that this
Melchizedek was a just man, and truly bore the image of Christ. Moved then by
a prophetic spirit, having understood the offering that was going to be
brought for the nations, he honored God with bread and wine, imitating the
Christ who was to come.

Since therefore the synagogue of the Jews offered sacrifice to God in the
order of Aaron, not bread and wine, but calves and lambs, and glorified God
with sacrifices through blood, God cries out toward =56.262= Jesus the Christ,
the Son of God, who was going to be born of the virgin Mary, and says: You are
a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek; not according to that
of Aaron, who served with calves and lambs; but, You are a priest forever
according to the order of Melchizedek, bringing continually with bread and
wine the offering of those who present it. Through whom to the Father be
glory, together with the all-holy Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of
ages. Amen.
