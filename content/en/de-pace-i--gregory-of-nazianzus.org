C. These both when present made me illustrious, my abundance, my good
delight, and when absent they compressed me. These things held my soul
together, these things troubled it. From these I went mourning and downcast;
because of these, along with the other delights, I also shook off the word;
because the beloved ones kicked away, and turned their backs upon me and not
their face, and became a flock more free than the shepherd, that I may not
say more insolent; because the true vine was turned to bitterness for me,
=35.725= that which had been most finely pruned for the good husbandman, and
was farming the good crop for the divine winepresses; because my friends and
my neighbors drew near over against me and stood, and those nearest me stood
far off; because we divided Christ, we the exceedingly God-loving and
Christ-loving, and for the sake of the truth we lied against one another,
and through love we practiced hatred, and for the corner-stone we were
dissolved, and for the rock we were shaken; because more than was well we
warred for peace, and for the one lifted up upon the wood we were brought
down, and for the one buried and risen we were put to death.

D. Formerly these things; and why would anyone, in a season of brightness,
rake up the unpleasantness, lingering in the painful things, of which the
experience is to be prayed against, and the memory to be fled? And silence
is better than speech, covering the fracture that fell upon us in the depths
of forgetfulness; except if someone should stir the memory of the painful
things for this only, that we may be trained by the example, and as in
diseases we may flee the causes from which we were led into these things.
But now, when pain and grief and groaning have fled away; when we of the One
have become one, and we of the Trinity connatural, and of one soul, and of
equal honor; we of the Word outside of unreason; we of the Spirit not
boiling against one another but with one another; we of the Truth thinking
and saying the same; we of Wisdom of good understanding; we of the Light
walking decently as in day; we of the Way all going straight; we of the Door
all within; we of the Sheep and of the Shepherd gentle, and of the same
fold, and of one shepherd; not in the gear of an unskilled shepherd
shepherding, nor of one destroying the sheep of the pasture, nor of one
casting them to the wolves and the cliffs, but of one very well considered
and skilled; when we of him who suffered for us have become compassionate,
and lightening one another's burdens together; we of the Head, being fitted
together as one body and compacted according to every joint that is in the
Spirit; when he who does all things and changes them toward the more
profitable turned the lamentation into joy for us, and gave in exchange the
gladness of the sackcloth, then I put off silence together with the things
that have passed =35.728=, and I bring forward the word to the present
season and to you, or rather to God a most fitting thank-offering, a gift
purer than gold, more precious than costly stones, more costly than woven
stuffs, holier than a legal sacrifice, more sacred than the firstfruits of
firstborn, pleasing to God above a young calf, incomplete in horns and hoofs
and without sense, above incense, above a whole burnt offering, above
myriads of fat lambs, with which the elementary law ruled still-infant
Israel, sketching in blood-sacrifices the one that was to come.

E. This I offer to God, this I dedicate, which alone I left to myself, in
which alone I am rich. For the other things I yielded to the commandment and
to the Spirit; and I exchanged the precious pearl for all that I once had,
and I have become a great merchant, or rather I have it by prayer to become
one, having bought with the small things, which will wholly perish, the
great things that are not dissolved; but of the Word alone I take hold, as a
servant of the Word, and I would never willingly neglect this possession;
but I both honor it, and greet it, and rejoice more than in all the other
things together in which the many rejoice; and I make it partner of all my
life, and a good counselor and companion, and guide of the way upward, and
an eager fellow-combatant; and since I dishonor every delight that remains
below, into this all my affection after God has been emptied; or rather even
into this one; because it carries toward God with understanding, by which
alone God is genuinely grasped, and is kept, and grows in us. I said that
Wisdom is my sister; and I honored her, and embraced her, as it was possible
for me; and I seek for my head the crown of graces and of delight, which
indeed are gifts of wisdom, and of the word in us that illumines the ruling
part, and lights for us the steps according to God.

F. With this I bridle anger carried away, with this I put to sleep envy that
melts, with this I rest grief, a bond of the heart, with this I chasten the
pouring-out of pleasure, with this I measure hatred, but not friendship; for
the one must be measured, but of the other no one is to know a bound. This
makes me moderate when I have plenty, and great-souled when poor; this
persuades me to run with the one who runs well, and =35.729= to stretch a
hand to the one who falls, and to be weak with the weak, and to rejoice with
the strong. With this, fatherland and a strange land are equal to me, and a
change of places is of alien things likewise, but not of my own. This
divides worlds for me, and leads me away from the one, and adds me to the
other; this also leads me through the right-hand weapons of righteousness,
not lifted up, and even with the left-hand and rougher ones it philosophizes
together, yoking the hope that does not shame and lightening the present by
the future. With this even now I greet my friends and brothers, and I set
forth a rational table, and a spiritual and ever-flowing mixing-bowl; not
with the things with which the table below flatters the belly that is being
abolished, and is incurable.

G. I was silent; shall I also be silent always? I endured as a woman in
labor; shall I endure for ever? For to Zacharias the silence is loosed by
John being born; for it was not fitting that the father of the voice be
silent, when this had come forth; but as, being disbelieved, it bound the
tongue, so, being made manifest, it gave the father freedom, to whom also
the good news was announced, and a voice and a lamp was born, forerunner of
the Word and of light; but for me the present benefaction looses the tongue,
and lifts the voice, as of a trumpet, and this fairest theater, the children
of God that were scattered, gathered into one, and resting under the same
wings, and going into the house of God in concord, and fitted to one
harmony, that of the good and of the Spirit; when we no longer bear one
another and lead, having been so stolen by the evil one, or forced, and shot
in the mind in a moonless night, which he himself raised against us; and I
know not what one must say, so as even to rejoice at one another's evils,
and not to think one another's overthrow a loss of the whole; when Judah and
Israel set for themselves one beginning, and Jerusalem and Samaria are
gathered toward the one Jerusalem above, and no longer of Paul, and of
Apollos, and of Cephas, for whom and against whom was the swelling, but we
have all become Christ's. =35.732=

H. But since you have both me and the word, and through love have tyrannized
one not unwilling, I will speak, even if with difficulty, since you command
this; and I will speak words of thanksgiving and of admonition. The
thanksgiving then is of this sort. Who will speak the mighty acts of the
Lord? And who will set before all hearing the praise upon all things? that
the two have become one, and the middle wall of the fence has been
dissolved; that you have made us cease from being a byword among the
nations, a shaking of the head among the peoples; that you afflicted us so
much as to make known the good of peace by the division, and having made us
to suffer, you restored us again; O the paradox of the healing! having
trained us toward peace through hatred that was hated as quickly as
possible, and having managed the contrary by the contrary, and having
separated us so much as to make us run to one another more warmly; just as
of plants those which are forcibly pulled aside by hands, then released, run
back again to themselves, and to their first nature, and show what is their
own, being bent aside by force, but not being set upright by force; that no
longer does the hand disdain the eye,

lamp under the bushel (which in a little while he will set upon the

rational flock, making it to dwell in a place of green grass by the

Spirit. This then we both hope and pray; but for me it is already time to
add to the thanksgiving also the exhortation; and I will make this too, as
far as possible, very brief, since you have already received the most of the
admonition through the deeds themselves, and those trained by experience do
not need longer words.

J. It was necessary, brethren, that we should neither have been divided at
first, nor that the ancient dignity and adornment should have been broken
down for us; by which, although our flock was small, and not even worthy to
be named among flocks that are numbered, they held equally with the greatest
and broadest; and there are some whom they even preferred in the power of
the Spirit. For so it was: of another, some other adornment, either small or
greater, but of our flock the most proper was the unbroken and unfactioned;
so that it was often even named the ark of Noah, alone having escaped the
flood of the whole world, and preserving in itself the seeds of piety. But
since we were proved to be men, and did not wholly escape the envy of the
evil one, nor have we appeared better than the disease that holds all; but
we ourselves also have taken a share of the common calamity, and the fair
and ancestral inheritance, the good of concord, we did not keep to the end,
yet even here we had no small advantage over the others (if one must even
boast against our enmity, having taken courage in Christ), both that we
suffered these things last, and that we were first to be set right. For to
fall sick is of the =35.736= common nature, and of the weakness of the
human, which touches all, even those very strong in body and in mind; but to
be healed, and to return to one another, is of reason and of grace, which
awarded us well and justly, and better than according to our prayers, and
the hopes of the others.

K. For we received in grace the heads given to the portion, as having been
newly cut for the sake of piety, and for the help of the right word that was
laboring, and we did not turn away as from enemies, but we embraced as
brothers those who had risen a little over a paternal inheritance, in a
brotherly way, but not wickedly; and we did not praise the enmity, but we
accepted the zeal; for a division for piety is better than a passionate
concord; and for this reason we have made the subtraction an addition of
ourselves, having stolen by love the device against us, and having so
exchanged the order, that the grace did not follow the vote, but the vote
the grace, and we used others' hands for this, having been anticipated a
little by the Spirit; and you, having left the suspicion against the letter,
ran to the spirit; not praising the simplicity upon the appearance of the
words, yet not conceiving impiety; but knowing that unfallen

L. For nothing is so strong toward concord for those genuine in the things
toward God, as the agreement about God; and nothing so ready toward
division, as disagreement about this. For even the most reasonable in other
things is hottest about this, and the meek truly becomes a fighter, when he
sees God being damaged by long-suffering, or rather damaging God by his own
fall, the one who both is rich in us and makes us rich. Thus then, as I
said, we even in the division more moderate; so that the concord became even
more conspicuous than the sundering, and the interval was all but stolen by
the right hands from both sides; but since the speed of the peace does not
suffice toward security, unless some word that holds it also appear, and God
come as ally to the word, from whom every good both begins and comes to an
end; come, both through prayers and through reasonings let us confirm this
as far as we can; having first considered this, that the fairest of beings
and the highest is God, unless it please someone even to lead him above
essence, or to set the whole of being in him, from whom it is also for the
others; and second, as many first things as are from God and about God, I
mean the angelic and heavenly powers, which, first drawing of the first
light, and being made distinct by the word of truth, are themselves also
light, radiances of perfect light; and of these nothing is so proper as the
unwarlike and unfactioned. For there is not faction in Godhead, because
neither is there dissolution; for dissolution is offspring of faction; but
so great is the concord both toward itself and toward the second things,
that even a name has come to be for God, among the others and before the
others by which he rejoices to be called, this advantage. For he is named
Peace and Love, and such things, granting us through the names to claim
these virtues as of God.

M. But of the angels, he who dared to revolt, and to be lifted above his
worth, stiffening the neck against the Lord Almighty, and devising the seat
above the clouds, as the word of the prophet, paid a penalty worthy of the
madness, having been condemned to be darkness instead of light, or, to speak
more truly, having become so from himself =35.740=; but the rest remain upon
their own worth, of which the first is the peaceful and unfactioned, having
received the being one from the praiseworthy and holy Trinity, from which
also the illumination. Since that too is and is believed to be one God, not
less through the concord than through the identity of essence; so that near
to God and the divine are as many as appear greeting the good of peace, and
hating and being displeased at the contrary, faction; but of the opposing
portion are as many as are warlike in manner, and hunting the well-reputed
by novelty, and adorning themselves with their own shame; since that one
himself both revolts against himself, and by the many-formed, and by the
passions, and in the others, works this same thing, as a man-slayer from the
beginning and a hater of the good, that in a moonless night he may shoot the
common body of the Church, hiding himself in the gloom of faction, as I
think, and he also approaches each one for the most part sophistically and
knavishly, and as it were opening a place in us for himself through the art,
that he may fall in at all, as a champion upon an army at the part of the
wall or of the array that is broken.

N. This then is one thing, and so great toward a necessity of goodwill and
of agreement, the imitation of God and of the divine things; toward which
alone it is safe for the soul made according to the image of God to look,
that as much as possible the nobility may be kept for it through the
inclination toward them, and the likeness as far as attainable; and second,
let us look up to the heaven above, and to the earth below, having heard a
divine voice, and let us learn laws of creation; that heaven, and earth, and
sea, and this whole world, the great and celebrated element of God, by which
also God is shown, being proclaimed in silence, so long as it stands firm
and is at peace toward itself, remaining in the proper bounds of nature, and
nothing rises up against the other, nor goes out of the bonds of goodwill,
with which the craftsman Word bound the whole, is both a world, as it is
called, and an unapproachable beauty, and no one would ever devise anything
more splendid or more magnificent than this. But at once it has ceased from
being at peace, and from being a world. For does it not seem to you that
heaven, sharing in good order with air and earth, with the one light, with
the other rains, is held by a law of goodwill? =35.741= and earth and air,
the one granting foods, the other breathing, to all living things, and
through this holding together the living, imitate the affection of parents?

O. And the seasons, being mixed mildly, and little by little giving way to
one another, and taming the austerity of the extremes by the mean, are they
not fittingly awarded by peace toward both pleasure and use at once? And
what of day and night, having obtained equal share toward one another and a
measured turn, the one raising us to work, the other giving rest? And what
of sun, and moon, and the beauty and multitude of stars, appearing and
withdrawing in order? And sea and earth, mixing mildly with one another, and
distributing usefully, and receiving in turn humanely, nourish man, and
supply to man the things from themselves richly and generously? And rivers
drawn through mountains and plains, and not overpassing, except toward the
useful, nor turning back to cover the earth? And mixtures and blendings of
elements, and symmetries and concords of members? and foods of living
things, and births, and dwellings divided? and the ruling and the ruled, and
those yoked under us, and the free? All these things being so, and being
steered and carried through according to the first causes of the harmony, or
rather of the flowing-together and breathing-together, what else ought they
to be thought than proclamations of friendship and of concord, and to
legislate for men through themselves the being of one soul?

P. But when matter revolts against itself, and becomes hard to hold,
practicing the dissolution through the faction, or God shakes something of
the harmony for fear of those who sin and for punishment, either the sea
giving way, or the earth seething, or strange rains being borne, or the sun
being covered, or a season overflowing, or fire boiling over, there is
disorder according to this, and fear about the whole, and the good of peace
is shown by the faction. And that I may pass over peoples, and cities, and
kingdoms, and further choruses, and armies, and houses, and ships' crews,
and yokes, and companies, held together by peace, and dissolved by faction,
I will go with the word upon Israel, and having reminded you of his
sufferings, and of the dispersion, and of the wandering, which they both now
have, and which they will have for the most part, for I am persuaded by the
predictions about them, then I will ask you who know exactly, what is the
cause of these calamities, =35.744= that we may be trained toward concord by
the evils of the others.

Q. Did they not, so long as they had peace both toward one another and
toward God, being pressed in Egypt the iron furnace, and being gathered by
the common affliction (for there is a time when even this is a good medicine
toward salvation, the affliction), hear themselves a holy people, and a
portion of the Lord, and a royal priesthood? And not so in the names, but
otherwise in the deeds; but they were also led by generals being led by God,
and they were guided by a pillar of fire and of cloud night and day; and the
sea was divided for them as they fled, and for the hungry heaven supplied
food, and a rock gushed for the thirsty, and for those at war a stretching
of hands was instead of myriads, raising trophies through prayer, and making
a way of the things in front; and rivers gave way, imitating the kindred
sea, and elements stood still, and walls were shaken down by trumpets. And
what need is there to speak of the plagues of the Egyptians granted to
these, and voices of God heard from a mountain, and a double legislation,
the one in letter, the other in spirit, and the other things with which
Israel of old was honored beyond their own worth. But when they began to be
sick, and raged against one another and were divided into many parts (the
cross driving them together toward the last destruction, and the madness
which they raged against the God and Savior of us, having not known the God
in man), and they drew upon themselves the iron rod long threatened to them
from afar, I mean the rule and kingdom now prevailing, what happens? and
what have they suffered?

R. Jeremiah laments them upon the former sufferings, and wails the captivity
to Babylon; for those too were truly worthy of laments and wailings; and how
not of the greatest, walls dug down, a city leveled, a sanctuary destroyed,
offerings plundered, profane feet and hands; the ones treading the
untrodden, the others luxuriating upon the untouchable, =35.745= prophets
silent, priests being led away, elders not pitied, virgins outraged, youth
falling, fire alien and hostile, rivers of blood instead of the holy fire
and blood, Nazirites dragged down, laments raised against the hymns; and
that I may say something from the Lamentations of Jeremiah themselves, the
sons of Sion, the precious and compared with gold, the delicate and
untouched by evils, going a strange road; and the ways of Sion mourning
because there are not those who keep feast; and a little before these: hands
of compassionate women, not stretching food to children, when the siege
prevailed, but tearing these for food, and making their dearest a medicine
of famine. How are these things not terrible and beyond terrible, not only
for those who suffered then, but also now for those who hear? I at least, as
often as I take up this book, and keep company with the Lamentations, and I
keep company as often as I wish to chasten prosperity by the reading, am cut
in the voice, and am poured together with tears, and as it were the
suffering comes before my sight, and I lament with the one who lamented. But
their last blow and migration, and the yoke of slavery now laid upon them,
and the celebrated humiliation under the Romans, of which nothing is so much
the cause as faction, who will lament according to the worth, of those who
know how to write laments, and to equal a word to a suffering? What books
will contain these things? One pillar of the calamity for them is the whole
inhabited world upon which they were sown, and the worship ceased, and the
very ground of Jerusalem scarcely known, of which so much only is accessible
to them, and so much they enjoy of their former glory, as, appearing in a
day, to lament the desolation.

S. And while it is so terrible to revolt, and cause of so many evils, as
both the things said show, and it is possible to learn through more
examples, still much more terrible is this: having dissolved smallness of
soul, and having tasted the goods of peace, to be carried down again to the
same disease, and to turn toward one's own vomit, as the saying is, not even
that which trains the mindless, having been chastened by experience. For I
see those thought light and mindless not those who remain in some evil, but
those easily carried upon both and flowing across, like breezes shifting, or
the changes and back-flows of Euripuses, or unstable waves of the sea.
=35.748= And I consider this too, that those remaining upon the faction, the
hope at least of concord makes easier, and lightens for them the most of the
calamity; for the greatest help to the unfortunate is hope of change, and
the better lying before the eyes; but those who have often been of one mind,
and always run back toward the vice, have been deprived along with the rest
even of the hope of the better; fearing always the concord no less than the
faction, and

T. And let no one think me to say that every peace is to be loved; for I
know, as there is a certain faction that is best, so also a most harmful
concord; but the fair one at least, and that which joins upon a fair thing
and upon God. And if one must distinguish briefly about them, I know thus,
that it is good to be neither more sluggish than the measure, nor hotter, so
as either through easiness to go along with all, or through disorder to
stand off from all; for likewise the sluggish is without deed, and the
easily-moved is without communion. But where the things of impiety are
clear, one must go together even with fire, and iron, and seasons, and
rulers, and all things first, rather than partake of the wicked leaven, and
consent with those who are in a bad state, and nothing of all things is so
to be feared, as to fear something else before God, and through this to
betray words about the faith and the truth, being slaves to truth; but where
what pains is suspicion and unexamined fear, long-suffering is better than
speed, and condescension than self-will, and it is much better and more
profitable, remaining in the common body, to restore one another as members
of one another, and to be restored, than, having condemned beforehand
through the standing-off, and having loosed the credibility by the
separation, then from command, as tyrants, but not as brothers, to legislate
the correction.

U. Knowing these things, brethren, let us embrace one another, let us clasp,
let us become genuinely one, let us imitate him who loosed the middle wall
of the fence, and through his blood gathered all things, and made peace; let
us say to this common Father, to the solemn grey hair, to the meek and
gentle shepherd: Do you see the wages of gentleness? Lift your eyes round
about, and see your children gathered, in the way you longed, and that which
alone you asked to be added to you night and day, that in a fair old age you
may dissolve the sojourning; behold all have come to you, and they rest
under your wings, and they circle their own altar, having gone away with
tears, and running to it with gladness. Rejoice and take delight, =35.749=
best of fathers and most child-loving, because you have put them all on and
wrapped yourself, as a bride an adornment. Speak you also toward us: Behold
I and the children whom God has given me. Add also another master's voice
and very fitting: Those whom you have given me I have kept, and I have not
lost one of them.

V. And let no one perish, but let us all remain in one spirit, with one soul
striving together for the faith of the Gospel, of one soul, thinking the one
thing, armed with the shield of the faith, having the loin girt in truth,
knowing one war only, that against the evil one, and those marshaled under
him, not fearing those able to kill the body, but not able to take hold of
the soul, but fearing the Lord of both soul and body; keeping the fair
deposit, which we have received from the Fathers, worshipping Father, and
Son, and Holy Spirit; knowing the Father in the Son, the Son in the Spirit,
into which we have been baptized, into which we have believed, with which we
have been ranged, dividing before joining, and joining before dividing,
neither the three as one (for they are not without hypostasis, or according
to one hypostasis, so that the wealth for us would be in names, but not in
things), and the three one. For one not in hypostasis, but in Godhead; a
