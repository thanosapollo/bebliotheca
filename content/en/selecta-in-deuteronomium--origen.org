ORIGEN'S SELECTIONS ON THE DEUTERONOMY.
=12.805=
Moses spoke to all the sons of Israel according to all things whatsoever the
Lord commanded him. Since God on the one hand was revealing the power, and the
prophet on the other was using his own tongue for the presentation of the
things made known. The prophetic voice therefore is called a movement, as
presenting the things spoken by God. And I said to you at that time. Moses
does not simply say, "I said to you;" but since after he withdrew Joshua the
son of Nave was about to succeed him, he says the matter is not out of place,
but even following. For having gone before, he says, I exhorted, and, as you
nodded assent, I set up judges; so that you also obey Joshua the son of Nave
who succeeds me according to prophetic and guiding grace. I shall not be able
to bear you alone. And remembering the former things, he does not begin from
Egypt; but from when he gave them wise judges and instructors. I gave to
Reuben and to Gad. The more inferior =12.808= come more quickly into the
promise; but the more decent receive later. For those under Moses first
received the inheritance; and those under Joshua later. But it is said also
concerning all the just, not ordinary ones; for as the Apostle says: "These
all, having been attested through faith, did not receive the promise, God
having provided something better concerning us, that they should not be
perfected apart from us." A voice of words which you heard, and what follows.
Not that the voice is visible, nor that God has a voice by nature; for this
would belong even to a composite nature; but since both the trumpets and the
voices, as God himself knows, were accomplished, being created from the
continuity of the elements that were seen; I mean of the fire and of the
gloom; so that a voice is both heard, and is not seen; yet in the miracles of
God the voice also is seen. And he swore that I should not cross this Jordan.
Moses, not having glorified God before the people, was prevented from passing
with them, lest the one they honored while living they should honor in the
worship of gods after he departed. Wherefore also when he had died He made the
tomb invisible. I call to witness against you today both the heaven and the
earth. God, not allowing those who worship him to be joined to demons, but
commanding those who have once known him to be outside the disposition toward
those, and making jealous in the apostasy of the peoples, foretells the
punishments that will come upon them because of the wickedness of their
practices. From a house of slavery. For truly also the bodily and material
things are a house of slavery, just as from the contrary there is a house of
freedom. For you are fewest among all the nations. It has been well said to
those of the portion: "You are fewest among all the nations." And kindred also
is, "Few are those who are saved," and, "Strive to enter through the narrow
gate." That man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word, and what
follows. And again that the manna was Word, even if it is indicated as bread
in the Exodus. "For this," he says, "is the bread which the Lord gave you to
eat. This is the word which the Lord commanded." And for this reason the manna
"was fine as white coriander, as frost upon the earth;" since the Word, of
which that manna was a symbol, is fine in its nature, and passing through all
intellectual, pure, most fine spirits. And wisdom also is an intellectual
spirit, holy, only-begotten, manifold, fine, and what follows. And it was also
as coriander toward being white. And they say that the nature of coriander has
throughout the principle of seed, so that not even in the cutting up of the
seed of coriander is its power destroyed, as no longer able to be sown. And
such throughout is the Word; and every portion of it is by nature to be
cultivated =12.809= in a good and noble earth, that is, of a soul. And the
manna is white. For the Word is light and bright, and according to this white,
to the one who understands it and is nourished by it. And it is also as frost;
for it is compacted, and nowhere flabby, nor dissolved, but firm, which indeed
is a word of heaven, and announcing to us the heavenly things. For the Lord
your God shall bring you into a good and large land. To us Christ promised not
to give these things, but a kingdom of the heavens, and a good and eternal
land, whose torrent is Christ, watering with the streams of wisdom. And the
torrents go forth through the plains and the humble and fertile men, and
through the high mountains, those stretching up through the height of
contemplation into the heavens. There is the wheat that strengthens the heart
of man, and barley feeding the more inferior. There is the true vine, Christ.
There is the oil that anoints the heads of the holy. Likewise also
pomegranates, bearing the sweet within, but having clothed themselves
outwardly with the austerity of the way of life. There is the fig tree, not
the fruitless one flourishing with leaves, but the one rich in the sweetness
of the Spirit. There is the desirable sweetness of the throat of the Savior,
and the lips of the bride in the Song dripping honeycomb, and the holy stones,
which having been rolled upon the earth are unyielding toward temptations, and
imitating the nature of iron. There are the mines of copper, from which they
constructed the intelligible panoply. And the sum of these, to bless God
without ceasing. Of the same. It is not, he says, to toil and be distressed
with clay and brickmaking, but to have stones, and these most stubborn, and
not easily dissolved, so as both to suffice for buildings and to last for
long. And he says these things wanting their mind to depart from Egypt. For by
saying "good" he showed the fertility; and by "large," the size and the
breadth. "Whose torrents of waters (this is not in Egypt), and springs of
abysses;" which is exceptional to the land of promise. And he added also
"through the mountains;" which does not belong to Egypt, nor does it flourish
with vines, nor has it abundance of oil; but the land of promise has all
things richly. Take heed to yourself, lest you forget the Lord your God. After
the promise of the good things he raises them to sobriety, to remember the
benefactor, and not to wax insolent with satiety, nor to despise the one who
granted these things, and that from the slavery in Egypt he transferred them
into freedom. You shall not do thus to the Lord your God. He accuses men
sacrificing their own children to the gods of the nations, as savage and
inhuman; as abominable. And those who say to sacrifice to gods of the nations
one must not only turn away from, but not even lend the ear to their words.
=12.812= You shall not add to it, nor take away from it. Men sinning, in a
general account, sin in two ways, either overstepping the commandments or
emptying them. For when we do not live from knowledge, nor knowing the
measures of righteousness we err, and sometimes overstepping the rule, with a
fancy of doing something better, we fall from what is set before us. Of the
same. Those therefore who do not do all things, or who do something more, are
lawless. Every beast that divides the hoof, and brings up the cud. The
dividing upon the hoof would show the walk according to which we come to any
of the good pursuits whatsoever; and the cud-chewing, the making the words of
the inspired Scripture go up and down, and as it were chewing them over and
refining by inquiries the hidden things of the contemplations. All things that
have fins and scales in them, you shall eat. Of the flying things I think as
many as have spurs below the knees above the feet he wants to be clean; and as
many as do not, unclean. You shall make for yourself a feast of tabernacles
seven days. We find in the Scriptures the name of the feast and of the
assembly, as in the prophet saying: "What will you do in the day of assembly
and in the day of the feast of the Lord?" The difference of feast and assembly
is to be sought. Every cheerful day, so to speak, in which it has been
commanded as on a holy day to do certain exceptional things besides the rest,
is called a feast; but not every feast is an assembly, but according to the
name, when the Hebrews from everywhere gather together to the same place, so
that according to this the assembly is a feast, but the feast is no longer
altogether an assembly; for example sabbaths and new moons are feasts; but
unleavened bread and the days of the weeks and tabernacles are not only the
feasts, but also assemblies, when every male has been commanded to appear
before the Lord your God in the place which he shall choose. You shall not
move the landmarks of your neighbor. Neighbor in many places is said of every
man. Of the same. For one must neither overreach those nearby, nor unsettle
the boundaries of the fathers as having been well fixed. For this will be a
cause of all confusion, the bordering being as if wronged, and those who set
the bounds being despised. And some say neighbor of every man. And if an
unjust witness stand up against a man. These things the law says toward the
weak Israelites; but the Lord who legislated these things says to his own
disciples: "You have heard that it was said, An eye for an eye," and what
follows. "But I say to you, not to resist the evil one." Of the same. These
things upon the sensible and the things according to the world, but upon the
things we have sinned, God needs nothing. For he himself is overseer of all
and judge. =12.813= Who is the man who is fearful. He who is given over to
earthly things and building a house, and planting vineyards, overcome by
flesh-loving pleasures, will be altogether both cowardly and without strength.
And if anyone have a disobedient son. He does not simply want them to be
destroyed when they embitter the parents; but when both parents agree against
the child. For if the father be angry, and rush to destroy the son, the mother
holds back; and if she herself be provoked, the father does not allow it; and
it will be rare for the parents to agree against their own child, God
commands, when both agree against the child, then to stone him, not the ones
who begot him, but the rest. You shall not sow your vineyard with diverse
seed. Lest the earth become weaker, so as neither to give back the seed full,
nor the fruit of the vine, but each by itself. You shall not plow with a calf
and an ass together. Do not yoke the high-spirited and labor-loving part of
the soul with the pleasure-loving and passionate. Of the same. And Isaiah
again writes: "Blessed are those who sow upon every water." What water, upon
which one must sow the good seed? This would be baptism of salvation, the
washing of regeneration. Sow upon the mystery of the washing, and you become
blessed. There an ox and an ass tread, an ox the clean one the Israelite, and
an ass unclean by race the one from the nations; that both animals may be a
symbol of the word that has been preached to the nations. Therefore an ox and
an ass tread, he says, this water where we wish to sow. And if there be a
virgin girl betrothed to a man. He calls the already betrothed a woman; thus
also it was said in the case of Joseph and the Theotokos. And even unto the
age. Because these too are from fornication from the daughters of Lot; and
they are not only until the tenth generation unacceptable to the synagogue of
Israel, but even unto the age. Thus he wants the enmity toward them to be
eternal; according to the meaning and the true cause of the divine Scripture,
because they hired Balaam to curse the Hebrews, and also their wives and their
daughters, as many as were fair of face and beautiful in form, they set
forward of the race, so as to give them for fornication to the Israelites, and
thereby to procure a most grievous fornication for the Hebrews; and this was
to depart from God, and to be initiated to Beelphegor; which also happened.
You shall not take a mill or an upper millstone as pledge. Because, he says,
it is impossible for a single stone to crush the wheat; for a mill is
constructed as a twin, male-female. Since therefore the Jews also have one
covenant; and likewise every heresy seeming to have this new one only, they do
not grind in this mill, that they may taste of the heavenly bread; for if
=12.816= he says, the wheat is not crushed and ground, they remain without
bread. Starting from this then I also clarify the law set forth, "You shall
not take a mill as pledge," that is, you shall not profane the old and you
shall not thrust it from your house; "nor an upper millstone;" instead of, the
new, "because this takes a soul as pledge," that is, by these the soul is fed
enjoying intelligible foods. They shall beat him with the number forty. We
have always observed the number forty to be afflicting; whence Moses the
great, having fasted forty days, and after him Elijah; but also our Savior
being tempted by the devil did not eat forty days and forty nights; and the
great flood upon the earth happens, God raining, forty days and forty nights.
You shall not lay iron upon them. It is to be inquired why he does not allow
iron to touch the stones that are useful for the building of the altar. But
also after these things he commands them to gather unhewn stones for the
building of the temple, and to build. For if it is out of place for iron to
touch the stone fitting for building, how then were the pillars, how then the
boards and their capitals worked? Or how were as many things as came from
bronze or gold or silver constructed without iron? And how is it possible?
Through these it is clear that he does not simply forbid iron to touch the
stones. What then is the cause? We speak not insisting. These things then. He
forbids laying hard upon hard and striking together. For great evils are wont
to be born from the mixing and collision of the hard things. Or he casts
murder as far as possible from the heart of men; and very justly. For if one
must not lay iron upon a stone being built into the construction of the altar,
or of the temple, how is it not much more to be forbidden to snatch iron, and
to murder against the neighbor? These shall stand to bless the people. It is
to be observed that those appointed to bless were born from the free women and
kinswomen of Jacob; so those set apart to curse from mount Gebal were from the
maidservants, except Reuben and Zebulun. And Reuben has been arranged with
those who curse, as also being cursed by the father. And if he also cursed
Levi, yet he himself has been redeemed by the priestly dignity. For it belongs
especially to a priest to bless the people. But also Simeon, as having dwelt
in the midst of the tribe of Judah, and having become as it were a part of it,
therefore where Judah is, there he too is numbered. And this is the blessing
with which Moses blessed. The predictions from Jacob were neither all
blessings, nor all curses; but those from Moses, all blessings. And he
hastened from mount Pharan. That is, =12.817= by no longer giving oracles to
Israel, but to those from the nations, and he established the law. For this,
"he hastened from mount Pharan," or else "he ceased," which another writing
contains, instead of, he will eagerly leave the shadow, and will manifest the
truth to us. With myriads of Kadesh. Now Kadesh does not mean the place, but
the holy ones. For Kadesh means the holy in the Hebrew tongue. And he spared
his people. That is, having come he saved those who were once not a people,
but now peoples of God, those not shown mercy, but now having received mercy.
"For in this," says the apostle Peter, "the love of God was manifested in us."
And holy John says in the catholic epistle, that "God has sent his
only-begotten Son into the world, that we might live through him." And blessed
Paul also says: "He who did not spare his own Son, but delivered him up for us
all." Therefore he spared the people. And all the sanctified under your hands.
For he holds all, as king of heaven and earth. Standing by as to a king,
ministering as to God, worshipping as to a maker, they are also under him;
"for he also subjected all things under his feet." Rulers of peoples having
been gathered together. The divine disciples, as David also says from their
person: "He subjected peoples to us, and nations under our feet." And again:
"Rulers of peoples were gathered together with the God of Abraham." Therefore
he indicates the calling of the nations, as God being about to rule at once
both Israel and the nations. And the Son is called "Beloved." "For this," he
says, "is my beloved Son." And because of this the Father rules us and reigns.
