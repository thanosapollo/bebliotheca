Of the same, that one must not go up to horse-races nor to theaters. And
having grieved them, then at the assembly on the Sunday after that having
yielded the speaking to a bishop arrived from Galatia, and having kept
silence, and in this too having grieved them, he spoke this homily in the
great church on the saying, My Father worketh hitherto, and I work.
αʹ. I laid hold of you very severely the other day, discoursing about theaters
and the horse-race; for this reason I rejoice and am glad according to the
apostolic voice that says: And who is he that maketh me glad, but he that is
made sorry by me? For I see much fruit sprouting from this grief. Since even
the medicines that correct the sores first sting the wounds, and then free them
from the putrefaction; so also the word, striking home to a hearer, makes a
beginning of correction. For just as in the case of our bodies, the flesh that
has once been deadened perceives neither cutting, nor medicines, nor cautery;
wherefore not even if ten thousand things should happen, can it return to
health, having lost the beginning and the ground of the healing; but that
which perceives both iron and fire and medicines would quickly return to being
healthy; so also in the case of souls, those that are disposed without feeling
would not easily be changed, but those that know both how to be ashamed and to
blush and to ache and to be in pain at the rebukes furnish us the greatest
proof that they will quickly stand off from wickedness. Wherefore also the
blessed Paul, knowing these things, received those who had been greatly
grieved, and rejoiced over them, but of those not so disposed he despaired,
saying: Who being past feeling have given themselves over to lasciviousness,
unto the working of all uncleanness in greediness. For he who does not know
how to ache, how would he ever be corrected, unless he should first learn this
very thing? Since then this has been accomplished for you, we are very
confident concerning your love; for if one discourse stung so, and put you
into such agony, so as even to be downcast and disturbed and troubled, it is
quite clear that a second and a third being added will free you from all the
sickness. And that you may learn that I do not say these things flattering
you, by what you displayed on the former day, by these you bore witness that
the things said by us are true. For you who were so stung, who were so pained,
who so ached and were downcast, when the Sunday after that came, you made the
theater more splendid for us, and the gathering more numerous and the
eagerness greater, and you were all in suspense, like swallow-chicks hanging
from the nest, and gaping toward our tongue. Then when we, honoring a brother
of ours who had arrived from Galatia, both on his account the law of the
church that commands so to receive strangers, and on account of the
illustrious gray hair, yielded the word to him, you were withdrawing with
outcry, taking it hard, as having endured famine for a long time, and longing
for ou =63.512= r tongue that cuts, that rebukes, that strikes home, that
causes pain; doing the same thing as if a child being struck, being rebuked,
would not even so stand off from the mother, but whimpering would follow,
holding the mother's garments from the side, and being dragged along with her
together with the wailings. For these things I leap and fly with pleasure, and
I say that I myself am blessed


contending among so many lovers, having you so hanging from our tongue. This
is sweeter to me than this ray, this more pleasant than light, this is life,
to meet with such well-disposed hearers, who wish not simply to clap, but to
be corrected, being rebuked, and not leaping away, but fleeing for refuge to
the one who rebukes. For this very reason I myself also with greater eagerness
take hold of the discourse toward you, and I wish today to pay back the
remainders of the things said to you the other day, now leaving aside the
rebuke, lest again by accusing the careless we should damage the zealous,
spending the whole discourse on the refutation of those. For even if nothing
had been said by us, what happened yesterday was sufficient even to make those
who are greatly mad and raging about the horse-race stand off from this
untimely desire. For the murder that happened yesterday in the hippodrome
filled our city with tragedy, set choruses of women round about, filled the
marketplace with many wailings, the one cut apart by the chariots being
carried so pitiably through the midst of the people. For this man, as I
learned, being about on the coming day to kindle the wedding torches, the
bridal chambers having been woven and all things for the marriage having been
made ready, being under the rank of the prefect, running below in the stadium,
when the charioteers came on and were contending against one another, being
caught in the middle, he endured this violent and pitiable death, his head
together with the extremities having been cut off. βʹ. Did you see the fruit
of the horse-race? And what is that to us who sit above, he says? To you
indeed most of all; for if you had not made it your eagerness to run together,
the things accomplished below would not have happened. But lest I should again
make the word more burdensome and scrape the sore, leaving these things to
your conscience, I shall try to pay back the remaining things of those said to
you the other day. What then were the things said the other day? I was saying
that Christ has been called a foundation as bearing all things and holding
them together and supporting them; this now I wish to prove from another
apostolic saying. For this one who said those things, interpreting that
expression, says: Who being the radiance of the glory and the impress of his
subsistence, and bearing all things by the word of his power, having made
purification through himself, sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on
high. What is, Bearing? Driving as a charioteer, steering, managing, holding
fast, holding toge =63.513= ther, supporting. For he did not only bring all
things from not-being into being, but also provides for them once they have
come to be, just as the Father who begot him. Wherefore also making this clear
he said: My Father worketh hitherto, and I work. Wherefore the Jews were
seeking the more to kill him, not only because he was loosing the sabbath, but
because he was also calling God his own Father, making himself equal with God.
Where now are the children of the heretics, who rave against their own
salvation, and split him off from the equality toward the Father? let them
hear the voice of the evangelist (and when I say the evangelist, I mean Christ
who moves his soul); let them hear, and be ashamed, and cease raving such
things. For the Jews were persecuting him for this, that he was saying himself
equal; and these hang themselves for this, that we offer him this praise,
having been taught by him. But this vote was not his, he says, nor the
evangelist's, but the Jews'. First of all, even if it were the Jews', this too
is your greatest charge and extreme accusation, that what the ungrateful Jews
understood as being constructed from the words, these things you yourself,
willingly deaf, do not see. For they were not introducing them from home, but
reasoning them from the things Christ was saying. And that you may learn that


this expression is not even of the Jews, but both vote and saying of the
evangelist (and when I say of the evangelist, I mean of Christ who moves his
mind), examine the words themselves. My Father. Is it not clear to everyone,
even to the very mindless, that he said My Father for this reason, that he
might declare him his own? And the adding again, He worketh, and I work, is
this not of one constructing equality? For he did not say, That one worketh,
but I minister; That one worketh, but I serve; but, That one worketh, and I
work. Do you see how what is said was not a judgment of Jews, but a
construction from his words? For if it were some deceived suspicion and vote
of Jews, the making himself equal with God, and he himself did not wish this
to be suspected, but the Jews, he not wishing it, suspected this, supposing
other things than what he himself wished to construct; the evangelist would
not have left it so uncorrected, but would have marked it and said it clearly.
For it is their custom to do this, both theirs and Jesus'; and this from one
and a second example I shall try to make clear to you, that when Christ says
something, and he himself says it in one way, but the Jews suspect it in
another way, the evangelist corrects. And that you may learn this clearly,
hear John himself making this very thing clear. For when he entered into the
temple of God, and cast out the money-changers having made a scourge, and was
saying: Make not the house of my Father a house of merchandise; the Scribes
and the Pharisees coming said: What sign do you show us, that you do these
things? What then does he? Destroy this temple, and I in three days will raise
it; speaking about his own body. But they did not understand it, but what do
they say? Forty and six years was this temple in building, and will you raise
it in three days? speaking about the Jewish temple constructed through stones;
for that one was built in forty and six years after the return from Babylon,
the =63.514= builders being hindered by the barbarian plot, and much time
being spent this temple was completed. Since then Jesus was speaking about his
own body, making clear the cross and the resurrection; for this is the,
Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it; but they were
suspecting about the Jewish temple; whence also they were saying: Forty and
six years was this temple in building, and will you raise it in three days?
see how the evangelist did not pass it by, but added the correction, saying:
But he was speaking about the temple of his body. For he did not say, Destroy
this body, but, The temple, that he might show the God who dwells inhab.?
destroy the temple much better than the Jewish. For the one had the law, the
other the lawgiver; the one the letter that kills, the other the spirit that
gives life; the one the rod of Aaron, the other the rod of Jesse. γʹ. Again
elsewhere, having done the miracle upon the loaves, having departed with them,
he was crossing to the other side, and was saying to the disciples: Take heed
of the leaven of the Pharisees and Sadducees. But they, hearing leaven,
suspected about loaves; but he was not speaking about loaves, but about the
teaching of the Pharisees. For since he himself said it one way, but they
suspected it another way, see how he corrects it, saying: Are you so still
without understanding? do you not remember the five loaves and how many
baskets you took, and the seven loaves and how many hampers you took? how do
you not perceive that I did not speak to you about loaves, but about the
teaching of the Pharisees, so as to take heed? Do you see how both in the case
of the temple the evangelist and here he himself corrects the suspicion of
those who were deceived? So then also in the case of making himself equal with
God, if he himself was not making himself equal with God, but the Jews this


were suspecting from the things said, the evangelist would have corrected this
suspicion, and would have said that the Jews thought he was making himself
equal with God, but he himself was not doing this nor constructing it. But
that you may learn from the words themselves, that this especially he wished
to fix in the minds of men, let us bring the word higher up and learn what in
the world, being accused, he was answering with these things. What then in the
world was that of which he was accused? That he was doing works on the
sabbath; For this, he says, the Jews were persecuting him, because he was
doing these things on the sabbath. What then were these? That he made fast the
paralytic, and commanded him to take the bed and go away into the house.
Wherefore also questioning him they were saying who it might be who had
commanded him to do these things on the sabbath; and he said, He who made me
whole, that one said to me: Take up your bed and go into your house. Being
displeased at these things, they were persecuting him, because he commanded
these things on the sabbath, which seemed a transgression and loosing of the
law. What then does Christ say in defense? My Father worketh hitherto, and I
work. And yet many other things were possible to say, if indeed he wished only
to make a defense, but not to show the equality. For the sabbath was often
loosed, and first in the case of the city of the men of Jericho. For when they
assaulted the walls, for seven days they were commanded, handling trumpets, to
go around the walls, and thus was brought down the =63.515= city. And that in
the seven days, from wherever we may make the beginning, the sabbath must
fall, is clear to everyone I suppose. Whence it is clear that the sabbath was
loosed. Again he was commanding a man to be circumcised on the eighth day;
here again of necessity the sabbath is loosed; for the one born on a sabbath
must of necessity be circumcised on the other sabbath. And the priests were
loosing it even with greater abundance, being commanded again to offer
sacrifice on the sabbath; and where there is sacrifice, there was necessity
also to flay and to burn, and to lay upon the altar, and to carry water, and
to break wood, and to carry out ash, and to do many other things, and through
this the sabbath to be loosed. Together with these the creation itself also
was loosing the sabbath; for the sun works on the sabbath, and the moon runs,
and the varied choir of the stars shines, and winds blow, and springs gush,
and rivers flow, and the sea is moved, and seeds and plants are put forth, and
the earth gives birth and all the irrational creatures, and still also the
race of men. For never, when a woman was in labor on the sabbath, did nature
wait for the law, and wait for the sabbath to pass, and then for the
birth-pangs to be loosed, but it was working its own work even when it was
sabbath. And the powers above also serve on the sabbath, and they do not leave
off fulfilling their own ministry. For what reason then, tell me, having so
many starting-points of defense, does he use none of these, nor say, Why do
you accuse me, that I loose the sabbath? for the priests in Jericho loosed it,
and those in the temple loose it; why do you accuse me, that I loose sabbath?
for the sun looses it and moon and stars and all the creation, both that above
and that below; but leaving all these, he came to the Father, saying: My
Father worketh, and I work. For he did not wish only to make a defense, but
also to show the equal honor. For this reason he did not say, I work, for the
creation also works; for he was not of the creation; but, I work, for the
Father also works. For he was of that substance and of that authority. δʹ. For
this reason he does not make a defense as being of the creation, but as a
genuine offspring of that one. And that you may learn that the things said are
not conjecture, the disciples also once loosed the sabbath, plucking ears and
eating, and the Jews accusing and saying: Do you not see what these are doing
on the sabbath? nowhere there did he mention the Father, but what does he say?
Have you not read what David did, when he hungered? how he entered, and ate
the loaves of the presentation, which it was not lawful for him to eat and for
those with him? have you not read that the priests in the


temple profane the sabbath, and are guiltless? Do you see how, when he makes
the wo =63.516= rd on behalf of the servants, he brings the servants into the
midst, David and the priests; but when on behalf of himself, the Father? And
if somewhere he has also used other defenses, as when he says: If a man
receives circumcision, are you angry with me, because I made a whole man
healthy on the sabbath? and again: Who is there of you, who does not loose his
sheep and the ox? it is nothing marvelous; for he does not speak all things as
God, but there are places where he also discourses as man, since he was both
God and man. Here however he speaks toward his own dignity, saying: My Father
worketh hitherto and I work. For this the evangelist says, The Jews were
persecuting him, not only because he was loosing the sabbath, but because he
was also calling God his own Father, making himself equal with God. And I
would gladly ask the heretic that also: Was he calling God his own Father, or
was he not calling? was this of the Jewish suspicion, or of the declaration of
Christ? for even if he should be shameless ten thousand times, he is convicted
by the truth. He was saying, that My Father worketh hitherto, and I work; was
he loosing the sabbath or was he not loosing? It is quite clear that he was
loosing; I work. he says, and that one also works. Therefore neither of these
was of the Jewish suspicion, but of the construction and declaration of
Christ, both the loosing of the sabbath, and the calling God his own Father.
How then, saying that these are of the declaration of Christ, do you say that
that is of the suspicion of the Jews, lying next in order and joined to this,
and reported in like manner by the evangelist? For all these things are a
report of the evangelist, interpreting the things said by Christ. Wherefore
also when the Jews were accusing he was saying, that they were persecuting
him, not only because he was loosing the sabbath, but because he was also
calling God his own Father, making himself equal with God. But the refutation
against the heretics has a sufficient end; and if you wish also to learn what
in the world the working is which the Father works, and which the Son, I would
say the providence of the things that are, the holding fast, the care. For all
the things that appear came to be in six days; And God rested on the seventh
day; but the providence of them did not cease. This providence then Christ
calls working, saying: My Father worketh, and I work, providing, caring,
holding fast, he says, holding together, letting nothing flow apart. Having
received therefore the proof of the right doctrines also from the things said,
let us add an exact way of life also agreeing with the doctrines; since
knowledge of the right doctrines alone is not sufficient for us toward
salvation, but there is need also of a most excellent way of life, that having
sent up glory to God through all things we may attain the promised good
things; to whom be the glory and the power unto the ages of ages. Amen.
