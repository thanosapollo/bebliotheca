OF THE SAME SAINT CYRIL A DIALOGUE WITH NESTORIUS THAT THE HOLY VIRGIN
IS THEOTOKOS AND NOT CHRISTOTOKOS.
=76.249=
{NESTORIUS.} When the divine Scripture discusses the birth of Christ
from Mary the virgin [or] death, it nowhere appears having set God, but
Christ or Lord or Jesus, since these three are significative of the two
natures, sometimes of this one, sometimes of that; as I say for example:
When the Apostle announces to us the birth of Christ from the Virgin
[cod. announcing], he says: God sent forth his own Son, come to be from
a woman; but he did not say, He sent forth God the Word; but he takes
the name that announces the two generations, that is God [cod. God] and
man, since Christ is double; for the Virgin bore a Son of God, inasmuch
as it said: You are gods and all sons of the Most High; whence we have
learned from Scripture to say Christotokos, Kyriotokos, anthropotokos,
but we have in no way been taught to call the holy Virgin Theotokos.
{CYRIL.} Isaiah cries in the Spirit saying: Behold the virgin shall have
in the womb, and shall bear a son, and they shall call his name
Emmanuel, which is =76.252= interpreted, God with us; therefore the one
born is God, whether you wish it or not. {NEST.} The angel said to
Joseph: Rise, take the child and his mother, and flee into Egypt, and be
there until I tell you; but he did not say, Take God and his mother.
{CYR.} But the archangel Gabriel says to the Virgin: The Holy Spirit
will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you;
wherefore also the holy one being born of you shall be called son of the
Most High. {NEST.} The Apostle said: He emptied himself, taking the form
of a servant; which is putting on our body, wherein he advanced in
wisdom and stature and grace with God and men, as a lordly man. {CYR.}
Everything that has come to be from the flesh is flesh; but it does not
take away the paradox of the birth; for as God begets in a godlike
manner, so also the God-befitting Virgin bore in flesh the God Word from
God. {NEST.} Every mother bears what is consubstantial with her; she is
therefore not his mother, unless he himself is consubstantial with her;
for how is she mother of him who is alien to her substance [cod. the
alien existing]? {CYR.} We have been taught to confess one Son from the
Virgin and from the Holy Spirit, consubstantial with the mother as with
the Father, as the Fathers have said. {NEST.} I divide the natures, I
unite the worship, since God is inseparable from the one who appears;
therefore I do not separate the honor. {CYR.} He who divides the
natures says two sons, not believing the Scripture that says, The Word
became flesh. {NEST.} Paul says that Jesus became high priest and
apostle of our confession, being faithful to him who made him, he
offered himself a sacrifice for himself as for the people; whence he is
appointed high priest in the things toward God. {CYR.} Paul says:
Christ, having been offered once to bear the sins of many, will appear a
second time without sin. And again: He did no sin, and deceit was not in
his mouth; and behold he offers himself for us; see then he did not
offer himself up for himself, but for the people. {NEST.} He himself
saying: He who eats my flesh, and drinks my blood, will live through me,
just as I live through the Father who sent me. =76.253= Whom then do you
eat, heretic? the Godhead, or the manhood? For Christ did not say, He
who eats my Godhead, but, The flesh. {CYR.} I for my part partake in
faith of the life-giving flesh of the life-giving Word; whence the
Savior said: Those who eat of this bread shall not taste death unto the
age; but those who partake unworthily will be judged through their
disobedience. {NEST.} The Lord has sent me and his Spirit; The Spirit of
the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me; and therefore he says:
God, your God, has anointed you with the oil of gladness beyond your
fellows; understand then the one anointed, confess the one who anoints,
and worship the temple because of the one dwelling in it.
{CYR.} I confess from the Holy Spirit and Mary the Virgin one Son from
two; I do not divide the one into two. {NEST.} How do you say that he is
ignorant of the hour, which the Son does not know, except the Father
alone? Or how, knowing, does he deny [cod. begins]? Or if not knowing,
how was he called wisdom and power of God, he who shares in ignorance
(cod. the place sharing in ignorance)? {CYR.} The Son of God is not
ignorant of the hour, knowing all things and having the Father in
himself, as he himself said: No one knows the Father except the Son; he
alone knows the nature of the Father which is before all, and does the
Son of God not know the end of the age? God forbid; but the son
according to grace is ignorant, not however the one according to nature;
according to grace we are, being ignorant of the whole, and knowing
nothing concerning the ends of the age. {NEST.} We hunger and thirst and
sleep by nature, not suffering this by choice, but being subject to
necessity of nature; what then? Shall God be found following compulsory
laws? God forbid. {CYR.} We indeed hunger and thirst and sleep by
nature, not by choice, but serving necessity of nature; but he not by
necessity, but by choice; for from five loaves he fed five thousand, and
from seven again four thousand; he himself said: No one takes my soul
from me, but when I will I take it; and no man can do this, except
Christ alone.
{NEST.} Do you say that the Son suffered? Then the Father also suffered;
but if you say that the Father did not suffer, you have made the Son of
a different substance from him. {CYR.} I neither say the Father is
passible, nor the Son impassible; but the Divine is impassible, because
it is also bodiless; but the Lord is passible because of the flesh.
{NEST.} I do not say that the Word became flesh, nor that he suffered;
for the Divine is impassible, and the impassible is =76.256= above
sufferings; but flesh is passible and human nature mortal. {CYR.} We
also confess that the Divine is impassible, but through the flesh he
accepted suffering; whence, Christ having suffered in flesh for us; but
Christ is not a name alien to God; for the apostle Paul says: From whom
is the Christ, who is God over all, blessed unto the ages. Amen.
