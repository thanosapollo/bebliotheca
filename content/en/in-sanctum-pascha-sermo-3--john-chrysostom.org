* On Holy Pascha, Sermon 3
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

1 The sacrifice of the Pascha, together with the unblemished victim,
showed the holiness of Christ; the salvation of the firstborn, given
through the sacrifice of the Pascha, showed the salvation of men in the
passion of Christ; and the eating showed the sanctification. These
things have already been said to us. Now from the remaining parts of the
legislation we must examine how the one who is to be sanctified must
prepare himself, how he must approach communion in holiness, and what
sort of life he must lead after he has become a partaker and sharer of
the Holy One.
2 The preparation, then, which the one about to partake of Christ must
make, the Lawgiver showed us through circumcision, speaking typically:
"This is the law of the Pascha. No foreigner shall eat of it; and every
household servant of anyone, and one bought with silver, you shall
circumcise 3 him, and then he shall eat of it." Circumcision then was
partial, and did not of itself display the benefit; for a man was shown
to be no fairer from circumcision than the uncircumcised. There was even
a reproach from God against the Israelites, that "All the nations," he
says, "are uncircumcised in flesh, but the house of Israel is
uncircumcised in their hearts." The true circumcision is of the whole
flesh, and it shows the one circumcised in heart, stripped of the
fleshly covering, as friend and household member to God. This covering
we have by succession from our fathers, and we need it stripped away.
The law prefigured this for us by circumcising the generative members,
making through this the symbols of generation and of the covering 4
that follows us according to birth.
No one, then, who is not circumcised as to the fleshly manner of life
will come to his proper communion with Christ. But "we are the
circumcision," he says, we who share in Christ, "who worship by the
Spirit of God and do not put confidence in flesh." For the heart,
stripped of everything fleshly, can worship God in truth and be mixed
with Christ in spirit.
5 This circumcision is begun by the passion of the Lord, who put off the
flesh for us and took circumcision itself upon himself for our sake.
The mystery is stamped upon us in baptism and is completed in the life
according to Christ. "You were circumcised," he says, "with a
circumcision not made by hands, in the putting off of the body of the
flesh, in the circumcision of Christ, having been buried with him 6 in
baptism." So long, then, as you do not put off the fleshly character,
you are a foreigner and a stranger; you have no share and no communion
with the Holy One, Christ who is present from heaven. For the one who
will approach the heavenly must become heavenly, and no one can become
heavenly unless he has stripped away the earthly.
7 So then, having first put off the old man in the grace of baptism and
in the eagerness of spiritual deeds, you will approach and partake of
Christ. Second, we must see of what sort the law sends forth all who
partake of the Pascha, so that we may also know of what sort we must be
when we partake of Christ. "Thus," he says, "you shall eat it: your
loins girded, and the sandals on your feet, and the 8 staffs in your
hands." The figure is that of a traveler, compact and well-girt, ready
to run toward the promises of God, just as Israel then was sent as a
traveler toward the promise of the good land. But consider for me how
great a superiority the spiritual traveler has over that one: he does
not hasten toward a promise of land below, he does not chase after
temporary goods, he does not exchange land for land, Judea for Egypt.
What does such a traveler say about his own journey? "Forgetting what is
behind, and stretching out to what is ahead, I press on toward the goal
for the prize of the upward calling."
9 A loin, then, girded with "truth," according to the apostle. For so
you will be well-girt for the good race, if you take all present things
as vanity and falsehood, according to David, and seek the truth that is
to come, and keep the longing for it.
10 And feet shod with "the readiness," he says, "of the gospel of
peace," prepared for every good race, through which we confirm for
ourselves the peace toward God, as the apostle says: "We have peace with
God through our Lord Jesus Christ"; and "Through endurance we run," he
says, "the contest set before us"; and "So I run, that I may lay hold."
11 And "staffs" for them "in their hands": <faith and> the hope that is
toward God, a support of the soul, not letting it be given over to
afflictions, as Paul has said, but supporting it firmly so that it is
not shaken.
12 "You shall eat" it "in haste; it is the Lord's Pascha": not a lazy
food, nor one for pleasure; not of a relaxed and reclining mind, but of
those whose mind is raised and taut, because it grants us a passing-over
of the destroying punishment, the Lord bestowing this. Therefore he
says, "It is the Lord's Pascha," which another, interpreting it
outright, has said: "It is a passing-over for the Lord."
13 After such a partaking of the divine food, let us see, third, of what
sort the life of the one who has shared in holy food must also be. The
14 unleavened breads and the days of unleavened bread show this. Let the
Jews, then, be eager about the unleavened breads and think that the
seven days grant them something marvelous, because they do not eat
leavened bread nor have it among themselves, though they can show no
benefit from the thing; for they fulfill a type, not the truth. But you
consider the unleavened, as was said beforehand by the apostle and as we
have noted: the soul sincere and clean from all malice, the new manner
of life that brings nothing of the old wickedness. Toward this also the
saying appears well spoken: "You shall make leaven disappear from your
houses. Anyone who eats leavened bread, that soul shall be cut off from
Israel." For a return to old wickedness is destruction for the one who
has been transferred to newness. "If you live according to the flesh,"
he says, "you are about to die."
15 And what is the symbol of the seven days? "Therefore," he says,
"seven days you shall eat unleavened bread." The whole of time happens
to be seven days; for after the seventh, which is the sabbath, a return
of the first comes about, and so the cycle of time proceeds through the
seven days, 16 turning back upon itself. You ought, then, to live the
whole of time "in simplicity and sincerity," and this is truly saving.
For "if by the Spirit," he says, "you put to death the deeds of the
body, you will live."
17 Simple is the manner of the one who does not seek the things of the
flesh: without malice, without cunning, thinking nothing evil, nothing
bitter. Why does he call the first day and the seventh especially holy,
saying, "The first day shall be called holy, and the seventh day shall
be a holy convocation for you"? Because the first day shows the
beginning, in which we are sanctified by receiving the Holy Spirit; and
the seventh signifies the end, when, having departed from the body, we
come to be with Christ and dwell with the Lord, leaving behind the
sojourn on earth, and wholly transferred toward holiness. For "being at
home in the body," he says, "we are away from the Lord."
18 The law wants these holy days to be free from works. For "you shall
do no work at all," he says, "on them," which shows that the soul is not
to be bent down about earthly businesses, nor to endure a slavery
unfitting to the service of God, but to make those things alone its
earnest work, and to strain the soul about those things that are for its
benefit. And a symbol of this again is: "Except what shall be done for
every soul, this alone shall be done for you." Therefore the Lord,
bringing the legal rest to its interpretation, that he was granting
idleness from works that strain toward vanity, not from those that come
to the benefit of the soul, put forward to the Pharisees: "Is it lawful
on the sabbath to do good or to do evil, to save a soul or to destroy
it?"
19 If you wish to keep the sabbath well and according to Christ, and the
festal rest, stand away from worldly pursuits and dealings, and take
thought for and do the things of God and those that are for the benefit
of the soul. And so you will keep a sabbath truly holy, and you will
keep a holy feast, an image of the one with Christ, to whom be the glory
unto the ages of ages. Amen.
