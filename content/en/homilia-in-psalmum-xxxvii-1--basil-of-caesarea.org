ON THE 37TH PSALM.
A teaching of confession.
A psalm of David, for remembrance.
The words of the God-inspired Scripture will stand at the judgment-seat of
Christ. For I will reprove, he says, and I will set your sins before your
face. So then let us also attend soberly to the things said according to the
Scripture, and let us be urgent to hasten to bring into work the =30.84=
commandments of the Lord, because we do not know at what hour or day our Lord
comes. O Lord, rebuke me not in your wrath, nor chasten me in your anger. With
these words and syllables the sixth psalm also begins similarly to this, and
the rest of the sixth happens to be akin to the things carried in the one
before us. For he said in the sixth that his bones had been humbled and his
soul had grown weary in his groaning, and he added, saying: I will wash my bed
every night; I will water my couch with my tears, and the things joined to
these. And those in the one before us would be close to those, through which
it has been said: There is no healing in my flesh, and there is no peace in my
bones from the face of my sins; for my iniquities have gone over my head, and
the things brought on through the whole psalm after these, in which he bewails
his own wretchedness. For he himself has used this very word, saying: I have
been wretched and I have been bowed down utterly to the end; all the day I
went mourning. But in the sixth the superscription had: Unto the end, in
hymns, for the eighth, a psalm of David; but in the present there is none of
these, only: The psalm of David, for remembrance, has been said. The addition,
at least, for remembrance, seems to send us back to the superscription in the
sixth; so that the present things are: Unto the end, and in hymns for the
eighth. For for remembrance would be indicative of all these. But since, going
through the things that fell in the sixth, we have spoken toward the
superscription, let us also for the present go off to the remembrance of those
things. And it seems to me that David, having confessed in various and many
places concerning the transgression done by him, has set apart the present
psalm privately for remembrance, so as always and continually to bear it on
his mouth; and to use it in place of a charm, for the healing of his own soul.
He sends up, at any rate, a supplication to God, through which he turns away
the wrath that hangs over all who sin, and propitiates the good Lord with
voices of exceeding confession. And saying, O Lord, rebuke me not in your
wrath, he does not decline the rebuke, but that which is with wrath; and the,
Nor chasten me in your anger, he does not flee being chastened, but he asks to
obtain the chastening apart =30.85= from anger; as if someone should say to a
physician, who brings helps through cautery and iron and bitter antidotes for
the healing of the underlying passion: Do not heal me through fire, nor
through iron and cuttings, but through gentle and milder drugs; for such a one
does not refuse the healing, but the toil of the helps. And it has often been
said that wrath and anger of God, being named in the God-inspired Scriptures,
do not signify passions; for the divine is alien to every passion; but the
word of Scripture is accustomed to name such things by metaphor, as also eyes
of God, and ears, and hands, and fingers, and feet, and the remaining members;
which things are economically arranged as useful toward men, condescending to
the quality of the hearers. So then also the punishments brought upon those
who sin according to the judgment of God,


being gloomy and painful to those who suffer them, it sketches as if brought
on from wrath and anger. And this the holy Apostle also teaches, saying: But
according to your hardness and impenitent heart you store up for yourself
wrath in a day of wrath, and of revelation and of the righteous judgment of
God, who will render to each according to his works. As if someone should also
name the punishments hung by the law over the murderer wrath and anger, the
law not truly being angry and wrathful, but the one being punished having
taken the vengeance as wrath of the law. And already also the wicked demons
who minister the punishments against the ungodly, and the ministering powers
of God, have been named such wrath and anger. So at any rate it has been said
in the word concerning the ten plagues brought against the Egyptians: He sent
upon them wrath and anger, a sending through wicked angels. So the right hand
of God has been named, the things supplied to the worthy through the
right-hand and good powers. David therefore beseeches not to be rebuked
through wicked powers, nor to be chastened through a sending of wicked angels,
but through saving words and beneficial lessons. And otherwise he entreats not
to be kept for a day of wrath, and of revelation, and of the righteous
judgment of God; but already from here and according to the present life,
before the end, to receive back the sins. And he did obtain the aim; he
appears, at any rate, to have paid the penalty, through the things he
suffered, before the end. And otherwise he also punished himself, subjecting
himself to all kinds of punishments in the manners of confession. For your
arrows have been fastened in me, and you have pressed your hand upon me. There
is no healing in my flesh from the face of your wrath. There is no peace in my
bones from the face of my sins. The great athlete Job, having endured all
kinds of trials, was not ignorant whence this came to be for him; therefore he
said: For the arrows of the Lord =30.88= are in my body, whose wrath drinks up
my blood. But David, not struck in the body like Job, but wounded mortally in
the soul itself, does not say like Job: For the arrows of the Lord are in my
body; but, For your arrows have been fastened in me, and you have pressed your
hand upon me. And Job makes mention of the hand of the Lord, saying: The hand
of the Lord is that which has touched me. And the devil, saying to the Lord,
Send forth your hand, and touch all that he has, was speaking. And again: Send
forth your hand, and touch his flesh and his bones. But it was some other
hand, and other arrows, that reached his flesh and his bodily possessions. But
here, since David had fallen sick in the soul itself, he signifies that other
arrows reached him, and another hand touched him. And the arrows spoken of
here seem to me to be rational, or rather the very words of God, pricking and
wounding his soul, and punishing and chastising the conscience itself; since
being so great a man, and having obtained so many goods from God, he gave
himself over to a shameful deed. And perhaps he also signifies the devil
himself, and the fiery darts of the evil one, the passionate and erotic, that
kindled him into desire of the wife of Uriah; concerning which the Apostle
also teaches, saying: Take up the panoply of God, that you may be able to
quench all the fiery darts of the evil one; for since he was found unarmed,
and did not hold out against the fiery darts of the evil one, reasonably,
wounded in the soul, he was taken by the desire. And just as Job named the
arrows of the devil, the sores laid upon him against the body, since it came
to be by concession of God, arrows of the Lord, saying: For the arrows of the
Lord are in my body, whose wrath drinks up my blood; so it is likely that
David also has here said arrows of the Lord, since by concession of the Lord


the adversary made war upon him, that he might learn not to say: I shall not
be moved forever. For since, being strengthened by the grace of God, he once
thought great things of himself, so as to be bold and to say: But I said in my
prosperity: I shall not be moved forever; for this reason he was reasonably
delivered to the tempter, who, having laid hold of his soul, harmed him more
than Job. And the words, reminding of his righteous judgment, and teaching of
the wrath hung over those who sin, =30.89= are sharper than arrows, pricking
the conscience, both wounding and piercing it. And so you would say that
David, being pierced by these holy and divine things, reasonably declines the
rebuke through wrath, and the chastening through the anger of God. Do not
rebuke me in your wrath, he says, O Lord, nor chasten me in your anger. Why?
For your arrows have been fastened in me. For the arrows that reach me from
you, having taken on the wrath and the anger, punish me sufficiently, and
chastise me adequately. Therefore he beseeches not to be tried with another
wrath nor with anger, For your arrows have been fastened in me, or, they reach
me, according to Symmachus. And you have pressed your hand upon me. And
understand the hand of the Lord that touched him as also that declared in the
case of Job. And this hand of the Lord shook the whole house of David from the
foundations, driving his firstborn of the children, Amnon, mad with love of
his sister, and kindling Absalom with wrath against his brother, so as to kill
Amnon, then also to rise up against the father himself; and all the remaining
things presented through the history, the hand that has been declared. Your
hand touched me through the successive calamities. I beseech, he says, not to
be rebuked with another wrath, nor to be chastened with a worse anger. And not
only these things, but also the things said next, which he teaches saying in
what follows: There is no healing in my flesh from the face of your wrath;
there is no peace in my bones from the face of my sins. I, he says,
anticipating, and not even waiting for someone from without to chastise my
body, punished myself with an evil sore, and I delivered my flesh, through
which the sin was done for me, to punishments, punishing myself and chastising
with all kinds of chastisements. For this he also made clear through other
things, saying: I humbled my soul with fasting, and my flesh was changed
through oil, and I forgot to eat my bread; from the voice of my groaning my
bones cleaved to my flesh. And, I will wash my bed every night; I will water
my couch with my tears. So then also now he says: There is no healing in my
flesh from the face of your wrath. And instead of wrath, both Aquila and
Symmachus in agreement have rendered, from the face of your indignation. For
the indignation from your divine Scriptures was sufficient for me, he says,
and the threat through your prophet Nathan. Therefore from the face of your
indignation I punished myself thus, so as to chastise my own flesh, and that
my bones should not have peace From the face of my sins. In place of which
Symmachus, Because of my =30.92= sins. For my iniquities have gone over my
head; as a heavy burden they have been heavy upon me. My bruises have stunk
and have festered, from the face of my folly. I have been wretched, and I have
been bowed down utterly to the end. Since such things have been done by me, he
says, for this reason I have been wretched, and I have been bowed down to the
end, and all the day I went mourning. And through these he presents what he
has suffered, and how he punished himself, not accusing himself of one sin,
but of many at once, and so many, that they were not even hidden within in his
own soul, nor escaped notice inside the body, but had gone over him, so that
they were known even to all. He was, at any rate, accuser


of himself in the first speech, and among all, so that it might be fulfilled
upon him: A just man is an accuser of himself, anticipating every human
accuser. He himself condemned himself, and stopped every mouth of enemies, who
had nothing else to say against him. And the word teaches us not to hide our
own evils, nor, like some blackness and rottenness, to hold the sins down in
the depth of the soul, cauterizing the conscience. As with fevers, the fever
going within and down in the depth works a greater disease, but having
advanced into the open it shows a hope of abatement; so also it would be in
the case of the soul. And David also teaches something else that has come to
be about himself, saying: As a heavy burden they have been heavy upon me. For
the things dared by him weighed upon his conscience, so that he was not able
to bear them, through nobility and good sense of soul. For the stubborn and
hardened man, according to his own hardness and impenitent heart, stores up
for himself wrath in a day of wrath, drawing sin upon sin, increasing his own
evils; For when a sinner comes into a depth of evils, he despises; but the
well-disposed man, even if he should once slip through some snatching of the
devil, is terribly weighed in the conscience, so that he cannot be silent, nor
hide his own evil. From the abundance of the heart, at any rate, the mouth
speaks. Whence, not bearing to be silent, he disclosed the things that held
him, saying: As a heavy burden they have been heavy upon me; and it is clear,
that my iniquities. And again: My bruises have stunk from the face of my
folly. And he names folly the mindless deed that came to be from folly. For
every sin comes to be according to folly; but prudence being a virtue, shows
all the things done according to itself worthy of praise and virtue. But also
the things that come to be according to prudence are productive of health for
the soul; but folly works wounds and bruises. Those then =30.93= who persist
in the sins, rejoicing in them and taking pleasure, are like swine rolling in
mire; but he who has slipped once, then recovering himself, abhors that
defiled deed as foul-smelling and unclean. Having suffered some such thing,
David, by a sound perception, confesses, saying: My bruises have stunk and
have festered from the face of my folly. Since then my iniquities were lifted
over, having been multiplied, and having become higher than the head were
heavy upon me, and have stunk and have festered; for this reason, healing
myself, I have been wretched, and I have been bowed down to the end; and not
even for one day, nor for a brief hour, but All the day I went mourning, or,
according to Symmachus, I walked gloomy; not being indifferent about the
things done by me, nor lifted up as a king, because I was subject to no judge;
but through your fear and your indignation, all the day I went mourning. For
my loins were filled with mockings, and there is no healing in my flesh. I was
afflicted and I was humbled exceedingly; I roared from the groaning of my
heart. Lord, all my desire is before you, and my groaning was not hidden from
you. Instead of, my loins were filled with mockings, Aquila has given, for the
flanks were filled with dishonor, and Symmachus, for my loins were filled with
dishonor. And for this reason, having come to remembrance of the shameful and
dishonorable deed, because the sense-organ of the soul was sound for him, he
said these things: My bruises have stunk and have festered from the face of my
folly. And why they have stunk, he adds, saying: For my loins were filled with
mockings, or, my flanks with dishonor; and through these he hinted at the form
of the shameful deed. Then recovering himself he said: There is no healing in
my flesh.


For since my loins were filled with mockings, for this reason, he says, having
come to repentance of the things done, I chastise and punish my own flesh;
therefore I say: There is no healing in my flesh; so that he might at some
time, having set it right, be able to say: Even if our outer man is corrupted,
yet the inner is renewed. And above he also said: There is no healing in my
flesh, and there is no peace in my bones. Then he adds: I was afflicted and I
was humbled exceedingly. And this he made clear otherwise in the things
before, having said: I have been wretched, and I have been bowed down to the
end. It is proper to prudent men, not to be lifted up over the things sinned
according to prepossession and snatching, but to be ashamed and to blush, and
to be struck in the conscience, and to be humbled. For David besides these
says next: =30.96= I roared from the groaning of my heart, or, according to
Aquila, I gnashed from the gnashing of my heart. For I do not confess with the
lips in order to become manifest to the many; but within, in the heart itself,
closing the eye, I display my own groanings to you alone who see the things in
secret, roaring in myself. For I had no need of long words toward the
confession; for the groanings of my heart were sufficient toward confession,
and the lamentations sent up from the depth of the soul toward you the God.
But also the desire, being good, was before you, O Lord, and this was that
concerning my own salvation. For when I became a desirer of evils, my desire
was not before you; but now, when I do all things being humbled and roaring
from the groaning of my heart, and desiring to obtain you the God as savior
and physician, my desire is before you, and you are not ignorant of the cause
of my groanings. For you know that I send up a groaning fitting to your
loving-kindness; since you do not will the death of the sinner, as his
repentance. Therefore I say: And my groaning was not hidden from you. My heart
was troubled, my strength forsook me, and the light of my eyes, it also is not
with me. My friends and my neighbors drew near over against me and stood. And
those nearest to me stood from afar; and those seeking my soul used violence;
and those seeking evils for me spoke vanities, and they meditated deceits all
the day. Having indicated beforehand in the superscription, that it would be
for remembrance, he reasonably goes back over, and takes up again in memory
the things that happened to him of old. And he teaches in what things he came
to be, having fallen in the slip of the sin; for which cause he also makes the
whole confession. I have been wretched then, and I have been bowed down, and I
went mourning, and I was humbled, and I roared from the groaning of my heart.
I have come into all these things; for since at a certain time My heart was
troubled, and my strength forsook me, and the light of my eyes, it also is not
with me. For at the time of the sin, his thinking part endured no small
trouble, being confused and darkened by the one working the sin, so as even to
fall into folly. Therefore he also said above: My bruises have stunk and have
festered from the face of my folly. His heart then was troubled, when he had
come to be in folly, and he fell out of the virtue according to prudence, and
did works of folly. And then also his strength forsook him; for he was no
longer able to say: I can do all =30.97= things in Christ who strengthens me,
having been conquered by the passion and having grown faint. For in the case
of those who succeed: The spirit is willing, but the flesh is weak; but in the
case of those who are conquered the flesh is lifted up and prevails, and the
soul grows faint and weakens. Then therefore, he says, when I was sinning, my
heart was troubled, and my strength forsook me, and some other grievous thing
came to be about me. For the light of my eyes (and this was the prophetic
Spirit, that


enlightens the sight of his soul), it also was not with me, having left me and
withdrawn, Since wisdom will not enter into a soul that devises evil, nor
dwell in a body involved in sin. For a holy Spirit of discipline will flee
deceit, and will remove from senseless reasonings. The Holy Spirit then having
withdrawn from him, left him deserted after his heart was troubled, and his
strength grew faint. For then also the light of his eyes left him deserted.
But also those formerly his friends, and his neighbors, who were with him the
whole time, having left him, Stood from afar, no longer wishing to be with
him, bewailing from far off, as it seems, a friend's calamity. And who were
they, but those who rejoiced at his former goods, good angels clearly and
ministers of God, accustomed to rejoice together at the salvation of men? if
at least, over a sinner repenting, joy comes to be in the heavens; And an
angel of the Lord will encamp round about those who fear him. And all these
friends of David, then, at the time of his sin, withdrew turning away; yet
they did not abandon him entirely, but standing far off they groaned; which
very thing he also teaches, saying: My friends and my neighbors drew near over
against me, and stood. And those nearest to me stood from afar. And when these
had become far from me, certain others, those working the sin, having become
near me, used violence, and mocked my soul. Therefore he adds, saying: Those
seeking my soul used violence; and those seeking evils for me spoke vanities,
and they meditated deceits all the day. For of old these, thirsting for my
blood, and from long times praying to behold my fall, as if seizing a time,
when they saw my strength leaving me, and the light of my eyes withdrawing
from me, and my friends and my neighbors becoming far from me; immediately
leaping upon me they used violence against my soul, and as being willfully
evil, They spoke vanities, suggesting within to my soul vain words; but also
charming and deceiving with deceit, they baited me as upon goods, dragging me
down into the sin. Therefore he adds: And they meditated deceits all the day.
=30.100= But I as a deaf man heard not, and as a dumb man not opening his
mouth; and I became as a man not hearing, and not having reproofs in his
mouth. Since those seeking my soul found it, then having found it they mocked,
and they came on scoffing at my evils; then they spoke vanities and deceits
all the day. Upon these I, having become deserted of my own friends, had
nothing to say, filled with shame; therefore as a deaf man I heard not; but I
who of old was most eloquent, and made wise with the present wisdom, became as
a deaf man not hearing, and as a dumb man not opening his mouth. For I had no
word toward a defense; nor had I anything to address to the enemies, once
having been conquered by them; but I was As a man not hearing, and not having
reproofs in his mouth. For I myself was being rebuked, but I did not rebuke,
nor had I boldness of speech, covered with shame. For in you, O Lord, I hoped;
you will hear, O Lord my God. For I said, lest ever my enemies rejoice over
me; and in the moving of my feet, they spoke great things against me. These
then are the things that have happened to me. Therefore I became as a deaf man
not hearing, and as a dumb man not opening his mouth, and as a man not having
reproofs; therefore I said nothing toward the enemies, as having been
conquered by them. Yet I did not for this despair of myself; but I gathered
myself again, having leapt away from the sin as from death, healing my own
wound with the drugs of confession. And I ask that the bruises that stank upon
me from my folly obtain


healing. Therefore In you, O Lord, I hoped; for not trusting myself, nor
setting any other hope before my eyes than you, I sketched for myself the
things of the healing. Since then I hoped, O Lord, and hope does not put to
shame, therefore hear, because you are a willer of mercy, and you do not will
the death of the sinner, as his repentance. Because I hoped upon you, I
beseech to obtain the help from you, Lest ever my enemies rejoice over me. For
they have not yet rejoiced utterly; but they will rejoice over me, if I should
not obtain your mercy; but that my enemies may not rejoice over me, who
already even before, in the moving of my feet, spoke great things, I fled to
you, and I say: May I not be put to shame forever, nor let my enemies laugh me
to scorn. And again I say: To you, O Lord, I will cry, be not silent from me,
lest ever you be silent from me, and I become like those who go down into a
pit. And the remaining things said by me variously in the other =30.101=
psalms, gathering them in the supplication toward you, I ask to obtain the
present help. For even if it especially happened that my feet were moved, at
the time when my heart was troubled, and my strength forsook me, and the light
of my eyes was not with me, yet I was not wholly overturned, nor did I fall
such a fall as those who became apostates. Since in that very time, in the
moving of my feet, my enemies spoke great things against me, that they may not
applaud against me still more, I fled to you as to a harbor of salvation, who
are able to heal even the passions of the soul that are incurable among men.
For I am ready for scourges, and my pain is before me continually. For I will
declare my iniquity and I will be anxious for my sin. Having said at the
beginning, Do not rebuke me in your wrath, at the present he says: For I am
ready for scourges. He therefore declines the wrath and the anger, but says
that he is ready to be scourged; for he knows that Whom the Lord loves he
chastens, and he scourges every son whom he receives. Therefore he also bore
nobly the scourges that came to him through the successive calamities after
the sin, which the history of the Kingdoms contains. And my pain is before me
continually. For the many make a forgetfulness of the former sins, being
indifferent about them, and despising, as if no vengeance will come to be; but
he who has the court of God before his eyes continually, and is persuaded that
we shall all stand at the judgment-seat of Christ, that each may receive the
things through the body toward what he has done, whether good or worthless;
has come to be in agony always, and taking thought to heal this pain in his
own soul, he accuses himself through the confessions toward God. Therefore he
says: My pain is before me continually, for I will declare my iniquity, and I
will be anxious for my sin. And such voices belong to a faithful soul and one
accurately persuaded concerning the judgment of God. But my enemies live, and
have been strengthened above me; and those who hate me unjustly have been
multiplied. Those who render me evils for goods slandered me, since I pursued
goodness. Do not forsake me, O Lord my God, do not depart from me. Give heed
to my help, O Lord of my salvation. He makes mention of all his enemies, who
dragged him down into the sin, and he beseeches and exhorts that the Lord not
depart from him. For he saw the enemies still even now standing about him
=30.104= and pressing upon him, not even being content with the former daring
against him. Therefore, in agony lest he also fall a second time into the same
things, he says very watchfully: But my enemies live. For they have not died,
nor having worked once have they passed by; therefore it has been written: Do
not count a man blessed before his end. They live; but they have also been
strengthened, being more than I.


For those who hate me have been multiplied, and those who render me evils for
goods; who even now have not yet ceased slandering me, since I pursue the good
and saving way, being envious of my salvation. For this reason I beseech and
ask, do not forsake me, O Lord; for alone toward so many I would not be able
to hold out. You then, my God, do not depart from me, but be present to me as
helper, and guardian of my salvation.
