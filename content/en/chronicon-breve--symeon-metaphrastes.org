BOOK 7. ON THE MAKING OF THE WORLD, OF SYMEON LOGOTHETE FROM VARIOUS
CHRONICLES AND HISTORIES. 1. Concerning the light. [1. Concerning the light.]
God without time, bringing this world that did not previously subsist into
being in time, made in the beginning heaven and earth; and together with the
earth there came to be both water and fire; and a clear proof of this is that
from stone and iron, being from earth, abundant fire leaps forth (and from
springs and wells rich water is given up); for when this [earth] had come to
subsist from things that were not, clearly also the things in it. All things
therefore came to be according to the counsel of God. And since heaven,
stretched round after the manner of a tent, darkened the place left within,
God said: Let there be light; and at once by the word this all-beautiful
nature of light was brought forth; and it scattered the darkness that had come
about from the interception of the heavenly body and suddenly showed the air,
which was without radiance from such a cause, most radiant. And that
everything that was before the constitution of this world was in light is
clear; for neither the choirs of the angels nor all the heavenly hosts lived
in darkness, but in light and all spiritual gladness they fulfill the liturgy
that befits them, upon which they too were brought from non-being into being.
This light then having been shut up above from the said cause, God again
created that which now shines upon and makes bright the place between heaven
and earth. These things came to be on the first day of the making of the
world. On this day then Moses says in the Little Genesis that the heavenly
powers were created. 903 [2] Concerning the firmament. Again on the 2nd day,
the abyss being poured around the earth boundlessly and infinitely, he
commanded a firmament to come to be from the waters in the midst of them,
which he also afterward named heaven; and with the command the work was
completed; and the created thing was called firmament because of the nature of
the waters lying above or also below, being fine and rare, but heaven because
it is seen after the manner of a hide stretched out over the head. 1264 [3]
Concerning the sea. And again on the 3rd day he commanded the waters left
below the firmament to come together into one gathering, which he also named
sea, so that the dry land might appear; and thus certain deep hollows having
come to be received it; dry [land, the earth, was] called, since this name
also belongs to it by nature; for [what is properly dry] is the earth; and
since as some weight it laid aside the [waters stored in it, then] it was
commanded to sprout herb of grass and se[ed according to kind and
fruit-bearing tr]ee; and at once having been stirred [toward fruit-bearing,
the countless kinds of sprouts] (205b) and of plants it put forth and appeared
fair and graceful together; for it was made bright not only from the
shooting-up of the standing crops, but was also sufficiently adorned with
cedars and cypresses together with the rest of the multitude of trees that
rose of themselves from it. [4] Concerning the luminaries. Again on the 4th
day he said luminaries should come to be; and there came to be both sun and
moon and stars, as he who commanded willed; 904 and that most pure light of
the 1st creation, in which having measured measures in these, he furnished
from them a many-parted benefit to the whole, one from shining and richly
illuminating the world with their own radiances, another from rising and
setting, being the cause of the numbering of the times, another that through
certain signs benefits in various ways those who conjecture piously and do not
over-inquire beyond measure,


such as are also the things often signified through the moon; for being fine
in its rays about the day and clear, it announces settled fair weather; but
appearing thick in its horns and somewhat red, it indicates that either
violent water from clouds or a storm will be; likewise also the sun becoming
coal-like and bloodshot in its rays or in its gleam shows that a violent
movement will come on; many therefore from such a sign have been benefited,
both sailors and farmers and travelers. [5] Concerning the fishes and the
birds. And on the 5th day he commanded the waters to bring forth creeping
things of living souls and birds flying along the firmament of heaven; and
with the command the work was completed, the fitness for generating life
having come to be in the waters. For at once the sea brought forth the
manifold kinds of swimming things, and rivers and lakes were filled with their
own and natural fishes. And the waters again also brought forth the birds,
having a certain manner and kinship toward the swimming things. For just as
the fishes in the waters go forward by the motion of the fins, and by the
frequent turning of the tail they steer their straight rushes, so also the
birds swimming through the air cut it, and pass wherever they wish. 1265 905
[6.] Concerning the cattle and the wild beasts. Again on the 6th day the earth
was permitted to bring forth a living soul according to kind, four-footed
things and creeping things and wild beasts; and at once it brought forth both
the wild and the tame animals. And he commanded it to bring forth a living
soul, so that from this the difference of the irrational soul and of the human
might be known; for the soul of the irrational things grew from earth, but
that of men was constituted from the divine inbreathing; and that the soul of
the irrational things is earthy, one may know again from Scripture itself; for
it has been said: The soul of every beast is its blood; and blood congealing
is changed into flesh; and flesh de[caying] is resolved into earth. Reasonably
then the soul of the irrational is earthy. [7] Concerning the fashioning of
man. And since [then] all things were already adorned with the [fitting]
beauty, heaven with the [rays] of the luminaries, [sea] ... and air with the
swimming and aerial of the animals, and earth with the [manifold differences]
of plants and of cattle, as some king [the maker of the all brings] man into
the world, not as a cast-off last (206a having created him, but as one who
ought to reign together with his coming to be. For what does Scripture say?
And God said: Let us make man according to our image and according to
likeness, and let them rule the fishes of the sea and the birds of heaven and
the wild beasts and all the earth. And all the other creatures were brought
forth by a word only; but man had something exceptional in the making compared
with these. For he was created after counsel had gone before, so that from
this it might be shown that he is some honorable thing; for the: Let us make
man according to our image and according to likeness, shows nothing other than
that the Father used as counselor his only-begotten Son in the fashioning of
this one, the 906 radiance of the glory, according to the Apostle, and the
impress of his hypostasis. For it was not possible to have used any other
counselor than his living and enhypostatic Word, the unchanging according to
image and consubstantial and equal in power. (22 works, as Moses says, God
created in the 6 days; wherefore also 22 letters and so many books among the
Hebrews and 22 genealogies from Adam until Jacob.) [8] On the, According to
image. Since then, as has been said, man was brought in as a king in the
world, reasonably then he has also come to be according to the image of God;
for it was necessary that he who was going to rule the others be as some
living image of the king's likeness, not showing the dignity in purple and
scepter and diadem, since neither is the archetype in these, but in being
adorned with incorruption and immortality and virtue; for having been honored
with these, man preserves the likeness toward the 1268 prototype image; and
further also self-determination shows most clearly the kingly and exalted of
the nature of man and the resemblance toward the


divine image. And according to this account man is also a work of the hands of
God because of the honor of the fashioning; for the whole is an energy of
counsel. Then after his formation what does Scripture say? And God finished on
the 6th day his works which he made, and he rested on the 7th day from all his
works, and he blessed the 7th day and sanctified it, because on it he rested
from his works which God began to make. [9] Concerning the Sabbath. For what
reason did God bless the 7th day alone, and not all of them? Because each of
the others had the blessing from the creation that came to be on it; on the
7th, no creation having come to be on it, 907 for this reason it too was
blessed by God. [10] Concerning paradise. Again then Scripture says: And the
Lord God planted a paradise in Eden toward the east, and he placed there the
man whom he formed. And God caused to spring up still from the earth every
tree beautiful to the sight and good for food, and the tree of life in the
midst of the paradise, and the tree of knowing the knowledge of good and evil.
God, having proposed to honor the rational hypostasis, also prepares a
splendid dwelling full of all delight, and he sets Adam there. And having come
to this point of the account, one may wonder at the unspeakable love of
mankind of God, that having all-ruling and eternal authority over the all, he
also gave himself to farming because of man. For he who by a word established
such a magnitude of heaven and earth and sea was far more able to plant a
paradise by a word and to root the all-blessed place by a word; but, as has
been said, (206b) because of the honor toward man, he who without toil brought
this world from non-being into being by will alone also becomes a planter by a
certain mystical account. [11] Concerning the naming of the animals. Then
again Scripture: And God brought all the wild beasts and the cattle and the
creeping things to Adam, to see what he would call them; and whatever Adam
called them, this was its name. One may see then from this also the honor of
man after these things, which he had from the very birth. For Adam sits forth
as some leader of the herd; and God was bringing to him the countless kinds of
wild beasts and of cattle and of birds, that he might appoint as leader this
one whom he had made according to his own image. And he, 1269 908 having been
inspired with a divine and prophetic spirit, set proper names on each of the
irrational things; and the animals, as having received some great honor from
the naming, thus withdrew in pairs. [12] Concerning the making of the woman.
And since from all these kinds there was nothing like Adam, God said again, as
Scripture says: It is not good for the man to be alone; let us make for him a
helper according to him, calling the female a helper because of the account of
childbearing, being about to cooperate with the man toward the increase and
continuance of the race. And again he adds: And God cast an ecstasy upon Adam,
and he slept, and he took one of his ribs, and filled up flesh in its place,
and he built the rib, which he took from Adam, into a woman. And the said aim
of Scripture is this: God who made all things in wisdom, having judged to give
Adam a helper according to him, as he promised, does not form the female from
earth, as also the man, but turns him to sleep, having made his mind to
imagine by a descent of spirit, and he calls forth the eye of the soul to an
insatiable sight, so that being held by the imagination of such beauty he may
not know what is coming to be; then he takes one of his ribs without toil, not
bringing upon him a sensation of pain; and at once as a craftsman and a swift
physician he filled up what was lacking through flesh. For while he was
drawing the soul aside with the insatiable divine pleasure, he built the rib,
which he took, into a woman, mystically forming her into a rational living
being, adorned with unattainable beauty and a most seemly form, and he brings
this to Adam. Then he, as being a partaker of a divine spirit


having known that she was from him, says: This now is bone of my bones,
flesh of my flesh; she shall be called woman, because she was taken from her
man. Adam then, seeing the woman, was not 909 affected as beholding a woman,
but as seeing himself, he rejoiced spiritually, seeing her similar and having
this same fashioning. And likewise also the woman, beholding the man, greatly
exulted, considering herself to be according to him. They being then in the
paradise of delight, God gives them a law for a benefit, both to show the
nature free in self-determination, and to indicate that they are under a
master and guardian, saying thus: From every tree that is in the paradise you
shall eat for food, but from the tree of knowing good and evil you shall not
eat from it; and on whatever day you eat from it, you shall die by death, that
is, you shall be mortal. For thus also Symmachus interpreted: On whatever day
he eats from the tree, you shall be mortal. 1272 (207a) [13] Concerning the
tree of knowing good and evil. But let no one suppose that the tree was of
such a nature as to furnish knowledge of both good and evil; for there was not
anything harmful in paradise; but a boundary was set upon the plant for an
exercise of freedom. And that the tree did not become a cause of knowledge for
Adam is clear; for the commandment was given as to one who knew both, both the
good and the evil, both the obedience and the disobedience, so that the
knowledge preceded the commandment. And it was given because, as has been
said, man being moved by a self-determining will, and having leave to partake
of all the other trees, but being commanded by a law to abstain from one only,
might know that he is under a master and the one who has given the
commandment. They were then living in this most pure place, both Adam and Eve,
and richly enjoying the variety of the immortal flowers. 910 [14] Concerning
the devil and the serpent. But the enemy of our nature, having envied the
great honor of man and having conceived envy, raised the war against him, and
having put on the serpent he stood by the woman, Adam being absent, and having
cunningly turned her aside from what was profitable through a lie and having
deceived Adam together with her, he prepared them to have become transgressors
of the divine law. For Scripture says concerning this thus: And the serpent
was most prudent beyond all the wild beasts of the earth, which the Lord God
made. And the serpent was called prudent, because he was about to deceive the
rational living being, man, who also differs from the others in shrewdness.
Then it adds: And the serpent said to the woman: Why is it that God said: You
shall not eat from every tree that is in the paradise? And the woman said to
the serpent: From every tree that is in the paradise we shall eat; but from
the fruit of the tree that is in the midst of the paradise, God said: You
shall not eat from it, nor shall you touch it, lest you die. And did the
serpent then also use uttered speech? Not at all, but the first-formed, being
unmixed with evil, had most accurate senses and much differing from ours, so
that their hearing was able to hear every voice; and it says that the wild
beasts and the cattle once spoke with one tongue, but when Adam was condemned,
then they too were muzzled, and the serpent also had the feet taken away and
creeps upon the earth, and venom was put upon his tongue. Then it adds: And
the serpent said to the woman: You shall not die by death, for God knew that
on whatever day you eat from it, your eyes will be opened, and you will be as
gods, knowing good and evil. And the 1273 woman saw that the tree was good for
food and that it was pleasing to the eyes to see and that it is beautiful to
contemplate; and having taken of its fruit she ate and gave also to her
husband, and they ate, and the eyes of the two were opened, and they knew that
they were naked. 911 [15] On the, The eyes of the two were opened. Not as of
the first-formed then looking up has it been said, The eyes of the two were
opened; for they are shown even before the eating seeing


from its being said: And the woman saw that the tree was good for food and
that it is pleasing to the eyes to see, but as having put off inexperience of
evil through the transgression, (206b) which also rendered the nakedness a
matter of indifference; and it was said that their eyes were opened. For every
eye is by nature stirred by the reasoning toward the things it sees; for it
has no understanding by itself, so that often, the mind being occupied with
other things, we pass by even what is very familiar after the manner of a
blind man; and when we are blamed, assigning the cause to the occupation of
the reasoning, we make defense. Thus then Adam and Eve were stirred by the
perception of the mind toward the sight; for the, They knew that they were
naked, signifies a revelation that came to be in the clothing, not a
looking-up of eyes. And that too will be said, that earlier, in
passionlessness, since the things of childbearing had no time, the observation
of nakedness had not been given by God, but they lived as fleshless, not yet
having a time of fleshly disposition; but when the disobedience having come on
brought in mortality, reasonably thereafter shame toward one another was
granted to them; and they understood later the difference of the forms that
had been foreseen by the fashioner for the necessity of the succession of the
race. [16.] Concerning the leaves of the fig. Adam and Eve then, perceiving
themselves to be naked after the transgression, made for themselves girdles
from leaves of a fig, hinting through the tree at the manner of the
evil-doing. For as the fruit of the fig is sweet, but the 912 leaf is rough
and most bitter, so every sin in the doing is shown sweet, but after the deed
furnishes pain to the one who has done it. What then again does Scripture say?
And they heard the voice of the Lord God walking in the paradise in the
afternoon, and they hid themselves, both Adam and his wife, in the midst of
the tree of the paradise, after the daring having come to awareness of the
fault, they are held by the judicial fear and they refuse the unerring eye and
decline the coming forward and shrink from the standing forth. And God, having
seen them thus shaken by dread, calls them back as an affectionate father and
says: Adam, Adam, where are you? And he said to him: I heard your voice
walking in the paradise, and I was afraid, because I am naked, and I hid
myself. 1276 [17] Concerning the condemnation of the first-formed and of the
serpent. It was then to be seen thereafter a tribunal and a sharp and fearful
court, the luxurious place of paradise having become such. For having brought
into the midst those who were harmed and the one who harmed, and having found
the serpent to have done injustice, and Eve to have been treated unjustly, he
defined for each the fitting punishment. For having declared the serpent
accursed, he condemned it to be dragged upon the breast and the belly and to
eat earth instead of edible food, and he judged Eve to have grief as a
companion and to bear in pains, and he condemned Adam to eat bread in the
sweat of his face until he return to the earth from which he was taken. Then
again Scripture: And God made for Adam and his wife tunics of skin and clothed
them, and God said: Behold, Adam has become as one of us to know (207b) good
and evil; and now lest he stretch out his hand and take of the tree of life,
and eat, and live unto the age. For what reason is man cast out of paradise
and concerning the tunics 913 of skin. It is then to be understood from what
has been said that man was able even after the transgression to live unto
ages; wherefore, so that evil might not be immortal, God declared man mortal,
wrapping him in deadness; for this the tunics of skin signify, from the
account of the deadness of the animals, so that through the loosing of the
body and the binding all sin might be destroyed from the very root. [18] On
the: Behold, Adam has become as one of us. And it has been said by God:
Behold, Adam has become as one of us, to know good and evil, not as of him
having advanced through the disobedience to the knowledge of virtue and of
vice, but as of one having been formed such from the beginning.


For Scripture said earlier that God made man, according to the image of God he
made him. And he who was formed according to the image of God clearly had
already laid down in his nature the knowledge of the good and of the evil;
wherefore, so that he might not use self-determination and take of the tree of
life and become an immortal evil one, he was cast out with Eve into the earth
of his creation. For what after all these things does the divine Scripture
say? And the Lord God sent him out of the paradise of delight to work the
earth from which he was taken; and he cast out Adam and settled him over
against the paradise of delight and set the Cherubim and the flaming sword
that turns, to guard the way of the tree of life. 914 [19] Concerning the

the tree of life, so that no one thereafter should be able to pass that place

draw near. And the earth bore all things of itself before the transgression of
