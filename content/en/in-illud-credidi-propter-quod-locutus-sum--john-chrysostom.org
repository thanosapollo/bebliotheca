* On that saying, I believed, therefore I spoke
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of our father among the saints John Chrysostom: On I believed, therefore I
spoke.
They say that the bee, flying with light wings upon trees and flower-bearing
plants, and harmlessly plundering the moisture that lies in the forms of the
flowers, from there furnishes to men the sweetest honeycomb of honey. Such are
the teachers of the Church; for flying with the light wing of the mind upon
the fair-green spring of the Scriptures, and drawing the moisture of the
Spirit that lies in the letter, they gently sprinkle the sweetest honey of the
faith into the hearts of those who hear. Come then, beloved, let us also,
imitating the bee and warming the mind with the rays of the sun of
righteousness, run down with warm spirit upon the most flowery meadow of the
psalmody. For what was the psalm just now chanted saying to us? I believed,
therefore I spoke. And what force does the saying have? It is necessary that
we, having drawn the honey-dripping thought that lies in the depth of the
letter, mix for all you who thirst, according to the strength we have, the
drinkable stream of the word.
I believed, therefore I spoke. Just as those who wish to narrate some matter
to others first come themselves to full assurance, and thus begin to teach the
matter in detail, so also the prophet, being about to teach us, first comes
himself to full assurance concerning the existence of God and the hope stored
up for men in Christ; he says: I believed, therefore I spoke. For it was not
352 safe for him, unless he had first believed and been fully assured
concerning the things he was about to narrate, to begin teaching; therefore he
necessarily prefixes the words: I believed, therefore I spoke. And what did I
believe? I believed that God is Father of Christ, not a creator and maker, but
a begetter; and having been fully assured of this I believed, therefore I
spoke; nor did I curiously pry into the nature of God, of what sort and how
great it is, but I believed that Christ is Son of God, begotten of the Father
before the ages inexpressibly, and having been fully assured of this I
believed, therefore I spoke. But this psalm, even if the Jews have it, they
cannot sing in truth; for they did not believe that God is Father of Christ,
nor did they know that Christ is Son of God; for if they had known, they would
not have crucified the Lord of glory. This psalm, even if the Eunomians and
Arians have it, yet they cannot sing it in truth; for they did not believe
that God is begetter of Christ, but creator and maker, nor again did they
believe that Christ is Son of God, saying that he is a creature and a work;
and rejecting Christ they reject the Father.
Hear Scripture saying: If anyone does not have the Son, this one does not have
the Father either. I believed, therefore I spoke. This psalm, even if the
Macedonians have it, they cannot sing in truth; for they did not believe that
the Holy Spirit who proceeds from the Father inexpressibly is God; and not
having the Holy Spirit, they do not have Christ either. Hear Paul saying: If
anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ, this one is not his. Everyone
therefore who believes rightly in Father and Son and Holy Spirit says this: I
believed, therefore I spoke. 353 Let us see also the next verse, what it means
to say; after I believed, therefore I spoke: But I was greatly humbled. But he
who does not understand thinks the verse is inconsistent; but if each of us
understands, as David also understood, he speaks concerning God and concerning
himself: But I was greatly humbled. Having seen with the eyes of the heart the
inexpressible and unutterable majesty of God, that as much as heaven differs
from an ant, so much does God differ from man himself, and having understood
this and
been struck with amazement he says: But I was greatly humbled. Abraham also,
having seen this, that in comparison with God he himself is earth and ashes,
said: But I am earth and ashes.
For the proud, not knowing God, do not even know themselves, what they are;
for how shall one who has not first recognized the Creator recognize himself,
that he is a formed thing? And that from not knowing the Lord pride arises,
let Scripture bear witness to me, saying: The beginning of pride is not to
know the Lord; for from him who made him his heart departed. David was not
such; but knowing that God will not despise a contrite and humbled heart, he
humbled himself, that he might be exalted; for everyone who exalts himself
shall be humbled, and he who humbles himself shall be exalted.
So great a good is humility, that our Lord himself humbled himself, and
wishing to lead us to the likeness of his own humility he says: Learn from me,
that I am meek and humble in heart. I believed, therefore I spoke, but I was
greatly humbled. I said in my 354 ecstasy: Every man is a liar. And how do we
hear Job called a true man? For God bears witness to him, saying: Have you
considered my servant Job, how there is none like him on all the earth? A man
blameless, righteous, true, abstaining from every evil deed? If then Job is
written as true and is witnessed by God, how does David, having pronounced
concerning all, say: But I said in my ecstasy: Every man is a liar? To this we
shall say, that as far as in comparison with other men Job was true, so far as
in comparison with God even he was a liar.
For no one is clean from filth, not even if his life be one day. And if no one
is without sin, it is clear that neither is there anyone without falsehood,
except God alone. Likewise we also hear that Barnabas is good; as far as in
comparison with other men we know him also as good, but as far as in
comparison with God this one too is not good; for no one is good except God
alone. And just as in the evening hour one calls the star bright as far as in
comparison with the other stars, but when the sun has appeared no one would
call the star bright, because the brilliance of the star is covered by the
surpassing radiance of the sun, so also we say that a man is true and good as
far as in comparison with the others, but as far as in comparison with God
there is no true man; for every man is a liar. But come, beloved, let us turn
the discourse, that we may find another grace of thoughts shining in it. But I
said in my ecstasy: Every man is a liar. Blessed David, in childhood 355 a
lover of letters, in manhood a lover of toil, in old age a philosopher,
blessed David, being a friend of God and wishing to behold God with the eyes
of the heart, of what sort he is, having gone outside fleshly desires and with
the mind run past the air and peered over the clouds and leapt over heaven and
flown past the angelic powers and entered into the very immaterial and most
pure form of God, and having seen that radiance and the lightning of the
Godhead, which is incomparable majesty, and having understood that no man nor
any human tongue is sufficient to narrate the greatness of God, having come
into such an ecstasy and having learned it exactly, he confidently pronounces,
saying: But I said in my ecstasy: Every man is a liar. But come, let us turn
the pearl of the word again and illuminate our mind by the radiance of the
Spirit that lies within.
I know, beloved, even an experienced farmer who from one field in a year takes
three harvests; let us then also imitate the farmers, with the mattock of the
Spirit let us hoe according to the prophet who says: At night I communed with
my heart and my spirit hoed. First then we considered a verse of the letter,
we took the second, let us still consider the third: But I said, he says, in
my ecstasy: Every man is a liar. Consider with me every man assigning to
himself many years of life, wishing to build and plant and rear his children
and to petition against those who wrong him and promising to avenge himself on
his enemies, to imprison, to bind, to banish, or even to do something else
more terrible, and not long after dying that very night or after two or three
days, and you will see how every man is a liar; for what he promised, he did
not do. But every 356 man who loves this life is a liar, while he who pursues
this life in virtues is not a liar; for such a one is not a man, but God, not
by his own nature, but by the grace of him who dwells in him, such as Paul and
Peter were, who had Christ speaking in them; for these were not liars, but
true; for they were not men either, for they had a divine spirit speaking in
them.
These did not care about money, nor about possessions; for how would they have
cared about these, when he said to them: Do not acquire gold nor silver nor
copper in your belts? Nor did they threaten to avenge themselves on enemies;
for they did not even have enemies. For they kept the commandment of the Lord
who says: Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you. These did
not assign to themselves many years of life; for each of them said: I die
daily, by your boasting. Hear the apostle saying: As deceivers and yet true,
as dying and behold we live. For as far as in appearance, we are as deceivers;
for we appear as men; but as far as in the preaching of the heart and of the
way of life, we are true. And dying, and behold we live. For as far as in our
purpose, we die each day, but as far as by the faith of the Lord, we live.
Then after this: But I said in my ecstasy: Every man is a liar, he adds: What
shall I render to the Lord for all that he has rendered to me? The prophet,
having seen the gift of the Lord given from 357 the beginning, and being at a
loss as to the repayment of the goods granted him by God, says: What shall I
render to the Lord for all that he has rendered to me? He stretched out heaven
for me, arranging it with light-bearing stars; he fixed the sun for me,
weaving it about with gold-like rays; he founded the moon for me, a
night-traveler lighting the dim things of the night, charioteer of sailors and
of wayfarers; he spread out the earth for me, crowding it with all kinds of
trees, fruit-bearing and barren, adorning it with flower-bearing plants; he
shut up the multitudes of waters that bring forth fish into one gathering; he
prepared springs of waters, salt-bearing and sweet-bearing, a healing of
thirst, to gush for men from their own recesses.
At a loss then before all these benefactions the prophet cries out saying:
What shall I render to the Lord for all that he has rendered to me? But the
prophet David, having thought still more deeply and more spiritually, and
having seen the benefactions that were going to be given to man in Christ,
proclaiming them as already present, says: What shall I render to the Lord for
all that he has rendered to me? For taking dust from the earth he both formed
me and brought me from not being into being; he honored me with his own image;
he put angelic glory about me, and when I had been undone by death through the
transgression, coming again he called me back; what shall I render to the Lord
for all that he has rendered to me? He put to death the dragon-serpent that
was against me; becoming himself in Hades he shattered the bronze gates of
death and crushed the iron bars; what shall I render to the Lord for all that
he has rendered to me? When I had been cast out of the delight of paradise he
brought me in again, not by my righteousness but by his grace; he opened the
gates of heaven for me; he promised the kingdom of heaven; he raised for me a
ladder from earth to heaven, the faith; what shall I render to the Lord for
all that 358 he has rendered to me?
But he granted me still more than these; for my sake he endured spittings
and blows, for my sake he wore a crown of thorns upon his head, he drank
vinegar mixed with gall, for my sake he suffered many things, that he might
also empty out the bitterness and malice of death that had been prepared; what
shall I render to the Lord for all that he has rendered to me? The prophet,
having sought many things and gone round in his mind and found no perfect
repayment worthy of God, seeing one thing in the matter of martyrdom, adds
saying: I will take the cup of salvation and call upon the name of the Lord.
And this cup makes known the passion of Christ. For, Father, if it is possible
for this cup to pass, let it pass. We have many cups written in Scripture; a
golden cup of Babylon, he says; the nations drank and were drunken. Babylon
too has a golden cup, but it has no good drink in it; for the golden cup of
Babylon was, understand for me, the most beautiful discourse of the Greeks who
are wise in conceit, having within a deadly mixture, the teaching of idolatry.
But such is not the cup which he who prays in the psalm seeks, but the cup
full of endurance and contest, sealed in the name of the Lord, of which he who
drinks, filled with the gladness of the Holy Spirit, shall be set in the holy
choir of the martyrs; in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory and the
might unto the ages of ages. Amen.
