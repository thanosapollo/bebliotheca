HOMILY 12.
On the beginning of Proverbs.
The reward of ready obedience is good. Let us then obey a kind Father, who
sets before us contests from the oracles of the Spirit; who, after the manner
of those experienced among hunters in hard-to-pass places, wants to take the
trial of the run as of some pup. And he has set before us for interpretation
the preface of the Proverbs.
And how hard to catch is the sense of this =31.388= saying, is known to
everyone who has given even a little attention. Yet we must not shrink from
the attempt, having set our hope upon the Lord, who, through the prayers of
the shepherd, will give us a word in the opening of our mouth. We have come to
know three treatises in all of the most wise Solomon: that of these Proverbs,
and that of Ecclesiastes, and that of the Song of Songs; each arranged toward
its own aim; yet all were written for the benefit of men. For the Proverb is a
training of morals, and a correction of passions, and in general a teaching of
life, containing dense counsels of the things that must be done; while
Ecclesiastes takes hold of the study of nature, and reveals to us the vanity
of the things in this world; so that we may not think the things that pass
away to be much sought after, nor spend the cares of the soul on vanities. And
the Song of Songs shows the manner of the perfection of souls. It contains the
harmony of bride and bridegroom; that is, the soul's kinship with God the
Word. But let us return to the matter at hand. Proverbs of Solomon son of
David, who reigned in Israel. The name of proverbs has been assigned among
those outside to the more popular sayings, and to those spoken in the roads,
for the most part; for oimos among them is the name for the road, whence they
also defined the proverb as a roadside saying worn in the use of the many, and
able to be transferred from a few things to more that are similar. But among
us a proverb is a beneficial word, given out with moderate concealment,
containing much that is useful at once, and also hiding much meaning in the
depth. Whence also the Lord says, These things I have spoken to you in
proverbs. An hour is coming when I will no longer speak to you in proverbs,
but I will speak to you plainly; as the proverbial word does not have the open
and published character of the meaning, but intimates its intent sideways to
those who are more quick. Proverbs then of Solomon: that is, hortatory words,
useful along the whole road of life. And he added the name of the writer, that
by the distinction of the person he might draw the hearer. For the
trustworthiness of the teacher makes the word readily acceptable, and prepares
those being taught to be more attentive. Proverbs then of Solomon, of that
Solomon to whom the Lord said: Behold, I have given you a prudent and wise
=31.389= heart; such as you, there has not been before you; and after you none
like you shall arise; and again: The Lord gave Solomon wisdom and
understanding very much, and largeness of heart as the sand that is by the
sea. And the wisdom of Solomon was multiplied above the prudence of all
ancient men, and above all the prudent of Egypt. Thus necessary is the
addition of the name: Proverbs of Solomon son of David.

The father also is added, so that you may know that Solomon was wise from a
wise and prophetic father, trained from infancy in the sacred writings, and
not having obtained the rule by lot, nor having forced his way into a kingdom
that belonged to him in nothing, but having received the ancestral scepters by
a just judgment of a father and by a vote of God. This man became king of
Jerusalem. Nor is this idle; most of all for the distinction of namesakes, and
then also because of the construction of the much celebrated temple; that you
may know its craftsman, and the cause of all the administration according to
the city and of the ordinances and of the good order. And it contributes
greatly toward the reception of the exhortations that the writer of the word
is a king. For if kingship is lawful overseeing, it is clear that the counsels
given by a king, at least one truly worthy of this title, have much that is
lawful, looking to what is profitable in common to all, and not arranged
toward the aim of his own benefit. For this is how a tyrant differs from a
king, that the one looks to his own from every side, while the other procures
what is beneficial to those ruled. And he also enumerates the useful things
from the book, what and how many accrue to those being taught. And first from
the Proverbs it is possible to know wisdom and instruction. And wisdom is
knowledge of things divine and human, and of their causes. He then who
theologizes successfully has known wisdom, as also the blessed Paul says: But
we speak wisdom among the perfect; yet not a wisdom of this age, nor of the
rulers of this age who are being brought to nothing; but we speak God's wisdom
in a mystery, the hidden one, which God predetermined before the ages. And he
who from the construction of the world conceives the craftsman, =31.392= has
himself also known God through the wisdom of the world. For his invisible
things from the creation of the world are seen, being understood by the things
made. And he brings us on to the thought about God, through the things he
says: God by wisdom founded the earth; and, When he was preparing the heaven,
I was present with him; and, I was with him fitting; and I was the one in whom
he rejoiced. For all these things, having personified wisdom for us, in order
to present clearly the knowledge of it, he discourses from it. And in general
the, The Lord created me the beginning of his ways, he says concerning the
wisdom that is shown in the world, all but sending forth a voice through the
things seen, that it has come to be from God, and that so great a wisdom does
not shine in the things completed of its own accord
. For as The heavens tell
the glory of God, and the firmament announces the work of his hands (and they
tell without voice); for there are no speeches, nor words, whose voices are
not heard; so there are also certain words of the original wisdom, that which
was laid down with the creation before the others in the making. This, being
silent, cries out its own creator and Lord, that through it you may run up to
the thought of the only wise. And there is also a human wisdom: the experience
of the affairs according to life, according to which we call wise those who
are skilled in each of the useful arts. Therefore also much of the book has
been spent by the writer in the exhortation concerning wisdom. Wisdom is
hymned in the exits, and in the streets she conducts herself with boldness;
and on the tops of walls she is proclaimed. For seeing the love of honor of
men, and that we all by nature welcome what is distinguished in it, through
the praises of wisdom he stirs the souls toward not claiming it sluggishly nor
as a side matter. Everywhere, he says, there is much speech of it, in roads,
in marketplaces, in the fortifications of the city. For this reason he says
gates and streets and walls, through the exits and the streets intimating its
distinction, and through the walls what is beneficial from it, and sufficient
toward every security of life. And wishing to make us akin to it: I said, he
says, that wisdom is your sister; and again:

Be in love with it, and it will keep you. Then showing its common benefit, and
that what is useful from it is open alike to all: She has slain, he says, her
=31.393= own sacrifices; that is, she has prepared solid food for those who
through habit have the senses of the soul trained. She mixed into a
mixing-bowl her own wine, that which gladdens a man's heart. And he calls a
mixing-bowl the common and public participation of the goods; from which it is
possible for all equally to draw off what is possible and proportionate for
each. And she prepared her own table. He says all things by emphasis, showing
us the spiritual through the bodily. For he names the rational food of the
soul a table, to which she calls with a high proclamation, that is, with
doctrines that have nothing low or thrown down. He who is foolish, let him
turn aside toward me. For as those who are sick have need of the medical art,
so the foolish stand in need of wisdom. And the: For it is better to traffic
in her than treasures of gold and silver; and the: And she is more precious
than costly stones; and every precious thing is not worthy of her; and the:
Son, if you become wise for yourself, you will be wise also for those near
you; and the: And for a wise son the deeds will be prosperous. And in general
it is possible for you to know the truth of the word, having gathered at
leisure the things said about wisdom by Solomon. And since wisdom will not
enter into a soul skilled in evil, he first purifies through the divine fear
the souls of those who are about to converse with wisdom. For to throw the
mysteries of salvation upon whoever happens by, and to receive all alike,
those who have used neither a pure life nor a word examined and exact, is like
as if one should also put the much honored myrrh into a filthy vessel. For
this reason, The beginning of wisdom is fear of the Lord. And fear is a
purifier of soul according to the prayer of the prophet saying: Nail my flesh
from your fear. As where fear dwells, there all purity of soul is established,
all wickedness and unholy action fleeing away, the members of the body not
being able to be moved toward unseemly operations, because they have been
nailed by fear. For as one who has the bodily nails driven into himself is
inactive, held by the pains; so one seized by the fear of God cannot use the
eye toward what he must not, nor move the hands toward forbidden deeds, nor in
general operate anything small or greater contrary to what is fitting, as if
pierced through by some pain, the expectation of the things threatened. And he
sends away the profane and the rabble from the divine teachings, saying: But
the fearless will lodge in gates; and the: You will seek wisdom among the
wicked, and you will not find it; and again: =31.396= The wicked will seek me,
and they will not find, because they have not been purified by the divine
fear. So that one who is about to approach the taking up of wisdom, having
purified the shames from vice by the saving fear, let him come forward. There
has appeared to us then also another good from the teaching of the Proverbs,
the taking up of fear, introduced to us through wisdom. Second among the
promises was to know instruction. And instruction is a certain beneficial
leading of the soul, often laboriously cleansing it of the stains from vice,
which For the present does not seem to be of joy, but of grief; but later it
yields a peaceful fruit to those trained through it unto salvation. To know
this instruction then is not of an ordinary mind; because many, giving up at
the laborious character of the things that happen, do not await the benefit of
the outcome through ignorance, but, being impatient of the austerity of the
care, they remained in the infirmities of ignorance. For this reason the
voices of the righteous are worthy of wonder, of those who say: Lord, do not
convict me with your wrath, nor instruct me with your anger. For they do not
decline the instruction, but the anger. Close to this is also the,

Instruct us, Lord, only in judgment, and not in wrath; and the: The
instruction of the Lord opens my ears. For as the infants among children,
neglecting the lessons, after the lashes which the teachers or pedagogues
apply to them, having become more attentive, receive the teachings, and the
same word before the blows was not heard, but after the pains from the lashes,
as if the ears had just been opened, was both received by the hearing and kept
by the memory; so also it happens in the case of those who disobey the divine
teaching and are disposed contemptuously toward the commandments. For whenever
the instruction is brought upon them from God, then especially the commands of
God that are always spoken and always disobeyed were received as having first
fallen upon their ears. For this reason, The instruction of the Lord, he says,
opens my ears. Since then instruction admonishes the disorderly, as Paul used
to do handing over to Satan, as to some public executioner twisting and
lashing, that they may be instructed not to blaspheme; and he brings back the
one who throws off the rein, as concerning whom it has been said, that
Afterward of his captivity he repented; it is necessary to know the power of
instruction toward how many things it is profitable. Knowing then the benefit
from it, Solomon exhorts: Do not hold back from instructing an infant, because
if you strike him with a rod, he will not die. For you will strike him with a
rod, but you will rescue his soul from death. For what son is there whom he
does not instruct. a father? This instruction is more precious than many
possessions to those who reckon rightly. Therefore Solomon says: =31.397= Take
instruction, and not silver; that in a time of circumstances, either of the
body laboring, or of the things according to the house having suffered, you
may never take a wicked thought about God, but in much endurance you may
receive his blows, being instructed for the things in which you sinned, and
you too may say, because of knowing instruction, the: I will bear the wrath of
the Lord, because I have sinned against him; and the: It is good for me that
you humbled me. Such was Paul saying: Being instructed, and not being put to
death; and the: Being convicted, we are instructed by the Lord, that we may
not be condemned with the world. And since also the taking up of lessons is
called instruction, according to what is written about Moses, that He was
instructed in all wisdom of the Egyptians, not to throw oneself upon whatever
lessons happen by, but to know the most useful instruction, is no small thing
toward salvation. For already some, either occupying themselves with geometry,
which the Egyptians invented, or with astrology honored among the Chaldeans,
or in general having their meteorology about figures and shadows, have
despised the instruction from the divine oracles. But also poetry, and
rhetoric, and the invention of sophisms have occupied many; whose material is
the false. For neither can poetry stand without the myth; nor rhetoric without
the art in speaking; nor sophistic without the fallacies. Since then many
through eagerness about these things have neglected the knowledge about God,
having grown old in the inquiry of vanities, the recognition of instruction is
necessary, both toward the choice of the beneficial instruction, and toward
the flight from the useless and harmful. It is also possible to understand
words of prudence for one who attends to the Proverbs, and does not receive
the benefit from them idly. Prudence then we understand to be one of the
generic virtues, according to which we humans become knowers of goods and
evils and things that are neither. And the prudent man has clearly been named
paronymously from prudence. How then has the serpent been testified to have
become most prudent of all the beasts? =31.400= And again the Lord: Become
prudent, as the serpents. And prudent is the steward of injustice. Or is it
clear that the name of prudence is double? For one is a guarding of one's own
advantage together with plotting against the neighbor, such as that of the
serpent, keeping his own head. And this seems to be a certain malignity

of manners, sharply finding out one's own profit, and snatching away those who
are more simple; such as that of the steward of injustice. But true prudence
is a discernment of the things to be done and not to be done; he who follows
it will never stand aside from the works of virtue, nor ever be pierced
through by the destruction of vice. He then who understands words of prudence
knows which are sophistical and deceptive, and which are those that counsel us
the best things concerning what must be done according to life; and as an
approved banker, he will hold the approved, and will keep away from every form
of wickedness. This prudence grants to the one building his own house to set
his foundation upon the rock, that is, to rest upon the faith in Christ, so as
to remain unshaken in rains and winds and onsets of rivers. For the unshaken
character in temptations, both those human and those brought upon us from
above, the Lord showed us through such words of the parable. And it also
teaches not to neglect the necessary things, but having prepared beforehand
the provisions of life, in the readiness of the heart to await the presence of
the bridegroom. For the prudent virgins, he says, because they had the oil in
the lamps, went in together with the bridegroom; but the foolish, because of
their unpreparedness, were alienated from the joy of the bridal chamber. After
this, let us see what it is to receive turns of words. The true word, and one
going forth from a sound mind, is simple and of one manner, saying the same
things always about the same things; but the varied and artful, having much
that is intricate and constructed, is shaped in countless ways, and is turned
with untold turns, transforming itself toward what pleases those who meet it.
Toward our being able then to hold out strongly against the onset from the
technical words the Proverbs grant a great help. Because the one who attends
to them, and does not admit the exhortations from them idly, as if armed with
the experience, receives unharmed the turns of the words, being neither
overturned31.401 by them, nor being moved anywhere from the truth. For
whenever the affairs have one nature, and the words persuade otherwise about
them, there is a certain turn, or rather a distortion, coming about from the
word concerning the truth. And he who appears one thing, and is another
according to truth, has used turns of words, deceiving those with him, as
hares and foxes do the dogs, showing one way and turning another. Or certain
turns of words are the oppositions from the falsely named knowledge. For not
accepting the simplicity of the spiritual teaching, those sharpened from
dialectic toward contradictions often overturn the strength of the truth by
the persuasiveness of the sophisms. He then who has been fortified by the
Proverbs receives these turns of the words. And if he ever finds problems
having equally matched attempts on either side, in which the finding of the
more persuasive is hard to distinguish; nevertheless because he has been
trained in the Proverbs, he will not have his mind confused, even if those
conversing seem most of all to bring the blows from the attempts equally
against one another. The Proverb also grants to understand true justice. Since
then justice is a habit distributive of what is according to desert; and this
is hard to catch, some, through deficiency concerning prudence, not finding
out how to distribute to each the equal, and others, through being preoccupied
by human passions, making the just disappear, whenever they despise the poor,
and do not convict rulers who do injustice, the book of the Proverbs promises
to give to its own students the knowledge of true justice. And since many,
hunting the praise from the many, in deed prefer injustice and the grasping as
beneficial, but in form and word they greatly admire equality and justice;
neither will the one trained in the Proverbs be ignorant of these, but he will
know which is the counterfeit and

adulterated, and which is the true and unmixed justice. Since however the wise
outside have also composed many words about justice, and persuasively knock
aside those who are not able to follow the true word about this inquiry, the
book promises to make known the true justice, so that we may escape the harm
from the sophisms. And already also the laws of the nations, having much
difference toward one another, produce confusion in the thoughts of those who
have not grasped the exact account of justice. For some of the nations set
patricide as just; but other nations turn away from every killing as unholy.
And some honor chastity above measure; and some rage after mothers and
daughters and sisters. And in general many, mastered by ancient custom, do
not distinguish the abominable character of the things that happen. But this
book, teaching the things about true justice, rescues men from the passions
from unreason.
And there is justice31.404, the one turning among us, the distribution of the
equal. For even if we do not attain it according to exactness, yet doing with
the most just intention, we do not fall away from the aim. And there is one
brought on from heaven from the just judge, both the corrective and the
retributive, of which there is much that is hard to behold because of the
height of the doctrines stored in it. For this I think the Psalmist says: Your
justice is as the mountains of God. It promises then to make this truly true
and divine justice clear to those trained in the proverbial teaching. Again
since of the things sinned some are involuntary, and some are sinned from a
wicked intention, and the definition of the just is not the same upon these.
For let us suppose that fornication is the thing being judged, and two
fornicating women. But the one, sold to a brothel-keeper, is in the evil by
necessity, furnishing the work from the body to the wicked master; and the
other through pleasure has given herself voluntarily to the sin. Otherwise
then the involuntary things meet with pardon, and otherwise the things from a
worthless choice are condemned. Again one has sinned, having been reared
wickedly from the beginning; for he was also brought into life by unjust
parents, and was reared together with both words and deeds that are unlawful;
but another, having many things that call him toward virtue: a most solemn
upbringing, admonition of parents, exactness of teachers, hearing of more
divine words, a settled way of life, the other things by which a soul is
trained toward virtue; then he too has slipped into the like sin; how will
such a one not justly be deemed worthy of a heavier punishment? For the one
will be charged only upon the saving starting-points sown in our thoughts, as
not having used them soundly; and the other, besides this, also as having
betrayed all the cooperation given to him, and having been dragged down
through inattention into the wicked life. To understand then the true justice
is of a truly great mind and a most perfect understanding. And perhaps it also
has a promise, that the one trained in the proverbial things will be able
thereafter to apply himself to the exactness of theology. For the true justice
is Christ, Who became for us wisdom from God, justice and sanctification, and
redemption. To know true justice has been yoked with to direct judgment. For
it is impossible that one not previously trained about the just be able to
distinguish the disputes rightly. For not even Solomon himself, if he did not
have the exact accounts about the just in himself, would have been able to
bring out so rightly and so hitting the mark that much celebrated judgment,
which he judged for the prostitutes about the child. For since the things said
by each were without witness, he went to nature, and through it he found the
unknown things; the alien one having accepted =31.405= without passion the
destruction of the child, and the true mother through natural affection not
even having endured the hearing of the suffering

. He then who knows the true justice, and through it has been taught to assign
what is proper to each, this one is able to direct the judgment. For as the
archer directs the arrow toward the mark, leading the shooting astray neither
by excesses, nor by deficiencies, nor by the deviations to either side; so the
judge aims at the just, neither taking faces (for to know a face in judgment
is not good), nor doing according to inclination, but bringing out judgments
that are straight and undistorted. And when two are being judged before him,
both the one grasping more and the one having the lesser, the judge stands
equalizing them toward one another, and taking away from the one who exceeds
as much as he finds the one being wronged diminished. But he who does not have
the true justice stored beforehand in his soul, but is either corrupted by
money, or granting to friendship, or avenging enmity, or being overawed by
power, cannot direct the judgment. To whom the psalm will say the: If then you
truly speak justice, judge straight things, sons of men. For a proof of the
disposition of the just is the rightness concerning judging. Whence he himself
also going forward forbids saying: A great weight and a small, abominable
before the Lord, hinting proverbially at the unequal concerning the judgment
by the name of the weights. And this is useful not only to those who judge,
but also to the choices about each of the things according to life. Since we
have with ourselves a natural criterion, through which we distinguish the good
from the wicked, it is necessary for us in the choice of the things to be done
to make right distinctions of the affairs; and, as some judge awarding to the
opposites with equal and most just intention, to obey virtue, and to condemn
vice. For example fornication and chastity are judged before you, and your
mind sits high, having been entrusted with the court; and pleasure advocates
for fornication, and the fear of God stands with chastity. If then you condemn
the sin, and give chastity to win, you have made the judgment of the matter
straight; but if, having given the inclination to pleasure, you declare the
sin more preferred, you have made the judgment crooked, having become liable
to the curse of the one who said: Woe to those who say =31.408= the bitter
sweet, and the sweet bitter; those who say the light darkness, and the
darkness light. Since then, according to Solomon himself, Reasonings of the
just are judgments, it is necessary to be eager in the hidden court of the
reasonings to make the judgments about the matter unswerving, and to have the
mind resembling a balance, weighing each of the things to be done without
leaning. Whenever each commandment is tried before you against the vice
opposed to it, give to the law of God the victory prizes against the sin.
Superabundance and equality are judged? Declare against the desire of others'
things, give the better vote to virtue. Abuse and long-suffering stand against
one another? Put abuse to shame, and prefer the long-suffering. Enmity and
love? Having made the one dishonored, banish it as far away as possible; and
having honored love, make it your own. Hypocrisy and simplicity, courage and
cowardice, prudence and folly, justice and injustice, chastity and
incontinence, and to speak concisely, every virtue is tried against every
vice? Then therefore show the straightness of the judgments, in the hidden
court of your soul, and having made the commandment as it were assessor to
yourself, show the hatred of wickedness; turning away from the sins, and
highly honoring the virtues. For if in each action you make the better things
win with you, you will be blessed In that day, when God will judge the hidden
things of men according to our Gospel, and the reasonings accusing or also
defending among one another, you do not go away condemned because of the
inclination toward

the base things, but you will be honored with the crowns of justice, with
which throughout all your life you crowned virtue. Of how great goods will the
book of the Proverbs become a procurer for you, teaching both the true justice
and to direct judgment? What then still besides these? That he may give, he
says, shrewdness to the guileless, and to a young child both perception and
thought. We understand guilelessness in two ways. For either the alienation
from sin accomplished by reasoning, and through long attention and study of
the goods having cut out as it were some root of vice, we receive the name of
the guileless according to a complete deprivation of it; or guilelessness is
the not yet experience of the evil, through youth often or a certain practice
of life some being inexperienced toward certain vices. For example the child
does not know pride, does not know deceit and knavery. Again there are some of
those inhabiting the countryside, not knowing the commercial malices, nor the
entanglements in court. Such we call guileless, not as having been separated
from vice from choice, but as not yet having arrived at experience of the
wicked habit. And guileless in the proper sense, such as David was, saying:
But I walked in my guilelessness; he who by the exercise according to virtue
has put away all wickedness from his own soul, who is also deemed worthy of
the inheritance of the goods. Because the Lord will not deprive of the goods
those who walk in guilelessness. Such a one trusting will say: Judge me, Lord,
because I walked in my guilelessness; and again: Judge me according to my
justice and according to my guilelessness upon me. And the simplicity of
character characterizes the guileless, the noble, the unconstructed. Such as
Jacob was: Unformed, he says, dwelling a house; that is, using the simplicity
from nature, putting around himself no fabricated form from art, like a mask,
toward the deception of those who meet him. Here however he seems to call
guileless the one inexperienced in evil, whom he also says to have need of the
praiseworthy shrewdness, that in addition to the integrity from nature he may
also acquire the intelligence from experience, and, fenced as with some
weapon, the good shrewdness, he may be hard to catch for the plots of the
adversaries. For it is necessary, I think, that the perfect be prudent toward
the good, and simple toward the evil. From here as from some spring let the
guileless draw the saving shrewdness. For a shrewd man is a throne of
perception; and, And a shrewd man, when evils come on, hid himself; and, And
he who keeps rebukes is more shrewd. Shrewdness then is an operation of all
things according to a technical practice; just as malice is the working of the
evil alone. Since then the shrewd man admits every working, and among all
things the base things also are, the name of the shrewd signifies two. And he
who uses the invention of the works for harm of others is wicked; but the
praiseworthy shrewd man is the one who sharply and intelligently finds out his
own good, and flees the harms craftily and plottingly prepared for him by
others. Attend then exactly to the name of shrewdness; and know that it is a
certain middle habit, according to which he who from a sound purpose uses it
for benefit of himself and of those near is acceptable, and he who handles it
for harm of the neighbors is condemned, using the practice as a starting-point
toward destruction. And the history is full of the use =31.412= of the
practice on either side. Good shrewdness of the Hebrews outwitting the
Egyptians, and both receiving the wage of the working of the cities, and
supplying themselves materials toward the tent. The midwives shrewdly in a
praiseworthy way preserved the male children of the Hebrews. Good shrewdness
of Rebecca procuring the great blessing for the son. Rahab shrewdly in a good
way; Rachel in a good way; the one having saved the spies, and the other
having outwitted the father, and having freed from idolatry. Wickedly

the Gibeonites outwitted the Israelites. Wicked shrewdness of Absalom in a
form of fairness drawing the subjects, and having gathered a multitude of
rebels toward a plot against the father. And some are charged, as having also
used shrewdness against the people of God in intention. Here however the word
admits the shrewdness taken up for benefit, as being a weapon toward the
affairs of life, and fortifying through itself the souls of the more simple.
For if Eve had had this shrewdness, she would not have become easy to catch
for the deceits of the serpent. The guileless then, the one easy to have the

fortifies, furnishing him as it were an alliance toward the affairs of life,
the benefit from the shrewdness. Next it is to examine how it gives to a young
child perception and thought. Since man is double, as the Apostle says; the
one being from outside, and the one from within, according to the one seen,
and according to the one understood in the hidden, it is necessary that we
also take the things of age similarly upon each. To say then that the newborn
child receives a bodily perception is not far from the laughable. For which of
the senses can the book grant, both sight, and hearing, and smell, and taste,
and touch being born together with us at once, and not accruing from teaching,
but nature completing the living being through these? Neither then is a child
to be understood bodily, nor perception as one of these that have been
enumerated, but the ages are to be transferred upon the inner man. For we have
been taught in many places of Scripture also a childlike condition of the
soul; and another in its prime; and another already of an old man. For
example, as we have learned from Paul, those in Corinth were infants;
therefore they still had need of milk, of the introductory and simpler
teaching of the Gospel, not yet being able to master solid food, that which is
in doctrines. And a young man according to soul is the one who in all the
parts of virtue has been fitted =31.413= toward the perfect, the one boiling
with the spirit, the one swelling toward the deeds of piety, and being in his
prime toward every vigor of good works; whom also the Gospel calls a violent
one, able to snatch the kingdom of the heavens; whom also the Holy Spirit
takes up into the hymns, as suitable: For young men, he says, and virgins, let
them praise the name of the Lord. And in Joel the young men have promises that
they will see visions. And an elder according to soul is the one perfected
according to prudence; such as Daniel was, showing in a young body the
solemnity from understanding, more venerable than all grey hair. Therefore
those full of wicked days say to him: Come, sit in the midst of us, and
announce to us, because God has given you the eldership. Therefore here too he
calls a young child the one regenerated through the washing of regeneration,
and nourished, and become as a little child, and through such a condition
being fit for the kingdom of the heavens. To the infant thus newborn then, who
has longed for the rational and unmixed milk, the book of the Proverbs gives,
to one trained through it, perception and thought; perception of the present
things, and
thought of the things to come. For it trains the human things, and makes them
perceptive of the affairs, so as neither to be enslaved to unseemly pleasures.
nor to be fluttered about the vain glories of this world. And it also produces
thought of the age to come, and brings on through the things it says to the
faith of the things promised. And since we transferred the differences of the
ages upon the inner man, it is consistent also to transfer the names of the
senses upon the powers of the soul. So that whenever he says, that Apply your
ear to my words, we must know that he seeks the ready hearing of the soul, as
the Lord: He who has ears to hear, let him hear; and the, A wise word into an

ready of hearing. Through these then and such things he gives to the young
child the healthy perception of hearing. And whenever he says, Do not attend
to a base woman; and again, Do not rest your eye toward her; and, Let your
eyes look straight things; he is clearly granting to the child as it were the
sight of the soul. And whenever he exhorts, that Eat honey, son, that your
throat may be sweetened (naming honey tropically the divine teaching; For how
sweet, he says, are your oracles to my larynx, above honey to my mouth);
through this exhortation he fits the spiritual taste of the soul, concerning
which it has been said: =31.416= Taste and see that the Lord is kind. And
there is also a certain touch of the soul, according to which wisdom lays hold
of it, as it were folding around it its own virtue. For be in love with it, he
says, that it may embrace you; and again Ecclesiastes, A time to embrace, and
a time to be far from an embrace. For bodies are defiled by unclean
entwinings; but a soul, united with it wholly throughout by the entwining of
wisdom, is filled with sanctification and purity. Thus then he grants
perception to a young child. And how does he produce thought? Whenever he
says: Possessions will not benefit in a day of evil. For he casts a thought
into your heart about that day, in which the multitude of the money will not
suffice for you, nor will it deliver you from the eternal punishment. Or
whenever he says: But the guileless will inherit earth; that earth, of which
also the meek are heirs, both according to the Psalmist who says: And the meek
will inherit the earth; and according to the beatitude of the Lord; For
blessed, he says, are the meek, because they themselves will inherit the
earth; and again: The wise will inherit glory. He raises the soul toward the
desire of the goods in promises. This thought he gives to the young child,
fear of the things threatened to the sinners, and desire of the things
prepared for the just. For having heard these a wise man will be wiser. The
word testifies a great power to the Proverbs, that it surpasses the wisdom of
the wise, and the things handed down from this book are greater than the
lessons stored in them. So that of the other teachers the inexperienced are
students; but of this book the hearers are the wise. And since the name of the
wise is homonymous (for wise also those of this world are called, wise also
those who have received the true wisdom our Lord Jesus Christ from the faith
in him), those who are outside our teaching, whenever they come forward to the
sound teaching of the Proverbs, the word promises that they will be wiser;
dishonoring the knowledge of vanities, and transferring the wonder toward the
truth. Or since the wise is said in many ways (for both the one desiring
wisdom, and the one already in progress of the contemplations of wisdom, and
the one perfected according to the habit, are similarly named, having heard
these the wise, whether the lover of wisdom, or also the one already advanced
in it, they will be wiser, having been trained in many things about the divine
doctrines, and being taught many things also about the human, the book in
various ways banishing vice, and in many manners =31.417= again introducing
virtue. It bridles an unjust tongue, it trains an eye that looks wicked
things, it does not permit unjust hands to rule; it drives away idleness, it
chastises unseemly desires, it teaches prudence, it teaches courage, it honors
chastity. He then who has been trained in these, and having with himself both
a strong denunciation of the worse things, and the desire of the good with a
greater impulse, as through some prompting, having acquired in addition, being
wise through his own impulse, has become wiser through the perfection from the
teaching. And the intelligent will acquire steering. It is necessary that the
one about to approach the taking up of some art have the fitnesses from nature
prepared; the wrestler the formation of the body and

the strength; the runner the harmony of the limbs and the lightness; so also
the steersman the sharpness of the mind and the easy following. For this
reason the word does not call just anyone toward the steering, but the
intelligent. And what is the steering, if not altogether a science of soul
concerning the unstable nature of human affairs, how it is fitting for it to
be carried across? For in many places we find this life named waters and sea
by the Scripture, as in the psalm: He sent from on high, and took me; he took
me from many waters. For it is clear that he calls the disturbance of life
waters. For neither do the things on the right and much sought after by the
many have the standing and permanent, nor have the circumstantial and downcast
of the affairs been firmly seated; but all things are subject to some tossing
and shaking and unexpected changes. As then it is impossible for a sea to last
the same for long (for the one now smooth and steady you will a little later
see being roughened by violences of winds, and indeed also the one raging, and
being boiled by the surge, a deep calm quickly laid it); so also the affairs
of life easily take the turns on either side. For this reason there is need of
a steersman, that both in a calm of life, all things going for him according
to stream, he may await the changes, and may not rest upon the present things
as upon immortal ones, and in the more gloomy condition he may not despair of
himself, nor having been swallowed by the more excessive grief be carried
under water. For neither health of body, nor flower of youth, not prosperity
of house, not the remaining fair weather of life remains for long; but being
in this fair weather of life, await sometime also a winter of affairs. For
disease will come, and poverty will come; the wind not always standing at the
stern; but also unexpected disgraces often overtake the one conspicuous in all
things and envied; and unwanted circumstances, like some reefs, have thrown
into confusion all the fair weather of life. And the succession of the evils
=31.420= is as waves for you, others upon others. being raised, roughening
your life, and setting around you a fearful surge of living. You will sometime
also see these having run past, and the life changed toward cheerfulness and a
truly white calm. This then is the intelligent steersman, who handling the
things that fall out with a view to the underlying nature, and remaining
always like himself to himself, being neither lifted up in the cheerfulnesses,
nor falling in the disasters. Again also toward other things the good from the
steering is useful to us. For I know also other waves and a fearful surge
rising against the soul, that from the passions of the flesh. For angers and
fears, and pleasures and griefs, having fallen as from some violent squall of
the mind of the flesh, have often sunk the unsteered soul. It is necessary
then that the mind, sitting above the passions as some steersman, and having
boarded the flesh as a boat, and turning the reasonings as rudders with
experience, both trample the waves nobly, and remaining high, and being hard
of access to the passions, in no way be filled with the bitterness from them
as with some brine, but always praying say: May I be rescued from those who
hate me, and from the deep waters. Let a storm of water not sink me, nor let a
depth swallow me. Do you want me to narrate for you also another voyage,
toward which the gift of the steering is necessary for us? The kingdom of the
heavens is like a merchant man. All then who travel the evangelical road are
merchants, through the working of the commandments trafficking for themselves
the possession of the heavenly things. It is necessary then that we gather
much and varied heavenly wealth, if we are not going to be put to shame in the
showing of the working, as those who received the talents, nor to hear, Wicked
and slothful servant; and having put in the cargoes, to try securely

to cross this life. For many having gathered many things from youth, having
come about the middles of life, temptations from the spirits of wickedness
having risen against them, did not bear the weight of the winter, because the
steering was not present to them, but they endured the loss of all those
things. Whence also some Were shipwrecked concerning the faith; and others
lost chastity, which they gathered from youth, some unexpected squall =31.421=
of the wicked pleasure having run upon them. A most pitiable spectacle after
fasting, after hard living, after intense prayer, after abundant tear, after
continence of some twenty or even thirty years, through inattention of soul
and neglect to be shown naked of all things, and for the one prospering by the
working of the commandments to become like some very wealthy merchant, who
promising by the multitude of the cargoes, the ship being carried for him with
a fair wind, having run through the fearful seas, at the harbors themselves
the ship having been broken, was shown all at once empty of all things; if
this one too should lose the things acquired by countless toils and sweats by
one onset of a demon, having been sunk by the sin as by some wild hurricane. A
fitting voice for the one who has shipwrecked all virtue together: I came into
the depths of the sea, and a storm sank me. Hold then securely the rudders of
life; steer your eye, lest ever through eyes the violent wave of desire fall
upon you; the hearing, the tongue, do not admit something harmful, nor utter
any of the forbidden things. Let the squall of anger not overturn you; lest
somewhere the astonishments of the fears flood you, let the weight of grief
not plunge you down. The passions are waves, holding yourself higher than
which, you will be a secure steersman of life. But if you do not turn aside
each of these scientifically and steadily, being carried about like some
unballasted boat by the things always falling upon, you will disappear in the
sea of sin. Hear then how the science of the steering may also accrue to you.
It is custom for those who sail to look up toward heaven, and from there to
take the guidance of the voyage, in day from the sun, and in night from the
Bear, or some other of the always-appearing stars; and by these always to
infer the straightness. You too then have the eye toward heaven, according to
the one who said: Toward you I lifted my eyes, the one dwelling in the heaven.
Look away toward the sun of justice; and being directed as by some conspicuous
stars, the commandments of the Lord, have the eye sleepless, not giving sleep
to your eyes, nor slumber to your eyelids, that you may have lasting the
guidance from the commandments. For a lamp, he says, to my feet is your law,
and a light to my paths. For if you never nod upon the rudders, while you are
in this life, in the unstable nature of the affairs of the world, you will
also receive the cooperation =31.424= from the Spirit, sending you forward
into what is ahead, and with gentle and peaceful breezes carrying you across
securely, until you are brought safe toward that waveless and fair-weather
harbor of the will of God; to whom be the glory and the power unto the
unending ages of the ages Amen.
