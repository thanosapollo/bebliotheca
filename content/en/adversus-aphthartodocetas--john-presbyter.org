* Against the Aphthartodocetae
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

I. 1. If the body of Christ is incorruptible by nature and incapable of
receiving corruption, how is it consubstantial with us, concerning whom it has
been said: "For this corruptible must put on incorruption"? For that which has
become incorruptible from a change has corruption by nature.

2. But perhaps the opponent will say: If the body of Christ, being
incorruptible before the resurrection, is not consubstantial with the bodies
of men that now are corruptible, then do you also, who confess that after the
resurrection the body of Christ is incorruptible, no longer say that it is
consubstantial with us?

3. To whom it must be answered that according to this it is consubstantial
with ours: for just as, being receptive of incorruption, it became
incorruptible from a corruptible nature, so also ours, being corruptible by
nature, will become incorruptible in the resurrection. And if the body of
Christ was incorruptible, how was it divided by the spear?

For it is proper to things that are divided to be corrupted, even if they
often would not be corrupted when held together by the will of God, as also
the body of the Lord, being corruptible by nature, by the advent of the Word
was not, according to its own nature, corrupted by being dissolved in the tomb
into the simple elements from which it was composed.

4. And that God the Word, having become incarnate, willed his own body to be
corruptible by nature, and that he assumed ours, procuring through himself and
in it salvation for us all, through the things which he began both to do and
to teach and to grant the things above nature to men, the blessed Cyril
says in the first chapter concerning the incarnation: "Then he became free
from sin, so that in him and in him alone human nature, being crowned with the
boasts of sinlessness, might be enriched with the Holy Spirit," and again to
the empresses, chapter 23: | "And inasmuch as he is God by nature and truly,
he receives in a human manner not so much for himself, but for the nature of
man, procuring it through himself and in himself first, and he gives in a
divine manner from his own nature to those worthy to receive."

5. In addition to these goods, as a pattern, as writing and teaching for us,
he assumed our body. But how is he a pattern for us who have a corruptible
body, while he himself has an incorruptible one, and according to us then in
appearance and not in truth? For those who dogmatize that Christ assumed an
incorruptible body are like those who say that the economy is a phantasy.

6. Since therefore after the transgression the body of Adam became corruptible
by nature because of the decree from God, which after Adam ran through the
whole of our race, Christ, wishing to save this corruptible, assumed a body.
For how did that which was not assumed enjoy healing?

7. But if they dare to say that the Word having become incarnate did not
become a pattern for us, perhaps fighting against all the teachers of the
Church and the very disciples and apostles of Christ, for everywhere they
proclaim that he became a pattern for us, how then does he grow, if it was
incorruptible? For quantitative alteration takes place only in a corruptible
essence. 8. Since therefore, not understanding, they put forward a scriptural
saying, that it has been said "His flesh did not see corruption," they are
indeed those who do not understand. For he did not say incorruptible by
nature, but that being corruptible it did not however undergo corruption and
dissolution into the elements, because it rose without corruption by the power
of the Word, wherefore also it did not see corruption.

9. And this is also to be found in the case of those living who are found at
the consummation, concerning whom it has been said in the first letter to the
Thessalonians: "That we who are alive, who remain unto the coming of the Lord,
shall not precede those who have fallen asleep." And again: "Then we who are
alive, who remain, shall be caught up together with them in clouds to meet the
Lord in the air."

10. You see that he says the dead and those whose body has been corrupted will
rise, but those living being caught up immediately in clouds undergoing the
change toward the immortal and incorruptible, the change from corruptibility
which has not come about for them in actuality; for they were not corrupted
into the elements, even if they had a corruptible nature as regards the body.
And the dead in Christ shall rise, as he also says in the first to the
Corinthians: "We shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed." It is
clear here also that at the consummation they do not die, as those before the
consummation, but in some instantaneous time they receive incorruption.

11. If then those, having a body truly corruptible, did not see corruption,
but only | a settling toward the incorruptible, how should we not also suppose
this in the case of the Master's body, consubstantial with bodies from earth,
differing from them in this: to men the natural passions come upon them
unwillingly, but to the Lord not by necessity, but rather himself yielding to
the nature to enact its own things, as the teaching of the holy fathers has
instructed us. 12. And how again shall we understand that in the Acts, "That
he raised him up, no longer about to return to corruption"? For the "no
longer" shows it receptive of corruption before the resurrection. And how
again did the apostle say: "Whence he ought in all things to be made like the
brethren, that he might become a merciful and faithful high priest"? and
again: "Since therefore the children have partaken of blood and flesh, he also
himself likewise partook of the children," who have a corruptible body. In no
way is the corruptible like the incorruptible.

II. Testimonies of the holy fathers that the body of Christ was corruptible
before the resurrection

1. Of Justin the philosopher from the third discourse against the Greeks: Man
having thus come to be and having looked at once toward the transgression,
received corruption naturally. And corruption having been added by nature, it
was necessary for him who wished to save it to destroy the corruption-making
substance. And this could not otherwise come to be, unless the life according
to nature were joined to that which had received the corruption, destroying
the corruption, and preserving henceforth the one who received it immortal.

2. Of Athanasius against Apollinarius: That by death he might destroy death in
the manifestation of a soul, and in the tomb abolish corruption in the burial
of a body; displaying immortality and incorruption from Hades and from the
tomb, having traveled our way in the form that is according to us.

3. From the same discourse: For this reason in the soul of God the hold of
death was being dissolved and the resurrection from Hades was taking place and
the good news was being announced to the souls, and in the body of God
corruption was being abolished and incorruption from the tomb was being shown.

4. From the third discourse against the heresies: For the things of the body
would not have come about in a bodiless one, unless he had taken a body
corruptible and mortal; for Mary was mortal, from whom was also the body.
Wherefore also it is necessary, he having come to be in a suffering and
weeping and toiling body, that these things also which are proper to the flesh
be said of him together with the body.

5. From the discourse on the cross: For the sake then of death being shown in
the body, he raised this on the third day; but that it might not, remaining
long and being completely corrupted, afterwards when he raised it be
disbelieved, as if he were bringing not it but another body, for this reason |
he did not endure more than the three days.

6. Of Ambrose: For other is the weakness of flesh and other the eternity of
the Godhead, and of flesh is death, but of the power the immortal; and if the
Godhead has done this so that the flesh should not see corruption, being
clearly subject by nature to corruption, how could the Godhead itself come to
an end?

7. Of saint Ephraim on love: Incorruption is the resurrection, but he did not
make the scars vanish, that they might become witnesses of God, that he
worked, not insulting the corruptible by the remaining of the scars, but on
the contrary glorifying it by incorruption, because from the corruptible body
he banished the corruption.

8. Of the Theologian on the Lights: For this reason the unmixable things are
mixed: not only God with generation, nor mind with flesh, nor the timeless
with time, nor the uncircumscribed with measure, but also birth with
virginity, and dishonor with him who is higher than all honor, and the
impassible with passion, and the immortal with the corruptible.

9. Of Epiphanius from the Ancoratus: He entered when the doors were shut, that
he might show the dense as fine and the mortal immortal and the corruptible
incorruptible; but that he might refute all who disbelieve in our salvation, I
mean the resurrection, he who changes the body into fineness and mixing the
resurrection with spirit did not wipe away the scars of the nails nor the mark
of the spear, but showed the very thing that suffered on the cross, that it
was not another that rose, nor another grown from it, but the very thing that
suffered, impassible, and the same fallen grain risen incorruptible.

10. Of Chrysostom from the interpretation according to John: He appeared then
in the evening and flew away; then after eight days and again flew away; then
after these things at the sea. And what is "He manifested"? From this it is
clear that he was not seen, unless he condescended, because the body was
henceforth incorruptible and undefiled.

11. From the same: Glorify me, you, Father, with yourself. What then does he
say here? The word is about the economy; since the nature of the flesh had not
yet been glorified, neither having enjoyed incorruption, nor having shared the
royal throne. And for this reason he did not say On the earth, but With you.
Of this glory we too shall partake according to our measure, if we are sober.
For this reason Paul also says: If we suffer with him, that we may also be
glorified with him.

12. From that according to Matthew on "I will not drink from now of the fruit
of the vine": With you bearing witness; for you will see me risen. And what is
new and newly? That is, strangely, not having a passible body, but henceforth
immortal and incorruptible and not needing food; for it was not through need
that after the resurrection he both ate and drank; for the body no longer
needed these, but through these confirming the things of the resurrection.

13. Of Cyril from the Gospel according to John: With the utmost economy then
our Lord Jesus Christ, not yet having transformed his own temple toward the
glory befitting him, was still appearing in the first figures, not wishing the
faith of the resurrection to be transferred to some other body than that which
he took from the holy Virgin, the power of death running against the flesh
alone, from which it has also been driven out. For if his dead body did not
rise, what death has been conquered, and where has the power of corruption
grown weak, when none of the rational creatures has died, not a soul, not an
angel, still less God the Word himself?

14. And after a little: And the entering, the doors being shut, will be ranked
together with his other signs by the sober.

15. From the twelfth book of the Gospel according to John: for the perforation
of hands and side still remaining and being preserved has a demonstration of
bodily corruption, forms of wounds and of piercings that have come about
through iron.

16. And after other things: for corruption being wholly no longer preserved
after the resurrection of the holy flesh especially, since it lived again
toward incorruption, but neither having been believed to need the customary
food, as before, he displayed the marks of nails and did not refuse to partake
of food, that he might root the great mystery of the resurrection and plant
the faith concerning this in the souls of us all.

17. From the second book of the Treasury: Firstborn also from the dead; for he
first raised his own flesh to incorruption and first led it up to the heavens.
Wherefore he also says: I am the way and I am the door. For through him human
nature is taught to walk the new way of the resurrection and as through a door
enters into heaven itself.

18. And again: Just as he was called firstborn among many brethren because
those saved through him have been called to the adoption of God and grace; and
again firstborn and firstfruits of the dead because he has been raised to
incorruption before all; so he is said to have been created the beginning of
ways because all can run through him to salvation.

19. From the first volume of that according to Matthew: How then did he die,
being immortal, that he might also be raised and put death to death, unless he
assumed a body subject to the laws of death? Or how would he have destroyed
the corruption, taking out the race that was acting under it and subject to
it, unless he took that which is naturally able to be corrupted?

20. From the letter to Succensus: And after the resurrection it was the same
body that had suffered, except it no longer had the human weaknesses in
itself; for no longer hunger or toil or anything else of such kind, but
henceforth incorruptible and not this only, but also life-giving.

III. 1. It is necessary to know that since among some of the fathers the body
of Christ has been called incorruptible |, one must know that the
incorruptible is understood in many ways.

For incorruptible is that which is in no way naturally able to be corrupted,
but is always held together in identity by God and never receives alteration
according to essence, such as are the intellectual essences, unchangeable
according to essence as existing according to will.

2. It is also called incorruptible that which is of a nature to be corrupted,
yet is not being corrupted in actuality and being dissolved into the simple
elements from which it was composed, such as will be those of whom Paul speaks
as also set forth: "That we who are alive who remain shall not precede those
who have fallen asleep." Even if they will not be corrupted in actuality,
nevertheless they are corruptible by nature.

3. Incorruptible is also often called the sinless. For David calls sin
corruption. For he says: They were corrupted and became abominable in their
practices, as also Moses says in Genesis: And the Lord saw the earth and it
was corrupted, because all flesh had corrupted its way upon the earth.

4. If then you find the incorruptible concerning the body of Christ among the
teachers of the Church, understand it as not having been corrupted in
actuality and dissolved into the simple elements from which it was composed,
or according to the sense of the sinless. But do not say incorruptible
according to nature, but rather corruptible; for Christ, wishing to heal the
sentence brought against the forefather Adam, received the body corruptible
after the transgression, that in it he might abolish the corruption, having
lived as a fellow-citizen with sinners. For if it were incorruptible by
nature, it would not have been receptive of any passion.

5. Again the blessed Basil said: "Just as therefore the Lord accepted hunger,
the solid food having been spent, and admitted thirst, the moisture in the
body having been consumed, and grew weary, the muscles and the nerves having
been overstretched through the hardship of the journey, not the Godhead being
subdued by the toil, but the body receiving the symptoms that follow from
nature." "For the Lord gave opportunity when he wished to the nature to enact
its own things," as the Theologian also says.

6. How then did the nature enact these things in Christ as being its own, if
it is incorruptible? For these things have no place as coming about concerning
a nature incapable of receiving corruption. But the Word, not wishing to
prevent these from his own flesh which was corruptible, did not prevent it
from enduring these passions, but yielded to the flesh to undergo its own
things for confirmation that it is true and not according to phantasy.
Besides, he accepted the flesh, not that he might flee the blameless passions,
but that he might abolish | together with the passions also the corruption in
his own flesh.

7. Of Hippolytus To the empress Mammaea: For the ark was a type of Christ,
from incorruptible woods signifying his incorruptible power, because it did
not see corruption. And it enclosed five and a half measures, the ark, not
idly; for through the measures he signified the number that fell upon the
time, as composed in this measure, having been filled ... bearing the
incorruptible ark, his own body, in which containing every righteousness he
takes rest.

8. Of Athanasius from the third discourse against the Arians: For it was
fitting that the flesh, being corruptible, should no longer remain mortal
according to its own nature, but through the Word who put it on should remain
incorruptible. For as he, having come to be in our body, imitated our things,
so we, having received him, partake of the immortality from him.

9. Of Methodius of Myra of Lycia on virginity: While Adam was still being
wrought in clay, so to speak, and being fusible and watery and not yet having
reached, after the manner of an earthen vessel, being strengthened by
incorruption, sin, dripping down like water and trickling, dissolved him.
Wherefore God again from above, fashioning the same in honor as in wax, having
strengthened him in the virginal and mixed him with the Word, brought him out
into life unmeltable and unbreakable, that he might not again, flooded from
without by the streams of corruption, fall apart having generated a wasting.

10. Of Gregory of Nyssa from the Catechetical: For a man is born from coupling
and after death comes to be in corruption. If the preaching contained these
things you would not at all have thought him to be God who is witnessed by the
properties of our nature. [Since you hear that he has been born, but has gone
out from the commonality of our nature both by the manner of generation and by
the incapacity for alteration toward corruption.

11. Behold they say the incorruptible of Christ is the sinless, as the
fathers. It was then the body's not being dissolved in the tomb into the
things from which it was composed, as held together by divine power and not
possessing the incorruptible by nature; even if sinless, yet he was
corruptible according to nature, as having put on Adam who came to be under
sin, not the one who had come to be in incorruption before the transgression.
And that from incorruptible Adam became corruptible, hear the teaching of the
fathers that Adam was incorruptible before the transgression...
