* On the Beginning of the Indiction
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

On the beginning of the indiction of the new year, and on the martyrs, and on
the woman with an issue of blood.
=59.575=
αʹ. A successive sign from the Master Christ is the downpour of good things,
and one wealth succeeds another. See, today too the victorious martyrs feast
us, having set forth their own contests as a common banquet of gladness. For
it was needful, it was needful that the martyrs who are always young in grace
should also at the year's entrance become for the pious an occasion of
welcome; it was needful that such exultation should meet the faithful at the
new year; it was needful that we should greet with so great a blessing the
preludes of the change of time; it was needful that we should receive the
festival of the present day, honored by the choir of the saints; it was
needful that the cycle of the year be adorned with the crown of the martyrs;
it was needful to use such a feast at the year that has come upon us, bringing
in also the honor of the martyrs. For they themselves are the cause of all
gladness to the race, the noble soldiers of Christ, who even after death are
heralds of his power, who trampled the deceiving serpent under the feet of
endurance, and strangled the tyrant-minded lion with the hands of love of God,
and made the truth manifest; who hated the present life as vain, and longed
for the kingdom of the heavens as sons for a mother; who through death opened
the gates of immortality, and by their own death put to death the begetter of
death; who in the body conquered the bodiless demons, not by power of body,
but by faith of soul; for through faith they overcame all the things of the
world; nothing is more desirable for Christians than faith.
For having acquired faith as a fellow-worker, they confirmed their words by
deeds. For truly great is the achievement of righteousness; and splendid also
is the virtue of temperance; useful likewise is the care of love for the poor;
and with these, fair is the exercise of self-control; but the mighty advantage
of faith carries the victory over all. This once tilled Sarah's barren womb
for Abraham, after the long spending of time, into a fruitful field; by this
Moses also made a sailing sea to be crossed on foot; through this Elijah gave
orders to heaven, and was heard; this drove away countless kinds of sufferings
in the time of the Savior; a leper through faith was delivered from the scales
of the disease; a blind man through it received eyes which nature had not
brought forth; the Canaanite woman, taking this as ally, compelled for her
child's sake, snatched her daughter from the demon that held her. And a faith
that was hidden also dried up streams of blood, and in other cases the gift
followed a request that came first, but here, being silent, she stole the gift
from one who, as she thought, was silent. O woman wretched and blessed at
once! wretched for what came first, blessed for what came second; For behold,
he says, A woman in a flow of her blood twelve years. If the number of days
had been as great as the time of years that had been spent, it would have
overpowered her, and not twelve years.
The affliction, as I think, had passed over into nature; she who, having spent
her whole life on physicians, added to the wasting of the body also the
diminishing of her wealth; and she could be healed by no one. O that word, She
could not! the woman had as antagonist the enemy of the affliction; so then
the woman's belly was welling up, an unfailing fountain of blood was being
carried down; nor did even so great a length of time spend the flow in the
channel of the stench. She was still being worn in mind by pains and cares,
how she would burst the bond of the grievous sickness, how she would shake off
the twelve-year affliction, how she would loose the unyielding pangs, how she
would stop the unbridled streams, how she would still the madness of the
veins; going by night and being dragged about, she was worn out at the doors
of all the physicians; for she declined the light, lest the descending drops
of blood, making a path, =59.576= should pillory the hidden shame of her as
she walked. As a fee to those who were willing to heal, she drained all the
substance she had, necessity forcing the gift; for the desire of health
persuades one not to spare money. And thus all the physicians, warring against
the disease, were exhausted; varied inventions of drugs could not tame the
wildness of the affliction; for like some hard-of-hearing beast it remained
unhearing of the art of those who chanted over it, as if seeming to answer
such things, vainly, to those who were forcing it: Why do you fight a stronger
beast? so great a time has not robbed me of my pasture in the body, I do not
yield to the sciences of those who heal, no one will buy off my power with
money, no one of those on earth will check my flow; one physician alone, born
of a virgin, will put me to flight; I too am kept for his glory; I am kept,
with the others, as a witness of his power. Why then do you display fruitless
labors, you slaves forcing yourselves to snatch the master's victory
beforehand? I will not spare the creature, unless I see the Fashioner. O how
many things were fighting against the woman! length of time, unyielding pains,
unprofitable confiscations, grief of a wasting body, a mind spent on empty
hopes. But so many enemies did not plunder the weapon of the faith of her who
was in danger; so many surging waves of terrors did not work a shipwreck for
the woman; so many assaults of trials did not throw down the woman who was
enduring.
She saw, he says, Jesus. The one perishing saw the one who saves, the one
bound saw the Redeemer, the one storm-tossed saw the world's anchor, she saw
the king of the afflictions who had come from above, she saw an unexpected
hope of joy, she saw a power unconquerable by treatments, she saw a fountain
of healings dividing streams without pay, she saw life that had come to dwell
among those being put to death; she saw, and she turned over such a thought of
good cheer. I have found, she says, a fitting healer; this physician will not
demand a wage of gold; one wage, as I think, he will demand: a genuine mind;
he will seek from me one gift of faith. But I have it; for of this alone the
disease has not robbed me. But I am pulled the other way by perplexities of
thoughts. What then? Shall I run up and take hold? I shrink from the danger
from the crowd. Shall I cry out the things concerning myself? They will drive
me away as unclean. Shall I hide the misfortune? But I cannot bear the pain.
Shall I run to him as to a physician of nature? But I fear him as a king. Will
he receive me as one who entreats? Lest he flee me as one who defiles. Will he
pity the affliction? Lest he condemn me if I appear.
βʹ. What then shall I do? Which thought shall I take up? Shall I appear
shameless? I am ashamed, lest I remain again unhealed. Let me not fear the
crowd, let me despise the dread, let me become a spectacle, let me flee the
laughter from without; I shall heal the war within. The law hates her as
defiled, the Lawgiver pities her as sick. Eagerness and hesitation were
dividing the woman's soul; but she found faith as mediator of the attempt. For
coming up, he says, behind Jesus, She touched the fringe of his garment. For
she said within herself, If I only touch the fringe of his garment, I shall be
saved. O theft that acquired faith as mother! O theft which he himself praised
who punishes thefts! O theft that delights the one stolen from! O plunderings
crowned by a judge! And see the wonder. Having robbed the gain, she is admired
as one who has granted a gift. For she said within herself: God, she says, is
Master who is ignorant of nothing; Master, not needing bodily eyes, he is a
physician healing souls, he is an eye that enters into thoughts; he sees me
even when I am hidden, he hears even when I do not speak, he touches, even if
I stand apart; he knows that, as unworthy, I could not at all bear the sight.
What then? I will make a hidden approach, =59.577= and honoring him with my
mind, and not insulting him with my face, I will come behind and touch the
tunic; this alone, if I succeed, is enough for me for healing; for surely it
too is filled with the energy of the one who wears it. For the mantle of life
cannot but itself send forth rays of salvation.
Great among prophets was Moses, and chastener of a difficult people, yet still
a man; this one is his Master, and a lover of men. If then the servant's rod
cut through the Red Sea, building a wall in the waters, how would the Master's
fringe not turn the streams of blood to stone? Elijah was a zealot, and a
fervent servant of God, but he too was one of nature, and yet he had a mantle
that was a worker of wonders. Again Elisha, having inherited the teacher's
grace with addition, bridled with the mantle the streams of the Jordan. If
then so great is the grace among the prophets, how great will be the power of
the one prophesied by them? Why do I not speak briefly? Peter's shadow heals;
and shall Christ's garment not heal? I perceive how much even the planning
alone has bridled for me the running disease. O faith, that granted no delay
to the affliction! For she only believed, and the thing that was warring fled;
she believed, and the contentious beast did not talk back; she believed, and
the ever-flowing fountain of blood was dried up, as if blinded by some
invisible cautery. But she was laying hold of a garment, and drawing near to
the things without; but he was recognizing the thoughts within. She, unseen,
drew the healing into the body; but the Savior made public her soul's beauty;
for he did not allow such a fairness of piety to go without a triumph; he did
not let a spark of righteousness be given over to a night of forgetfulness.
The narrative of the Gospels granted to life the faith of one woman, who
robbed God well with an undoubting mind. Who is she who is honored for theft
as for faith? The woman's faith anticipated the thief; before that one's
tongue, this one's palm is praised; =59.578= before he by voice plundered
paradise, she by silence robbed the Giver of the kingdom.
For Jesus, knowing, he says, the power that had gone out from him, asked the
disciples, Who touched me? how did you not know the theft? how were you
ignorant of the robbery? who touched me? who from my treasures has grown rich
in health out of disease? who touched me? What do you say, O Master? you have
given, and you do not know the one who received? you saw the faith, and you
did not recognize the one who believed? you perceived the hidden mind, and you
did not look at the appearing face? Who touched me? Who touched you? on whom
did you lay the medicine of the energy? to whom did you send out the healing,
Master? You drove out the affliction, and you did not know the one who was
saved? Who touched me? who handled me with the mind before the hands, what
thought laid hold of my Godhead? who is this just robber of my energy? who
kindled the lamp of his extinguished salvation from my torch? Who touched me?
Either I may learn from you, or I teach. I wanted to run to the end of the
subject; but the time from here on keeps another day of teaching for the
miracle. For if she, warred against by the affliction for a twelve-year time,
did not loose the tension of endurance, shall we not wait out one more day for
the long contests of the woman? whose faith even the people of the martyrs
emulated, not touching the Master's fringes, but even stripping off the body
itself for Christ. Let us too then, beloved, raise our minds toward zeal for
like faith; let us not become worse than one woman of another race, if we have
enjoyed a greater sight toward piety than she. She was content only once to
lay hold of a garment; but we, holding the Savior's body every day, as you
know, even as being fed with the Master's members, let us offer thanksgiving;
for to him belongs glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
