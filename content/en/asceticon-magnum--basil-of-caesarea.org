QUESTION 3. Concerning love toward the neighbor. It would be next to go
through the second commandment both in order and in power. ANSWER. That then
the law is a cultivator and nourisher of the powers that exist in us
germinally has been said by us in the words before; =31.917= and since we have
been commanded to love the neighbor as ourselves, let us learn whether we also
have power from God toward the fulfillment of this commandment. Who then does
not know that man is a tame and social animal, and not a solitary one, nor
wild? For nothing is so proper to our nature as to share with one another, and
to need one another, and to love the same kind. Of those seeds then which the
Lord himself gave us beforehand, he also seeks the fruits in consequence,
saying: A new commandment I give you, that you love one another. And wishing
to stir our soul toward this commandment, he did not demand as a proof of his
disciples signs and strange powers (although he granted the working of these
also in the Holy Spirit); but what does he say? In this all will know that you
are my disciples, if you have love among one another. And thus everywhere he
joins these commandments, so that he transfers the well-doing toward the
neighbor to himself. For I hungered, he says, and you gave me to eat, and the
things that follow. Then he adds, that Inasmuch as you did it to one of these
least of my brothers, you did it to me. Therefore through the first it is
possible to accomplish also the second; and through the second to return again
to the first; and loving the Lord, to love consequently also the neighbor. For
he who loves me, said the Lord, will keep my commandments. And this is my
commandment, he says, that you love one another, as I loved you; and loving
the neighbor again, to fulfill the love toward God, who receives the grace
into himself. Wherefore Moses, the faithful servant of God, showed so great a
love toward the brothers, that he chose even to be blotted out from the book
of God in which he had been written, if the sin were not forgiven the people.
And Paul dared to pray to be anathema from Christ on behalf of his brothers,
his kinsmen according to the flesh, wishing to become an exchange for the
salvation of all, according to imitation of the Lord; and at the same time
knowing that it was impossible for the one who, through love toward him, was
giving up the grace from God on behalf of the greatest of the commandments to
be alienated from God, and that for this reason he was going to receive back
many times what he gave. At any rate that the saints reached even to this
measure of love toward the neighbor, the things said have sufficient proof.

QUESTION 4. Concerning fear of God. ANSWER. For those then who are just now
being introduced into piety the elementary teaching through fear is more
useful, according to the counsel of the most wise Solomon, who said: The
beginning of wisdom is fear of the Lord; but for you, as having passed beyond
the infancy in Christ, and no longer needing milk, but able by the solid food
of the doctrines to perfect the inner man, there is need of the more capital
commandments, in which all truth of the love in Christ


is accomplished, being guarded, clearly, lest ever the abundance of the gifts
of God become for you a cause of heavier condemnation, having been disposed
ungratefully toward the benefactor. For to whom they committed much, he says,
they will demand of him the more.
QUESTION 5. Concerning the unwandering according to thought. ANSWER. That at
any rate one must know, that neither the keeping of any other commandment, nor
love itself toward God, nor that toward the neighbors, can we accomplish,
wandering at other times about other things with the thoughts. For neither is
it possible to make exact a craft or a science, passing at other times upon
another; nor even to master one, not having recognized the things proper to
the end. For the actions must be consequent upon the aim, since none of the
things according to reason is accomplished through the unfitting. Since
neither is the end of bronze-working naturally mastered through the works of
pottery, nor are athletic crowns accomplished from zeal about flute-playing;
but for each end the proper and fitting labor is sought. So that also the
ascesis of well-pleasing toward God according to the Gospel of Christ is
accomplished for us in withdrawal from the cares of the world and in complete
alienation from the distractions. Wherefore the Apostle, even though marriage
has been conceded and has been deemed worthy of blessing, set the occupations
from it against the cares about God, as these not being able to come together
with one another, saying: The unmarried man cares for the things of the Lord,
how he will please the Lord; but the one who has married cares for the things
of the world, how he will please the wife. Thus also the Lord testified to the
disciples after the sincere and unwandering disposition, saying: You are not
of this world. And from the contrary he testified that the world is unable to
receive the knowledge of God, nor to contain the Holy Spirit; For Father,
=31.921= he says, righteous, also the world did not know you; and, The Spirit
of truth, which the world cannot receive. One must then be loosed from the
bonds of attachment to life, at least the one who is truly going to follow
God; and this is accomplished through complete withdrawal and forgetfulness of
the old customs. So that unless we alienate ourselves both from fleshly
kinship and from communion of life, as it were passing over to another world
through the relation, according to the one who said, For our citizenship
exists in the heavens, it is impossible for us to master the aim of
well-pleasing toward God, the Lord having said definitively, that Thus
everyone of you who does not renounce all the things that belong to himself
cannot be my disciple. And having done this, it is fitting to keep our own
heart with all guarding, so as never to cast out the thought about God, or to
defile the memory of his wonders with imaginings of vain things; but through
continuous and pure memory to carry about, stamped in our souls, as an
inerasable seal, the holy thought of God. For thus love toward God comes about
for us, both stirring toward the working of the commandments of the Lord, and
being itself again preserved by them toward the lasting and unfalling. And
this the Lord shows, saying at one time: If you love me, keep my commandments,
and at another, that If you keep my commandments, you will remain in my love,
and still more compellingly: As I have kept the commandments of my Father, and
I remain in his love. From which he trains us, always setting forth as an aim
the will of the one who commanded for the work at hand, to direct the things
of the zeal toward him, as also elsewhere he says, that I have come down from
heaven, not that I may do my will, but the will of the Father who sent me. For
just as the crafts according to life, having set up certain aims proper to
themselves, fit together the particular workings consequently upon those; so
also for our works, one definition and rule


being laid upon, to do the commandments in a way well-pleasing to God, it is
impossible otherwise for the exactness of the work to be accomplished unless
it is completed according to the wish of the one who delivered it. And in the
exact zeal about the work toward the will of God it will be possible to be
joined to God through the memory. For just as the bronze-worker in the
working, if it should happen, of the axe, remembering the one who delivered it
to him, and carrying him in the thought, both conceives that shape and size,
and directs the working toward the =31.924= wish of the one who proposed it
(for if he forgets, he will make something else, or of another kind

the acceptance of the one who surpasses as of more worth, he despises the
blame of the lesser. And if it is so among men, the soul that is truly sober

QUESTION 6. That it is necessary to live apart. ANSWER. And living apart
according to the dwelling also contributes toward the unwandering for the
soul. For that to have the life mixed with those who are disposed fearlessly
and contemptuously toward the exact keeping of the commandments is harmful,
the word of Solomon also shows, teaching us that Do not be a companion to a
wrathful man; and do not dwell with a friend who is angry, lest you learn of
his ways, and take snares for your soul, and the, Come out from the midst of
them, and be separated, says the Lord, bears toward the same. Therefore, in
order that we may not receive provocations into sin either through eyes or
through ears, and according to what is hidden become accustomed to it, and as
it were certain types and characters


of the things seen and heard may remain in the soul unto destruction and
perdition, and in order that we may be able to remain in prayer, let us first
live apart according to the dwelling. For thus we would also master the custom
that went before, in which we lived as aliens to the commandments of Christ
(and this contest is not small, to master one's own habit; for a custom
confirmed through a long time takes the force of nature), and we will also be
able to wipe out the stains from sin by labor-loving prayer and by persistent
study of the wills of God; which study and prayer it is impossible to master
among the many things that drag the soul about and produce occupations of
life. And the, If anyone wishes to come after me, let him deny himself, when
would anyone being in these things be able to fulfill it? For we must, having
denied ourselves, and having taken up the cross of Christ, thus follow him.
And denial of oneself is the complete forgetfulness of the things that have
passed, and the withdrawal from one's own wills, which it is most difficult
for one living in the indifferent custom to accomplish, that I may not say
that it is altogether incapable of receiving it. But also toward taking up
one's own cross and following Christ the mixing with such a life is a
hindrance. For the preparation toward death on behalf of Christ, and the
putting to death of the members upon the earth, and to be arrayed toward every
danger =31.928= that comes upon us on behalf of the name of Christ, and to
have oneself without attachment toward the present life, this is to take up
one's own cross; toward which we see great hindrances coming to be for us from
the custom of the common life. And besides all the other things which are
many, a soul looking toward the multitude of those who transgress the law,
first does not have time to perceive its own sins, and to crush itself through
repentance upon the faults; and in the comparison with the worse it even
acquires a certain imagination of accomplishment; then, being pulled away from
the more worthy memory of God by the tumults and the occupations which the
common life naturally produces, it is not only deprived of leaping in God and
being gladdened in God, and of luxuriating in the Lord, and of being sweetened
by his words, so as to be able to say: I remembered God, and I was gladdened;
and, How sweet to my throat are your oracles, beyond honey to my mouth; but it
is also accustomed unto contempt and complete forgetfulness of his judgments,
than which it would suffer no greater nor more destructive evil.
QUESTION 7. Concerning the need to live with those of like mind toward the aim
of well-pleasing toward God; and that it is both difficult and dangerous to
live alone. Since then the word has given us full assurance that the life with
those who have themselves contemptuously about the commandments of the Lord is
dangerous, we consequently think it right to learn whether the one who has
withdrawn from these ought to live apart by himself, or to live with brothers
of like mind who have chosen the same aim of piety. ANSWER. I learn that the
way of life of the more upon the same is more useful toward many things.
First, that not even toward the needs of the body is each of us sufficient to
himself, but in the providing of the necessary things we need one another. For
just as the foot has one power, and is in need of another, and without the
help of the remaining members it neither finds its own working possible or
sufficient to itself toward continuance, nor has consolation of what is
lacking; so also in the solitary life both what is present to us becomes
useless, and what is lacking is without consolation, the creator God having
defined that we need one another, as it is written, in order that we may also
be joined to one another. And without this the word of the love of Christ also
does not permit each to look at his own. For love, he says, does not =31.929=
seek the things of itself. But the life that lives apart has one aim, the care
of the needs


of each. And this is clearly fighting against the law of love, which the
Apostle fulfilled, not seeking his own advantage, but that of the many, in
order that they might be saved. Next, in isolation neither will each easily
recognize his own deficiency, not having the one who convicts him, and who
sets him right in meekness and compassion. For conviction might often come
even from an enemy, producing a desire of healing in the well-disposed; but
the healing of a sin is accomplished knowledgeably by the one who has loved
sincerely. For he who loves, he says, trains carefully. Whom it is without
resource to find in solitude, not having been previously united according to
the life; so that that thing said happens to him: Woe to the one, because if
he falls, there is not the one who raises him. And the commandments also come
to be more easily by the more upon the same, but no longer by the one; for in
the working of the one the other is hindered. For example, in the visiting of
the one who is sick the reception of the stranger, and in the giving and
sharing of the needs (and especially when the services come to be through a
long time), the zeal about the works; so that from this the greatest and
intense commandment toward salvation is left lacking; neither the hungry being
fed, nor the naked being clothed. Who then would choose to prefer the idle and
fruitless life to the fruitful one accomplished according to the commandment
of the Lord? And if also all who have been taken on in one hope of the calling
are one body, having Christ as head; and each members of one another, if we
are not fitted together from agreement toward the harmony of one body in the
Holy Spirit; and each of us chooses the solitude, not serving the management
toward the common advantage according to what is well-pleasing to God, but
fulfilling his own passion of self-pleasing; how, being split off and divided,
can we preserve the relation and service of the members toward one another, or
the subjection toward our head, which is Christ? For it is possible neither to
rejoice with the one being glorified, nor to suffer with the one suffering, in
the standing-apart of the life, each not being able reasonably to know the
things of the neighbor. Then also, one not sufficing =31.932= to receive all
the spiritual gifts, but according to the proportion of the faith in each the
supply of the Spirit coming to be, in the communion of the life the proper
gift of each becomes common of those living together. For to one is given a
word of wisdom, and to another a word of knowledge, to another faith, to
another prophecy, to another gifts of healings, and the things that follow; of
which each the one who receives has not more through himself than through the
others. So that of necessity in the social life the working of the Holy Spirit
in the one passes over unto all together. The one then who lives by himself
perhaps has one gift, and he makes this useless through idleness, having
buried it in himself; which how great a danger it has, you all know who have
read the Gospels; but in the living-together of the more he both enjoys his
own, multiplying it by the giving, and he harvests the things of the others as
his own. And the life upon the same also has more good things, which it is not
easy to number all. For both toward the keeping of the good things given us by
God it is more useful than solitude, and toward the guard against the plot
from outside of the enemy the waking by those who are watchful is more safe,
if ever it should happen to the one to nod off that sleep unto death, which we
were taught by David to pray not to happen to us, saying: Enlighten my eyes,
lest I sleep unto death; and for the one who sins the withdrawal from the sin
is easier, being put to shame by the condemnation that comes to be from the
more in agreement, so that one might fit to him the, Sufficient for such a one
is this punishment, that by the more; and for the one who accomplishes there
is much full assurance both in the testing of the many and in the consent of
the work. For if upon the mouth of two and three witnesses every word will
stand, much more clearly


more firmly will the one who completes the good work be confirmed in the
testimony of the more. And danger attends the monastic life besides the things
said. First and greatest is that of self-pleasing. For having no one who will
be able to test his work, he will think he has reached the perfect of the
commandment; then, having shut up the habit always unexercised, he neither
recognizes his own shortcomings, nor comes to know the progress in the works,
=31.933= by having taken away all matter of the working of the commandments.
For in what will he show humility, having no one than whom he will show
himself more humble? In what compassion, having been cut off from the
communion of the more? And toward long-suffering how will he train himself, no
one opposing his wills? And if someone says he is content with the teaching of
the divine Scriptures toward the accomplishment of the manners, he does a like
thing to the one who learns to carpenter, but never carpenters, and who is
taught bronze-working, but does not choose to bring the teachings forward into
work. To whom the Apostle would say: Not the hearers of the law are righteous
with God, but the doers of the law will be justified. For behold the Lord,
through excess of love of man, was not content with the teaching from the word
alone, but so as to deliver to us exactly and clearly the example of humility
in the perfection of love, having girded himself he washed the feet of the
disciples. Whom then will you wash? whom will you serve? of whom will you be
last, yourself living by yourself? And the good and pleasant, the dwelling of
the brothers upon the same, which the Holy Spirit likens to oil of the head of
the high priest giving off scent, how will it be fulfilled in the dwelling by
oneself? A stadium then of contest, and a good journey of progress, and
continuous training, and study of the commandments of the Lord, is also the
dwelling of the brothers upon the same; having as aim the glory of God
according to the commandment of our Lord Jesus Christ who said: Thus let your
light shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your
Father in the heavens; and preserving the character of the saints related in
the Acts, concerning whom it is written: And all who believed were upon the
same, and they had all things common; and again: And of the multitude of those
who believed the heart and the soul was one; and not one said that any of the
things belonging to him was his own, but all things were common to them.
QUESTION 8. Concerning renunciation. Whether one must first renounce all
things, and thus approach the way of life according to God. ANSWER. Our Lord
Jesus Christ, after much =31.936= and through more things a strong proof,
saying to all: If anyone comes toward me, let him deny himself, and take up
his cross, and follow me; and again: Thus then everyone of you who does not
renounce all the things that belong to himself cannot be my disciple; we
consider that the command stretches upon more things, from which the
alienation is necessary. For we also renounce the devil before all, and the
passions of the flesh, we who have refused the hidden things of shame, and
bodily kinships, and fellowships of men, and a custom of life that fights
against the exactness of the Gospel of salvation. And more necessary than
these, he renounces himself, the one who has stripped off the old man with his
deeds, the one being corrupted according to the desires of deceit. And he also
renounces all attachments of the world that are able to hinder the aim of
piety. Parents then such a one will consider true those who have begotten him
in Christ Jesus through the Gospel, and brothers those who have received the
same Spirit of adoption, and he will attend to all possessions as to things
belonging to another, which is the case. And in one word, he to whom through
Christ the whole world has been crucified, and he himself to the


world, how can he still be a sharer of the cares of the world? our Lord Jesus
Christ leading both the hatred of the soul and the denial of himself to an
extreme

=31.937= Thus the one who is strongly held by the desire of following Christ
can no longer be turned toward anything of this life, not toward the affection
of parents or of household, when it is opposing the commands of the Lord (for
then even the, If anyone comes toward me, and does not hate his father, and
the mother, and the rest, has time); not toward human fear, so as through it
to shrink from some of the things that are advantageous, which the saints
accomplished, saying: One must obey God rather than men; not toward the
laughter from those outside upon the good things, so as to be defeated by
their belittling. And if someone should wish more exactly and more clearly to
recognize the tension yoked with the desire of those who follow the Lord, let
him remember the Apostle relating the things according to himself unto our
teaching, and saying: If anyone thinks to have trusted in flesh, I more;
circumcision on the eighth day, from the race of Israel, of the tribe of
Benjamin, a Hebrew from Hebrews, according to law a Pharisee, according to
zeal persecuting the Church, according to righteousness that in law having
become blameless; but whatever things were gains to me, these I have
considered a loss through Christ. But then I also consider all things to be a
loss through the surpassing of the knowledge of Christ Jesus our Lord, through
whom I was deprived of all things, and I consider them to be dung, that I may
gain Christ. For if (that I may say something bold, but true), if to the most
cast-out things of the body, and those to be spat upon, and of which we
especially press toward the alienation, he likened even the legal advantages
given by God toward a time, as hindrances of the knowledge of Christ, and of
the righteousness in him, and of the conformation toward his death, what would
one say about the things that have been customary among men? And what need is
there to confirm the word by our reasonings and by the examples of the saints?
it being possible to set beside the words of the Lord themselves, and through
them to put the fearful soul to shame, he clearly and unanswerably testifying
in saying: Thus then everyone of you who does not renounce all the things that
belong to himself cannot be my disciple; and elsewhere after the, If you wish
to be perfect, having first said: Go, sell your belongings, and give to the
poor; and then having added the, Come, follow me. And the parable of the
merchant also is clear to every well-disposed person as bearing toward this.
For it is like, he says, the kingdom of the heavens to a man who is a
merchant, seeking good =31.940= pearls, who having found one very costly
pearl, having gone away sold all things whatever he had, and bought it. For it

art? and not rather a danger, being carried away from the right word into the
care of the body? For those at any rate who have gathered the weakness for
themselves from a wicked way of life one must use the healing of the body as
some type and underwriting toward the care of the soul, as has been said
before. For the holding-off from the things that harm according to the medical
word is also beneficial for us, the choice of the useful, the keeping of the
commands. And let even the change itself of the flesh from weakness toward
good condition become a consolation toward our not despairing of the soul, as
not being able from the sins toward its proper completeness again through the
repentance to return. Neither then must one flee the craft wholly, nor is it
consequent to have all the hopes upon it. But as we use the farming, and we
ask the fruits from God; and we entrust the rudder to the helmsman, and we
pray to God to be saved from the sea, so also introducing a physician, when
reason concedes, we do not stand away from the hope toward God. And to me the
craft also appears to contribute not little toward self-control. For I see,
that it both cuts off luxuries, and accuses fullness, and a variety of way of
life, and an invented fuss of seasonings, as


unprofitable, it sends away; and the whole, it calls lack a mother of health,
so that even according to this the counsel from it is not useless for us.
Whether then we sometimes use the commands of the medical art, or we refuse,
according to some of the words given beforehand, let the aim of well-pleasing
toward God be kept, and let the benefit of the soul be managed, and let the
command of the Apostle be fulfilled, who said: Whether then you eat, or drink,
or do something, do all things unto the glory of God.
