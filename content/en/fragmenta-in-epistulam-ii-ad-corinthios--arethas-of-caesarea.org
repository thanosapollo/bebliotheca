* Fragments on the Second Epistle to the Corinthians
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

{2 Cor 2:1-2} Not to come to you again in grief. for if I grieve you. he looses
the opposing through these; that no one may say: if you are heavy and burdensome
sojourning by plunging us with grief you ought not come at all. he says then:
and indeed for this reason one must come, making a sign of your love toward me
that my rebuke reach you sharply. necessarily then one must go to those who love
me, that I may also share the gain with you. since this grief of yours is joy to
me and unsurpassed gladness, I must give myself to you; that I may have you
rejoicing together of the joy that comes to me from your grief taking you as
fellow-dancers. If their grief is a bringer of joy to him, and the joy of the
teacher is common toward the disciples, how is the declining of the sojourn
toward them judged for him? but we say that while they are still more incomplete
he is borne around with the weakness of the disciples, and he disposes the word
together with the things appearing to them, drawing them from everywhere as he
can and pulling them toward being persuadable to himself.
{2 Cor 5:4} Not because we have feared death, and it comes upon us. for this
reason we groan, but we groan longing to put on the incorruption with glory.
{2 Cor 12:4} The not permitted for a man to speak I think more exactly upon no
one of men being able to take such things to speak—as also in the 661 Gospels he
says concerning the Lord himself, that he was teaching according to authority
but not as the scribes—or upon not speaking out to another the things heard. for
if this, how does this same one who stretches himself to tell these things to no
one, set beside many of these in the present letters? for not having been
discipled to Christ according to the sojourn in flesh how does he say: I
received from the Lord, and concerning virgins I do not have a command of the
Lord, and other such things. but he would not have heard these at another time
or then. how then would it not have been permitted him to say, he has said? he
does not then say that it was not permitted to say the things heard to others,
but he says these things are such, and so showing the greatness of the one
mystagoguing as they both are and are thought of the magnificence of God only,
but not of human cheapness and humility.
