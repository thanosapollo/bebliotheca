1 {3Jο 1, 1}3 The Word knows one beginning, not two, as the Manichaeans say,
nor a first cause and a second cause and a third cause, as Plato, Basilides,
Marcion, Arius, Eunomius say; for the Father is called beginning and the Son
beginning and the Spirit beginning because of the co-eternity of the Trinity,
not because there are three beginnings, since we also call the Father God and
the Son God and the Spirit God, not as worshiping three gods, but because of
the consubstantiality of the one Godhead of the three hypostases. 2 {3Jο 1,
1}3 Through this he overthrows the heresy of Sabellius, signifying the
hypostases of Father and Son; for neither does the church glorify a
son-fatherhood, as the Libyan said while mythologizing. Sabellius, Noetus,
Novatus, Paul of Samosata, Marcellus say one hypostasis of the Trinity. He was
not uttered, but in-hypostatic, and not only in-hypostatic, but also God.
Photinus says that a Word from the Father, being without hypostasis as our own
is, was changed into flesh. Since some of the heretics, hearing that the
Father begot the Son, are scandalized, thinking that a flowing or a passion
comes about concerning the Father, which things are accustomed at times to
happen in the case of dense bodies. The evangelist, forestalling their
simplicity, from the proem called the Son Word once and a second time and a
third time, in order that he might present the passionless character of the
begetting; for as our word is begotten from the mind without flowing and
without diminution and without passion (and likewise also our spirit), so also
is the begetting of the Son; wherefore it was also said: "My heart belched
forth a good word," which is God, who alone "is good." 3 {3Jο 1, 2}3 Instead
of: he was co-eternal with the Father, existing in the nature of the Father,
having as most ancient place the nature of the one who begot; for the
beginning oversteps the ages; for the Father is called beginning of the Son
according to the from-whom alone; for in the cause the Father will be
preconceived of the Son, yet he will not be pre-beheld in existence. And
everywhere he inserts the "was," sending the Son back upon the beginningless;
for he did not say he had a beginning, but in the beginning he was. 4 {3Jο 1,
3}3 He casts out another heresy, teaching that through the Son all generated
things were created, yet not through angels good or evil as some badly
supposed, of whom are Plato, Simon, Basilides, Nicolaus, Menander, the
Borborian, Satornilus, Cerinthus. Wherefore some also forbid marriage and
begetting, Satornilus and the Apostolics. 5 {3Jο 1, 3}3 The saying has
emphasis, that not the Son alone created all things, but also the Father and
the Spirit; for whatever the Father does he works through the Son in the Holy
Spirit, and where one of the three hypostases is said to do, the remaining
hypostases of the one essence are also co-conceived. 6 {3Jο 1, 4}3 All things
whatever the Father made through a Son were made alive from him. 7 {3Jο 1, 4}3
The Word did not come to be in a human being, but in a certain manner he
himself, having strengthened the virgin through the Spirit and having dwelt in
her womb, became a foundation. And in him he built the rational ensouled flesh
united to him from Mary, which is life. For as he himself is life, so also the
flesh united to him, of which those who partake are made alive, being
delivered from death and corruption and being sent into the enjoyment of the
unceasing goods of the age to come, which is the truly life. 8 {3Jο 1, 4}3 The
incarnation of the Only-begotten light-leads human beings toward the true
knowledge of God and toward every form of virtue, through which the making
alive comes about, which is the enjoyment of the goods. 10 col 1 {3Jο 1, 5}3
Darkness is the whole world by comparison with the inexpressible and
unapproachable light. Yet also the Son lights the mind toward the things to be
done and the truth always prevails, yet the falsehood does not get the better
of the truth. Wherefore he himself also said: "I have overcome the world,"
instead of: I have refuted every deceived heresy and have gotten the better of
all the passions, as many as are adventitious, yet not natural and necessary.
Which things he underwent willingly, in order that he might show the truth of
the


incarnation, and that the natural and necessary things are not base. For
through this it was drawing near to the flesh of the Word of God, not in order
that it might be strengthened, but in order that it might be destroyed in him;
for the unseemly passions he did not undergo even according to imagination.
Darkness and the world now are to be understood by comparison with the
inexpressible and unapproachable light. 11 {3Jο 1, 6}3 John is sent as having
passed from the desert into the inhabited country, in order that he might
proclaim the Christ to the peoples. And the Lord also himself was called sent,
not as having passed from places to places, for the divine is unmoved, but as
having been moved as it were from the bosom of the Father and having come
toward our poverty, on account of which he is also said to have humbled
himself. Whenever therefore the scripture says the words fitting to the
economy, if it says that "he emptied himself," it signifies the self-sovereign
and kingly, that he was incarnate willingly, but if it says that he was sent,
it teaches that he was not incarnate without the Father. And these things we
speak as human beings; for there is one will of the Father and of the Son,
even if we conceive the difference of the hypostases of the one Godhead and
essence; for thus we say that the Son alone has been incarnate, not the
Father, not the Spirit. 12 {3Jο 1, 7}3 Through this John is greater than the
other prophets, that those indeed proclaimed the one expected, but this one,
having seen him and having been persuaded through signs that this is he,

things did not know him at all, who were the idolaters and slaves of the

having been incarnate, he was made known to those who beheld him with their
eyes, or that he came toward his own, who seemed to be his own according to
the "from whom is the Christ according to the flesh." 22 {3Jο 1, 13}3 There is
no longer need of the sacrifice of oxen as pleasing to God according to the
type of the law, as the blood of the Christ suffices toward justification. 23
{3ςο 1, 14}3 When the Word became flesh, he was not changed as to the essence,
but even having become visible through the flesh he remained having his own
glory as only-begotten Son of God and Father; for the one pitching tent is
other according to essence than the tent. And he did not say body, lest some
say that he wore a heavenly body, nor did he say he became a human being, lest
others say he took not the outer but the inner human being, nor did he say
flesh came to be from the Word, lest others say he was changed from this into
that. The flesh then is also called body, but not in every way is a body also
flesh. Wherefore the Lord also said: "he who eats my flesh" and elsewhere:
"handle me and see, that a spirit does not have flesh and bones as you behold
me having." 25 {3Jο 1, 14}3 How did we behold? Through the star of the magi,
the angels, the shepherds, Anna, Simeon, Gabriel, the unutterable birth of the
virgin, the voice of the Father that bears witness to him, the visitation of
the Spirit upon him and the myriad God-signs and healings. 26 {3Jο 1, 16}3 As
root of all the goods and a spring distributing to many of his own goods he is
not diminished, yet from the things of which he indeed supplies, and the
creation receives, it is clear that the Christ is God. 27 {3Jο 1, 17}3 Instead
of the grace in Adam, from which we fell, the Christ through the washing has
given us indwelling of Holy Spirit. 28 {3Jο 1, 18}3 Not only the Father is


invisible, but also the Son; for even if he was seen in flesh, yet not
according to the essence, which not even angels behold as it is; for many
indeed know God, but the essence no one knows. Not the hidden body is called
this invisible, but the incorporeal which is not by nature to be seen even by
mind. 29 {3Jο 1, 18}3 Through the trustworthy person he establishes this
doctrine as lofty and above human; "for who knows the things" of God and
Father to say, except the Son begotten from him and his Holy Spirit that "also
knows the depths of God." 30 {3Jο 1, }3 Instead of: he spoke these things
bearing witness that Jesus is the Christ, yet not John himself, as some badly
suspected concerning him.

31 {3Jο 1, 23}3 It is a work of virtue not to be lifted up by the praises of
those men and to be beautified in ornaments not one's own. He said: I am a
voice of one crying in the wilderness; make straight the way of the Lord, as
Isaiah the prophet said. 32 {3Jο 1, 23}3 As a voice of a word runs before, so
also, the Christ being Word, John runs before as a voice. 33 {3Jο 1, 23}3 If
Isaiah had not pre-announced me to proclaim the Christ, who is Lord, I would
not have dared to speak. 34 {3Jο 1, 24}3 As more exact concerning the
knowledge of the scriptures these came, whom John gently convicted of being
uneducated and mindless as being ignorant of the passage said concerning him
of the prophet well-worn among them. 35 {3Jο 1, 25}3 As it seems they had such
traditions from their teachers that it was permitted only to those to baptize,
that is either the Christ or Elijah or the prophet. 36 {3Jο 1, 26}3 John
himself is a trustworthy witness of his own things, seeing that he uttered
nothing great concerning himself, but even something very humble, having
called the water of his own baptism bare. 37 {3Jο 1, 26}3 Until the time of
the economy the Christ, being present in the midst of all, was unknown. And
after a little; and standing in the midst and unknown he says the Christ
because of the wrapping of the flesh. 38 {3Jο 1, 29}3 He is a lamb as being
given for remission of sins of those being regenerated "through a washing of
regeneration," and having said taking away he shows that he always takes away.
39 {3Jο 1, 30}3 The Christ has come to be before John as loosing "the sin of
the world" and baptizing in Holy Spirit as the water of the baptism of John
being bare. And he is called a man as having been born from a woman "according
to the flesh" or that he was thirty years old; (for they call a man the one
who has stepped upon this time) or that he was perfect in every virtue both
practical and contemplative or that he is bridegroom of all the rational
nature, which is the church. As the church has the person of a woman the
Christ is called a man as being bridegroom of the souls of the faithful. 41
{3Jο 1, 31}3 For the sake of proclaiming the Christ and making him evident to
the people John was baptizing. 42 {3Jο 1, 32}3 This has been said by way of
economy; for it is inseparable from the Son as also from the Father; for to
see something of the Trinity sensibly is impossible. 43 {3Jο 1, 33}3 As
spending time from first age in the deserts he establishes the unsuspicious
character of the testimony as having learned him through a revelation. 45 {3Jο
1, 35}3 This is found a third day, from which the Pharisees made the meeting
toward John, in which they were inquiring of him who he is. 46 {3Jο 1, 36}3
Because of the slackness of the hearers he uses the same words; for it is a
virtue of a teacher to use the same things toward the words being fixed in the
souls of the hearers. 47 {3Jο 1, 38}3 Since those following Christ are not
able to overtake him, he turns toward them condescendingly, showing them his
own face, in order that they may know that he is the one who fulfills "the
law" and teaches the things in it. 48 {3Jο 1, 38}3 And he asks not as not
knowing, but calling them out into converse. 49 {3Jο 1, 39}3 This is not
contrary to the thing said "the Son of man has not where he may lay the head";
for even if he remained in a house, yet he did not acquire a lodging of his
own. 50 {3Jο 1, 45}3 Him whom you evangelized to me, he says, how is it
possible that the Christ is from Nazareth, whom the


scripture said is to come from Bethlehem? But there indeed in Bethlehem he was
born, and he was enrolled Nazareth as fatherland as having been reared there
and spending time until perfect age. 51 {3Jο 1, 47}3 Great glory and boast is
to have from the truth itself, which is Christ, a testimony of a pure heart,
which sees God with the mind and is guided toward the saving faith to run
toward the Christ without guile not as a Jew or as Herod. 52 {3Jο 1, 49}3
Nathanael was wholly ready. Wherefore having been persuaded from this bare
saying alone he confessed him to be the Christ, who is Son of God by nature
and truth and king of those truly faithful; for as still imperfect he did not
call him king of the creation, but only of Israel according to the thing said:
"and the people of Jacob became the Lord's portion, Israel a measured line of
his inheritance." 53 {3Jο 1, 51}3 Sometimes simply, and sometimes doubled the
savior uses it, presenting the firmness of the things said. And the word is
Hebrew, making clear the "may it be so" that you be found faithful so as to
see the heaven opened and the things following. And not sensibly, he says, is
it possible to see the heaven opened, but only with a mind looking at the
angels approaching upon ministering to Jesus. And the amen is also said
instead of really and truly, which is also better here. 54 {3Jο 1, 51}3 Since
Nathanael named the Christ king of Israel alone, he educates him through this,
that the Christ was not a bare human being, since somehow angels serve him. 55
{3Jο 1, 51}3 He shows through this that not in seeming, but in truth he became
a human being. Having been incarnate from Mary as being alone sinless he was
saving for himself the "according to image" which the first human being had
who had come to be "according to image and according to likeness" of God. 56

marriage. 57 {3Jο 2, 4}3 And he finds fault with the mother as having reminded
out of season God who has no need of a reminder, instead of saying do not
think me to be only a human being, but also God. And the time of my

4}3 He finds fault with the mother urging him toward wonder-working out of

the marriage in Cana the first sign, but first of all. 62 {3Jο 2, 11}3 He was
opening to them a door of faith through the God-signs, revealing himself, or

sacrificed according to law, in order that he might bring in instead the
spiritual worships. 65 {3Jο 2, 16}3 And the Christ calls God his own Father as
himself alone existing from him according to nature. 66 {3Jο 2, 16}3 The house
of God is indeed a house of merchandise, but not of these fleshly things that
go "into a belly," but of the honorable and beautiful pearls, which adorn the
face of the soul. And these are the pure faith and the remaining virtues. 67

3, 5}3 The water cleanses the body and the Spirit hallows the invisible soul.
The water is taken in the rank of a mother, and the Spirit in the rank of God
who forms. The sensible water is recast toward a divine power and hallows
those in whom it is regenerated. The water has a difference toward the Spirit
in thought alone, since it is the same in the energy; the water is a symbol of

Spirit, which is God and Father, begets spirit, which is the Son and God. The
Christ therefore is consubstantial with the Father according to the Godhead
and consubstantial with the mother according to the flesh, even if the Christ
is one from both, unchangeably and unconfusedly. And according to the word now
canonically set forth by the Lord we are reshaped through Christ, our mind
being formed into a spiritual quality. 76 {3Jο 3, 6}3 If Mary is rational
ensouled flesh, also her fruit is consubstantial with her, rational ensouled
flesh and not a phantom or something other besides the flesh that bore, the
flesh of the Word that has a rational soul, in which "all the fullness of the
Godhead dwelt bodily." 77 {3Jο 3, 8}3 One hears the voice of the Holy Spirit
through the prophets. 78 {3Jο 3, 11}3 He said the "our" as the Christ having
in himself by nature the Father and the Spirit. 79 {3Jο 3, 12}3 He calls the
baptism earthly, being divine and spiritual, since it is completed on earth
toward hallowing of the human beings who are on earth. The birth of the
washing is called earthly by comparison with the begetting of the Son that
came about from the essence of the Father. 80 {3Jο 3, 13}3 From the lesser
essence he names his whole self just as from the Godhead he calls his whole
self. 81 {3Jο 3, 13}3 After the union according to hypostasis he makes common
the things of the flesh and of the Godhead according to the energies, not
according to a change of the things from which is the Christ. And both the
descent and the ascent are energies. 82 {3Jο 3, 13}3 The body of the Lord is
not heavenly; for it is subject to passions. And as being one even with flesh
he is called Son of man. 83 {3Jο 3, 14}3 Just as the bronze serpent was not
the venomous beast, but a likeness, so also the flesh of the Lord. It was
flesh according to truth, yet not flesh of sin, but a likeness of flesh of
sin. 84 {3Jο 3, 14}3 The being lifted up makes clear the Christ becoming
manifest and notable. 85 {3Jο 3, 16}3 Here again he makes the whole common,
not knowing a separation after the union; for the flesh having been sacrificed
for the life of the world and given into death the Son is said to have been
given into death although remaining passionless as God, his own flesh alone
suffering its own things, when he conceded and wished. 86 {3Jο 3, 16}3 The
Arians say that through this the Son is called only-begotten, that he alone
has come to be from God. Toward whom it must be said that some also call the
sun only-begotten inasmuch as it is sun alone, but it is no longer also a son.



87 {3Jο 3, 17}3 If the Christ had come upon judging the world, he would have

was then handing over the evangelical baptism, which had much difference
toward that of John; for the Christ was baptizing through the hand of the
apostles as he says clearly in the things following. 92 {3Jο 3, 23}3 Neither
the baptism of John nor that of the disciples was providing the Holy Spirit,
but in order that they might not, going about each one, proclaim the Christ,
they set up the baptism, in order that they might lead the crowds running
toward them to the Christ. 93 {3Jο 3, 23}3 The "water of sprinkling" that has
"the ash of the heifer" was making a cleansing of involuntary sins and was
cleansing those from a touch of dead; and the baptism of John was providing
remission also of the voluntary to those genuinely repenting, and that of the
Christ of all the sins, but it was also providing Holy Spirit and worth of
adoption. 94 {3Jο 3, 25}3 By economy a seeking came about while John was still
living, in order that through his voice the difference of the baptisms might
appear. 95 {3Jο 3, 29}3 The Christ is bridegroom and the church bride and the
bridal chamber the place of the baptism, where the spiritual joining comes
about, since all things are bright and full of joy and gladness. For what is
more blessed than to be cleansed of sins and to be glued to God and "to become
a partaker of divine nature" through the participation of the Holy Spirit; for
these things are the "earnest," and the more perfect things in the future,
when again the bridegroom having entered leads in with him the virgin souls
and the door of the bridal chamber is shut and only those being within enjoy
the gladness and no longer does anyone enter there. Until then therefore today
the present age, either the particular death of each or the door of the bridal
chamber has been opened to the souls wishing to be wedded to Christ. 96 {3Jο
3, 29}3 Through the baptism the Christ is wedded to the church that has been
regenerated. And this spiritual yoking through the teaching word joins to God.
The bride is a chaste virgin through the rightness of the doctrines, the same
is also a woman of the Christ as begetting good deeds not from herself, but
through the Christ who sows in her, of whom none of the generated can be
bridegroom, since none of them is a giver of goods prototypically. Every
rational nature therefore of both angels and human beings is a bride. 97 {3Jο
3, 30}3 Neither does the Christ increase nor is John lessened, but after the
manner of a morning star John is covered by the intelligible sun. 98 {3Jο 3,
30}3 The Christ as God increases the more being made manifest, and I must
remain in the things in which I am. 99 {3Jο 3, 31}3 Having said coming he does
not signify a passing from the things above toward the things below, but that
always remaining in Father above he comes by a relation of condescension
toward these, of whom he is above. Whenever therefore he says coming, he
speaks as concerning God, and whenever he says having come, he speaks as
concerning a human being, since he himself is God and a human being. He who is
from the root of the Father has by nature the preeminence of the Father, being
above all the things that have been made as their God and maker. 100 {3Jο 3,
32}3 He speaks more humanly because of the flesh; for being all-perfect by
nature he knows all things from himself. 101 {3Jο 3, 32}3 He said this not as
of some not having been persuaded, but as marveling at the mindlessness of
those who disbelieve. 102 {3Jο 3, 33}3 The "he sealed" instead of he was fully
assured and has a demonstration and a seal. 103 {3Jο 3, 34}3 The Son is said
to have been
