* Fragments on the Epistle to the Philippians
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

In this epistle he writes to clerics, not to laymen; for which reason he did
not even need more ethical [matters], but his zeal through the epistle is that
humility be accomplished in them. And through the exhortation itself he shows
their exactness of the rest of life. For rarely does anyone both sin and is
puffed up; for the matter of puffing-up touches those living most finely and
by this very thing for the most part being lifted up. Phil 1:7 Everywhere and
through all things it is shown that my sufferings are not unprofitable nor the
defenses. From your faith I make a proof, your persistence. For whether I make
a defense being accused on account of those who believe, of whom you also are,
or I wish to confirm the gospel to some, from your persistence; or to show
that I do not unprofitably endure bonds, from your faith. Phil 2:7 And being
found in fashion as a man. The as is not of likeness but of intensification,
such as is How good is God to Israel, and How I loved your law, Lord, instead
of I loved very much. Phil 2:16 Instead of holding to the word of life. And to
Timothy he also writes: take heed to yourself, instead of attend to yourself.
Phil 3:8a If through Christ he considers [them] loss, they were not evils; for
they were gains. And he said I have considered; for it was not loss, but by
comparison of the excess of the better things he considers [them] loss. 314
Phil 3:8b The dung is a part of stalk, and the stalk and the ear once from the
seed. This then the dung once most useful for the seed to be born. For just as
from seed root and stalk and ear, so from the faith of Abraham as if from seed
that from those in the law, and the stalk of the prophets and the ear already
announcing beforehand Christ in the secret. And the harvest of these and the
threshing in the first coming of the savior, whose winnowing-fan is in the
hand and he will thoroughly cleanse the threshing-floor. When then the stalk
of the law ceased, and as dung circumcision was separated, keeping of sabbaths
and of the others, and the pure wheat of faith that was hidden away in the
prophetic stalk was stored in the storehouses of the common master. This then
the name of the dung shows the law as proper; and just as without the dung it
was not possible for seed to come to be, so neither without the law for the
perfection of grace to be accomplished. Phil 3:13 Forgetting the things
behind, not as evils, but what he says is of this sort: I am not occupied in
recalling my past contests, but in taking thought, what I shall accomplish
into the things following. Phil 4:2 And some say Euodia to be the wife of the
jailer, and Syntyche the husband.
