Come then, let us also make mention of the earlier things, if we are able at
all; of which at the beginning it occurred to us to write nothing, from
disbelief that we would survive; and then the body, being in such a state, did
not allow leisure for these things. And when time had again gone on, it seemed
one of the impossible things both to remember each thing and to tell it with
accuracy; better then to be wholly silent than to mar so many deeds. And many
refusals came to me about these things, both toward the god and toward the
companions who were always asking me to speak and to compose about them. But
now, so many years and times later, dream-visions compel us to bring them
somehow into the open. And yet this much at least I can say, that from the
very beginning the god commanded that the dreams be written down; and this was
the first of the prescriptions. And I made the record of the dreams, dictating
whenever I could not do it with my own hand; yet I did not add either in what
circumstances each came, or what sort of things resulted from them, but it was
enough for me as it were to discharge the rite toward the god, both because of
the weakness of the body, as I said, and because I would never have hoped that
the god would go so far in providence; and let Adrasteia be invoked by me
together with him. And still, because I did not begin writing everything from
the start, as if stung I even let the rest go, some as one would willingly,
some unwillingly; and I found other paths of thanks toward the god; since I
think the record is no fewer than thirty myriads of lines, but it is not easy,
I suppose, to go through them, nor to fit them to each thing as the times
stood; and besides this there are some that were also scattered in the
manifold ruin and disorder of the household about these times. It remains then
to tell chief points, some from one place

For when I was brought from Italy, having gathered many and all kinds of
things in the body from the continuous toils and storms which I met going away
through Thrace and Macedonia, still being ill when I left home, there was much
perplexity for the physicians, not that they could not help, but they could
not even recognize what the whole thing was. And the hardest and most
perplexing of all was that I was shut off from breath, and with much trouble
and disbelief I would scarcely ever breathe, forcibly and barely, and
continuous chokings attended in the neck and the sinews had bristled and there
was need of more covering than I could bear; and besides, other things beyond
telling troubled me. It seemed then needful to use the hot waters, if I might
become somewhat easier, or somehow better endure the air; for it was already
the season of winter, and they are not far from the city. Here first the
Savior began to give oracles. For he commanded me to go forth unshod, and I
cried out in the dream as if it were waking and upon a dream fulfilled, Great
is Asclepius, the command is fulfilled. These things I seemed to cry out as I
went forward. After this a summons and an arrival from Smyrna to Pergamum with
good fortune. And the things from here it is not according to man to narrate,
but one must attempt, as I proposed, to go through some of them in a rush. And
if anyone shall wish to know the most accurate of the things that have come to
us from the god, it is time for him to seek the parchments and the dreams
themselves. For he will find healings of every kind and certain dialogues and
speeches at length and all sorts of visions and all the predictions and
oracles about all kinds of matters, some in prose, some that came in verse,
and all of them worthy of greater thanks to the god than one would guess. But
now let us begin from somewhere here, how when we were in the temple, on the
first of the nights the god appeared to my foster-father in the form of
Salvius, now consul (and who Salvius was we did not yet then

wish of necessity to mention the paradoxical.

From where then would one begin, when they are many and of all kinds, and at
the same time not all in memory, except at least of the gratitude for them? He
sent me to Chios, saying he was sending me for purification. So we went away
by the way toward Smyrna, and making it in much unpleasantness and thinking we
were without a patron and were in truth sailing on our own, once we were
outside the sanctuary. And in what way all were struck in Smyrna, when they
saw me appear contrary to their hopes, what would one say? And when we were at
Clazomenae, it seemed needful to cross straight to Phocaea. And when we were
about the islands, Drymousa and Pele, some breeze of the east wind began, and
as we went further a bright east wind already, and at last an extraordinary
blast broke out. And the ship, lifted from the prow, squatted on the stern and
nearly sank; then it was flooded from this side and from that; then it was
turned out toward the open sea. And sweat and uproar of sailors and all the
cries of those sailing, for some of the companions were also sailing; and for
me it was enough to say, O Asclepius. And having been in danger in many ways
of all kinds, and at last about the landing itself overturned and driven off
countless times and causing much agony to those watching, we were saved barely
and with difficulty. And when night came, the god commands the purification to
be made, indicating from what. And it was indeed no less than from

And these are the chief points of the manifestation, as I would have valued it
above everything even to be able to go through each thing with accuracy. And
it was indeed the middle of winter and a great north wind and frost, and the
pebbles were so bound to one another by the ice as to resemble a continuous
crystal, and the water as one would expect in such air. And when the
manifestation was announced, our friends escorted us, and of the physicians
both the familiar ones and others, some as if in agony, some also for the
story; and another considerable crowd came on, for there happened to be some
distribution outside the gates and everything was visible from the bridge. And
there was a physician Heracleon, our companion, who confessed to me the next
day that he had persuaded himself to go as if, even if I should fare in the
strongest way, I would be seized with opisthotonos, or some other such thing.
When we were at the river, there was no need of anyone to exhort, but still
full of the warmth from the vision of the god I threw off the cloaks and
without even needing to be rubbed, I rush in where the river was deepest. Then
as if in a pool of very mild and mixed water I spent time, swimming and
washing myself about in every way. And when I came out, then indeed the whole
skin was blooming and the body was light in every part, and there was a great
shout of those present and those coming on, crying that much-hymned thing,
Great is Asclepius. And the things from this who could display? For all the
rest of the day and of the night

the other things I did not seem to see them; so wholly was I toward the god.
But from here it is already yours, O Master, to show and set beside us what we
should say next and where we should turn so as both to do things pleasing to
you and to go forward with the speech as most finely. Whether, since I made
mention of a river and such a winter and a bath, I should tell next other
things of the same kind and make as it were a catalogue of baths, winter ones
and divine and in every way paradoxical? Or dividing the speech should I also
narrate some of the things in the middle? Or is it best even to pass over all
the things in the middle and give the end to the first speech, how the matter
of the oracle about the years stood and how everything turned out? For many
other things too the god signaled, snatching me from the dangers that always
stood over me, which were dense each night and day, attacking now these now
those, then the same ones returning, and whenever one was gone, others taking

day would be, for he gave of what sort the air and where the charioteer would
appear and other signs of the truth; but I must do thus: first mounting the
team go to the river flowing through the city, and when I was where it is
already outside the city perform

things, good hope standing beside the fear. And consistent with these and
tending to the same were also the things that happened later in time, from
which I drank the wormwood.

That it is in every way more shuddering and clearer to narrate the visions
themselves purely is plain, but in most I must use the plan which I set up,
and run over chief points, as they meet the speech. Philadelphus was one of
the two temple-wardens; to him there comes on the same night a dream-vision
the same as to me, but it differed a little somehow. Philadelphus seemed (for
this much then I can remember) that in the sacred theater there was a crowd of
people in white clothing gathered according to the god, and standing among
them I was speaking to the people and hymning the god, saying all kinds of
other things and that he had diverted my fates in many other ways, and
recently when he even invented wormwood and commanded me to drink it diluted
with vinegar, so that I would not find it hard. And a ladder, I think, he
announced, a sacred one, and a presence and certain marvelous powers of the
god. These things Philadelphus

Come then, let us give back the speech from the beginning, and join how the
matter of the oracle about the years stood. For that this whole time he
himself was the one saving and granting day upon day, or rather even now he
himself is the one saving, those who know even a little of our affairs know.
But when the time of the prediction had passed, such things happened. And I
will take up a little. I happened to be in the suburb at the height of summer,
and a pestilential disease took hold of almost all the neighbors. And of the
household first two and three were ill, then another and another; then all
lay, both younger and older; and last I was taken. And physicians came from
town and we used their followers as servants, and some even attending
themselves were in place of servants. And the draft-animals were ill too, and
if anyone anywhere moved, he immediately lay before the doors; so that it was
no longer easily possible even to use a boat about what was happening. And
everything was full of despondency, wailing, groaning, every distress, and
there were terrible diseases in the city too. For a while then I held out,
taking thought no less for the others' salvation than for my own, then the
disease intensified and I was seized by a terrible fire of bile of every kind,
which continuously night and day troubled me, and I was shut off from food and
the strength was dissolved. And the physicians withdrew and finally despaired
altogether, and it was announced that I would be gone at once. Yet here too
you would have said Homer's, /yet the mind at least was sound./ So I followed
myself, as I would another, and I perceived the body always failing, until I
came to the extreme. And things being such I happened to be turned to the
inside of the bed, and I seemed as in a dream; but this itself was then the
release;

And the matters of the prediction about the years and of the later weakness
that contributed to this and of the manifestations about these things stood
thus. And it would perhaps fit now to speak about the baths with which he
brought us through, since also from the beginning together with the prediction
he prescribed the bath in the river. There were the catarrhs and the
difficulty about the palate and everything was full of frost and fire, and the
suffering of the stomach was at its height and many other things of all kinds,
and I was shut in at the season of summer. And these things happened at
Pergamum in the house of the temple-warden Asclepiacus. First then he
prescribed to take blood from the elbow and he added, as I remember, one
hundred and twenty pounds; and this was then to show that there would be need
of not a few phlebotomies, and it appeared from what followed. For the
temple-wardens being of this age and all the attendants about the god and
those holding ranks agreed that they had never yet known anyone at all cut so
many times, except Ischyron, and that his case was among the paradoxical, but
even so what happened with us surpassed it, apart from the other paradoxes
that attended the phlebotomies, such as indeed then immediately happened. For
one day later, I think, or two at least, he commanded again to take from the
forehead, and to one of those from the Roman senate attending and himself he
commanded this same thing, signaling that it had been prescribed also to
Aristides (Sedatus was his name, best of men, who himself narrated these
things to me); and in the middle of these phlebotomies he prescribes to me the
bath in the Caicus, and I had to throw off the wools and travel and bathe; and
I would see also a horse bathing and the temple-warden Asclepiacus standing on
the bank. These things had been foretold and these things happened. Still
approaching the river I see the horse bathing. And while I was bathing the
temple-warden was present and standing on the bank he watched. And the
lightness after this and the refreshment is very easy for a god to know,

From where then so great a matter was assembled perhaps someone would need to
hear. And it is beyond Alcinous' tale, but I will try somehow to tell it in a
rush. I went out to Rome in the middle of winter, being ill from home
immediately from waters and chilling, making no account of the present season,
but trusting in the training of the body and in the good fortune about all
things. And going forward as far as the Hellespont I was excessively ill in
the ear and otherwise was not as it happened, and having recovered a little I
went on further. After this rains, frosts, ices, all the winds; the Hebrus
just cut, so as to be passable for boats, or if not, the whole was mainland
from ice; and plains flooding as far as the eye held; and lack of lodgings,
and more water flowing from the roofs than from Zeus outside; and upon all
these haste and running contrary to the established season and the power of
the body. For not even the soldiers carrying the messages passed us, that I
may say no more, and of the household the greater part traveled at leisure.
And I myself even sought out the guides, if anywhere there should be need, and
this too did not happen from the easiest. For one had to drag them, slipping
away like barbarian men, partly persuading, and in some way even mastering.
From all these then the disease was raised. And this, about the teeth I was in
every extremity, so that I held my hands under, as always about to receive.
