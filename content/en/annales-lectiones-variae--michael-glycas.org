254 5. post codex claromontanus: Abraham with Sarah begot Isaac, and with
Hagar Ishmael. Isaac with Rebecca begot Esau and Jacob, who because of
Esau's threat migrated to Laban, his maternal uncle, whose daughters
Leah and Rachel he also took as wives. This Jacob then begot the twelve
patriarchs, that is the twelve tribes of Israel. It should be known that
after the three men had been entertained by Abraham, two of them arrived
for the burning of Sodom, and they also came to Lot's dwelling. When the
wanton Sodomites learned of this, they arrived there seeking the
strangers in order to fulfill their unlawful practice. Lot met them at
the door; and being much forced by them he offered his daughter for that
abominable abuse, only that no such shameful thing should happen to the
strangers. But the wanton men would not be persuaded, and seeking to
seize the strangers by force they fought with Lot. The strangers opened
the doors of the gateway and, merely appearing to them, burned them all
with fire. Astonished at what had happened, Lot fell on his face and
begged them that he might learn who they were. They said to him: "Take
up all who are with you, and go out from this place; for tomorrow all
will be burned, and the place will be winnowed away and vanish because
of the inhabitants' abominable wantonness." Lot quickly did this and
fled to Mount Segor. The Lord rained fire behind, and all were burned
according to the angels' prediction. But Lot's wife, being a woman and
not fearing the angels' command, turned to see what had happened, and
was immediately turned to stone and became as a pillar of salt. Lot's
daughters, thinking that the whole world had already perished and that
seed would no longer come to them, made their father drunk with wine,
and he had intercourse with them, not knowing from the drunkenness what
he had done. When his uncle Abraham learned this from him and was
grieved, he sent him away to the regions of the river Nile, to fetch
from there, as if it were, three firebrands, but in truth so that he
might be eaten by wild beasts and atone for the sin. But Lot, running
swiftly by God's providence, brought Abraham three firebrands, namely of
cypress, of pine, and of cedar. And Abraham, marveling that he had been
saved from the beasts 255 unharmed, glorified God. Both going up to the
mountain with the firebrands, Abraham fixed them on a rock apart from
one another, yet near, so as to form a triangle. He ordered Lot to go
and draw water from the Jordan and water them each day; which he did.
The mountain was about twenty-four miles from the Jordan. Within three
months the firebrands sprouted. Lot came down and told this to Abraham.
Going up to see what had happened, they found that the three firebrands
had been united and become one, while only the roots remained separate,
as they had been planted. Abraham, seeing what had happened in the dry
woods, fell and worshiped the Lord, and said: "This wood shall be a
sentence of sin." The wood became a great tree, lasting even until the
reign of Solomon. Abraham received assurance concerning the forgiveness
of Lot's sin. When the temple of Jerusalem was being built, this tree
too was cut down with the others, and having been hewn it lay unused in
the sanctuary, this too by God's providence. When Christ our God was
about to suffer the saving passion for the world, he was crucified on it
by the Jews.

295 18. post elegen Labbei Codex: concerning Amalek. After such things
Amalek came with thousands and tens of thousands to join battle with the
Hebrews, who were unarmed. Moses went up on the mountain and stretched
out his hands in prayer. And when Moses lifted his hands on the
mountain, he put Amalek to flight; but when they grew numb and he
lowered them, Amalek turned the Jews. Aaron and Hur, observing this,
came, one from the right and the other from the left, and they supported
the hands of Moses on high, and so Amalek was destroyed at last. And
David's word was fulfilled: "In the works of his 296 hands the sinner
was caught." When the Jews reached the desert of Kadesh, the serpents
were devouring them, and they were dying. Then Moses also nailed the
bronze serpent crosswise on high, and the biting serpents stopped; and
as many as were bitten by the serpents, gazing at the bronze serpent,
were delivered from death. 387 9. aux. refer quae p. 211 ima post
ὑποκείμενον habet φ: "And he knew her not until she had borne her
firstborn son." He has therefore set here the "until," so that we may
learn that before the birth-pangs the Virgin was truly untouched; for
what came after he left us to infer, that she who had thus become a
mother and had been deemed worthy of new pangs and strange childbearing,
that just man would not have endured to know her even afterward. And so
the great Athanasius in divine things [spoke] on this evangelical and
divine saying. But the Paulicians, heirs of the accursed Manes, being
able to understand none of this, were also led, as has been said before,
to say of the spotless and all-blameless mother of the Lord, that after
the saving birth she had other sons from Joseph. After these things et

the Metaphrast, in the martyrdom of the holy apostles Bartholomew and
Barnabas written by him, says these things. When the Savior had just
healed the paralytic at the Sheep-pool, he had Barnabas as a spectator
of the miracle; therefore, looking into the depths of his heart, he also
imparted to him speech and boldness toward himself. Barnabas, then,
arriving at the house of Mary the mother of John, who was called Mark
(and the account held this house of his to be a divine one), says to
her: "He who is about to come, whom our fathers longed to see, behold,
he has arrived." And at once leaving all she reached the temple, and
falling before Christ she begged him to enter her house. And indeed he
came. And this is the house in which he celebrated the Passover with the
disciples. A tradition has come down to us from of old that the one
carrying a jar of water, whom Christ bade the disciples follow, was the
wise Mark, the son of this 396 Mary. In such a house also after the
Ascension the apostles went up on their return with the rest of the
brethren, among whom were also Barnabas and Mark. A certain Epiphanius
et reliqua.
