* On that saying, And as he went
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of the same Athanasius, archbishop of Alexandria, on the saying: "And as he
went they spread their garments in the way." "And the greatest multitude"
rejoiced "with great joy" and cried out: "Hosanna in the highest." 1.1 If we
examine the particulars of the things that have come to be from the beginning,
they are infinite. If then one is not able to remember the things seen and
that have come to be from God, how could anyone be able to understand the
things hidden of him? 1.2 "For the greater part," he says, "of the works of
God is in hidden things." There are then invisible works of God which are "in
hidden things," concerning which the divine apostle also bears witness and
says: "For his invisible things from the creation of the world are clearly
seen, being understood by the things that are made." For truly "how
unsearchable are his judgments and his ways past tracing out." 1.3 Indeed the
prophet also, holding to the same judgment, said: "I considered your works and
I was astonished." 1.4 Let us then also seek "first his kingdom, and" thus
"all these things shall be added" to us; for thus also our Lord Jesus Christ
teaches in the gospels saying: "Seek and you shall find." 1.5 But we seek
neither pearls nor hyacinths, which the tasteless have marveled at; but we
seek "the wisdom of God in a mystery, the hidden wisdom," "which none of the
rulers of this age knew; for if they had known," he says, "they would not have
crucified the Lord of glory." 1.6 And this is the wisdom, concerning which
Solomon also said in prophecy: "Send forth to me the wisdom that sits beside
your throne." 1.7 And that Christ is also proclaimed as wisdom, Paul will bear
witness for me saying: "Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God." 1.8
"Seek then first the kingdom and his righteousness and" thus "all these things
shall be added to you." 1.9 For the things that follow are second to the
things that lead; for even if the shadow according to the law has a reason,
yet it is a pledge of the good things to come. 2.1 The Jews therefore even
until today, attending to the letter rather than to the spirit, have gone
astray. For hear them saying: "This man came to destroy the law and the
sabbaths." 2.2 But if a poor man speaks against money, he will not be
believed; nor if a licentious man concerning temperance will his testimony be
true. 2.3 For they do not affirm these things from disposition; for the one
declares against wealth because he does not have it; and the other, not having
practiced self-control, nor having exercised temperance with toil, mocks those
who long for it. 2.4 Indeed our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, convicting their
folly, said: "I came not to destroy the law nor the prophets but to fulfill."
2.5 Wherefore also the blessed Paul writes saying: "Christ then is the
fulfillment of the law unto righteousness to everyone who believes." 2.6 Let
us then examine more deeply concerning the disciples, beloved; and let us
examine in the wisdom that is called so in the proper sense and without
addition. 2.7 For this has knowledge of divine mysteries; this dwells in our
mind as in a house, if especially it is pure, occupying itself with the
intelligible things. 2.8 For "wisdom will not enter into a soul that devises
evil, nor dwell in a body that is in debt to sin." Let us then "cleanse
ourselves, beloved, from every defilement" of soul and body. 2.9 Let us hear
the prophet crying out: "Purify your hands, you sinners, cleanse your hearts,
you double-minded." =2.10= For thus someone would be able both to prove the
perceptible things and to hold the intelligible things, and to choose the one,
and to be far from the other. =2.11= As "approved money-changers" let us
select the good, "let us abstain from every form of evil." Let us then
approach the good things as necessary, but the indifferent things as far as
need requires. =2.12= For wealth in its own account is neither good nor base,
but by the choice of the one who uses it it is judged toward either. =2.13=
For example the great Abraham was exceedingly rich, but he did not on this
account turn aside from virtue. =2.14= Likewise also the poor Lazarus, being
in poverty, was anointed unto courage and greatness of soul by wisdom and by
reasoning. =2.15= Therefore, as I said, neither is poverty a good nor is
wealth base, but the use together with the disposition of the one who uses.
=2.16= Indeed of the faithful man is the whole world of possessions; but of
the unfaithful not even an obol. For to the faithful all things are subjected
and all things are slaves. Since he both rules all and despises all. =2.17=
But the unfaithful is a slave of all, being mastered by mammon; ruling nothing
but rather being ruled. =2.18= And all the works of God are good; but the
disposition of those who use them has much difference. 3.1 Indeed the wise
Ecclesiastes, having set his own mind with wisdom upon all things, examined
and discerned how he should use wealth, how he should bear poverty, how he
should endure disease, and how he should mix health. And these are the virtues
which take care for the human race. 3.2 But contrary to the things said the
one who is not wise will be disposed. And this is that "an evil distraction"
has been given "to the sons of men" from God, that from the toilsome labor and
the helplessness in the things that are seen they may flee for refuge to the
providence and wisdom of God. 3.3 For this reason "God has given an evil
distraction to the sons of men," that is, a toilsome one. 3.4 And that the
"evil" is toilsome, hear what is written in Job. For he says: "He struck Job
with an evil sore," that is, a toilsome one. And the plagues of Egypt have
been called evil, that is, toilsome. 3.5 That men may also move their own mind
toward the divine oracles and, having become always more prudent in these, may
set their own mind upon the life-giving Spirit. For you know that "the letter
kills, but the Spirit gives life." 3.6 Let us then with toil and exercise
embrace the benefit of virtue. For the good is accomplished by toil, as also
the blessed Paul writes saying: "In afflictions, in necessities, in stripes,
in tumults" and the rest. 3.7 And again the same says: "I bear all things, I
endure all things," "that we may not give any hindrance to the gospel of
Christ." 4.1 And we have set these things forth in the discourse, since the
reading seems to have a certain reason. For it says: "And as he went they
spread their garments in the way," that having set Jesus upon the colt they
might begin to go. And this the evangelical saying teaches us. 4.2 And how
many garments at all did the apostles have of their own, so as to throw some
of them upon the donkey, that they might set Jesus upon it, and to spread
others in the way; men without possessions and having left all things and
having kept, together with all the other things, also this commandment, "not
to have two tunics"? 4.3 And what is this so great softness, that as a donkey
walks they should not wish it to step on the ground, but upon garments? And
these things belong to those who live delicately and live in luxury. 4.4 Did
Jesus also occupy himself with luxury, that he should not wish, as he rode,
the donkey to tread the ground? Beloved, the teaching is not of those who live
in luxury. 4.5 But neither has anyone of those ever recorded been told to have
done this, for not even Nebuchadnezzar among the Greeks has done this; nor
Baltasar who succeeded him; nor indeed the great Solomon, when he was in his
kingdom. 4.6 For we have said beforehand that he knew "how he should use
wealth, how he should bear poverty" and the rest. 4.7 For it is his to hear
saying: "Lord, give me neither wealth nor poverty, but measure out to me the
things that are needed and that suffice." 4.8 But he rather took care for
wisdom. Wherefore he cries out and says: "I chose to have her instead of" gold
and silver, "I loved her above health and beauty." 4.9 And whence also so many
garments for the disciples, so as from Bethany until he entered into Jerusalem
to spread them for the donkey as she walked? 5.1 But the discourse goads me to
come to the higher sense and to seek out the apostolic garments. Let us then
lead ourselves up a little from the earth. 5.2 I say then that the garments
spread in the way are the ethical teachings, thrown under his feet. 5.3 And
these are: "You shall not commit fornication, you shall not commit adultery,
you shall not murder, you shall not steal, you shall not bear false witness,
honor your father and your mother" and the commandments like these. 5.4 Let us
then no longer tread the ground. For when apostolic garments have been spread
for us we ought not to tread the ground, lest we defile the feet of our own
soul from the dust of the earth, but even if someone calls us to this, let us
say to him: "I have washed my feet, how shall I defile them?" 5.5 And perhaps
for this reason Jesus washes the feet of the disciples, that, having cast off
the dust which they had before, they may no longer tread the ground nor defile
their own feet from the dust of the earth, but having become clean they may
step upon garments. 5.6 For "how beautiful," he says, "are the feet of those
who preach good news of good things." Let us then also gird "the loins of" our
"mind" and shoe "our feet in readiness of the gospel of peace." 5.7 For it
happened that we also once made trial of earth, when according to the time of
the captivity we were carried captive into the land of the Chaldeans. For this
reason also the prophet, groaning, said: "My delicate one has gone rough
ways." 5.8 For before the presence of the Savior the foot of the delicate soul
took experience of walking upon the earth. 5.9 But when our Lord and Savior
Jesus Christ sojourned "to preach release to the captives," then he put "water
into the basin" and lays aside "the garments" and girds himself with a "towel"
and washes "the feet of the disciples" and after washing their feet he puts on
"his garments" and being clothed he reclines with them. =5.10= So that for
this reason the disciples spread their garments in the way, that we may not
walk upon the earth, nor go upon the breast and the belly, but instead of
reclining our breast upon the earth we may rather recline it, as John, upon
the breast of Jesus. =5.11= For even if such a one still spends time upon the
earth, he does not have his feet dragged upon the earth, but as Paul, walking
upon the earth, "has his citizenship in the heavens." 6.1 "They set" then, he
says, "Jesus upon the colt." "And as he went they spread their garments in the
way." "And the crowds that went before him and those that followed cried out
saying: Hosanna to the son of David, blessed is he who comes in the name of
the Lord." 6.2 A new and paradoxical wonder, beloved. Was there then a
multitude of disciples with Jesus, were not the twelve alone? And how does the
gospel here signify a multitude? 6.3 But perhaps angels also were present with
him and divine powers were disciples to him? For before Peter and John and the
rest Gabriel is a disciple to him. 6.4 For he himself is the one sent to the
virgin Mary betrothed, from the seed of David. He himself is the one who says:
"Take the child and flee into Egypt." 6.5 He himself is the one who said: "The
Holy Spirit shall come upon you and the power of the Most High shall
overshadow you." 6.6 He himself is the one who also at the tomb rolled away
the stone. He himself is the one who also announced good news to the women and
said: "He whom you seek is not here; he has risen from the dead, as he said."
6.7 "Come," he says, "see the place where" the Lord "lay." "Behold he goes
before you into Galilee, there you shall see him." 6.8 Wherefore also the
prophet long before cries out saying: "Galilee of the nations, the people
sitting in darkness, see a great light." 6.9 And angels then, being disciples
to him, minister; "and behold," he says, "the angels came and ministered to
him." And all who are disciples to him rejoice; for "the crowds," he says,
"and all the multitude of the disciples began to rejoice with great joy." 7.1
I seek then at what the disciples and the divine powers rejoice and "they
rejoice praising God with a great voice." 7.2 Is it that the Lord sat upon a
mere little donkey? And for this do the disciples and the divine powers
rejoice, that the Lord was riding upon a little donkey? 7.3 What is this so
great tastelessness? If someone sits upon a donkey, is it worthy of joy? And
ought someone to rejoice at this, and these the disciples of the Lord? 7.4 I
do not think that the disciples of Jesus rejoice for this, especially with
angels also being with them as we have proposed. For those know for what
things they rejoice. 7.5 For they altogether see certain things, at which it
was worthy for them to rejoice. For attend to what the gospel says: that they
rejoiced "at all the mighty works which they saw." 7.6 And if the disciples
beheld mighty works in Jesus sitting upon the donkey, the thing was not mere,
but through the history they beheld certain mysteries and divine powers. 7.7
For hear the Savior saying that "there is joy in heaven over one sinner who
repents more than over ninety-nine righteous who have no need of repentance."
7.8 Wherefore also in another place he taught them saying in parables: "A
certain man having a hundred sheep of his own, if one of them goes astray,
does he not leave the ninety-nine that have not gone astray and go seeking the
one that has gone astray? And having found it he rejoices with joy over it
more than over the ninety-nine that have not gone astray." For those that have
not gone astray "have no need of repentance." 7.9 This colt then is called
both "sheep" and "younger son." For "my people," he says, "has become a
wandering sheep." =7.10= And again: "The ox knew his owner and the donkey the
manger of his lord." And: "This my son," "the younger," "was lost and was
found, he was dead and he lived again." 8.1 The Lord then, having taken all
these things upon himself: he put on the man and "was led as a sheep to
slaughter" and sat upon a little donkey and "died" and "was buried according
to the scriptures." 8.2 And his body lay in the tomb, but the Godhead spoiled
Hades. And he loosed those who were bound and redeemed the captives and gave
life to those who had been put to death. 8.3 For this reason he also goes up
upon the cross, triumphing over the enemy and becoming a curse for us and
being crowned for us from thorns, that he might set free from the sentence
that came against us. 8.4 And it was said to Adam that the earth "shall bring
forth thorns for you." Wherefore he receives the crown from thorns and they
prepared him to hold a reed in the right hand, that through this he might
easily accomplish the destruction of the serpent. 8.5 For thus again instead
of the poison of the serpent, of which Adam partook in paradise, he himself is
given to drink "gall with vinegar" for us; and instead of the side, from which
Eve came forth, he himself gave his own side unto slaughter. 8.6 A woman Eve,
a woman Mary, from whom Christ was born. A man Adam, a man whom Christ wore. A
paradise, from which Adam was cast out, a paradise which Christ opened to the
thief. 8.7 This colt then "is the one sinner who repents," whom the Lord
loosed "from the snares of the devil," concerning whom there is joy not only
upon the earth but also in the heavens. 8.8 After then the colt was loosed by
the disciples they bring him to Jesus and "they rejoice with great joy." And
further also angels and the divine powers rejoice over the one that was lost
and has been found. 8.9 He then who carefully attends to the things said and
reads the evangelical words mystically will see that this colt is the sinner
Adam. And if I say Adam, it is all humanity. =8.10= This colt then before the
sojourning of the Savior had been subjected to beasts, as we also proposed
before this. And I call beasts all the opposing power and the great demon
Satan himself. 9.1 When then the Lord sojourned toward us and "has given us
authority so as to tread upon serpents and scorpions and upon all the power of
the enemy," then let us also, taking courage, say with the prophet: "You will
not deliver to the beasts a soul that confesses you." 9.2 For the Lord "has
crushed the heads of the dragons." Wherefore also the prophet cries out
saying: "Upon asp and basilisk you shall tread and you shall trample lion and
dragon." 9.3 These then, as I said before, ruled over the colt. And these were
the ones who bound him and the ones who enslaved him. 9.4 For I think that the
prophet Isaiah also for this reason says: "A lion and a whelp, lions from
there and asps and flying offspring of asps, who carried their wealth upon
donkeys and camels." 9.5 The prophet then, wishing to instruct us, made known
the opposing powers through the beasts; which opposing powers loaded humanity
with their own destructive wealth. 9.6 And what is the wealth of the devil?
Wickedness and idolatry and pleasure and folly and every sin. 9.7 For this
very wealth the donkey and the colt bore according to much necessity and was
bound with the bonds of sin. 9.8 For nothing is heavier than sin. Wherefore
also it has been likened to lead by the prophet. From all these things then
Jesus, having sojourned, set us free and, having unloaded their wealth and
having loosed, sat upon the colt. 9.9 Wherefore also the disciples throw some
of the garments upon him, and some they also spread in the way. 10.1 And these
are the ethical teachings as we said before. The disciples then rejoice with
great joy. For indeed according to truth it is worthy of great joy at all
these things, with which Jesus, having sojourned, did good to humanity. 10.2
Let us then also take care of the eyes of the mind and become disciples of
wisdom, that we may see the powers that each hour minister to Jesus. 10.3
Having then been counted worthy of such great good things let us also rejoice
with great joy, because our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ deigned to sojourn
toward us and to suffer for us. 10.4 He rose on the third day from the dead,
having spoiled Hades, having trampled the enemy, having broken the bonds of
the sin that afflicted us. 10.5 Wherefore also "he raised us up together and
seated us together in the heavenly places," "at the right hand of the majesty"
of the Father, "where Christ himself entered as forerunner for us," "having
found eternal redemption." 10.6 Let us then also say with a great voice:
"Hosanna in the highest, blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord."
10.7 Let us then worship Father with Son and Holy Spirit; proclaiming three
hypostases, confessing one Godhead. 10.8 Let us prefer virtue, beloved, and
let us willingly embrace the toils for it; let us practice self-control, let
us remember the poor. 10.9 For thus living we shall also inherit the kingdom
of heaven in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory and the power unto
the ages of ages. Amen.
