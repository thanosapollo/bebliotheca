THE LETTER OF SAINT POLYCARP, BISHOP OF SMYRNA AND HOLY MARTYR, TO
THE PHILIPPIANS.
Polycarp and the elders with him, to the church of God sojourning at
Philippi: mercy and peace from God Almighty and Jesus Christ our
Savior be multiplied.
1.1 I rejoiced with you greatly in our Lord Jesus Christ, that you
received the patterns of true love and sent on their way, as was due
from you, those wrapped in bonds befitting the saints, which are the
diadems of those truly chosen by God and our Lord; 1.2 and that the
firm root of your faith, proclaimed from ancient times, remains until
now and bears fruit unto our Lord Jesus Christ, who for our sins
endured even unto death, whom God raised, having loosed the pangs of
Hades; 1.3 in whom, though you have not seen him, you believe with
joy unspeakable and glorified, into which many desire to enter,
knowing that you have been saved by grace, not from works, but by the
will of God through Jesus Christ.
2.1 Therefore, having girded up your loins, serve God in fear and in
truth, leaving empty chatter and the error of the many, and believing
in him who raised our Lord Jesus Christ from the dead and gave him
glory and a throne at his right hand; to whom all things in heaven
and on earth were subjected, whom every breath worships, who comes as
judge of living and dead, whose blood God will require from those who
disobey him. 2.2 And he who raised him from the dead will raise us
also, if we do his will and walk in his commandments and love what he
loved, abstaining from all injustice, greed, love of money, slander,
and false witness; not returning evil for evil, or insult for insult,
or blow for blow, or curse for curse; 2.3 but remembering what the
Lord said as he taught: Do not judge, that you may not be judged;
forgive, and it will be forgiven you; show mercy, that you may
receive mercy; with the measure you measure, it will be measured back
to you; and that blessed are the poor and those persecuted for
righteousness' sake, for theirs is the kingdom of God.
3.1 These things, brothers, I write to you concerning righteousness,
not as one who has taken the matter on himself, but because you
invited me first. 3.2 For neither I nor anyone like me can keep pace
with the wisdom of the blessed and glorious Paul, who, when he was
among you in the presence of the men of that time, taught the word of
truth accurately and firmly, and who, when he was absent, wrote you
letters; if you look closely into them, you will be able to be built
up in the faith that was given you, 3.3 which is the mother of us
all, with hope following and with love going before, love toward God
and Christ and toward the neighbor. For if anyone is within these, he
has fulfilled the commandment of righteousness; for the one who has
love is far from all sin.
4.1 The beginning of all troubles is the love of money. Knowing then
that we brought nothing into the world, and that we cannot take
anything out, let us arm ourselves with the weapons of righteousness
and teach ourselves first to walk in the commandment of the Lord;
4.2 then teach your wives also, in the faith given them and in love
and purity, to cherish their own husbands in all truth and to love
all equally in all self-control, and to train the children in the
discipline of the fear of God; 4.3 the widows to be sober-minded
concerning the faith of the Lord, interceding without ceasing for
all, far from all slander, evil speech, false witness, love of money,
and every evil; knowing that they are God's altar, and that
everything is examined for blemish, and that nothing escapes him,
neither reasonings nor thoughts nor any of the secrets of the heart.
5.1 Knowing then that God is not mocked, we ought to walk worthily of
his commandment and glory. 5.2 Likewise the deacons, blameless before
his righteousness as servants of God and Christ and not of men; not
slanderers, not double-tongued, not lovers of money, self-controlled
in all things, compassionate, diligent, walking according to the
truth of the Lord, who became the servant of all; if we please him in
the present age, we shall also receive the age to come, as he
promised to raise us from the dead; and that if we live worthily of
him, we shall also reign with him, if indeed we believe. 5.3 Likewise
also the younger men, blameless in all things, taking thought above
all for purity and bridling themselves from every evil. For it is a
good thing to be cut off from the desires that are in the world,
because every desire wars against the spirit, and neither the
sexually immoral nor the effeminate nor those who lie with men will
inherit the kingdom of God, nor those who do what is unseemly.
Therefore it is necessary to abstain from all these things, being
subject to the elders and deacons as to God and Christ; and for the
virgins to walk in a blameless and pure conscience.
6.1 And the elders also, compassionate, merciful toward all, turning
back those who have gone astray, visiting all who are sick, not
neglecting widow or orphan or the poor; but always taking thought for
what is good before God and men, abstaining from all anger,
partiality, and unjust judgment, being far from all love of money,
not quickly believing anything against anyone, not harsh in judgment,
knowing that we are all debtors of sin. 6.2 If then we ask the Lord
to forgive us, we too ought to forgive; for we are before the eyes of
the Lord and God, and all must stand before the judgment seat of
Christ, and each must give an account for himself. 6.3 So then let us
serve him with fear and all reverence, as he himself commanded, and
the apostles who preached the gospel to us, and the prophets who
proclaimed beforehand the coming of our Lord; being zealous for what
is good, abstaining from offenses and from false brothers and from
those who in hypocrisy bear the name of the Lord, who lead empty men
astray.
7.1 For everyone who does not confess that Jesus Christ has come in
the flesh is antichrist; and whoever does not confess the witness of
the cross is of the devil; and whoever twists the sayings of the Lord
to his own desires and says there is neither resurrection nor
judgment, this one is the firstborn of Satan. 7.2 Therefore, leaving
the emptiness of the many and the false teachings, let us turn back
to the word handed down to us from the beginning, being sober toward
prayers and persevering in fasting, asking in our petitions the
all-seeing God not to lead us into temptation, as the Lord said: The
spirit is willing, but the flesh is weak.
8.1 Let us then persevere without ceasing in our hope and in the
pledge of our righteousness, which is Christ Jesus, who bore our sins
in his own body upon the tree, who did no sin, nor was deceit found
in his mouth; but for us, that we might live in him, he endured all
things. 8.2 Let us then become imitators of his endurance, and if we
suffer for his name, let us glorify him. For this pattern he set us
in himself, and this we believed.
9.1 I exhort you all, then, to obey the word of righteousness and to
endure every kind of endurance, which you also saw with your own eyes
not only in the blessed Ignatius and Zosimus and Rufus, but also in
others from among you, and in Paul himself and the rest of the
apostles; 9.2 being persuaded that all these did not run in vain, but
in faith and righteousness, and that they are in the place due them
with the Lord, with whom they also suffered. For they did not love
the present age, but him who died for us and for our sake was raised
by God.
13.1 You wrote to me, both you and Ignatius, that if anyone is going
to Syria, he should also carry your letter; which I will do, if I
find a convenient opportunity, either I myself or the one I send as
envoy also on your behalf. 13.2 The letters of Ignatius that were
sent to us by him, and as many others as we had with us, we have sent
to you, as you commanded; they are appended to this letter; from them
you will be able to receive great benefit. For they contain faith and
endurance and all edification that belongs to our Lord.
