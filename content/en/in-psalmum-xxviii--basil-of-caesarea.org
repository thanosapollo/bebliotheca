ON THE 28TH PSALM.
Worship the Lord in his holy court.
Outside the holy court one must not worship the Lord, but after having
come inside; that is, the one who ought truly to serve God must not go
out =30.73= of the constitution of the Church, and by himself begin a
conventicle and schisms. For it is not fitting. And just as someone
prepares himself as a house of the Lord, so as to become a temple of
God, so also he can put a court around himself, walling himself about
with his life, and with the word, and with the divine thoughts,
worshipping God in himself, taking as a sufficient occasion for worship
that which is present in him, from both the bodily construction and from
the economy concerning the soul, having understood the one who created
him. And already the Lord also called the court that of the Jews,
saying: And other sheep I have which are not of this court, making
clear, of course, that there is also another court, the truly holy one,
which is going to gather the sheep of Christ, those from the nations
predestined for salvation; that is, this Church, in which the true
worshippers worship in spirit and in truth. And no one who deifies
anything else besides God can worship God in his holy court. And some
deify the belly, of whom the god is the belly; and others, silver: those
devoted to greed, which is idolatry; and each one, whatever he has
honored more than all things, to this he ascribes the glory of God. And
such a one is not in the holy court, nor does he worship God, even if he
seems inseparable from the visible assemblies. The voice of the Lord is
upon the waters.
It is worthwhile, in order to understand what the voice of the Lord is,
for us to gather as far as possible from the divine Scripture the things
written concerning voice. We found, then, in Genesis a word of the Lord
written to Abraham, saying in a vision: Fear not, Abraham; I am your
shield, your reward is great; and Abraham answered: Master, what will
you give me? And I am going away childless. And a little later: And
immediately a voice came to him. And again in Exodus he says: Let them
be ready for the third day. And, When the voices and the trumpets and
the cloud have departed from the mountain, they shall go up onto the
mountain. And after the Decalogue: And all the people saw the voice and
the lamps, and the voice of the trumpet. And in Isaiah it is written: A
voice of one saying: Cry. Voice, then, is a blow upon air, or some form
that comes about in the air, toward which the one who utters voice
wishes to shape it. Those who converse with one another through flesh,
striking the air, and as it were imposing some type and form =30.76=
upon it, push it by the breath so that it reaches as far as the hearing
of the one toward whom it is directed; but the voice of the Lord is of
another kind, God producing an impression in those whom he wishes to
hear his voice, so that this impression has an analogy to that which
occurs in dreams. For there too, it is not that the outer air has been
struck, and the perception of the things said has entered through the
hearing, but when the ruling faculty itself has received the thoughts,
they naturally remain in the memory of those who have seen them.
A voice therefore came to Abraham, because he had ears able to hear the
commandments of God, the divine will sounding in him invisibly. But the
voice that came to the people was visible; that is, well known,
intelligible to all. For it was not, of course, colored by some color
and outlined, so that the spoken voice became perceptible to the eyes,
but the people saw the voice; because the commandment of the Lord is
far-shining, enlightening the eyes, no unclarity surrounding the things
spoken. And to Isaiah also a voice was coming, commanding: Cry; that
is, the ruling faculty of the prophet was being shaped from the power
that was coming to be in him from God. The voice of the Lord is upon
the waters. What voice? Upon what waters? Let us take the saying as
prophecy.
Remember John, who, when asked by the Jews, Who are you? what answer
shall we give to those who sent us? answered: I am a voice of the Lord
crying in the wilderness. Therefore John is a voice of the Lord, a
messenger sent from God before the face of the Lord, to prepare for the
Lord a people made ready. This voice, then, was upon the waters, at the
Jordan, in which he baptized, preaching the baptism of repentance; and
not in the Jordan only, but also in Aenon near Salim, because there was
much water there. Therefore the voice of the Lord upon the waters is
John at the baptism. There also the God of glory thundered. For a voice
came from heaven saying: This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well
pleased. Then also the Lord was upon many waters, having deigned to
come down to the baptism of John, that he might fulfill all
righteousness that is in the law. The voice of the Lord in power. For
he takes away the weaknesses of the people through the baptism of
repentance, baptizing through it in water unto repentance. The phrase,
In power, is the voice that says: Repent, for the kingdom of heaven has
drawn near; and, Make fruits worthy of repentance. Or perhaps the voice
is in power, because John bears witness to the one who truly is the
strong Lord, saying: I baptize in water; but he who comes after me is
stronger than I. This voice, then, was bearing witness to the power.
The voice of the Lord in magnificence: John discoursing the great
mysteries concerning Jesus =30.77=, saying: I saw the Holy Spirit as a
dove coming down and remaining upon him; and again: For from his
fullness we have all received, and grace for grace. For the waters are
high and indicative of the greatness of the glory concerning the
Only-begotten. Therefore, The voice of the Lord in magnificence.
And it is also magnificent to say: No one has ever seen God; the
only-begotten Son, who is in the bosom of the Father, he has declared
him. And he appears to recount many things magnificently concerning the
Lord, in saying: A man can receive nothing, unless it has been given him
from heaven. And magnificent also is: He who comes from above is above
all. And: He who has received his testimony has sealed that God is
true. For he whom God has sent speaks the words of God. And: He who
believes in the Son has eternal life; and as many such things as these.
Nothing, then, lowly, nor petty, but all things spoken by John
concerning the Lord have been rendered magnificently, with the fitting
greatness, in a manner that is proper. Therefore, The voice
of the Lord in magnificence. The voice of the Lord breaking cedars. It
is possible to say that, preparing for the Lord a people made ready,
breaking and pulling down the impieties that lift themselves up and are
exalted against the knowledge of God, he was making the crooked things
into a straight way. For he who brings low every hill and every
mountain, this one was the one breaking the cedars, and making the way
smooth for the Lord's sojourn, by leading into repentance the high and
lifted-up and unhumbled heart of the people. Hence, succeeding to his
preparation, the Lord, through his own sojourn, breaks the opposing
powers, which are named figuratively cedars of Lebanon. For the Lord
must reign, until he puts the enemies under his feet. And these cedars
he will grind fine, as the calf of Lebanon. What then is the calf
Lebanon? They say that, with much elaborate craft, those who serve the
idols, forming images of calves out of frankincense, used thus to offer
these upon the fire. Thus then he says that the opposing powers will
worthily be brought to nothing. Or a calf that is melted by the fire
through frankincense, according to the saying: As wax melts from the
face of fire, so shall the sinners perish =30.80=. And it is also
possible to understand in this way that the cedars are ground fine, and
Lebanon itself, the leader of the lifting-up of the cedars. The calf
was once ground fine at the Exodus through Aaron, when the people,
having revolted from God and turned aside into idolatry, fashioned it;
which Moses took, ground into fine pieces, and threw into the water.
Thus then the Lord will grind fine the whole of Lebanon, that is, the
first beginning of evil, as that calf was ground fine by Moses. And he
will grind them fine as the calf of Lebanon. And the beloved as a son
of unicorns. After the opposing powers have been destroyed, then the
love toward the Lord will appear, and his strength will become manifest,
none of the adversaries overshadowing. Therefore he says after the
grinding: The beloved shall be as a son of unicorns. And a unicorn
is a princely animal, unsubdued to man, unconquerable in strength,
always dwelling in wildernesses, trusting in one little horn.
For this reason the unconquerable nature of the Lord was likened to a
unicorn, both because of the rule over all, and because he has one
beginning of himself, the Father; for the head of Christ is God. The
voice of the Lord dividing a flame of fire. John was cutting through
the sin that, like fire, was feeding upon Israel, saying: Repent; for
the kingdom of heaven has drawn near. The flame of the passions, then,
that comes to be in the souls from sin, he was cutting through,
recounting to the Jews the things concerning the eternal fire, in
speaking the severe things of the judgment, that The winnowing-fan is in
the hand of the Lord, and he will thoroughly cleanse the threshing-floor;
and the wheat he will store into the storehouses, but the chaff he will
burn up with unquenchable fire. That flame, then, he was cutting
through by bringing the fearful things before the sight of the people.
And the Lord will shake the wilderness of Kadesh. For John was
baptizing in the wilderness. That wilderness, then, he shook by the
coming of the peoples from everywhere; which, because those who came
were repenting, was called a holy wilderness. For Kadesh is interpreted
"the holy". This voice also prepares hinds, the holy souls, those that
seek the high things, those that consume the serpents, those that make
their sojourn upon the high mountains. And these would be souls of a
great nature, running up from the low things to the heights of the
heavenly way of life, light and lifted up; having war against the
opposing powers, as the hinds against the serpents. But he also
uncovers thickets, so that the serpents may become visible, those
covered by the shade of the thickets, in order that they may be consumed
more easily by the hinds =30.81=. Perhaps, then, the one who convicts
the evils in secret uncovers thickets; that is, he brings into the open
the sins that nest in the material nature, which are called serpents
because of the varied character of pleasure. And he will uncover on a
day, when he will uncover the hidden things of the darkness, and the
counsels of their hearts. And perhaps also John, being a voice of the
Lord, was speaking to Israel in a riddle, that Already the axe is laid
to the root of the trees. Therefore every tree that does not make good
fruit is cut out, and is thrown into fire. He was uncovering the
thickets, that is, he was making manifest the uselessness of the fruits;
for a thicket is unfruitful material. Showing them the harm of this, he
was calling them to the fitting fruit-bearing, saying: Make the fruits
worthy of repentance. From all of which there is one result, that the
temple of the Lord be built, where everyone who has come to be can truly
bring glory to the Lord. But also the Lord will dwell in the flood.
For through baptism is the dwelling of the Lord into the souls of those
who have been sanctified. The soul, then, that is washed of sin, this
God inhabits. For this reason he is said to dwell in the flood. For
that he named the remission from baptism a flood, we have also learned
from the thirty-first psalm, in the saying: Nevertheless in a flood of
many waters they shall not come near him. For the sins of the sinners,
in the redemption through baptism, will no longer be permitted to draw
near. And the Lord will sit as king unto the age. Upon all these things
it will be ours to come to know the rule over all of the Master, who
gives strength to his people, so that, having conquered the evil one, we
may have room for the blessing given by the Lord in peace to those
worthy of him; because to him is the glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
