On love.
=60.773=
No speech is sufficient to speak of love as it deserves, because it is not
earthly, but heavenly. And I think even the tongue of the angels is at a
loss toward the most exact inquiry into it, since it proceeds unceasingly
from the very magnificence of the mind of God. Shall we then, since neither
the tongue of men is sufficient for its most exact exposition, ourselves
shrink back as well? But by no means; since with God even the very great
offerings of those rich in silver are acceptable; but also those of the
unfeigned, with a cheerful mind, even if they happen to be exceedingly
small, as also the offering of that widow who cast the two mites into the
treasury was preferred to those who laid down much gold.
And we, having set our hope on the Lord as helper and corrector of what is
said, let us with eager heart cast the two denarii of love into the
treasury of your mind. For I think these two denarii are those two
commandments, on which the whole law hangs and the pro =60.774= phets. For
it has been said by the Lord in the Gospels, You shall love the Lord your
God with all your heart, and your neighbor as yourself. In these two
commandments are the law and the prophets. Love toward God, and love toward
the neighbor, two commandments that hold to one another; he who has one of
these, but does not have the other, this one does not even possess the one
he seems to have. For as a soul without a body is not called a human being,
nor again a body without a soul; so neither love toward God, if it does not
have following it also the love toward the neighbor, happens to be love;
nor again the love toward the neighbor, if it does not have that toward
God, is called love. Therefore it is rare to find perfect love. The Greeks
are ignorant of this, even if they seem to have love toward one another;
but being ignorant of God, they are strangers to this. The Jews, even if
they have love toward one another, having imitated the Lord of the law who
says, You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, themselves,
instead of =60.775= love, having taken hatred to their bosom, crucified the
Lord of glory, as it says in the prophet: And they set hatred instead of my
love. Neither do those who say the Son of God is a creature or a made thing
possess this love, even if they have love toward one another.
For how will the one who loves God call this one creator or maker of
Christ, and not begetter? Or how will he say that he loves the Son, who
claims that this one is a creature or a made thing? Even if he should say
that he loves the Father, not loving the Son, he has been deprived of the
love toward the Father, as the Apostle says: He who does not have the Son
does not have the Father either. Those who say the Spirit is a creature or
a made thing do not have this love, even if they seem to confess and love
the Father. For if anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ, this one is
not his. As many, then, as think orthodoxly concerning Father and Son and
Holy Spirit, and keep the love toward one another, these possess the
perfection of love. Love confesses the Father, worships the Son, and
glorifies the Holy Spirit; love does not divide the concord of the Trinity.
O love, companion of angels, foster-mate of the fathers, tent-fellow of the
prophets, fellow-athlete of the martyrs, fellow-chorister of the apostles,
bond of the Church.
Love does not know war, has no enemy, awards peace; love soothes murderous
rages, dignifies rational peoples; love, which is not contained in the
world, and which dwells in a humble heart; love sweeter than honey and
milk, having the sweet not only in the mouth, but also in the depth of the
heart, to possess; love fits all things, through which the earthly things
have been constituted, and the heavenly increase; love, on account of which
God sent his own Son into the world; For God so loved the world, that he
gave his only-begotten Son into the world for us; love taught to endure the
things from dangers both by land and by sea; love perfects martyrs, blesses
them, crow =60.776= ns them; love sees the neighbor as itself, and holds
its own things as common to all; love sets the neighbor's want as its own;
love sets a common table for all, for rich and poor, for wise and
unlearned; love sweetens the embittered soul, heals the afflicted, makes
the humbled bloom again; love carries about the unquenchable lamp of
virginity, and busies herself seeking her own; love opens the doors of the
kingdom, and leads in virginity wearing a crown, and does not hinder those
who live in honorable marriage from entering; love loves almsgiving, and
prepares gold-gleaming crowns for its cultivator; love does not know pride,
but glorifies the humble; love flees evil-speaking, and is fond of
blessing; love advises men and women joined in marriage to be of one mind,
and never wishes them to be separated from one another; love urges fathers
to love the children, and the children to serve their parents as masters;
love exhorts masters to be merciful toward their household servants, and
advises the household servants to minister to their masters without deceit;
love puts fear outside, and clasps to the breast boldness toward God; For
perfect love, it says, casts out fear; love wove this crown of the fathers
for us, and planted for us the stem of hope, sweet and fruit-bearing; love
leads the flock into the fold of the Church, and feeds on the verdant
luxury of paradise; love, having received the hearing of us all into one
love of listening, ordered it, and widened your tongue; love trains the
body, warms the spirit, whitens the soul; love dignifies the labors of the
ascetics, and opens to them the smiling bosoms of the Son; of which may the
Master of all also deem us worthy, unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
