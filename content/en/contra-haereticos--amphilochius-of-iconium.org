mind, nor do they dare to come forward into the light or to converse with a
prudent man, or to set foot in a city of Christians, as the leaders of the
other heresies do, who try through philosophy and empty deceit to lead some
astray. For through the cunning that is theirs they also try to drag the
scriptures toward their own wishes and from there to deceive the simpler; for
the scriptures are like medical remedies, which when well prepared heal, but
when given badly or unskillfully kill; therefore they are reasonably called
little foxes. Now the remaining heresies one can find that they have both
argument and plausibility and by syllogisms and sophisms they get the better
of certain simpler people; and I think that concerning these also the prophet
prays saying: He will deliver me from my powerful enemies. But the little
foxes, that is these heresies, what power do they have for deceiving? Not
intelligence, not strength, not boldness of their doctrine, not the justice
that is in cities, able to deceive those who are divided in the faith; they
have one thing only, the craftiness of these beasts. For when they enter a
vineyard, that is a people, they do not enter with boldness, but secretly as
thieves, as these beasts also, the foxes. When they see the vinedresser
occupied in another part, they enter unsparingly into others' labors, which
they also destroy. And one may understand the scriptural saying thus as well,
that they are not only named little foxes, but also destroyers of little
vineyards. For no man among them who is strong and firm in the faith and
founded upon the rock, that is Christ, is deceived, but if anywhere someone
like chaff and carried about by every wind is winnowed out of the church.
Concerning such the Lord also says in the Gospels: Whoever shall cause one of
the little ones who believe in me to stumble, it is better that a millstone of
a donkey be hung on his neck, and so on, clearly of one who is great and
established in the faith and holding the head of the church, Christ, not being
able to be made to stumble. Concerning these same the apostle Paul also
clearly hinted saying: Of these are they who creep into houses and take
captive little women heaped with sins, led by various desires, always learning
and never able to come to knowledge of truth. By the word creeping in he
showed the hole of the serpent that deceived the first woman; and he also made
plain the crafty and cowardly character of their ways and the weakness of
their preaching and the rottenness of those deceived; for he mentions no man,
except only little women easy to deceive. Therefore that preaching of theirs
deceives that sex and if anywhere someone is womanish; and one can see in
their synagogues more little women being deceived, following after other
women's husbands and living with them without distinction; and what follows
from these things is shameful even to speak. Who is the teacher of these
heresies? The teacher of all the heresies is the devil. For as Christ is
teacher of the catholic church and gave himself up for her, that he might
sanctify her and present the church to himself not having spot or wrinkle or
any such thing, so also the devil, being ready to deceive and scatter, has
become teacher of all the heresies. For as soon as the race of men was taught
to worship God, the devil made many falsely named gods; and why do I say many,
he deified the whole creation, that he might lead wretched man astray from the
God who truly is. For it was


possible to see likenesses of animals and beasts and reptiles and birds being
worshipped, and still almost all matter being honored as God. And for this
reason the Lord took up the incarnate economy on behalf of the race of men,
that he might abolish the polytheistic error and turn them to the worship of
the God who truly is through the washing of regeneration, and gather all
humanity that had been scattered into the church and named it his body, and
his word be fulfilled: There shall be one flock, one shepherd. But again the
envious devil by his own wickedness and his ancient craft does not cease
deceiving the unstable; for as before, having renamed idols gods, he led men
astray from the God who truly is, so also now, having named many churches, or
rather, if one must speak the truth, having set up idols of churches, those
who obey him into the renunciation of Christ, so that just as then through the
so-called gods he made them depart from God, so also now through the
so-called churches among them he may tear them from the church that truly is.
For it is the devil's custom to deceive through great promises; for
approaching the first man, or rather the woman, he said: If you eat of the
tree of knowledge, you shall be as gods, promising to make them gods, that is
immortal, and he took away even their very life. His deceit then has still
more; for when the Savior appeared and many believed in him the evil one
raised up persecution against the Christians and those whom he could no longer
hold by the deceit of idolatry, these he forced through tyranny to become
apostates. And it was possible to see a fearful thing: kings raging against
the Christians, laws full of lawlessness, judges avengers of impiety. For
beyond every murder and every sorcery piety was an accusation, and only the
Christians inhabited the prisons, were racked, beaten, fried, scraped, thrown
to beasts. And though all these things were brought upon them by the evil one
and his servants, that they might deny the church of God, they bore all things
nobly and eagerly through love for the Savior and died gladly, exchanging the
temporary death for the everlasting life. The devil, having learned these
things and perceived that his violence was being defeated and all his devices,
changed into another form of deceit, and again a fair promise and a humble
figure and a devout religion. For when continence and renunciation are
preached, who would not easily be deceived by this craft? He knows that it is
a heavy thing for a Christian to hear: Stand away from God, deny the Savior,
run away from the church, abandon the blood of Christ by which you were
bought. If he had said these things, who would have listened to him? But now
by the ancient method, again promising great things, he casts the man out of
the true paradise, the church of Christ. For truly the church of Christ is a
paradise, in which are both the tree of life and the tree of knowledge, and a
spring going up from the earth and watering the paradise, from which also the
four rivers flow; and it has also a great variety of fruits. For this reason
it is a paradise, because there are many kinds of fruits; for a paradise of
one kind does not exist, as the apostates say. And each, according as he has
faith and zeal, grows and bears fruit; only let him remain in the paradise of
Christ, as it is written: Planted in the house of the Lord they shall flourish
in the courts of our God. And if a time of martyrdom also comes, let us give
over the body, leave the temporary life, overlook family, despise money; let
all go, let faith, hope, love remain in us, as the apostle says. And in
another place: I am persuaded, he says, that neither life nor death nor things
present nor things to come nor any other creation will be able to separate us
from the love of Christ. And what this love is, our Lord Jesus Christ himself
said: He who eats my flesh and


drinks my blood, he remains in me, and I in him. And through his great love
toward us he paid down his own blood as the price for us; for you were bought
with precious blood, Peter the apostle says. He therefore who denies the blood
of Christ has insulted his coming and has set aside the price paid down for
him. And what will they plead, those who have denied the blood of God and run
away from the church? For if, as we said before, the apostles and martyrs
suffered so many things for the blood of Christ and were perfected in this
confession, of what punishments are these worthy who without blows, who
without a sword, without any danger have denied the price paid down for them?
Let them not even think they have anything of a Christian while they deny
this, for all the mysteries of the Christians hang upon this; whether you
speak of churches, or scriptures, or catecheses, or baptism, or an altar, or
the body itself, without the blood it is nothing; for a body without the blood
is dead, and no one offers a dead thing upon an altar. For one can find in the
old covenant also blood offered everywhere, and almost all things are cleansed
in blood according to the law. How much more will the blood of Christ cleanse
our conscience from dead works, the apostle says; and again: Having therefore,
brethren, boldness for the entrance of the saints in the blood of Christ,
which he renewed as a fresh and living way. He showed you everywhere the power
of the mystery of the Christians, that it is not possible either to flee sin,
or the devil himself, or to enter into the entrance of the saints, which he
renewed for us as a fresh and living way, to be recognized by the king if one
does not bear the seal. For if soldiers contending in wars and struggling for
their very salvation keep the token and through this show their goodwill
toward the king, reasoning that if they lose this they will no longer be able
to behold the face of the king, what will they do who have lost not a lifeless
sign, but have been stripped of the very blood of Christ? But if one examines
closely, more pitiable than every man who has gone astray, and worthy of many
lamentations, are those who have gone astray in these heresies. For so much
has the evil one prevailed over them and darkened their senseless heart, that
they do not even recognize what they have suffered. For they ought to groan
and weep, having been deprived of so many and so great goods and of life
itself. They have suffered the opposite: they name themselves righteous and
higher than all men; and why do I say than men, even holier than the very
blood of Christ. For as soon as the devil lays hold of one of them and casts
him out of the church, one can see from that hour such a one speaking
injustice against the height unsparingly, no longer recognizing either the
priest who baptized him, or the spiritual mother, the church, who bore him and
reared him, or the altar from which from childhood he was fed and given drink,
or the holy things themselves that are offered. But at once the first progress
and a great achievement is reckoned by them to be the denial and the blasphemy
against the mysteries; for such a one begins to say, or rather the devil
speaking in him: "What is a church? What is baptism? And is that the blood of
Christ? Let it not be that that should enter my mouth." Wretched man! If you
had kept your heart with all watch, if you had prayed laboriously until the
last breath, the safeguard would have remained with you. If you had not given
the devil entrance into your heart, you would not have been taken alive by him
into his will. To this progress continence and renunciation have brought you
up; they taught you nothing else than to renounce the saints alone. The blood
of Christ unclean, and you clean? I thought you had another renunciation,
concerning which the Lord said that If anyone does not renounce all


that belongs to him, he is not my disciple, and If you wish to be perfect, go,
sell your possessions and give to the poor, and so on; and he did not add If
anyone does not renounce my church or my mysteries, he is not my disciple. For
it is clear that he taught us through his incarnate economy first to renounce
the devil and his idols and all his error, and to enlist with him alone
through his church and the mysteries. Did you make the first renunciation or
the second? The first is abstinence from idols, the second is contempt of
money and disdain of every fleshly pleasure. Let us see then what renunciation
you mean, that of money. And who has been so enslaved to the passion of love
of money as the heretics? For not only do they hold with security the money
that falls to them, but they also become stewards and treasuries of what
belongs to others as if it were their own, and they gather interest and
increase and procure the money for others, while they themselves gain the
lawless deeds, only that they may increase the passion of greed. For no one
ever heard that a heretic sold all that belonged to him and gave to the poor
and became a doer of this commandment. You have renounced the saints alone
then; this alone you were taught by your continence and your renunciation, not
to know your priest who always feeds you through the mystical and holy food,
having made no great progress. For if you call this progress, even the mad
reach this; for they too, when they are held by this passion, do not know
those who begot them, do not recognize a brother, do not remember benefits,
but even if someone wishes to help, he is taken by them as an enemy and a foe,
and when asked by their own people to tell the cause of the grief, uttering
inarticulate and disconnected things they have nothing. It is clear then that
they are held by the passion of madness, and the apostates of the church also,
insulting toward all, keeping aloof, calling the father who begot them an
adulterer, thinking the mother a harlot, not keeping the commandment of God
that says He who speaks evil of father or mother, let him die the death. But
perhaps making an excuse he will say: "I revile the fleshly parents." Not even
this do you do well; for the law says this and our Lord Jesus Christ seals
this; for blaming the Jews he says, when they were accusing him saying: Why do
your disciples transgress the tradition of the elders and eat with unwashed
hands, the Lord says to this: Why do you also transgress the commandment of
God and your tradition? For God said: Honor your father and your mother, and
he who speaks evil of father or mother, let him die the death. You see how he
sealed the commandment, or rather the law of nature through his own word? And
one could say many things about honor and fear of parents, but the old and new
covenant suffice, bearing witness about this. Let us see whether they honor
even the spiritual parents. Who then is the spiritual father? Entirely after
God the priest who baptized you; for it is written: In Christ Jesus through
the gospel I begot you, and Behold I and the children whom you have given me,
you the God; and to those who have fallen My little children, he says, with
whom I again travail, until Christ be formed in you. Which we also pray may
come to pass for them; and the mother entirely is the church, the one who also
reared them. Does the heretic then recognize these, does he know the benefit
from them, that through these he fled the tyranny of the devil, through them
he was named a Christian? Why do I say a Christian? He put on Christ himself;
for the scripture says: As many of you as were baptized into Christ, you put
on Christ; only if you have not been stripped again by the serpent. What then
to these things? Do you recognize these, or have you denied these too? And
what will you do? For if you have dishonored those who begot you according to
the flesh, and have denied those who regenerated you through the washing of
regeneration, whence is


your life? But since you have truly gone away into a far country and have
spent the portion of the paternal substance that fell to you, you are also fed
on the husks and you feed the pigs of the leading citizen. For he has no sheep,
for there is one good shepherd and one his flock. And what is the substance
which you lost? It is plain and again evident: the faith and the gift which
you received through baptism. For as when baptized into Christ you put on
Christ, so having gone away from the church you put off Christ. And lest you
think what is said is a sophism, when that son had turned back the father
commanded nothing else than that the first robe and the ring be given to him.
Imitate this man, brother; rise from earthly thoughts as he from the earth,
come to yourself, as he also said, you too say: How the hired men of my father
have bread to spare, but I perish here with hunger? I do not have bread able
to strengthen a man's heart, but only the food of the pigs, the husks. And
what are these, but the diabolical writings, as we shall show in another
discourse? Remember from where you fell, how you were led astray, enticed by
the serpent that promises great things, that he might deprive you of the
present goods. For in the beginning, the word taught, that he so deceived the
first man, or rather the woman, saying: You shall be as gods. And you too by
the same deceit through the name of continence and of renunciation he led away
from the paternal house to the far country, in which there were not sheep, but
only pigs. And if you had kept the master's commandment that says to you: I
will put enmity between you and the serpent, you would not have listened to
that counsel. Yet God is a lover of men, he does not know how to remember
evil; only if you turn back, he is ready through his own goodness to meet you
also as he did that one. He knows that you have been deadened in so long a
time, not tasting bread; embracing you he will kiss your neck, which the devil
hardened, and he will feed you again through the fatted calf, the mysteries of
Christ. And there will be great joy over you also and the father will say: It
is necessary to rejoice and be glad, because my son was dead and lived again,
and was lost and was found. You will find also all of us rejoicing with the
father and making glad together over your salvation; for among us there is not
even one envy. If you hear our counsel, you will find these things done both
by the common father concerning you and by the brother; if you begin, because
of the little women deceived with you, to make excuses in sins: "After so long
a time shall I return to the church? Shall I hear from the little women living
with me destroyer*?" For after many things this has been said among them, and
this is the more bitter craft of the devil, that he not only trips the man at
the beginning, but even after the fall he does not allow him to turn back to
God and be healed. What is dissolution? And what is transgression? Tell me.
Entirely dissolution is, when someone leaves the faith and its mysteries
handed down in the church of God and goes over into unbelief. These things you
ought to have said at the beginning, when you were in the father's house, when
the devil and his workmen were persuading you to depart from the paternal
wealth of the life that truly is, then you ought to have said: "I do not
become a destroyer, I do not become a transgressor, no one will separate me
from the love of Christ; he himself underwent so many things for me, subject
to no one, that he might buy me who was formed with his own blood from sin.
And how shall I run away from the church, having denied the price paid down
for me?" And he kept persuading you through the fair promise and the solemn
names, as is the custom of that one and of his workmen, hastening to make you
depart from the faith in the guise of continence and renunciation or some
other solemn name. You ought then to have said: "It is permitted me, being a
Christian and remaining in the church and keeping the mysteries, to renounce
money,


to renounce wickedness, to renounce the whole world, that is worldly cares;
and if it should be needed, also to be continent from foods and every fleshly
pleasure; and that I may not say each thing, even if I must keep virginity and
purity, it is permitted me, being a Christian and keeping the faith, to build
all these things upon it. For if I take away the foundation of the faith,
which is Jesus, where shall I build the way of life? For these things I hear
every day in the church of Christ proclaimed by the fathers who also
enlightened me; but to you I will say: Depart from me, all you who work
lawlessness; if anyone evangelizes you contrary to what you received, let him
be anathema." If ever you had sobered, if ever you had kept watch, you would
not have been dragged under by the teacher of the heresies, you would not have
become truly a destroyer; for neither when you return to the church will
anyone force you to eat and drink fleshly food, nor even to marry or to be
given in marriage, but if it seems good neither to eat bread nor anything that
fattens the body. Only do not deprive yourself of the body and blood of
Christ, nor abhor the creatures of God. That from Simon Magus these heresies
Each of men has authority to abstain from the evil and to come to the good,
and to receive from the good as much power and grace as he himself wishes; for
of whatever sort the vessel is found, such also is the grace given to the one
who asked, as the scripture says concerning spiritual and holy men that The
spiritual man judges all things, but he himself is judged by no one. And this
is great, that he received such authority from God to judge all things,
himself judged by no one; but already having become higher than all human
nature and before the resurrection having received from God the judging. But
that surprises the hearers more, that there is a progress even beyond this
measure; for to say He who is joined to the Lord is one spirit, and he did not
say: he is spiritual or he is holy, but he is one spirit, shows that such a
one was blended with the Godhead; he became one with God, he can no longer be
separated. By the same word also the unbeliever and the fleshly man, to his
own measure, as much as he gives himself over to fleshly pleasures and to
unbelief, so much he finds lawlessness multiplied in himself. And one can find
this also in the scripture; to the fleshly he says: I was not able to speak to
you as to spiritual, but as to fleshly. And those who have fallen into extreme
wickedness he [does not] call fleshly, as if the Holy Spirit no longer has
even the slightest beginning toward them; for he says thus in the creation: My
spirit shall not remain in these men, because they are flesh. And it was
necessary to set beside this more testimonies of the scriptures, but we
thought it superfluous, the two persons of the believer and the unbeliever
being plain. For you will find having reached the first measure of piety the
holy prophets and apostles and martyrs and many children of the church of
Christ, and in the second person you will find many others in the scriptures
workmen of lawlessness and children of the devil and named by many and various
names; and one of them is Simon, the chief of these heresies, whom we shall
show as having received all the wickedness of the devil into his own heart.
For this man, having come to Philip the apostle, preaching in Samaria, and
having been catechized by him in the word of truth and having renounced the
devil and enlisted with God, is also counted worthy of baptism, and he was
remaining with the apostle and seeing the signs and the powers accomplished
through him he was amazed; for he did not simply listen to the preaching, but
the scripture shows him remaining even for a time, so that he was fully
assured both by eye and by ear. And these things the love of God for men was
working, that he might gain him; and if he had wished, he would have obtained
this, but instead of this he thinks the opposite. For when there had been made
known


by Saint Philip to the fellow apostles in Jerusalem, that Samaria had received
the word of God and that it was time for them to be given priests also, and
when Saint Peter and John had come down from Jerusalem and were doing the
laying on of hands, those counted worthy of this grace were receiving the Holy
Spirit. And Simon, seeing that through the laying on of the apostles' hands
the Holy Spirit is given, having fallen sick with love of rule, offers gifts
to the apostles saying: Give me also this authority, that on whom I lay hands
he may receive the Holy Spirit. But Peter said to him: Your silver with you
into destruction, because you thought to acquire the gift of God through
money, and so on. This is Simon who before had renounced the devil and his
angels and all his works; this is the one who received baptism from the
apostle Philip; but praiseworthy up to this point, and after these things
pitiable. For having missed the aim and such grace and having been judged
unworthy of the ordination, he begins to war against the apostles, no longer
recognizing the workmen, no longer recognizing the one who begot him, having
forgotten all together; the one who a little before was a disciple and now an
enemy. What have you suffered, O man? Who made you so savage? Who brought you
up into this very frenzy? Why have you given yourself over wholly to the evil
one, so that your whole heart was blinded and you no longer receive the
apostles' exhortation, but think yourself more righteous than the apostles?
And he does not cease until death warring against the apostles, and he did not
shrink from crossing from Samaria as far as Rome and there cooperating with
the devil and warring against Christ. We have shown through the facts the
leader of the heresies; and it would have been enough through these things to
persuade those who are deceived whose disciples they are, whose zealots they
are, whose work they pursue, for whose sake they fight against Christ. But
that we may put them to more shame, we will persuade them that even to this
day they hold that man's teaching; and taking this proof from those very men
we will refute them from their own refutations. For as the catholic and
apostolic church saves the genealogy of Christ, so also the heresies keep the
succession of Simon. It is written in a book kept among them which they call
Acts of Peter, that a certain Gemellus became a genuine disciple of Simon; he
remaining with him until the last shame and death. And this man's name lies
upon the heresy of the false Apotactites even until now, for they are surnamed
Gemellites. This Gemellus after the fall of Simon and the bitter death (for
the death of sinners is evil), not finding a place to harm anyone or to
deceive in Rome, as the writing testifies, for neither is there such a heresy
in it, takes these parts and finding the nation simple and easy and
inexperienced in the error he begins to teach and to ordain and to maltreat
all the mysteries of the Christians and names himself an Apotactite. And he
was a heresiarch himself, having succeeded to the heresy from Simon, neither
having received baptism from anyone, nor having been named a Christian at all,
or having been a disciple of Simon alone and having been benefited with that
man's things. This beginning the heresy had. And as time went on and certain
people were deceived because the leaders of the churches of Christ were being
driven by those then persecuting the church, and being one heresy they seemed
to escape notice, and again their teacher the devil, having them once under
his hand and obedient in all things, divides them into two thus; certain among
them being found who had animals were abhorred by those who did not have them,
for those who did not have them thrust these out as unclean and unholy of such
a renunciation, and they alone inherited the name of their leader of
destruction, Gemellus. But indeed the devil cuts even them again; for having
once taken hold of them, as a toy


he uses them. For finding certain among them wearing sackcloth he separated
them from those wearing woolen garments, as from the impious. But not even up
to this was the devil filled with the deceit against them; for again he splits
those who have put on the falsely named renunciation. And up to this there was
even some pretext for the devil, through which he made the separation for
them; for on the pretext of four-footed animals or of a sackcloth the
separation was coming about among them. But now there is no pretext of the
separation, for all are equal; for all bear not the name of Christ but of
Simon and Gemellus, and those are Apotactites, and all successors of Simon and
Gemellus. And what is the separation? And what is the cause? Tell us, that we
may know; you have renounced Christ and all his mystery, then also one
another, no longer finding anyone to renounce. Once deprived of all goods they
renounce one another; for abhorring one another and separating from one
another they think they are separating as from enemies, which they also name a
renunciation. Let us see also the other figure which these have thrown away as
useless. What name will they invent for them, having been cast out of such a
renunciation? But nevertheless those too find a name for themselves easily;
for they name themselves Encratites because they array themselves against
those. For having been separated from one another they have an irreconcilable
war against those, so that we may see the continence of these and from what
they are continent; and to promise continence or renunciation in word is
nothing wonderful nor a cause of labor; but not word only is sought, but also
the work. For the Lord says in the Gospels: Why do you call me, Lord, Lord,
and do not do what I say; and Isaiah the prophet: This people honors me with
the lips, but their heart is far from me; and they worship me in vain, and so
on. For the Lord having shut up all the power of the scripture of the old and
new covenant into two commandments in this: You shall love the Lord your God
with all your heart and with all your strength and with all your mind, and
your neighbor as yourself. Do these then keep these commandments? But entirely
none of the intelligent will trust the promises, unless he learns in you the
fruits of the earth. For you are ready to promise great things and through
these to deceive the unstable. And above we showed this, that Simon himself,
having apostatized from the apostles, said he was the power of God. See how in
short he found himself a name able to strike those who are infants. And his
disciple Gemellus, having preached renunciation, made an apostasy. Perhaps
then you too, following your teachers consistently, promise these things in
word, being content with the works; for you say that: "I love God, I love the
neighbor also and I am an Encratite." First if you wished to be continent, of
whatever you would have wished; for by this it was possible both to benefit
yourself and the neighbor; for we find in the scriptures that to the righteous
sinners were granted, but with sinners righteous men were condemned together.
And one could say many things about these, but let us take hold of what is
before us. Whom do you call the neighbor? Whom you love? Entirely according to
the word of the Lord every man is your neighbor; for this is what the Gospel
shows. If then you loved the neighbor, you would not have fled the church,
leaving all together, having imitated the Pharisee and having fallen sick with
that man's pride, having condemned all by your word and having thought
yourself alone righteous. For that man too, having gone up into the temple,
was praying these things: I thank you, God, that I am not as the rest of men,
robbers, unjust, adulterers, and as this tax collector; I fast twice in the
sabbath, I tithe all that I acquire. Of these he lied in nothing, but even if
he seemed to speak the true things, he is condemned; not because he fasted or

John himself, says: it is the Lord. Simon Peter then, having heard that it is
the Lord, took the outer garment and girded himself, for he was naked, and
threw himself into the sea; and the other disciples came in the little boat,
for they were not far from the land but about two hundred cubits, dragging the
net of the fishes. When then they disembarked onto the land, they see a
charcoal fire lying and a fish lying upon it and bread. Jesus says to them:
bring of the fishes which you caught now. Simon Peter went up and dragged the
net onto the land full of large fishes, a hundred fifty-three; and being so
many the net was not split. Jesus says to them: come, breakfast. And none of
the disciples dared to examine him: who are you? knowing that it is the Lord.
Jesus comes and takes the bread and gives to them and the fish likewise. And
when they had breakfasted, then consequently he commands Peter and the
remaining apostles how they will shepherd humanity. You see the goodness of
God? You see the love of men of the master? He did not condemn the apostles
nor blame these nor strike them saying: "Why did you leave the preaching and
turn to the catch of the fishes?" But he also visits them and exhorts them and
cooperates with them toward the hunt and prepares breakfast for them, having
made ready bread and the fish, that in this too he may show that he himself is
craftsman of earth and sea. And what also does the blessed Luke the evangelist
say even after the resurrection? For first he says the holy women had seen the
tomb opened and had heard from the angels: Why do you seek the living with the
dead? He is not here, but as he said he was raised from the dead. Having
learned these things from the angels they announced to the eleven disciples,
that the Lord was raised from the dead. And Saint Peter, having heard, ran to
the tomb and having stooped he sees the linen cloths lying and he went away to
himself wondering. And two certain of them were going to a certain village, to
whom also the Lord, having appeared and having refuted their unbelief, was
teaching them, that the Christ the Lord must suffer and rise. And when they
had returned to Jerusalem and were relating to the eleven disciples how the
Lord appeared to them on the road, and while the disciples were still troubled
and reasoning about this, the Lord himself stood in their midst, saying peace
to you, it is I, do not fear. And having been terrified and become trembling
they thought they were beholding a spirit. And he said to them: Why are you
troubled, and why do reasonings go up in your hearts? See my hands and my
feet, that I myself am; handle me and see, that a spirit does not have flesh
and bones as you behold me having. And having said this he showed them the
hands and the feet. And while they still disbelieved from the joy and were
wondering he said to them: do you have anything eatable here? And they gave
him a portion of a broiled fish and from a honeycomb of a bee; and having
taken it he ate before them, that he might persuade them also through the
food, that he himself is the one who even before the passion was with them and
living with them.


Who will still dare to fight against God? Who will be brought down into such
unbelief as to disbelieve these things? If everywhere it has been shown
through both the old and the new covenant that the creatures of God are
sanctified, whether they are offered to God, or even the Christ who became man
partakes, through all things his own creatures are sanctified. Let the
heretics then cease correcting God. For to say: "This he did well, this he
erred in doing," is not of a man who has a mind, but of one who is mad and
fights against God. Let us see, whether they themselves abstain from all
things with souls according to the teaching of the unclean Manichees. For
those men's chiefs, having once legislated to abstain from things with souls
because of the impiety dwelling in them, said even the things that grow from
the earth are with souls; and they tore testimonies from the scriptures; for
as it says in Daniel: Bless the Lord, you beasts and cattle and all the birds
of heaven, so it says: Bless the Lord, all things that grow from the earth.
And having followed their own impiety consistently they were dashed down the
cliff of error; for it is no longer even possible for those very men who say
these things to live in flesh. And it is consistent for the imitators of those
men also to follow the teachers; for that the seeds have a vital power and
bodies, this we also say; but to abstain from things with souls, this you
legislated for yourself; and since you have stood against the Lord's
ordinance, keep at least his confession, abstaining from the things with
souls and from other bodies. For the Lord says in the Gospels concerning the
seeds: And if it dies, it bears much fruit. It is clear that what is living
dies; and you once watched against eating the things that have acquired the
vital power. And the apostle Paul: What you sow is not made alive, unless it
dies; and what you sow, you do not sow the body that will come to be, but a
naked grain, perhaps of wheat or of some of the rest; and God gives it a body,
