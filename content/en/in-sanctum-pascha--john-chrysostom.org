A Discourse on Holy Pascha.

=52.765=

αʹ. It is timely today for us all to cry out that which was spoken by the
blessed David: Who shall speak the mighty acts of the Lord, shall make all his
praises to be heard? For behold, the longed-for and saving feast has come to
us, the resurrection day of our Lord Jesus Christ, the ground of peace, the
occasion of reconciliation, the abolition of wars, the dissolution of death,
the =52.766= defeat of the devil. Today men have been mixed with the angels,
and those who wear a body now offer the hymns together with the bodiless
powers. Today the tyranny of the devil is dissolved; today the bonds of death
have been loosed, the victory of Hades has been made to vanish; today it is
timely again to speak that prophetic voice: Where is your sting, O death?
where is your victory, O Hades? Today our Master Christ has crushed the
brazen =52.767= gates, and he has made even the very face of death to vanish.

And why do I say the face? He has changed its very name; for it is no longer
called death, but falling-asleep and sleep; for before the coming of Christ,
and the economy of the cross, even the very name of death was terrible. For
the first man, when he came into being, was condemned to this as a great
penalty, hearing: In the day you eat, you shall die by death. And the blessed
Job also addressed it by this name, saying: Death is rest to a man. And the
prophet David said: The death of sinners is evil. And not only was the
dissolution of the soul from the body called death, but also Hades. For hear
the patriarch Jacob saying: You shall bring down my old age with sorrow into
Hades; and the prophet again: Hades has opened his mouth; and again another
prophet saying: He shall deliver me from the lowest Hades; and in many places
you will find the departure from here called death and Hades in the Old
Testament. But when Christ our God was offered as a sacrifice, and the things
of the resurrection went forward, the Master who loves mankind took away even
the names themselves, and introduced a new and strange way of life into our
life; for instead of death the departure from here is now called
falling-asleep and sleep. And from where is this clear? Hear Christ himself
saying: Lazarus our friend has fallen asleep, but I go to awaken him. For just
as it is easy for us to wake and raise one who is sleeping, so also for the
common Master of us all is the raising. And since what was said by him was new
and strange, not even the disciples understood what was spoken, until,
condescending to their weakness, he spoke it more plainly.

And the teacher of the inhabited world, the blessed Paul, writing to the
Thessalonians, says: I do not want you to be ignorant concerning those who
have fallen asleep, that you may not grieve, as also the rest who have no
hope. And again elsewhere: Then those who have fallen asleep in Christ have
perished. And again: We who are alive, who are left, shall not precede those
who have fallen asleep. And again in another place: For if we believe that
Jesus died and rose, so also God will bring those who have fallen asleep with
him.

βʹ. Have you seen death now called falling-asleep and sleep everywhere? and
him who before this had a terrible face, now become contemptible after the
resurrection? Have you seen the bright trophy of the resurrection? Through
this the countless goods have been brought in to us; through this the deceit
of the demons has been dissolved; through this we laugh at death; through this
we look down on the present life; through this we are urged on toward the
desire of the things to come; through this, though we wear a body, we have
nothing less than the bodiless, if we will. Today our bright victory-rites
have come to pass; today our Master, having set up the trophy against death,
and having dissolved the tyranny of the devil, has granted the way through the
resurrection unto salvation. Let us all therefore rejoice, leap, be glad. For
even if it is our Master who conquered and set up the trophy, yet the gladness
and the joy are common also to us.

For he worked all things for our salvation; and through the very things by
which the devil wrestled us down, through these =52.768= Christ prevailed over
him. He took the very weapons, and with these he contended against him; and
how, hear. A virgin and a tree and death became the symbols of our defeat. For
Eve was a virgin; for she did not yet know a man, when she underwent the
deception; the tree was wood; death was the penalty against Adam. Have you
seen how a virgin and a tree and death became for us the symbols of the
defeat? See then how these same things have again become the causes of the
victory. Instead of Eve, Mary; instead of the tree of the knowledge of good
and evil, the tree of the cross; instead of the death of Adam, the Master's
death. Have you seen him being defeated through the very things by which he
conquered? At the tree the devil contended against Adam; at the cross Christ
wrestled down the devil. And that tree sent men into Hades, but this tree, the
tree of the cross, even called back again from Hades those who had departed;
and that tree hid the one who had been defeated as a captive and naked, but
this showed the victor naked, nailed on high, to all. And the one death
condemned even those after him; but the other truly raised even those before
him. Who shall speak the mighty acts of the Lord, shall make all his praises
to be heard? From death we have become immortal, from a fall we have risen,
from a defeat we have been set as victors.

γʹ. These are the achievements of the cross, this is the greatest proof of the
resurrection. Today angels leap, and all the heavenly powers are glad, sharing
the joy at the salvation of the common race of men.

For if there is joy in heaven and on earth over one sinner who repents, much
more over the salvation of the inhabited world. Today, having freed human
nature from the tyranny of the devil, he has brought it back to its former
nobility. For when I see my first-fruits thus prevailing over death, I no
longer fear, I no longer shudder at the war; nor do I look to my own weakness,
but I consider the unspeakable power of the one who is to fight with me. For
he who prevailed over the tyranny of death, and took away all its strength,
what will he not now work concerning his own kind, whose form he also deigned
to take on through much love of mankind, and through this to make the
wrestling against the devil?

Today there is joy everywhere in the inhabited world and spiritual gladness.
Today both the people of the angels and the choir of all the powers above are
glad because of the salvation of men.

Consider then, beloved, the greatness of the joy, that even the powers above
keep the feast with us; for they rejoice together with our goods. For even if
the grace from the Master is ours, yet the pleasure is theirs also. Therefore
they are not ashamed to keep the feast with us. And why do I say that our
fellow-servants are not ashamed to keep the feast with us? The Master himself
of them and of us is not ashamed to keep the feast with us. And what did I
say, he is not ashamed? He even desires to keep the feast with us. From where
is this clear? Hear him saying: With desire I have desired to eat this Pascha
with you; and if he desired to eat the Pascha, then clearly also to keep the
feast together. When therefore you see not only angels and the people of all
the heavenly powers, but even the Master of the angels himself keeping the
feast with us, =52.769= what is left to you any longer in the account of
gladness? Let no one then be downcast today because of poverty; for it is a
spiritual feast. Let no rich man be lifted up because of wealth; for he cannot
even bring anything into this feast from his money. For in the case of the
feasts outside, I mean the worldly ones, where there is much display both of
outward dress and of luxury at the table, there reasonably the poor man will
be in despondency and downcastness, and the rich man in pleasure and
brightness. Why? Because the one puts on a splendid garment and sets out a
more luxurious table; but the poor man is hindered by poverty from showing the
same munificence. But here there is nothing of this sort, but all this
inequality is out of the way, and there is one table both for rich and poor,
both for slave and free.

And if you are rich, you have nothing more than the poor man; and if you are
poor, you will have nothing less than the rich, nor is your spiritual banquet
diminished because of poverty; for the grace is divine, and has not regarded a
difference of persons. And why do I say that the same table is set before rich
and poor? Even for him who wears the diadem and has the purple, who has taken
the authority of the inhabited world, and for the beggar who sits for alms,
one table is set. For such are the spiritual gifts: they do not divide the
communion by ranks, but by purpose and mind. With the same freedom of speech
and honor both the king and the beggar rush toward the enjoyment and communion
of these divine mysteries. And why do I say, with the same honor? Often the
poor man with more freedom of speech. Why? Because the king, circled by cares
of affairs, and surrounded by many circumstances, as though he were on a sea,
is thus splashed from every side by successive waves, and many sins are rubbed
upon him; but the poor man, delivered from all these, and caring only for
necessary food, and pursuing the unmeddlesome and quiet life, sitting as in a
harbor and a calm, comes to the table with much reverence.

δʹ. And not this only, but also from many other things various despondencies
are born for those occupied with the worldly feasts. For there again the poor
man is in downcastness, and the rich man in brightness, not through the table
alone and the luxury, but also through the bright garments and the display of
clothing. What they suffer then at the table, this they also undergo in the
case of the garments. When therefore the poor man sees the rich man clothed in
a more luxurious robe, he is struck with pain, he counted himself wretched, he
uttered countless curses. But here even this despondency itself has been taken
away; for there is one garment for all, the saving clothing; and Paul cries
saying: As many of you as were baptized into Christ have put on Christ. Let us
not then, I beg, put this feast to shame, but let us take up a mind worthy of
the things given us by the grace of Christ. Let us not give ourselves over to
drunkenness and gluttony, but having considered the munificence of our Master,
and that he has honored rich and poor alike and slaves and free, and has
poured out the gift in common upon all, let us repay the benefactor for his
goodwill toward us; and a sufficient repayment is a life that is pleasing, and
a soul sober =52.770= and wakeful. This feast and festival does not need
money, does not need expense, but purpose alone and a pure mind.

Nothing bodily is to be bought from here, but all things spiritual: the
hearing of divine oracles, the prayers of Fathers, the blessings of priests,
the communion of the divine and unspeakable mysteries, peace and concord, and
spiritual gifts worthy of the munificence of the giver. Let us therefore keep
this feast in which the Lord rose. For he rose, and raised the inhabited world
together with himself. And he himself rose, having torn apart the bonds of
death; but he raised us, having loosed the chains of our sins. Adam sinned and
died; Christ did not sin, and he died. A new and paradoxical thing: that one
sinned and died; this one did not sin, and he died. For what reason, and why?
In order that the one who sinned and died might be able, through the one who
did not sin and died, to be freed from the bonds of death. Thus it often
happens also in the case of those who owe money; someone owes silver to
someone, and does not have it to pay down, and for this reason is held;
another, not owing, but able to pay down, having paid, has released the one
liable. So also it has happened in the case of Adam and in the case of Christ.
Adam owed death, and was held by the devil; Christ did not owe, nor was he
held; but he came and paid down death on behalf of the one held, that he might
release that one from the bonds of death. Have you seen the achievements of
the resurrection? have you seen the love of mankind of the Master? have you
seen the greatness of care? Let us not then become ungrateful toward so great
a benefactor, nor, since the fast has passed, become more careless; but now
rather than before let us make greater care of the soul, lest when the flesh
is fattened this become weaker, lest while caring for the maidservant we
neglect the mistress.

For what profit, tell me, is there in bursting beyond need, and overstepping
due measure? This both injures the body and betrays the nobility of the soul.
But let us become of self-sufficiency and of need, that we may fulfill what is
fitting both for soul and for body, that we may not pour out all at once the
things gathered from the fast. Do I forbid enjoying food and being relaxed? I
do not forbid this, but I advise becoming of need, and cutting off much
luxury, and not, overstepping the measure, injuring the health of the soul.
For neither will such a one any longer enjoy pleasure who has overstepped the
bounds of need; and this those who have come through the experience itself
know most exactly; having begotten for themselves from this countless kinds of
diseases, and having undergone much disgust. But that you will be persuaded by
our exhortations I do not doubt; for I know your obedience.

εʹ. And for this reason, having set the exhortation about this here, I wish to
turn the word toward those who in this light-bearing night have been counted
worthy of the gift of divine baptism, these fair plants of the Church, the
spiritual flowers, the new soldiers of Christ. Before yesterday the Master was
on the cross, but he has risen now; so also these, before yesterday were held
by sin, but now they have risen together with Christ; he died in the body and
rose, they had died by sin, and they have risen from sin. The =52.771= earth,
then, at this season of spring puts forth for us roses and violets and other
flowers; but the waters today have shown us a meadow more delightful than the
earth. And do not wonder, beloved, if meadows of flowers have been shown from
the waters; for neither did the earth from the beginning put forth the growth
of the plants according to its own nature, but yielding to the command of the
Master. And the waters then also put forth living creatures that move, when
they heard: Let the waters bring forth creeping things of living souls; and
the command became a work; the soulless substance cast out ensouled living
things.

So also now the same command has worked all things. Then he said: Let the
waters bring forth creeping things of living souls; but now they have not put
forth creeping things, but spiritual gifts. Then the waters brought forth
irrational fishes; but now they have borne for us rational and spiritual
fishes, caught by the apostles. For Come, he says, after me, and I will make
you fishers of men. New indeed is the manner of this fishing. For those who
fish cast the fishes out from the waters, and they kill the things that are
caught; but we cast them into the waters, and those who are caught are made
alive. There was once also among the Jews a pool of water; but learn what it
was able to do, that you may know exactly the Jewish poverty, and may have
knowledge of our wealth. An angel came down there, it says, and troubled the
water, and the first who went down after the troubling enjoyed the healing.
The Master of the angels came down into the streams of the Jordan, and having
sanctified the nature of the waters he healed the whole inhabited world.
Therefore there, after the first, the one who went down was no longer healed;
for the grace was given to the Jews who were sick, who were crawling on the
ground; but here after the first the second goes down, after the second the
third and fourth; and if you should name countless, and if you should cast the
whole inhabited world into these spiritual streams, the grace is not spent,
the gift is not consumed, the streams are not defiled, the munificence is not
diminished. Have you seen the greatness of the gift? Hear, you who today and
in this night have been enrolled as citizens into the Jerusalem above, and
show a guarding worthy of the greatness of the gifts, that you may also draw a
more abundant grace; for gratitude for the things already done calls forth
the munificence =52.772= of the Master.

It is no longer permitted you, beloved, to live indifferently; but set for
yourself laws and canons, so as to accomplish all things with exactness, and
to show much guarding even concerning the things supposed to be indifferent.
For the whole present life is a contest and a wrestling, and those who have
once entered this stadium of virtue ought to be temperate in all things; For
everyone who contends is temperate in all things. Do you not see in the
gymnastic contests how much care of themselves those make who take up the
wrestling against men, and with how much temperance they display the training
of the body? So then here also. Since our wrestling is not against men, but
against the spiritual things of wickedness, let our training also and our
temperance be spiritual; since our weapons also, which the Master has clothed
us with, are spiritual. Let the eye then also have bounds and canons, so as
not to leap simply upon all things that fall in its way; and let the tongue
have a wall, so as not to run ahead of the mind. For this reason also the
teeth and the lips have been created for the security of the tongue, that the
tongue may never simply throw open the doors and go out, but when it has well
arranged the things concerning itself, then with all good order it may go
forth, and may bring forth such words as to give grace to those who hear, and
may utter those things which tend toward the building of the hearers. And one
must also entirely turn away from disorderly laughter, and have the gait calm
and quiet, and the dress modest, and through all things simply it is fitting
that the one who has enrolled himself into the stadium of virtue be ordered;
for the good order of the outward members is a certain image of the state in
the soul.

ςʹ. If from the beginning we set ourselves into such a habit, walking
thereafter in the way with ease, we shall accomplish all virtue, and we shall
not even need much toil, and we shall draw much help from above. For thus we
shall be able both to run through the waves of the present life with safety,
and, having become superior to the snares of the devil, to attain the eternal
goods, by the grace and loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom to
the Father together with the Holy Spirit be glory, might, honor, now and
always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
