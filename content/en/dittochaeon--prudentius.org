Dittochaeon edited by H.J. Thomson

Eve was then a white dove; thereafter black,
made by the snake's ill-counseling fraud through venom,
she stained even innocent Adam with filthy spots;
the victorious serpent then gives fig leaves to the naked.

God with differing nod judges the offerings of two brothers,
accepting the living and refusing the earthly.
The countryman in envy lays the shepherd low: in Abel
the form of the soul is expressed, our flesh in Cain's gift.

As messenger of the flood already lessening, the dove
brings back to the ark in her mouth a branch of greening olive.
For the raven, caught by gluttony among foul corpses,
had stuck; she brings back the new joys of granted peace.

This is the Lord's lodging, where the leafy holm-oak of Mamre
covered the old man's herdsman roof; in that
cottage Sarah laughed that the late joys of offspring
were being brought her, and that her decrepit husband could thus believe.

Abraham bought a field in which he might lay the bones
of his wife, since justice and faith dwell as strangers
in the lands: this cave bought by him for thousands
is where rest was granted to the holy ash.

Twice seven ears, as many cows, seen by Pharaoh
in a dream, by unlike form of plenty and of famine
portend that two times through seven-year spans
are at hand; this the patriarch solves, with Christ as interpreter.

sold by his brothers' snares, the boy himself in turn
orders a cup to be hidden secretly in the sack of grain,
and Joseph holds them guilty of theft; the deceitful sale
is betrayed; they recognize their brother and blush at pardon.

God, fiery, flying among the thorns with flashing mouth,
addresses the young man, then by chance master of the flock.
He, commanded, takes the rod; the rod becomes a viper.
He looses the bonds of his feet; he hastens to Pharaoh's citadel.

The just man makes a safe journey even through the great sea.
Lo, the red waters split apart for the servants of God,
while the same waters drown raging sinners.
Pharaoh is overwhelmed; a free way lay open for Moses.

The mountain's peak smokes with divine fires, on which
a page of stones written with ten words is handed
to Moses; he, having received the law, revisits his people,
but for them the form of a calf is their only god, and god is gold.

The tents of the fathers grow white with angelic breads;
sure is the faith of the deed: a golden jar thereafter holds
the saved manna; for the ungrateful another cloud comes
and a piled quail saturates those greedy for flesh.

The dry way of the desert boiled with black serpents,
and already poisoned bites through livid wounds
were wasting the people, but the prudent leader hung
on a cross a snake polished in bronze, to temper the venom.

A pool harsh to taste, while the people thirsted,
held gloomy waters with stagnant gall.
Holy Moses says: 'Give wood, throw it into this flood;
the bitter things will be turned into a sweet savor.'

The men arrived under Moses' lead, where six springs
and by chance six others watered from glassy dew
seventy palms; which mystic grove of Elim
also painted the apostolic number in the books.

The Jordan is borne into a spring with rcfluent flood,
while it leaves dry fords to be trodden by the peoples of God;
twice six stones as witnesses, which in the river itself
the fathers set up in the form of the disciples.

Jericho fell; Rahab's halls alone stand.
The harlot hostess of the saints (so great is the force of faith),
safe with her house unharmed, offers conspicuous scarlet
against hostile fires as a sign of blood.

Samson sets out to break the lion unconquered by virtue of mane;
he kills the beast, but from the lion's mouth
honeys flow; the jaw of an ass of its own accord vomits a fountain:
folly overflows with waters, virtue with sweetness.

Samson takes three hundred foxes, arms them with fires,
binds torches behind to their tails, sends them into the crops
of the foreigners and burns the harvests: so the cunning fox
of heresy now scatters the flames of vices into the fields.

David was small, last of his brothers, and now Jesse's
care of the flock, forming the lyre at the paternal sheepfold,
thence to the king's delights; soon he joins horrid wars
and with whistling sling lays Goliath low.

The royal insignia of wondrous David shine,
scepter, oil, horn, diadem and purple and altar.
All things suit Christ, the cloak and the crown,
the rod of power, the horn of the cross, the altar, the olive.

Wisdom builds a temple through Solomon's
service; the queen of the south heaps heavy gold.
The time is at hand when Christ shall build a temple of man
under the breast, which the Greek may worship, which the barbarian may
enrich.

By chance while the sons of the prophets were cutting wood
on the river's bank, the shaken two-edged axe fell.
The iron was sunk in the flood, but soon a light wood
thrown into the pools made the iron recallable.

The people of the Hebrews, caught by frequent sin,
had wept exile at the streams of dire Babylon;
then, commanded, it refuses to sing the ancestral measures
and hangs the organs on the branches of the bitter willow.

Here good Hezekiah deserved through thrice five years
to put forward the prescribed day and the law of dying

to stretch, which the sun, turned toward the rising, taught
when the steps which evening had covered with shade were poured with
light.

As God was approaching, the messenger Gabriel came down from on high
from the Father's throne and suddenly
enters the virgin's seat. /The Holy Spirit,/ he says,

/will fill you, Mary; you will bear Christ, holy virgin./

Holy Bethlehem is the head of the world, which brought forth Jesus,
the beginning of the world, the head itself of beginnings.
The city begot the man Christ, who as Christ was acting
as God before the sun was made, before the morning star was.

Here the Magi bring precious gifts to Christ
the boy under the virgin's breast, of myrrh and of incense and of gold.
The mother wonders at so many honors of a chaste womb,
and that she has borne God as man, and also the highest King.

The force of angelic light fills the watchful eyes of the shepherds,
celebrating Christ born of a virgin.

they find him covered with cloths; the manger for the one lying
was a cradle; they exult eager and adore the godhead.

The impious enemy Herod rages with countless slaughters of infants,
while he seeks Christ among them.
The cradles smoke with the milky blood of the little ones
and the pious breasts of mothers drip with warm wounds.

The Baptist, fed on locusts and the honeycombs of the woods
and clothed in a camel's garment, pours over in the river;
he had also dipped Christ, but the Spirit sent from heaven
testifies that the one dipped is he who grants crimes to the dipped.

At the destruction of the old temple a pinnacle stands surviving;
for an angle built from that stone remains even
unto age of age, which the builders rejected;
now it is the head of the temple and the joining of new stones.

They were celebrating the covenants of marriage with an auspicious
gathering, Galileans by chance; already wines were lacking to the
servants;

Christ orders the water-jars to be filled quickly with waters;
from there a wave of old wine is drawn.

a water, medicine of diseases, which the spirit at various
hours belches forth, poured by a hidden law;
they call it Siloam, where the Savior ordered the eyes of the blind man,
smeared with spittle, to be washed from the spring.

The dancing virgin demands funeral prizes,
the head of John, cut off, which she may carry on a platter
to the lap of her unchaste mother; the royal singing-girl
bears the gift, her hands sprinkled from just blood.

The Lord goes through the midst of the sea and, treading the liquid
waves with his heel, orders the disciple to come down from the unstable
boat, but mortal trembling sinks his soles; yet he guides the hand and
firms the footsteps.

The demon had broken iron bonds under the sepulchral prison:
he bursts out and rolls at Jesus' feet.
But the Lord claims the man for himself and orders the enemy
to madden the herds of swine and to be sunk through the waters.

God broke five loaves and twin fishes;
with these he richly saturated five thousand of men.
Baskets are filled with too great a fragment of crumbs,
twice six; so great is the opulence of the eternal table.

The place in Bethany, conscious of the notable deed,
saw you, Lazarus, returned from the infernal seat.
The tomb appears split, its doors broken,
from which the limbs of the rotting buried one returned.

The field of Akeldama, sold for the wage of unspeakable crime,
full of mounds, receives funerals to be buried.
This is the price of Christ's blood. Unhappy Judas at a distance
tightens his neck with a noose for so great a crime.

Lo, the impious house of blasphemous Caiaphas has fallen,
in which the holy face of Christ was struck with blows.
This end awaits sinners, whose overwhelmed
life will lie without end in ruinous tombs.

bound, the Lord stood in these buildings, and fastened
to the column he gave his back as a slave to the whips.

the venerable column still stands and bears a shrine,
and teaches us to live immune from all scourges.

Pierced through either side, Christ drives forth water and gore:
blood is victory, the water is a washing.
Then two thieves on neighboring crosses on this side and that
disagree: that one denies God, this one bears the crown.

Stone did not hold Christ, nor the bars of the tomb;

death lies conquered for him; he trampled the abyss.

the people of the saints went at once to the upper shores;

and he gave himself to many to be proved by touch and by eyes.1

From the peak of the olive-bearing mount Christ went up
back to the Father, marking the footprints of peace.
From ever-living foliage a very rich moisture is melted,
which proves the gift poured on the lands from the chrism.

Stephen first enters upon the reward with a rain of blood,
struck with stones; yet he, bloody, among the rocks
asks Christ that the stoning not be a charge
to the enemies, O wondrous piety of the first crown!

The gate of the temple remains, which they called Beautiful,
the excellent work of Solomon; but in it a greater
work of Christ shone forth; for the lame man, ordered to rise
by Peter's mouth, marveled that his loosened 1 steps ran.

Peter dreams a disk slipped down from high heaven
filled with animals of every kind: he refuses
to eat, but the Lord orders him to think all things clean.
He rises and calls the unclean nations to the mysteries.

Here the wolf once rapacious is clothed in a soft fleece:
he who had been Saul, with light taken away, becomes Paul.
Soon he receives sight, becomes apostle and teacher
of the peoples and powerful in mouth to change ravens into doves.

The twice twelve seat of elders, gleaming with bowls and lyres
and with the badges of as many crowns, praises the Lamb
bloodied by slaughter, who alone could unroll the book
and open the seven seals.
