* Homily in Praise of Mary the Theotokos
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of our father among the saints Epiphanius, bishop of the city of Constantia,
of the island of the Cyprians, an encomium on the holy Theotokos.
Having spoken of the flashing radiances of the Theotokos, the dread rays and
incomprehensible powers, the mystery of heaven and of the inhabited earth, a
wonder, a mercy-seat =43.488=, I the wretched one, the thought that leapt
within my heart toward a more exact conception of deep memory, trying to bear
the wonder of the highest contemplation, a great and terrible trembling and
fear seized me, O beloved. For the memory of the fearful contemplation,
bringing my soul to trembling and my heart to dread, did not punish me
moderately, but tortured me mightily. For the memory of the greatest
contemplation strikes my mind, as of an incomprehensible mystery, not having
strength to speak. For who is sufficient to speak such a mystery? And what
mouth shall utter it, or what tongue shall tell it? It has escaped notice; it
has no strength to speak. But now I shall dare concerning the only Theotokos
according to what I grasp; and again I am afraid.

I have no tongue to speak worthily the things that are exceedingly great. For
I am thin-voiced and slow of tongue, and not eloquent, that I may so speak,
concerning the greatly-named and holy and unwedded and Theotokos Mary, mother
of the Lord; concerning whom the tongue of men will not easily speak. For
toward her the tongue grows thin-voiced and draws back, not having worthiness
to speak. For she also astonished the powers of the heavens; all angels,
archangels, principalities, authorities, thrones, dominions, the cherubim and
the seraphim and every army of angels were amazed, seized with fear and
terrible trembling. For they beheld on earth in her the one who is in the
heavens, and they shuddered. They saw the Virgin, heaven and throne, and they
were held by fear, beholding the beginningless one seated from the cherubic
throne into a virginal womb. O blessed root; from where did it sprout this
one? Concerning her the prophet Isaiah warbles as a swallow, crying with fiery
tongue: A rod shall arise from the root of Jesse, and a flower shall go up
from the root, and the Spirit of the fear of God shall rest upon it. And from
the root of Jesse is David the king, and from the tribe of David the king is
the holy Virgin, the holy daughter of holy men, whose parents are Joachim and
Anna, who in their life were well-pleasing to God, who also sprouted such a
fruit, the holy Virgin Mary, temple of God and mother together; and Joachim
and Anna and Mary, the three, clearly ministered to the Trinity. For Joachim
is interpreted preparation of the Lord; for from him was prepared a temple of
the Lord, the Virgin. And Anna likewise is interpreted grace; for Joachim and
Anna received grace, that they might sprout such a fruit through prayer,
having obtained the holy Virgin. For Joachim was praying on the mountain, and
Anna in her garden. And Anna, having conceived, bore heaven and a cherubic
throne, the holy maiden Mary. For she is found to be heaven and temple and
throne. For Mary is interpreted lady, but also hope. For she bore the Lord,
the hope of the whole =43.489= world, Christ. Again Mary is interpreted myrrh
of the sea; and it will say myrrh, which I also say, concerning immortality,
because she was about to bear the immortal pearl in the sea, that is, in the
world. And it will call the whole world a sea, to which the Virgin granted
calm, having borne the harbor, Christ.

Therefore again the blessed name of the august maiden Mary is interpreted one
being enlightened, who was enlightened by the Son of God, and enlightened
those who believed unto the ends of the earth in the Trinity. For a bride of
the Trinity is the holy Theotokos maiden


Mary, the all-unspeakable treasure of the economy; to whom Gabriel: Hail, full
of grace, the Lord is with you. Gabriel put her in remembrance, and the Father
from heaven sent the Holy Spirit as a pledge, and moved the Virgin toward the
only-begotten Son, a heavenly bride, whom the Father loved, the Son dwelt in,
the Holy Spirit longed for. For she is bride and bridal chamber, and from her
the bridegroom Christ going forth, the virginal garment, according to the
melody of the prophet David: In the sun he set his tabernacle, and he as a
bridegroom going forth from his chamber. O desire of the bridegroom's
chastity! Gabriel hastens to his own handmaid, bride, and mother; he became
the sponsor of the Virgin, joining the incense of heaven and earth; whom the
prophets clearly described, proclaiming the order of the marriage, how the
Virgin is found a heavenly bride and mother together, she who received before
the marriage the gifts, the Holy Spirit, and as dowry heaven and paradise
together.

The Virgin therefore was seeking to see the one she longed for, and she said:
Where is the bridegroom whose beauty I desire? Where is the beauty brighter
than the sun? Where is the unspeakable glory of all comeliness? Where is the
unquenchable light which, longing, I seek? Where is the sun that seeks a ray?
Where is the lyre of the cherubim? Where is the unsleeping eye of the
seraphim, Christ? Where is the worship of the angels, which Gabriel announced?
Where is the only only-begotten Son of the only Father, whom the Virgin,
longing and loving together and seeking, greets? To whom Gabriel: Hail, full
of grace. The boundless grace of the holy Virgin. Hail, full of grace, adorned
with many virtues, O Virgin bearing as a lamp the unquenchable light brighter
than the sun. Hail, full of grace, the bait of the noetic hook; for in you the
divinity is a hook. Hail, full of grace, the intellectual ark of glory; Hail,
full of grace, the golden jar, having the heavenly manna. Hail, full of grace,
who filled the thirsty with the sweetness of the ever-flowing spring. Hail,
full of grace, intellectual sea, having the heavenly pearl, Christ; Hail, full
of grace, the bright heaven, who have God who is uncontained in =43.492= the
heavens. Hail, full of grace, who flash forth the cherubic throne of the
divinity. Hail, full of grace, having the circle of heaven, and God
uncontained, but in you contained, and not straitened. Hail, full of grace,
pillar-like cloud, who have God, who guided the people in the wilderness. What
shall I say? And what shall I speak? How shall I bless the glory that is
wholly rooted? because apart from God alone she is above all. For she is more
beautiful than the cherubim and seraphim, and than every angelic army; for
whom the tongue of heaven and earth will not suffice, and perhaps not even of
angels. For they too offered hymn and praise and honor and glory; but not even
so were they able to speak according to worth. And the angels were rejoicing,
as themselves alone having God, compared with whom the all-holy Virgin is
greater, having conceived on earth the God who is in heaven, that she might
thus draw armies of angels upon earth with men.

For she has become a mediator of heaven and earth, having made unity. O
blessed Virgin, pure dove, the heavenly bride Mary, heaven and temple and
throne of the divinity; who have Christ the sun flashing in heaven and on
earth; the luminous cloud, who shone upon the world from heaven the
all-shining lightning, Christ; the heavenly cloud, who brought down to the
world the thunder of the Holy Spirit hidden in herself, who dashed upon the
whole earth the rain of the Holy Spirit toward a fruit-bearing of faith. Hail,
full of grace, the gate of the heavens, concerning whom the prophet cried
saying: Behold the gate shut, and no one shall enter through it, nor shall go
out, except the Lord God alone; and the gate shall be shut for the ruler,
because he himself shall be called ruler, and in him


shall all the nations hope.

Concerning this gate also in the Song the prophet is clearly and plainly
recounted crying in the saying: A garden shut, my sister, bride; a garden
shut, a fountain sealed. The holy and august maiden Mary remained in
virginity, the luminous cloud, who has within the God Word; to whom the
loud-voiced Isaiah: Behold a light cloud, and it shall come into Egypt; the
unhewn mountain, who have the sharp-cut rock, Christ, concerning whom the most
wise Daniel says: A stone was cut from a mountain without hands; that is,
without a man the Virgin bore the solid rock, Christ, the uncompounded temple,
who have the heavenly stone, Christ, the thunder-like cloud, who bore the
lightning within in the womb; and the divine Scripture bears witness to my
word saying, Your belly is a heap of wheat fenced with lilies; the untilled
field, who received the Word as a grain of wheat, and sprouted the sheaf, the
intellectual oven, who have the fire and the bread of life; the holy mother of
the Savior, =43.493= who bore the Word of the Father made flesh from you, to
whom Isaiah: Behold the virgin shall have in the womb, and shall bear a son,
and they shall call his name Emmanuel. O holy Virgin, holy mother of the Lord;
blessed bride of the inseparable Trinity; blessed are you among women, who
bore on earth as an infant the God who is creator of all; blessed are you
among women, who alone on earth conceived the heavenly God; blessed are you
among women, whose breasts the one who feeds all things sucked; blessed are
you among women, who now have given birth to the one who once fashioned Adam
with clay in paradise, holy Theotokos virgin Mary. For you are Theotokos, who
bore the Word made flesh from you; you are Theotokos who conceived the God
Word in the form of a servant; you are Theotokos, because having received the
God Word made flesh you bore him; you are Theotokos who alone bore the only
only-begotten Son of God; not having given birth to a temporary God, but the
eternal God who is before you and before all, made flesh from you; the
spotless sheep, who bore the lamb, Christ; the heifer unyoked, who gave birth
to the calf; the intellectual table of faith, who supplied the bread of life
to the world. What shall I say? And what shall I speak of the glory that is
wholly rooted? Concerning this holy maiden who is ever-child and virgin, God
says through the prophet speaking: From you shall come forth for me a ruler,
to be for a prince in Israel; and his goings forth are from the beginning,
from the days of eternity. Therefore he shall give them until the time of her
who gives birth; she shall bear, and the remnant of his brothers shall return
upon the sons of Israel; and he shall stand, and shall see, and his flock in
strength, the Lord.

O holy Virgin, being mother of eternal light; of the light that enlightened in
the heavens the armies of the angels; of the light that enlightened the
unspeakable eye of the seraphim; of the light that enlightened the sun with
bright lamps; of the light that enlightened the ends of the inhabited world in
the Trinity; of the light that said: I am the light of the world; of the light
that has said: I have come as light into the world; of the light that was
taken up and enlightened all things in the heavens and on earth. O all-holy
Virgin, who astonished armies of angels! For a strange wonder in the heavens,
a woman bearing the light in her arms; a strange wonder in the heavens,
another cherubic throne; a strange wonder in the heavens, a son of a woman,
being father of her and of the ages; a strange wonder in the heavens, a
Virgin's chamber, having the Son of God, God the bridegroom, Christ; a strange
wonder in the heavens, the infant of the Virgin has become Master of the
angels. O all-holy Virgin, mother of the Savior, who gave birth to the
beginningless Word, the Son who shares the throne of the Father, the
consubstantial of the Father and of the Holy Spirit, the pre-eternal with the
Father and the Holy Spirit, who vaulted the heavens, who founded the earth.
Hail, all-holy Virgin, who hold the fire of the divinity =43.496= unburnt, as
an intellectual bush, the intellectual oven, who brought to the world for food
the fire and the hot bread of life; concerning which


the Savior of the world, Christ, says: Take, eat; this is my body, which is
broken for you for the forgiveness of sins.

Rich is the all-virtuous table of the virginal one, O beloved; of all manner
of good foods toward enjoyment the holy Virgin and mother of Christ granted to
the inhabited world, who bore the luminous lamp that shines in heaven and on
earth, Christ; to whom the prophet Zechariah: Behold a golden lampstand and
the little lamp upon it. Therefore David also cries this melodious song: Your
word is a lamp to my feet, and a light to my paths. O virginal lampstand, who
drive away the darkness and shine forth the light. O virginal lampstand, who
brought the fire and the oil inseparable toward illumination. O virginal
lampstand, a three-lighted one unquenchable fire, consubstantial, having
received from the highest throne, and having shone forth toward the
illumination of the inhabited world. O virginal lampstand, concerning whom God
says through the Prophet: There I will make a horn to spring up for David, I
have prepared a lamp for my Christ. The royal crown, having the pearl-like
precious stone, Christ; the royal purple, who clothed the king of heaven and
earth with the inhabited-world purple-like garment of the body; the unstained
faith of the Christians; the incomprehensible book, who read forth to the
world the Word and the Son of the Father. The blessed among women, who bore
the Word made flesh from you, Word the Son of the Father, Word the pre-eternal
God, Word beginningless and everlasting, Word who is worshipped with the
Father and the Holy Spirit, Word who shares the throne of the Father, Word who
sits upon the cherubim, Word whom the four-formed living creatures glorify,
Word who created the angels, Word who made principalities and authorities to
be, Word who stretched out the heavens and founded the earth, Word the true
God Jesus Christ our Lord, whom you bore, all-holy Virgin, and after
childbirth again a virgin, who bore the pearl, and wove the royal crown. O
blessed Virgin, who sprouted the heavenly glory, who from many flowers of
paradise filled the world with fragrance. The Virgin is a spotless lily, who
gave birth to the unfading rose, Christ, the vine of the truth, fruitful and
unharvested in virginity, who was not dug, and bore clusters, who sprouted the
ripe cluster, Christ. How then did you sprout, persuade us, all-holy Virgin;
how were you shown mother of God on earth, having received the pre-eternal
Word. I the Virgin who bore in the womb have become incorrupt, an undefiled
temple of the Word of God who dwelt in me, unwedded.

=43.497= Bearing Emmanuel in an incorrupt womb, into an undefiled belly, being
likened to a cherubic throne; into whom the Word of the Father became flesh,
the beginningless and invisible; visible economically, the God who became man;
having known no experience of a man, I bore the pre-eternal God Jesus Christ.
For I am a virgin even now after the childbirth, purer than before. I bore
without pain, not as every woman; for the things between me and them are
unlike. The nature of men did not know my childbirth, except only the God who
dwelt in me. O Virgin, fearful treasure of the Church, the great mystery that
has come to pass, calls the virgin priest and altar together; who, bearing as
a table, gave us the heavenly bread, Christ, for the forgiveness of sins. O
undefiled belly having the circle of heaven, and God uncontained, but in you
contained, having borne him. O belly wider than heaven, who did not straiten
God who is in you. O belly a seven-circled heaven, and greater than them. O
belly higher and wider than seven heavens. O belly the eighth heaven, above
the firmaments. O belly having the unquenchable light of seven-lighted grace.
What shall I say, or what shall I speak concerning the august and holy Virgin?
Desire draws me to speak concerning the Theotokos, and fear holds me to be
silent, not having worthiness


to speak. The mind summons, and the fear thrusts away; the one draws me, and
the other draws me back. Since therefore I am held by both, it is expedient
for me to speak concerning the greatly-named and holy Virgin. For I call her
heaven and throne together and a cross; for having stretched out her holy
arms, the cherubic throne, cross-formed, heavenly, bore the Master, concerning
whom I stoop through the Scriptures in the heavens, and I see her worshipped
by angels. Whence first Gabriel greets the Virgin: Hail, full of grace, the
Lord is with you; hail, full of grace, the bright heaven; hail, full of grace,
who have the sun flashing from heaven with bright lamps, a ray, Christ.

The Virgin has become a seraph, the many-named, and many-eyed, of the
incomprehensible vision; a certain intellectual covering, found through a body
of the inhabited world, surpassing the cherubim. For those indeed turn away,
not being able to gaze toward the noetic fire of the divinity. But she, gazing
with clear eyes upon the incomprehensible and unsleeping eye of Christ,
longing and loving, greeted him. Armies of angels, falling as a footstool of
the feet of the Savior, are able neither to see nor to touch; but she, joining
lips to lips, greeted the incomprehensible one. O Virgin, bearing the wonder
of an incomprehensible mystery, having proclaimed a thrice-desired faith to
the inhabited world. The Virgin has become higher than angels, greater than
the cherubim and the seraphim, pleasing to the king Christ, honored by God as
a worthy handmaid and mother. Holy undefiled mother, who gave birth to the one
before you =43.500= Christ who said: Before Abraham was, I am; who glorified
the cave, and magnified the manger. For virgin and cave and manger, having the
circle of heaven, bore God uncontained, the three clearly ministering to the
Trinity, when the Virgin without pain, having conceived the Master of heaven
and earth in the cave, laid him in the manger, then also orders of angels
surrounded the Virgin crying and saying: Glory to God in the highest, and on
earth peace, goodwill among men. Whence also shepherds abiding in the field
heard such a doxology of the angels concerning the Savior, and having gone
with a run, they worshipped the light, having seen a strange wonder, the
handmaid of the Master and the earthly virgin, a heavenly bride. For she was
shown both bride, and bridal chamber, and mother of God in the cave, when she
bore the infant Christ, according to the prophetic voice of Isaiah: that a
Child has been born to us; a son also has been given to us, whose government
is upon his shoulder, and his name is called Angel of great counsel, mighty
God, one having authority, Prince of peace, Father of the age to come. Then he
says after these things: Magi from the east, shone upon and guided by a
luminous star, having walked the length of the road from the east, have come
to the worship of the one born in Bethlehem. Whence also having reached the
city Jerusalem, the star is hidden from them; and they, troubled, having lost
the guide, asking those in the city by force of necessity, inquired of them:
Where is the Christ born? And it was thought a strange wonder to all.
Concerning which Herod having heard, was greatly troubled, and having called
the priests, inquired of them, where the Christ is born. And they said and did
not deny; they confessed and did not lie. They were not able to hide the rays
of the sun; they testified the voice concerning the Savior made clear through
the prophet, saying: In Bethlehem the Christ is born. For thus he cries
through the prophet: And you, Bethlehem, are by no means least among the
rulers of Judah; for from you shall come forth one to be for a prince in
Israel. O the disobedient Israel! He confessed the matter of the law, and
denied the lawgiver; they themselves testified where the Christ is born, and
they themselves again denied the Christ who was born. What then of the magi?
whose own mind was altered, whose heart was greatly troubled, and was quenched
in grief, whose groanings as


with the force of waves were sinking the ship, whose eyes were sprinkling the
earth with pearl-like tears, whose affliction was unbearable, seeking the
guide of the east, whom they did not have, having him they were seeking: Where
is the star, the guide of the magi?

And in what manner the star is hidden from them, consider the truth, and see
the wonder, and the announcement. For this reason the star is hidden from
them, that when these ask of the unbelieving priests, the mystery of heaven
and earth may become manifest to all. Whence after =43.501= all have come to
know, the star is again made manifest to the magi, until coming it stood above
the infant in the cave, or rather in heaven. For where Christ is, there is
heaven; for the cave was shown a heaven upon earth. And they, having seen the
star, or rather the Savior, rejoiced with great joy, who also having bowed
worshipped, and having brought out the gifts, gold and frankincense and myrrh.
Gold indeed as to a king; frankincense as to God; and myrrh as to a mortal; or
rather that they might also announce the burial of Christ the creator and
fashioner of all, whom the heavenly bride Mary bore, the heaven and temple and
throne of the divinity, the unspeakable treasure of paradise. Angels were
blaming Eve, but now they glorify Mary; she who has properly glorified the
weakness of women, who raised up the fallen Eve, and sent Adam who had been
cast out of paradise into the heavens, who opened the closed paradise, and
through a robber planted Adam again.

For through you he destroyed the middle wall of the fence, the enmity, O holy
Virgin; through you the heavenly peace was granted to the world, through you
the ends of the inhabited world were enlightened, through you men have become
angels, through you men were called friends, and servants, and children of
God, and through you men were deemed worthy to become fellow-servants of
angels and companions; through you heavenly knowledge, and doxology, is sent
up from earth to the heavens; through you men have boldness in heaven toward
the Most High; through you a cross has shone throughout all the inhabited
world, on which your infant was hung, Christ our God; through you death is
trampled, and Hades is spoiled; through you the idols have fallen, and
heavenly knowledge has been raised; through you we have known the
only-begotten Son of God, whom you bore, all-holy Virgin, our Lord Jesus
Christ, to whom all angels and men, worshipping, say, beginningless the
Father, beginningless the Son, beginningless the Holy Spirit, glorifying an
inseparable and consubstantial Trinity unto the ages of ages. Amen.
