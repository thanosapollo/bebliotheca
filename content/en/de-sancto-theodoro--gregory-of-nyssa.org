OF THE SAME, AN ENCOMIUM ON THE GREAT MARTYR THEODORE.
You, the people of Christ, the holy flock, the royal priesthood, the crowds of
townsfolk and country folk who have streamed together from every side, from
where did you take the signal of the road, that you have been led as a people
to this sacred place? Who laid upon you so earnest and timely a necessity of
being here? And this in the season of winter, when even war is at rest, and
the soldier lays aside his panoply, and the sailor sets his rudder above the
smoke, and the farmer is still, tending the ploughing oxen at the manger? Or
is it not plain that the holy martyr sounded the trumpet from the military
rolls, and, having stirred many from various homelands, called them to his own
rest and =46.737= hearth, not making them ready for a warlike preparation, but
gathering them toward the sweet peace that is especially fitting for
Christians?
For this man, as we believe, both laid to rest the barbarian storm of the past
year, and stopped the dreadful war of the wild Scythians, having already
shaken over them, when they were in sight and drawing near, something terrible
and fearful, not a triple-crested helmet, nor a well-sharpened sword flashing
toward the sun, but the evil-averting and all-powerful cross of Christ, for
which he himself also suffered and acquired this glory. And now, having set
your mind upon it, consider with me, you servants of this pure religion and
lovers of the martyrs, how great a thing a righteous man is, and of how many
rewards he is counted worthy (both of those in the world, I mean, and of those
among us; for of the invisible ones no one is able to reckon the
magnificence); and having distinguished the fruit of piety, emulate the mind
of those so preferred. And desire the prizes which Christ distributes
according to worth to the athletes. And for now, if it seems good, while the
enjoyment of the good things to come is still at leisure, which a good hope
stores up for the righteous, whenever the judge of our lives shall come, let
us look at the present condition of the saints, how it is most fair and
magnificent.
For the soul, having gone up, dwells about its own lot, and lives bodilessly
with its like; but the body, that august and unstained instrument of hers,
which nowhere by its own passions harmed the incorruption of her who dwelt in
it, having been wrapped with much honor and attendance, lies augustly in a
holy place, like some highly prized treasure kept for the time of
regeneration, having much that is incomparable toward the other bodies, which
were dissolved by a common and ordinary death, and this in the same matter of
nature. For the other remains are even abominable to most, and no one passes a
tomb with pleasure, or even, if he happens upon one opened contrary to
expectation, and having cast his eye upon the shapelessness of those laid in
it, filled with every disgust, and having groaned heavily for humanity, he
runs past. But having come into a place like this, where today our assembly
is, where there is a memorial of a righteous man and a holy relic: first he is
led in soul by the magnificence of what is seen, looking at a house as a
temple of God, brilliantly furnished by the size of the building and by the
beauty of the adornment, where both the carpenter has shaped the wood into the
likeness of living things, and the stone-cutter has polished the slabs to the
smoothness of silver. And the painter too has colored the flowers of his art,
having drawn in an image the exploits of the martyr, the resistances, the
pains, the beast-like forms of the tyrants, the insults, that flame-feeding
furnace, the most blessed perfection of the athlete, the impress of the human
form of Christ the president of the contest, having crafted all these for us
as in some speaking book through colors, he has clearly set forth the contests
of the martyr, and has made the shrine bright as a splendid meadow; for a
silent painting too knows how to speak on a wall, and to benefit the greatest
things; =46.740= and the composer of the tesserae has made the floor that is
trodden worthy of a history.
And having thus delighted his sight in the perceptible works of art, he then
desires to draw near to the coffin itself, believing the touching to be a
sanctification and a blessing. And if someone should also give him to carry
away the dust that lies upon the surface of the resting-place, the soil is
taken as a gift, and the earth is stored as a treasure. For how much desired
it is to touch the relic itself, if ever some such chance should grant the
power, and how it is a gift of the highest prayer, they know who have tried it
and have been filled with such desire. For those who look kiss it as a living
and blooming body, bringing it to the eyes, to the mouth, to the ears, to all
the senses, then pouring over it the tear of reverence and of affection, they
offer the supplication of intercession as to the martyr whole and appearing,
calling upon him as a bodyguard of God, invoking him as one who receives the
gifts whenever he wills.
From all these things, O pious people, learn that Precious in the sight of the
Lord is the death of his holy ones. For one and the same is the body of all
men, having its constitution from one lump; but that which has simply died is
thrown away as a chance thing; while that which has been graced by the passion
of martyrdom is so lovely and so contested, as the preceding word has taught.
Therefore let us believe the invisible things from the things that appear, the
promise of the things to come from the experience in the world. For many who
set the belly, and vainglory, and the refuse here above all things, count the
future as nothing; and they think that all things are brought to an end with
the end of life. But you who think thus, learn the great things from the
small, understand the archetypes from the shadows. Which of the emperors is
honored with such honor? Which of those who have appeared among men to excess
is glorified with such a memorial? Which of the generals who have taken walled
cities and enslaved countless nations is so celebrated, as this soldier, the
poor man, the recruit, whom Paul armed, whom angels anointed for the contest,
and whom, when he had conquered, Christ crowned?
But rather, since I have come near in the word to the contests of the martyr,
come, leaving the common things, let us make the special account of the saint;
for what is one's own is dear to each. The homeland, then, of the noble man is
the country toward the rising sun; for he too, like Job, is well-born of those
from the sun's risings, and sharing that man's homeland he was not left behind
in the imitation of his character. But now he is a martyr common to all the
inhabited world, a citizen of the world under the sun; and having been taken
from there into the hoplite =46.741= rolls, he thus crossed over with his own
company to our country, the winter rest for the soldiers having been ordered
here by those in power.
And when a war was suddenly set in motion, not from a barbarian assault, but
from a Satanic law and a God-fighting decree (for every Christian was being
driven by the impious writing, and was being led to death); then indeed, then
this thrice-blessed man, being known for piety, and carrying about everywhere
the faith toward Christ, all but having written the confession on his
forehead, was no longer a recruit in courage, nor inexperienced in war and
battle; but noble in soul, having made a strong stand toward the dangers, not
shrinking, not fearing, not putting forward a word without boldness of speech.
For when that wicked tribunal sat, the governor and the commander having come
together to the same place, as Herod once and Pilate, they set the servant of
the Crucified into a judgment like that of the Master. "And say," he says,
"whence does boldness and daring arise in you, that you insult the imperial
law, and do not submit trembling to the commands of the Master, nor worship
according to what seems good to those in power?" For those around Maximian
then held the empire.
Who, with a firm face and an unstricken mind, set a well-aimed answer upon
what was said: "Gods I do not know (for they are not, according to the truth);
but you are deceived, honoring deceiving demons with the appellation of God;
but for me God is Christ, the only-begotten Son of God. For piety, then, and
for the confession toward him, let the one who wounds cut, and let the one who
scourges flay, and let the one who burns bring on the flame, and let the one
who is vexed by these voices of mine take out the tongue; for according to
each member the body owes endurance to the Creator."
The tyrants were defeated by the words; and they did not bear the first
assault of the champion, seeing a young man swelling toward the passion, and
drawing death to himself as some sweet drink. And when those men held back a
little, at a loss and deliberating what must be done, one of those in office,
a soldier thinking himself to be clever, mocking at the same time the martyr's
answer, said, "Is there then, Theodore, a Son to your God? And does that one
beget, as a man, with passion?" "With passion my God did not beget," he said;
"but I both confess a Son, and I say the generation is God-befitting; you, O
childish in reasoning and wretched man, are you not ashamed, nor do you hide
yourself, both confessing a female goddess, and worshipping the same as a
mother of twelve children, some many-bearing demon, easily both conceiving and
giving birth after the manner of hares or sows?"
And when the saint had thus turned a double ridicule back upon the idolater,
the tyrants, having feigned a form of humanity, say, "Let a little time for
consideration be granted to the madman; perhaps, having given himself counsel
at =46.744= leisure, he may change toward the better." For the mindless called
prudence madness, and reverence ecstasy and derangement; just as when the
drunken reproach the sober with their own passion. But the pious man and
soldier of Christ used the leisure given him for a manly deed. What deed is
this? It is time for you to receive the story with gladness: There was a
temple to the fabled mother of the gods in the metropolis of Amaseia, which
those then wandering had built in vanity somewhere there about the banks of
the river. This the noble man, in the time of the given license, having
watched for a timely hour and a favoring breeze, set on fire and burned down,
giving in deed to the accursed the answer which they were wholly awaiting
after the consideration. And when the deed quickly became evident to all (for
the fire was kindled most visibly in the middle of the city), he did not hide
the undertaking, nor did he take care to escape notice; but he was
conspicuous, and greatly indeed exulting in the success, and overjoyed at the
disturbance of the godless, with which they were disturbed, grieving over the
temple and the image.
And indeed he was informed against to the rulers as having himself done the
burning; and again a tribunal more fearful than the former; as was indeed
likely, when so great a thing that provoked them had occurred. And they went
up upon the judgment seat. But Theodore, free of speech in the midst, having a
ruler's courage in the stance of the one being judged, being questioned, and
cutting off the question by the speed of the confession. And since he was
unstricken, and spared none of the terrible things threatened, they changed to
the opposite manner; and speaking humanely, they tried to draw the righteous
man aside by promises. "And know," they say, "that if you should be willing to
receive our counsel obediently, we will at once make you known from the
unknown, honored from the inglorious; and we promise you the dignity of the
high-priesthood."
And when he heard of the dignity of the high-priesthood, the thrice-blessed,
laughing long, said: "I, judging even the priests of the idols wretched, and
as servants of a vain practice, pity them; but the high-priests I pity and
abhor still more; for among the worse, the greater and first-ranked is more
wretched; as among the unjust, the more unjust; as among murderers, the more
savage; as among the unchaste, the more wanton; and for this reason already
from here bring me down by the destructive promises; for you have escaped my
notice, O you men, having promised the summit of evils; but for the one who
chooses to live piously and rightly, it is good to be cast aside in the house
of God rather than to dwell in the tents of sinners. I pity even these
emperors, whose lawless law you continually read out, because, having among
men a sufficient dignity, the power of empire, they have assigned to
themselves the appellation of high-priest, and from there they put on the
mourning and dark purple, in imitation of the ill-starred high-priests,
wearing a gloomy garment with a bright dignity; and there are times when,
drawing near even to a polluted altar, instead of emperors they become
=46.745= cooks, sacrificing birds, and searching through the entrails of
wretched beasts, and staining the garment with the gore of blood, like some
meat-sellers."
After these voices of the righteous man, the rulers no longer displayed even
the feigned and figured humanity; but calling him also most impious toward the
gods, and further an insulter and blasphemer of the emperors, first having
hung him on the wood of the torture, they flayed the body; but he, while the
executioners were working fiercely, was stout and unyielding, and sang a verse
from the Psalter upon the tortures: I will bless the Lord at every time, his
praise shall be continually in my mouth. And they were scraping off the flesh;
but he was singing, as if someone else were undergoing the punishment. The
prison succeeded this torment, and again there a wonder was accomplished about
the saint, and by night a voice of a multitude singing was heard, and of lamps
appearing, so that a gleam as in a vigil was seen by those outside, so that
even the jailer, disturbed at the unexpected sight and hearing, leapt into the
cell, and found no one except the martyr at rest, and the other prisoners
sleeping.
And since, while many things were happening, he was at his height toward the
confession and the piety, the condemning vote came upon him, and having been
commanded to be perfected by fire, he himself went away on the fair and
blessed journey toward God; but to us he left the memorial of the contest as a
school, gathering peoples, teaching the church, driving out demons, bringing
down peaceable angels, seeking from God on our behalf the things that are
profitable, having made this place a surgery of various diseases, a harbor of
those storm-tossed by afflictions, a storehouse of the poor being made to
prosper, a bearable lodging of travelers, an unceasing festival of those
keeping feast. For even if we keep this day with yearly feasts, still the
multitude of those arriving in earnest never ceases, and the highway that
leads here keeps the likeness of ants, some going up, and others giving way to
those coming.
We then, O blessed one, having reached the circle of the year by the
loving-kindness of the Creator, have gathered for you the festival, the sacred
assembly of the lovers of the martyrs, both worshipping the common Master, and
having fulfilled the victory-memorial of your contests; but you, come now to
us, wherever you may be, as overseer of the feast (for we call you who have
called us); and whether you dwell aetherially in the height, or whether you go
about some heavenly vault, or, ranked with choirs =46.748= of angels, you
stand by the Master, or with powers and authorities you worship as a faithful
servant; having excused yourself a little from the things there, come to those
who honor you as an unseen friend; survey the things being accomplished, that
you may double the thanksgiving toward God, who in return for one passion, and
one pious confession, has granted you so many rewards; and may you be
gladdened by the blood and by the pain of the fire. For as many peoples as you
then had as spectators of the punishment, so many you now have as servants of
the honor. We have need of many benefactions; intercede for the homeland
toward the common king; for a martyr's homeland is the country of the passion;
and citizens and kinsmen are those who wrapped him and have him and honor him.
We suspect afflictions, we expect dangers; not far off are the accursed
Scythians, travailing with the war against us; as a soldier fight in our
defense; as a martyr use the boldness of speech on behalf of your
fellow-servants. Even if you have passed beyond life, still you know the
passions and the needs of humanity; ask for peace, that these festivals may
not cease, that a raging and lawless barbarian may not revel against temples
or altars, that a profane man may not trample the holy things.
For we also reckon to you the benefaction for those things in which we were
kept without suffering; and we ask also the security of the future. If there
should be need even of more entreaty, gather the choir of your brothers the
martyrs, and with all make petition; let the prayers of many righteous loose
the sins of peoples and crowds; remind Peter, raise up Paul, likewise John the
theologian and beloved disciple; that they may be anxious for the Churches
which they established, for which they wore the chains, for which they bore
the dangers and the deaths; let not idolatry lift a head against us, let not
heresies sprout up as thorns in the vineyard, let not tares having risen choke
the wheat; let not some rock come to be against us, lacking the richness of
the true dew, and show the power of the fruitfulness of the word to be without
root; but by the power of your intercession and of those with you, O marvelous
one, and shining above among the martyrs, may the polity of the Christians be
shown a crop remaining until the end, in the rich and fruitful field of the
faith in Christ always bearing fruit, the eternal life, that in Christ Jesus
our Lord. With whom to the Father together with the Holy Spirit, glory, power,
honor, now and always and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
