Cynthia was the first to take wretched me with her eyes, untouched before by
any desires. Then Love cast down my look of stubborn pride and pressed my head
with his feet set upon it, until the shameless one taught me to hate chaste
girls and to live with no counsel. Woe is me, already this madness has not
failed for a whole year, and yet I am forced to have the gods against me.
Milanion, Tullus, by fleeing no labors crushed the cruelty of hard Iasis's
daughter. For now he wandered mad in Parthenian caves, and again he went even
against shaggy wild beasts; he too, struck by the wound of Hylaeus's club,
groaned wounded on the Arcadian rocks. Therefore he was able to tame the swift
girl: so much in love do faith and good deeds avail. In me slow Love thinks of
no arts, nor remembers to go, as before, the known ways. But you, whose work
is the lure of the drawn-down moon and the labor of atoning rites on magic
hearths, come now, turn our mistress's mind, and make her grow paler than my
face! Then I would believe that you can lead the Shades and the stars with
Cytinaean songs. Or you, friends, who too late call back one who has fallen,
seek aids for a mind not sound. Bravely I will suffer both iron and savage
fires, if only there be freedom for anger to say what it will. Carry me
through the farthest nations and carry me through the waves, where no woman
may know my path. You remain, to whom the god has nodded with an easy ear, and
be always matched in a safe love. For our Venus works me with bitter nights,
and Love is never wanting, empty at no time. This evil, I warn you, avoid: let
each man's own care detain him, and let him not change his bed from accustomed
love. But if anyone turns slow ears to the warnings, alas, with how much pain
he will recall my words!

What helps it, my life, to go forth with dressed hair and to move the thin
folds of a Coan gown, or what to soak your locks with Orontean myrrh, and to
sell yourself with foreign gifts, and to lose nature's grace by bought
adornment, and not let your limbs shine in their own goods? Believe me, there
is no medicine for your figure: naked Love does not love an artificial beauty.
See what colors the untrenched earth puts forth, how ivy comes better of its
own accord, and the arbutus rises fairer in lonely caves, and untaught water
knows how to run its ways. Shores gleam painted with native pebbles, and birds
sing more sweetly with no art. Not so did Phoebe, daughter of Leucippus,
inflame Castor, nor her sister Hilaira Pollux by adornment; not so, once a
strife to Ida and eager Phoebus, the daughter of Evenus on her father's
shores; nor did Hippodamia, carried off on foreign wheels, draw her Phrygian
husband by a false brightness: but a face was there, bound to no gems, such as
the color is in Apelles's pictures. They had no zeal to win lovers by dye: for
them chastity was beauty ample enough. I do not now fear that you may be
cheaper to yourself than those: if a girl pleases one man, she is adorned
enough; especially when Phoebus gives you his songs and Calliope gladly the
Aonian lyre, and unique grace is not wanting to pleasant words, all that Venus
approves, and all that Minerva approves. By these you will always be most dear
to our life,

As the Cnossian girl lay faint on deserted shores when Theseus's keel was
departing; and as Cepheus's daughter Andromeda lay in her first sleep, already
free from the hard rocks; and no less as a Thracian woman, weary with
unceasing dances, fell by grassy Apidanus: so Cynthia seemed to me to breathe
a soft rest, her head propped on joined hands, when I was dragging steps drunk
with much Bacchus, and the boys were shaking the torch late at night. Her, not
yet even with all my senses lost, I try to approach, the couch gently pressed;
and though a double heat seized me and Love on this side and Liber on that,
each a hard god, ordered me to try her as she lay, my arm slipped lightly
under, and to take slow kisses with a hand brought near, yet I had not dared
to disturb my mistress's rest, fearing the abuse of a cruelty I had known; but
so I clung, fixed with intent eyes, as Argus on the unknown horns of Inachus's
daughter. And now I was loosing the garlands from our brow and setting them on
your temples, Cynthia; and now I was glad to shape the fallen hair; now I was
giving stolen apples with hollowed hands: all which gifts I was lavishing on
ungrateful sleep, gifts often rolled from a sloping bosom; and as often as you
drew sighs with a rare motion, I was stunned, credulous of a vain omen, lest
some vision should bring you unaccustomed fears, or lest someone should force
you, unwilling, to be his: until the moon, running before the opposite
windows, the moon busy with lights that would linger, opened the composed eyes
with light rays. So she speaks, her elbow fixed on the soft couch: 'Has injury
at last brought you back to our bed, driven from another's closed doors? For
where have you spent the long hours of my night, faint, ah me, when the stars
were spent? O that you may pass, shameless one, such nights as you always bid
wretched me have! For now I was cheating sleep with purple thread, and again,
weary, with the song of an Orphean lyre; sometimes, deserted, I was lightly
complaining to myself of the long delays often in a stranger's love: until
Sleep struck me, tired, with pleasant wings. That was the last care for my
tears.'

Why, Bassus, by praising so many girls to me do you force me, changed, to
leave my mistress? Why do you not let me lead whatever of life shall follow in
this more accustomed slavery? You may recall with praises the beauty of
Antiope, Nycteus's daughter, and of Spartan Hermione, and whatever girls the
age of a beautiful time bore; Cynthia would not let them have a name: still
less, if she were compared with slight figures, would she go off ugly even by
a hard judge. But this beauty is the last part of my madness; there are
greater things, Bassus, for which it is a joy to perish: a free-born color and
grace in the moving of her limbs and the joys it is a pleasure to learn under
a silent dress. The more you strive to unbind our loves, the more each of us
deceives with accepted faith. You will not go unpunished: this mad girl will
know it and will be your enemy with voices not silent; nor will Cynthia after
this trust me to you nor seek you; she will be mindful of so great a crime,
and angry she will spread you among all other girls: alas, you will be dear at
no threshold. She will scorn no altars with her weepings, and whatever stone
is sacred, of whatever kind, wherever. Cynthia is tried by no loss more
heavily than when, love snatched away, her glory fails her, especially ours.
May she so remain always, I pray, and may I find nothing in her to complain
of!

Envious one, at last check your troublesome voices and let us go matched in
the course where we are! What do you want, madman? To feel my furies? Unhappy
one, you hurry to know the last evils, and, wretched, to carry your steps
through unknown fires, and to drink poisons from all Thessaly. She is not like
wandering girls when compared: she will not know how to be angry at you
softly. But if by chance she is not contrary to your prayers, yet what
thousands of cares she will give you! She will leave you now neither sleeps
nor eyes: she, fierce in spirit, alone binds men. Ah, how often, despised, you
will run to my threshold, when strong words will fall from you with a sob, and
a trembling horror will rise with sad weepings, and fear will draw an ugly
mark on your face, and whatever words you wish will flee you as you complain,
nor will you be able, wretched, to know who you are or where! Then you will be
forced to learn the heavy slavery of our girl and what it is to go home shut
out; nor will you any longer wonder so often at our pallor, or why I am
nothing in my whole body. Nor will nobility be able to help you as a lover:
Love does not know how to yield to ancient images. But if you give small
traces of your fault, how quickly you will be a rumor from so great a name! I
will not then be able to bring comforts to you asking, when I have no medicine
for my own evil; but equally wretched we will be forced in a shared love to
weep mutual tears in one another's bosom. Therefore, Gallus, cease to ask what
my Cynthia can do: she does not come asked without punishment.

I do not now fear to know the Adriatic sea with you, Tullus, nor to draw sails
on the Aegean brine, with whom I could climb the Rhipaean mountains and go
beyond Memnon's homes; but the words of a girl who has embraced me hold me
back, and often heavy prayers with changed color. She argues fires at me
through whole nights, and complains that, left behind, there are no gods; she
already denies herself to me, she threatens the things a sad mistress is used
to say to an ungrateful man. I cannot last an hour under these complaints: ah,
let him perish, if anyone can love slowly! Would it be worth so much to me to
know learned Athens and to see the ancient riches of Asia, that Cynthia should
make abuse when the ship is launched and mark my face with mad hands, and say
that the kisses owed her have been given to the opposing wind, and that
nothing is harder than an unfaithful man? You try to outstrip your uncle's
deserved axes, and bring back old laws to forgetful allies. For your age has
never paused for love, but the care of an armed fatherland has always been
yours; and may that boy never bring our labors upon you and all things known
to my tears! Let me, whom fortune has always wished to lie low, give my last
breath to this worthlessness. Many have perished gladly in a long love, in
whose number let the earth cover me too. I was not born fit for praise, not
fit for arms: the fates wish me to undergo this warfare. But you, whether
where soft Ionia stretches, or where the water of Pactolus dyes Lydian
plowlands, whether you will seize lands with feet or the sea with oar, you
will go and be part of a received empire: then if any hour comes not forgetful
of me, you will be sure that I live under a hard star.

While Cadmean Thebes is told by you, Ponticus, and the sad arms of a brother's
warfare, and, so may I be happy, you strive with Homer first (if only the
fates are soft to your songs), we, as we are accustomed, drive our loves, and
seek something against a hard mistress; nor am I forced to serve talent so
much as pain, and to complain of the hard times of my age. Here the measure of
my life is worn away, this is my fame, from here I wish the name of my song to
go. Let them praise me, that I pleased a learned girl, Ponticus, and often
bore unjust threats; let a neglected lover read me constantly after this, and
let our known evils profit him. You too, if this boy has shaken you with a
sure bow (I would not have wished our gods to have violated me!), far off,
wretched, you will weep that the camp, far off that the seven armies, lie in
eternal deaf decay; and in vain you will wish to compose a soft verse, nor
will late Love supply you with songs. Then you will often wonder at me as no
humble poet, then I will be preferred to Roman talents. Nor will young men be
able to be silent at our tomb: 'You lie, great poet of our passion.' Beware of
scorning our songs with your pride: often late Love comes with great interest.

Are you then mad, and does my care not delay you? Or am I cheaper to you than
cold Illyria? And does that man, whoever he is, already seem worth so much to
you that you wish to go without me on any wind? Can you hear the murmurs of
the raging sea, brave, and can you lie in a hard ship? Can you support the set
frosts with tender feet, can you, Cynthia, bear unaccustomed snows? O that the
times of winter's solstice may be doubled, and the sailor idle with the slow
Pleiades, and the rope not loosed for you from Tyrrhenian sand, and no hostile
breeze lift my prayers and suffer me, fixed on an empty shore, often to call
you cruel with a hostile hand! But in whatever way you deserve of me, false
one, let Galatea not be a stranger to your voyage; and may I not see such
winds subside when the wave carries your advanced ships away, so that, the
Ceraunia conquered by a lucky oar, Oricos may receive you on calm waters. For
no things will be able to corrupt me from complaining of you, my life, at your
threshold; nor will I fail to ask hurried sailors 'Tell me, in what port is my
girl shut?', and I will say 'Though she settle on Artacian shores, and though
on Hylaean, she will be mine.'

She will be here! Here she remains, having sworn! Let the envious burst! We
have won: she has not borne constant prayers. Let greedy envy lay down false
joys: our Cynthia has ceased to go new ways. I am dear to her and through me
Rome is said to be most dear, and she denies sweet kingdoms without me. She
has preferred to rest with me even on a narrow bed and to be mine in whatever
way, rather than the ancient kingdom of dowered Hippodamia for herself, and
the wealth Elis had prepared, fit for horses. Though he gave much, though he
was going to give more, yet she, not greedy, has not fled my bosom. I was able
to bend her not with gold, not with Indian shells, but with the obedience of a
coaxing song. Therefore there are Muses, and Apollo is not slow for a lover,
on whom I, relying, love: rare Cynthia is mine! Now I may touch the highest
stars with my soles: whether day or night has come, she is mine! Nor will a
rival steal sure loves from me: that glory will know my white hair.

I used to tell you, mocker, that loves would come, and that free words would
not be yours forever: see, you lie and come a suppliant to a girl's laws, and
now any woman just bought commands you. Chaonian doves would not surpass me in
love in saying which young men each girl tames. Pain and tears have deservedly
made me skilled: and would that I may be called untrained, with love laid
aside! What good is it to you now, wretched, to speak a heavy song or to weep
the walls of Amphion's lyre? Mimnermus's verse avails more in love than Homer:
gentle Love seeks soft songs. Go, I ask, and put aside those sad little books,
and sing what any girl may wish to know! What if you had no easy plenty! Now
you, mad, seek water in mid-stream. Not even yet do you grow pale, nor are you
touched by a true fire: this is the first spark of the coming evil. Then you
will wish more to approach Armenian tigers and more to know the bonds of the
infernal wheel, than to feel the boy's bow so often in your marrow and to be
able to deny nothing to your angry girl. No Love has so easily offered wings
to anyone that he has not pressed him with the other hand. And let it not
deceive you that she is ready enough: she comes on more sharply, Ponticus, if
she is yours, inasmuch as it is not allowed to lead empty eyes aside, nor does
Love yield to keep watch at another threshold. He who is not open before,
until the hand has touched the bones: whoever you are, flee constant
blandishments! To them even flints and oaks could yield, still less could you,
that light breath. Therefore, if there is shame, confess the errors as soon as
possible: to say for what you perish often lightens in love.

O pleasant rest, when I had been present as a witness to first love, conscious
in your tears! O pleasant pleasure to me to remember the night, o how often
that night is to be called in my vows, when we saw you, Gallus, dying in a
girl's embrace and drawing words with a long delay! Though sleep was pressing
my slipping eyes and the Moon was reddening with her horses in mid-sky, yet I
could not withdraw from your play: so great was the heat in the alternating
voices. But since you have not feared to trust us, receive the gifts of a joy
committed: I have not learned to keep your griefs silent; there is something
in us greater, friend, than faith. I can join divided lovers again, and I can
open a mistress's slow doors; and I can heal another's recent cares, nor is
the medicine in my words light. Cynthia taught me what should always be sought
by each and what avoided: Love has not done nothing. Beware of wishing to
fight a sad girl, or of speaking proudly, or of being silent long; and if she
has asked anything, do not refuse with an ungrateful brow, and do not let kind
words fall for you as empty. She comes provoked when she is scorned, and,
wounded, she does not remember to lay down just threats: but the more humble
you are and subject to love, the more often you may enjoy a good result. He
will be able to remain happy with one girl who will never be free with an
empty breast.

Does any care steal upon you, Cynthia, lingering in the midst of Baiae, where
the path lies on Hercules's shores, and now wondering at the waters next to
noble Misenum, subjected to Thesprotus's kingdom, to bring on nights mindful
of us? Does any place remain in the last of love? Or has some unknown enemy
with feigned fires taken you from our songs, Cynthia, as a girl is used to
slip when the guardian is removed, faithless, and not remember the common
gods? And would that rather a little boat, trusting small oars, may delay you
on Lucrine water, or that the water easy to yield to alternate hands may hold
you shut in Teuthras's thin wave, than that you be free to hear another's
coaxing whispers, softly settled on a silent shore! Not because you are not
known to me by a fame seen through, but because in this quarter every love is
feared. You will therefore forgive if our little books have brought you
anything sad: the fault will be of fear. Ah, let me have no greater watch of a
dear mother, or any care of my life without you! You alone are my house, you,
Cynthia, alone my parents, you all the times of our joy. Whether I come sad or
on the contrary glad to friends, whatever I shall be, I will say 'Cynthia was
the cause.' Only leave corrupt Baiae as soon as possible: those shores will
give many a rupture, shores that were hostile to chaste girls: ah, let Baiae
perish, the waters, a crime of love!

Why do you not cease to invent a charge of idleness against me, that Cynthia
makes a delay for us, Rome? She has been divided from my bed by so many miles
as Hypanis is distant from Venetian Eridanus; nor does Cynthia nourish
accustomed loves for me with an embrace, nor does she sound sweet in our ear.
Once I was welcome: at no time has it befallen anyone that he could love with
like faith. We were a thing of envy: has a god overwhelmed me? or what herb
gathered on Promethean ridges divides? I am not who I had been: a long road
changes girls. How great a love has fled in a little time! Now first I am
forced to know long nights and myself to be heavy to my own ears. Happy he who
has been able to weep to a girl present (Love sprinkled with tears rejoices
not a little), or, if despised, has been able to change his heats (there are
joys also in transferred slavery). For me it is not right either to love
another or to leave off from this one: Cynthia was the first, Cynthia will be
the end.

You will rejoice, as you often are used, at our fall, Gallus, that I am empty,
love snatched away. But I myself will not imitate your words, faithless one:
may a girl never wish to deceive you, Gallus. While your fame is increased by
deceived girls, and sure you seek delay in no love, lost over a certain one
you begin to grow pale with slow cares, and, slipped, to leave the first step.
This will be the punishment of the pain of those who were scorned: one girl
will exact the wretched turns of many. She will check those common loves of
yours, nor will you always be a friend by seeking new ones. I have not been
taught this by an evil rumor, not by an augur; I have seen: I ask you, can you
deny me as a witness? I have seen you languish, bound by the whole neck, and
weep long, Gallus, with hands thrown on, and wish to lay down your life on
longed-for lips, and what then my shame, friend, conceals. I could not draw
your embraces apart: so great was the mad fury between you both. Not so did
the Taenarian god, mixed with Haemonia's Enipeus, press Salmoneus's daughter
with an easy love, nor so did Hercules's burning love for heavenly Hebe feel
the first joys from the Oetaean pyre. One day has been able to outrun all
lovers: for she has not put lukewarm torches under you, nor has she suffered
past pride to succeed, nor will she allow it: your heat will drive you bound.
Nor is it a wonder, since she is nearest to Leda worthy of Jove, and more
pleasing than Leda's offspring, one than the three; she would be more coaxing
even than Inachian heroines, she would force Jove to love by her words. But
you, since you are once going to perish by love, use it: you were worthy of no
other threshold. Let the error that has fallen on you, since it is new, be
lucky for you; and however many you wish, let that one be yours.

Though you, thrown softly by the Tiber's wave, drink Lesbian wines from
Mentor's work, and now wonder that boats run so swift and now that ships go so
slow by ropes; and though the whole grove stretch planted woods with its peak,
with as great trees as Caucasus is pressed; yet those things could not contend
with my love: Love does not know how to yield to great riches. For whether she
draws the longed-for rest with me, or leads the whole day in easy love, then
the waters of Pactolus come under my roof, and a gem is gathered under the Red
seas; then my joys pledge that kings will yield to me: may they remain, until
the fates wish me to perish! For who rejoices in riches when Love is adverse?
Let there be no prizes for me with a sad Venus! She can break the great
strengths of heroes, she can be a pain even to hard minds: she neither fears
to cross an Arabian threshold nor fears, Tullus, to go under a purple couch,
and to turn a wretched young man on the whole bed: what do silks with varied
weavings relieve? While she is present, appeased, to me, I will not fear to
despise any kingdoms or the gifts of Alcinous.

Often I feared many hard things of your lightness, Cynthia, yet this treachery
excepted. See with how great a danger fortune snatches me! Yet you come slow
in our fear; and you can arrange yesterday's hair with your hands and seek
your face in long idleness, and no less vary your breast with eastern pebbles,
as a beauty who prepares to go to a new man. But not so was Calypso moved at
the Ithacan's departure, who once had wept on deserted waters: many days she
had sat sad with uncombed hair, having spoken much to the unjust brine, and
though she would never see him after this, yet she was in pain, conscious of a
long joy. Nor so did Hypsipyle, anxious as the winds snatched the son of
Aeson, stand in an empty chamber: Hypsipyle felt no loves after those, once
she wasted with the Haemonian guest. Evadne, carried out through her husband's
wretched fires, fell, the fame of Argive chastity. Alphesiboea avenged her own
brothers for her husband, and love broke the bonds of dear blood. None of whom
has been able to turn your ways, so that you too might become a noble story.
Cease now to recall perjuries with your words, Cynthia, and spare to move
forgotten gods; ah, too bold, you will suffer at our danger, if anything
harder by chance befalls you! Sooner will deep rivers slide back from the sea,
and the year lead inverted turns before, than your care be changed under our
breast: be whatever you wish, yet not another's. Let those eyes not seem so
cheap to you, by which treachery has often been believed by me! These you used
to swear, if you had lied in anything, would fall out with hands put under
them: and can you raise these against the great Sun, and do you not tremble,
conscious of admitted worthlessness? Who was forcing you to grow pale with
many colors and to draw weeping from unwilling eyes? I now perish by these,
about to warn like lovers that it is safe to trust no blandishments.

I who had once been opened for great triumphs, a door vowed to Patrician
Chastity, whose gilded thresholds chariots celebrated, wet with the suppliant
tears of captives, now I, wounded by the nightly brawls of drinkers, often
complain, beaten by unworthy hands, and shameful garlands do not fail to hang
on me and torches, signs of one shut out, always to lie. Nor can I defend my
infamous mistress's voices, a noble door handed over to obscene songs; and yet
she is not called back to spare her own fame, and to live more shameful than
the luxury of the age. Among these complaints I am forced to weep more
heavily, sadder from a suppliant's long watches. He never suffers my posts to
rest, bringing back songs with clear blandishment: 'Door, even more cruel than
the mistress herself, why, shut, are you silent to me with so hard doors? Why,
never unbarred, do you not admit my loves, not knowing how, moved, to return
stolen prayers? Will no end be granted to our pain, and will there be shameful
sleep on a lukewarm threshold? Midnight grieves for me, the setting stars
grieve for me lying, and the cold breeze with eastern frost. O that my little
voice, thrown through a hollow crack, may turn into my mistress's struck
little ears! Though she be more enduring than Sicilian rock, though she be
harder than iron and steel, yet she will not be able to check her eyes, and a
breath will rise in unwilling tears. Now she lies leaning on another's lucky
arm, but my words fall to the night Zephyr. But you alone, you the greatest
cause of my pain, door never conquered by my gifts, you alone never having
pitied human pains, answer mutually with silent hinges. No petulance of my
tongue has hurt you; what is used to say so much in an angry joke, that you
suffer me, hoarse with so long a complaint, to keep watch through anxious
delays at the crossroads? Yet I have often drawn down songs for you in a new
verse, and leaning I have given pressed kisses to the steps. How often I have
turned myself before your posts, faithless one, and brought owed vows with
hidden hands!' These things he, and if any you wretched lovers have known, and
he makes a noise against the morning birds. So I am now pulled different ways
by the vices of a mistress and always by a lover's weepings, with alternating
envy.

And deservedly, since I was able to have fled a girl, now I address the
deserted kingfishers. Nor will Cassiope see my keel with me safe, and all vows
fall on an ungrateful shore. Nay even the winds profit you, Cynthia, in my
absence: see how savage the threats the breeze scolds. Will no fortune of an
appeased storm come? Will this small sand cover my funeral? Yet do you turn
savage complaints to the better: let night and the unjust shallows be enough
punishment for you. Or will you be able to demand my fate with dry eyes, and
hold none of our bones in your bosom? Ah, let him perish, whoever first
prepared ships and sails and made a way on an unwilling gulf! Was it not
lighter to overcome a mistress's ways (though hard, yet she was a rare girl),
than so to see shores surrounded by unknown woods and to seek the longed-for
Tyndarids? If any fates had buried my pain there, and a last stone stood with
love laid down, she would have given dear hair at my funeral, and would softly
set the bones on a tender rose; she would have called my name at the last
dust, that the earth might be of no weight for me. But you, sea-born daughters
of beautiful Doris, loose the white sails with a lucky chorus: if ever
slipping Love has touched your waves, spare a companion on gentle shores.

These are certainly deserted places and silent to one complaining, and the
breeze of Zephyr holds an empty grove. Here it is allowed to bring forth
hidden pains without punishment, if only lonely rocks can keep faith. From
where shall I first take up your pride, my Cynthia? What beginning of weeping
do you give me, Cynthia? I who just now was numbered among lucky lovers, now
in your love I am forced to have a mark. What have I deserved so much? What
charges change you toward me? Or is a new girl the cause of your sadness? So
may you bring yourself back to me, light one, as no other has brought
beautiful feet into our threshold. Though this pain of mine owes you many
harsh things, yet my anger will not have come so savage that I should always
be a fury to you deservedly, and your eyes be ugly with weeping, tears cast
down. Or because I give small signs with changed color, and no faith cries in
my face? You will be witnesses, if any tree has loves, the beech and the pine
dear to the Arcadian god. Ah how often my words resound under your shades, and
Cynthia is written in the tender barks! Ah how many cares your injury has
borne us, which were known at silent doors! I have been accustomed, fearful,
to bear all the commands of a proud girl and not to complain of deeds with a
clear pain. For which continuous mountains and a cold crag are given and a
hard rest on an untilled path; and whatever my complaints can tell, I am
forced to speak to shrill birds. But however you are, let the woods resound
'Cynthia' for me, and let not deserted rocks be empty of your name.

I do not now fear the sad Shades, my Cynthia, nor do I delay the fate owed to
the last pyre; but lest by chance my funeral lack your love, this fear is
harder than the funeral rites themselves. The boy has not clung so lightly to
our eyes that my dust should be empty with love forgotten. There the hero,
Phylacus's son, could not be forgetful of a pleasant wife in the blind places,
but, eager to touch joys with false palms, the Thessalian shade had come to
the ancient house. There, whatever I shall be, I will always be called your
image: a great love crosses even the shores of fate. There let a chorus of

A praetor has just come from Illyrian lands, Cynthia, a greatest spoil for
you, a greatest care for me. Could he not have laid down life on Ceraunian
rock? Ah, Neptune, what gifts I would give you! Now without me banquets are
made with a full table, now without me the door lies open the whole night.
Therefore, if you are wise, do not desert the harvests offered and pluck the
stupid flock with a full fleece; then, when he remains poor with the gift
consumed, say let him sail again to other Illyrias! Cynthia does not follow
fasces nor care for honors, she alone always weighs lovers' bosoms. But you
now, o Venus, succor our pain, that he may burst his limbs with constant
lusts! Therefore does anyone buy love with gifts? Jupiter, a girl perishes by
an unworthy merchandise. Always she sends me to Ocean to seek gems, and orders
me to take gifts from Tyre itself. And would that no one at Rome were rich,
and the leader himself could dwell in a straw hut! Never would girlfriends be
for sale at a gift, and a girl would grow white in one house; never would you
lie apart seven nights, white arms poured on so foul a man, not because I have
sinned (I call you to witness), but because commonly lightness has always been
a friend to beauties. A barbarian drives his steps with loins shut out, and

the Campus, nor does my table please. But let there certainly be shame, let
there be shame! unless perhaps, as they say, a shameful love is used to have

Caesar's virtue and this is Caesar's glory: with that hand by which he
conquered he stored the arms. But whatever clothes, whatever emeralds, or what

you. Not always does placid Jupiter laugh at perjured lovers and neglect
prayers with a deaf ear. You have seen sounds run through the whole sky, and

nothing; then he is used to punish perjured girls, since the god himself also
wept, deceived. Therefore let a Sidonian dress not be worth so much to you

To lie about a night, to lead a lover with promises, this will be to have
hands stained with blood! I am the seer of these things, as often as deserted
I have filled bitter nights, broken on each couch. Either you may be moved at
Tantalus's lot at the rivers, that liquor from a dry mouth cheats thirst; or
you may wonder at Sisyphus's labors, that he rolls a hard burden on the whole
mountain; nothing harder lives on earth than a lover, nor, if only you were
wise, what you would wish less to be. I whom just now they were calling lucky,

pleases, impious one, to throw the body from a hard rock, and to take worn
poisons into our hands; nor is it allowed to rest at the crossroads under a
dry moon, or to send words through cracked doors. Though it is so, I will
beware of changing a mistress: then she will weep, when she has felt that
there is faith in me.

Constant complaints have borne hatred to many: a woman is often broken in a
silent man. If you have seen anything, always deny that you have seen it! Or
if anything has by chance hurt, deny that it hurts!

Now even, mad, do you imitate the stained Britons, and play with your head
dyed with a foreign gleam? As nature has given, so every figure is right: a
Belgic color is shameful on a Roman face. Let many evils be made under the

you will certainly be able to seem beautiful to me; you are beautiful enough
for me, if only you come often. Or if some woman has dyed her temples with
dark-blue dye, is a dark-blue beauty therefore good? Since you have neither a

your own bed always be a watch for you, and do not wish to sit with a forehead

Though you leave Rome against my will, Cynthia, I am glad that you will till
out-of-the-way fields without me. There will be no young corrupter in the
chaste fields, who may not allow you by blandishments to be honest; nor will
any brawl rise before your windows, nor will sleep be bitter for you from
shouting. You will be alone and you will look at lonely mountains, Cynthia,
and a flock and the bounds of a poor farmer. There no games will be able to
corrupt you, and shrines a most cause of your sins. There you will constantly
look at bulls plowing, and the vine putting hair with a skilled sickle; and
there you will bring rare frankincense at an untilled shrine, where a kid will
fall before rustic hearths; and at once you will imitate dances with a naked
calf; let all things only be safe from a stranger man. I myself will hunt:
already now it pleases me to take up Diana's rites and to lay down Venus's
vows. I will begin to catch wild beasts and to give horns back to the pine and
myself to warn bold dogs; yet not so that I would dare to try huge lions or go
swift in close combat against rustic swine. Let this softness therefore be my
boldness, to take hares and to pierce a bird with a set reed, where beautiful
Clitumnus covers his streams with his grove, and the wave washes snowy cattle.

Morning Stars. Here neither lonely woods will be able to turn me, nor
wandering streams poured from mossy ridges, that I should not change your
names on a constant tongue: no one would not wish to have harmed one absent.

Why do you weep more heavily than Briseis snatched away? Why do you weep more

troubled Sipylus. Though bronze knots bind my arms, or your limbs be stored in

neither leave off quickly nor begin rashly.

Ah how much Panthus's page has invented about me to you, so much may Venus not

Dodona. That handsome lover of yours has a wife! So many nights have perished:

sick equally or well we are.

You know yesterday many girls equally pleased me; you know, Demophoon, many
evils come to me. No crossroads are traversed in vain by my soles; o theaters
born too much for my destruction, whether someone draws white arms with a soft
gesture, or sings various measures with the mouth! Meanwhile our eyes seek a
wound for themselves, if any sits white with breast not covered, or if
wandering hair strays on pure brows, which an Indian gem holds at mid-peak. If

from the whole brow. You ask, Demophoon, why I am so soft toward all? What you

created: fortune has given me always to love something. Though the fates of
the singer Thamyras may follow me, never, envious one, will I be blind toward

tried that my duty avails the whole night. Jupiter had put the twin Bears to

fierce Hector. See how now the sun now the moon serves in the sky: so even for
us one girl is too little. Let another hold me and cherish me with greedy

has been made angry at my service, that she may know there is another who
wishes to be mine! For two cables defend a ship better, and an anxious mother
more safely nourishes twins.

Or if you are hard, deny: but if you are not hard, come! What helps it to set
these words in no place? This one pain out of all is sharp for a lover, if any
who is hoped for suddenly denies to come. How great sighs turn him on the
whole bed, when she herself forbids to be received, who has not come? And
again by seeking he wearies the boy with things heard, whom she orders to seek
fates which she fears to know.

She for whom the path of the untaught crowd was to be fled, now water sought
from a lake is sweet to me. Does any free-born man give gifts to another's
slave, that he may bear promised words to his mistress? And he seeks so often
'What portico now covers her?' and 'On the Campus when does she move her
feet?' Then, when you have borne through the labors which fame calls
Hercules's, that she may write 'Have you anything of a gift?' that you may be
able to see the face of a bitter guardian, and often captured hide in a filthy
hut? How dearly once in a whole year a night is turned! Ah let them perish, if
a closed door pleases any! On the contrary, does she who goes free with wrap
thrown back, fenced by no fear of guardians, please? For whom the Sacred Way
is often worn with a filthy slipper, and she does not allow there to be delay,
if anyone wishes to approach; she will never put off, nor will she, talkative,
demand what a tight father often weeps to have given, nor will she say 'I am
afraid, hurry now to rise, I ask: unhappy one, today my husband comes from the
country.' And those whom Euphrates and those whom Orontes has sent me, may
they please me: I would not wish chaste thefts of a bed; since liberty now
remains for no lover: no one will be free, if anyone wishes to love.

'Do you speak, when you are already a story by a known book and your Cynthia

these words? Either free-born shame, or a love to be kept silent. But if
Cynthia breathed so easy for us, I would not be called the head of
worthlessness, nor so would I be led infamous through the whole city, and
though I were burning, I would give words with a name. Therefore let it not be
a wonder to you that I seek cheap women: they defame more sparingly: is the
cause light for you? [omitted] And now fans of a proud peacock's tail and to
have cold with a hard ball in the hands, and she wishes me angry to demand
ivory dice, and cheap gifts that shine on the Sacred Way. Ah may I perish if
those expenses move me, but I am already ashamed to be a joke to a deceitful
mistress!

Was this among the first things that you were ordering me to rejoice in? Does
it not shame you, so beautiful, to be light? One or another night has not yet
been finished in love, and already I am said to be heavy to your bed. Just now
you were praising me and reading our songs: has that love of yours turned
feathers so quickly? Let him contend with me in talent, let him contend also
in art, let him learn in first to love in one house: if it will be a pleasure
to you, let him fight at Lerna's hydras and bring you apples from the western
dragon, let him gladly drink foul poisons and as a shipwrecked man the waves,
and never refuse to be wretched for you: (which labors would that you, my
life, may try in us!) Already that bold one will be from the fearful for you,

for you. But the whole age of the Sibyl will not change me, not Alcides's
labor, not that black day. You will compose me and will say 'Bones,
Propertius, are these yours? Alas you were sure for me, you were sure alas,
though not noble by ancestral blood and though you were not so rich.' I will
suffer nothing not, injury never changes me: I think it no burden to bear a
beautiful woman. I believe not a few have perished by that figure, but I
believe many have not had faith. Theseus loved Minos's daughter for a small
space, Demophoon Phyllis, each a bad guest. Already Medea is known to you by
Jason's keel and just now left alone with the man saved. She is hard who
feigns a pretended love for many, and if any can prepare herself for more than
one. Do not compare me with nobles, do not with the blessed: scarcely he comes
who may gather bones on the last day. We will be these for you: but I pray
rather that you beat naked breasts for me with hair let down.

Most beautiful care born unique for my pain, since my lot shuts out 'come
often,' that beauty will become most known by my little books, Calvus, by your
leave, Catullus, by your peace. A soldier full of years lies apart with arms
laid down, and aged oxen refuse to draw plows, and a rotting ship rests on
empty sand, and an old war-shield is vacant in a temple: but no old age will
lead me from your love, whether I will be Tithonus or I will be Nestor. Was it
not enough to serve a hard tyrant and to groan in your bull, savage Perillus?
And it was enough to grow hard at the Gorgon's face, even if we suffered
Caucasian birds. But yet I will resist. An iron point is worn by rust and
flint often by a small liquor: but love is worn under no charge of a mistress,
which remains and sustains threats with an undeserving ear. Unasked the
scorned one asks, and wounded confesses to have sinned, and himself returns
with unwilling feet. You too, who take on pride in a full love, credulous one,
no woman has weight long. Or does anyone pay vows in the midst of storms, when
often a broken keel swims in port? Or does he demand prizes before with the
course unfinished, before the seventh wheel has worn the turning-post with
art? Lying following blasts play in love: if any comes late, a great ruin
comes. Yet you meanwhile, though she loves you, keep joys shut in a silent
bosom. For somehow in his own love always his own greatest words are used to
harm each one. Though she call you very often, remember to go once: what has
envy is not used to be long. But if ages pleasing to ancient girls existed, I
would be what now you are: I am conquered by time. Yet those ages will not
change my ways: let each one know to go his own way. But you who call back
duties into many loves, how great a pain so tortures your eyes! You have seen
a tender girl with full brightness, you have seen a dark one, each color
leads; you have seen a certain one come forth with an Argive figure, you have
seen our women, each form snatches; and let her be in a plebeian wrap or of
scarlet: this and that is one way of an evil wound. When one is enough to
bring sleeplessness to your eyes, one woman is enough many evils for anyone.

I saw you in sleep, my life, with a broken keel drawing tired hands in Ionian
dew, and confessing whatever you had lied against me, and no longer able to
lift hair heavy with wet, such as Helle driven by purple waves, whom a golden
sheep carried on a soft back. How I feared that by chance the sea might have
your name, and a sailor slipping might weep at your water! What then I
undertook to Neptune, what then with Castor the brother, and what to you,
already a goddess, Leucothoe! But you, scarcely lifting first palms from the
gulf, often already about to perish call my name. But if by chance Glaucus had
seen your eyes, you would have been made a girl of the Ionian sea, and the
Nereids would scold you for envy, white Nesaee, dark-blue Cymothoe. But I saw
a dolphin run to your aid, who, I think, had carried Arion's lyre before. And
already I was trying to send myself from the highest rock, when fear shook
such visions from me.

Now let them wonder that so beautiful a girl is a slave to me and that I am

my things, she says she hates the blessed: no girl cultivates songs so
sacredly. Much in love does faith profit, much constancy: he who can give many
things can also love many. [omitted] Or if my girl should think to go through
a long sea, I will follow her and one breeze will drive two faithful. There
will be one shore for the sleeping and one tree for a roof, and we will often
drink from one water; and one plank will be able to compose two lovers,

The rhombs twisted under a magic song fail, and a laurel lies burned at an

Pegasean back, nor if Perseus's wing has moved your feet; or if cut breezes

sweet thefts of ancient Jove, how he was burned by Semele, how he was lost by
Io, finally how as a bird he flew to Troy's roofs. But if no one exists who
has conquered the Winged one's arms, why am I driven as the only defendant of
a common fault? Nor are you moved, reverence, at Virgins' faces: this chorus
too is not ignorant what it is to love; if yet a certain one pressed by
Oeagrus's figure once lay on Bistonian rocks. Here when they set me in the
first part of the dance, and Bacchus will be in the middle with a learned
thyrsus, then I will suffer sacred ivy-berries to hang from my head: for
without you our talent does not avail.

You ask why I come to you later? The golden portico of Phoebus was opened by
great Caesar. So great it was arranged for show with Punic columns, among
which the woman-crowd of old Danaus. Here indeed a marble one seemed to me
more beautiful than Phoebus himself, to gape a song with a silent lyre; and
around the altar Myron's herds had stood, four oxen of the artist, living
signs. Then in the middle the temple was rising with bright marble, and dearer
to Phoebus than fatherland Ortygia: in which there was the Sun's chariot above
the gables; and the doors, a noble work of Libyan tooth, one was mourning
Gauls thrown down from Parnassus's peak, the other the funerals of Tantalus's
daughter. Then between his mother and between his sister the Pythian god
himself sounds songs in a long dress.

He who sees, he sins: he therefore who has not seen you will not desire: eyes
have the crime of the deed. For why, o Cynthia, doubtful lots of Praeneste,
why do you seek the walls of Aeaean Telegonus? Why so do Herculean chariots
carry you to Tibur? Why so often the Appian way you to Lanuvium? Would that
you may walk in this place, whenever you will be free, Cynthia, but a crowd
forbids me to believe you, when it sees you devoted run with torches lit into
the grove and carry lights to the goddess Trivia. Of course the Pompeian
portico is cheap with shady columns, noble with Attalic awnings, and a thick
row of planes rising equally, and streams which fall from a put-to-sleep Maro,
and when with nymphs lightly rustling in the whole city suddenly Triton hides
the water with his mouth. You are deceived, that road shows the theft of your
love: not the city, mad one, you flee our eyes! You do nothing, you compose
empty ambushes against me, idle you stretch known nets for me who am taught.
But about me it is less: the loss of a chaste fame will be as great for
wretched you as it deserves. For lately a rumor about you has hurt me to our
ears, and it was not good in the whole city. But you ought not to believe an
enemy tongue: always a story has been a penalty for beauties. Your fame has
not been condemned by poison caught: you will be a witness, Phoebus, that you
see pure hands. But if one night or another has been consumed in long play,
small charges do not move me. Tyndareus's daughter changed her fatherland by a
stranger's love, and without a decree she was brought back home living. Venus
herself is said to have been corrupted by Mars's lust, nor was she less always
honorable in the sky. Though Ida say that Paris the shepherd loved and that a
goddess lay among the flocks, this even the crowd of Hamadryad sisters watched
and old Sileni and the father of the chorus himself; with whom you gathered
apples under an Idaean cave, Naiad, catching the falling with a hand put
under. Or does anyone in so great a swarm of debaucheries ask 'Why is this
woman so rich? Who gave? From where did he give?' O Rome too lucky in our
time, if against customs one girl does it! These same things Lesbia already
did before her without punishment: she who follows is certainly less
invidious. He who seeks old Tatii and hard Sabines has lately set foot in our
city. You will be able sooner both to dry marine waves, and to pluck high
stars with a mortal hand, than to make that our girls refuse to sin: this
custom was when Saturn was holding kingdoms, both when Deucalion's waters
flowed through the world, and after Deucalion's ancient waters. Tell me, who
has been able to keep a bed chaste, which goddess to live alone with one god
alone? The wife once of great Minos, as they say, a white form of a grim bull
corrupted; nor less Danae surrounded with a bronze wall was able, chaste, to
deny great Jove. But if you have imitated Greek women and you Latin women,
always live free in my judgment!

Sad solemnities now return again to us: Cynthia has now performed rites ten
nights. And would that those rites perish which Inachus's daughter sent from
the warm Nile to Ausonian matrons! Which goddess has so often divided eager
lovers, whoever she was, she was always bitter. You certainly, Io, in Jove's
hidden loves, felt what it is to go many ways, when Juno ordered you to have
horns as a girl and to lose words by a hard sound of a herd. Ah how often you
hurt your face with oak leaves, and you chewed arbutus fed in your stalls! Or,
since Jupiter drew the rustic figure from your face, have you therefore been
made a proud goddess? Or is Egypt with dark alumni not enough for you? Why has
Rome been sought by you by so long a way? Or what profits it you that girls
sleep husbandless? But for you, believe me, there will be horns again, or we,
savage one, will drive you from our city: when there was no favor of the Nile
with the Tiber. But you who have been too much appeased by our pain, in these
nights empty let us make the way three times.

You do not hear and you allow my words to play, when already Icarius's oxen
turn the slow stars. You drink slow: midnights cannot break you. Or is the
hand not yet weary of throwing dice? Ah let him perish, whoever found
undiluted grapes and first corrupted good waters with nectar! Icarus,
deservedly slaughtered by Cecropian farmers, you have known how bitter the
vine-leaf odor is! And you o Eurytion, Centaur, perished by wine, and you too,
Polyphemus, by Ismarian wine. By wine beauty perishes, by wine age is
corrupted, by wine a girlfriend often does not know her own man. Wretched me,
how she is nothing changed by much Lyaeus! Now drink: you are beautiful: wines
harm you nothing, when your garlands hang down into the cups, and you read my
songs with a drawn-out voice. Let the table be wet more largely for you with
Falernian poured, and foam more softly in a gilded cup. Yet no woman receives
herself on a bed alone gladly: there is something that Love forces you to
seek. Always a tide is luckier toward absent lovers: a long plenty lowers
constant men.

Why should anyone now trust a mistress's face to Love? So my girl was almost
snatched from me. Having tried I say, no one is faithful in love: rarely does
each not seek a beauty for himself. That god pollutes kinsmen, looses friends,
and calls those well agreeing to sad arms. A guest adulterer came into
hospitality to Menelaus: and did not the Colchian woman follow an unknown man?
Lynceus, were you able, faithless one, to touch my care? Did not your hands
then fall? What if she had not been constant and so sure? Could you live in so
great a disgrace? Destroy my breast either with iron or with poison: only take
yourself away from my mistress. You may be a partner of life, you of the body,
I admit you as master, friend, of my affairs: I beg you off from one bed, you
from one bed: I cannot bear Jove as a rival. I myself rival shadows, because
it is nothing, foolish, because I often tremble with a foolish fear. Yet there
is one cause by which I remit such great charges, that your words were
wandering with much wine. But a wrinkle of a severe life will never deceive
me: all already know how good it is to love.

Lynceus himself, mine, is mad with late loves! I am glad that you approach our
gods. What will your wisdom in Socratic books now profit you, or to be able to
speak the ways of things? Or what do songs of Erechtheus read profit you? Your
old man helps nothing in a great love. You would better imitate Philitas
mindful of the Muses and the dreams of uninflated Callimachus. For though
again you may recount Aetolian Achelous, how the liquor flowed broken by a
great love, and also how deceitful Maeander wanders in the Phrygian field and
the wave itself deceives its own ways, and of what kind Adrastus's vocal Arion
was, a winning horse sad at Archemorus's funeral: the fates of Amphiaraus's
four-horse team would not profit you, or Capaneus's ruin pleasing to great
Jove. Cease also to compose words with an Aeschylean buskin, cease, and loose
your limbs to soft choruses. Begin now to enclose verses on a narrow lathe,
and come, hard poet, into your fires. You will not go safer than Antimachus,
not than Homer: a straight girl despises even great gods. But a heavy bull
does not yield to the plow before horns have stuck in strong snares, nor will
you already suffer hard loves by yourself: yet fierce you must first be tamed
by us. None of these is used to seek the reason of the world, nor why the Moon
labors with her brother's horses, nor if something will remain after Stygian
hardships, nor if thunderbolts sent on purpose thunder. See me, for whom a
small fortune has been left at home and no triumph of a grandfather in ancient
Mars, how I reign as a guest among mixed girls by this talent, by which I am
now lowered to you! Let it please me to languish set with yesterday's
garlands, whom a sure god has touched to the bones with a throw. That Virgil
can speak Actian shores of guardian Phoebus, and Caesar's strong ships, who
now raises the arms of Trojan Aeneas and walls thrown on Lavinian shores.
Yield, Roman writers, yield, Greeks! I know not what greater is being born
than the Iliad. You sing under the pine-woods of shady Galaesus Thyrsis and
Daphnis with worn reeds, and how ten apples can corrupt girls and a kid sent
from pressed udders. Lucky you who buy loves with cheap fruits! To this
ungrateful one Tityrus himself may sing. Lucky Corydon who tries to pluck
untouched Alexis, the delights of a farmer master! Though he rest weary from
his own oat-pipe, he is praised easy among the Hamadryads. You sing the
precepts of the old Ascraean poet, where a crop is green in a field, where a
grape on a ridge. You make such a song with a learned tortoise-shell as the
Cynthian tempers with fingers set on. Yet these things will not come
ungrateful to any reading, whether he will be untrained in love or skilled.
Nor did this one less in spirits, that he may be less in mouth, the tuneful
swan yield to the goose's untaught song. These things too Varro was playing
when Jason was finished, Varro the greatest flame of his Leucadia; these
writings too wanton Catullus sang, by which Lesbia herself is more known than
Helen; this even the page of learned Calvus confessed, when he was singing the
funeral of wretched Quintilia. And just now with how many wounds from
beautiful Lycoris dead Gallus washed in infernal water! Cynthia too even
praised in Propertius's verse, if Fame will wish to set me among these.

Shades of Callimachus and rites of Coan Philitas, allow me, I ask, to go into
your grove. I first enter as a priest from a pure spring to carry Italian
orgies through Greek choruses. Tell, in what cave did you alike thin the song,
or with what foot did you enter? Or what water did you drink? Ah farewell,
whoever delays Phoebus in arms! Let the verse go exact with thin pumice, by
which Fame lifts me sublime from earth, and the Muse born from me triumphs
with crowned horses, and with me in the chariot little Loves are carried, and
a crowd of writers has followed my wheels. Why in vain do you contend with me
with reins let in? A broad way is not given to run to the Muses. Many, Rome,
will add your praises to annals, who will sing Bactra as a future end of
empire. But this work, which you may read in peace, our page has brought down
from the Sisters' mountain by an untouched way. Give soft wreaths, Pegasids,
to your poet: a hard crown will not do for my head. But what an envious crowd
has drawn from me living, Honor after death will return with double interest;
after death old age fashions all things greater: a greater name comes on
mouths from the funeral rites. For who would know towers struck by a fir
horse, and rivers that went in close combat with a Haemonian man, Idaean
Simois with Jove's offspring Scamander, that wheels stained Hector three times
through the fields? And Deiphobus and Helenus and Paris of whatever kind in
Polydamas's arms his own soil would scarcely have known. You would now be of
scanty talk, Ilion, and you Troy twice captured by the divinity of the Oetaean
god. Nor less that Homer, recounter of your fall, felt his work grow by
posterity; and Rome will praise me among late grandsons: I myself augur that
day after ashes. It has been provided that a stone not indicate my bones with
a scorned tomb, the Lycian god approving the vows.

Meanwhile let us return into the circle of our song, that a girl touched may
rejoice at the accustomed sound. They say Orpheus soothed wild beasts and held
excited rivers with a Thracian lyre; they report that Cithaeron's rocks,
driven by Theban art, of their own accord joined into members of a wall; nay
even, Polyphemus, wild Galatea under Aetna turned her dripping horses to your
songs: should we wonder, with Bacchus and Apollo right for us, if a crowd of
girls cultivates my words? That my house is not propped with Taenarian
columns, nor an ivory vault among gilded beams, nor do my orchards equal
Phaeacian woods, Marcian liquor does not water laborious caves; but Muses are
companions and songs dear to a reader, nor is Calliope weary of my choruses.
Lucky, if any has been celebrated by my little book! Songs will be so many
monuments of your beauty. For neither the expenses of pyramids led to the
stars, nor Jove's house at Elis imitating the sky, nor the rich fortune of
Mausolus's tomb are vacant from the last condition of death. Either flame or
rain will withdraw honors from them, or conquered by the silent weight of
years they will fall. But a name sought by talent will not fall from age: by
talent glory stands without death.

I had seemed, reclining in Helicon's soft shade, where the wet of
Bellerophon's horse flows, to be able to gape your kings, Alba, and the deeds
of your kings, so great a work, with my sinews; and I had already moved small
lips to great springs (from where father thirsty Ennius drank before, and sang
the Curian brothers and Horatian spears, and royal trophies carried on an
Aemilian ship, and Fabius's victorious delays and the unlucky Cannae fight and
gods turned to pious vows, and the Lares putting Hannibal to flight from a
Roman seat, and that Jove was safe by a goose's voice), when Phoebus watching
me from a Castalian tree so speaks, leaning to the caves with a gilded lyre:
'What have you, madman, with such a stream? Who ordered you to touch the work
of heroic song? No fame is to be hoped by you from here, Propertius: soft
meadows are to be worn by small wheels; that your little book may often be
tossed on a bench, which a girl alone waiting for her man may read. Why has
your page been carried out of prescribed circuits? The boat of your talent is
not to be loaded heavy. Let one oar scrape waters for you, the other sands,
you will be safe: the greatest crowd of the sea is in the middle.' He had
spoken, and with an ivory plectrum he shows me a seat, where a new path has
been made in mossy soil. Here was a green cave with pebbles fixed, and drums
were hanging from hollow pumices, orgies of the Muses and a clay image of
father Silenus and your reeds, Tegeaean Pan; and the birds of mistress Venus,
my crowd, doves, dip purple beaks in a Gorgonean lake; and the nine Girls
allotted diverse rights exercise tender hands on their own gifts: this one
gathers ivies for thyrsi, this fits songs to sinews, but that weaves a rose
with each hand. From whose number one of the goddesses touched me (as I guess
from the face, it was Calliope): 'Content you will always be carried by snowy
swans, nor will the sound of a strong horse lead you to arms. Let it be
nothing for you to blow martial proclamations with a hoarse horn, nor to dip
the Aonian grove with Mars; or in what fields battles stand with Marian
standard and Rome breaks Teutonic wealth, or barbarian Rhine soaked with
Suebian blood carries wounded bodies on a grieving water. For you will sing
lovers crowned at another's threshold and the drunken signs of a nocturnal
delay, that through you he may know how to charm out shut girls, whoever
wishes to strike austere men with art.' Such things Calliope, and with waters
sought from the spring she wet our lips with Philetean water.

The god Caesar meditates arms toward rich Indians, and to split the straits of
a gem-bearing sea with a fleet. A great wage, Quirite: the farthest land
prepares triumphs; Tigris and Euphrates will flow under your laws; late, but a
province will come to Ausonian rods; Parthian trophies will grow accustomed to
Latin Jove. Go act, prows tried in war, give linens, and lead the accustomed
duty, armed horses! I sing lucky omens. Atone the Crassi and the disaster! Go
and consult Roman history! Father Mars, and the fateful lights of sacred
Vesta, before my deaths I pray that day may be, on which I may see Caesar's
axles loaded with spoils, and captured leaders sit under the arms, weapons of
a fleeing horse and the bow of a trousered soldier, horses often halt at the
crowd's applause, and leaning in the bosom of a dear girl I may begin to look
and read captured towns in titles! Guard your own offspring, Venus: let this
remain into the age, the head you see surviving from Aeneas. Let this spoil be
for those whose labors have deserved: it will be enough for me to be able to
applaud on the Sacred Way.

Love is a god of peace, we lovers worship peace: enough for me my hard battles
with a mistress. Nor is fat Campania plowed for me with a thousand yokes, nor
does our thirst drink from a rich gem, nor yet is my breast plucked by hated
gold, nor do I prepare mixed bronzes by your disaster, Corinth. O first earth
unlucky for fashioning Prometheus! He drove the work of the breast too little
cautiously. Arranging bodies he did not see the mind in a narrow place: the
way of the spirit ought first to have been straight. Now we are tossed on so
much of the sea by wind, and we seek an enemy, and we bind new arms to arms.
You will carry hardly any wealth to Acheron's waves: naked, fool, you will be
carried on the infernal ship. The victor is mixed equally with the conquered
in the shades: you sit with consul Marius, captured Jugurtha. Lydian Croesus
is not distant from Dulichian Irus: the best death is that which comes plucked
before the day. It pleases me to have cultivated Helicon in first youth and to
have folded hands in the Muses' choruses; it pleases me also to bind the mind
with much Lyaeus, and always to have the head in a spring rose. And when
already heavy age has intercepted Venus, and white old age has scattered black
hair, then let it please me to learn thoroughly nature's ways, which god
tempers this house of the world with art, where the rising comes, where it
fails, from where with horns forced the monthly moon returns into the full,
from where winds overcome on the brine, what the East Wind catches with a
blast, and from where perennial water into clouds; whether a day is to come
which may undermine the world's citadels, why a purple bow drinks rainy
waters, or why the peaks of Perrhaebian Pindus trembled, and the sun's disk
mourned with darkened horses, why Bootes is late to turn oxen and wagons, why
the chorus of Pleiades gathers with thick fire, or why the deep sea does not
go out of its bounds, and a full year goes into four parts; whether under the
earth there are laws of gods and torments of defendants, whether a wheel,
whether cliffs, whether thirst among waters, or Alcmaeon's furies or Phineus's
fasts, if Tisiphone's head rages with a black snake, whether Cerberus guards
the infernal cave with three throats, and few acres for Tityos nine, or a
feigned story descends on wretched nations, and fear can be hardly beyond the
pyre. Let this end of life remain for me: you, to whom arms are more pleasing,
bring Crassus's standards home.

Tell me what true things you have felt about our girl: so may a mistress's
yokes be taken from you, Lygdamus. For every messenger ought to be without
vanity, and a slave to have greater faith from fear. Now begin to tell me, if
you hold any, from the first origin: I will drink those things with hanging
ears. Do you deceive me swollen with empty joy, bringing back these things
which you think I wish to believe? Did you so see her weep with uncombed hair?
Was much water falling from her eyes? Nor did you see a mirror on the spread
bed, Lygdamus, and boxes lying closed at the bed's feet, and a sad dress
hanging from tender arms? Was no gem adorning snowy hands? The house was sad,
and sad maids were plucking their tasks, and she herself was spinning in the
middle place, and was drying wet eyes with wool pressed, and brought back our
abuses with a complaining sound? 'Is this the wage promised me with you as
witness, Lygdamus? There is a penalty even for a slave to break faith with a
witness. Can he leave wretched me with nothing done, and have at home such a
one as I do not wish to say? Does he rejoice that I waste alone on an empty
bed? If it pleases, let him insult, Lygdamus, at my death. She has conquered
me not by ways, but the shameless one by herbs; he is led by the threaded
wheel of a rhomb. Portents of a swelling toad with gore and bones gathered
from sucked snakes draw him, and feathers of a screech-owl found through lying
tombs, and a woolen fillet girt to a funeral bed. If my dreams do not sing
vain things, Lygdamus, I witness, the penalty will be late but ample before my
feet; and a rotting spider will be woven on an empty bed: Venus herself will
sleep on their nights.' If the girl has complained these things to you with

me, Lygdamus, you will be free.

Therefore you are the cause, money, of an anxious life! Through you we
approach the unripe way of death; you offer cruel fodder to men's vices; seeds
of cares have risen from your head. You overwhelm Paetus stretching linens to
Pharian ports three times and four times with a mad sea. For while he follows
you, wretched he fell from first age and swims a new bait for distant fishes.
But if content he were turning fields with a paternal ox, and had held that my

Sweet had been the quarrel for me at yesterday's lamps and so many curses of

Maecenas, knight from the Etruscan blood of kings, who wish to be within your
fortune, why do you send me into so vast a sea of writing? Great sails are not
fit for my raft. It is shameful to commit to the head a weight which you
cannot, and soon pressed to give the back with a knee bent. Not all things of
affairs are equally fit for all, nor is any palm drawn from an equal yoke. It
is Lysippus's glory to fashion spirited statues; Calamis boasts himself to me
with exact horses; in a tablet of Venus Apelles demands the summit for
himself; Parrhasius claims a place with small art; arguments are more added to
Mentor's form; but Myos's acanthus bends a scanty way; Phidian Jove adorns
himself with an ivory statue; a stone from his own city sells Praxiteles.
There are those for whom the palm of an Elean four-horse team runs together,
there are those for whom glory has been born in swift feet; this one born for
peace, this useful for camp arms: each follows the seeds of his own nature.
But I have received your precepts of life, Maecenas, and I am forced to
surpass you by your own examples. When it is allowed you to set mistress axes
in Roman honor and laws in mid-forum; or to go through the fighting spears of
Medes, and to load your house with arms fixed; and Caesar gives you strengths
to the effect, and at every time so easy wealth is insinuated; you spare and
gather yourself humble into thin shades: you yourself withdraw the full bosoms
of sails. Believe me, those judgments will equal great Camilli, and you too
will come on men's mouths. I do not split a swollen sea with a sail-bearing
keel: our delay is all under a scanty stream. I will not weep that Cadmus's
citadel has sat into paternal ashes, nor battles always with equal disaster;
nor will I recount Scaean gates and Pergama, Apollo's citadels, and Danaan
ships returned in the tenth spring, when a wooden horse, victor of Palladian

break bonds with the yoke broken? A sailor better presages the way of winds, a
soldier has learned from wounds to have fear. Those words I tossed in youth
gone by: you now learn to fear by my example. The Colchian woman drove burning

hands undeserving of cold rocks; Hypermnestra tells that the sisters dared a

tears we sanction life's loves: I hide many charges of your treachery. But now
we give commands to you, if by chance you are moved, if Chloris's herb does
not hold you wholly: let the nurse Parthenie not lack anything in trembling
years: she was able, nor was she greedy to you. And my delight Latris, whose
name is from use, let her not hold out a mirror to a new mistress. And
whatever verses you have made in my name, burn for me: cease to have my
praises. Drive ivy from the mound, which with a pregnant cluster binds soft
bones for me with twisted hair. Where fruit-bearing Anio lies on branching
fields, and ivory never grows pale by Hercules's divinity, here write a song
worthy of me on a middle column, but short, which a runner from the city may
read: 'here golden Cynthia lies in Tiburtine land: praise has been added to
your bank, Anio.' Nor do you scorn dreams coming from pious gates: when pious
dreams have come, they have weight. At night we are carried wandering, night
frees shut shades, and Cerberus himself wanders with the bar thrown down. At
light laws order to return to Lethean pools: we are carried, a sailor reviews
the carried load. Now let others possess you: soon I alone will hold: you will
be with me, and I will wear bones with bones mixed.' After she accomplished
these things with me under a complaining suit, the shade fell among my
embraces.

Learn what put watery Esquiline to flight this night, when a neighboring crowd
ran on new fields. Lanuvium is the old guardianship of an aged serpent, here,
where an hour of so rare a delay does not perish, where a sacred descent is
snatched by a blind opening, where (virgin, beware every such way!) the honor
of a fasting serpent penetrates, when he demands annual fodder and twists
hisses from the lowest ground. Such things girls let down grow pale at the
rites, when a hand is rashly trusted to a snaky mouth. He snatches foods moved
to himself by a virgin: the baskets themselves tremble in the virgin's palms.
If they have been chaste, they return onto parents' necks, and farmers shout
/it will be a fertile year./ Here my Cynthia was carried off with manes
clipped: Juno was the cause, but more Venus was the cause. Appian way, tell I
ask, how great a triumph with you as witness she drove with wheels poured
through your rocks! A shameful brawl sounded when in a secret tavern; if
without me, not without a stain of my fame. A spectacle herself sitting hung
on the first pole, having dared to move reins through impure places. For I am
silent of the silk carriages of a plucked spendthrift and Molossian dogs
armleted at the necks, who will give saleable fates to filthy stuffing, when a
shameful beard will conquer scraped cheeks. When injury was so often being
made to our bed, I wished to move camp with the bed changed. There is a
certain Phyllis neighbor of Aventine Diana, sober too little pleasing: when
she drinks, all becomes her. Another Teia is among Tarpeian groves, white, but
for her drunk one will not be enough. With these summoned I resolved to soothe
the night, and to renew my thefts with unknown Venus. There was one little bed
for three on secret grass. You ask the lying-together? I was between each.
Lygdamus at the ladles, and summer furniture of glass and Greek spit of
Methymnaean wine. Nile, your piper was, Phyllis a rattle-player, this one easy
to be sprinkled, neat without art, with a rose, and a dwarf himself briefly
thickened into his own limbs was tossing truncated hands at hollow boxwoods.
But neither was the flame standing with lamps filled, and the table fell
supine onto its own feet. I too seeking a second Venus through dice, always
harmful dogs leapt up. They were singing to a deaf man, they were baring
breasts to a blind: at Lanuvium's gates, ah me, I was; when suddenly hoarse
posts sounded on the hinge, and light murmurs were made at the first Lar. And
no delay, when Cynthia throws back all the folding-doors, not laborious in
hair, but raging becoming. Cups fell for me among fingers let go, and lips had
grown pale by the wine itself. She lightens with eyes and how much a woman
rages, the spectacle was not less than a city captured. She throws angry nails
into Phyllis's face: frightened Teia shouts neighboring waters. Lifted lights
disturb sleeping Quirites, and every path sounds with a mad night. Them with
hair snatched and tunics the first tavern of a dark way receives. Cynthia
rejoices in spoils and as victor runs back and wounds my face with a turned
hand, and sets a mark on the neck and makes it bloody with a bite, and
especially strikes the eyes, which have deserved. And when already she has
wearied arms with our blows, Lygdamus hiding at the left headboard of the
couch is dug out, and drawn forth he worships my genius. Lygdamus, I could
nothing: I was captured with you. Then at last with suppliant palms I came to
pacts, when she scarcely offered feet to be touched, and says 'if you wish me
to forgive an admitted fault, receive what the formula of our law will be. You
will neither walk cultivated in Pompey's shade, nor when sand spreads the
wanton Forum. Beware of bending necks oblique to the highest theater, or let
an open litter give itself to your delay. Lygdamus in first, all cause of
complaint to me, let him be sold and drag twin bonds at the feet.' She
declared laws: I answered /I will use the laws./ She had laughed made proud by
a command given. Then, whatever place foreign girls have touched, she
fumigates, but wipes thresholds with pure water, and orders to change all
lamps again, and three times touched my head with sulfur's fire. And so with
the bed changed through each coverlet I answered, and we loosed arms on the
whole bed.

The son of Amphitryon at what season he had driven bullocks from your stalls,
o Erythea, came to the unconquered Palatia of a flocked mountain, and set
tired cattle, himself also tired, where Velabra were pooling with their own
stream and a sailor was sailing through urban waters. But they did not remain
unharmed with Cacus as an unfaithful host: he polluted Jove by theft. Cacus
was an inhabitant, a rapist from a fearful cave, who was giving sounds divided
through three mouths. Here, lest sure signs of a manifest rape be, he dragged
cattle turned away by the tail into caves, nor without a god as witness: the
bullocks sounded a thief, and anger destroyed the thief's unplacid doors.
Cacus lay struck three temples by a Maenalian branch, and Alcides so says:
'Go, cattle, go cattle of Hercules, last labor of our club, twice sought by
me, twice my spoil, cattle, and sanctify the Bovarian fields with a long
lowing: your pastures will be Rome's noble Forum.' He had spoken, and thirst
twists the mouth with a dry palate, and pregnant earth ministers no waters.
But far off he hears shut girls laugh, where a grove had made a wood in a
shady circle, closed places of a female goddess and springs to be atoned and
rites uncovered without punishment by no men. Out-of-the-way thresholds purple
fillets were veiling, a rotting hut had shone with a scented fire, and a
poplar was adorning the shrine with long leaves, and much shade of a singing
bird was covering. Here he rushes with dust heaped on a dry beard, and throws
before the doors words smaller than a god: 'I pray you, o who play in a
grove's sacred cave, open hospitable shrines to weary men. Needing a spring I
wander and around sounding with waters; and a hollow palm with a stream taken
up is enough. Have you heard anyone who lifted a globe on his back? I am that
one: received earth calls Alcides. Who does not hear the strong deeds of
Hercules's club and weapons never idle at huge wild beasts, and that Stygian
darkness shone for one man? He receives: this land scarcely lies open to weary
me. But if you were making a rite for bitter Juno, the stepmother herself
would not have shut her waters. But if my face and a lion's bristles terrify
anyone and hair burned through by a Libyan sun, I the same have done slave
duties with a Sidonian cloak and daily tasks with a Lydian distaff, and a soft
band took a shaggy breast for me, and I was a girl fit with hard hands.' Such
things Alcides; but with such the kind priestess, white hair bound with a
purple thread: 'Spare the eyes, guest, and withdraw from a grove to be
revered; yield now and leave safe thresholds by flight. Forbidden to men it is
atoned by a law to be feared, which altar vindicates itself with the hut
removed. At a great price Tiresias the seer looked on Pallas, while she washes
strong limbs with the Gorgon laid down. Let gods give you other springs: this
water for girls, a pathless wave of a secret path, flows.' So the old woman:
he struck shady posts with shoulders, nor did a shut door bear angry thirst.
But after he had already conquered heat with the stream exhausted, he sets sad
laws with lips scarcely dry: 'This corner of the world now receives me
dragging my fates: this land scarcely lies open to weary me. The Greatest
Altar which is devoted with flocks found, an altar' he says 'made greatest
through these hands, let this never lie open to be worshipped by any girls,
lest the thirst of excellent Hercules be unavenged.' Hail, holy father, whom
already harsh Juno favors: Holy one, may you wish to be present right in my
book. For this one, since he had sanctified a world purged by hands, so the
Cures of Tatius composed him Holy.

Now I will begin to open the causes of Feretrian Jove and triple arms received
from three leaders. I climb a great way, but glory gives me strengths: a crown
gathered from an easy ridge does not please. You first imbue the example of
this first palm, Romulus, and you return full of spoil from an enemy, at the
time when Caeninan Acron seeking the gates you as victor pour onto an
overturned horse with a spear-point. Herculean Acron, leader from Caenina's
citadel, was once a horror, Rome, to your bounds. This one having dared to
hope spoils from Quirinus's shoulders himself gave, but not dry with his own
blood. Romulus sees this one balancing darts before hollow towers and occupies
first with vows ratified: /Jupiter, this victim Acron will fall for you
today./ He had vowed, and that spoil fell for Jove. So the parent of the city
and of virtue was used to conquer, who bore cold camps from a spare Lar. The
same was a horseman both fit for bridles, the same for plows, and a wolf
helmet dressed with a shaggy crest. Nor was a painted shield gleaming with
pyropus led on: slaughtered cattle were offering tough belts. Not yet was the
sound of war beyond the Tiber, the last spoil Nomentum and three captured
acres of Cora. But Cossus follows with the slaughter of Veientine Tolumnius,
when to be able to conquer Veii was of labor; alas old Veii! and you then were
kingdoms, and a golden chair was set in your forum: now within walls a horn of
a slow shepherd sings, and they reap fields on your bones. By chance the
Veientine leader stood on the citadel of the gate and gave a colloquy relying
from his own city: and while a ram was beating the wall with a bronze horn,
where a long vine was covering the work drawn, Cossus says /it is better to
run together on a strong field./ Nor is delay made, each sets a step on the
plain. Gods helped Latin hands, Tolumnius's neck cut washed Roman horses with
blood. Claudius kept off enemies thrown across from the Rhine, when the Belgic
shield of a huge leader Virdomarus was brought back. This one was boasting a
race from the Rhine itself, mobile to pour javelins from upright wheels. For
him as he goes from the line throwing with striped trousers a hooked torque
fell from a throat cut. Now three spoils are stored in a temple: the cause of
Feretrius, because by a sure omen a leader strikes a leader with a sword; or
because they were bearing these conquered arms on their own shoulders, from
here the proud altar of Feretrian Jove has been named.

Cease, Paullus, to press my tomb with tears: the black door is opened to no
prayers; when once funerals have entered infernal laws, the ways stand with
adamant not entreated. Though the god of a dark hall hear you praying: of
course deaf shores will drink your tears. Vows move the upper ones: when the
porter has received bronzes, a lurid gate bars grassy pyres. So sad trumpets
sang, when a hostile torch put under was drawing our head from the couch. What
did marriage of Paullus profit me, what a chariot of grandfathers or so great
pledges of my fame? Cornelia had Fates no less unsoft: and I am a load which
may be gathered with five fingers. Condemned nights and you, slow fords,
marshes, and whatever wave folds my feet, though unripe, yet I have come here
not harmful: let the Father here give soft laws to my shade. Or if any Aeacus
sits as judge with an urn set, let him claim my bones with a lot-drawn ball:
let brothers sit by, and next to Minos's seat a severe crowd of Eumenides with
the forum intent: Sisyphus, be vacant from the mass; let Ixion's circles be
silent, let deceitful Tantalus liquor be snatched; and let wicked Cerberus
seek no shades today; and let a chain lie loose with a silent bar. I myself
speak for me: if I deceive, let the sisters' penalty, an unlucky urn, press my
shoulders. If fame has been for anyone a glory through ancestral trophies,
African kingdoms speak Numantine grandfathers: another crowd equals maternal
Libones, and each house is propped by its own titles. Soon, when already the
bordered dress has yielded to marriage torches, and another fillet has bound
accepted hair, I am joined, Paullus, to your bed so about to depart: on this
stone I will be read to have been married to one. I call to witness ashes of
ancestors to be cultivated by you, Rome, under whose titles, Africa, you lie
beaten, and Perses stirring the breast with ancestor Achilles, and he who
broke your houses with ancestor Achilles, that I neither softened the law of
the censorship nor by any stain of mine made your hearths blush. Cornelia was
not a loss to such great spoils: nay she was even a part of a great house to
be imitated. Nor has my age been changed, it is wholly without a charge: we
have lived distinguished between each torch. Nature gave me laws drawn from
blood, nor could you be better by fear of a judge. Let any urn bear austere
tablets about me: none will be more shameful by sitting with me, either you,
Claudia, who moved slow Cybele with a rope, rare minister of a towered
goddess, or she whose white linen showed living hearths when Vesta was
demanding back shaved fires. Nor did I harm you, sweet head, mother Scribonia:
what would you wish changed in me except the fates? I am praised by a mother's
tears and the city's complaints, and my bones defended by Caesar's groan. He
chides that a sister worthy of his own daughter has lived, and we have seen
tears go from a god. And yet I have earned generous honors of a dress nor has
my rape been made from a sterile house. You, Lepidus, and you, Paullus, my
relief after fates: our lights have been stored in your bosom. We have seen
also a brother double a curule chair, in whose consulship, a feast time, a
sister was snatched. Daughter, you born a specimen of a father's censorship,
make that imitating us you hold one man. And prop the race with a series: for
me willing the boat goes from so many of mine about to increase my deeds. This
is the last wage of a woman's triumph, where free fame praises a deserved
pyre. Now I commend to you common pledges, the sons: this care even breathes
branded in my ash. Perform a mother's turns, father: that whole crowd of mine
will have to be borne on your neck. When you have given your kisses to them
weeping, add a mother's: the whole house has now begun to be your burden. And
if you will be about to hurt anything, without those witnesses! When they
come, cheat kisses with dry cheeks! Let nights be enough for you, which you
weary about me, Paullus, and dreams often believed into my face: and when in
secret you speak to our images, cast single words as to one about to answer.
Yet if a door has changed an opposing bed, and a cautious stepmother has sat
on our couch, boys, praise and bear a father's marriage: taken she will give a
hand to your ways; nor praise a mother too much: compared with the prior she
will turn free words into her own offenses. Or if that mindful one remains
content with my shade and has held my ashes worth so much, learn already now
to feel old age about to come, nor let any way be vacant to a bachelor's
cares. What has been drawn from me, let it be added to your years: so let it
please by my offspring that Paullus be an old man. And it is well: never did I
as a mother take mourning; a whole troop has come to my funeral rites. The
cause has been pleaded. Rise weeping, witnesses, while grateful earth pays
back the price of life. By ways even the sky has lain open: let me be worthy
by deserving, whose bones may be carried to honored grandfathers.
