Homily on the Pharisee.
=59.589=
Just as a father who loves his children is drawn by his own love to converse
with his dearest ones, and they being infants unable to hear mature words
because of the childishness of their condition, the father, having changed
his voice and laid aside the fluency of eloquence, begins to lisp to them,
leading his dearest ones from speaking and hearing toward a greater grasp of
words; in the same way our Lord also, through his great love for his own
formation, being God the Word, having taken up human speech, has come to us,
that from his spea =59.590= king and spending time with us, having shown a
heavenly revelation, he might lead us upon the heavenly ladder, that is, of
hope.
He is invited, then, to a meal by a Pharisee, as we just now heard the
Gospel proclaiming, and he does not turn away the one who invites; for he
did not come to display the dignity of his divinity, but to rescue fallen
humanity from the underworld. He is invited to a meal by a Pharisee, and he
does not decline, that he may both train the rich not to look down on the
tables of the poor, and teach the righteous not to spit upon the tables of
sinners. =59.591= For many who think themselves righteous spit upon the
tables of sinners, and the rich upon those of the poor. For the Lord,
wishing all to be dearest to one another, brother-loving, affectionate,
humble-minded, first begins the work, that from the doing he may teach.
Therefore he says: Learn from me, that I am meek and humble in heart. You
then who are exercised in piety, whenever you are invited by some sinner, do
not decline; and if you are reproached by someone saying, Why do you eat and
drink with sinners? answer in return, saying: Those who are well have no
need of a physician, but those who are ill. For I am righteous, not that I
may save the righteous, but that I may turn sinners. The Lord is invited to
a meal, and he does not decline, he who from five loaves fed five thousand
to fullness in the wilderness, who dried up the impassable sea, and led
Israel through the sea as through a grass-bearing plain, who rained the
manna upon them from heaven, and all day long snows from the brightness of
the manna. The Lord is invited to a meal, and he does not decline, teaching
those who invite him, at the meals, the best of lessons; for he goes away to
a meal, not that he may be filled with the Pharisee's foods, but that the
Pharisee may be filled with the Lord's teachings. He is invited to a meal,
and he comes into the house of the Pharisee, covering the divinity with
humanity, lest the one who invited the Master, suddenly seeing the flashing
radiance of the divinity, flee from the house.
He enters into the house of the Pharisee, the heavenly king toward the
captive, and he covers the royal dignity with the leather armor of the
flesh, lest the captive, having seen the heavenly king, tremble and sink
into the earth. He enters into the house of the Pharisee, the Sun of
righteousness, covering the gold-gleaming splendors of the rays with the
cloud of the body, lest the man, having seen, lose his sight. He enters into
the house of the Pharisee, the heavenly shepherd; having left the
ninety-nine sheep upon the mountains, he comes after the lost one; he enters
into its fold, covering the shepherd in the lamb-form of the body. He does
not carry a staff, nor a rod threatening the former wandering, lest the
sheep, having grown timid and been startled, run away again from its own
flock; but he enters into the house of the Pharisee, not as God, not as
king, not as Sun of righteousness, but as man toward man, as lamb toward
lamb. He lives with him, grazes with him, sits with him, enters, reclines
with the one already fallen because of sin, that in reclining with him he
may raise up together the one already fallen. And having entered he was not
washed according to the custom of the Pharisees. And this he did in two
ways, that he might give the sheep an occasion to speak to the shepherd; and
further that he might dissolve a Jewish ordinance and confirm a spiritual
one. For the Pharisee, it says, saw and marveled that he was not first
washed, not praising the teacher, but being at a loss at what was happening,
not praising, but judging. And it was possible to see, beloved, the
fashioner of life judged by the formation, and the potter being corrected by
the clay.
This the Pharisee suffered, not seeing with the hidden eyes of the heart the
commander of the angels within. If he had beheld this one, he would have
fallen at his kne =59.592= es, and washed his feet with tears, and said, Be
merciful to me the sinner. For he would have said: You who brought all
things from non-being into being; by your word the heavens were made firm,
and by your spirit their powers were established. You gather the waters of
the sea as into a wineskin, setting abysses in treasuries; from you the
whole inhabited world is afraid. But having neither thought nor said any of
these things, he judged the Master; but the Lord, having given a pretext, as
to that Samaritan woman, from asking water of her, gave an occasion to ask
of him the heavenly water. The Lord therefore begins gently to turn the
sheep, and to lead him by the hand from error toward the truth. For now you,
the Pharisees, wash the outside of the cup and of the platter; but the
inside is full of rapine and deceit; that is, You now adorn the body, but
you maltreat the souls. And how will you even take pleasure, even if the cup
be golden, when it has within a death-bearing drink? Are not the harlots
such, who, coloring their faces with paints, destroy their souls? For do
they not, reddening their faces with crimson, work with their own fingers an
image of the flame that is about to receive them? whereas they ought to
sprinkle their hearts with the blood of Jesus, or rather to make them
rose-colored, and to whiten their hearts with the Spirit; that the flower of
the soul might be flashing like a rose mixed in a lily. But now they set
their faces ablaze, that they may kindle the hearts of the young toward
madness. Now you, the Pharisees, wash the outside of the cup and of the
platter, but your inside is full of rapine and wickedness; as if he were
saying, Why do you wash the body with water, but do not wash the faults in
the Spirit?
For I dare to say, beloved, a man will be benefited in nothing, even if he
wash the body, unless he have the Spirit as fellow-worker, and almsgiving as
companion. For as a cup washed both outside and inside, but not having
drink, would not heal the desire of the thirsty, and likewise also a
platter; so also a man, even if he be washed inside and outside, will not
delight the Lord, unless he feed the fellow-servant. Therefore the Lord adds
and says: But the things that are within, give alms; and behold, all things
will be clean for you. But the things that are within, he says; for he is
not delighted by the bringing of things rich in gold, so much as by the
unfeigned offering of the guileless. Do not you, the poor man, raise the
plea that you have no gold, and so many cargoes of silver; cast into the
treasury what you have; and two mites justify. Remember that widow, that she
was justified above those rich in gold. For God does not know how to weigh
money, but he tests conscience. Almsgiving wipes away stains of souls, and
treasures up wages of good things; almsgiving is the vital provision of the
soul, almsgiving whitens the souls, almsgiving is the soul's boldness of
speech, a fair generalship. Cast gold, and receive the Lord; give silver,
and receive the holy; scatter bronze, and with it the rust of sin will go
out together; bedew the furnace, and give garments that you may receive
healings, in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory and the might unto
the ages of ages. Amen.
