Of holy Justin
Apology on behalf of Christians
to Antoninus the Pious.

To the Emperor Titus Aelius Hadrianus Antoninus Pius Augustus Caesar, and to
Verissimus his son, a philosopher, and to Lucius the philosopher, son of
Caesar by nature and of Pius by adoption, a lover of learning, and to the
sacred Senate and to the whole people of the Romans: on behalf of those from
every race of men who are unjustly hated and abused, I, Justin, son of
Priscus, son of Baccheius, of those from Flavia Neapolis of Syria Palaestina,
being one of them, have made this address and petition.

Reason dictates that those who are truly pious and philosophers honor and love
only the truth, declining to follow the opinions of the ancients if they are
worthless. For not only does sound reason dictate not to follow those who have
done or taught anything unjust, but the lover of truth must choose in every
way, even before his own soul, even if death is threatened, to speak and to do
the just things.

You, then, are heard everywhere to be called pious and philosophers and
guardians of justice and lovers of learning. Whether you also are so will be
shown. For we have come forward not to flatter you through these writings nor
to speak for favor, but to demand that you make judgment according to accurate
and examining reason, not held by prejudice nor by the man-pleasing of the
superstitious, nor, by an irrational impulse and a long-prevailing evil
rumor, bringing the vote against yourselves.

For we have calculated that we can suffer nothing evil from anyone, unless we
are convicted as workers of vice or are recognized as wicked. You can kill,
but you cannot harm.

But lest anyone think these things an irrational and bold voice, we ask that
the things charged against them be examined, and if they are shown to be so,
that they be punished as is fitting, or rather that they punish. But if no one
has anything to prove, true reason does not dictate that through an evil rumor
innocent people be wronged, or rather yourselves, who think it right to drive
affairs not by judgment but by passion.

And every sound-minded person will declare this a fair and the only just
challenge: that the ruled furnish the account of their own life and speech
unassailable, and likewise that the rulers, following not force nor tyranny
but piety and philosophy, cast the vote. For thus both the rulers and the
ruled would enjoy the good.

For somewhere one of the ancients also said: Unless the rulers philosophize,
and the ruled, the cities would not be happy.

It is our work, then, to furnish to all the inspection of both life and
teachings, so that we ourselves may owe the punishment on behalf of those who
think they do not know our things, for whatever they may offend while they
themselves are blind. And it is yours, as reason requires, to be found good
judges when you hear. For thereafter, once you have learned, if you do not do
the just things, it will be without defense before God.
By the mere additional name, then, neither good nor evil is judged, apart from
the deeds that fall under the name. Since, so far as it is from the name
charged against us, we are most kind. But since we do not think this just, to
ask to be released on account of the name if we are convicted as evil, again,
if we are found doing no injustice either through the appellation of the name
or through our way of life, it is yours to be in agony, lest, punishing
unjustly those who are not convicted, you owe punishment to justice.
For from a name neither praise nor punishment would reasonably come, unless
some virtuous or worthless thing can be shown through works. For indeed you do
not punish all who are accused before you before they are convicted. But in
our case you take the name as a conviction, although, so far as it is from the
name, you ought rather to punish those who accuse.
For we are accused of being Christians. And it is not just that the kind be
hated. And again, if one of those accused becomes a denier, saying with his
voice that he is not, you release him as having nothing to prove him sinning;
but if he confesses that he is, you punish him because of the confession.
Whereas it is necessary both to examine the life of the one who confesses and
that of the one who denies, so that through the deeds it may appear of what
sort each is.
For in the way that some, having received from the teacher Christ, are
exhorted not to deny when examined, in the same way those who live badly
perhaps furnish occasions to those who choose otherwise to charge impiety and
injustice against all Christians. And this too is not done rightly. For indeed
some inscribe upon themselves the name and figure of philosophy who do nothing
worthy of the promise. And you know that even those among the ancients who
held and taught opposite opinions are addressed by the one name as
philosophers.
And some of these taught atheism, and the poets who have arisen announce Zeus
as licentious together with his children. And those who pursue their teachings
are not barred by you; rather you set prizes and honors for those who insult
these with fine voice.
What then would this be? In our case, who promise

to do no injustice and not to hold these godless opinions, you do not examine
judgments, but, driven by irrational passion and by the whip of worthless
demons, you punish without judgment, taking no thought.
For the truth will be spoken. Since of old worthless demons, making
appearances, both committed adultery with women and corrupted boys and showed
terrors to men, so that those who did not judge the things that were happening
by reason were struck, and, seized by fear and not knowing that they were
worthless demons, they named them gods, and they addressed each by the name
which each of the demons set for himself.
And when Socrates tried by true reason and in an examining way to bring these
things into the open and to lead men away from the demons, the demons
themselves, through men who delight in vice, worked that he be killed as
godless and impious, saying that he was bringing in new divinities. And
likewise in our case they work the same thing. For not only among the Greeks
were these things refuted by reason through Socrates, but also among
barbarians by the Reason himself, having been formed and become a man and
called Jesus Christ, by whom we, having been persuaded, say that the demons
who did these things are not only not right, but evil and unholy demons, whose
deeds are not like those of men who long for virtue.
Hence we have also been called atheists. And we confess that we are atheists
of such supposed gods, but not of the most true God, the Father of justice and
of temperance and of the other virtues, unmixed with vice. But both him and
the Son who came from him and taught us these things, and the host of the
other good angels who follow and are made like, and the prophetic Spirit, we
worship and adore, honoring with reason and truth, and handing them on without
grudging to everyone who wishes to learn, as we were taught.
But someone will say, Already some who were taken have been convicted as
evildoers. For indeed you often, whenever you examine the life of each of
those accused, condemn many, but not because of those previously mentioned. In
general, then, we confess that also: that just as those among the Greeks who
taught what pleased them are addressed from every side by the one name of
philosophy, although the teachings are opposite, so also among those who have
arisen among barbarians and have been thought wise the name charged upon them
is common. For all are addressed as Christians.
Hence we ask that the deeds of all who are announced to you be judged, so that
the one convicted as unjust may be punished, but

not as a Christian. And if anyone appears unconvicted, let him be released as
a Christian who does no injustice. For we will not ask you to punish the
accusers. For they are sufficient with the wickedness that is already theirs
and with ignorance of the good things.
And consider that we have said these things on your behalf, from the fact that
it is in our power to deny when examined. But we do not wish to live speaking
falsehood. For desiring the eternal and pure life we lay claim to the sojourn
with God the Father and Maker of all, and we hasten toward the confession, we
who are persuaded and believe that those who have persuaded God by works that
they followed him and loved the sojourn with him, where vice does not strike
against, can obtain these things. So then, to speak briefly, the things we
expect and have learned through Christ and teach are these.
And Plato likewise said that Rhadamanthus and Minos would punish the unjust
who come to them. And we say the same thing will happen, but by Christ, and to
the same bodies coming to be with the souls, and that they will be punished
with eternal punishment, and not a thousand-year period only, as he said. If
then someone will say this is unbelievable or impossible, this error is toward
us and not toward another, until we are convicted by deed of doing no
injustice.
But neither with many sacrifices and plaits of flowers do we honor those whom
men, having shaped and set up in temples, have named gods, since we know these
are soulless and dead and do not have the form of God (for we do not think God
has such a form as some say has been imitated for honor), but that they have
the names and shapes of those evil demons who appeared.
For why must we say to you who know what the craftsmen do with the matter,
scraping and cutting and melting and striking? And often from dishonorable
vessels, having changed the shape only by art and having formed them, they
name them gods. Which we think not only irrational, but also to happen as an
insult to God, who, having unspeakable glory and form, is named upon things
that are perishable and in need of care.
And that the craftsmen of these are licentious and have every vice, that we
may not number them, you know accurately. And they corrupt their own servant
girls who work with them. O the thunderstruck folly, that unbridled men are
said to shape and recast gods to be worshipped, and that such men are set as
guardians of the temples where they are dedicated, not seeing that it is
unlawful

even to think or to say that men are guardians of gods.
But we have received that God does not need the material offering from men,
seeing that he himself provides all things. And we have been taught and are
persuaded and believe that he accepts those only who imitate the good things
that belong to him, temperance and justice and love of man and whatever is
proper to God, who is called by no set name.
And we have been taught that he, being good, made all things in the beginning
out of formless matter for the sake of men. Who, if they show themselves by
works worthy of his counsel, we have received will be counted worthy of the
sojourn with him, reigning together, having become incorruptible and
passionless. For in the way that in the beginning he made them when they were
not, in the same way we think that those who choose the things pleasing to
him, because they have chosen, are counted worthy of incorruption and of
communion.
For to come to be in the beginning was not ours. But to follow the things dear
to him, choosing through the rational powers which he himself granted, both
persuades and leads us into faith. And we think it is for all men not to be
barred from learning these things, but even to be urged toward them.
For what human laws were not able to do, these the Word, being divine, has
worked, unless the worthless demons scattered many false and godless charges,
taking as ally the evil desire toward all things that is in each and is
various by nature, of which none belongs to us.
And you, having heard that we expect a kingdom, have assumed without judgment
that we are speaking of a human one, although we speak of that with God, as is
also clear from the fact that when examined by you we confess that we are
Christians, knowing that death is laid down as the penalty for the one who
confesses. For if we expected a human kingdom, we would also deny, so that we
might not be destroyed, and we would try to escape notice, so that we might
obtain the things expected. But since we do not have our hopes toward the
present, we have not taken thought of those who destroy, of that which is owed
to die in any case.
And we are helpers and allies to you for peace more than all men, we who hold
these opinions, that it is impossible for a wrongdoer or a greedy man or a
plotter or a virtuous man to escape God, and that each goes to eternal
punishment or salvation according to the worth of the deeds. For if all men
knew these things, no one would choose vice for a little while, knowing that
he goes to an eternal condemnation through fire, but in every way he would
restrain himself and adorn himself with virtue, so that he might obtain the
good things from God and be released from the places of punishment.

For it is not because of the laws and punishments laid down by you that they
try to escape notice when they do injustice, but knowing that it is possible,
being men, to escape your notice, they do injustice. If they had learned and
been persuaded that it is impossible to escape God in anything, not only what
is done but also what is planned, they would have been orderly in every way
even because of the consequences, as you too will agree.
But you seem to fear that all may act justly, and you will no longer have
people to punish. And such a work would belong to public executioners, but not
to good rulers. And we are persuaded that these things also, as we said
before, are worked from worthless demons, who even ask sacrifices and services
from those who live irrationally. But we have not supposed that you, who reach
after piety and philosophy, would do anything irrational.
And if you also, like the foolish, honor the customs before the truth, do what
you can. And rulers who honor opinion before the truth can do only so much as
robbers in a desert. And that you will not sacrifice well, the Word
demonstrates, whose most kingly and most just ruler after the God who begot
him we know no one to be.
For in the way that all decline to inherit paternal poverties or sufferings or
disgraces, so also whatever the Word dictates must not be chosen, the man of
sense will not choose. All these things, I say, our teacher, who is also Son
and Apostle of God the Father and Master of all, Jesus Christ, from whom we
have also had the naming as Christians, foretold would happen.
Hence we also become firm toward all the things taught by him, since whatever
he previously foretold would happen is shown in deed to be happening. Which is
a work of God, to speak before it happens and thus to be shown happening as it
was foretold. It would have been possible, then, even stopping at these
things, to add nothing, calculating that we ask just and true things. But
since we know that it is not easy for a soul held by ignorance to change
quickly, we have been eager to add a little for the persuasion of those who
love truth, knowing that it is not impossible, when truth is set beside, to
flee ignorance.
That we are not atheists, then, worshipping the Maker of this all, saying, as
we were taught, that he is in need of neither blood nor drink-offerings nor
incense, praising him, as much as power allows, with the word of prayer and
thanksgiving for all that we are offered, having received this as the only
honor worthy of him, not to consume in fire the things that have come from him
for nourishment, but to offer them to ourselves and to those in need,
and being thankful to him to send processions of word and hymns both for our
having come to be and

for all the means toward vigor, qualities of kinds and changes of seasons, and
sending requests also to come to be again in incorruption through faith in
him: who that is sound-minded will not confess it?
And that we honor with reason Jesus Christ, who became our teacher of these
things and was born for this, who was crucified under Pontius Pilate, who was
procurator in Judaea in the times of Tiberius Caesar, having learned that he
is the Son of the true God and holding him in the second place, and the
prophetic Spirit in the third rank, we will show. For here they declare it our
madness, saying that we give second place after the unchangeable and always
existing God and Begetter of all to a crucified man, being ignorant of the
mystery in this, to which we urge you to give heed as we explain.
For we foretell to you to be on guard, lest the demons who have already been
slandered by us deceive you and turn you away from even reading and
understanding the things said (for they struggle to have you as slaves and
servants, and sometimes through appearances of dreams, sometimes again through
magical turns they overpower all who do not struggle somehow for their own
salvation), in the way that we also, after being persuaded by the Word, stood
away from those, and follow only the unbegotten God through the Son.
We who formerly delighted in fornications, but now embrace temperance alone;
and those who used magical arts, having dedicated themselves to the good and
unbegotten God; and those who loved resources of money and possessions more
than all, now bringing even what we have into common and sharing with everyone
in need;
and we who hated one another and murdered one another, and would not make
common hearths with those not of the same tribe because of the customs, now
after the appearing of Christ becoming table-companions, and praying for our
enemies, and trying to persuade those who hate us unjustly, so that those who
have lived according to the good counsels of Christ may be of good hope with
us to obtain the same things from God who is master of all.
And lest we seem to you to be playing the sophist, we have thought it well,
before the demonstration, to mention a few of the teachings from Christ
himself, and it will be yours as powerful kings to examine whether we have
truly been taught these things and teach them. And short and concise words
have come from him. For he was not a sophist, but his word was the power of
God.
Concerning temperance, then, he said this much: Whoever looks at a woman
toward desiring her has already committed adultery in the heart before God.
And: If your right eye is a stumbling-block to you, cut it out. For it is
profitable for you one-eyed

to enter into the kingdom of the heavens, than with the two to be sent into
the eternal fire. And: Whoever marries a woman divorced from another husband
commits adultery. And: There are some who have been made eunuchs by men, and
there are some who were born eunuchs, and there are some who made themselves
eunuchs because of the kingdom of the heavens. Yet not all receive this.
Just as also those who make second marriages by human law are sinners before
our teacher, and those who look at a woman toward desiring her. For not only
the one who commits adultery in deed has been cast out before him, but also
the one who wishes to commit adultery, since not the works only are manifest
to God but also the thoughts. And many men and many women, sixty-year-olds and
seventy-year-olds, who from childhood were made disciples to Christ, remain
uncorrupted. And I pray to show such from every race of men.
For what should we even say of the innumerable multitude of those who have
changed from licentiousness and have learned these things? For Christ did not
call the just nor the temperate to repentance, but the impious and the
licentious and the unjust. And he spoke thus: I did not come to call the just,
but sinners to repentance. For the heavenly Father wishes the repentance of
the sinner rather than his punishment.
And concerning loving all he taught these things: If you love those who love
you, what new thing do you do? For even the fornicators do this. But I say to
you: Pray for your enemies and love those who hate you and bless those who
curse you and pray for those who abuse you.
And toward sharing with those in need and doing nothing toward glory he said
these things: To everyone who asks give, and the one who wishes to borrow do
not turn away. For if you lend from those from whom you hope to receive, what
new thing do you do? This even the tax-collectors do. And do not treasure up
for yourselves on the earth, where moth and eating make unseen and robbers dig
through. But treasure up for yourselves in the heavens, where neither moth nor
eating makes unseen. For what is a man profited if he gain the whole world,
but lose his soul? Or what will he give in exchange for it? Treasure up
therefore in the heavens, where neither moth nor eating makes unseen.
And: Become kind and merciful, as also your Father is kind and merciful, and
he makes his sun rise upon sinners and just

and wicked. And do not be anxious what you shall eat or what you shall put on.
Do you not differ from the birds and the beasts? And God feeds them. Do not
therefore be anxious what you shall eat or what you shall put on. For your
heavenly Father knows that you have need of these. But seek the kingdom of the
heavens, and all these things will be added to you. For where the treasure is,
there also is the mind of the man. And: Do not do these things to be seen by
men. Otherwise you have no reward from your Father who is in the heavens.
And concerning being patient of evil and serviceable to all and without anger,
the things he said are these: To the one who strikes your cheek offer also the
other, and the one who takes your tunic or cloak do not hinder. And whoever is
angry is liable to the fire. And to everyone who impresses you for a mile
follow two. And let your good works shine before men, so that seeing they may
wonder at your Father who is in the heavens.
For one must not resist. Nor has he wished us to be imitators of the
worthless, but through endurance and gentleness he has urged to lead all from
shame and desire of the evil things. Which we can also show in the case of
many who have happened among you: they changed from violent and tyrannical
men, having been defeated either by having followed the endurance of life of
neighbors, or by having observed the strange patience of fellow-travelers who
were being defrauded, or by having made trial of those who do business with
them.
And concerning not swearing at all, but always speaking the true things, he
exhorted thus: Do not swear at all. And let your yes be yes, and your no no.
And what is more than these is from the evil one.
And that one must worship God alone, he persuaded thus, saying: The greatest
commandment is: The Lord your God you shall worship and him alone you shall
serve from your whole heart and from your whole strength, the Lord God who
made you. And when someone came to him and said, Good teacher, he answered
saying: No one is good except God alone, who made all things.
And those who are not found living as he taught, let them be known not to be
Christians, even if they speak with the tongue the teachings of Christ. For he
said that not those who only speak, but those who also do the works, will be
saved. For he spoke thus: Not everyone who says to me Lord, Lord will enter
into the kingdom of the heavens, but

the one who does the will of my Father who is in the heavens. For whoever
hears me and does what I say hears the one who sent me. And many will say to
me: Lord, Lord, did we not eat and drink in your name and do powers? And then
I will say to them: Depart from me, workers of lawlessness. Then there will be
weeping and gnashing of teeth, when the just shine as the sun, and the unjust
are sent into the eternal fire.
For many will come upon my name, outwardly clothed in sheepskins, but inwardly
being ravening wolves. From their works you will know them. And every tree
that does not make good fruit is cut out and thrown into fire. And we ask that
those who do not live in accordance with his teachings, but are only called
Christians, be punished also by you.
And taxes and contributions to those appointed by you we try everywhere before
all things to bring, as we were taught from him. For at that time some came
and asked him whether one must pay taxes to Caesar. And he answered: Tell me,
whose image does the coin have? And they said: Caesar's. And again he answered
them: Render therefore the things of Caesar to Caesar and the things of God to
God.
Hence we worship God alone, but in the other things we serve you with joy,
confessing you kings and rulers of men, and praying that you be found having,
with the kingly power, also a temperate reason. And if, even when we pray and
set all things into the open, you take no thought, we will be harmed in
nothing, believing, or rather also being persuaded, that each will pay
penalties through eternal fire according to the worth of the deeds, and
according to the proportion of the powers he received from God the account
will be demanded, as Christ made known, saying: To whom God gave more, more
also will be demanded from him.
For look toward the end of each of the kings who have been, that they died the
death common to all. Which, if it went into insensibility, would have been a
windfall for all the unjust. But since also sensation remains for all who have
come to be, and eternal punishment lies stored, do not neglect to be persuaded
and to believe that these things are true.
For necromancies and the inspections of uncorrupted children and the callings
of human souls and the so-called dream-senders and assistants among the magi
and the things that happen by those who know these things, let them persuade
you that even after death the souls are in sensation,

and the men who are taken and thrown by souls of the dead, whom all call
demon-possessed and mad, and the oracles said among you of Amphilochus and of
Dodona and of Pytho, and as many other such things as there are, and the
teachings of the writers, of Empedocles and Pythagoras, of Plato and Socrates,
and the pit in Homer and the descent of Odysseus toward the inspection of
these, and of those who have said the same things as these: by whom, even if
you receive us similarly, we believe God not less than those but more, we who
expect to receive again our own bodies, even those that are dead and thrown
into earth, saying that nothing is impossible for God.
And for one who considers, what would seem more unbelievable, than if we were
not in body and someone said that from a small drop of human seed it is
possible for bones and sinews and flesh, formed as we see, to come to be? For
let it now be said on a hypothesis. If someone said to you, not being such nor
of such, showing the human seed and a painted image, affirming that from such
a thing it is possible to come to be, would you have believed before seeing it
come to be? No one would dare to contradict.
In the same way, then, because you have not yet seen a dead man raised,
unbelief holds you. But in the way that in the beginning you would not have
believed it possible for such to come to be from the small drop, and you see
them coming to be, in the same way consider that it is not impossible for the
human bodies, dissolved and spread into earth like seeds, to be raised at the
time by the command of God and to put on incorruption.
For what power worthy of God they speak of, who claim that each goes into that
from which it came, and that besides these things nothing else can, not even
God, we cannot say. But this we see, that they would not have believed it
possible for such ever to come to be, of the sort they see themselves and the
whole world also generated from such things.
And we have received that it is better to believe even the things impossible
to their own nature and to men, than to disbelieve like the others, since we
have also known our teacher Jesus Christ saying: The things impossible with
men are possible with God. And: Do not fear those who kill you and after these
things are not able to do anything, he said, but fear the one who after dying
is able to throw both soul and body into gehenna. And gehenna is a place where
those who have lived unjustly and do not believe that these things will happen
which God taught through Christ are going to be punished.

And the Sibyl also and Hystaspes said there would be a consumption of
perishable things through fire. And the so-called Stoic philosophers teach
that even God himself is resolved into fire, and they say the world again
comes to be according to change. But we think the God who is Maker of all
something better than the things that change.
If therefore we say some things like the poets and philosophers honored among
you, and some things even more greatly and divinely and we alone with
demonstration, why are we hated unjustly beyond all? For by saying that all
things have been ordered and have come to be by God we will seem to say a
teaching of Plato. And by a conflagration coming to be, of the Stoics. And by
the souls of the unjust being punished in sensation even after death, and
those of the serious living well, released from the punishments, we will seem
to say the same things as poets and philosophers.
And by not needing to worship works of human hands we speak the same things as
Menander the comic and those who have said these things. For they declared the
Maker greater than the thing being crafted.
And by our claiming that the Word, which is the first offspring of God, was
begotten without mixing, Jesus Christ our teacher, and that this one, having
been crucified and having died and having risen, has gone up into heaven, we
bring nothing new compared with the so-called sons of Zeus among you.
For how many sons they claim of Zeus, the writers honored among you, you know:
Hermes, the interpreting word and teacher of all; and Asclepius, who also
became a healer, having been struck by lightning has gone up into heaven; and
Dionysus having been torn apart; and Heracles having given himself to fire in
flight from labors; and the Dioscuri from Leda; and Perseus from Danae; and
Bellerophon from men upon the horse Pegasus. For why do we speak of Ariadne
and those who are said similarly to have been set among the stars? And why of
the emperors who die among you, always thinking it right that they be made
immortal, and bringing forward someone who swears he has seen the burned
Caesar going up from the pyre into heaven?
And of what sort the deeds of each of the so-called sons of Zeus are related,
there is no need to say to those who know, except that these things have been
written for difference and exhortation of those being educated. For all think
it is fine to be imitators of gods. But far be such a thought concerning gods
from a temperate soul, as that even Zeus himself, the leader and begetter of
all according to them, became both a father-killer and of such a father, and,
having become weaker than love of evil and shameful pleasures,

came upon Ganymede and the many women who were committed adultery with, and
received his own children who did the like things. But, as we said before, the
worthless demons did these things. And we have been taught that only those who
live holily and virtuously near God are made immortal, and we believe that
those who live unjustly and do not change are punished in eternal fire.
And the Son of God, the one called Jesus, even if he were only a man in
common, would be worthy because of wisdom to be called Son of God. For all
writers call God the father of both men and gods. And if we say that he has
been begotten from God in a special way, beyond the common generation, as Word
of God, as we said before, let this be common to you who say that Hermes is
the word, the angelic one from God.

is significant of those men remaining even having died and being men of this
Christ. For also first of all men those were occupied about a seeking
concerning God, Abraham being father of Isaac, and Isaac of Jacob, as Moses
also wrote.
And that the demons worked also to raise the idol of the so-called Kore upon
the springs of the waters, saying she is a daughter of Zeus, imitating the
thing spoken through Moses, you can understand from the things previously
said. For Moses said, as we wrote before: In the beginning God made the heaven
and the earth. And the earth was unseen and unconstructed, and spirit of God
was borne upon the waters. In imitation, then, of the Spirit of God spoken as
borne upon the water they said Kore a daughter of Zeus. And Athena similarly,
acting wickedly, they said a daughter of Zeus,

not from mixing, but, since they knew that God, having thought, made the world
through Word, they said Athena as the first Thought. Which we think is most
laughable, to bring forward an image of the thought in a form of females. And
similarly the deeds refute the other so-called sons of Zeus.
And we, after thus washing the one who has been persuaded and has agreed, lead
him to the so-called brothers, where they are gathered, to make common prayers
both for ourselves and for the one who has been illuminated and for all others
everywhere intensely, so that we may be counted worthy, having learned the
true things and through works being found good citizens and guardians of the
things commanded, so that we may be saved the eternal salvation.
We greet one another with a kiss, having ceased from the prayers. Then there
is brought to the one who presides over the brothers bread and a cup of water
and mixed wine, and this one, having taken, sends up praise and glory to the
Father of the wholes through the name of the Son and of the holy Spirit, and
he makes thanksgiving at length for having been counted worthy of these things
from him. And when he has completed the prayers and the thanksgiving, all the
people present shout assent saying: Amen. And Amen in the Hebrew voice
signifies Let it be.
And when the one who presides has given thanks and all the people have shouted
assent, those called among us deacons give to each of those present to partake
from the bread and wine and water that have been thanked, and they carry away
to those not present.
And this food is called among us thanksgiving, of which it is permitted to no
one else to take a share except the one who believes the things taught by us
to be true, and who has been washed the washing for forgiveness of sins and
into regeneration, and who lives thus as Christ handed over.
For not as common bread nor common drink do we take these. But in the way that
through Word of God Jesus Christ our savior, having been made flesh, had both
flesh and blood for our salvation, so also the food that has been thanked
through a word of prayer from him, from which our blood and fleshes are
nourished according to change, we have been taught to be flesh and blood of
that Jesus who was made flesh.
For the apostles in the memoirs that came to be by them, which are called
gospels, handed over thus that they were commanded: Jesus, having taken bread,
having given thanks, said: Do this into my remembrance, this is my body. And
the cup likewise, having taken and having given thanks, said: This is my
blood. And he gave a share to them alone. Which also in

the mysteries of Mithras the wicked demons handed over to happen, having
imitated. For that bread and a cup of water are set in the rites of the one
being initiated, with certain additional words, you either know or can learn.
And we after these things thereafter always remind one another of these. And
those who have help all who are lacking, and we are always together with one
another. And upon all things we are offered we bless the Maker of all through
his Son Jesus Christ and through the holy Spirit.
And on the day said to be of the sun there is a gathering of all who remain
according to cities or fields to the same, and the memoirs of the apostles or
the writings of the prophets are read, as long as it is allowed. Then, when
the one who reads has ceased, the one who presides makes through word the
admonition and challenge toward imitation of these good things.
Then we stand up all in common and send prayers. And, as we said before, when
we have ceased from the prayer, bread is brought and wine and water, and the
one who presides similarly sends up prayers and thanksgivings, as much as
power is to him, and the people shout assent saying the Amen, and the
distribution and the partaking from the things that have been thanked happens
to each, and to those not present it is sent through the deacons.
And those who are well-resourced and who wish, each according to choice, give
what he wishes of his own, and the thing collected is deposited with the one
who presides, and he himself helps both orphans and widows, and those lacking
through disease or through another cause, and those who are in bonds, and the
strangers who are sojourning, and simply he becomes a caretaker for all who
are in need.
And on the day of the sun we all in common make the gathering, since it is the
first day, on which God, having turned the darkness and the matter, made a
world, and Jesus Christ our savior on the same day rose from the dead. For on
the one before the Kronos-day they crucified him, and on the one after the
Kronos-day, which is the day of the sun, having appeared to his apostles and
disciples he taught these things, which we have also given over to you for
inspection.
And if they seem to you to hold to reason and truth, honor them. And if they
seem to you nonsense, despise them as nonsense things, and do not define death
as against enemies against those who do no injustice. For we say beforehand to
you that you will not flee the judgment of God that will be, if you persist in
the injustice. And we will cry out: What is dear to God, let this come to be.
And from a letter also of the greatest and most manifest

Caesar Hadrian, your father, having to demand of you that you command the
judgments to come to be just as we asked, we did not ask this rather from its
having been judged by Hadrian, but from knowing that we ask just things we
have made the address and explanation. And we have also subjoined the copy of
the letter of Hadrian, so that you may also recognize by this that we speak
truly. And the copy is this:

Letter of Hadrian on behalf of Christians.

To Minucius Fundanus.

I received a letter written to me from Serenius Granianus, a most illustrious
man, whom you succeeded.

It does not seem to me, then, to leave the matter uninvestigated, so that
neither the men may be troubled and a supply of villainy be furnished to the
sycophants. If therefore the provincials are able to support this request
clearly against the Christians, so as also to answer before a tribunal, let
them turn to this only, but not to petitions nor to shouts alone. For it is
much more fitting, if someone should wish to accuse, that you decide this.

If therefore someone accuses and shows them doing something contrary to the
laws, thus determine according to the power of the offense. As, by Heracles,
if someone should put this forward for the sake of sycophancy, take it
according to the dreadfulness, and take thought how you might avenge.
