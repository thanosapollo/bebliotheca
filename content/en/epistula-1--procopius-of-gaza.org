You carry the Graces themselves truly upon the tongue, and you seem to me as
their precinct, and a temple is founded here somewhere for you and charms the
goddesses, a soul variously sounding under the tongue with a pure plectrum;
whence they rejoice at this more than seeing Naxos, which report also holds to
be an island of the Graces; where also Dionysus was overcome by Ariadne and
was a housemate. But Semele's [son] has come into our word at a good time;
who, if he were not a god, of how much accusation would he have enjoyed from
us, having overlooked a man poor and from the very mattresses dreaming the
taxes, but otherwise given over to Muses and Graces. He had the little plot to
a small thing, O Dionysus, and he hung upon the hopes from there, waiting to
take this, which he seemed to give to the vines; why did you not come on in
bulk and show them heavy with fruit? Would that you had also gone upon the
vats, and danced a bacchic dance, and nothing of the must had been empty;
whence having wealth well, he would have danced from pleasure and been more
musical. Do you wish to learn also what Dionysus says toward these things?
that "it is not possible to be rich in words and in money together; whence,"
he says, "I withdrew the clusters, that I might rather train his tongue; since
also the cicadas, being fed by dew alone, have room in themselves for the
whole Muses, and you would say hearing that they were men and have become
cicadas". Therefore it is fitting to be content with the present, and even if
the collectors annoy, only sing to them, and they will immediately forget the
money.
