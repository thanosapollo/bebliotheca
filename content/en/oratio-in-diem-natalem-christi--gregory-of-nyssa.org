OF GREGORY BISHOP OF NYSSA ON THE NATIVITY OF THE SAVIOR

1128 Blow with a trumpet in the new moon, says David, in the well-marked day
of our feast. And the commands of the God-inspired teaching are altogether a
law for those who hear. Therefore since the well-marked day of our feast is
present, let us also fulfill the law, becoming trumpeters of the sacred month.
And the trumpet of the law, as the apostle commands us to understand, is the
word. For he says that the sound of the trumpet must not become indistinct,
but distinguished in its notes toward the clearness of those who hear.
Therefore let us also sound, brothers, some bright and far-heard sound, in
nothing more dishonorable than the horn. For the law also, which in the types
of the shadow pre-sketches the truth, legislated the sound from the trumpets
at the Feast of Tabernacles. And the present subject of the feast is the
mystery of the true Feast of Tabernacles. For in this the human tabernacle is
pitched for him who for our sake put on the human being. In this our
tabernacles that had fallen apart under death are again compacted by him who
from the beginning built our dwelling. Let us also say the word of the
psalmody, dancing with the loud-voiced David, that Blessed is he who comes in
the name of the Lord. How coming? Not as through some ship or vehicle, but
having crossed over to human life through virginal incorruption. Thus our God,
thus the Lord has appeared to us, to establish this feast among those who are
decked even to the horns of the altar.

We are not altogether ignorant, brothers, of the mystery in the things said,
that all creation is one palace of the Master of creation. But since, when sin
entered, the mouths of those mastered by wickedness were shut 1129 and the
voice of exultation was silenced and the symphony of those keeping feast was
torn apart, the human creation not keeping feast together with the nature
above the world, for this reason the trumpets of the prophets and apostles
came, which the law calls horns because their fashioning is from the true
unicorn, which according to the power of the Spirit sounded the taut word of
truth, so that, the hearing of those stopped up by sin having been opened,
there might come to be one concordant feast through the decking of the
tabernacle-feast of the creation below sounding together toward the powers
that stand out and project about the altar above. For the horns of the
intelligible altar are the projecting and outstanding powers of the noetic
nature, both principalities and authorities and thrones and lordships, to
which by the communion of the feast human nature is joined through the
tabernacle-feast according to the resurrection, being decked by the renewal of
the bodies.

For to be decked is equal to being adorned or being clothed, as those who know
these things interpret. Come then, having stirred our souls toward the
spiritual dance, let us set David as leader and guide and chorus-master of our
choral standing, and let us say with him that sweet voice which we hymned just
now. And let us take it up again, that This is the day which the Lord has
made; let us exult and be glad in it, in which the darkness begins to be
lessened and the measures of the night are crowded toward eclipse by the
increasing of the ray. It was no chance thing, brothers, that came about of
itself, this economy concerning the feast, that the divine life should now
appear to human life. But some mystery through the things that appear is
narrated to those more clear-sighted by the


creation, all but sending forth a voice and teaching him who is able to hear,
what the day increasing and the night being shortened wants in the presence
of the Master. For I seem to myself to hear creation going through some such
things, that seeing these, O human, understand what is shown to you as hidden
through the things that appear. Do you see the night having gone forward to
the utmost length and standing from its onward course and turning back to the
reverse? Understand that the wicked night of sin, having increased as far as
it was possible and having reached through every invention of evils to the
utmost magnitude of wickedness, today has been checked from further grazing,
and from this point is driven together toward eclipse and disappearance. Do
you see the ray of light more lasting and the sun higher than custom?
Understand the presence of the true light, which with evangelical rays lights
up the whole inhabited world. And perhaps also of the Lord's not appearing at
the beginnings but in the last of the times granting to human life the
manifestation of his Godhead, one might reasonably suspect this as the cause,
that he who was about to be mixed with human life for the overthrow of
wickedness necessarily waited for all 1132 the wickedness that had been
rooted by the enemy to sprout up, and then thus brought, as the gospel says,
the axe to the root.

For even among physicians those who attend to the art, while the fever is
still smoldering the body from within and being kindled little by little by
the disease-making causes, yield to the illness until the passion comes to its
peak, bringing no help from foods to the sick one. But when the evil becomes
stationary, then they apply the art, the whole disease having been made
manifest. Thus also he who heals those who are badly off in soul waited for
the disease from wickedness, by which the nature of human beings was mastered,
to be wholly uncovered, so that none of the hidden things might remain
untreated, if the physician treated only what had appeared. For this reason he
does not, in the times of Noah when all flesh had been corrupted in injustice,
bring the healing through his own manifestation, because the shoot of
Sodomitic wickedness had not yet sprouted, nor at the time of the overthrow of
Sodom does the Lord appear, because many of the remaining evils were still
hidden in human nature. For where was the God-fighting Pharaoh? Where the
untamed wickedness of the Egyptians? Nor even then was it timely for the
corrector of the all, I mean at the Egyptian evils, to be mixed with life. But
the lawlessness of the Israelites also had to appear; the kingdom of the
Assyrians and the arrogance of Nebuchadnezzar, still smoldering in life, had
to become manifest; the murder of the holy ones had to run up as some wicked
and thorny shoot of the evil root of the devil; the frenzy of the Jews against
the saints of God had to be made manifest, of those who kill the prophets and
stone those sent and at last work the pollution concerning Zachariah between
the temple and the altar. Add to the catalog of the wicked sprouts also the
child-murder of Herod. When therefore all the power of wickedness had been
given forth from the wicked root and had grown in many forms in the choices of
those known for wickedness in each generation, running wild, then, as Paul
says to the Athenians, God having overlooked the times of ignorance arrives in
the last days, when there was none who understands and seeks out God, when all
turned aside, they became unprofitable together, when all things were shut up
into sin, when lawlessness abounded, when the gloom of wickedness increased to
the utmost measure, then grace appeared, then the ray of the true light rose
again, then the sun of righteousness dawned upon those sitting in darkness and
the shadow of death, then he crushed the many heads of the dragon, having set
his foot through human flesh and having crushed them against the earth and
trampled them.

And let no one, looking toward the


evils now in life, think the word to be lying, according to which we said
that in the last times the Lord shone upon life. For the one contradicting
will perhaps say that he who waited the times toward the manifestation of
wickedness, so that having increased it he might take it out by the roots,
has reasonably destroyed it all, so that no remnant of it be left in life.
But now both murders 1133 and thefts and adulteries and all the most wicked
things are dared. But let him who looks toward this dissolve the doubt about
these things by some example of things known. For as in the destruction of
reptiles one may see that the trailing part behind is not at once dead
together with the head, but the one has died, while the <tail> is still
animated with its own rage, and has not been deprived of vital movement, thus
also the destroyer of the dragon, when the beast had become great, grown
together with all the generations of human beings one by one, having taken
away the head, that is the inventive power of evils which has many heads in
itself, made no account of the trailing part behind, having caused the
movement in the dead beast to be left behind as an occasion of exercise for
those after. What then is the crushed head? That which brought in death by
the wicked counsel, that which put the death-bearing venom into the human
being through the bite. He therefore who dissolved the power of death crushed
the power in the head of the serpent, as the prophet says. But the remaining
trail of the beast, sown together with human life, so long as the human is in
the movements according to wickedness, always roughens life with the scale of
sin. In power it is already dead, the head having been made useless. But when
the time has passed and the things that move stand still according to the
expected consummation of this life, then both the tail and the last of the
enemy is abolished (and this is death) and thus the complete disappearance of
wickedness will come to be, all having been called back to life through the
resurrection, the righteous being at once transferred to the portion above,
and those held in sins being purged by the fire of Gehenna.

But let us return to the present joy, which the angels announce as good news
to the shepherds, which the heavens narrate to the magi, which the spirit of
prophecy proclaims through many and various things, so that even the magi
became heralds of the grace.

[ For he who makes the sun rise upon righteous and unrighteous, who rains upon
wicked and good, brought the ray of knowledge and the dew of the Spirit even
into alien mouths, so that by the witness from the opponents the truth might
rather be confirmed in us. You hear the soothsayer Balaam, by a better
inspiration prophesying to the foreigners, that A star shall rise out of
Jacob? You see the magi who draw their race from him watching according to the
prediction of the forefather for the rising of the new star, which alone,
contrary to the nature of the remaining stars, both partook of movement and of
standing, taking each of these according to the need? For of the remaining
stars, some having been fixed once for all in the unwandering sphere and
having been allotted an unmoved standing, and others never ceasing from
movement, this both moves going before the magi and stands announcing the
place. You hear Isaiah crying that A child has been born to us, a son also has
been given to us? 1136 Learn from the prophet himself how the child was born,
how a son was given. Was it according to the law of nature? No, says the
prophet, the Master of nature is not a slave to the laws of nature. But how
was the child born, tell. Behold, he says, the virgin shall conceive in the
womb and shall bear a son and they shall call his name Emmanuel, which is
interpreted, God with us.

O the wonder, the virgin becomes a mother and remains a virgin. Do you see the
innovation of nature? For in the case of other women, so long as she is a
virgin, she is not a mother. And when she becomes a mother, she does not have
the virginity. But here the two


names ran together at the same time. For the same is both mother and virgin.
And neither did the virginity hinder the birth, nor did the birth loose the
virginity. For it was fitting that he who came to be in human life for the
incorruption of the all should begin from the incorruption that served his
birth. For the custom of human beings knows to name the unwedded one
incorrupt. This seems to me that great Moses had understood beforehand in the
theophany that came to him through the light, when the fire was kindled of
the bush and the bush was not withered. For I will cross over, he says, and
see this great sight. Not indicating, I think, a local movement through the
crossing, but the passing-through of time. For what was then prefigured in
the flame and the bush, the middle time having crossed, was clearly unveiled
in the mystery concerning the virgin. For as there the shrub both kindles the
fire and is not burned, so also here the virgin both bears the light and is
not corrupted.

And if a bush prefigures the God-bearing body of the virgin, do not be ashamed
at the riddle. For all flesh, through the reception of sin, in this very
thing, that it is flesh only, is sin. And sin is named by the Scripture with
the appellation of the thorn. And if we are not carried far from the things
set before us, perhaps it is not untimely to present also Zachariah, who was
murdered between the temple and the altar, as a witness of the incorrupt
mother. This Zachariah was a priest, and not a priest only, but also in the
gift of prophecy. And the power of the prophecy is proclaimed as incontestable
in the book of the Gospel. When the divine grace, making a way before for
human beings that they not think the birth from a virgin unbelievable, trains
beforehand the assent of the unbelieving by lesser wonders, a child is born to
the barren and over-aged. This becomes a prelude of the wonder concerning
virginity. For as Elizabeth does not become a mother by a power of nature,
having grown old through life childless, but the birth of the child is
referred to the divine will, so also the unbelief of the virginal labor has
its credibility by the referring to the divine. Since therefore the one from
the barren woman precedes the one from virginity, he who at the voice of her
who was carrying the Lord, before coming forth into light, leaped in the
maternal womb, as soon as the forerunner of the Word passed into coming-to-be,
then the silence is loosed for Zachariah through the prophetic 1137
inspiration. And whatever Zachariah goes through was prophecy of what was to
come. This man therefore, being led by the prophetic spirit toward the
knowledge of hidden things, having understood the mystery of virginity at the
incorrupt birth, did not separate in the temple the unwedded mother from the
place allotted by the law to the virgins, teaching the Jews that the craftsman
of the things that are and king of all creation has human nature, with all
things, subject to himself, leading it by his own will toward what seems good,
not himself being dominated by it, so that it is in his power to craft a new
birth, which will not take away from the one who became a mother that she is a
virgin. For this reason he did not separate her in the temple from the chorus
of the virgins. And this place was the interval between the temple and the
altar. When therefore they heard that the king of creation had come forth
economically into a human birth, from fear of not becoming subject to a king
they slay the one who witnesses these things about the birth, sacrificing the
priest at the altar itself. But we have wandered far from the things set
before us, when we ought to run back with the word to Bethlehem in the Gospel.

For if we are truly shepherds and keep watch over our own flocks, the voice of
the angels that announces as good news this great joy is altogether toward us.
Therefore let us look up to the heavenly army, let us see the choral standing
of the angels, let us hear their divine hymnody. What is the sound of those
keeping feast? Glory in the highest to God they cry. Why does the voice of the
angels glorify


the Godhead contemplated in the highest? Because, it says, also on earth
peace. The angels have become overjoyed at what appears. On earth peace. She
who was formerly cursed, the productive of thorns and thistles, the place of
war, the exile of the condemned, this one has received peace. O the wonder,
truth has sprung from the earth. Such fruit has the earth of human beings
given, and these things come to be for the good pleasure among human beings.
God is mixed with human nature, that the human may be lifted together to the
height of God. Having heard these things let us pass through to Bethlehem,
let us see the new spectacle, how the virgin exults at the birth, how the
unwedded one nurses the infant. But first who this woman is and from where,
let us hear from those who narrate the things concerning her.

I heard then some hidden history setting forth such narratives concerning her.
There was a man notable in the exact way of life according to the law and
known for the fairest things, the father of the virgin, but he grew old
through life childless, because his wife was not fit toward childbearing. And
there was some honor from the law for mothers, of which the barren did not
partake. Therefore this woman also imitates the narratives concerning the
mother of Samuel. And having come within the holy of holies she becomes a
suppliant of God not to fall outside the blessing from the laws, having failed
in nothing concerning 1140 the law, but to become a mother and to dedicate to
God the one being born. And having been strengthened by a divine nod toward
the grace which she asked, when the child was born, to name her Mary, so that
even through the appellation the God-given of the grace might be signified.
And when the girl had already grown, so as no longer to need to attend to the
breast, to give her back to God and fulfill the promise and bring her to the
temple. And the priests for a time, after the likeness of Samuel, rear the
child in the holy places, but when she had grown they bring a counsel, what
they might do so as not to sin against God concerning that sacred body. For to
yoke her to the law of nature and enslave her through marriage to the one who
takes her seemed one of the most out-of-place things. For it was thought
outright temple-robbery that a human being become lord of a divine offering,
since the man was appointed from the laws to be lord of the woman who dwells
with him. And for a woman to be together with the priests within the palaces
and to be seen in the holy places was neither lawful and at the same time the
solemn did not attach to the deed. And as they were counseling about these
things a counsel comes from God to give her to a man in the name of betrothal,
and that this man be such as to be fit toward the guarding of her virginity.
Joseph then was found, such as the word was seeking, from the same tribe and
family as the virgin. And he betroths the girl to himself according to the
counsel of the priests. And the joining was as far as betrothal.

Then the virgin is initiated by Gabriel. And the words of the initiation were
a blessing. Hail, he says, you who have been graced, the Lord is with you.
Opposite to the first voice toward the woman the word now comes to be toward
the virgin. That one was condemned to pains in the labors through the sin, but
upon this one the pain is cast out through the joy. Upon that one the pains of
labor led the way, but here joy midwifes the labor. Fear not, he says. Since
the expectation of labor furnishes fear to every woman, the promise of the
sweet labor casts out the fear. You shall conceive in the womb, he says, and
bear a son and you shall call his name Jesus. What then does Mary? Hear the
voice of a pure virgin. The angel announces the birth as good news, and she
clings to the virginity, judging the incorruption more honorable than the
angelic appearance, and she is able neither to disbelieve the angel nor to go
out from the things decided. Intercourse with a man, she says, has been
forbidden me. How shall this be to me, since I do not know a man? This voice
of Mary is a proof of the things narrated according to the hidden. For if she
had been taken by Joseph for marriage, how would she have been astonished at
the one who was announcing the birth beforehand to her, herself also
altogether and in every way expecting to become a mother according to the law
of nature? But since


the flesh dedicated to God had to be kept untouched as some of the holy
offerings, for this reason, even if you are an angel, she says, even if you
come from heaven, 1141 even if what appears is above a human being, yet for
me to know a man is among the impossible things. How shall I be a mother
without a man? For Joseph I know as a betrothed, but a man I do not know.
What then does the bridal-escort Gabriel? What sort of bridal chamber does he
present for the pure and undefiled marriage? The Holy Spirit, he says, shall
come upon you and the power of the Highest shall overshadow you. O that
blessed flesh, which through excess of purity drew to itself the goods of the
soul. For in the case of all others a pure soul would scarcely receive the
presence of the Holy Spirit. But here the flesh becomes a receptacle of the
Spirit. But also the power of the Highest shall overshadow you. What does the
word signify through unspeakable things? Christ is the power of God and the
wisdom of God, says the apostle. Therefore the power of the most high God,
which is Christ, through the coming of the Holy Spirit is formed in the
virginity. For as the shadow of bodies is shaped together with the type of
the things going before, so the character and the marks of the Godhead of the
Son will show through in the power of the one being born, being shown as
image and seal and shadowing and radiance of the prototype through the
wonder-working of the operations.

But the angelic good news urges us to run back with the word to Bethlehem and
to look down upon the mysteries in the cave. What is this? A child wrapped in
swaddling-clothes and resting upon a manger, and the virgin after birth, the
incorrupt mother, tending the offspring. Let us shepherds say the voice of the
prophet, that just as we heard so also we saw in the city of the Lord of
powers, in the city of our God. Did these things come about for Christ by
chance according to what happened, and have they been narrated, and is there
no account over the history? What does the lodging in a cave want for the
Master, the lying-down upon a manger, the being mixed with life at the time of
the registration of the taxes? Or is it clear that as he takes us out of the
legal curse, himself becoming a curse for us, and transfers our bruises onto
himself, that by his bruise we might be healed, so also he comes to be in tax,
that he might free us from the wicked tributes, to which the human was
subject, being taxed by death? And the cave in which the Master is born,
understand as the unlit and underground life of human beings, in which he
comes to be who appears to those sitting in darkness and the shadow of death.
And he is bound with swaddling-clothes who puts around himself the cords of
our sins. And the manger is the dwelling-place of the irrational, in which the
Word comes to be, that the ox may know his owner and the ass the manger of his
lord, the ox being the one yoked to the law, the ass the burden-bearing
animal, loaded with the sin of idolatry. But the suitable food of the
irrational animals is grass. For he makes grass to spring for the cattle, says
the prophet. But the rational animal is fed with bread. For this reason
therefore to the manger, which is 1144 the hearth of the irrational, the bread
that came down from heaven is added, that the irrational also, having partaken
of the rational food, may come to be in reason. Therefore the Lord of both
mediates at the manger for the ox and the ass, that having dissolved the
middle wall of the fence he may create the two in himself into one new human
being, and having taken off from this one the heavy yoke of the law, and
having unburdened that one of the load of idolatry.

But let us look up to the heavenly wonders. For behold not prophets only and
angels announce this joy to us as good news, but also the heavens through
their own wonders proclaim the glory of the gospel. From Judah Christ rises
for us, as the apostle says, but the Jew is not lit up by the one who has
risen. The magi are strangers of the covenants of the promise and alien to the
blessing of the fathers, but they anticipate in knowledge the Israelite people
and


having recognized the heavenly luminary and not having been ignorant of the
king in the cave. Those bring gifts, these plot. Those worship, these
persecute. Those, having found the one sought, rejoice, these are troubled at
the birth of the one announced. For the magi, it says, having seen the star
upon the place where the child was, rejoiced with exceeding great joy. But
Herod having heard the word was troubled and all Jerusalem with him. The ones
as to God bring frankincense and greet the royal dignity with the gold, and
they signify the economy according to the passion by some prophetic grace
through the myrrh. But the others condemn all the youth to utter destruction.
Which seems to me to have the accusation not of bitterness only but also of
their utmost folly. For what does the child-murder want for them, and toward
what aim has such a pollution been dared by the murderers? Since some new
sign, he says, of the wonders according to heaven announced to the magi the
showing-forth of the king, what then? Do you trust the announcing sign as
true, or do you suspect the thing being rumored as vain? For if he is such as
to dispose the heavens together with himself, he is altogether above your
hand. And if it lies upon you to make him live or die, you have feared such a
one in vain. For he who so acts as to be subject to your authority, for what
is he plotted against? Why is that dreadful command sent down, the wicked
vote against the infants, that the wretched babes be taken away, having done
what injustice? Having furnished what cause of death and punishment against
themselves, having one charge only, that they were born and came into light?
And for these things it was necessary that the city be filled with
executioners and a people of mothers be gathered and a people of infants,
their begetters being present with them, and all who share the race, as is
likely, being assembled at the suffering?

Who could sketch the disasters with the word? Who could bring the passions
before the sight through the narrations, that mixed lament, the wailing
concert 1145 of children, mothers, fathers, kinsmen crying pitiably toward the
threat of the executioners? How could one describe the executioner standing by
the infant with bare sword, looking sharp and murderous and uttering other
such things and drawing the babe to himself with the hand and stretching the
sword with the other? And the mother from the other side drawing the child
back to herself and holding out her own neck to the edge of the sword, that
she might not see with her eyes the wretched child being destroyed in the
hands of the executioner; how could one narrate out the things of the fathers,
the callings-back, the wailings, the last embracings of the children, and many
such things happening at once at the same time? Who could make a tragedy of
the many-formed and many-turned disaster, the double labors of the newly
delivered, the sharp burnings of nature, how the miserable babe both clung to
the breast and received the death-blow through the inward parts, how the
wretched mother both held the nipple to the mouth of the infant and received
the blood of the child in her bosom? And often somewhere by the rush of the
hand the executioner with one impulse of the sword drove through the child
together with the mother and one stream of the blood came to be, mixed both
from the maternal wound and from the death-blow of the child. And since this
too is added to the defiled voice of Herod, that not only was the
death-bearing vote brought out against the newly born, but if anyone had also
come forward to a second year this one too was to be snatched away, for it is
written, from two years old and under, the word sees in these another
suffering according to likelihood, that often the time between made the same
woman mother of two children. What then again was the spectacle in such cases,
two executioners being occupied about one mother, the one drawing to himself
the one running beside, the other tearing the suckling from the bosom? What is
the wretched mother likely to suffer at these things, nature being split
toward two children, each equally kindling the fire in the maternal inward
parts, not having, which or whom of the wicked executioners she will follow,
the one from here, the other


from the other side drawing the infants toward the slaughter? Is she to run
to the newborn still sending forth a markless and inarticulate wail? But she
hears the other already speaking and with a lisping voice calling the mother
with tears. What is she to suffer? What is she to become? To whose voice will
she cry in answer?

To whose wailing will she wail in return? Which death will she mourn, perhaps
being whipped at each by the goads of nature? But let us lead the hearing away
from the laments over the children and turn the mind toward the more cheerful
things and those more fitting to the feast, even if Rachel according to the
prophecy cries over the slaughter of the children. For on a day of feast, as
the wise Solomon says, a forgetting of evils is fitting. And what could become
more well-marked for us than this feast, in which the sun of righteousness,
having scattered the wicked gloom of the devil, shines upon the nature through
our own nature itself, in which the fallen 1148 is raised, the one that had
been made war is brought into reconciliations, the one that had been disowned
is led back, the one that had fallen from life returns into life, the one
enslaved to captivity is taken up again into the dignity of the kingdom, the
one bound with the bonds of death runs back free toward the land of the
living? Now according to the prophecy the bronze gates of death are crushed,
the iron bars are broken, by which the human race was formerly shut in the
prison of death. Now is opened, as David says, the gate of righteousness. Now
a concordant sound of those keeping feast is heard throughout all the
inhabited world. Through a human being death, through a human being the
salvation. The first fell into sin, the second set the fallen one upright. The
woman has made defense for the woman. The first gave the entrance to sin, but
this one served the entrance of righteousness. That one drew upon herself the
counsel of the serpent, this one presented the destroyer of the serpent. That
one through the wood brought in the sin, this one through the wood brought in
the good in return. For the wood was the cross. And the fruit of this wood
becomes ever-living and unfading life for those who taste. And let no one
suppose that such thanksgiving is fitting to the mystery according to the
Pascha only. For let him reckon that the Pascha is the end of the economy. And
how would the end have come to be, if the beginning had not led the way? What
is more originating of what? The birth, clearly, of the economy according to
the passion. Therefore the goods of the Pascha also are a part of the praises
concerning the birth, whether one catalogs the benefactions of the things
narrated in the Gospels or goes through the wonders according to the healings,
the food from things without resource, the return of the dead from the tombs,
the improvised farming of the wine, the flight of the demons, the change of
the various sufferings into health, the leapings of the lame, the eyes from
clay, the divine teachings, the legislations, the initiation through the

For this began the goods that follow. Therefore let us exult and be glad in
it, not fearing the reproach of human beings and not being defeated by their
contempt, as the prophet charges, who mock the word of the economy, as if it
were not seemly that the Lord should come under the nature of a body and mix
himself through birth with human life. For you are not altogether ignorant of
the mystery concerning this, how the wisdom of God economized the salvation
for us. We were sold willingly to the sins, we were enslaved after the manner
of those bought with silver to the enemy of our life.

What was most according to your heart to come to be for you from the Master?
Was it not to be taken out of the disaster? Why do you over-busy yourself
about the manner? Why do you legislate to the benefactor the form of the
benefaction, as if someone even thrusting away the physician should blame him
for the well-doing, because he worked the healing 1149 not thus but otherwise?
And if you seek the account of the economy


from love of busyness, it is enough for you to learn this much, that the
divine does not have one only of the goods, but everything that is good
according to conception, that it is, powerful, just, good, wise, all whatever
names and thoughts belong to the God-befitting signification. Consider then
whether all the things said did not run together about what came to be for
us. The goodness, the wisdom, the justice, the power. As good he loved the
apostate. As wise he devised the invention of the return of those who had
been enslaved. As just he does not force the one who enslaved, who justly
acquired by purchase, but he gives himself as an exchange in place of those
who have been mastered, that as some surety transferring the debt onto
himself he may free from those who hold the one who is held. As powerful he
was not held in Hades nor did his flesh see corruption. For it was not
possible that the originator of life be held by corruption. But is it
shameful to come into human coming-to-be and to undergo the trial of the
passions of the flesh? You are naming the excess of the benefaction. For
since otherwise the human could not be taken out of the evils, the king of
all impassibility endured to exchange his own glory for our life. And the
purity comes to be in our filth, but the filth does not attach to the purity,
as the Gospel says, that the light shone in the darkness, and the darkness
did not overtake it. For the gloom disappears at the presence of the ray, the
sun is not dimmed by the gloom. The mortal is swallowed by the life, as the
apostle says, the life is not spent out in the death. The corrupted is saved
together with the incorrupt, but the corruption does not attach to the
incorruption. For these reasons a common concert of all creation comes to be,
all sending up a concordant doxology to the Master of creation, every tongue
of heavenly and earthly and subterranean crying that the Lord Jesus Christ,
to the glory of God the Father, is blessed unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
