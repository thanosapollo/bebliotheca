HOMILY I
ON THE GENERATION OF MAN AND ON ACCORDING TO THE IMAGE AND
ACCORDING TO LIKENESS

I have come to discharge an old debt, whose repayment I postponed not by
ingratitude of choice but by weakness of body, a most necessary debt and one
owed to your hearing. For it would indeed be unjust that we who have been
taught the things concerning beasts and swimming things and birds and cattle,
and heaven and the things in heaven, and concerning earth and the things in
the earth, should not also be enlightened from the God-inspired 3 Scripture
concerning our own generation. For just as our eyes, looking at the things
outside, do not see themselves, unless they happen upon something smooth and
solid and from there the sight, being reflected as from a reflux, makes them
see their own things behind them, so also our mind does not otherwise see
itself, unless it stoops into the Scriptures. For the light here, being
reflected, becomes the cause of each of us being seen, since we are without
understanding of ourselves and without survey of the constitution, not knowing
what we are and who we are. For we are most neglectful of ourselves, we who
have no knowledge of the things ready at hand for knowledge and of the
smallest things in us.

Many treatises concerning our human body have spent all their 4 zeal, and if
you go upon medicine, you will find how much it recounts to us concerning the
need of our <members>, how many hidden ways it discovered concerning our
inward constitution in the anatomical operations, bored through in the unseen
into one common breathing from the body: channels of breath, water-courses of
blood, the drawing of respiration, the dwelling of the hearth of the heat upon
the heart, the continuous motion of the pericardial spirit. Myriad things have
been philosophized among them, of which none of us is experienced, by having
assigned no leisure to this part of the inquiry, nor does each know himself,
who he is. We are ready to know heaven rather than ourselves. So do not
despise the wonder in you; for you are small, 5 as you think, but the account
coming upon you will find you great. For this reason wise David, knowing how
to examine himself accurately, says, Your knowledge was made wonderful from
me. I discovered the knowledge concerning you wonderfully. From where? From
me. Your knowledge was made wonderful from me; and considering the art in me,
with how much wisdom my body was constructed, from this small construction I
conceived the great craftsman.

Let us make man according to our image and likeness. It was shown recently in
passing, and sufficiently shown, whose the word is and to whom the word is.
The Church has the proofs concerning these things, or rather it has a faith
more firm than proof.

Let us make man. Learn yourself beginning from here. Not yet concerning
another of the constructions is this voice recorded. Light came to be, and a
simple command 6. God said, Let there be light. Heaven, and heaven without
counsel; luminaries, and he took no counsel beforehand concerning the
luminaries; sea and infinite deeps, and by command they were brought into
being. Kinds of fishes of every sort, being commanded, came to be, beasts and
cattle, swimming things and birds; He spoke and they came to be. Here not yet
man, and there is counsel concerning man. He did not say as concerning the
others, Let there be man. Learn the honor of yourself. He did not toss your
generation to a command, but there is a council in God how the honored living
being is about to be brought into life. Let us make; the wise one takes
counsel, the craftsman considers. Does something perhaps lack of his art, and
from the care does he wish to make his work of art complete and perfect and


exact? Or in order to show you that you are perfect with God? You learned
that there are two persons, the one speaking and the one to whom the word is.
Why did he not say, I will make, but, Let us make man? So that 7 you may
understand <equal> lordship, so that recognizing the Father you may not deny
the Son, so that you may know that the Father made through the Son and the
Son created by the paternal will, and you may glorify the Father in the Son
and the Son in the holy Father. Thus you have become a common work, so that
you may be a common worshiper of both, not splitting the worship but uniting
the Godhead. See a history in form, but theology in power. And God made man.

Let us make; and he did not say, and they made, so that you may not take
occasions of polytheism. For if the person had been introduced as plural, men
would have become unsparing, heaping up for themselves many multitudes of
gods. Now however: Let us make, so that you may know Father and Son and Holy
Spirit; God made man, so that you may unite the Godhead, but you will unite
not the hypostases but 8 the power, so that you may have one glory, not being
divided about the worship, not being divided into polytheism. He did not say,
the gods made man, but, God made. A proper hypostasis of the Father and a
proper of the Son and a proper of the Holy Spirit. Why then not three gods?
Because there is one Godhead; for the Godhead which I see in the Father, this
also in the Son, and that which is in the Holy Spirit, this also in the Son;
because there is one form in each and the beginning from the Father in the Son
is the same. For this reason also from us there is one worship and doxology. A
true theology is the prelude of our generation. Let us make man according to
our image and likeness. We have come to be according to the image of God. How
then according to the image of God? Let us cleanse an uninstructed heart, an
uneducated preconception, ignorant assumptions about God. If we have come to
be according to the image of God, he says, is God of like form with us? Eyes
about God 9 and ears, a head, hands, a haunch upon which he is seated (for it
is said in Scripture that God sits), feet through which he walks? Is God then
such? Put away from the heart unseemly imaginings. Cast out from yourself a
thought that does not fit the greatness of God. God is without shape, simple;
do not imagine a form about him. Do not diminish the Great in Jewish fashion,
do not encompass God with bodily thoughts, do not circumscribe him with your
mind. He is ungraspable in greatness. Consider the Great and to the great add
more than what has been thought, and to the more the still more, and persuade
your own reasoning that it will not reach the boundless. Do not think a shape;
from 10 power God is conceived. How great a magnitude in simplicity of nature?
He is everywhere and overflows beyond the all and is intangible, unbounded,
falling beyond your grasp; he is neither limited by magnitude nor encompassed
by shape nor measured by power nor squeezed by times nor defined by limits.
There is nothing about God such as about us.

How then did Scripture say that we have come to be according to the image of
God? Let us be taught the things about God, let us understand the things about
ourselves, that we do not have the According to the image in the form of the
body. For the form of a perishing body, and the immortal is not shaped in the
perishable; the perishable is not an image of the immortal. The body grows,
diminishes, ages, is altered; it is one thing in youth and another in old age,
another when it is in good condition and another when 11 it is in sufferings,
another fearing and another rejoicing, another in want and another in
fullness, another in peace and another in battle. There is one color of one
awake and another of one sleeping; of the one the ruddier blooms as the heat
goes toward the outside, of the other the heat has been cooled toward the
depth. Therefore also the bodies of those sleeping are somewhat pale. How then
can the changing be like the unchanging? That which never stands still, like
that which always has itself in the same things? It escapes us like flowing
things; before being seen it has slipped under, the human body appears as one
thing from another. According to our image. The flowing image of the unmoved
nature? The formed of the formless? How then shall we seek the According to
the image? In the things which the Lord himself said 12. If I say something of
my own, do not


receive it; if it is of the Master, accept it. Let us make man according to
our image and according to likeness and let them rule the fishes. With body
or with reasoning? Is the ruling in soul or in flesh? The flesh is weaker
than many animals. What comparison of the flesh of a man and a camel, of a
man and an ox, of a man and each beast you may wish? The flesh of man is
easily caught when it strikes against the flesh of a beast. But in what is
the ruling? In the abundance of reasoning. As much as is lacking in the power
of the body, so much is present in the constitution of reasoning. Whence does
man shift great burdens, by invention or by bodily strains?

Let us make man according to our image. He says Let us make man, meaning the
inner man. But you will say: why does he not speak to us about reasoning? He
said man according to 13 the image of God; but is reasoning man? Hear the
apostle saying: Even if our outer man is being destroyed, yet the inner is
being renewed day by day. How? I recognize two men, one the appearing and one
the hidden, invisible to the appearing, the inner man. We have then an inner
man and we are in a way twofold. And indeed what is said is true, that we are
within; for I am according to the inner man; the outer things are not I but
mine. For the hand is not I, but I am the rational of the soul, and the hand
is a part of the human being. So that the body is an instrument of the human
being, an instrument of the soul, but a human being is properly according to
the soul itself. Let us make man according to our image, that is, we will give
him an abundance of reason. And let them rule. Not: 14 Let us make man
according to our image, and let them be angry and desire and be grieved; for
the passions were not taken into the image of God, but the reasoning which is
master of the passions. And let them rule the fishes. You were created at once
and you were created a ruler. And let them rule. Having received authority of
one year from a king, a man from a man, a mortal from a mortal, having
received from the one who does not have, for what authority of soul does a man
receive? You however received from God, not written on woods nor on perishable
tablets, consumptions of moths, but nature has the divine voice inscribed. Let
them rule; all these things belong to human rule. Let them rule the fishes,
the beasts of the earth, the birds of heaven, the cattle, the reptiles that 15
creep upon the earth. He did not say: Let us make man according to our image
and according to likeness, and let them eat every fruit-bearing tree which has
fruit in itself. Second are the things of the flesh, first the things of the
soul, the leading things. First the power of ruling was voted together with
us. You are a ruling living being, O man, and why do you serve passions? Why
do you cast down your own dignity and become a slave of sin? Why do you make
yourself a captive of the devil? You were appointed ruler of creation, and
have you thrown away the dignity of your own nature? You were called a slave?
Why does the slavery of the body grieve you? Why do you not think greatly of
the lordship given you by God, that you have reasoning as master of passions?
When you see your master being a slave of pleasure, and yourself temperate,
know that you are a slave in name only, but that one has the lordship in name,
and in deed has the slavery confirmed. You see that one being dragged with the
harlot, and yourself despising the harlot; how are you not master of the
passion, and that one a slave of the pleasures trampled by you? So then Let us
make man and 16 let them rule; where the power of ruling is, there is the
image of God.

Let them rule the fishes. First the rule of the things dwelling apart was
given to us. He did not say, Let them rule the animals reared with us, but, Of
the fishes; for their way of life is in water. The rule of the fishes, then,
was given to us first. And how do we rule the fishes? If ever you have known
yourself appearing beside a lake, how your shadow frightened all. Who is so
much a master of the house, when the house is in uproar, that having stood by
suddenly he made quiet and changed all into good order by the presence of the
one who holds power? How does all the watery


creation, when one man has appeared, change its form. It no longer has the
free way of life, it does not dare to swim upon the backs of the sea or of
the lake. The dolphin, when it sees a man being somewhere near, although a
dolphin is the most kingly of the sea-monsters, is ashamed. Thus the rule
over the swimming things was given to man. But when you see your reasoning
extending through all and holding 17 all, how do you not rule the
sea-monsters? I myself have seen human invention, that they make a
construction of certain hooks and putting upon these baits commensurate with
the size of the sea-monsters that swallow them. Then of those ends of the
ropes, from which the hooks hang at the other extremity, stretching bladders
with breath and binding them they send those ends aloft over upon the deep.
The wild sea-monsters, then, being borne to the baits and receiving the hooks
upon themselves, drag the bladders into the depth, but the nature of the
bladders, being upward-tending, again pulls against them to the surface. And
being stuck fast in their own food, raging up and down and wandering they
search through the depths. They measure deeps of deeps and having much toil
ineffectual they at last become capturable by that hook, tamed by the toil,
consumed by hunger, a dead 18 catch being dragged with the bladders they
become to the hunter; the great by the small, the immense by the weak. Why?
Because having received power of ruling by the abundance of reasoning he
brings the most disobedient as bad runaways to exactness; those who cannot be
led by gentleness he enslaves through necessity. Thus everywhere the power of
ruling exists, being born in man from the one who created. From this
swordfish and hammerheads and whales and sawfishes and oxen and all those
fearful names of sea-monsters have become subject to man. Let them rule the
fishes of the sea and the beasts of the earth. Do you not see the lion, the
heavy, the roaring one, whose eye also is unbearable and whose roar makes the
earth tremble? To whom is he so bearable, so as to endure his charge? To none
of the animals. He trusts in such an abundance of power that he despises all
the beasts. But nevertheless 19 you see that one shut up in a small cage. Who
is the one who confined him, the one who devised the small prison for the
great animal? Who is the one who by the sparseness of those wooden threads
prepared breath for the beast, so as not to choke it with its own panting,
but both giving it free breathing and managing the security? Who? Is it not
man? They make playthings of the most difficult beasts. Does he not mock the
leopards, when he stretches a paper shaped into a man, and the one tore the
paper, but the man lying below laughed at the folly of the beast? Does not
man hold all by abundance? How shall I speak to you about the flying things?
That man does not go up into the air, but he flies along with the birds by
the power of reasoning. For nothing holds reasoning. The things in the depth
it searches through, the things above earth it hunts, the things in the air
it anticipates 20. Have you ever seen a bird sitting on the tip of a branch
laughing at men? For it trusts in the lightness of the wing. But nevertheless
one may see a talkative boy putting reeds under reeds and attaching birdlime
at the tip of the reeds, then stealthily through the branches and the leaves
stealing the presence of the birdlime. And † the eye of the bird being lifted
aloft † by a small touch he mastered the air-traveling, the winged thing
borne through the aether he brought bound by birdlime. A man lies below and
the hand is below, but the mind goes up and through art all things become
reachable for man. Nets are set for the winged, archers take aim at the
flying things, the more gluttonous are hunted by bait. Have you never seen
even an eagle falling greedily upon a catch, then being pierced through by
Thus the high becomes below, drawn by human baits. He brought all under the
hand


of man and filled the creation and did not make man without a share 21 of the
lordship. Do not say: the things borne in the air, how do they concern me?
Through reasoning those also become subject to you. †

And of the reptiles that creep upon the earth. Do you see where you have the
having come to be According to the image of God? And God made man. What then
is man? Let us define from what we have read and from what we have heard. For
we no longer have need to borrow foreign definitions nor to introduce the
things from vanity into the reasoning of the truth. Man is a rational work of
God that has come to be according to the image of the one who created him. If
anything is lacking to this account, let those who have spent much on the
taking up of the perishing wisdom inspect it. Man has come to be according to
the image of God. And God blessed man and said, Increase and multiply and fill
the earth. This is also a blessing of fishes; And God said, Let the waters
bring forth reptiles of living souls. And it was so. Then And God said,
Increase and multiply and fill the waters. What then is extra? These things of
necessity. He both granted you the common things 22 and the proper things
await you. For you increase as also the remaining animals, from the small of
the first you come to completion by additions little by little. So also horses
and dogs, so also eagles and swans and all that you may name, having received
the completion of growth through a small first conception by addition little
by little, again through diminution turn back to the less. The things, then,
that were common of nature, he granted also to us.

Increase, that is: be nourished, being born small become large and let there
be a limit of the increase. For it is not that since in the first seven-year
period we are nourished and a change of age takes us upon the second week of
years, we already ought similarly in every part of our life to change age by
weeks. The first week has the childhood age 23. The limits of the childhood
age are clear, the change of the teeth; some fell, others grew under. Growth
took a second beginning. At the ends of the fourteenth the second then, that
of the boys. For first a little child, then a boy, then an adolescent, then a
man. † from the fourteenth year † here are the limits of the increase.

Increase then. Not if you become a hundred years, do you admit increase from
the first to the hundredth year, but this Increase, one word spoken wisely,
has been arranged providentially; Increase up to a certain point. † but not †
measures of the increase. According to the first constitution that was laid
down in the womb the principles of the increase also were laid down. For it is
not that afterwards the gift of age is something newer coming on, but the
maternal deposits have the aptitudes toward increasing laid down together.
Then it came forth from the womb, it was increased as much as in the arms of
the mother. The teeth fell, we knew 24 that it was increased such a measure.
The father measured the three-year-old child; he knows that it will receive
double this size in the completion. For as much as it is in the three-year
period, twice so much it will be in the filling up of the increase. This is
the measure of human nature as far as the limit of a bound from the first week
to the second. There then the heat is more, the formation of the body more
sparse. The moisture fails, the members are strengthened. In the beginning the
members are more flabby than those of the grown, the humans not yet having the
complete. For their flesh is still newly compacted and unsuited toward
enduring labors. The living being has now received the light and easily moved.
But in the third week then it receives the completion of the increase. Still
the rising of the body is into length. After the third week, when nature has
breathed from the labors of the increase toward the above, 25 it begins then
to pass into the breadths and as it were to found around the things that have
been raised and to put thicknesses around them and to make the members of the
body strong. These things nature does according to its own sequence. But it
came to be from the beginning


by a master's command and what was then spoken proceeds through all the
creation as far as the end. Increase and multiply. Increase † so that the
creation may not be limited in one; Multiply, so that not into one but also
into many; † And fill the earth. But fill not by the dwelling; for thus we
would have been straitened living, if the earth were as much as measures us;
but fill by the authority. He gave us the dominion of the earth. Fill the
earth. Not surely also the burned and uncultivated and the frozen and
impassable, not surely also this 26 do men fill by necessity, but properly he
made us fill and we fill according to that by reasoning. When we see how
great a measure of the burned and uninhabited earth, when we see how great
the northern clime, which because of the exceeding cold is uncultivated and
useless, have we not filled the earth? Have we not, choosing the useful,
dismissed as much as is useless for the way of life of men? So that the Fill
the earth made us lords. Not because we do not have the use of all, do we not
have the ruling of all. For neither when you have bought the grain are you
not lord of the whole, because in the grain you have the edible and the
thrown away, or you throw away the stones as useless and if anything else has
been mixed in unsuited toward food, and having blown off the chaff and
separated the tares, having chosen as much as is clean 27 toward the
constitution of life? Thus then the earth, the better was granted as suited
toward dwelling, and that necessary toward farming, another was let go toward
pasturage of the four-footed. Tell me: is it possible to arrange what I wish,
since I have become lord according to the gift of the master who created me?
And fill. And let them rule the fishes of the sea and the birds of heaven and
the beasts of the earth. This then is a blessing, this a legislation, this
the dignity given to us by God.

And God made man, in the image of God he made him. Did you not mark that the
demonstration was incomplete? Let us make man according to our image and
according to likeness, the counsel had two: According to the image and
According to likeness. The crafting has one. Did he will otherwise and change
his counsel otherwise? Did some regret follow the creation? Was it weakness of
the creator, having chosen other things and having made other things? Or
garrulity in the words? For perhaps 28 he says the same thing, Let us make man
according to image and according to likeness; for having said According to the
image he did not say According to likeness? Whichever we choose, we will
accuse what is written. For if he says the same thing: it is superfluous to
say the same thing twice. To say an idle word in Scripture is a terrible
blasphemy. But indeed he does not talk idly. Therefore it is necessary that
man come to be According to the image and According to likeness.

Why has it not been said: And God made man according to the image of God and
according to likeness? Did the maker grow weak? The account is impious. Did
the one commanding repent? The thought is more impious. But he spoke and
changed his counsel? No. Therefore neither does Scripture say the same thing,
nor does the craftsman grow weak, nor was the counsel made vain. What is the
account of the silence? Let us make 29 man according to our image and
according to likeness. The one we have by the creation, the other we
accomplish from choice. In the first construction the having come to be
According to the image of God coexists with us; from choice it is accomplished
for us to be According to likeness of God. And this according to choice exists
in us in power, but in activity we bring it upon ourselves. If the Lord had
not said beforehand making us Let us make also according to 30 likeness, if he
had not granted us the power of coming to be according to likeness, we would
not have received the likeness toward God by our own authority. Now however in
power he made us able to be likened to God. And having given power toward
being likened to God he left us to be workers of the likeness toward God, so
that the reward of the work may be equal to angels, so that we may not be as
images having come to be from a painter lying in vain, so that the things of
31 our likeness may not bring praise to another. For when you


see the image accurately formed toward the prototype, you do not praise the
image, but you marvel at the painter. In order then that the marvel may
become mine and not another's, he left to me the coming to be According to
the likeness of God. For According to the image I have being rational, but I
become according to likeness in becoming kind. Become perfect as your
heavenly Father is perfect. Did you see where the Lord 32 restores to us the
According to likeness? Because he makes his sun rise upon evil and good and
rains upon righteous and unrighteous. If you become a hater of evil, not
remembering evil, not remembering yesterday's enmity, if you become a lover
of brothers, sympathetic, you have been likened to God. If you forgive the
enemy from the heart, you have been likened to God. If such as God is toward
you the sinner, you become such toward the brother who has offended against
you, by compassion toward the neighbor you have been likened to God. So that
the According to the image you have from being rational, but you become
According to likeness from taking up kindness. Take up Bowels of compassion,
kindness, so that you may put on Christ. For through the things by which you
take up sympathy, through them you put on Christ and by the kinship toward
him you have become kin to God. Thus the history is an education of human
life. Let us make man according to image; let him have in the creation the
According to the image. Let him also come to be According to likeness; of
this he gave the power. If he had also made according to likeness, where is
your grace? Why are you crowned? For if the craftsman had given back the
whole 33, how would the kingdom of the heavens have been opened to you? But
now the one was given, the other was left incomplete, so that having
perfected yourself you may become worthy of the recompense from God. How then
may we become according to likeness? Through the Gospels. What is
Christianity? Likeness of God according to what is possible to the nature of
man. If you have accepted being a Christian, hasten to become like God, put
on Christ. But how will you put on, you who have not been sealed, how will
you put on, you who have not received baptism, who have not received the
garment of immortality? Do you send away the likeness toward God? If I were
saying to you, Come, become like a king, would you not have judged me a
benefactor? Since I wish to make you like God, do you flee the word that
makes you 34 god, having blocked your ears, so that you may not even hear the
saving word? And God made man according to image. The man, says the woman,
what is it to me? The male came to be. For he did not say the woman, she
says, but by the addition of the article he indicated the masculine. But so
that no one may ignorantly have used the appellation of man of the male only,
he added: Male and female he made them. And the woman also has the having
come to be According to the image of God as also the man likewise. The
natures are of equal honor, equal the virtues, equal the prizes, the
condemnation alike. Let her not say, I am weak. The weakness is of flesh, in
soul is the strong. Since then of equal honor 35 is the image according to
God, let the virtue also be of equal honor and the display of good works.
There is no excuse for the one who wishes to allege the body as weak. For
this very reason it is simply tender, but through sympathy it is vigorous in
endurances, and intense in vigils. When can the nature of a man rival the
nature of a woman living her life enduringly? When can a man imitate the
vigor of women in fasts, the labor-loving in prayers, the abundant in tears,
the ready toward well-doing? I myself have seen a woman secretly stealing
good thefts and without the husband doing the well-doings on behalf of a
husband, on behalf of the increase of a house, on behalf of the remaining of
children giving and escaping the knowledge of the husband, putting out on his
behalf and 36 hiding him. For what she was doing, she was doing for the one
who sees in secret, not publishing her own well-doing. The good woman has the
According to the image. Do not attend to the outer man, this is a surrounding
plaster. The soul sits within under a curtain and the tender body. Soul
however and soul are of equal honor, the difference is in the curtains. You
have then become like God through kindness, through forbearance, through
sharing, through being a lover of brothers and a lover of one another, a
hater of evil, mastering the passions of sin, so that to you


there may belong the ruling. And let them rule the fishes. The ruling of the
irrational fishes was given to you, so that you have become ruler of the
irrational passion.

And let them rule the beasts. You rule every beast. What then, you will say,
do I have beasts in myself? Both myriads and a great 37 crowd of beasts you
have in yourself. And do not think what is said to be an insult. Is anger a
small beast, when it barks in the heart? Is it not more savage than every dog?
And the deceit lurking in the deceitful soul, is it not harder to tame than
every lurking bear? Is not hypocrisy a beast? And the one sharp toward insult,
is he not a scorpion? And the one rushing secretly toward revenge, is he not
more difficult than a viper? And the greedy, is he not a rapacious wolf? What
beast is not in us? The woman-mad, is he not a horse raging? For Horses, it
says, became woman-mad, each was neighing upon the wife of his neighbor. He
did not say he was speaking to the woman but, He was neighing. He transferred
him upon the nature of the irrational through the passion to which he was made
akin. Many beasts then in us. You have then become ruler of beasts, if you
rule the outer, and will you leave the inner unruled? What sort will you be in
reasoning, holding the lion 38 and despising his roar, but grinding the teeth
and sending forth an inarticulate voice, of the anger within † all at once
striving to fall forward and not even allowing it to remain within? What is
more difficult than this, when a man is mastered by the passion, when the
anger, having thrust aside the reasoning, itself takes over the dominion of
the soul? You were created a ruler then, ruler of passions, ruler of beasts,
ruler of reptiles, ruler of birds. Have neither high-flying reasonings nor be
light and unstable in mind. You were judged to rule birds. You are out of
place, bringing down the flying things with birdlime, but yourself being light
and aloft. Do not be puffed up, do not be lifted above, do not think greater
than human nature, do not be inflated being praised, do not glorify yourself
nor think yourself to be something great. For thus you will be an unstable
bird with unsupported nature 39 being carried about here and there. Rule the
reasonings in you, so that you may become ruler of all the things that are.
Thus the rule given through the animals trains us toward ruling ourselves. For
it is out of place for one being ruled at home to be king of nations, for one
being held by a courtesan within to be publicly a patron of a city. One must,
arranging the things at home well and having ordered the good order of the
inner things, thus take the oversight of the others, since the account will be
turned around by those being ruled, if he has the things at home unordered and
unarranged, that Physician, heal yourself. Let us first heal ourselves. No one
has been condemned for not having hunted a lion; but not having mastered anger
he is altogether laughable. So that the one who has not prevailed over his own
passion is brought into condemnation, but the one who has not been able to
prevail over wild beasts will appear as not yet having done anything worthy of
blame. And the Lord who arranged that these things be written, who also
allowed our small and weak tongue to converse with you as far as 40 this, who
through our weak mind has indicated to you great treasures in the few shadows
of the truth, may he give you through the small things the great, through the
few seeds the completion of knowledge, and to us the full reward of the choice
and to you a filled fruit of the enjoyment of the divine oracles, because to
him is the glory and the power unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
