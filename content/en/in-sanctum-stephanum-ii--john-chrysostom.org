On Saint Stephen, II.
=63.931=
Those from without who wish to weave crowns for men are eager to gather roses
and lilies and the remaining flowers of the earth; for such is the law of the
earthly crown-weavers. But we, fashioning a crown for the blessed Stephen, let
us harvest the spiritual flowers from Stephen himself, that we may weave a
crown of graces for the head of Stephen.
For the =63.932=.70 blessed Stephen is a meadow, filled with every fragrance,
watered by the blood of Christ, perfumed by the Holy Spirit, and making all
things fragrant. For behold, the memory of Stephen, and all things have been
filled with sanctification: heaven having received the spirit, the earth
having received the body, the air together with all things has been filled
with the myrrh of the fragrance. And we have him interceding on =63.933= our
behalf, the ornament of the Church, the athlete of piety, the disciple of
Christ, the refutation of the Jews, the leader of the martyrs, the protector
of the widows, the nourisher of the hungry, the helper of those who are worn
down, the guardian of the holy city of Christ, the crown of the good deacons,
the pillar of the Church, the splendor of the martyrs, the boast of the
inhabited world, the foundation of the faith; the guardian of our souls, who
prays for those who persecute, and who makes defense on behalf of those who
stone, and for this
reason is glorified in his face with angelic grace.
For all, it says, who sat in the council saw his face as the face of an angel.
Let us now crown as athlete Stephen, the first athlete of Christ, and let us
cease with the flowers of words. He was then the ornament of the choir of the
=63.934= apostles, and he was enrolled among the disciples of piety; by
gentleness he held sway over all men, and he had an exceptional love of
mankind, and he was not so much pricked by his own labors as by those of
others, and he considered the neighbor's want his own, and he treated the
suffering that belonged to another as belonging to himself. And in the first
preachings of the word, while faith was blossoming abundantly, immediately
those who were rich laid aside the burden of their possessions, and those in
need received what was lacking.
Of so fair a harmony and order Stephen was the steward, and poverty had him as
protector and ally, and wealth as charioteer. The power of the Spirit rested
upon him, and grace had the man as its own house. Let us then also hymn,
beloved, together with the first martyr Stephen, the one who bestows the
crowns, the holy and consubstantial Trinity, because to it glory is fitting,
now and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
