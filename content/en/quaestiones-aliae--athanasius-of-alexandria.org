OF OUR FATHER AMONG THE SAINTS ATHANASIOS, ARCHBISHOP OF ALEXANDRIA, CERTAIN
OTHER QUESTIONS.

QUESTION 1. What is God? ANSWER. God is an intellectual essence, both unseen
and inexplicable. God is an immaterial spirit, an unsleeping eye, and an
unmoved mind. God is a creative essence of all the unseen and seen creatures.
QUESTION 2. And why is God called God? ANSWER. He is called God from seeing
all things, as if a spectator, and God, that is, a beholder of all. And again
he is called God from running and racing intellectually, without time,
through all things. For God is always present everywhere. QUESTION 3. And how
many gods are there? ANSWER. One God of gods, and Lord of those who lord it,
and besides him there is no other. But men also are called gods by grace; as:
"I said, You are gods, and all sons of the Most High." =28.776= QUESTION 4.
And since he is one, how do we call this one God tri-hypostatic, Father, and
Son, and Holy Spirit? For behold we say three, and not one. But teach us how
he is one God; and how again we speak of three persons concerning God. This
seems marvelous to us, how both God is one, and his persons are three.
ANSWER. Hear with understanding, and you will grasp the mystery of the holy
Trinity, as far as the mind of men is able to grasp. As it seems to me, at
any rate, this is said according to the power of words that is ours. For God
is inexplicable, and for this reason we cannot comprehend his nature; nor is
he like us, single-personed. For if he were single-personed, we would know
him, as we know one another. But God is one, and his persons are three. And
see now what is said. Just as the sun is one, but the sun has a ray and
light, and there are in the sun three persons, disk, ray, and light; and the
disk is the orb of the sun, the ray coming down lamp-like and striking toward
the earth; and light that which illuminates, and into the shadowed places
without a ray. And behold three persons, disk, ray, and light. Yet we do not
say three suns, but one sun, nor do we say one person, but three persons. For
if you are asked how many suns are in heaven, you will say that there is one
sun; but if you are asked how many persons of the sun there are, you will say
three, disk, ray, and light; so understand also concerning God. God is one,
but the persons of the one God are three, of Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.
And from this know that, just as the sun is three-personed, so also the one
God is tri-hypostatic. For the solar disk is a type of the Father, the ray is
a type of the Son, the light of the sun is a type of the Holy Spirit. And say
thus: In the sun, disk, ray, and light; yet we do not say three suns, but one
and only; likewise also in God, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are one God, and
not three. And again say thus: In the sun the disk and the ray and the light
are inseparable; for they are not separated from one another, and for this
reason he is called also one God, and not three; because the three persons,
of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, of the one God, are
not separated from one another. And just as the solar disk begets the ray,
and sends forth the light; so also God the Father begets the Son and also
sends forth the Holy Spirit. See with understanding: Just as the ray of the
sun comes down from heaven toward the earth, and is neither separated from
the solar disk, nor is it absent from heaven, nor from the earth, but is both
in the


solar disk, and in heaven, and on earth, and everywhere, and is absent
neither from the things above nor from the things below; so also the Son and
Word of God came down toward the earth, and was absent neither from the
Father, nor from the heavens, nor from the earth; but he was also in the
bosom of the Father, inseparable, both above and below, and everywhere; and
he was absent from nothing. And just as the solar light is both in the solar
disk =28.777= and in the ray, and in heaven, and on earth, and enters into
the houses and everywhere, and illuminates; so also the Holy Spirit is both
with the Father, and with the Son, and above and below, and illuminates every
man, and never is absent. "For the Holy Spirit," says the apostle Paul,
"searches all things, even the depths of God." Behold then, just as one whom
we have never known, nor are we able to know of what sort he was; but when we
see his image, then we understand a little the character of his face; so
understand also concerning God. "For no one has ever seen God." How then can
anyone comprehend him? But since God is light and is named light, from this
sensible light we image him, so that then, as we said before, we characterize
the holy Trinity in the type of the sun, saying that the Father is disk, the
Son ray, and the Holy Spirit light from light. And just as the disk and the
ray and the light are one and three, being divided without division, so also
God the Trinity, the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit, is one and
three, divided in the persons after the sun, but remaining undivided in
nature. And just as the disk of the sun alone is cause and unbegotten, but
the ray is caused and generated from the disk, and the light is proceeding
from the disk alone, being sent through the ray, and illuminating the things
about the earth; so also God the Father himself alone is cause to the two and
unbegotten; but the Son is caused and begotten from the Father alone; and the
Spirit itself is caused and proceeding from the Father alone, but sent into
the world through the Son. And so hold, and understand, and believe
concerning God. But if the example of the sun is not enough for you toward
knowledge of the tri-hypostatic godhead, see also another image of God; and
it is the soul of man. For when God was about to form man, he said: "Let us
make man according to our image and according to likeness." Behold then, man
is an image of God, that is, the soul of man. And the soul of man is one, but
tri-hypostatic; the soul has three persons; and how? listen. The soul is one
person; and the soul begets the word, and behold the word is another person.
The soul also sends forth the breath, and behold the breath is another
person. Behold three persons, soul, word, and breath. For the word and the
breath are of the soul, not of the body, since, when the soul has gone out
from the body, neither word remains in the body, nor breath, but the body
lies both breathless and wordless. But the word and the breath are with the
soul; and from this it is clear that both the word and the breath are from
the soul, and are persons of the soul; and behold then the soul is one
person, and the word another person, and the breath another person. Behold
three persons of the soul, but one soul, and not three. For if you are asked,
how many souls does a man have? you will say, one. But if you are asked also
how many =28.780= persons of the soul there are? it is fitting that you
should say, three; since soul, word and breath, one soul, and three persons.
And the soul is as a type of the Father; and the word of the soul as a type
of the Son and Word of God; and the breath of the soul as a type of the Holy
Spirit. For as the soul is unbegotten, so also God the Father is unbegotten;
and just as the word is begotten from the soul, so also the Word of God is
begotten from the Father; and just as the breath


is proceeding from the soul, so also the Holy Spirit is proceeding from the
Father. And I said thus: Soul, word and breath, one soul, and not three; for
the word and the breath are inseparable from the soul. Likewise Father, and
Son, that is Word, and Spirit, one God, and not three; for the Word and the
divine Spirit are inseparable from the Father. And just as the soul is
invisible, so also God is invisible. And so whenever you hesitate in your
mind, and say, how is God one, and is also tri-hypostatic? remember your
soul, and say: Just as my soul is one, but also tri-hypostatic, soul, word,
and breath; so also God is one, but is also tri-hypostatic, Father, Word, and
Holy Spirit. And say in your mind that, if the soul, the creature of God, and
likewise also the sun, is tri-hypostatic, but their nature is one; how much
more God the maker of these! Is it not admissible that it be so, that he is
one in nature, and tri-hypostatic in the persons? and truly it is admissible.
For as soul, word and breath are three persons, and one nature of soul, and
not three souls; so Father, Word and Holy Spirit, three persons, and one God
in nature, and not three gods. If you reason thus always, you will not
blaspheme against the holy Trinity. But hear still also another image of God,
beloved. Behold fire is one, yet also otherwise tri-hypostatic. For the
underlying fire itself is one, but its burning is another person, and its
illuminating is another person. Behold then three persons of the one fire,
that is, the underlying fire, and the burning, and the illuminating; but one
nature of fire, and not three. Likewise also in the case of God; for the
Father is the fire, the Son the burning, and the Holy Spirit is the
illuminating. And just as in the case of fire, the fire, the burning, and the
illuminating, and the underlying fire itself, we call one, and not three; so
we say also in the case of God the three persons, both the Father, and Son,
and the Holy Spirit, we call one God, and not three. And concerning the
knowledge of the holy Trinity let these things suffice for the one who
believes. Yet hear also another mystery besides these. The word of man has a
double generation, and is generated in two courses; one course it is
generated from the soul, and another from the lips. And the word of man is
generated from the soul, whenever someone thinks to say something. For the
thought is called the first generation of the word, since he thinks to speak
that word; but he does not speak this through the lips, =28.781= but the soul
keeps the word in her bosom; and that thought is the first generation of the
word from the soul. Nevertheless, even if the word is generated from the
soul, a first generation whenever the soul thinks it, yet it does not make it
manifest, but still keeps it. But when it wishes to make the word manifest,
then it generates it from the lips. And this generation of the word from the
lips, this makes the word manifest in all, and no longer then is the word
called unmanifest, but manifest. But when the word is generated through the
lips, then all hear it, and it becomes manifest to all; and this generation
through the lips is called the second generation of the word; so that there
are two generations of our word; one from the soul, whenever it thinks to
speak the word, which is also called first generation; and another from the
lips, which makes the word manifest to all men, which is also called second
generation. Learn then accurately that, just as the word of man has two
generations, one from the soul, and another from the lips, so also the Word
of God has two generations, one from God the Father, which is also called
first generation; and another from the flesh, which is also called second
generation. And just as our word, even if it is generated the first
generation from the soul, yet is unmanifest, and is not made manifest, but
again


after the generation remains in the bosom of the soul; so also the Word of
God, even if he was generated before the ages from God the Father, yet was
not made manifest to men; but he remained again in the paternal bosom. And
just as our word, whenever we wish, is generated from our lips, and is made
manifest among all those who draw near to us; so also the Word of God,
whenever he was well pleased, was generated from the lips of the prophets,
and from the all-pure Mary, and then became manifest in the whole world. For
as our word is made manifest when generated from our lips, so also the Word
of God, generated from the flesh of the ever-virgin Mary, was made manifest
to all creation, and those who have believed in him are saved. And just as
our word, generated from our lips, whenever it is generated from the lips
themselves, is absent neither from the soul, nor from its own lips, nor from
the ears of those who hear, but is both in the soul, and in our lips, and in
the ears of those who hear, and is absent from nowhere, even if thousands of
thousands hear the word, the word is not diminished, but was always most
complete among us; so also the Word of God, even if he was generated from the
Virgin Mary, and from the lips of the prophets, yet was absent neither from
the Father, nor from the flesh, nor from all men, nor from all creation, but
was present everywhere, and was not diminished, but was most complete. And so
understand concerning God the Word, that he has a double generation, one from
God the Father, and one from the flesh; and the one from the Father is before
the ages, and the one from the flesh in the last of the days; just as also
our word is first generated from the soul, and then from the lips. And these
things concerning the two generations of the Word of God. =28.784= QUESTION
5. What is common to the holy Trinity? ANSWER. Common is the essence; common
the beginningless; common the power, the goodness, the wisdom, the justice.
For the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit have all things equally,
except their own properties. For the property of the Father is the
unbegotten, of the Son the begotten, and of the Holy Spirit the proceeding.
QUESTION 6. In the case of God how many essences do you confess? ANSWER. I
say one essence, one nature, one form, one kind, one glory, one dignity and
lordship. QUESTION 7. And how many hypostases do you confess in the case of
God? ANSWER. I confess three hypostases, three persons, three properties,
three individuals, and three characters. QUESTION 8. Why is the Father called
Father? ANSWER. God is called Father, as keeping all things; as if a keeper
of all. QUESTION 9. And why is the Son called Son? ANSWER. He is called Son
from "of what sort," that is, of what sort and like; for of what sort the
Father is, such also is the Son, and by change of o into u, son. For the Son
is like the Father. QUESTION 10. And why is the Spirit called Spirit? ANSWER.
It is called Spirit from conceiving every inclination swiftly, that is, every
inclination is conceived through it in brief. "For the Holy Spirit searches
all things, even the depths of God." QUESTION 11. In the case of God how many
causes? ANSWER. I say one cause in the case of God, and this is the Father.
For the Father himself begets the Son, and also sends forth the Spirit. Know
then that the Father alone is cause; but the Son is not cause, but caused. So
that the Father alone is cause; and the caused are two, the Son and the
Spirit. And the Father is called cause, because he begets, and is not
begotten, sends forth and is not sent forth. He begets the Son; and he also
sends forth the Holy Spirit. And for this reason the Father is called cause.
QUESTION 12. And how many caused? ANSWER. Caused two, the Son and the Spirit.
The Son does not beget, but is begotten, and for this reason is called
caused. Then if someone asks, how many causes do you confess in the case of
God? say, I say one cause. And what is this? The Father. And why is the
Father called cause? Because he begets the Son and also sends forth the
Spirit. But if he asks you, how many caused do you say in the case of


God? say two. And what are these? The Son and the Spirit. And why are they
called caused? Because =28.785= the Son is begotten, and does not beget; and
the Spirit also proceeds, and does not send forth. QUESTION 13. In the holy
Trinity who is first? ANSWER. In the holy Trinity no one is first, and no one
later, but together Father, together Son, together Holy Spirit; and for this
reason they are also called co-beginningless, and beginningless. And
beginningless is said of that which is before the beginning. Beginningless
then is the Father, beginningless the Son, beginningless the Holy Spirit. And
not one first, and another later, but the three together, Father, Son and
Holy Spirit. For this reason they both are and are named co-beginningless.
QUESTION 14. Make this also clear to us, how the Son and Word of God is
generated from the Father; for this amazes us. ANSWER. Since God is unseen
and inexplicable, we cannot interpret this either. For how can anyone
interpret what he himself has never yet seen, or heard from others at any
time? Yet from his works, I say, let us speak typically of the Word and God,
as far as is according to power. It must be understood that, just as the word
of man is generated from the soul without seed and incomprehensibly, so also
the Word is generated from the Father. And as fire is generated from fire,
and light from light, so the Son and Word of God is generated from the
Father. QUESTION 15. And how does the Holy Spirit proceed from the Father?
ANSWER. It is fitting that you not ask about this either. For this too is
inexplicable. Yet learn also about this. Just as then the breath of man
proceeds from the soul, so also the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father. And
as Eve is neither begotten nor unbegotten, but in a middle way, so also the
Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father. For Adam is unbegotten, but Seth is
begotten, and Eve is proceeding. For Eve was neither generated, as Seth was
generated, nor was she unbegotten, as Adam was; but she went forth from the
side of Adam. And Adam was unbegotten as a type of the unbegotten Father; and
Seth begotten as a type of the begotten Son; and Eve proceeding from the side
of Adam as a type of the all-holy Spirit. For the holy Trinity was typed in
our forefathers. But Adam, and Seth, and Eve were bodies, and they were
separable from one another; but God the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit,
are neither bodies, nor are they separable from one another. But only the
type of the unbegottenness of the Father is seen in the unbegotten Adam, and
the type of the generation of the Son in the begotten Seth, and the type of
the procession of the Holy Spirit is seen in the proceeding Eve. And so
understand also concerning this. QUESTION 16. Is God contained in one place,
or not? ANSWER. Apply your mind, and understand that God is unseen and
uncontainable light; God is neither seen, nor is he contained anywhere. And
since he is not contained in the all, how is it possible for him to appear or
=28.788= to be grasped by anyone? Of whom neither Moses the God-seer, nor the
disciples of his Word on that mountain of his transfiguration, nor anyone
else at any time was able to see the godhead naked; so that it is clear that
he is not contained in one place, but God is present everywhere. And these
things concerning theology; but now we will begin to speak also concerning
the economy, that is, concerning the incarnation of the Son and Word of God.
QUESTION 17. Behold I ask you a question, and you answer me. You taught us
concerning the holy Trinity, that God is tri-hypostatic, and that Father,
Son, and Holy Spirit. But now I ask you: This Christ, who is he? About this I
wish to learn. ANSWER. You have altogether heard concerning the holy Trinity,
that one person is called Father, and the other Son, and the other person is
called Holy Spirit. Behold then know that this Son and Word of God the Father
put on the flesh of a man, and walked as a man on the earth. He was named
then Christ, because he was anointed, that is, he wore the flesh of man. And
for this reason he is called


incarnate God, and Christ the Son and Word of God and God, because he wore
the form of man. QUESTION 18. And what reason, or what need did God have,
that is, the Son and Word of God, that he should become incarnate, and walk
as a man on the earth? ANSWER. God had no need to become incarnate, but
humanity was in need of healing. For since God made the heaven and the earth,
and all things in them, and he also formed man, and set him in the midst of
paradise, and appointed him king over all the creatures, that all things
might serve him, and that he might dwell in paradise, as an image of God. The
devil knowing this, and envying the dignity of man, advised this man to
disobey God, that through the disobedience he might drive him out of paradise
and of the dignity of God, and make the immortal mortal. But man, as not
having experience of the wickedness of the evil one, heard the counsel of the
devil, and disobeyed God, and from then received the death of sin, and
likewise all the men who came from him, as having received the sin from their
ancestors, held it; and sin prevailed against men. And God sent prophets and
teachers into the world; that they might teach the world, that is, men, and
they might turn from sin; and the prophets were not able to set things right.
For this reason God said in himself: I will come down, and I will wear flesh,
and I will become man-formed, and I will teach my creature, and it will turn
from the counsel of the devil, and every man will be as God not according to
nature, but according to adoption. And God having willed this by the good
pleasure of the Father, and the cooperation of the Holy Spirit, =28.789= the
Son and Word of God came down, and entered into a pure womb of a virgin
woman, and taking flesh from her, he became man; and again coming out from
the woman, and leaving her a pure and sealed virgin undefiled, just as
before, he walked in the world with men, as a man, and then was named Christ,
because of being anointed and wearing the flesh of man. And this is the
Christ the Son and Word of God, the one wearing flesh. QUESTION 19. And how
was it possible for a virgin to generate flesh, and again remain a virgin?
and this too seems marvelous to us and altogether extraordinary; teach us
also concerning this, we ask. ANSWER. Even if this seems marvelous to us, yet
it is not the work of man, but of God; for where God wills, the order of
nature is conquered; and again: "God is marvelous," and "Marvelous are his
works." Yet hear concerning the Virgin who generated various and most evident
symbols; remember that Adam being a virgin generated Eve; and again he
remained a virgin, just as before. And just as Adam being a virgin generated
and remained a virgin, so also the Theotokos Mary, being a virgin, bore
Christ, and again remained a virgin. But hear also another mystery toward
this. Just as a house fenced round on every side, having toward the east a
glass window both pure and most thin, when the sun has risen its rays pass
through the glass and enter illuminating the whole house; and again when the
sun is passing and its rays going out, the glass is not shattered, but
remains unharmed from the incoming and outgoing collisions of the sun's rays;
so understand also concerning the ever-virgin Mary. For this all-pure one,
being as a house fenced round, the Son and Word of God like a divine ray
coming down from the sun of righteousness the Father, and entering through
the glass window of her ears, illuminated her all-holy house, and again as he
himself knew, he went out, not injuring her virginity at all; but as before
the birth, so also


in the birth and after the birth he kept her a pure virgin. And with these
hear also another light-shining and most marvelous impression like those. The
pearl comes to be in the Red Sea; and it comes to be thus. The extremities of
the Red Sea are dry, having a small flow of water. And there are found there
certain sea shell-skins in all that Red Sea, named pinnas. Those pinnas then
stand always gaping, and awaiting foods for their nourishment. So then, as
they stand gaping and awaiting their own food, when the lightnings become
frequent there, the lightning comes down toward the pinna, and finding the
shells of the pinna =28.792= opened, it enters into it; and the pinna,
immediately contracting and squeezing itself together, shuts the lightning
within itself. And the lightning, being rolled in the sockets of the eyes of
the pinna, makes them bright, and makes them pearls. And the pearls coming
out from the pinna fall toward the shore of that sea, and so those who seek
them find them. So understand also concerning the incarnation of the Word.
The world is a sea, and the Virgin a shell. And the Virgin stood like a pinna
in the temple, awaiting the heavenly bread, Christ God, who also coming down
and entering into her, as lightning, and being wrapped in the flanks of her
virginity, prepared as bright the all-holy flesh, which he took when he
became incarnate; and so the Word of God was generated from her as a pure and
most precious pearl without male intercourse. And just as the pearl is
heavenly and earthly, heavenly according to the lightning, and earthly
according to the intertwining of the eyes of the pinna; so also Christ is
heavenly and earthly; heavenly according to the godhead, and earthly
according to humanity. And just as the pearl is prepared without intercourse
of male and female from lightning and the pinna, so also Christ was generated
without intercourse of male and female from the godhead, and the flesh of the
Virgin. And just as when the pearl is crushed, often the matter of the eyes
of the pinna is crushed and dissolved, but the brightness of the lightning
does not suffer, but the brightness of the lightning remains impassible, and
the matter of the eyes of the pinna alone is crushed and suffers; so also you
must understand in the case of Christ. For when our Savior himself was being
scourged, and suffering under the unbelieving Jews on the cross, the matter
of the flesh, that is, the humanity, alone suffered, but the godhead remained
impassible. And saying these things, you stop the mouths of Theopaschites,
and you teach the incarnate economy. From here, beloved, the doubtful thing
is dissolved for you by the grace of Christ. QUESTION 20. Behold all these
things, God-like Father, you have taught us fittingly. But we ask you, that
you interpret for us also this difficulty, why the godhead alone did not
teach the man, and save him without flesh; for what did he want with wearing
the flesh? ANSWER. And you ask well about this; therefore hear also the
answer well. At the beginning, when God made man, and man was led astray by
the devil, the devil did not conquer the godhead, but the humanity. Wherefore
it was fitting again that this same conquered humanity should itself again
conquer its enemy the devil, and again receive the divine paradise which it
had formerly lost through him. For if the godhead had come forward naked, and
conquered the devil, the devil would have been about to boast, that it is not
marvelous, even if he was conquered; for having attacked he was conquered by
the godhead. And for this reason God did not consent that his godhead naked
should wrestle with the devil, but he willed that the humanity which had been
conquered by the


devil, this =28.793= also alone should conquer the devil who had conquered
it. The wise God then knowing beforehand again that the humanity alone was
not able to conquer without the godhead, the godhead was hidden in that very
flesh, so that the devil, seeing the flesh, and not knowing that in that very
flesh the godhead is hidden, might come forward and wrestle with Christ, and
so be conquered by the hidden godhead. Which also came to pass. For just as
the fisherman, wishing to hunt a fish, does not cast the hook naked into the
sea, but by a bait clothes the hook outside with a worm, and so throws it
into the sea clothed with the worm; and the fish, seeing the worm only, and
not knowing that that worm in its midst has the hook, but thinking the worm
to be most alone without a hook, being led astray is held by the hook; so
also Christ did. For wishing to hunt the venomous fish lurking in the
boundless waters of the abyss, or rather the great dragon the devil, he did
not offer himself to the devil with his godhead naked, but by a bait the worm
his all-holy flesh, which from the Ever-virgin Mary, the most holy earth, he
put on without mixing, according to the divine David who says: "I am a worm
and not a man," he covered the most holy hook, his world-saving cross, being
fixed upon it, and through it wishing to hide his own godhead, by which this
venomous and snake-biting fish, the great dragon devil, the one who drove man
out of paradise and put him to death, being led astray and held, being
conquered perished. So that the godhead is as a type of the hook, and the
humanity as a type of the worm. The devil then having seen the human from
outside and not having understood the godhead hidden within it inside, was
led astray, and coming forward to the humanity, was held by the incomparable
and unconquerable hook of the godhead; and so the great dragon devil was
conquered. For this reason the Son and Word of God did not come with his
godhead naked to hunt the devil, but he wore the flesh, and so he entered.
And this is the cause of the incarnation coming to be. And just as the devil
himself, wishing once to lead man astray, and to cast him out of paradise,
did not come to Eve with his own devilry naked, but he wore flesh, the
serpent, and so he came, and led her astray; for the deceitful one knew that,
if he came to her with his devilry naked, he would not be able to lead her
astray; and for this reason he put on the serpent as flesh, and through the
flesh-bearing serpent he led Eve astray. And the serpent appeared, but the
devil did not appear. And through the appearing serpent the unseen serpent
devil was at work. For two natures in one person were then bringing about the
fall among the forefathers. And just as then devilry and serpent-nature, two
natures, were united in one person, and the one nature, that is, the
serpent-nature appeared, but the other, namely the devilry, did not appear,
