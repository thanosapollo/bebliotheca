OF THE SAME. A CHAPTER. On the immaculate body of which we partake.
αʹ. The body of our Lord and God of which we partake, brethren, is itself the
body which he took from our substance, which he assumed from the immaculate
Theotokos. For we must not assign two bodies to Christ; his body is one, and
he offered one sacrifice for us to God the Father. For even if we often
celebrate the mystical unbloody sacrifice, so that every believer may partake
of the divine body and blood in every place and time, yet it is the same
sacrifice; for we offer the same body, the same Lamb of God who takes away the
sin of the world. For if that were one thing and this another, many
sacrifices, and not one, would have been offered for us, as the divine apostle
has said in various places in the Epistle to the Hebrews:
"For such a high priest became us, holy, harmless, undefiled, separated from
sinners, and made higher than the heavens; who has no daily need, as the high
priests, to offer sacrifices first for his own sins, then for those of the
people. For this he did once for all, when he offered up himself." And after
other things: "But Christ having come as high priest of the good things to
come, through the greater and more perfect tabernacle, not made with hands,
that is, not of this creation, nor through the blood of goats and calves, but
through his own blood, entered once for all into the holy places, having found
eternal redemption." And a little after: "For Christ did not enter into holy
places made with hands, copies of the true, but into heaven itself, now to
appear before the face of God for us; nor that he should offer himself often,
as the high priest enters into the holy places year by year with blood not his
own; since then he would have had to suffer often from the foundation of the
world.
But now once, at the completion of the ages, he has been manifested for the
putting away of sin through his sacrifice; and inasmuch as it is appointed for
men to die once, and after this the judgment, so also Christ, having been
offered once to bear the sins of many, will appear a second time without sin
to those who wait for him unto salvation." And again: "And every high priest
stands daily ministering, and offering the same sacrifices often, which can
never take away sins. But this one, having offered one sacrifice for sins, sat
down for ever at the right hand of the Father, waiting thereafter until his
enemies be made a footstool for his feet; for by one offering he has perfected
for ever =95.408= those who are being sanctified. And the Holy Spirit also
bears witness to us. For after having said before, This is the covenant that I
will make with them after those days, says the Lord, giving my laws upon their
hearts, and upon their minds I will write them, and their sins and their
lawlessnesses I will remember no more. And where there is forgiveness of
these, there is no longer an offering for sin."
βʹ. As therefore the infant born of a woman is a complete human being, and by
eating and drinking it grows, and another body is not taken on, and we do not
say that it has two or many bodies, but one, that which is born of the mother,
and nature works the growth through the natural economy: so also now, since
Christ is our head, and we have become of one body with him, and we are his
body always growing.
And we have become of one body with him by participation in his flesh and his
blood; "For taking bread and a cup of wine and water, he gave thanks and
blessed, and said: This is my body." And in place of the natural economy, the
bread and the wine with water have become, through his word, his body and
blood. Just as his body before the resurrection from the dead was corruptible,
broken, eaten and drunk, yet without dissolution; for why did he not do this
after the resurrection, but before the resurrection? Because the body made
incorruptible through the resurrection is not broken, nor eaten, nor drunk;
nor does the incorruptible body possess blood, nor would it justly even be
named flesh, as Gregory, surnamed the Theologian, says in the oration on
baptism: "Believe that Christ the Son of God will come again with his glorious
advent to judge living and dead; no longer flesh, yet not bodiless, with such
reasons as he himself knows of a more Godlike body, that he may both be seen
by those who pierced him, and remain God outside of thickness." And this body
and blood of our God of which we partake is therefore corruptible, broken and
poured out, eaten and drunk; and just as the increase of our body fulfills
every natural economy of the original constitution of our body, so also the
celebration of the holy mysteries fulfills the whole spiritual and
supernatural economy of the incarnation of God the Word.
γʹ. For in our case the seed of the father is a beginning of the constitution
of the infant's body; and nourishment is supplied from the mother's blood, and
there comes to be a change of the underlying material into an organic body,
according to the power given to nature by the Creator of our nature and power,
and so flesh and blood and bones and the rest of the members are constituted
through the attractive and retentive and alterative, that is nutritive, power;
likewise also the addition to the body through eating makes the growth of the
body through the nutritive, that is attractive, and retentive, and alterative
power. It was necessary, then, also in the increase of the Lord's body to
behold the whole divine economy of his incarnation, and crucifixion, and
burial, and resurrection, and incorruption. For the Lord's body did not become
incorruptible from the beginning, but corruptible and passible until the
resurrection, and after the burial it became incorruptible, having risen from
the dead by the divine power and made us incorruptible together with it.
δʹ. How then did it come to be? There the holy Virgin was the table, having
the matter of the body; then according to the voice of the angel the Holy
Spirit came upon her. Then the power of the Most High overshadowed her, the
divine Word, the divine hypostasis, and assumed flesh from her; and here there
lies, as in the womb of the Virgin, upon the mystical table, as matter the
bread and the mixture of wine and water. For the mother also was nourished
from these, and supplied to the infant the matter of the body. The priest
says, as the angel, "That the Holy Spirit coming upon them may sanctify and
make this bread the holy body of Christ, and this cup the precious blood of
Christ;" and not through a natural economy, but a supernatural one, it
becomes, for the increase of the Lord's body and blood, one body, and not two.
εʹ. Then it is lifted up in the hands of the priest as upon a cross, and is
distributed broken, and so is buried in us, and makes us incorruptible
together with it. For this is the end of the economy. It is not destroyed in
us, as the Lord's body was not destroyed in the tomb. But until our communion
it undergoes all things of corruption. And it is broken by us honorably and
faithfully, and by lawless hands it is often dishonored and cast down, and by
mice and worms; but it is not destroyed, nor does it pass into non-being. For
it is gathered invisibly into one body. For the Lord also was circumcised, and
on the cross he poured out blood from his feet and hands and side, but it was
gathered into one =95.412= and the same; for the whole body rose complete.
Christ said: "Do this in my remembrance. For as often as you eat this bread
and drink this cup, you proclaim the death of the Son of Man." And the
remembrance of death is corruption. For death is corruption, but not
dissolution ....
And this chapter was found on a very old paper, damaged, drafted from his own
hand in letters according to sigla (edit. in abbreviated signs); and as far as
was possible, the surviving parts were gathered, even if incompletely, so that
they might not perish altogether.
