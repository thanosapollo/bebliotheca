i1 set apart for the gospel of God. Third it is to see the "set apart for the
gospel of God"; and in the [letter] to the Galatians the apostle says such
things about himself: but when God was well pleased, who set me apart from my
mother's womb, to reveal his Son in me. And those who do not understand the
cause predetermined from the foreknowledge of God, which is the cause of the
things known beforehand coming to be, lay hold of such expressions; and they
think through these to introduce those who are saved from constitution and
nature. And some also abolish what is in our power from such sayings, using
also the "the sinners were alienated from the womb" said in the Psalms. Toward
this, then, it is easy to meet, asking about the next expression; for it is
written "the sinners were alienated from the womb, they went astray from the
belly, they spoke falsehood; their wrath is according to the likeness of the
serpent." And we will ask those who attack the expression as if it were clear,
whether the sinners alienated from the womb, at the same time as they came out
from their mother's belly, went astray and failed of the saving way,
themselves working toward this. And how did the sinners alienated from the
womb go astray from the belly and speak falsehood? For they will not show that
at the same time as being born they sent forth articulate voice, putting forth
some falsehood. But if we attend to the things prefixed of the predestination
in the letter being examined, we will be able, having taken down the things
that drag the simpler toward acceptance of that which accuses injustice
against the divine doctrine, to make a defense concerning the one who sets
apart from the mother's womb and sets apart for the gospel of God the slave of
Jesus Christ, called apostle Paul. And the sayings are thus: and we know that
to those who love God all things work together for good, to those who are
called according to purpose; because whom he foreknew, he also predetermined
conformed to the image of his Son, that he might be firstborn among many
brothers; and whom he predetermined, these he also called; and whom he called,
these he also justified; and whom he justified, these he also glorified. Let
us attend then to the order of the things said. God justifies having first
called, not having justified those whom he did not call; and he calls having
predetermined before the calling, not having called those whom he did not
predetermine; and the beginning of the calling and of the justification is for
him not the predestination; for if this were the beginning of the things that
follow, those who smuggle in the absurd account concerning nature would most
persuasively prevail; but higher than the predestination is the foreknowledge;
for whom he foreknew, he says, he also predetermined conformed to the image of
his Son. God then, having looked beforehand at the sequence of the things that
will be, and having perceived an inclination of what is in our power of
certain ones toward piety and an impulse toward this after the inclination,
and that they will give themselves wholly to living according to virtue,
foreknew them, knowing the things present and knowing beforehand the things
future; and those whom he thus foreknew, he predetermined as going to be
conformed to the image of his Son. There is then someone who is the Son of
God, being image of the invisible God, and image of this is the so-called
image of the Son of God; which we think is the human soul which the Son of God
took up, which became through virtue an image of the image of God. And of
this, which we suppose is an image of an image [of the Son] of God, God
predetermined that those become conformed whom he predetermined because of the
foreknowledge concerning them. One must not then think that the foreknowledge
of God is a cause of the things that will be; but because they were going to
come to be according to the proper impulses of the one doing, for this reason
he foreknew, knowing all things before their generation; and as knowing all
things before their generation he foreknew certain ones and predetermined them
as going to be conformed to the image of his Son, and others he saw alienated.
If someone


should object toward these things, whether it is possible that the things
which God knew beforehand would be such not come to be; we will say that it is
possible they not come to be; but not, if it is possible they not come to be,
that it is necessary they not come to be or come to be; and they come to be
not altogether from necessity, but it being possible also that they themselves
not come to be. And the topic concerning the possibles belongs to rational
quickness and contemplation, so that the one who has wiped the eye of his own
soul may be able, having followed the subtlety of the demonstrations, to
perceive how even as far as chance things it is not hindered that something be
possible unto many, one of the many being that which will be, and not going to
be according to necessity; and known beforehand thus, that it will be, yet
will not be from necessity, but it being contingent that it not come to be,
that will be which was said not by guesswork but truly known beforehand. And
let no one think we have been silent about the "according to purpose" as if it
pressed our account; since Paul says "and we know that to those who love God
all things work together for good, to those who are called according to
purpose." But let him attend that the apostle has also immediately given the
cause of their being called according to purpose, saying "because whom he
foreknew, he also predetermined conformed to the image of his Son." And whom
rather was it necessary to enroll into the justifying call by the purpose of
God than those who love him? And the "and we know that to those who love God
all things work together for good" altogether presents the cause from what is
in our power of the purpose and of the foreknowledge. For he has almost said
that all things working together for good work together for this reason, since
those who love God are worthy of co-working. And at the same time we will also
ask those who say the contrary, and let them answer us toward these things.
Let it be by hypothesis that there is something in our power; and we will say
this to them who abolish what is in our power, until from the given hypothesis
their account be convicted as not sound. There being then what is in our
power, will God, having applied himself to the sequence of the things that
will be, know beforehand the things that will be done from what is in our
power for each of those who have what is in our power, or will he not know
beforehand? To say he will not know beforehand belongs to one ignorant of the
mind over all and of the greatness of God. And if they will grant that he will
know beforehand, again we will ask them, is his having known a cause of the
things that will be coming to be, what is in our power being granted? Or,
because they will be, he knew beforehand, and his foreknowledge is in no way a
cause of the things that will be from what is in our power for each? It was
possible then, certain things having met, that the one created with
self-determination work not this but that. These things then and such being
going to be said, there is saved also the "well done, good and faithful slave;
you were faithful over few things, I will set you over many; enter into the
grace of your Lord"; and every praise. And there is saved also the
reasonableness of the "wicked and slothful slave, you ought to have put my
silver to the bank." And thus only will the things justly said be saved,
toward those on the right "come before me, you blessed of my Father, inherit
the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world; because I
hungered, and you gave me to eat" and the things following; and toward those
on the left "go away from me, you cursed, into the eternal fire, that prepared
for the devil and his angels; because I hungered, and you did not give me to
eat" and the things following. But also if the "set apart for the gospel of
God" and the "who set me apart from my mother's womb" contained some
necessity, how would he reasonably have said "I bruise my body and lead it in
slavery, lest having preached to others I myself become unapproved" and the
"for woe is me if I do not preach the gospel"? For from these he clearly
presents that if he did not bruise his body and lead it in slavery it was
possible that he, having preached to others, become unapproved, and that it
was possible that woe come to him if he did not preach the gospel. Perhaps
then together with these God set him apart from his mother's womb, and set him
apart for the gospel of God, together with having seen the cause of the just
setting-apart, that he will bruise the body and lead it in slavery, being
cautious lest somehow having preached to others he himself become unapproved,
and that, knowing woe will be to him if he does not preach the gospel, by the
fear


toward God toward not coming to be in the woe he did not keep silent but
preached the gospel. And these things also the one who set him apart from his
mother's womb saw, and the one who set him apart for his own gospel, that he
will be in labors more abundantly, in prisons more abundantly, in stripes
exceedingly, in deaths often; from Jews five times he will receive forty save
one, three times he will be beaten with rods, once he will be stoned; and he
will suffer such and such things, boasting in the afflictions and knowing that
the affliction works endurance and enduring. And because of these things it
was worthy that he be set apart for the gospel of God as known beforehand that
he would be, and that he be set apart from his mother's womb. And he was being
set apart for the gospel of God not because of the nature having something
exceptional and, as far as concerns the constitution, above the natures of
those not such, but because of the deeds known beforehand earlier, which later
came to be, each from the preparation and the apostolic choice. And now to
give an account toward the saying from the Psalm was not timely, for it was
digressive; wherefore it will be given into its proper order, God granting,
when we expound the Psalm. And these things too, having been spoken at some
length, will suffice toward the "set apart." 2 i 9–11 for God is my witness,
whom I serve in my spirit in the gospel of his Son, how unceasingly I make
mention of you always in my prayers, asking if somehow now at last I may be
prospered in the will of God to come to you; for I long to see you. And it is
possible also without the will of God to come to someone, just as Balaam was
going away to Balak to curse Israel for a wage; and I think that also every
Gentile, or whoever is in any way alien to piety, not ministering to saints
something of the things profitable to them, is not also prospered in the will
of God to come to be toward those toward whom he comes, even if he is
sometimes prospered; for the Scripture says there is prosperity of a man in
evils. 3 11, 12 that I may impart some spiritual gift to you, so that you may
be established; and this is, to be encouraged together in you through the
faith that is in one another, both yours and mine. As there being also a gift
that is not spiritual I think that it is necessarily added; for there are
certain gifts, not spiritual, as also marriage; for the spiritual would never
hinder prayer; but the coming together of man and woman to the same place
prevents. And that marriage is a gift the apostle makes clear having said I
wish all men to be as also I myself, but each has his own gift from God; one
thus, and one thus. 4 i 13–15 and I do not want you to be ignorant, brothers,
that I have often intended to come to you and was prevented until now, that I
might have some fruit among you also even as among the rest of the nations,
both to Greeks and to barbarians; both to wise and to foolish I am a debtor,
so, as far as it depends on me, I am eager also to you who are in Rome to
preach the gospel. [And there is here according to a hyperbaton, which if it
is not observed fights with the sequence; for who would say that Paul has been
prevented from coming to the Romans for this reason, that you may have fruit?
For the contrary, if he was not prevented he had fruit, and being prevented he
was deprived of the fruit; how then must it be read? I have often intended to
come to you that I might have some fruit among you also even as among the rest
of the nations, both to Greeks and to barbarians, both to wise and to foolish,
and I was prevented until now; why then was he compelled to set the
hyperbaton? Because he was going to join many things to the "that I may have
some fruit" (such as the "among you also even as among the rest of the
nations, both to Greeks and to barbarians, both to wise and to foolish I am a
debtor" and the things following) so that he might not cut it by the
continuity, having inserted the "I was prevented until now" he joined to the
former, the "I intended to come to you," the "that I may have some fruit," and
thus he gave the order to the sequence of the account.] 5 i 18 for wrath of
God is revealed from heaven upon all ungodliness and unrighteousness of men
who hold down the truth in unrighteousness. And wrath seems to be said also
the minister of the pains upon the things that have been sinned and those
under him, servants and liturgists of the same, as when the wrath of God was
set forth to be kindled in Israel, when David, having been stirred up by it,


commanded Joab to number the people; and the second is presented from the "he
sent into them a fury of wrath, affliction and wrath, a sending through wicked
angels." And when, the pains themselves through sin; for thus I hear the
"according to your hardness and unrepentant heart you store up for yourself
wrath in a day of wrath," and the "and the wrath has come upon them unto the
end." In addition to these examine whether the things that happen to the
righteous, the wicked one tempting them, are wrath, yet neither of God nor
from heaven, but from somewhere below either of the devil or of some one of
his angels; and someone will not unpersuasively object to these the "our
wrestling is against the spiritual things of wickedness in the heavenlies";
for these too will seem, being angry, to wrestle with the saints and to bring
upon them wrath from heaven. Perhaps then there is some wrath from heaven and
another from below as has been shown, thus also for us there is wrath of God
from heaven, and another from heaven but not of God? And if God through the
starting-points in the natural account manifested the truth to all men, and
what is known of himself as each has had room when he has completed the
reason, it is clear that it will be necessary, because of all the things that
have come to be ungodly and that have been done unrighteously, more to these
and less to those, according to proportion that the revelation of the wrath of
God from heaven come. 6 ii 7–9 to those who by endurance of good work seek
glory and honor and incorruption, eternal life; but to those from faction and
who disobey the truth but are persuaded by unrighteousness, fury and wrath,
affliction and anguish upon every soul of man who works the evil, of Jew first
and of Greek. And wrath is the pain that follows those who sin unto
punishment; and they define fury as wrath that is fuming up and swelling; and
this fury has as its work to convict, whenever through the sins someone
suffers certain things, either their conscience being struck or certain
painful things following the sin. But also affliction follows such, not the
one that works endurance, nor if being fasting he will boast, but if it has
been yoked with anguish. And no one would have been in anguish if someone had
made for himself from wisdom breadth of heart and had heard the apostle saying
be you also enlarged. For the evils are accustomed to gather and to narrow the
soul, so that the devil as in a hole according to the serpent lurks. 7 ii 10,
11 glory and honor and peace to everyone who works the good, to Jew first and
to Greek; for there is no respect of persons with God. He then who works the
good and showing himself honorable will receive honor, ascending through
repentance, from which he fell having been likened to the senseless beasts;
and having understood the deeds worthy of incorruption he is raised in glory
unto eternal life; and everyone is in peace who is undistracted and not
dragged about by anything to think or to do beside the truth, according to the
"and the peace of God will guard your hearts and your thoughts in Christ
Jesus," and "peace be multiplied to you," and the "my peace I give to you";
and the prophet also says a multitude of peace will be in the sojourn of the
Lord until the moon be taken away, so that no longer there exist bastard light
and nocturnal. 8 ii 12, 13 for as many as sinned without law will also perish
without law; and as many as sinned in law will be judged through law; for not
the hearers of the law are righteous with God, but the doers of the law will
be justified. To be judged is according to law and through law; but to perish
does not admit law; for neither will anyone else destroy them, that he may
judge them by a law of destruction, but destruction holds those who have
sinned without law; and as many as sinned in law, not altogether standing away
from it but either overlooking or also being overcome, with proposing to live
according to law, these will be judged through the law itself. 9 ii 15, 16
their conscience bearing witness with them and the reasonings between one
another accusing or also defending, in a day when God will judge the hidden
things of men according to my gospel through Jesus Christ. [Or also thus:] God
will judge according to the gospel; which gospel is of Paul, and of those


near him and who have made themselves worthy of nothing gloomy but of that
over which one must rejoice. Wherefore the gospel is theirs, having used the
judgment of God as a minister together with it. 10 ii 21–25 you then who teach
another, do you not teach yourself? You who preach not to steal, do you steal?
You who say not to commit adultery, do you commit adultery? You who abhor the
idols, do you rob temples? You who boast in law, through the transgression of
the law do you dishonor God? For the name of God is blasphemed through you
among the nations as it is written; for circumcision indeed profits, if you
practice law; but if you are a transgressor of law, your circumcision has
become uncircumcision. One must seek in how many ways the law is said, and
especially with the apostle, so that he may not seem to contradict himself; at
one time "if you are circumcised, Christ will profit you nothing"; and at
another time that circumcision profits if someone practices law. There is then
law according to one way the bare letter of Moses, according to which
signification it has been said "if you are justified in law you have fallen
away from grace"; and the "the law has become our pedagogue unto Christ."
There is also the law according to spirit properly named, according to which
it has been said "so that the law is holy and the commandment holy." There is
also the natural, according to which it has been said "for when nations that
do not have law do by nature the things of the law" and the things following.
Law is said also the history of Moses, as when in the [letter] to the
Galatians he says "tell me, you who read the law, do you not hear the law, for
it is written, Abraham had two sons" and the things following. And he also
calls the prophetic things law, as in the "in the law it is written, in other
tongues and in other lips I will speak to this people" in Isaiah. And perhaps
he also calls the teaching of Christ concerning the things to be done law, as
when he says that he himself is not without law of God but in-law of Christ.
When then he says "whoever are justified in law you have fallen away from
grace," he overturns us from the letter of Moses. Circumcision then, which he
himself now says, he says another besides the cutting-down; and law, the
spiritual. And now law can also be understood the one according to the natural
notions; for according to the betterment of the soul the one spiritually
circumcised is profited according to the taking-away of the worthless things,
and through practicing the law working the things to be done. And someone can
also according to the letter apart from anagogy expound the saying, and he
says that speaking toward those from Jews he says this; for he became to the
Jews as a Jew that he might gain Jews, having circumcised Timothy and having
shaved and being purified according to law, so as also to say toward them "for
circumcision indeed profits if you practice law," and speaking toward those
from the nations he forbids this, not contradicting himself; and since it has
been said in the account concerning this to Abraham that those from the
nations are to be circumcised, if not those from his seed, and those home-born
and bought with silver; and likewise also in Leviticus it has been said only
concerning the sons of Israel; and it is not necessary that the proselytes
also be understood along with these, since neither the things specially
legislated concerning the Levites, or the things concerning the priests, or
the things concerning the senate, grant that others also be understood along
with them. For when he also wants the proselytes to accomplish something, he
specially mentions them too, as when he speaks concerning sacrifice. Indeed
even while the so-called sanctuary still stood, proselytes also used to offer;
as also concerning the ten lepers who were cleansed the Lord said "go show
yourselves to the priests"; of whom one was a Samaritan. To whom the Savior
bearing witness says "were not the ten cleansed? And the nine, where? They
were not found having returned to give glory to God except this foreigner."
And if someone will object the things from Exodus concerning the proselytes
wanting to eat the passover, that they must be circumcised, we will say that
in one place God commanded the passover to be sacrificed, and this not
standing now the circumcision is superfluous for those from the nations. 11
iii 1–3 what then is the surplus of the Jew, or what is the profit of the
circumcision? Much according to every way; first that they were entrusted with
the oracles of God. For what if some disbelieved? Will their unbelief make
void the faith of God? May it not be. The being entrusted with the oracles


of God is not characterized in books and letters being entrusted, but in the
mind in them and the mysteries stored in them being known. For according to
the sound account the wise will understand the things from his own mouth; and
upon his lips he will wear knowledge. Such was Moses and the prophets, being
Jews and entrusted with the oracles of God, and if anyone among them is near
these. And the apostles also of our Lord Jesus Christ had the surplus of the
Jew and the profit of the circumcision beyond those who learned Christianity
from them. And there is also for those from the nations much surplus beyond
those from circumcision; for from those the kingdom of God was taken that it
might be given to a nation doing its fruits. And if those first were entrusted
with the oracles of God, there are those also entrusted second; for the first
is first of someone or of some. Who then were entrusted second it is to see
from the "for to one through the Spirit is given a word of wisdom" and the
things following. And if someone being confused thinks the oracles of God the
same as the letters of God, let him attend to the Psalm that says "the oracles
of the Lord are pure oracles, silver tried in the fire, tested for the earth,
purified seven times." For I do not think the pure oracles are the same as the
letter that kills. And just as the Jews mentioned before were first entrusted
with the oracles of God, so also some disbelieved; but not the unbelief of
such, whether toward God or toward his oracles, will make void the faith of
God. And what faith of God? Whether that by which God entrusts the oracles to
some? Or that by which those entrusted with the oracles believe God, as
Abraham believed God and it was reckoned to him unto righteousness? I think
each account has a point; for one must remember both the one who believes God
and the one entrusted with his oracles, especially at a time of the mockery of
the unbelievers which they mock those who have believed, that the faith of God
not be made void by the account of those who disbelieve. 12 iii 4 and let God
become true and every man a liar; as it is written: that you may be justified
in your words and may conquer when you are judged. The "let it come to be"
then has been said instead of an optative, as the "may your name be blessed
unto the ages." 13 ιιι 9–18 what then? Do we excel? Not altogether; for we
have already charged both Jews and Greeks all to be under sin as it is
written; there is not a righteous one, not even one; there is not the one who
understands; there is not the one who seeks out God; all turned aside,
together they became useless; there is not one doing kindness, there is not
even as far as one; their throat is an opened tomb; with their tongues they
were dealing treacherously; poison of asps is under their lips; whose mouth is
full of curse and bitterness; their feet are sharp to pour out blood; crushing
and misery are in their ways and a way of peace they did not know; there is
not fear of God before their eyes. He wants to gather sayings how all Jews and
Greeks have come under sin, beginning from the "as it is written there is not
a righteous one, not even one, there is not the one who understands, there is
not the one who seeks out God"; which in these very words we have not found
written somewhere, but we think the sayings have been recast by the apostle
from the thirteenth Psalm and from the 52nd; for in the 13th Psalm it has been
said "the Lord looked down from heaven upon the sons of men to see if there is
one understanding or seeking out God"; and in the 52nd "God looked down from
heaven upon the sons of men to see if there is one understanding or seeking
out God." He seems then to have understood the "to see if there is one
understanding or seeking out God" as equivalent to the "there is not the one
who understands, there is not the one who seeks out God." And the "there is
not a righteous one, not even one" I think has come from the "there is not one
doing kindness, there is not even as far as one"; so that in force he has set
it twice, once having recast, and once using the same words, and having
omitted the "there is not" said second. And the "poison of asps is under their
lips," the citation lies in these very words in the 139th Psalm; and the upon
these "whose mouth is full of curse and bitterness" seems to have come from
that said thus in the ninth Psalm "whose curse


his mouth is full of and bitterness and of deceit." Then next this is: their
feet are sharp to pour out blood; and this you will seek in Isaiah, or in the
other editions of the Proverbs, in the "swift to pour out blood," which was
added with asterisks in the edition of the Seventy. And the "crushing and
misery are in their ways" until now I do not know where having read it; and I
think in some one of the prophets it has been written "a way of peace they did
not know," and in Psalms "there is not fear of God before their eyes." And
there being a multitude of sins, not altogether is each liable to all, but
some to this sin and some to that, and thus all to all. 14 ιιι 19, 20 for we
know that as many things as the law says it speaks to those in the law, that
every mouth may be stopped and all the world become under-judgment to God;
because from works of law all flesh will not be justified before him; for
through law is knowledge of sin. What is common toward all flesh the works of
the law of Moses? And how also does knowledge of sin come through the law of
Moses, many even before Moses having known their own sins? For Cain says "my
cause is greater than for me to be forgiven"; and the patriarchs who went down
into Egypt toward Joseph say upon the words of Joseph toward them "we are in
sins concerning our brother" and the things following; but also if Job is
shown to have come to be before Moses, he says "and if also having sinned
willingly I hid my sin" and the things following. So that it seems to me that
not the law of Moses is now being said by the apostle but concerning the
natural, which also has been written in the hearts of men. And who would those
in the law be? Or every man who has completed the reason? For those still
living without law are not in the law, of which sort Paul also once was.
Except the saints whom he foreknew and predetermined and called and justified
and glorified are law and not in law; wherefore their mouth will not be
stopped, nor will they become under-judgment to God, for they are not flesh;
and if they are justified, not from works of law, for law will not be
justified from works of law, being righteous from itself. And thus through law
is knowledge of sin, as through medicine knowledge of disease, and especially
of that which escapes even the one who is sick. [And otherwise also] as far as
concerns the law it was necessary that the one committing adultery be punished
and the one murdering be chastised altogether for one who wants it, but the
"the Lord has taken away your lawlessness" was no longer a will of the law but
grace of the one who has given the law. For this reason having said accurately
the "from works of law" he did not add Every man but All flesh. For by
disposition it is possible to be justified, when by purpose one inclines
toward the will of the law, but by works no longer possible; and the works of
flesh whatever a soul may choose. 15 21, 22 but now apart from law
righteousness of God has been manifested, being witnessed by the law and the
prophets; and righteousness of God through faith of Jesus Christ unto all and
upon all who believe. What he says is such: not as through law is knowledge of
sin, so also manifestation of righteousness of God comes through law, for
apart from law righteousness of God has been manifested; for the righteousness
of God is greater than the natural starting-points, which are not
self-sufficient toward perceiving righteousness, not simply but that of God
himself, whose justifying judgments are unsearchable. Wherefore now apart from
law the righteousness of God has been manifested, of which Christ is teacher,
taking along in nothing the law of nature unto presentation of the
righteousness of God. And this was being witnessed, just as by the prophetic
words said enigmatically from Holy Spirit, also by the law of the prophet
Moses. And do not wonder if two significations of the one name of the law have
been taken along in the same place; for we will find this custom also in other
scriptures; such as "do you not say there are still four months and the
harvest comes? Lift up your eyes and behold the fields that they are white
already toward harvest." For twice there the harvest having been named,
according to the former it is referred to the bodily, and according to the


second to the spiritual. And if the same law has been taken along according to
some, if it has been manifested apart from law it is not witnessed by law, and
