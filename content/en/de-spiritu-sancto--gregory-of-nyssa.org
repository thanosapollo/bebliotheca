OF THE SAME, A DISCOURSE ON THE HOLY SPIRIT.
David of himself makes every occasion of a feast more radiant, always
retuning that many-stringed lyre to fit the need. So let the same Prophet
brighten for us also the great feast of Pentecost, striking the melody
with the plectrum of the Spirit on the strings of wisdom. Let him speak,
then, from that inspired song, what is fitting for the present grace,
that Come, let us rejoice in the Lord.
First we must know what the grace is, and then fit to the subject the
matching voice from the prophecy. And grant me, so far as it is possible,
to make the account of these things clear through some consecutive order.
At the beginning humankind had gone astray as to the knowledge of God,
and having abandoned the Lord of creation, some through deceit submitted
to the elements of the world, others made the nature of demons an object
of worship, and to many even the handmade form of the idols seemed the
Divine, for whom altars and temples and rites and sacrifices and precincts
and images and all such things were set up by them in service of the
falsely named gods. So the Master of nature saw with the eye of
loving-kindness the corruption of human nature, and through a certain
sequence he leads human life back from error toward the knowledge of the
truth. For just as those who, with a certain medical skill, restore people
wasted by a long famine do not lead them all at once to satiety, out of
care for their weakness, but when their strength has been recovered
through measured food, then they allow them to take their fill =46.697=
of plenty according to their capacity, in the same way, when human nature
had been wasted by the fearful famine, the sharing in the food of the
mysteries is portioned out to them by the economy, so that, always
receiving what is perfect through a consecutive order, they may in this
way reach the end of perfection.
For what saves us is the life-giving power, believed in the name of the
Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. But those who could not
contain the whole, because of the weakness that had come upon them from
the famine of souls, were first transferred from polytheism by the
prophets and the law, and they are trained to look toward one Godhead,
and in the one Godhead they grasp only the power of the Father, being, as
I said, unable to contain the perfect food. Then through the Gospel the
only-begotten Son also is revealed to those already perfected through the
law, and after this the perfect food of our nature comes to us, the Holy
Spirit, in whom is life. This is the subject of the feast. For these
reasons it is well that we, having become dancers of the Spirit, obey
David, the leader of this spiritual choir, who says: Come, let us rejoice
in the Lord. And the Lord is the Spirit, as the Apostle says.
For today, according to the yearly circuit of the year, Pentecost being
completed, at this hour, if indeed we are around the third hour of the
day, the unspeakable grace came to pass. For the Spirit was mixed again
with human beings, which earlier, because man had become flesh, had
withdrawn from our nature; and through that violent blast, the spiritual
powers of wickedness and all the filthy demons having been scattered from
the air, in the descent of the Spirit those who had been left in the
upper room became full of the divine power, in the form of fire. For it
is not otherwise possible for anyone to become a partaker of the Holy
Spirit unless he dwells in the upper room of this life. For as many as
think on the things above, having transferred their citizenship from
earth to heaven, being inhabitants of the upper room of the high way of
life, come to share in the Holy Spirit. For so the history of the Acts
says, that when they were gathered in the upper room, that pure and
immaterial fire is divided into forms of tongues, according to the number
of the disciples. Those men, then, spoke to Parthians and Medes and
Elamites and the rest of the nations, retuning their own voices at will
to every national tongue. But I, as the Apostle says, would rather speak
five words in the church with my mind, that I may also benefit others,
than ten thousand words in a tongue. Then it was useful to become of one
speech with those of other tongues, so that the preaching might not be
idle among those who did not understand, hindered by the voice of the
preachers. Now, however, since there is a common speech as to tongue
=46.700=, we must seek the fiery tongue of the Spirit for the illumination
of those darkened through deceit.
So let David lead us toward this also, taking the Apostle as a fellow
dancer with himself. For in this psalmody, whose beginning grants us the
rejoicing in the Lord, saying Come, let us rejoice in the Lord, we are
not through this led to the doxology of the Holy Spirit; but much more in
the remaining parts we are taught the things concerning his Godhead. I
will speak to you the Prophet's own words, to which the great Apostle
also agrees. And the saying runs thus: Today if you hear his voice, do
not harden your hearts, as in the provocation, according to the day of
temptation in the wilderness, where your fathers tempted me. Recalling
these things the divine Apostle speaks thus: Therefore as the Holy Spirit
says. And having said this, he added these sayings of the Prophet, fitting
them to the person of the Spirit. Who then is the one whom their fathers
tempted in the wilderness? Who is the one they provoked? Learn from the
Prophet himself, who says that they tempted God the Most High. But the
Apostle, having first set the person of the Holy Spirit, ascribes those
words to him, saying: Therefore, as the Holy Spirit says, that according
to the day of temptation in the wilderness, where your fathers tempted
me. Therefore the one whom the Prophet named Most High God, this one the
divine Apostle says is the Holy Spirit. And if you do not believe, examine
again what has been said: Therefore, as the Holy Spirit says: Do not
harden your hearts, as in the provocation, according to the day of
temptation in the wilderness, where your fathers tempted me. If then the
Holy Spirit says, Your fathers tempted me in the wilderness, and the
Prophet testifies that the one tempted in the wilderness is Most High
God, the mouths of the Pneumatomachi have been stopped, those who speak
injustice against God, since both the Apostle and the Prophet clearly
proclaim through what has been said the Godhead of the Spirit: the one
saying that they tempted God the Most High, and bringing forward that
saying to the Israelites as from God, that In the wilderness your fathers
tempted me; and the great Paul fitting these words to the Holy Spirit, so
that through these things it is clearly shown that the Holy Spirit is
Most High God. Do the enemies of the glory of the Spirit see the fiery
tongue of the divine oracles that lights up the hidden =46.701= things,
or will they mock as if we were filled with new wine? But even if they
say these things against us, I advise you, brothers, not to fear the
reproach of such people, nor to be overcome by their contempt.
Would that this new wine might someday come also to them, this newly
pressed wine poured out from the vat which the Lord trod through the
Gospel, that he might make the blood of his own cluster drinkable for
you. Would that they too had been filled with this new wine, which they
named must, which has not suffered the mixing of heretical water from the
tavern-keepers. For they would surely have become full of the Spirit as
well, through whom those who boil with the Spirit foam off from themselves
the thick and muddy part of unbelief. But such people cannot receive the
new wine in themselves, still carrying about the old wineskin, which,
unable to hold such wine, bursts in a heretical way. But we, brothers, as
the Prophet says, Come, let us rejoice in the Lord, drinking also the
sweets of piety, as Esdras commands, and being made bright in the choirs
of the apostles and prophets, according to the gift of the Holy Spirit
let us rejoice and be glad in this day which the Lord has made, in Christ
Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory unto the ages. Amen.
