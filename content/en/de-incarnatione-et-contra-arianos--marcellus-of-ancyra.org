OF OUR FATHER AMONG THE SAINTS ATHANASIUS ARCHBISHOP OF ALEXANDRIA, ON THE
INCARNATE MANIFESTATION OF GOD THE WORD, AND AGAINST THE ARIANS. Those who
wish to understand the divine Scriptures with evil craft wish to drag aside
the human words of the poverty of the Son 985 of God in order to establish
their own blasphemy. But all the exactness of Christianity is found in the
humble words and deeds. If therefore they were able to hear the blessed Paul
writing to the Corinthians: For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ,
that for our sake he became poor, being rich, that we by his poverty might
become rich; they would never have dared to say that the Son is unlike the
Father; if indeed they had known what is the manner of his poverty, and what
is the power of his cross. Whence also elsewhere the same Paul says: That you
may be able to comprehend with all the saints what is the breadth and length
and depth and height, and to know the surpassing love of the knowledge of
Christ, that you may be filled unto all the fullness of God. For this reason
in writing he is bold in his poverty and his sufferings, saying: But far be it
from me to boast, except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, through which
the world has been crucified to me, and I to the world; and again: I judged to
know nothing among you except Jesus Christ, and him crucified; and, If they
had known, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory. And now these, if
they had known the divine Scriptures, would not have dared to blaspheme the
Creator of all things as a creature or a work. For they bring them forward to
us, saying: How can he be like, or of the essence of the Father, when it is
written: As the Father has life in himself, so he has given also to the Son to
have life in himself? there is a superiority, he says, of the giver over the
receiver; and, Why do you call me good? he says; no one is good, except one,
God; and again: My God, my God, why have you forsaken me? and again:
Concerning the last day no one knows, not even the Son, except the Father; and
again: Whom the Father sanctified, and sent into the world; and again: Whom
the Father raised from the dead. How then, they say, can he who is raised from
the dead be like or consubstantial with the one who raised him? These things
we have set down as few from many, so that, when these are solved, the rest
also may be easily understood. We therefore ought according to our power to
explain the force 988 of them. When therefore the blessed Paul says, The
Father raised his Son from the dead, John relates to us that Jesus said:
Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it. But he was speaking,
he says, of the temple of his body. It is therefore clear to those who attend
that, when the body is raised, the Son is said by Paul to have been raised
from the dead; for the things of his body are said unto his person. Thus then
also when he says, The Father gave life to the Son, one must understand the
life as given to the flesh. For if he himself is the life, how does the life
receive life? And if he himself promises saying: My sheep hear my voice, and I
know them, and they follow me, and I give them eternal life, and they shall
never perish; and if he himself is the wisdom of God, and if he himself made
every stature, how is it said: And Jesus advanced in wisdom and stature? And
if the Father made all things through his own Word and Son, it is clear that
he also made the raising of his flesh through him. Through him therefore he
raised him, and through him he gives him life. He is raised according to the
flesh as man, and receives life as man, being found in fashion as a man. And
he himself is the one who raises his own temple, as


God, and giving life to his own flesh. When therefore again he says, Whom the
Father sanctified, and sent into the world, elsewhere he says: For them I
sanctify myself, that they also may be sanctified in truth; and when he says:
My God, my God, why have you forsaken me? he speaks from our person; because
having taken the form of a servant, and having become in the likeness of men,
and being found in fashion as a man, he humbled himself, becoming obedient
unto death, and death of a cross, and what follows. And as Isaiah says: He
takes our infirmities, and is in pain concerning us; so that he is not in pain
for himself, but for us; and he himself was not forsaken by God, but we, and
for us who were forsaken he came into the world. And when he says: Therefore
God also highly exalted him, and granted him a name, that which is above every
name, he speaks concerning the temple, which is his body. 989 For the Most
High is not exalted, but the flesh of the Most High is exalted; and to the
flesh of the Most High he granted a name which is above every name. And the
Word of God did not receive by grace to be called God, but his flesh was
theologized together with him. For he did not say that the Word became God,
but, The Word was God; Always, he says, was the God Word, and this same God
became flesh, that his flesh might become God Word; as also Thomas, having
touched his flesh, cried out: My Lord, and my God, theologizing the both
together; and likewise John also wrote: That which was from the beginning,
which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we beheld, and
our hands touched, concerning the Word of life. It is therefore manifest that
in flesh the Son and Word of the Father was touched, and the divine Scripture
has handed down that the both together, Word of life, was touched. And when he
says: The Holy Spirit was not yet, because Jesus was not yet glorified, he
means his flesh not yet glorified. For the Lord of glory is not glorified, but
the flesh of the Lord of glory; this receives glory going up together with him
into heaven. Whence also the Spirit of adoption was not yet in men, because
the firstfruits taken from us had not yet gone up into heaven. As many things
therefore as Scripture says, that the Son received, and the Son was glorified,
it says on account of his humanity, not on account of the divinity. And when
he says, My Father, who sent me, is greater than I, since he became man, he
calls the Father greater than he. But being Word of the Father, he is equal to
him. For I, he says, and the Father are one; and He who has seen me has seen
the Father. And he does not have it from robbery that he is equal to the
Father, but by nature he is equal and consubstantial with the Father; because
he has been begotten from the paternal essence. And when the Apostle says,
That in Christ dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily, one must
understand that all the fullness of the Godhead dwells in his flesh. And when
he says, Who did not spare his own Son, but delivered him up for us all,
elsewhere he says: As also Christ loved the Church, and delivered himself up
for her. 992 For the immortal God did not come to save himself, but those who
had been put to death; and he did not suffer for himself, but for us; so that
for this reason he took upon himself our cheapness and our poverty, that he
might grant us his wealth. For his passion is our impassibility; and his death
is our immortality; and his tear is our joy; and his burial is our
resurrection; and his baptism is our sanctification; For them, he says, I
sanctify myself, that they also may be sanctified in truth. And his bruise is
our healing; For by his bruise we were healed. And his chastisement is our
peace; For the chastisement of our peace was upon him; that is, for the sake
of our peace he himself is chastised; and his dishonor is our glory; whence he
asks for the glory for our sake, saying: Glorify me you, Father, with yourself
with the glory which I had, before the world was, with you. For we are the
ones who are glorified in him; and his descent is our ascent, according to
what is written: And he raised us together and seated us together in the
heavenly places in Christ Jesus; and the, That


he might show in the ages to come the surpassing wealth of his grace in
kindness, in Christ Jesus toward us. And his saying on the cross: Father, into
your hands I commit my spirit, he commits all men to the Father through
himself, those who are made alive in him. For they are his members, and the
many members are one body, which is the Church; as also the blessed Paul
writes to the Galatians: For you are all one in Christ Jesus; he therefore
commits them all in himself, Thus then also when he says, The Lord created me
the beginning of his ways, he speaks concerning the Church, which is created
in him. For the maker of all things is not a creature and a work, but the work
is renewed in the maker, according to what is said by Paul: We are his
workmanship, created in Christ Jesus. And as again he says: That now there
might be made known to the principalities and the authorities in the heavenly
places through the Church the many-colored wisdom of God, according to the
purpose of the ages, which he made in Christ Jesus our Lo 993 rd; in whom we
have boldness, and the access in confidence through his faith. And again he
says: As he chose us in him before the foundation of the world, that we should
be holy and blameless before him in love, having predestined us unto adoption
through Jesus Christ unto himself. And again he says also concerning the two
peoples: That he might create the two in himself into one new man, making
peace, and might reconcile both in one body to God through the cross, having
slain the enmity in him. And when he says: Why do you call me good? no one is
good, except one, God; God, having numbered himself with men, said this
according to his flesh, and toward the mind of the one who came to him. For
that man thought him a man only and not God, and the answer has this mind: For
if, he says, you think me a man, and not God, do not call me good; for no one
is good. For the good does not belong to human nature, but to God. And he
adds: You know the commandments? And when that man had said: Which? he said:
You shall not commit adultery, you shall not murder, and what follows. He
answered saying: All these I have kept from my youth, what do I still lack? He
said to him: If you wish to be perfect, go, sell all that you have, and give
to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven, and taking your cross
follow me. Through these things therefore he showed that the good belongs also
to him; for he who does not follow the good how can he inherit eternal life?
And when he speaks concerning the last day, that No one knows, not even the
Son, except the Father, he speaks humanly. For if he knows the Father, how is
he ignorant of the last day? For no one knows the Father, he says, except the
Son, and him to whom the Son may wish to reveal him. And if the Holy Spirit
knows all the things of God, how is the Son ignorant of the day which he
created? And if the ages came to be through him and the times, it is manifest
that the last day also is found among the ages and times, and it is impossible
for him to be ignorant. 996 As many humble words therefore as have been spoken
by the Lord belong to his poverty, that we in him might become rich, not that
in them we might blaspheme against the Son of God. And according to this the
Son of God became son of man, that the sons of man, that is of Adam, might
become sons of God. For the Word begotten from above from the Father
unspeakably, inexpressibly, incomprehensibly, eternally, the same is begotten
in time from below from the Virgin Theotokos Mary; that those who were first
begotten from below might be begotten from above a second time, that is, from
God. He therefore has a mother only upon earth, and we have a Father only in
heaven. And for this reason he calls himself Son of man, that men may call God
Father in the heavens; Our Father, he says, who is in the heavens. As
therefore we the servants of God have become sons of God, so the Master of the
servants became a mortal son of his own servant, that is of Adam, that the
sons of Adam, being mortal, might become sons of God, according to what is
said: He gave them authority to become children of God. Whence


the Son of God also tastes of death because of his fleshly father, that the
sons of man may partake of the life of God, because of their Father God
according to the Spirit. He therefore is Son of God by nature, but we by
grace. And again he by economy and our grace became son of Adam, but we by
nature are sons of Adam. Whence he says, I go to my Father and your Father,
and my God, and your God. For God is his Father, as I said before, by nature,
but ours by grace; and he became his God by economy, because he is man, but of
us he is by nature Master and God. And for this reason the Word and Son of the
Father, having been united to flesh, became flesh, a perfect man, that men,
having been united to Spirit, might become one Spirit. He therefore is God
bearing flesh, and we men bearing spirit. For having taken firstfruits from
the essence of men, that is from the seed of Adam (which is the form of a
servant), 997 and having become in the likeness of a man, he gave us from the
essence of the Father firstfruits of Holy Spirit; that we all might become
sons of God, in the likeness of the Son of God. He therefore, the true and by
nature Son of God, bears us all, that we all may bear the one God. It is
therefore impious to say that the Spirit of God is created or made; seeing
that all Scripture, both Old and New, numbers it together with Father and Son
and glorifies it; because it is of the same Godhead, and of the same
authority; as the Lord himself says: He who believes in me, rivers of living
water shall flow from his belly. But this he said concerning the Spirit, which
those who believed in him were about to receive; as also in Joel he says from
the person of the Father: I will pour out from my Spirit upon all flesh, and
your sons and your daughters shall prophesy. And for this reason he breathes
it into the face of the apostles saying: Receive Holy Spirit; that we may
learn that the Spirit given to the disciples is from the fullness of the
Godhead. For in Christ, he says, dwells, that is in his flesh, all the
fullness of the Godhead bodily; as also John the Baptist says: We all received
from his fullness. For in bodily form, that is as a dove, the Holy Spirit was
seen, descending and remaining upon him. For in us firstfruits and a pledge of
Godhead dwells, but in Christ all the fullness of the Godhead. And let no one
think that not having it he was receiving it; for he himself was sending it
from above, as God; and he himself was receiving it below, as man. From
himself therefore it was coming down unto himself, from his Godhead unto his
humanity. And Isaiah cries from the person of the Father saying: Thus says the
Lord your God, who made you and formed you from the womb: Fear not, my servant
Jacob, and beloved Israel, whom I have chosen; because I will give water in
thirst to those who walk in a waterless place; I will put my Spirit upon your
seed, and my blessings upon your children. But in the Gospel the Son prom 1000
ises to give water to those who walk in thirst, saying to the Samaritan woman
concerning the Holy Spirit: If you knew the gift of God, and who it is that
says to you, Give me to drink, you would have asked him, and he would have
given you living water. And after a little he says to her: Everyone who drinks
of this water will thirst again; but whoever drinks of the water which I will
give him will never thirst; but the water which I will give him will become in
him a fountain of water leaping up unto eternal life. And for this reason
David singing to God says: Because with you is a fountain of life, in your
light we shall see light; for he knows that with God the Father the Son is the
fountain of the Holy Spirit. And through Jeremiah the Son says: Two evil
things my people have done; they have forsaken me, a fountain of living water,
and have dug for themselves broken cisterns, which will not be able to hold
water. And when the seraphim glorify God, saying thrice: Holy, holy, holy Lord
Sabaoth, they glorify Father and Son and Holy Spirit. And for this reason,
just as into


the name of the Father and of the Son we are baptized, so also into the name
of the Holy Spirit, and we become sons of God, not of Gods. For Father and Son
and Holy Spirit is Lord Sabaoth. For the Godhead is one and one God in three
hypostases. And for this reason, the things which the Father said in Isaiah,
John says. that the Son said; but in the Acts Paul says that the Holy Spirit
said. For Isaiah speaks thus: I saw the Lord Sabaoth sitting upon a throne
high and lifted up; and the house was filled with his glory, and the seraphim
stood around him; six wings to the one, and six wings to the one; and with two
wings they covered the faces, and with two the feet, and with two they flew.
And one cried to the other, saying: Holy, holy, holy Lord Sabaoth, the heaven
and all the earth are full of his glory. And after a little the same: I heard
the voice of the Lord saying: Whom shall I send? and who will go to this 1001
people? And I said: Behold I, send me. And he said to me: Go to this people,
and I said to him: Hearing you shall hear, and shall not understand; and
seeing you shall see, and shall not perceive. For the heart of this people has
grown fat. And with the ears they heard heavily; and they closed the eyes;
lest they should see with their eyes, and hear with the ears, and understand
with the heart, and turn, and I should heal them. But the evangelist John
says: For this reason the Jews were not able to believe in Jesus, because
Isaiah said that He blinded their eyes, and hardened their heart; lest they
should see with the eyes, and understand with the heart, and turn, and I
should heal them. These things Isaiah said when he saw his glory, that is of
the Son; so that the Son also is Lord Sabaoth. For Lord of hosts is
interpreted Lord Sabaoth; But the Lord of hosts, he himself is the king of
glory, as David said; But the Lord of glory is the crucified Christ according
to Paul. And as David said: The Lord shepherds me, and nothing shall be
lacking to me; and elsewhere: You who shepherd Israel, give heed, who lead
Joseph like a sheep, who sit upon the cherubim, appear. But in the Gospels the
Son says: I am the good shepherd; he therefore is the one who sits upon the
cherubim, who shepherds Israel. But Paul in the Acts says that the Holy Spirit
has said these things through Isaiah, whichhesaid Isaiah, that Lord Sabaoth
said to me. The Father therefore and the Son and the Holy Spirit is Lord
Sabaoth. And when Scripture says concerning the Father, that He created all
things, the visible and the invisible; elsewhere Scripture teaches us that it
was created through the Son. The Father did not create some things apart, and
the Son others; but the things which the Father does, he does through his own
power, which is the Son; for all things came to be through him. Thus then also
when the Son says: Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it; but
he was speaking, he says, concerning his own body; another Scripture says that
The Father raised his Son from the dead; and it says well; for he raised
through his own Word and Son the flesh 1004 of his Son. And we do not say that
the body is Son of God by nature, but the body of the Son of God. When
therefore the body is raised, the Son is understood to have been raised from
the dead. For this reason we say according to the flesh that he both died, and
was buried, and was raised from the dead; but according to the spirit he was
both in heaven and on earth and everywhere. Since therefore formerly being
rich, that is God; but later he became poor, that is man, and was likened to
us in all things except sin; and for this reason as man he receives life, he
who has life, for he himself is the life; but he receives it for our sake, and
as man the eternal power and wisdom of God advanced in wisdom and stature, and
as man the holy one is sanctified, not having need of sanctification; For
them, he says, I sanctify myself, that they may be sanctified in truth. And as
man he is exalted having received a name which is above every name, which he
always has by nature. For God was, it says, the Word.


As many things therefore as Scripture says that the Son received, it says on
account of his body, which body is firstfruits of the Church; For firstfruits,
he says, is Christ. When therefore the firstfruits received a name which is
above every name, the lump also was raised together in power, and was seated
together, according to what is said: And he raised us together and seated us
together. And for this reason men received by grace to be called gods and sons
of God. First therefore the Lord raised his own body from the dead, and
exalted it in himself; after these things he will also raise the members of
his body, that he may grant them all things, as God, as many as he himself
received as man. He therefore grants life to himself, and he himself
sanctifies himself, and he himself exalts himself. When therefore he says that
the Father sanctified him, and raised him, and granted him a name which is
above every name, and gave him life; it is clear that the Father does all
things through him. Through him therefore he raises him, and through him he
sanctifies him, and through him he exalts him, and through him he gives him
life. And when he commits his spirit into the hands of the Father, as man he
commits himself to God, that he may commit all men to God. For he himself is
the hand, and the hands of the Father. And when he says: Before all the hills
he begets me, he speaks from the person of the Church, which, having been
created first, after these things is begotten from God. For this reason first
is set in the Prov 1005 erb, The Lord created me, and later, he begot. And
when Scripture says concerning the Father: Did not one God create us? likewise
also concerning the Son it says that through him all things were created; and
the same things are said also concerning the Holy Spirit; You will take away,
it says, their spirit, and they will fail, and they will return unto their
dust. You will send forth your Spirit, and they will be created, and you will
renew the face of the earth. And when the Son says concerning the Father to
Peter: Blessed are you, because flesh and blood did not reveal it to you, but
my Father who is in the heavens; he says the same things concerning himself:
No one knows the Father except the Son, and him to whom the Son may wish to
reveal him. And likewise also concerning the Holy Spirit Paul says: But to us
God revealed it through his Spirit. For the Spirit searches all things, even
the depths of God; for who of men knows the things of the man except the
spirit of the man which is in him, Thus also no one has known the things of
God except the Spirit of God. As therefore the spirit of the man is not
separated from his humanity and his essence, so also the Spirit of God is not
alien from his Godhead and his essence. And when the Lord says in Isaiah: I
begot sons and exalted them; but they set me at nought, in the Gospel he says:
That which is begotten of the flesh is flesh, and that which is begotten of
the Spirit is spirit. And again: The Spirit blows where it wills, and you hear
its voice; but you do not know whence it comes, and where it goes. So is
everyone who is begotten of the Spirit. But in the beginning of the Gospel
John says: But as many as received him, he gave them authority to become
children of God, to those who believe in his name; who were begotten not of
bloods, nor of the will of a man, nor of the will of flesh, but of God. As
many therefore as were begotten of Holy Spirit, these were begotten of God.
And as many as were baptized into Christ, these were baptized into Father and
Holy Spirit. And again, when Peter says to Ananias: Why has Satan filled your
heart, that you should lie to the Holy Spirit, and keep back from the price of
the field? You did not lie to men, but to God. So that he who lied to the Holy
Spirit lied to God, who dwells in men through his Spirit. 1008 For where the
Spirit of God is, there God is; For in this, he says, we know that God remains
in us, because he has given us of his Spirit And when Scripture says that Holy
Spirit was speaking in the prophets, elsewhere the blessed Paul says that the
Father was speaking in the prophets; For in many parts and in many ways of old
God having spoken to the fathers in the


prophets, in the last of this days he has spoken to us in a Son. And elsewhere
he says that the Son speaks; If you seek, he says, a proof of Christ who
speaks in me. But the Son said that the Spirit is the one speaking in the
apostles, When, he says, they deliver you up to councils, do not be anxious
how, or what you shall speak; for it will be given you in that hour what you
shall speak. For you are not the ones speaking, but the Spirit of your Father,
the one speaking in you. And at one time the Apostle says that the bodies of
the faithful are a temple of the Holy Spirit, at another that their members
are members of Christ, at another that they themselves are a temple of the
Father; For you, he says, are a temple of the living God, as God said: I will
dwell in them, and walk among them, and I will be God to them, and they shall
be my people; and, If anyone corrupts the temple of God, God will corrupt this
one. And again: Do you not know that you are a temple of God, and the Holy
Spirit of God dwells in you? For if anyone is a temple of the Spirit, this one
is a temple of the Son and of the Father; for where the Spirit of God dwells,
there God dwells. And, As the Father raises the dead and makes alive, so also
the Son makes alive whom he wills. And likewise also concerning the Spirit he
says that The Spirit is that which makes alive, the flesh profits nothing. And
to the Corinthians Paul writes: But the Spirit makes alive. You see that the
things which are works of the Father, these Scripture says belong to the Son,
and to the Holy Spirit; as also the Apostle taught saying: There are
distributions of gifts, but the same Spirit; and there are distributions of
minis 1009 trations, and the same Lord; and there are distributions of
operations, but the same God who operates all things in all. Having said
therefore that the Father is the one who operates all things in all, even
after a little he says that the Holy Spirit is the one who operates all things
in all. But all these things, he says, the one and the same Spirit operates,
distributing its own to each, as it wills. And when the blessed Paul teaches
concerning the Holy Spirit, that the Holy Spirit is a pledge of the
inheritance, David says that the Lord is the inheritance; For the Lord, he
says, is the portion of my inheritance and of my cup. And elsewhere he says: I
cried to you, Lord, I said, you are my hope, you are my portion in the land of
the living. But Jeremiah says that He who formed all things,he himself is the
inheritance of Jacob, Lord is his name. When therefore the prophets say that
the Lord is the inheritance of the saints, Paul said that the Holy Spirit is
inheritance; In whom, he says, having also believed, you were sealed with the
Holy Spirit of promise, which is a pledge of our inheritance; as also the face
of Moses was sealed with the Holy Spirit, when he received law from God, and
none of the sons of Israel was able to look intently at him; for there was
marked upon him the light of the face of the Lord; as also in the Gospel it is
written: Then the righteous will shine as the sun in the kingdom of their
Father. For God is light; and likewise the Son also is light, because he is of
the same essence of the true light, as also Isaiah says: And the light of
Israel shall be as fire, and it shall sanctify him in a burning flame. But in
the Gospel John the Baptist says concerning the Lord: He will baptize you in
Holy Spirit and fire. And elsewhere he says the Lord who sanctifies. I the
Lord who sanctify them. And when Scripture calls God fire: Our God is a
consuming fire, likewise also concerning the Holy Spirit it says; that There
appeared to the apostles tongues distributing as of fire, and it sat upon each
one of them, and they were all filled with Holy Spirit, and began to speak
with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them to utter. For as many as bear
Spirit of God, bear light; and those who bear light have put on Christ; and
those who have put on Christ have put on the Father. For this 1012 corruptible
must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality. For those
who bear the Spirit of God bear incorruption; and incorruption is God. And
when David says: Many say, Who will show us the good things? concerning the
Holy Spirit


it speaks; and elsewhere it has said: The Lord will not withhold the good
things from those who walk in innocence. And Matthew concerning the Father
speaks thus: If therefore you, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your
children, how much more will your Father who is in the heavens give good
things to those who ask him? But Luke narrating in the same manner interprets
what the good things are, saying: If therefore you, being evil, know how to
give good gifts to your children, how much more will your Father who is in the
heavens give Holy Spirit to those who ask him? For if the Holy Spirit were not
of the essence of the only good, it would not have been called good, seeing
that the Lord declines to be called good, inasmuch as he became man, saying:
Why do you call me good? no one is good except one, God. But Scripture does
not decline to call the Holy Spirit good, according to David who says: Your
good Spirit will guide me in a straight land. And when again the Lord says
concerning himself: I am the living bread which came down from heaven.
Elsewhere he calls the Holy Spirit heavenly bread saying: Give us this day our
super-substantial bread. For he taught us in the prayer in the present age to
ask for the super-substantial bread, that is the coming one, of which we have
firstfruits in the present life, partaking of the flesh of the Lord, as he
