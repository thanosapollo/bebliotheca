* On Measures and Weights, Greek Excerpt 7

Aquila was known, who was father-in-law of the emperor Hadrian. And he was a
Greek, as Hadrian, setting out from Sinope of Pontus, and later he was
baptized, having been persuaded by the apostles. And he was also an
astronomer, and being accused concerning this from those of the Church, and
not having been persuaded, afterwards having set aside the things of the
Christians, he becomes a proselyte to Jews and is circumcised a Jew, and
having learned with toil their dialect and the letters he interpreted an
interpretation for himself not with a right reasoning, but so as to distort
some of the sayings.
Under the consuls set forth a certain Theodotion of Pontus from the succession
of Marcion the heresiarch who set out from Sinope itself, and he himself being
angry at his own heresy and having turned aside into Judaism and having
learned their letters and tongue, privately also himself published the most
things agreeing with the 72 interpreters =92.652= In these times a certain
Symmachus a Samaritan, of the wise among them, not having been honored by his
own nation, having fallen ill with love of rule and being angry at his own
tribe, comes to Jews and becomes a proselyte and is circumcised a second
circumcision. This one then, having interpreted toward a distortion of the
interpretations among the Samaritans, published the third interpretation.
