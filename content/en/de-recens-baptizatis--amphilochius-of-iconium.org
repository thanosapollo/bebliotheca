* On the Newly Baptized
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of the blessed Amphilochius, bishop of Iconium, concerning the newly illumined
and on the resurrection of our Savior Jesus Christ.

Whenever the season of spring succeeds the gloom of winter, various birds
flying through the aether signal to men the sweetness of the season with
honey-voiced songs; then also sweet-voiced swallows, cutting the air with
sharp flight and skimming past the heads of men, cast their own cry like a
flower into the ears of men. Then one may see the air clear and windless, and
the faces of men made bright, remodeled into an imitation of the calm; for the
notes of birds delight the hearing, and clear air brightens the eye, and the
gleam of varied flowers sweetens the sight, and the mixed breath of herbs
restores the sense of smell. And this delight, beloved, the earthly and
perishable spring furnishes to men; but our divine and unstained spring,
Christ, having packed the meadow of the church with spiritual violets and
roses and lilies, brightens the sight by faith and fills the reservoir of our
heart with divine spices. For who of the faithful is not now gladdened? And
who now does not exult, when he sees the newly illumined shining in the image
of lilies with the bright calyxes of their robes, and possessing in the midst
of the heart the gold-gleaming faith? Here you will find, beloved, if you gaze
with the eyes of the heart, the heart of the faithful made purple with the
blood of Jesus like a very deep violet; for the flame-leaved rose blossomed
incorruptibly from the virgin Mary. What meadow possesses such fragrance as
our meadow possesses, the grace of the Spirit that gives forth spice? Smell
the newly illumined and you will find in him the immortal fragrance of the
Spirit, I mean the myrrh of the heavenly chrism. Here the palms of the
fathers, crowned with the victory-palms, put forth the sweeter fruits of love;
here the fair-voiced birds of the psalm-singers sing the psalms to God with
one voice as on a lyre; here virginity, offering to God the pure incense of
prayer like storax, rejoices; here temperance, sacrificing to God the cheerful
branch of chastity as Abraham sacrificed Isaac, takes delight; here the trunks
of the olives, putting forth to the poor the fruit-laden branch of well-doing,
delight the Lord and feed the beggar. Let virginity sing, saying: "Let my
prayer be set forth as incense before you, Lord." Let the merciful say: "But I
am like a fruitful olive tree in the house of God; for the righteous shall
flourish as a palm tree, he shall be multiplied as a cedar in Lebanon." Now
the gloom of the devilish winter has fled, and the cheer of the heavenly
meadow has shone forth; now the mourning of the dead has been put to flight,
for the radiance of the resurrection has come. Let us all sing together the
new song, for the new and blessed song befits the new way of life. Let us sing
the new song, for behold Adam has been renewed, for the old sin has been made
to vanish, behold all things have become new. Let us sing the new song, for
behold Adam has been renewed and Eve has been carried up into the heavens, for
the devil has been tripped into the fire. Let us borrow from Miriam the sister
of Moses that song, for it befits us now as it did them then. Let the choir of
those holy ones also stand with us and say what it then sounded forth at the
Red Sea: "Let us sing to the Lord, for he has been gloriously glorified." For
what has he done? "Horse and rider he has thrown into the sea." Horse and
rider: the horse, the sin that rages after women, and the rider, the demon
seated upon the sin, he has sunk in the baptism of the washing. Let us
therefore sing to the Lord, for he has been gloriously glorified; for "Pharaoh
and his host he has thrown into the sea," that is, he has drowned the devil
and the dark and accursed camp of his demons in the washing of baptism.
I no longer fear to hear: "You are earth and to earth you shall go"; for in
baptism I have put off the earth and put on heaven, and I hear: you are heaven
and to the heavens you shall go; "for as many of you as were baptized into
Christ have put on Christ," and: "as is the earthy, such also are the earthy,
and as is the heavenly, such also are the heavenly." We must mount upon clouds
and run up into the heavens; the word is not without witness; hear Paul
saying: "We shall be caught up in clouds to meet the Lord in the air, and so
we shall always be with the Lord." Therefore, just as we have just now heard
the psalmist exhorting us and saying: "This is the day which the Lord has
made, let us rejoice and be glad in it." Let us therefore rejoice, not being
darkened with drunkenness and surfeit, for that would not be gladness but
folly and a moonless night of the heart. Let us rejoice, leaping in the
Spirit; let us rejoice, feasting in love, rejoicing in hope. And if we must
also think of another gladness of heart, having received the heavenly bread
with clean palms, let us send it on to the soul, since it is provision for
eternal life; with clean palms, I say, not washed with common water, but made
bright with well-doing. And let us draw the divine and heavenly mixture with
rose-colored lips, not reddening them with some crimson dye, but making them
purple with the blood of Jesus Christ. O truly great and good day, in which
the lamb is sacrificed and the world is bought and our shepherd lives; "For I
am," he says, "the good shepherd." O new mystery and paradoxical wonder! A
cross was set up and Christ was stretched upon it, and unjust death was what
appeared, and a holy bridal chamber was what was taking place; a cross was
what appeared and a marriage-chamber was what was being accomplished;
yesterday a marriage-chamber arose and today a people was born. O death of
Christ, death of death, and one that wells forth sweet life in the most bitter
death! O mystery of Abraham being accomplished in Christ! Isaac was bound upon
the altar and a lamb was slain in his place, and the Son of God was lifted
upon a cross and was crucified in the flesh for us, and while the flesh
suffered the Godhead did not suffer. The mast of the cross was set in the ship
of the world and the world remained unshipwrecked; he who sails by this mast
of the cross knows no shipwreck of death, but sails up into the heavens. Eve
no longer fears the reproach of Adam, for in Mary her fault has been
healed; Adam no longer fears the serpent, for Christ has crushed the head
of the dragon; "For you," he says, "crushed the head of the dragon upon the
water," that is, upon baptism. I no longer mourn, I no longer weep saying: "I
was turned to misery when a thorn was stuck in me"; for Christ, coming, having
uprooted the thorn of our sins, set it upon his own head. My ancient grief has
been loosed; the old curse has been loosed that says: "Thorns and thistles
shall the earth bring forth for you"; for the thorns have been dried up, the
thistle has been uprooted, and a triple-plaited crown has been set upon my
head. Which of the Jews and Greeks believes that a tree was set up and life
blossomed forth? and every day it is harvested by the faithful and the fruit
remains inexhaustible; and every tribe and every nation of the faithful climbs
this tree with the step of the soul and fills the bosom of the mind with
immortal fruit, and the tree bears all and fills all and seals all and
enriches all and after these things hurls them into the heavens. For what has
Christ not endured to be called for our sake? We cannot number his names, but
I shall attempt to speak, beloved: door, way, sheep, shepherd, worm, stone,
pearl, flower, angel, man, God, light, fountain, sun of righteousness. The
many-named Christ, even while being one; the Son is many-named and being one
is not changing, not being altered, for the Godhead is unchangeable; but
fitting himself to each thing according to the operation, having set a proper
name upon each operation. And let us see, beloved, if we can set forth the
operation for each name. He was called Way: "For I am," he says, "the way and
the truth," since he himself has become the pioneer of all the virtues and of
the ascent into the heavens. The same is Door: "For I am the door of the
sheep," since through him, as through a door, we enter into the kingdom of the
heavens. He was called Salt, since he wiped away the stench of men's idolatry
and seasoned our souls toward the savor of piety by faith and bound together
the loosened members of the mind by the truth. He was called Sheep: "For he
was led as a sheep to the slaughter, and as a lamb before its shearer is
silent"; he was called sheep because of the slaughter and because of the
partaking of his holy members and his immortal protection that comes from
the seal of his holy blood. He was called Shepherd: "For I am the good
shepherd," since he turned back the sheep that had gone astray and killed the
lion that is our adversary with the staff of the cross. He was called Pearl
because he was born, as a pearl in a mussel, between two valves of a virgin
body and soul, without the seed of a man; or because he is an ornament of the
soul; or because of his many-shining and pure nature, flashing from every side
with the radiance of the Godhead. He was called Light, "for he was the true
light, which lights every man coming into the world," since he drove away from
our mind the gloomy error of the old moonless night and opened the eyes of our
governing mind, that we might truly see those to whom we formerly bowed down,
that they are not gods but works of men's hands, stones and woods. He was
called Cornerstone, since having built the two peoples as two walls by faith
he lies upon them, gathering them into one joint of salvation. He was called
Sun of righteousness because of his brightness and heat (for there is none who
shall hide from his heat), and because of the twelve-rayed light of the
apostles. He was called a grain of mustard, since he makes himself small and
condescends to our habits at the beginnings of the faith, then whenever,
having nested in the furrows of our soul, he is poured out into the depth,
being raised to a heaven-high height he is warmed toward piety and
understanding. He was called Worm: "For I am a worm and not a man," because,
having hidden the flashing Godhead like a hook in the worm of the body, having
let it down into this depth of life, and having drawn up like a good
fisherman, he fulfilled what is written: "You shall draw the dragon with a
hook." He was called Man, since he wore a body not composed of pleasure and
sleep, but come to be from a virgin and the Holy Spirit. But since each of us,
beloved, also awaits the honey-flowing teaching of the fathers, let us here
seal our purse of words with the Holy Spirit, that they themselves, coming,
may fill the reservoirs of our hearts from the honey-dropping wisdom of our
Lord Jesus Christ, and that each of you may sprout much fruit of piety, and
one may make thirty, another sixty, another a hundred, the crown of the
angels, in Christ Jesus our Lord, with whom to the Father be glory, honor,
power, together with the Holy and life-giving Spirit, now and unto all the
ages of ages. Amen.
