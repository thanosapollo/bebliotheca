OF GREGORY BISHOP OF NYSSA
A REFUTATORY DISCOURSE AGAINST THE EXPOSITION OF EUNOMIUS
1 The faith of the Christians, the one preached into all the nations
according to the command of the Lord by the disciples, is neither from men nor
through men, but through our Lord Jesus Christ himself, who, being Word and
life and light and truth and God and wisdom and all things that he is by
nature, for this reason especially became in the likeness of a man and shared
our nature in all things according to likeness without sin, and according to
likeness by taking up the whole man with soul and body, 2 so that through both
salvation might come to be for us. This one was seen upon the earth and
consorted with men, in order that men might no longer form opinions about the
existent according to their own judgments, making a dogma of the suspicions
that come to them from certain guesses, but, persuaded that God was truly
manifested in flesh, we might believe that that alone is the true mystery of
piety which was handed down to us through the Word and God himself, who spoke
through himself to the apostles. And the teaching about the transcendent
nature that came from the more ancient scripture, from law and prophecy and
proverbial wisdom, we receive through a certain mirror and riddle as a
testimony of the truth revealed to us, taking the meaning of the sayings
piously, so that it agrees with the faith set forth by the Master of all,
which we keep on the wording pure and unadulterated as we received it, and
judging even a small turning-aside of the words handed down 3 to be utmost
blasphemy and impiety. We believe then just as the Lord set forth the faith to
the disciples, the one who said, Going, make disciples of all the nations,
baptizing them into the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy
Spirit; this is the word of the mystery, in which through the birth from above
our nature is recast from the corruptible toward the incorruptible, being
renewed from the old man according to the image of the one who created in the
beginning the God-like likeness. This faith then having been handed down from
God to the apostles, we make neither subtraction nor alteration nor addition,
knowing clearly that the one who dares to twist the divine voice through
sophistic villainy is from the father the devil, who, leaving the words of the
truth, speaks from his own, becoming father of a lie. For everything said
besides the truth is altogether a lie and not truth.
4 Since then this dogma is set forth by the truth itself, if the inventors of
the wicked heresies devise anything for the setting-aside of this divine
voice, so as to name him, instead of Father, creator and craftsman of the Son,
and instead of the Son a work and creature and made thing, and instead of the
Holy Spirit a creature of a creature and a work of a work and all the things
that it seems good to the God-fighters to say about him, we name all such
things a denial of the godhead revealed to us in this dogma and a
transgression. For we have once for all learned from the Lord toward what we
must look with the mind, through which the transmutation of our nature from
the mortal toward the immortal comes to be; and this 5 is the Father and the
Son and the Holy Spirit. We say then that it is fearful and ruinous to reason
away these divine voices and to invent instead the ones found beside them for
their setting-aside, as if correcting the God Word who legislated that we
should have these voices in the faith. For each of these appellations,
understood through its proper meaning, becomes a canon


of truth and a law of piety for Christians. For though there are many other
names as well by which the divine is indicated in history and prophecy and
law, the Master Christ, leaving all of them, sets before us these voices as
more able to lead us toward the faith concerning the existent, declaring it
sufficient that we remain in the appellation of the Father and of the Son and
of the Holy Spirit for the understanding of the 6 truly existent, which is
both one and not one. For by the account of the essence it is one, wherefore
also the Master legislated that we look to one name; but by the identifying
properties of the hypostases it is divided into faith in Father and Son and
Holy Spirit, being partitioned without interval and united without confusion.
For when we hear Father, we take up this meaning, that this name is not
thought on itself alone, but also signifies through its own force the relation
toward the Son. For a father would not be thought separated on himself, unless
a son were joined through the utterance of father. Having learned Father then,
by the same 7 voice we were taught together the faith also toward the Son.
Since then the divine by nature, which is just as it is, always has itself in
the same way, neither ever not being something that it now is, nor ever going
to be something that it now is not, and the true Father was named Father by
the Word, and with the Father the Son also is co-indicated, we necessarily
believe that the one who admits no turning or alteration in the nature was
also altogether always what he now is, or, if ever he was not something, he is
not altogether now either. Since then he is named Father by the true Word, he
was altogether always Father and is and will be as he was. For it is not
lawful to say that upon the divine and unstained nature the good is not always
8 about it. For if he was not always what he now is, he was altogether turned
either toward the worse from the better or toward the better from the worse;
and of these the impiety is equal on either side, whichever of the two is said
upon the divine nature. But the divine is unreceptive of turning and
alteration. Therefore everything that is beautiful and good is always
contemplated about the fountain of the beautiful; and the only-begotten God
who is in the bosom of the Father is beautiful and beyond every beautiful,
being in the bosom, not coming to be in it. 9 Therefore it has been shown
through these things that the Son is contemplated from eternity in the Father
in whom he is, being life and light and truth and every good name and thought,
apart from which to say that the Father ever is on himself is of the utmost
impiety together with madness. For if the Son, as the scripture says, is power
of God and wisdom and truth and light and sanctification and peace and life
and such things, before the Son was, as it seems good to the heretics, these
things altogether were not either. And if these were not, they will altogether
think the paternal 10 bosom empty of such goods. In order then that the Father
may never be thought barren of his own goods, and that the dogma may not fall
out into this absurdity, necessarily according to the voice of the Master the
faith concerning the Son is co-contemplated with the eternity of the Father.
For which reason, leaving all the names that are said toward indication of the
transcendent nature, he sets forth to us in the faith the appellation of
Father as more indicative of the truth, which, as has been said, by the
relative meaning co-indicates also the 11 Son with itself. And since the Son,
who is in the Father, always is what he is, as has been said also in the words
before, because the divine by nature does not admit increase toward the
greater (for it sees no other good outside itself of which by participation it
takes on the more) but always having itself in the same way, neither throwing
off what it has nor taking on what it does not have (for it has nothing of the
things to be thrown off, and if anything is blessed and unstained and true and
good, it is altogether about him and in him, we necessarily see that the
Spirit, the good and holy, which is upright, princely, life-giving, holding
around and sanctifying all the creation, which works all things in all as it
wills, did not come to him from after-acquisition, so that we think no
interval


in between of the Christ toward the chrism or of the king toward the kingdom
or of the wisdom toward the spirit of wisdom or of the truth toward the spirit
of truth or of the power toward the spirit of  power) but, the Son being
contemplated from eternity with the Father, who is wisdom and truth and
counsel and strength and knowledge and understanding, from eternity the Holy
Spirit also is co-contemplated with him, which is spirit of wisdom and of
truth and of counsel and of understanding and all the other things that the
Son is 12 and is named. For this reason we say that the mystery of piety was
handed down to the holy disciples as both conjoined and distinguished, that
one must believe into the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy
Spirit. For the peculiarity of the hypostases makes the distinction of the
persons clear and unconfused, but one name set forward of the exposition
according to the faith clearly interprets for us the unity of the essence of
the persons in the faith, I mean of Father 13 and Son and Holy Spirit. For
through these appellations we are not taught a difference of nature, but only
the identifying properties of the hypostases; so that we know that the Father
is not Son, nor the Son Father, nor the Holy Spirit Son or Father, but that we
recognize each in the distinctive character of the hypostasis, contemplated in
unbounded perfection both on itself and not divided from the one joined with
it. 14 What then does the unnameable name signify, about which the Lord,
saying Baptizing them into the name, did not add the very significative voice
indicated by the name? We have this meaning about it, that all the things that
are in the creation are comprehended through the signification of names. For
the one who said heaven led the mind of the hearer upon the creature indicated
by the name, and the one who mentioned a man or some other of the animals by
name at once stamped the form of the animal upon the one who heard, and all
the other things likewise through the names laid upon them are painted in the
heart of the one who received through hearing the appellation laid 15 upon the
thing. But the uncreated nature alone, the one believed in the Father and Son
and Holy Spirit, is better than every nominative signification. For this
reason the Word, saying name in the tradition of the faith, did not add the
what (for how would a name be found upon a thing that is above every name?),
but gave authority, however our mind, moving piously, may have room to find
some name indicative of the transcendent nature, to fit this alike to Father
and Son and Holy Spirit, whether the good or the incorruptible, whatever each
thinks worthy to be taken up toward indication 16 of the unstained nature. And
this too the Word seems to me to legislate through this exposition, that we be
persuaded that the name of the divine essence is both unspeakable and
incomprehensible; for it is clear that the appellation of Father is not
presentative of essence, but signifies the relation toward the Son. Therefore
if it were possible for the human nature to be taught the essence of God, the
one who wills all to be saved and to come into knowledge of truth would not
have kept silent the knowledge 17 about this. But now, by saying nothing about
the essence, he showed that the knowledge of that is impossible, and that
having learned what we have room for we need nothing of the uncontainable,
having sufficient for our salvation the faith of the teaching handed down. For
to learn that that one is the truly existent, with whom through the relative
thought the majesty of the Son is co-revealed, is the most perfect teaching of
piety, the Son, as has been said, co-indicating conjoinedly with himself the
Spirit of life and of truth, since he himself is both life and truth. 18 These
things having been distinguished thus, anathematizing every heretical
supposition in the divine dogmas we believe, as we were taught by the voice of
the Lord, into the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,


confessing with this faith also the economy that came to be from the Master of
the creation on behalf of men, who, being in the form of God, did not count it
plunder to be equal with God, but emptied himself, taking the form of a slave,
and being made flesh in the holy virgin ransomed us from the death in which we
were held, having been sold under our own sin, giving as an exchange for the
ransom of our 19 souls his precious blood, which he poured out through the
cross; and having made a road for us through himself of the resurrection from
the dead he will come in his own times in the glory of the Father to judge
every soul in righteousness, when all who are in the tombs will hear his
voice, and will come out, those who did the good things into a resurrection of
life, and those who practiced the worthless things into a resurrection of
judgment. And in order that the heretical corruption now being scattered by
Eunomius, falling in unexamined upon some of the simpler, may not harm the
unmixed, we will necessarily set out in parts the exposition circulated among
them and will try to refute the wickedness of their dogma. The wording of
their dogma then is as follows: we believe into the one and only true God
according to the teaching of the Lord himself, honoring him not with a lying
voice (for he is without lie), but as truly being by nature and glory one God
without beginning, eternally, endlessly, only. Let the one who professes to
believe according to the teaching of the Lord not twist the exposition of the
faith that came from the Master of all toward what seems good to himself, but
let him follow the voices from the truth. There then, the word of the faith
containing the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, what
agreement do the things now set forth have with the voices of the Master, so
as to refer such a 21 dogma to his teaching? Where then in the gospel words
the Lord said that one must believe into the one and only true God, they would
not be able to show, unless there is some new gospel among them. For the
things that from ancient times until now are read in succession in the
churches do not contain this voice that says one must believe or baptize into
the one and only true God, as these say, but into the name of the Father and
of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. And as we were taught from the voice of the
Master, this we say, that the one does not signify the Father only, but the
Son also is co-indicated with the Father, the Lord saying thus, I and the 22
Father are one. And likewise the name God is invoked equally upon the
beginning in which the Word was and upon the Word who is in the beginning (for
he said, And the Word was toward God, and the Word was God), so that in the
thing signified of the godhead also the Son is co-contemplated likewise with
the Father. And besides these the true cannot be thought something other
besides the truth. And that the Lord is truth, no one will altogether
contradict, unless he should be alien from the truth. 23 If then the Word also
is in the one and is God and is truth, as is proclaimed in the gospel, to what
teaching of the Lord does the one who has used these distinguishing voices
refer the dogma? For the contrast of the only is toward the not only, and of
God toward the not God, and of the true toward the not 24 true. If then
looking toward the idols they make the opposition of the words toward those,
we too agree; for the name of the godhead is said of the idols of the nations
by homonymy; for all the gods of the nations are demons [and otherwise: the
only is opposed toward the multitude and the true toward the false and the
gods that are not toward the God who is]; but if the opposition comes to be
toward the only-begotten God, let the wise learn that truth has the contrast
toward falsehood 25 only, and God toward the one who is not God. And since the
Lord, who is truth, is God and is in the Father and is one toward the Father,
these distinguishing voices have no place upon the dogma. For the one who
truly believes into


the one sees in the one the one united to him through all things in truth and
godhead and essence and life and wisdom and simply all things; or, if he does
not see in the one the one who is these 26 things, he has the faith into
nothing. For without a Son a Father neither is nor is named, nor indeed
without power the powerful, nor without wisdom the wise; for Christ is power
of God and wisdom of God; so that the one who imagines he sees outside the
power or the wisdom or the truth or the life or the true light either sees
nothing or altogether sees the evil. For the taking-away of the goods becomes
a positing and existence of the evil. 27 Not with a lying voice, he says,
honoring him; for he is without lie. I pray that Eunomius remain in this
voice, bearing witness to the truth that it is 28 without lie. For if he
should think thus, that everything said by the Lord is separated from the lie,
he will be altogether persuaded that the one who said I am in the Father and
the Father is in me speaks truly, that is, whole in whole, neither the Father
overflowing in the Son nor the Son falling short in the Father, and that the
Son must be honored thus, just as the Father is honored, and that The one who
has seen me has seen the Father, and that No one knows the Son except the
Father, and that And no one recognizes the Father except the Son; through all
of which neither a variation of glory nor of essence nor of anything else is
suspected upon Father and Son by those who have received these voices as true.
Truly being, he says, by nature 29 and glory one God. The truly existent is
opposed toward the not truly existent. And each of the things that are is
truly existent just as it is; but what seems to be as far as a certain
imagination and suspicion, yet is not, this is not truly, as the
dream-apparition or the man upon the image; for these and such things, being
as far as imagination, do not have the truly to be. If then according to the
Jewish supposition they construct that the only-begotten God is not at all,
they do well to bear witness the truly to be to the Father alone; but if they
do not deny that the maker of all is, let them be persuaded not to deprive the
one who truly is of the truly to be, who named himself being in the theophany
that came to Moses, saying I am the one who is, as Eunomius agrees with this
in the following words, saying that this is the one who appeared to Moses. 30
Then he says by nature and glory one God. If then God is without being God by
nature, the one who says these things would himself know; but if the one who
is not God by nature is not God, let them learn from the great Paul that those
who serve the gods that are not by nature do not serve God, but we serve a
living and true God, as the apostle says, and the one whom we serve is Jesus
the Christ. For the apostle Paul also boasts to serve this one, saying: Paul,
a slave of Jesus Christ. We then who no longer serve the gods that are not by
nature have recognized the one who is God by nature, to whom every knee bends
of heavenly and earthly and subterranean. Whom we would not have served, if we
had not believed that this is the living and true God, whom every tongue
confesses, that Jesus is Lord unto glory of God the Father. 31 One God, he
says, without beginning, eternally, endlessly, only. Again Understand, you
guileless, villainy, says Solomon, lest being deceived you fall out into a
denial of the godhead of the only-begotten. The endless is what is unreceptive
of death and corruption, and likewise the eternal is said of what is not
seasonal. What then is neither eternal nor endless, this is altogether
contemplated in the corruptible and perishable nature. Therefore the one who
bears witness the endless to the one and only God, but does not include the
Son in the signification of the endless and the eternal, constructs through
such a word that he is corruptible and seasonal, the one who is 32
distinguished against the eternal and endless. But we, even if we hear that
God alone has immortality, think the Son through the immortality (for
immortality is the life, which is the Lord who said I am the life); and if it
is said that he dwells in light unapproachable,


again we do not doubt that the true light, which is unapproachable to the lie,
is thought the only-begotten, in whom we learned from the truth itself that
the Father is. And from these let the hearer choose the more pious, whether
one must thus form opinions about the only-begotten in a manner befitting God,
or say that he is corruptible and seasonal, as the heresy suggests. 33 Not the
essence, he says, according to which he is one, being separated or partitioned
into more, or becoming another at another time, or being displaced from being
what he is, nor being split from one essence into a threefold hypostasis; for
he is altogether and once for all one, remaining only according to the same
things and in the same way. And from these things set forth it befits the
intelligent hearer first to distinguish the empty words thrown into the
discourse without meaning from those that seem to have a mind, and after these
to inspect the meaning of the things said that is found 34 in the remainder,
whether it coincides with the piety concerning Christ. The first then of the
things set forth is separated altogether from every meaning, both good and
bad. For what meaning does Not the essence, according to which he is one,
being separated into more, or becoming another at another time, or being
displaced from being what he is have, neither would he himself say, nor do I
think any of those allied with him would be able to find some shadow of
meaning in the things said. Not the essence, according to which he is one,
being separated. Does he say that he is not separated from his own essence, or
does he say that the essence is not partitioned upon him? This insignificant
word is a certain noise and vain sound put together at random. And why must
one remain in the examination of these unintelligible 35 sayings? For how does
someone, having been separated from his own essence, remain in being? Or how
is the essence of someone, having been partitioned, shown on itself? Or how is
it possible that one who has gone out from that in which he is become another,
having become outside himself? But neither, he says, being split from one
essence into three hypostases; for he is altogether and once for all one,
remaining only according to the same things and in the same way. That there is
the unintelligible in the things said I think is clear to everyone even before
our words; let the one who thinks there is some mind or meaning in the things
said speak against these; for the word that has been trained to judge the
power of a discourse will 36 refuse to be entangled with the insubstantial.
For what force against our dogma has the saying not the essence, according to
which he is one, being separated or partitioned into more, or becoming another
at another time, or being displaced from being what he is, nor being split
from one essence into three hypostases, things that are neither said by the
Christians nor believed nor thought as a consequence through the things
confessed among us? For who ever said this or heard someone saying in the
church of God that the Father either is separated or is partitioned as to the
essence or becomes another at another time, becoming outside himself, or 37 is
split into three hypostases? Eunomius says these things to himself, not
fighting toward us, but stringing his own twaddle, mixing much mindlessness
with the impiety of the things said. For we say it is equally impious and
godless both to say that the Master of the creation is created and to think
that the Father, just as he is, is separated or cut or stands out of himself
or is split into three hypostases, as if some clay or wax being molded in
different forms. 38 But let us examine the following words: for he is
altogether and once for all one, remaining only according to the same things
and in the same way. If he says this about the Father, we too agree with this;
for the Father is truly one, only, and altogether once for all having himself
according to the same things and in the same way, and never, what he is,
either not being or not going to be. If then such a voice looks toward the
Father, let him not fight with the dogma of piety, agreeing with the church in
this part. For the one who confesses that the Father always has himself
according to the same things and in the same way, being one and only, confirms
the word of piety, seeing in the Father the Son, without whom a Father neither
is nor is named. But if he fabricates some other God besides the Father,


let him converse with Jews or with the so-called Hypsistians, whose difference
toward the Christians is this, that they confess there is some God, whom they
name highest or almighty, but do not accept that he is Father; but the
Christian, if he should not believe in the Father, is not a Christian. 39 And
the things he adds next to these are these. Not having, he says, a partner of
the godhead, not a sharer of the glory, not a co-heir of the authority, not a
co-enthroned of the kingdom; for one and only God is the Almighty, God of
gods, king of those who reign, Lord of those who lord. I do not know toward
whom Eunomius is looking when he testifies that the Father does not share the
godhead with him. For if he says such things toward the vain idols and the
deceived supposition of the idol-worshippers, as Paul also cries that there is
no agreement of Christ toward Beliar nor communion of a temple of God with
idols, we too agree; but if through the things said he marks off the
only-begotten God from the paternal godhead, let him learn that he constructs
against himself a two-horned 40 accusation of impiety. For either he denies
altogether that the only-begotten God is God, in order to keep for the Father
the unsharedness toward the Son in the godhead, and through this he is
convicted as a transgressor, having denied the God of the Christians, or if he
should grant that that one also is God, not coinciding according to nature
with the true God, there is every necessity to confess that he advocates gods
split from one another by the difference of the natures. Let him choose of
these which he wills, either to deny the godhead of the Son or to introduce
gods into the dogma; for whichever of these he chooses is equal unto impiety.
For we, being initiated from the God-inspired words of the scripture, do not
see a sharing of godhead in Father and Son, but unity, the Master having
taught this through his own words in which he says I and the Father are one,
and The 41 one who has seen me has seen the Father. For if he were not of the
same nature, how would he either have had in himself the alien, or how would
he have shown upon himself the unlike, the foreign and alien nature not
receiving the character of the heterogeneous? And he says that he does not
even have a sharer of the glory. And this he says as it is, even if he does
not know what he says; for the Son is not partitioned toward the Father as to
the glory, but has the whole glory of the Father, as also the Father has all
the glory of the Son. For thus he said toward the Father that 42 All my things
are yours and yours mine. Wherefore also in the time of the judgment he says
he will appear himself in the glory of the Father, when he is about to render
to each according to his works. And he shows through this voice the united of
the nature; for as one is the glory of sun and another the glory of moon
because the nature of the elements does not coincide toward one another (as if
the same glory were upon both, no difference of their nature would have been
thought), thus the one who foretold that he would appear in the glory of the
Father showed through the identity according to the glory the communion of the
nature. 43 And to say that the Son is not co-enthroned of the kingdom with the
Father bears witness to Eunomius much study of the divine oracles, who from
the excessive attention to the God-inspired scriptures has not yet heard that
Think the things above, where Christ is, sitting at the right of the Father,
and that He has sat at the right of the throne of God, and many other such
things, of which one would not easily number the multitude; which Eunomius,
not yet having learned, forbids that the Son is co-enthroned with the Father.
And the co-heir of the authority one must rather run past as mindless than
refute as impious. For what meaning the word co-heir has, it is not possible
to find from the common misuse of speech. They cast a lot upon the clothing of
the Lord, as the scripture says, those who did not wish to split the Master's
tunic, but to make it a possession of one of them, to whom 44 the lot might
grant it. Those then who cast lots toward one another over the tunic can
perhaps be called co-heirs; but here upon the Father and


the Son and the Holy Spirit the authority being in the nature (for the Holy
Spirit blows where it wills and works all things in all as it wills, and the
Son through whom all things came to be, visible and invisible, in the heavens
and upon the earth, did all things whatever he willed and makes alive whom he
wills, and the Father set the times in his own authority, and from the times
we think all the things that have come to be in time to be subject to the
Father); if then the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit have been shown,
as we said, in authority to do what they will, what mind the word 45 co-heir
of the authority has, it is not possible to see. For the heir of all things,
the craftsman of the ages, the one who shines together with the glory of the
Father and characterizes in himself the hypostasis of the Father, himself has
all things whatever the Father has and is lord of all the authority, the
dignity not being displaced from the Father into the Son, but both remaining
with him and being in that one. For the one who is in the Father is in the
Father with all his own power, clearly, and the one who has the Father in
himself encompasses all 46 the paternal authority and power. For he has the
Father whole in himself and not a part of him; and the one who has the whole
has his authority also altogether. What then Eunomius is thinking when he
declares that the Father does not have a co-heir of the authority, the
disciples of his vanity would perhaps say; for the one who knows how to listen
to words confesses that he cannot understand the things said without meaning.
The Father, he says, does not have a co-heir of the authority. For who is the
one who says that the Father and the Son fight toward one another from a
casting of lots over the authority? For whom the holy Eunomius, mediating
through a friendly settlement, without a lot assigns to the Father alone the
prerogatives of the 47 authority. See the laughable and childish of this
low-to-the-ground exposition of dogmas. Does the one who bears all things by
the saying of his power, saying what he wills to come to be and doing in the
power of the command what he wishes, whose power is concurrent with the will
and whose wish becomes the measure of his power (For he, it says, spoke and
they came to be, he commanded and they were created), the one who made all
things through himself and established them in himself, without whom none of
the things that are either came to be or remains in being, this one waits from
some casting of lots 48 to have the authority? Judge, you who hear, whether
the one who says these things has his mind in a settled state. For one, he
says, and only God is almighty. If then through the appellation of the
