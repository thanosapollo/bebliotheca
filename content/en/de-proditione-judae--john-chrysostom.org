ON THE BETRAYAL OF JUDAS, And on the Pascha, and on the delivery of the
mysteries, and concerning not bearing malice. On the holy and great Thursday.
Homily 1.
αʹ. It is necessary today to say a few things to your love; and it is
necessary to say a few, not because you are wearied by the multitude of what
is said; for it is not possible to find another city so lovingly disposed
toward the hearing of the spiritual oracles. We do not then say few things for
this reason, because we wear you out with the multitude, but because the cause
of brevity today is necessary; for I see many of the faithful hurrying toward
the communion of the dread mysteries. So that they may neither miss that table
nor fall away from this one, it is necessary to make the feast moderate, so
that the gain may come to you from both sides, and having been furnished from
this and from our words, you may thus go away, and with fear and trembling and
the fitting reverence approach the fearful and dread communion. Today,
beloved, our Lord Jesus Christ was betrayed; for on this coming evening,
taking him, the Jews went away. But do not become downcast when you hear that
Jesus was betrayed; or rather do become downcast, and weep bitterly, but not
for Jesus who was betrayed, but for Judas the betrayer. For the one who was
betrayed saved the inhabited world, but the one who betrayed lost his own
soul; and the one betrayed sits at the right hand of the Father in the
heavens, but the one who handed him over is now in Hades, awaiting the
inescapable punishment. For this man then weep and groan, for this man mourn,
since even our Master wept for this man. For seeing him, it says, he was
troubled and said: One of you will betray me. O how great the compassion of
the Master! the one betrayed grieves for the one who betrayed. For seeing him,
he was troubled, it says, and said: One of you will betray me. For what reason
was he downcast? At once both displaying his own affection and teaching us
that it is just everywhere to lament not the one who suffers evil, but the one
who does evil. For this is worse than that; or rather that is not even an
evil, to suffer evil, but to do evil is an evil. For to suffer evil procures
the kingdom of the heavens, but to do evil becomes for us a cause of Gehenna
and of punishment. For blessed, it says, are those persecuted for
righteousness' sake, for theirs is the kingdom =49.374= of the heavens. Do you
see how suffering evil has as wage and prize the kingdom of the heavens? Hear
how doing evil brings punishment and vengeance. For when Paul said concerning
the Jews that They killed the Lord, and persecuted the prophets, he added:
Whose end shall be according to their works. Did you see how those persecuted
receive the kingdom, while those who persecute inherit wrath? These things
have not been said by me now simply, but so that we may not be angry with
enemies, but may pity them and lament and suffer with them; for they are the
ones who suffer evil, those who are hostile to us. If we so prepare our soul,
we shall be able even to pray for them. For this is why I have been
discoursing to you this fourth day about prayer for enemies, so that the word
of the teaching may become lasting, rooted by the continuity of the
exhortation. For this reason I continually draw off with words, so that the
swelling of anger may subside, and the inflammation be allayed, so that the
one


approaching prayer may be clean of anger. For Christ did not exhort these
things only for the enemies, but also for us who forgive them their sins; for
you receive greater things than you give, when you let go anger against the
enemy. And how do I receive greater things? he says. If you forgive the sins
against the enemy, the offenses against the Master are remitted to you. Those
are incurable and unpardonable; these have much consolation and pardon. Hear
at any rate Eli saying to his sons: If a man sinning sin against a man, they
will also pray for him; but if he sin against God, who will pray for him? So
that that wound is not easily loosed even by prayer; it is not loosed by
prayer, but it is loosed by the forgiveness of the sins against the neighbor.
Therefore Christ called those ten thousand talents, the sins against the
Master; and these a hundred denarii. Forgive then a hundred denarii, so that
ten thousand talents may be forgiven you.
βʹ. But the things concerning prayer for enemies have been said sufficiently;
let us return, if it seems good, to the account of the betrayal, and let us
see how our Master was betrayed. Then going, one of the twelve, Judas who is
called Iscariot, to the chief priests, said: What are you willing to give me,
and I will betray =49.375= him to you? And what is said seems indeed to be
clear, and to hint at nothing more; but if someone examine accurately each of
the things said, it has much contemplation, and much depth of thoughts. And
first, the time. For the evangelist does not mark it simply; for he did not
say going simply, but added Then going. Then? tell me, when? and for what
reason does he mark the time? what does he wish to teach me? For Then has not
been said simply, for speaking by the Spirit he speaks neither simply nor at
random. What then is Then? Before that time, before that very hour a harlot
came, having an alabaster flask of ointment, and poured that oil upon the head
of the Lord. She displayed much attendance, she displayed much faith, much
obedience and reverence; she was changed from her former life, she became
better and more temperate. And when the harlot repented, when she drew the
Master to herself, then the disciple betrayed the teacher. For this reason he
said Then, so that you may not accuse the teacher of weakness, when you see
the disciple betraying the teacher. For so great was the power of the teacher
as even to draw harlots into his own obedience. What then, he says, did he who
draws harlots not have strength to draw a disciple? He had strength to draw
the disciple, but he did not wish to make a good man by necessity, nor to drag
him to himself by force. Then going. This too has no small contemplation, the
Going; for he was not summoned by the chief priests, not compelled nor forced,
but he himself from himself and from home begot the deceit, and brought out
this purpose, having no counselor of this wickedness. Then going, one of the
twelve. What is, One of the twelve? This too shows the accusation against him
to be very great, the saying One of the twelve. For there were other disciples
of Jesus, seventy in number; but those had the second rank, those did not
enjoy so much honor, nor share so much freedom of speech, they did not share
in secret things so much as the twelve. These were especially approved, and
this was the chorus about the king; this was the company about the teacher,
and from this Judas leaped out. So that you may learn that it was not a
disciple simply who betrayed him, but one of the most approved order, for this
reason he says: One of the twelve. And Matthew who wrote these things is not
ashamed. For what reason is he not ashamed? So that you may learn that
everywhere they speak all things with truth, and hide nothing, not even the
things that seem to be disgraceful. For these things that seem to be
disgraceful, these show the Master's love of man; that he counted the
betrayer, the robber, the thief, worthy of so many goods, and kept bearing
with him until the last hour; for he admonished and exhorted and took care in
every way. And if


he did not attend, the Lord is not the cause; and the harlot is a witness; for
she, attending to herself, was saved. Do not then despair of yourself, looking
to the harlot; nor be confident in yourself, looking to Judas. For both of
these are deadly, both being confident, and despairing; for being confident
makes the one who stands fall, and despairing does not let the one who lies
rise. For this reason Paul exhorted saying: Let him who thinks he stands look
=49.376= lest he fall. You have the examples of both, how the disciple
thinking he stood, fell; and how the harlot lying, rose. Our purpose is
slippery, our choice easily turned; for this reason we must fence and wall
ourselves from every side. Then going, one of the twelve, Judas Iscariot. Do
you see fromwhat chorus he fell? do you see what teaching he despised? do you
see how great an evil listlessness and neglect is? Judas who is called
Iscariot. Why do you tell me his city? Would that I did not know this man!
Judas who is called Iscariot. For what reason do you say his city? There was
another Judas a disciple, surnamed the Zealot. So that no error may arise
through the sameness of name, he divided this one from that one, and named
that one from virtue, Judas the Zealot; but he did not name this one from
vice; for he did not say, Judas the betrayer. And yet he ought, just as he
called that one from virtue, so also to address this one from vice, and say,
Judas the betrayer; but so that he may train you to keep the tongue clean of
accusation, he spares even the betrayer himself. For going, it says, one of
the twelve, Judas Iscariot, to the chief priests, he said to them: What are
you willing to give me, and I will betray him to you? O that polluted voice!
And how did it leap out of the mouth? how did it move the tongue? how did the
whole body not grow numb? how did the mind not go out of itself?
γʹ. What are you willing to give me, and I will betray him to you? Did Christ
teach you these things, tell me? was it not for this he said, Do not acquire
gold, nor silver, nor bronze in your belts, checking beforehand from above the
occasion of your love of money? did he not exhort these things continually and
with them say, If someone strike you on the right cheek, turn to him the other
also? What are you willing to give me, and I will betray him to you? O the
madness! In return for what, tell me? having what small or great charge, do
you betray the teacher? that he delivered to you authority over the demons?
that he made you able to loose diseases? to cleanse leprosies? to raise the
dead? that he set you over the tyranny of death? in return for these
benefactions do you give these repayments? What are you willing to give me,
and I will betray. him to you? O the madness! or rather, O the love of money!
for that begot all the evils; having desired that, he himself betrayed the
teacher. For such is that wicked root, it drives the captured souls more
fiercely than a demon into frenzy, and makes all ignorant, both of themselves
and of neighbors and of the laws of nature, and casts them out of their very
wits and makes them beside themselves. For see how many things it cast out of
the soul of Judas; the converse, the familiarity, the sharing at table, the
miracles, the teaching, the exhortation, the admonition; all these love of
money then threw into forgetfulness. For this reason Paul reasonably said that
The love of money is a root of all evils. What are you willing to give me, and
I will betray him to you? Great is the madness of the saying. Do you betray,
tell me, the one who holds all things, who has authority over demons, who
commands the sea, the Master of the nature of all? So then that he may check
his madness, and show that if he had not wished, he would not have been
betrayed, hear what he does. At the very time of the betrayal, when they came
upon him, having staves =49.377= and torches and light, he says to them: Whom
do you seek? and they did not know the one they were about to seize. So far
was Judas from being able to betray him, that he did not even see him present,
the one he was about to betray, and this though there were lamps


and so much light. For that the evangelist hinted this, he says: They had
torches and light, and they did not see him. And each day he kept reminding
him, both through works and through words, showing that he would not escape
notice in betraying. And he neither convicted him openly before all, so as not
to make him more shameless; nor was he silent, lest thinking he was hidden he
should attempt the betrayal without fear, but he kept saying continually, One
of you will betray me; yet he did not make him manifest. And he went through
many words also about Gehenna, many also about the kingdom, and displayed the
power in both directions, that in punishing those who sin, and that in
honoring those who succeed. But that man knocked all these aside, and God did
not drag him by force. For since he made us masters of the choice both of base
and of good actions, and wishes us to be good willingly; for this reason, if
we do not wish, he does not force nor compel; for to be good by being forced
is not to be good. Since then that man too was master of his purpose, and had
authority not to be persuaded, and not to decline toward love of money, for
this reason he was blinded in mind, and betrayed his own salvation; and he
says, What are you willing to give me, and I will betray him to you? So then
convicting the blindness of his mind and the madness, the evangelist says: At
the time of the assault Judas stood near them, that one who said: What are you
willing to give me, and I will betray him to you? And it is possible to behold
the power of Christ not from this only, but that even when he let out a bare
voice, they went away and fell to the ground. And when not even so they stood
off from shamelessness, he then delivers himself, all but saying: I have done
my whole part, I have uncovered my power, I have shown that you attempt
impossible things. I wished to check your wickedness; but since you did not
wish, but remain out of your wits, behold I deliver myself. These things have
been said by me, so that some may not accuse Christ, saying: Why did he not
change Judas? why did he not make him temperate and reasonable? How was he
bound to make him reasonable? by necessity, or by purpose? If by necessity,
not even so was he going to be better; for no one would become good by
necessity; but if by purpose and choice, he brought in all things able to
correct choice and purpose. And if he did not wish to receive the medicine,
the charge is not the physician's, but of the one who thrust the healing away.
See then how many things he did, so as to recover him and save him through. He
taught him all philosophy through deeds, through words, he made him superior
to the demons, he prepared him to work many miracles, he frightened him with
the threat of Gehenna, he urged him with the promise of the kingdom; he
convicted continually his secret counsels, and convicting he did not publish
them; he washed his feet with the others, he shared salt and table with him;
he left out nothing, not small or great; but he remained willingly
uncorrected. And so that you may learn that being able to change he did not
wish, but the whole came from his own listlessness, hear. For when he had
betrayed him, he threw =49.378= the thirty silver pieces, and says: I have
sinned betraying righteous blood. What has happened? When you saw him working
wonders, you did not say, I have sinned betraying innocent blood; but, What
are you willing to give me, and I will betray him to you? But when the evil
had advanced, and the betrayal had reached its end, and the sin had been
completed, then you recognized the sin. What then do we learn from this? That
when we are listless, not even exhortation benefits us; but when we are in
earnest, we are able even ourselves from ourselves to rise up. For this man
too, when the teacher was exhorting, did not hear; but when there was no one
exhorting, his own conscience rose up, and with no teacher present he changed,
and condemned what had been dared, and threw the thirty silver pieces. What
are you willing to give me, and I will betray him to you? And they set, it
says, for him thirty silver pieces. They paid a price


of blood that has no price. Why do you take thirty silver pieces, O Judas?
Christ came freely to pour out this blood for the inhabited world, over which
you make covenants and shameless contracts. For what is more shameless than
this contract?
δʹ. Then the disciples came. Then; when? When these things happened, when the
betrayal advanced, when Judas destroyed himself, The disciples came to him
saying: Where do you wish we should prepare for you to eat the Pascha? Did you
see a disciple? did you see disciples? That one betrays the Master, these are
anxious about the Pascha; that one makes covenants, these were preparing for
service. Both that one and these enjoyed the same miracles, the same
teachings, the same authority; from where then the change? From choice; this
is the cause of all things everywhere both of goods and of evils. Where do you
wish we should prepare for you to eat the Pascha? This evening was then; since
then the Master had no house, for this reason they say to him: Where do you
wish we should prepare for you to eat the Pascha? We do not have a defined
lodging, we do not have a tent, nor a house. Let those who build the splendid
houses, and the broad colonnades, and the long enclosures, learn that Christ
had nowhere to lay his head. Therefore these ask: Where do you wish we should
prepare for you to eat the Pascha? What Pascha? Not this one of ours, but the
Jewish one for the time; for that one the disciples prepared, but this one of
ours he himself furnished; and not only did he himself furnish it, but he
himself also became Pascha. Where do you wish we should prepare for you to eat
the Pascha? That Pascha was Jewish, that one took its beginning in Egypt. For
what reason then does Christ eat it? Because he fulfilled all the things of
the law. For even when he was being baptized, he said: For thus it is fitting
for us to fulfill all righteousness. I came to redeem man from the curse of
the law. For God sent his own Son born of a woman, become under the law, so
that he might redeem those under the law, and might give the law itself rest.
So that no one may say that he abolished it for this reason, because he was
not able to fulfill it, as being burdensome and grievous and hard to
accomplish, having first fulfilled all of it, then he dissolved it. For this
reason he also kept the Pascha; for the Pascha was a command of the law. And
for what reason did the law command to eat the Pascha? The Jews were
ungrateful toward the =49.379= benefactor, and even at the very benefactions
they forgot the ordinance of God. For even when they came out of Egypt, seeing
a sea splitting and coming together again, and ten thousand other wonders,
they said: Let us make gods for ourselves, who will go before us. What do you
say? the wonders are still in your hands, and you forgot the benefactor? Since
then they were so without feeling and ungrateful, God bound the memorials of
the gifts into the occasion of the feasts, and for this reason he also ordered
them to sacrifice the Pascha, So that, if your son ask you, it says, What is
this Pascha? you may say that Our ancestors once smeared the blood of the
sheep on the doors in Egypt, so that the destroyer coming and seeing might not
dare to leap in, and bring a plague. And the feast was thereafter a continual
memorial of salvation. Or rather they did not gain this only, that it reminded
them of the ancient benefactions, but also another greater thing, that it
prefigured the things to come. For that lamb was a type of another spiritual
lamb, and a sheep of a sheep; and the one was shadow, the other truth. And
when the sun of righteousness appeared, the shadow thereafter ceased; for when
the sun rises, the shadow is hidden. For this reason at that very table both
Paschas take place, both that of the type, and that of the truth. For just as
painters on the very tablet both draw the lines around and paint the shadow,
and then lay the truth of the colors upon it; so also Christ did; on the very
table he both sketched the typical Pascha, and added the true: Where do you
wish we should prepare for you


to eat the Pascha? It was the Jewish Pascha then; but when the sun has come,
let a lamp no longer shine, when the truth has arrived, let the shadow
thereafter be idle.
εʹ. These things I say to Jews, since they seem to keep Pascha, since with
shameless purpose the uncircumcised in hearts put forward the unleavened
bread. How, tell me, do you celebrate the Pascha, O Jew? The temple has been
razed, the altar has been taken away, the Holy of holies has been trampled,
every form of sacrifice has been dissolved; for what reason then do you dare
to do these unlawful things? You went away to Babylon once, and there those
who took you captive said: Sing to us from the songs of Zion; and you did not
endure. And David showing these things said: Upon the river of Babylon, there
we sat and wept; upon the willows in the midst of it we hung our instruments,
that is, psaltery, cithara, lyre, and the rest; for they used these of old,
and through these they sang the psalms. And these things they took when they
went away into captivity, so that they might have the memorial of the way of
life in the fatherland, not so that they might use them. For there, it says,
those who took us captive asked us words of songs. And we said: How shall we
sing the song of the Lord on a foreign land? What do you say? you do not sing
the song of the Lord on a foreign land, and you celebrate the Pascha of the
Lord on a foreign land? Did you see the ingratitude; did you see the
lawlessness? When those compelling were enemies, they did not dare even to say
a psalm on a foreign land; but now from themselves, with no one compelling nor
forcing, they introduce war against God. Do you see how unclean the unleavened
bread is? how unlawful the =49.380= feast? how it is not a Jewish Pascha,
There was a Jewish Pascha once, but it has been dissolved now, and the
spiritual Pascha has come upon it, which Christ was then delivering. For as
they were eating and drinking, it says, taking bread, he broke it and said:
This is my body which is broken for you for remission of sins. The initiated
know the things said. And again the cup, saying: This is my blood, which is
poured out for many for remission of sins. And Judas was present, as Christ
said these things. This is the body which you sold, Judas, for thirty silver
pieces; this is the blood, over which a little before you were making the
shameless covenants with the ungrateful Pharisees. O the love of man of
Christ! O the frenzy of Judas! O the madness! for he sold him for thirty
denarii; but Christ even after this did not refuse to give that very blood
that was sold for remission of sins to the one who had sold him, if at least
he had wished. For Judas too was present, and shared in the sacred table. For
just as he washed his feet with the other disciples, so he also shared in the
sacred table, so that he may have no pretext of defense, if he remain in the
wickedness. For he displayed and brought in all things from himself; but the
other remained keeping the wicked purpose.
ʹ. But it is time thereafter to approach this dread table. Let all then
approach with the fitting temperance and sobriety; and let no one still be
Judas, let no one be wicked, let no one have venom, not bearing some things on
the mouth, and others in the mind. Christ is present, and now that one who
arranged that table, this one also arranges this one now. For it is not a man
who makes the things set forth become the body and blood of Christ, but Christ
himself who was crucified for us. The priest stands fulfilling a figure,
uttering those words; but the power and the grace are God's. This is my body,
he says. This word reshapes the things set forth; and just as that voice which
says: Increase, and multiply, and fill the earth, was spoken once, but through
all time it becomes a work empowering our nature toward childbearing; so also
this voice, spoken once, at each table in the Churches from then until today
and until his coming, works the sacrifice completed. Let no one then be
underhanded, let no one of wickedness


full, let no one have venom in the mind, so that he may not partake unto
condemnation. For even then, after receiving the offering, the devil leaped
upon Judas, not despising the Master's body, but through the shamelessness
despising Judas, so that you may learn that upon those who share unworthily in
the divine mysteries, upon these especially the devil leaps and mounts
continually, just as also upon Judas then. For honors benefit those who are
worthy, but throw those who enjoy them beyond their worth into greater
punishment. And these things I say not so that I may frighten, but so that I
may make you secure. Let no one then be Judas, let no one entering have venom
of wickedness. For the sacrifice is spiritual food; and just as bodily food,
when it falls into a stomach that has wicked humors, increases the sickness
more, not from its own nature, but from the weakness of the stomach; so also
=49.381= it is accustomed to happen in the case of the spiritual mysteries.
For these too, whenever they fall into a soul full of wickedness, corrupt it
more and destroy it, not from their own nature, but from the weakness of the
soul that received them. Let no one then have wicked thoughts within, but let
us cleanse the mind; for we approach a pure sacrifice; let us make the soul
holy; for it is possible for this to happen even in one day. How and in what
manner? If you have something against the enemy, take out the anger, heal the
wound, loose the enmity, so that you may receive healing from the table; for
you approach a dread and holy sacrifice. Reverence the occasion of the
offering itself; Christ lies slain. And for what reason was he slain, and why?
So that he might make peace of the things in heaven and the things on earth,
so that he might make you a friend of angels, so that he might reconcile you
to the God of all, you being an enemy and a foe, so that he might make you a
friend. That one gave his soul for those who hate; but you remain hostile to
the fellow-servant? and how can you walk to the table of peace? And that one
did not refuse even to die for you; but you do not endure to let go anger
against your fellow-servant for your own sake? and of what pardon are these
things worthy? He insulted, he says, and overreached in the greatest things.
And what is this? The loss is altogether in money; for he has not yet wounded,
as Judas did Christ; but nevertheless even that very blood which he poured out
he gave for salvation to those very ones who poured it out. What equal to this
do you have to say? If you do not forgive the enemy, you have not wronged that
one, but yourself; for that one you often harmed for the present life; but
yourself you have made unpardonable toward the defense on the coming day. For
God hates nothing so much as a man who remembers wrongs, as a swelling heart
and an inflamed soul. Hear at any rate what he says: When you offer your gift
at the altar, and there remember standing above at the altar, that your
brother has something against you, leave your gift at the altar, and go away,
be reconciled to your brother, and =49.382= then offer your gift. What do you
say, I shall leave it, Yes, he says; for for the sake of the peace toward your
brother this sacrifice also has come to be. If then the sacrifice has come to
be for the sake of the peace toward the brother, and you do not accomplish the
peace, you share in the sacrifice in vain; for the achievement has become
unprofitable for you. Do that first then, for the sake of which the sacrifice
has been offered, and then you will enjoy it well. For this reason the Son of
God came down, so that he might reconcile our nature to the Master; for this
reason he did not come himself only, but so that he might also make us who do
such things sharers of his own name. For blessed, it says, are the
peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of God. For what the only-begotten
Son of God did, this do you also according to human power, becoming a procurer
of peace both for yourself and for the others. For this reason he also calls
the peacemaker a son of God; for this reason also at the time of the sacrifice
he mentioned no other commandment, except the reconciliation toward the
brother, showing that this is greater than all. I wished to stretch out the
word, but even the things


said suffice for those who attend, if they remember. Let us then remember
throughout these words, beloved, and the holy kisses, and the most dread
greeting toward one another. For this weaves our minds together, and makes all
become one body, since we all also share in one body. Let us then mix
ourselves into one body, not blending the bodies with one another, but joining
the souls to one another by the bond of love; thus we shall be able with
freedom of speech to enjoy the table set forth. For even if we have ten
thousand justifications, but are mindful of wrongs, all is in vain and to no
purpose, and we shall be able to harvest nothing from them toward an account
of salvation. Knowing these things then, let us dissolve all anger, and
cleansing our conscience, with meekness and all reasonableness let us approach
the table of Christ, with whom to the Father be all glory, honor, power,
together with the Holy Spirit, now and always, etc.

On the divine and mystical supper of the Savior, and concerning the betrayal
of Judas, and on the Pascha, and on the delivery of the divine mysteries, and
concerning not bearing malice. Spoken on the holy and great Fifth-day.
Homily 2.
αʹ. I wished, beloved, to take hold again of the subject concerning the
patriarch, and from there to set before you the spiritual feast; but the
ingratitude of the betrayer draws our tongue toward the subject concerning
him, and the season of the day urges us to speak the madness of his daring.
For today our Lord Jesus Christ was betrayed into the hands of the Jews by his
own disciple. But you, hearing this, beloved, do not become downcast, nor be
gloomy hearing that the Master was betrayed; or rather both groan and weep,
not for Jesus who was betrayed, but for Judas who betrayed him. For Jesus who
was betrayed saved the inhabited world, but Judas who betrayed lost his own
soul; and Jesus who was betrayed sits at the right hand of the Father in the
heavens, but Judas who betrayed is now in Hades, awaiting the inescapable and
eternal punishment. For this groan, for this =49.382= weep; since even our
Master Jesus Christ himself seeing Judas was troubled and wept. For seeing
