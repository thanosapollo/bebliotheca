ON THE 118TH PSALM,

That is, the blameless. Stasis 1.

=55.675=

1. A great treasure and spiritual wealth and a most welcome profit of souls,
both toward the praise and doxology of God, and toward the security of our
life and a pattern of good works, at the turning of the week on the first day
(which is also named the Lord's day among us, because of the Lord who rose on
it) the renowned leaders and teachers, our holy fathers, handed down to us to
sing and chant the hundred and eighteenth psalm. For it is an elementary
instruction and a guidance of the life according to God. For thus it also has
its inscription, since it trains those who attend and search out its meaning
exactly toward perfection of life, and according to its name it makes them
blameless. And blameless is the one who has been set free from every pollution
and defilement and filth, and is outside every stain and lawlessness and sin;
and the same is also blessed, because after the departure from this life he
will be counted worthy of the true blessedness, and will be made splendid with
the light of divine knowledge, and will lodge in rest and eternal delight,
which is the kingdom of the heavens. Whence also the hymnographer David,
perceiving with prophetic eyes the grace of holy baptism, cried out: Blessed
are they whose lawlessnesses have been forgiven. But those who have kept this
grace, and have acted according to the covenant, and are brought to God and to
holy baptism, these he blesses in this psalm as succeeding in practical
virtue. And the subject of this psalm has also another most lofty meaning. For
this same divine David had many and various changes of affairs. For he fled
enemies, and he pursued enemies, and he fell into despondencies, and again he
had a more welcome life; and he ran the divine way, and he stumbled while
traveling, and again he followed the divine laws. These things then he
gathered all together in this psalm, and fitting to one another the prayers
offered by him to God at each season, he set before men one profitable
teaching, and he teaches through the things he says how it is possible to
succeed in practical virtue. Nor does he neglect the exactness of the
doctrines, but to the ethical counsels he also adds the teaching concerning
these, so that this psalm suffices both to perfect in it those who desire
perfect virtue, and to awaken to zeal those who live with ease, and to lead
the despondent, and to train the slothful, and in a word to put every kind of
healing into the varied passions of men. =55.676= Blessed are the blameless in
the way, who walk in the law of the Lord. As if we should say, blessed are
those who in this present life walk in the law of the Lord. For way in the
prophet is said of the present life, because it has nothing standing or firm.
For our life is a shadow, and after the manner of smoke it passes quickly. For
so the apostle James said: For what is our life? For it is a vapor that
appears for a little, then vanishes. And something of this sort the theologian
Gregory also philosophized: a certain circle of our affairs runs round,
bringing changes now this way now that in a single day, and sometimes even in
an hour. We brought nothing then, brethren, into this world, as the divine
apostle says, nor shall we be able to carry anything out. Let us take thought
for the things to come;


for these remain. He blesses then those who have traveled the royal way
without turning, and have not been turned aside to the right or to the left
according to the counsels of the law. And who these are, he teaches: Who walk
in the law of the Lord. For those who live according to these have the
blameless. For way, as I said before, he calls the life. For this we all
travel from birth-pangs to the tomb. Blessed are they who search out his
testimonies; they will seek him with the whole heart. And interpreting the
eighteenth psalm we have said how law differs, and testimonies, and
justifications, and judgments, and commandments; yet we will recall in summary
what was said. He calls law that given by the God of all through the inspired
Moses; and commandments and ordinances again the same, as commanded and
ordained in kingly fashion; justifications, as able to justify the one who
succeeds; and judgments, as showing the divine votes and the due recompenses
of those who live lawfully and of those who live unlawfully; and testimonies,
as witnessing and showing what punishments those who transgress will undergo.
He blesses then here also those who continually search the testimonies of God,
and who seek God with the whole heart. And this is not of everyone, but of the
one who has reached the very summit of virtue. For such a one does not divide
the mind between God and cares of life, but offers his whole self to God. For
both through reading and through hearing of the divine Scriptures our life is
set right. The prophet then blesses those who search out his testimonies. For
this the Lord also said: Search the Scriptures, because you think you have
eternal life in them. And the one who advances in this life by the search of
the divine Scriptures, and does not divide his life into pleasures and
passions of sins, but both living by the Spirit and walking by the Spirit,
searching the Scriptures, then as perfect seeks God with the whole heart.
=55.677= And since this psalm densely sets forth law and commandments and
justifications and judgments and testimonies, it is useful to treat of these
from the beginning. Law then was given by God, because he himself is king of
those who reign and Lord of those who lord it. And law is a common charge upon
all men: rulers, ruled; slaves, masters; rich, poor. And it is named from
distributing. For it has been legislated for all men, You shall not commit
adultery, you shall not murder, you shall not steal, you shall not bear false
witness, you shall not covet your neighbor's field, honor your father and your
mother. And the same is also called commandment, because God commands you to
do the good and to keep from the base. And justifications and judgments,
inasmuch as the righteous who keep the commandments are justified before God,
and those who transgress are condemned as sinners. And testimonies are so
named, since God testifies through his divine Scriptures to keep all his
commandments. For they who work lawlessness have not walked in his ways. He
has made this division also in the first psalm. For there too, having blessed
the one who meditates in the law of the Lord day and night, and having shown
the fruit of the meditation, he added: Not so the ungodly, not so. So here
too, having blessed the blameless, he teaches that the workers of lawlessness
have prepared another way for themselves, and have left the one set by God.
The prophet then, having blessed those who seek God with the whole heart, adds
saying: For they who work lawlessness have not walked in his ways. You have
commanded your commandments to be kept exceedingly.

2. This both through the law and the prophets and through the holy Gospels the


Master charges, and all the divine Scripture is full of such legislation. For
he does not simply pledge to keep, but to keep Exceedingly, that is, with all
exactness; and not to keep one and set another aside, but he has said to keep
all the commandments Exceedingly. Wherefore another prophet also said: I will
not pass from your commandments, that I may not die. O that my ways might be
directed, to keep your justifications. For this reason, he says, I entreat
that my way be directed by your law as by a certain rule, so that I may keep
your justifications. And O that is an utterance of prayer, i.e. to say, may it
be granted me to be directed to keep all your commandments. Since every man,
though he has a desire to keep the commandments of God, if he does not have
him as fellow-worker of well-doing, would not arrive at the end toward what is
desired; for it is written, It is not of him who wills, nor of him who runs,
but of God who shows mercy. Therefore the beginning of the good is from us,
but coming to the end needs the help of God; for Draw near, he says, to God,
and he will draw near to you. Then I shall not be ashamed, when I look upon
all your commandments. For shame is the fruit of transgression. So also the
divine apostle said: What fruit then did you have then, in the things of which
you are now ashamed? But those who fulfill all the commandments of God have
the boldness that comes from conscience. Then: when? When, according to the
prophet Daniel, The righteous will rise to eternal life, and the sinners to
eternal shame. The prophet then entreats not to be ashamed then. And how does
this come about? By looking upon all the commandments: not doing one and
leaving another. For he who said, You shall not commit adultery, said also,
You shall not fornicate; and he who said, You shall not fornicate, said also,
You shall not do injustice. And Whoever will fulfill the whole law, but
stumble in one, has become guilty of all, according to the apostle James. I
will confess to you in uprightness of heart, when I have learned the judgments
of your righteousness. He calls confession thanksgiving. Then I shall be able
to offer you a pure hymn, when I have learned all your judgments, and live
according to them. For he who has good deeds, that one confesses In
uprightness of heart, repenting from the whole heart, as David over the
adultery, and as the publican crying, I have sinned, and, Be merciful. Judas
also confessed, saying: I have sinned in betraying innocent blood; but not In
uprightness of heart; for he was held by love of money. The Pharisee also
confessed; but =55.678= having a heart high and not upright, he went down not
justified, saying that I am not as this publican. I will keep your
justifications; do not abandon me unto exceeding. Often the divine grace
leaves some for a little, devising benefit for them from this. Thus the great
Elijah, being abandoned, fell into the passion of cowardice, and learned the
weakness of human nature; but at once again he enjoyed the divine grace. Thus
the divine Peter slipped in denying, but at once the Master undergirded him.
But Judas, becoming entirely stripped of the divine providence, became a ready
prey of the devil. The blessed David also was abandoned after that sin, and
fell into those hard calamities, but again he recovered boldness toward God,
and enjoyed the care from there. But Saul, becoming entirely empty of this,
was stripped of the grace of the Spirit, and was yoked to an evil spirit.
Fitly then the prophet entreats not to be abandoned unto exceeding; that is,
not to be stripped altogether of the grace that provides. And again then we
will use the same thought, through the prophet's saying, Do not abandon me
unto exceeding. Abandonment by God then comes about in many ways through
certain economies and benefactions; as when Job was left that he might become
more perfect.


Elijah also was left, as we have already said, withdrawing for a little from
the face of Ahab and Jezebel; but something else was being economized, that he
might come to Zarephath of Sidon, and be entertained by a widow woman, and
furnish her food. For the handful of meal shall not fail, he says, nor the oil
of the cruse; and that through the wickedness of the Jews he might shut the
heaven three years and six months not to rain. For he says: As the Lord lives,
there shall be no rain upon the earth, except by the word of my mouth. To be
left by God unto exceeding, this is terrible, as Saul was left and Absalom,
who no longer turned back toward salvation; for they were stripped of the
providence of God. Whereby shall a young man direct his way? By keeping your
words. Youth is quick to turn toward sin, being led away into pleasures and
passions of dishonor; but if it is held by the keeping of the commandments of
God, it remains unharmed; for what a bridle and a horse-breaker is to a herd
colt, this the divine word becomes to the young. Since Daniel also was young;
but he kept the commandments of God, and was counted worthy of prophetic
grace, and became an initiator of the unspoken mysteries. Likewise the three
children also, being young, by their faith and works changed the fire into the
contrary nature; for they were cooled in the midst of it, and were dewed with
the boldness of faith and the breeze of the Spirit. Thus then it is present
also for us. Let those who are young adorn themselves through the keeping of
the commandments of God, and not become idle of age, as Absalom, who lived
with wickedness; who also had accompanying it a head of long hair, the one
without sense in his wits. For hair is a symbol of deadness. And for him the
hairs became causes of destruction, when he was hung from them in the oak, as
the history of the Kings narrates. But it is hard, that even now some,
emulating this man, hang down very long curls and plaits, spreading the works
of the women's quarters over the loin in sluggishness, puffed up in vain in
their own mind; and they do not know that they rather bring dishonor on
themselves, the Apostle saying: If a man wear long hair, it is a dishonor to
him. And Greeks are recorded in Homer as doing this; for he says, Long-haired
Achaeans. Since the wise among physicians also say this. For seemliness of the
head then, having measured hair according to the adornment of the ancient men,
let us be content, and let us not wish these to lie over the loin, nor over
the back; for it is a dishonor to the one who does such a thing, as we have
heard the Apostle counseling, With my whole =55.679= heart I have sought you;
do not thrust me away from your commandments.

3. For many seek God, but not with the whole heart; for they divide this not
only into cares of affairs of life, but also into unseemly desires and envies
and deceits and plots against neighbors. But the lover of the divine things
consecrates the whole mind to God, and enjoys the providence from there, not
falling from his commandments into a pit of destruction, but walking with the
living God, and offering his whole mind to him. In my heart I have hidden your
oracles, that I might not sin against you. He who has acquired gold or silver
or precious stones does not set these outside, but hides them within in the
treasuries and the chambers, so as to escape the hands of house-breakers; so
he who has the wealth of virtue hides this in the soul, so that the robbers of
souls may not plunder it through empty glory. And otherwise: I have not
hidden, he says, gold or silver or a soft and flowing garment, where moth and
worm make away, and thieves dig through and


steal; but in the treasuries of the heart I have hidden your oracles, says the
prophet, that I may not fall into sin. And the word teaches also another
thing; for it pledges not to offer the divine oracles to all: for Do not give,
he says, the holy things to the dogs, nor cast your pearls before the swine.
And even if these things have been said by him, yet we who sing are debtors to
keep the confession of our hymnody. And the word has also another sense. I do
not do the good things to be seen by men, since I have my reward; but I do the
fair thing for myself, that God who sees in secret may repay me in the open.
Blessed are you, O Lord; teach me your justifications; i.e., you are gentle
and loving toward man, and worthy to be hymned by all; for which cause I
entreat to learn from you the things that can justify. Or thus: you are
blessed and glorified above all, because you are loving toward man,
compassionate and merciful, long-suffering and of much mercy; I entreat, teach
me your justifications. I do not desire wealth, I do not seek gold, nor
silver, nor rank, nor any of the delights of this world; but I seek to learn
your justifications, because in keeping them there is much recompense. For
this is a Christian's seeking. In my lips I have declared all the judgments of
your mouth. For it is not fitting for one who knows the judgments of God to be
silent in himself, but to declare them, and to set others right; for to
announce the works of God is glorious. For whatever I may learn from your
goodness, these I will teach those who do not know. In the way of your
testimonies I was delighted, as over all wealth. The way of virtue is
toilsome, but thrice-desired to the perfect. For this very reason the Master
Christ also said: Take my yoke upon you, because my yoke is kind, and my
burden is light. This the prophetic word also said: that the possession of
your testimonies is more welcome to me than all wealth. And, Over all wealth,
is not simply added, but it signifies wealth of every kind. For many are the
forms of wealth. For one who has gold is named rich; another silver; another
very much land, some planted, some also sown; another cattle. But the prophet,
taking all these together, and comparing them with the divine testimonies,
said that the divine testimonies are worthy of love to him, as to lovers of
wealth all the forms of wealth; and as the one who is rich in these is
gladdened, so also very fitly he exults over the justifications of God, as
over all wealth. In your commandments I will meditate, and I will consider
your ways. The prophet promises to be at leisure and to meditate in the
commandments of God, and not to have these as a side-work. For this cause, he
says, I will have the meditation of your words continually, and I will make my
journey in your ways. For this reason he also considered his ways; wherefore
he also adds: In your justifications I will meditate; I will not forget your
words; that is, I will keep in myself an unforgettable memory of your oracles.
Recompense to your servant. Not all of us make this entreaty; for the entreaty
is of the righteous. Those who live soberly and piously and justly, these say
with confidence: Recompense to your servant; but those in =55.680= sins and
pleasures and passions of dishonor cannot say: Recompense to your servant. For
no one carrying about an evil conscience stirs up the judge to recompense; but
he who uses clean reasonings uses this voice with boldness. I shall live, and
I will keep your words. I ask this life, that I may walk in the keeping of
your words; for enjoying your aid, I will escape the death of sin, and I will
pluck the life from your words. Uncover my eyes, and I will consider the
wonders from your law. Not all who meet with the divine oracles


consider the wonders of them, but those who enjoy the brightness from above;
for which cause the blessed Paul also said: But when he turns to the Lord, the
veil is taken away; and the Lord is the Spirit. He himself then, being shone
upon by the divine light, fitly cried: But we all, with unveiled face,
mirroring the glory of the Lord, are transformed into the same image, from
glory to glory, even as from the Lord the Spirit. And it befits us to entreat
the Master, that taking away the veil of the eyes of our mind, he may show the
power of the divine oracles. For we mirror and understand the divine
Scriptures according to anagogy and contemplation; as that the lamb of old
slain by the Israelites was a type of the true lamb, Christ our God, who was
sacrificed for us; and as there the anointing of the blood of the doorposts
saved those who anointed, so the blood of the true lamb, Christ our God,
redeemed us from the curse of the enemy and the continuance of death. And
again the Israelite people, having crossed the Jordan and entered into the
land of promise, became a type of us Christians; because we through the water
of holy baptism are led into the promised land, the kingdom of the heavens.
But Hebrews consider no such thing, because A veil lies upon their eyes, as
the divine apostle Paul says. I am a sojourner on the earth; do not hide from
me your commandments.

4. A voice of a righteous man, and of one living according to God, and not
reckoning the affairs of the world. Wherefore he also calls himself a
sojourner; as also Abraham and all the saints are sojourners in this life,
having their dwelling in the kingdom of the heavens. For this earth we all do
not inhabit, but sojourn; for having lived a short time, we pass over into
another life. And this not all are willing to know, but they swagger over the
present goods as over things lasting and enduring; but the one trained in the
divine things knows the temporary character of life. Wherefore he also names
himself a sojourner, and entreats in no way to be ignorant of the divine
commandments. My soul has longed to desire your judgments in every season.
Many desire the divine judgments, and long for the kingdom of the heavens, but
not always, but now they are pleased with the divine things, and now they
follow the passions of the body; but the prophet longs to have this desire
continually. In every season then he says, not as longing at one time, and at
another not. For some know that a piercing comes to them, at one time to long
for the commandments of God through fear of eternal punishment and through
desire of the kingdom of the heavens, and at another they are led away by
fleshly desires. Such then do not long for God in every season; but those who
do this through the love of God do not at all stand away from the judgments of
God. You have rebuked the proud. Those who despise the divine laws fall into
the disease of pride, and receive the punishment from the just judge. Some
then were lifted up against God, but they were rebuked by him, as Pharaoh
perished in the Red Sea having been proud, and Sennacherib, lifted up on high,
perished by his own sons. Likewise also Nebuchadnezzar, having lifted up his
heart, fed like cattle. For such are the wages of the boastful; for the Lord
arrays himself against the proud, as it is written in Proverbs. Accursed are
they who turn aside from your commandments. By the inspiration of the divine
Spirit prophets spoke, as also in the symbol of our faith we confess, Who
spoke through the prophets. The Holy Spirit itself then


cries. =55.681= Accursed are they who turn aside from your commandments. Let
us fear then, brethren, lest the curse of the Holy Spirit come upon us. This
curse the law also offers to the transgressors. For Accursed, he says, is
everyone who does not remain in the things written in the book of the law, to
do them. And we also, singing, become of one voice with the Holy Spirit,
sending with the curse those who turn aside from the commandments of God. Let
us take heed then, lest while calling others accursed, we ourselves become so
by turning aside from the commandments of God. Take away from me reproach and
contempt, because I have sought your testimonies. Nabal reproached the
prophet, calling him a runaway slave; the Gittites also reproached him.
Grieved then at these things, he entreats that the reproaches be wiped off,
inasmuch as he has sought the divine testimonies. The present sayings then do
not fit the reproaches from Shimei. For those came after the sin; but those of
Nabal and the others before the sin. In another psalm then the same prophet
says: Reproach to those who prosper, and contempt to the proud; and, These are
sinners and prospering they have held wealth forever; and again another
prophet says: Why do sinners prosper, and they have grown old also in wealth?
The prophet then prays that this reproach and contempt be taken away from them
(perhaps from him). For the affairs of this world, wealth and glory: these he
calls reproach and contempt, thinking this fame and honor, to seek the
testimonies of God. For princes also sat and spoke against me. According to
the letter Saul and Abner and Ahithophel reviled David; but according to the
mind the verse is referred to Christ. For Herod and Pontius Pilate and Annas
and Caiaphas took counsel to kill him; but he was with his disciples in the
garden conversing of the things of God and the Father. For he said to them:
The things I heard from my Father, these I have announced to you. And as
having become a perfect man, and having taken the form of a slave, he has been
named also a slave. And your servant was meditating in your justifications.
But I, confessing your mastery, had as meditation your justifications. For
your testimonies also are my meditation, and my counsels your justifications.
For having thrust away every human suggestion I follow your counsels, and I
speak nothing else, nor am I in meditation of other affairs, except concerning
your testimonies and your justifications. For this is of a perfect man. My
soul has cleaved to the ground. Through these he showed the excess of
humiliation. For so also in the forty-third psalm he said: Because our soul
was humbled into dust; our belly cleaved to the earth. That is, I was not
lifted up, I did not become proud, but lowly-minded. For this is, My soul has
cleaved to the ground. Wherefore I ask: Make me live according to your word.
That is, according to your law direct my life. And it shows first the
communion of the soul with the body from the ground, being of dust, then also
his voluntary humiliation, which he showed over the things in which he sinned,
having expected to live through it because of the word of God who lifts up the
one who humbles himself. Make me live according to your word. He does not ask
life indefinitely, but the life according to the law. I have declared my ways,
and you heard me; teach me your justifications.

5. All my deeds, Master, I have made plain to you, and I have not endured that
any of mine should escape you. For this very reason you would nod to the
entreaties. I entreat to learn exactly your justifications. And the same
prophet says also in another psalm: I said, I will declare against myself my
lawlessness to the Lord; and you forgave the impiety of my heart. And in
another prophet it is said: Say your sins first, that you may


be justified. We then who sin must declare the sins before God, and cut these
off; for cutting off of sin and declaration works forgiveness of it; and
especially to declare to some teacher, and to receive from him teaching, what
we ought to do over our sins. And without toil repentance is idle. For the
same prophet said =55.682= in another psalm: See my humiliation and my toil,
and forgive all my sins. And over his sin he lamented saying: I will wash my
bed every night, I will wet my couch with my tears. Make me understand the way
of your justifications, and I will meditate in your wonders. Those who walk in
the ways of God and are made to understand by him continually always (for this
is I will meditate) meditate in the wonders of God, considering that he
himself is maker of heaven and earth, he himself our nourisher, he himself the
supplier of goods, he himself sends rain, he himself brings spring and summer,
doing all things toward our nurture, he himself as maker keeps all things in
harmony and order. For we need also divine understanding, so as to pursue
virtue according to the divine law, and that the one who uses love toward man
may not look toward empty glory, and that the ascetic may not hunt human
praise, that the sober man may have sobriety not through the shame of
incontinence, but through the desire of virtue. Fitly then the prophet
entreats to partake of divine understanding, so as to discern the way of
righteousness. My soul slumbered from acedia; confirm me in your words. The
continual assault of sin often dissolves the tension of the reasonings, and
prepares the athlete to give up, and works the so-called acedia; and the soul
being slackened receives sleep, and sleep brings on death. Wherefore the
prophet entreats to be confirmed and established. And to fall into sin, and to
be held by sloth and neglect and acedia, and not to be eager toward the
commandments of God, is slumber. And in another psalm the prophet prays this
thought saying: Enlighten my eyes, lest I sleep into death, calling death sin.
Remove from me the way of injustice, and in your law have mercy on me. I have
chosen the way of truth, I have not forgotten your judgments. This petition
also is necessary. For it befits us to ask help from the God of all, so as to
turn from the way of injustice, and to choose the journey of truth, and to
carry about an unforgettable memory of the testimonies of God. And the prophet
prays that every wicked deed be taken away from him (for this is injustice),
and to be shown mercy by his law and his commandments. For he says, I have
chosen the way of truth; that is, I have loved, I have selected; and for this
reason I have not forgotten your judgments, and I have not become forgetful of
the memory of your testimonies. I have cleaved to your testimonies, O Lord; do
not put me to shame. He did not simply say, I followed your testimonies, nor
as a side-work, but I have cleaved; that is, I did not even for a little
separate my reasoning from these, but through all I was nourished in them, not
being torn away from them. Wherefore do not put me to shame, when you repay
each according to his works. And I seek to pluck the fruit of such a
disposition, to take experience of no shame. I ran the way of your
commandments, when you enlarged my heart. There is need of both, both of our
eagerness, and of the providence of God. For neither is the divine grace given
to those who do not have the good eagerness, nor can human nature succeed in
virtue without the help from above. This the prophet also teaches, that I ran
the way of your commandments unhindered, receiving from him the ease of the
course. For he called breadth the ease; and


elsewhere he says: In affliction you enlarged for me; and again: You enlarged
my steps under me, and my footprints did not weaken, when you made the things
concerning me easy and ready, and you drove away from me every tangled and
crooked assault of the enemy. For this is, When you enlarged my heart, I ran
the way of your commandments. Such was Paul saying: So I run, as not
uncertainly; so I box, as not beating air; but I bruise my body and bring it
into slavery. Likewise also all the saints. Legislate for me, O Lord, the way
of your justifications, and I will seek it continually. Legislate Symmachus
has as Show; and Aquila and Theodotion, Enlighten. =55.683= Continually, he
says, I need your illumination and legislation, so as both to know the way of
your justifications, and to travel this accordingly. Every good giving then,
and every perfect gift, is from above, coming down from the Father of lights.
And we, choosing to do some good, if we do not have the cooperation from
there, our desire is incomplete. The prophet then prays, and we who sing (for
as many things as were written beforehand were written beforehand for our
salvation), to have God as fellow-worker. Wherefore he also adds: Make me
understand, and I will search out your law, and I will keep it with my whole
heart. This verse also holds to the same contemplation, and does not need
another thought for interpretation. For the Lord also commanded to search the
Scriptures; but those who search need the brightness from above, that they may
both find what is sought, and keep what is hunted. Guide me in the path of
your commandments, because I have willed it. He does not simply ask that the
God of all become his guide, but showing the desire of the journey. Incline my
heart to your testimonies, and not to greed. The nature of waters is borne
toward the slope, and if it takes hold of some outlet, there the whole goes;
so, the devil having opened the way toward sin, the nature of men has inclined
toward the worse. Very fitly then the prophet entreats that the heart be
inclined toward the divine testimonies, and that he escape the experience of
the impulse toward the other. For the mind of man lies carefully upon the evil
things from his youth, as it is written. Let us pray then, being taught by the
prophet, that our hearts be inclined to his testimonies, and not to greed, to
seizures and injustices, and to distractions of this world. Because the whole
life of the ungodly is in care, according to the much-wrestling Job. For from
these wealth also is gathered, which is also called mammon. Turn away my eyes,
not to see vanity; in your way make me live.

6. The prophet entreats not to have the eyes lifted up through certain
spectacles, races of horses and of suchlike, and dancing twists, which are of
vanity, and without profit and useless; since also all the affairs and the
many cares of this world that tend toward fleshly passions are vanities.
Wherefore also in Ecclesiastes it is said: Vanity of vanities, and all is
vanity, the earthly things, that is; for he adds: What surplus is there to a
man in all his toil, in which he toils under the sun? A generation goes, and a
generation comes; and again: I built myself houses, I planted myself
vineyards, I made myself gardens and parks, pools of waters to water from
them. I acquired slaves and maidservants, and home-born became mine, and also
possession of herds and flocks much became mine above all who were before me
in Jerusalem. I gathered myself also silver and gold, and the abundances of
kings and of the countries. I made myself singing men and singing women, and
the delights of the sons of man, cupbearers and cupbearing women; and I did
not withhold my


heart from every gladness. And behold all is vanity, and a choice of spirit.
And vain is the unprofitable and useless; and such are the seeming delights of
life. The prophet then entreats that the eyes of the mind incline toward
another contemplation, and not be charmed by the vanity of the present things.
And, In your way make me live, give me to live according to your laws.
Establish for your servant your oracle unto your fear. Show me firm in your
words, keeping me unturned by your fear, i.e., let your fear lie upon me,
terrifying, and not letting me be turned from your oracles, toward taking away
from me every wickedness and sin. Thus then I ask that it come about in me
your servant. For the fear of God, it is written, is unto life for a man; and,
By the fear of the Lord everyone turns from evil; and in the following of the
same psalm the prophet says: Nail my flesh from your fear. Let your fear then
lie upon me, not letting me do the will of flesh. For the will of the flesh is
enmity toward God, according to the divine Paul the apostle. Take away my
reproach, which I suspected, because your judgments are kind. And Aquila
instead of I suspected has said I shrank =55.684=, and so also Theodotion; and
Symmachus, Which I reverence. And reproach here he calls that which has come
about from sin. For this he both shrank from and feared, and he lived a
suspected life. For this reason he did not add as usual, because I have sought
your judgments, or I have kept, but, Because your judgments are kind. And he
calls judgments the just votes of God. And, I suspected, this is, I knew
myself to have sinned; and this is a reproach to me, that having been created
according to your image I slipped into sin. Take this then away from me,
because you do not only bring punishment upon those who sin, but also more
salvation upon those who repent. For your judgments are kind, being according
to an intensification of the good; for when goodness abounds, kindness comes
about, as those wise concerning these things say. Behold I have desired your
commandments; in your righteousness make me live. A perfect desire is mine to
walk in your commandments; and to me who long, grant the life in
righteousness. For doing this, I have acquired the head of all the virtues.
And let your mercy come upon me, O Lord. For we all are in need of the divine
love toward man; and even the one who has reached the very highest summit of
virtue needs this continually; since both we who sin and those who have set
life right need the mercy of God. For all are in need of the divine love
toward man, and All fall short of the glory of God, being justified freely by
his grace. For with the cooperation of God both the one who sins turns from
his wicked way, and the good is made good and is kept in goodness. Your
salvation according to your word. And I will answer a word to those who
reproach me.

7. The salvation which you promised, Master, in your sacred words, grant, that
I may converse with those who mock me with boldness. For the one who is in
calamities bears the reproaches in silence; but the one who lives the welcome
life refutes those who reproach badly. For perhaps some unbelievers, seeing me
in life in afflictions and calamities, reproach me; but I will answer them a
word: that Christ our God has set a day, in which he is about to judge the
world, and to repay each According to his works; to those who by endurance of
good work seek glory and honor and incorruption, eternal life; but to those
from faction, who disobey the truth, but obey injustice, wrath and anger and
distress, and upon every soul of man who works the evil, of Jew first and of
Greek. Wherefore the rich man also heard: You received in your life your


good things, and likewise Lazarus the evil things; for this reason that one is
comforted here, but you are in pain. And, I have hoped in your words, what is
it? For I hang upon your promise, and I travel upon this hope. And do not take
away from my mouth a word of truth unto exceeding, because I have hoped upon
your judgments. Truth is precious, so that Christ himself also said: I am the
truth and the life. And another prophet teaches: These are the words which you
shall do; speak truth each to his neighbor, and do not love a false oath. And
the contrary of truth is the lie, which is also from the evil one. For he
himself first having lied whispered to Eve. The prophet then teaches us to
love the truth, and to ask this from the God of all with much eagerness. And I
will keep your law continually, unto the age, and unto the age of the age.
From here in this life I will keep your law and I will observe your
commandments; and the goods lie stored for me doing this; for thus I will also
become more eager concerning the keeping of your laws. Through Unto the age,
and unto the age of the age, he showed the life to come, in which a pure and
unmixed keeping of the divine laws will be given to all, and the goods, Which
eye has not seen, and ear has not heard, and has not gone up upon a heart of
man. For these God has prepared for those who love him. And I walked in
enlargement, because I have sought your commandments. And I spoke in your
testimonies before kings, and I was not ashamed. With much ease, he says, and
living according to your commandments, I did not even fear kingly power, but
with much boldness I made conversation toward them. For the lawful life is a
maker of boldness; =55.685= and of this the great David is a witness. For
before the sin he converses with Saul with much boldness; but after the sin,
even fleeing the father-striking son, he walked bending to the earth and
covering the head. And enlargement here he calls the readiness and the ease,
in which those who live well walk, and they do not turn aside to the right nor
to the left, unless they have their mind toward the way of God. For to the
crooked the Lord sends forth crooked ways. And since I walked in enlargement
of your commandments, I spoke in your testimonies before kings, and I was not
ashamed. Such was Elijah the great, trusting in righteousness, refuting the
lawlessness of Ahab. Such was the inspired Daniel, saying to Nebuchadnezzar:
And now, O king, let my counsel please you, and redeem your lawlessnesses in
alms, and your injustices in compassions of the poor. Such was John, refuting
the adultery of Herod, saying to him: It is not lawful for you to have the
wife of Philip your brother. Thus the divine Paul conversed toward Agrippa and
Festus and Felix. Thus the fair-victorious martyrs despised the impious kings.
And I meditated in your commandments, which I have loved exceedingly. And I
lifted my hands toward your commandments, which I have loved, and I meditated
in your justifications. For the kingdom of God is not in word, but in power;
and, He who has done and taught, this one will be called great in the kingdom
of the heavens. For this very reason the divine prophet also has added the
deed to the meditation. For having said, I meditated in your commandments,
which I have loved exceedingly, and having shown the warm desire, he added:
And I lifted my hands toward your commandments, which I have loved, and I
meditated in your justifications. For the things I was taught through the
divine words, these I pursued through the works. For the meditation of the
divine words and the hearing of the reading arrives at good works. For this
is, I lifted my hands toward your commandments. For the hands are indicative
of deeds; for the Apostle said: Not the hearers of the law are righteous
before God, but the doers of the law


will be justified; and, Faith without the works is dead, and the works without
the faith. Wherefore the Lord also said: Everyone who hears these my words,
and does not do them, will be likened to a foolish man, who built his house
upon the sand; and the rain came down, and the rivers came, and the winds
blew, and they beat upon that house, and it fell, and the fall of it was
great. But whoever hears these my words, and does them, I will liken him to a
prudent man, who built his house upon the rock; and the rain came down, and
the rivers came, and the winds blew, and they fell upon that house, and it did
not fall; for it had been founded upon the rock. And, I meditated in your
justifications, continually, he says, I made the meditation of these. Remember
your words to your servant, in which you have given me hope.

8. God, legislating, both promised his own goodwill to those who keep the law,
and the punishment to the transgressors. He entreats then that God remember
his own promises. For upon those, he says, you taught me to hope. Remember
then, Master, the promises which you promised us. For you said to your
disciples: Behold I am with you all the days until the completion of the age.
And not only to them did you promise this, but also to us all who believe
through them into your name. Remember us then, and be with us, and do not
stand away from us, that we may not perish. This comforted me in my
humiliation, because your oracle has made me live. For this I had as cooling
in the calamities and in the afflictions. This promise itself became a comfort
to me and a refreshment, and riding upon this I sailed over the waves.
Wherefore I ask to make my life in your words. The proud acted lawlessly unto
exceeding; but from the law I did not turn aside. He calls proud Saul,
Absalom, the king of the Ammonites, who did those unlawful things to those
sent by him. But those embraced an unlawful life, and I followed your laws.
And these things the =55.686= fair-victorious martyrs also would say, having
suffered those terrible and hard things from the harsh and impious =55.686=
tyrants. And the Pharisees also were exceedingly proud, not choosing to hear
the teaching of Christ. Wherefore he also said to them: Woe to you, scribes
and Pharisees, hypocrites, because you shut the kingdom of the heavens. For
not only yourselves, but you also hinder those who wish to enter. Still the
rulers and the priests of the Jews were proud against the apostles, saying to
them: Did we not charge you with a charge to speak this word to no one? And
behold you have filled Jerusalem with your teaching. And they answered them:
If it is just before God to hear you rather, or God, judge. I remembered your
judgments from the age, O Lord, and I was comforted. Being persecuted then the
choir of the apostles and of all the martyrs remembered the judgments of God
from the age: what Abel suffered from the brother-murdering Cain; what
Abraham, what Moses; the things concerning Isaac, the things concerning Jacob,
the things concerning Joseph, the things concerning Job; and these became a
comfort and consolation to them; and how they were allowed to fall into varied
temptations, and later became conspicuous and looked upon. Taking these then
into mind I also reaped from here a sufficient leading of the soul.
Despondency held me from sinners who abandon your law. I, suffering badly, was
led in soul by the memory of your judgments; but I was altogether pained and
continued distressed, seeing many transgressing the laws without fear. These
things the


apostle Paul also said; for the word is of the perfect: Who is weak, and I am
not weak? who is scandalized, and I am not on fire? And being in Athens, His
spirit was provoked; for he saw the city to be full of idols. Let us then
also, brethren, seeing some leaving the law of God and his commandments, come
into despondency, since it is written: You shall love your neighbor as
yourself; and later in this psalm it is said: I saw the without-understanding,
and I wasted away, because they did not keep your oracles. Your justifications
were songs to me in the place of my sojourning. The blessed David sojourned,
being persecuted by Saul, among the Foreigners; but I think he calls
sojourning not that time, but the whole life. For not as inhabiting, but as
sojourning, he continued it, and singing the justifications of God, he
traveled through the life. For so the remaining interpreters also interpreted:
Your ordinances were songs to me in the place of my sojourning. All the saints
then and those who live according to God had this life as a sojourning, having
the dwelling that is to come. Songs then to them are the justifications of God
in this life. For unto this man also has been created, that he may praise and
glorify God. I remembered in the night your name, O Lord, and I kept your law.
For not only spending the day have I done this, but also in the night, when
there is rest of the body, and it dreams toward unreal appearances, and
unpleasant reasonings are formed; I did not give room to these, but I
remembered your name. Wherefore Also I kept your law. And the choir of the
saints also, always rising at night, both prays and hymns the God of all. And
I think night here is named the season of temptations; for it brings on gloom,
and comes upon men after the manner of night. And in this the memory of the
God of all is especially necessary, leading the soul and encouraging and
driving out the opposing reasonings. This has come about for me, because I
have sought your justifications. What, This? The nightly meditation. For thus,
he says, I was able to prevail, and to choose the memory or the possession of
your justifications, since I have sought your justifications, and in the night
I did not cease making memory of you. Therefore, brethren, let us be taught to
spend the night in prayer and psalmody. You are my portion, O Lord; I have
said to keep your law. God is not the portion of all, but of those who embrace
the perfect things. Thus he became portion and lot of the priests and Levites.
For to the sons of Levi, he says, there shall not be a portion in the midst of
their brothers, because I the Lord am their portion. Thus also in the
fifteenth psalm he said: =55.687= The Lord is the portion of my inheritance,
and of my cup. He says then here also, that having overlooked all the other
things, I have you as portion and abundance and wealth. Wherefore I also
hasten to keep your laws. This then is a voice of the perfect, who offer their
whole selves to God, and are not led away into pleasures and many cares. For
the Lord said: You cannot serve God and mammon. I entreated your face with my
whole heart. The word teaches us what sort we must stand, when we entreat God,
as that we stand face to face. For I entreated your face. Or also the Son is
face of the Father, as the divine apostle said: Radiance of the glory, and
impress of the hypostasis; and through him we offer A well-pleasing sacrifice
to God, our reasonable worship. And everywhere David adds, With the whole
heart, to the law, and in this being persuaded to be attached to God, teaching
us to love him with the whole heart. For You shall love the Lord your God from
your whole heart, and from your whole strength, and from your whole mind. And,
Have mercy on me according to your oracle, is nothing else than, that in


you I offer all the things concerning me; and as you will have mercy on me.
Have mercy on me according to your oracle.

9. He does not entreat indefinitely to be shown mercy, but to obtain the mercy
according to the divine oracle. And this a prudent sick man also would say to
the physician: as you know, heal; as the art declares. This a prudent merchant
also would say to the helmsman: according to the laws of the art steer the
vessel; as the science commands, pilot. Thus it also befits us to profess the
divine mercy. For the one asked knows the manner of the mercy. For often
through discipline he works salvation, and the punishment becomes love toward
man. Thus a physician also both cuts and burns, working health for the one who
is ill. Thus we also must ask the mercy. For there is a time when mercy is
supplied even through poverty, and love toward man is provided through
sickness, and according to the medical word he heals contraries by contraries.
I considered your ways, and I turned my feet to your testimonies. Since I have
offered my whole choice to your commandments, for this cause I have traveled
the way of your testimonies. And the Hebrew has, My ways; and likewise the
other interpreters also interpreted, and the Seventy in the Hexapla. And he
says that auditing my ways, I directed my feet toward your testimonies, and I
did not let them turn from the way that bears toward you. I was prepared, and
I was not troubled, to keep your commandments. For having set myself ready
toward the onsets of the terrible things, I did not endure a storm from the
sudden assault. Such was the blessed Daniel who bridled mouths of lions; such
were the three children, for whom the furnace became a diet of dew; thus the
wonderful Maccabees; such were the holy apostles, prophets, and martyrs. Thus
all conquered the assaults of the impious. For before the dangers they
prepared themselves, dying each day through the love of God, holding all
things second and as nothing. For this the Lord pledges in the holy Gospels,
Watch, saying, because you do not know at what hour the thief comes. Cords of
sinners have been wrapped around me, and I have not forgotten your law. He
called the plots, whether of demons or of men, cords, taking the
starting-point of the figure from the plaiting. Thus also Isaiah: Woe they who
draw their sins, as with a long cord. And of these, he says, the cords having
been stretched around me, I did not shake off the memory of the divine law.
And cords of the sinners are to be taken as the assaults of the demons, and
the wicked reasonings, the fleshly movements; for Each is tightened with
chains of his own sins; and whenever someone has sinned, if he does not
quickly call himself back and come into awareness, he falls again into another
sin. Thus then Cain having sinned, having killed his own brother, had a lie
following, as a cord plaited together. For God said to him: Where is Abel your
brother? And he answered: Am I a keeper of my brother? and he lied to God who
searches all things, and the depths of our hearts. =55.688= Thus the Hebrew
people, having sat To eat and drink, rose up to play, and stringing sin upon
sin, they made a calf worshiping an idol, in the wilderness. Thus then also
Judas, being a lover of money, became a betrayer of the teacher, and at the
end hanged himself. But Peter having denied, having wept bitterly over the
denial, was justified, not having added sin. Thus then we also, brethren, if
we sin, let us turn, and let us not become despisers, that sin upon sin may
not follow us, as a long cord. At midnight I rose, to confess to you over the
judgments of your


righteousness. For not only by day, but also at night, at the very midmost of
the night, in which sleep comes upon men with more pleasure, I continued
hymning you, and I praised your just votes. It has been said then, when all
have the rest, when they rest the body with sleep, I rose to confess to you
over the judgments of your righteousness. And what are these? That Christ our
God is about to judge each according to his works at his second coming. For
this is the righteousness; and he teaches us, that we also both by day and by
night confess and entreat God over our sins. For the evangelist Luke also
says: And Jesus was spending the night in the prayer of God, teaching us to do
this. This the divine apostle also did with Silas. For being fettered with
Silas, and bound to the stocks after outrage and scourges, he mixed the
hymnody with the prayer, and plucked the fruit of the fair vigil; for they
enjoyed the divine generosity. I am a partaker of all who fear you, and of
those who keep your commandments. And Symmachus instead of Partaker has said
Joined; and the Syrian, Friend. For turning away those who choose the things
contrary to yours, I had as partners and familiars those who make much of your
fear and choose to live lawfully. This he said also in another psalm, But to
me your friends have been honored exceedingly, O God. In proverb-fashion then
we have heard: He who walks with the wise will be wise; but he who walks with
fools will be known. As many then as have acquired the hospitality of Abraham,
have become partakers of him; likewise of the zeal of Elijah, of the meekness
of Moses and David; since as many as have become imitators of wicked works
hear: If you saw a thief, you ran with him, and with an adulterer you set your
portion. Let us not then become, brethren, fellow-partakers of these, but of
those who have done the good things. For in many ways some have perished doing
sin, and destroying others with them. But when someone calls you toward sin,
answer him: I did not sit with a council of vanity, and with those who act
lawlessly I will not enter; and, I have hated those who do transgressions. The
earth is full of the mercy of the Lord; teach me your justifications.

10. Upon all, he says, you pour richly the springs of your mercy, and you make
your sun rise upon wicked and good, and you rain upon righteous and
unrighteous, and you do not bring on the wrath each day, but you bear those
who dig through others' marriages, and those defiled with unjust blood, and
others appropriating what does not belong, and those who pollute the tongue
with blasphemies, and others daring some other of the unlawful things. Using
then so great a love toward man, count me also worthy of this, Master,
providing the knowledge of your justifications. Like this then the prophet
Isaiah also says: The whole earth will know the Lord. For when Christ our God
came upon the earth, all the ends were filled with his grace, and his
justifications were taught by his disciples. And the word has also another
thought, that we all enjoy his mercy. For he rains upon righteous and sinners,
and he bears long-sufferingly over our sins, and does not lead away wrath. You
have done kindness with your servant, O Lord, according to your word. I have
taken experience of your goodness, wherefore again I entreat to enjoy this,
and I ask to live through according to your laws. And since the earth has been
filled with your mercy, You have done kindness with the human race, as you
promised through your prophets.


Good =55.689= then is God, and he has gifted us goods, and when we sin he
disciplines us, turning us toward admonition. For whom the Lord loves, he
disciplines, and he scourges every son whom he receives, according to the
proverbial word. Therefore we stumble, and he justly chastens, that we may
turn from the wicked way. For it is written: And the people did not turn,
until they were struck. And we are taught through his kindness both discipline
and knowledge, and that in his commandments salvation will be given us if we
believe. Teach me kindness and discipline and knowledge, because I have
believed your commandments. The prophet knows the usefulness of discipline,
and that the Master, using love toward man, brings this upon men; for which
cause he asks to partake of this. For God, using kindness, brings on the
discipline; and the discipline the knowledge. Thus the prophet Jeremiah also
entreats: Discipline us, O Lord, only in judgment, and not in anger, that you
may not make us few. Thus also a sick man desiring health seeks both a cut of
iron and a cautery. Then he teaches from where he knew the usefulness of
discipline. Before I was humbled, I offended; for this reason I have kept your
oracle. Since prospering in this world and being rich I altogether sinned; for
this is shown by, Before I was humbled; for wealth is altogether gathered from
injustice; but turn me, that I may keep your words. For I, he says, procured
discipline for myself. For the vote of injustice is free of the punishment.
Discipline followed the sin, and to the discipline the keeping of the laws. I
was sick, I was cut, I was made well. You are kind, O Lord. For in truth you
are kind and loving toward man, good and good to excess; and by your goodness
keep me in your justifications. For from you are all things saving. And in
your kindness teach me your justifications. For this reason, trusting, I ask
to receive from you the knowledge of your justifications. Injustice of the
proud has been multiplied upon me; but I with my whole heart will search out
your commandments. All who do not keep the commandments of God, and who lift
themselves up against his knowledge, these are proud. And even if I turn about
in the midst of such men, he says, yet I will not follow these, but I will
search out with my whole heart your commandments, remaining fixed in them. For
those who use boastfulness stitch every form of plot against me; but
nevertheless I am not provoked toward their lawlessness, being trained by your
commandments. And these things were written toward our admonition, that we may
be rooted and founded unto the good. Their heart has been curdled as milk; but
I have meditated your law. This is like the prophetic saying: For the heart of
this people has been thickened; and with the ears they have heard heavily, and
they have closed their eyes. And it is like also the things said in Exodus
concerning Pharaoh: And the heart of Pharaoh was hardened. The Jews then were
set as cheese; but the choir of the apostles, and those who believed through
them, turn about in meditation of the words of God, having obeyed the gospel
of God. Those then, he says, have had the heart as a counter-type, themselves
having worked its softness hard, and as having set milk and curdled it; but I
have been attached to the meditation of your law. It is good for me that you
have humbled me, that I may learn your justifications. The prophet confesses
thanks for the more austere drugs of the physician, having learned exactly the
health that has come about from here. For above he said, Before I was humbled
I offended; but here he says, It is good for me that you have humbled me. For
toward discipline and turning one falls into afflictions and calamities; and
those who receive these in endurance say: It is good for me that you have
humbled me, that I may learn your justifications. The law of your mouth is
good to me


above thousands of gold and silver.

11. For even if I was driven from the kingdom by the child, yet through the
discipline I took up your law, which I hold more precious than all wealth. And
the same prophet in the following says, I will exult over your oracles, as
finding many spoils; but here he =55.690= says, Above thousands of gold and

ON THE 118TH PSALM.

Stasis 2.

Your hands have made me and fashioned me; make me understand, and I will learn
your commandments.

1. The prophet David, having come into experience of many affairs, of ruling
authority, of kingship, of wealth, of glory, of honor, of luxury, and of valor
of wars, having thought all dung, looked toward the truly beloved and desired
and loved, saying: The law of your mouth is good to me above thousands of gold
and silver. And we, brethren (for these things were written beforehand toward
our admonition), having reckoned all things as nothing, let us keep the law
and the commandments of God. And wishing to show of how great honor man has
been counted worthy, having sung he said: Your hands have made me and
fashioned me. For by a word God constituted all things, and brought them from
not being into being. For having made the heaven and the earth he said, Let
there be light, and there was light; and, Let there be a firmament, and let it
be dividing in the midst of water and water; and, Let the earth put forth herb
of grass; and, Let there be lights in the firmament of the heaven; and, Let
the waters bring forth creeping things of living souls; and, Let the earth
bring forth a living soul, cattle, creeping things, beasts according to their
kind; but man, having taken dust from the earth, he fashioned. The prophet
then, being moved by a divine Spirit, reminds the maker of the affection
toward the human nature. For having crafted all things by a word he himself is
said to have fashioned the nature of men, not using hands (for he is bodiless
and without shape; for God is Spirit, according to the voice of the Lord), but
having shown the greater disposition and appropriation concerning this work,
which we men have; for we were created according to his image; wherefore this
living being, man, alone is made to understand. He entreats then the one who
fashioned to supply understanding to the work; for he added: Make me
understand, and I will learn your commandments. Those who fear you will see
me, and they will be gladdened, because I have hoped in your words. I will
become, he says, a pretext of gladness to the pious, having hoped in you, and
having found the end corresponding to the hope. And since God rejoices over
one sinner repenting according to the Lord's voice, the


righteous, as being imitators of him, are gladdened over a man understanding
and hoping in the words of God. I have known, O Lord, that your judgments are
righteousness, and in truth you have humbled me. These things he says
variously, showing his own good acknowledgment. I know, he says, exactly, that
you voted against me rightly and justly, and you threw around me with
calamities of every kind. For when we sin, there is a time when God is angry,
and there is a time when he also is long-suffering, awaiting our repentance.
That we then may not remain in the sin, toward our discipline and correction
he brings on wrath, and he judges justly. For it is written through the
prophet: In a little anger I struck you, in eternal mercy I will glorify you;
and, The Lord makes to be in pain, and again he restores; and, The people did
not turn, until they were struck. For in truth he brings on calamities and
afflictions. For this shows the humiliation. And let your mercy come about, to
comfort me. For it is then a season of love toward man and of leading of the
soul. Mercy of God then is the presence in flesh of Christ our God. For he has
done mercy with us, and he himself is advocate toward the Father, and
propitiation of our sins, as John the apostle says, And another Advocate he
promised, the Holy Spirit; for he himself said to the disciples: When the
Advocate comes, the Spirit of the truth, that one will witness concerning me.
The prophet then asks that this mercy come about unto the human race, as he
promised through the patriarchs, the law and prophets; for this =55.691= is:
According to your oracle to your servant. For to those who use repentance you
promised your goodwill. This he said also through Isaiah: When you have turned
and groaned, then you will be saved; and through Jeremiah he cries: Turn to
me, and I will turn to you. Let your compassions come to me, and I shall live,
because your law is my meditation. He thinks himself dead, having been
deprived of the divine goodwill; for which cause as it were he entreats to
live again through the divine love toward man. These compassions themselves
then he says, which the Lord has given us at his coming, the gospel word, the
apostolic teachings, that living in these we may have them as meditation
continually. Let the proud be ashamed, because they have acted lawlessly
against me unjustly; but I will meditate in your commandments. He does not
curse the enemies, but he prays over them. For the shame works the salvation.
Of which healing then he has enjoyed, he asks that those also obtain. The
devil then was proud and the opposing powers under him against the human race
through the transgression; but they were ashamed at the coming of Christ our
God. For they themselves said: Permit us to go away into the herd of swine;
and, What have we to do with you, Son of God? Have you come before the season
to torment us? And, I will meditate in your commandments, one of the
interpreters rendered, I narrated in your commandments; another, I will speak;
and another, I will converse. Let those who fear you turn me, and those who
know your testimonies. As having become somewhere far from the choir of the
righteous through the sin, and all having turned away from him similarly to
God, he entreats to be united to them again, and to recover the communion
toward them. For this Symmachus more clearly indicated: Let those who fear you
turn me back. And the prophet converses from the person of the human race. For
the apostles and those who fear God turned all the nations into knowledge of
the truth. For the Lord said to them: Going, make disciples of all the
nations. For they in truth have known the testimonies of God. Let my heart
become blameless in your justifications, that I may not be ashamed.

2. He desires to have a heart free of every blemish; for such a one has been
set free of shame.


For this very reason also, having begun the psalm at once, he blessed the
blameless. When then is our heart blameless, except when we have been born
again through water and Spirit, and have become clean in soul and body, and
through holy baptism we live in the justifications of God, that we may not be
ashamed, when the righteous will rise unto their honor and glory, and the
sinners unto eternal shame? Since also in this life it is a shame for a
Christian to be called a fornicator, or a doer of injustice, or a reviler, or
a perjurer, or a hater of brothers, or a drunkard, or a babbler, or a liar, or
anything of such a sort. My soul fails unto your salvation; in your words I
have hoped. Those who desire something exceedingly, and have been deprived of
the desired, say they fail. Thus those awaiting some of their acquaintances
from a journey always long, and are wasted by the desire as they miss; thus
those who thirst greatly and lack water; thus also those wrestling with some
calamities and awaiting the divine goodwill, seeing this delayed, fail.
Salvation then of the human race is the condescension toward us of the one of
the Holy Trinity. Since also in another psalm he says: Show us, O Lord, your
mercy, and give us your salvation; thus then he also prayed that this
salvation, which was stored, come about more quickly. My eyes have failed unto
your oracle, saying, When will you comfort me? Oracle is called both the
divine promise and the God-inspired Scripture. He fails then not only who
awaits the divine promise, and who looks for the loosing of the evils that lie
upon him, but also he who meets with the divine oracles, and who desires to
learn their mind exactly, and who takes the finding as comfort and leading of
the soul. He intensifies then the word, awaiting this comfort, which is the
gospel word. For the Savior himself having come comforted us saying: Come to
me all who toil and are heavy-laden, and I will give you rest; and, My yoke is
kind, and my burden is light. Because I have become as a wineskin in frost;
your justifications =55.692= I have not forgotten. The wineskin being warmed
is slackened, and being blown is swollen; but in the frost it is hardened and
set. Those then who have received the gospel word throw off every luxury and
slackenings and swellings of bodies, and they live according to this life in
seemliness and in reverence and in self-control. For thus is the nature of the
body; it is slackened by luxury and swollen, and it rises up against the soul;
but by the ascetic ill-treatment it is humbled and pressed. And of this Paul
is a witness crying: But I bruise my body and bring it into slavery, lest
somehow, having preached to others, I myself become unapproved. Therefore also
the prophet, since being persecuted by Saul he was stronger than the passions,
but having enjoyed peace he was harmed by the leapings from luxury, having
humbled the body he renewed the memory of the divine laws. How many are the
days of your servant? When will you make me a judgment from those who
persecute me? Few then are the days of our life, and we are persecuted by
wicked reasonings, and by fleshly desires and the cares of the world; For the
flesh struggles against the spirit, as the Apostle says; For the days of our
years in them are seventy years. When then will you judge for me, and set me
free from the hostile? Judge then, and drive these away from us. Lawless men
have told me meditations, but not as your law, O Lord. To the prophet they
advised to destroy the enemies; but he, being persuaded by the divine law,
awaited the divine vote. But the teaching of these oracles is most useful to
us also. It befits then to flee not only the myths of the Greeks and the
unbelief of the Jews, but also the blasphemous doctrines of the heretics.
Since Jews and Greeks


and heretics also narrate, and they seem as teachers, having the lie unto
alliance, and they utter nothing from the divine Scriptures; and if they also
utter, according to their own desires, yet as lawless they babble and deceive;
because this has come about also in our generation. The accusers of
Christians, that is the iconoclasts, have snatched many, and the Paulicians
who partake of the heresy of the Manichaeans. For they have not spoken from
the divine Scriptures, but they are covered with the lie, and they insist upon
it; but we who follow the Gospel and believe orthodoxly, as the holy catholic
Church of God has received, we say. All your commandments are truth. For this
reason I choose your law, since I see all your commandments adorned with
truth. They have persecuted me unjustly; help me. They have almost made an end
of me on the earth; but I have not abandoned your commandments. Great, he
says, is both the injustice and the power of the hostile; for they have all
but deprived me of life itself. And he teaches us to hold the traditions which
we have received; and he also guides toward another teaching, not to avenge
ourselves, nor to repay evil for evil, but to need the help from God, and to
give the judgment to him. For it is written through the prophet: Vengeance is
mine, I will repay, says the Lord. But I have not abandoned your commandments.
Since I drew near to death by a little both from the assault of the evil one
and from the bodily pleasures, yet I have not abandoned your commandments.
Such are the boasts of the saints, which the prophet declares toward our
admonition. According to your mercy make me live, and I will keep the
testimonies of your mouth. What mercy? Which you have done, Master, by your
incarnate economy. Therefore give me this grace, that I may keep your
testimonies. And the prophet adorned the word with humility. For he did not
ask the life as a recompense of righteousness, but he entreated to receive
this as a gift of mercy, having promised to keep the testimonies of God. Unto
the age, O Lord, your word remains in the heaven. For all things, he says, are
easy to you the Master and possible. For your command keeps the hollows of the
heavens unmoved. And this he said also in the hundred and forty-eighth psalm:
He established them unto the age, and unto the age of the age; he set a
command, and it will not pass. The heaven itself then remains, as it has been
created by you, Master, and the angels who dwell in it keep the divine law,
and they are free of every transgression. =55.693= For they are mighty, and
they do your word in strength. Unto generation and generation is your truth.

3. Of old a law was given to the Israelites, who are a first generation; and
after this to us the nations the gospel one; this is the truth. For this you
have kept both for the generation of the Jews, and for the other that has come
about after them. And he speaks of the people from the nations who have
obtained salvation through our Savior Christ. You have founded the earth, and
it remains. By your arrangement the day remains, because the sum of things are
your slaves. You have given the earth the lasting for the most part, and it
has remained, as you commanded. You have marked off the day from the night,
and it travels according to your laws. The earth also remains according to
your creation, putting forth according to its season herbs, plants, trees,
bearing fruits, seeds toward the feeding of all the living beings; and By your
arrangement the day remains. For the sun rising travels toward setting. For
all things slave to you, and they yield to your nods. For you are the maker of
all, and all things obey you the God. Unless that your law is my meditation,
then I would have perished in my humiliation. These things


each of the pious would say having fallen into a calamity, and Joseph fleeing
slavery and adultery and slander, and Daniel being hindered from praying, and
those three children being compelled to worship the image, and the
victory-bearing martyrs having endured forms of every kind of punishment. And
fitly the blessed David said these things, having been driven out by Saul, and
being compelled to live with foreign and hostile men. For he would have shared
the impiety, if he had not carried about the meditation of the divine laws;
wherefore he also adds: Unto the age I will not forget your justifications,
because in them you have made me live. Blessed is this one, who sends up this
voice in truth toward God, and lives in his justifications in this world. For
the sinner cannot say, Unto the age I will not forget your justifications,
transgressing the commandments of God; for the prophet, having learned by
experience, says that your justifications produce life, and I will keep an
unquenchable memory of these. I am yours, save me, because I have sought your
justifications. Unto various things I am yours is understood in addition; for
I am your slave, your attendant, your son according to grace. If anyone then
trusts to keep the commandments of God, he himself says: I am yours, save me.
Why? Because I have sought your commandments. For those who do the works of
the opposing one do not seek the commandments, and they cannot say: I am
yours, save me. Not all then can use this voice. For the slave of sin lies,
naming himself of God. For by whatever one has been defeated, to this he has
also been enslaved. If then, having been set free of that, we choose the
divine laws, we can use this voice. Sinners have awaited me, to destroy me; I
have understood your testimonies. Awaited, Symmachus and Theodotion have said
Expected. Those then hoped to give me over to death; but I, attending to your
testimonies, reaped the life from here. And in many ways those who live in
sins rise up against the righteous, not expecting the judgment and recompense,
nor an equal justice of their wickedness, holding the present things as firm,
gathering wealth from injustice, living luxuriously from others'; but the
righteous seek the testimonies of God, and if anything comes about unto them,
they do not turn aside from his commandments. Of every completion I have seen
an end; your commandment is exceedingly broad. And Symmachus thus: Of every
construction I have seen an end; your commandment is exceedingly roomy. All
human things, he says, both wealth and abundance and power, governorships and
generalships, tyrannies and kingships, have an end, and a swift end; but your
commandment is broad and indestructible, and for those who keep it it procures
the unbounded and eternal wealth and the goods that receive no change. For the
sinners themselves being rich and being gladdened in this life, and passing in
prosperity and power, reckon the present things to be an end of the goods; but
the prophet not so, but he sings: Of every completion I have seen an end; the
commandment of God is exceedingly broad, =55.694= and it remains unto the age,
and it has the recompense of the eternal goods. How I have loved your law, O
Lord! The whole day it is my meditation. Not everyone who fulfills the divine
law fulfills it loving this; but some by dread and fear of the punishment, and
some hunting the glory from men; but the genuine lovers of virtue hasten to
fulfill the divine ordinances by the disposition toward the good things. Let
us attend then, brethren, to the things said. He commands us to meditate the
whole day the divine law and the commandments of God; and we do not hear two
hours or three of divine reading, through which our life is set right. For
there are some who after the manner of a deaf asp shut their ears, and
withdraw, not wishing to hear divine words and


teaching profitable to the soul; and the more terrible, that some clerics also
do this, and brothers and fellow-liturgists of ours. And if we did not have a
commandment from the Apostle saying, Reprove, rebuke, entreat, we would have
set a door upon the mouth, and have borne and carried their burdens. Yet they
are wise and understanding men, and they can set themselves right. For it is
written: The wise are judges of themselves. Above my enemies you have made me
wise your commandment, because unto the age it is mine.

4. The prophet, having confessed to love the divine law warmly, offers the
whole to the divine grace. For from you, he says, having received wisdom and
knowledge, I have loved your law. For you have given me more knowledge than
the enemies; wherefore they hasten to destroy me, but I do not even endure to
avenge myself on those who do injustice; for I imagine the eternal gain of
virtue. And the choir of the apostles cries these things; for their enemies
were the scribes and the Pharisees. They then, having been made wise by Christ
the God, preached the gospel word, and unto the age their teaching has
prevailed; that of the scribes and Pharisees was temporary. Above all who
teach me I have understood, because your testimonies are my meditation. Above
elders I have understood, because I have sought your commandments. It is
proper to kings to teach; and to be taught, of those ruled. Saul was king, and
he was elder also in time; but nevertheless neither the authority of the
kingship, nor the time of the age prepared him to be sober, but he desired to
destroy the benefactor. But the great David, still ranked with the subjects
and leading a young age, loved the philosophy, and he neither endured to begin
unjust hands, nor to avenge himself on those who do injustice. The prophet
then holds to the same thought. For the teachers of the law and the scribes,
wishing to be called by men Rabbi, taught what is not lawful; but the
apostles, understanding the true knowledge witnessed by the law and the
prophets, preached. These teachers of the Jews then are named elders, because
they are in time before the apostles; but the apostles understood both the law
and the prophets, understanding these things spiritually. For this reason the
commandments of God also were in truth sought out by them. From every wicked
way I have hindered my feet, that I may keep your words. For it is not
possible at the same time to travel two ways, of incontinence and of sobriety,
of righteousness and of injustice, but one must flee the contrary, and travel
the straight; for thus it is possible to become keepers of the divine words.
He then who keeps all the commandments, he himself says, From every wicked way
I have hindered my feet. For neither the doer of injustice can say this, nor
the reviler, nor the greedy, nor he who has envy or hatred of brothers, or he
who repays evil for evil. From your judgments I have not turned aside, because
you have legislated for me. The word teaches us never to turn from the
commandments and the judgments of God. For knowing you as legislator of these,
I embraced them with all disposition. Wherefore he also added: How sweet to my
throat are your oracles, above honey to my mouth. Honey is sweet as far as the
throat, since it goes into the belly and into the privy; but the oracles of
God sweeten heart and soul. For also in another psalm he says: Desired above
gold and much precious stone, and sweeter above honey and honeycomb. For not
so does the sweetness of honey sweeten the sense of my =55.695= mouth, as the
meditation of your oracles gladdens my soul. For he has set the throat, as
being an organ of voice; and through voice is the meditation; and the soul,
using these, reaps the benefit from here. From your commandments I have
understood;


for this reason I have hated every way of injustice. For having understood
with what things you are pleased, I have turned away from all the forms of
wickedness. And the whole character of the thought of the psalm set before
shows us the keeping and the observation of the commandments, and the phrasing
is not some legal and shadowy one, but evangelical, and a safeguard of the
grace of us Christians, and a pattern and security of our life. For
continually there is set forth law, commandments, justifications, judgments
also and testimonies, concerning which in the interpretation of the psalm at
the beginning it has been said what the showing of these is; but now let us go
to what is set before. From your commandments I have understood; as if we
should say, from your commandments we have attended and have understood; and
for this reason we have hated every way of injustice. For the commandments
themselves arrange, You shall not murder, you shall not commit adultery, you
shall not fornicate, you shall not steal, you shall not do injustice, you
shall not lie, you shall not revile, you shall not desire the things of
others, nor your neighbor's field, nor the boy, nor the maidservant, nor any
beast of his; You shall not lend to your brother interest of silver and
interest of gold and interest of foods. The commandments of God, which we hear
each day through the reading both of the holy Gospel and of the apostolic
lessons; for these themselves guide us toward hating every way of injustice.
For all the sins and the forms of wickedness are named injustice; through
which the wrath of God comes upon the sons of men, or rather upon the sons of
disobedience. From which may you be kept, brethren, and be established unto
the will of God; and he himself will gift you his rich mercies and
compassions, because he is our Savior. Your law is a lamp to my feet, and a
light to my paths. I walk in darkness, but as by a certain lamp, being
enlightened by your law. One must know however, that the law has been named a
lamp; but our Savior and Lord, sun of righteousness. For the one, as a lamp,
scarcely shone upon one nation; but the other has enlightened the whole
inhabited world. Thus also the Master Christ named the inspired John a lamp,
having appeared to the Jews while it was still night. And since the true
light, which enlightens every man coming into the world, has risen, he also
named the sacred apostles light, as having partaken of that light, which has
enlightened all men. And since, according to the voice of the Lord, The whole
world lies in the evil one, and is in confusion and in sins, which are
darkness (for the same David said, as it is written in the Kings: Night was
about me the wretched, and I did not know), we need a lamp. The law is given
us unto help, and it becomes a light in our journeys; for it is written:
Because your ordinances are a light upon the earth. And the word has also
another thing. A lamp is the old Covenant which was given once to the
Israelites; for it enlightened one nation only. But when the sun of
righteousness came, Christ our God, the true light, which enlightens every man
coming into the world, he himself enlightened the ends, and he said to his
disciples: You are the light of the world; from which light we all have
received. I have sworn, and I have established, to keep the judgments of your
righteousness.

5. Since the most of affairs are confirmed by an oath, the firm and
unchangeable which the prophet had toward the commandments of God he called an
oath. For this is customary to the divine Scripture; as, The Lord God swore to
Abraham; and, The Lord swore truth to David, and he will not set it aside,
because of the firmness of the divine promise; since the oath is sung against
by all the divine Scripture, and by the Old: You shall not take the name of
the Lord your God upon vanity; and, You shall not swear my


name upon injustice; and, You shall not accustom your mouth with an oath, that
is upon worldly affairs; for Ecclesiastes said: I acquired gold, silver,
clothing; I made myself gardens, vineyards, water-conduits; and behold all is
vanity, and a choice of the spirit. =55.696= He who upon these carries the
name of God upon the tongue takes the oath upon vanity and upon injustice. And
again the Lord: But I say to you, not to swear at all; and his disciple James:
Before all, brethren, do not swear. As then there is a commandment, you shall
not murder, you shall not do injustice; so also, you shall not swear. I have
been humbled unto exceeding, O Lord; make me live according to your oracle. He
did not simply say he had been humbled, but to have been humbled exceedingly,
and this being a king and a prophet, and having the boldness from virtue, and
flourishing in wealth, and prevailing over the enemies. But he never endured
to trust either in the wisdom, or in the courage, or in the righteousness; he
took these as gifts of the divine grace. And asking life also of the one able
to give, he does not ask indefinitely, but the reasonable, the lawful, that
made splendid by the divine law. And since altogether living we slip into sin
(for no one is entirely clean from filth before the Lord, not even if his life
be one day), let us embrace groaning, tears, self-control, wasting of body.
For this is Humiliation unto exceeding. And when the prophet says, Make me
live, he does not entreat concerning this life that is in foods and drinks,
but the reasonable, the practical, the virtuous, which keeps the divine
commandments. The voluntary things of my mouth be well-pleased, O Lord, and
teach me your judgments. Of the successes of virtue the most the divine laws
declare, and some the judgment also adds. Thus also of the sacrifices some are
legal, and some of judgment. For those concerning sin and offense and
concerning ignorance, which the law commanded to offer, these also as a
certain debt were repaid to God; but those offered by the generosity of the
judgment he named gifts. Thus also now sobriety and righteousness the gospel
words declare; but virginity, and self-control after marriage, and
possessionlessness, and a solitary life, and the conduct in deserts, are works
of the judgment, leaping over the laws. Such things then the prophet named
voluntary. For the things that are not subject to necessity of laws, but are a
fruit of a God-loving judgment, are fitly named voluntary. And in many ways
falling into circumstances and afflictions, we arrange with God, as, I will
repay you my vows, which my lips distinguished, and my mouth spoke in my
affliction. These we must altogether fulfill. And the word has also another
thought. Voluntary then are as many as are according to excess of the
commandments of God. A commandment of God is to be yoked to one woman; for
Marriage is honorable, and the bed undefiled; but God will judge fornicators
and adulterers. And if someone wishes to practice virginity, this is voluntary
of his. Likewise we are permitted to have foods and coverings and garments;
but if we choose to practice possessionlessness, it is voluntary of ours. And
if we long to practice the solitary and monastic life, it is voluntary of
ours. In these then the prophet prays that those who wish to do them be sped
well. My soul is in your hands continually; and I have not forgotten your law.
For being guarded by your providence, I have banished the forgetfulness of
your laws. For everyone who has offered himself to God deposits his soul into
his hands, as also in another psalm he says: Because you are my defender, O
Lord, into your hands I will deposit my spirit. But if someone divides himself
toward pleasures and bodily passions, he in no way sets his soul in the hands
of God. For if at the season of the divine liturgy we are led away into cares
of life, and our leisure in these at that


hour comes about, and we leave the praise of God, we have not offered our
souls in his hands. If we fail the morning hymns sleeping and resting upon a
bed; and at the evening praises we are given to surfeit and drunkenness and as
it were are fixed to them, we have not deposited our souls in the hands of
God. If at the season of the divine reading, and especially on the days of the
holy Lord's day, we leave the church, and we go about striking bargains and
trading, and we spend time in the ways and the squares, we have not offered
our souls in the =55.697= hands of God. If standing in the divine liturgy we
leave both the prayer and the praise, and we converse with one another
concerning worldly affairs and cares, we have not deposited our souls in the
hands of God. Sinners have set a snare for me; and from your commandments I
have not wandered. Many and of every kind both men and demons craft plots for
me; but I have chosen to travel the unwandering journey of your commandments.
For both demons and reasonings in many ways snare those who live according to
God, then also wicked men; but they themselves boast not to have wandered from
the commandments of God. For Saul, snaring David himself and seeking to
destroy him, did not make him stand away from the commandments of God. And yet
David, having found Saul both once and twice, did not repay him evil for evil.
And of old Egypt set a snare for Joseph, but he himself did not wander, having
kept his own sobriety. This Susanna also suffered from the elders. I have
inherited your testimonies unto the age, because they are an exultation of my
heart.

6. The saints and those who live piously exult, and they rejoice over the
keeping of the commandments of God, and having God as Father they themselves
have also become heirs of his testimonies, and as a paternal inheritance they
hold these exulting; for sweet is the inheritance of parents. For this reason
then the prophet also, praying, says that believing your testimonies to be an
eternal and inviolable lot, at the memory of these I exult and am gladdened. I
have inclined my heart to do your justifications unto the age for a
recompense. The council-chamber of the soul is like a balance; and the mind
holds the balance, having been allotted to govern. If then it is added to the
pious reasonings, the scales must incline toward the better. This the prophet
says he has done. And not I have inclined at one time, and at another not; but
Unto the age, that is, all the days of my life. For in these I exult, finding
the recompense, Which eye has not seen, and ear has not heard, and has not
gone up upon a heart of man. For these are a recompense. For goods have been
created for the good. I have hated the lawless, but I have loved your law. For
having shaken off the reasonings that suggest acting lawlessly, I have
preferred your law. For such are the boasts of the saints. For also in another
psalm he says: Have I not hated those who hate you, O Lord, and have I wasted
away over your enemies? With a perfect hatred I hated them; they have become
enemies to me. As he who loves the light hates the darkness; so he who loves
the law of God hates the lawless reasonings and deeds, and the turning away
from these is a love toward the promises of the law. He then who has these
things thus cries out: You are my helper and my supporter; in your words I
have hoped. And I have not succeeded in this without your help, but having
enjoyed your inclination, and being nourished by your hope. And I have not
hoped in wealth, nor in honor and worldly glory and in help of men, but in
your words.


Whence also one of the wise said: Do not give me help of men. Turn aside from
me, you who work wickedness, and I will search out the commandments of my God.
He commands to turn aside every assault of demons and of wicked reasonings,
that he may keep the commandments of God in himself in work and in word. For
this is, I will search out. For these things, he says, I said also to the
unseemly reasonings and to those who attempt to counsel the base, that you
suggest no such counsel to me; for a life according to the divine commandments
is to be chosen by me. And I will not simply follow these, but I will search
them out with all exactness, so that none of these may escape me, but that I
may fulfill all, as many as the Lord of all has commanded. =55.698= Support me
according to your oracle, and make me live; and do not put me to shame from my
expectation. Count him then who has this aim worthy of your providence, that
having missed the hope I may not be filled with shame. And Oracle here he
calls the promise, i.e., grant the salvation that has been promised me. As
perfect then and having kept the commandments of God the prophet expects honor
and glory at the second coming of Christ our God, where he also hopes to live
the eternal life, and not to be put to shame with the sinners, and those who
have disobeyed, and those who have made themselves far from the commandments
of God. Help me, and I shall be saved, and I will meditate in your
justifications continually. For even having obtained the salvation, I will not
neglect your justifications, but I will make the meditation of these
continually. And he calls meditation not that in words only, but also that in
works. For the meditation of the justifications of God that comes about always
draws his help to us, and from it the salvation is born. You have set at
nothing all who revolt from your justifications, because their thought is
unjust. And I will have this eagerness concerning your commandments, knowing
those who transgress these to have become reproached and ridiculous. And in
another psalm he says concerning the sinners: They will be set at nothing as
water passing through. And again, Reproach to those who prosper, and contempt
to the proud. I have reckoned all the sinners of the earth as transgressing;
for this reason I have loved your testimonies. Transgressing, Theodotion has
said Unto nothing, and Symmachus, Dross. And the prophet has shown, that
himself also setting at nothing, similarly to God, the transgressors, he has
brought all eagerness unto the fulfillment of the divine oracles, inasmuch as
he has known the penalty of the lawlessness. John the apostle then said: He
who does the lawlessness also does the sin. Among those then who live piously
the sinners are known as transgressors of the law. Nail my flesh from your
fear; for I have feared from your judgments. My soul, he says, is clothed with
your fear; but since the body rises up against this, I entreat that its
members also be nailed to this fear, so that they, having become dead to the
sin, may follow the guiding of the soul. Like this is that apostolic saying:
Put to death your members that are upon the earth, fornication, uncleanness,
passion, evil desire, and the greed, which is idolatry. And again: I have been
crucified with Christ. And I live no longer I, but Christ lives in me. For as
the one nailed in a stone cannot be moved, so neither can the one nailed in
the fear of God be separated from the fear of God. And again the same apostle
said: I bruise my body and bring it into slavery, lest somehow, having
preached to others, I myself become unapproved. I have done judgment and
righteousness; do not give me over to those who do me injustice.

7. Having chosen, he says, to follow your laws, let me not become to the


hostile easy to take. And who now has such a purity of soul, as to use such
words with boldness? Yet the divine apostle also has said things like these:
For this is our boast, the testimony of our conscience. Great is he who trusts
to converse these things to God. For through saying, I have done judgment and
righteousness, he has drawn the success of all the virtues and of the
commandments. From here he also entreats not to be given over to those who do
injustice, to wicked demons and irrational impulses and disorderly reasonings.
Receive your servant unto good. And Symmachus, Take me up your servant unto
good; and Aquila and Theodotion, Be surety; i.e., I speak truth, I do not lie;
trust your servant who promises; become yourself surety of my promise, that I
will hasten to fulfill your laws. Take me up, Master, he says, and trust your
servant, and become my surety unto the good, because I keep your ordinances;
and let me not become slandered by the proud. For all who do not keep the
commandments of God are named proud, according to the saying that says: The
proud acted lawlessly unto exceeding. Let the proud not slander me. Slander is
painful, even if it has a great wage. The wonderful Joseph also has fallen
into this, =55.699= and countless others also. And the Lord also charges to
pray not to enter into temptation. The prophet then has done nothing unseemly
in having asked this asking. For especially the slander of the boastful and
powerful is harder. For injustice, having taken on power, works a greater
harm. My eyes have failed unto your salvation, and unto the oracle of your
righteousness. Again here he calls oracle the promise. Continually, he says, I
await the salvation from you, and I await the end of the promise. And he has
set They have failed, showing the intensified desire. This he showed also
above saying: My soul fails unto your salvation. And what salvation? Which
Simeon, taking the Lord in his arms, said: Because my eyes have seen your
salvation, which you have prepared before the face of all the peoples. This
then the prophet prays to come about more quickly for the salvation of the
race of men. For My soul fails is nothing else than the intensification of the
desire. Do with your servant according to your mercy, and teach me your
justifications. The worker of so great virtue asks to obtain mercy, and he
asks to enjoy salvation; and he does not demand a wage, but he asks love
toward man. For even if the prophet advanced in virtues, yet he asked the
mercy of God. Thus we also, brethren, even if we do some good, let us ask him
to become our fellow-worker, and to be saved by his mercy. I am your servant;
make me understand, and I will know your testimonies. All men by nature are
slaves of God; but by disposition, those who genuinely choose the mastery of
God. Of this company then the prophet names himself a slave, and he entreats
to be counted worthy of understanding, so as to know the testimonies of God.
It is a season to do for the Lord; they have scattered your law. God who
administers all things by weight and measure

bears the sins of men for the most part; but when he sees the wickedness
increased by the long-suffering, then he brings on the punishment. This then
he says here also: it is a season for you to rise unto aid of those who are
done injustice, O Master; for the enemies have utterly trampled your law. And
the apostle Paul also said concerning the coming of our Lord: Behold now is an
acceptable season, behold now is a day of salvation. It was a season also for
the Jews to believe; but they did the contrary, having disbelieved and having
set aside the Gospel. For this is, They have scattered your law. For this
reason I have loved your commandments above gold and topaz. Those then


have despised your law; but I think your commandments more worthy of love
above gold and a costly stone. For of the precious stones is topaz; and from
the one he has indicated all the stones. And it is likely that at that time
this was preferred to the other stones. And elsewhere also he said: The law of
your mouth is good to me above thousands of gold and silver. And the word
hints, above all wealth, both that according to the whole, and that from a
part, to love the commandments. For this is perfection of a Christian; for the
Lord said: When you keep my commandments, you will remain in my love. For this
reason I was set right toward all your commandments, I have hated every unjust
way. For the desire works the eagerness; and the eagerness prepared to counsel
the straight way, and it abhorred the path of injustice. He then who succeeds
in all the commandments altogether also hates an unjust way. For whoever will
fulfill the whole law, but stumble in one, has become guilty of all. For he
who said, you shall not murder, said also, you shall not fornicate; and he who
said, you shall not do injustice, said also, you shall not revile; and he who
said, you shall not lie, said also, you shall not swear. Wonderful are your
testimonies; for this reason my soul has searched them out. He has shown that
he has not loved these in vain. For they are, he says, worthy of admiration
and sufficient to charm and to awaken into love those able to look upon them.
And how then are they not wonderful? For they guide toward a life set right
and seemly, and a virtuous conduct, toward sobriety and self-control, meekness
and long- =55.700= suffering, almsgiving, righteousness and goodness. For this
reason the prophet also boasts to have searched them, not to speak with the
mouth, and for the mind to wander; but as we have been commanded to love God
from the whole soul, so we have been taught both to desire and to do his
commandments. The showing of your words will enlighten and will make infants
understand. Being shone upon, he says, by your light I have received this
knowledge, and your law fills with understanding all who are held by ignorance
and are like infants. And the showing of the gospel words enlightens not only
the perfect, but also those who have what is lacking. And the word teaches
also another thing. For the Lord said I confess to you, Father, Lord of the
heaven and of the earth, because you have hidden these from wise and
understanding, and you have uncovered them to infants, speaking concerning the
apostles; for in wickedness they were infants. I have opened my mouth and I
have drawn spirit, because I have longed for your commandments. Mouth here he
calls the eagerness of the mind; for this itself draws the grace of the
Spirit. For also elsewhere he says: Widen your mouth, and I will fill it. And
the divine apostle entreated that a word be given in opening of the mouth; and
the prophet himself also said in another psalm: The Lord will give a saying to
those who evangelize with much power. And this he said here also I have opened
my mouth, and I have drawn spirit, because I longed for your commandments. For
having seen me longing for your commandments, you have counted me worthy of
your grace. Those then who long for the commandments of God, opening the
mouth, fill with Spirit. And David, longing for the commandments of God, has
been counted worthy of the prophetic grace, and having widened the mouth, he
filled with Spirit. But to those who disobey the commandments of God it has
been said: With a muzzle and bridle you will squeeze their jaws of those who
do not draw near to you; and, Many are the scourges of the sinner. From which,
brethren, attending to the narration of the things spoken, may you be
delivered by the help and support of God, and may the Lord God save you both
in the present age and in that to come. Amen, so be it; to whom befits glory
and the honor now, and unto the ages.

STASIS 3.

Look upon me, and have mercy on me according to the judgment of those who love
your


name.

1. It is customary, brethren, to the blessed David to invent many-turned
emphases upon the things sung by him prophetically. For also in this psalm he
utters the same things concerning the same things in many ways. And his aim
is, the holding off from sin and the turning away of the evil, and keeping and
guard of the commandments of God, and to refute those who transgress, and to
turn away from them, and to need the help of God and his mercy. Wherefore he
says: Look upon me, and have mercy on me. There is a time when God looks in
wrath, as it is said in the prophet Habakkuk: He looked, and nations melted;
but when he looks upon those who love him, he works mercy and salvation. This
then the prophet sought; for this reason he also added: According to the
judgment of those who love your name. He entreats then to obtain the divine
goodwill, not simply, but as you are accustomed, he says, to provide the mercy
to those who love you. For this he said, According to the judgment of those
who love your name; i.e., I entreat to enjoy the equal vote with these. Direct
my steps according to your oracle; and let not every lawlessness lord it over
me. For when we bring in beforehand the eagerness, and God providing the help
and directing the journey, the power of sin has no room. When God then directs
us in his ways, and we providing the eagerness, sin will not have power against
us, nor lawlessness. For he promises in another psalm to the one who fears
him: You will not fear from fear of night, from an arrow flying by day, from a
thing passing through in darkness; which things are the lawlessness. Redeem me
from slander of men, and I will keep your commandments. According to the
saying David endured many temptations from men, both of Saul, and of certain
others. For he says also in the following psalm: Lord, deliver my soul from
unjust lips and from a deceitful =55.701= tongue. What would be given you and
what would be added to you toward a deceitful tongue? And again in another
psalm: Sons of men, their teeth are weapons and arrows, and their tongue a
sharp sword. He prays then to be redeemed from them. And the Master Christ
called those who are reproached and slandered enviable and blessed; but he
also charged to pray not to enter into temptation. The prayer of the prophet
then also agrees with the gospel laws. And the holy Athanasius, interpreting
the saying according to the mind, calls slander of men the unbridled mouths
and garrulity of the heretics and the lawless. Let us then also, brethren,
following the father, flee from every heresy, and for the present from those
now prevailing among us, both of the iconoclasts and of the Paulicians and
Manichaeans; and if they say to any of us, come with us, let us turn aside the
foot and pass by, according to the proverbial word, and let us not travel with
them. Make your face shine upon your servant, and teach me your
justifications. The divine is bodiless, the simple and uncompounded; but the
divine Scripture converses concerning him more bodily and more thickly,
measuring the words to the nature of men; since the Son is impress of the
hypostasis of the Father, and radiance, as it seems to the truth and to the
blessed Paul. He longs then to look upon his presence unto salvation of the
human race. For he himself having appeared to us has taught all righteousness.
Therefore the appearing of the divine face is to be understood as the loosing
of the painful things and the supply of the goods. Outlets of waters have sunk
my eyes, since they have not kept your law. This law also is apostolic. For if
one member suffers, he says, all the members suffer together.


The prophet then pursues the gospel perfection, lamenting the lawlessnesses of
the others; and he called outlets of waters the multitude of the tears, i.e.,
I bore the tears as from a spring, looking upon the lawlessnesses of men. With
such grief the saints are held over those who have transgressed. For Moses
also lamented, when the Israelite people turned aside from God having made a
calf, saying to God: If you forgive them the sin, forgive; but if not, wipe me
also out from the book of the living. And again the Apostle, seeing the Jews
being alienated from the Gospel, said: I could wish myself to be anathema from
God over my brothers, my kinsmen according to the flesh. You are righteous, O
Lord, and your judgments are straight. You have commanded righteousness, your
testimonies, and truth exceedingly. You administer all things justly, O
Master, both caring for men, and wishing to show them workers of
righteousness. And you have given a law, and you testify to those who
transgress what penalties they will pay, and you promise to those who keep the
good recompenses, and you confirm the promises by the works. For this the,
Truth exceedingly, shows. For he has set a day, in which he is about to judge
the world, and to repay Each according to his works; to those who by endurance
of good work seek glory and honor and incorruption, eternal life; but to those
who obey injustice, but disobey the truth, wrath and anger and affliction and
distress upon every soul of man who works the evil. And through saying, Truth
exceedingly, he has shown the firmness and the outcome of the affair. Your
zeal has wasted me, because my enemies have forgotten your words. And the
prophet laments those who live with lawlessness, and seeing the legislator
despised he is justly indignant. This zeal proclaimed Phinehas; this made the
great Elijah of famous song; being burned with this the fair-victorious
Stephen refuted the unbelief of the Jews. And it has the thought of the verse
above; for what he said there, Outlets of waters have sunk my eyes, here he
says, Your zeal has wasted me, because my enemies have forgotten your words.
Such was, as I said, Phinehas, having pierced the one who fornicated; And it
was reckoned to him unto righteousness. Such was the inspired Paul, carrying
about the whole Christ in himself; wherefore he also said: =55.702= Who is
weak, and I am not weak? who is scandalized, and I am not on fire? And the
blessed Luke also says concerning him, that in Athens His spirit in him was
held, seeing the city to be full of idols. Your oracle has been fired
exceedingly, and your servant has loved it.

2. Approved, he says, and unadulterated is your word and free of every
blemish; and I have a warm love concerning this. And in another psalm he says:
The oracles of the Lord, oracles pure; silver fired, a testing for the earth,
purified sevenfold. Nothing then of the judgments of God is unapproved, or
bastard, or adulterated, but all are free of every blemish. In their purity
then, brethren, let us direct our life. I am younger and set at nothing; I
have not forgotten your justifications. David was small, and least among the
sons of his father; but through the desire which he had toward God he was
chosen, and he received the kingship and prophetic grace. For God knows to
repay wages to those who love him both in the present age, and more in that to
come. When then God charged the divine Samuel to appoint one of the children
of Jesse king, the others adorned both with strength and with size and with
beauty of body he rejected all, and he asked


Jesse, if the boys had failed; and when he had said a lad was left, who
shepherds the flocks, and as unfitted for a king, the prophet commanded this
one to be brought, and he anointed him at once when he arrived. Of this gift
here the prophet has remembered, and he says: even being young and thought
cheap by those who had been born you have counted me worthy of so great grace,
and you have made me a prophet together and a king; for which cause with all
eagerness I desire to keep your justifications. Your righteousness is
righteousness unto an age; and your law is truth. He who is persuaded by human
laws does not even according to the present life enjoy the honor from those
who legislate; but he who follows the divine commandments has as wage of the
toils the eternal life. And the human laws also do not all have the just, but
the law of God is made splendid with truth. Fitly then he said: Your
righteousness is righteousness unto the age; and your law is truth. The
righteousness of God then and the law is truth unto the age. Wherefore also to
the Jews the blessed Paul said: Seeking to establish their own righteousness,
they have not been subjected to the righteousness of God. Afflictions and
necessities have found me. For these have been yoked to the workers of virtue.
And this is like the thing said in Paul: Who will separate us from the love of
Christ? affliction, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness?
Because for your sake we are put to death the whole day, we have been reckoned
as sheep of slaughter. Such are the contests and the wrestling-grounds of the
saints. Your commandments are my meditation. For from the teaching of these I
learn the courage, and I bear the painful things nobly. Righteousness are your
testimonies unto the age; i.e., procurers of the eternal goods. Make me
understand, and I shall live. For being enlightened by you, I will enjoy the
true life. I have cried with my whole heart; hear me, O Lord; I will seek your
justifications. Again he calls cry the eagerness of the soul. For this reason
he has added, With my whole heart. Thus Moses also cried being silent, and to
him praying according to the mind God said: Why do you cry to me? And With the
whole heart he says, showing the eager of the soul, not the cry through the
mouth. For the divine Paul also said: Singing and chanting in your hearts to
the Lord. I have cried to you; save me, and I will keep your testimonies. For
receiving the salvation from you, I will become a keeper of your laws. Those
then who keep the testimonies of God are altogether also saved by him. I have
anticipated in an untimely hour, and I have cried; in your words I have hoped.
Being a king the prophet, and being washed around with countless cares, and
having taken up in addition to these warlike contests, not only when day was
dawning did he offer the entreaties to God, but also in the middle of night,
not awaiting the songs of the cocks; so he longed for the God who had made
him; so eagerly he asked the help from there. For the prophet has not known a
season of praising and =55.703= giving thanks to God, but both by day and by
night (for this the, In an untimely hour, shows), teaching us also always to
carry God in memory. For one must remember God rather than breathe, as the
theologian Gregory said. My eyes have anticipated toward dawn, to meditate
your oracles. And not only at the season of the sweetest sleep, but also
toward the dawn he had made the meditation of the divine laws; but we, living
with poverty, and set free of every care, spend the night rolling on the bed,
and not even when day begins do we offer the hymn to the giver of the goods.
The prophet then secures us beforehand to meditate the oracles of God at the
hour of the dawn. And the Lord said, Watch, lest your hearts be weighed down
in surfeit and drunkenness and cares of life. And one of the prophets,
lamenting such, said: The whole


night of sleep, Ephraim. Hear my voice, O Lord, according to your mercy;
according to your judgment make me live. He who flourishes with so many goods
asks to obtain mercy; and he does not ask the mercy indefinitely, but
according to the profitable vote. He entreats then and beseeches God, that he
may have the life in the judgments of God, and through himself he also may
guide us to pray this. Those who persecute me have drawn near in lawlessness;
but from your law they have been made far. For far from the divine laws are
those who attempt to war against the righteous. For the scribes and the
Pharisees also persecuted the apostles, drawing near to the lawlessness, to
whom the Lord said: He who sets you aside, sets me aside, and he who sets me
aside, sets aside the one who sent me; and, I will be an enemy to your
enemies, and I will oppose those who oppose you. For this reason they have
also been made far from the gospel law, having disobeyed the teaching of the
apostles. You are near, O Lord; and all your ways are truth.

3. You see all things, he says, Master; for you have not stood away from us,
but you fill the inhabited world, and using truth you administer the creation.
This God himself also says through the prophet: I am a God who draws near, and
not a God from afar. For he knows and searches all things, even the depths of
our hearts. And the blessed Paul: For in him we live and are moved and are.
From the beginning I have known from your testimonies, that unto the age you
have founded them. He calls testimonies the heaven and the earth. For having
come down, he says, testify to them the heaven and the earth. For this reason
the great Moses also, beginning the ode, said: Attend, heaven, and I will
speak; let earth hear words from my mouth. And in these the providence of God
is seen continually; for all things travel with much order. From here, he
says, I have known exactly, that you are near, and you are present to the
things that come about, and nothing of the things done escapes you. And
otherwise from the prefaces, he says, I have known that the things spoken by
you are firm, Master, and your commandments are continual. For not as the laws
of the kings and of the cities are transferred, so also those of God; but as
the heaven and the earth remain unto the age, so also the commandments of God.
For upon them, he says, there is not to add, and from them there is not to
take away. And the Lord also said: The heaven and the earth will pass; but my
words will not pass. See my humiliation, and deliver me, because I have not
forgotten your law. He who humbles himself will be lifted up, according to the
voice of the Lord; and Blessed are the poor in spirit, because theirs is the
kingdom of the heavens. With this the divine prophet also has adorned the
other virtues, and putting this forward he professes the divine help. And many
are the forms of humiliation; for someone is humbled both in affliction and in
calamities and by human insolences. But the true humiliation is, when we turn
through our sins; as David himself was humbled having said and having done: I
will wash my bed every night, I will wet my couch with my tears; and, See my
humiliation, and my toil; and forgive all my sins. Likewise, also if we are
humbled under the mighty hand of God, =55.704= loving and keeping his
ordinances, concerning which the Lord says: Blessed are the poor in spirit.
Judge my judgment, and redeem me; through your word make me live. Since from
the original-evil enemy through the ascent the human race has stood away from
God, the prophet prays that the human race be judged and obtain salvation; and
this comes about through the gospel word. For the Gospel having been preached
in all the inhabited world, the devil has been abolished, and the human race
has been saved having been made alive by Spirit. He reminds then the Master of
promises, and from him he asks the


salvation, and he entreats him to judge for him and for the hostile. Salvation
is far from sinners, because they have not sought your justifications. Those
who live with lawlessness, he says, have deprived themselves of the salvation
from you, not having wished to learn exactly what things you have legislated
to do. And this is a cause of the destruction, that we do not seek the
justifications of God. And he has not said only, that they have not believed,
but, Because they have not sought your justifications. For in our despising
the commandments of God, he makes the salvation far from us. Your compassions
are many, O Lord; according to your judgment make me live. This verse has the
mind obvious, and it does not need words unto interpretation; for he says:
much is the wealth of your love toward man, just is your vote; I ask the
salvation from these. Many are those who pursue me and afflict me; from your
testimonies I have not turned aside. Being surrounded, he says, by many
calamities, I have not endured to transgress anything of the things legislated
by you. And having said the subject of the psalm we have said, that the divine
David gathered all the things that had happened to him in this psalm, and that
some fit the painful things that had come about to him from Saul, and some
those from Absalom. These however it is clear he has said being persecuted by
Saul; for at that time he was without experience of the sin. Many then
persecuted the saints, and led them away unto death, but they themselves did
not turn aside from the testimonies of God. For they remembered the Lord
saying: Blessed are those who are persecuted for the sake of righteousness,
because theirs is the kingdom of the heavens; likewise also of the apostle
Paul: That through many afflictions we must enter into the kingdom of the
heavens. For this reason the blessed and of famous song martyrs passed through
fire and water, and they gave their bodies through the confession and love of
God; but they have a recompense, having become of one body and
fellow-partakers of Christ our God. I saw the without-understanding, and I
wasted away, because they have not kept your oracles. Thus the blessed Paul
lamented Jews; thus the Master himself lamented Jerusalem; thus the divine
Jeremiah lamented the lawlessness of the people. Such we must be, brethren; to
waste away and to grieve over those who walk disorderly, and to refute them
and to entreat, directing our own life as obvious; For he who turns a sinner
from wandering of his way will save a soul from death, and will cover a
multitude of sins; and if they remain uncorrected, withdraw from such, as the
Apostle teaches; For withdraw, he says, from every brother who walks
disorderly; and, If anyone named a brother is a fornicator, or greedy, or a
drunkard, or a reviler, or a robber, with such a one not even to eat together.
See that I have loved your commandments, O Lord; in your mercy make me live.
Continually he remembers commandments and justifications and laws and
testimonies, showing the desire concerning these. And the saying teaches us,
that, according to James the brother of God, we love not in word and tongue,
but in work and truth. For thus we would live in the mercy of God. The
beginning of your words is truth, and unto the age all the judgments of your
righteousness.

4. You have promises without lie, you adorn the promises with truth, you
confirm your words by work. In the cosmogony at the beginning then God said to
Adam: From every tree that is in the paradise you shall eat for food; but from
the tree of knowing good and evil, you shall not eat from it; and on whatever
day you eat from it, by death you shall die. This sentence then has prevailed
unto the age; for to disobey God brings death in. And the word has also
another thing. =55.705= For it is written: God said to Abraham: In your seed
all the nations will be blessed; who is Christ.


Through him then unto the age the righteousness has come about to the race of
men. And he calls beginning of words the promises toward Abraham. For from
that one the race of the Jews was constituted; but he has fulfilled the
promises, and he has increased the people, as the stars of the heaven in
multitude, and in his seed also he has blessed, according to the promises, all
the nations. For this he indicated having said: And unto the age all the
judgments of your righteousness. For being a prophet he saw the eternal power
of Christ. Wherefore he also said that the beginning of the divine oracles has
been adorned with truth. And he says also in another psalm: Works of his hands
are truth and judgment. Rulers have persecuted me freely, and from your words
my heart has been timid. Those who war, he says, and persecute I have not
feared; but I have feared your laws. Wherefore even having found the enemy
sleeping, I did not dare to destroy him. And pitying that father-striking and
abominable child I said: Spare me the lad. These things David said being
persecuted by Saul; but according to contemplation and mind rulers are the
wicked spirits, as the blessed Paul teaches us: Our wrestling is not against
blood and flesh, but against the principalities, against the authorities,
against the world-rulers of this world, against the spiritual things of
wickedness. These then assault us, wishing to deceive; but let us fear from
the words of God. For the Lord said: Do not fear those who kill the body, but
are not able to harm the soul at all; but fear rather the one able both soul
and body to destroy in gehenna. I will exult over your oracles, as finding
many spoils. Since he has remembered enemies persecuting, fitly he has also
brought in the memory of the spoils. And he says that if I had destroyed all
those and having spoiled had gathered very many plunder, I would not have been
so gladdened, as I exult in your laws. And what he said above, this he says
here also. For there he said: The law of your mouth is good to me above
thousands of gold and silver; and, I have loved your commandments above gold
and topaz. He teaches then, that above all gladness we rejoice and exult over
the commandments of God. I have hated injustice and I have abhorred it, but I
have loved your law. This word also is true. For he who is disposed warmly
concerning the divine things abhors the contrary. For he who hates the
injustice altogether draws the righteousness. For two contrary words
concerning the same affair do not have a nature to be true. And the Lord also
said: You cannot serve God and mammon. Seven times of the day I have praised
you over the judgments of your righteousness. Some have interpreted Seven
times as Very many times. And both this and that shows the warm love of the
prophet. For he did not take a fill of the divine hymnody. Continually then we
also must praise God, that is, over the judgments of his righteousness. And
the more reasonable and reverent make their standing toward God seven times by
night and by day; first spending the night, which we also name dawn; then
thus, as the sun rises, they give thanks to the one who has brought the day;
at the third hour, when the Holy Spirit sojourned upon the apostles; at the
sixth, when Christ our God was nailed in flesh upon the cross, and wiped out
the handwriting against us; at the ninth, when he poured out the blood and
gave over the spirit, and redeemed us from the hold of the devil; about
sunsets, which we also name the lamp-office, because we give thanks over the
passing of the day; and again, when we go toward sleep. These then being
numbered together become Seven times. There is much peace to those who love
your law, and there is not a scandal for them. Those who are burned with the
divine love, and who acquire the peace toward him through the keeping of the
commandments, even if they are warred by all men, live a welcome life. And a
witness is the inspired Paul crying: In


all things being afflicted, but not distressed; being at a loss, but not
utterly at a loss; being persecuted, but not left behind; being thrown down,
but not being =55.706= destroyed. And the Lord also, having sent the sacred
apostles as sheep in the midst of wolves into all the inhabited world, said to
them: My peace I give you, my peace I leave you. For they themselves both
received the gospel law, and preached everywhere. For this the, Much, shows.
And there is not a scandal for them. For Satan has been crushed under their
feet. I expected your salvation, O Lord, and I have loved your commandments.
Not even being surrounded by calamities have I cut off the better hopes, but I
have continued awaiting your salvation, and following your commandments; since
those who love the commandments of God seek a recompense of the love and of
the keeping, and they expect the enjoyment of the heavenly goods to lie stored
for them. My soul has kept your testimonies, and has loved them exceedingly. I
have kept your commandments and your testimonies, because all my ways are
before you, O Lord. For knowing that you look upon all things, as living under
you as spectator, I have not only fulfilled your commandments, but I have also
done this with a warm disposition. And these things the prophet converses
toward our admonition and comfort, that we may keep the commandments of God
and his testimonies. As many then as do not through trade and shameful gain,
nor through being seen and being praised by men, do good things, these say:
All my ways are before you, O Lord. Let my petition draw near before you, O
Lord; according to your oracle make me understand.

5. Having offered all the entreaty, he asks that it be received, and that the
petition not be thrown away. And he entreats to receive understanding, not
human, but the knowledge of the divine words. He then who lives in sins and
turns about cannot say: Let my petition draw near before you, O Lord. For God
said through the prophet: Your sins stand as a divider in the midst of me and
you. So that one must first turn from the sins, and then say: Let my petition
draw near before you, O Lord; since it is not even permitted a sinner to sing;
for it is written: Praise is not fair in a mouth of a sinner; and, To the
sinner God said: Why do you declare my justifications, and take up my covenant
through your mouth? But you have hated discipline, and you have cast my words
behind. And, Sing to the Lord, you his holy ones, not the unholy. Let my
request enter before you, O Lord; according to your oracle deliver me. He
calls request the supplication. For this I have found also in the other
interpreters, that the thought may be thus: let my supplication and my
petition enter before you, O Lord. And since you receive my petition according
to your oracle, which you promised to repenting sinners, to loose me of my
sins. Using various words however the prophet soothes the Master. My lips will
belch a hymn, when you teach me your justifications. The belch is
corresponding to the food. For learning, he says, your justifications, I will
offer the hymnody to you the teacher of such things. When then we succeed in
the virtues and the commandments of God, we belch this hymn of praise, and we
draw a Spirit of understanding; since living in sins we in no way belch a
hymn. Let my tongue utter your oracles, because all your commandments are
righteousness. I will consecrate the tongue unto meditation of your oracles,
knowing your commandments to have been adorned with all righteousness. And the
voice is optative, teaching us of how great condemnation those become liable
who utter the fornicating songs, who offer flatteries and praises to men, who
shout comic


babblings, who whisper through reviling, those of false-speakings, those who
chatter shouts in hippodromes. For one must utter nothing else, except the
oracles of God. For this reason he also adds saying: Because all your
commandments are righteousness. Let your hand come about to save me, because I
have chosen your commandments. I have chosen, Symmachus has as I have taken,
and Aquila and Theodotion, I have selected. And he calls hand the divine
energy, by which he entreats to obtain the divine salvation. For this is a
hand of God. For those who select the commandments of God altogether also
bring the salvation from him. I have longed for your =55.707= salvation, O
Lord, and your law is my meditation. I long, he says, to obtain salvation from
you, having your law as a continual meditation. And what salvation? Which has
come about, when the one of the Holy Trinity became man, Christ our God. For
then we were saved from the hold of the enemy, and we learned to meditate the
gospel ordinances. My soul will live, and will praise you; according to your
judgments help me. Life here he calls the godly that comes about by praise of
God; and he asks that the judgments of God follow him unto help. And I know
that I will not miss the hope, but I will obtain from you the true life, and I
will praise you the giver of the goods. I have wandered as a sheep that is
lost. This both fitted the prophet to say after the sin, and all the nature of
men. Seek your servant; because I have not forgotten your commandments. Adam
having fallen from the paradise through the transgression, the sin passed
through unto all, as the Apostle said, and as a lost sheep they wandered upon
the mountains and the cliffs and the ravines of the sin, where there was not a
visitation of God, serving demons and idols. But the good shepherd came, who
lays down his soul over the sheep, to seek and to save the lost; and having
sought the wandering, he found, and he called this back, and he bore it upon
the shoulders, and he mixed it with his own flock. For he made peace the
earthly things with the heavenly in his presence in flesh. Wherefore also
shepherds with angels at his birth glorified saying: Glory in the highest to
God, and upon earth peace. Because I have not forgotten your commandments. For
even if he has transgressed some, yet he has not received a complete
forgetfulness of these. But the prophet has offered this prayer, and all the
nature of men has obtained the salvation. And as has been said above =55.708=
the good shepherd ran upon the wandering sheep, and having sought he found,
and having taken upon the shoulders he brought it back, and he was gladdened
over it more than over the ninety-nine that have not wandered; the word
showing this, as often as we do not forget his commandments, God seeks us
calling us back unto himself. And let us look at the beginning of the psalm
and the end. For he says in the prefaces: Blessed are the blameless in the
way, who walk in the law of the Lord; and at the end of this: Seek your
servant, because I have not forgotten your commandments. Those then who walk
in the law of the Lord altogether do not forget his commandments; and those
who do not forget the commandments walk in the law of the Lord. And we, having
made the interpretation of the psalm in summary, entreat those who meet with
it, not to be content with the things written, nor to think that these fit the
prophet alone, but each to take the suitable benefit from these, and to
prepare a defensive drug for his own passions. And these, brethren, are the
memorials and the teachings of the fathers, from which we also having drawn
have given a share to you with the due thoughtfulness, that the commandments
of God may advance unto you. For this is proper of a Christian, that according
to our sincere faith the deeds also be good. In which the Lord God will
establish you,


gifting you his rich mercies and his compassions, keeping you from every evil,
and counting you worthy of his heavenly kingdom, by the intercessions of our
undefiled mistress the Theotokos and ever-virgin, and of all his saints, unto
glory of Father and Son and Holy Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of
the ages. Amen.
