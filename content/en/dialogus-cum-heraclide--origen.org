Dialogue of Origen with Heraclides and the bishops with him
concerning the Father and the Son and the soul.
1 When discussions had been set in motion by the bishops present concerning
the faith of Heraclides the bishop, in order that before all he might confess
how he believes, and when each had said what occurred to him and had inquired,
Heraclides the bishop said: "I too believe the things the divine scriptures
say: 'In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word
was God. This one was in the beginning with God. All things came to be through
him, and without him not even one thing came to be.' Do we then agree in the
faith also according to this, and do we believe that Christ took flesh, that
he was born, that he went up into the heavens in the flesh in which he rose,
that he sits at the right hand of the Father, being about to come from there
and 'to judge the living and the dead,' God and man?" Origen said: "Since once
for all an examination is taking place, I will say something into the place of
the examination. The whole church is present listening. A church ought not to
have a difference from a church in knowledge, since you are not a church that
lies. I entreat you, papa Heraclides: God is the Almighty, the unbegotten, the
one who over all made the universe. Does this please you?" Heraclides said:
"It pleases me; for so I too believe." Origen said: "Christ Jesus, 'being in
the form of God,' being other than the God in whose 'form' he was, was he God
before he came into a body, or not?" Heraclides said: "He was God before."
Origen said: "Was he God before he came into a body, or not?" Heraclides said:
"Yes." Origen said: "A God other than this God in whose 'form' he himself
was?" 2 Heraclides said: "Clearly of some other, and being <in> the form of
that one he is of the one who created all things." Origen said: "Was he then
God, Son of God, the only-begotten of God, 'the firstborn of all creation,'
and do we not scruple in one way to say two Gods, and in another way to say
one God?" Heraclides said: "This you say clearly; but we say that God is the
Almighty, God without beginning, without end, encompassing all things and not
encompassed, and his Word the Son of the living God, God and man, through whom
all things have come to be, God according to spirit, and man according to his
having been born from Mary." Origen said: "You did not seem to have said
what I asked. Clarify then; for perhaps I did not follow. Is the Father God?"
Heraclides said: "By all means." Origen said: "Is the Son other than the
Father?" Heraclides said: "For how can he be a son, if he is also a father?"
Origen said: "Being other than the Father, is the Son also himself God?"
Heraclides said: "He himself also is God." Origen said: "And do the two become
one?" Heraclides said: "Yes." Origen said: "Do we confess two Gods?"
Heraclides said: "Yes; the power is one." Origen said: "But since our brothers
take offense at there being two Gods, the word must be treated, and <it is
necessary> to show in what respect they are two and in what respect the two
are one God. And the divine scriptures have taught that many two things are
one, and not only two but also some things more than two, and even if far more
in number they have taught them to be one. The thing now set before us is not,
taking a 3 problem, to pass by and string it together, but to chew it through
for the simpler ones as it were the meats and little by little to put the word
into the ears of those who hear. Many two things, then, according to the
scriptures are said to be one. What scriptures? Adam is other, the woman is
other. Adam is other than the woman and the woman other than the man; and it
has been said at once in the making of the world that the two are one: 'For
the two shall be into one flesh.' It is possible then at times for two to be
one flesh. But keep for me that in the case of Adam and Eve it has not been
said that the two shall be into spirit


one, nor that the two shall be into one soul, but 'the two shall be into one
flesh.' The righteous man again, being other than Christ, is said by the
apostle to be one toward Christ: 'For he who is joined to the Lord is one
spirit.' Is there not one of a lesser or descended and smaller substance, and
Christ of a more divine and more glorious and more blessed one? Are they then
no longer two? But the man and woman 'are no longer two but one flesh,' and
the righteous human being and Christ one spirit; so our Savior and Lord toward
the Father and God of the universe is not one flesh, not one spirit, but what
is above both flesh and spirit, one God. For it was necessary that in the case
of human beings joined to one another the name 'of flesh' be set, and in the
case of a righteous human being joined to Christ the name 'spirit' be set, and
in the case of Christ being united to the Father neither the name 'flesh' nor
the name 'spirit' 4 be set, but what is more honorable than these, 'God.'
Hence 'I and the Father are one' let us understand thus. Let us pray, for the
sake of those who keep the dyad, and for the sake of those who introduce the
monad, and thus we neither fall into the opinion of those who have split from
the church into a fantasy of monarchy, who take away the Son from the Father
and in power take away also the Father, nor do we fall into another impious
teaching that denies the divinity of Christ. How then do the divine scriptures
say, as in 'Before me another God did not come to be, and after me there will
not be' and in 'I am, and there is no God besides me'? By these voices one
must not suppose the one upon the God of the universe † to the undefiled
without Christ; nor yet of Christ without God; but let us so say that they are
as Jesus says, 'I and my Father are one.' <In> these words one must study,
because many things have been set in motion in this church. Often they write
to subscribe, both the bishop to subscribe and those under suspicion, and to
subscribe before the whole people, so that there may no longer be a faction or
any inquiry about this. God then permitting, second also the bishops, third
the presbyters and the people too, I will say again what moves me into the
place. Always an offering is made to God Almighty through Jesus Christ, as
bringing his divinity as fitting to the Father; let an offering not come to be
twice, but to God through God. I shall seem bold to speak, praying to remain
in the agreements; if it does not come to be, 'you shall not take a person's
face nor marvel at a 5 ruler's face,' he is a bishop αν † thus and he stands
up before all if this does not come to be ουτ † these agreements would furnish
occasions for inquiries και ναι † a bishop or a presbyter is not a bishop, is
not a presbyter; if a deacon, he is not a deacon nor a layman; if he is a
layman, he is not a layman nor is he gathered. If it seems good, let these
agreements come to be. Some add that the things concerning the divinity,
offering thus in substance the divinity to Jesus Christ, I have confessed a
resurrection of a dead body before the church. And since our Savior and Lord
took a body, let us examine what the body was. The church alone <beside> all
the heresies that deny the resurrection confesses a resurrection of a dead
body, as following from the firstfruits having been raised from the dead that
the dead are raised. 'Christ the firstfruits'; for this reason his body became
dead. For if his body had not become dead, being able to be wrapped in linen,
to receive myrrh and whatever else belongs to the dead, and to be laid in a
tomb, these things a spiritual body cannot suffer; for in every way it is not
possible for the spiritual to be put to death, or for the spiritual to become
without sensation; for if the spiritual can be put to death, there is fear
lest after the resurrection of the dead, when our body is raised according to
the apostle saying 'It is sown a soulish body, it is raised a 6 spiritual,' we
all die. 'For Christ, having been raised from the dead, dies no more.' And not
only 'Christ, having been raised from the dead, dies no more' but also 'those
of Christ,' having been raised from the dead, no longer


die. If these things please, these too before the testimony of the people
shall be legislated and fixed. What else concerning the faith? Do these things
seem good to you, Maximus, † to have said?" Maximus said: "Would that all
might become like me. Before God and church, I both <sub>scribe and I
anathematize; only, however, lest I be wholly made to doubt and of two minds
† about something I inquire; for the brothers also know this, that I was
saying: 'I have to be benefited by the brother and to be taught this.' The
spirit having been handed over to the Father according to 'Father, into your
hands I commit my spirit,' and the spirit being apart from the flesh that died
and lies in a tomb, as it is opened and the dead, how do they rise?" Origen
said: "That the human being is composite we have learned from the sacred
scriptures. For the apostle says: 'And may God sanctify your spirit and the
soul and the body;' and 'May he sanctify you completely, and may your spirit
and the soul and the body be kept entire, blamelessly, in the coming of our
Lord Jesus Christ,' this spirit is not the Holy Spirit, but a part of the
constitution of the human being, as the same apostle teaching says: 'The
Spirit bears witness with our spirit.' For if it were the Holy Spirit, he
would not have said: 'The Spirit 7 bears witness with our spirit.' Our Savior
and Lord then, wishing to save a human being as he wished to save, for this
reason so wished to save a body, as he likewise wished to save also a soul, he
wished also to save what remained of the human being, the spirit. And the
whole human being would not have been saved, if he had not taken up the whole
human being. They set aside the salvation of the human body by saying the body
of the Savior is spiritual; they set aside the salvation of the human spirit
concerning which the apostle says: 'No one of humans knows the things of the
human being, except the spirit of the human being that is in him.' Wishing to
save the spirit of the human being, concerning which the apostle said these
things, he took up also a human spirit. These three at the time of the passion
were divided, these three at the time of the resurrection were united. At the
time of the passion they were divided. How? The body in the tomb, the soul in
Hades, the spirit he committed to the Father. The soul in Hades: 'You will not
abandon my soul into Hades.' If he committed the spirit to the Father, he has
given the spirit as a deposit. It is one thing to grant as a gift, and another
to hand over, and another to deposit. The one who deposits deposits in order
to receive back the deposit. To whom then was it necessary that the deposit,
the spirit, be committed, to the Father? It is above me and my habit and my
mind, for I am not of such stature as to say that just as the body was not
able to go down into 8 Hades, even if those who say the body of Jesus is
spiritual say this, so neither was the spirit able to go down into Hades,
wherefore he gave the spirit as a deposit until he should rise from the dead
to the Father. Having committed this deposit to the Father, he receives it
back. When? Not together with the resurrection, but immediately after the
resurrection. Bring me as witness the evangelical scripture. The Lord Jesus
Christ rose from the dead; Mary met him, and he says to her: 'Do not touch
me.' For he wished the one touching him to touch him complete, so that
touching him complete he might be benefited, body from the body, soul from the
soul, the spirit from the spirit; 'For I have not yet gone up to the Father';
he goes up to the Father and comes to the disciples. He therefore goes up to
the Father. For the sake of what? To receive back the deposit. The things
concerning the faith, as many as have shaken us, have been examined together;
but one must know that we are judged in the divine court not concerning faith
only, as if the life were not examined, nor concerning life only, as if the
faith were not searched. From both being set right we are justified, from both
not being set right we are punished for both. And there are some who will not
be punished for both, but for the one, some for the faith as having stumbled,
but not for the life as not 9


having been set right, and others conversely will not be punished for the
faith, but for the life as having lived contrary to the right word they will
be punished. I think that in the Proverbs of Solomon these two generic things,
I mean that concerning how we believe and know and that concerning how we
live, are spoken by Solomon in this way: 'Who will boast having a pure heart?
Or who will stand saying he is clean from sins?' For we take the difference of
these thus, the heart concerning thought, and the sins concerning the deeds.
'Who will boast having a pure heart' not stained with 'falsely named
knowledge,' not stained with a lie? 'Or who will stand saying he is clean from
sins' having sinned nothing in practice? If then we wish to be saved, let
us not, becoming concerned about the faith, neglect the practice of life, nor
again let us trust in the life; for from both let us know, let us grasp, let
us believe either that we shall have the blameless or even the blessed, or the
contrary of these. Of those then who are punished there are not only the
terrible and fearful things, which ought not even to be named in both, but
also the things supposed to be lesser. For this reason the apostle seems to
have listed with the (if one must so name them) the unlawful 10 and
abominable and polluted things those despised by the many. For what does he
say? 'Do not be deceived; neither fornicators, nor adulterers, nor the soft,
nor men who lie with males, nor thieves, not drunkards, not revilers shall
inherit the kingdom of God.' You see that with such great evils, with the one
who lies with males, the soft, the adulterer, the fornicator, he numbered the
drunkard, the reviler, sins despised by all of us, so that we may be taught
that we are not cast out of the kingdom of God for those only, but also for
these that are supposed to be smaller. Therefore let us neither revile, nor
get drunk, nor seize, nor steal, nor do the things that are not lawful, even
if we go astray. If anything is lacking concerning the canon, remind us; we
will still speak into the scripture." Dionysius said: "If the soul is the
blood?" Origen said: "It has come into my hearing, and I say, being fully
assured, that some of those here and of those in the neighboring towns think
that after the departure from here the soul perceives nothing, but is in the
tomb, in the body. And I know that I was carried more roughly about this
toward the other Heraclides and Celer who was before him, and so roughly that
he left and wished to go away; and yet for the sake of honor and of the word
he had sent for us; we came down together into 11 the word, he gave a voice as
that one had cleansed himself as toward us with voices of faith † that as
toward God. Necessarily then the beloved Dionysius inquires also this. The
texts by which they are drawn aside, lest any of them escape me, I will first
set out, and to each, God giving, according to your prayer we shall answer.
The thing that has drawn aside is that 'The soul of all flesh is blood'; and
this text has terribly moved those who have not understood it; and 'You shall
not eat the soul with the flesh; take strong heed not to eat blood; you shall
not eat the soul with the flesh.' This is what has drawn aside; for the other
things that have drawn aside are far lesser than the emphasis of the thought
according to these. And I, according to my parts, praying to be helped in
reading the divine things (for we need help so that we may not think something
other than the truth) ... I found things not bodily named homonymously with
all the bodily things, so that the bodily things may be according to the outer
human being, and the things homonymous with the bodily according to the inner.
Scripture says that the human being is two human beings: 'For if also our
outer human being is being destroyed, yet our inner is being renewed day by
day'; and 'I delight in the law of God according to the inner human being.'
These two human beings the apostle everywhere 12 presents according to each.
And it seems to me that he himself has not dared to innovate this word, but
from the scriptures that have spoken more unclearly, having understood more
nakedly, has spoken. Some think that in the


making of the world there is a repetition after the creation of the human
being, the 'God took dust from the earth and formed the human being.' To these
it follows that the 'according to the image' is the body and that they give
God a human-shaped form, or that the form of God is according to this shape.
But we are not so mad as to say either that God is composite from worse and
better, so that the 'according to the image' <may be> upon both, or complete,
† God being, the 'according to the image' has come to be rather in the worse,
and not in the better. The words are more subtle; we need hearers who have a
lean understanding. I entreat the hearers then to attend to themselves, lest
they ever become causes for me of being accused that I cast the holy things to
dogs, to shameless souls. For the barking ones, the dog-like, those stretched
toward fornications, toward revilings, do nothing else than bay, and I must
not cast the holy things to such. And so I also entreat the hearers not to
become causes for me of being accused as setting such pearls, which we try to
gather as good 13 merchants, before those who roll in the uncleannesses of
bodies, and for this reason they are styled swine; for I would say that the
one who is always rolling in bodily things and tumbling upon the mires of life
and not hastening toward the clean life, toward the holy, this one is wholly a
swine. If then, 'the kingdom of the heavens being likened to a merchant human
being seeking good pearls,' I find the good pearls, and buying them even with
toils and watchings I cast them to pleasure-loving souls, and to those rolling
in the bodily mires and in the uncleannesses, I too shall have transgressed
because I threw the pearls to the swine. And the swine, having received the
pearls, inasmuch as they do not see their beauty nor perceive their
superiority, trample them, because of speaking badly the things well spoken,
and not only do they trample the pearls, but also turning they tear the
ministers of the pearls. I beg you then, be transformed; do you wish to learn
that it is in you to be transformed, and to put off the form of the swine,
which is according to the unclean soul, and the form of the dog, which is
according to the barking and baying and speaking-evil human being. And it is
14 possible to be transformed from serpents; for an evil human being is called
'serpent' and 'offspring of vipers'; if then we wish to hear that it is in us
to be transformed from serpents, from swine, from dogs, let us learn from the
apostle the transformation that is in our power. For he says thus: 'And we all
with unveiled face mirroring the glory of the Lord are transformed into the
same image.' If you were barking and the word formed you and remade you, you
were transformed from a dog into a human being. If you were unclean and the
word reached your soul and you offered yourself to the forming of the word,
you changed from a swine into a human being. If you were beast-like, hearing
the word that tames, that domesticates, that changes you into a human being
upon the will of the word, you will no longer hear 'serpent, offspring of
vipers.' For if it were impossible for these serpents, <the> serpents
according to soul through wickedness, to change, the Savior would not have
said (or John): 'Produce then fruits worthy of repentance.' Having repented
you are no longer 'serpent, offspring of vipers.' Since then it is set before
us to speak concerning a human being, and to inquire concerning the soul of
the human being whether it is not blood, and the word required us to arrive at
the word concerning two human beings according to each, and we have come upon
a mystical word, we entreat you that I may not be accused through you as
having thrown the pearls to swine, as the holy things to dogs 15, as having
cast the divine things to the serpents, as having given a share of 'the tree
of life' to the serpent. That I may not be accused, be transformed, 'put away
wickedness,' faction, 'anger,' fights, 'wrath,' griefs, discord, so that there
may no longer be 'schisms among you but you may be fitted together in the same
mind and in the same judgment.' I am in agony both to speak, and in agony not
to speak. For the sake of those who are worthy I wish


to speak, lest I be accused as having deprived of the word those able to hear;
for the sake of those not worthy I hesitate to speak, because of the things
said before, lest I ever cast the holy things to dogs and throw the pearls to
the swine. It was the work of Jesus alone to know how to separate among the
hearers those outside from those inside, so that to those outside he may speak
in parables, and to those who have entered his house he may explain the
parables. The outside and the entering into the house is mystical. 'For what
is it to me to judge those outside?' Everyone who sins is outside; for this
reason let it be spoken in parables to those outside, if somehow they may be
able, leaving the things outside, to become inside. The entering into the
house is mystical; the genuine disciple of Jesus enters into Jesus' house, he
enters through thinking the things of the church, through living
ecclesiastically. The inside and the outside is spiritual. You see how many
things I say in a preface preparing the hearers. I hesitate to speak, being
about in the speaking to postpone. What do I wish to do? To take a word that
heals the souls of those who hear. The human being then being created, first
the 'according to the image' was created, whose matter is not found; for
neither is the 'according to the image' from matter; 'And God said: Let us
make a human being according to our image and according to our 16 likeness;
and let them rule' and the rest; 'And God made the human being' not taking
'dust from the earth' as in the case of the second, but 'he made him according
to the image of God.' And that the 'according to the image of God' Moses alone
does not know as immaterial, better than every bodily substance, but also the
apostle, his diction presents when he says: 'Having put off the old human
being with his deeds and having put on the new, the one being renewed unto
knowledge according to the image of the one who created.' There are then two
human beings according to each of us. How is it said 'the soul of all flesh is
blood'? The problem is great. As then the outer human being is homonymous with
the inner, so also his members, so that one may say that every member of the
outer human being is named also according to the inner human being. The outer
human being has eyes, and the inner is said to have eyes: 'Enlighten my eyes,
lest I sleep unto death.' He does not speak concerning these eyes, nor
concerning perceptible sleep, nor concerning the common death. 'The
commandment of the Lord is far-shining, enlightening eyes'; not from keeping
the commandments of the Lord do we become sharper-sighted according to the
body, but from keeping the divine commandments we become sharper-sighted
according to the mind. The eyes of the inner human being see through more than
ours: 'Uncover my 17 eyes, and I will perceive the wonders of your law,' as of
his eyes having been covered, not so, but our eyes are the mind. It belongs to
Jesus alone to uncover, so that we may be able to behold the things written
and to understand the things spoken hiddenly. The outer human being has ears,
and the inner human being is said to have ears. 'He who has ears to hear, let
him hear.' All had the ears of sensation, but not all had succeeded in having
the inner hearings purified. These ears are not in our power, those are in our
power; and since it is in our power to have those ears, for this reason the
prophet says: 'Deaf ones, hear, and blind ones, look up to see; and who is
deaf but my children, and who is blind but those who lord it over them? And
the servants of God were blinded.' That it is in our power to be made deaf ...
(let us attend; the things said are about to touch all of us; one must
describe the inner human being so that the blood may be found), that it is in
our power to be made deaf according to those hearings, hear the prophet
saying: 'The sinners were alienated from the womb, they went astray from the
belly, they spoke false things. Wrath to them according to the likeness of the
serpent, as of 18 a deaf asp that stops its ears, which will not listen to the
voice of those charming it, even of a drug being prepared by a wise one.' And
you, as many as know with yourselves that you are in fault, if you hear a word
and 'a drug being prepared by a wise one' and you hear words of those
charming, so that he may put down your wrath and wickedness, then


you stop the ears, not opening nor spreading them toward reception of the
things said, the 'Wrath to them according to the likeness of the serpent as of
a deaf asp that stops its ears, which will not listen to the voice of those
charming, even of a drug being prepared by a wise one' will come upon you. The
outer human being smells with nostrils, perceiving fragrance and stench; and
the inner human being perceives a fragrance of righteousness and a stench of
sins with other nostrils, a fragrance as the apostle teaches saying: 'We are
the fragrance of Christ to God in every place, among those being saved and
among those perishing, to some a smell from death unto death, to others a
smell from life unto life.' And Solomon too says in the Song of Songs from the
person of the maidens the daughters of Jerusalem: 'After you we will run into
the smell of your ointments.' As then with the 19 nostrils we perceive a
sensible fragrance and a sensible stench, so according to the inner human
being there is a perception of the one who is healthy according to the divine
senses, a fragrance of righteousness, which the apostle had, and a stench of
sins. What stench of sins? Concerning which the prophet says thus: 'My bruises
have stunk and have rotted from the face of my folly.' The outer human being
has the tasting, and the inner human being has the spiritual tasting,
concerning which it has been said: 'Taste and see that the Lord is kind.' The
outer human being has perceptible touch, and the inner human being has touch,
that touch with which the woman with the flow of blood touched the hem of
Jesus. She touched; for the one who said bore witness: 'Who touched me?' And
indeed a little before Peter was saying to him: 'The crowds press you and you
say Who touched me?' He thinks that those who touch bodily touch, not
spiritually. Those then who were pressing Jesus were not touching him; for
they were not touching him by faith. But she alone, having a certain divine
touch, touched Jesus and for this reason was healed; and since she touched
with his divine touch, for this reason power 20 of Jesus went out upon her
divine touch. He says then: 'Who touched me? For I knew power having gone out
from me.' Concerning this more divine touch John speaks. 'And our hands
handled concerning the word of life.' Thus we have other hands, concerning
which it is said: 'The lifting of my hands is an evening sacrifice.' For if I
lift these hands, but the hands of my soul lie below, and I do not lift them
through holy and good deeds, 'a lifting of the hands' does not become for me
'an evening sacrifice.' I have also other feet, concerning which Solomon
promised me saying: 'Let your foot not stumble.' Something <out of place> is
said in Ecclesiastes; for to the one who does not understand it will seem to
be mindless, but to the wise it has been said from Ecclesiastes: 'The eyes of
the wise man, his eyes are in his head.' In what head? For of every human
being, both of the mindless and of the foolish, the perceptible eyes are in a
perceptible head, but 'the eyes of the wise' the ones spoken of before, the
ones enlightened by the commandment of the Lord, 'in his head,' in Christ,
since 'the head of a man is Christ,' the apostle says. The thinking faculty is
in Christ. 'My belly, my belly I am in pain,' said 21 Jeremiah. What belly is
he in pain in? The one we too are in pain in, by which, since he was in
travail with the people, 'I am in pain in the belly' and 'the senses,' not
these but
'of my heart.' If I come also upon the fine parts of the body, I see them
fleshless upon the soul. 'Lord, do not rebuke me in your wrath, nor discipline
me in your anger. Have mercy on me, Lord, for I am weak; heal me, Lord, for my
bones were troubled.' What bones of the prophet were troubled? The harmony of
the soul and the firmness of his understanding was troubled, and he entreats
concerning the restoration of those bones. 'Our bones were scattered beside
Hades.' What bones of the one speaking were scattered beside Hades? If you see
for me the one who has sinned and you see his harmonies in the region of sin,
in the region of the dead, in the region of


wickedness, you will say concerning such a one that his bones were scattered.
'All my bones will say: Lord, who is like you?' These bones speak, converse,
say, perceive God, and these are without sensation as physicians' attendants
present when they saw bones of the one who does not perceive in the sawing.
'All my bones then will say: Lord, who is like you?' Bones 22 all the inner
human being has. The inner human being has a heart: 'Hear me, you who have
lost the heart.' Those had this heart of the body; for that heart is not lost.
But when someone neglects the cultivation of the noetic habit, and from much
idleness puts away the thinking faculty, he has lost the heart, and it is said
to such a one. 'Hear me, you who have lost the heart.' 'And even all the hairs
of your head are numbered.' What hairs? On account of which they were
spiritually Nazirites. Since you have all these things of the perceptible body

that homonymously with the perceptible blood, as also the other members of the

and to be with Christ.' But according to you who say that the soul lies in the
tomb with the body, it has not gone out from the body, it does not rest, it
has not come to be in the paradise of God, it does not rest in Abraham's
bosom; but according to you who say such things it is not 'better to depart
and to be with Christ'; for he is not with Christ together with departing, if
the soul is blood. If the soul lies in the tombs, how can it 'be with Christ'?
But according to me and the word of God, the soul that has departed from the
toils, that has departed from the sweats, that has been released from the
body, that is able to say 'Now you release your servant, master, in peace,'
goes out in peace and rests with Christ. Thus the soul of Abraham heard: 'And
you shall go away to your fathers in peace, having been nourished in a good
old age.' He goes away to the fathers. What 24 fathers? Concerning whom Paul
says: 'For this reason I bow my knees to the Father from whom every family.'
Thus according to us 'Aaron was released'; for also in Ecclesiastes it has
been written concerning the righteous one who has contended well, who has gone
out from the bodily bond, that 'From the house of the prisoners he shall go
ou[t] to be king.' Thus I am persuaded to die for truth, thus I despise the
so-called death when it is ready, thus bring beasts, bring crosses, bring
fire, bring tortures. I know that together with being released I go out from
the body, I rest with Christ. For this reason let us contend, for this reason
let us wrestle, let us groan being in the body, not as being about to be again
just now in the tombs in the body, but as about to be released, and changing
the body unto the more spiritual; and departing we shall also be with Christ,
how do we groan who are in the body?" When Philip the bishop had entered,
Demetrius, another bishop, said: "Brother Origen teaches that the soul is
immortal." Origen said: "The word of papa Demetrius has given us a beginning
of another problem. He was saying that we have said that the soul is immortal.
Toward this 25 word I will say that the soul is both immortal and the soul is
not immortal. Let us first distinguish the name of death and as many things as
are signified from the


voice of death. I will try to present as many things as are signified not
according to Greeks, but as many as are signified according to the divine
scripture. Perhaps someone wiser than I will also present other
significations; I at any rate for the present know three deaths. What are
these three deaths? Someone 'lives to God' and 'has died to sin' according to
the apostle. This death is blessed; someone dies to sin; this death my Lord
died. 'For that which he died, he died to sin.' I know also another death
according to which someone dies to God, concerning which it has been said:
'The soul that sins, it shall die.' I know also a third death according to
which we commonly think those released from the body have died; for Adam
'lived nine hundred and thirty years, and he died.' There being then three
deaths, let us see whether the human soul is immortal according to the three
deaths, or not according to the three deaths, but according to some of these
immortal. According to the middle death we all human beings die; which we
think to be dissolution. <This> death no soul of a human being 26 dies; for if
it died, it would not be punished after the death. 'Human beings will seek, he
says, death and they will not find it.' For the souls being punished will seek
death, they will wish rather not to be than that they be, but be punished; for
this reason 'human beings will seek death and they will by no means find it.'
According to this signification every human soul is immortal. But according to
the remaining significations, according to the one the soul is mortal, and
blessed if it dies to sin. Concerning this death Balaam prophesying was
saying, praying in a divine spirit: 'May my soul die in souls of the
righteous.' Concerning this death Balaam prophesied paradoxically, and for
himself in a word of God he prayed the finest things; for he was praying to
die to sin so that he might live to God; and for this reason he was saying:
'May my soul die in souls of the righteous, and may my seed become as the seed
of these.' And there is another death, according to which we are not immortal,
but we are able from being guarded not to die. And perhaps the mortal of the
soul is not always mortal; for as long as it admits of sinning such a sin that
it becomes 'the soul that sins, it shall die,' the soul is mortal of the real
death; but if it comes to be 27 in a confirmation of blessedness so as to be
incapable of admitting death, and the one having the eternal life is no longer
mortal, but has become also according to this signification immortal. How has
it been said by the apostle 'Who alone has immortality' upon God? I seek that
I may find that Christ Jesus 'died for all apart from God.' You have how God
alone has the immortality. Let us then take up the eternal life; let us take
it up from what is in our power. God does not give it to us, but he sets it
before us: 'See, I have given before your face the life.' It is in our power
to stretch out the han[d, to do good deeds and to take the life and to store
it in the soul. This life is Christ who said: 'I am the life,' this life which
now is present to the righteous in a shadow, 'but then face to face'; for
'Spirit before our face is Christ [the Lord] of whom we said: In his shadow we
shall live among the nations.' If your shadow of the life furnishes such good
things, such as Moses had prophesying, such as Isaiah had seeing 'the Lord
Sabaoth sitting upon a throne high and lifted up,' such as Jeremiah had
hearing 'Before I formed you in the belly, I know you, and before I formed you
in the belly, I know you, and before 28 you came out from the womb, I have
sanctified you,' such as Ezekiel had seeing the Cherubim, seeing the wheels,
the unspeakable mysteries, how shall we live, when we no longer live under the
shadow of the life, but become in the life itself. For now our 'life is hidden
with Christ; but when Christ is manifested, our life, then' we also 'with him'
shall be manifested 'in glory.' Toward this life let us hasten, groaning,
grieving that we are in


the tent, that we are at home in the body. As long as 'we are at home in the
body, we are away from the Lord.' Let us long 'to be away from the body and to
be at home toward the [Lord],' so that being at home with him we may become
one toward the God of the universe [and] his only-begotten Son [we may see]
being saved toward all things and becoming blessed, in Jesus Christ to whom is
the glory and the power unto the ages of the ages. Amen."
