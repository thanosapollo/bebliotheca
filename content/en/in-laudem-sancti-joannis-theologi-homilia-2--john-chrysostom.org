On Saint John the Apostle and Evangelist, the Theologian.
=61.719=
Again our John, O Ephesians, or rather the teacher of the whole inhabited
world, has spread out the spiritual net, and has gathered us all from
different places, not that we might honor him with crowns of roses, but that
we might magnify him with good and righteous works. Again he has drawn his
spiritual springs, not that he might give drink to idle thirsty ones, but that
he might strengthen toiling peoples; again the second after Peter has gone up
into the temple, not that having healed one lame man he might make him leap,
but that having strengthened us all in piety he might prepare us to have our
citizenship in the heavens. No longer does he recline upon the Lord's breast,
but having reclined upon each one of us, having received us all as of equal
honor, he will offer his own gain to God, and thus he has cried out saying:
Behold I and the children whom God has given me. Children, of what sort? Those
whom grace has caused to sprout, and has shown to be heirs of the kingdom of
the heavens.
As many as Artemis once led captive, more than these the apostle gathers. She,
feeding dogs, hunting, sowed the error; John, stewarding the poor and
assembling peoples, sowed the knowledge of God; she darkened the air with
processions of idols, John illumined Asia with commemorations of saints; Egypt
remembers the wonders of Moses, Ephesus proclaims the benefactions of John;
Moses with ten plagues scourged Egypt, but John with the God-inspired Gospel
adorned Ephesus, or rath =61.720= er also had mercy on all; Moses through a
rod split the sea, John through the cross overthrew Artemis; when the sea was
split Pharaoh and his chariots were drowned; when Artemis fell, her priest and
the abominations were crushed. David narrates and says, The sea saw and fled;
today the Church in the same manner cries out and says, Artemis saw and fell.
She saw the theologian and herald of God, she saw the God-bearer, and the
sinful one, the unhonored, fell to the ground.
She saw him whom Jesus loved, and she had not strength to remain. What is it
to you, Artemis, that you have fallen? I saw the son of thunder, and my temple
of mediation was shattered; I saw the divine friend, and I was dissolved; I
saw him who reclined upon the divine breast, and I was brought down upon my
idolatrous breast. Marvelous is Elijah, beloved, in that he overthrew Baal;
more marvelous is John, in that he banished Artemis. Elijah killed four
hundred and fifty; John, having made alive the priest slain by the pillar,
raised him in Christ. O the wonder! A stone pillar put to death the stone-like
priest, whom John through grace raised up. For having taken up his body, he
says, carrying it they brought it, having set it at the door of John; and him
whom he did not wish to see while living, this one having died he sought,
asking for life and faith. Thus once also the utterly wicked Jews did; they
de =61.721= nied Christ while living, and having died they confessed him Son
of God; they denied him at the blind man, saying, We are disciples of Moses;
but this man we do not know whence he is. The blind man saw again, and the
senseless people were darkened. They confessed him Son of God at the cross,
when having bowed his head he gave up the spirit, and with a spear they
pierced his celebrated side, and there came out a multitude of blood and
water; and the children of the Jews were crying with a great voice, and
saying,

Truly this was the Son of God. John, the living one, the herald of the
knowledge of God, who received the grace of the Holy Spirit, the orator of the
Church, the luminary of piety, the eye of Asia, overthrew the demon Artemis,
and proclaimed God the Word with a great voice in the whole world: In the
beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and =61.722= the Word was
God. Let us ask the fisher of fishes, and writer of the holy Gospels, who
taught him this? What father advised him to utter this, into what book having
stooped did he find it and speak it forth?
But neither did anyone teach him, nor having stooped into a book did he find
it and speak it forth; but he leaned upon the Savior's masterly breast, and
having been enlightened in mind unto the depth of wisdom and knowledge he
cried out, In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the
Word was God. What more than the things written do I have to say? You have
heard that In the beginning he was, that is, in the Father and with the
Father, and he was God from God and the Father, begotten ineffably and
impassibly before the ages. To him be the glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
