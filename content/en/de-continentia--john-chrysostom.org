Of our father among the saints John Chrysostom: On continence.
575
The discourse on continence always seems to me to be most useful and fitting
for the servants of Christ, but most of all we shall now make this one toward
you in season, when the children of the church, having put on Christ, ought
above the other goods to possess continence especially; since whoever has seen
athletes, reckoned fit at Olympia, going down to the stadium after they have
anointed themselves, and then has discoursed with them about wrestling and
endurance and victory, 576 would justly be thought by all to have done this in
season; and we too, having seen the athletes of the Savior receiving power
from the Spirit through the divine mysteries, would reasonably, after
discoursing on continence, send them forth to the spiritual contest.
In human contests, then, the crowns come after the victory, but in the stadia
of Christ the crowns come before the victory. For what reason, then, did
Christ crown us and send us forth to the contest? Wishing at once to cast fear
into the adversaries, and at the same time also rousing our own spirits, so
that, looking toward the honor given by Christ, we may both do and say all
things worthily of the honor from the Master. For if some king, having put on
the purple, his head adorned with a crown, then, being carried away by the
passions of nature to do something unworthy of the kingship, at once looked
upon the robe of that dignity and became stronger than the shameful things;
but you who have put on Christ, whenever you see a shameful desire harassing
the soul, look at once upon the divine robe and you will quickly become
stronger than the device of the evil one.
It is a good thing, then, to praise continence, but a better to possess it;
and those who continually both hear and speak about it receive no small
exhortation to self-control from the words. For this reason God also commanded
that the virtues of the saints become celebrated through the divine
scriptures, that he might draw all men to the zeal of those and persuade them
to practice continence, following accurately in the tracks of the righteous.
For if in the gymnastic contests many, having often seen the athletes crowned,
quickly stripped and took hold of the contests and 577 endured many sweats and
toils, that they might be crowned with branches of olive or of laurel, with
how much eagerness ought we to endure the sweats for continence, whenever we
see others crowned by God, that we may bind on the heavenly crowns for the
noble and saving acts of valor? And yet how is it not terrible and worthy of
much wrath, if a leaf of olive or of laurel and empty glory, about to perish
together with this life, exhorts the athletes toward victory, while the gifts
of Christ do not persuade us to drive away every pleasure and to set the fear
of God over our desires? It is not, then, among men only that one may see the
zeal of like for like, but doves also, having often seen one only beginning
the flight, follow swiftly; and a noble colt, having pranced in a herd of
horses, has drawn the whole herd to the course. Therefore since we all are a
herd of Christ, and there is among us a good colt, the most continent Joseph,
with heavenly prancings calling his fellow-servants to his own zeal, let us
dance with the fair youth the spiritual dance, praising his continence not
with words only, but also by imitation of the deeds.

That one, then, was so exact and firm a guardian of continence, that it was
possible for him, with wantonness, both to be rich and to live in luxury and
to rule his mistress; but nevertheless, although he was about to become master
of so many and such things, he chose rather to die than to betray continence,
considering that wealth and power and glory are finished together with this
life and have a temporary gain, while of virtue alone death is not by nature
able to take hold.
For this reason he set the fear of Christ upon the pleasures as a kind of
bridle, and of the wealth and the luxury and 578 the promises of the mistress
he made a mockery, counting the misery in the prison sweeter than the splendid
dwellings; and yet it is hard for those who have come to the height of beauty
to master pleasure; but he furnished such a proof of self-control, as to hide
the bloom of the body by the beauty of the soul, resembling a fair star in
form, but angels in soul. And it befits us not only to admire the continence
of the youth, but also the dangers which he endured for it, judging slavery to
pleasures more fearful than death. And one would know his virtue exactly, if
he examined the times in which he appeared keeping the soul pure; for before
the Master and Creator of the universe appeared upon the earth he kept the
soul free, being reared in a house of the impious, having many who exhorted
him toward the worse, no teacher of continence, all enslaved to pleasure,
indulging the belly, doing nothing pious or holy; but nevertheless, surrounded
by so many and such evils and seeing the unbridled mistress pressing upon him,
he did not become a betrayer of the heavenly treasures, but kept the temple of
the Spirit inviolate, choosing rather to die than to be enslaved to pleasures.
This one did not hear Paul saying that Your bodies are members of Christ; but
nevertheless, before hearing the divine voice, he appeared no less than those
honored with the heavenly commands, teaching us who continually contend in the
churches with this word, how it is fitting to keep the soul uncorrupted.
For if I, says Joseph, having come to be before the appearing of God, not yet
having seen the Master taking the form of a servant, not yet having heard the
chief of the apostles crying that Your bodies are members of Christ,
nevertheless judged it fitting for the servants of God to master pleasure and
did not appear a betrayer of continence, although seeing many dangers hanging
over me, 579 how ought you to live together with continence with fear and
trembling, lest you appear unworthy of the honor, lest you make the members of
Christ members of a harlot? This saying is able to wall every soul with
continence, this saying quickly drives out every pleasure, this saying easily
cools the inflammations of desire; not so easily does a rainstorm, having
fallen into a fire, quench the flame, as this divine word, having entered into
a soul, quickly withers the evil desires. The great Job also is able to make
the same words toward us, who not only appeared an exact practitioner of
continence, but also laid down a law for his own eyes not to look upon the
face of a virgin, lest beauty appearing by the way seize the reasoning. And
yet who would not be struck and wonder, seeing this man wrestling with the
devil nobly and exposing every device of the evil one, yet fleeing the face of
a virgin and quickly leading the eyes away from a well-formed girl? Seeing the
devil coming on he did not flee, but remained like a lion trusting in his
strength, but having seen a girl he did not stand nor linger about the sight
of the beauty, but withdrew quickly, thinking that the war against the demons
requires courage and a bold soul, while the practice of continence is easily
mastered not from associating with girls, but from fleeing virgins.

Whoever, then, professes virginity, let him receive the counsels of the most
continent of all men, who himself also, having come to be before the appearing
of Christ, became so exact a guardian of continence. And let no one simply
hear that the righteous, having appeared before the appearing of Christ,
furnished so great a proof of self-control; for they did not have so great a
necessity of continence laid upon them, nor did the neglected possession 580
of continence bring so great a reproach upon the saints. How? Because for this
cause the most high God, the Creator of all things, took our form, that he
might bring down from heaven the holiness of the angels.
Whenever, then, men appear, after so great an honor, enslaved to pleasures, it
is not possible to speak the greatness of the daring, that they show the
Master's loving-kindness vain, at least as far as they are concerned, making
the members of Christ members of a harlot. Let demons hear and be afraid, that
God does not refuse to join us to himself and unite us; yet some of the
faithful dare, having torn themselves away from Christ, to be joined to
harlots? It is not so great an evil, having been brought down from heaven, to
fall into mire, as it is an evil, having become a member of Christ, to fall
from the divine honor and become a member of a harlot. Whenever, then, an evil
desire burns up the soul, remember Christ quickly, think that Paul stands
beside you admonishing and saying: Do you not know that your bodies are
members of Christ? Shall I then take the members of Christ and make them
members of a harlot? If you remember these words, you will quickly see the
pleasure running away. For if a mistress, modest and continent, having
appeared, by a look alone quickly brought unseemly maidservants to order, why
do you wonder if the memory of Christ quickly puts to sleep a harassing
pleasure? Always have the cross shining before your eyes, and you will pass
through the time clean of sins. For as the pillar of cloud, being a type and
prophecy of the cross, gave rest to the multitude of the Hebrews and did not
allow them to suffer anything terrible from the Egyptians, so the cross, seen
before the eyes, quickly drives away every evil pleasure and does not allow
one to suffer anything unworthy of piety.
This is salvation of the soul, this is a wondrous antidote of shameful desire;
for the sicknesses in the bodies 581 are stopped by the arts of the
physicians, but a sick soul is quickly healed by the oracles of Christ.
Wherefore we also ask and beseech those who have already sinned and are
enslaved to pleasures to rise up and be sober and not to be wholly overcome by
the passion, nor to be carried as by a current, nor, having enslaved the
reasoning to the pleasures, to carry about a bitter tyranny in the soul, but
to resist and draw up in array and, having strengthened the mind with the fear
of Christ, to cast out from the acropolis a harsh mistress, that, having
thrown off every stain and having cast away the multitude of the things that
have been sinned, we may be able with a pure and uncorrupted soul to approach
the divine and fearful mysteries of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ, to
whom be the glory and the power unto the ages of ages. Amen.
