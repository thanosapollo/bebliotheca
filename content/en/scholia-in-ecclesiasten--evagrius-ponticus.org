SCHOLIA ON ECCLESIASTES

The Church is true knowledge of ages and worlds and of the judgment and
providence in them, of pure souls. And the Ecclesiast is Christ, the begetter
of this knowledge; or the Ecclesiast is the one who through ethical
contemplations purifies souls and leads them to natural contemplation.

2 1, 2 Vanity of vanities, said the Ecclesiast, vanity of vanities, all things
are vanity. To those who have entered the intelligible Church and marvel at
the contemplation of the things that have come to be, the Word says: do not
think this to be the last end, O you, that which is stored up for you in the
promises; for all these things are vanity of vanities before the knowledge of
God himself. For just as medicines are vain after perfect health, so after the
knowledge of the Holy Trinity the reasons of ages and worlds are vain.

3 1, 11.1 There is no remembrance for the first things. If there is no
remembrance for the first things, how does David say: "I remembered days of
old" and "I remembered eternal years"? Or forgetfulness of all these things
will come about at that time when the rational nature receives the Holy
Trinity; for then God will be all in all. For if the thoughts of things,
coming to be in the understanding, bring the intellect to remembrance of the
things, and the intellect is separated from all thoughts when it has seen God,
then the intellect that has received the Holy Trinity forgets all the things
that have come to be.

4 1, 13.4-6 that God has given an evil distraction to the sons of men, to be
distracted in it. He calls "evil" what is toilsome, not what is opposed to the
good; for God gives that to no one; for he is not a cause of evils, being a
fountain of goodness, unless he is said to give as permitting according to the
reason of abandonment.

5 1, 15 What is crooked will not be able to be adorned upon, and what is
lacking will not be able to be numbered. He calls "crooked" the unclean
intellect; "for a crooked heart," he says, "devises evils." And he named
wisdom "adornment" in Proverbs, where he says: "wisdom is an adornment to
young men"; "wisdom will not enter a soul that crafts evil." And he did not
say: it will not be able to be adorned, but: it will not be able to be adorned
upon; for a right life adorns someone, but the wisdom of God adorns upon.
Therefore a crooked intellect can be adorned, but it cannot be adorned upon,
unless it purify itself through the virtues and make itself a useful vessel
for the master.

6 The number of God by which he numbers the saints indicates a certain
spiritual and defined order; "for numbering," he says, "multitudes of stars
and calling them all by name." And with this number the Lord also commands
Moses to number the sons of Israel; but David, concerning men who crawl and
are enslaved to pleasures, what does he say? "there creeping things of which
there is no number"; and in Proverbs Solomon says concerning wickedness: "for
having wounded many she has cast them down, and they are numberless whom she
has slain." So the "lack" spoken of here and those who have been slain and the
creeping things are of the same state, which does not fit the spiritual
number. But if David also says that there is no number of the understanding of
God, he does not write this as though it were unworthy of number, but as not
being of a nature, because of its incomprehensibility, to be subjected to
number. For just as the invisible is spoken of in two ways, both that which is
not of a nature to be seen, as God, and that which is of a nature to be seen,
yet is not seen, as iron in the deep because it is covered by the water, so
also the numberless is spoken of in two ways, both that which is not of a
nature to be numbered and that which is not numbered for some cause.

7 2, 6 I made myself pools of waters to water from them a grove sprouting
trees. It has been said with hyperbaton; and the straight reading of it is
this: to water from them the trees of a grove that are sprouting.

8 2, 10.1-4 and whatever my eyes asked, I did not take away from them; I did
not withhold my heart from all gladness. A soul does not ask knowledge by a
word, but by purity; "for not everyone who says to me, Lord, Lord, will
enter," he says, "into the kingdom of the heavens, but the one who does the
will of my Father"; for we receive knowledge according to the proportion of
the state, if indeed with the measure we measure it will also be measured back
to us; an intelligible asking, then, is impassibility of a rational soul
drawing holy knowledge. Thus then the one who makes himself receptive of all
knowledge takes nothing away from the eyes; and I mean all knowledge, that
which is of a nature to accrue to a soul bound together with blood and flesh.

9 The one who sins nothing does not withhold his heart from all spiritual
gladness.

10 2, 11.4 and behold all things are vanity and preference of spirit. He names
the soul "spirit"; for preference is a certain kind of movement of intellect.
And David: "into your hands I will commit my spirit"; and Stephen: "Lord
Jesus," he says, "receive my spirit"; and in Kingdoms: "David," he says, "did
not grieve the spirit of Amnon his son."

11 2, 14.1 The eyes of the wise man are in his head. If "the head of every man
is Christ," and the wise man too is a man, the head of the wise man is Christ;
but Christ is our wisdom, "for he was begotten for us wisdom from God"; the
head of the wise man, then, is wisdom, in which the wise man has the eyes of
the understanding, contemplating in it the reasons of the things that have
come to be.

12 2, 22 For it happens to the man in all his toil and in the preference of
his heart, with which he himself toils under the sun. From this it is shown
that the preference of the spirit is preference of heart.

13 2, 25 For who will eat and who will drink apart from him? For who without
Christ will be able to eat his flesh or drink his blood, which are symbols of
virtues and of knowledge?

14 2, 26.4-7 and to the one who sins he has given a distraction, to add and to
gather, to give to the one who is good before the face of God; for this too is
vanity and preference of spirit. This is like the proverb that says: "he who
multiplies his wealth with interest and increase gathers it for him who has
mercy on the poor" and "a grudging man hastens to be rich and does not know
that a merciful one will take hold of him"; wherefore let us be content with
the scholia lying there. But this must be known, that he declared the
gathering of sins to be vanity, not his obtaining from God a good teacher.

15 3, 10 I saw the distraction which God has given to the sons of man to be
distracted in it. 3, 11 He has made everything beautiful in its time, and he
has also given the age in their heart, so that man may not find the work which
God has done from beginning even to end. 3, 12 I knew that there is no good in
them, except to be glad and to do good in his life; 3, 13 and also every man
who will eat and drink and see good in all his toil, this is a gift of God. I
saw, he says, the sensible things distracting the understanding of man, which
God gave to men before purification so that they might be distracted in them.
And he says their beauty is temporary and not everlasting; for after
purification the pure man no longer looks upon the sensible things only as
distracting his intellect, but as lying in them toward spiritual
contemplation. For the intellect is formed in one way when it applies itself
to sensibles through the senses in a sensible manner, and it is disposed in
another way when it contemplates the reasons stored up in the sensibles; but
this knowledge accrues only to the pure, while the grasping of things through
the senses belongs both to the pure and to the impure. Wherefore he also
called it a temporary distraction given by God; for God, providing for the
passionate soul, gave it senses and sensible things, so that being distracted
in them and occupying itself with thoughts it might escape the reasonings that
are going to be thrown into it by the opposing powers. And he gave them, he
says, also the age, that is, the reasons of the age; for this is the kingdom
of the heavens which the Lord said we have within us, which, being covered by
the passions, is not found by men. I knew then, he says, that the things are
not good, but the reasons of the things, upon which the rational nature is
also of a nature to be glad and to work the good; for nothing so feeds and
waters the intellect as virtue and knowledge of God.

16 We do the good through the timely use of the things given us by God; for
thus also all things will be beautiful in their time and "behold all things
are very beautiful."

17 3, 14 I knew that all things whatsoever God has made, they will be unto the
age; upon them there is no adding, and from them there is no taking away, and
God has made them, that they may fear from his face. If "all things whatsoever
God has made will be unto the age," and God did not make wickedness, then
wickedness will not be unto the age.

18 From the manifold wisdom there is no taking away, and to it there is no
adding. And God made it, he says, so that men, longing for knowledge, may
cease from wickedness; "for by the fear of the Lord everyone turns away from
evil."

19 3, 15 What has come to be already is, and as many things as are to come to
be have already come to be, and God will seek the one who is pursued. If
"blessed are those who have been persecuted for the sake of righteousness, for
theirs is the kingdom of the heavens," and the kingdom of the heavens is the
reasons of the ages that have come to be and will come to be, then blessed are
those who have been persecuted, because they themselves will know the
contemplation of the things that have come to be. For God is said to seek this
one whom he illuminates with knowledge, and not to seek this one whom he does
not illuminate with knowledge; "I have gone astray," says David, "like a lost
sheep; seek your servant, because I have not forgotten your commandments"; for
he himself was being pursued; "many," he says, "are those who pursue me and
afflict me; I have not turned aside from your testimonies."

20 3, 18 There I said in my heart concerning the speech of the sons of man,
that God will distinguish them, and to show this, that they themselves are
cattle. He named the life of man now "speech," if indeed concerning every idle
word we will give account in the day of judgment, in which both the pure and
the impure are made manifest.

21 3, 19 And also for them there is a meeting of the sons of man and a meeting
of the cattle, one meeting for them; as the death of this one, so the death of
this one, and one spirit in all; and what has man in abundance beyond the
cattle? nothing, because all things are vanity. 3, 20 All things go to one
place; all things came to be from the dust, and all things return to the dust;
3, 21 and who knows the spirit of the sons of man, whether it goes up above,
and the spirit of the cattle, whether it goes down below into the earth? 3, 22
And I saw that there is no good except that a man should be glad in his works,
because this is his portion; for who will bring him to see in what will come
to be after him? He calls "meeting" the things that happen commonly to all
men, both just and unjust, in this world, such as life, death, disease,
health, wealth, poverty, losses of limbs, of wives, of children, of
possessions, from which it is not possible before the judgment to distinguish
the just and the ungodly. And he also says it is common to them both to be
from the dust and to return again to the dust and to have one soul, not in
number, but in nature; "for there is," he says, "one spirit in all." And he
now called "cattle" the man who came to be in honor and did not understand,
but through irrational pleasures was compared to the mindless cattle and was
made like them. But neither through the things which the just and the unjust
do will they be clearly known before the judgment, many of the unjust having
passed over to righteousness and been exalted, and many of the just having
fallen from virtue and been humbled. What then did I find in abundance in
these things? now, nothing, he says. For all things are vanity except
spiritual gladness, which is of a nature to come about upon the works and the
virtues of man; for the one who has fallen from this gladness will not come
back again here to do the things that contribute toward the acquisition of it.

22 That the irrational soul also is called spirit.

23 4, 1 And I turned and I saw all the calumnies that have come to be under
the sun; and behold a tear of those who are calumniated, and there is no one
comforting them, and from the hand of those who calumniate them there is
strength, and there is no one comforting them. He calls "calumnies" those who
oppose us; "for wait," he says, "for your servant unto good; let not the proud
calumniate me"; and again concerning the Savior Christ he says: "and he will
humble a calumniator and he will remain with the sun." And those who were
calumniated before Christ were men who had not the comforter, of the sort of
the one who says these things: "I the prisoner in the Lord exhort you to walk
worthily of the calling with which you were called, with all humility and
meekness, with longsuffering, bearing with one another in love."

24 4, 2 And I praised the dead, those already dead, above the living, as many
as they themselves live until now; 4, 3 and better than these two is whoever
has not yet come to be, who has not seen the evil work that has been done
under the sun. And I praised those who have already died together with Christ
and been delivered from those who calumniate them, above those who live in
wickedness and persist in it until now. And good is the one who neither needed
such a death separating him from wickedness nor came to be in wickedness, who
did not know the evil toil that is done by those who calumniate those under
the sun.

25 4, 4 And I saw all the toil and all the manliness of the work, that it is a
man's jealousy from his companion; and this too is vanity and preference of
spirit. I saw, he says, all wickedness and the one who is manly in it as evil;
for this one he also calls manly in ungodliness, calumniating the poor, and
again a work as "made to be mocked by the angels" of God. I also saw all his
jealousy which he possesses toward men, being vain and filling his heart; for
God must altogether become all in all and the prayer of Christ be fulfilled
which says: "give them that they too may be one in us, just as I and you are
one, Father."

26 4, 5 A fool folded his hands and ate his flesh. If the hands are a symbol
of practical work, everyone who does not work righteousness folds his hands;
wherefore such a one also, he says, eats up his own flesh, being filled with
the evils that grow from the flesh.

27 4, 6 Better a fullness of a handful of rest than fullnesses of two handfuls
of toil and preference of spirit. The preference of the spirit seems to me to
be as a passionate psychic will; whence also the good handful of virtue is
preferred to the two handfuls of wickedness and of ignorance and of the
preference of the spirit. Such also is "better a little to the just than much
wealth of sinners" and "better a little taking with righteousness than many
produce with injustice." Like these also is "I chose to be cast aside in the
house of my God rather than to dwell in tents of sinners" and "better to dwell
upon a corner of a housetop than with a railing woman in a common house." And
to these will also follow "better a hospitality of vegetables toward
friendship and grace than a setting of calves with enmity," as if someone were
saying: better to learn one spiritual contemplation than a multitude of
contemplations of the wisdom that has been made foolish.

28 4, 8 There is one and there is not a second, and he has neither son nor
brother; and there is no end to all his toil, and his eye is not filled with
wealth. and for whom do I toil and deprive my soul of goodness? and this too
is vanity and an evil distraction. If someone does not have a brother, this
one has not received a spirit of adoption, and if someone is not a father,
this one is evil; "for offspring will not come to be," he says, "for the
evil." And reasonably such a one is not even filled with wickedness, depriving
his own soul of knowledge of God. And I say father and brother here according
to the sense of Scripture; for I am not unaware that the propositions laid
down do not hold true when they are set down of sensible brothers and fathers.
But if someone should wish also to take the bare sense of these sayings, he
will altogether condemn the childless rich who are eager to procure for
themselves a multitude of money, especially if they are also hard of access to
friends; for these sayings are very true when spoken against men.

29 4, 11 And also if the two should sleep, there is warmth for them; and the
one, how shall he be warmed? Without the Lord no one would become fervent in
the spirit; "for the Lord is the Spirit."

30 4, 12 And if the one should be overpowered, the two will stand against him,
and the threefold cord will not quickly be broken. I think the one who is
overpowered is the evil one, against whom the two stand, both the man and the
angel of God, so that the man, having conquered the devil, may be deemed
worthy of knowledge of God and become a threefold cord not quickly broken. And
this can be clearly learned also from the patriarch Jacob who, blessing the
children of Joseph, says: "my angel who delivers me from all evils, may he
bless these children." Like this also is what is laid down in David: "an angel
of the Lord will encamp round those who fear him and will deliver them."

31 A threefold cord is an impassible intellect being filled with spiritual
knowledge, or a wise intellect having an angel of God attending it. And it is
well that he did not say "it will not be broken," but "it will not quickly be
broken"; for the rational nature is changeable.

32 4, 13 Better a poor and wise child than an old and foolish king who does
not know still to take heed. A child is the one who has kept the teaching from
youth, and an elder is the one who has left the teaching from youth and
forgotten the divine covenant and grown old in wickedness; and the former
belongs to Christ, the latter to the evil one.

33 4, 14 For from a house of prisoners he will come out to reign, for even in
his kingdom he was born poor. A house of prisoners is the sensible world, in
which "each is bound with the chains of his own sins."

34 4, 17 Guard your foot, in whatever you go into the house of God, and near
to hearing; your sacrifice is above a gift of fools, because they are not
knowing how to do evil. "They do not know how they stumble," not even knowing
this very thing, that they transgress.

35 5, 1 Do not be hasty upon your mouth, and let your heart not be quick to
bring forth a word before the face of God; for God is in heaven, and you upon
the earth; upon this let your words be few. 5, 2 For a dream comes in a
multitude of trial and a voice of a fool in a multitude of words. "For what we
should pray as we ought we do not know." Or perhaps now he does not wish to
say this, but he commands not to theologize without consideration; for it is
not possible for one who is among sensibles and taking thoughts from them to
discourse without stumbling concerning God who is among intelligibles and who
escapes all sense. For this reason he says: "and let your words be few," that
is, true and considered; for "few" seems to me to signify something of this
sort, as also "better a little to the just than much wealth of sinners" and
"better a little taking with righteousness." For to those who do not keep this
he says: "a dream comes in a multitude of trial and a voice of a fool in a
multitude of words," calling "dream" the demon who stands over sleeping souls
with a multitude of trials and throws the soul into confusion, concerning whom
Job also says to the Lord: "you terrify me with dreams and you astound me with
visions"; and David, turning aside from this enemy, entreats the Lord saying:
"enlighten my eyes, lest I sleep unto death, lest my enemy say: I have
prevailed against him"; and in Proverbs: "give not sleep to your eyes nor let
your eyelids slumber, that you may be saved as a gazelle from snares and as a
bird from a snare." And he also calls him a voice of the fool, coming with
false words and deceiving the soul; and this is "from a voice of one who
reproaches and slanders." And it is possible also to fit to the voice of the
fool this: "from much speaking sin will not escape." And the Savior also in
the Gospels commands the man to keep watch and to pray, that he may not fall
into trial; for sleep of a rational soul is ignorance and wickedness; whence
Paul also wakes those who sleep thus, saying: "awake, you who sleep, and arise
from the dead, and Christ will shine upon you."

36 5, 3 As you vow a vow to God, do not delay to pay it; for there is no will
in fools, you then whatever you vow, pay. 5, 4 Better that you not vow than
that you vow and not pay. Of good gifts some are offered to God from the soul,
some from the body, some from the things around the body. And from the soul we
offer him right faith and true doctrines, righteousness and courage and
temperance; and from the body continence and virginity and monogamy; and from
the things around the body sons and daughters and slaves and money and
possessions. Let us then see the promising and delaying; perhaps the delay
does not signify the great interval of this time, but the complete denial of
the promise; for Jacob appears after many years to have paid to God the tenths
which he promised to give him as he was going to Mesopotamia, and Anna
bringing Samuel to God after a long time. Thus for the gifts around the body;
but for the gifts of the soul and of the body, how is it to be taken? I think
that the one who has promised right faith and says that one of those from the
Holy Trinity is a creature delays, and the one who has professed to confess
that all things have come to be through God and again introducing automatism
delays; and likewise for the other doctrines. And for the virtues thus: the
one who has promised righteousness and is unjust delays to pay, and the one
who has professed temperance and again commits fornication delays. And for
those of the body: the one who has promised continence and again partakes of
various foods delays, and the one who has professed virginity or monogamy and
has married or married a second time delays to pay. And as for how "better not
to vow than to vow and not pay," we will solve it by bringing forward from the
Gospel that the servant who has not known and has not done will be beaten with
few, but the one who has known and has not done will be beaten with many.

37 5, 5 Do not give your mouth to make your flesh sin and do not say before
the face of God that it is ignorance, lest God be angry at your voice and
destroy the works of your hands; 5, 6.1 for in a multitude of dreams and
vanities and many words. "In a multitude," he says, "of evil dreams and
vanities and false words" a man comes to be, his works having been destroyed
from the abandonment of God, which has happened to him through his own
transgression.

38 5, 7 If you see calumny of a poor man and seizure of judgment and of
righteousness in a country, do not wonder at the matter; for a high one
watches over a high one and high ones over them. 5, 8 And an abundance of
earth is upon all, a king of a worked field. 5, 9 One who loves silver will
not be filled with silver; and who has loved produce in his multitude; and
this too is vanity. 5, 10 In a multitude of goodness those who eat it have
been multiplied; and what is manliness to him from it? because it is a
beginning of seeing with his eyes. 5, 11 Sweet is the sleep of the slave,
whether he eat little or much; and to the one filled with being rich there is
none letting him sleep. If you see, he says, among men some being calumniated,
some being wronged in judgment and others practicing righteousness, do not
wonder at what comes about as though there were no providence. For know that
God through Christ watches over all things and this one again provides for all
through the holy angels who abound in knowledge of the things upon the earth.
For God is king of the world that has come to be through him, who will render
affliction to those who have preferred greed and the vanity of this life to
the knowledge of Christ, but to those who have lived in goodness and served in
courage and in righteousness he will give knowledge of God and sweet rest,
even if they have known some few or many reasons of the things here, having
known in part and having prophesied in part; and such an end will succeed
these, but those filled with wickedness the worm born from it will not allow
to rest. And that the Lord has entrusted this world to angels, Moses shows
saying: "when the Most High divided nations, as he scattered sons of Adam, he
set bounds of nations according to the number of angels of God." And our Lord
himself also named the world a field in the Gospels, saying: "the field is the
world." And he calls the abundance of the earth the knowledge of the things
upon the earth, if indeed "blessed are the meek, for they shall inherit the
earth"; and what other inheritance is there of the rational nature besides the
knowledge of God? And he called the angels high, since they partake of the
high Lord; "for high," he says, "is the Lord over all the nations."

39 5, 12 There is a sickness which I saw under the sun, wealth being kept for
its owner unto his wickedness. The multitude of wickedness is now indicated by
the wealth; and this wickedness is signified by the sickness. Everyone then
who keeps this wealth for himself will not know wisdom of God nor will he
apply his heart to understanding nor will he apply it unto admonition for his
son; for he has not received sayings of commandments of God nor has he hidden
them in his heart.

40 5, 13 And that wealth will perish in an evil distraction, and he has
begotten a son, and there is nothing in his hand. An evil distraction is
ignorance with punishment separating the unclean from spiritual contemplation.

41 5, 14 Just as he came out from his mother's womb naked, he will return to
go as he came and he will take nothing in his toil, that he may go in his
hand. 5, 15.1-2 And this too is an evil sickness; for just as he came, so also
he will go away. And Job says: "naked I myself came out from my mother's
belly, naked also I will go away there"; but here "just as he came out from
his mother's womb naked, he will return to go as he came." But Job as a just
man goes away naked of wickedness and of evil; but this one, with the
ignorance with which he came into this world, with this he will also go away
there.

42 5, 17 Behold what I have seen as good, which is beautiful, to eat and to
drink and to see goodness in all his toil with which he may toil under the
sun, the number of days of his life which God has given him; because this is
his portion. 5, 18 And also every man to whom God has given wealth and
possessions and has given him authority to eat from it and to take his part
and to be glad in his toil, this is a gift of God. 5, 19 For he will not
remember much the days of his life; because God distracts him in gladness of
his heart. The knowledge of God is called both food of the intellect and drink
and goodness and portion and wealth and possessions and gladness and
distraction of God and light and life and gift; and the Holy Spirit sets many
other names upon knowledge, which it is not possible now to list, the rule of
the scholia not permitting.

43 Toward a distinction from those who have received wealth of wisdom and
knowledge, yet have not kept it, it has been said "and he has given him
authority." For Judas the betrayer also received intelligible wealth and
spiritual possessions, but he did not have authority over them, having
betrayed for the sake of gain the wisdom and the truth of God.

44 When a man receives spiritual knowledge from God, he rarely remembers this
life and sensible life, his heart always being occupied about contemplation.

45 A distraction of God is true knowledge separating the purified soul from
sensible things.

46 6, 1 There is an evil which I saw under the sun, and it is great upon the
man; 6, 2 a man to whom God will give wealth and possessions and glory, and he
is not lacking in his soul from anything which he desires, and God will not
give him authority to eat from it, because a strange man will eat them; and
this too is vanity and an evil sickness. 6, 3 If a man should beget a hundred
and live many years, and as great as will be the days of his years, and his
soul will not be filled from goodness, and also a burial has not come about
for him, I said: the miscarriage is better than him, 6, 4 because he came in
vanity and he goes in darkness, and in darkness his name will be covered, 6, 5
and also he has not seen the sun and he has not known, there are rests for
this one above that one. 6, 6 And if he has lived a thousand years of descents
and has not seen goodness, do not all things go to one place? In this chapter
he speaks concerning those who have been deemed worthy of impassibility and of
knowledge and have again fallen by the envy of the devil. And the second
chapter contains a long life of an unclean man with many children who has not
known God, of whom he also sets the miscarriage as more honorable, and as
obtaining the same place as him after death.

47 The one who destroys the wealth of the one who has been enriched in all
knowledge and all wisdom is the evil one, whom he also calls a stranger and
alien to the knowledge of God. And "to eat" is said both of eating and of
destruction; "if you are willing and you hear me," he says, "you will eat the
good things of the earth; but if you are not willing nor hear me, a sword will
eat you up": instead of, will destroy. "For the mouth of the Lord has spoken
these things." And this stranger, having come also to David, persuaded him to
sacrifice the ewe-lamb of the poor man; for Nathan the prophet then also named
him a stranger.

48 6, 7 All toil of man is into his mouth, and also a soul will not be filled.
All wickedness of man remains in his heart, and he will not say "from his
fullness we have all received."

49 6, 8 For what is the abundance for the wise above the fool? because the
poor man knows how to go opposite life. Of the one who said: "I am the life."

50 6, 9 Better a vision of eyes than what goes with a soul, and this too is
vanity and preference of spirit. Good is the one who follows knowledge of God
above the one who follows the wills of the soul; or, knowledge of God is good
above perishable pleasure. And according to Symmachus: better to look toward
the one who is coming than to be glad upon the present things.

51 But it is not fitting to apply the vanity and the evil distraction and the
preference of the spirit to all the things in the chapter, but to apply them
to the blameworthy things, and not to apply them to the praiseworthy. And I
say this because he does not bring in according to common the vanity and the
distraction and the preference of the spirit upon the whole chapter, in which
some praiseworthy things also exist; for the poor man who goes opposite life
is not vanity, but the one who has lived a thousand years of descents and has
not seen goodness; nor again is the good vision of the eyes vanity, but the
one who goes with the soul.

52 6, 10 If anything has come to be, already its name has been called, and it
has been known what man is, and he will not be able to be judged with the one
stronger than he; 6, 11 for there are many words multiplying vanity. what is
more for the man? 6, 12 For who knows what is good for the man in life, the
number of days of his life of vanity? and he has made them in shadow; for who
will announce to the man what will be after him under the sun, and as it will
be, who will announce to him? Of names some belong to bodily nature, some to
the incorporeal; and the names of bodily nature signify the quality of each
thing, which consists of size and color and shape; and the names of
incorporeals indicate the state of each rational being, being either
praiseworthy or blameworthy; but the former names have been set upon things
simply, the latter not simply; for they depend upon preference. For it belongs
to free will to incline toward virtue and to be deemed worthy of knowledge
which makes him an angel or archangel or throne or lordship, or to incline
toward wickedness and to be filled with ignorance which makes him a demon or
satan or some other world-ruler of this darkness. If then, he says, anything
has come to be according to the time of the making of the world, it received a
name signifying its state, and man also received the name proper to his state.
Let not then, he says, the man say: for what reason have I been yoked to such
a body and why have I not become an angel, unless perhaps there is respect of
persons with God or we have not become free-willed? for such words multiply
vanity. And how will the thing formed say to the one who formed it: why have
you made me thus? or how does he answer back to God? but let him cease from
these words, and let him do the things that contribute toward virtue and
knowledge, so long as he is in this shadowy age, reckoning all things here
vain and a shadow and that the things of this life will be covered with
forgetfulness after the departure.

53 7, 1 A good name is above good oil and the day of death above the day of
his birth. Names by their own nature are neither good nor evil; for they
consist of various letters, and no stroke is good or bad; but being set upon
good things they are called good and upon evil things evil. Here then the good
name signifies a good thing; and Solomon says nothing of the things that have
come to be is good except virtue and knowledge of God. And the oil now
signifies bodily luxury which appears to those who live in luxury to be good.
For what is good by nature is said to be good as virtue is, and what is good
to some as gold and silver; thus also the rich man received his good things in
his life "and Lazarus likewise the evils." And that oil is taken instead of
luxury, David shows saying concerning men that "they have been multiplied from
fruit of wheat and wine and oil of theirs."

54 If the praiseworthy death according to which the just die together with
Christ is of a nature to dissolve a soul from wickedness and ignorance, so
again the generation opposed to this death joins a soul to wickedness and
ignorance; wherefore such a death is more honorable than such a generation.

55 7, 2 Better to go into a house of mourning than to go into a house of
drinking, inasmuch as this is the end of every man, and the living will give
good in his heart. The end of man is blessedness. And if the Lord blesses
mourning in the Gospels: "for blessed," he says, "are those who mourn, for
they shall be comforted," Solomon well says mourning is the end of man, which
fills those living in it with the spiritual goods.

56 7, 3 Better anger than laughter, because in wickedness of face a heart will
be made good. 7, 4 The heart of the wise is in a house of mourning, and the
heart of fools in a house of gladness. 7, 5 Better to hear a rebuke of a wise
man than a man hearing a song of fools; 7, 6 for as a voice of thorns under a
kettle, so is the laughter of fools; and this too is vanity. 7, 7 For calumny
carries about a wise man and destroys the vigor of his heart. When anger,
contending for virtue, boxes with the demons, it is strong and praiseworthy;
but when it fights with men over perishable things, it becomes blameworthy.
Here then what he says is of this sort, that the fool is made good in
wickedness and laughs and is glad upon it, neither refusing shameful songs nor
laughters that destroy his soul like a fire kindled in thorns; but the just
man both is angry against such passions and is indignant and considers
mourning more honorable than such gladness, and a rebuke of a wise man than
such songs; and he names such a life vanity and calumny, easily deceiving a
heart of a wise man and dissolving its vigor in the virtues.

57 7, 8.1 Better a last of words than their beginning. Better, he says, the
doer of the law than the hearer of the law; for the first words of teaching
are called words, but the last words are called words of the works, if indeed
the words also through the good works are said to be good words.

58 7, 9 Do not be hasty in your spirit to be angry, because anger will rest in
the bosom of fools. It is to be noted that here he names the soul "bosom"
outright; for no one would say that anger rests in the sensible bosom.

59 7, 10 Do not say: what has come to be that the former days were good above
these? because you have not asked about this in wisdom. If "fear of the Lord
adds days," it is in our power to enjoy good or better days of the holy
knowledge. But to think that the first places of knowledge are also given to
those older in time is not of the wise; for not everything ancient is also
worthy of honor, since wickedness too is altogether old, but it is not also
worthy to be honored because of time, if indeed "it is not the long-lived who
are wise nor do the old know judgment."

60 7, 11 Wisdom is good with an inheritance, and an abundance to those who
contemplate the sun; 7, 12 because in its shadow wisdom is as a shadow of
silver, and an abundance of knowledge of wisdom will make alive the one from
it. Just as those who contemplate the sun have something more than those who
have contemplated, but no longer contemplate, so those who have obtained
wisdom and have had authority over it (for this is "with an inheritance")
possess something more than those who have received and fallen from it through
their own transgression. That everyone who has acquired wisdom and lost it
first acquired a shadow of wisdom and not wisdom, and then also resembles a
man who has held a shadow of silver, but has not acquired silver; for wisdom
is of a nature, not when it is taken, but when it is present, to make alive
through knowledge the one who possesses it.

61 7, 15.2-3 There is a just man perishing in his just thing, and there is an
ungodly man remaining in his wickedness. Abandonment for testing is also
called destruction, as in the case of Job: "for I have perished," he says,
"and I have become without a house."

62 "But as for me, my feet," says David, "were almost shaken, my steps were
nearly poured out; because I was jealous of the lawless, seeing the peace of
sinners."

63 7, 16 Do not become just much and do not be wise overmuch, lest you be
struck. 7, 17 Do not be ungodly much and do not become hard, lest you die in
not your time. 7, 18 It is good that you hold to this, and also from this do
not defile your hand, because the one who fears God will come out of all
things. Let not, he says, an ungodly reasoning linger in your heart, lest your
soul having become ungodly die in ignorance. The Sodomites and the Gomorrhites
died not in their time. And if this time is a time of right-doing, those who
die in this time and are separated from the life that said: "I am the life,"
do not die in time.

64 It is good that you hold to not being just much, and also from ungodliness
do not defile your heart, because fearing God you will come out from all
wickedness.

65 8, 2.1 Guard a mouth of a king. He now calls "mouth" the word or the
commandment.

66 8, 12.3-5 For I also know that there will be good for those who fear God,
that they may fear from his face; 8, 13 and there will not be good for the
ungodly, and he will not lengthen days in a shadow who is not fearing from the
face of God. Now he calls good the knowledge of God.

67 8, 14 There is a vanity which has been made upon the earth, that there are
just men that it comes upon them as a work of the ungodly, and there are
ungodly that it comes upon them as a work of the just; I said that this too is
vanity. There is a vanity, he says, which comes about upon the earth, that
there are just and they fall into misfortunes as ungodly, and there are
ungodly and they enjoy goods as just. For concerning these the prophet also
says to the Lord: "yet I will speak judgments toward you: why is it that the
way of the ungodly prospers?"; and David: "my steps," he says, "were nearly
poured out; because I was jealous of the lawless, seeing the peace of
sinners."

68 9, 1.1-2 For I have given all this into my heart, and my heart has seen all
this. The man brings the things to the heart, turning aside toward the
investigation of them, and the heart after these things knows the things; and
this is "I went around, and my heart to know." For both the one who brings the
thing to the heart through examination goes around the thing, and the heart
again knowing it. But this must be known, that not all things which the man
goes around the heart also knows; for we examine many things, but we know few.

69 9, 10.3-4 For there is no work and reasoning and knowledge and wisdom in
Hades, where you go there. If there is no reasoning in Hades, how does the
rich man entreat Abraham to send Lazarus to him?

70 9, 12.1 And also man has not known his time. Man has not known that time is
a time of right-doing; for "time" indicates the opportune time.

71 11, 9.3-4 and walk in ways of your heart, blameless, and in vision of your
eyes. In practice and in contemplation.

72 11, 10.1-2 and put away anger from your heart and lead wickedness away from
your flesh. From this we know that the irascible is yoked to the heart, and
the concupiscible to the flesh.

73 Now he calls wickedness fornication and gluttony.
