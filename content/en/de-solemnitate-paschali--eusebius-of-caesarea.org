OF EUSEBIUS OF CAESAREA CONCERNING THE FEAST OF THE PASCHA.

αʹ. Perhaps it would not be untimely to treat again of the Pascha, which from
of old was handed down to the children of the Hebrews in a figure. When then
the Hebrews, performing shadows of things to come, first celebrated the feast
of Phasek, a young animal from the flock was taken for them; and this was a
lamb or a sheep; then they themselves sacrificed this through themselves; and
thereafter with the blood first each anointed the lintels and doorposts of
their own houses; in this way bloodying thresholds and roofs for the overthrow
of the destroyer. And using the flesh of the sheep for food, and having bound
their loins with a belt, =24.696= and partaking of the food of unleavened
breads, and taking to themselves the herb of bitter things, they passed from
place to place, that from the land of the Egyptians to the wilderness. These
things then had been legislated for them to do together with the slaughter and
the food of the sheep. Hence the passing over from Egypt filled out for them
the name of the rites of passing over. But these things happened to those men
in a type, and they were written for our sake. And indeed Paul interprets,
making clear the truth of the ancient symbols, through the things he says: For
indeed our Pascha, Christ, has been sacrificed. And the cause of his being
sacrificed the Baptist presents, saying: Behold the Lamb of God who takes away
the sin of the world. For indeed as a sacrificial victim, an apotropaic of all
evils, the saving body was given over to death, which after the manner of a
purification took away the sin of the whole world; Isaiah therefore has cried
out clearly: This one bears our sins and suffers pain concerning us.

βʹ. Being nourished then by the rational flesh of this saving victim who by
his own blood rescued the race of all men, that is, by teachings and by words
announcing the kingdom of the heavens, we reasonably luxuriate in the luxury
according to God. But also by faith of his blood, which he gave in exchange as
a ransom for our salvation, marking the houses of the soul, the bodies, we
drive away from ourselves every kind of plotting demons; and celebrating the
feast of the rites of passing over, we train ourselves to pass over to the
divine things, as of old having passed from Egypt to the wilderness. In this
way then we too set out on a certain path untrodden and deserted by the many,
making the old leaven of godless error an exile from the soul itself, and
taking to ourselves the true bitter herbs through the bitter and painful way
of life. And the time of the feast too is well-timed, not brought in in the
midst of the winter season; for this is gloomy; nor yet was it suitable in the
midst of summer, in which the turning, being inflamed, takes away the beauty
of those who spend their time in the fields; and the things of the hours also
take more than their share, not being balanced in equal portion; for the sight
of the autumnal turning is not pleasant, the country being widowed, and
deprived of its own fruits as of children. Spring then is left, the bright
one, which as a certain head of the whole year, as of a body, leads, the sun
just now riding through the first section, and the moon from a parallel
course, with full-shining light, changing the course of the night into a
bright day. This looses the terrors of winter crashings, and looses the long
intervals, and turns the floods of waters. And now as a fresh fair weather
shines forth, it sets the seas in calm for those who sail, and grants a mild
air to those who travel on land. And in this the lands in the fields are
pregnant with seeds, and plants swelling with fruits, exulting in the gifts
from God, provide to farmers the returns of their toils in blessings.

γʹ. This is the time of the feast, which for Egyptians, the friends of demons,
procured destruction, but for Hebrews celebrating to God, freedom from evils;
this was that very one, which was also observed at the first cosmogony of the
whole, in which the earth sprouted, in which the luminaries came to be, in
which heaven and earth were brought forth and all the things in them;
according to this also the Saviour of the whole world accomplished the mystery
of his own feast, and the great luminary brightened the inhabited world with
rays of piety, and indeed the time seems to contain the birthday of the world.
And according to this time also a type was celebrated, the ancient Pascha,
which is also called the rite of passing over; but it also bore a symbol of
the slaughter of a sheep; but it also hinted an image of food of unleavened
breads. And all these things were fulfilled upon the saving feast; for the
sheep was himself according as he was clothed with a body. He himself also is
sun of righteousness, a divine spring indeed and a saving turning having taken
hold of the life of men from worse things to the better. God-driven scourges
are sent down even now upon the demons of the Egyptians, and peoples dwelling
everywhere on earth celebrate their own freedom from much-wandering
godlessness. And the people-deceiving spirits having ceased, and the winter of
evils, an abundance of new fruits crowns the Church of God with various gifts
of Holy Spirit; and simply the whole race of men has been changed over to our
side; and all lands, having obtained from the husbandman Word the husbandry
according to the soul, have sprouted the seasonable flowers of virtue; but
also having been freed from the evils of darkness, we have been deemed worthy
of light in a day of knowledge of God.

δʹ. Such are the new teachings, of old indeed overshadowed through symbols,
but recently unveiled into light. And we too rekindle the chief point of the
feast by periods of cycles each year, taking up beforehand, for the sake of
preparation, the forty-day training according to the zeal of the holy Moses
and Elijah, and renewing the feast itself unto an unforgettable age. Setting
out then on the journey toward God, we bind our loins well with the bond of
self-control, and guarding the steps of the soul in safety, as in sandals we
prepare the course of the heavenly calling; and using a staff of divine word
in the power of prayers for defense against enemies, with all eagerness we
pass over the passage that bears to the heavens, hastening from the things
here to the heavenly things, and from mortal life to the immortal; for thus
another greater feast will succeed us from here, when we have passed over the
rites of passing over well and finely; the children of the Hebrews name it by
the surname Pentecost, bearing an image of the kingdom of the heavens. Moses
then says =24.700=: When you begin the sickle upon harvest, you shall number
for yourself seven weeks, and you shall set before God new breads from new
harvests. And he was making clear then by a prophetic type the harvest as the
calling of the nations, and new breads as the souls brought to God through
Christ, and the churches from the nations, upon which a greatest feast is
accomplished to the God who loves mankind; who having been harvested by the
rational sickles of the apostles, and having been gathered as into
threshing-floors, the Churches everywhere on earth, under one, and having been
embodied by a like-voiced disposition of faith, and having been seasoned with
salts, the teachings from the divine words, and having been reborn through
water and fire of Holy Spirit, we are brought through Christ as nourishing
breads, gentle and well-pleasing to God.

εʹ. In this way as the prophetic symbols beside Moses proceed into works with
more solemn results, we ourselves at any rate have received to keep the feast
more brightly, as having been raised together with the Saviour, and enjoying
his kingdom. Wherefore we are no longer permitted to toil according to this
feast, and we are taught to bear the image of the rest hoped for in the
heavens; whence neither in the prayers do we bend the knee, nor are we worn
out with fastings; for those deemed worthy of the resurrection according to
God it is no longer possible again to fall upon the earth; nor those freed
from the passions to suffer the equal things with those who have been
enslaved. Wherefore after the Pascha we celebrate the Pentecost in seven
complete weeks, having manfully accomplished the former period of the
forty-day joint training before the Pascha in six weeks; for the hexad is
practical and active; wherefore also in six days God is said to have made the
universe. And the toils in that one the second feast will reasonably succeed
in seven weeks, the rest being multiplied for us, of which the hebdomad wishes
to signify the symbols. Yet the number of the Pentecost does not stand upon
these; but having overshot the seven weeks, it seals with the last monad after
these the all-festive day of the ascension of Christ. Rightly then in the days
of the holy Pentecost, tracing out the rest that is to come, we are gladdened
in our souls, and we rest the body, as already being with the Bridegroom
himself, and not able to fast.

ςʹ. But that the holy evangelists related that the saving passion came to be
according to the days of the Jewish Pascha of the unleavened breads, no one
would doubt; for this was the cause of the law spoken beside Moses concerning
the Pascha. For since the Lamb of God was about to be led as a sheep to
slaughter among the Jews themselves, and to suffer this for the common
salvation of all men, at no other time than according to the time being shown,
God, anticipating the future through symbols in a figure, at that very time
which was going at some point according to periods of times to be established,
commanded a sensible lamb to be sacrificed among the Jews; and this was
accomplished for them year by year, until the truth, having been fulfilled,
circumscribed the ancient images. Whence from that time among the nations the
true feast of the mysteries has prevailed, but among the Jews not even the
memory of the symbols themselves =24.701= is kept, the place having been taken
away from them in which the things of the feast had been legislated to be
accomplished. Rightly then the divine writing of the Gospels says that the
Saviour suffered according to the time of the Jewish unleavened breads; since
then also he was led as a sheep to slaughter, following the prophetic voices.

ζʹ. And they according to Moses once of the whole year sacrificed a sheep of
the Pascha on the fourteenth of the first month, toward evening; but we of the
New Covenant, accomplishing our own Pascha on each Lord's day, ALWAYS WE ARE
FILLED WITH THE SAVING BODY, ALWAYS WE PARTAKE OF THE BLOOD OF THE SHEEP.
always we have girded the loins of our own soul with purity and self-control,
always we have prepared the feet in readiness of the Gospel, always we have
the staves in the hands, and we rest upon the rod that came forth from the
root of Jesse, always we are being set free from Egypt, always we pursue the
wilderness of human life, always we set out on the journey toward God, always
we celebrate the rites of passing over. For the evangelical word wishes us to
do all these things not once of the year, but always and through every day.
Wherefore also according to each week we accomplish the feast of our Pascha
according to the saving and Lord's day of the true sheep, through whom we were
ransomed, fulfilling the mysteries; and neither do we circumcise the body with
iron, but with the cutting word we take away every evil of the soul; nor do we
use bodily unleavened breads, but only those of sincerity and truth. For
grace, having freed us from the custom that had grown old, handed over to us
the new man created according to God, and the new law, and a new circumcision,
and a new Pascha, and the Jew in secret; thus it also left us free of the
ancient times.

ηʹ. But when the emperor most beloved of God was seated in front in the midst
of the holy synod, as the question concerning the Pascha was brought into the
midst, there was said as much as was said; and the triple portion of the
bishops of the whole inhabited world had the advantage in number, stretching
against from the other side those of the East; for northern together with
southern, and the nations toward the setting sun, having fenced themselves
with concord, pulled the opposite against those of the dawn who were defending
the ancient custom. And at the end of the word the Easterners yielded; and
thus there came to be one feast of Christ; and thus they stood away from the
Lord-killers, and were joined to those of the same doctrine; for nature
=24.704= draws like things toward like things. And if someone should say that
it is written: On the first day of the unleavened breads the disciples having
come said to the Saviour, Where do you wish that we prepare for you to eat the
Pascha? And he sent to such-and-such a man, charging them to say: I make the
Pascha toward you; we shall say that this is not a command, but a history of a
thing that happened according to the time of the saving passion. And it is one
thing to narrate an ancient deed, and another to legislate and to leave
commands to those after these things.

θʹ. But also the Saviour did not accomplish the Pascha with Jews according to
the time of his own passion. For not when those men were sacrificing the sheep
did he himself then also keep his own Pascha with his disciples; for they did
this according to the Preparation, in which the Saviour suffered; whence they
did not even enter into the praetorium, but Pilate goes out toward them; but
he himself a whole day before, on the fifth of the Sabbath, was reclining with
the disciples, and eating with them he said: With desire I have desired to eat
this Pascha with you. Do you see how the Saviour was not eating the Pascha
with Jews? Since that was new and strange beside the customary and Jewish
manners, he necessarily set it in, saying: With desire I have desired to eat
this Pascha with you, before I suffer; for the things being old manners and
having grown old, which he used to eat with Jews, were not desirable; but the
new mystery of his New Covenant, which he was imparting to his own disciples,
was desirable to him reasonably; since many prophets and righteous men before
him desired to see the mysteries of the New Covenant; and the Word himself
also, always thirsting for the common salvation, was handing down a mystery
through which all men were going to celebrate, and he confessed this to be
desirable to him. The Pascha of Moses did not fit all the nations then; for
how? when it had been legislated to be accomplished under one place, that in
Jerusalem; wherefore it was not desirable; but the saving mystery of the New
Covenant, fitting all men, was reasonably desirable to him.

ιʹ. But he himself, before suffering, ate the Pascha with his own disciples,
and accomplished the feast, not with the Jews; and when he had celebrated at
evening, the chief priests together with the traitor having come upon him,
laid the hands upon him; for they themselves were not eating the Pascha
according to the evening; for otherwise they would have had the leisure
concerning him; and then having seized him they led him away into the house of
Caiaphas, where having spent the night, when day came to be, they were
gathered, and they examine him first; then after these things having risen
together with the multitude they led him to Pilate. And then the Scripture
says that they did not enter into the praetorium, in order that they might
not, having entered under a Hellenic roof, as they supposed, be defiled, but
remaining clean, the all-defiled ones, when evening came on, might eat the
Pascha, they who strain out the gnat, but swallow the camel, they who having
been defiled in souls together with bodies against the Saviour with bloodguilt
=24.705=, feared to enter under the roof; but they on that very day of the
passion ate the Pascha destructive of their own soul, having demanded the
saving blood not on behalf of themselves, but against themselves. But our
Saviour not then, but a day before reclining with the disciples, was keeping
the feast desirable to himself.

ιαʹ. Do you see how from that time he was being separated from them and was
withdrawing from the Jewish bloodguilt, and was being joined to the disciples,
celebrating together with them the desirable feast? Therefore for us too the
Pascha is to be eaten with Christ, purifying from our own mind all leaven of
evil and wickedness, and being filled with unleavened breads of truth and
sincerity, having in ourselves within in the soul the Jew in secret and the
true circumcision, and anointing the doorposts of our mind with the blood of
the sheep sacrificed for us for the warding off of the destroyer against us.
And this not toward one period of the whole year, but also through every week.
Let Preparation for us be a fast, a symbol of mourning, for the sake of our
former sins, and for the sake of memory of the saving passion.

ιβʹ. The Jews at any rate I say have from the beginning slipped from the
truth, from when they have plotted against the truth itself, driving the Word
of life away from themselves. And the writing of the holy Gospels presents
this clearly. For it testifies for the Lord that he has eaten the Pascha on
the first of the unleavened breads; nor, as Luke says, on the day in which the
Pascha had to be sacrificed have they eaten the Pascha customary to them, but
on the one next after that, which was the second of the Unleavened Breads, and
the fifteenth of the moon, in which, our Saviour being judged by Pilate, they
did not enter into the praetorium. Not then on the first of the unleavened
breads, in which it had to be sacrificed, have they eaten it according to the
law; for otherwise they too would have made the Pascha with the Saviour; but
from that time together with the plot against our Saviour, having been blinded
by their own wickedness, they have also slipped from all truth. But we keep
the same mysteries through the whole year, on every pre-sabbath making the
remembrance of the saving passion through a fast, which the apostles then
first fasted, the Bridegroom having been taken from them; and through every
Lord's day being made alive by the consecrated body of the same saving Pascha,
and being sealed in the souls with his precious blood.
