Discourse on the Hippodrome.
=59.567=
A spiritual stadium and a heavenly course have been opened for us today
to those who are ready for the spectacle of the Spirit, not according to
the earthly man of dust, but according to the heavenly man of the
Spirit; in which one must run upon the earth, but look toward heaven. I
have been moved to set this forth for the refutation of the
spectacle-lovers, who overlook the spiritual spectacle of salvation, and
prefer the bodily hippo =59.568= drome of wickedness, or rather the
satanodrome. But consider earnestly, beloved, rather the unadorned
hippodrome of the Church, where Paul contends, not running the circuits
of the euripus and the turning-posts, but from Jerusalem round about as
far as Illyricum fulfilling the Gospel of Christ; where Elijah drives
the chariot, of whom it has been said: Chariot, chariot, charioteer of
Israel, and his horseman; not racing the mile from the white line to the
sphendone, but daring from earth =59.569= to heaven on a fiery chariot,
where a people of heaven-citizens is present for the spectacle, and a
spiritual judge sits, and a king looks on, and a heavenly gift of
victory is set, and the scourges of a fearful judgment lie ready. God
wishes you yourself to contend.
Therefore He has also given you the four-horsed chariot of the Gospels,
in which chariot there are white horses according to Zechariah: the
yoke-horses are light and immortality, the trace-horses righteousness
and truth, who go behind the chariot that goes toward the land of the
north; of which Wisdom says, A hard wind of the north; for truly a hard
and wicked spirit is the devil. In his chariot there are black horses:
the yoke-beasts are Hades and death, and the trace-horses darkness and
destruction, whom one must drive with the chariot of Christ, because The
chariot of God, it says, is ten-thousandfold, thousands of those who
steer, whom one must drive in the storm-waves of life, as in a sea. For
He has mounted His horses upon the sea, troubling many waters, and
overturning the chariots of the intelligible Pharaoh, and his power,
into the Red Sea. And He has also an outrider angel who pursues and
darkens the horses of your enemy. For let their way, it says, become
darkness and slipping, and an angel of the Lord pursuing them. You have
opposite you those who proclaim his gifts, Canaanites, Pharisees,
Sadducees; and you too have Moses, Samuel, and David, who proclaimed
beforehand concerning the coming of the Righteous One. He has mid-course
starters, demons, Bel and Dagon and Baal and Chemosh, who threw Israel
from their legs, and cast their limbs in the wilderness; and you too
have gate-openers, Paul and Silvanus and Timothy, as they themselves
say, that a great and evident door has been opened to us, and many
adversaries, who also sought that a word might be given them in the
opening of their mouth. He has Philetus, Judas, Demas and Hermogenes,
Hymenaeus and Alexander the coppersmith, full of hypocrisy; and you too
have spectators, the host of the angels, the ranks of the righteous
looking down from above: how all run, but one receives the prize.
One is the people of Christ according to the truth, one baptized in
Christ and having put on Christ; and no longer Jew nor Greek, male and
female, slave or free, Scythian, Arian, Eunomian, Marathonian, but one
in Christ. Run worthily of God, and live as a citizen, people of God, in
the spectacle-place of the all-holy Spirit. Let not the pre-race
pairings delight you, lest the dangers before the judgment overtake you.
If you wish to make pairings and set your arrays, you can do this rather
in the spiritual spectacle. Yoke against the lust-mad horses of the
devil your pious reasonings; array against the strong ones
long-suffering. For better, it says, is the long-suffering man than the
strong; against Judaism, Christianity; against Hellenism, godliness;
against fornication, chastity; against greed, almsgiving; against
falsehood, truth; against anger, meekness and gentleness. Again, against
the swift yoke the submissive, that is, against wrath and grief a gentle
spirit (For a gentle-spirited man, it says, is a physician of the
heart); against the swift, gentleness of spirit; against enmity,
friendship; against hatred, love; against affliction, thanksgiving;
against pride, humility; against wickedness, goodness; and against every
opposing thing once for all, have the orthodox and saving faith.
But you who drive the chariot must also have the self-control of the
Spirit, lest falling into nausea you grow despondent. For the one who
contends, it says, is temperate in all things; they then that they may
receive a corruptible crown, but we an incorruptible. Take also the
helmet of salvation, and the scourge in your hand, which our Lord Jesus,
having made of cord, drove from the =59.570= temple the merchants of the
devil. Stand firmly upon the vessel of election; mount the chariot of
the eunuch, O Philip, of him who has been delivered from all
uncleanness; mount the chariot; without distraction gaze at the
mapparius who ministers to the reading of the Gospel. Let no one think I
am speaking in jest; I do not lie. For as in the hippodrome the eyes of
all gaze upon the mapparius, when the signal has been set, as they wait
in his decision for the opening; no less also we who gather together
unto the same for the ecclesiastical spectacle, when the deacon is about
to open the four-doored Gospel, all gaze at him, keeping silence, and
when he begins the course of the reading, immediately we stand up,
crying out: But glory to you, O Lord. Mount the chariot, hunt, run the
course, bring down, cast out, turn back, seize, and make from the mouth
the good confession; run the course against the adversary; For he who
goes simply, goes with confidence. Bring him down as David did Goliath,
cast him out as Elisha did Gehazi, seize him as Elijah did Ahab in the
vineyard of Naboth, turn him back into the outer darkness, where there
is weeping and gnashing of teeth; take heed to yourself.
For take heed to yourselves, it says, lest your hearts be weighed down
in surfeiting and drunkenness. Bear all things; For bear one another's
burdens, it says. Having trackless ground, keep watch, Lest you dash
your foot against a stone; not turning back, remembering Lot's wife.
Keep the middle path of righteousness; do not turn aside to the right,
nor to the left. Seek from within, from paradise, seek from within. For
from the heart come forth evil thoughts, murders, adulteries, thefts,
false witnessings, slanders, blasphemies, the things that defile the
man. Do not go up with him, as God did not with Israel, because he is
stiff-necked; do not go up with him; he calls you into sin. He himself
will make you swerve. And get before him, saying to him: Get behind me,
Satan, for you do not mind the things of God, but the things of men.
Stay outside, as Elijah fleeing Jezebel; hasten toward the prize of the
upward calling. Let him not overtake you, as neither the servants of
Absalom overtook those around Jonathan, and Ahimaaz the priests of the
Lord as they fled; let him not seize you, as neither the servants of the
king of Jericho seized the spies; but if possible, deceive him as Rahab
the harlot deceived those who were searching. Let him not subdue you, as
neither Pharaoh subdued those around Moses and Aaron; but turn the bend
around him, as the magi did Herod. Do not be anxious; for you have God
as the arranger of the race, and Christ as physician. Be frightened at
nothing; for you have the Holy Spirit as trainer, who anoints you with
the myrrh by the mystical horn of Samuel. Nor fear thirst; for you have
upon you the water of rest of baptism through the gourd, that coming
forth to you, that is, through the preaching of Jonah, it may take you
up. Turn the chariot of his tyranny; cast him into the pit which he
made; cast him into the deep there. Cry out in the words of the Psalm,
Out of the depths I have cried to you, O Lord; Lord, hear my voice. Cast
him into the deep, having bent him down, yourself quite alone, out of
his destruction. Lift holy hands on high toward the king, having
received the prize of the palms after the manner of the children, and
having shaken them worship him, because Bel has fallen, Dagon has been
shattered. But thanks be to God who gives us the victory through our
Lord Jesus Christ.
By this victory you will also be able to open paradise as a public bath,
where there are four rivers, much water, as in a bath, in a place of
green pasture, so that you too may say in due season: In a place of
green pasture there he made me dwell, beside the water of rest he
nourished me; and upon all these things, boasting, you may say, I have
fought the good fight, I have finished the course, I have kept the
faith; henceforth there is laid up for me the crown of righteousness, in
Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be glory unto the ages of ages, Amen.
