Discourse of Amphilochius, bishop of Iconium, on the holy Saturday.
Today we celebrate a funeral feast of our Savior. And he, below among
the dead, is loosing the bonds of death and filling Hades with light
and awakening those who have fallen asleep, but we dance above the
earth, imagining the resurrection, and we do not fear corruption, lest
it prevail over incorruption. For "You will not give your Holy One to
see corruption," it says. And perhaps Jews and Greeks laugh at our
philosophy: the former expecting another Christ, the latter having
shut up their hopes in tombs, concerning whom the prophet reasonably
says: "Their graves are their houses forever." But those who laugh
groan (for they will groan later, seeing him whom they pierced and
insulted), but we, weeping, let us mix the Passion with delight.
Death snatched the Master Christ, but he will not hold Life with
himself. He swallowed him in ignorance, but he will vomit forth many
together with him. Willingly he is held now; after this day, having
despoiled Hades, he rises. Yesterday, being crucified, he darkened
the sun, and night came in at the middle of the day; today death is
paralyzed, having received a strange corpse. Yesterday creation
mourned, seeing the madness of the Jews, and put on darkness as a
mourning garment; today "the people sitting in darkness have seen a
great light." Yesterday the earth was shaken and deliberated about
flight and threatened to split apart for those who dwell in it, and
the mountains were smitten, and the rocks were split; and the temple
was stripped, as if a living thing tearing its garment, showing by
what it suffered that the holy things had been profaned. The lifeless
things perceived the outrage, but those who dared these things had
unfeeling souls. The elements were indignant and all but left their
order and would have wrought confusion of the universe, had they not
perceived the will of the Creator, that he was not being insulted
unwillingly. O new and paradoxical things! He is stretched on wood
who stretched out the heaven by a word, and he is wrapped in bonds who
bound the sea with sand, and he is given gall to drink who granted the
springs of honey, and he is crowned with thorns who crowned the earth
with flowers, and he is struck on the head with a reed who struck
Egypt with the ten plagues and covered the head of Pharaoh with the
waters, and they spit upon the face which the cherubim cannot bear to
look upon. And suffering these things he prayed for those who were
crucifying him, saying: "Father, forgive them, for they do not know
what they are doing." By goodness he conquers wickedness, he makes a
defense for the slayers of Christ, netting them for salvation, he
dissolves the charge by blaming ignorance, he is not angry at becoming
the plaything of their drunken outrage, he accepts the drunkenness and
by love of mankind calls them to repentance. What need is there to
say more? Having gained nothing from the goodness, they shut in a
tomb him whom none of existing things contains, and they set seals,
keeping salvation for us, and fearing the resurrection they station
soldiers as guards at the tomb. Who has seen a dead man being
guarded? Or rather, who has seen a dead man being warred against?
Who has heard of a death that is doubted and that produces fear in
those who wrought it? Who has not ceased toward an enemy after taking
away his soul? Who has not dissolved enmity, content with the death
of the foe? Why do you cower at him whom you have consumed, O Jew?
Why do you fear him whom you killed? Why do you tremble at him who
has departed? Why do you dread him who was slain? Why are you in
agony over him whom you crucified? You have security from the
slaughter. Take courage: if the one who died is a mere man, he does
not rise; if a mere man, you have well

planted the cross; if a mere man, he is not telling the truth when he
said "Destroy this temple and in three days I will raise it"; if a
mere man, he is held by death; if a mere man, why do you seal the
tomb, you fool? Wait for the third day and you will see the
refutation of your own madness. Cease being meddlesome and you will
behold the outcome of the events; cease raging against the truth;
cease warring against God and wounding yourself by the war; cease
insulting the sun of righteousness and thinking to quench those rays;
cease contending to choke up the fountain of life; cease troubling
the rulers and discoursing about a guard; cease buying the lie and
stirring the armed men. Do not toil in vain, do not spend your goods
on impiety, do not imagine victory against God, do not give pay to
soldiers to say one thing instead of another, do not lodge the crowd
at the tomb, do not put your trust in weapons. Resurrection is not
hindered by weapons, is not blocked by the seals, is not shut out by
soldiers, is not stolen by money. But you disbelieve? Did you not
see Lazarus of old shaking off death as if it were sleep? Did you
see how the dead man followed the command, and the bond did not
hinder? Did you see how he walked with the grave clothes themselves,
having heard "Come out"? Did you see how the voice fitted together
him who had been dissolved by death? He who was able to do those
things will also be able to do these; he who raised the servant is
himself raised all the more; he who gave life to the one who had
decayed will not leave himself dead. But indeed the blindness of the
Jews is great, and seeing the wonders it does not see; they have eyes
and do not look, they have ears and do not hear. For the god of this
age has blinded their hearts, so that the gospel of righteousness
should not shine upon them. But let us leave those for now to their
unbelief, and let us say with those around Mary the faithful,
fashioning for ourselves in thought the tomb of the Savior: "They
have taken away our Lord, and we do not know what they have done with
him." To him be the glory together with the undefiled Father and the
Holy Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
