TO THOSE WHO MAKE WAR ON THOSE WHO LEAD MEN TO THE MONASTIC LIFE
=47.319=
αʹ. When the children of the Hebrews, returning from the long captivity,
wished to raise the temple in Jerusalem that had lain on the ground for many
years, then certain barbarous and savage men, neither reverencing God, for
whom those men were raising the temple, nor broken by the misfortune of the
people, from which they had at last and with difficulty recovered, nor fearing
the punishment that follows from God upon those who dare such things, first
attempted through themselves to hinder them as they built; but when they
accomplished nothing, sending letters to their king, in which they called that
city a rebel and an innovator and a lover of war, they persuaded him to permit
them to hinder the building. And having received this authority from him, and
falling upon them with many horsemen, they both interrupted the work for a
time, and thought highly of the victory, at which they ought to have been cut
down, and supposed that their plot had reached its end. This then was a
prelude and beginning of the evils that were about to overtake them at once.
For the work was accomplished, and received a splendid end; and they learned,
and through them all men, that they were not after all making war on men,
neither Mithridates then, nor anyone else who should choose to make war on men
who have chosen to do some good thing, but on God himself before them, who is
honored through them. And it is not possible for the one who makes war on God
ever to come to a good end; but such a one at the beginning of his daring will
perhaps suffer nothing terrible, if indeed he should not suffer, God calling
him to repentance, and granting him to recover as from a kind of drunkenness;
but if he persists in the drunken outrage, gaining nothing from so great
long-suffering, he will at least benefit others greatly, teaching them by the
punishment upon him never to take hold of the fight against God, since it is
not possible to escape that unconquered hand. Such misfortunes then overtook
them at once, as to hide by the magnitude of the tragedy all the other
misfortunes. For after the murders and the countless =47.320= slaughters which
the hands of the Jews who were then being hindered worked upon them, the earth
was soaked with the blood of the slain through a great depth, and much mud
came from this blood; and the bodies of horses and of men being mixed
together, from these and from the wounds rubbed together in them so great a
multitude of worms was born, that the earth was hidden by the multitude of the
dead, and these themselves again by that of the worms. One would have said,
seeing that plain, that it was not bodies of the dead lying below, but that
there were many springs, bringing that race of animals from many places; so
much more violently than any downpour did the stream of worms well up from
that putrefaction. And this did not happen for ten and twenty days only, but
for a long time. And the things here were such; but the things there that will
succeed them are far more grievous than these. For not for a thousand years,
nor for ten thousand only, nor for twice as many, and thrice, but for infinite
ages the bodies, having received souls, then receive those torments and those
unspeakable pains. And both of these the blessed Isaiah knows, and Ezekiel the
spectator of paradoxical visions knows, who also divided the punishment of
these men, the one narrating that here, the other that there. βʹ. And I have
not remembered them now simply, but because someone coming announced to us a
bitter and grievous message, and one having much insolence against God. For
there are some men even now the same things as those barbarians


daring as those men did, or rather even far more lawless than those; for they
drive from every side those who lead men to our philosophy, and with much
threat they forbid altogether that they should not speak, nor teach any man
ever any such thing. I therefore both cried out at once when I heard it, and
kept asking the speaker continually whether he were not jesting in saying
these things. And he said, Away with you; for I would never jest at these
things, nor even speak and fashion such things in speech, things which I would
have valued highly, and often prayed, not even now, when they have happened,
to receive even in hearing alone. Then groaning more bitterly I said, Truly
these things are so much more unholy than what was dared by Mithridates and
all those men, as this temple is far more august and holier than that one. But
who, tell me, and from where, are those who have dared these things? and why,
and from =47.321= what cause, and looking toward what, do they throw stones
into the height, and shoot arrows toward heaven, and make war on the God of
peace? For Sameas and the Pharathaeans and the rulers of the Assyrians and all
those men were barbarians, as one can see even from the names themselves, and
they were trained far from the Jewish way of life, and they did not wish,
dwelling beside them, to see men growing; for they thought that the power of
those men would thereafter overshadow their own authority; but these men, with
what freedom of theirs being diminished, with what security being cut off,
having which of those in power cooperating, dared these things? For those men
had the kings of the Persians wishing the same things as they; but our own, as
I persuade myself, both desire and pray the opposite of these. Whence I have
also been filled with much perplexity, that while the kings are living in
piety, you say that such things are dared in the midst of the cities. And he
says: If you should learn even what is more paradoxical than this, that those
who do these things also wish to be pious, and call themselves Christians, and
many of them are even of those already initiated; and one of these very men,
the devil having blown much into him, dared to say through that polluted
tongue that he would even fall away from the faith, and would sacrifice to
demons; for he was choked seeing free and well-born men, able to live in
luxury, being led to this hard life. Hearing these things I was struck with a
more grievous blow, and guessing how many evils would come from this, I both
mourned the whole inhabited world, and said to God: Take my soul from me, and
lead me out of my necessities, and release me from this perishable life, and
transfer me to that place, where no one else will say any such thing, nor will
I hear it. For I know that when I have gone from here the outer darkness will
succeed, where there is much weeping and gnashing of teeth; but it is sweeter
for me to hear teeth gnashing than words sounding such things. And he, seeing
me mourning vehemently, said: But this is not the time for these things now;
for you will never be able by these tears to recover those who have perished
and are perishing; for I think the terrible thing will not even stop. But we
must see how we may quench the pyre, and stop the plague, at least as far as
our part goes; and if you would obey me at all, leaving these lamentations,
work a discourse having exhortation for those who are sick and in faction,
both for their own salvation and for the common salvation of all men. And I,
taking this book, will put it as some other drug into the hands of those who
are ill; for I have many friends among those sick with such things, and they
will bear to go over it both once and twice and many times, and I know that
they will quickly be released from the plague. You, I said, measure even the
things of our power by the measure of love; but I have neither any strength of
words, and I am ashamed to use even the strength that seems to be there for
such a subject, no one else so much, but all the Greeks, both those who are
and those who will come after, whom I have always, no less than for the
doctrines, upon =47.322= the slackness of life been mocking, and now I am
forced to teach our own evils. For if


some of those men perceive that among Christians there are some men so at war
with virtue and philosophy, that they not only stand away from the labors on
behalf of it, but do not even bear the words on behalf of these things, and
they do not even bear to stop the madness here, but if someone else should
counsel and converse about these things, they drive this man too from every
side; if they perceive these things, I fear that they may never even think us
to be men, but beasts and human-shaped monsters, and certain avenging demons,
and enemies of the common nature, and that they may bring this vote not about
those who are liable only, but also about our whole nation. And he, laughing,
said, You are jesting in saying these things; for I will now tell you
something more timely than this, if indeed you fear that from your words they
may learn what all have long since learned through the deeds. For just as if
some wicked spirit had filled the souls of all, so the mouths of all are full
of these words; and if you throw yourself into the marketplace, you will see,
and in the physicians' shops, and in every part of the city, where those who
wish to do nothing are accustomed to sit together, much laughter being moved
by all. And the subject of this laughter and comedy is the narratives of what
has been done against the holy men. For just as certain warriors who have
accomplished many battles, and have set up trophies, rejoice in narrating
their own exploits, so also these men are delighted at their own daring deeds;
and you will hear one saying, I first both laid hands on so-and-so the monk,
and inflicted blows; and another, I myself found the lodging before the
others; But I more than the others exasperated the judge, says another;
another puts the prison and the terrible things in the prison, and the
dragging of those holy men through the marketplace, in the rank of an
encomium; and another another thing. Then all burst out laughing at these
things. And these things in the assemblies of the Christians; but the Greeks
laugh both at these men, and at those laughed at by them; at the ones, for
what they did, at the others for what they suffered; and all things have been
filled with a kind of civil war, or rather these things are far more grievous
than that. For those who fought that war, whenever later they remember it,
both curse much those who stirred it, and ascribe all that happened in it to
some wicked demon; and those men are ashamed more than the others, by as much
as they may have done more than the others in it; but these men even pride
themselves on the daring deeds. And not this only, that this war is more
accursed than that one, nor that it happens against holy men who have done no
injustice only, but that it is also against men who know how to do no one
harm, and are prepared only to suffer. γʹ. Hold, I said, hold; for we have
enough even of these narratives, if I should not have to breathe out.
entirely; but make me go away having at least some small strength. For what
you have ordered will certainly come to be; only add no other narrative to us,
but go away and pray that the cloud of my despondency be scattered for me, and
=47.323= that I receive some inclination from the God who is being warred upon
toward the healing of those who make war on him; and he will give it
altogether, being both philanthropic, and not wishing the death of the sinner,
but that he turn and live. Having thus sent that man away, I took hold of
these words. For if this alone were the terrible thing, that those holy and
wonderful men of God should be dragged and torn, being hauled to courts, and
beaten, and suffering the other things which I narrated just now, and no harm
from these things were turning upon the head of those who dare these things, I
was so far from being pained at what was happening, that I would even have
laughed a very sweet and abundant laugh at them; since even small children,
when they strike their mothers without danger, move much laughter in the very
women being struck, and by as much as they do this from more anger, by so much
more for them


does it intensify the pleasure, so that they even dissolve and break with
laughter; but if by doing this continually and violently he should ever be
wounded, either a needle having been pinned in the mother's little tunic about
the girdle, or the shuttle before the mother's breast having received the
child's hand, then indeed, then, the mother, having let go the laughter, is
possessed by a greater anguish than the one who was struck; and for the time
she treats the wound, but thereafter with vehement threat she forbids them not
to do such things, so as not to suffer the same things again. And we too then
would have done the same things, if we saw this childish anger, and the blow
of infants, not bringing great destruction upon them; but since a little
later, even if they do not perceive it now, being possessed by anger, they
will weep and wail and lament, not such a lament as small children, but that
in the outer darkness, that in the unquenchable fire; let us do the same thing
again as the mothers, differing from them by so much, that we will not
converse with these children with threat and insolence as those women do, but
with much flattery and gentleness; since indeed for the saints there is no
harm from these things, but even greater the reward and more the confidence.
If we should speak the future goods, perhaps you will even laugh greatly, as
being always practiced in laughing such a laugh; but as to the present things,
even if you be lovers of laughter ten thousand times, you will not disbelieve;
for not even if you should wish will you be able, the facts themselves thus
speaking against you. You certainly hear of Nero, for the man became notable
from his wantonness, first and alone having found in such a rule certain new
ways of licentiousness and indecency; this Nero the blessed Paul, for he
happened to have been born in the same times as that man, accusing him of such
things as you accuse these holy men, for having persuaded a concubine of his,
greatly beloved, to receive the word about the faith, he was persuading her at
the same time also to be released from that unclean intercourse; accusing him
then of such things, and calling Paul a pest and a deceiver and the same
things which you utter, he first bound him, but when he did not persuade him
to abstain from the counsel toward the girl, at last he killed him. What harm
then came from this to the one who suffered evil? and what benefit to the one
who did evil? what was not a benefit to Paul then slain47.324? and what was
not a harm to Nero who killed him? is not the one celebrated everywhere in the
inhabited world as an angel, for I will speak the present things for now; and
the other slandered to all as truly a pest and a savage demon? δʹ. And the
things there, even if you disbelieve, it is necessary to speak for the sake of
those who believe; and yet you ought from the present things to believe those
also; nevertheless, however you may be disposed toward them, they will be
spoken by us, and will not be hidden. What then will the things there be? That
wretched and miserable man, squalid and downcast, full of shame and gloom, and
bending down, will be led away where the worm is unending and the fire
unquenchable; but the blessed Paul will stand with much confidence beside the
very throne of the king, flashing brightly, and clothed with so much glory as
to fall short in nothing of the angels and the archangels, and he will receive
so great a reward as it is likely a man should who has given over body and
soul for the sake of the things that seem good to God. For so it is: much
recompense lies for those who work the good things, but this becomes greater
and more when those who work these good things also go through dangers and
much dishonor; and even if the achievement be equal for the one who has
accomplished it without toil, and for the one who has accomplished it with
labors, the things of honor and of the crowns will not be equal. Since even in
wars the one who has set up a trophy is crowned, but still far more greatly
the one who can also show the wounds through which he set up the trophy. And
why do I speak about the living, when even those who have shown this only, to
die well in war, and have benefited their own no further, are celebrated
everywhere in Greece as saviors and protectors? even if you are ignorant of
these things, attending always to laughter and luxury


attending? But if Greek men, thinking nothing at all sound, were able to see
this, and have honored with much honor those who only died for them, and
worked nothing further, how much more will Christ do this, who everywhere
conquers those who run risks for him with the recompenses of gifts from a
great surplus? For not of persecutions only, nor of blows, nor of prisons and
murders and slaughter, but even of insolence alone and of reproachful words he
has set a great reward. For blessed are you, he says, when men shall hate you,
and when they shall separate you and reproach you, and cast out your name as
evil, for the sake of the Son of man; rejoice in that day and leap; for
behold, your reward is great in heaven. If then to suffer evil and to hear
evil intensifies the reward both for those who suffer and for those who hear
evil, the one who hinders them from suffering and hearing evil has not
benefited these, but those who speak and those who do. For he has harmed
these, cutting off from them the greater part of the reward, and taking away
the ground of the joy and of the many leapings. So that for the sake of these
it was even necessary to be silent, and to let these things happen which make
for them the great wealth of goods, and prepare greater confidence; but since
we are members of one another, even if they themselves again refuse the grace,
those so disposed must not take thought for one part and overlook the other.
For for those there will be even another ground of good repute ὑπό47.325, even
if they do not now suffer evil; but these, if they do not abstain from this
fight, will no longer be able to be saved. For this reason then, overlooking
their part, I stand toward yours, and I beg and entreat both that you be
persuaded by us as we exhort, and that you no longer thrust the sword against
yourselves, nor kick against the goads, nor, thinking you are grieving men,
grieve the Holy Spirit of God. For that you will praise this judgment of ours,
if not now, yet afterward, I know and am persuaded; but I wish you to do this
now, so that you may not do it in vain after these things. Since even that
rich man, when he was here, held the prophets and the law and the exhortation
from these to be a kind of myth and nonsense; but when he went there, he so
admired their counsel that, when he perceived that he could be benefited
nothing from the praises, he begged the patriarch to send someone of those in
Hades announcing to those on the earth, fearing this, lest they too should
suffer the same thing, and having mocked the divine Scriptures, should then
admire them, when there is no benefit to them from the admiration. And yet
that man did nothing so great as you. For he did not share his own things with
Lazarus, yet he did not also hinder others who wished to share, or drive them
away as you do now. And not by this cruelty only have you outstripped him, but
also by another. For just as it is not equal himself to do nothing good, and
to hinder others who wish to do it, so it is not equal to lack bodily food,
and, lying in a great famine of philosophy, to hinder being fed by others. So
that you have doubly surpassed that cruel rich man, both by hindering others
from quenching the famine, and by displaying this inhumanity while the soul is
being strangled. This the Jews also once did, and they hindered the apostles
from speaking to men the things toward salvation. Nevertheless you are even
worse than these. For they, having taken up the rank of enemies, thus did all
those things; but you, having put on the mask of friends, display the things
of enemies; they then scourged and insulted and threw a wicked reputation
around the holy apostles, calling them sorcerers and deceivers. But such a
punishment overtook them, that no misfortune could thereafter be equaled to
their terrible things. For these first and alone of all men under the sun
suffered such things as no other. And a trustworthy witness of these things is
Christ, speaking thus: There will be great tribulation, such as has not


happened from the beginning of the world until now, nor shall happen. To go
through all their sufferings then is not of the present time, but it is
necessary to speak a few from many. And I will speak not my own word, but that
of a Jewish man who has recorded their things accurately. What then does this
man say? After he narrated the burning of the temple, and announced those
novel misfortunes. εʹ. The things about the temple, he says, were in these;
but of those perishing by the famine throughout the city an infinite multitude
was falling, and unspeakable sufferings were happening. For in each house, if
anywhere a shadow of food should appear, there was war, and the dearest came
to hands against one another, snatching the wretched provisions of the soul.
And there was not even for the dying a trust of want, but even those breathing
out the =47.326= robbers searched, lest someone having food under the fold
should be pretending death for himself. And those gaping from want like mad
dogs were stumbling and being carried aside, and dashing against the doors in
the manner of drunkards, and from helplessness they leaped into the same
houses twice or thrice in one hour. And necessity brought all things under the
teeth, and collecting things not even suitable for the filthiest of the
irrational animals, they endured to eat; at last they did not abstain from
belts and shoes, and stripping the hides of the shields they chewed them. And
food for some was also shreds of old grass; for some collecting the fibers
sold a very small weight for four Attic drachmas. And why must one speak of
the shamelessness of the famine upon lifeless things? For I am going to show a
work of it, such as has been recorded neither among Greeks nor among
barbarians, terrible to speak, unbelievable to hear. And I at least, lest I
should seem to the men after us to be telling marvels, would even have gladly
omitted the misfortune, if I did not have countless witnesses of those in my
own time; and besides I would be paying a cold favor to the fatherland if I
understated in words the deeds which it has suffered. A certain woman of those
dwelling beyond the Jordan, Mary by name, of a father Eleazar, of the village
Bethezō, and this means house of hyssop, notable for family and wealth, having
fled with the rest of the multitude into Jerusalem was being besieged with
them. The tyrants plundered the rest of her property, as much as she had
packed up from Peraea and transferred into the city; and the remnants of the
treasures, and if anything of food should be devised, the spearmen leaping in
each day seized. And a terrible indignation came upon the woman, and often
reviling and cursing the plunderers she provoked them against herself. But
when neither anyone being exasperated, nor pitying her, killed her, and to
find some food she toiled in vain, and from every side even finding was
already impossible, and the famine was going through bowels and marrows, and
the angers burned more than the famine, taking anger as counselor together
with necessity, she went against nature, and seizing the child, and she had a
child at the breast, she said, Wretched infant, in war and famine and faction,
for whom shall I keep you? The things from the Romans are slavery, even if we
live under them; but the famine outruns even slavery, and the factionists are
more grievous than both; come, become food for me, and a Fury for the
factionists, and a tale for life, the only thing lacking to the misfortunes of
the Jews. And saying these things at once she kills the son, then having
roasted him, she eats half, and covering the rest she kept it. And immediately
the factionists were present, and having drawn in the unlawful smell, they
threatened, if she should not show what had been prepared, to slaughter her at
once. And she, saying she had kept even a good portion for them, uncovered the
remnants of the child. And immediately both horror and a standing-out of the
mind seized them, =47.327= and they were frozen at the sight. And she said,
This is my genuine child, and the deed is mine; eat, for I too have eaten; do
not become either softer than a woman, or more compassionate than a mother.
And if you are pious, and turn away from my sacrifice, I have eaten half, and
let the rest remain for me. After these things they went out trembling,
cowards toward this one thing, and yielding to the mother this food alone. And
immediately the whole city was filled


of the abomination, and each taking the suffering before his eyes shuddered as
if it had been dared by himself; and the haste of those starving was toward
death, and a blessing of those who had arrived before hearing and seeing such
evils. And quickly the suffering was announced also to the Romans. And of them
some disbelieved, some pitied, and it happened that the many proceeded to a
more vehement hatred of the nation. ςʹ. And they suffered these things, and
far more grievous than these, not because they crucified Christ only, but
because even after these things they hindered the apostles from speaking the
things toward our salvation. Which also the blessed Paul, having accused them,
prophesied these evils to them, saying that The wrath has come upon them to
the end. And what are these things to us? he says; for we do not lead away
from the faith, nor from the preaching. And what is the benefit of the faith,
tell me, if there is no pure life? But you perhaps are ignorant even of these
things, as being unhearing of all our things; but I will enumerate for you the
sentences of Christ. And you observe whether life is nowhere accused then, but
the things of punishments have been defined for faith only and doctrines. For
when he went up into the mountain, seeing a great crowd flowing around him,
after the other exhortations he said: Not everyone who says to me, Lord, Lord,
will enter into the kingdom of the heavens, but the one who does the will of
my Father; and, Many will say to me in that day: Did we not prophesy in your
name, and in your name cast out demons, and in your name do many powers? And
then I will confess to them: Depart from me, you who work lawlessness, I do
not know you. And the one who hears, but does not do his words, he said is
like a foolish man building the house upon the sand, and making it easy to
take for rivers and rains and winds. And in another place speaking publicly,
Just as the fishermen, he says, when they have drawn the net, separate the
worthless of the fish, so it will be on that day, the angels throwing all who
have sinned into the furnace. And discoursing about wanton and unclean men he
said that They will go away, where the worm is unending, and the fire
unquenchable. And again, A king, he says, made a marriage for his son, and
seeing a man clothed in filthy garments, he said to him: Friend, how did you
come in here not having a wedding garment? And he was muzzled. Then he says to
his deacons47.328: Binding his hands and feet, throw him into the outer
darkness; threatening these things to the unrestrained and wanton. And virgins
too who were shut out of the bridechamber suffered this through cruelty and
inhumanity. And others again for the same cause go into the eternal fire
prepared for the devil and his angels. And those who speak simply and at
random are condemned; For from your words, he says, you will be justified, and
from your words you will be condemned. Do we then seem to you to have feared
in vain for life, and to make much zeal for the more ethical part of
philosophy? I at least do not think so, unless you should say that Christ has
said all these things at random, and more than these; for not all have been
said. And if I did not shrink from making the discourse long, I would have
taught also from the prophets and from the blessed Paul and from the other
apostles how much zeal God has made for this part. Nevertheless I think even
these suffice; or rather not even these only, but even a small part of what
has been said. For when God pronounces, even if he should say it once, one
must receive what has been said as having been said many times. ζʹ. What then,
those remaining at home, he says, will they not be able to accomplish these
things, which not being accomplished bring so great a punishment? I myself
also wished no less than you, but even much more, and I have often prayed that
the need of the monasteries be taken away, and that so great good order come
to be in the cities, that no one should ever need the refuge into the desert;
but since the things above have become below, and the


cities, where there are courts and laws, are full of much lawlessness and
injustice, but the desert teems with much fruit of philosophy, it is not those
who lead out of this storm and confusion those who wish to be saved, and guide
them toward the harbor of quiet, who would justly be accused by you, but those
who make each city so impassable and unfit for philosophy, that those who wish
to be saved are forced to pursue the deserts. For tell me, if someone at
midnight taking a torch burned a great and populous house, plotting against
those sleeping within, whom would we have said to be evil, the one who wakes
those sleeping, and leads them out of that house, or the one who kindled the
fire from the beginning, and set so great a necessity around both those men
themselves and the one who led them out? And what if someone, seeing a city
being tyrannized and sick and in faction, persuaded those he could of those
dwelling in this city to run up to the peaks of the mountains, and having
persuaded at the same time, also cooperated with them toward this withdrawal,
whom would you have accused? the one who transferred from this confusion
toward that calm the men being stormed in the midst, or the one who worked
these shipwrecks? Do not think that the affairs of men are now disposed better
than a city being tyrannized, but even far more grievously. For not a man, but
some wicked demon, having occupied the whole inhabited world like a savage
tyrant, with all his phalanx has burst into the souls of men; then from there,
as from a kind of acropolis, he sends down each day to all the polluted and
accursed commands, not tearing marriages, nor sending and carrying money only,
nor working unjust slaughters, but the things far more grievous than these,
the soul =47.329= once betrothed. to God, leading it away from converse with
him, and giving it over to his unclean spearmen, and forcing it to lie down
with them; who, whenever they once receive it, so come together with it
shamefully and insolently, as it is likely wicked demons should who love
vehemently and madly both our destruction and our indecency. For having
stripped it of all the garments of virtue, and putting around it the
rag-passions of vice, the filthy and torn and foul-smelling, and making it
