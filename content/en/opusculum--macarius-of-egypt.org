A discourse of Macarius the Great on the guarding of the heart. 1. How can the
chance man say that fasting, and living as a stranger, and feeding with my
possessions, I am holy? Is he holy, not having been purified as to the inner
man? =34.824= For the abstinence from evils is not the purification, but the
perfect purification according to conscience. For enter, O you, through the
straining of your thoughts toward your mind, captive and slave of sin, and see
the one lower than the mind and deeper than the thoughts, in the so-called
storehouses of your soul, the creeping and lurking serpent, who murdered you
through the more vital members of your soul; for the heart is an
incomprehensible abyss; and if you kill this one, boast to God concerning
purity; but if not, being humbled as one in want and a sinner, praying to God
for your hidden things. 2. The true death is hidden within in the heart, and
the man has been put to death within. If anyone then has passed from death
into life in the hidden place, this one truly lives unto the ages, and does
not die; but even if the bodies of such are dissolved for a time, yet they are
sanctified and are raised with glory; wherefore we also call the
falling-asleep of the saints a sleep. 3. The whole contest of the adversary is
this, that he may be able to tear our mind away from the memory of God and
from love, using earthly baits, and turning it aside from what is truly good
toward things that seem, but are not truly, good. For whatever good a man may
do, the evil one wishes to stain and defile, and he strives to mix his own
after-sowing of vainglory or conceit with the commandment; so that the good
that is done may not come about through God and only according to a good
eagerness. 4. What then? We who have not entered into the heart at all, how
shall we begin? Through fasting and prayer, standing outside, we knock, as the
Lord commanded saying: "Knock and it shall be opened to you." For if we remain
in the word of the Lord, and in poverty, and in humble mind, and in all the
virtues of the commandments, knocking night and day at the spiritual door of
the Lord, we shall be able to obtain what is sought. For through this door
everyone who wishes to flee the darkness can obtain redemption. For there he
finds the freedom of the soul, and receives back its thoughts, and acquires
the heavenly king Christ. 5. When the mind has forgotten the noetic and pious
affliction, then it has forgotten the commandments also; and from wherever it
seems to run, it turns aside from the smooth way, and conducts itself
crookedly. Therefore it meets beasts. For if we did not cut off the labor of
prayer and hope, we would not have sinned; for to those affli34.825cted
concerning the soul Scripture says: "God is faithful, who will not let you be
tempted beyond what you are able"; but evils will meet the uninstructed. 6.
Just as the outer eyes see from far off the thorns and the precipices; so also
the foreseeing mind, being swift, foresees the arts and the devices of the
opposing power, and makes the soul secure, being as it were its eye. 7. There
is need of much contest and hidden and invisible toil, to make a search of
thoughts, and to exercise the weakened sense-organs of our soul toward the
discernment of good and evil; one must always also rekindle, through the
mind's urgency toward God, the members that have been weakened in the soul;
our mind being called together with the Lord always, that it may become one
spirit according to the saying of Paul. And this hidden


contest and meditation of the Lord and toil night and day we must have toward
every practice of a commandment, whether praying, or eating, or serving, or
drinking, or doing anything else, that every good practice that comes about
may come about unto the glory of God. For the whole sequence of the
commandments also is sanctified; and it is purely accomplished by you, through
the unceasing memory of God, and fear and love toward God, and we become
outside of the one who defiles the commandments of God. 8. For the patriarch
Abraham also offered from the first-fruits to Melchizedek the priest of God,
and thus received the blessing from him. And what else does the Spirit hint at
through these things, but showing somewhere a higher contemplation, the
extremities and the bones of the whole compound of our nature? that is, the
mind itself, the conscience itself, the disposition itself, the thought
itself, the loving power of the soul itself, that we must always first bring
to God the first-fruits of our whole man, the holy sacrifice of the heart;
occupying the first-fruits, and the extremities of the good thoughts, always
in the same memory and meditation. And thus we are able day by day to receive
increase and progress, being helped by the divine grace, and the burden of the
righteousness of the commandments will appear light to us, accomplishing them
purely and blamelessly, being co-worked by the Lord himself through our faith
toward him. And concerning the apparent ascesis, and which good practice is
greater and first, know this, beloved, that all the virtues hang from one
another, and are bound to one another, and like some holy spiritual chain, one
hangs from one; prayer from love, love from joy, joy from meekness, meekness
from humility, humility from service, service from hope, hope from faith,
faith from obedience, =34.828= obedience from simplicity. Just as also the
contrary, in one the evils are given out; hatred from anger, anger from pride,
pride from vainglory, vainglory from unbelief, unbelief from hardness of
heart, hardness of heart from neglect, neglect from slackness, slackness from
little-caring, little-caring from acedia, acedia from small-souledness,
small-souledness from love of pleasure, and the remaining parts of wickedness
are hung from one another. Thus in the good part the virtues are hung. And the
head of every virtue and the summit of the achievements is perseverance in
prayer, through which we can also acquire the remaining virtues day by day
through asking from God. 9. If humility, and simplicity, and goodness do not
adorn us, the form of prayer will benefit us nothing. And we say this not only
concerning prayer, but also concerning every labor, or toil, or virginity, or
prayer, or whatever labor and work accomplished for the sake of virtue; if we
do not find in ourselves abundantly the fruits of love and peace and joy and
meekness and humility, both simplicity and guilelessness, faith and
longsuffering, our labors have come about in vain and to no purpose; for all
that work, and the labors, ought to come about for the sake of the fruits. And
if the fruits of love and peace are not found in us, the whole work comes
about in vain and to no purpose. For those who work without these are shown in
the day of judgment according to the five foolish virgins, who, because they
did not bring from here in the vessels of the heart the spiritual oil, which
is of the aforementioned virtues, were named foolish, and were shut out of the
spiritual bridechamber of the kingdom. And the labor of virginity, through the
lack of the virtues and the deprivation of the manifest indwelling of the
Spirit, was reckoned as nothing. For just as in the farming of the


vine every care also comes about for the enjoyment of the fruits, and if
fruits are not found in the vine, all the toil of the work comes about in vain
and to no purpose; so if we do not recognize in ourselves, through the energy
of the Spirit, fruits of love and peace and joy and humility, and of the rest
spoken by the Apostle, in all full-assurance and spiritual perception, the
labor of virginity, and of prayer, and of psalmody, and the toil of fasting
and vigil, is shown to be nothing. For these toils of soul and body ought to
be accomplished in hope of spiritual fruits; and the fruit-bearing of the
Spirit in the virtues is a spiritual enjoyment in incorruptible pleasure, in
faithful hearts, being energized by the Spirit; for in much discernment of
understanding let the work and toil and labors of nature be led, through faith
and hope, being energized by the Holy Spirit for the worthy. Fasting is good,
vigil, sojourning; but these are still of a good way of life =34.829=; yet the
rank of Christians is more inward than these, and one ought not to have
confidence in these alone. And it happens that there are some who are
partakers of grace; and wickedness, still being, crafts, and yields of its own
will, and does not act, but makes the man think that his mind has been
purified; and then it brings him conceit, that I am a perfect Christian, and
after these things, when the man thinks that I am free, and is without care,
then in a robber-like manner wickedness comes to him making an ambush, and
tempts him, and brings him down to the lowest parts of the earth. For if men
often, being robbers or soldiers against enemies, know how to craft, and lie
in wait, and make an ambush, and take the backs of the enemies, and surround
them suddenly, and kill them; much more does wickedness, being of so many
thousands, having this work, having destroyed so many souls, know then to make
an ambush in the heart, at times not acting, that it may bring the soul into a
conceit of perfection? 10. The foundation of Christianity is this, that if a
man does righteousnesses, he should not rest in them, and hold himself great,
but be poor in spirit; and if he becomes a partaker of grace, he should not
think that he has attained something, and suppose himself to be somebody, and
begin to teach; but that being of a good sojourning and way of life, fasting
much, living as a stranger, praying, partaking of grace, he should not reckon
his soul precious; but then especially, when a beginning of grace comes about,
that he should have the toil, the thirst, that being filled he should not hold
himself righteous or rich in grace; but that he should be mourning and
weeping. And in the way that if there is a mother, and she has an
only-begotten son and has trained him, and when he becomes a man, it happens
to him to die; and then as many as are comforting the mother rather raise up
the mourning in her, and she is inconsolable; so the Christian must weep his
fall, and weep unceasingly; and before all things have a crushed heart. 11.
Just as if a house of a king having many apartments and various courts, and
many vestibules, and inner houses, where the king remains, and the purple, and
the treasures are laid up; then he who enters into the outer courts should not
think that he has already come into the inner houses, where the glory of the
king, and the purple, and the treasures; so in the spiritual, those who fast,
those who keep vigil, those who psalm and pray, should not think that they
have entered into the rest, but as still they enter into the vestibules and
into the courts, not where the purple and the treasures. Brothers therefore
ought not to take courage in the outer way of life, and say that I am
somebody; and if one also becomes a partaker of grace, to suppose as having
attained, or having become nearest to the king; for still he spends time in
the outer houses. Each ought not to sear34.832 ch whether he found in the
earthen vessel the treasure, or put on the purple of the Spirit, or found the
king, and rested. For the


soul is such; it has a certain depth and many members; and then sin, having
entered, holds all its members, and the thoughts of the heart. Then, as the
man seeks, grace comes to him, and holds two members of the soul. The
inexperienced then, being comforted by the grace, thinks that the grace having
come has taken hold of all the members of the soul, and that sin has been
rooted out; then therefore the greater part is held by sin and one part by
grace, and he is stolen and does not know; for grace is often acting
unceasingly, in the way that the eye in the body has been made; and sin is
present together, stealing the mind; and the undiscerning, as having already
attained, thinks something about himself and is puffed up as having been
freed. For things are not so; for as I said, Satan makes an ambush, at times
not acting, that he may make the man think that I am pure and perfect; does
the one who plants a vineyard immediately receive a grape, or farm wine, and
the one who sows the seed in the earth, has he already harvested and received
fruits, and can the child being born immediately come to a perfect man, or has
the conscript soldier already become an officer? for he must first toil, and
having entered war receive the prizes of victory and be crowned. 12.
Possessionlessness then is good, and psalmody, and fasting and vigil, and the
receiving of the grace of God; but he still has not yet begun as he ought to
dig and to lay a foundation. And in such things the mind must be blameless, a
partaker of grace; then he thinks about himself, and holds the soul precious.
Look to Jesus, from what glory, being Son of God and God, he came into what
sufferings and crucifixion. Through this humility then he was highly exalted,
sitting at the right hand of the Father; and the serpent from the beginning
sowed in Adam height, that "You become as God"; through this high-mindedness
you saw then into what dishonor the race of Adam came down. I seek then a man
poor in spirit, and I do not find; just as if someone should be rich having
treasures in his house, and should be in want, and poor and seeking from all.
Thus there are Christians having grace, rich toward God, they are poor in
spirit, as having nothing; there are toward a measure a hundred pounds of gold
which he ought to acquire; he who has taken five pounds has been filled. And
there are some having toward a measure ten ounces of grace, and they make them
a hundred, boasting. And there are often those having five ounces of sin,
=34.833= and they make them half-ounces; and this happens to them, either
through ignorance, because being stolen in the grace itself they do not know.
Or through vainglory, being ashamed to say that the evil is acting; for
already they proclaim themselves to be perfect. But the just thing is that the
man should do and say; both confess the things of grace, and the energy of
sin. For such a one lies saying that he has a pure heart; for things are not
so, that as soon as grace takes up residence the man has already become pure;
for they have been handed over to the enemies and to the pirate-bands for
exercise and training, in the way that Job also to the temptation. For the
evil co-works with the good, not with a good choice; but the Christian, having
five ounces of sin, must say that I have twenty, and I am filled with the
evil. And having toward a measure twenty ounces of the good, say that I have a
half-ounce. And if through vainglory he is ashamed to say this, let him at
least say the just thing. Let him confess both the energy of grace; and let
him confess also the sin in part. As then I said before concerning the soul,
that it has depth, we use examples drawing its members. Just as if there
should be a sun, and from it many rays; or if there should be a tall tree, and
it should have branches; or if there should be a great city, and it should
have many neighborhoods; so is the noetic essence, the immortal soul, a
precious beauty above all the created things, image and likeness of God. Then
grace having come, shines upon two rays of the soul; or from the whole


tree upon two branches, or from the whole city upon two neighborhoods. And
still the greater parts of the soul are held by sin; and he thinks that it has
become wholly through and through of the good part and of grace and of
brightness; having acquired toward a measure five pounds of gold, it seems to
him that he acquired a hundred pounds; has the embryo in the womb already
become a man? or because he has set one stone of the foundation, has he
already completed the building? or has the seed that was buried upon the earth
already become an ear? does the merchant, at the same time as beginning the
trade, already fill the storehouse? and has he who partakes of a small grace,
by this become a Christian? still the first and the great ones are thus toward
perfection, as the official toward a governor; or as a small stream toward the
river Euphrates. He who has a purpose to go to a city far off of thirty
stations, if he goes two or three, let him not think that he has entered into
the city; since then the opposing power is persuasive, not compulsive; and
divine grace is persuasive because of the self-governing and simple character
of the nature, then if the man does evils being persuaded by Satan, Satan is
not judged instead of him; but the man himself is punished and chastised, as
having become obedient to wickedness by his own will. Likewise also if the man
is turned to the good, and grace of God should prevail, grace does not ascribe
the good to itself, but renders it to the man and glorifies him; since the man
became for himself the cause =34.836= of the good; for this belongs to the
nature itself; and it is not as if grace coming to him binds his will with
compulsive power, and then whether willing or unwilling makes him unchangeable
of good, but it yields to the self-governing, the divine power also being
present, that the will of the man may be shown, whether he honors the soul or
does not honor; whether he agrees or does not agree; for many honored and
agreed. And others were turned aside. For the Apostle says, "Having begun in
spirit; now you complete in flesh"; for a law is not laid down for the nature,
but for the self-governing choice able to be turned toward the good and the
evil. Wherefore the Lord says; "I came to cast fire upon the earth, and I
wished if it were already kindled." The Lord wishes the heavenly fire to be
kindled in the hearts of men, and some wish, and some do not wish; likewise
again he says; "How often I wished to gather you, as a hen her own chicks, and
you did not wish"; you see that the Lord wishes; and again men do not wish to
draw near to the Lord, and to find mercy. 13. He who wishes to come to the
Lord, and to be deemed worthy of eternal life, and to become a dwelling-place
of God, and to be deemed worthy of the Holy Spirit, that he may be able to do
his fruits according to the commandments of the Lord blamelessly and purely,
ought thus to begin. First to believe the Lord firmly, and to give himself
wholly to the words of his commandments, and to renounce the world in all
things, that the mind may not be occupied at all in any of the things that
appear. And that he should persevere always in prayer, and not despair, always
awaiting the visitation of the Lord and the help, having the aim of his mind
toward this through all. Then he must always force himself toward every good,
and toward all the commandments of the Lord, even if the heart is not willing
because of the sin present with it; as to force himself toward being
humble-minded before all men, and to reckon himself lesser and worse than all,
not seeking honor or praise or glory from anyone, as it is written in the
Gospel, but only always to have the Lord before his eyes, and his
commandments, wishing to please him alone. Toward meekness likewise let him
force himself, even if the heart is not willing, as the Lord says; "Learn from
me, that I am meek and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your
souls." Likewise toward being merciful, kind, compassionate, good, as much as
is in his power, to accustom himself with force, as the Lord says; "Become
good and kind, as your


heavenly Father is compassionate"; and again he says; "If =34.837= you love
me, you will keep my commandments." And again; "Force yourselves; for the
violent seize the kingdom of the heavens"; and this: "Struggle to enter
through the narrow gate." And always let him have before his eyes the humility
and way of life and conduct of the Lord, as a pattern in every unforgetful
memory; and as much as is in his power let him force himself to persevere in
the prayers, always asking and believing; that the Lord having come may dwell
in him and prepare and strengthen him in all his commandments, and that his
soul may become a dwelling-place of Jesus Christ; and thus the things which he
now does with force and the heart not willing, to accustom himself through all
toward the good and always to remember the Lord, and to expect him through all
in much goodness and love. And then the Lord, seeing such a choice of his and
the good eagerness, how he always forces himself toward the memory of him, and
always toward the good, and toward humility, and meekness and love; and he
chokes the unwilling heart, and leads, as much as is in his power, himself
with force, he does his mercy with him; and redeems him from his enemies, and
from the indwelling sin; filling him with the Holy Spirit, and thus then
without force and toil he always does all the commandments of the Lord from
truth; or rather the Lord does in him his own commandments, and the fruits of
the Spirit, when he bears fruit purely. And one must first, having come to the
Lord, thus force even himself toward the good, and the heart not willing,
always expecting in unwavering faith his mercy, and force himself toward
having a compassionate and merciful heart, force himself toward being
despised, toward being longsuffering, when set at naught or dishonored not to
be indignant; according to what was said; "Not avenging yourselves, beloved";
and to force himself toward prayer, not yet having prayer of the Spirit. And
thus God, seeing him who thus contends, and by force leading himself toward
the good, and the heart not willing, gives true prayer of Christ, gives bowels
of compassions, true kindness, and in a word he keeps for him the fruit of the
Spirit. But if someone only forces himself toward prayer, not having prayer,
that he may have prayer of grace, and does not force himself toward humility,
and love and the remaining commandments of the Lord, sometimes prayer of grace
is given to him, and in rest and gladness partially according to his request,
but in his ways he is like to him to whom he was formerly like. For he does
not have meekness, because he did not seek laboriously, and did not prepare
himself beforehand thus to become. He does not have humility, since he did not
ask, and force himself toward this; he does not have love toward all, since he
did not have care and contest concerning this in the asking of the prayer; for
each must, just as he forces himself toward prayer and chokes, and the heart
not willing =34.840=; so toward love, toward meekness, and toward all
endurance and longsuffering with joy, according to what is written; so to
force himself toward being set at naught, and to reckon himself worse and last
of all, as being not useful, not to converse; but always to meditate the words
of the Lord and to speak with mouth and heart; so toward not being angry
according to what was said; "Let all bitterness, and wrath, and clamor be
taken away from you, with all wickedness"; that thus the Lord seeing his
choice may give him all these things without toil and without force, which
before he was not able thus to keep with force, because of the sin dwelling in
him; and all these practices of virtue become for him as nature; for then the
Lord having come, having come and having become in him, himself also does in
him his own commandments without toil, filling him with the fruits of the
Spirit. 14. But if someone forces himself toward prayer, until he receives
some gift from God, but toward these, that is toward humility, toward love,
toward meekness, and toward the


remaining virtues, he likewise does not force himself, and choke, sometimes
divine grace comes to him as he asks and prays; for God is good and kind, and
gives to those who ask him the requests; but not having prepared himself
beforehand, and not having accustomed himself to the aforementioned virtues,
he either loses the grace, or receives and falls, or does not progress having
become high-minded; since he does not give himself from choice to the
commandments of the Lord. For the dwelling-place or rest of the Spirit is
humility, and love, and meekness, and the remaining commandments of the Lord.
Those then who wish in truth to well-please God, and to receive from him the
heavenly grace, and to increase and be perfected in the Holy Spirit, ought
also first to force themselves to keep all the virtues, even if the heart is
not willing, according to what was said; "Therefore toward all your
commandments I was set right." For as one forces himself toward perseverance
of prayer and chokes until he accomplishes this; likewise toward all the
practices of virtue he forces himself and chokes, and accustoms a good habit;
and thus asking and praying the Lord always, and having obtained the request,
the gift of the Spirit grows and flourishes in him, the gift that was given
him resting in his humility, and in the remaining virtues, and it teaches him
true love, true meekness, which he forced beforehand and sought; and thus
having increased and been perfected in God he is deemed worthy as heir of the
kingdom of the heavens. For the humble never falls. For where has he to fall,
being below all? Great exaltation is humility; and honor, and dignity is
humility of mind. Let us then also accustom ourselves, and choke ourselves
toward humility, even if our heart is not will34.841 ing, asking and calling
upon God in faith and hope and love unceasingly in this expectation, that he
may send his Spirit into our hearts; that we may pray and worship in Spirit to
the Father; and the Spirit itself may pray in us; that the Spirit itself may
teach us true prayer, humility, meekness, love, which now with force we cannot
do, bowels of compassions, kindness, and may teach us to do all the
commandments of the Lord from truth, without toil and without force; as the
Spirit itself knows to fill us with its fruits; and thus the commandments of
the Lord having been fulfilled upon us through his Spirit, who alone knows the
will of Christ, and cleansing us from the stain of sin, he may present us as
fair and pure and blameless brides to Christ, we resting in God, and Christ
resting in us unto the endless ages. Glory to his compassions, and to the
mercy, and to his love, because he deemed the human race worthy of such honor,
deeming and addressing them as his own brothers and sons of the heavenly
Father. Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, unto the
ages of ages. Amen.
