* Homily on "Now is my soul"
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

=26.1240= The benefit of the Lord's likeness toward us is great, because we
too are likened to him. For if he himself had not been made like unto men,
men would not have been able to be made like unto God. But men are likened
to God who has become man. And it was necessary that the one who descended
into our likeness be likened most of all according to that in which we had
need =26.1241= of change. And we had need according to the passions, in
order that these might be turned into impassibility. Let no one then
disbelieve concerning the passions, thinking that the Godhead is changed,
but let him consider the passion as having come upon it from the fleshly
and soulish movement, both coming upon it and being overcome. For it has
come upon it for this reason, that it might be overcome; and from this it
has been overcome, in order that the whole nature of men might be benefited
according to kinship. For beginning from there we are all transformed from
those who suffer into the impassible, and from the dead we are established
as living. Let no one then be troubled into unbelief, if the Lord, being
God and at the same time also man, says: Now is my soul troubled. For this
took place, the Godhead yielding, and the flesh being raised up; it being
possible for the Godhead to hinder the trouble, but it not willing, in
order that it might not dissolve the likeness toward us. Something of this
sort then must be understood from the things among us: honey mixed with
water; the honey has not been changed, but the water has become
predominant. When then there is some movement of the flesh in the Lord, let
us take some such likeness, as a predominance of water in honey-water. But
when again the divine is manifested, and works powers, and shows the
authority, let us take such a likeness, as of the honey prevailing over the
water. For this is possible for the Godhead, at one time to yield to the
flesh so as to predominate, at another time itself to prevail, destroying
the passion and the weakness of the flesh. And a proof: for forty whole
days he abstains from bodily food and the body does not hunger. For after
these things, as it is written, he has hungered. Here is a predominance of
flesh; then he hungered after so great a time. That of the flesh
predominated for this reason, in order that a trial might also be brought,
and the tempter be put to shame, and the man from the beginning who fell
through food might rise through abstinence from food. And he also bears the
trouble of the flesh at the approaching death. And how was he going to
become obedient, fulfilling our obedience in our stead, if he did not also
have an opposing impulse, and prevail over the opposition? For in our case,
whenever some opposition from the flesh occurs, if we disobey the
commandment, having been overcome by flesh, in this manner sin comes about.
Therefore in the Lord the opposition from the flesh had to occur, but
obedience had to conquer. For even if he is God, yet in flesh and according
to flesh he fulfills the obedience, and he masters the will of the flesh by
the will of the Godhead, as he has said: Because I have come down from
heaven, not that I may do my own will, but the will of the Father who sent
me, calling his own will that of the flesh. Since the flesh also has become
his own; for there is no divine will of the Son separated from the will of
God. And it was necessary that the will of the flesh be moved, but be
subjected to the divine will. And thus the disobediences of men are loosed
through this paradoxical obedience, which Christ obeyed =26.1244= on our
behalf. See then, when the fleshly movement has occurred, what he says: And
what shall I say? Father, save me from this hour. But for this I have come
to this hour. Father, glorify your name. The, What shall I say? Father,
save me from this hour, he uttered according to the movement of the flesh;
the, for this I have come to this hour, he utters as the Godhead
prevailing, almost not even allowing the fleshly will to appear, the divine
conceding. For the, What shall I say? is as it were a word of one
interrupting, so that it might appear to us that there is also true flesh,
but subjected, and not able to drag off, as a horse the rider, nor even so
much as to make the guiding hand give way. And indeed what does he reckon
the passion to be? Not dishonor, not shame, but glory of himself and of the
Father. And he rather hastens on, rather than turns aside, the passion.
Father, glorify your name; there will be, he says, no refusal from me, but
that which refuses will yield, and that which does not refuse will prevail.
For fleshly nature refuses death, and this was in Christ, since the Word
has also truly become flesh; but the divine will chooses the salvation of
the world, which the death accomplished. Then that appears fulfilled, That
I may do the will of him who sent me. Christ says: Father, glorify your
name; and an answer from heaven: I have both glorified it, and I will
glorify it again; in order that we may know that there is no distance, nor
separation of the Father and the Son; or rather in order that we may gain
our conjunction with God that has come about in Christ. For there is no man
who has not torn himself away from the will of God according to some part
or deed or time. But in Christ alone the inseparability of the willing was
preserved; following along with whom we gain the conjunction toward God,
and the saving union according to his likeness, according to the likeness
of the divine union, as he himself has said: I and the Father are one, so
also may these be one in us.
