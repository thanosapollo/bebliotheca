* On the Saying, The Kingdom of Heaven Is Like a Grain of Mustard Seed

VII. Of the same, on the saying, "The kingdom of heaven is like a grain of
mustard seed."
=64.21=
What is greater than the kingdom of heaven, and what is smaller than a grain of
mustard seed? How did he liken the immeasurable kingdom of heaven to the easily
measured and tiniest grain of mustard seed? But if we consider what the kingdom
of heaven is, and what the grain of mustard is, we shall find how well and
aptly each has been likened to each. What is the kingdom of heaven, if not
altogether Christ? For he says concerning himself: Behold, the kingdom of
heaven is within you. And what is greater =64.22= than Christ according to the
Godhead? as one may hear the prophet saying: This is our God; no other shall be
reckoned beside him.
He found out every way of knowledge, and gave it to Jacob his servant and to
Israel his beloved. After these things he was seen upon the earth, and
consorted with men. Isaiah also speaks concerning him, using a voice of the
same mind. For what does he say? Egypt has toiled, and the merchandise of the
Ethiopians, and the Sabaeans, men of stature, shall pass over =64.23= to you,
and they shall be your servants, and they shall follow behind you bound in
fetters and they shall worship you, for in you is the Lord God, and there is no
God besides you. For you are God, and we did not know it, God of Israel, the
Savior. And blessed Peter also utters a voice having the same power, saying:
Brothers; for there is no other name, he says, under heaven, in which we must
be saved. And what is smaller than Christ according to the economy of the
incarnation, who has become lesser even than angels and than men? Hear David
saying how he became smaller than angels: What is man, that you remember him,
or the son of man, that you visit him? You have made him a little lower than
the angels. And that David says these things concerning Christ, Paul interprets
for you, saying: But we see Jesus, who was made a little lower than the angels,
because of the suffering of death. Thus he has become smaller than men also.
For what does Isaiah say? We saw him, and he had no form nor beauty, but his
form was without honor and failing beside all the sons of men; for this reason
he also named himself a worm. For what does he say? But I am a worm and not a
man. And in Isaiah the Father says to him: Fear not, worm Jacob. Then,
remembering also his celebrated death, he said: And your remnant is a worm.
For it was necessary that he, like a wise fisherman, should set the worm of the
flesh around the flashing hook of the Godhead and let it down into the depth of
this life, and so hook the dragon, that what is written in Job might indeed be
fulfilled: Will you draw the dragon with a hook? The kingdom of heaven, then,
is like a grain of mustard seed. Someone among the hearers will therefore say:
how has the same one become both kingdom of heaven and a grain, both great and
small in relation to himself? Because through excess of compassion toward his
own creature he has become all things to all, that he might gain all. He was
God, as he also is and will be according to his own nature, and he has become
man for our salvation.
O depth of the riches and wisdom and knowledge of God! how unsearchable are his
judgments, and his ways past tracing out! O grain, through whom the world has
come to be, through whom darkness has been scattered and the Church has been
made new! This grain, hung upon the cross, had so great a magnitude of strength
that even while he was bound, by a word alone he snatched the thief from the
wood and rolled him into the delight of paradise; this grain, his side pierced
with a spear, caused a drink of immortality to spring forth for those who
thirst; this grain of mustard, taken down from the wood and placed in a garden,
covered all that is under heaven with its branches; this grain of mustard,
placed in a garden, and sending forth its own roots into Hades, and seizing the
souls there, in three days brought them up to heaven; this grain of mustard,
crushed upon the wood, stung and darkened our adversary the serpent, and having
awakened Hades turned him back again to the place from which he had departed.
The kingdom of heaven, then, is like a grain of mustard seed, which a man took
and sowed in his own garden.
Sow this grain of mustard in the garden of your soul, that you too may say:
Awake, O north wind, and come, O south, blow through my garden, and let my
spices flow.
If you have this grain of mustard in the garden of your soul, the prophet will
say to you also: And you shall be as a drunken garden, and as a spring whose
water has not failed; if you have this grain in the garden of your soul, and
partake of its myrrh, it is said to you also: The scent of your garments is as
the scent of frankincense; that is, the scent of your fleshly things is as the
scent of frankincense. A garden enclosed, a fountain sealed, your shoots. This
grain of mustard, crushed upon the wood, and having become leaven by the
all-crafting and immortal Wisdom, mixed into three measures of meal, that is,
into soul and body and spirit, leavened all humanity into one faith. The image
of this mystery Abraham and his wife Sarah, dwelling in the tent under the oak
of Mamre, painted with their own hands. For we are compelled, beloved, to
digress from the discourse: who Abraham is, who Sarah is, what the oak is, what
the tent is, or what the ash-cakes =64.24= are, why there three measures of
fine flour and here of meal; it is necessary to go through the discourse with
exactness.
Who would Abraham be? He is interpreted, chosen father of a multitude. And who
would the chosen father be, if not God? concerning whom, Their sound has gone
out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world. And what of
Sarah? She is interpreted, ruling and governing. And who would she be who rules
and governs us, if not altogether the Wisdom of the Father? or what is the tent
under the oak of Mamre? altogether the Church sheltered by the cross. The cross
has been likened to the oak because the wood is strong and unyielding. What are
the three measures of fine flour? or altogether, as I said before, humanity,
which is composed of soul and spirit and body. For so the Apostle taught us,
saying: And God will keep your body, and your soul, and your spirit.
The Wisdom of God, then, Sarah, the governess or ruler, takes body, soul, and
spirit, and hides them in the fire, that is, torch-bearing with the
unquenchable Godhead, and makes three ash-cakes, that is, in the hidden place
of our flesh, the confession of Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. Fittingly,
beloved, has the Gospel been read to you. A grain of mustard, and leaven; the
leaven is transmuted into loaves, the grain into herbs; and loaves and herbs
are provisions for fasts, toward which may Jesus the Lord lead us blamelessly,
as we fulfill the course of the fast. The kingdom of heaven is like a grain of
mustard seed. We shall not be able to understand the power of the parable in
any other way, unless we first consider the substance, the color, and the size.
For mustard is small in size, but when sown and growing it surpasses every
increase of herbs, and excelling in height, and by the multitude of its
branches it overcomes the others, and by the abundance of its leaves; for it is
productive of shade, so that even birds flying to it can settle upon it and
rest.
And it is also very good to the taste, and heating in quality, and healing of
the inward parts. And it becomes food of no other bird, but of man only. And
outwardly it is reddish, but inwardly it appears white. And this is the nature
of the plant, and the praise of the seed. But if we should wish to examine with
exactness, we would find the parable fitting upon the Savior himself. For this
is he who is small in appearance; this is he who is little in the world, and
great in heaven; this is both son of man and God, and being Son of God, this is
the innumerable, the eternal; this is the invisible, the heavenly, who is eaten
by believing men only; this is he who was crushed, and after the passion became
white as milk; this is he who is greater than all the other herbs; this is the
undivided Word of the Father; this is he in whom the birds of heaven nest, that
is, prophets and apostles and all the called; this is he who purges the
passions of the soul through his own heat, under whom we are refreshed with
dew, under whom we are sheltered from the worldly burning; this is he who
through death was sown into the earth, and there bearing fruit, on the third
day raised the saints from the burial of the dead; this is he who through
resurrection appeared greater than every prophet; this is he who by the outflow
of the Father saves all things; this is he who blossomed from earth into the
heavens, who was sown in his own field, who in the world also brings those who
believe in him to the Father. O seed of life sown into the earth by God the
Father! O plant of immortality, reconciling to God those who are fed by you! O
lofty tree, cultivated by the Father alone!
This tree sprang up from the paternal heart; this plant has its roots in the
heavens, but appearing in the world it is harvested by men. O planting seen
upon the earth, and blossoming into the heavens! O seed seen as small, but with
the Father greater than all things! This plant sprang up also for the dead in
Hades; its fruit has become the resurrection of men; this brought down upon
death a death-bearing dart; to this the walls of Tartarus, bending a knee hard
to bow, made worship; its branches covered the whole world; this is moved by
the Holy =64.25= Spirit; the burning does not touch it; under this plant be
glad and dance with angels, =64.26= glorifying the Father and the Son and the
Holy Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
