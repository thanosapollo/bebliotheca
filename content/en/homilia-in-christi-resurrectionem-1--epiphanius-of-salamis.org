* Homily on the Resurrection of Christ
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of Saint Epiphanius, bishop of Cyprus, a discourse on the holy resurrection of
Christ:
1 Now the mourning of the dead has been banished and the light of the
resurrection has come. For this is a proof of love, that the death which man
owed, this Christ loosed by dying, giving as a ransom soul for soul and body
for body, a whole man for man, and death for death. 2 Who ever died, being
just, for the unjust? What father died for a child, what son for a father?
What the Lord did for us, not only having formed us by hand in Adam, but also
having regenerated us through the Spirit in his own passion. 3 And the impious
people of the Jews killed their own benefactor, rendering to him "evil things
for good things" and affliction for joy and death for life; for the one who
raised their dead and healed the lame and brought light to the blind, this one
they killed, hanging him upon a tree. 4 See, you men, see, all you tribes, the
new daring deeds: they hanged the one who hung the earth, and they fastened to
a tree the one who fixed the world, and they measured the one who measured the
heavens, and they bound the one who loosed the sinners, and they gave vinegar
to drink to the one who gave righteousness to drink, and they fed gall to the
one who fed life, and they destroyed the hands and feet of the one who healed
their hands and feet, and they compelled to close his eyes the one who made
them look up, and they buried the one who raised their dead. 5 O new mystery
and paradoxical wonder: the judge was judged, and the one who looses those who
have been bound was bound, and the one who fixed the earth was fastened, and
the one who hung the world was hanged, and the one who measured the heavens
was measured, and the one who fed life was fed gall, and the one who gives
life to all things died, and the one who raises the dead was buried. 6 For
when the Lord was hanging upon the tree, the tombs were split, and Hades was
opened, and the dead rose, and souls leaped forth, and many of those who were
raised appeared in Israel, when the mystery of Christ was being accomplished;
for upon the cross he lifted up the flesh, that the flesh might be shown
lifted up and death fallen under the feet of the flesh. 7 Then the angels were
astonished and the powers of the heavens were struck with amazement at the
passion of Christ, creation was struck with amazement, being astonished and
saying: What then would this new mystery be? The judge is judged and is
silent, the invisible is seen and is not ashamed, the ungraspable is grasped
and is not indignant, the immeasurable is measured and does not resist, the
impassible suffers and does not repay, the immortal dies and endures, the one
in the heavens is buried and submits; what is this new mystery, or is it
altogether for the sake of man? 8 But the Lord who suffered by will rose from
the dead, having trampled down death, and having bound "the strong one", and
having loosed man; and death, having been changed, fell under the feet of
Christ, and Hades was dragged captive, being led in triumph, and all his
powers were overthrown when they heard the voice of Christ, as the Scripture
says: "His form we did not see, but his voice we heard". For Hades did not see
the face of the Lord, but heard his voice saying: Come out, souls that have
been bound and that sit in the shadow of death; I preach life to you; I am the
Christ, your life.


9 Then Hades, having heard, was overthrown, and his bronze gates were
shattered, and his iron bars were crushed, and the souls of the saints leaped
forth following the footprints of Christ; then was fulfilled what was written:
"There he shattered bronze gates, and crushed iron bars". 10 And the earth
also cried out saying: Master, spare me from the evils, lighten me of the
wrath, loose me from the curse, because I received bloods and bodies of men,
and moreover also your body; Master, take back your Adam. 11 The Lord
therefore rose after three days, teaching us to worship a trinity in a monad;
and all the tribes of the nations were saved in Christ; for one was judged and
myriads were saved; for the Lord died for all. 12 And this one likewise,
having put on the whole man, went up into the heights of the heavens, bringing
as a gift to the Father not gold, not silver, nor a precious stone, but man
whom he formed "according to his image and likeness". 13 The Father, having
exalted this one at his right hand, seated him "upon a high throne", "until
his enemies be placed under his feet"; for he is about to come as judge of
living and dead, "of whose kingdom there shall be no end", because glory is to
the Father and to the Son and to the Holy Spirit, now and always and unto the
ages of the ages. Amen.
