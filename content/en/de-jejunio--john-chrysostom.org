On Fasting.
=61.787=
I cry out, raising my voice higher than a trumpet, not that I may deceive the
hearing with the delight of pleasure, but that I may quench pleasure by the
speech of truth; and I will stop the desire of the belly by speech, but I will
awaken through longing the work of fasting. For the longing of fasting works a
heavenly gain, but the gain of luxury works a damaging harm. For he who occupies
himself with eating, and fills the desire of the belly, damages the spiritual
fruit of the soul; but he who withers the flesh by fasting, and flees the
gluttony of foods, treasures up the expectation of the better things.
For fasting teaches self-control, and makes one enduring; fasting anoints for
piety, and trains for patience; fasting makes the way of life angelic, and
renders the body spiritual; fasting is a safeguard of the soul, and an
unconquerable weapon of the body. For it neither admits the treacherous
pleasure, =61.788= and it overthrows the desire that wars against us. Fasting
leads the mind up to heaven and wings it toward the immortal nature. Let us
fast, then, beloved, not going through the days of the fasts with gloomy faces,
but disposing ourselves toward them with bright souls as servants of Christ. For
if you have come to the fast with desire, do not be sullen, but rejoice; for it
cleanses the poison from your soul.
Do not take the fast as being about good things whose foods are pleasant; but as
you endure, be long-suffering, receiving teachings sweeter than foods. For this
reason you fast from foods, that you may be fed through the Spirit; and for this
reason you abstain from bodily meats, that you may be filled with spiritual
offerings. Fasting is a medicine that destroys sin, and a softening plaster of
the soul, washing toward piety. And just as the worse passions, when they are
sprinkled with certain most pungent and most astringent =61.789= things, throw
off the rotting of the body; in the same way also, when fasting most astringent
comes in upon the sin that lurks within and spreads against the soul, it dries
it up, thinning it little by little, and utterly destroys it by cleansing it
away.
For this reason, even if fasting is laborious, it is nevertheless beneficial and
accessible. Do not, then, fast while grieving, lest you lose the labor; nor
murmur that you delay a little, lest through a little you suffer the loss of the
whole. For as much as fasting constricts the body, so much is the benefit from
the fast. Run back to the ways of life of the righteous, and you find that they
have mostly been approved through fasting. First Moses, having fasted, entered
into the darkness, and through fasting became a companion of God. Having joined
forty days of fasting, he was named a lawgiver through fasting; and he neither
ate bread nor drank wine; for he was content with food, being taught the
legislation from the Lawgiver. But that you may learn how much fasting benefits,
and how much drunkenness harms, warring against fasting: consider the things
done by Moses, and the things happening among the people, and you will find the
power of fasting. Above, Moses was receiving the law, and below the people were
overturning the law. For Moses, being grieved, shattered the law, not thinking
it reasonable that the law be entrusted to those who were drunk, and that
oracles be handed over to minds weighed down with wine. See how much drunkenness
wrongs, opposing the fast. But again fasting, having set right so great an evil,
using the same days, he called the law back again. He has left us a memorial for
the security of life and conduct, perhaps also stretching out a mystery through
this: that the first legislation will be idle, being shattered because of the
unbelief of the former people; but the second has been in force, being given
everywhere, the second people faithfully receiving the second law and keeping it
together.
And Elijah also, having fasted forty days, and having traversed the desert with
one meal, did not feel labor; for fasting was carrying him, lightening the toil;
and after fasting he beheld God, as far as it is possible for human nature to
perceive. He did not behold the bodiless with bodily eyes, but having perceived
the invisible with the heart, according to the purity of the soul. For blessed
are the pure in heart, for they shall see God. Thus having fasted, he shut the
heaven against the unrestrained, and those who were wasting their own life in
luxury, so that what they did not choose to pursue by their own purpose, this
they might learn by being trained through the fasting of famine, and might know
how beneficial fasting is, even when it displays its work in a time of luxury.
And Daniel himself, having fasted three weeks, and not tasting bread of desires,
coming into a den of lions through the jealousy of the unrestrained, even taught
lions there to fast. For the wild beasts were put to shame before the faster,
becoming tamer than the rational beasts, and they shut their own mouth, not
daring to touch the holy body. For as one who had set fasting as a weapon to his
own body, and had fortified his whole self through it, he deceived the eyes of
the lions, displaying not a human body but an angelic body. And the three
children in Babylon, not willing to share in the lawless table of the king, but
to follow the fast according to the law, entering into the kindled fire of the
furnace quenched the flame, making the furnace dew instead of fire through
fasting; and the fire dared against those inexperienced in fasting, for it
consumed all the Chaldeans standing around; but it withdrew from those in the
habit of fasting, not being able to touch their body. For there was not any
matter in the body, fasting having made it spiritual.
Likewise =61.790= the Ninevites also, having fasted, drove off death; for having
persevered intensely in fasting for three days, converting eternal punishment
through a few days, they have left eternal praise to the world. You see,
beloved, how much fasting has power when it is accomplished with prayer and
works. For fasting and petition deliver from death; but righteousness is above
both. Fasting is both a destruction of death and a ransoming from wrath; fasting
is a cleansing of the mind, a companion of self-control, a gymnasium of piety, a
yoke of righteousness. Fasting knows how to make the poor man rich; for it does
not allow untimely spending, furnishing the sufficiency of measure; it knows
also how to make the rich man a steward of his own money, not allowing him
without fear to fatten himself; fasting also knows the prime of youth. It
teaches a bridle of foods, it puts on self-control, and wisely charioteering
toward piety and chastity. It knows also how to make elders wise, and to keep
the eye of the mind pure. Fasting also keeps the body healthier; for it does
not, being weighed down by foods, receive the matter of diseases, but being
lightened from meats, it becomes vigorous for the reception of the gifts.
For this reason let us fast, beloved, that we may obtain the spiritual gifts.
The one who fasts also trains the soul toward piety, taking as an image of
bodily self-control the care according to the soul, so that just as he abstains
from foods that enter from without, so also from the evil thoughts that rise up
in the mind. For if, it says, the spirit of the one who has authority rises up
against you, do not leave your place, because a healing will put a stop to sins.
Just as now you abstain from foods, not tasting them, so abstain from sins by
being changed. Do you fast, not taking wine? Fast also by abstaining from
wickedness; for evil knows how to work a greater drunkenness in the soul. Do you
fast, not touching meats? Fast also by abstaining from slander, lest through it
you eat the flesh of the brothers, tasting of his own flesh. For true fasting is
abstinence from sins. Hear the Prophet saying: I have not chosen this fast, nor
a day for a man to humble his own soul. But what? Loose, he says, every bond of
injustice, dissolve the knots of violent contracts, break your bread for the
hungry, bring the homeless into your house; if you see one naked, clothe him.
This, beloved, is true fasting, acceptable to God, and saving for man, and
pleasing to all. The possession of the heavens follows this, and the unfailing
promise of the Master. For see what he adds: Then your light shall break forth
early, and then, when you cry, he will hear you; while you are still speaking,
he will say: Behold, I am here. Do you see how great are the gifts of fasting,
and of how much piety it holds, so as even to put God himself to shame to come
to human nature?
Fast, then, remain in the prayers, in the assemblies, in the admonitions, not
being absent from the gathering, as is the custom of some; nor in businesses or
law-courts through the whole day; lest you gather money which you ought to have
scattered; or plead against your brother, whom you ought to have served. You
have come to evening, or the ninth hour, not that you may complete the purpose
of fasting, but that you may fill the appetite of the belly. And do not suppose
that by abstaining from wine for the sake of sobriety, yet being filled with
wickedness, you are drunk, lest you hear, Woe to those who are drunk, not from
wine! Drunkenness is not a companion of fasting, but enmity and an adversary.
That we may arrive at the hope of the resurrection prepared for us, enjoying
there the goods laid up, and here rejoicing in the things expected in Christ
Jesus, to whom be the glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
