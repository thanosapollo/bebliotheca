* On the Meeting of the Lord
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of Amphilochius, bishop of Iconium, on the Theotokos and on Simeon and Anna.
Many of the great among men marvel at virginity; for it is marvelous as
kindred to the angels, as a companion of the powers above, as a fellow-runner
with the bodiless natures, as lamp-bearer of the holy Church, as conquering
the world, as treading down the passions, as bridling the appetites, as having
no share with Eve, as delivered from grief, as having laid hold of purity, as
redeemed from groanings, as not receiving the condemned sentence that says:
"Multiplying I will multiply your griefs and your groaning; in griefs you
shall bear children, and your turning shall be toward your husband, and he
shall rule over you." Marvelous, therefore, is virginity as an unenslaved
possession, as a free dwelling, as an ascetic adornment, as superior to the
human condition, as released from the passions that come by necessity, as
having entered in with the bridegroom Christ into the chamber of the kingdom
of the heavens. And these are the glories of virginity, and the things like
them.
But honorable marriage stands above every earthly gift, as a fruitful tree, as
a most noble plant, as the root of virginity, as a husbandman of the rational
and living branches, as a blessing of the world's increase, as a comforter of
the race, as a craftsman of humanity, as a painter of the divine image, as
possessing the Master who blesses, as receiving to bear the whole world, as
conducting itself according to him whom it also prevailed upon to become man,
as able to say with boldness: "Behold, I and the children whom God has given
me." Take away honorable marriage and you will not find the flower of
virginity; for from there and from nowhere else is the flower of virginity
gathered. But in saying these things we do not introduce a battle between
virginity and marriage; we marvel at both as mutually indebted. Since the
Master is the provider of both, he does not weigh one against the other; for
both sides, in all things, hold to the fear of God. For apart from pious fear
of God, neither is virginity venerable nor is marriage honorable.
These things have been brought forward in my discourse because of what was
written in the law and confirmed in grace, and spoken universally and
universally not found, and fulfilled in the Lord alone: I mean concerning the
first-produced fruit of marriage. What fruit is this? You were just now
hearing the evangelist saying: "And it came to pass, when the eight days were
fulfilled for the circumcising of the child, his name was also called Jesus,
which was called by the angel before he was conceived. And when the days of
their purification were fulfilled, they brought him up into the temple and
presented him to the Lord, as it is written in the law of the Lord: Every male
that opens the womb shall be called holy to the Lord." Did you see the
blessing of marriage, and what was spoken in common found in the Lord alone?
That saying, "Every male that opens the womb shall be called holy to the
Lord," has its reference to the Lord alone, and not also to any other, even
though it was spoken universally. For every nature of a virgin is first opened
from intercourse with a man; the womb, and then thus she gives birth. But in
the case of our Savior it was not so, but without intercourse he himself,
having opened the womb of the virgin, came forth inexpressibly, so that the
saying: "Every male that opens the womb shall be called holy to the Lord,"

is referred to the Lord alone. Is Cain then holy, who tore life away in
profaneness, because he before all first came forth from the maternal womb?
Is Esau then holy, the hostile one and heir of the sword, because he too
first fell from the maternal belly? Is Reuben then holy, who
insulted his father's bed and boiled up a sentence of curse, because he too
first came forth from the many-bearing womb of Leah? None of these is holy;
all these are under penalties. From which it is shown that the saying has its
reference to the Lord: "Every male that opens the womb shall be called holy to
the Lord," according to Gabriel who said to the virgin: "The Holy Spirit shall
come upon you and the power of the Most High shall overshadow you, therefore
also the holy thing that is born shall be called Son of God." And someone of
those who contradict will certainly say, that if then the saying has its
reference to the Lord: "Every male that opens the womb shall be called holy to
the Lord," the virgin did not remain a virgin. For Scripture has cried that
"Every male that opens the womb"; the virginal womb was certainly opened, if
it has its reference to the Lord. Hear with understanding: toward the virginal
nature the virginal gates were not opened at all, by the will of the one just
now carried in the womb, according to the saying that speaks concerning him:
"This is the gate of the Lord, and he shall enter and he shall go out, and the
gate shall be shut." As toward the virginal nature, then, the virginal gates
were not opened at all; but as toward the power of the Master who was born,
nothing is shut to the Lord, but all things have been opened. Nothing that
hinders, nothing that troubles; all things have been opened to the Lord.
Whence also the highest powers above, charging those below, have cried saying:
"Lift up the gates, you rulers, and the King of glory shall enter."
Marvelous, therefore, is virginity, the true virginity, since there is also a
difference in virginity: some having grown drowsy, and others having watched.
Marvelous also is marriage, the true and honorable marriage; for many have
kept it and many have shaken it. Marvelous also is widowhood; for it is good
to make mention of the three orders. And, as I said before, marvelous also is
widowhood, the true widowhood, which has won the contest of the undefiled
prizes; such as was Anna the prophetess, who was just now read out to us, who
grew old well and was renewed as an eagle. You were just now hearing the
evangelist Luke saying: "And there was Anna a prophetess, daughter of Phanuel,
of the tribe of Asher; she was advanced in many days, having lived with a
husband seven years from her virginity; and she was a widow of about
eighty-four years, who served in the temple in fastings and prayers night and
day." Did you see in what things and of what sort the widow Anna has been
adorned in the divine Gospels? Truly Anna is grace; the appellation of the
name ran together with the good repute of her ways. Let the women hear and
emulate the glorious Anna; let them run the like course, that they may enjoy
the equal crowns. Let no one plead lack of protection as a pretext, lest she
shake off the purity of single marriage. Anna is the crown of widows: a woman
in form, a prophetess in order; living in widowhood, nourished in the kingdom;
slackened in body, sinewed in soul; wrinkled in face, stretched taut in
understanding; bent with old age, walking upright in knowledge; aged by many
years, thriving in the knowledge of God; fasting and not gorging; praying and
not wandering; attending to the temple, not running about other houses;
singing psalms, not babbling; prophesying, not telling myths; meditating on the
divine things, not introducing unseemly things. Anna surpassed the widow blessed
by
the apostle. Which one is this? Hear Paul saying: "Let a widow be enrolled not
less than sixty years, if she has brought up children, if she has washed the
feet of the saints, if she has

helped those in need, if she has followed after every good work." She
surpassed the one blessed by the apostle both in years and in ways of life.
For she who was
counted worthy of prophecy and became a vessel of the Holy Spirit and made
known the signs of the presence to all who were awaiting the incarnate
presence of the Lord, how did she not surpass them all, the evangelist having
said concerning her, as you just now heard: "And she," he says, "this Anna,
coming up at that very hour, gave thanks to the Lord and spoke to all who were
awaiting redemption in Jerusalem."
Did you see the greatness of Anna? She advocated for the Lord, speaking his
things in his presence. O the paradoxical things! A widow, but she
stung the chief priests and scribes, and in stinging she won the whole people,
making known the redemption that was going to be in Jerusalem by the Lord's
coming, speaking to those present and making known the signs of the Lord. Anna
saw the Lord as an infant, gifts and purifications being offered for him and
with him, and she did not stumble at the smallness of the age; Anna confessed
the infant as God, physician, strong redeemer, destroyer of sins. Do not
simply run past the words of Anna. Making known the Lord's working to all
present, she was saying to those gathered: Do you not see this child drawing
the breast, borrowing the nipple, resting upon the mother's bosom, not yet
deigning to step the earth with feet, being circumcised on the eighth day? Do
you not see this child? This one founded the ages, this one vaulted the
heavens, this one broadened the earth, this one fenced the sea with sand.
This child sends forth winds from his treasuries, this child in Noah's time
opened the floodgates of the deluge, this child works the warp-threads of
rain, this child shakes out snow as wool, this child freed our forefathers
from the land of Egypt through the Mosaic staff, this child cut through the
Red Sea and led them through as through a grass-bearing plain and, raining the
manna in the wilderness, bequeathed a land flowing with milk and honey; this
child prepared this temple to be raised on high through the fathers, this
child, setting oaths toward Abraham, was saying: "Multiplying I will multiply
your seed as the stars of heaven and as the sand by the lip of the sea."
Concerning this child the prophetic choir, offering a plea, was saying: "Stir
up your might and come to save us." Do not, because he is a child, stumble at
the child. The same is both a child and co-unoriginate with the Father, the
same both numbered with times and without genealogy, the same both a child
lisping and granting mouth and wisdom; the one because of the birth from a
virgin, the other because of the incomprehensibility of his existence. Since
Isaiah also presented this, saying: "A child has been born to us, a son also
has been given to us." The one "has been born," the other "has been given";
the one because of what is seen, the other because of what is understood.
These are the prophesyings of Anna, these the noble utterances of the woman,
these the achievements of the widow, of the true widow, who exchanged her life
well, who sent her husband forth and received the Lord. She lived with her
husband seven years, having fulfilled a week of years from her virginity, and
in the seventh year having ceased from marital intercourse. She kept sabbath
well, she reached well the Lord's day of grace. Anna took the order of the
turtledove; she did not forget her spouse, she did not set
aside the first faith, she did not bring an insolent one up onto the bed, she
did not lay the yoke of single marriage upon another. She had the memory on
the bed, as if the dead man were living;

she did not soil the bridal linen, Paul's rebuke did not touch her, saying:
"But when they have grown wanton against Christ, they wish to marry, having
judgment because they have set aside the first faith." Truly worthy of
judgment is she who has buried the memory together with the God-yoked spouse,
and especially when a child or children are present, for which reason also the
law of marriage has been planted; but if a child is not present to the young
widow, it is likely that she rushes toward a second marriage, being goaded by
the desire of love of children. Since the blessed Paul, exhorting this also,
has cried: "I wish younger widows to marry"; then, showing what is the sign of
the progress of the second marriage, he added saying: "I wish younger widows
to marry, to bear children, to rule the house." So the addition of the second
marriage is concerning childbearing; but when children are present to the
young widow who has rushed toward marriage, such a sowing is then superfluous,
the rational ears of grain already rivaling.
Let us then return again to the discourse: what then does the evangelist say?
For it is good to take hold of the whole matter. You were just now hearing the
evangelist Luke saying how Simeon said to the virgin: "Behold, this one is set
for the fall and rising of many in Israel and for a sign spoken against, and a
sword shall pass through your own soul also." What is "And a sword shall pass
through your own soul also"? Hear with understanding. When Simeon had said
concerning the Lord, to the hearing of the virginal ears, the saying: "Behold,
this one is set for the fall and rising of many in Israel and for a sign
spoken against," the mother of the Lord was likely indignant against Simeon,
saying to him: You do not know what you are declaring, O man. Do you announce
gloomy things upon the Christ? You do not know the conception of the child,
and you make known a sign of contradiction as concerning a common birth. There
is no fall in him, but much lifting up and condescension for those who are
benefited. Why then do you not bless, saying: Behold, this one is set not for
a fall, but for the rising of many in Israel; and why "And for a sign spoken
against"? But Simeon to the virgin: It is enough for you, O virgin, to be
called mother; it is sufficient for you to be found nurse of him who nourishes
the world; it is great for you to bear in flesh him who bears all things. The
Christ who has now dwelt in you, the same one has now also in me prepared the
things concerning him to be spoken, that "This one is set for the fall and
rising of many in Israel": for the fall of the unbelieving Jews, and for the
rising of the believing nations. "Behold, this one is set for the fall and for
the rising of many in Israel and for a sign spoken against": having named the
cross a sign of contradiction.
Since on that very cross many of the unbelievers brought words of
contradiction to the Lord, mocking in words and deeds, striking with a reed,
giving vinegar to drink, offering gall, putting on a crown of thorns, piercing
the side with a spear, slapping with hands, crying with reproachful words: "He
saved others, he cannot save himself." Interpreting this, then, he was saying:
"And for a sign spoken against." Many spoke against when Peter was denying and
all the apostles were being scattered as unshepherded sheep. In the sign of the
cross, then, the heart of the virgin herself also had been filled with grief;
therefore she was also saying: Why did I not die beforehand? And why have I
reached this day? I have remained a virgin and I am bitten in the inward parts
more than mothers. These boundless reasonings of the virgin Simeon named a
sword, as piercing the inwards, as bringing scandals, as the Lord foretold:
"All of you will be scandalized in me this night." Whence Simeon also added,
saying: "And a sword shall pass through your own soul also, that the thoughts
of many hearts may be revealed." Do you see how the boundless

reasonings he named a sword, as piercing the inwards, as touching kidneys and
marrows? And the virgin fell among these, not yet knowing the power of the
resurrection, and that the resurrection was close at hand she did not yet
know. Whence after the resurrection there is no longer a two-edged sword, but
gladness and exultation. Simeon therefore named the sign of the cross a sign of
contradiction, in which time a sword of thoughts passed through the soul of
the virgin. And someone will certainly say: And from where is the proof for
us? From the Lord's own teachings. Hear him saying: "This generation is an
evil generation; it seeks a sign and a sign shall not be given it, except the
sign of Jonah the prophet. For as Jonah was in the belly of the sea-monster
three days and three nights, so also shall the Son of man be in the heart of
the earth three days and three nights."
Do you see how the passion of the cross was named a sign, not only in the new
covenant, but also in the old? For the old things agree exceedingly with the
new. Since it is one and the same God who both legislated there and awards
the prize here. And who is witness of this? The Lord himself. Hear him saying
in the
prophet Ezekiel to the reaping angels, making known the universal
consummation: "Spare not with your eyes and do not pity; slay to extinction
old man and young man and virgin and child and women, but do not touch those
who bear the sign upon their foreheads." See the sign of the old and of the
new. For it is the cross that saves the world, through Jesus Christ our Lord,
to whom be the glory and the might unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
