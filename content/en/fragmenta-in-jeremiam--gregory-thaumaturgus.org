* Fragments on Jeremiah

He speaks peaceable things to his neighbor, and in himself he has enmity, etc.
The scribes and Pharisees of the Jewish people would not have inherited so
great a woe from the Savior, if it were not through hypocrisy; for in the
rebukes of their sins the Lord did not cease, on every charge, from declaring
beforehand: Woe to you, 592 scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites, calling them
whitewashed tombs, who outwardly appear beautiful to men, but inwardly are
full of dead men's bones and of all uncleanness. For if we do any good toward
our neighbor through unfeigned love, we hear from the Savior: Inasmuch as you
did it to one of these, the least of my brothers, you did it to me. So also if
we show anything base toward the brother through deceitful and polluted
hypocrisy, or through any other sin whatsoever, we likewise sin against God as
well. Hypocrisy is therefore a terrible thing, brothers, and an enemy; and
deceit hidden in the heart, for the deception of those who approach simply,
speaking one thing from the mouth but carrying another in the breast, entices
those who possess love.
Pride is not a good thing, having much false glory; for it is clear, clear,
that every perfect good comes from God; and it is not otherwise obtainable by
a man, so that of all things the saying is truly most true, that the one who
boasts in anything ought to glory in this alone, and in understanding and
knowing that the Lord himself is the one who does mercy and judgment and
righteousness, and in this is the will of God. For the divine is pleased with
such a man, and he carries lasting and sure goods through all, whenever he
ascribes the cause of the present goods to this one who is truly the first
cause, to whom one must also refer the cause of the goods remaining. How,
having named them circumcised, does the word then not name Jews only, nor
Israelites, nor the house of Jacob, nor even the proper name that lies there,
Judah, 593 but ranks with these also Egypt, and Edom, and the sons of Ammon,
and the sons of Moab, as all uncircumcised in their heart? Does the word then
wish to say something of this sort, that the sons of Israel seem indeed to
have the law of circumcision, but differ in nothing from the rest of the
uncircumcised nations, themselves also being uncircumcised in their hearts,
not having cut away their vices? Does the word, I say, wish to say something
of this sort, that the sons of Judah have the word of circumcision and the
rest of the oracles of God, yet they differ in nothing from those who do not
have the law of God, Egypt and Edom, the sons of Ammon and the sons of Moab?
Or does it at some time call all together circumcised, not only the Israelites
and the Jews, but also the remaining nations, as the text being read also
makes clear? For it called all circumcised, then it listed Egypt and Judah and
the remaining nations. And how is it possible to be brought to say that all of
them are circumcised?
All these named nations set before us, if one were to reason from the
beginning, one would find to be from the seed of Abraham. Abraham was
circumcised being ninety-nine years old, himself and his sons, Ishmael and all
the men of his house, both those born in the house and those bought with
silver, those from foreign nations; he circumcised them. For these are the
things written word for word in Genesis. How then does it bring circumcision
down upon Egyptians, and upon Edom, and upon the sons of Moab, and upon the
sons of Ammon? Ishmael has been circumcised, as I said, according to law,
being about thirteen years old, inasmuch as he was the son of Abraham from
Hagar the Egyptian maidservant.
A little later. And Ishmael, it says, was settled in the wilderness, and
became an archer, 594 and he dwelt in the wilderness. And his mother took for
him an Egyptian wife. For what reason have all these things been told me as a
story? I seek those circumcised according to Egypt, and I seek those
circumcised who are in the wilderness, all whom the word records. They
therefore seem to be of the race of Ishmael, who dwelt in the wilderness and
took the Egyptian wife; and I no longer doubt that these are they, namely
those circumcised in Egypt, and those shorn about the face, and dwelling in
the wilderness. For it is likely that Ishmael, having received the ancestral
law of Abraham, as being of a God-loving father, and having withdrawn from his
own land, would do also upon his own descendants those things which he thought
safest for himself, being handed down from the fathers. In this way, then, it
is possible to find Egyptians circumcised, or those who dwell in the
wilderness. And how also Edom? This Edom also descends from the seed of
Abraham; for to Abraham there was born a child, Ishmael, from Hagar, and Isaac
also was born from Sarah; and to Isaac again two children, Esau and Jacob.
And Jacob did not take a wife of foreign blood, nor of the Canaanites, his
father having looked to these things. Esau, however, took as wife the daughter
of Ishmael, sister of Nebaioth, taking as wife, of course, his own cousin on
the father's side, but a foreigner on the mother's side, and he becomes father
of many children. And his firstborn is Edom, and the sacred oracle will bear
me witness, saying: And these are the generations of Esau, father of Edom.
Therefore the things that were said a little earlier about Ishmael, that
having received a law from the father, that of circumcision, he handed it on
to his own descendants, these things it is possible for us 595 to say about
Esau, that being a child of Isaac the son of Abraham, the law which he
received coming to him both from the grandfather and from the father, he also
handed on to the children, and he too circumcised Edom, I suppose; is it no
longer unclear, then? And how also the sons of Ammon and the sons of Moab? The
more industrious know these, I suppose, to be the sons of Lot, the nephew of
Abraham. And a little later.
Ammon and Moab were therefore born from Lot, each from his daughters, when,
having supposed the earth to be desolate, the daughters of Lot thus wished to
preserve the race of men; for to the elder was born Moab, interpreted "from
the father," and to the second Ammon, interpreted "the son of the race." All
these things having been said persuade us without difficulty to agree that all
the circumcised are thus called by the sacred word, Jeremiah himself also
bearing witness concerning the sons of Moab and concerning the sons of Ammon,
that he ranked these also among the circumcised. And a little later. And if
some benefit ought also to come to us from these things that have been said,
one would rightly say thus, that the many races of men have a pretense of
righteousness, and some traces of a law of God, and somehow put on an
appearance, and take care of virtue in a counterfeit way, yet the things of
their choice stand only as far as outward form, as far as pretense and playing
the hypocrite, yet we have not also fully been eager to cut vice out of
ourselves and the reproaches upon Egypt and upon Idumaea.
Chapter x, 2. According to the ways of the nations, do not learn. And what do
you learn? Learn the law of God, which says: There shall not be for you other
gods besides me; gods who did not make heaven and earth, let them perish from
the face of the earth and from under heaven.
