* Fragments on the Translation of the Relics of the Martyrs Cyrus and John
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

I. Epiphi 2. Address of the Blessed Cyril to the Tabennesiote monks who
are in Canopus in the place called Metanoia, concerning the holy
martyrs Cyrus and John. Courage and endurance, a fair and most
well-formed pair of virtues, and most fitting especially for the
saints; to whom the blessed David addressed, "Be of good courage, and
let your heart be =77.1101= strengthened, and wait for the Lord"; and
the disciple of the Savior: "You have need of endurance, that, having
done the will of God, you may receive the promise." For we must as at
the beginning come to be of the good things, and seek the end, that we
may also inherit the kingdom of heaven, in Christ Jesus our Lord, to
whom be the glory and the power unto the ages. Amen.
II. Epiphi 7. Of the same, first exposition on the holy martyrs Cyrus
and John who lie here, through which the Father indicates both their
honorable passion, and the deposition here of their holy relics. And
the present first exposition was spoken in the Metanoia, that is, in
that of the Holy Apostles. We have not become sluggish concerning the
accustomed word, but as the Savior has said, the spirit is willing, but
the flesh is weak. And we believe that through your prayers by the
mercy of God the affliction will ease. And what we both planned and
have done for what is useful, we have judged it necessary to say to you
again. That these parts needed physicians healing through God. In order
then that we might benefit all the places, and especially those lying
around the church of the Holy Evangelists, for they were going away not
having a martyr-shrine to certain other places, and being Christians
they were being led astray, for this reason we necessarily sought
relics of holy martyrs. We found them then, and have learned
accurately, that at the time at which the holy virgins suffered
martyrdom, whose divine spring we have in those of the holy evangelist
Mark, two certain ones, of whom the one was a monk, an ascetic, and the
other a soldier, were present urging them and anointing them toward the
contest of the struggle; so that with noble mind they might undergo
danger for our Savior; and they themselves also were called together
with those women to the martyrdom; and they entered nobly and suffered
martyrdom for Christ, and laid down for him their own souls. The relics
of the holy martyrs then were together lying in one place; but since
the relics were undistinguished, for it was not clearly known who was
this one and who that one, necessarily taking up both we transferred
them and have placed them in the church of the Evangelists, having made
the customary memorial, as they bear witness. Let us assemble =77.1104=
then, Christ willing, tomorrow together, honoring both the holy
evangelists and the blessed martyrs; and they are called Cyrus and
John.
III. Epiphi 8. Of the same, another exposition in the church of the
evangelists, where he also deposited in a tomb the relics of the holy
martyrs Cyrus and John, two signs to the east of Canopus, near
Menouthis. And great crowds were going along with him, and turning he
said to them: "If anyone comes to me, and does not hate his father and
his wife and the children and his brothers, and still even his own
soul, he cannot be my disciple; whoever does not bear his cross and
come after me, cannot be my disciple." The words of the Savior demand
us to be fervent unto piety; and they persuade those who wish to love
him to consider nothing better than him, not affection of bodies, not
love toward fathers, not reverence toward mothers and brothers; for he
knows that those who have despised these things find a great and
splendid recompense; and that I may also say something else besides
these, knowing how great a love he had toward us, he himself did not
make the things concerning himself better than our affairs; he has not
preferred the things concerning himself to the salvation and life of
all. Being God, he became man for us and endured a cross, having
despised shame; but those who suffer with him will also reign with him;
those who have been dishonored with him, will altogether also be
glorified with him. Thus we believe that the holy martyrs Cyrus and
John also have found favor; for they entered eagerly into the contests
on behalf of piety toward Christ. And the most terrible beast of all
leaps upon them, I mean death; but they remembered their own Master
saying: "Whoever does not take his cross, and follow after me, cannot
be my disciple." They have fulfilled the commandment, they also took
the cross, they followed their own Master.
And they were led not alone to the contests, but there was also a choir
of holy virgins; and they were women, but unbreakable in mind; the fair
pair of these athletes then has also been perfected together with
=77.1105= them; and they have as reward of their love toward Christ,
the trampling of Satan and the driving out of the wicked demons; let
those then who of old were being led astray come; let them come to a
true and unadulterated healing-place; for no one fabricates dreams for
us; no one says to those who come: "The Lady has said: Do this and
that"; is a Lady at all able to be God, and does she wish to be
worshipped? Among the demons there is neither male nor female. And see
what sort of choice they have; they wish to be called by names of
women. Having therefore trampled the old-wives' fables and the old
mockeries of the sorcerers, let them come to the true physicians from
above; to whom the God who is able in all things has granted the
authority to be able to heal, saying: "Heal the sick; freely you
received, freely give." Having then enjoyed every healing from the
Savior, they hymn their own Master, that they may also be counted
worthy of the kingdom of heaven, in Christ Jesus our Lord, through whom
to God and Father with the all-holy Spirit be glory, honor, power, now
and always and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
