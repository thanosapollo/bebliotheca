OF SAINT CYRIL THAT CHRIST IS ONE {Α} Of the sacred lessons there would be
satiety altogether none for those at least who are truly sound of mind and
have gathered into mind the life-giving knowledge. For it is written: That man
shall not live on bread alone, but on every word that proceeds through the
mouth of God. For food of mind is the word that is from God and spiritual
bread strengthening a man's heart, according to what is hymned in the book of
Psalms. {Β} You speak well. {Α} The wise then of the Greeks and the choice
among them have marveled at fine speaking, and of the things most eagerly
pursued among them is to speak well, and they boast in mere refinements of
words, and they pride themselves in the pomp of diction. And the matter for
poets is the false, turned on rhythms and metres into the graceful and
tuneful, but altogether little care of the truth, and they have also been sick
with scarcity of right and profitable opinion, that concerning, I mean, the
God who is by nature and truly, or rather as the most holy Paul says: They
became vain in their reasonings, and their senseless heart was darkened.
Saying they were wise, they became fools, and they exchanged the glory of the
incorruptible God in the likeness of an image of corruptible man and of birds
and of four-footed things and of creeping things. {Β} True. God said then
concerning them through the voice of Isaiah715: Know that their heart is
ashes, and they go astray. {Α} But thus far their affairs. Those however who
have become inventors of unholy heresies, the profane and apostates, and
widening their unbridled mouth against the divine glory and speaking the
things that are distorted, would be caught not in lesser writings of the
Greeks' folly as having slipped from madness, or perhaps even in things
beyond. For it was better for them not to have known the way of the truth
than, having known, to turn back from the holy commandment given to them. For
the thing of the true proverb has come to them, A dog that turned to his own
vomit, and a sow that washed to wallowing in mire. For they have divided the
charges of blasphemy against Christ, and as some wolves sharp and bitter they
ravage the flocks for whom Christ died, and they snatch his things,
multiplying for themselves the things that are not theirs, as it is written,
and making their yoke heavy stoutly, of whom it would be said also very
fittingly: They went out from us, but they were not of us. {Β} Not at all
then. {Α} But in season the word concerning such is judged for us. For some
senselessly bring down the only-begotten Word of God from the highest
preeminence, and they lower him from the equality toward the Father and God,
saying he is not consubstantial, nor yet willing to crown him with natural and
unvarying identity. And others, coming as it were the same path as those
named, and slipping down into a snare of death and into the trap of hades,
distort the mystery of the economy with flesh of the Only-begotten, and they
practice a madness sister as it were to the first. For the ones, all but
dragging down the Word born of God the Father, not yet incarnate, from the
heights of the godhead, as far as it lies with them, would be caught. And the
others have resolved to war even against him incarnate, all but even rebuking,
the bold ones, the philanthropic grace, stretching perhaps that he has not
counseled rightly, because he endured flesh and the measures of the emptying,
that is that he became man and was seen upon the earth and consorted with men,
although God


being by nature and sitting with the Father. {Β} You have spoken rightly. {Α}
The God-inspired Scripture then will cry down against the ignorance in both,
advocating the true, and showing the 716 account with those men feeble and
silly, and setting upon the paths of the godhead those who are accustomed to
behold the mystery upon him with a thin and accurate eye of thought. Who then
would they be who have so unholy adulterated the so august and ineffable
economy of the Saviourfor you seem not moderately to be distressed at this
very thing, I would wish to inquire of you. {Β} You conjecture well. For I
have been jealous with jealousy for the Lord, and toward this I am whetted and
I am greatly at a loss. And where ever the words from them will turn, I fear
as I look. For they adulterate the faith handed down to us, using inventions
of a newly appeared dragon, and pouring into the souls of the simpler, in the
manner of poison, some cold and distorted things and full of folly. {Α} Then
who the newly appeared dragon is, and of what sort the things prated by him
against the doctrines of the truth, tell me as I ask. {Β} A newly appeared
dragon, this crooked one and having a tongue drunk with poison, who has all
but said farewell to the tradition of the mystagogues of the world, or rather
even to all the God-inspired Scripture, and has innovated what seemed good to
him, and saying that the holy Virgin is not God-bearer, but rather
Christ-bearer and man-bearer, and besides these other dissonant and stricken
things, having brought them in upon the right and sincere doctrines of the
catholic Church. {Α} Nestorius, I think, you mean. For I already somehow
understand, but what the affair of his words is, my friend, I cannot grasp.
And how does he say the holy Virgin is not God-bearer? {Β} For she has not, he
says, brought forth God. For the Word was even before her, or rather even
before every age and time, being coeternal with God and Father. {Α} They will
deny then this also openly, that Emmanuel is God, and in vain as it seems the
evangelist interprets the name saying: Which is interpreted: God with us. For
thus the God and Father confirmed clearly through the voice of the prophet
that the one born according to flesh from the holy Virgin must be named, as
God who became man. {Β} And yet it seems to those men that these things are
not thus, but they would say God came to be with us, that is the Word from
God, according at least to the modes of succour. For he saved the world under
heaven through the one born of a woman. {Α} For was he not, tell me, also with
Moses of the 717 country of the Egyptians and the greed there, releasing
Israel in a mighty hand and in a high arm, as it is written? And shall we not
also after these things find Jesus saying clearly: And as I was with Moses, so
I will be also with you? {Β} True. {Α} For what reason then has none of those
been named Emmanuel, but the name fitted only the one born according to flesh
from a woman in the last times of the age paradoxically? {Β} Shall we then
suppose in what manner God has been born of a woman? as if the Word has
partaken of the existence in her and from her? {Α} Away with so cold a random
counsel. For they are words of one beside himself, and of a mind that has
fallen sick, turned toward things that do not belong, to think that the
ineffable existence of the Only-begotten has become a fruit of flesh. But he
was, as God, coeternal with the Father who begot him, and from him by nature
ineffably begotten. And if they wish to know clearly how and in what manner he
has appeared in the form that is according to us and has become man, the
divine evangelist John will make it clear saying: And the Word became flesh,
and tabernacled among us, and we beheld his glory, glory as of an
Only-begotten from a Father, full of grace and truth. {Β} But if the Word
became, they say, flesh, he has no longer remained Word, but has rather
departed from being what he was. {Α} And yet this is sophistry and nonsense


and inventions of a mind that has gone out of itself, and nothing else. For
they think, as it seems, as from an inescapable and necessary account, that
"became" is significant of turning and alteration. {Β} Yes, they say, and they
even confirm their own account, taking it from the God-inspired Scripture

became a pillar of salt. And indeed also upon the rod of Moses that he cast it
upon the earth, and it became a serpent. For in these a natural turning has
come to be. {Α} Therefore, when some sing: And the Lord became to me a refuge,
and again: Lord, you have become a refuge to us from generation to generation,

change into being a refuge, and has he stood over naturally toward something
else besides what he was not in the beginnings? {Β} And how is the affair not
unfitting and incongruous to the one who is 718 by nature God? For being
unchangeable by nature, he remains somewhere altogether what he was and is
always, even if he should be said by some to have become a refuge. {Α} You
have spoken most excellently and very rightly. Therefore, when the memory of
God has been brought into the midst, if "Became" should be said by someone,

turning, and not rather to try to understand it according to some other
manner, and to turn it sensibly toward what is most fitting and like to the
unchangeable God? {Β} How then do we say the Word has become into flesh,
keeping always the unchangeable and unalterable as proper and essential and
inborn to him? {Α} The all-wise Paul, the steward of his mysteries, the priest
of the evangelical preachings, will make it clear saying: Think this each in
yourselves which also in Christ Jesus, who being in the form of God, did not
consider being equal to God a thing to be seized, but emptied himself, taking
a form of a slave, becoming in likeness of men, and being found in fashion as
a man, he humbled himself, becoming obedient as far as death, and death of a
cross. For although being God and from God by nature his only-begotten Word,
the radiance of the glory, and the impress of the hypostasis of the one who
begot, he became man, and not turned into flesh, or mixture, or blending, or
having endured some other of such things, but rather letting himself down into
emptying, and instead of the joy set before him, having despised shame, and
not having dishonored the poverty of the humanity. For he willed, as God, the
flesh held by death and sin, also to show it better than death and sin, and to
bring it back toward what was in the beginnings, having made it his own, and
not soulless at least according to some, but rather ensouled with a rational
soul. And not having disdained to go the path fitting to the affair, he is
said to have endured the birth according to us, remaining what he was. For he
has been born paradoxically according to flesh from a woman; for it was not
otherwise for one who is God by nature, or to be seen in the form that is
according to us to those upon earth, the intangible and bodiless. Except that,
having judged to become man, and in himself and alone to show our nature
distinguished by the dignities of the godhead; for he was the same God 719
together and man, and in likeness of a man, insofar as with this he was also
God, and in fashion as a man. For God was in the form that is according to us,
and in a slave's form Master. For thus we say he became flesh. And in this way

flesh, according to the Scriptures, and was seen upon earth and consorted with
men, having the form of the slave as his own, he will also be called Son of
man, even if some are distressed at this, they would be caught throwing
themselves into the charges of ignorance. For it was not otherwise for the
flesh to become life-giving, although according to its own nature brought
under the need to be corrupted, unless it became proper of the Word who gives
life to all things. For thus it was energizing his things, being in
birth-pangs with his life-giving power. And it is no wonder, for if it is true
that the fire having consorted with the matters, although they are not hot
according to their own nature, shows them hot; for it sends even very richly
into them the energy of the power inbeing to it. How does not the Word being
God rather send into his own flesh his own life-giving power and energy,
having been united to it and having shown it his own, unconfusedly and
unchangeably and according to the manner which he himself knows? {Β} It is
then necessary to confess together, nothing else mediating, that the body of
the Word being from the Father has become altogether proper, even if it should
be thought ensouled with a rational soul. {Α} Entirely so, if we rightly778
divide the unerring account of the faith, and we are lovers of the doctrines
of the truth, and we track the faith of the holy fathers, not being carried
away from the straight, nor yet having left the royal path, and being carried
off by the random-talks of some into a counterfeit mind, but rather being
built upon the foundation itself, that is ChristFor another foundation no one
is able to lay besides the one lying, as the wise master-builder and priest of
his mysteries has written truly. We believe then that the Son of the God and
Father is one, and our Lord Jesus the Christ thought in one person, having
been begotten from the God and Father divinely as Word before every age and
time, and in last times of the age the same having become according to flesh
from a woman, and we shall assign to him both the things proper to God and the
human, and we say the birth according to flesh and the suffering upon the
cross have become of him, appropriating all the things of his own flesh, yet
remaining impassible in the nature of the godhead. For thus every knee bends
to him, And every tongue will confess that Lord Jesus Christ unto glory of God
the Father. Amen.
