544 1 Cor 1:1-3 Do not think that, claiming for myself the dignity of
teaching, I am about to pull you down from the swelling over this; for see, I
even rank Sosthenes with myself, and I call him brother, and I write with him,
making my own things common. Why then do I cut you short? Because your
swelling over teaching is harmful; for it profits you nothing, and it drives
those who are persuaded out of the healthy teaching, and it divides the common
body of the church. With all who call upon the name of the Lord. As much as
those men were splitting even from one another, so much did he himself contend
to join them even to those who dwell far off. Wherefore, being about to
address to them the grace and the peace from God, and having said called
saints, he at once joined with all who call upon and so on; and to you, he
says, and to all together be the grace and peace from God the Father. In every
place, theirs and ours. Since having said with all who call upon the name of
the Lord, he added only ours, correcting himself and showing him to be common
Lord and master of all, he adds: aὐτῶν τε καὶ ἡμῶν. And if he is common Lord
of all, not only of us but also of those everywhere, why, splitting from one
another and insulting the common lordship from which you cut yourselves, do
you distribute yourselves to these and to those? 1 Cor 1:5 From where is it
clear that you were enriched in word and knowledge? From the preaching of the
faith toward Christ being confirmed in you; for the confirmation of this in
you becomes a testimony of you both of word and of knowledge. 545 1 Cor 1:6-7
Testimony of Christ: either the voluntary and saving passion, and simply the
emptying and incarnation of the Word for us; or testimony of Christ is the
grace and gift of the signs. For these too are a testimony of the faith toward
Christ and of its divinity and majesty; for the Corinthians were rich in

confirmation, well, he says, we said before that you were enriched in every
word and all knowledge; for both the word and the knowledge are a part of the
gifts, the word contributing to teaching, and the knowledge to understanding.
And the so that you are not lacking rather agrees with the second
interpretation. 1 Cor 1:9 Through whom you were called. For no one comes to
the Son, unless the Father draw him, as neither does anyone come to the
Father, except him to whom the Son may reveal. Through whom you were called
instead of by whom you were called, that is, the one who called you. And mark
how the divine Paul sets the through whom also upon the Father, and in place
of what he sets it; so that when you hear it also of the Son, you may think
not a difference of essence but identity. Into the fellowship of his Son. You
were called into the fellowship of Christ; why, setting over yourselves now
these, now those as patrons and teachers, do you contend to split yourselves
and cut yourselves off from that wondrous and saving fellowship? 11 Cor 1:14.
17a Why do you give thanks, Paul, that you baptized none of them except those
you named? Not setting baptism in a slight account? God forbid, he says, but I
give thanks for these two causes: one, that by not baptizing, he says, I did
not even give some a pretext to say that I baptized many for this, that,
having gathered a chorus of disciples for myself, I might procure for myself
the name of teacher and guide. And for another, that I did not even have a
commission from Christ for this, showing that, if I who was sent by 546 Christ
to preach and announce his gospel baptized with sparing and rarely although
baptism is akin and is bound together with the preaching, you who were not
sent at all, how do you dare to declare yourselves teachers? And how do you
even baptize? Or at all what do you do of things for which you did not receive
grace and commission? 11 Cor 1:17b He did not say not in wisdom of knowledge,
but not in wisdom of word. Well; for he was evangelizing in wisdom of
knowledge, wherefore also elsewhere: unskilled in word, but not in knowledge.


For what is wiser or better and more splendid toward knowledge than to learn
and to teach that death has been abolished by a cross and by death, and that
our race of men has been set free from the long and most grievous tyranny of
the devil? Lest the cross of Christ be emptied. For the cross of Christ, that
is, his voluntary passion for us, is emptied and cheapened by those who try by
a word of wisdom and by persuasiveness to set forth its majesty and divinity.
For how is it possible to set forth what is beyond word and nature by words
and by some art? And what need is there even of word and persuasion, when the
signs and wonders cry louder than every voice and lead by the hand everyone
who is willing toward piety and the preaching? 1 Cor 1:18 He shows next that
it was not even possible to draw some to the preaching by a word of wisdom,
but only by the cooperation and grace from above. For if the cross is the
chief point of the preaching, and the word about the cross not only does not
draw, but even seems folly to those who are perishing, what could one have to
say about the preaching if grace were not supplying him all things? 1 Cor
1:25-31 Do you wish to learn that the foolish thing of God is wiser than men
