* On Saints Peter and Paul

Hesychius, presbyter of Jerusalem, encomium on the holy
apostles Peter and Paul.
1 Fair indeed is the rose, when the spring season offers it to
be smelled, but if it is twined with the lily it becomes, as is
fitting, more fragrant. For myrrh is mixed with myrrh, and from
this there comes a double enjoyment of the sweetness. Peter is
brilliant in himself, and more brilliant than the brilliant, as
chief of the apostolic pair, as the eye of discipleship, as a
memorial of zeal and the face of the faith, as a trumpet of the
mystery, as a tongue that taught many tongues, as a blameless
shepherd, as a sleepless helmsman, as an unerring charioteer, as
an apothecary who prepared the abiding eye-salve of repentance,
as a fisherman of the sea by whom the fishes are fished into
heaven (whose net has miracles for linen, sufferings for bait,
the cross for a hook, and the resurrection for its bosom), as
keeper of the keys of the treasury, in which the Father has the
treasures, the Son the bridal chambers, the Spirit the table,
the angels the choirs.
2 But when Paul is added, the wall is double and cannot be
taken, the spring is two-mouthed, watering the whole earth
without toil, the lake is two-faced, and every merchant-ship
that sails past is saved. The turtledove speaks with the
swallow, the dove sounds with the cicada, flute and syrinx
together sing a holy and gentle melody. "A young man with a holy
one, and their way is straight," as the wise man proclaimed in
Proverbs. A lamb and a wolf graze together, and the shepherd is
glorified, and the wonders of the worker of marvels are crowned.
A bull and a lion feed in one pasture, and he who strengthened
the bull and bridled the lion gathers the praises. Was Paul not
a lion when he was Saul, since by killing he scattered the fold,
struck the shepherds with terror, roared within Jerusalem, and
frightened the cities, threw the marketplaces into uproar, and
made the fields uninhabited, and did not cease "dragging men
into prison and women" in bonds, yet was himself defeated by the
endurance of the men and proved weaker than the courage of the
women? He ran in vain, for he did not finish the road; he boxed
to no purpose, since he bloodied his fingers, and was not able
to find the adversary. Christ then, being himself wise, and
himself again good, coming forward uses gentleness and, leaving
the lofty things aside, puts forward the humble. He did not
kindle fire, did not stir whirlwinds, did not shake the
foundations of the mountains, did not bare an angelic two-edged
sword, but with a meek and humane voice he called, saying,
"Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?" What wealth of yours did
I take away? Of what glory did I strip you? Of what first prizes
did I deprive you? Did I shut paradise against you? Have you
been clothed with the fig-leaves because of me? Have I become
the cause of your turning back to the earth? Did you fall from
your rule because of me, diminish the inheritance, lessen the
image?
3 "Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?" You had the Law, and
I, as one who is good, added grace. You were weary with the
letter, and I set the Spirit beside you. The shadows were
causing grief, and coming I showed the truth. You were stumbling
at the types, and I brought one healing in place of many, the
unbought in place of the money-loving; I, interpreting the
sabbath for you, glorified it; I stopped the wound of your
circumcision; I freed the altar from groanings; instead of the
much-groaning sacrifices I showed the ever-joyful, the
ungroaning one. "Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?" Be
ashamed before the tent of Abraham, and remember the altar of
Isaac; take up into your memories the wrestling of Jacob. Why
have you given Joseph's dreams over to forgetfulness? How are
you not put to shame by the rod of Moses, the mantle of Elijah,
the courage of Elisha? These are types of me; their prophecies
concern me. "Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?" I showed
Jeremiah the almond staff; I painted for Zechariah the golden
lampstand; I pointed out to Daniel the uncut stone; I widened
for Jonah the belly of the sea-monster; me the great Ezekiel saw
as charioteer of the cherubim; I am Isaiah's much-celebrated
sheep.
4 "Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?" Attend where you
stretch your bows. "For heaven is my throne, and the earth a
footstool." Where do you send your arrows? "For I am in the
Father and the Father in me." Against whom do you throw the
stones? I "measured the water with the hand and the heaven with
a span and all the earth with a handful." Against whom do you
arm your tongue? I make clear the tongues of those who stammer;
I made the hard of hearing and the deaf; I fashioned as a potter
both the seeing and the blind. See whom you call a deceiver: "I
multiplied visions and was likened in the hands of the
prophets." "Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?" For David
sings me, but you revile; he hymns, but you blaspheme; he plays
the kithara, but you kick; he shakes the strings, but you use
weapons. "Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?" Why do you make
war on me with what is mine, you champion the Law and campaign
against me, you run for my sake and punish those who are mine?
You avenge my glory, but you do not know from where one must
take hold of the battle, from where it is fitting to join the
war.
5 These things Christ said to Saul; and he answers and says,
"Who are you, Lord?" Good is the zeal of Paul, since when called
he obeyed at once. He received the word and at once perceived
the dignity of the one speaking, and said, "Who are you, Lord?"
For I have been filled with the radiance, I have been captured
by the light, I have become a prisoner of the ray. "Who are you,
Lord?" For I long to see your beauty, I am eager to fly up
toward the clouds, I desire to learn whence this "radiance of
glory," who is the "impress of so great a hypostasis," who this
one is who "bears all things by the word of his own power." "Who
are you, Lord?" For I have heard your voice and I have had my
fill of the Law; I no longer have longing for the Law; I no
longer wish there to be a gold-wood ark; I long for a pure
manger, wholly royal, gold-gifted. Let the children of all the
heretics be ashamed, and let the mouths of the heretics be put
to shame. For see: Paul, when called, does not pry but only
believes; he worships and does not fight; he hymns, and blame
has been done away. "Who are you, Lord?" he says. He would not
have called him Lord if he had not perceived God in the one
calling. Whom he knew as God he confessed as Maker; whom he
confessed as Maker he proclaimed impassible; he held him
incorruptible. He reverenced as immortal that one whose cross he
knew and whose tomb he recognized. He was not ignorant of the
blows, the spear of the side, the slaps.
6 What then does Christ say to him? "I am Jesus the Nazarene,
whom you persecute." He receives a brief answer to the question:
"I am Jesus the Nazarene, whom you persecute"; I am Jesus who
once was and now is, who is before all and after all, who is in
the beginning and at the last, the son of David but himself
before David, from Abraham yet Maker, from Adam but he himself
fashioned Adam as a potter, from Isaac but he himself crowned
the altar of Isaac, from Jacob but he himself was seen upon the
ladder, surrounded by the companies of the angels. "I am Jesus
whom you persecute," but in persecuting you do not overtake him.
For where or how will you hunt "the radiance of glory"? What net
can contain "the impress of the hypostasis"? Who can lay hold
with the hand of him "who bears all things by the word of his
own power"? "I am Jesus whom you persecute." I keep you as the
bridesman of my bride. Why do you persecute me, for whose sake
you are soon to be persecuted? But you weary your feet in vain,
you sweat to no purpose, you toil needlessly; he who is
persecuted by you sits in the bosom of the Father. "I am Jesus
whom you persecute," not knowing that you fight against your own
life. Why do you war against the Wise One? You shoot arrows at a
benefactor such as you have no other; you kick the nourisher
through whom the earth, without hesitation, raises up for you
the table of fruits? You slight a Savior; if he should close his
hand, who frees you from sins, who looses you from bonds, with
whom will you find the handwritten bond?
7 Thus then this Paul, once a persecutor, now an evangelist;
once a pirate, now a helmsman; once a wolf, now a shepherd; once
lead, now gold; once a blasphemer, now blessing and blessed by
all; this man is a leader among teachers, exceptional among the
God-bearers, clear among prophets, wiser among apostles,
unwounded among athletes, more glorious than the heralds of the
truth, more powerful than the soldiers of piety. For who "was
beaten with rods three times" and prayed for a fourth? Who "five
times received forty minus one from the Jews," as he boasts? Who,
"having spent a night and a day in the deep," offers the
shipwreck instead of a bridal bath, "in journeyings often," but
all of them profitable, having made each one much-gaining? Who,
"in dangers from robbers, dangers from his own people, dangers
from rivers, dangers in the wilderness, dangers from the
nations, dangers among false brothers," did not scatter the
wealth of endurance? Who carries about the care of all the
churches, yet is not straitened by the trial of the burdens, nor
weighs down the shoulders, nor is the back bent? Who then so
greatly abhors sin, yet possesses such bowels of compassion
toward sinners? No one. For he always intercedes that we may
obtain the eternal goods, in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be
the glory and the power unto the ages of ages. Amen.
