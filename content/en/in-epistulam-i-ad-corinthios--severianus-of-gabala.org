1,22–25 For showing the absurdity of those who have trusted in eloquence and
suppose they adorn piety through fluency, he says that one must not only not
answer with wisdom to the Greeks who seek it, but not even give signs to the
Jews who ask for them. And indeed the signs are of the Spirit, but the wisdom

wisdom from outside (for they do not ask by putting to the test but they ask
in faith): yet speech is cast out altogether as plotting against the glory of
the cross. But we preach Christ crucified, and what follows. Clearly he says
Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God according to the word of the
cross, as above; for the word of the cross is foolishness to those who are
perishing, but to us who are being saved it is the power of God. And what
follows shows this more clearly; for after saying: but to those who are
called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God,
he added: because the foolishness of God is wiser than men, and the 230
weakness of God is stronger than men. But who would say that the God Word has
been called the weakness of God, and that the foolishness of God is what is
said about Christ crucified? The God Word is powerful as the Father, not a
power that is a completing part of the paternal substance, and likewise wise;
for since what the visible creation did not have power to do, nor angels nor
archangels coming down from heaven, patriarchs and prophets, the law through
Moses, the voices brought from the midst of the fire to the people, because he
did not constrain the choice of the ungrateful, this the foolishness of the
cross had power to do. For this reason Christ crucified is the power of God
and the wisdom of God, more effective than all the economy and more
trustworthy than all creation. And this too is the power of the one who
ordered the economy and accomplished it, in that he saved men in what is
nothing and led them to faith in him. 229,ξολ2 Since Jews put the Spirit of
God to the test, but it is to be granted to those who believe, neither indeed
must one answer Greeks who suppose they adorn piety with eloquence, then he
teaches how the preaching seems to be foolishness; for those seek signs and
wisdom, we preach Christ crucified, the power and wisdom of God, and from the
crucified we promise salvation to those who believe. 230,ξολ2 One must not say
that the God Word has been called the weakness of God, but the cross of
Christ; for the Word is powerful as the Father, not a power as a completing
part of the Father's substance. So also the Son is wise as the Father. And
since what angels did not have power to do, nor law, nor prophets, because God
allowed the choice of the ungrateful, this the seeming foolishness of the
cross had power to do, Christ crucified is preached as the power and wisdom of
God, as having appeared stronger and more effective and having saved those who
believe. 1 Kor 1,26 He added "according to the flesh" contrasting it with the
Spirit, and what follows likewise construing in common. 1 Kor 1,29 "That no
flesh should boast before him," that is, so that they may not ascribe the
success of the preaching to their own advantages. And he said flesh and not
soul, cheapening human boasts. And why "before him" and not before men?
Because the boast in our own advantages was set against the boast in God; for
what they ought to have ascribed to God, 231 they ascribed to men. For this
reason he said "before him"; for the boast was also impious when it took place
before the one who sees all things. And that he did not say "before him" of
the presence, the whole subject shows; for he must also make his speech to
those who boast in words and boast in baptizing, and in certain other things.
1 Kor 1,30 "And of him you are." All alike say that this has now been said
according to having been saved, not according to the account of creation, as
he says elsewhere: who were born not of


bloods nor of the will of flesh but of God. If then you are of him, how shall
we boast in our own advantages? And "in Christ Jesus, who became for us wisdom
and sanctification and redemption": it was not the wisdom of men that saved
(for it was going astray) but he himself became wisdom for us unto knowledge
of God; it was not the understanding of men that seized, but Jesus crucified.
Men did not redeem, so that the boast may come from the one who saved, not
from those who were saved. 1 Kor 2,3–4 I was always afraid that you would be
puffed up by the education from outside; at once I did not use the wisdom that
persuades. And "in weakness," he says, that from persecutions (for this is his
custom to say everywhere): in weakness and in fear and in trembling, the
weakness from the things by which I was persecuted. I displayed the fear and
the trembling concerning you; I was altogether humble, so that you might be
taught by this and not be lifted up. Since he said "my speech," lest he seem
to present the opposite to himself, that is fluency, he says: "and my
preaching not in persuasive words of wisdom," since there is a wisdom not of
phrases but of divine knowledge, of spirit and power. For since the Holy
Spirit both supplies a word of wisdom and also works wonders, he said: 232 "in
demonstration of spirit and of power"; for what is merely said is not yet
demonstration, but the wonder has the demonstration. 231,ξολ2 He shows that he
did not preach trusting in any human dominion; for he was in weakness and fear
and trembling and in such things, as being in persecutions and afflictions.
232 1 Kor 2,5 For since by speaking alone he differed in nothing from those
who use wisdom and trust in their own speech, but by working powers he showed
the strength of God, and from there the preaching, he well added: "but in
demonstration of spirit and of power, that your faith may not be in the wisdom
of man but in the power of God." 1 Kor 2,6 That is, not among the eloquent,
but among those perfect according to piety. And he said "among the perfect,"
not indeed "to the perfect," because the one speaking to whoever happens by
often also receives contradiction, but the one introducing the saving word to
the perfect and pious, as depositing and treasuring up the words in their
souls, does not talk. 1 Kor 2,7–8 If it had been hidden, it is clear that it
was not for reasonings to discover it, but truly it belonged to God to reveal
it. And the proof of their not understanding is, instead of saying: for if
they had known, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory. And he calls
"rulers" those around Pilate and Herod, and "Lord of glory" the God who became
incarnate. 1 Kor 2,9 This citation is not carried in the old [scripture] in
these very phrases, but in other words signifying the same; for Isaiah says:
Lord, who has believed our report? and to whom has the arm of the Lord been
revealed? and who will recount his generation? and Moses: the hidden things
belong to the Lord God. And the hidden thing of God clearly escaped both eye
and heart. Yet one must not think that God without judgment revealed the
mystery to some and allowed others to perish in ignorance, but one must know
and be persuaded that by his foreknowing power he measured out and supplied to
each what was according to desert toward what was expedient; for he foresees
each one's choice even before he comes to be. 233 1 Kor 2,10 He called "depths
of God" his deepest economies that escape us. Through these he shows that he
searches, not as one who does not know but as one who understands. And since
some say that "spirit" has now been said of the Father, and others of the Son,
one must speak to both. Now it is impossible that the Father has been meant;
for he says: "and we have not received the spirit of the world but the Spirit
that is from God," and no one is from himself; for if it had been said of God,
from whom is God? And the Son may be suspected, but it is not possible that he
is the one about whom the speech is; for who would say that the God Word
searches in order to learn the things of God? And if they should say that the
Son is taught, how did they not understand from what follows that as a man's
spirit knows the things in him, so also the Spirit of God? And apart from
this, throughout the whole passage the Holy Spirit is shown. And one must
observe that he says the Holy Spirit is from God, but he did not say the
unclean is from the world but of the world; for the


Paraclete is not a part of God, but the devil is as a part of creation
according to having been generated. But the Holy Spirit is from God. 1 Kor
2,11–13 Not in order to show that, as in us our spirit is better than we, so
also in God the Spirit is better than he; at once having said: "for who among
men knows the things of a man except the spirit that is in him?" But what is
the "in" of God? For the "in him" is a completing part of the whole man, but
the "from him" shows the cause. And one must observe that God and the Holy
Spirit are two persons, but a man and the spirit in him are not two persons
but one man. So showing the exact identity of knowledge 234 of Father and
Spirit, he compared the knowledge of the spirit in us to the knowledge of
Father and Spirit, so that it may be shown that, as in us in the one and the
same spirit, that is in the soul and the mind, there is no discord of union,
but we know what we know, so also in Father and Holy Spirit there is no
discord of knowledge but identity. "That we may know the things granted us by
God," the Spirit having given, the mind having grasped. "Which things we also
speak." Again he shows another thing: not only, he says, do we not find in
reasonings the things revealed to us, but we also preach them, not beautifying
with words but comparing spiritual things with spiritual, both understanding
and preaching in order by the power of the Spirit his revelations. "Comparing
spiritual things with spiritual": for when the second revelation is an
interpretation of the former and a clarification, those who receive the
revelations, comparing the second with the first and finding the same things,
took a greater assurance of the revealed truth. 233,ξολ2. God and the Holy
Spirit are two persons, but a man and the spirit in him are not two persons
but one man. But he says this: as in a man there is no discord of knowledge,
so also upon Father and Spirit knowledge is one, so that according to this the
Spirit would not have searched as one who does not know. Then again, upon the
man he said "the spirit that is in him," but upon the Holy Spirit he did not
say "that is in God" but "that is from God." And there is a great difference
between the "in him" and the "from God"; 234,ξολ2 for the "in him" is
completing of the whole man, but the "from him" shows the cause. Accurately he
said the Holy Spirit is from God as a living and hypostatic substance, but the
unclean of the world as completing of its substance; for the devil is a part
of his creation according to having been generated. Yet we do not find by
reasonings how to beautify the things revealed to us, but we compare spiritual
things with spiritual, when the second revelation becomes an interpretation
and clarification of the first teaching or vision or wonder; for the second
are compared with the first and are found the same. We take the greatest
assurance of the truth from the agreement. And this also "comparing" makes
clear from the contrary, also this distinguishing and separating; but the
former is better. 1 Kor 2,14–15 "But the soulish man does not receive the
things of the Spirit." He calls soulish the one who follows his own
reasonings. 235 "For it is foolishness to him." So great is the depth of the
spiritual revelation that it appears foolishness to the wise of this world; so
incapable of containing it is the revelation of God for them. For the grace of
the Holy Spirit, displaying power, convicted the craftsman, not by craft but
by the strength of the wonders. The spiritual do not overturn reasonings by
reasonings, but receiving the mind from God and the revelation from there they
discourse, so that the one who sets himself against them does not convict
reasonings, but opposes the knowledge of God. For this reason he says: "for
who has known the mind of the Lord, who will instruct him? But we have the
mind of Christ," that is, the knowledge. 234,ξολ2 And he calls soulish the one
who follows his own reasonings or the one who is sick in the reasonings of the
soul and not sound concerning the 235,ξολ2 knowledge of the truth, just as he
also calls fleshly the one who is sick concerning fleshly desires and


a slave of pleasures. So then the soulish man cannot be receptive of the
truth, but he has so harmed the soul as to consider the economy of God and the
grace foolishness. And not being able to know that he is examined spiritually.
The soulish man, seeking craftsmanlike reasonings upon the cross, thrusts away
the preaching as foolish and unpersuasive, and not looking toward the
wonder-workings of the Spirit he can no longer even understand that he is set
against the truth, being convicted by the power and wonder-working of the
Spirit; for "is examined" makes clear that he is convicted accurately and by
scrutiny. But the spiritual man, the one who has obtained the Spirit through
the signs he displays, convicts all the things that among the others convict
badly, but he himself is convicted by no one. Then also confirming the word he
sets beside it the things from the old [scripture] and says. 1 Kor 2,16
"Instruct": correct. We have the mind of Christ our God. And if men do not
succeed in knowing the mind of God, then neither ours who have the mind and
wisdom of God. 1 Kor 3,1–3 The unbelievers rather follow the wonder-workings;
therefore I spoke a word to you, not indeed the perfect hidden wisdom, the
economy concerning the passion which the perfect alone, believing, accept.
236 Still the teaching concerning morals and believing through the signs he
figuratively calls milk, but meat the announcement of the divine doctrines.
And milk can also be the light commands, honoring father and mother, and you
shall not commit adultery and the like, and come follow me. Then having said
"you are fleshly, and you walk according to man," he means this: you honor the
things among men and you do not seek the things of God. 1 Kor 3,5 i.e. equal
according to the ministry. 1 Kor 3,6–10 Some things have been said by him as
upon farming, others as upon building. And he said he had planted and given
the beginning and laid a foundation of God. He charges those who after him
build on the foundation not to make the building a side-work. And he calls
foundation the faith and the preaching. 1 Kor 3,11–15 Christ is not called
foundation of creation, as the Arians falsely insist, but of the faith; for
Christ was preached, and all the building comes to be upon this foundation.
And those who teach preach one faith and announce the evangelical life, and it
is ours to make ourselves either precious stones or wood or hay or stubble.
When then he says "and each let him see how he builds upon it," not according
to the life, but according to what? For no one can lay another foundation
besides the one that is laid. The teacher announces Christ and gives his
commands; it remains to be likened either to gold through purity, or to hay
through weakness. If then he charges that each let him see, and what follows,
it is toward those who wish to be named from those who baptize or teach. "If
anyone's work remains which he built upon," you see that the one who builds
builds well; but that does not remain according to a change sometimes of a
worse choice. "If anyone's work shall be burned, he shall suffer loss, but he
himself shall be saved, yet so as through fire." 237 It is a hyperbaton; for

those who receive another teaching from the wisdom from outside, these have

having left the indwelling teacher, they have gone astray about the wisdom
outside, which is sufficient as a provision for deceit. 238,ξολ2 Through the
things by which he forbids the wisdom of this world as being sufficient for

charged them not to receive the wisdom outside, he teaches them not to boast
in wise men either; for this boast is cheap where all things are yours, both
the apostles (for they were sent on account of those who are benefited), and
the world is yours, for whose sake it came to be for enjoyment, and life is
yours as of those being saved, and death (for if the things of death were not
fearful, the things of the life to come would not have been preferred); for

have become Christ's, and those who are made intimate with Christ are pleasing

become Christ's, and in order to show that those who are made intimate with
Christ are pleasing to God, he added: "and Christ is God's." 1 Kor 4,1 It is
enough for us to be honored as servants of Christ, but for some to be named
from us is burdensome. 1 Kor 4,2 You indeed see us as servants of God, but who
is a faithful servant, do not you seek (for this is at risk among you), and
neither is it fitting that the teachers be judged by the disciples. And why
does he say these things? Because he perceived that some of the teachers
unworthy were being honored by them, but the worthy were being overlooked,
whence he also foresaw that schisms would be among them. Therefore he also
charges more vehemently, hastening to check the passion. 1 Kor 4,3 And lest he
seem to say these things disdainfully as one who has been despised, he all but
says this: do not think that I send these things on my own behalf; for I do
not so conduct myself as to be examined by you; for I consider this unworthy
as toward you and every man. And he calls the man a human 239 day, making
clear the temporary and ephemeral character of the life of men. Then since it
seems a great thing to say "I am conscious of nothing against myself," he
added "but I am not justified in this"; for it is possible that one who has
stumbled in ignorance is not struck in conscience. Then it is not not doing
what is base that justifies, but succeeding in virtue that perfects. And it is
likely that he also said this as one of moderate feeling: so do not think
things greater than according to yourselves, but wait for the judge. 1 Kor 4,5
Throughout he holds to the aim, bringing to their senses those who wish to be
named from this one, but not to be named from that one; for this belongs to
those who judge both the worthy and the not worthy. So do not think things
greater than according to yourselves, but wait for the judge; for a feigned
character can also deceive you, but nothing of the hidden things would escape
the one who lights up the hidden things of darkness. And he said these things,
as that many things among them were happening by deceit and the unworthy were
being honored. Those who were honored were not altogether good, but they held
virtue as far as a figure, and those good by nature were overlooked; for he
would not have said these things. 1 Kor 4,6 He brings forward himself and
Apollos, having kept silent


the persons of those who demand to be honored and that the disciples be named
from them, so that you may take from us a type and sequence of dealing
moderately with the teachers. And do not think about us above what is written,
but consider us servants of Christ and stewards of the mysteries of God, and
do not dishonor some of the teachers and honor others. More clearly in these
things he convicted their mind; for the one named from this man, but
overlooking that man, was puffed up on behalf of the former, and had the
puffing up against the latter. 1 Kor 4,7 You who wish to be called from this
one or to leave that one aside, who distinguishes? Do you not wait for the
judge? You who obey wait 240 for the judge, and you subject the teacher to
your judgment? 239,ξολ2 You the layman, who distinguishes you upon the things
you think or do, that you dare to distinguish others, and these teachers? Is
it not the Lord? 240,ξολ2 If then you the disciple wait for the Lord as judge,
how do you subject the teacher to your judgment, saying the one worthy, the

indeed think great things of yourself and be lifted up as putting forward an

4,21 Since amendment is twofold, in one way through admonition for the

humanely made the choice of these things rest upon them, so that repenting
they will correct themselves. And he called "rod" the compelling power of the
Spirit, which Paul used against Elymas and God against Paul himself. 1 Kor
5,1–5 Not because that man had a woman in the manner stated, but 243 because
he was eloquent, and they were not mourning that he had his father's wife, but
they were puffed up that he was well educated; for their mourning would either
have separated the one who fornicated if he persisted, or would have driven
out the absurdity of the deed and restored him. Not simply having worked but
having worked out; for he did not fall by being snatched away, but he used the
evil as a work. And he says he is present in spirit, voting with them and
condemning with them (and he calls spirit the gift), when they have been
gathered together with the power of Christ. Those puffed up over the one who

to be gathered together toward the condemnation upon the fornication. 244 They

have separated the one who fornicated if he persisted in the fornication, or
would have separated the absurdity itself and would have restored the man
himself, so that the spirit may be saved. He did not say having accomplished
but having worked out, even in this in order to indicate that having used much

griefs, mournings, other circumstances, as the apostle himself also says

manner then he now also commands to hand him over to Satan, that is to the
difficulties of life. 244,ξολ2 He hands him over to the harms of life; for

resurrection. So what he says is such, that flesh being flesh does not
inherit, and blood being blood does not inherit; for as long as the flesh is
flesh, it is corruptible; but then having put on incorruption, having put away
the weakness of the flesh, it becomes something else, that is incorruptible.
He proved what he was saying before this; for he calls incorruption the
kingdom, and corruption the mortality in this age. So the opposites do not
become heirs of the opposites. 1 Kor 15,52 He made clear that there are many
trumpets; for having recalled a last he also put together the first. "And the
dead will be raised incorruptible, and we shall be changed." If the dead are
incorruptible, they themselves also will clearly be changed. What need of "and
we shall be changed"? Or altogether somewhere he said the incorruptible as
common of all, but "we shall be changed" now hints the change toward the
better; therefore he also said "we" about the righteous. Having said this and
having shown the body he shut in the heretics who say there is not a
resurrection of flesh but of a soul; for these also are impious about the
divine economy itself, thinking that Christ has taken up the flesh not in
truth but in appearance, which is mindless. For if he did not have flesh, what
is that which died and was buried and rose? And if these things in appearance,
then we also die in appearance. So the incorruptible is common of those who
will be raised, but to be changed is proper only of the holy, which indicates
the change toward the better. And the "we" is superfluous, unless perhaps he
wishes to signify about the holy, i.e. the righteous around us, as also
elsewhere having said that we who are living, he signified the holy around
him. 1 Kor 16,1 Collection is called the contribution that comes to be by
gathering unto consolation of the poor.
