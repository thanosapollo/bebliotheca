Of Saint Athanasius, Concerning the Unleavened Breads.
The Jews used to celebrate the Passover in three materials and in three
virtues; and in three witnesses according to the order of the Law. And the
three materials were the lamb, the unleavened breads, and the bitter herb;
and the three virtues, being girded, and sandals on the feet, and a staff
in the hands. And the three witnesses, scribes standing at the table, and
knowing exactly the outpouring of the moon, through a certain cup being
shaken and being signaled; the priest immediately trumpeting that it is
the Passover of the Lord; and then after the hearing of the voice of the
trumpet, they made the leaven without bread for seven days. But before the
sign of the cup and of the trumpet, they did not have authority to prepare
for seven days a lamb, or unleavened bread, or bitter herb; for thus also
the great David says: Blow with a trumpet in the New Moon. And our Lord
Jesus Christ on the fifth day made the tradition of the holy mysteries at
the seventh hour of the night; and the entrance of the eaters of unleavened
bread happened on Friday evening after the Lord had been buried. For this
reason the four Evangelists say that the Lord handed over perfect bread in
the holy mysteries and not unleavened; that he might confirm that it is a
perfect body from the holy Virgin; I mean ensouled and endowed with mind;
and not as the heretic Apollinarius said, that he assumed a body only; but
we say that the bread being made perfect is that which consists of flour
and leaven and salt; just as the spiritual to the body, and to the mind;
for a body, without breath, is dead; and the unleavened alone not having
leaven is dead and is not living bread; since the leaven gives life to the
dough and union just as the spirit to the body, and the salt to the mind
and for this reason we celebrate the mystery through bread being made
perfect, that we may not fall into the blasphemy of the impious
Apollinarius. And the outpouring of the light happens in fourteen and a
half days and three hours; but toward the passion of our Lord Jesus Christ
I went beyond the sixteenth day; that it might bear witness that he is
true and eternal life. It is to be known therefore that in the age of
Christ and of his holy apostles to eat or to offer the fine flour of the
Unleavened no =26.1329= Church received; since neither did Christ himself
at the time of his passion eat the legal Passover, hear John the
Evangelist saying: Now before the feast of the Passover, Jesus knowing
that his hour had come that he should depart out of this world to the
Father, ... and supper having taken place, the Devil having already put
into the heart of Judas Simon Iscariot that he should betray him; and
when, he says, the hour came, that is when the time stood present for him
to be betrayed, the twelve apostles also reclined with him; and he said to
them: With desire I have desired to eat this Passover with you before I
suffer. You see, Before I suffer, he says; since therefore he had to
suffer on the day of the legal Passover, on which also the lamb was
sacrificed by the Jews, and the passion was about to take place by the
slayers of God on Friday, as John again says: Since it was Preparation,
for that Sabbath was a great day, on which at that time also the
fourteenth of the moon, according to the first month, happened to fall in
the five hundred and thirtieth year, so that having been betrayed he might
not fail to hand over his own Passover to the disciples, reclining at the
supper late, and slowly about the night according to the evening, namely
of the fifth, he did eat, but his own Passover before the feast of the
Unleavened; and this is clear from his reclining; for it was not even
legislated; since standing and girded and shod to eat the Passover with
haste, and having the staff upon their hands the law commanded the Jews;
he brought to an end, in the times of his own preaching, the legal
Passover, and he handed over his own through the supper; therefore after
dining. taking bread, and breaking it, he handed over to the apostles the
mystery of his New Covenant and Liturgy; in the night namely of the fifth,
in which he was betrayed. And it is to be known that at the hour in which
the slayers of God crucified the Lord of glory, at that same they also,
having sacrificed the lamb, ate the legal Passover; and this is clear from
the Evangelist John saying: that they themselves did not enter into the
Praetorium, that they might not be defiled, but that they might eat the
Passover; so that on one day together with the lamb Christ was sacrificed;
but what does Luke say? He said that Christ took bread and not unleavened;
for it was not yet, it being then the fifth day; the moon was still the
thirteenth and there were no unleavened breads, because they had not yet
come to be; for on the fifteenth of the moon it was legislated to eat the
unleavened; and on the fourteenth the lamb alone to be sacrificed;
therefore also on the fourteenth of the first month of the moon the law
names Passover; and on the fifteenth it names first Sabbath; if therefore
Christ was sacrificed according to the fourteenth of the moon according to
the day of Preparation, and the Sabbath was the feast of the Unleavened,
when did he eat the unleavened breads? and when did he hand these over to
the apostles in the New Covenant? because the imitators of the Jews say
that we received from the apostles =26.1332= to make the unleavened; for
Christ did not even arrive in time to eat the legal Passover at that time;
and that the truth is thus, for confirmation of the things said, hear also
the legislation of Moses in which he legislated concerning the Passover
and the Unleavened: These, he says, are the feasts of the Lord, holy
convocations, which you shall call them in their seasons. In the first
month on the fourteenth day of the month, between the evenings, a Passover
to the Lord. And on the fifteenth day of this month a feast of Unleavened
Breads to the Lord; seven days you shall eat unleavened breads. See, you
learn also from the legislation of Moses, that the unleavened breads were
not; for after dining according to the fifth day Christ handed over
through the breaking of the bread the mystery of the New Covenant; since
unleavened breads on that day were not ...
