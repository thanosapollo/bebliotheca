* Song on Great Saturday
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

A sticheron sung on the holy and great Saturday; made by kyr the great
logothete Acropolites. plagal second mode. for the epitaphios greetings.
Joseph, having seen the sun hiding its own rays, and the veil of the temple
torn at the death of the Savior, came to Pilate and entreated saying: "Give me
this stranger, who from infancy as a stranger was a guest in Egypt; Give me
this stranger, whom those of the same race, hating, scourge as a stranger;
Give me this stranger, I am not a stranger to seeing the death of the
stranger; Give me this stranger, who was present to entertain the poor and
strangers. Give me this stranger, whom Judas deceitfully alienated from the
world. Give me this stranger, whom the friend denies not to know as a
stranger. Give me this stranger, [that I may hide him in a tomb,] because the
stranger has not a head where to lay it. Give me this stranger, whom the
mother as living entreats crying: Even if I am torn in the inward parts and
wounded in the heart seeing you dead and breathless, yet I take courage to be
magnified by your resurrection." Having prevailed upon Pilate with these
words, the honorable one takes the body of the Savior, and having wrapped it
honorably in linen and myrrhs he laid it in a memorial, furnishing to the
faithful eternal life and the great mercy.
