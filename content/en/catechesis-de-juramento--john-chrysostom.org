* Catechesis on the Oath
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Catecheses of John Chrysostom. Of the same. Having discoursed in the former
catechesis concerning oaths, he spoke this again on the same subject, showing
that not only perjury, but also true-swearing, has punishment, and that Christ
usefully rose after three days. Discourse 2.

Have you driven out from your mouth the wicked custom of oaths? For I have not
forgotten either the things I myself discoursed to you, or the things you
promised to me concerning this subject. For I also discoursed and you
promised, even if not in word, yet by the praises of the things said. And this
promise is greater =155= than that through the word; for the one who promises
in word often nods with the tongue, but does not nod with the mind; but the
one who praises the things said makes the consent from the soul. Have you then
wiped the tongue clean from the grievous stain? Have you banished the ruin
from your sacred soul? I guess that you have wiped it clean; for you are about
to receive a great king and to enjoy much and spiritual teaching of the wiser
fathers. And the time too is sufficient, and the appointed term given for the
correction is now toward the end, and you are easy to guide and obedient; "for
obey those who lead you and submit," says the apostle, and in everything you
hearken to them. Guessing from all these things I think the whole has been
accomplished. But I did not wish to guess and suppose, but to know clearly;
for I would have taken hold more eagerly of the more mystical words, having
laid aside the care concerning oaths, and with more freedom I would have led
you by the hand toward this dread mystagogy, and I would have brought you into
the very inner sanctuary and shown the holy of holies and all the things
there, not a golden jar having manna, but the Master's body, the heavenly
bread. I would have shown you not a wooden ark having stone tablets and a law,
but a blameless and holy flesh having the lawgiver himself. I would have shown
within not a blameless sheep slaughtered, but the lamb of God sacrificed, a
mystical sacrifice, which even the angels themselves tremble seeing. I would
have shown you not Aaron entering with a golden robe, but the only-begotten
entering, having the firstfruits of our nature, showing to the Father himself
the greatness of the achievement; "for not into handmade," he says, "holy
places did Christ enter, antitypes of the true, but into =156= heaven itself,
now to appear to the face of God." There is a veil there not such as the
Jewish temple had, but much more dreadful. Listen then what this veil is, that
you may learn of what sort those holy of holies were, and of what sort these.
"Having," he says, "much boldness for the entrance of the holy places in the
blood of Jesus, which he dedicated for us, a fresh and living way through the
veil, that is, of his own flesh." Did you see how this veil is more dreadful
than that? Unto all these things I wished to initiate you today.

But what am I to suffer? The care of oaths does not let me go, wasting my
soul; and I know that many will condemn the excess of the things said, having
heard that it wastes my soul; for they think the sin to be small; but I on
this account rather lament. For the other sins both are grievous and are
thought to be grievous; as murder and adultery both is grievous and is thought
to be grievous, but the oath is grievous, yet is not thought to be grievous.
On this account I lament and fear the sin; for this, this is of the wiles of
the devil, to bring in the sin covered, and as mixing poison with customary
food, so he contrives to hide the oath in the preconception of men. Shall I
then spend all the teaching and the time on the oath? By no means; for I also
guess that there are some who have succeeded.

For as when the sower went out not all the seed fell into the thorns, nor all
onto the rocks, but much also was brought down into the good earth, so also
now it is impossible, so much teaching having taken place in so great a
multitude, that there be no one able to display the fruit. Therefore since
many have succeeded, but not yet all, let us also divide the word. For those
=157= who have not succeeded ought not even at all to hear mystical words; but
that those may not be treated despitefully, let us grant it to the more
slothful on account of the more earnest; for it is much better to grant this
to these on account of those, than to treat the more earnest despitefully on
account of the more slothful.

I wish to remind you of a debt, which in the former discourse I promised, but
did not pay, the word having pressed us toward more necessary things. What
then was that debt? I was seeking to say to you, for what cause our fathers,
having run past all the time of the year, legislated in this season that your
souls be initiated; and I was saying that not simply nor at random has the
observation of the season taken place. For always the same grace is, and it is
not hindered by time; for the grace is divine; yet the observation of the
season also has something mystical. For what cause then did the fathers now
legislate this feast? Now our king has conquered the war against the
barbarians; for barbarians they are, and wilder than barbarians, all the
demons. Now he has dissolved sin, now he has quenched death and subjected the
devil, he has taken the captives. Of those triumphs therefore we keep the
present day as memorials. On this account the fathers now legislated that the
royal gifts be distributed; for this is the law of triumphs. So also the kings
outside do; they honor the days of the triumphs with many honors. But the
manner of that honor is full of dishonor; for what honor are theaters and the
things done and said in theaters? Is not everything filled with shame and much
laughter? But this honor is worthy of the munificence of the one who honored.
On this account they now legislated, that they may remind you through the
season of the victory of the Master, that in the triumphs there may be some
wearing the bright garments and entering unto the honor of the king; and not
for this alone, but that =158= you may also share with the Master according to
the season. He was crucified, he says, on the wood; you be crucified through
baptism; for the cross, he says, is also baptism and death, but a death of
sin, but a cross of the old man.

Listen then what Paul says, how he says both things concerning baptism, that
it is a death of sin and the cross: "Or do you not know that as many as were
baptized into Christ, were baptized into his death?" And again: "Our old man
was crucified with him, that the body of sin might be abolished." Therefore
lest, having heard death and having heard cross, you be afraid, he added that
the cross is a death of sin. Did you see how baptism is a cross? Learn that
Christ also called the cross baptism, giving you in return and taking in
return the name of baptism. Your baptism he called a cross; my cross, he says,
I call baptism. And where does he say this? "I have a baptism to be baptized
with, which you do not know." And from where is it clear that he speaks
concerning the cross? The sons of Zebedee came to him, or rather "the mother
of the sons of Zebedee" saying: "Say that these my two sons may sit, one on
the right and one on the left in your kingdom." The request is of a mother,
even if thoughtless. What then does Christ say? "Are you able to drink the cup
which I am about to drink, and the baptism which I am baptized to be baptized
with?" Do you see that he called the cross baptism? From where is this clear?
"Are you able," he says, "to drink the cup which I am about to drink"? He
calls the passion a cup, and on this account he says: "Father, if possible,
let this cup pass from me." Did you see how he called the cross baptism and
the passion a cup? And he called it so, =159= not because he himself was being
cleansed (for how, "who did no sin nor was guile found in his mouth"?), but
because the blood that flowed from there cleansed the whole inhabited world.
On this account Paul also says: "If we have become grown-together in the
likeness of his death through baptism." He did not say "in the death," but "in
the likeness of his death." For that is death and this is death, but not of
the same thing; for that is of a body, but this of sin; on this account a
likeness of death.

What then? Do we only die with the Master and share only in the gloomy things?
And yet not even this is gloomy, to share in the Master's death. But wait a
little and you will see yourself also sharing in the good things; "for if we
died with him," he says, "we believe that we shall also live with him." For
together both burial and resurrection is in baptism at the same season; he
leaves below the old man and takes the new and rises, "as Christ was raised
through the glory of the Father." Do you see how again he speaks of the
resurrection? But why is our resurrection and the burial and the death in the
same season (for together we are both buried and rise), but that of the Master
delayed? For in three days he was raised. Why then is our resurrection sudden,
but the Master's slower? Yes, usefully, that you may learn that the slowness
is not through weakness; for he who was able to raise the slave in a brief
turn of time was much more able to raise himself. For what cause then the
slowness? For what cause the three-day burial? That, death having delayed,
even through the slowness the proof of the resurrection might become
undisputed. For where even now after so great a proof there are men =160=
saying that he suffered in appearance, if such slowness had not taken place,
what would those not have said? For the devil wished to plot not only against
the word of the resurrection, but also against the faith of the death; for he
knew, he knew clearly that the saving death was a common medicine of the
inhabited world and he was eager to take this out of the faith of men, that he
might destroy the salvation. On this account the Master delays in the
resurrection and the Jews come saying: "Give us soldiers, that we may keep the
tomb."

O the shamelessness; when did you see a dead man, O Jew, being kept? For if
the one crucified was a common dead man and a mere man, why do you do strange
and paradoxical things? Why do you fear and tremble and gather guards? Yet not
even this did God hinder, but he allowed him to be guarded, that the sinner
might be caught in the works of his own hands. For they said: "Give us
soldiers, lest his disciples steal him and say that he rose." But the opposite
happened; for they took soldiers, that they might not say, when he had risen,
that his disciples stole him and he did not rise, and the things being
contrived against the resurrection became for the resurrection, and Christ
made the plotters themselves witnesses of the resurrection, that he might cut
out their defense on that day.

For what cause then the fathers commanded that we be initiated at this season,
we have sufficiently (I at least think) set before you through the things
said. But I wish also to pay you another debt, if you have not grown weary
toward the hearing, and to say for what cause we send you from here naked and
unshod toward the voices of those who exorcize; for here too the same cause
will appear again, that the king, having conquered a war, took captives. And
such is the form of the captives. Listen then what =161= God says to the Jews:
"In the manner in which my servant Isaiah went naked and unshod, so shall the
sons of Israel go into the captivity naked and unshod." Wishing then to remind
you of the former tyranny of the devil, through the form he sends you toward
the memory of the old ignoble birth. On this account you have not only stood
naked and unshod, but also having the hands upturned, that you may also
confess the lordship of God after these things, to which you now come. You are
all spoils and booty; and of these spoils Isaiah makes mention from afar
before the outcome of the things, proclaiming beforehand thus: "He himself
will divide the spoils of the strong." And again: "He came to proclaim release
to captives." And David with this, prophesying this captivity, was saying:
"You went up on high, you took captivity captive." But do not be gloomy
hearing captivity; for nothing is more blessed than this captivity. For the
captivity of men leads from freedom into slavery, but this transfers from
slavery into freedom; and that of men deprives of fatherland and leads toward
the foreign land, but this captivity casts out from the foreign land and leads
toward the fatherland, the Jerusalem above. The captivity of men deprives of a
mother, but this leads you toward the mother, the common one of us all. That
also separates from kinsmen and citizens, but this leads toward the citizens
above; for "you are fellow-citizens," he says, "of the saints." The form then
has this account.

And for what cause the voices of the exorcists, those fearful and dreadful
ones, remind you of the common Master, of the punishment, of the vengeance, of
gehenna? On account of the shamelessness of the demons. For the catechumen is
an unsealed sheep and a deserted inn and a doorless lodging laid open simply
to all, =162= a raid of robbers, a refuge of beasts, a dwelling of demons.
Since then it seemed good to the king through much love of mankind that this
deserted inn, the doorless, the refuge of robbers, become royal courts, on
this account he sent us who teach and those who exorcize, preparing beforehand
the lodging. And we who teach make the walls that are rotten strong through
the teaching; "for everyone," he says, "who hears these my words and does
them, shall be likened to a prudent man, who built his house upon the rock."
Let us lay down sure foundations, until the king arrives. If somewhere we see
some filth and mire, we draw it out; for such is the custom of sin,
foul-smelling and unclean. Listen then how David speaks its nature: "As a
heavy burden they were heavy upon me; my bruises stank and rotted from the
face of my folly." We draw out the foul smell and set the spiritual myrrh, but
those who exorcize through those fearful voices look around, lest somewhere a
beast, lest somewhere a snake, lest somewhere a viper, lest somewhere a
scorpion; for after hearing that fearful voice, even if the beast be grievous,
it cannot sink down nor nest, but it comes up and runs away even unwilling.

I wished to say also another thing, which I did not promise to say; but it was
necessary to say, for what cause we are faithful, but the uninitiated
catechumens. For it is truly shameful and ridiculous, receiving an honor, not
even to know the name of the honor. But what am I to suffer? The care of oaths
has stood over me again and accused slowness and draws the word toward itself.
On this account, putting off these words unto the coming day, let us now turn
toward the exhortation concerning oaths. Terrible is the oath, beloved,
terrible and harmful; a destructive drug, a grievous poison, a hidden =163=
wound, an unseen sore, a shaded spreading that brings the venom upon the soul;
a satanic dart, a javelin set on fire, a two-edged sword, a sharpened
broadsword, a sin without pardon, a lawlessness having no defense, a deep pit,
a sheer cliff, a strong snare, a net stretched out, a bond unable to be
loosed, an inescapable noose. Are these enough, and do you believe that the
oath is terrible and more grievous than all sins? Be persuaded by me, be
persuaded, I beseech; but if anyone disbelieves, I already furnish the proof
also; for what no other sin possesses, this this sin has. For the other
commandments, not transgressing, we are released from punishment, but the
oath, often both keeping and transgressing, we are punished alike. Perhaps you
did not understand the thing said? Therefore it is necessary to say it again
more clearly. Often someone swore to do a lawless deed and fell into an
unloosed noose; for it is necessary thereafter either, having kept the oath,
to act lawlessly, or, not having kept the oath, to be caught in the charge of
perjury; and from both sides the cliff has become deep, from both sides the
death inexorable both for those who keep the commandment and for those who do
not keep it. Is there anything more destructive than this transgression, when
it is kept and is not kept?

And that you may learn that this is so and that many often not only having
transgressed the oath, but also having kept the oath, made themselves liable
to punishment, I will tell you such a narrative. Herod once kept a birthday
and was celebrating the day on which he was born; and wishing to make it
bright he brought in the daughter of the queen to dance, not knowing that he
rather shamed it; for it was necessary to give thanks to the God who loves
mankind, that, he not being, he made him, that he gave a soul, that he brought
him into this solemn theater of creation, that he made him =164= a spectator
of this most fair and wondrous workmanship, it was necessary to honor the day
with hymns and thanksgivings toward the Master, but he honored it with
dishonor. For what is more dishonorable than dancing? On this day the daughter
of Herodias danced. Hear, both men and women, as many as honor your own feasts
with such dances and such songs; these evils are not small, even if they seem
indifferent; for on this account they are great evils, since they seem to be
indifferent; for they do not enjoy much providence; for the great disease,
even meeting with care, is quenched, but that which seems to be small, being
despised on this very account, becomes great. What do you say? Does someone
dare to bring a dance into the house of a faithful man and not fear lest a
thunderbolt brought down from above burn up everything? These things I say
also toward the women, that they may also chasten the men and lead them away
from such delight. On this day the daughter of the queen also entered and
danced. Blessed be God; toward how much sobriety he has changed our life.
Hear, you faithful, to what sort of bridegroom you come, who adorned our life
that was unseemly before this with shamefastness and sobriety and gravity; for
the things which the queen then was not ashamed to do, now a cheap little
servant-girl would not choose to endure. She danced then, and after the dance
she did another more grievous sin; for she persuaded that foolish one to
confess to her with an oath to give whatever she should ask. Do you see how
the oath also makes men foolish? Whatever she should ask, he simply swore to
give. What then, if she asked your head? And what if the whole kingdom? Yet he
was aware of none of these things. For the devil stood by making the snare
strong, and =165= from the time he completed the oath and set the snare and
stretched the net everywhere, then he brings on that request, that the catch
might become inescapable. "Give me," she says, "on a platter the head of John
the Baptist." Shameless the request, foolish the giving and harmful. Of both
of these the oath is the cause. What then was it necessary to do? Remember
what I was saying, that both keeping the commandment and transgressing we are
punished alike. Was it necessary to give the head of the prophet? But the
punishment is unbearable. But not to give? But the charge of perjury will
await. Did you see how from both sides the cliff? "Give me," she says, "here
on a platter the head of John the Baptist." O the accursed request; yet she
persuaded and thought to muzzle that sacred tongue, but it cries even until
now; for every day, or rather every church, you hear John crying through the
Gospels and saying: "It is not lawful for you to have the wife of Philip your
brother." She cut off the head, but she did not cut off the voice; she muzzled
the tongue, but she did not muzzle the rebuke.

See what the oath does; it cuts off heads of prophets. Did you see the bait,
fear the destruction; did you see the net, do not fall in. But it is necessary
hereafter to spare, so that the cut may not become deeper; it is necessary
hereafter to hold back the hand and the bloodied iron and to lay the word away
from the wounds of perjury into silence. Remember this then and you will never
sin, that both keeping the oath and not keeping it you are punished alike.
Where now are those who say, "If I swear upon a just thing"? For where is this
just, when there is a transgression of law? Where just, when God forbids, and
you do it? But bear with us hereafter as we bind up the wounds; for the
bandage also has something painful. For grievous is both the punishment of
perjury and =166= of true-swearing even before our teaching, but more grievous
also after our teaching. "For if I had not come," he says, "and spoken to
them, they would not have sin; but now they have no pretext concerning the
sin." This it is possible to say also upon you, that there is not even one
defense left for you who transgress. Now then baptism, even if it find
perjury, even if true-swearing, even if fornication, even if adultery, even if
all wickedness, washes it off and cleanses with much exactness; and may it be
that also hereafter you keep this purity, being released from every stain, and
that we be able to share some boldness by your prayers; for it is permitted
you also to pray henceforth on behalf of teachers. For after a little you are
about to appear to us from the heavens, shining more brightly than the stars
themselves. May it be then by your prayers that we all share boldness before
the judgment-seat of Christ, through whom and with whom to the Father be the
glory together with the Holy Spirit, now and always and unto the ages of ages.
Amen.
