* Letter on the Trisagion Hymn
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Letter of the blessed John, monk and presbyter, written to Jordan the
archimandrite concerning the Trisagion hymn. To the God-honored lord Jordan
the archimandrite, adorned with divine zeal, John. 1 The height of my longing
toward you, O best of fathers, does not have its occasions from an unstable
pretext, but from divine love, which is not wont to fall. For we know the
orthodox character of your faith and the right-cutting of the word of truth,
the fiery quality of your zeal, the unmixed purity of your love toward God,
from which and through which you are bound to your neighbor; for the one is as
a first fountain, and the other is second and a token of the first. For love
toward the neighbor is able to characterize the desire toward God. We know
your longing for and delight in the words of God, which is reckoned by you
sweeter than honey; for you have envisioned the divine beauty and have flown
up to it on wings and have become an unstained mirror of God and of the divine
manifestations, having virtue as beauty and becoming, by its most exact lines,
an unlying impression of the divine beauty as of the archetype of every good.
Therefore longing for you we long for virtue and for God, from whom your
advantages of virtue are. But, woe is me, O father, woe is me. How the enemy
of the church is always in haste to throw it into confusion, and what is
indeed hard, if it be through those nurtured in piety. For our most
God-fearing brother, abba Sergius the townsman and familiar and friend, has
reported to us by letters, through his genuine brother the most holy abba Job,
that our most sacred father, the lord abba Anastasius, the renowned superior
of Euthymius the blessed, has brought forward certain sayings of the holy
fathers as though referring the Trisagion hymn to the Son alone. Which being
granted, nothing will prevent the blight that entered badly from the fuller
from utterly ruining all. But I have marveled, I have marveled greatly, if
these things are so. And again I have marveled, if these things are not so;
for we know the orthodox character of the venerable man and we are not
ignorant of the seemliness of his in word and in virtue according to practice
[.....] I mean of the lord abba Anastasius. Nevertheless we also know clearly
the unlying character of the accuser. For longing for God and living for him
he would not, I think, so lie, and be a slanderer of a guiltless man who even
shines with the advantages of virtue. Which has not moderately tortured our
reasoning. And he has pressed the charge most of all through the most devout
abba Job, his genuine brother, and has pressed us without any delay or
postponement to signify our opinion about this. And he has written this also,
that he has confirmed that we too have submitted to this opinion, having
become consenting and of one vote with him about this, which I have marveled
at more and more (for he brought forward the sayings to us, I will not lie,
and we declared that they are of the holy and select fathers; but we did not
consent that the sayings show the Trisagion hymn to be referred to the Son
alone) and that the thrice-blessed patriarch of the holy city of Christ our
God, John, the most divine and the man of God, has been of the same mind with
him; for this reason he has written that he declared it. We therefore thought
it necessary to make the matters about these things quite clear to


your God-honored holiness and how we are of opinion, so that it may be
ignorant of none of our things. For this is dear and customary to us. 2 We say
then that the definitions and the allotments that come about by a fitting word
are most firm. For the divine Scripture says that "God brought all the animals
to Adam to see what he would call them. And whatever Adam called it, a living
soul, this was its name." We therefore see names that separate species from
species and names imposed that separate hypostasis from hypostasis, and
definitions and laws laid down and prevailing until now. Thus I say that the
one trihypostatic Godhead and lordship has been definitionally revealed to
Isaiah from God through the seraphim and through the Trisagion hymn as through
a certain arithmetical manifestation. For why did they not hymn "holy" twice
or four times or ten thousand times, and why did the seraphim not utter "Lord
of Sabaoth, the heaven and the earth are full of his glory" twice or thrice or
four times or ten thousand times, but "holy" thrice, "holy, holy, holy," and
"Lord" monadically, either showing three holy hypostases, and one lordship of
the three and one beginning and glory and essence and Godhead, and that the
three hypostases are one holy God, holy the Father, holy the Son, holy the
Holy Spirit, but one holy, not three holies, one Lord, not three lords? For
the three hypostases are God and God and God, but one God and not three Gods;
holy, holy, holy, but one holy and not three holies; Lord and Lord and Lord,
but one Lord and not three lords. Therefore he also added: "The heaven and the
earth are full of his glory," and not of theirs. The Godhead then, that is the
essence, is unfolded triadically, undividedly, being known in three
hypostases, and the three hypostases are united and joined into one essence,
that is Godhead. And the Godhead is sanctified and glorified triadically,
"holy, holy, holy," and the three hypostases are doxologized monadically,
"Lord of Sabaoth; the heaven and the earth are full of his glory." For there
is one God in three hypostases, and the three are one, that is one nature, not
one hypostasis, and each of the hypostases is not doxologized and sanctified
triply. For the Godhead, being a triad of hypostases, is sanctified
triadically as is proper and fitting; holy the Father, holy the Son, holy the
Holy Spirit, Lord of Sabaoth. For the three are one God and Lord. But each of
the hypostases, being one hypostasis and not three, for what reason will it be
sanctified thrice and not once or twice or four times or ten thousand times?
For each of the hypostases of the Godhead is not a triad; for thus the Godhead
would be an ennead and not a triad. For why did they not hymn a twice-holy or
four-holy or ten-thousand-holy hymn, but a trisagion, according to the number
of the hypostases? For just as we say the Father a hypostasis and the Son a
hypostasis and the Holy Spirit a hypostasis and the Godhead, that is the
essence, trihypostatic as containing the three hypostases, and it is not
lawful to say either the Father trihypostatic or the Son trihypostatic or the
Holy Spirit trihypostatic, so neither is one of the hypostases trisagion, but
holy the Father, holy the Son, holy the Holy Spirit, and trisagion the
trihypostatic Godhead. If then the Trisagion hymn is not significative of the
three hypostases, let us hymn "holy" twice and add "Lord of Sabaoth." But it
is not lawful, nor did the seraphim so hymn. Let us not sophistically overdo
extras, nor seem to be wiser than the seraphim. 3 And if the God-bearing
fathers, whose word is law, should say that they hymn the Son "holy, holy,
holy," if they said that they hymn the Son alone, it would have suspicion also
that the Trisagion hymn had been said of the Son alone. But if the Son is
inseparable from the Father and the Spirit, the hymn also is inseparable; for
the Son is co-worshipped with the Father


and with the Spirit and is co-hymned and co-glorified. Then we say that a hymn
is said of the Son alone, when we say the things indicative of his hypostasis,
such as "the Son of God," "the Word," the wisdom of God and power, the one
begotten from the Father, the one incarnate, the one crucified in flesh, the
one risen, the one who went up into the heavens, the one sitting at the right
hand of the Father, and such things. As therefore these are indicative of the
one hypostasis and have no place at all upon another hypostasis or upon the
common of the Godhead, so also the Trisagion hymn, being indicative of the
three hypostases, has no place upon one of the hypostases. For just as we call
each hypostasis of humanity a man, and we say the city containing many men is
many-manned, but we cannot say each man many-manned, so also upon the
trisagion we call each of the hypostases holy, but trisagion the trihypostatic
Godhead, and we cannot say one of the hypostases trisagion; and just as
counting we say one and one and one and cannot say upon each thrice-numbered,
so also upon the trisagion; and just as we say light and light and light and
joining them we say a thrice-shining brightness, but we do not say each
thrice-shining, so also upon the trisagion. All things that are said unitarily
and simply and absolutely upon the whole Godhead are said also upon each of
the hypostases, but the number three upon the Holy Trinity is declarative of
the three hypostases. For well and very seasonably the God-bearing Epiphanius
said that "why do the seraphim not say "3holy\"3 twice or four times, but
thrice, if not because they indicate the number of the hypostases united into
one lordship and Godhead?" "Holy God" also Father, from whom God the Son and
God the Holy Spirit, "holy strong" the Son the hypostatic power of God, "holy
immortal" the Holy Spirit the life-giving, as the apostle says: "The law of
the Spirit of life." Not that the Son or the Holy Spirit is not God, nor that
the Father or the Holy Spirit is not strong, nor that the Father or the Son is
not immortal, but, as the apostle, wishing to show the hypostases, says: "But
for us one God and Father, from whom are all things, and one Lord Jesus
Christ, through whom are all things, and one Holy Spirit, in whom are all
things," so also here. And this David also proclaimed beforehand saying: "As
the hart longs for the springs of the waters, so my soul longs for you, O
God." See, having spoken the Godhead commonly, he added also, wishing to
signify the hypostases: "My soul thirsted toward the strong God, the living,"
that is the immortal, the always living; for the always living is immortal.
Having said: "My soul longs for you, O God," he signified the one Godhead.
Then having said: "My soul thirsted toward the strong God, the living," he
showed the triadic of the hypostases. And again he returned to the unitary of
the nature: "When shall I come and appear to the face of God?" 4 And if
someone should say that often even upon one hypostasis "thrice-blessed" and
"thrice-longed-for" and "thrice-blessed" is said, let him know that "three,"
when it is written through the diphthong ει, signifies the triadic number, but
when through a bare iota, it is set instead of "often," as we say three men
and "there are three that bear witness, the water and the blood and the
Spirit," and thrice through the iota adverbially instead of often, as the
apostle says: "Concerning this I besought the Lord thrice," instead of often.
And it must be known that upon the triadic number, when it is said
adverbially, it is written through a bare iota, such as once and twice and
thrice and four times. And in composition too, whether it signifies the
triadic or the much number, it is written through the iota; three-named the
one having three


names, thrice-longed-for the much-longed-for, thrice-blessed the often
blessed, thrice-happy the often happy. And these have place also upon one
much-longed-for and much-happy and much-blessed person. But the significative
of the triadic number cannot be said except upon the number itself properly,
as it is impossible to call one not having two names two-named and one not
having three colors three-colored. When therefore we say three-named, we show
that the one addressed as three-named has three names only. If we say
trisagion, it has place upon the Holy Trinity; for having three hypostases
only, each being holy and hymned, it would properly be called trisagion. But
if it should be said upon one hypostasis, someone would suppose either that it
possesses three holy essences or three holy hypostases; of which either is
unlawful and impious to say upon one of the hypostases of the Godhead. And
this also we would say, that it is absurd to assign the Trisagion hymn, being
manifestative of the three hypostases of the Godhead, to one of the same
hypostases. For if it were significative of the common of the essence, it
would be said upon each of the hypostases; but being significative of the
number of the three hypostases it is not timely to say it upon one, lest we
declare the Holy Trinity nine-holy. For the number three multiplied upon
itself is wont to generate the number nine. 5 We said that the one possessing
two names is called two-named, and three-named is the one having three names
and neither more nor fewer. Why then is the Father only holy a third time, or
the Son, or the Holy Spirit, or is the Father alone trihypostatic or the Son
alone or the Holy Spirit alone, that each might be called trisagion? May it
not be. But also infinitely infinitely. But the holy Godhead, as having three
holy hypostases only, would reasonably be called trisagion. So that one must
not say the Trisagion hymn upon one of the hypostases, it being indicative of
the three hypostases of the Godhead; for it is not possible to say a defined
number upon the undefined Godhead except a monad of essence, and a triad of
hypostases. If someone should ask us, in return for what we make the immersion
a third when baptizing, what else would we say, than that according to each of
the hypostases of the Godhead one immersion, and three because of the triple
of the hypostases, and not three immersions according to each of the
hypostases, or thus we shall make nine immersions, which is unlawful?
Therefore according to each of the immersions we add the name of one of the
three hypostases. Thus also, if we are asked, for whose sake "holy" is said a
third time and not twice or four times, we would say, speaking truly, because
of the trihypostatic Godhead. How then would we say the Trisagion hymn upon
one of the hypostases? For we establish ourselves as liars and transgressors
of our words; "each of the hypostases of the Godhead," as the divine Basil
says, "is announced monadically." One Father is the Father and one holy,
because one hypostasis; one trisagion nature is the Godhead, because three
hypostases. The Father is not trisagion, because one hypostasis; the Son is
not trisagion, because one hypostasis; the Holy Spirit is not trisagion,
because one hypostasis. The Holy Spirit and all-holy the whole choir of the
God-bearing and God-inspired fathers always uttered with every breath. But
trisagion this we have not yet even now found in any discourse of the sacred
fathers. This is the twaddle of the fuller, who from very much madness dared,
with bare head as the saying is, which indicates the shamelessness of unchaste
women, as supposedly wiser than the seraphim and higher in initiation, to full
the Trisagion hymn as some unlovely weaving. For if we shall say the Trisagion
hymn of the Son alone, the ambiguous is dissolved without doubt, and let us
become Maronites, let us add the crucifixion to the Trisagion. But may it not
be mine to bring this through the lips, God being propitious, death is more
choiceworthy. Take from me this


death. "Death is in this pot." 6 And if someone shall say: What I have heard
many saying, that for whose sake saying "glory to the Father and to the Son
and to the Holy Spirit" we often add "to the one crucified by will and risen
from the dead" or some other such thing, but saying the Trisagion we do not
bring on "who was crucified for us," we shall say to him that the customs and
the traditions of the church must prevail. For "do not move," it says,
"eternal bounds, which your fathers set." And "everything that differs by
antiquity is venerable," the great Basil declared. There is then a custom in
the church handed down from above that "glory to the Father and to the Son and
to the Holy Spirit" is said in the rank of a text. As therefore saying a text
of a psalm perhaps or of an ode we often add a troparion melody not holding to
the sense of the text, so, when we say in the rank of a text upon "glory to
the Father and to the Son and to the Holy Spirit," we add a troparion fitting
the Son alone, not holding to the sense or the syntax of "glory to the Father
and to the Son and to the Holy Spirit." For "glory to the Father and to the
Son and to the Holy Spirit" is said toward the dative, but the melody often
toward the genitive or accusative or nominative or vocative. And from this it
is clear that the melody does not hold to the sense of "glory to the Father
and to the Son and to the Holy Spirit." But when we say "glory to the Father
and to the Son and to the Holy Spirit" alone not as in the rank of a text, we
by no means add the cross or any of the things defining of the hypostasis of
the Father alone or of the Son or of the Holy Spirit. "Holy God" the Father,
"holy strong" the Son who was incarnate and crucified in flesh and rose, "holy
immortal" the Holy Spirit. Having said the hypostases and wishing to show that
the three hypostases are one God I join and say: "Have mercy on us," as God
says legislating to Moses: "The Lord, God, the Lord is one," and the seraphim
"holy, holy, holy, Lord of Sabaoth." God the Father, God the Son, God the Holy
Spirit, but one God, said the God-speaking fathers, and holy the Father and
holy the Son and holy the Holy Spirit, but the three are one holy, and Lord
and Lord and Lord, but one Lord. But God and God and God the Father or God and
God and God the Son or God and God and God the Holy Spirit or holy, holy, holy
or Lord, Lord, Lord, upon one of the hypostases none yet of our approved
fathers has proclaimed. For just as it is impossible to say the Trinity one
hypostasis, so it is not holy to say any of the hypostases a Trinity. Thus
also upon Proclus the thrice-blessed they hymned the Trisagion hymn from a
divine revelation, "holy God, holy strong, holy immortal, have mercy on us,"
when they say the child was snatched from the midst of the multitude of those
processing and was initiated into the hymn having come to be in the air from a
certain angelic power and the ceasing of the visitation became a testimony of
the divinity of the hymn. Thus also the most divine band of the six hundred
thirty, inspired by the divine Spirit, hymned. And what is this new and empty
teaching that now throws into confusion the people of God and disorderly
disturbs? 7 But someone perhaps will say again: Why saying hypostasis and
hypostasis and hypostasis we say three hypostases, but saying God and God and
God we do not say three Gods? To which we shall answer, that the particular
things are not made common, but the common things, even if they are
participated by each of the particulars, yet unitarily and commonly, and each
hypostasis is contemplated individually, and there is not one common
hypostasis of them. But the Godhead and the sanctification and the lordship,
even if they are participated by each of the hypostases, yet are common of the
three hypostases, being one. For there is not another Godhead of the Father
and another of the Son and another of the Holy Spirit, nor sanctification or
lordship, but one and the same


being wholly and undividedly in each of the hypostases, all of the Father, all
of the Son, all of the Holy Spirit, one of Father and Son and Holy Spirit. But
the hypostases not so, but another of the Father, another of the Son, another
of the Holy Spirit. Each hypostasis then by itself is called God and holy and
Lord and whatever such; but when they are numbered together, not three Gods or
holies or Lords, but the three one God, one Lord, one holy, because God and
his powers, Word and Spirit, one God, one holy, one Lord, and not three. But
God and God and God because of the perfection of each hypostasis, and holy and
holy and holy and Lord and Lord and Lord, but not three Gods or holies or
Lords. Because the Word and the Spirit are powers of the Father, but
hypostatic powers inseparable from the Father, from whom and in whom they are
and to whom as cause they are referred; for they are not separated by place or
will or energy or strength or authority or by any of as many things as are
said upon God, except only by the hypostases and the characteristic properties
of each hypostasis. For we being begotten are cut through and through from the
fathers and are separated, and showing this the Theologian Gregory said:
"Leaving and being left." For among us the Son does not stand as power of the
father nor word or wisdom, but upon God the two run together, both that the
Son and the Spirit are powers of the Father, as word of mind and spirit, and
perfect hypostases, which is fitting to the supersubstantial God alone. But we
are cut also by judgment and by power and by place. Therefore we also are
called men, not man, this one and that one; but upon God not so, as we have
said before. But come, let us also inspect the sayings they bring forward and
make the exact examination of them. For these are paternal, and we shall by no
means contradict. But it is necessary to show the holy fathers and teachers as
concordant and not contrary to one another, and no less not contradictory and
fighting against themselves, whose mind about the faith the one power and
illumination of the Spirit showed unitary and unchanging. 8 Athanasius then of
blessed end, the marvelous, in the discourse concerning incarnation and
Trinity says these things: God then is the one begotten and become man, and we
say that God is the one who became man for us. But not God and God and God the
one of the Trinity, the Son of God and holy God, but not holy and holy and
holy, nor thrice only holy, but indefinitely and infinitely holy and
superholy. And again elsewhere he says: "We saw him, and he had no form nor
beauty." For he had beheld him before this in the divine and glorious form
sitting upon a high and lifted throne, when the cherubim were glorifying him
saying: "Holy, holy, holy, Lord of Sabaoth," and after these he sees him
"having taken the form of a slave" and "having become in the likeness of men."
These they bring forward, which we also embrace. But let them say: Did he see
the Son only sitting upon a throne or sitting together with the Father?
Glorified only or with the Father and the Spirit? If then only, let them allot
to the Son alone also the Trisagion hymn. But if with the Father and the
Spirit, let them recognize that the praise expressive of the three hypostases
and of the one essence and lordship belongs to the Trinity as inseparable. For
"holy, holy, holy," the seraphim were hymning and offering the hymn to the
Trinity they hymn the Son; for the Son is one of the Trinity. But those
assigning the Trisagion hymn to the Son alone set the Father and the Spirit as
without share of the hymn. For the common things include the particular, but
the particular no longer the universal. For the one who said man signified all
the men according to part, but the one who said Peter indicated him only,
leaving the remaining hypostases of humanity. 9 Again they put forward of
Saint Epiphanius from the discourse on the holy Theotokos:


The blessed among women who bore the Word incarnate from you, Word the Son of
the Father, Word the pre-eternal God, Word the beginningless and everlasting,
Word the consubstantial of the inseparable Trinity, Word the one worshipped
with Father and Holy Spirit, Word the co-throned of the Father, Word the one
sitting upon the cherubim, Word whom the four-formed living beings glorify
saying: "Holy, holy, holy, Lord of Sabaoth; the heaven and the earth are full
of his glory." If his glory is other besides that of the Father, we shall say
the hymn also of him alone. But if the doxology is one and common of the Holy
Trinity, the hymn also is one and common. For saying "holy, holy, holy" they
glorify the Son, but not the Son only, but as one of the Holy Trinity, in the
midst of Father and Spirit. 10 Then of Chrysostom on the Blessed, whose
beginning: From wonders to wonders: Today the king of glory is glorified upon
earth prophetically and shows the earth-born as sharers of the heavenly feast,
that he may show that he himself is Lord of both as being hymned in concert
from either side. Therefore those above, indicating the salvation upon earth,
were singing: "Holy, holy, holy, Lord of Sabaoth, all the earth is full of his
glory," but those below, celebrating together with the heavenly gladness, were
crying: "Hosanna in the highest," "hosanna to the son of David." And who in
his right mind would say that the son of David has been separated from the
trisagion hymnology? For he is one of the Holy Trinity co-glorified with
Father and Spirit, but the Trisagion is not referred to him alone. For the
seraphim saying "holy, holy, holy" did not signify the Father only or the Son
only or the Holy Spirit only, but the trihypostatic Godhead as one trisagion
lordship. The same then was glorified above with Father and Spirit, the
seraphim hymning him not only, but with the Father and the Spirit and crying:
"Holy, holy, holy." Let him also be hymned upon the earth: "Hosanna in the
highest," "hosanna to the son of David," all but being shown through the hymn
as one of the highest hypostases of the Godhead, even if he was named son of
David. 11 And if the God-bearing father should also say that "those above
indicating the salvation upon earth were singing," he speaks very excellently
and most inspiredly. For we know that heaven and earth have always been filled
with the divine glory. For all the works of the Lord bless the Lord and tell
his glory. But since in the heavens the angels were recognizing the glory of
the God who is, but human nature which had obtained the dwelling of the earth
had run aground, and exchanged the glory of the incorruptible God in the
likeness of an image of a corruptible man and of birds and of four-footed
things, the God-bearing man says that the earth has been filled according to
knowledge of those inhabiting the earth with divine glory, which (and not
another) has become salvation for those upon earth. And the divine glory is
clear, that it is of Father and Son and Holy Spirit. For thus also the unlying
Lord said to the Father: "Father, the work which you gave me, I have
completed; I have manifested your name," and again: "This is eternal life,
that they may know you the only true God and Jesus whom you sent." Thus, as
the nature, so also the glory is common of the three hypostases of the
Godhead. 12 Further of Saint Cyril of Alexandria from the sixth discourse
concerning the Holy Trinity, whose beginning: Of equality then: Isaiah then
the marvelous says that he has not beheld the Son in lesser glory. For "I
saw," he said, "the Lord of Sabaoth sitting upon a high and lifted throne and
the house was full of his glory. And seraphim stood round about him; six wings
to the one and six wings to the one, and with two


they were covering the face and with two they were covering the feet and with
two they were flying, and they were crying one to the other," the
sanctification in a triple beginning, but ending into a henad and into one
lordship. See, he said clearly that they were hymning "the Lord of Sabaoth,"
who is one God glorified in three hypostases, they were saying the hymn, "the
one in a triple beginning," that is the hymn triple in beginning of the three
hypostases, "but ending into a henad and into one lordship sanctification,"
that is, that a henad of essence and one lordship shown by the triple
sanctification is the triad of the hypostases. So that "holy, holy, holy" is
indicative of the three hypostases, not of one, and "Lord of Sabaoth"
manifestative of the one Godhead and lordship. 13 Further of the same Cyril of
Alexandria from the ninth discourse concerning the worship in spirit: And the
as-it-were intent and insatiable of the highest powers toward the vision of
God seems to suggest the cherubim always looking upon the mercy-seat in such a
figure. And the prophet Isaiah also writes saying: "I saw the Lord of Sabaoth
sitting upon a high and lifted throne, and seraphim stood round about him,"
and he says six wings had grown to each, and they cover with two the feet, and
with two the faces, and fly with the others. And after a little: The
mercy-seat then is Christ, who even having appeared in flesh is no less God
and Lord by nature and in truth, having the highest powers standing round
about in a circle in slave-fashion, and he himself also. If someone has not
been maimed in the senses of the mind, he would be able to know that "and he
himself" is indicative of his not only having the powers singing in a circle
nor of the Trisagion hymn being sent up to him alone. For to say "and he
himself" through the conjunction "and" shows, as also the Father and the
Spirit; for "and" shows an addition being a connective conjunction.
Confessedly then, the Father having the powers singing in a circle, he said
that Christ himself also with the Father has the same powers singing in a
circle. But if the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit have the powers
singing in a circle "holy, holy, holy," one must not apply to the Son alone
the Trisagion hymn which is most manifestative of the three hypostases. For if

Godhead is in a triad the number three is perfect. For a monad from a

beings the number received this power, that, as the Father is begetter of a
