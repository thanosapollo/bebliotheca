611 Eph 1:3-13 Who has blessed us. He blessed through the Son, he chose
through the Son, he adopted through the Son, he graced through the Son. And
how through the Son? O wonder: through his blood, he says. Did you see the wealth
of grace? Or rather abundance and unspeakable mercy? And not only these
things, but he also made known to us the mystery of his will. Through whom
this? This too through the Son: all things through the Son, the good pleasure
of the Father being present together in all; for in nothing is the paternal
will severed from the beloved Son. And since he treated of the mystery being
made known to us, he again mentions the adoption, saying, through whom we were
also allotted an inheritance; for those who have been adopted are counted
worthy of the inheritance of the adoption, which came to be through the Son
and was predestined, the good pleasure and the paternal will being present
together. And this came to be in order that we also might so conduct ourselves
that it become unto praise of his glory, that is, that our life and conduct be
seen by all as his praised glory and well-pleasing; for that they may see, he
says, your good works, the Lord in the Gospels, and glorify our Father who is
in heaven. For he blessed us not only in every spiritual blessing, but also in
spiritual and heavenly things. Through whom? Through Jesus Christ. How? Even
as he chose us before the foundation of the world: so ancient is our election
and blessing. And unto what did he choose us? Unto being holy and blameless,
honorable and pure in life. Moved from where unto this? From love alone. In
order that what may come to be? That he may adopt us to himself through
Christ; for this also had been predestined. From what working of ours? From
none, except through the good pleasure of his will; for he was well pleased
and willed this, we contributing nothing at all. For how was it possible for
those who had not even somewhere received a beginning of being to contribute?
And what is the gain from this? Unspeakable praise and encomium arises of his
glorified grace in which he graced us. Through whom? Or is it clear that this
too is through his beloved Son. How? Because he himself made our redemption;
for we were captives through our trespasses, and giving his blood as a ransom
for us he redeemed us from the tyranny in which we were held. And how did this
fearful thing come to be? It is not possible to say, except that it is wealth
of grace, abundance of grace, excess; there is nothing else to say. And this
unspeakable mystery concerning us he not only worked, but also manifested it
to us. How? By giving us mind and making us wise 612 unto the grasp of it. All
things are of that one's benefaction, the working concerning us of that
unspeakable mystery, the making it known again to us; for this too is of great
and unspeakable affection and care, to make known to us the mystery of his
will, namely his inmost and hidden purpose. Do you see of what things he counts us
worthy? And this was the mystery of his will in his Son, that is, predestined
through his Son to come to be according to good pleasure. And reasonably he
said again "according to good pleasure," and that it had been purposed to come
to be through the Son; for since, having said it at the beginning, and
gathering causes upon causes in between, he had drawn the hearer far from the
word, again as if reminding him and taking it up, that all things came to be
through the Son by the good pleasure of the Father, he fittingly joins this on
and sets it down. Then these things, he says, unto an economy, that is, upon
unspeakable providence and administration of the times of the consummation,
that is, of the things at the consummation; for as many things as were done
were done because of the glory that will then be revealed to us. Wherefore
also Christ is then manifested through the outcome of the things, summing up
all things in himself. Or that not only the being done of the mystery
concerning us was predestined to come to be through the Son according to the
good pleasure of the Father's will, but also our being counted worthy to learn
this likewise came to be for us through the Son according to the good pleasure
of the Father. For the Son did and suffered all things, that he might adopt,
and he adopted and revealed that he adopted; but also in all these things the
Father was well pleased and willed. Either therefore the divine Paul's saying
a second time "the mystery of his will according to the good pleasure" and so
on is to be taken as was written before, or as it has now been unfolded; for
in either sense it does not stand apart from what is reasonable. Eph 1:14 Who
is the earnest of our inheritance. Who is the Spirit, he says. And he made the
change of the article not from the utterance according to "spirit," but from
the sense from God. For since he said "in spirit," and this is God, he adds,
fittingly and more deeply arranging: who is the earnest and so on. Eph 1:15 By
hyperbaton the faith among you in the Lord Jesus; which you, he says, possess.
613 Eph 1:19-21 613 And what the exceeding greatness. For the greatness
according to excess of his unspeakable power is displayed also unto us who
believe. How? Because having all the senses deadened by the passions and
reason itself buried in them and covered over, he raised and made alive so
that we might receive the radiance and brightness of his faith. Truly this is
the might of his strength. Wherefore he also prays that they understand this
through the wisdom of the Spirit; for if we understand what we have been
benefited, we shall both embrace the gift more and be disposed thankfully
toward the benefactor. And "according to the working which he worked in
Christ," either that of the same strength and working is our rising from the
death of the soul and the rising of Christ and our God from the bodily; or
"according to the working," he says, assigning the cause of our raising, that
is, through which we have believed and through which we rose from the death of
the soul. For the working which he worked in Christ raising him and so on,
this became the cause of our believing and being brought up from the former
error; for he rose, because he died; and he died, because he was incarnate;
and he was incarnate, that he might make us sons. And all these things are of
the same working of the one who raised him from the dead. Therefore "according
to the working," that he may say: through the working of the might of his
strength; for according to that and through that we have both believed and
been saved. Or also "what the exceeding greatness of it unto us who believe"
he says this, that raising Christ he seated our firstfruits above every
principality and power and authority and dominion at his right hand. For even
if from the whole human lump our Word and God assumed our assumed nature,
nevertheless he is firstfruits of those who believe only and follow him, just
as also firstborn among many brothers who believe, but not among all; for not
also among the unbelievers. And when it is taken thus, "and what the exceeding
greatness" and so on, this also most of all presents the greatness according
to excess of him, the raising earthly nature above the heavens and showing it
worshipped by the incorporeal orders. The sense of the saying having been
taken thus, therefore, the meaning of what follows also is consistent and
clear; for from the working which he worked raising Christ, there also came to
be both our firstfruits sitting with the Father and for us the faithful the
being exceedingly honored and exceedingly adorned upon this. 614 And he seated
him at the right hand. Again and in another way he shows the might of the
Father's strength, not only by raising the Lord but also by seating him at his
right hand. Above every principality. i.e. every thing spoken and nameable,
not only that named here but also that able to be spoken and named from there;
for the Son is above all things, even if he has been incarnate. Eph 1:22-23
And he gave him as head. Having said Christ head of the faithful, having said
the faithful his body and his fullness, and many benefits of the philanthropy
and economy concerning us, he adds also a token of his divine and unstained
essence. But of whom are you body? he says; of whom fullness? of whom? Of him
who fills all things. Not by one working and administration, but in all things
he himself created all things. He himself gave breath to the living, he
himself holds together, he himself provides, he himself fills and governs all
things in all matters. Of this one then, so great and of such magnitude, who
spreads out his workings into all things and in all, we have been counted
worthy to be called body and fullness. But if "of him who is filled" were
understood thus, it will be taken as "of him who fills." More consistent
therefore with the wording as those before us understood. Eph 2:1 The apodosis
after much connected construction. And you being, he says, dead in the
trespasses; then having filled the word with many expansions, after a
resumption of the same saying, as: and we being dead in the trespasses, he
gave the sequel at once, he made us alive together with Christ. And much is
his use of this figure. Eph 2:2-5 According to the prince of the authority of
the air. For Satan rules and has authority over the aerial and wicked spirits;
according to whom, that is, according to whose counsel and will you walked and
were deadened and became children of wrath, by the rich mercy of God and the
unsurpassable love we rose and were made alive. God being rich in mercy. For
among those who obey Christ he is inactive, he has no strength, he has no
authority; but those who have made themselves 615 sons and heirs of his
disobedience, these both are worked upon and are ruled by him. And we were
under his authority. How? In doing his wills. But God by wealth of mercy and
surpassing love, as he delivered from there, also raised those who had been
deadened. Eph 2:9-10 Not of works, lest anyone take a pretext of arrogance, as
having been saved from his own works. And yet even if it were possible to be
saved from works, not even so, he says, was it right to fall into arrogance
and boasting. Why? Because we are a workmanship of God. And not this only, but
also created unto doing good; so that even if we were saved from works, we
would be right to refer the cause and the thanksgiving to God, because he
himself created us for this, and not to confirm the things of arrogance toward
ourselves. But when, having been created for good works, we not only did
nothing good, but were also carried off to the contrary, and having been
carried off we did not turn back from ourselves, but by grace and gift of God,
how great a thanksgiving is owed by us to God? And "created for good works" is
at once also of one exhorting and raising up unto good actions, that he may
say: you are debtors to do the good, and the debt is ancient; for you were
created for this. But you also weighted it and increased it with many
interests, paying back nothing but even adding through transgression. There is
need therefore of much zeal and care unto paying the things owed. Eph 2:12 And
atheists in the world. In your conduct and worship according to the present
life; for in the future even unwillingly all will recognize God. And not only,
but also according to the present, as creatures and ones provided for, they
were not atheists. How then atheists? From the conduct according to the world,
from the worship, from the profane choice. Eph 2:14 And the middle wall of the
fence. Double was the enmity and double the middle wall, rather even triple;
for the human was also at war toward angels. But now for the present he treats
of the two, that which those from the nations had toward those from the Jews,
and that which both had toward the God of all. But each enmity and each middle
wall Christ our God dissolved. 616 Eph 2:16 And that he might reconcile both.
Through "in one body" he indicates the reconciliation toward one another, and
through "to God" that of both toward God. Eph 3:1 For this cause I Paul the
prisoner of Christ Jesus on behalf of you the nations. For it is possible to
be a prisoner of Christ, that is, to be bound and punished because of
unchangeable faith toward him, but not also on behalf of the nations, that is,
because of the teaching and the preaching which one preaches among the
nations, as many of the martyrs; for they were punished not because they
taught the nations, but because they did not deny. But Paul for both, both on
behalf of Christ and on behalf of the nations. Eph 3:8 To me the less than the
least of all. "For" is lacking, as: for to me. Or rather nothing is lacking,
but it is the apodosis of "for this cause," as: for this cause to me the less
than the least of all saints was given and so on. And consider that having
begun the period according to the straight figure, in the apodosis he made it
oblique, shaping the apodosis toward the type of the expansions. Thus also
Thucydides and Demosthenes in many places. And "for this cause" he says as:
for the cause of our being built together into a dwelling-place of God. Eph
3:10-11 The manifold wisdom. The manifold wisdom of God
according to the purpose of the ages; for this was both purposed before the
ages and predestined, even if in last times it was made known through the
church. And "which he made," that this too through Christ he both purposed
before the ages and predestined; for nothing without the Son did he either
will or define or make. Eph 3:13 Wherefore I ask not to faint in my
afflictions on your behalf; for the syntax is by hyperbaton. 617 Eph 3:18-19
Rooted in love. "Rooted and founded" is either a scribal error, it being
necessary to write in the accusative "rooted and founded"; or if not this, the
divine Paul used according to the custom of archaic syntax a participial
nominative instead of accusative. And much also among those outside is such a
figure, and especially in Thucydides. And if one must take the nominative as
nominative and not by interchange of case, the sequence will still be
preserved, making the syntax by hyperbaton, which figure the divine Paul
especially appears to have used, as that the saying be arranged thus: that
rooted and founded in love you may be able to comprehend with all the saints
what is the breadth and length and depth and so on. From the common
construction "that you may be able," as: that you may be able to comprehend
and to know. What the breadth and length. Of what the breadth and length and
depth and height? Either of the mystery, so that it has been said by ellipse;
or also without ellipse, but by hyperbaton: that you may be able, he says, to
comprehend what is the breadth and length and depth and height of the
knowledge of Christ, and to know his love that exceeds toward us. And
knowledge of Christ is the manifestation of his economy concerning us. And
knowledge of Christ is, both that he himself and not another worked his
beyond-marvelous act on our behalf, and that he himself is the supplier to us
of this knowledge, and that through it especially we came to know Christ. But
this is knowledge of Christ. And its length is that from the ages. It to have
been predestined. And breadth, that its benefaction is spread out unto all,
unto those before the law, unto those in the law and after the law, unto the
living, unto those who have died, unto the Jews, unto the Greeks, unto the
barbarians, unto all the nations, unto the heavenly, unto the earthly; for
Christ worked peace in them, the angels and the men having formerly been at
war toward one another. He prays therefore that they comprehend these things,
and not these only but also its depth and its height, that as far as the
treasuries of Hades he displayed his unspeakable power and benefaction, having
abolished and taken that one captive, and having rescued and freed the
captives. Then also the height, that he who descended is himself also he who
ascended, that above the heavens 618 he led up our firstfruits, that above
every principality and authority and power and dominion. You see unspeakable
height of the economy of Christ and of his knowledge; these things Paul prays
that they consider, these that they comprehend. And if they comprehend these,
what? Then they will know his love that exceeds toward us. And if we know,
what? Who will contradict that we are debtors to be filled in all the fullness
of God, that is, to receive in through the works the whole of him who so
loved us? Eph 4:3-4 Endeavoring to keep the unity of the Spirit. In being bound
to one another peaceably according to one body and according to one Spirit;
for this is the unity of the Spirit with which that one united us and of which
you were called. Or endeavoring to keep and guard one another as one body and one
Spirit according to the unity of the Spirit, as the Holy Spirit united us, as
also you were called. And the bond of love guards such a union. But if as the
former, the syntax would be only elliptical, lacking the preposition
"according to," as: according to one body and according to one Spirit. And if
as the second, it has three modes, hyperbaton and from the common with zeugma
and third the elliptical, and here too the same preposition being lacking, as:
according to the unity, and "one another" being taken from the common with
zeugma; for "one another" will be understood. And the hyperbaton is evident.
Eph 4:4 Or as being one Spirit and one body; for thus you were called. Eph 4:8
Having ascended on high. And he means that of the cross, as he himself
promised: when I am lifted up, I will draw all to myself. He took the devil
captive, and instead of punishment he gave gifts and charisms to those who had
been taken captive, since he who ascends either in the ascending takes the
beginning of coming to be above, as when someone begins to ascend not being
above from the beginning, or he does not now take the beginning of the ascent
but from the beginning he was above, and having descended from there now again
619 he goes up. And having said "having ascended," he interprets how one must
understand "having ascended": that not as now, he says, beginning he ascends,
for he was from the beginning in the height of the divine dignity and of the
paternal glory, but as having come up again from the condescension according
to the economy and as far as the descent into Hades unto the fitting
manifestation of the divine height, he is said to have ascended. Eph 4:10 The
same, he says, is he who descended and he who ascended; for he descends into
the incarnation as God, and he ascends as man. And he descends into Hades as
man, and he rises as God. And not only, but he also comes to be above the
heavens, and not simply, but counting our assumed nature worthy of all the
portion and seat there. Eph 4:13 Of the fullness. For not just anyone becomes
a part of the whole fullness, that is, of the body of Christ, but he who has
arrived at a perfect man of his knowledge, and at a measure of stature worthy
of the fullness of Christ. Eph 4:16 From whom the whole body, that is, the
faithful, being joined together and compacted makes its own increase unto its
own building and perfection, and not for the sake of some other. And "in
love," because not even any other of the good things is constituted and
perfected apart from love, and especially the unity according to faith and the
joining. But how are the faithful joined and compacted? Through every joint,
he says, lest the one be joined to this one, and the other to that, but all to
all; for through the conjunction of all the common increase of the whole body
comes to be. And this joint and conjunction, from where does it grow? It is of
the supply of Christ, he says; and "supply," because along with his other
benefactions he himself also supplies the conjunction with one another. And
"according to working," because not only the being fitted toward the
conjunction, but also the very being joined is of the same supply. And "in the
measure of each one part," lest having heard that he himself joins us to one
another, he himself supplies also the conjunction according to working, all
things are of his gift and supply; lest 6 having reasoned these things you
yourself decline into slackness as having all things from there freely. He
adds these things, because even if it is supplied freely, nevertheless the
distribution comes to be according to the measure of the eagerness of each one
of those receiving. Eph 4:17-18 In vanity of mind. Not thinking and believing
and accepting the things of the truth, but whatever their mind vainly
fabricates and reasons. And what of the good things would he even reason who
has the understanding darkened? Eph 4:19 They delivered themselves. Either
that they delivered themselves to two passions, wantonness and greed, which
are more prevailing than the other sins; or that they worked the wantonness
and uncleanness simply in greed, that is, according to excess and
unyieldingly. Eph 4:21-23 According to the former. You were taught to put off,
you were taught to put on; for "you were taught" is from the common
construction. The old man. The old man according to the former manner of life.
Eph 5:11-14 And do not have fellowship. He said works of darkness, naming
generally every sin. And why, he says, did I not go through each of their
deeds by name? Because even in the very saying of them he who goes through
them is also filled with shamefulness. Or thus: reprove, he says, by the
setting beside of your own life. For I do not say to reprove them going
through their deeds to them and reviling, for I know that to the earnest even
the wholly remembering of them is reckoned shamefulness; for no less, he says,
than if someone uncovered them through words will they be reproved, your works
flashing with the virtues. But even more all their works through the light of
your life are reproved and uncovered and made manifest, and being made
manifest they are enlightened and are brought over and recast unto the better;
for the manifestation of the hidden things through the virtuous conduct urges
unto condemnation, and the beginning of conversion comes to be. And this 621
ends in the working of virtue, which is light, as procuring the light from
there. And light again, because, formerly being blind in us concerning the
passions, this, having shone upon the ruling part, makes it see clearly the
unseemliness and shamefulness and formlessness in sin. And "for everything
that is made manifest is light," either is to be taken as has been said, or as
constructive of the earnest being able through their own life to reprove the
conduct of the base. For the life of the earnest is made manifest, he says,
and being made manifest it is light, and when light shines the darkness is
reproved, which is the life of the base. But rather even reprove. Or thus:
you yourselves reprove through your bright and earnest life, and do not wait for
those to denounce themselves and to make comedy of their own things and to
bring them into the midst and thus to be bettered. For even if they do what is
not lawful, nevertheless by the inborn knowledge, knowing their shamefulness,
they are ashamed, although it is necessary to uncover their own unseemliness.
For everything that is made manifest. That, he says, what is made manifest and
uncovered becomes light, the usage also witnesses; for "awake," he says, "you
who sleep," that is: rise up from the reclining and attendance toward the
passions. And arise from the dead: allow them, dead and shameful as they are,
to be made manifest. And if these things come to be, then Christ will dawn
upon you and light you and flood you with light, so that what is made manifest
becomes light and is enlightened. Eph 5:17-21 But understanding.
Understanding, walk; for from the common construction, just as also "be
filled" again is from the common toward "speaking" and "singing" and
"psalming" and "giving thanks" it is given, and toward "being subject."
