Of the same, on meekness.

=63.549=

1. I think that you all know clearly that those who hear the oracles of God
intelligently ought to practice a good character and virtue of manners. For
this is that by which the servants of Christ most of all differ from those who
are ignorant of the truth, by living together with kindness and furnishing a
life worthy of piety. For it is nothing unlikely that those who are ignorant
of Christ should be both wicked and depraved in manner; but let the servant of
Christ be named rather from the gentleness of his manners than from the name
set upon him by his parents. For the race of men is not so =63.550= loved by
God from virginity, nor from fasting, nor from despising money, nor from
readily stretching out a hand to those in need, as from gentleness and from
good order of manners; and yet even the ready succour of those in need comes
to all who love Christ from no other source anywhere, but from kindness. And
just as streams from springs, so the care of the poor from the gentleness of
manners.

For every mild and meek man is easily drawn into love of mankind, and =63.551=
would not bear to overlook those who have been set in want, counting the
poverty of others his own misfortune. Envy, then, is the worst of all things;
but a kind and meek man does not admit this wicked disease into the soul, but
seeing his brothers in good repute, he rejoices with them and is glad with
them, counting the good repute of others his own good repute, holding the
things of friends as common, rejoicing with them in good things, and sharing
their pain in grievous things. Such are the gifts of meekness, such is the
life of those who live together in mildness: the meek man is a father of
orphans, a protector of widows, a guardian of poverty, a helper of those
wronged, everywhere making the power of justice prevail. And a father living
together with meekness is venerable to a child, and a child to a father, and a
slave to a master, and nothing else makes the master enjoy the goodwill of his
household. For when they see him taking pleasure in mildness, and conducting
himself meekly toward all, they hold him in much account and marvel, counting
the one who possesses them a father rather than a master. Come then, let us
examine accurately according to our power how many goods come to the mild from
the gentleness of manners.

Therefore the meek man is a friend to those who see him, and a friend also to
those known by hearing; and there is no one who, hearing a gentle man praised,
would not desire to see him and greet him and look upon him continually,
counting the friendship of such a one as a kind of gain. And if some should
differ with one another over money, they hurry to the kind and meek man,
thinking that every strife and contention will quickly be dissolved by that
man's kindness. For even if it should happen that men are hostile toward one
another, the gentleness of the one judging puts to shame those who are easily
carried out into anger and wrath, so that they become meek. But if some others
should appear wishing to bring those who are fighting into friendship, they
would not be able to appear venerable. For in each of the affairs, I think, a
counselor is persuasive when he is the first to take hold of the deeds which
he advised others to hold to. Whenever, then, the teacher of mildness first
appears confirming the precepts, as soon as he is seen he has also tamed those
who are raging, and he needs no admonition and words, but before he utters
anything he has brought in peace. And just as a ray of the sun, having
appeared, quickly drives away the darkness, so the kind and meek man quickly
reshapes the turmoil and the fight into peace and quiet. It is worth marking
this too, then, that Christ says that only those who are peaceful in manner
are sons of God.

For what reason, then, did he share his own appellation with the mild alone?
Because only the one who becomes a cause of friendship and peace to others
imitates the Son of God according to his power. For that one, as God and lord
of all, blended into friendship the holy angels who had been set at war with
men, having appeared to us through his divine flesh; but a kind man persuades
brothers and kinsmen and household who are warring against one another to
reverence the laws of God, and, having thrown off the enmity, to love one
another according to the divine command. And indeed, what the sinews are in
our bodies, this a kind man is in a house and a church and a city. For it is
Paul's custom to name the brothers a body and members of one another; and the
one who brings those who have been torn apart and are fighting into friendship
has confirmed Paul's word, persuading all not to be ignorant of the members,
nor to devour one =63.552= another. Therefore that word is true; so that the
one who becomes a cause of peace to the brothers has the same power in church
and house and city which the sinews have in the bodies, gluing together and
joining those who have been torn apart and are warring against one another.
This power, however, each receives over the others whenever he himself first
appears living together with mildness; but the one who is easily carried out
into anger would seem ridiculous to the many, whenever he attempts to make
those who are wrathful meek. For what could be worse than men enslaved to
anger? And who, seeing such a one, does not turn away his eyes? An irascible
man is unpleasant to those who meet him, and unpleasant to his neighbors, and
if he should fall into need of money, he does not easily find those who
lighten his poverty; he is a common enemy both of citizens and of strangers,
shouting and crying out, aiming blows at those who meet him, saying
things speakable and unspeakable; in such a one the glance is troubled, the
face swells, the tongue rages, the hands are easily carried against all
things, such a one does the works of those possessed by demons; and someone
seeing a man in wrath would be at a loss whether the demon is driving the
wretched man, or whether he has been carried out willingly into such madness.

Anger is a willing demon, a self-chosen madness, a desolation of the mind; the
man in wrath is the devil's plaything, a workshop of demons, a worthy servant
of their wickedness. Tell me, what do the demons most of all wish to make men
do? Is it not to insult parents? Is it not to lay their polluted hands upon
those who begot them? Is it not to blaspheme God? These, then, are all the
works of those in wrath. So that wrath easily becomes a cause of impiety also
to those enslaved to anger; for those who have given themselves to wrath
cannot quickly master themselves; but just as in a storm and a swell of the
sea, when a helmsman is not present, the waves, taking the ship deserted, make
it quickly submerged; so wrath, having fallen upon the soul like some
thunderbolt and storm, quickly makes the one enslaved to it out of his mind
and beside himself. What then? It belongs to men of sound mind to check the
disease beforehand, and before being carried out into anger, to hold the soul
in check, and to put the wrath to sleep by the admonitions from Scripture. For
if the beasts easily yield to the charms of enchanters, why do you marvel if
the oracles of Christ quickly reshape a soul growing savage into calm and
evenness of soul? Let all bitterness and anger and wrath and clamor, he says,
be taken away from you, with all malice; and become kind to one another,
tenderhearted, forgiving one another; if anyone has a complaint against
anyone, even as God in Christ also forgave you. Become therefore imitators of
God. Do you see of what sort the meek man is? Of whom is he named an imitator?
Not of angels, not of archangels, but of the Master of all. And yet those too
are most meek and full of every virtue; but nevertheless Paul named the gentle
emulators of God, in order that through the greatness of the honor he might
train those who hear to be most meek, in order that everyone who is insulted
or suffers some other terrible thing may bear the revilings meekly, and may
master anger, as being about to follow God in mastering anger. For what then
are the things of God? From above he both sees and hears all things, but in
the things said and done upon earth there are many injustices. And there are
some who have come to such a pitch of impiety as even to blaspheme God,
whenever they are grieved by some of their affairs, and looking toward heaven
they refrain from no word; but God en =63.553= dures, and though he has such
power to take vengeance from those who act impiously, he does not take it? And
if God punished all who sin, the part that is saved would be exceedingly
small, and the living would not even suffice for the burials. For this reason
David says: If you should mark iniquities, Lord, Lord, who shall stand?
because with you there is propitiation. But now God shows his own love of
mankind even in this very thing, by refraining even from just punishment.
Whoever therefore wishes to be like God, let him be meek and gentle according
to human power, and let him bear it nobly whenever he suffers some terrible
thing from others.

Love your enemies, he says, do good to those who hate you, pray for those who
abuse you and persecute you, that you may become like your Father who is in
the heavens, because he makes his sun rise upon evil and good, and sends rain
upon just and unjust. There are, then, many virtues that befit Christian men,
but greater than all is meekness; for only those who shine in this does Christ
name emulators of God. It befits us, then, whenever someone insults or strikes
or does some other terrible thing, to endure all things nobly with a quiet
soul, considering that meekness makes imitators of God. For even our Master
and Savior himself, being insulted, receiving blows, being nailed, being
crucified, bore meekly the madness of the Jews, and though he had such power
to take vengeance from those who acted impiously, he did not take it, but he
showed the power in shaking the earth and raising the dead and hiding the sun
and making the day night, and the meekness and love of mankind in punishing
none of those who had acted impiously; in order that all who were laying
impious hands upon the Master might learn that he who so easily shook the
earth and commanded the sun to become suddenly unseen could easily have
punished those who had gone mad; but now, since he is gentle, and bears meekly
the madness of the impious, not he alone gently endured those who were
crucifying, those who were blaspheming, but he also begged the Father not to
use the heavenly darts against the impious. Whenever, then, anger and wrath
stand beside you as you suffer something terrible and unbearable, remember the
meekness of Christ, and you will quickly be gentle and meek, not you alone
gaining the greatest things from gentleness, but also benefiting the enemy and
training him to be kind. For when he sees you suffering the utmost
things meekly, bearing with the anger, he himself also will quickly be mild
and kind, and having thrown off the wrath he will desire to become an emulator
of your gentleness.

Whoever therefore would wish to conquer those in wrath, let him bear the
insults and the blows nobly, and he will see a great change in the one who is
raging and striking; but if he himself also, having been defeated by anger, is
eager to defend himself against the enemy with equal things, there will never
be an end of the anger and the fight, but always raging and desiring to
conquer one another with revilings and with blows, they are carried out into
endless war and fight, plotting against one another, kindling slanders, daring
to swear falsely, practicing to accuse things which they have neither seen nor
heard, by day like dogs raging mad, and seeking the enemy, and spending the
greater part of the night in counseling some evil and stitching together
=63.554= plots. For such men not even sleep is clean of passions, but thinking
that the enemy is present and is reviling and striking, they leap up from
sleep, loving one another's slaughter more than a table; and others, drunk
with anger, hiring slanderers, and furnishing one another with countless
troubles, the more evils they furnish one another, by so much more are they
provoked into madness. For what they say those who are drunk suffer, being
carried out by insatiable drunkenness into a harsher thirst, this those
enslaved to anger suffer, being carried out by measureless contention into a
greater and harsher madness; the one called him a slave, the other a harlot's
son, the one a fool, the other a madman, the one a thief and unjust, the other
filled with every wickedness.

Then, drunk with anger, having grappled with one another, they all but imitate
the beasts, striking, kicking, biting, cutting the bodies to pieces, sparing
no part, not eyes, not ears, not nose, not face, not any other member, but
having dashed together against one another and having cut themselves to
pieces, and what evil have they not done? they withdraw from the fight, some
with an eye knocked out, some with lips, some with some other part of the
body; more corrupted in the soul than in the body, raging, out of their
senses, raving, ignorant of the law of God and Savior, which commands those
who are slapped and struck and suffer the other things to bear all things
nobly with a quiet soul, and to conquer by meekness the wrath of those who do
wrong. For the one who does this is praised by men, and is crowned by God and
Savior. Wherefore one must be steadfast, and gently bear with those who are
angry, looking toward the crown which God prepares for the mild and the kind.
For it is absurd that the athletes of gymnastic contests should bear even dust
and blows and wounds, looking toward empty glory and a crown worth nothing;
but that we who are about to be crowned by Christ should not master anger, nor
bear all things nobly, but should prefer a brief pleasure and an unjust wrath
to the honor and good repute in the heavens. And indeed we know this too, that
though there are many and great things with which the life of the righteous is
adorned, this before the others has made the men illustrious, living together
with kindness and conducting themselves gently toward those who do wrong.

For by this Moses, meekly bearing the madness of the Jews, and saving the
enemies by his prayers, enjoyed before the others the honor and good repute in
the heavens, and was called a friend of God; and by this David, having spared
his enemies and foes, drew God into much goodwill and friendship. For this can
adorn every man, but especially those in authority. For this, when kingship
permits one to do all things, to restrain oneself, and to make the law of God
the guide of one's deeds, is a great thing for good report and glory. And
indeed Paul also and Peter, having touched the heavenly vault through the
virtue with which they lived, shone out from gentleness more than from the
other goods, honoring those who reviled them, doing good to their enemies.
often saving with their prayers those who had stoned them; for they knew that
they were doing this which is most pleasing to God, and which is marveled at
in the heavens before the other goods. Wherefore they themselves appeared
adorning their own life with gentle =63.556= ness, and exhorting the other men
toward this. Since we also, seeing Stephen in the dangers for piety praying
for the enemies, marvel likewise both at the meekness and at the contests.

Since, then, we have so many and such leaders of mildness, let us exhort
ourselves and one another to follow in their steps, and to minister to God
through meekness, as to one who receives this ministry more gladly than all
the other virtues. For the most divine Paul also, wishing to show the virtue
of an apostle, wishes the minister of Christ to appear most of all from
gentleness; and yet he had many things to say, if indeed he wished to speak
the things that adorn an apostle, the driving out of demons, the raising of
the dead; but nevertheless, having passed over all those things, he goes
through the mildness in manners up and down, going through and saying how he
conducted himself gently toward the enemies, in order that he may persuade us
also to follow in his own steps, and to be kind and meek.

It is just, then, to follow those saints, and to make them a pattern of life,
in order that, shining out in all the good things, we may obtain the promised
goods from our great God and Savior, through whom and with whom to the Father
together with the Holy Spirit be all glory, honor, and power unto the ages of
ages. Amen.
