On the birthday of our Savior Jesus Christ, a discourse.
=56.385=
I behold a strange and paradoxical mystery; shepherds resound my ears, not
blowing an empty (sic) pastoral melody, but singing a heavenly hymn. Angels
sing, archangels make melody, the Cherubim hymn, the Seraphim give glory, all
keep festival beholding God upon earth, and man in the heavens; the one above
below by economy, and the one below above by loving-kindness. Today Bethlehem
imitated the heavens; having received in the place of stars angels hymning,
and in the place of the sun having welcomed the Sun of righteousness
uncircumscribably. And seek not how; for where God wills, the order of nature
is overcome. For he willed, he was able, he came down, he saved; all things
run in step with God. Today he who is =56.386= is born, and he who is becomes
what he was not; for being God, he becomes man, not having departed from
being God. For neither has man become man by an ecstasy of deity, nor again
has God become God from man by progress; but being the Word, because he is
impassible he became flesh, the nature remaining unchanged. But when he was
born, the Jews denied the strange birth-giving, and the Pharisees
misinterpreted the divine Books, and the scribes spoke things contrary to the
law. Herod sought the newborn, not that he might honor him, but that he might
destroy him. For today all things saw things contrary.
For he was not hidden, according to the psalmist, from their children unto
another generation. For kings came, marveling at the heavenly king, that how
he came upon earth not having angels, not archangels, not =56.387= thrones,
not lordships, not powers, not authorities, but having walked a strange and
rough road, he came forth from an untilled womb, leaving neither his angels
bereft of his oversight, nor departing from his own deity by his incarnation
toward us; but kings came to worship the heavenly king of glory, and soldiers
to serve the commander of power; the women him born of a woman, that he might
change the sorrows of the woman into joy; the virgins the child of the
virgin, that how the maker of milk and breasts makes the fountains of breasts
flow streams of themselves, took nourishment as an infant from a virgin
mother; the infants him become an infant, that from the mouth of infants and
sucklings he might perfect praise; the children the child having wrought
martyrs because of the madness of Herod; the men him incarnate and having
served the ills of slaves; the shepherds the good shepherd, him who laid down
his soul for the sheep; the priests him become high priest according to the
order of Melchizedek; the slaves him who took the form of a slave, that he
might honor our slavery with freedom; the fishermen him who works hunters of
men from fishermen; the publicans him who made an evangelist from publicans;
the harlots him who yielded his feet to harlot tears; and that I may say
briefly, all sinners came to see the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of
the world, Magi attending, shepherds blessing, publicans evangelizing,
harlots bearing myrrh, the Samaritan woman thirsting for a fountain of life,
the Canaanite woman having faith unshamed.
All things therefore leaping, I also wish to leap, I wish to dance, I wish to
keep festival; and I dance, not striking a lyre, not moving a thyrsus, not
having flutes, not touching torches, but bearing in the place of musical
instruments the swaddling clothes of Christ. For they are hope to me, they
are life to me, they are salvation to me, they are a flute to me, they are to
me a lyre. Wherefore also I come bearing them, that taking strength of words
by their power I may say with angels: Glory to God in the highest; and with
shepherds: And on earth peace, among men good pleasure. Today he who was
begotten unspeakably of the Father is born of a virgin, unspeakably for me.
But then indeed he was begotten according to nature of the Father before the
ages, as he who begat knows; today again beyond nature he was born, as the
grace of the Holy Spirit knows. And his birth from above is true, and his
birth below is without falsehood, and truly God from God was begotten, and
truly man the same from a virgin was born. Above the Only-begotten alone of
the Alone, below the same the Only-begotten alone of a virgin alone. For as
upon his birth from above it is impious to conceive a mother; so it is
blasphemous to suppose also a father upon his birth below. The Father begot
without flux, and the virgin bore without corruption; for neither did God
undergo flux begetting; for he begat godly; nor did the virgin undergo
corruption bearing; for she bore spiritually.
Wherefore neither does his birth from above admit of explanation, nor does
his procession in =56.388= later times endure to be searched into. For that
the virgin bore, today I know, and that God begot timelessly, I believe; but
the manner of the birth-giving I have learned to honor in silence, and I have
not received to search into with words. For concerning God one must not
attend to the nature of things, but believe in the power of the doer. For it
is a law of nature, when a woman having consorted with marriages bears; but
when a virgin unmarried having borne again appears a virgin, the thing is
beyond nature. Let the thing according to nature therefore be sought, and the
thing beyond nature be honored in silence, not as fled from, but as
unrevealed, and worthy to be honored in silence. But grant me pardon, I
beseech you, wishing to pause the word in the preface. For being cowardly
toward the inquiry of the greater things, how or where I shall turn the
rudders of my words I have not. For what shall I say, or what shall I speak?
I see her who bore, I behold him who was born, but the manner of the
birth-giving I do not see together; for nature is overcome, and the term of
order is overcome, where God wills. For the thing did not happen according to
nature; but the wonder is beyond nature; for nature was idle, and the will of
the Master energized. O grace unspeakable! He who before the ages is the
Only-begotten, the unutterable and simple and bodiless, entered into me, into
my corruptible and visible body. Wherefore? That being seen he might teach,
and having taught might guide toward the unseen. For since men make the eye
of hearing more faithful, and doubt what they see not, therefore he endured
also to present his own sight to eyes through the body, that he might
dissolve the controversy. And he is born of a virgin not knowing the thing;
for she neither cooperated toward the coming to be, nor contributed toward
the doing, but was a bare instrument of his unrevealed power, only knowing
what she learned when asked by Gabriel: How shall this be to me, since I know
not a man? And he says: Will you learn this? The Holy Spirit shall come upon
you, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow you. And how was he with
her, and a little after of her?
As a craftsman finding a most useful material works out a most fair vessel;
so also Christ finding the holy body and soul of the virgin, adorned for
himself an ensouled temple, having fashioned man in the virgin in the manner
he willed, and having clothed himself with it, today came forth, not being
ashamed of the unsightliness of nature. For it bore no outrage to him to wear
his own work; and the creature reaped the greatest glory, becoming the
garment of the craftsman. For as at the first fashioning it was impossible
for man to be constituted before the clay came into his hands; so also the
corrupted vessel is impossible to be transformed, unless it has become the
garment of the maker. =56.389= But what shall I say, or what shall I speak?
For the wonder astonishes me. The Ancient of days has become a child, he who
sits upon a high and lifted up throne is laid in a manger, the unutterable
and simple and uncompound and bodiless is swathed by human hands, him who
breaks the bonds of sins is entangled in swaddling bands, because he wills
it. For he wills to make dishonor honor, to clothe ingloriousness with glory,
to show the manner of virtue, the limit of outrage. Wherefore he undergoes my
body, that I may receive his Word; and taking my flesh, he gives me his
Spirit, that giving and receiving he may traffic for me a treasure of life.
He takes my flesh, that he may sanctify me; he gives me his Spirit, that he
may save me. But what shall I say, and what shall I speak? Behold, the virgin
shall conceive. It is no longer said as about to be, but is marveled at as
done. It was done among the Jews, among whom also it was said, but by us is
believed, among whom it was not even named. Behold, the virgin shall conceive.
The letter is of the synagogue, but the possession of the Church. That one
found the diptych, this one found the pearl; that one dyed the wool, this one
put on the scarlet. For the Jewish woman bore him, and the inhabited world
received him. The synagogue nursed him and fostered him, and the Church took
hold and reaped. With that one the vine branch of the vine, and with me the
cluster of truth. That one harvested the cluster, and the nations drink the
mystic draught. That one sowed the grain of wheat in Judaea, and the nations
reaped the ear with the sickle of faith.
The nations reverently cropped the rose, and to the Jews the thorn of
unbelief remained; the fledgling flew away, and the foolish sit by the nest;
the Jews interpret the leafage of the letter, and the nations pluck the fruit
of the Spirit; Behold, the virgin shall conceive. Tell me, O Jew, tell me
then, whom did she bear? Be of good courage toward me, even as toward Herod.
But you are not of good courage. I know wherefore; because of the plot. For
to him you said it, that he might slay him; but to me you say not, lest I
worship him. Whom then did she bear? Whom? The Master of nature. For even if
you keep silent, nature cries out; for she bore, as he who was born willed to
be born. For it was not permitted by nature, but as Master of nature he
introduced a strange manner of birth-giving, that he might show that being
made man he is not born as a man is born, but is begotten as God begets. From
a virgin therefore today he came forth, her who overcame nature, having
surpassed marriage. For it was fitting for the president of holiness to come
forth from pure and holy births. For he is the same who of old formed Adam
from a virgin earth, and from Adam without a woman fashioned a woman. For as
Adam without a woman brought forth a woman, so today the virgin without a man
bore a man. Man is he, it says, and who shall know him?
Since the female race owed the grace to men, as Adam brought forth from
himself without a woman a woman, =56.390= therefore today the virgin bore
without a man, discharging for Eve the debt toward men. For that Adam might
not be haughty, having brought forth without a woman a woman, therefore also
the virgin without a man bore a man, that she might show by the common wonder
the equal honor of nature. For as from Adam he took away the rib, and
diminished Adam nothing; so also in the virgin he both fashioned the ensouled
temple, and did not undo the virginity. Adam remained safe also after the
taking away of the rib; the virgin also remained incorrupt after the
procession of the infant. Wherefore also he made not from elsewhere a temple
for himself, nor having fashioned another body clothed himself with it, that
the clay of Adam be not thought to be insulted. For since man having been
deceived became an instrument of the devil, therefore he took up the same
ensouled temple who had been tripped up, that by the closeness toward his
maker he might estrange him from the fellowship of the devil. Still also
being made man he is not born as a man is born, but is begotten as God
begets. For if he had come forth from common marriages, as I, it would have
been thought a lie by the many; but now therefore he is born of a virgin, and
being born keeps the womb unchanged, and preserves the virginity unhurt, that
the strange manner of the conception may become to me a source of great
faith. Wherefore whether a Greek ask me, or a Jew, that Christ being God by
nature became man beyond nature, I will say, Yes, calling the seal of the
virginity the unspotted witness of the word; for thus is God overcoming the
order of nature; thus is he a potter of a womb, and a discoverer of
virginity, because he had the manner of his birth-giving unstained, and built
for himself unspeakably a temple in the manner he willed. Tell me therefore,
O Jew, did the virgin bear, or not? For if she bore, confess the strange
birth-giving; but if she bore not, why did you deceive Herod? For you, when
he inquired where Christ is born, said, In Bethlehem of Judaea.
Did not I then know the village, or the place? did not I know the dignity of
him who is born? did not Isaiah mention him as God? For she shall bear, he
says, a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel. Did not you ungrateful
enemies expound the truth? did not you the scribes and Pharisees, the exact
keepers of the law, teach us all the things concerning him? did we not know
the tongue of the Hebrews? did not you interpret the Scriptures? After the
virgin bore, and before she bore, that what is said be not thought to be
interpreted in favor of God, did not you, being asked by Herod, bring forward
Micah the prophet as witness, that your word be confirmed? For And you, he
says, Bethlehem, house of Ephratha, art by no means least among the governors
of Judah; for out of you shall come forth a leader, who shall feed my people
Israel. Well said the prophet, Out of you. For out of you he came forth, and
came into the inhabited world. For he who is, goes forth; but he who is not,
is created, or becomes. But he both was, and went forth, and ever was; but he
was ever as God, governing =56.391= the world; today he went forth, as man
indeed shepherding the people, as God saving the inhabited world. O kind
enemies! O benevolent accusers! who, unawares, showed the God born in
Bethlehem, who recognized the Master hidden in a manger, who betrayed him
sitting in a cave unwillingly, and benefited not willing, having uncovered
him wishing to cover him. Do you see unlearned teachers? What they teach they
know not, hungering they feed, thirsting they give drink, being poor they
enrich.
Come therefore, let us keep festival, come let us celebrate. For strange is
the manner of the feast, since also the account of the birth is paradoxical.
For today the long-lasting bond was loosed, the devil was put to shame, the
demons fled, death was loosed, paradise was opened, the curse was made to
vanish, sin was put to flight, error was driven out, the truth returned, and
the word of piety was sown everywhere and ran; the citizenship of those above
was planted in the earth, angels share with men, and men converse fearlessly
with angels. Wherefore? Because God came upon earth, and man in heaven; all
things have become mixed together. For he came upon the earth, being wholly
in the heavens; and being wholly in heaven, he is wholly upon the earth.
Being God, he became man, not having denied to be God; being the impassible
Word, he became flesh, because he dwelt among us he became flesh. For he did
not become God, but was. Therefore he became flesh, that him whom heaven
could not contain a manger might receive. Therefore he was laid in a manger,
that he who feeds all things might take the nourishment of an infant from a
virgin mother. Therefore the Father of the ages to come carries in his arms
as a burden the tender infant of virgin arms, that he might become
approachable even to Magi. For today Magi also came, having taken a beginning
to deny the tyrant, and the heaven boasts, announcing its own Master by a
star, and the Lord sitting upon the light cloud of the body runs into Egypt,
seemingly fleeing the plot of Herod, but in truth fulfilling what was said by
Isaiah: For it shall be, he says, in that day Israel shall be third among the
Assyrians, and among the Egyptians blessed shall be my people in the land
which the Lord of hosts blessed, saying: Blessed shall be my people in Egypt,
and among the Assyrians, and in Israel. What say you, O Jew? have you become
the first third? Have Egyptians and Assyrians been set before, and Israel the
firstborn is numbered after? Yes; fittingly the Assyrians shall be first,
since they also first worshipped him through the Magi; the Egyptians after
the Assyrians, since they also received him fleeing the plot of Herod; last
Israel is numbered, since after the ascent from Jordan through the apostles
they knew him. He entered into Egypt, then, shaking the handmade things of
Egypt, not simply, but because he shut the front doors of Egypt with the
destruction of the firstborn; therefore today he entered as firstborn, that
he might dissolve the mourning of the =56.392= ancient grimness. And that
Christ is called firstborn, Luke the evangelist testifies today, saying: And
she bore her son the firstborn, and swaddled him, and laid him in the manger,
because there was not for them a place in the inn. He entered therefore into
Egypt, that he might dissolve the mourning of the ancient grimness, casting
upon joy in the place of scourges, granting the light of salvation in the
place of night and darkness. It was then unclean the water of the river with
the slaughter of untimely infants. He entered therefore into Egypt who of old
made the water red, and he made the streams of the river generative of
salvation, having purified their blood-defilement and uncleanness by the
power of the Spirit. The Egyptians were made wretched, and having raved
denied God. He entered therefore into Egypt, and filled God-beloved souls
with the knowledge of God; and to the river he furnished witnesses,
nourishing ears of grain more fertile. But because of the narrowness of the
time I wish to pause the word here; here I shall pause, having filled the
word with the account of how, being the impassible Word, he became flesh, the
nature remaining unchanged. What shall I say, or what shall I speak? I behold
a craftsman and a manger, and an infant, and swaddling clothes, the lying-in
chamber of a virgin empty of needs, all holding poverty, all filled with want.
Did you see wealth in much poverty? how being rich he was made poor for us?
how he had neither bed nor couch, but was cast upon a dry manger? O poverty,
fountain of wealth! O wealth unmeasured, bearing the pretext of poverty! In a
manger he lies, and he shakes the inhabited world; he is entangled in
swaddling clothes, and he breaks the bonds of sins; he has not yet broken
forth an articulate voice, and he taught the Magi, and moved them toward
conversion. What shall I say, or what shall I speak? Behold an infant
entangled in swaddling clothes, and lying in a manger; and Mary is present,
being both virgin and mother; and Joseph was present, named father. This one
is called man, that one is addressed woman; names of lawful wedlock, yet
empty. Understand me as far as words, but not as far as deeds. This man alone
betrothed her, and the Holy Spirit overshadowed her. Wherefore Joseph being
perplexed knew not what to call the infant. To say from adultery he dared
not, to pour out a blasphemous word against the virgin he was not able, to
say the child his own he did not endure; for he knew well that he knew not
how or whence the infant was born; wherefore to him perplexed at the thing an
oracle from heaven through the voice of the angel was brought, that Fear not,
Joseph; for that which is born of her is of the Holy Spirit. For the Holy
Spirit overshadowed the virgin. Wherefore then is he born of a virgin, and
keeps her virginity unhurt? Because of old the devil deceived Eve being a
virgin, therefore Gabriel evangelized Mary being a virgin. But Eve having
been deceived bore a word, the cause of death; but Mary having been
evangelized brought forth the Word made flesh, the source of eternal life for
us. The word of Eve showed the tree, through which it thrust Adam out of
=56.393= paradise; but the Word from the Virgin showed the cross, through
which he brought in the thief into paradise, into the face of Adam.
Since Greeks believed not, nor Jews, nor children of heretics, that God begot
without flux and without passion, therefore today having come forth from a
passible body, he kept the passible body impassible, that he might show that
as being born of the virgin he did not undo the virginity, so also God, the
holy essence remaining without flux and =56.394= unchanged, begat God as God,
godly. Since men having left him carved images in the likeness of men, and
worshipped them in outrage of the maker, therefore today the Word of God,
being God, was seen in the form of a man, that he might both dissolve the
falsehood, and unperceived transfer the worship to himself. To Christ
therefore, who wrought this way out of impossibilities, let us send up glory
with the Father and the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto the ages of ages.
Amen.
