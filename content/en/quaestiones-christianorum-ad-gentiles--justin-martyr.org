Of Saint Justin the Philosopher and Martyr Christian Questions to the Greeks.
First Christian question to the Greeks. If to the greatest good among men the
greatest evil is opposed, and the greatest good among men is to know the God
who truly is, then the greatest evil is to be ignorant of this one. But since
this is an evil in some of men, which is more becoming to the God who is
creator of the world, to change the constitution of the present life and to
deliver men from the greatest evil, as the word of the Christians wills, or to
let the world remain upon the same constitution and men always held by the
greatest evil? Greek answer to the Christians. First, toward the first
difficulty it must be said that there is no greatest evil. For if God is the
greatest of the goods, it is clear that, if someone should suppose a greatest
evil, another principle contrary to God will be found, which the Manicheans,
speaking badly, say. For it is also absurd that God should not comprehend all
things, should not shine his own goodness upon all. There is therefore no
greatest evil. For ignorance too is often given to men for good; we see then
in many that it often happens that they rather despise the things known than
the things unknown. Otherwise also, being ignorant of the God who truly is,
the soul suffers not from some other thing but from its own forgetfulness. And
knowledge of God also comes even to the souls here; just as then we also see
here men knowing God. And if someone should wish to speak the truth, there is
not even ignorance concerning God at all; for all confess by common notion
that God is. And that men being here too are able to know God, the faith of
the orthodox also clearly shows, saying that God himself has come down and
been known to men. If then it is possible for men being here too to know God,
no greatest evil happens to them from being here. For to those who are
ignorant, as has been said, being ignorant of God happens from the unbelieving
nature. And if someone will also grant, which is absurd, that the things here
are the greatest evil, and that for the things here not to be is better than
to be, if he says that the creator has made the things here evil through
weakness, he is foolish, asserting that the power of God does not comprehend
all things. But if, being able to make the things here good, he allowed others
to make them evil, this too will be an accusation of God; for he who is able
to stop, but overlooks, more truly himself does it. And if God himself is the
one who made the things here, it is clear that the same God remaining,
afterward also and always the same things will be. For what he was able of old
he is able also now. And if now he is able something more besides the things
before, such a

power of his will be temporal, which is absurd; for the temporal is
corruptible. And if, being able of old also to prevent so great an evil, he
did not prevent it, he would be envious; which it is even impious to imagine.
He must therefore, remaining the same upon the same things, do the same
things. Refutation of the answer not rightly made. The argument itself, being
understood, was sufficient for refutation of the inconsistent argument. For by
saying at the beginning of the argument that there is no greatest evil, but at
the end of the argument that it is impious even from imagination alone to
think the things unworthy concerning God, how is the greatest evil not
introduced as judged, that which was indiscriminately cast out by the one
answering? And if there should be added to such an impiety from imagination
alone also the saying, the impiety becomes greater; and if with the saying
also the writing, the impiety of necessity has every excess of vice. And if
these things are so, and in all the things unworthily said and written
concerning God, or even only imagined, there is an impiety destructive of the
glory of God, there is therefore from ignorance of God the greatest evil among
men. But since it seemed good to your piety that the inconsistency of the
argument be also refuted by us in writing, for this reason I write in brief

numbing both to the one writing and to the one reading. The one answering set

nature; if then the craftsman is ungenerated and the paradigm ungenerated, so

and agreed to all, and from the thing itself it is easy to know. For the right
and left are of the relatives. As then it is not possible for the right to be
the left not being, nor the left outside the right, so it is not possible for
the craftsman to be without the artifact or the artifact without the
