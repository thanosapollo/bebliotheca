ON THE HOLY HIEROMARTYR PHOCAS, AND AGAINST HERETICS

And on the 141st psalm, "With my voice I cried unto the Lord, with my voice
unto God I made supplication."

=50.699=

αʹ. Bright has the city become for us yesterday, bright and conspicuous, not
because it had columns, but because it had a martyr processing, arrived among
us from Pontus. He saw your hospitality, and he filled you with the blessing;
he praised your eagerness, and he blessed those who had come. I counted
blessed those who assembled and took part in his fragrance, and I called
wretched those who were left behind; but that their loss may not become
incurable, we proclaim him again on another day, so that those too who were
left behind through sloth may double by their diligence the blessing that
comes from the martyr. For what I have often said, this also I will not cease
saying: I do not demand accounts of sins, but I prepare medicines for the
sick. Were you left behind yesterday? Come even today, that you may see him
being led away to his own place. Did you see him being led through the
marketplace? See him also sailing through the sea, so that both the elements
may be filled with the blessing from him. Let no one be left behind from this
sacred festival; let no virgin remain at home, let no woman hold the house,
let us empty the city, and let us put in at the martyr's tomb; for even
emperors dance in common with us. What pardon then has the private man, when
emperors leave the imperial courts and sit beside the martyr's tomb? For such
is the power of the martyrs, as to net not only private men, but even those
who wear the diadems; this is the shame of the Greeks, this the reproach of
their error, this the destruction of the demons; this is our nobility, and the
crown of the Church. I dance with martyrs, and I leap, seeing their trophy
instead of meadows, because instead of springs blood flowed; their bones have
been spent, and their memory becomes fresher every day. For just as it is
impossible for the sun to be quenched, so also a memory of martyrs; for Christ
himself declared: Heaven =50.700= and earth shall pass away, but my words
shall not pass away.

But let us wait for the encomia of the martyr until the fitting time; for even
these things have been said sufficiently for the eagerness of those who ought
to be gathered, and to make the day of the feast conspicuous. For what I was
saying yesterday also, I say again today: no glory will come to the martyr
from the presence of the many, but there will be more blessing for you from
coming to the martyr. For just as he who looks toward the sun does not make
the star brighter, but lights up his own eyes; so also he who honors a martyr
does not make that one more splendid, but himself draws from him the blessing
of the light. Let us make the sea a church again, going out there with lamps,
both wetting the fire and filling the water with fire. Let no one fear the
sea; the martyr did not fear death, and do you dread the water? But we have
spoken this word sufficiently; come then, let us also set before you from what
has been said today the accustomed table. For even if our bodies are cramped,
yet let the mind be given wings; for I do not look at your crowding, but at
your eagerness. Sweet also to a steersman is a sea that is swelling, and to a
teacher a church that is like a sea; for in these waters there is neither
brine, nor reef, nor beasts, but a sea and a deep full of fragrance; here
ships are not shifting harbor from land to land, but from earth to heaven,
having not goods, not gold, nor silver, but faith, and love, and zeal, and
wisdom.

βʹ. Come then, therefore, let us with exactness launch the ship that is never
lost, nor suffers shipwreck. But attend with exactness to what is said; for
the psalm today leads us out into battle-array against the heretics, not that
we may strike them as they stand, but =50.701= that we may raise them as they
lie; for such is our war; it does not make dead men from the living, but it
fashions living men from the dead, being full of gentleness and of much
forbearance. For I do not drive by a deed, but I pursue with the word, not the
heretic, but the heresy, I do not turn away from the man, but I hate the
error, and I wish to draw him in; I have no war against substance; for
substance is a work of God; but I wish to correct the mind, which the devil
corrupted. So also a physician healing a sick man does not war against the
body, but destroys the evil of the body.

I too then, if I war against heretics, do not war against the men themselves,
but I wish to cast out the error, and to purge away the rottenness. It is my
custom to be persecuted and not to persecute, to be driven and not to drive.
So also Christ prevailed, not by crucifying, but by being crucified, not by
striking, but by being struck. If I have spoken ill, he says, bear witness
concerning the evil; but if well, why do you beat me? The Master of the
inhabited world makes a defense to the servant of the high priest, and having
been struck on the mouth, from which a word went forth, and he bridled the
sea, and raised Lazarus of four days from the dead; from which wickedness
fled, from which diseases and sins were loosed; this is the wonder of the
Crucified. For though able to send a thunderbolt, and to shake the earth, and
to wither the servant's hand, he did none of these things, but he even makes a
defense, and prevails by meekness, training you, being a man, never to be
angry; even if you are crucified, even if you receive a blow, that you may say
the same things as your Master: If I have spoken ill, bear witness concerning
the evil; but if well, why do you beat me? And see his love of mankind, how he
avenges the things of the servants, but passes by his own. There was once a
prophet, and he was rebuking an impious king, and coming he says: Altar,
altar, hear. For since king Jeroboam was standing offering sacrifice to the
idols, the prophet coming converses with the altar. What are you doing, O
prophet? Have you left the man, and do you converse with the altar? Yes, he
says. Why? Because the man has become more unfeeling than a stone, I leave
that one, and I converse with this, so that you may learn that the stone
hears, and the man does not hear. Hear, altar, hear, and immediately the altar
was split. The king stretched out his hand, wishing to seize the prophet, and
he was not able to draw it back. Do you see that the altar heard more than the
king? do you see that he left the rational one, and converses with the
irrational, so that through the obedience of this he may correct the
unfeelingness and wickedness of that one? The altar was split, and the king's
wickedness was not split. But see what happened; the king stretched out his
hand to take the prophet, and immediately his hand was withered.

Since by the punishment of the altar he did not become better, he is trained
to the obedience of God by his own chastisement. For I, sparing you, wished to
send the wrath upon a stone; but since the stone did not become a teacher for
you, take the punishment yourself. And he stretched out his hand, and at once
it was withered. There stood then the trophy of the prophet, and the king was
not able to draw it back. Where is the diadem? where the purple robes? where
the breastplates? where the shields? where the army? where the spears? God
commanded, and =50.702= all those things perished; the satraps stood by, not
able to help, but becoming spectators of the blow only. And the king stretched
out his hand, and it was withered; when it became dry, then it bore fruit.
Look for me at the example of the tree in paradise, and of the tree on the
cross. For just as that tree, being green, bore death, but the tree of the
cross, being dry, generated life; so also it happened in the case of the
king's hand: when it was green, then it bore impiety, when it became dry, then
it offered obedience; see the paradoxical works of God. But as I was saying,
when he himself was being struck, he did nothing evil to the one striking; but
when the servant was about to be insulted, he chastised the king, training you
to avenge the things of God, but to pass by your own. For just as I pass by my
things, but avenge yours; so you also avenge my things, and pass by your own.
But stretch your hearing for me; for where it is a time of contests, there is
need of hearers who have their hearing stretched, so that you may know with
exactness how I bind, how I loose the faults of the adversaries, how I
wrestle, how I break. For if those sitting in the theatre lean forward when
two are wrestling, stretching their gaze and their stature, that they may see
wrestlings full of shame, that they may see wrestlings which it is shame to
imitate, much more must we attend to the hearing of the divine Scriptures. For
if you praise the athlete, why do you not become an athlete? but if you are
ashamed to become an athlete, why do you imitate the praise of these? But the
wrestlings here are not such, but common to all and profitable both to those
who speak and to those who hear. For I wrestle with the heretic, so that I may
make you athletes as well, so that not only by singing psalms, but also by
conversing, you may stitch up their tongue. What then does the prophet say?
With my voice I cried unto the Lord, with my voice unto God I made
supplication.

Attend; the saying does not seem to have an occasion for wrestlings, does it?
See how I weave the crown and the matter of the contests. With my voice I
cried unto the Lord; with my voice unto God I made supplication. Call the
heretic here for me; whether he is present, or whether he is not present. For
if he is present, let him be trained by our voice; and if he is not present,
let him learn through your hearing. But as I said, I do not drive him when he
is present, but I receive him when he is driven, not by us, but from his own
conscience, according to the one who says, The ungodly flees when no one is
pursuing. The Church is a mother of her own children, both receiving these,
and spreading her bosom to the strangers. The ark of Noah was a common
theatre, but the Church is even better than this. For that one received
irrational things, and kept irrational things; but this receives irrational
things, and changes them. What I mean is this: If a heretic fox comes in here,
I make this one a sheep; if a wolf comes in, I make him a lamb, as far as my
part is concerned; but if he is not willing, it is nothing from me, but from
his =50.703= own ingratitude; since Christ also had twelve disciples, and one
became a traitor, but not from Christ, but from that man's corrupted mind;
since Elisha also had a money-loving disciple, but not from the weakness of
the teacher, but from the sloth of the disciple. I cast down the seeds; if you
are rich soil receiving the seeds, you also give back the ear; but if you are
a barren rock, it is nothing from me; whether you hear, or whether you do not
hear, I will not cease singing you a spiritual song, pouring upon the wounds,
so that I may not hear on that day: Wicked servant, you ought to have put my
money with the bankers. With my voice I cried unto the Lord; with my voice
unto God I made supplication.

γʹ. What do you say, heretic? concerning whom does the prophet speak, and whom
does he call Lord and God? for the word is concerning one person. For those
men, citing the Scriptures against their own head, and always seeking premises
against their own salvation, do not perceive that they are thrusting
themselves into a pit of destruction; for neither does the one who praises
make the Son of God more glorious, nor does the one who blasphemes harm him;
for the bodiless substance has no need of our praise, but just as he who says
the sun is bright does not add to its light, nor does he who says it is dark
lessen its substance, but he brings forth his vote as a proof of his own
blindness; so he who does not call the Son of God Son but a creature brings
forth a demonstration of his own madness, but he who recognizes his substance
displays his own good-mindedness; and neither does this one benefit him, nor
does that one harm him, but the one contends against his own salvation, and
the other on behalf of his own salvation. But as I was saying, citing the
Scriptures, they pass by the rest, and they seek if they might somewhere find
a premise seeming to run with their disease. And do not tell me that the
Scripture is the cause; the Scripture is not the cause, but their ingratitude,
since honey also is sweet, but the sick man thinks it bitter; yet the charge
is not against the honey, but the accusation is of the infirmity. So also the
mad do not see the things that are seen, but the charge is not against the
things seen, but the corrupted mind of the madman. God made the heaven, so
that we might see the work, and worship the Creator, but the Greeks made a god
of the work; yet the work is not the cause, but their ingratitude; and just as
the ungrateful man is benefited by no one, so the well-minded man is benefited
even from himself. What is equal to Christ? and Judas was not benefited. What
is more wicked than the devil? and Job was crowned; and neither did Christ
benefit Judas, because he was ungrateful, nor did the devil harm Job, because
he was well-minded.

These things I say, so that no one may slander the Scriptures, but the
ingratitude of those who interpret badly the things well said. For the devil
also was conversing with Christ from the Scriptures. But the Scripture is not
the cause, but the mind that =50.704= interprets badly the things well said.
For wishing to show the Son lesser than the Father, they go about busying
themselves with foreign names, and they say concerning God and Lord, that the
Father is God, but the Son is Lord, and they divide the names, and they assign
God to the Father, but Lord to the Son, as being dividers of the Godhead and
bequeathers. Does the Scripture say Lord of the Son? does it say these things?
How did you not hear the psalm speaking today toward one person, With my voice
I cried unto the Lord, with my voice unto God I made supplication? Therefore
it names him both Lord and God. Whom do you wish to be God, the Son, or the
Father? You will certainly say that these names belong to the Father.
Therefore the Son is God, and the Father is Lord. Why then do you divide the
names, and throw the one there, and divide the other here? And Paul also says,
would that you heard Paul, and you would be blessed: But to us there is one
God the Father, from whom are all things, and one Lord Jesus Christ, through
whom are all things. He did not call the Son God, did he? But what then? Lord.
For what, tell me, is God more august than Lord, and Lord lesser than God?
Stretch yourselves, I beseech you, with exactness; if then I show that Lord
and God are one, what do you say? Do you say that God is greater, but Lord
lesser? Hear the prophet saying: This is the Lord who made the heaven; this is
God who framed the earth; Lord the heaven, and God the earth; this one has set
both Lord and God upon the same person. And again: Hear, O Israel, the Lord
your God, the Lord is one. Twice Lord, and once God; and first Lord, and then
God, and again Lord. But if this is inferior, and that greater, he would not
have set the lesser first upon a great and greater substance in the saying,
but having said the greater he would have been content, and would not have
thrown in the lesser.

Did you understand what I said? I teach again; for it is not a theatre for
display, but teaching for compunction, not so that you may go away unarmed,
but so that you may withdraw having been armed. Do you say, heretic, that God
is greater, and Lord lesser? I showed you the prophet saying: The Lord who
made the heaven, God who framed the earth. I show you again Moses saying:
Hear, O Israel, the Lord your God, the Lord is one. How one, if the names are
two, the one of a lesser, and the other of a greater substance? For the
substance is not greater than itself and lesser, but equal and joined; The
Lord your God, the Lord is one; and I show you that the name Lord, this is
God; and in general, if Lord is inferior, but God greater, which ought to have
been his name? Lord the smaller, or God the greater? If he should say: What is
my name? what do you say, O heretic? do you say that Lord belongs to the Son,
but God to the Father, as more proper? If then I show you that Lord, the small
one, is his identifying name, what do you do? Let them know that your name is
Lord, the prophet says; he did not say God, did he? And yet if God is greater,
why did the prophet not say, Let them know that your name is God? For if God
is his own and proper, but Lord is =50.705= foreign to him as inferior, why
did he say: Let them know that your name is Lord, the small, the lesser, the
inferior to the dignity, and he does not say that great and high one, the one
worthy of the substance? And so that you may learn that this is not small, nor
inferior, but has the same force: Let them know that your name is Lord; you
alone are Most High over all the earth.

δʹ. But still you do not stand off from the fight, but you say again that God
is greater, but Lord lesser. If then I show the Son called by the greater
name, what do you say? do you dissolve the fight? do you stand off from the
contention? do you recognize your salvation? are you delivered from the
madness? did you understand what I said? since they throw the name Lord to the
Son, but God to the Father as greater. If then I show you the Son called by
the greater name, God, the fight is loosed; for I master you with your own
weapons, and I prevail over you with your own wings. You said that God is
greater, but Lord lesser; I wish to show that the lesser would not have fitted
the Father, if he were greater, and the greater would not have fitted the Son,
if the Son were lesser. Hear the prophet saying: This is our God, no other
shall be reckoned beside him; he found out every way of knowledge; afterward
he was seen upon earth, and consorted with men. What do you say to this? do
you contradict these words? But you cannot; for the truth remains, sending
forth its own brightness, and blinding the eyes of the here =50.706= tics who
do not wish to believe it.

Even if the wrestlings are deep, and the heat is much, yet the word is greater
and prevails over the difficulty of the hearers, and comforts the heat with
the dew of the teaching; even if we are gathered once or twice in the week,
the hearer must not be slothful. For if you go out from here, and someone says
to you, what did the teacher discourse? you should say, against heretics; if
he says to you, what did he discourse? and you do not find it, it is a very
great shame to you; but if you say it, you sting him, and if he is a heretic,
you correct him, and if he is a slothful friend, you draw him, and if it is a
licentious woman, you make her more temperate; for you are also accountable to
the wife; For let the women, it says, be silent in church; but if they wish to
learn anything, let them ask their own husbands. If you go into the house, and
the wife asks you: What did you bring me from the church? say to her, not
meat, nor wine, nor gold, nor ornaments that beautify the body, but a word
that makes the mind wise.

When you go in to the wife, set forth the spiritual table; say first, while
the memory is still ringing: Let us enjoy the spiritual things, and then let
us enjoy the sensible table; for if we thus order the things that concern
ourselves, we shall also have God in the midst both blessing the table, and
crowning us. For all these things then let us give thanks to him, the Father
with the Son and the Holy Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of ages.
Amen.
