* On the Consubstantial
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

To those who were absent from the assembly, and a demonstration that the Son
is consubstantial with the Father, and that the things said and done by him in
a lowly way were not done and said through weakness of power, nor through
diminution, but through various economies. From On the Incomprehensible, and
following.

Homily seven.
=48.755=
1. Again there are horse-races, and again our gathering has become smaller; or
rather, so long as you are present, it would not become smaller. For just as a
farmer, if he should see the wheat ripe and complete, does not make much
account of the leaves falling; so also I now, with the fruit present to us, do
not feel so great a pain, seeing the leaves being
snatched away =48.756=. For I grieve also because of those men's sloth, but
nevertheless the eagerness of your love consoles the pain over them. For even
if those men should come sometime, they are not even then present, but their
body is seated here, while their mind wanders outside; but you, even if you
should be left behind sometime, even then are present; for your body is
outside, while your mind is here. I wished, then, to stretch a long discourse
against those men, but lest I should seem to fight with shadows, rebuking
those who are not present and do not hear, having kept these words for their
presence, I will try by the divine grace to lead =48.757= your love to the
accustomed meadow and the sea of the divine Scriptures. But rise up, and keep
watch; for in the case of those who sail, even if all should sleep, and the
helmsman alone should be awake, there is no danger, his sobriety and skill
sufficing the ship in place of all; but here it is not so, but even if the
speaker should be sober ten thousand times, and those who hear should not show
the same watchfulness, the discourse will go down for us and be gone, not
finding a mind that received it. Therefore one must be sober and keep watch;
for our merchandise is for greater things; for we do not sail for gold and
silver and things that perish, but for the life to come, and the treasures in
the heavens, and here the ways are more than those in the sea and those on the
land; and if one does not know how to cut them with accuracy, he will suffer a
most grievous shipwreck. All of you then who sail with us, show not the
carelessness of passengers, but the watchfulness and care of helmsmen; for
those men, while all the rest sleep, sitting at the tillers, not only look
around at the ways in the waters, but also looking toward heaven from so great
a distance, as if the course of the stars were some hand leading them, they
steer the vessel with safety, and no unskilled man could sail the deep so
fearlessly by day as they, with all quiet, in the middle of the night, when
the sea appears more terrible, being awake, show their skill unshaken, and
they look around not only at the paths in the waters, nor the courses of the
stars, but also the goings-forth of winds, and so great is the wisdom of those
men, that often, when a more violent rush of wind has fallen in and is about
to overturn the ship, receiving it with frequent shifts of the sails, they
skillfully dissolve all the danger, and


setting their own skill against the violent onsets of the winds, snatch the
vessel from the surge. And if those men, sailing a perceptible sea for things
of this life, have the soul thus continually awake, much more must we be so
prepared; for both the danger is greater for those who are slothful, and the
safety more for those who are sober. For neither is this vessel of ours
constructed from planks, but it is glued together from the divine Scriptures,
nor do stars lead it from above, but the sun of righteousness directs this
voyage for us, and we sit at the tillers, not waiting for breaths of the west
wind, but for the gentle breeze of the Spirit.

2. Let us be sober then and look around at the ways with accuracy; for our
discourse is again concerning the glory of the Only-Begotten. Formerly, then,
we were showing that the comprehension of the essence of God exceeds, with a
great surplus, the wisdom of men, and of angels, and of archangels, and simply
of all creation, and is known and clear to the Only-Begotten alone and to the
Holy Spirit; but now the discourse is trans =48.758= ferred to another part of
the wrestlings for us. For we are inquiring whether the Son is of the same
power, whether of the same authority, whether of the same essence as the
Father; or rather we are not inquiring, but we have found this through the
grace of Christ, and we hold it with security; but for those who are shameless
about these things we are now preparing to demonstrate this very thing. I am
ashamed then and I blush, being about to throw myself into these discourses.
For who will not laugh at us, as we try to establish and demonstrate things so
evident? And what condemnation would there not be in inquiring whether the Son
is consubstantial with the Father? For this fights not only with the
Scriptures, but also with the common opinion of all men, and with the nature
of things. For that the one begotten is consubstantial with the one who begot,
one may see this not only in the case of men, but also in the case of all
animals, and in the case of trees. How then is it not absurd that this law
remain unmoved in the case of plants and men and animals, but in the case of
God alone to move it and overturn it? Nevertheless, lest we should seem to
insist on these things from the affairs among us, come, let us demonstrate
this from the Scriptures themselves, and let us set in motion the discourses
concerning these things. For it is not we who have been persuaded, but those
who disbelieve who will incur the laughter, those who stand against things so
evident, and who look against the truth. What things so evident? he says. For
if, since he is called Son, he is consubstantial with the Father, we too will
be able to be consubstantial; for we too are called sons; For I said, he says,
you are gods, and all sons of the Most High. O the shamelessness! O the utter
folly! How through all things they display their madness! When we were setting
in motion the discourse on the Incomprehensible, they were contentious to
claim for themselves that which belonged to the Only-Begotten alone, the
knowing God so accurately as he knows himself; but now since our discourse is
concerning the glory of the Only-Begotten, they are contentious to bring him
down to their own cheapness, saying that we too are called sons. And this does
not at all make us consubstantial with God. You are called son, but that one
is; here a word, there the reality. You are called son, but you are not called
Only-Begotten as that one also is, nor do you dwell in the bosom of the
Father, nor are you the radiance of the glory, nor the impress of the
hypostasis, nor do you exist as the form of God. If then the former does not
persuade you, let these persuade you, and many other things more than these,
bearing witness to that nobility of his. For when he wishes to show the
unvarying of his essence toward the one who begot him, He who has seen me, he
says, has seen the Father. And when the distinctive of the power, I, he says,
and the Father are one; and when the matching of the authority, For as the
Father raises the dead and gives life, so


also the Son gives life to whom he will; and when the identity of the worship,
That all may honor the Son as they honor the Father; and when the authority in
the recastings of the laws, My Father, he says, is working, and I am working.
But these men, having run past all these things, and not taking the name Son
in the proper sense, since they too have been honored with the appellation of
sons, bring the Son down to the same cheapness, saying, I said, you are gods,
and all sons of the Most High. Therefore since through the appellation of son
you say the Son has nothing more than you, and according to this is not even
=48.759= a true son, you will perhaps also, through the appellation of God,
since he has given you a share of the name, contend that the Father has
nothing more than you; for as you were called son, so also you were called
god; but as, having been called god, you do not dare to say that the name is
bare there, but you confess that the Father is true God; so neither in the
case of the Son dare to bring yourself forward and say, I too have
been called son; and since I am not of the same essence, neither ought that
one to be of the same; for all the things enumerated show that he is true Son
and of the same essence as the one who begot him. For when he says that he is
of the same form as he, and of the same impress, what else does he make clear,
if not the unvarying of the essence? For there is not form around God, nor a
face; but as you say these things, he says, say also the contrary. What
contrary things? Such as that he prays to the Father; for if he is of the same
power, and of the same essence, and does all things from authority, for what
reason does he pray?

3. But I will not say these things only, but also as many other lowly things
as have been said concerning him, I will set them all forth with accuracy,
saying that first, that I have many just causes to give of the things said
lowly concerning him, but you would have no other pretext to give of the high
and glorious things, except the wishing to show us his own nobility. Since if
this should not be so, there is battle and war in the Scriptures; for the
saying that As the Father raises the dead and gives life, so also the Son
gives life to whom he will, and as many other things as I said, and again the
praying, whenever it is necessary to do this, is contrary; but if I should say
the causes, all dispute is then taken away. What then are the causes of his
having said, and the apostles having said, many lowly things concerning him?
First then and greatest cause is that he has been clothed with flesh, and
wishes to make all, both those then and those afterward, believe that
what is seen is not some shadow, nor a mere figure, but truth of nature. For
if, when so many lowly and human things have been said concerning him both by
the apostles and by himself concerning himself, nevertheless the devil was
able to persuade some of the wretched and miserable men to deny the account of
the economy, and to dare to say that he did not take flesh, and to destroy the
whole ground of the loving-kindness; if he had said none of these things, how
many would not have fallen into this pit? Do you not still even now hear
Marcion denying the economy, and Manichaeus, and Valentinus, and many others?
For this reason he utters many human and lowly things, falling short of that
unutterable essence, in order that he may confirm the account of the economy.
For the devil has been very eager to destroy this faith from men, knowing that
if he should destroy the faith of the economy, the greater part of the affairs
concerning us will be gone. There is also another cause after this, the
weakness of those who hear, and their not being able, seeing him then for the
first time, and hearing then for the first time, to receive the higher words
of the doctrines. And that what is said is not a guess, I will try to present
this to you from the Scriptures themselves =48.760= and to show, that if ever
he uttered something great and high and worthy of his glory; why do I say
great and high and worthy of his glory? if ever he said something more than
human nature,


they were thrown into uproar and were scandalized; but if ever something lowly
and human, they ran to it and received the word. And where is this to be seen?
he says. Especially in John; for when he said, Abraham your father rejoiced
that he might see my day, and he saw and was glad, they say: You do not yet
have forty years, and have you seen Abraham? Do you see that they were
disposed as concerning a mere man? What then does he? Before Abraham came to
be, he says, I am. And they took up stones that they might throw them at him.
And when he stretched out long discourses concerning the mysteries, saying,
And the bread which I will give for the life of the world is my flesh, they
were saying, This word is hard; who is able to hear it? And from this many of
his disciples went away backward, and no longer walked with him. What then was
one to do? tell me; to linger continually in the higher words, so as to scare
away the catch, and to beat all back from the teaching? But this was not of
the loving-kindness of God. For again when he said, He who hears my word shall
never taste death, they were saying, Did we not say well that you have a
demon? Abraham died, and the prophets died, and you say that He who hears my
word shall not taste death? And what wonder if the multitude was so disposed,
when even the rulers themselves had this opinion? Nicodemus at any rate, being
himself a ruler, and having come to him with much goodwill and having said, We
know that you have come from God as a teacher, was not able to receive the
word concerning baptism, it being much greater than that man's weakness; for
when Christ said that Unless one is born of water and spirit, he cannot see
the kingdom of God; he fell into such human suspicions, and says, How can a
man be born when he is old? can he enter a second time into his mother's womb
and be born from above? What then does Christ? If I told you earthly things
and you did not believe, how if I tell you heavenly things will you believe?
all but apologizing and saying for what reason he was not continually
discoursing with them concerning the birth from above. Again near the very
time of the cross, after the ten thousand signs, after that great
demonstration of his power, when he said that You will see the Son of man
coming upon the clouds, the high priest, not bearing what was said, tore his
own garments. How then was one to discourse with these, who bear none of the
high things? For that he did not say anything great and high at all concerning
himself is no wonder to men who crawl on the ground, and are so weakly
disposed.

4. The things said would then have sufficed to show that this was the cause
and the pretext of the cheapness of the things said then; but I will try to
make this clear also from the other side. For just as you saw them scan
=48.761= dalized, thrown into uproar, leaping away, railing, fleeing, if ever
Christ uttered something great and high, so I will try to show them to you
running up and accepting the teaching, if ever he said something lowly and
cheap. For they themselves, they themselves who were leaping away, when he
said again that I do nothing from myself, but as my Father taught me I speak,
immediately ran up. And the Evangelist, wishing to show us that they believed
because of the lowliness of the words, notes it saying: When he had spoken
these things, many believed in him. And elsewhere in many places one would
find this happening so. For this reason he uttered many things and often in a
human way, and again not in a human way but in a manner befitting God, and
worthy of his own nobility, on the one hand condescending to the weakness of
those who hear, on the other taking thought for the correctness of the
doctrines. For lest the condescension, happening continually, should


harm those afterward in the opinion concerning his worth, he did not
neglect that part either; but although he knew beforehand that they would not
hear, but would even rail at him and leap away, nevertheless he spoke, both
establishing this very thing which I said, and showing the cause for which he
also mixed lowly words for them. And this was, that they were not yet able to
receive the greatness of the things said. And if he had not wished to
establish this, the teaching of the high doctrines was superfluous for those
who would not hear nor pay attention; but now even if it did not benefit those
men at all, yet it was educating us, and preparing us to have the fitting
opinion concerning him, and was persuading that because those men were not yet
able to receive the greatness of the things said, he transferred the discourse
to the lower. When then you see him saying lowly things, do not think the
condescension to be of cheapness according to essence, but of the weakness
according to the mind of the hearers. Do you wish me to say a third cause
also? For it was not only because of the clothing of the flesh, nor because of
the weakness of the hearers, but also because he wished to teach those who
hear to be humble-minded, that he both did and said many lowly things; and
this again is a third cause. For he who teaches to be humble-minded educates
this not through words only but also through the deeds, being moderate in word
and in work; For learn, he says, from me that I am meek and humble in heart;
and again elsewhere: The Son of man did not come to be served, but to serve.
He then who teaches men to become humble, and nowhere to run after the first
places, but to accept being lessened everywhere, and leading to this through
words and through deeds, had many pretexts for uttering lowly things. It is
possible to say a fourth cause also, not less than those said. And what is
this? That we should not, because of the great and unspeakable nearness of the
hypostases, fall sometime into a suspicion of one person,
seeing that even now, when he has said something of this sort few times, some
have already slipped into this impiety. Sabellius the Libyan at any rate,
having heard him saying: I and the Father are one, and, He who has seen me has
seen the Father, seized the nearness toward the one who begot him that appears
from these words as a ground of impiety, =48.762= and a suspicion of one
person and one hypostasis. But these are not the only pretexts, but also that
no one should think him to be the first and unbegotten essence, and suppose
him greater than the one who begot him. For Paul also appears fearing this
very thing, lest someone should sometime suspect this impious and wicked
doctrine. Having said at any rate, For he must reign until he puts the enemies
under his feet, and having added that He subjected all things under his feet,
he brought on: Except the one who subjected all things to him; he would not
have brought this on if he had not feared lest somewhere some such diabolic
thought should come to be. And in many places also, soothing the envy of the
Jews, he lets down from the height of the words, and often answering toward
the suspicion of those discoursing with him, as when he says: If I bear
witness concerning myself, my witness is not true. For stretching toward their
suspicion he spoke thus; for he did not wish to show this, that it is not
true, but as you think and suspect, he says, you who do not wish to accept him
speaking concerning himself.

5. And it is possible to find still more other causes. Of the lowliness of the
words then we would have many pretexts to say; but you, say one other pretext
of the high doctrines, besides the one we have said; and this was his wishing
to show us his own nobility; but you would not have another to say. For the
great one could say even something small concerning himself, and there is no
charge; for this is of fairness;


but the small one, if he should ever say something great concerning himself,
will not escape accusation; for it is boastfulness. For this reason we all
praise the high one, when he says lowly things concerning himself; but no one
will praise the lowly one, if he should ever say something great concerning
himself. So that if the Son were much inferior to the Father, as you say, he
ought not to have been saying words through which he showed himself equal to
the one who begot him; for this was boastfulness; but for one who is equal to
the one who begot him to say some lowly and cheap things, there is no blame,
nor cause; for this is praise and a very great wonder. And that the things
said may become clearer, and that you all may learn that we are not guessing
at the divine Scriptures, come then, let us now take in hand the first of
these causes that have been said, and let us show where, because of the
clothing of the flesh, he clearly utters words inferior to his own essence;
and, if it seems good, let us bring forward the prayer itself, which
he prayed to the Father. But attend to us with accuracy; for I wish to narrate
the whole to you a little from above. There was a supper once on that holy
night, on which he was about to be delivered up; for I call it holy, because
the ten thousand goods which came to be throughout the world took their
beginning from there. So then the betrayer also was reclining with the
eleven disciples, and as they were dining, Christ says, One of you will
deliver me up. Remember these words for me, so that when we come to the
prayer, we may see for what reason he prays thus. And consider for me the care
of the Master; he did not say, =48.763= that Judas will deliver me up, so as
not to make him more shameless by the openness of the rebukes; but when that
man, pricked by conscience, says, Is it I, Lord? he says to him, You have
said; and not even then did he endure to accuse him, but he set him as a proof
against himself; but nevertheless not even so did he become better, but taking
the morsel he went out. When then he had gone out, Jesus taking the disciples
says: All of you will be scandalized in me; and when Peter contradicted and
said, Even if all should be scandalized, yet I will never be scandalized,
again Jesus says, Amen I say to you, before a cock crows, you will deny me
three times. And when he contradicted again, he let him be thereafter. You are
not persuaded, he says, through the words, but you contradict; you will be
persuaded through the deeds themselves, that one must not contradict a Master.
And remember these words for me again; for the memory of them will be useful
to us as we examine the prayer. He named the betrayer, he foretold the flight
of all, and his own death; I will strike, he says, the shepherd, and the sheep
will be scattered; he foretold the one who was about to deny him, and when and
how many times, and all things with accuracy. And after all these things,
having given a sufficient demonstration of his foreknowledge of the things to
come, having come to a certain place he prays; and those men say that the
prayer is of the godhead, but we say that it is of the economy. Judge then,
and before the very glory of the Only-Begotten bring a vote granted as a favor
to no one. For even if I am now being judged among friends, yet I entreat and
beg that the judgment become unbribed, and neither toward my favor, nor toward
hatred of those men. For it is especially clear even from the thing itself
that the prayer is not of the godhead; for God does not pray, for it belongs
to God to be worshipped; it belongs to God to receive a prayer, not to offer a
prayer; but nevertheless since they are shameless, I will try to make it clear
to you from the very words of the prayer, that the whole is of the economy,
and of the weakness according to the flesh. For when Christ utters something
lowly, he utters it so lowly and cheap, that the excess of the lowliness of
the things said can persuade even those who are very contentious, that the
words fall far short of that unutterable and ineffable essence. Let us go then
upon the very words of the prayer: Father, if


it is possible, let this cup pass from me; yet not as I will, but as you. Let
us then ask them here: does he not know, whether it is possible or not
possible, he who a little before was saying at the supper, One of you will
deliver me up? he who a little before was saying, It is written, I will strike
the shepherd, and the sheep will be scattered; and that All of you will be
scandalized in me; he who said to Peter that You will deny me, and, You will
deny me three times; does this one not know now, tell me? And who of those
even in any way of sound mind would say this? For when the thing unknown is
clear to no one, neither of prophets, nor of angels, nor of archangels,
perhaps it will furnish some handle to the contentious; but when the thing
unknown is so evident and known to all, that even men know it with accuracy,
what defense and what pardon is there for those who say that he himself said
these things not knowing? This account then of which I speak =48.764= even his
servants appear knowing with accuracy, both that he must die, and that he must
suffer this through a cross, and so many years before David, making both these
things clear, was saying from the person of Christ: They dug my hands and my
feet; and he announced the thing about to be as having come to be, showing
that, just as it is impossible for the things that have happened not to have
come to be, so also it is impossible for this not to have come to be. Again
Isaiah, proclaiming this very thing beforehand, was saying: He was led as a
sheep to slaughter, and as a lamb before the one who shears him is voiceless.
Again John, seeing this lamb, was saying, Behold the lamb of God who takes
away the sin of the world, that one who was proclaimed beforehand, he says.
And see, it is not said simply the lamb, but of God is added. For since there
was also another lamb, the Jewish one, making clear that this one is of God,
for this reason he spoke thus. That one was offered for the nation only, this
one was offered for the whole world; and the blood of that one hindered a
bodily plague from Jews only, but the blood of this one became a common
cleansing of the whole world. And this blood of the Jewish lamb had the power
it had, not from its own nature, but since it was a type of this one, it had
that power.

6. Where then are those who say that he too is called Son, and we are sons,
and from the communion according to the appellation attempt to bring him to
our cheapness? For behold, lamb and lamb, and the name is one, but the
interval of each nature is infinite. As then when you hear here a communion of
appellation, you imagine nothing equal, so also here when you hear son and
son, do not bring the Only-Begotten down to your cheapness. But what need is
there to speak concerning the clear things? For if the prayer is of the
Godhead, he himself will be found falling upon himself and contradicting and
fighting. For this one who said here, Father, if it is possible, let this cup
pass from me, and shrinking and drawing back from the passion, elsewhere
having said that the Son of man must be delivered up and scourged, when he
heard Peter saying, Be it far from you, this shall not be to you, so strongly
rebuked him as to say, Get behind me, Satan; you are a scandal to me, because
you do not think the things of God, but the things of men. And yet a little
before he had praised him and blessed him, but nevertheless he called him
Satan, not wishing to insult the Apostle, but wishing to show through the
insult that what was said was not according to his mind, but so alien, that he
did not shrink from naming the one who had said these things, although he was
Peter, Satan. Again elsewhere he says: With desire I have desired to eat this
Passover with you. For what reason then does he say this Passover? And yet at
other times also he celebrated this feast with them; for what reason then?
Because after it was the cross. And again, Father, glorify your Son, that your
Son also may glorify you. And =48.765= in many places we find him both
foretelling the passion, and desiring that it


come to be, and for this reason having come. How then does he say here, If it
is possible? He shows us the weakness of the human nature, which does not
simply choose to be torn away from the present life, but draws back and
shrinks because of the friendship toward the present life that was put into it
from the beginning by God. For if, when he had said so many and such great
things, some dared to say that he did not take on flesh, if none of these
things had been said, what would they not have said? For this reason he
foretells there as God, and desires that the passion come to be; but here he
flees and declines it as man. For that he was coming to the passion willingly,
he says, I have authority to lay down my soul, and I have authority to take
it. No one takes it from me, I lay it down from myself. How then does he say,
Not as I will, but as you? And why do you wonder, if before the cross he made
so much haste to confirm the truth of the flesh, when even after the
resurrection, seeing the disciple unbelieving, he did not refuse to show him
both wounds and prints of nails, and to submit the scars to the touch of a
hand, and to say, Search and see that a spirit does not have flesh and bones?
For this reason he did not from the beginning take on the man of complete age,
but he endured also to be conceived, and to be born, and to be nursed with
milk, and to spend so much time upon the earth, in order that by the length of
the time, and by all the other things, he might confirm this very thing. For
since angels often appeared upon the earth in the form of a man, and God
himself also, and what appeared was not truth of flesh, but condescension;
lest you should think this presence also to be such as those came to be, but
that you might truly be made to believe that it was true flesh, he was also
conceived, and was born, and was fed, and was laid in a manger, not in some
little house, but in an inn, an infinite crowd being present, in order that
his birth might be made public. For this reason also swaddling-clothes, and
prophecies from above saying this very thing, not that he will be man only,
but that he will also be conceived, and will be born, and will be fed by the
law of children. And Isaiah cries this saying: Behold the virgin shall have in
the womb, and shall bear a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel; he
shall eat butter and honey; and again, A child was born to us, a son also was
given to us. Do you see how they also proclaimed beforehand his former age?
Ask then the heretic: Does God cower, and draw back, and shrink, and grieve?
and if he should say yes, stand off thereafter, and set him below with the
devil, or rather even lower than that one; for not even that one will dare to
say this; but if he should say that none of these things is worthy of God,
say, therefore neither does God pray; for apart from these things there will
be another absurdity also, if the words should be of God. For the words do not
show agony only, but also two wills, one of the Son, and one of the Father,
contrary to one another; for the saying, Not as I will, but as you, is of one
showing this. But not even those men ever granted this, but when we always say
the, I and the Father are one, of the power, those men say this was said of

7. I wished also to go through the remaining causes, and to show upon the
deeds themselves, that just as here he prayed convicting the weakness of the
flesh, so also elsewhere again correcting the weakness of those who hear; for
one must not think that all the things said in a lowly way have been said only
because of the clothing of the flesh, but also because of the other causes
which I said; but lest I should flood the multitude of the things said with
the surplus of the things about to be said, having stopped the discourse
toward those men here, and putting off the things that remain to another day,
I will turn again to an exhortation of prayer. For even if it has often been
said by us concerning this subject, yet it is necessary to say it now also.
For of garments, those dyed once have the dye fading, but those which the
dyers let down and draw up many times and continually keep the bloom of the
color unmoved; this then happens also in the case of our souls. For when we
hear the same words often, having received the teaching as some dye, we would
not easily spit it out. Let us not then hear as a side-matter; for there is
not, there is not anything more powerful than prayer, nor equal. A king
wrapped in purple is not so splendid as the one who prays, being adorned with
converse toward God. For just as if, an army being present, generals, rulers,
many consuls, someone coming forward should converse with the king privately,
he turns the eyes of all toward himself, and by this becomes more august; so
also it would be in the case of =48.767= those who pray. For consider how
great it will be, angels being present, archangels standing by, the Seraphim,
the Cherubim, all the other powers, that he himself, being a man, is able to
approach with much boldness, and to converse with the king of those powers; of
how much honor would this not be worth? And not honor only, but also a very
great benefit would come to us from the prayer, even before we receive what we
ask. For as soon as one has stretched the hands toward heaven, and has called
upon God, he has immediately stood off from all human affairs, and has
transferred in mind to the life to come, and imagines thereafter the things in
the heavens, and has nothing toward the present life, at the time of the
prayer, if he prays with accuracy; but even if anger should boil up, it is put
to sleep easily, even if desire should burn, it is quenched, even if envy
should waste, it is driven away with much ease; and this comes to be, which
the prophet says, when the sun is rising. And what does that one say? You set
darkness, and it became night; in it all the beasts of the thicket will go
through, young lions roaring to snatch and to seek from God food for
themselves; the sun rose, and they were gathered, and they will lie down in
their dens. As then when the ray is rising all the beasts are put to flight,
and sink down toward their own lairs; so also when the prayer, as some ray,
has appeared from our mouth and tongue, the mind is enlightened, and all the
irrational and beastly passions desert and are put to flight, and


sink down toward their own lairs, only if we pray with accuracy, if with a
soul that has been awakened and a mind that is sober. Then even if the devil
should be present he is put to flight, even if a demon, he withdraws. For if
when a household-servant is conversing with a master, none of the
fellow-servants, nor any of those who have boldness, would dare to come
forward and annoy, much more these, being of those who have offended and are
without boldness, would not be able to annoy us when we are conversing with
God with the =48.768= fitting eagerness.

Prayer is a harbor of those in a storm, an anchor of those tossed by waves, a
staff of those shaken, a treasure of the poor, a security of those who are
rich, a destruction of diseases, a guard of health; prayer both keeps the
goods unmoved for us, and quickly changes the evils; and if a trial should
come upon us, it is easily beaten back, and if a loss of money, and if any
other of the things that grieve our soul, it drives all away quickly; prayer
is a place of flight for all grief, a ground of good cheer, a starting-point
of continual pleasure, a mother of philosophy. He who is able to pray with
accuracy, even if he should be poorer than all, is richer than all; just as he
who is again deprived of prayer, even if he should sit on the royal throne
itself, is poorer than all. Was not Ahab a king, and did he have unspeakable
gold and silver? but since he did not have prayer, he was going about seeking
Elijah, a man who did not even have a lodging, nor a garment, except a
sheepskin only. What is this? tell me; you who have so many storehouses, do
you seek the one who has nothing? Yes, he says. For what benefit to me of the
treasuries, when this man has shut the heaven, and has made all things
useless? Did you see how this one was more well-supplied than that one? For
when this one had spoken, the king was in much poverty with the whole army. O
the wonder, he did not even have a garment, and he shut the heaven. For this
very reason then he shut the heaven, since he did not have a garment; since he
possessed nothing here, for this reason he displayed much power; for opening
the lips only, he made ten thousand treasures of goods be brought down from
above. O mouth that has springs of rainstorms; O tongue that sends forth
flakes of rains; O voice that gushes ten thousand goods. Looking then always
toward this poor and rich man, the one rich for this reason, because he was
poor, let us look past the present things, let us desire the things to come.
For thus we shall attain both the goods here and all those there; which may it
come to pass that we all attain, by the grace and loving-kindness of our Lord
Jesus Christ, with whom glory to the Father, together with the Holy Spirit,
now and always, and unto the ages of the ages, Amen.
