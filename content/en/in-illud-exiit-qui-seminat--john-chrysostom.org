On the saying, A sower went out to sow his seed.
=61.771=
The virgin field, furrowed by the ploughs of skilled farmers with the help of
the oxen, receives the seeds, and having folded them in the clods, first
sprouts the blade, then lengthens the ear, then puts forth the grain full in
the ear of wheat. Then the farmers, seeing the bright crop of the ears, honor
the hopes of their own toils, and they hasten to beseech God sincerely, that
he may keep unharmed the fruits of the goods that appear. For often, when the
height of harvest draws near, and the hope is within easy reach, and the farmer
is overjoyed, either a hot wind blowing upon it has winnowed the ear, or rust
has rotted it, or the locust has eaten it, or hail has shredded it, and has
withered the untimely end of hope: so that the farmer, seized by great
despondency, having put the sickle to sleep in the tent, wastes himself away
with grief at the =61.772= untimely end of the harvest.
But whenever the temperateness of the airs has made the ear bright and ripe,
and the crop, whitening like an image of old men, is bowed, awaiting the
cutting of the harvest, then country folk, and palms stretched out with sharp
sickles, add toils to toils, and they lay their longing to rest, feeding their
hopes with the end itself. Then, while the other animals rest at home, the ox
alone goes to the threshing-floor with cheerful step and grim gaze. And the
other animals, goats and swine and sheep, as many as did not submit their own
neck to the yoke of the plough, being choked by their own conscience,
approaching the threshing-floor with slavish approaches, plunder the sheaf of
the ears; but the ox alone, having stretched out a free tongue, boldly draws
the fruits of his own toils.
You saw =61.773= there the reapers, you saw there the ear-bearing field, the
threshing-floor, the ox: pass over with me from there in the discourse to the
good order of the Church, and you will see the husbandry of the things already
said in the mystery of the Church. A sower went out, he says, to sow. For we
just now heard this in the Gospel. Who went out? Christ, coming from heaven
into the world. For Christ is the sower, the seed is the divine word, the field
is humanity, the oxen are the apostles, the plough is the cross, the yoke is
concord, the yoke-collar that binds the necks of the apostles is sweet love. A
sower went out to sow. Christ went out, not that he might reap, but that he
might sow; for the seeds of the resurrection had not yet been sown in the
hearts of men. A sower went out to sow, not wheat, not barley, not millet, nor
any other earthly thing, but faith in Father and Son and Holy Spirit, hope of
resurrection, love toward God and men unfeigned and without distinction.
Christ went out sowing from the Father's loins, he who is in the bosom of the
Father, and he came to us, as he himself says, speaking: I came forth from the
Father and have come. But he went out from the Father, not being separated
from him, yet being with us. The sower went out, that he might root out the
tares which the devil had sown evilly in the souls of men. For every plant, he
says, which my heavenly Father has not planted, shall be rooted up.
The sun of righteousness went out, that he might sprinkle the light-casting
rays of his Godhead in the hearts of darkened men. The shepherd of the sheep
went out, that having sown the salt of piety he might season for the sheep the
grass of the legal letter and make it palatable; the sower went out, having
the ten yokes of oxen, that he might make the one jar, and having sown six
artabas, might make three measures. Speak now, Isaiah, do not be silent. For
where ten yokes of oxen work, it shall make one jar. Ten yokes of oxen are
twenty oxen; the ten rational yokes of oxen of Christ are the twelve apostles,
and the seven whom the apostles chose. Who are these? Stephen, Philip,
Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon, and Parmenas, and Nicolas a proselyte of Antioch;
and the apostle who farmed from Jerusalem as far as Illyricum.
These ten yokes of oxen made, in the field of humanity, the confession of
Jesus, who put on the earthen vessel of our body. And that the divine word
calls the incarnate sojourning of Christ a jar, hear. When he was about to go
away to suffer, his disciples say to him: Where do you want us to prepare the
Passover? And he to them: Go into such a city, and a man carrying a jar of
water will meet you, and say to him: Our Teacher said: I keep the Passover at
your house. And he will show you an upper room furnished, and there prepare
the Passover for us. And who is the man who bears the jar of water, or what is
the city into which he sends them, or what is the furnished upper room, it is
necessary that you inquire and learn thoroughly. The man carrying the jar of
water is John the forerunner of Christ, proclaiming Jesus who put on the jar
of our body, having in himself the water of baptism. And the furnished upper
room he calls the rest that will be in the heavens for the saints after the
resurrection. Go into such a city: not into the old synagogue of the Jews,
where the old law went sour in the old wineskins; for mixing vinegar with gall
they offered it to the Lord. But go into the new city, into the new Jerusalem,
where the new word is, where the new grace of the Spirit in the new wineskins
gushes forth the must of faith; not where the old Passover is, but where the
new resurrection; not where the lamb is sacrificed, but where Christ delivers;
not where the old leaven is, but where the heavenly bread is perfected upon
the cross. For where ten yokes of oxen work, it shall make one jar.
Where ten yokes of oxen work, of the apostles (for the apostles were likened
to oxen, as Paul bears witness to me, saying: It is written, he says, in the
law, You shall not muzzle an ox treading out the grain. Does God, he says,
care for oxen? But he says it for our sake); where then =61.774= these ten
yokes of oxen work, they make the confession of Jesus, who put on the jar of
our body. And he who sows six artabas shall make only three measures. Has such
barrenness of farmers ever happened, that six artabas should be sown, and make
only three measures? But let us seek the mind of Scripture, beloved; let us
enter into the inner veil of the letter, and the divine knowledge of the
Spirit will meet us. He who sows six artabas shall make three measures. Six
artabas are the New Testament: that is, the four evangelists, and Acts, and
apostles.
He who has sown these six artabas in a certain soul makes the three measures,
the confession of Father and Son and Holy Spirit. The sower went out to sow,
not wheat that makes bread, but faith that makes alive; not barley for the food
of irrational beasts, but nourishment for the breath of men; not millet that
feeds birds, but a divine word, fiery, likened to a grain of mustard, strongly
heating our souls toward piety and understanding. The sower went out to sow.
And some fell by the way, and was trodden down, and the birds of heaven came
and ate it; other fell among the thorns, and the thorns choked the sprout;
other fell on the rocky places, and because it had no root, having sprung up
it immediately withered.
Those by the way are Arians, the thorny are Eunomians, those on the rocky
places are Marathonians. And learn accurately, beloved. A sower went out to
sow; and some fell by the way. He did not say, In the way, but, By the way.
What is the way, if not altogether Christ who says, I am the way and the
truth? Those by the way, then, are the Arians. They are not in the way; for
they are not orthodox; nor again far from the way, as Greeks or Jews, but by
the way: that is, beside Christ, beside the right faith. Their confessing
Christ is what makes them near the way; their denying that Christ is Son of
God is what prevents them from being in the way. For this reason the birds of
heaven coming down, that is, the spiritual hosts of wickedness, gather the
divine word from their hearts, and make them altogether empty of right faith.
And the thorny are Eunomians; for they have in themselves the thorns of
blasphemies; and they sprout the blade of faith, but they do not bring to
completion the ear of confession. Their confessing Christ makes them sprout
the blade of faith; but their saying again creature and made thing is what
makes the ear of their faith wither untimely. For pricking and choking the
word of truth with the Hellenic doctrines of creature-worship, they make it
fruitless and altogether barren of right faith. And those on the rocky places,
these are Marathonians. Scripture did not say that some fell on the rock; for
the rock was Christ; but On the rocky places. For they are neither good earth;
for they are not orthodox; nor are they rock, that being hewn by teachers they
may build the temple of God; but what? They are tufa. There is a kind of stone
named tufa (poros); it is neither earth nor solid stone; soft beside the
stone, hard beside the earth. Their confessing the Son of God like the Father
according to essence is what makes them softer; their denying the Holy Spirit
is what makes their hearts to be calloused as tufa. Therefore they are neither
good earth, that they may sprout flourishingly and be adorned with the
ornament of faith; nor solid rock, that they may keep unshaken and unmoved the
confession of Father and Son and Holy Spirit. And they receive this word and
sprout; but immediately they dry it and chill it, because they do not have the
roots of their reasonings in the depth of the Spirit. These, even if they
confess Christ to be Son of God, denying the Holy Spirit, do not even have the
Son, the apostle bearing witness to me and saying: If anyone has not the
Spirit of Christ, this one is not his. The others, he says, fell on the good
earth, and gave fruit, and some made thirty, some sixty, some a hundred.
This is the good earth, the heart of the orthodox, which is outside =61.775=
the thorns of the heretics, which has been worked by perfect teachers and as
it were furrowed, and which receives the words of the Gospel that are seeds of
the soul. Such a heart first sprouts the blade of faith, then lengthens the
ear of hope, then shows forth the ripe fruit of perfect love. Of these three
fruits of the soul the blessed Paul also makes mention, saying: Faith, hope,
love. He who believes makes the thirty; he who hopes makes the sixty; he who
is perfected in love makes the hundred; he who is anointed makes the thirty;
he who goes down into the water makes the sixty; he who is perfected in the
mystery makes the hundred. Thus from one seed it brings forth fruit threefold:
it glorifies God, it exalts the Church, it saves itself. This one will receive
the rewards of the good Trinity: understanding with the spirit, looking
forward with the soul, enduring with the body.
This one is blessed threefold: he is glorified on earth, he rises from the
dead, he rests in the heavens. Such a one is honored in three surpassing
orders: thirty, as in the heavens; sixty, as among angels; a hundred, as with
God. Such a one is meek, quiet, humble-minded, merciful, compassionate,
=61.776= just, a stranger to the world, alien to this life, appearing on earth
in image only, but dwelling in the heavens in heart and deeds. But certain
others of the heretics, according to their own thankless mind, finely
craft a ladder of blasphemy with their words; saying what? Faith in the Spirit
makes the thirty; that in the Son makes the sixty; that in the Father makes
the hundred; not knowing that as they wish to belittle, they magnify the Holy
Spirit.
For they put the Spirit before Father and Son, saying: He who believes in the
Spirit makes the thirty; and first they put the Spirit before the Father, then
the Son, then the Father. But we, thinking the opposite of them, say the
opposite. For he who believes does not first believe in the Spirit, but in the
Father; he who believes in the Father makes the thirty; he who takes on in
addition the Son doubles the number and makes the sixty; he who has also put
on the Holy Spirit has filled the hundred, in Christ Jesus our Lord; to whom
be the glory and the might unto the ages of ages. Amen.
