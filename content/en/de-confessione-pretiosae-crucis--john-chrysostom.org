* On the Confession of the Precious Cross
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of the same, a discourse: That one must not be ashamed to confess the precious
cross, and that through it Christ saved us, and that we must boast in it, and
concerning virtue, and how he longs for our salvation, and concerning
almsgiving.
=52.841=
1 This, brethren, is the sign which the Master promised to give to all,
saying: An evil and adulterous generation seeks a sign, and no sign shall be
given it except the sign of Jonah the prophet; meaning the cross, and the
death, and the burial, and the resurrection. And again, declaring the power of
the cross in another way, he said: When you have lifted up the Son of Man,
then you will know that I am. When you crucify me, he says, and suppose that
you have got the better of me, then especially you will know my power. And
Christ spoke well.
For after Christ was crucified the Jewish customs ceased, the preaching
blossomed, the word was stretched out to the ends of the inhabited world; and
earth and sea, and inhabited and uninhabited, continually proclaim his power.
Let no one then be ashamed of the august symbols of our salvation, and the
chief of good things, through which we both live and through which we are; but
as a crown, so let us carry about the cross of Christ. For all that concerns
us is accomplished through it; if we must be born again, the cross is present;
if we must be fed with that mystical food, if we must be ordained, if we must
do anything else whatsoever, everywhere the symbol of our victory stands by.
For this reason both in houses, and on the walls, and on the doors, and on the
forehead, and in the mind, we inscribe it with great eagerness. For this is
the sign of the salvation wrought for us, and of our common freedom, and of
the gentleness of our Master: For he was led as a sheep to the slaughter. When
therefore you seal yourself, consider the whole matter of the cross, and
quench anger, and all the other passions; when you seal yourself, fill the
forehead with great boldness, make the soul free. And you know well what
things they are that furnish us with freedom.
Wherefore Paul also, leading us to this, I mean to the freedom that belongs to
us, led us up thus, having reminded us of the cross and of the Master's
blood: For you were bought with a price, he says; do not become slaves of men.
Consider, he says, the price paid down for you, and you will be slave of no
man; calling the price the blood through the cross. For one must not simply
carve it with the finger, but first with the will in much faith, and so
imprint it upon the face. None of the unclean demons will be able to stand
near you, seeing the sword by which he received the blow, seeing the blade in
which he took the mortal wound. For if we, seeing places in which the
condemned are =52.842= beheaded, shudder, consider what the devil will suffer,
seeing the weapon by which Christ dissolved all his power and cut off the
dragon's head.
2 Do not then be ashamed of so great a good, lest Christ be ashamed of you,
when he comes with his glory, and the sign appears before him, shining above
the ray of the sun. For the cross will then appear, sending forth a voice
through its appearance, and making a defense before the whole inhabited world
on behalf of the Master, and showing that he left undone nothing of the things
that belonged to them. This sign also opened closed doors among our ancestors,
this quenched deadly poisons, this dissolved the power of hemlock, this healed
the bites of venomous beasts.
For if it opened the gates of Hades, and unfolded the vaults of heaven, and
renewed the entrance of paradise, and cut out the sinews of the devil, what
wonder if it prevails over deadly drugs and beasts and the rest of such
things? Engrave this then upon your mind, and embrace the salvation of our
souls. For this cross converted the inhabited world, drove out error, brought
back the truth, made the earth heaven, fashioned men into angels. For this
reason the demons are no longer fearful, but contemptible; nor is death death,
but sleep. For this reason all things that war against us have been thrown
down and trampled. If then someone says to you, Do you worship the crucified?
say with a bright voice and a rejoicing face, Both I worship, and I will never
cease worshipping; and if he laughs, weep for him, because he is mad. Give
thanks to the Master, that he has bestowed such benefits upon us, which no one
can even learn without revelation from above. For this is why he too laughs,
because The natural man does not receive the things of the Spirit; since
children also suffer this, when they see something great and wonderful; and if
you bring a child into the mysteries, he will laugh. To these children the
Greeks also are like, or rather they are even more immature than they, and
therefore more wretched, because they suffer the things of children not in an
unripe age but in a mature one, whence they are not even worthy of pardon. But
let us, crying out with a bright voice, loud and high, shout and say, even if
all the Greeks are present, let us speak with greater boldness, that Our boast
is the cross, and the chief of all good things, and the boldness and the whole
crown. I wished I were able to say also with Paul, that The world has been
crucified to me, and I to the world; but I cannot, being held by various
passions. Wherefore I exhort both you and, before you, myself to be crucified
to the world, and to have nothing in common with the earth, but to love the
fatherland above, and =52.843= the glory from there, and the eternal good
things; for we are soldiers of a heavenly king, and we have put on spiritual
weapons. Why then do we take up the life of hucksters and mountebanks, or
rather of worms? For where the king is, there the soldier also must be. For we
have become soldiers, not of those far off, but of those near. For the king
upon earth would not endure that all should be in the palaces, nor at his very
sides; but the king of the heavens wishes all to be near the royal throne. And
how is it possible, someone says, for those who are here to stand by that
throne? Because Paul also, being upon earth, was where the Seraphim were,
where the Cherubim were, and this man was nearer to Christ than those
shield-bearers are to the king. For these carry their eyes about in many
directions; but nothing made a show before him, nor dragged him about, but he
had his whole mind stretched toward the king and Lord.
3 So that if we wish, this is possible for us also. For if he were separated
by place, you would well be at a loss; but if he is present everywhere, he is
near to the one who is eager and intent. For this reason the prophet also
said, I will fear no evil, for you are with me. And God himself again: I am a
God who draws near, and not a God far off. As then sins set us apart from him,
so also righteous deeds gather us to him. For while you are still speaking, he
says, I will say, Behold, I am here. What father would ever so hearken to his
offspring? what mother is so prepared and continually standing, if ever the
children should call her? There is none, neither father nor mother. But God
stands continually waiting, if ever one of his servants should call him, and
he never, when we have called as we ought, failed to hear.
For this reason he says, While you are still speaking; I do not wait for you
to finish, and I will hearken at once. Let us then call him as he wishes to be
called. And how does he wish? Loose, he says, every bond of injustice, undo
the knots of violent contracts, tear apart every unjust writing. Break your
bread to the hungry, and bring the homeless poor into your house. If you see
one naked, clothe him, and you shall not overlook those of your own seed. Then
shall your light break forth early, and your healings shall speedily arise,
and your righteousness shall go before you, and the glory of God shall cover
you. Then call upon me, and I will hear you; while you are still speaking, I
will say, Behold, I am here. And who, someone says, can do all these things?
And who cannot, tell me? For what of the things said is hard? what is
toilsome? what is not easy? For they are so not only possible but even easy,
that many have even overshot the measure, not only tearing apart unjust notes,
but also stripping themselves of all they had; not receiving the poor with
roof and table, but even toiling with the sweat of the body so as to nourish
them, benefiting not kindred only but even enemies. And what of the things
said is at all difficult? For he did not say, Cross the mountain, pass through
the sea, dig so many and so many acres of earth, remain without food, put on
sackcloth; but, Share the house, share the bread, tear apart the notes that
lie unjustly.
What is easier than these, tell me? And if you think them difficult also,
consider also the prizes, and all will be easy for you. For just as kings in
the horse-races set crowns and =52.844= garments and prizes before the
contestants; so also Christ sets the prizes in the midst of the stadium,
stretching them out as it were through many hands, the words of the prophet.
And the kings, being men, and having a wealth that is spent, and an ambition
that is consumed, are ambitious to make the little appear much; wherefore
putting each single thing into the hands of each of the attendants, they thus
bring them into the midst. But our king, on the contrary, having gathered all
together at once, since he is exceedingly wealthy, and does nothing for
display, thus sets forth in the midst things which, when stretched out, will
be infinite, and will need many hands to hold them. And that you may learn
this, examine each of them with accuracy. Then shall your light break forth
early, he says. Does it not seem to you to be one gift? But it is not one. For
it has many things within, both crowns and prizes, and other rewards, and if
you wish, having unloosed it we will show all the wealth as far as it is
possible for us to display, only do not grow weary. And first let us learn
what Break forth is. It indicates to us the swift and the abundant, and how
greatly he longs for our salvation, and how he is in labor that the good
things themselves should come forth, and he is urgent; and there will be
nothing hindering the unspeakable rush. Through all which he shows their
abundance, and the infinity of the surplus. And what is Early? That is, not
after coming to be in trials, nor after the onset of evils, but to appear
beforehand.
For just as in the case of fruits we call early that which appeared before the
season, so here also indicating again the swiftness he spoke thus, as he said
above, While you are still speaking, I will say, Behold, I am here. And what
light does he mean, and what is this, The light? Not this sensible one, but
another far better, which shows us heaven, the angels, the archangels, the
Cherubim, the Seraphim, the thrones, the dominions, the principalities, the
powers, the whole army, the royal cities, the tents. For if you are counted
worthy of that light, you will both see these things, and you will be
delivered from Gehenna, and from the venomous worm, and from the gnashing of
teeth, and from the painful bonds, and from the straitness, and from the
affliction, and from the lightless darkness, and from being cut asunder, and
from the rivers of fire, and from the curse, and from the places of pain, and
you will go away, where pain and sorrow have fled, where there is much joy,
and peace, and love, and delight and gladness; where there is eternal life,
and unspeakable glory, and inexpressible beauty; where there are eternal
tents, and the ineffable glory of the king, and those good things which Eye
has not seen, and ear has not heard, and which have not entered into the heart
of man; where the spiritual bridechamber and the nuptial chambers of the
heavens, and the virgins who have the bright lamps, and those who have the
wedding garment; where there are palaces of the Master, and the royal
treasuries. Did you see how great the prizes are, and how many he displayed
through one saying, and how he gathered all together? Thus also, unfolding
each of the following sayings, we shall find much surplus and an open sea.
Let us not then put off, and be slow to have mercy on those in need; do not, I
beseech you; but even if we must throw all away, even if we must be cast into
fire, even if we must dare the sword, even if we must leap upon knives, even
if we must suffer anything whatsoever; let us bear all easily, that we may
obtain the garment of the kingdom of the heavens, and that unspeakable glory;
of which may it be that we all obtain, by the grace and loving-kindness of our
Lord Jesus Christ, to whom be the glory and the power unto the ages of ages.
Amen.
