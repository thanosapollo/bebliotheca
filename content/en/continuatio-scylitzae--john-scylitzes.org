hinted at the onset and dissolution of the said nation. For in the divine
signs not the present only, but also something future has been seen beforehand
and is signified. And in the month of May of the 4th indiction a comet

becomes full-moon. And it seemed then to send forth smoke and mist, and on the
following day it began to show forth certain locks, and as much as these were
spread out the magnitude of the star abated. And it stretched its rays toward
the east and proceeded toward that and was prevailing until forty days. And
Constantine the patriarch having died, John the monk surnamed Xiphilinos is
appointed, having started from Trebizond, a wise man and having gone to the
peak of education and having become conspicuous in political affairs and
attending to virtue with good sense, so as in the prime of prosperity and of
age to embrace the monastic way of life and to choose the anchoretic life for
a time not small beside the mountain of Olympus. Whence no one except him was
thought worthy toward the high rank of the patriarchate, even if refusing and
fleeing he was pursued toward the honor. But indeed also Theodoulos the high

Michael having appeared to them with the brothers they announce that the event

also proclaimed him with great and piercing voices. As then it appeared from

passage to the opponents. For these reasons therefore after a delay of two

given out by the previously said raids, were in extreme dangers. And in the

serviceable toward nothing noble, so that even the standards themselves
silently uttered this, being seen squalid as if darkened from the soot of
smoke, and having attendants few to count and poor. These things being seen by
those present brought and produced much despondency, as they reckoned from

advantage judged, as it was possible, to array himself against the enemies and
somewhat to check and restrain them from the great onset. For the opponents,
not knowing the things at home pressing and distressing the emperor, the
sudden onset and attack of this man rather astounded, having thought, as we
later learned, that this man has made no account of dangers, but being a pupil
of Ares he will make new and rejuvenate the Romans' affairs and will
counterpoise to the enemies the wages. <Through> these reasons therefore the

and he commands the other to become more northern. And the emperor, having

them take courage again with dignities and gifts, and having carefully set the
best captains and taxiarchs over each tagma, in a short time established a
considerable army and with it more eagerly drove upon the Persians. And when
the more northern camp of the Persians, having previously imagined a certain
raid, furnished an opinion of withdrawal from the emperor's advance, Diogenes
thereafter knew to fall upon the southern ones, who were plundering the parts
about Coele Syria and Cilicia and Antioch itself. And having left going
straight of Koloneia and Sebasteia he sojourned in the theme of Lykandos at
the time of summer; for in the autumn he planned to approach the Syrian parts,
so that the camp, not having been distressed by the burning heat, might not
fare badly and be maimed. Meanwhile the Turks, having come upon Neocaesarea

bringing a weight of booty 127 not least. Which also having fallen into the
emperor's ears dripped much grief into him, that, although he had campaigned
out, the enemies no less devour his country before his face.

behind at full rein. And having drawn near to Sebasteia he leaves the military
baggage and all his infantry somewhere there with his own stepson Andronikos

enjoy their own life, having become a work of the sword. The whole booty

done was reckoned wonderful, how the emperor of the Romans drove against them

used the affairs ambitiously from fear of failure (for he was differing in
prudence and being cautious about the future) a nearly double war occurred to
the emperor being deprived of such a force. For often when the enemies called

having fallen in with a certain least portion going out for a market of grain,

things of the outcomes, whether they are gathered toward the better or toward
the worse, he does not wholly miss the right and the true discernment; and
well has it been apophthegmed by the ancients that it is better that a lion
rule deer than that a deer rule lions. But the emperor having thus been saved
