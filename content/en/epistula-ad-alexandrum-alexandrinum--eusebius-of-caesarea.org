* Letter to Alexander of Alexandria
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

1 With how much agony and with how much care I came to this letter; 2 Your
letter accuses them as saying that the Son has come to be from the not-being
as one of all. And they brought forward a writing of themselves, which they
have made toward you, in which having set out their own faith they confessed
these things in their own words: "the God of law and prophets and new covenant
who begot an only-begotten Son before eternal times, through whom he has also
made the ages and all things, having begotten not in seeming but in truth,
having given subsistence by his own will, unchangeable and unalterable, a
perfect creature of God, but not as one of the creatures". If then the letter
from them speaks truly, and it is altogether also carried with you, in which
they confess the Son of God before eternal times, through whom he has also
made the ages, to be unchangeable and a perfect creature of God, but not 3 as
one of the creatures. And your letter accuses them as if saying that the Son
has come to be as one of the creatures. They not saying this, but clearly
defining that "not as one of the creatures", see if not immediately again a
starting-point is given to them unto 4 taking hold and setting out to slander
whatever they also wish. Again you blamed them saying that "the one who is
begot the one who is not". And I wonder if anyone can speak otherwise. For if
the one who is is one, it is clear that from him has come to be everything
whatsoever also is after him; and if not he alone is the one who is, but the
Son also was the one who is, and how did the one who is beget the one who is?
For thus there would be two beings.
