On the beheading of the Forerunner and Baptist John, and on Herodias.
=59.485= Again Herodias rages, again she is troubled, again she dances, again
she seeks the head of John the Baptist to be lawlessly cut off by Herod.
Again Jezebel goes about seeking to seize the vineyard of Naboth, and to hunt
down the holy Elijah upon the mountains. And I think that not I only am beside
myself, but also all of you who hear the voice of the Gospel, and that you
marvel with me at the boldness of John, and the lightness of Herod, and the
beastly madness of the godless women. For what did we hear? For Herod, having
seized John, put him in prison. Why? Because of Herodias, the wife of Philip
his brother. And one might blame the lightness of Herod, driven by wretched
women. But what could one say, or how could one express the unbridled
wickedness of those women? It seems to me that there is no beast in the world
that matches a wicked woman. But now my word is about a wicked woman, not
about a good and sober one. For I know many seemly and good women, whose life
I must recall for the building up and love of what is fair.
There is then no =59.486= beast in the world that matches a wicked woman. What
is more terrible than a lion among four-footed beasts? Nothing. And what is
more savage than a dragon among creeping things? Nothing. Yet both lion and
dragon are lesser in evil. And the most wise Solomon bears witness to my word,
saying: I have been pleased to dwell with a lion and a dragon, rather than
with a wicked and talkative woman. And lest you think the prophet has spoken
in irony, learn it accurately from the facts themselves. The lions in the den
reverenced Daniel, but Jezebel murdered the just Naboth; the sea-monster kept
Jonah in its belly, but Delilah, having shaved and bound Samson, delivered him
to the foreigners; dragons and asps and horned vipers trembled at John in the
wilderness, but Herodias beheaded him at a banquet; the ravens nourished
Elijah on the mountain, but Jezebel, after the benefaction of the rain,
pursued him toward murder. For what did she say? If you are Elijah, I also am
Jezebel: So may the gods do to me, and so may they add, if by tomorrow at this
hour I do not make your soul as one of the dead. And Elijah was afraid, and he
went according to his soul, and he went away into the wilderness a journey of
forty =59.487= days. And he came under a broom-tree, and he asked that his
soul might die, and he said: Lord God, it is enough for me now; take my soul
from me, for I am not better than my fathers. Woe is me! Did the prophet
Elijah fear a woman? He who bore the rain of the inhabited world upon his
tongue, who brought down fire from heaven, and raised the dead by prayer,
feared a woman? Yes, he feared. For no wickedness is compared with a wicked
woman.
And Wisdom bears witness to my word, saying that There is no head above the
head of a serpent, and there is no wickedness above the wickedness of a woman.
O the evil of the devil, and his sharpest weapon! Through a woman from the
beginning he wounded Adam in paradise; through a woman he drove the most meek
David mad toward the murder of Uriah; through a woman he laid low the most
wise Solomon toward transgression; through a woman, having shaved the most
manly Samson, he blinded him; through a woman he dashed to the ground the sons
of Eli the priest; through a woman, having bound the most noble Joseph, he
shut him in prison; through a woman he beheaded John, the lamp of the whole
world. But why do I speak of men? Through a woman he cast the angels down from
heaven; through a woman he slaughters all, murders all, dishonors all, insults
all. For a shameless woman spares no one; she does not honor a Levite, she
does not revere a priest, she does not respect a prophet. O evil of evils, a
wicked woman most evil! If she is poor, she is rich in wickedness; but if she
has wealth cooperating with the wickedness, the evil is double, the creature
unbearable, an incurable disease, an untamed beast. I know even asps, being
flattered, to be tamed, and lions and tigers and leopards, being domesticated,
to be made gentle; but a wicked woman, even when insulted, rages, and when
flattered she is puffed up. If she has a ruler as husband, night and day,
prying him with words, she whets him toward murder, as Herodias did Herod; if
she has a poor husband, she stirs him toward angers and fights; if she happens
to be a widow, she herself through herself dishonors all. For she is not
bridled in the tongue by fear of the Lord, she does not look toward the
judgment to come, she does not look up to God, she does not know how to keep
the laws of friendship.
It is nothing to a wicked woman to deliver her own husband to death. At any
rate, his own wife delivered the just Job toward the death of blasphemy,
saying: Say some word against the Lord, and die. O wicked nature! O unholy
choice! She did not pity, seeing the inward parts of her own husband burned by
the boiling blisters, as by spark-casting coals, and all the flesh seized by
worms; she was not bent toward pity, seeing him wholly through and through
writhing, and toiling and in agony, and bringing continual gasps with pain
through a gaping mouth. She was not softened toward compassion, seeing the one
who once approached in royal purple then lying upon a dung-heap, stripped in
body; she did not remember the ancient intimacy toward him, nor how many
glorious and fair things she herself had blossomed through him. But what? Say
some word against the Lord, and die. O the grace of a wife! O softening
poultice of pains! O the law of a yoke-fellow's friendship! Did he ever, when
you were sick, utter such a word, and did he not wipe away your disease with
prayers and good works? Was the temporary chastisement not enough for him, but
you also procure for him eternal punishment through blasphemy? Or do you not
know that Every blasphemy and sin shall be forgiven men; but the blasphemy
against the Holy Spirit shall not be forgiven, neither in this age nor in the
one to come? Do you wish to see another yoke-fellow of this wickedness? Look
at Delilah for me. For she too, having shaved and bound the most manly Samson,
delivered him to the foreigners, her own husband, her own bedfellow, whom she
warmed, whom she fawned upon, whom she flattered, whom she pretended to love
above herself; whom yesterday she loved, today she deceived; whom yesterday
she warmed loving, today she was burying deceiving. And was he not fair? And
who was fairer than he at that time, who bore seven locks =59.488= upon his
head, bearing the image of the seven-lighted grace? And was he not manly? And
who was manlier than he, who alone throttled a fearful lion on the road, and
with one jawbone of an ass laid low a thousand foreigners? But was he not also
holy?
He was so holy that once, when he thirsted in scarcity of water, he prayed,
and from the dead jawbone held in his hand water sprang forth, and from there
he drew the healing of his thirst. And this one so fair, so manly, so holy,
his own wife, having bound him as an enemy, delivered to the foreigners. And
from where then did a woman prevail over one so manly? From the husband's own
goodness. For having stolen by night the mystery of his strength, she
overthrew him naked with strong thongs. For this reason Wisdom charges you:
Guard yourself from your bedfellow, from committing anything to her. What
beast, tell me, ever plotted such things against its own male? What she-dragon
wishes her own yoke-fellow to perish? And what lioness delivers her own male
to slaughter? Do you see that Wisdom spoke hitting the mark, that There is no
head above the head of a serpent, and there is no wickedness above the
wickedness of a woman? And in a word, let him who has a wicked wife know
already that the wages of his lawlessness have been paid. The word is not
without witness; hear Wisdom saying: A wicked woman shall be given to a
lawless man in return for wicked works. But let the word about a wicked woman
have its limit up to this.
2. But we must also recall the good women, especially on account of those
present. For the good see the virtues of the
good as their own, and they reckon those women's labors to be their own
crowns. A good and hospitable woman was the blessed Shunammite, who, having
exhorted her husband, built a little room for Elisha, that as he passed he
might have rest unhindered, having set for him a bed and a lampstand and a
table; a bed not empty of garments, but having coverings fitting for the
prophet; a lampstand not without a lamp, but with the oil that waters the
light; a table not barren of breads, but fruitful of foods. And what could one
say about that blessed widow who received the prophet Elijah? whom the poverty
of money in no way hindered, because of the richness of her choice? who had
present neither grain, nor wine, nor cooked food, nor any other of the things
of earth for the consolation of poverty. No grain-bearing land offered her
seed of bread; no vine farmed for her a sweet-flowing cluster; no tree offered
her a most pleasant fruit of autumn. For how, when she had neither a span of
arable earth as a place, nor a cubit's ground for the planting of a vine? but
always in the time of harvest, stooping in the fields, gleaning with her palms
the ears that fell from the reapers, she stored up food measured to the yearly
cycle of time. To this woman Elijah went away in the time of the famine, when
the whole earth was almost crumbling from the drought; when heaven was set on
fire, and the air was bronzed, and the clouds were bridled; when no herb, no
flower, no shoot of fruit-trees, no dewy breeze, no tip of an ear of grain was
stirred; when rivers were thinned, and springs squeezed the breasts of the
waters with the burning heat, and the sea was made briny, since sweet waters
were not entering it and rains had been held back. Then Elijah went away to
the poor woman and the widow. And what a widow suffers even in a time of
plenty, you know. And the prophet left the rich, those who had breads in
abundance, and having come down from the mountain, he came to her. For why
rather did he who brought down fire from heaven by words not bring down breads
for himself? Was he not able? Yes, he was able; but he did not do this. Why?
That he might not deprive the widow of the fruits of hospitality; and besides,
he multiplies the handful of meal and the scanty oil through the blessing.
For the prophet did not go away so much to be fed as to feed the poor woman,
and to prove a hidden and well-disposed heart. This God does, being able to
feed all the saints who are in the world through himself; he holds back the
gift, that he may distinguish the well-disposed hearts in the time of
hospitality =59.489= from the fruits. But whenever there are not some who
ought to receive them, either he feeds through birds, as Elijah on the
mountain, or through a foreign prophet, as Daniel in the den, or through a
sea-creature, as Jonah through the sea-monster, or he himself through himself
rained down the food, as for our fathers in the wilderness; for when there
were not those who ought to receive them, he rained manna from heaven, and
from a rock he made water spring; but when the saints are in the world living
among the others, he draws back his right hand; and even if he sees them
afflicted, he lets them be, that he may give much grace through the
good-doing that happens toward them to those who wish to harvest salvation.
So Elijah went away to the widow, who had nothing present except a handful of
meal, which with toil was enough for her and her children at the time of a
meal. And what to her? Take for me a little water into a vessel, and I will
drink. And as she was going, he cried out behind her: And you shall take for
me also a morsel of bread in your hand.
And she said what she did not have; what she had, she confessed. For what? As
the Lord lives, if there shall be for me a cake baked in the ashes, but only
so much as a handful of meal, and a little oil in the cruse.
It is marvelous that in such scarcity of foods she did not deny the remnant of
poverty present to her. How many now, having gold and silver as mud, when
asked refuse the benefactions of friends, and when flattered deny that they
have? and if by entreaty they are bent toward the giving, they compose
writings stronger than iron, and they first bind the receiving hand with the
documents, taking sureties and mediators of the gold. But she from one voice
did not deny the handful of meal. And what did the prophet say to her? Make
haste, and make me a cake, first for me, and last for yourself and your
children. The word of the prophet was a trial, a testing of heart, a proving
of choice; and the heart of that blessed woman was as if lying under a yoke,
and being tested. Where then does it incline? Toward =59.490= goodwill for
her own children, or toward hospitality for the prophet? And she chose rather
to straiten herself and her children, and to receive the prophet.
For she knew that He who receives a prophet in the name of a prophet shall
receive a prophet's reward; and he who gives a cup of cold water to drink in
the name of a disciple shall not lose his reward. And what is it that the
prophet said, Make haste? Did he hunger so much as to need haste? Not at all,
but the eager and cheerful and warm of the good-doing, not with grief, or with
necessity; for God loves a cheerful giver. Make haste and make first for me,
and last for yourself and your children. Make haste, as Abraham in the sojourn
of the angels hastened upon the oxen, upon the calf, that he might receive the
lamb; and as Sarah hastened upon the cakes, that she might receive the bread
hidden in the heavens. Make haste, and make as Abraham the sacrifices to God;
not first for yourself, and then for me, as Cain, and Hophni, and Phinehas,
sons of Eli the priest, who insulted God, taking first the firstfruits of the
gifts offered to God. And she did the thing commanded eagerly. And he, having
seen the rich ash-cake of bread, received it, spoke, and filled her house with
good things. For the handful of meal, he says, shall not fail from the jar,
and the oil from the cruse, until the Lord gives rain upon the earth. Why
until then? And of necessity. For it was necessary, when the new grace came as
rain, that the old law should take an end. And the work followed the word. Do
you see how the good women harvested the fruits of hospitality? For of good
labors the fruits are of good report, and unfailing is the root of prudence.
You have heard, women, the deeds of the wicked women, and the virtues of the
good. Love the latter then, and do not desire the former; and imitate the
latter, and hate the former, that having tracked their course, I mean of the
fair ones, you may be numbered in the same choir of the saints in Christ Jesus
our Lord, to whom be the glory and the might unto the ages of ages. Amen.
