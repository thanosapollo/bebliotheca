A Discourse of the Same on Fasting and Almsgiving.
=48.1059=
Fasting is good, and the reading of the Scriptures is good; but it is good
when the deed that comes from it also follows. For if you read and do not do,
the reading becomes for you a judgment, and a provision for punishment. For
the hearers of the law, he says, are not righteous before God, but the doers
of the law shall be justified; and again Christ: If I had not come and spoken
to them, they would not have sin; but now they have no excuse for their sin.
Blessed is he who speaks into the ears of those who hear, especially when they
pay the interest; and interest is obedience, and the working of the
commandments of God, as the Lord says: And I, when I came, would have demanded
my own with interest. What then have you gathered, brother, from the fast? For
the farmer sows for this, that he may reap, and the merchant makes his
journeys for this, that he may gather money, and the helmsman crosses many
seas for this, that he may fill the ship with cargo. Do not tell me, I fasted
so many days, I did not eat this and that, I did not drink wine, I endured
going unwashed; but show me whether, being wrathful, you became gentle, and
whether, being harsh, you became humane. For if you are drunk with anger, why
do you afflict the flesh? If envy is within and greed, what is the profit of
drinking water? I do not ask now what sort the table was, but whether there
was a change of evil disposition. If the mistress, I mean the soul, commits
fornication, why do you scourge the maidservant, I mean the belly? If the soul
is swept away, why do you waste the body? =48.1060= I say these things not
accusing you, but because of the careless. For as much as I see you flying, I
still want you to fly higher; such is the tyranny of love. And as the
money-lovers, as much gold as they gather, still seek more; so we too long
still more for your progress. If then you wish to be received by God, fast as
the Ninevites did, brother; they did not receive a law; of whom Paul says: For
when Gentiles who have not the law do by nature the things of the law, these,
not having the law, are a law to themselves. Do not then show the fast idle;
for fasting does not go up to heaven alone, if it does not have its sister,
almsgiving; for she is its yoke-fellow, and not only sisterhood and
yoke-fellow, but also a chariot.
And from where is this? The angel said to Cornelius: Your prayers and your
alms have gone up as a memorial before God. For almsgiving is a wing of
prayer; if then you do not make a wing for the prayer, it does not fly; but
when your soul is winged, it flies to heaven. How long the love of money and
the desire of possessions? All these things, brother, are dissolved together
with the present life. But you will surely say to me: Say these things to
yourself. And I say them to myself, brother, and to you; for the counsel is
common.
I, hearing these things and being set right, receive a favor from you; and if
it is a slave who speaks, I accept the counsel, and if he is free, I listen
eagerly. =48.1061= For it is not the difference of persons, but the use of the
counsel, that makes me accept what is said. For if that Moses, so great a man,
who spoke with God, did not turn away from the counsels of his father-in-law,
and that though he was a barbarian, but he too accepted them, and God
confirmed them: how much more we? I do not say to you, Become without
possessions, but spend the surplus on the poor, that your abundance may become
for you a ground of salvation.
Do you not see many poor along the road, how they remain sick and naked? and
one is younger, another older, and another is supported by another, and great
is their tragedy. Give then to the fellow-servant, that you may have the
Master as debtor, the one who gladly owes, who gives the principal with the
interest; for among those outside interest is a charge, but with God it is
praise. You do not give to the poor man? Attend to the one who asks through
him, and reverence the dignity of the one who receives; the poor man receives,
and God borrows. Consider where your Master came down for this, that he might
bend you not to be harsh and inhuman. For hungry, he says, you saw me, and you
did not feed me; thirsty, and you did not give me drink; a stranger, and you
did not take me in; naked, and you did not clothe me, and what follows. Will
you not then give to Christ when he hungers? You and the poor man partake in
common of his body from the table, you share his cup likewise; he shares with
you in the great and fearful things, and you do not share with him of the
small?
For is it your own that you give him? For even if you received it from your
parents, even if from your ancestors, they are God's. Why do you bury them in
the earth? Give to the poor man, and the Master keeps them for you with much
security. Do you not see what the husbandmen do? that often from poverty, not
having seed, he pawns his cloak, and takes what he wants, and trusts it to the
earth; and yet often an unseasonable time came, and he went away having reaped
nothing, and still he trusts the earth because of hope. What then the earth
does, can the Master not do this? Imitate that widow in the Old Testament, who
had a handful of flour in the jar, and a little oil in the cruse, because from
these she entertained the prophet; or this one in the Gospel, who casting two
obols outstripped all; for she cast her whole wealth. But what do you say? I
am poor and I have no money. Do you not have two obols? And if you do not even
have them, God seeks an abundance of good will. For this he said, Whoever
gives a cup of cold water shall not lose his reward. See what he says: of cold
water, not hot, lest through the expense of the wood you be deprived of the
reward. And a king, as much gold as he lays on you, hanging, being racked, you
give what he wants; and if you are poor, he does not attend to the power of
the persons, but to his own gain. But God is not so, but he requires according
to each one's ability. For why are there poor? Was God not able to send down a
rain of gold? But that that man's poverty might become for you a consolation
for your sins. A man is great, and a merciful man is honorable; see how great
almsgiving is.
God compares the merciful man to himself; For become, he says, compassionate,
as also your heavenly Father is compassionate. If death comes, the money stays
here; why then do you not send it on ahead for yourself there, that on that
day the poor may stand by you, where there is no orator, nor advocate? For the
poor display the garments and the bands, and they snatch you from the fire.
The sun, coming upon ice, does not so melt it, as almsgiving, falling upon a
multitude of sins, makes them vanish. And that you may learn the height of
almsgiving, what is more toilsome than virginity? Nothing; there are many
virtues, some great, some greater, some lesser, but nothing is more toilsome
than virginity; it wrestles against nature; this war has no truce; it is a
battle that never has peace, except through the mercy of Christ. The virgin
stands gathering the furnace against herself, and is not burned; =48.1062= a
virgin standing upon the coals is not consumed; being in the furnace she is
not set on fire, having dew like the three children; competing with the
invisible powers, she imitates Michael, and she contends with Gabriel.
Virginity was in paradise, and it was corrupted by the dragon. For this reason
it did not appear in the middle times; but when Jesus Christ the Son of God,
who was born of a virgin, came, then it appeared again. And do you wish to
know the greatness of virginity? Moses split the sea, changed the air, brought
down manna, and they spoke against him concerning his own wife, the Ethiopian,
because he had a wife; and Abraham too and Isaac and Jacob, and Joseph also,
even if he was chaste, still after these things he had a wife. Do you wish to
learn how great virginity is? Christ when he came did not bind it with
necessity; for while he intensified the other virtues, this one he did not
intensify, so that if you accomplish it willingly, you may be crowned.
Those who practice self-control take up possessionlessness first. For there
are eunuchs who were born so; and there are eunuchs who were made eunuchs by
men; and there are eunuchs who made themselves eunuchs for the kingdom of
heaven, not cutting off the members, but cutting away the unbridled desire. He
who is able to receive it, let him receive it. And the Apostle, having gone
through all the virtues, knowing its ocean, says: But concerning virgins I
have no commandment of the Lord.
Did you see how great virginity is? And yet without almsgiving it did not
save; but almsgivings without virginity saved. The five foolish virgins,
because they had no oil, but virginity only, did not enter into the
bridechamber; for they say to the wise: Give us of your oil. And they were
justly called foolish, because having accomplished the more toilsome, they did
not do that which is without toil; they brought down the great antagonist, and
they were defeated by the lesser. And when the bridegroom came, the wise
entered into the bridechamber; and those others came knocking; and he says to
them: I do not know you. Why? Because you saw me hungry, and you did not feed
me.
But may it not be that we hear such a voice; but to those who have
accomplished almsgiving he says: Come, you blessed of my Father, inherit the
kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world. Why? Because they
kept virginity? By no means; but because I hungered, and you gave me to eat; I
thirsted, and you gave me drink; I was a stranger, and you took me in; naked,
and you clothed me; sick and in prison, and you visited me.
And that you may learn how great almsgiving is, I wish again to remind you of
this history. There were ten virgins, five wise and five foolish; and a voice
came at midnight: Rise, the bridegroom is coming. Midnight is the
resurrection, when the angels wake us all. And the virgins, having risen,
trimmed their lamps. And the lamp is virginity, the pure, the keen, the
luminous. The foolish say to the wise: Give us of your oil. The wise say to
them: We fear lest it not suffice for us and for you (but it was not from envy
that they did not give; for what need of gold remains there? for there is
neither rich nor poor), but go away and buy. And who are the
sellers? The poor; for you rather receive than give; you give him earthly
things, and you receive heavenly. So as they went away to buy, the bridegroom
came; and the ready ones entered into the bridechamber, and the doors were
shut. And those others came and knocked, and he says to them: I do not know
you.
You see, because they did not have almsgiving, they failed of the labor of
virginity also, and they remained outside the bridechamber. Having learned
then the gain of almsgiving, brothers, let us do almsgiving, that we may both
attain the bridechamber, and enjoy the eternal good things, by the grace of
our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom to the Father be glory together with the Holy
and Good and Life-giving Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of ages.
Amen.
