OF OUR FATHER AMONG THE SAINTS ATHANASIUS, A BRIEF SYNOPSIS OF
DIVINE SCRIPTURE, OLD AND NEW TESTAMENT.
All Scripture of us Christians is God-breathed. And it has the books not
undefined, but rather defined and canonized. And of the Old Testament they
are these: Genesis, of which the beginning: "In the beginning God made the
heaven and the earth." Exodus, of which the beginning: "These are the names
of the sons of Israel who were going into Egypt." Leviticus, of which the
beginning: "And he called Moses, and the Lord spoke to him in the tent of
the testimony." Numbers, of which the beginning "And the Lord spoke to
Moses in the wilderness of Sinai in the tent of the testimony." =28.285=
Deuteronomy, of which the beginning: "These are the words which Moses spoke
to all Israel beyond the Jordan, in the wilderness, toward the west, near
the Red Sea." Joshua the son of Nun, of which the beginning: "And it came
to pass after the death of the servant of the Lord, the Lord said to
Joshua, son of Nun, minister of Moses, saying." Judges, of which the
beginning: "And it came to pass after the death of Joshua, and the sons of
Israel inquired in the Lord saying: Who shall go up against the Canaanites
as leader?" Ruth, of which the beginning: "And it came to pass in the
judging of the judges, there was a famine in the land." First and Second of
Kingdoms, numbered as one book. And of the first the beginning: "And a man
from Armathaim Sipha, from the mountain of Ephraim, and his name Elkanah,
son of Jeremeel, son of Heli." And of the second the beginning: "It came to
pass after Saul died, and David returned striking Amalek." Third and Fourth
of Kingdoms, numbered again as one book. And of the third the beginning:
"And king David was older, advanced in days; and they covered him with
garments, and he was not warmed." And of the fourth the beginning: "And
Moab rebelled in Israel, after Ahab died." First and Second of
Paralipomena, numbered as one book. And of the first the beginning is:
"Adam, Seth, Enos, Cainan, Maleleel, Jared, Enoch, Mathusala, Lamech, Noah."
And of the second the beginning: "And Solomon son of David was strengthened
upon his kingdom, and the Lord his God was with him, and he magnified him
unto a height." First and Second Esdras, numbered as one book. And of the
first the beginning: "And Josiah kept the Passover in Jerusalem to his own
Lord on the fourteenth day of the first month." And of the second the
beginning: "And in the first year of Cyrus the king of the Persians, that
the word from the mouth of Jeremiah might be fulfilled, the Lord stirred up
the spirit of Cyrus the king of the Persians." The Davidic Psalter, having
151 psalms; of which the beginning, that is the first psalm: "Blessed is
the man who has not walked in the counsel of the ungodly." Proverbs of
Solomon, of which the beginning: "Proverbs of Solomon son of David, who
reigned in Israel, to know wisdom and instruction." Ecclesiastes of the
same, of which the beginning: "Words =28.288= of Ecclesiastes, son of
David, king of Israel in Jerusalem. Vanity of vanities, all things vanity."
Song of Songs of the same, of which the beginning: "Song of Songs, which is
Solomon's. Let him kiss me from the kisses of his mouth, for your breasts
are better than wine." Job, of which the beginning: "There was a certain
man in the land of Ausitis, whose name was Job, and that man was blameless
and godly." Twelve prophets, numbered as one book. And they are these,
Hosea first, of which the beginning: "A word of the Lord, which came to
Hosea, the son of


Beeri, in the days of Uzziah, and Jotham, and Ahaz, and Hezekiahkings of
Judah, and in the days of Jeroboam the son of Joash, king of Israel.
Beginning of the word of the Lord in Hosea." Then Amos, of which the
beginning: "Words of Amos, which came in Accarim, from Thecue, which he saw
concerning Jerusalem in the days of Uzziah king of Judah and in the days of
Jeroboam the son of Joash, king of Israel, two years before the earthquake."
Micah, of which the beginning: "And the word of the Lord came to Micah the
son of Morathi in the days of Jotham, and Ahaz, and Hezekiah kings of
Judah, concerning the things which he saw about Samaria and Jerusalem."
Joel, of which the beginning: "A word of the Lord, which came to Joel the
son of Bathuel. Hear these things, all you elders, and give ear, all you
who dwell in the land." Obadiah, of which the beginning: "Vision of
Obadiah: Thus says the Lord God to Idumaea." Jonah, of which the beginning:
"And the word of the Lord came to Jonah the son of Amathi, saying: Arise
and go to Nineveh the great city." Nahum, of which the beginning: "Burden
of Nineveh. Book of the vision of Nahum the Elkesite." Habakkuk, of which
the beginning: "The burden which Habakkuk the prophet saw." Zephaniah, of
which the beginning: "A word of the Lord, which came to Zephaniah the son
of Chus, son of Godolias of Amorias of Hezekiah, in the days of Josiah son
of Amos king of Judah." Haggai, of which the beginning: "In the second year
under Darius the king in the sixth month, on the first of the month, the
word of the Lord came in the hand of Haggai the prophet saying." Zechariah,
of which the beginning: "In the eighth month of the second year under
Darius the word of the Lord came upon Zechariah, the son of Barachias, son
of Addo the prophet, saying." Malachi, of which the beginning: "Burden of
the word of the Lord upon Israel in the hand of his angel." These twelve
then are one book. And next four others, each toward one book, these:
Isaiah, of which the beginning: "A vision, which Isaiah =28.289= son of
Amos saw, which he saw against Judaea and against Jerusalem in the reign of
Uzziah, and Jotham, and Ahaz, and Hezekiah, who reigned over Judaea. Hear,
O heaven, and give ear, O earth, for the Lord has spoken." Jeremiah, of
which the beginning: "The word of God, which came upon Jeremiah the son of
Chelkias, who was of the priests." Ezekiel, of which the beginning: "And it
came to pass in the thirtieth year in the fourth month, and I was in the
midst of the captivity upon the river Chobar, and the heavens were opened,
and I saw visions of God." Daniel, of which the beginning: "And there was a
man dwelling in Babylon, and his name Joakim, and he took a wife whose name
was Susanna, daughter of Chelkias, very beautiful and fearing the Lord."
Together the canonized books of the Old Testament are twenty-two, equal in
number to the letters of the Hebrews. For so many are the elements among
them. And besides these there are again other books of the same Old
Testament, not canonized, but only read to the catechumens, these: Wisdom
of Solomon, of which the beginning: "Love righteousness, you who judge the
earth." Wisdom of Jesus son of Sirach, of which the beginning: "All wisdom
is from the Lord, and with him it is unto the age." Esther, of which the
beginning: "In the second year, when Artaxerxes the Great was reigning, on
the first of Nisan Mordecai the son of Jairus son of Semei son of Kisaios,
of the tribe of Benjamin, saw a dream." Judith, of which the beginning:
"In the twelfth year of the kingdom of Nabuchodonosor, who reigned over the
Assyrians in Nineveh, the great city, in the days of Arphaxad, who reigned
over the Medes in Ecbatana." Tobit, of which the beginning: "Book of the
words of Tobit, the son of Tobiel, of Ananiel, of Adouel, of Gabael, of the
seed of Aseel, of the tribe of Nephthaleim, who was taken captive in the
days of Ennemesaros the king of the Assyrians." So many also are those not
canonized. Some of the ancients however have said that Esther also is
canonized


among the Hebrews; and that Ruth, being joined with Judges, is numbered as
one book; and Esther as another one; and thus again the number of the books
canonized among them is filled up to twenty-two. And the books of the Old
Testament, both the canonized and those not canonized, are of such a kind
and so many. And those of the New Testament, again defined and canonized
books, these: According to Matthew, of which the beginning: "Book of the
generation of Jesus Christ, son of Abraham." =28.292= According to Mark, of
which the beginning: "Beginning of the Gospel of Jesus Christ, Son of God;
as it is written in Isaiah the prophet: Behold I send my angel before your
face, who shall prepare your way." According to Luke, of which the
beginning: "Since many have taken in hand to set in order a narrative
concerning the things that have been fulfilled among us." According to
John, of which the beginning: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word
was with God, and the Word was God. This one was in the beginning with God.
All things came to be through him, and without him came to be nothing that
has come to be." Acts of the Apostles, of which the beginning: "The first
word I made concerning all things, O Theophilus, which Jesus began both to
do and to teach." Catholic Epistles of various apostles, all seven,
numbered as one book. One of James, of which the beginning: "James, servant
of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ, to the twelve tribes in the
dispersion, greeting." Two of Peter; and of the first the beginning: "Peter
apostle of Jesus Christ, to the elect sojourners of the dispersion of
Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia, according to the
foreknowledge of God the Father, in sanctification of Spirit, unto
obedience and sprinkling of the blood of Jesus Christ, grace to you and
peace be multiplied." And of the second the beginning: "Symeon Peter both
apostle of Jesus Christ to those who have obtained a faith equally precious
with us in the righteousness of our Lord Jesus Christ." Three of John; and
of the first the beginning: "That which was from the beginning, which we
have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, and our hands have handled,
concerning the word of life." And of the second the beginning: "The elder
to the elect lady and to her children, whom I love in truth." And of the
third the beginning: "The elder to Gaius the beloved, whom I love in
truth." One of Jude, of which the beginning: "Jude, servant of Jesus
Christ, and brother of James, to those beloved in God the Father, and kept
for Jesus Christ, called." Epistles of Paul the apostle 14, numbered as one
book. In the first, that to the Romans, of which the beginning: "Paul
servant of Jesus Christ, called Apostle, set apart unto the Gospel of God.
Two to the Corinthians; and of the first the beginning: "Paul called
Apostle of Jesus Christ through the will of God, and Sosthenes the brother,
to the Church of God that is in Corinth." And of the second the beginning:
"Paul apostle of Jesus Christ through the will of God, and Timothy the
brother, to the Church of God that is in Corinth." Fourth, to the
Galatians, of which the beginning: "Paul apostle not from men, nor through
men, =28.293= but through Jesus Christ and God and Father." Fifth, to the
Ephesians, of which the beginning: "Paul apostle of Jesus Christ through
the will of God, to the saints who are in Ephesus, and faithful in Christ
Jesus." Sixth, to the Philippians, of which the beginning: "Paul and
Timothy, servants of Jesus Christ, to all the saints who are in Philippi,
with bishops and deacons." Seventh, to the Colossians, of which the
beginning: "Paul apostle of Christ Jesus through the will of God, and
Timothy the brother, to the saints in Colossae and faithful brethren in
Christ." Two to the Thessalonians; and of the first the beginning: "Paul
and


Silvanus and Timothy, to the Church of the Thessalonians, in God the Father
and the Lord Jesus Christ." And of the second the beginning: "Paul and
Silvanus and Timothy, to the Church of the Thessalonians, in God our Father
and the Lord Jesus Christ." Tenth, that to the Hebrews, of which the
beginning: "In many parts and in many ways of old God having spoken to the
fathers in the prophets, at the last of the days has spoken to us in a
Son." Two to Timothy; and of the first the beginning: "Paul apostle of
Jesus Christ, according to the command of God our Savior and of Christ
Jesus our hope, to Timothy genuine child in faith." And of the second the
beginning: "Paul apostle of Jesus Christ through the will of God, according
to the promise of life that is in Christ Jesus, to Timothy beloved child."
Thirteenth, that to Titus, of which the beginning: "Paul servant of God,
and apostle of Jesus Christ, according to the faith of the elect of God,
and knowledge of truth, that is according to piety in hope of eternal
life." Fourteenth, that to Philemon, of which the beginning: "Paul prisoner
of Jesus Christ and Timothy the brother, to Philemon the beloved and our
fellow-worker. Upon these there is also the Apocalypse of John the
theologian, received as of that one and approved by saints of old and
Spirit-bearing Fathers, of which the beginning: "Apocalypse of Jesus
Christ, which God gave him to show to his servants the things that must
come to be quickly, and he signified it, having sent through his angel to
his servant John." So many also are the books of the New Testament, at
least those canonized, and as it were the first-fruits or anchors and
supports of our faith; as having been written and set forth by the apostles
of Christ themselves, who both were with him and were discipled by him.
Since later, according to their sequence and agreement, other countless
and innumerable books were worked out by the great and most wise
God-bearing Fathers from time to time unto witness of the things that went
before and unto illumination; concerning which there is not now a word, as
being very many and undefined, and at once =28.296= all holding to the same
sequence as these ancient ones, and themselves also explaining and making
clear the same things. As then has been said, in these are all the books,
both of the Old and of the New Testament. Except that so far it has been
announced thus narrowly with a view to number only. And from this point one
must take up again concerning the same books more broadly, by whom each of
these was composed, and from where it has the naming it obtained, and what
it contains, in heads and synoptically, according to the order already set
forth, each book being described with its own naming.
Book 1. Genesis. This book of Genesis it is Moses who spoke and wrote it
from a command of the Lord. And it is called thus, because it contains also
the genesis of all things, of heaven and earth, and of men and of all these
visible things, and the very planting of paradise. And it narrates that on
the first day the light came to be, and on the second the firmament, which
God called heaven; on the third the waters were gathered, and the dry land
appeared; and the gatherings of the waters he called seas, and the dry land
he called earth; and the earth put forth herb of grass, and every tree; on
the fourth there came forth sun and moon, and all the choir of the stars;
on the fifth the genesis of winged things and fishes and sea-monsters in
the waters; on the sixth of four-footed things and wild beasts and reptiles
and cattle, and the fashioning of man according to the image of God and
likeness; and on the seventh God rested from the works, and blessed it. And
there is in this book the transgression of Adam, and from paradise


expulsion, and the killing of Abel by his brother Cain, and the translation
of Enoch, and the construction of the ark, and the flood that came to be
upon Noah, and the tower-building, and the division of the tongues, and the
dispersion of the nations, and the going out of Abraham from the Chaldeans.
And it contains also the overthrow of Sodom, and the legislation concerning
circumcision, and the oath of the promise of God to Abraham, and the birth
of Isaac, and of Jacob, and of the patriarchs from him; and that Abraham
lived 180 years, and Ishmael, who was born from Agar, died at 137 years;
and how Joseph was sold by the brothers into Egypt, and being slandered by
his mistress was cast into the prison, and later was set as ruler of all
Egypt; and next the descent into Egypt of Jacob and his sons in number 75;
and that Jacob, having come there, and having blessed the patriarchs his
own sons, died at 147 years, and his body was carried over, and was buried
in Canaan. And Joseph also, after the famine that came to be in Egypt,
having lived on 60 years, died at 110 years, having first commanded
concerning his own bones, that they too should be carried over to the tomb
of the fathers. =28.297=
Book 2, Exodus. And this book of Exodus the aforesaid Moses composed. And
it is called thus, because it contains also the going out of the sons of
Israel from Egypt, which came to be after 430 years of the promise spoken
to Abraham, and after 215 years of the sojourning in Egypt (for so many
years they sojourned there), and after the ten-plague against Pharaoh the
tyrant of Egypt. For God brought these ten plagues against him: first, he
changed the river into blood; second, he brought frogs in all Egypt; third,
gnats; fourth, dog-fly; fifth, death of cattle; sixth, sores, blisters
boiling upon the men and the cattle; seventh, hail and fire flaming;
eighth, locust; ninth, darkness in all Egypt for an interval of three days
apart from the land of the sons of Israel; tenth, death of all the
firstborn of Egypt from man unto cattle. And when Pharaoh pursued the
people after they had gone out, then Israel crossed the Red Sea dry-foot,
it having been divided on this side and on that; and the Egyptians who were
pursuing the people together with Pharaoh himself were drowned in that same
sea. In this book there is contained also how the magicians of the
Egyptians, Jannes and Mambres, at the beginning stood against Moses, but
later, being put to shame, withdrew; and the legislation concerning the
Passover, and the change of the bitter water into sweet, and the gift of
the quail, and the supply of the manna from heaven, and the water given
forth to them from a rock, which Moses also named temptation and reviling;
when also the observation of the sabbath had its beginning, and the
legislation that came to be from God on the mountain Sinai through Moses
on two tablets of stone, and the construction of the tent and of the
garment, both of the high priest and of the priests, is signified here.
Those then who worked all things are Beseleel of the tribe of Judah, and
Liab of the tribe of Dan. And what was spent on the works is of gold 29
talents, and 700 shekels; and of silver 100 talents (and shekels
#22αψοεʹ); of bronze 70 talents, and 2500 shekels. And this book contains
also the transgression of the people, when they made a calf below the
mountain, and how Amalek was struck by the stretching out of the hands of
Moses, and then ordinances and just things, and before them


these ten commandments on tablets: first, I am the Lord your God; second,
You shall not make for yourself an idol, nor a likeness of anything; third,
You shall not take the name of the Lord your God in vain; fourth, Remember
the day of the sabbaths; fifth, Honor your Father and your mother; sixth,
You shall not murder; seventh, You shall not commit adultery; eighth, You
shall not steal; ninth, You shall not bear false witness against your
neighbor a false witness; tenth, You shall not desire the wife of your
neighbor, nor as many things as are your neighbor's. And the tent, having
been completed, =28.300= was set up in the second year of their going out
on the new moon of the first month. And in these the book of Exodus is
concluded.
Book 3, Leviticus. And this Levitical book was composed by the
aforementioned Moses. And it is named thus, because it contains also
peculiarly the whole arrangement of the Levitical ministry, and how Aaron
and his sons are chosen from the tribe of Levi, and are anointed as
priests; and the whole difference of the sacrifices and of the offerings,
and the quality of all the worship in the temple, and of the Levitical and
priestly ministry; and the law of each sacrifice; and concerning the
peace-offering and the voluntary and involuntary trespass, and how each of
the things offered ought to be divided and offered up, it narrates. And
there is also treated in this book the anointing of high priest and of the
priests, and the discernment and knowledge of signs concerning leprosy of
men and of garments, and of walls of a house, and the law concerning their
cleansing; and a law and distinction concerning lawful marriages, and which
are unlawful; and again a distinction of clean and unclean cattle and
winged things and fishes; namely of which of these the Hebrews ought to
taste, and from which to abstain. And it declares the day of the trumpets
on the new moon of the seventh month, and the command of the great fast in
the same seventh month, they do this on the tenth of the month, which it
calls sabbaths of sabbaths, and remission of sin. And it contains the
Tabernacles on the 15th of the same month. And concerning feasts also, and
the things that ought to be offered in them; and concerning remission of
the Hebrews who are sold, and cutting off of debts, and rest of the land,
which ought to come to be every seven years, it narrates. And again a
reminder of ordinances and commands, and testimonies; it contains also
promises to those who keep them, and most terrible threats to those who
transgress. And the distinction and the cause of the clean and unclean
foods it makes thus. The clean from the cattle it says are such: those that
part the hoof, and have claws of two hoofs, and bring up rumination; such
as are calf, sheep, goat, deer, gazelle, oryx, buffalo, pygarg, goat-stag,
camel-leopard, and the like. And if from these something was lacking to
any, this was unclean; such as are camel, and hare, and hedgehog; for these
ruminate, but do not part the hoof; and the swine; for this also parts and
has claws, but does not ruminate. And again from the winged things, those
it commands not to eat, are these: the eagle, the griffin, the sea-eagle,
the vulture, the kite, and those like them; and raven, and those like it,
and ostrich, and owl, and gull, and those like them, and hawk, and those
like it, and night-raven, and cormorant, and ibis, and purple
gallinule, and pelican, and swan, and heron, and charadrius, and those like
them, and hoopoe, and =28.301= bat. And of the winged reptiles it says all
are unclean that go upon four, having legs above the feet, so as in


them to leap from the earth; such as are the locust, the attagas, the
grasshopper, and those like them, and the snake-fighter. And the things in
the waters, those generated both in the sea, and in the rivers, and in
winter-torrents, these it says are clean, as many as have fins and scales;
such as are red mullet, parrot-wrasse, glaucus, grey mullet, and those like
them. And those lacking one of these are unclean, or even not to be eaten;
such as are cuttlefish; for they have fins, but do not have scales. And of
the reptiles, those upon the earth, it says these are unclean: the weasel,
the land crocodile, the shrew-mouse, the chameleon, the gecko, the lizard,
and the mole. For concerning the serpent and those like it, it was
superfluous to make mention; for it says clearly that these are abominable
and hateful to all; nevertheless it also made mention of them in
Deuteronomy.
Book 4, Numbers. Of the same Moses is this book also, the composition of
Numbers. And the book is called thus, because the number of the whole
people also is contained in this, each tribe of the Jews having been
measured from a command of the Lord. And the people was numbered from
twenty years and above, every man going out to war, apart from the tribe of
Levi; for this was measured by itself; and the number of those measured was
found 603,550. And those numbering were, the people, Moses and Aaron, and
with them a ruler of each tribe. And the names of the rulers are these, and
this the number: Of Reuben Elisur, son of Sediour; and their number was 46
thousand and 500. Of Simeon Salamiel, son of Surisadai; and their number
was 74 thousand and 600. Of Issachar Nathanael, son of Sogar; and their
number was 54 thousand and 400. Of Zebulun Eliab, son of Chelon; and their
number was 57 thousand 400. Of the sons of Joseph, of Ephraim, Elisama son
of Emioud; and their number was 42 thousand and 500. Of Manasseh Gamaliel,
son of Phadassour; and their number was 32 thousand and 300. Of Benjamin
Abida, son of Gedeon; and their number was 35 thousand and 400. =28.304= Of
Dan Achiezer, son of Amisadai; and their number was 62 thousand and 700. Of
Asher Phageel, son of Echran; and their number was 41 thousand and 500. Of
Gad Elisaph, son of Ragouel; and their number was 45 thousand and 650. Of
Nephthaleim Achire, son of Ainan; and their number was 53 thousand and 400.
This is the number of the warlike people, 603,550. And the tribe of Levi
they numbered by themselves through a voice of the Lord, Moses and Aaron
alone, from a month old and above; and their number was 22 thousand. And
Moses alone also numbered the male firstborn of all the other tribes, and
their number was found 22 thousand and 273. And in place of these firstborn
the Levites were reckoned to God; for so many also were the Levites. And
they too are commanded to be purified through Moses, and they are set apart
thereafter through Aaron to work the works of the Lord in place of the
people, and to take up the vessels of the tent, and to minister to Aaron,
and to his sons. And there is in the same book also the arrangement of the
journey of the people, and the raising of the tent, and the command
concerning trumpets, for the people to be summoned in them. Here is
signified also the dedication of the altar, and how he anointed the tent,
and sanctified it and all the things in it; and the ordering of the
offerings for the dedication of the


altar, and a limit of the times of the ministry of the Levites; so that from
twenty-five years they begin to minister; and having become fifty years,
they withdraw from the ministry, and only keep the tent. And it is written
also concerning the water of conviction, that which is accursed; and the
law concerning those who vow to abstain from wine, and not to shear the
head; and that to the people who of old murmured for meat God gave quail,
and to the people thirsting and murmuring again he gave water from a rock,
which Moses called water of contradiction. And he sent spies one according
to tribe, among whom also Joshua the son of Nun, and Caleb the son of
Jephonne. Here also the synagogue of Core rose up against Moses and they
were swallowed living into the earth with Dathan and Abeiron; when also
Maria the sister of Aaron was made leprous; and the rod of Aaron sprouted a
nut. And after these things Aaron dies on the mountain Hor, and Eleazar his
son succeeds him. Here also Balaam and his ass that spoke with a human
voice are related; and that Balak king of Moab was put to shame; and when
the people had been profaned from a suggestion of Balaam, Phinees slew the
Midianite woman and =28.305= the Israelite Zambres of the tribe of Simeon
because of the fornications, and thus the wrath was stayed. And when the
people spoke against God against Moses, they were bitten by serpents, and
later when they asked, God commanded a bronze serpent to be made, and for
it to be hung; that the one bitten, seeing it, might live. Here God
commands Moses and Eleazar the priest to measure the people again from
twenty years and above, and their number was found 601,730. And the Levites
again were numbered by themselves, and were found 23 thousand. This
visitation then came to be in Araboth Moab, upon the Jordan over against
Jericho. And among these there was none of those formerly visited; because
they died, except Caleb and Joshua the son of Nun. In this book Moses wrote
through words of the Lord their departures and their stations, in which
journeying from Egypt they lodged resting. And they are these: From
Ramesses of Egypt they went out in the first month on the morrow of the
Passover, and came to Sochoth, from Sochoth to Bouthan, from Bouthan to
Beelsepphon, over against Magdolus; from Beelsepphon they crossed the midst
of the sea; from sea to Pikriai, and from Pikriai to Elim, where the palms;
and from Elim to Seir the wilderness; from the wilderness Seir to Raphaka;
from Raphaka to Elim, and from Elim to Raphideim; from Raphideim to Sinai
the wilderness; from wilderness of Sinai to Graves of Desire; from Graves
of Desire to Asioth; from Asioth to Rathama; from Rathama to Remon Phares;
and from Remmon Phares to Lemona; from Lemona to Dessan; from Dessan to

Book 14, Proverbs of Solomon. Solomon son of David, having succeeded to the
kingdom of the father, prayed to God to receive wisdom above wealth, and
above vengeance of enemies. Having received then and having become wise
above all the men before him and after him, and having been marveled at in
all things which he both did and said, he spoke three thousand parables and
five thousand odes; and he physiologized concerning all the things growing
from the earth and all the animals. And he composed books, three canonized;
that is both this one called Proverbs, and Ecclesiastes, and the Song of
Songs; and another besides these, not canonized, as has been said before,
which


is named Wisdom of Solomon. And he titled the present book Proverbs,
because proverbs are words of the wise, as riddles, which are declaring one
thing from themselves, and report another in a hidden sense. And of such
the Proverbs are a form. For thus also the disciples of the Lord say in the
Gospel according to John, when, Christ having first said many things in a
hidden meaning, later they say to him: "See now you speak with boldness,
and you say not even one proverb;" as of the proverbs being spoken not from
the open, but hiddenly. Since then in this book most words are of such a
kind, for this reason he wrote the book Proverbs. And it was named
Proverb, because such words were written beside the ways toward correction
and teaching of those passing through in the ways. And they were written
beside the ways, since not all had room for the words of the truth; that
even passing through and seeing they may search out the things written, and
thus men may be educated. Some then define them thus: a word by the way
from some one thing into many =28.341= being taken over. There is then in
this book knowledge of wisdom and of instruction, understanding of words of
prudence, turns of words, understanding of true righteousness, to make
judgments straight, and a promise of knowing from these parables, sayings
of the wise, riddles, dark words. And they are knowledges of wisdom and of
instruction; because also Greeks profess to have wisdom, and heretics think
they have instruction. For this reason this one teaches the true wisdom and
instruction, that one may not by the homonymy of wisdom fall into the
sophisms of the Greeks and of the heretics. For also Greeks, some thinking
themselves to be, and "saying they are wise, became foolish." And heretics,
thinking they are being instructed, have been turned aside, and they sin,
being self-condemned. And the one who has heard of the divine words, being
wise, will be wiser. For having heard the ordinances of God, and having
kept these, and not having neglected the instruction of the Lord, nor
having been faint upon it, he will become a wise son, and having become
instructed, he will quickly receive also the knowledge concerning God. For
this the wise are instructed. And understanding of words of prudence is the
teaching concerning the only and true God. For among Greeks some said God
is a body; and some even likened him to idols. And heretics, they too were
beside themselves concerning the one who is. For this reason this one
narrates the true knowledge concerning God, the inexpressible of him:
"Glory of God hides a word." And the providential of him he says: "In every
place eyes of the Lord look upon both evil and good;" and again: "Rich and
poor have met one another; and the Lord has made both;" and, "Of a lender
and a debtor having come together with one another, the Lord makes a
visitation of both;" and, "For before the eyes of God are ways of a man;
and into all his tracks he looks." And the judicial of him he says:
"Sacrifices of the ungodly are an abomination of the Lord; and prayers of
those making straight are acceptable with him;" and again: "Houses of the
insolent the Lord pulls down, and he has set a bound of a widow." And the
creative of him not simply, but that through his own Word and wisdom he
makes all things; which especially also characterizes the true God, that he
is Father of a Son, he says then: "God by wisdom founded the earth;" and
again: "The Lord made countries, and uninhabited places, and inhabited
extremes of the under-heaven. When he was preparing the heaven, I was
present with him, and when he was marking off his own throne upon winds,
when he was making the upper clouds strong, I was with him fitting. I was
she in whom he was glad, and daily I was being gladdened in his face."
Turns of words have been said; because one turning them in the mind opens
and


finds the mind lying in them; such as are: "A prudent son has been saved
from the heat, and a lawless son becomes wind-spoiled in harvest;" and
again: "Take care of the green things in the field, and you shall shear
grass, and gather mountain fodder, that you may have sheep for clothing;"
=28.344= and again: "If you sit to dine at a table of rulers, understand
intelligently the things set before you." And things like these
understanding of true righteousness has been said; because some have taken
the just variously, some saying, it is just to give back what one received
to keep; and some, to give evil in place of evil, and good in place of
good. And it happens that such definitions are not accurate. For do not
say, In the manner he used me, I will use him, and I will pay him what he
wronged me. For this reason this one teaches the true righteousness, that
this is the truly just, to give back to each his own. "First then honor
the Lord from your just labors, and first-fruits to him from your fruits of
righteousness." Then: "Honor a king, and to the parents render the fitting,
and to all the equal." And this is one type; and a second, to justify one's
own soul, to keep equality, so as not to incline into inequality; but to
the rational: Let not an evil counsel overtake you, that which leaves
teaching of youth, but with counsel do all things, and let your reasonings
be judgments; so that each may judge himself, and have every desire good;
for desire of the just is all good, and desire of a just one is acceptable.
And according to the spirited: "Be not a companion to a wrathful man, and
with a passionate friend do not lodge. For a fool brings out all his
wrath; but the wise stores it according to part." And a man thus ordering
himself, and keeping the own action of each part of the soul healthy and
unhurt, will be knowing the true righteousness. And to make judgments
straight is first to judge justly according to the law of God, as he says:
"Open your mouth in a word of God, and judge all healthily. And discern a
poor and weak one. For the one who has said the ungodly, He is just, is
accursed to peoples, and hated unto nations. For to respect a face in
judgment is not good." This must be said from the open; and then that what
one judges others, this judgment also he may make straight unto himself,
and may test himself, rebuking himself, if the wrath takes more; and
holding, if the desire abounds; and raising, if the reasoning part sleeps,
saying: "How long, sluggard, do you lie;" For thus leading himself justly,
and becoming an accuser of himself, he will learn to make a judgment
straight, and will not hear from another: "You who teach another, do you
not teach yourself? You who say, Do not commit adultery, do you commit
adultery?" for just as the one journeying and making the way straight
arrives at the end; so the one making judgments straight will be known just
and wise. And parables are called words, as images of the things said. For
from them through the likeness the thing said is grasped. For thus the Lord
was saying, as Mark says: "To what shall I liken the kingdom of God? or in
what parable shall I compare it?" as of the things said according to
likeness being a parable. For also saying: "The kingdom of God is like,"
later he was adding: =28.345= "For this reason I speak to them in
parables." Such then are the parables: "Just as an outgoing of snow in
harvest a burning benefits, so a faithful messenger those who sent him;"
and again: "Just as winds and clouds and rains, so those who boast upon a
false gift;" and things like these. And sayings of the wise are words not
sophistically made, nor deceiving in the persuasive, but accurately
wrought; not as being said according to a command, but as having been known
by themselves, and as being brought forward as their decisions. Such as
are: "Of a heart


being gladdened, a face flourishes; and being in griefs, it is sullen;" and,
"A right heart seeks sense; and a mouth of the uninstructed will know
evils;" and, "Hatred raises strife; and friendship covers all those who do
not contend;" and, "Who despises a matter, will be despised by it; and the
one fearing a commandment, this one is healthy." For the words of the other
men happen to be for the most part ambiguous; but those of the wise, such
as these are, are true, and have been accurately wrought from every side,
so as not even to be contradicted. And the other men recognize the matter
from the end of the action, for example: that The unjust is base, and the
adulterer is evil; and this would be clear even to anyone; but the wise
foretell the beginnings of the actions, and as it were announce beforehand
symbols of the movements of the soul; that each, knowing the beginnings of
the evils, may guard himself, such as are the things said before: "Hatred
raises strife; and friendship covers all those who do not contend." For the
contentious has from hatred the beginning of such a vice, and one would not
say that he loves the one who contends. And again from the contrary, the
one who does not contend has a mark of love; and one would not say that the
one who loves is contentious, as again the: "In desires is every idle one,
and a quick-tempered acts with lack of counsel." For of the idle he
signified that the soul has been sunk in all desires, and he declared that
the quick-tempered has not even one right counsel. And wholly these report
the ethical things rather, saying the beginning of the action from where it
is constituted, that of contention hatred leads the way; and of not
contending love leads the way; and of idleness an evil pleasure leads the
way; and of the quick-tempered lack of counsel leads the way; and of the
one set at nought a beginning is that he himself despises the law; and of
health the fear of God leads the way; and of the destruction of the soul a
beginning is to have the mouth unguarded; and of one being terrified
rashness leads the way. And thus analyzing each saying, you will find it
said and written for those who hear and learn the beginnings of the evils
and of the goods to flee the base things, and to do the good things. And
riddles and dark words, which are so secure, as to pain the one who
encounters them by showing nothing, nor furnishing any hidden sense; but
being searched they nevertheless show the mind in them. Such as are: "The
leech had three daughters =28.348= being loved with a loving; and these
three, and the fourth was not satisfied to say, Enough. Hades, and love of
a woman, and tartarus, and earth not being filled with water, and water,
and fire, shall not say, It suffices;" and again: "And three things are
impossible for me to understand, and the fourth I do not know; tracks of an
eagle flying, and ways of a serpent upon a rock, and paths of a ship
sailing the sea, and ways of a man in youth," and as many such as are
riddles; for they declare some other things, and present a thought of other
things; and they are unclear, but have the mind hidden
Book 15, Ecclesiastes. And this book also Solomon wrote, just as has been
said before. And it is called Ecclesiastes, because the one who wrote named
it thus, having said in the beginning: "Words of Ecclesiastes son of David
king of Israel in Jerusalem; Vanity of vanities, said Ecclesiastes." And
Solomon might have been called variously: wise, because he partook of
wisdom; and prudent, because prudence of truth was in him; and instructed,
because he had the true instruction from the Lord. He was called then
Ecclesiastes, because he himself was assembling the peoples, and was
speaking the things of the spirit. And in this book he appears philosophizing
concerning the natural contemplation. For since


Egyptians were imagining, and Greeks were about to mythologize concerning
these things; for this reason this one, being moved by Holy Spirit, stays
those who attempt to seek concerning these things, saying: "A man will not
be able to find the work that has been made under the sun; as many things
as a man shall labor to seek, and he will not find;" and he also slanders
the understanding of the Egyptians concerning genethlialogy, saying: "One
who keeps a wind will not sow, and one who looks in the clouds will not
harvest, in which there is not one knowing, what is the way of the spirit.
As bones in a womb of one conceiving, so you will not know the works of
God, as many as he will make all things." Then also coming to be about the
contemplation of each he understood, that it is in vain to seek concerning
such things, and such things only generate labor. For not from knowing such
things, but from the practical life the wise and just is constituted. For
he says: "And even as many things as a wise one may say to know, he will
not be able to find. Because I have given all this into my heart, and my
soul has seen all this, as the just and the wise and their works are in a
hand of God." Fittingly then having passed over a seeking concerning such
things, and occupying himself about the human way of life, and seeing many
and various the accidents of men, he establishes, that all things are
administered by providence, saying: "Not to the light is the course, and
not to the intelligent [mighty] is the war, and even not to the wise
=28.349= is the bread, and even not to the intelligent is the wealth, and
even not to those who know is the grace; because a season and an encounter
will meet all these. For in every thing there is a king of a worked field.
For also no one is lord, he says, in these of his own counsel. Because who
is a man, who will go after the counsel the things as many as it has made
it? and that a man in this life would have authority over nothing more,
than to do evil and good, and to prepare for the going-out his works. For
also what he shall eat and drink, this is a gift of God. Whence whether
there is a seeking concerning the, how the visible things have come to be,
or there is an acquisition of money, or a luxury of abundance, all these
things are vanity of vanities, and nothing more than a surplus of counsel
and an attempt of choice." And in saying these things vanity, he indicates
the eternal of the things to come. Wherefore also having gone beyond all
things, he exhorts to remember the eternal things, and to know that there
will be an end of this world, in saying, that the grinding women are idle,
"in a day in which keepers of the house shall be shaken, and men of the
might shall be twisted, and the grinding women have been idle." And the
things after the end also he narrates: "Because God will bring all the work
in judgment in every thing overlooked, whether good, and whether evil."
From these then he shows, that what he was saying, "Vanity of vanities," he
did not say slandering the creation, but that, contemplating the eternal
things, he was saying that the desire of the temporary things is vanity.
And teaching a judgment also, he signifies the resurrection of the dead,
and a recompense of the deeds. Then saying, "The grinding women have been
idle," he shows again that the creation itself is not vain, but rather that
it is a teacher to all unto piety. For its order and its arrangement is a
narrative concerning the one who has made and created it, in which also the
prophets and the evangelists, and the teachers are, as grinding for each
food through the teaching the knowledge unto piety; who grinding will
thereafter be idle in the consummation. For they will no longer teach as
grinding, but from the deeds of each the judgment comes to be.
Book 16, Song of Songs. Of Solomon is this book also a composition, as has
been shown before. And the whole has been written for him from beginning
until end mystically


with an enigmatic allegory. For not in the obvious, but in a secret the
mind of the doctrines in it has been hidden. And being searched it is
found, because it is fitting for the intelligent to encounter this whole
with allegory; that the things said may not through ignorance of the
unlearned fall into dishonor. And it is called Song of Songs for this
reason, because after the other songs this, and after this one must no
longer expect another; and I say another not a book, but the thing
signified from the book. And what I say is of such a kind: All the divine
Scripture prophesies concerning the descent of the Word unto us and his
incarnate presence. For this is the will =28.352= of God; and to the
prophets and to all the divine Scripture this was first to report. And they
make as accompaniments to this divine narrative words concerning the
nations of Babylon, of Damascus, of Moab, and of the others. And through
each Scripture they rebuke those who sin; and they exhort those
preoccupied to repent, threatening the coming judgment of fire. The other
Scriptures then foretell, as Moses: "A prophet the Lord our God will raise
up for you," saying, "from our brothers, as me;" and Isaiah: "Behold the
Virgin shall have in womb, and shall bear a son;" and Micah: "In the last
days the mountain of the Lord shall be manifest;" and another foretells
otherwise, according to the grace of the Spirit. And of all these speaking
the Lord being reported was expected. For this reason also many were
reporting; and until he came, those prophesying were not ceasing. And all
these are songs. And the Song of Songs is not as prophesying, nor as
signifying beforehand; but him whom others announced beforehand, this one
it shows as already having come, and already having taken up the flesh;
wherefore also as upon a yoking of the Word and of the flesh the Song of
Songs sings a wedding-song. And the other Scriptures speak concerning the
Savior, and in between they report also concerning some others; and this
book sings only the yoking of the Word toward the body. Wherefore also in
the other Scriptures through the things shown in between there are also
words of wrath and of anger, and threats of fears; and this book, since it
signifies nothing other than the presence alone of the Word, has words of
gladness and of joy, and of good-cheer alone. For a bridegroom being
present, it is fitting for all to rejoice, and for no one to mourn, as the
Lord himself has said. For he himself being present bore our weaknesses,
and turned the griefs into joy. As then after the economy of the Savior we
no longer expect a prophet, so after the things signified in the Song of
Songs there is not some other newer thing signified to expect. For also the
things signified in the Song, these are found being said also in the
prophets and in the other Scriptures from intervals. And again just as when
John shows the Lamb, until John the law and the prophets are; so the things
signified in the Song of Songs are an end of all things signified in all
the divine Scripture. For what else after the presence of Christ must one
expect than judgment and recompense? And for this reason those according to
the Phrygians introducing prophets after the Lord, are tripped, and as
heretics they were condemned. A Song of Songs then, because of expecting
nothing other after the things from this. And just as according to the law
there were Holies, and after the Holies Holies of holies, and after the
Holies of holies there was no longer an inner place; so after the songs the
Song of Songs, and after the Song of Songs one must no longer expect an
inner and newer promise. For once the Word became flesh, and has perfected
the work. And this whole book is full of dialogues =28.353= of the old
toward the Word, and in general of the human


race toward the Word, and of the Church from the nations toward him, and of
the Word again toward her and toward the human race; then of the nations
toward Jerusalem, and of Jerusalem concerning that from the nations and

Book 8. Apocalypse of John. Thus the book is called, because John the
evangelist and theologian himself also saw this apocalypse in the island
called Patmos, on a Lord's day, and having been commanded he wrote it, so
as to send to these seven Churches, to Ephesus, to Smyrna, to Pergamum, to


Thyatira, to Sardis, to Philadelphia, and to Laodicea. And the things which
are in the vision, are many and various. And an end he saw the destruction
of the Antichrist with the devil. And he is commanded first to write to
each =28.429= angel of the aforementioned Churches according to the deeds
of each. Many then and paradoxical visions he saw, and he saw seven golden
lampstands, and in the midst of them one like a Son of man; and they were
interpreted to him, that the lampstands are the seven Churches; and the one
in the midst of them the Lord. He saw also a door opened in the heaven, and
the Lord sitting on a throne, and 24 elders, sitting upon thrones and
worshipping the Lord. He saw also seals being loosed, and as each was being
loosed, some vision was coming to be. Then he saw seven angels having seven
trumpets, and as each was trumpeting, a sign was coming to be; and as the
seventh was trumpeting, he heard them saying, that "The kingdom of the
world has become of the Lord." And he saw the ark of the Covenant in
heaven. Then he saw a woman in birth-pangs, and a dragon of fire pursuing
her. The woman then was saved into the wilderness; and the dragon was
thrown into the fire. Then he saw a beast having ten horns, and seven
heads, and its diadem full of blasphemy; and its name he did not declare,
but the number of its name 660. He heard also of virgins singing, and
following the Lamb, and he saw an angel flying in mid-heaven, and another
following, and another, and a white cloud, and upon it one sitting like a
Son of man, having upon his head a golden crown, and in his hand a sharp
sickle. Then he saw seven angels, having seven last plagues, and golden
bowls full from the wrath of God. Then he was carried away by the spirit
into a wilderness, and he saw a woman sitting upon a scarlet beast, having
seven heads; and he signified that from this one the Antichrist will be.
Then he saw the heaven opened, and a white horseman, and his name was
called the Word of God. Then he saw the beast and the kings of the earth,
wishing to make a war with the one sitting upon the white horse. And the
beast was seized, and with it the false prophet, who was the Antichrist,
and he saw them being thrown into the lake of the fire. Then he saw an
angel coming down from the heaven, and throwing Satan and those having his
mark into the abyss, and shutting over him, that he may no longer lead the
nations astray until a thousand years. Then he saw the martyrs in glory
reigning in Christ. Then he saw, that after a thousand years Satan will be
loosed, and he will be thrown into the lake of the fire with the Antichrist
unto the ages of the ages. Then he saw a new heaven, and earth, and the new
Jerusalem, and he saw a river of water of life, and the servants of God
beholding his face, and having upon the forehead his name. John having seen
these things fell, wishing to worship the angel showing him all these
things. And the angel hindered him saying: "Do not worship me, but God; for
I am a fellow-servant of yours." And seeing these things, he was hearing

himself is the Word of God, who later through us having been fleshed; I
