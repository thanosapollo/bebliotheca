CLEMENT THE STROMATEUS
EXHORTATION TO THE GREEKS
Amphion the Theban and Arion the Methymnaean "were both skilled in song, but
both are myth" (and this song
of the Greeks is still sung by a chorus), by the art of music the one luring a
fish, the other walling Thebes. And another Thracian sophist (this too is a
Greek myth) tamed the wild beasts by bare song and even transplanted the
trees, the oaks, by his 1.1.2 music. I could tell you yet another myth brother
to these, and a singer, Eunomus the Locrian and the Pythian cicada; a Greek
festival was being assembled at Pytho over a dead serpent, Eunomus singing a
funeral song for a reptile; hymn or dirge of a snake the song was, I cannot
say. And there was a contest and Eunomus was playing the cithara in the season
of heat, when the cicadas under the leaves were singing over the mountains,
warmed by the sun. They were singing, then, not to the dead serpent, the
Pythian, but to the all-wise God an autonomous song, better than the nomoi of
Eunomus. A string snaps for the Locrian; the cicada flies onto the yoke; it
trilled as upon a branch on the instrument; and the singer, fitting himself to
the cicada's song, filled in the 1.1.3 missing string. So it is not by the
song of Eunomus that the cicada is led, as the myth would have it, having set
up at Pytho a bronze Eunomus with the cithara itself and the Locrian's fellow
contestant; but it flies willingly and sings willingly. And to the Greeks it
seemed to have become an actor of music. 1.2.1 How is it, then, that you have
trusted empty myths, supposing animals to be charmed by music? While the
bright face of truth, it seems, appears to you to be only a fiction, and has
fallen under the eyes of unbelief. Cithaeron, then, and Helicon and the
mountains of the Odrysians and the precincts of Thracians, the mysteries of
error, have been deified and 1.2.2 hymned. I, for my part, even if they are
myth, find it hard to bear so many disasters being made into tragedy; but for
you even the records of evils have become dramas, and the actors of the dramas
spectacles of delight. But come, the dramas and the Lenaean poets, already
quite drunk, having crowned them somewhere with ivy, raving strangely in
Bacchic rite, together with the satyrs themselves and the mad thiasos, and
with the rest of the chorus of daemons, let us shut up on Helicon and
Cithaeron grown old, and let us bring down from above from the heavens truth
together with brightest wisdom onto the holy mountain 1.2.3 of God and the
holy prophetic chorus. And let her, flashing forth a light as far-shining as
can be, illuminate everywhere those who wallow in darkness and deliver men
from error, stretching forth the highest right hand, understanding, unto
salvation; and let those who have lifted their heads and looked up leave
Helicon and Cithaeron, and dwell in Zion; "for out of Zion shall go forth a
law, and a word of the Lord from Jerusalem", a heavenly word, the genuine
contestant crowned in the 1.2.4 theater of the whole cosmos. And my Eunomus
sings not the nomos of Terpander nor that of Capion, nor yet Phrygian or
Lydian or Dorian, but the eternal nomos of the new harmony, the one named
after God, the new song, the Levitical, "without grief and without wrath,
making one forget all evils"; a sweet and true drug of persuasion is mixed
into the song. 1.3.1 To me, then, that Thracian Orpheus and the Theban and the
Methymnaean seem to have been certain men who were no men, deceivers, who
under a pretext of music ruined life, by a certain artistic sorcery


being possessed toward corruptions, celebrating outrages as rites, deifying
griefs, the first to lead men by the hand to the idols, yes, and with stones
and woods, that is statues and paintings, to rebuild the clumsiness of custom,
yoking that truly beautiful freedom of those who lived as citizens under
heaven into the last slavery by songs and incantations. 1.3.2 But my singer is
not of that kind, nor has he come to dissolve at long last the bitter slavery
of the tyrannizing daemons, but as one transferring us to the gentle and
philanthropic yoke of piety he calls back again to the heavens those 1.4.1 who
have been thrown upon the earth. He alone, at any rate, of all who have ever
been, tamed the most troublesome beasts, men: birds those of them who are
light, reptiles the deceivers, and lions the passionate, swine the
pleasure-loving, wolves the rapacious. And stones and woods are the foolish;
and besides, a man baptized in ignorance is more unfeeling than stones. 1.4.2
Let a prophetic voice come forward as our witness, singing in concert with
truth, pitying those worn down in ignorance and mindlessness; "for God is able
from these stones to raise up children to Abraham". He who took pity on the
great ignorance and the hardness of heart of those who had been turned to
stone against the truth raised up a seed of piety that perceives virtue 1.4.3
out of those stones, of the nations that had trusted in stones. Again, then,
certain venomous and shifty hypocrites who waylay righteousness he has called
somewhere "offspring of vipers"; but if one even of these serpents should
repent willingly, following the Word he becomes a "man of God". And other
"wolves" he allegorizes as clothed in sheepskins, hinting at the rapacious in
the forms of men. And all these wildest beasts, then, and such stones, the
heavenly song itself has transformed into tame human beings. 1.4.4 "For we too
were once foolish, disobedient, going astray, enslaved to pleasures and
various desires, living in malice and envy, hateful, hating one another", as
the apostolic writing says; "but when the kindness and the philanthropy of God
our Savior appeared, not from works in righteousness which we ourselves did,
but according to his mercy he saved us". See how much the new song has
availed; it has made men out of stones and men out of beasts. And those who
were otherwise dead, who had no share in the life that truly is, having become
hearers of the song only, came back to life. 1.5.1 This, you see, also ordered
the universe tunefully and stretched the discord of the elements into an order
of concord, so that the whole cosmos might become a harmony for him. And the
sea he let go loose, yet forbade it to advance upon the land, and the earth in
turn he made firm as it was being carried and fixed it as a boundary of the
sea; yes, and the rush of fire he softened with air, as if blending a Dorian
harmony with a Lydian; and the harsh cold of air he tamed by the interweaving
of fire, mixing these 1.5.2 lowest notes of the whole tunefully. And the
undefiled song, support of the whole and harmony of all things, stretched from
the middles to the limits and from the extremes to the middles, tuned this
universe, not according to the Thracian music, the one akin to Jubal, but
according to the ancestral will of God, which David imitated. 1.5.3 And he who
is from David and before him, the Word of God, having looked down on lyre and
cithara, the soulless instruments, and having tuned this cosmos and indeed the
little cosmos, man, both his soul and his body, with the Holy Spirit, psalms
to God through the many-voiced instrument and sings along to the instrument,
man. "For you are cithara and pipe and temple to me;" cithara because of the
harmony, pipe because of the spirit, temple because of the Word, so that the
one may strike the strings, and the other


may breathe, and the other may contain the 1.5.4 Lord. Yes, and David the
king, the citharist, of whom we made mention a little before, was exhorting
toward the truth, and was turning away from idols, far be it that he should
hymn the daemons who were being driven away by the true music from him, by
which, when Saul was being possessed, he by singing only healed him. A
beautiful breathing instrument the Lord wrought, man, according to his own
image; and indeed he himself also is an instrument of God, all-harmonic,
tuneful and holy, supercosmic wisdom, heavenly Word. 1.6.1 What then does the
instrument, the Word of God, the Lord, and the new song wish? To open the eyes
of the blind and to open the ears of the deaf and to lead by the hand those
who limp in the feet or go astray into righteousness, to show God to men who
are out of their minds, to stop corruption, to conquer death, 1.6.2 to
reconcile disobedient sons to a father. Philanthropic is the instrument of
God; the Lord has mercy, educates, exhorts, admonishes, saves, guards, and as
a surplus wage of our learning he promises a kingdom of the heavens, enjoying
this alone from us, that we are saved. For vice feeds on the corruption of
men, but truth, like the bee harming nothing of the things that are, exults in
the salvation of men 1.6.3 alone. You have then the promise, you have the
philanthropy; take a share of the grace. And do not suppose my saving song to
be new in this way, as a vessel or as a house; for it was "before the morning
star", and "in the beginning was the Word and the Word was with God and the
Word was God". 1.6.4 Error is old, but truth appears new. Whether then mythic
goats teach that the Phrygians are ancient, or again the poets who record
those before the moon teach that the Arcadians are, or again those who dream
that this land first showed forth gods and men teach that the Egyptians are;
yet not one of these was before this cosmos, but before the foundation of the
cosmos we were, we who had been begotten beforehand to God by the necessity of
being going to be in it, we the rational plasters of the Word of God, through
whom we are ancient, because "in the beginning the Word 1.6.5 was." But that
the Word was from above, a divine beginning of all things he was and is; and
that now he has taken the name long since consecrated, worthy of power,
Christ, it has been called a new song 1.7.1 by me. The Word, then, Christ, is
cause both of our being long ago (for he was in God), and of our being well
(now indeed he has appeared to men): this same Word, the only one who is both,
God and man, cause of all good things for us; from whom, being taught to live
well, we are sent on to eternal life. 1.7.2 For according to that inspired
apostle of the Lord "the grace of God that brings salvation has appeared to
all men, educating us, that denying ungodliness and worldly desires we should
live soberly and justly and piously in the present age, awaiting the blessed
hope and appearing of the glory of the great God and 1.7.3 our Savior Jesus
Christ." This is the new song, the appearing that has now shone forth in us of
the Word who was in the beginning and was before; and recently the Savior who
was before appeared, he who is in that which is appeared, because "the Word
was with God," a teacher, the Word appeared by whom all things have been
created; and having provided life in the beginning together with the
fashioning as creator, he taught living well when he appeared as teacher,
1.7.4 in order that later he might furnish living always as God. And it is not
now first that he pitied us in our error, but from above from the beginning,
and now already, appearing when we were being destroyed, he has saved us round
about. For the wicked and creeping beast, enchanting, still even now enslaves
and maltreats men, as it seems to me, punishing in a barbarian way, they who
are said to bind the captives to dead 1.7.5 bodies, until they rot together
with them. This wicked tyrant then and dragon, as many as he might be able to
appropriate from birth, with stones and woods


and statues and such idols, having bound them tight with the wretched bond of
superstition, this indeed which is the saying, carrying them still living
1.7.6 he buried them together, until they should also be corrupted together.
For which reason (for one is the deceiver, from above dragging Eve, and now
already the other men too, down into death) one also himself is our helper and
ally, the Lord, signaling beforehand from the beginning prophetically, and now
already also clearly calling unto salvation. 1.8.1 Let us flee then, obeying
the apostolic charge, "the ruler of the authority of the air, of the spirit
now at work in the sons of disobedience", and let us run to the Savior the
Lord, who both now and always was exhorting unto salvation, through wonders
and signs in Egypt, in the desert both through the bush and through the cloud
that followed the Hebrews like a maidservant of philanthropic 1.8.2 grace. By
this fear then he was exhorting the hard-hearted; and already also through
Moses the all-wise and through Isaiah who loves truth and through the whole
prophetic chorus he turns more rationally toward the Word those who possess
ears; and in one place he reviles, and in another he also threatens; and some
of men he even laments; and to others he sings, just as a good physician of
sick bodies plasters some, and smooths others, and pours over others, and some
even cuts with iron, and cauterizes others, and in some place even saws off,
if somehow it is possible 1.8.3 even by a part or a limb to make the man
healthy. Many-voiced indeed is the Savior and many-turned toward the salvation
of men; threatening he admonishes, reviling he turns, lamenting he pities,
psalming he exhorts, through a bush he speaks (those men had need of signs and
wonders) and with fire he frightens men, kindling the flame from a pillar, a
token of grace and fear together; if you obey, the light, if you refuse to
hear, the fire. And since flesh is more honorable than both pillar and bush,
prophets speak after those things, the Lord himself speaking in Isaiah, 1.8.4
himself in Elijah, himself in the mouth of prophets; but you, if you do not
believe prophets, and suppose both the men and the fire a myth, the Lord
himself will speak to you, "who, being in the form of God, did not count it
plunder to be equal with God; but he emptied himself", the God who loves pity,
eager to save man; and already the Word himself speaks to you clearly, putting
unbelief to shame, yes I say, the Word of God become man, in order that you
too may learn from a man, how at last a man may become God. 1.9.1 Then is it
not strange, friends, that God is always exhorting us toward virtue, and we
shrink back from the benefit and put off salvation? For does not John too call
unto salvation and become wholly an exhorting voice? Let us inquire of him
then: "Who and from where among men are you?" Elijah he will not say, and that
he is Christ he will deny; but a voice he will confess, crying in the
wilderness. Who then is John? To take it as a type, let it be permitted to
say, an exhorting voice of the Word crying in the wilderness. What do you cry,
O voice? "Tell us too." 1.9.2 "Make straight the ways of the Lord". John is a
forerunner and the voice is a forerunner of the Word, a comforting voice,
preparing beforehand unto salvation, a voice exhorting unto an inheritance of
the heavens; through which the barren and the desert is no longer unfruitful.
This pregnancy an angel's voice prophesied to me; that too was a forerunner of
the Lord, evangelizing a 1.9.3 barren woman, as John the desert. Through this
voice of the Word then the barren is well-childed and the desert bears fruit;
the two forerunner voices of the Lord, of an angel and of John, hint to me the
salvation stored up, that when this Word has appeared we should carry off a
fruit of good childbearing, 1.9.4 eternal life. Bringing both voices then to
the same point, the writing makes the whole clear;


"Let her who does not bear hear; let her who does not travail break forth a
voice, because the children of the desert are more than of her who has the
husband." An angel was evangelizing us, John was exhorting us 1.9.5 to
understand the farmer, to seek the husband. For this is one and the same, the
husband of the barren, the farmer of the desert, who filled both the barren
and the desert with the divine power. For since the children of the well-born
were many, but the Hebrew woman who from of old had many children was
childless through disobedience, the barren receives the husband and the desert
the farmer; then the one of fruits, the other of believers, and both mothers
through the Word; but to the unbelieving even now both barren and desert
remains. =1.10=.1 John, the herald of the Word, was in this way calling them
to become ready for the presence of God the Christ, and this was what the
silence of Zacharias was hinting, awaiting the forerunner fruit of Christ, in
order that the light of truth, the Word, might loose the mystic silence of the
prophetic riddles, having become a gospel. =1.10=.2 And if you long to see God
truly, take a share of god-befitting purifications, not of laurel leaves and
certain fillets variegated with wool and purple, but having bound on
righteousness and having put around you the leaves of self-control, busy
yourself about Christ; "for I am the door", he says somewhere; which those who
have wished to understand God must learn thoroughly, in order that =1.10=.3 he
may fling open for us the gates of the heavens all at once; for the gates of
the Word are rational, opened by a key of faith; "no one has known God, except
the Son and he to whom the Son may reveal." And a door, I know well, that has
been shut until then, the one who opens later reveals the things within and
shows what it was not even possible to know before, except to those who have
traveled through Christ, through whom alone God is beheld. =2.11=.1 Do not
then busy yourselves about godless inner shrines, nor mouths of pits full of
marvel-mongering, or a Thesprotian cauldron or a Cirrhaean tripod or a
Dodonaean bronze; and leave an old stump honored in desert sands and the
oracle there, withered together with the oak itself, to myths grown old. The
spring of Castalia, at any rate, has been silenced, and another spring of
Colophon, and the other mantic streams likewise have died, and empty of the
puffery they have been exposed late indeed, yet still they have been exposed,
having flowed out together with their own =2.11=.2 myths. Tell us also the
useless oracles of the other mantic art, or rather manic, the Clarian, the
Pythian, the Didymaean, Amphiaraus, Apollo, Amphilochus, and if you wish,
question also the portent-watchers and bird-watchers and the judges of dreams
along with them, and set together beside the Pythian the flour-diviners you
bring and the barley-diviners and the ventriloquists still honored among the
many; yes, and let the inner shrines of Egyptians and =2.11=.3 the
necromancies of Tyrrhenians be given over to darkness. These are truly manic
sophistries of unbelieving men and gambling-houses of unmixed error;
fellow-traders of this sorcery are goats trained for divination and ravens
taught by men to give oracles to men. =2.12=.1 And what if I should list the
mysteries for you? I will not dance them out, as they say Alcibiades did, but
I will quite strip, by the word of truth, the sorcery hidden in them and your
so-called gods themselves, whose rites are mystic, as upon the stage of life I
will wheel them round for the spectators of truth. =2.12=.2 Bacchants
celebrate raving Dionysus with raw-eating, conducting the sacred madness, and
they complete the flesh-distributions of the murders wreathed with the
serpents, crying Eva, that Eve through whom the error followed; and a sign of
Bacchic orgies is a serpent that has been initiated. Straightway, at any rate,
according to the precise speech of the Hebrews the name Hevia


aspirated is interpreted the female serpent; and Deo and Kore have already
become a mystic drama, and Eleusis torch-bears their error and the rape and
their grief. =2.13=.1 And it seems to me one must etymologize the orgies and
the mysteries, the one from the wrath of Deo that came to be toward Zeus, the
other from the defilement that happened concerning Dionysus; and if also from
some Attic Myon, whom Apollodorus says was destroyed in a hunt, there is no
envy; =2.13=.2 your mysteries have been glorified with tomb-honor. And it is
possible also otherwise to think the mysteries as myth-eries for you, the
letters corresponding; for such myths, if some others also hunt, hunt at any
rate the most barbarous of Thracians, the most mindless of Phrygians, the
superstitious of Greeks. =2.13=.3 May he perish then who began this deceit for
men, whether Dardanus, who showed forth the mysteries of the Mother of the
gods, or Eetion, who underpinned the orgies and rites of the Samothracians, or
that Phrygian Midas, who learned from the Odrysian, then distributed to those
subject to him an artful deceit. =2.13=.4 For the Cyprian islander Cinyras
would never persuade me, having dared to hand over from night to day the
wanton orgies about Aphrodite, ambitious to deify a citizen =2.13=.5
prostitute. And Melampus son of Amythaon others say brought over from Egypt to
Greece the festivals of Deo, a grief being hymned. These I would call
first-evils, fathers of godless myths and of destructive superstition, who
planted in life as a seed of vice and corruption the mysteries. =2.14=.1 And
now, for it is time, I will expose your orgies themselves, full of deceit and
marvel-mongering. And if you have been initiated, you will laugh still more at
these myths of yours that are honored. And I speak openly the things that have
been hidden, not ashamed =2.14=.2 to say what you are not ashamed to worship.
She then the "foam-born" and "Cyprus-born", dear to Cinyras (I mean Aphrodite,
the "philomedea, because she appeared from medea," those medea of Uranus that
had been cut off, the lewd ones, which after the cutting forced the wave), as
a fruit worthy of shameless parts [Aphrodite] comes to be for you, in the
rites of this sea pleasure a lump of salt is given as a token of the
generation and a phallus to those being initiated into the adulterous art; and
those being initiated bring coin to her as lovers to a courtesan. =2.15=.1 And
mysteries of Deo and aphrodisiac intertwinings of Zeus toward mother Demeter
and wrath (I do not know what I am to say remaining, of mother or of wife) of
Deo, on whose account she is said to have been addressed Brimo, supplications
of Zeus and a drink of gall and heart-drawings and unspeakable doings; the
same things the Phrygians complete =2.15=.2 for Attis and Cybele and the
Corybantes. And they have babbled that Zeus, having torn off the twins of the
ram, carrying them threw them into the midst of the lap of Deo, paying a false
penalty of the violent intertwining, as if he had castrated himself. =2.15=.3
The symbols of this initiation set out from surplus I know will move laughter
even in you who are not going to laugh, because of the exposures; "I ate from
a drum; I drank from a cymbal; I carried the kernos; I went under the bridal
chamber." Are these symbols not outrage? Are the mysteries not mockery?
=2.16=.1 And what if I should add also the remainders? Demeter conceives, and
Kore is reared, and this begetter Zeus mingles again with Pherphatta, his own
daughter, after the mother Deo, having forgotten the former defilement, father
and corrupter of a girl, Zeus, and he mingles having become a serpent
=2.16=.2, he who had been exposed. A symbol then of Sabazian mysteries for
those being initiated is the god through the lap; and this is a serpent, drawn
through the lap of those being initiated, =2.16=.3 an exposure of the
incontinence of Zeus. Pherphatta too conceives a bull-formed child; indeed,
some idolatrous poet says, ... a bull is father of a serpent and a serpent
father of a bull, on the mountain the secret,


herdsman, the goad, a bucolic goad, I think, calling the fennel-wand, which
indeed the bacchants wreath. =2.17=.1 Do you wish that I narrate to you also
the flower-gatherings of Pherphatta and the basket and the rape by Aidoneus
and the split of the earth and the sows of Eubouleus that were swallowed down
together with the two goddesses, for which reason in the Thesmophoria they
throw pigs when they make megara? This mythology the women celebrate variously
by city, Thesmophoria, Skirophoria, Arretophoria, tragically rendering in many
ways the rape =2.17=.2 of Pherphatta. For the mysteries of Dionysus are quite
inhuman; whom, still a child, while Curetes were dancing around in armed
movement, and Titans had slipped under by deceit, having deceived him with
childish playthings, these Titans tore apart, while he was still an infant, as
the poet of the Rite, Orpheus the Thracian, says; cone and rhombos and
joint-bending toys, and beautiful golden apples from the clear-voiced
Hesperides. =2.18=.1 And it is not useless for condemnation to set out for you
the useless symbols of this rite; knucklebone, ball, spinning-top, apples,
rhombos, mirror, fleece. Athena then, having stolen the heart of Dionysus, was
addressed Pallas from brandishing (pallein) the heart; and the Titans, who
also tore him apart, having set a certain cauldron on a tripod and thrown in
the limbs of Dionysus, were boiling them first; then having pierced them with
spits =2.18=.2 "they held them over Hephaestus." And Zeus later appearing (if
he was a god, perhaps having taken a share of the steam of the roasting meats,
of which indeed your gods confess they "obtained the privilege") maltreats the
Titans with a thunderbolt and deposits the limbs of Dionysus with Apollo his
child to bury. And he, for he did not disobey Zeus, carrying them to Parnassus
deposits the torn-apart corpse. =2.19=.1 And if you wish to behold also the
orgies of Corybantes, these, having killed the third brother, covered the head
of the corpse with a purple cloth and, having crowned it, buried it, carrying
it on a bronze shield under the foothills of Olympus =2.19=.2 (and these are
the mysteries, to speak concisely, murders and tombs). And the priests of
these, whom those who care to call them call Anaktotelestai, add
marvel-mongering to the disaster, forbidding celery to be set on a table root
and all; for they think that from the blood that flowed away of the Cory
=2.19=.3 bantic the celery has grown; just as indeed also the women keeping
the Thesmophoria guard against eating the grains of the pomegranate; for they
think the pomegranates have sprouted from the drops of the blood of Dionysus
that fell to the ground. =2.19=.4 And calling the Corybantes Cabeiri they
proclaim a Cabeiric rite; for these two brother-killers, having taken up the
chest in which the private part of Dionysus was stored, brought it down to
Tyrrhenia, traders of a glorious cargo; and there they spent time, being
fugitives, having set before Tyrrhenians the much-honored teaching of piety,
to worship private parts and a chest. For which reason not unreasonably some
wish Dionysus to be addressed Attis, deprived of private parts. =2.20=.1 And
what wonder if the Tyrrhenians the barbarians are initiated in sufferings so
shameful, when for Athenians and the rest of Greece, I am ashamed even to say,
the mythology about Deo is full of shame? For Deo, wandering in search of her
daughter Kore about Eleusis (and this place is of Attica), grows weary and
sits down by a well in grief. This is forbidden to those being initiated even
now, in order that those who have been initiated may not seem to imitate the
=2.20=.2 lamenting one. And at that time the earth-born were dwelling in
Eleusis; names for them Baubo and Dysaules and Triptolemus, and still Eumolpus
and Eubouleus; Triptolemus was a herdsman, and Eumolpus a shepherd,


and Eubouleus a swineherd; from whom this hierophantic race of the Eumolpidae
and of the Kerykes =2.20=.3 flourished at Athens. And indeed (for I will not
hold back from saying it) Baubo, having received Deo as guest, holds out a
kykeon to her; and when she refused to take and was not willing to drink (for
she was in mourning) Baubo, becoming greatly pained, as if overlooked, draws
up her private parts and shows them to the goddess; and Deo is delighted at
the sight and at last receives the =2.21=.1 drink, pleased at the spectacle.
These are the secret things of the Athenian mysteries. These things Orpheus
too records. And I will set out for you the verses of Orpheus themselves, in
order that you may have the mystagogue as witness of the shamelessness; so
speaking she drew up her robes, and showed all the shape of the body not even
seemly; and the child was Iacchus, and with his hand he kept tossing it,
laughing, under the lap of Baubo; and when then the goddess smiled, she smiled
in her heart, and received the glancing vessel in which the kykeon lay.
