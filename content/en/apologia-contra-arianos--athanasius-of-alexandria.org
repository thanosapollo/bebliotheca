* Apology against the Arians

Of the same, a second apology 1.1 I for my part thought that after so many
proofs made on our behalf the enemies would at last hide themselves and rather
from these things condemn themselves, for the things on which they also
calumniated the others; but since even when thus condemned they do not blush,
but through insensibility they creep on with their slanders, thinking that all
things become unjudged, not that they may be judged (for they flee), but that
they may harass us and the souls of the simple, I thought it necessary to make
a defense to you, so that you may no longer endure their murmuring, but may
condemn their wickedness and their calumny. For to you who are genuine I make
my defense, but 1.2 toward those who love contention I speak freely with the
proofs against them. For our affairs no longer need judgment, for they have
been judged not once, not twice, but even many times. First in the synod
assembled in our country by nearly a hundred bishops, second in Rome when
Eusebius wrote and they and we were summoned and bishops were assembled there
too, more than fifty, and third in the great synod assembled at Sardica by
command of the most God-loving emperors Constantius and Constans; in which
also those who arose against us were deposed as calumniators, and with the
things judged on our behalf there voted bishops more than three hundred from
the provinces of Egypt, Libya and Pentapolis, Palestine, Arabia, Isauria,
Cyprus, Pamphylia, Lycia, Galatia, Dacia, Moesia, Thrace, Dardania, Macedonia,
the Epiruses, Thessaly, Achaia, Crete, Dalmatia, Siscia, the Pannonias,
Noricum, Italy, Picenum, Tuscia, Campania, Calabria, Apulia, Bruttium, Sicily,
all Africa, Sar 1.3 dinia, the Spains, the Gauls, the Britains. And Ursacius
and Valens bore witness to these things, who at first slandered, but later
recanted. For they not only accepted the things judged on our behalf, but also
confessed themselves and the others who arose against us to be calumniators.
For those who recanted thus and wrote thus are clearly also convicting those
around Eusebius; for together with them they jointly 1.4 engineered the plot
against us. That the things then examined and judged by so many and such great
bishops and clearly proven should be superfluous to examine again, everyone
would confess, lest even if they are now investigated they be examined again
and investigated once more and thus the things of the busybodies become
endless. 2.1 For even the vote of so many bishops was enough to put to shame
those who still even wish to pretend against us; but when even enemies bear
witness for us and against themselves, saying that the things against us are a
plot, who still remaining in doubt 2.2 is not ashamed? For when the law
commands that judgments stand "upon the mouth of two and three witnesses,"
behold so great a multitude of witnesses is on our behalf, having as an
addition also the proof from the enemies, so that even those who remain should
no longer attend to those things on which they judged as they wished, but
should now be forced even, instead of the reasonable things, to wrong those
who convict them. For this especially grieves them, because the things which
they themselves hid when they did them and composed by themselves in a corner,
these Valens and Ursacius brought out and uncovered. And they know accurately
that the repentance of these condemns 2.3 those men, and justifies those who
were wronged by them. For this reason then they were also deposed in the synod
at Sardica, as I said before, and reasonably so; for thus also those then

Pharisees, making a defense on behalf of Paul, convicted their own conspiracy
and that of the Jews which had been made against him; thus the blessed David
was shown to be persecuted in vain, when the persecutor confessed saying: "I
have sinned, child David"; thus these too, conquered by the truth, thought it
right and, writing, delivered it to Julius 2.4 the bishop of Rome. And they
wrote also to us, asking to have peace with us, they who had babbled so many
things and perhaps even now are blushing, because those whom they sought to
destroy they see living by the grace of the Lord. And they also anathematized
Arius and the heresy, doing this consistently. For knowing that those around
Eusebius plotted against us for no other reason than their own impiety, once
they decided to confess the calumny against us they immediately also denied
the Christ-fighting heresy, for the sake of which they also played the things
against us. 3.1 The things then written by the bishops on our behalf according
to various synods, and first those of the Egyptians, are these: The holy synod
assembled in Alexandria from Egypt and the Thebaid and Libya and Pentapolis to
the beloved and most longed-for bishops of the catholic church everywhere,
brothers in the Lord, greeting. 3.2 We were able, beloved brothers, even at
the beginning, as soon as our fellow minister Athanasius was plotted against,
or even after he entered Alexandria, to make a defense concerning the things
those around Eusebius plotted against him and to accuse concerning the things
he suffered from them and to show all the calumnies that had been made against
him; but since then the affairs did not permit it, as you also know, and now
we thought that after the return of Athanasius the bishop they would be put to
shame and blush at such 3.3 manifest injustices of theirs, for this reason we
persuaded ourselves to be silent. But since after the man suffered so many
things, after the sojourn into the Gauls, after the stay in a foreign land
instead of his own and very far away, after he almost died from their
slanders, if he had not met with a humane emperor, at which even every enemy
would have been sated even if most savage in wrath, they do not blush, but
again play the young against the church and the man and, being angry at his
release, they dare worse second things. 3.4 And they accuse readily, but they
do not fear what is written in the holy scriptures: "a false witness shall not
go unpunished" and "a mouth that lies slays a soul." For this reason we are no
longer able to be silent, but we marvel at their wickedness and their in 3.5
satiable love of contention for plots. For behold they do not cease again
moving imperial ears against us, they do not cease writing letters of
destruction toward the destruction of a bishop who is the enemy of their
impiety. For again they wrote to the emperors against him, again they wish to
plot against him, charging slaughters that did not happen, again they wish to
murder him charging murders that did not happen. For then too they would have
murdered him by slandering, if we had not met with a humane emperor. Again
him, to say the lesser, they press to go into exile, pre 3.6 tending to lament
the misfortunes of exiles as if they had happened from him. And they lament
among us the things that did not happen, but they are not content with the
things that happened against him, but they wish also to attach second things
that are worse. Thus they are gentle and humane and moderate in manner, or
rather (for the truth shall be said) wicked and bitter and honored from fears
and threats rather than from piety and moderation, which befit bishops. For
such words as not even one of those outside who is a busybody would utter they
have dared 3.7 to pour out in what they wrote to the emperors. And they
accused of so many murders and slaughters not before a governor, not before
some other greater person, but before the three Augusti, not shrinking even
from the distances of the roads, only that all greater

courts may be filled with their accusation. For truly, beloved, the things
done by them are an accusation and the highest of all accusations, since
indeed the courts too are highest of all human things. For what other end of
the examination on these things than death brought on from imperial movement?
4.1 Therefore not the things from Athanasius, but the things done by them are
worthy of lament and wailing; therefore one would more justly lament them; for
over these one must mourn, since it is also written: "do not weep for the
dead, nor lament him, weep with weeping for the one going out, because he will
not return again." For their whole letter looks to nothing other than death,
and they attempt to murder, if they are permitted, and to make 4.2 exiles. For
they were permitted, when the most God-loving father of the emperors fulfilled
their wrath by a change of place instead of death. That these things then are
not even works of simple Christians, and rarely even of pagans, much less of
those seeming to be bishops and to teach the others the just things, we think
your conscience in Christ perceives. 4.3 For how do those who prevent the
others from accusing themselves become accusers even as far as emperors? How
do those who teach to pity misfortunes not even after our exile keep quiet?
For it has become, as is confessed, a common exile of us bishops, and we all
held ourselves as exiles and now, as restored with Athanasius to our
homelands, we have received again instead of the former wailings over him and
laments a very great cheerfulness and 4.4 grace, which may the Lord preserve
and not permit those around Eusebius to overturn. These things, even if the
things they accused him of were true, were certain accusations, because
contrary to the command of Christianity and after the exile of the trial they
again, trampling, accuse of murders and slaughters and other charges and echo
these things to imperial ears against the bishops; but when they lie in all
things and calumniate in all things and truth is not present in their mouths
nor in their letters, how many times over is the evil 4.5 among them, or of
what sort do you suppose the men to be? Let us then already proceed to the
things themselves 4.5 and meet the accusations of theirs that are now being
made. For from these it will be proven that not even the former things babbled
by them at a synod and a judgment were well done, or rather not even truly
said, and again on these they will be condemned. 5.1 We are then put to shame
making a defense about such things; yet since the rash accusers bring
themselves against all things and after the entrance of Athanasius they charge
that murders and slaughters have happened, we ask you to bear with the
defense, even if it is through 5.2 more words; for the facts compel it. Murder
has happened neither from Athanasius nor because of him, since the accusers,
as we said before, also force us into this inglorious defense; but slaughters
and bonds are foreign to our church; Athanasius has handed no one over to an
executioner; and the prison, as far as it depends on him, has never been
troubled 5.3 by him. Our priesthoods, as always, are even now pure, gloried in
only by the blood of Christ and by piety toward him. No presbyter, no deacon
was destroyed by Athanasius; the man committed neither murder nor exile. But
would that those men had not done this against him, bringing the trial in
truth. For no exile has happened here because of him except the bishop
Athanasius of Alexandria himself from them, whom, having been released, they
seek again to surround with the same things or even worse, 5.4 urging their
tongue toward all false and death-bearing words. For behold now they even
transfer the acts of the judges upon him. And they openly confess in the
letter that the prefect of Egypt pronounced against certain persons, but again
they are not ashamed, attaching these sentences too to Athanasius, and this
when he had not yet

set foot in Alexandria, but was still holding to the road from exile and was
in Syria, if indeed one must even bring a long sojourn into a defense for the
point that no one is even liable for whatever a governor or prefect of Egypt
may do; for even if he was at Alexandria, what have the acts of the prefect to
do with Athanasius? But nevertheless he was not even residing in the places;
and the things done were not done by the prefect of Egypt for ecclesiastical
pretexts, but for certain ones which you will know from the minutes, which
after learning the things written by these men we inquired into and sent to
you. 5.5 Therefore when even now they cry out as having happened the things
that happened neither from him nor because of him, and they testify to so many
evils as if fully assured, let them say from what synod they also learned
these things? From what proofs? From what judgment? But if having nothing of
this sort they simply affirm, how the former things happened or how they
speak, we have left it for you to consider. For it is nothing other than
calumny and a plot of enemies and a wrath that thinks ungovernable things and,
against piety, the impiety on behalf of the Ariomaniacs raging, so that the
orthodox may be put out of the way, and with license thereafter the patrons of
impiety may preach what they wish. For so it is. 6.1 When Arius who had acted
impiously, from whom the heresy of the Ariomaniacs is also named because of
him, was cast out of the church by the blessed bishop Alexander, those around
Eusebius, being disciples of his impiety and partners, thinking themselves to
have been cast out, wrote many things, asking Alexander the bishop not to
leave the heretic 6.2 Arius outside the church. But when Alexander, because of
piety toward Christ, did not receive the impious one, they were grieved at
Athanasius who was then a deacon, because inquiring they heard that he was for
the most part with Alexander the bishop and was honored by him. And having
taken a trial of him and of his piety toward Christ from the synod assembled
at Nicaea, in the things in which he spoke freely against the impiety of the
Ariomaniacs, 6.3 they increased the hatred more. But when God led him to the
episcopate, rekindling the wickedness long nourished in them and fearing his
orthodoxy and his stand against impiety (and Eusebius even more, being struck
by the conscience of the things he knows) they plotted in every way and
conspired against the man, they moved 6.4 an emperor against him, they often
threatened synods, and at last they assembled at Tyre. And until now they do
not stand off from writing against him, and they are so unrelenting that they
even blame the appointment of his episcopate, showing themselves everywhere
enemies and haters of the man and speaking false things, only that they may
cheapen him in their falsehoods. But even from these very things which they
lie they convict the former things as false and full of plot. "For after the
death," they say, "of Alexander the bishop, when a few made mention of
Athanasius, six or seven bishops ordain him secretly and in a hole." These
things they wrote even to emperors, they who do not decline to 6.5 write
nothing false. But that the whole multitude and all the people of the catholic
church, as from one soul and mouth having come together, shouted, cried asking
for Athanasius as bishop for the church, this they prayed publicly to Christ
and this they adjured us to do for very many days and nights, neither
themselves standing away from the church nor permitting us to stand away from
this, and we are witnesses of this and the whole city and the province, saying
nothing contrary against him, as those men wrote, but all the best things,
naming him zealous, reverent, a Christian, one of the ascetics, a true bishop.
6.6 And that the majority of us ordained him under the eyes and shouts of all,
we again are more trustworthy witnesses than those who are absent and lie, we
who ordained; but nevertheless Eusebius the appointment

of Athanasius blames, a man who perhaps has not even had an appointment at
all, and if he ever even had one he himself has invalidated it. He was in
Berytus from the beginning, but leaving Berytus he went to Nicomedia, leaving
the one contrary to law, and stepping into the other contrary to law, and
having left his own lovelessly, and holding the other's unreasonably, and
having overlooked the affection of the first by desire of the other's, 6.7 and
not even keeping for the second that which he had from desire. For behold
having departed from there too he again holds another's, everywhere looking
with desire at others' cities and thinking piety to be in wealth and in the
greatness of cities, and considering as nothing the lot of God according to
which someone was appointed, not knowing that even "where two or three are
gathered in the name of the Lord, there the Lord is in the midst of them," not
considering what was said by the apostle that "I will not boast in others'
labors," not perceiving the command from him: "you are bound to a wife, do not
seek release." And if the saying is about a wife, how much more about a church
and the same episcopate, which the one bound to it ought not to seek another,
lest he also be found an adulterer in the divine scriptures. 7.1 But even with
such things on his conscience he has dared to slander the appointment of
Athanasius, which is witnessed by all for good things, and he dares to
reproach him with deposition, himself having been deposed and having as
witness of the deposition the appointment in his place. How then could either
he himself or Theognis depose another, themselves having been deposed and
being convicted by the appointments in 7.2 their place? For you know
accurately that Amphion was appointed in Nicomedia, and Chrestus in Nicaea, in
their place, because of their own impiety and communion with the Ariomaniacs
who were rejected by the ecumenical synod. And wishing to undo that synod
which is truly a synod they attempt to name their own unjust combination a
synod, and not wishing the things from those to prevail they wish the things
among themselves 7.3 to prevail, and they name a synod who do not obey so
great a synod. Thus they have not cared for a synod, but they pretend to care,
so that having destroyed the orthodox they may dissolve the things decided by
the true and great synod against the Arians, of whom being champions they
always and now dare to lie against Athanasius the bishop. 7.4 For those things
too are like the present things which they lie, that at his entrance there
were factions and laments and wailings of the peoples, who were impatient at
receiving him. For nothing of this sort happened, but entirely the opposite,
joy and cheerfulness and a running of the peoples hastening toward the
longed-for sight of him; and the churches were full of gladness and 7.5
thanksgivings being offered to the Lord everywhere. And ministers and clerics
all saw him so that their souls were gladdened and they believe that that day
was more cheerful than all. For what need is there to write the unspeakable
joy of us bishops? For we have said beforehand that we thought ourselves to
suffer with him. 8.1 These things then being thus confessed and being put
forward by them in the contrary way, what faith does the synod or judgment
babbled by them have? For those who dare thus to step upon things they did not
see, things they did not judge, things for which they did not assemble, and to
write as if fully assured, how can they be believed concerning the things they
say they assembled for, and not rather be believed to have done those things
too according to enmity 8.2 and these things? For what synod of bishops even
was there then? What council holding to truth? Who of the majority among them
was not our enemy? Did not those around Eusebius rush against us because of
the madness of Arius? Did they not bring on the others who thought with them?
Did we not always write against them as thinking the things of Arius? 8.3 Was
not Eusebius in Caesarea of Palestine accused of sacrifice by the confessors
with us?

Was not George proven as having been deposed by the blessed Alexander? Were
not others on other and various things being called to account? How then did
these men wish to come together against us? And how do they dare to name a
synod, of which a count sat as president and a speculator was present and a
commentariensis brought us in instead of deacons of the church? That man spoke
and those present were silent, or rather they obeyed the count, and what moved
those seeming to be bishops was hindered by his counsel. That man commanded,
we were led by soldiers, or rather when those around Eusebius commanded, that
man served their 8.4 opinions. And the whole, beloved, what synod is it where
the end, exile and murder, was for glory to an emperor? And what even were the
charges? For on these things the marvel against them is greater. There was a
certain Arsenius, whom they blamed as having been murdered, and they slandered
that a mystical cup had been broken. But Arsenius lives, and prays to be
assembled with us and does not wait for other testimonies, that he may appear
living, but he himself confesses that he lives, writing with his own letters
to our fellow bishop Athanasius, whom they affirmed to be his murderer. And
the impious were not ashamed, accusing that a man at so great a distance and
very great length away by sea and by land had been murdered by him, whose
country in those times the man had not even 8.5 seen. But they have also dared
to hide him and to make him disappear though he suffered nothing. And if it
were possible, they would have transferred him to another world, or rather
they would even have truly led him out of living, so that whether proving true
or fabricating a murder they might truly kill Athanasius. But in this too
thanks be to the divine providence that permits no unjust thing to prevail,
but brings Arsenius under the eyes of all living and clearly convicting the
plot then and the calumny. For he does not turn away as from murderers nor
hate as those who have done injustice; for he has suffered nothing at all, but
he thinks it right to communicate with us and wishes to be numbered with us,
through the things he wrote. 9.1 And nevertheless Athanasius was plotted
against by them as having murdered the living man, and he was exiled by the
same men. For not even the father of the emperors exiled this man, 9.2 but
their slanders. For consider whether it is not so. When nothing was found
against our fellow minister Athanasius, but the count was using force and was
much in zeal against him, bishop Athanasius, fleeing the violence that had
happened, went up to the most pious emperor, at once declining both the count
and their plots, and asking that a lawful synod of bishops be assembled or
that he himself also receive the de 9.3 fense of the things they brought
against him. And the emperor writes, being angry, and having summoned them and
promising that he himself would hear, he who had also commanded the synod to
happen. But those around Eusebius going up slander Athanasius no longer with
the things babbled by them at Tyre, but about grain and a holding of ships, as
if Athanasius had promised that he was able to prevent the conveyance of grain
from Alexandria to Constantinople; these things some of ours who were inside
with Athanasius heard the em 9.4 peror threatening. Then when Athanasius was
lamenting over the slander and affirming that this was not true (for how could
a private and poor man be able to do such great things?), Eusebius, not even
declining the slander publicly, swore that Athanasius was rich and powerful
and sufficient for all things, so that from these things Athanasius might be
thought also to have said those. These things the solemn bishops were
accusing, but the grace of God has become stronger than their wickedness, for
it moved the piety of the emperor to humanity and instead of death it provided
exile. 9.5 Therefore the slanders and nothing else are the causes of these
things. For the emperor before this, writing, blamed the plot, accused the
conspiracy, voted against the Meletians, calling them in writing unlawful,
worthy of a curse, the most terrible things. For he was moved having heard of
a dead man, who

was among the living; he was moved having heard of a murder upon one living
and not deprived of life, whose letter also we have sent. 10.1 But those
around Eusebius, the admirable ones, so that they may seem also to undo the
truth and the things written, pretend the name of a synod and receive its work
from the emperor. And a count for this, and soldiers as bodyguards of the
bishops, and imperial let 10.2 ters compelling those whom they asked to
assemble. Here consider the paradox of their plot and the irregularity of the
things dared by them, so that from somewhere, in every way, they may snatch
the man from us. For if they entrusted the judgment to themselves alone as
bishops, what need of a count and soldiers? Or how were they assembled under
imperial letters? But if they needed an emperor and wished to have the
authority from him, why 10.3 did they dissolve his judgment? Or how, when that
man judged the Meletians calumniators, un 10.3 lawful, and Athanasius most
pure through the things he wrote, and treated as tragedy the fabricated murder
upon the living man, did these men determine both that the Meletians spoke
truth and that that man was liable, and not being ashamed they made the living
man a corpse, living even after the judgment of the emperor, and when they
assembled, and until now being with us? 11.1 These things concerning Arsenius;
but a mystical cup, what sort or where broken by Macarius? For this they
babble going around up and down. And yet not even the accusers have dared to
charge Athanasius, except those who were put forward by them, but nevertheless
they themselves bring the end of the blame upon him, which 11.2 ought not even
to have gone against Macarius who was not convicted. And they are not ashamed,
making a tragedy of these mysteries before catechumens, and what is worst,
before Greeks, although it is necessary, as it is written, "it is good to hide
the mystery of a king," and as the Lord commanded: "do not give the holy
things to the dogs nor throw the pearls before the swine." For one must not
make a tragedy of the mysteries to the uninitiated, lest the Greeks, not
knowing, laugh, and the catechumens becoming busybodies be scandalized. 11.3
But nevertheless what sort or where or among whom was a cup broken? For the
accusers are Meletians, who ought not to be believed at all. For they have
become schismatics and enemies of the church not now, but from the blessed
Peter who became bishop and martyr, who also plotted against Peter himself and
slandered Achillas who was after him 11.4 and accused Alexander as far as the
emperor himself. For having thus practiced they then passed over also to
Athanasius, doing nothing foreign to their own wickedness, for just as they
calumniated those before him, so also this man. But now their calumny and
slanders have prevailed and not before, because they had those around Eusebius
as fellow workers and patrons because of their own impiety of the Ariomaniacs,
11.5 for the sake of which as against many bishops so also they plotted
against Athanasius. For even that place in which he says the cup was broken
was not a church; the one dwelling in the place was not a presbyter; the day
on which they say Macarius did this was not the Lord's day. Since then there
was neither a church there nor the one performing the sacred rite nor the day
11.6 requiring it, what sort or when or where has the mystical cup been
broken? For that there are many cups both throughout the houses and in the
midst of the market is clear, and the one who breaks none of these acts
impiously, but the mystical cup, which even if it is broken by one willing,
makes the one who has at tempted impious, is found only among those who
preside lawfully. This is the manner of this 11.7 cup alone, no other. This
you lawfully offer beforehand to the peoples, this you received from the
ecclesiastical canon, this alone belongs to those who preside over the
catholic church. For it is yours alone to offer beforehand the blood of
Christ, and of none of the others. But as much as the one who breaks the
mystical is impious, so much more impious is the one

who insults the blood of Christ; for he insults who does this contrary to the
ecclesiastical ordinance. (And we say these things not because even a cup of
schismatics has been broken by Macarius, but because there was nothing at all
there; for how? where there was neither a place of a Lord's house nor anyone
of the church there, nor even was it the time of mysteries.) And this is the
much-babbled Ischyras, who was not even ordained by the church and, when
Alexander received the presbyters appointed by Meletius, was not even numbered
with those; thus he was not even appointed from there. 12.1 From where then is
Ischyras a presbyter? Who appointed him? Colluthus perhaps? For this is what
remains. But that Colluthus died being a presbyter and every hand of his has
become invalid and all those appointed by him in the schism have become laymen
12.2 and are assembled thus, is clear and has been made doubtful to no one.
How then would a private man, dwelling in a private little house, be believed
to have a mystical cup? But then they named the private man a presbyter and
they granted him this title because of the injustice against us, and now as a
wage of the accusation they procure the building of a church. 12.3 Thus the
man did not have a church, but now as a wage of malice and of obedience toward
the accusation he receives what he did not have, and perhaps they even gave
him an episcopate in return; for he babbles these things going around and thus
puffs himself against us. Thus thereafter such great prizes are added by the
bishops to the accusers and calumniators; reasonably, for having had him as a
fellow worker toward the things they wished, as a partner of the affairs, they
thus thought him worthy also of a like episcopate. But not yet these things,
but still give your ears to the things then done by them. 13.1 Not being able
against the truth, although prepared against it, and when Ischyras proved
nothing at Tyre, but was being shown a calumniator and their plot of calumny
was being dissolved, they postpone to proofs and promise to send 13.2 from
themselves those who would inquire throughout Mareotis. And those whom we
declined openly for many reasons and as thinking the things of Arius and for
this reason our enemies, they themselves sent secretly, using the power,
Diognius, Maris, Theodore, Macedonius, two younger in age and in manner
Ursacius and Valens from Pannonia, and these after so great a road, which they
endured in order to judge the enemy, again from Tyre were hastening even into
Alexandria, not even declining that the judges become witnesses, but taking on
every manner of plot openly and undergoing every toil and every road, so that
they might fulfill the plot being constructed. 13.3 And they left bishop
Athanasius held in a foreign land, but they themselves entered into the city
of the enemy as if reveling against the church and the people, and what is
more unreasonable, taking Ischyras the accuser with themselves they did not
permit Macarius the accused to follow, but left him under guard at Tyre; for
up and down Macarius the presbyter of Alexandria was being brought by them
under the charge. 14.1 Alone then having entered Alexandria with the accuser,

cause because of which we were sent into the Gauls, who again does not
perceive the choice of the emperor, and the murderous soul of those around
Eusebius, and that the emperor did this, lest they counsel some greater plot?
For he hearkened simply. This is the act of those around Eusebius, this is the
device of the plot against us. 88.2 Who perceiving these things would not say
that nothing has happened according to favor on our behalf, but that justly
and consistently so great a multitude of bishops both privately and in common

Valens and Ursacius reasonably condemned themselves and recanting wrote such

our blessed fellow ministers also, doing justly and ecclesiastically, since

90.1 If anyone then wishes to learn both the things concerning us and the
calumny of those around Eusebius, let him read the things written on our
behalf and let him have as witnesses not one or two 90.2 or three, but so
great a multitude of bishops. And of these let him again take as witnesses
those around Liberius and Hosius and those with them, who seeing the things
that happened against us underwent to suffer all things rather than to betray
the truth and the judgment on our behalf. And this they have done counseling
well and holily; for the things these have suffered show 90.3 the necessity
which the other bishops also have suffered. And there are minutes and a
posting as a pillar against the Arian heresy and the wickedness of the
calumniators, and a pattern and type for those who happen after these things
"to contend on behalf of the truth until death," and to turn away from the
Arian heresy as being Christ-fighting and a forerunner of the antichrist, and
not to believe those who attempt to speak against us. For a trustworthy and
sufficient testimony of such and so many bishops is the defense and vote on
our behalf.
