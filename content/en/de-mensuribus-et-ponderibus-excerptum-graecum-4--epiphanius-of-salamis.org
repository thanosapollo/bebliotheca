82.1 Up to this point, most excellent one, having been contented with all
that was said before, we have set forth the subject of the interpreters;
then next, as we promised, to the things that remain, at your prayers, O man
of God, let us direct our mind, making clear the weights and measures and
numbers in the divine scriptures, whence each is named, and on account of
what it is so called, and whence it took the occasion of its naming, and
what is the quality and the weight and the capacity of each of these. 82.2
A measure then is a kor, and it is laid down in the Gospel according to
Luke, whence the Savior praises the prudent steward who transcribed so many
for the debtors in their own handwriting instead of so many kors, and made
so many baths of oil instead of so many. 82.3 For the names of measures are
these: lethek, gomor, bathos, saton, modios, kabos, choinix, hyphē [of fine
flour], drax of flour, artabe, three measures of fine flour, three kana of
groats, nebel of wine, kollathon, alabastron of myrrh, kampsakēs of water,
kotyle of oil, kyathos, metretes of wine, metretes of oil, tryblion,
xestes, amphoreus, aporryma, sabitha, in, chous, the golden jar in which
the manna was laid up, marēs, kypros, kongiarion. 82.4 The kor then is
taken from the Hebrew dialect, which is called chor. And it is thirty
modii. And chor is said from the supposition of the hill; for a hill is
called choria. And the thirty modii, being heaped up, make a camel's load.
82.5 And lethek, as it is said in Hosea the prophet: "I hired for myself a
lethek of barley." And in the copies "a gomor of barley" is the same. For
fifteen modii are signified. And lethek is called in the Hebrew tongue,
which is "lifting up," from the youth being able to lift the fifteen modii
and place them on the donkey. 82.6 And the same is also called gomor. And
there are two gomors, great and small, of which the great is of equal
measure with the lethek, being itself also of fifteen modii, and the small
of twelve. Bathos. And this too has come from the Hebrew dialect,
synonymous with the oil-press called bith. For bathos is interpreted
oil-press, and it is of fifty xestai. And the measure is of the work of the
oil-press. 82.7 Mnasis or medimnos, I think, have come from the Roman
tongue. For medionum is interpreted "middle" in that tongue. Mnasis then is
measured among the Cyprians and among other nations. And it is ten modii of
wheat or of barley, into the modius of the seventeen xestai among the
Cyprians. And the medimnos differs among the Cyprians themselves. For the
Salaminians, or rather the Constantians, have the medimnos of five modii,
but the Paphians and the Sicilians measure it of four and a half modii.
82.8 Saton is called, taken from the Hebrew dialect itself, and pronounced
in the feminine; but in the Greek in the neuter: for it is said saton and
not satos. And it is a modius overflowing, so that the modius is filled,
and yet a fourth of the modius must still overflow. And it is called sata
in the same dialect, pronounced "taking" and "lifting," from the measurer
taking and heaping the measure in some manner. 82.9 And the name of the
modius was found by the Hebrews with much exactness, being of twenty-two
xestai; and not simply nor as it happened, but with much exactness. For the
just modius, as the law is accustomed to say, was measured according to the
holy measure. And the holy measure is nothing else than the twenty-two
works which God made in the six days of the week. =82.10= These twenty-two
numbers, found everywhere and numbered in different forms, in the
twenty-two works which God made in the six days of the cosmopoeia, and in
twenty-two generations from Adam to Israel, and in the twenty-two elements
of the letters from aleph to tau, and in the twenty-two books from Genesis
to Esther, a measure was generated to us of twenty-two xestai, which is
called among the Hebrews modē, among the Greeks modia on account of
clearness. Similarly also the Egyptians indeed call it this, and likewise
the Syrians and Arabs say modia, which is interpreted "agreement." For the
modius, being filled, agrees that it is filled. And it is also called
gnōmōn. =82.11= And kabos is from the same dialect a distinct measure, that
is, the fourth of a modius. =82.12= And choinix and hyphē are one, but
called by a double name. And it is of two xestai and a postemorion.
=82.13= Drax of flour. This is the handful and plain to all. =82.14=
Artabe. This measure was called among the Egyptians. And it is of
seventy-two xestai. And with much exactness this too was so formed.
Seventy-two men then were building the tower, when also they were joined
into seventy-two tongues from one. Whence also they were called merores
into the divided voice. And the same measure the metretes has according to
the holy measure. =82.15= Three measures of fine flour, which Abraham
commanded Sarah to prepare for the angels, of which measures he commanded a
hidden cake of bread to be made. And each of these measures held a gomor.
=82.16= And the gomor was a tenth of the great measure, that is, of the
artabe, which becomes seven xestai and a fifth. =82.17= Three kana of
groats, where they were accustomed at that time to store the groats. And it
is a kind of bread cut in two. =82.18= Nebel of wine, which is a measure of
one hundred fifty xestai. =82.19= And the saton is of fifty xestai.
=82.20= Alabastron of myrrh is a glass vessel holding a litra of oil. And
the measure is half of a xestes. And it was called alabastron because of
its easily-broken nature. =82.21= Kampsakēs of water is a measure of twelve
xestai of water. And the kampsakēs prepared for Elijah is of four xestai.
=82.22= There is a measure, kotyle. It is half of a xestes. And kotyle is
called from the xestes being cut into two. =82.23= A kyathos is a sixth of
a xestes. And the writing calls the kyathoi methekōth; and the masmarōth it
calls the strainers, which we also call sieves. And both are used with the
same name, since they also have the same operation. =82.24= Tryblion is an
oxybaphon in shape, or a side-dish, and holds the measure of half a xestes.
=82.25= The Alexandrian xestes carries a weight of two litrai in the oil.
=82.26= And aporryma is measured among the Thebans alone. For it is half of
the saites. And the true saites is of twenty-two xestai. =82.27= Sabitha.
This is a Syrian name, which is interpreted "monthly drawing." Among the
Ascalonites of twenty-two xestai. =82.28= In. The great in is of eighteen
xestai, and the holy in of nine xestai. =82.29= Chous is of eight xestai.
And the so-called holy one of ʹ xestai, which is a twelfth of a metretes.
=82.30= Marēs is a measure among the Pontics of two hydriai. And the hydria
among them is of ten xestai; so that the kypros is of twenty Alexandrian
xestai. =82.31= Kypros among the same Pontics is a measure of dry produce,
two modii, which is said to be among them of five choinikes. And the
choinix among them is of five xestai; so that the kypros is of twenty
xestai. For the great modius among them is of twenty-four xestai. =82.32=
And kollathon among the Syrians is the half of the liquid saton. And it is
of twenty-five xestai. =82.33= And kongiarion is a measure of liquid also,
pronounced among the Hebrews. This measure is shown in the chronographies
of Eusebius and the other chronographers, as each of the kings then in
bounty granted it to the Roman people for refreshment; which is interpreted
"joined together" or "twisted together." =82.34= A talent is the surpassing
measure of all that is weighed. And according to the litrismos it is of one
hundred twenty-five litrai. =82.35= †Six lepta of a talent are seven
assaria. And the seven lepta of the assaria are twenty denarii†. =82.36=
And the argyros was first stamped as a coin. And it was stamped from the
Assyrians. And they say Abraham brought the type into the land of Canaan.
=82.37= And of the talent, the one hundred and twenty-fifth part is one
litra. And the litra is twelve ounces. =82.38= And the ounce has two
staters. =82.39= And the stater is half of an ounce, having two didrachms.
=82.40= Concerning the shekel. The shekel, which is also called kodrantes,
is a fourth of the ounce, and half of the stater, having two drachms. For
the drachm was the eighth of the ounce. =82.41= And the drachm was called
holkē of the same weight as the shekel, weighing at the time the hair of
Absalom when he was shorn, having a weight of one hundred twenty-five
shekels, which is an ounce and one shekel added, becoming two litrai and a
half. =82.42= The obol also was stamped among the silver coins. And it had
also an eighth of the ounce, not made of silver, but of iron. And there was
another obol stamped of silver, being a coin, which was most thin, an
eightieth of the ounce. For it says in Leviticus: "and the didrachm is
twenty obols." That the didrachm is a fourth of the ounce we have already
taught. =82.43= Chalkoi. The Egyptians invented these. And there are two
stamped coins. And among the Alexandrians the coins are called chalkina.
And the chalkous in weight is an eighth of an ounce, as the drachm.
=82.44= Mna, which is called manē in the Hebrew. And the Italian mna is
forty staters, which is twenty ounces, of one litra and two-thirds.
=82.45= And there have been many types of coins at times. =82.46= And the
noumion was stamped from a certain Numa, king of the Romans. =82.47= And
the ancients called dichryson the half of the argyros. And the argyros is
that which the Romans call miliarision, which is interpreted "military
payment." =82.48= And the dichryson also was a coin, which was afterwards
called kibdēlos on account of the king of the time being killed and his own
type cast out of the dichryson, which was resembling it. For from then,
wherever it was found bearing the image of the killed king, it was named
kibdēlos, that is, unapproved. =82.49= Phollis, which is also called a
purse. And it is double, composed of two argyroi, which become two hundred
denarii. And a phollis is two lepta according to the denarism, but not
according to the argyrism.

83.t Concerning talents.

83.1 Talent. This is of one hundred twenty-five litrai, and according to
the thinnesses in the coin, having been cut into a lepton, it is divided
into lepta, which are called assaria; which is interpreted from the Hebrew
"diminished." And the argyros was sixty assaria; and those were two denarii
thrown by the widow into the treasury, which were also called two lepta;
for the assaria were very thin. 83.2 Kentinariom from the Latin centum,
which is one hundred. 83.3 And litra from the Hebrew; for li is said "to
me," and tra "the difference." 83.4 The ounce has two staters; and in
Hebrew it is called choutra; and the stater is of one ounce, and two
didrachms, which is called epikephalaia, and in the Roman dialect kapition;
for they call the head kapout. And the didrachm is two drachms. 83.5 Shekel
from the Hebrew sekel, which is "weight." And it has two lepta, which are
two drachms. 83.6 And two didrachms are two shekels according to the holy
shekel, which make one stater. 83.7 And the stater or holkē of two
didrachms completes the measure. 83.8 And it is called kodrantes [for the
shekel, that is, one ounce, is a sixth]. And it is interpreted from the
Hebrew kodrantes, that is, "loosing." 83.9 And the shekel itself is the
fourth of it, and the half of the stater. And it has two drachms; for the
drachm is the eighth of it. And the drachm is also called holkē. =83.10=
And the hair of the shearing of Absalom was of a weight of one hundred
twenty-five shekels; which is thirty and one shekel, or two ounces, of
litrai ... and one shekel. =83.11= Obol. This is an eighth of it, made of
iron. And this was an arrow; for before the presence of Christ, because
their life was joined in war, they had need of it against the enemies, and
they made their administrations by such things, buying each five arrows and
ten, and bread or something else. And this in weight is an eighth of it.
And there was also another obol struck of silver, a coin, which was most
thin, and an eightieth of it. And the didrachm is twenty obols, which is a
fourth of it. =83.12= And chalkos is a stamped coin, whence among the
Alexandrians the coins are called chalkina. =83.13= And the drachm is the
eighth of it. =83.14= Mna instead of manē; for in the Hebrew the silver is
called manē. And the Italian mna is forty staters, that is twenty-one and
two-thirds; and the Theban of sixty staters, that is two and a half litrai.
=83.15= And many types of coins of old, which they called noummoi from
Noummos, whence also the coin. =83.16= And miliarision is the silver one,
which is [also a] military payment; for milita is the soldiering. =83.17=
And the phollis fills one hundred twenty-five coins; and it is called among
the Romans a purse. =83.18= And maris is a Pontic measure of two hydriai.
And the hydria among them is of ten xestai, so that the kypros is of twenty
Alexandrian xestai. =83.19= And the kypros is a measure of two modii, and
is said to be among the Pontics of five choinikes. And the choinix is of
two xestai; so that the kypros is of twenty. For the great modius among
them is of twenty-four xestai. =83.20= Litra among the Romans is
interpreted libra, which is etymologized among them as "equality" or "equal
rule." And it has twelve ounces. And the name has come from the Greek from
onkos. =83.21= And the litra is two hundred eighty-eight grammata. And each
gramma is of ʹ keratia; these are bones from the fruit of the carob. And
this bone, when it should be perfect, makes a weight of well-fruited barley
ˉβ; so that the litra is of grains of barley γυνʹ, of keratia αψκηʹ, of
grammata two hundred eighty-eight, of ounces two. And the ounce is of
twenty-four grammata. =83.22= And again the ounce is divided among the
Hebrews into two staters. And the stater has two shekels; and the shekel
has two lepta. And the lepton is one holkē, an eighth of it. =83.23= And
among some also an obol is a coin, from the world being governed by the
kings in this coin. We call it argyrion from being stamped of silver. And
it is great, which was called argyros, of one hundred denarii. And each
denarion is of sixty assaria. =83.24= And the argyros is called manē among
the Hebrews. =83.25= The Greek xestes from the large measures being seethed
into thinness.

84.t Concerning measures.

84.1 A kor is thirty modii; and among the Hebrews it is called chor. 84.2 A
lethek is fifteen modii. 84.3 A gomor likewise fifteen modii. 84.4 A batos
is a measure of fifty xestai. 84.5 And mnasis is ten modii of wheat or
barley, taken from the Roman medionum, which is "middle." 84.6 Medimnos:
the Salaminians five modii, and the Sicilians four and a half. 84.7 Saton
is a heaped modius; among the Hebrews in the feminine, among the Greeks in
the neuter. And a heaped modius is among the Hebrews, and through the
heaped the fourth of the modius, the overflow, is called sao, that is
"taking" or "lifting"; a modius among the Hebrews of twenty-two xestai.
84.8 Kabos is here the fourth of the modius, there the fifth, and there the
ʹ. And kaba in Hebrew is "to cut," whence it was so named because the
modius is cut into small parts. And among the Greeks it was called kabos
for clearness. 84.9 Choinix and hyphē are one measure, but called a double
name, in the Hebrew in the masculine, and in the Greek in the feminine. And
it is the eighth of the modius among the Cyprians, which is a modius among
them of seventeen xestai and † of a cup. And the hyphē is said from the
Hebrew dialect itself ophi, which is the measure of the two drakōs of the
hand. =84.10= Drax, the hand-numbers of one hand. =84.11= Artabe among the
Hebrews of seventy-two xestai. And likewise the metretes is of seventy-two
xestai; and the holy measure, both the liquid metretes and the artabe of
the produce. And the artabe was called from the Egyptian ortob, which is
"well put together." =84.12= Three measures, the small gomor, which is of
ʹ xestai; so that it is the tenth of the artabe. =84.13= Three measures of
fine flour. These three measures each held a gomor. And the gomor was a
tenth of the great measure, that is, of the artabe; which becomes seven
xestai and a fifth; so that the same measure is of the gomor. Again there
were three measures, which were made from two xestai; and they become
fifteen. And the measure has this manner.

85.1 Concerning the koros, which is also called chor.

The koros is taken from the Hebrew dialect, which is called chor; and it is
thirty modii. And chor is said from the supposition of the hill; for a hill
is called chorion; for the thirty modii, being heaped up, make a camel's
load. 85.2 Concerning the lekeith or thekel. Lekeith, as it is said in
Hosea the prophet: "I hired for myself a lekeith of barley." And in the
antistrophe "a gomor of barley" is the same, and fifteen modii are
signified. And lekeith is said from the Hebrew tongue, which is "lifting
up," from the youth being able to lift fifteen modii of wheat or barley and
place them on the donkey. And the same is also called gomor; for it is the
great, which is called gomor from the Hebrew dialect. And there is also a
small gomor of twelve modii. 85.3 Concerning the badou or batou. A badon is
called liquid of fifty xestai, and it also has come from the Hebrew
dialect, synonymous with the oil-press called bēth; for bada is interpreted
oil-press; and it is of fifty xestai; and the measure is of the work of
oil. 85.4 Concerning the mnasis or medimnos. Mnasis and medimnos, I think,
have come from the Roman tongue; for medionum is interpreted "middle" in
that tongue. Mnasis then is a metretes among the Cyprians and among other
nations. And it is ten modii of wheat or barley. 85.5 Concerning measures
and weights. And the saton is a heaped modius; and because of the overflow
it is called saton. 85.6 A talent was called a thin litra. And lepta were
called the assaria, that is the ten noummia; and the denarion was then of
sixty assaria, that is the keration. And the argyros was of one hundred
denarii. †And the coin was first stamped of silver. And the talent
according to the litrismos is one hundred twenty-five litrai. And the
talent again is the surpassing measure of all that is weighed. 85.7 An
alabastron is a glass bission, holding one litra of oil. And the alabastron
is said to be carried for oil because of its easily-broken nature. 85.8 And
a drachm is the eighth of an ounce; and it is also called holkē; and it has
three grammata. And the obol has grammata aʹ #25. And the half of the
drachm is the kyathos, of two ounces.

86.t Of Saint Epiphanius of Cyprus.

86.1 A talent is of one hundred twenty-five litrai. 86.2 And the kodrantes
is of three nomismata. 86.3 And the assarion, as some say, is a decanummion
and the same a lepton. 86.4 And the didrachm is the fourth of the ounce.
86.5 And the stater is the half of the ounce, as Epiphanius of Cyprus hands
down. 86.6 A denarion is the eighth of an ounce. 86.7 And an alabastron is
a vessel narrow-long, fit for much drinking, pleasant for drinking. 86.8 A
tryblion, the dish called in common use. 86.9 And korbanōn is interpreted a
gift-receiving place. =86.10= Golgotha, where the Hebrews say Adam was
buried, whence also it has the name; or as much as the place is higher than
the whole inhabited world. =86.11= The Beelzeboul, prince of the dead,
those around Aquila the interpreter have handed down, as we found in the
second. =86.12= And the milion has seven stadia, and forty-six feet,
called Philetaireioi among us. =86.13= And the pēchys has one and a half
feet. That called "solid" among us.

87.t Concerning the underlying weights of the Hebrews.

87.1 A nebel is twenty-four xestai. 87.2 An in is ten xestai. 87.3 A gomor
is thirteen xestai, that is the tenth of the three sata. 87.4 And the saton
is a measure having ten shekels. 87.5 And the shekel is one weight, and two
the coin. And they become twenty obols. For the royal shekel is twenty
obols, and among others the fourth of the ounce. 87.6 An assarion of bronze
was a coin drawing a trilitraion weight. 87.7 And a talent is the sixty
mnai. 87.8 And a mna is the one hundred drachms. 87.9 And a drachm is the
six obols. =87.10= And the stater has two didrachms. =87.11= A denarion was
the sixty assaria, which are also called lepta. =87.12= And the saton again
among others is a modius, †koumoulos. =87.13= And the medimnos is one
hundred and eight measures. =87.14= And the talent is one nomisma.
