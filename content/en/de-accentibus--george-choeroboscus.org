On accents, from Choeroboscus, Aetherius, Philoponus, and others. 1.1 Every
monosyllabic neuter noun is circumflexed, e.g. =πᾶν=, =πῦρ=, =κῆρ=, =ἦρ=,
=ὦς=, =φῶς=, =σταῖς=, =κρῖ=, =σκῶρ=, and their vocatives; for the vocatives
also follow the circumflexed nominative. The monosyllabic names of the letters
are also circumflexed, =μῦ=, =νῦ=, =ξῖ=, =πῖ=, =ῥῶ=, and the rest. Among the
masculines, =Θρᾷξ= and =βῶξ= are circumflexed, for they are from =Θράϊξ= and
=βόαξ=; yet common usage makes =Θρᾴξ= oxytone. Of those ending in =σ=, =λᾶς=,
=πᾶς=, =φθοῖς=, =εἷς= are circumflexed; but the disyllable =οὐδείς= and
=μηδείς= are oxytone. 1.2 All monosyllables in =υς= with simple =υ= are also
circumflexed: =μῦς=, =σῦς=, =ὗς=; but their oblique cases have the =υ= short
and are paroxytone: =σύες=, =μύες=, and the rest. These feminines are
circumflexed: =μνᾶ=, =γῆ=, =γραῦς=, =Κῶς= the island, =βῶς= the shield; but
=δώς= the gift and =κλείς= are oxytone; and =σῶς= and =παῖς=, whether
masculine or feminine, are circumflexed. All the remaining monosyllables are
oxytone: =αἴξ=, =Πάν= a proper name, =φθείρ=, =Ζεύς=, =ῥήξ=, =μήν=, =χήν=,
=σής=, =κτείς=, =θώψ=, =Τρώς=, =φώς= the man; the participial forms are
oxytone as well: =στάς=, =βάς=; =στάν=, =βάν=. 1.3 Those in =ας= declined by
dropping of =σ=, or through =ντ=, are circumflexed: =βορρᾶς βορρᾶ=,
=Θωμᾶς Θωμᾶ=, =ἀλλᾶς ἀλλᾶντος=, except =ἱμάς ἱμάντος= and
=ἀνδριάς ἀνδριάντος=; the participles are oxytone as well: =ἱστάς ἱστάντος=,
=βιβάς βιβάντος=. As many as are declined neither by dropping of =σ= nor
through =ντ= are oxytone: =ἐθάς ἐθάδος=, and the feminines: =λαμπάς=, =τριάς=.
1.4 Those in =αν= of more than one syllable are oxytone: =ὁ παιάν=,
=ὁ Ἀλκμάν=; =ὦ παιάν=; =ὦ Ἀλκμάν=; but their genitives are properispomenon:
=τοῦ παιᾶνος=, =τοῦ Ἀλκμᾶνος=. 1.5 All those in diphthongal =εις= are oxytone:
=οὐδείς=, =μηδείς=, =καθείς=, and the participles: =ὁ τυφθείς=, except the
monosyllable =εἷς=, as we also said before. 1.6 All those in =ευς= are
oxytone: =βασιλεύς=, =Πηλεύς=; and their vocatives, dropping the =σ=, are
circumflexed. 1.7 Those in =ης=, if they are compounded from =κλέος=, are
circumflexed; for =Σοφοκλέης= becomes =Σοφοκλῆς=, and the like; also
circumflexed are as many as have the genitive in =ου=: =Ἑρμῆς Ἑρμοῦ=,
=Ἀπελλῆς Ἀπελλοῦ=; those besides these are oxytone, whether masculine or
feminine: =ψευδής ψευδέος=, =ἀληθής ἀληθέος=, =προβλής προβλῆτος=,
=ἀβλής ἀβλῆτος=. 1.8 All those in =ους= are circumflexed, except =πούς= and
=ὀδούς=. 1.9 Those in =υς=, whether masculine or feminine, are oxytone:
=τραχύς=, =βραδύς=, =ἰχθύς=, =χλαμύς=, =νηδύς=. =1.10= Those in =ων= declined
through =ντ= are circumflexed: =Ξενοφῶν Ξενοφῶντος=, =Χαιρεφῶν Χαιρεφῶντος=,
because such are also from contraction; but if they are declined otherwise,
they are oxytone: =Τυφῶνος=, =ἡγεμόνος=, except =Ποσειδῶν Ποσειδῶνος=; for it
is contracted from =Ποσειδάων=. The second futures in the participles are also
circumflexed: =ὁ τυπῶν=, =ὁ λιβῶν=. =1.11= Those in =ως= are oxytone: =ἱδρώς=,
=λαγώς=; the participles are oxytone as well: =ὁ πεποιηκώς=, =ὁ τετυφώς=,
=ὁ ἑστώς=; for even if this last is contracted, it is not from acute and
grave, but from grave and acute, and therefore it is again oxytone according
to the rule. =1.12= No feminine or neuter noun of more than one syllable is
circumflexed when it is uncontracted, but those that have been circumflexed
were circumflexed from contraction: =συκία συκῆ=, =φακέα φακῆ=; likewise also
the neuters: =ἀργύρεον ἀργυροῦν=, =χρύσεον χρυσοῦν=; those not from
contraction are all oxytone: =ἡ Λητώ=, =ἡ λαμπάς=, =ἡ αἰδώς=, =τὸ ἑστάν=,
=τὸ ζευγνύν=; but such feminines or neuters as come from

masculines ending in =ους= are circumflexed: =ἡ ἁπλῆ=, =τὸ ἁπλοῦν=, because
also =ἁπλοῦς=, and the like.

2.τ On ethnics and Hebrew names. =2.13= Ethnic and Hebrew names are
indeclinable and oxytone in all the cases: =ὁ Σαμψών τοῦ Σαμψών=,
=ὁ Ἰακώβ τοῦ Ἰακώβ=, =ἡ Ἐσθήρ=, =ἡ Ἰοδήθ=, =ἡ Μαριάμ=, and the rest. =3.14=
Participles ending in =ων= and having the accent on the ultima are
distinguished thus: those whose genitives have a naturally long penult, their
nominatives are circumflexed: =τοῦ τυποῦντος ὁ τυπῶν=,
=τοῦ σπεροῦντος ὁ σπερῶν=, =τοῦ κτενοῦντος ὁ κτενῶν=; those in whose genitive
penult =ο= is found with =ντ= are oxytone: =τοῦ λαβόντος ὁ λαβών=,
=τοῦ εὑρόντος ὁ εὑρών=. =3.15= The participial dative plurals, all that have a
variable vowel in the penult, are properispomenon: =τοῖς βᾶσι=, =τοῖς στᾶσι=,
=τοῖς δεικνῦσι=, =τοῖς ζευγνῦσι=, since the dative plural wants to keep the
same quantity in the penult as the other oblique cases, and sometimes it even
wants to have a greater quantity, so that =ἀνδριᾶσι= and =ἱμᾶσι= are also
properispomenon.

4.τ On those that have the accent on the penult. 1 =4.16= Pure masculines in
=ας= having the accent on the penult all have the =ας= long and are
paroxytone, e.g. =Αἴας=, =Αἰνείας=, =Ποίας=, =κοίας=, and the like. =4.17=
Another rule: nouns in =ας= declined through =τος= =4.17= have the =ας= long,
except the neuters: =κρέας=, =κέρας=, and with these =γέρας= and =οὖδας=.
=4.18= Participles in =ας= have a long ultima and are paroxytone: =ὁ ποιήσας=,
=ὁ νείμας=; and their vocatives as well: =ὦ ποιήσας=, =ὦ νείμας=; for the rule
says that participles and participial nouns have the same nominatives and
vocatives. The neuters of these are properispomenon: =Τὸ ποιῆσαν=,
=τὸ λαλῆσαν=. =4.19= Those in =ος= having =α= in the penult and the accent on
it are paroxytone: =κάρος=, =σκάρος=, =βάδος=; =πρᾶος= alone is
properispomenon. =4.20= Those in =ιξ= have a short ultima and are
properispomenon: =Φοῖνιξ=, =ἧλιξ=, =ὁμῆλιξ=; along with these also =κῆρυξ=
with simple =υ=. =4.21= Disyllabic nouns in =ιλος= are paroxytone, e.g.
=φίλος=, =κτίλος=, =σπίλος=; likewise also those of more than two syllables:
=ποικίλος=, =ὀργίλος=; in some, =σπῖλος= and =πῖλος= have been found
properispomenon. =4.22= All those in =ινος= are properispomenon:
=Κωνσταντῖνος=, =Πλωτῖνος=, =Ἰουστῖνος=; in some, =πίνος= the dirt has been
found paroxytone. Note that common usage makes =καρκίνος= and =ἐχίνος=
paroxytone. =4.23= All those in =ιτης= have the =ι= long: =πολίτης=,
=ὁπλίτης=, hence their nominative plurals are properispomenon: =οἱ πολῖται=,
=οἱ ὁπλῖται=, =οἱ Θερσῖται=; and =ἡ χρυσῖτις= earth, feminine. =4.24= Those in
=ιος= are paroxytone: =ἐναντίος=, =νυμφίος=, except =δῖος= and =Χῖος= a man.
=4.25= Those in =ος= not having pure =ι= in the penult and having the accent
on it are paroxytone: =τρίτος=, =κρίκος=, and the rest, except =σῖτος=,
=ἶσος=, =μῖμος=, =γρῖφος=; common usage makes =γρίφος= paroxytone. =4.26=
Those of two endings in =ιν= and in =ις= have the =ι= long and are
properispomenon in the genitive: =δελφῖνος=, =ἀκτῖνος=; but the monosyllables
of these are oxytone in the genitive and dative, and in the other cases, where
the penult receives the accent and

the ultima is short, they are properispomenon: =ῥίν ῥινός=,
=ῥῖνα ῥῖνε ῥῖνες ῥῖνας=; so also in the like. For this reason =εὔριν= is not
properispomenon either, since the =ι= is long. =4.27= Those in =υξ= are
properispomenon if they have a long penult, e.g. =δοῖδυξ=. =4.28= Those in
=ος= not purely having =υ= in the penult and having the accent on it are
paroxytone: =κύβος=, =λύγος=, =στύλος=, =Κύρος= a proper name, except =κῦρος=
authority, neuter, =μῦθος=, =θῦμος=, =τῦφος=; but common usage gives the acute
to =τύφος=. Those in =υνος= are properispomenon: =φρῦνος=, =θῦνος=.

5.τ On feminines. =5.29= Feminines in =αια=, if they come from a noun,
lengthen the =α= and are paroxytone, e.g. =δίκη δικαία=, =σελήνη σεληναία=,
=εὐκταία= from =εὐχή=, =ἀπευκταία=, =περικεφαλαία= from =κεφαλή=, =δειλαία=
from =δειλός=, =κεραία= from =κέρας=; along with these also =ἐλαία= and
=νεολαία= the multitude; but if they are not derived from nouns, they have a
short ultima and are properispomenon, e.g. =μαῖα=, =γαῖα=, =γραῖα=, =αἶα=, and
=Μαῖα= the proper name. =5.30= Simply, as many as come from a verb have the
=α= short and are properispomenon if the penult is long, e.g. =Μοῦσα= from
=μῶ= "I seek", =βῆσσα= from =βῶ=, and =νῆσσα= from =νῶ=, and =ἧσσα= from
=ἡσσῶ=; along with these also the participial forms, as themselves from a
verb: =σχοῦσα=, =τετυφυῖα=, =πεποιηκυῖα=; with them =στᾶσα=, =βᾶσα=, and the
like are properispomenon. =πᾶσα= also followed these. =5.31= Another rule
says: feminines ending in =α=, if they have a variable vowel in the penult,
lengthen it; therefore also =πᾶσα=, =στᾶσα=, =τλᾶσα=, =γνοῦσα=, =δεικνυοῦσα=
are properispomenon; but =πρύμνα=, =πυράγρα= have an acute, and likewise those
ending in a double consonant: =ῥίζα=, =σχίζα=, =μύξα=. =5.32= Feminines in
=ρα= having the accent on the penult, if they have a diphthong in the penult,
are properispomenon, e.g. =σφαῖρα=, =μοῖρα=, =στεῖρα=; but if they have a
single vowel in the penult, they are paroxytone: =Ἥρα=, =χήρα=, =χώρα=, =ὥρα=;
and those with a variable vowel in the penult: =λύρα=, =θύρα=, =σφύρα=. =5.33=
Feminines in =εια= have the =α= long, even if they come from verbs in =ευω=,
e.g. =πρεσβεία= and =δουλεία=, except if they come from masculines in =υς=
declined through =εος=; such have the =α= short and are reasonably
circumflexed. =5.34= Feminines in =ας= are oxytone and are declined through
=δος=: =μονάς=, =τριάς=, =λαμπάς=; =τῆς μονάδος=, =τῆς λαμπάδος=,
=τῆς τριάδος=. =5.35= Feminines in =ις= with a long penult are
properispomenon: =ῥῆσις=, =λῆξις= and =θραῦσις=; but if the penult is a
variable vowel, they have the variable short and are paroxytone: =τάσις=,
=φράσις=, =κλίσις=, and all the rest, except =ῥῦσις=, =λῦσις=, =πρᾶξις=,
=χᾶτις= and =πρᾶσις=; this last common usage makes paroxytone. 5.32βις still
paroxytone also are =οὐρήθρα=, =δακτυλήθρα=. Those with the =αυ= diphthong in
the penult are paroxytone: =σαύρα=, =λαύρα=, =αὔρα= the slave-woman; but
=αὖρα= the finest breeze is circumflexed; along with these also =Φαίδρα= a
proper name. 5.33βις Feminines in =ια= are paroxytone, e.g. =κοιλία=, and
those with a diphthong in the penult, e.g. =θεία=, =χρεία=, and those from
verbs in =ευω=, e.g. =πρεσβεύω πρεσβεία=, and the like. Feminines in =α=
formed beside masculines keep the same quantity as their genitive; and if the
genitive ends long, the feminine also has the =α= long and is paroxytone; but
if the masculine genitive ends short, the feminine itself also has the =α=
short and is properispomenon, e.g. =ὀξύς ὀξέος ὀξεῖα=,
=γλυκύς γλυκέος γλυκεῖα=; for the masculine genitive ends short and therefore
it too is short and properispomenon; but =θείος θείου θεία= and

=ἀστεῖος ἀστείου ἀστεία= are paroxytone, because the masculine genitive ends
in =ου=. Note that =γενναῖα= and =ἡ σπουδαῖα= and =τὰ γενναῖα= are
properispomenon. 5.35βις Feminines in =ις= having the accent one syllable
before and with a long penult are properispomenon: =μῆνις=, =ῥῆσις=, =καῦσις=,
and the accusative =τὴν μῆνιν=, =τὴν ῥῆσιν=, =τὴν καῦσιν=; for the rule says:
every accusative ending in =ν= wants to have the same accent and the same
quantity as its own nominative, and the vocatives: =ὦ μῆνις=, =ὦ ῥῆσις=; and
the rule: those ending in =ις= have the same nominative and vocative. Those in
=ις= with a variable vowel in the penult are paroxytone: =τάσις=, =βάσις=,
except =ῥῦσις=, =λῦσις=, =πρᾶξις= and =πρᾶσις=; this last common usage makes
paroxytone. I also found =μῦσις= circumflexed in some. =5.36= =Ἡ Χίος=,
=ἡ Κίος=, =ἡ Τίος= are paroxytone. But =Χῖος= a man is properispomenon. =5.37=
Feminines in =ινη= lengthen the =ι=: =κλίνη=, =ὑσμίνη=, =ἡρωίνη=; and their
nominative plurals are properispomenon: =ὑσμῖναι=, =κλῖναι=, =ἡρωῖναι=, and
the like. =δωτίνη=; the proper names =Στρατονίκη=, =Βερονίκη=; the feminine
nominatives of these, then, as short-ending, are circumflexed: =αἱ ὑσμῖναι=,
=αἱ κλῖναι=, =αἱ ἡρωῖναι=. The genitives in =ινος=, namely =ἀκτῖνος=,
=Σαλαμῖνος=, were written above among the masculines, that is among
=δελφῖνος=, =ἑρμῖνος=; and these are circumflexed. =5.38= Oxytone words in
=ις= declined through =δος=, being disyllabic and having =η= in the penult,
have a long ultima and are properispomenon in the genitive, e.g.
=κηλίς κηλῖδος=, =ψηφίς ψηφῖδος=; but as many disyllables as do not have =η=
in the penult in the genitive are paroxytone: =ῥανίς ῥανίδος=,
=βολίς βολίδος=; and of those of more than two syllables, as many as are
shortened from the dative: =Αἰθίοπι Αἰθιοπίς=, and =Πέλοπι Πελοπίς=. The
grammarians among us for the most part lengthen only those that have a
naturally long penult and make their genitives properispomenon: =αἰγῖδος=,
=σφραγῖδος=, =καταιγῖδος=; we said "naturally long" on account of =ἀτμίδος=,
=βαλβίδος=. =5.39= Disyllables in =υνη= have the =υ= long, e.g. =φρύνη=,
=αἱ φρῦναι=; hence it is properispomenon, and the like; but of those of more
than two syllables, as many as have =σ= before the =υ= have it long: therefore
=βριθοσῦναι= and =γηθοσῦναι=, and the like, are properispomenon; but if they
do not have the =σ= before the =υ=, they have it shortened: =αἱ κορύναι=,
=αἱ τορύναι=, =αἱ αἰσχύναι=, and the like are paroxytone. =5.40= Of oxytone
words in =υς=, those declined in =δος= have the =υ= short also in the
genitive; for this reason the =μυ= of =χλαμύδος= is short and it is
paroxytone. =ἰσχύος=, =Ἐριννύος=, =πληθύος= are paroxytone, and the rule:
those in =υς= having the accent on the ultima and declined with pure =ος=
always shorten the variable vowel in the genitive, and in the other cases,
both in monosyllables and in those of more than one syllable: =σῦς συός=,
=οἱ σύες=; =μῦς μυός=, =οἱ μύες=; =αἱ δρύες=, =αἱ ὀσφύες=, and the like.

6.τ On neuters. 1 =6.41= Disyllabic neuters in =μα= having simple =υ= in the
penult shorten it: =χύμα=, =πλύμα=, except =κῦμα=, =θῦμα=, =φῦμα=. =6.42=
=Γῆρας=, =οὖδας=, =οὖας=, and the like are properispomenon, and the rule:
neuters in =ας= shorten the =α=. =6.43= Neuters in =αρ= are properispomenon:
=ἧπαρ=, =οὖθαρ=, =εἶλαρ=, =πῖαρ=; along with these also the adverb =εἶθαρ=,
i.e. immediately. =6.44= Disyllabic neuters in =ος= having =α= in the penult
shorten it and are paroxytone: =χάος=, =φάος=, =νάπος=, =κράτος=, =λάχος=, and
the like, except =πρᾶγος=; for this has the =α= long, as being from =πράσσω=.
=6.45= All those in =ιον= are paroxytone: =παιδίον=, =θριγγίον=, =κηπίον=, and
the like. =6.46= Those in =ον= not having pure =ι= in the penult

are properispomenon: =κρῖνον=, =γρῖφον=, =ἶσον=. =6.47= Those in =ος= having
=ι= in the penult are paroxytone: =ξίφος=, =στίφος=, except =δῖνος=, =σῖνος=,
=πνῖγος=, =νῖκος=, =κλῖτος=, =μῖσος=, =λῖπος=, and =μῦσος= written with simple
=υ=; but common usage makes =ῥίγος= paroxytone, even though the ancients make
it properispomenon. =6.48= Neuters ending in =υ= shorten it, so that =πῶυ=,
=μῶλυ=, and the like are properispomenon. =6.49= Neuters in =υλον= are
properispomenon: =σκῦλον=, =φῦλον= the race, =σῦλον= the theft, except =ξύλον=
and =φύλλον=. =6.50= Those in =ος= having =υ= in the penult are
properispomenon: =μῦσος=, =κῦτος=, =σκῦτος=, =φῦκος=; but =ψύχος= and =τρύχος=
are paroxytone. =6.51= Neuters in =αον= coming from the =αι= diphthong have
the =α= long and are properispomenon: =τὸ κλαῖον τὸ κλᾶον=,
=τὸ καῖον τὸ κᾶον=.

7.τ On verbs. 1 =7.52= Of verbs in =ανω=, those of more than two syllables are
shortened: =μανθάνω=, =τυγχάνω=, so that the neuters also, =τὸ μανθάνον=,
=τὸ τυγχάνον=, and the like are paroxytone; but =ἱκᾶνον= and =κιχᾶνον=
irregularly have the =α= long and are properispomenon; =τὸ φθάνον= and the
remaining disyllables are paroxytone. For the rule says: if however the future
is circumflexed, the aorist also lengthens its penult; accordingly their
infinitives are properispomenon: =φᾶναι=, =λιπᾶναι=, and the like; along with
these also =δρᾶναι ἀποδρᾶναι=; but =φάναι= i.e. to say is paroxytone, and
=ἱστάναι=; for the =στα= is short, following =τιθέναι=, =διδόναι=; and indeed
also =ζευγνύναι=, =δεικνύναι=. =7.53= =Γελάω γελάσω=, the variable vowels are
short. Why? Whenever the present has =α=, then the future too is formed with
short =α=: =γελάω γελάσω=, =πελάω πελάσω=; from the future, therefore, the
aorist of such verbs is also shortened in the penult, and is paroxytone in the
infinitives: =ἐγέλασα γελάσαι=, =ἔθλασα θλάσαι=; for the rule says: in general
the aorist has a long penult, unless it has the short from the future. And you
may recognize the future with a short penult from the passive perfect having
=σ= before the last syllable: =ἡτοίμασμαι=, =οὔτασμαι=, =πέφρασμαι=,
=γεγέλασμαι=; for the rule says: when the penult of the future is short, the
passive perfect takes on the =σ=. =7.54= =Κρίνω κρινῶ=, the aorist =ἔκρινα=
and the infinitive =κρῖναι=; so also =κλῖναι=, =σῖναι=; for the rule says: if
however the future is circumflexed, the aorist also lengthens the penult.
