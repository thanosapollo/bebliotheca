* Caput 1
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of the two children whom Septimius Severus left, Geta and Bassianus, of whom
one Antoninus

the army named and the father the other, Geta was judged a public enemy,

but Bassianus obtained the empire, concerning whose ancestors we think it vain
to go over the matter again, since all of it in the Life of Severus

has been said enough. his boyhood, then, was winning and clever, affable to
his parents, pleasant to his parents' friends, acceptable to the people,
pleasing to the senate, and even for winning

affection it was of service to him. he was not slow in letters, not sluggish
in kindnesses, not tight-fisted in largess, not slow in mercy, at least as he
seemed under his parents.

finally, if ever he saw condemned men thrown to the beasts, he wept or turned
his eyes away, which was more than lovable to the people.

as a boy of seven, when he had heard that a playmate of his had been beaten
rather harshly for the Jewish religion, he long would not look at either his
own father or the boy's father as the authors

of the beating. by his own intervention he restored their ancient rights to
the people of Antioch and Byzantium, with whom Severus had been angry because
they had helped Niger. a hatred of Plautianus

he conceived because of cruelty. what he had received from his parents at the
festival of the Sigillaria he of his own accord gave either to clients or to
teachers.

:CUSTOM_ID: h2

But this was as a boy. once he had left boyhood, whether by his father's
warnings or by cunning of nature or because he thought he must be made equal
to Alexander the Great of Macedon, he became more reserved, more severe, and
even more savage in his face, so much so that many who had known him as a boy
did not believe he was the same.

he always had Alexander the Great and his deeds on his lips. Tiberius and
Sulla in assembly he often

praised. he was haughtier than his father; his brother, for his great modesty,
he despised.

After his father's death, going to the Praetorian Camp, he complained before
the soldiers that he was being trapped by his brother's plots,

and so he had his brother killed in the Palace. his body

he ordered to be burned at once. he said besides in the Camp that his brother
had prepared poison for him, and that he had been irreverent to their mother;
and he publicly thanked those who had killed him.

finally he added to these, as if

more loyal toward him, a donative.

part of the soldiers at Alba took Geta's killing very badly, all of them
saying that they had pledged

faith to both the children of Severus, and ought to keep it for both.

and with the gates shut the emperor was long not admitted until their minds
were soothed, not only by complaints about Geta and charges brought out but by
the hugeness of a donative, the soldiers being, as usual, placated; and from
there he returned to Rome.

then, wearing a cuirass under his senatorial robe, he entered the Senate-house
with armed soldiers. he placed these in a double line in the middle among the
benches and so made his speech.

he complained of his brother's plots in a tangled and clumsy way toward

accusing him, and toward excusing himself.

which indeed the senate did not gladly receive, when he had said that he had
granted his brother everything, had freed his brother from plots, and that the
other had nevertheless made the gravest plots against him and had not

returned the like for brotherly love. after this he restored to those who had
been relegated and deported the right of return to their fatherland.

From there he went on to the praetorians and stayed in the Camp.

the next day he made for the Capitol, and those whom he was preparing to kill
he addressed in a friendly way

and leaning on Papinian and

on Cilo he returned to the Palace. when he had seen Geta's mother weeping and
other women after his brother's murder, he tried to kill the women, but was
held back from this, lest there be increased

the cruelty of his brother's killing. he forced Laetus to death by sending him
poison himself; for he had been first among those who advised Geta's death,
and he was also the first killed.

he himself very often wept over that death. many who had been privy to the
killing he put to death, and likewise

the man who honored Geta's image.

After this he ordered that his cousin Afer, to whom the day before he had sent
portions

from dinner, be killed. when the man had thrown himself down for fear of the
assassins and had crawled to his wife with a broken leg, still in mockery he
was

caught by the assassins

and killed. he also killed Pompeianus, grandson of Marcus, born of his
daughter and of that Pompeianus to whom Lucilla had been married after the
death of the emperor Verus,

whom Marcus had also made consul twice and set over all the wars which were
then the gravest, and in such a way indeed

:PROPERTIES:

that he seemed to have been killed by robbers. then in his sight Papinian was
struck with an axe by the soldiers and killed. when this was done he said to
the slayer, /You

ought to have carried out my order with a sword./ Patruinus also was killed by
his order

before the temple of the Deified Pius, and their corpses were dragged through
the street without any reverence of humanity. he also killed Papinian's son,
who three days before, as quaestor, had given a rich show.

in those same days countless men were killed who had favored his brother's
party. the freedmen also were killed who had managed Geta's affairs.

then there was slaughter in all places, and slaughter was done in the baths,
and some were killed even while dining, among them also Sammonicus Serenus,
whose books

remain in great number for learning. Cilo, twice prefect and consul, also came
into the utmost danger for this,

that he had urged harmony between the brothers. and when this same Cilo, his
senatorial robe stripped off, had been seized barefoot by the city troops,
Antoninus put down the riot.

he afterward made many further killings in the city, some men being seized
here and there by the soldiers and killed, as if

he were punishing a sedition. Helvius Pertinax, a suffect consul, for this
alone, that he was an emperor's son,

he killed, and he never ceased, under various occasions, to put to death those
who had been his brother's friends.

often against the senate, often against the people, he inveighed haughtily,
either by edicts posted or by speeches published, even showing that he would
be a Sulla.

* Caput 5
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5
:END:

When these things had been done he made for Gaul, and as soon as he

came into it he killed the proconsul of Narbonensis. then, with all who were
managing affairs in Gaul thrown into confusion, he earned a tyrant's hatred,
although he

would at times pretend even

to be kindly, when by nature he was savage. and when he had done many things
against men and against the rights of cities, he was seized with illness and
suffered heavily, and toward those who tended him he was most cruel.

Then, preparing a setting-out for the East, he dropped the journey and settled
in Dacia. about Raetia he killed not a few barbarians

and his soldiers, as if they were Sulla's soldiers, he both

exhorted and gave gifts. he forbade himself to be called by the names of the
gods, which Commodus had done, although many

called him Hercules, because he had killed a lion and other wild beasts,

and when he had subdued the Germans he called himself Germanus, either in jest
or in earnest, as he was foolish and mad, asserting that if he had conquered
the Lucanians he ought to be

called Lucanicus. at that time those were condemned who had made water in a
place where there were statues or images of the prince, and those who had
taken crowns from his images in order to put others in their place, and those
also were condemned who wore remedies for quartan and tertian fevers, tied to
the neck,

and carried them.

He made a journey through Thrace with the prefect of the guard. from there,
when he was crossing into Asia, he met the danger of shipwreck, the yard-arm
being broken, so that he went down into a skiff with his bodyguards.

from which, taken up into a trireme by the prefect of the fleet, he escaped.

he often took boars, and he even stood against a lion, when

he boasted in letters sent to friends and vaunted that he had come near the
prowess of Hercules.

* Caput 6
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h6
:END:

After this, turning to the Armenian and Parthian war, he made a war-leader who
matched his own character. from there he made for Alexandria, called the
people into the gymnasium and rebuked them; he also ordered that the

strong be chosen for military service. but those whom he had chosen he killed,
after the example of Ptolemy Euergetes, who was called the eighth of that
name. a signal besides being given to the soldiers to kill their hosts, he
made a great slaughter at Alexandria.

Then, having entered through the Cadusii and the Babylonians, he joined battle
in a disorderly way with the satraps of the Parthians,

wild beasts even being sent against the enemy. letters having been given to
the senate as if after a victory, he was called Parthicus; for he had obtained
the name Germanicus while his father was alive.

then, when he wished to bring war on the Parthians again and was wintering at
Edessa and had come from there to Carrhae for the sake of the god Lunus, on
his birthday, the eighth day before the Ides of April, on the very Megalensia,
when he had gone aside for the needs of nature, by a plot laid by Macrinus,
prefect of the guard, who

after him seized the empire, he was killed. privy to the killing were
Nemesianus and his brother Apollinaris and Triccianus,

who was serving as prefect of the Second Parthian Legion and who was over the
irregular cavalry, Marcius Agrippa, who was over the fleet, not being ignorant
of it, and besides most of the staff, at the instigation of Martialis.

* Caput 7
:PROPERTIES:

He was killed, however, in the middle of the road between Carrhae and Edessa,
when he had gotten down from his horse to relieve his bladder and was standing
among his bodyguards, the conspirators

of the killing. finally, when his groom was lifting him onto his horse, he
stabbed his side with a dagger, and it was cried out by all that Martialis had
done it.

And since we have made mention of the god Lunus, it should be known that this
has been handed down to memory by all the most learned, and so

is held even now especially by the people of Carrhae, that whoever has thought
the Moon should be named by a woman's name and sex

is bound to women and always their servant; but whoever has believed the god
to be male, he is master of his wife

and suffers no woman's plots. hence, although the Greeks or the Egyptians, in
that way in which they call a woman also a human being, call the Moon a god,
yet in the mysteries they say Lunus.

* Caput 8
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h8
:END:

I know that many have so put Papinian's death into writing that they have not
admitted a cause of the killing, some relating one thing and others another;
but I have preferred to set out the variety of opinions

rather than keep silent about the killing of so great a man. it is handed down
to memory that Papinian was most friendly to the emperor Severus, and that he
studied under Scaevola with Severus and succeeded Severus in the advocacy of
the fiscus,

as some say, related also through

the second wife; and that to this man especially both sons were commended by
Severus, and that for this reason he favored the harmony of the Antonine
brothers;

that he even pleaded that Geta not be killed, when Bassianus was already
complaining of his plots; and that therefore, together with those who had been
Geta's supporters, he was killed by the soldiers, not only with Antoninus
permitting it but even urging it.

many say that Bassianus, after his brother was killed, charged him to wash
away the crime for him both in the senate and before the people,

but that he answered that it is not so easy for parricide

to be excused as to be done. there is also this tale, that he would not
dictate a speech in which the brother was to be attacked so that the case of
him who had killed might become better; and that he, refusing, answered that
that

was parricide

and another thing to accuse an innocent man killed. but this does not agree at
all; for a prefect could not dictate a speech, and it is agreed that he was
killed as a supporter

of Geta. and indeed Papinian is said, when, seized by the soldiers, he was
being dragged to the Palace to be killed, to have foretold, saying that he

would be most foolish who should be put in his place, unless he avenged the
prefecture so cruelly

attacked. which came to pass; for

Macrinus killed Antoninus,

as we have set out above. he, made emperor in the camp with his son, called
his son, who was named Diadumenus, Antoninus, for this reason, that Antoninus
was much desired by the praetorians.

* Caput 9
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h9
:END:

Bassianus lived forty-three years, he ruled

six years. he was borne out with a public funeral. he left a son, who
afterward was himself also called Marcus Antoninus Heliogabalus; for so had
the name of the Antonines taken root that it could not be torn from men's
minds, because it had occupied the breasts of all like the name of Augustus.

He was of evil manners and more cruel than his hard father. greedy of food,
eager even for wine, hateful to his own and, except the praetorian soldiers,
hated in every camp, in short nothing alike between the brothers.

At Rome he left baths of his own name, outstanding, whose solar hall the
architects say cannot be made by any

imitation of the way it was made. for gratings of bronze or copper are said to
have been placed underneath, to which the whole vaulting was entrusted, and
there is so much space that the learned in mechanics deny that that very thing
could have been done.

he also left a portico in his father's name, which would contain that man's
deeds

and triumphs and wars. he himself received the name Caracallus from a garment
which he had given to the people,

reaching down to the ankles, which had not been so before. whence even today
cloaks of this kind are called Antoninian caracallae,

used especially in the custom of the Roman plebs. the same man paved a new
street, which is below his baths, the Antoninian of course, than which among
Roman

streets you would not easily find anything more beautiful. he brought the
rites of Isis to Rome and made magnificent temples everywhere to that goddess,
and he celebrated the rites also with greater reverence

than they used to be celebrated before. in which indeed it seems wonderful to
me

how the rites of Isis are said to have first come to Rome through this man,
when Antoninus Commodus so celebrated them that he even carried Anubis and
made the ritual pauses; unless perhaps this man added to the celebrity, and
did not first bring it in.

His body was brought into the tomb of the Antonines, that that seat might
receive his remains which had added the name.

It is of interest to know how he is said to have taken his stepmother

Julia as wife. she, being most beautiful and having, as if by carelessness,
bared a very great part of her body, and Antoninus having said,
/I should like to, if it were allowed,/
is related to have answered,
/If it pleases you, it is allowed. or do you not know

that you are emperor and give the laws, not receive them?/
when this was heard, a wild passion was strengthened toward the carrying out
of the crime, and he celebrated those nuptials which, if he knew that he truly
gave the laws,

he alone ought to have forbidden. for he took his mother as wife (she was not
to be called by another name) and joined incest to parricide, if indeed he
joined in marriage the woman whose son he had lately killed.

It is not off the point to add also a certain mocking thing said against him,

for when he was writing the name Germanicus

and Parthicus and Arabicus and Alamannicus (for the nation of the Alamanni

he had conquered) Helvius Pertinax, son of Pertinax, is said to have said in
jest,
/Add, if you please, also Geticus Maximus,/
because he had killed Geta his brother, and the Goths were called Getae, whom
he, while he crossed to the East, had conquered in disorderly battles.

* Caput 11
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h11
:END:

Many prodigies of Geta's being killed existed, as

we shall set out in his life. for although that man departed from life first,
we have nevertheless followed order, so that he who was both born first and
who had begun to rule first should be written first.

At that time, indeed, when he was named Augustus by the army while his father
was alive, because that man, sick in the feet, seemed unable to govern the
empire, the minds of the soldiers and tribunes having been crushed, Severus is
said to have turned over in his mind that he should kill this one also, had
not the

prefects

resisted,

grave

men. some on the contrary say the prefects wished it done, but Septimius did
not, lest his severity also be stained with the name of cruelty, and lest,
since the soldiers had been the authors of the crime, the young man pay the
penalties of a foolish rashness under the title of so heavy a punishment, that
he should seem to have been killed by his father.

Yet this man, the harshest of all and, that we may embrace it in one word, a
parricide and incestuous, enemy of father, mother, and brother, was by
Macrinus, who had killed him, through fear of the soldiers and especially of
the praetorians, enrolled among the gods.

he has a temple, he has Salii, he has an Antoninian brotherhood, he who
snatched from Faustina her temple and divine name,

at least the temple which her husband had founded for her at the roots of the
Taurus, in which afterward this man's son Heliogabalus Antoninus made a temple
for himself or for the Syrian Jupiter or for the Sun (that is uncertain).
