Chapter 27. On the [text]: That they may know you, the only true God, and
Jesus Christ whom you have sent? Another. If the Father has made all things
through his own Word, and the blessed evangelist speaks truly saying: All
things came to be through him, and without him nothing came to be; and again
if all things spoken through the prophets have been spoken through the Word,
and not through anyone else, how is it not absurd to think that the Word of
God himself uttered such voices against himself? Therefore God the Father says
such things through the Son for the overthrow of the falsely named gods?
Another toward the overthrow of the doctrine of the Arians, overturning the
force of the made thing. When the Father says that he himself is one and only
true God, he does not overturn that the Son has been begotten from 39 him, and
is true God, just as he himself also is, but rather he casts out of the
godhead that one whom you would invent for yourselves as God or son from
outside his essence; for this one truly is not true, but a discovery of the
ignorance that is present in you.
Another. If the Father is called true and only God, the Son will not
reasonably be banished from likeness toward him, nor will he be cast out from
being true God. For if he does not lie when he says: I am the truth, and he
is, in thought, that which he says himself to be, how will he not of necessity
be ranked together with the true Father? he himself also being truth;
therefore it has been said for the overthrow of those who are not, nor are
called, gods by nature nor according to truth: that they may know you, the
only true God?
Another. If the Father alone is true God, but the Word from him does not have
such a nature as the one who begot him, but is of generated essence, even if
he receives from us that he is set above all the other made things, how, being
of another essence, and not true, as you say, does he join himself to the God
who is according to truth and wish to be known together with him, saying: That
they may know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent? For
what reason has the creature, according to you, to be known together with the
uncreated, and the thing that has been made to be numbered together with that
which has not been made nor come to be? For if you should grant that this
holds and can be so, 40 what will prevent even some other of the rational
creatures from using such a voice? But if no such thing is brought forward of
any other, and the saying is the Son's alone, it is clear that knowing himself
not as one of all things, but as the very own of the Father's essence, he both
ranked himself together with the one who begot him, and being understood
together with him, he has also given us to know that he would be a true
offspring of a true Father; for that the Son is true, hear John saying: And we
are in his true Son, Jesus Christ. Chapter 32. Selections of sayings with
syllogisms from the New Testament, that the Son is God by nature, and if this,
not a made thing, nor a creature.
Another. That from his fullness we all have received: if the all-perfect is
reserved to the divine nature alone, inasmuch as it also needs nothing from
another, but rather has and is able to fill whatever things of it would be
capable of receiving; and this is in Christ, how would he be alien to the
divine essence, who works the things of that [essence], and is this very thing
which we would define that [essence] to be, at least according to the account
of perfection? How then would one accept that the nature of the Son, being
thus, is generated? For he will hear together with us: For what do you have
that you did not receive! which is most impious.


41 Scholium. Well did the great Cyril say, that the things owed to God are to
be rendered to God; for it is clear that to rule and to reign and to govern
one's own mind, as the great Athanasius said. And indeed also to lead through
him the flesh and the fleshly motions both divinely and without sin, according
to Gregory the wonder-worker, but also, he says, to the flesh the things that
befit it.
And it befits the flesh of the Word, as the great Eustathius interpreted, who
became bishop of Corycus, to be led even in all things in which it is
naturally moved by the spirit and the will and the power and authority of its
own hypostasis, and this is to say of the Word, and to work nothing from its
own impulse and by itself, because it has not even come to subsist by itself
at all, altogether.
