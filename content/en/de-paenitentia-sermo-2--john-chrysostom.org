On Cheesefare Sunday, concerning repentance and almsgiving. Sermon 2.
=60.699=
αʹ. The trumpets, sounding, prepare the soldiers of the earthly king for
battle; but the reading of the divine Scriptures stirs the soldiers of the
heavenly King, Jesus Christ, toward doxology and the fear of God. Yet those it
prepares for array against perceptible enemies and foes; us, against invisible
enemies and demons. As Paul says, that Our wrestling is not against blood and
flesh, but against the principalities, against the powers, against the
world-rulers of the darkness of this age, against the spiritual things of
wickedness in the heavenly places. See, brethren, what enemies we have; see
with what foes we fight. For they are wicked, as you see, and shameless, and
they force us toward every sin.

And if they fail at this, they come upon another; and if they are at a loss
from that, they pass over to another; and, to speak simply, going from one
thing to another they do not stand off, until their will is done. And one they
move toward fornication, another toward adultery; and another to seize what
belongs to others, and another to steal the neighbor's goods; and one toward
greed and insatiability, another toward the evil eye and envy; another toward
murder and reviling, and another toward slander and brother-killing; another
toward gluttony and the corruption of children, and another toward sacrilege
and shamelessness; and one toward man-pleasing and love of glory, another
toward lack of compassion and inhumanity; another toward denial and
oath-taking, and another toward insolence and dishonor; and one toward anger
and wrath, and another toward usury and toward jealousy and strife, and
another toward blasphemy and uncleanness; and some they persuade not to pity
the poor, others to move the neighbor's landmarks; others they make to deprive
hired men of their wages, than which what could be more pitiable? others they
make to strip orphans, others to oppress widows, others not even to enter the
church, others they hurl down into countless passions, of which there is no
number; which even in relating them the time of my life would fail me.

See, brethren, into how many evils we fall every day, and we do not take heed.
But, woe is me, what am I to become as I relate these things? Believe me,
brethren, that in the very act of writing these things alone I am frightened
and trembling, and a shudder has fallen upon me and I am beside myself, and it
comes upon me to weep, when I consider in how many wicked and demonic passions
we are baited. Why then do we neglect, why do we wait? what season do we look
for? why do we despise our own salvation? why do we not take thought for the
things of our departure? why do we neglect, why do we hang back? how long
listlessly, how long luxury, how long drunkenness, how long pleasures, how
long self-indulgence, how long insatiability, how long seizures, how long
injustice? how long the running-down of the poor? how long the plunderings of
the needy? how long wealth, how long money, how long gold? how long love of
money, the noose of souls? how long envy, how long falsehood, how long
slander, how long reviling, how long shameful and unbridled desires? Why are
we led astray? why do we wait? why do we toil out of season? why do we mock
ourselves? why do we cut ourselves down in vain? why do we run after what
belongs to others? why are we troubled? why do we toil? why do we treasure up
things of a season?

Let us repent henceforth, let us wake, let us watch, I beseech you, brethren,
let us shake off the deep sleep of sin. Come, hear, and I will declare to you,
all you who fear God; come, hear spiritual and saving deeds and words; come,
learn a counsel that saves and is well-pleasing to God; come, know how we
shall confess to God our Savior; come before the festival of life is
dissolved; come before the doors of life are shut, before death comes and
finds you unprepared; come before the =60.700= sun sets, before the light
grows dark; Come, hear, all you nations, and give ear, all you who dwell in
the inhabited world; come, Earth-born and sons of men, rich and poor together;
come, the well-born and the glorious, slaves and free; come every race, and
every age, men together and women, small and great, young and old; come,
priests of the Lord, and all the fullness of the Church from the ends to the
ends of the inhabited world; come, Young men and maidens, elders with younger;
come, kings of the earth, both the wise and the unlearned; come, Let us fall
down before the Lord who made us; for he is our God, and we are the people of
his pasture, and the sheep of his hand. Come, brethren, and let us know that
the ends of the earth will profit us nothing. Come, see that we entered naked,
and again we are about to go away there naked. Come, let us confess to the
Lord in tears and groanings, in almsgivings, in compassions for the poor; and
he himself will teach us how we may be saved.

Let us too say to him: Lord, we have sinned against heaven and before you;
receive our turning and our repentance, receive our groaning and our tears,
receive the repentance of us sinners, the mourning and weeping unto death;
receive us the wretched, who have lived shamefully; receive us, O Lover of
mankind, who have many times provoked you; receive us, Master, who have spent
the whole of life in prodigality and every wickedness and uncleanness;
receive, Lord God, those who have disobeyed your commandments; receive us,
Master, your unworthy servants, and do not render to us according to the works
of our hands.

We confess, Master, that we are not even worthy to live because of our sins;
we confess, Lord, that we are not even worthy to look upon this sun, because
there is no sin, no form of every kind of evil, which we wretched ones have
not done; but receive us, Master, as the prodigal son, receive us as the
robber, receive us as the harlot and the publican. Lord, turn us; Lord, give
us understanding, and do not be angry, but be propitious to our sins. You are
our God, and besides you we know no other. Lord, deliver us from our enemies,
and do not enter into judgment with your servants. Lord, you are God, and we
are your people. For we have sinned, we have acted lawlessly, we have done
injustice, and we have done the wicked thing, and we have gone astray in all
things, and we have not heard your commandments, and for this reason we have
wandered far from you. But have mercy, Lord, on the works of your hands; have
mercy, Master, on those who by the deceit of the serpent were cast out of
paradise; have mercy on us, and clothe us with a tunic of gladness, and a
garment of salvation; have mercy on us, whom the devil stripped of your help.
Compassionate One, have mercy on those who have left you, and served that one;
have mercy on those who have gone astray, have mercy on those who have not
kept your charges, and have served the knaveries of demons; have mercy on
those defiled in sins, have mercy on those tyrannized by the devil, have mercy
on those soiled in prodigality; have mercy, merciful One, have mercy, good
One, have mercy, long-suffering One, because every evil and every uncleanness
and greed and injustice our hands have done. For we have defiled the soul,
which you made according to your image; we have defiled the body, we have
defiled sense. Our tongue has become a sharp sword against the neighbor, our
eyes look with fire, our hands are full of blood, our feet are swift unto evil
to do someone harm.

Our mouth has been defiled with revilings; and, to speak simply, we have
defiled the earth and the air; for our injustices have drawn near to heaven,
our seizures have been lifted above the mountains, our greedinesses have been
raised above the clouds, our vices have reached as far as heaven, our =60.701=
sins are unpardonable, our faults have no consolation, our failure is
inconsolable, and behold the earth cannot bear our evils; and for this reason,
Master, we have need of your mercy. You know the weakness of our nature; have
mercy, Lord, on the work of your hands. For we call upon your compassion; do
not leave us lacking of your help; send forth your mercy to us the unworthy;
grant your succor to us sinners, and make your face to shine, and we shall be
saved. Saying and doing these things, my brethren, let us flee every vice and
every uncleanness, let us flee every injustice and greed, let us flee
vainglory, drunkenness and every insatiability, let us flee gluttony the
mother of all evils, let us flee love of money the beginning and root of every
sin, let us flee hatred of the brethren and lack of compassion, let us flee
slander and reviling; let us flee fornication, adultery, and every blasphemy;
let us flee theft, seizure; let us flee love of glory and respect of persons
and strife and jealousy and the remaining deceit of this vain life; lest our
Lord Jesus Christ come, and find us slothful, and cut us in two, and declare
against us saying: Binding their hands and feet, take them up, and cast them
into the outer darkness; there shall be the weeping and the gnashing of teeth.
O how much we are then about to weep, brethren, and mourn and lament and cut
ourselves down, profiting nothing any longer? Woe in that fearful hour, if we
are found thus unrepentant; it were good for us not to have been born, if we
are going to be found thus.

Let us weep bitterly then, O brethren, that we may not be punished there with
immortal punishments; let us have mercy, that we may be shown mercy, that we
may be forgiven; let us ourselves scatter money, lest others after our end,
eating and drinking and living in luxury upon our labor, sing saying: The rich
man treasures up, and does not know for whom he will gather them; and again,
Behold a man who did not set God as his helper. For what profit, brethren,
when we toil, and others enjoy our labor? Let us send ahead there, that we may
find. For what a man sows, this he will also reap. Let no one make the excuse
that I have a wife and children, and I cannot because of them scatter the
money, lest dying I leave them poor.

βʹ. Do not, brethren, do not, I beseech you all, do not be anxious about this.
For there is a God in heaven who is compassionate, who cares for and governs
the wife and the children, who also made them; only let us take thought for
ourselves, let us weep for ourselves, let us make ourselves ready, let us make
ourselves secure beforehand toward the tribunals and judgment-seats there. For
in that hour neither children nor wife, not father, not mother, not brother,
not kinsman, nor even Abraham himself, or Noah, or Daniel, or Peter, or Paul
will be able to redeem us from those incomprehensible torments, unless our
works. From heaven he comes to judge the living and the dead, he comes who
searches hearts and kidneys; he comes, and who then shall escape his hands? he
comes sitting upon a throne of Cherubim, and looking upon the abysses; he
comes the God of gods, and Lord of those who lord it; he comes the Ruler of
rulers, and Judge of judges; he comes the King of those who reign, and
Justicer of justicers; he comes who publishes and convicts every base and
hidden mystery; he comes who makes manifest and reveals the faults of secret
things; he comes who triumphs over the thoughts and the deeds and the words
and every conceiving before all; he comes the true and unsleeping eye, the
impartial judge, the unbribable examiner, who burns up every filthy deed and
shameful pleasure, and sends it on to Gehenna; he comes who loves peace, and
hates every wicked word, and every seizure, and every sin, and punishes those
who work scandals eternally; he comes, from whose face the earth flees, the
sea is dried up; the heaven is rolled up as a book, the stars fall, the air is
shaken, the sun is darkened, the moon will not give her light, the powers of
the heavens will be shaken, the Cherubim tremble, the Seraphim are beside
themselves, angels shudder, archangels tremble, the things above, the things
below are shaken, all things are shaken; =60.702= he comes who does not take a
man's person, and does not pity a poor man in judgment; he comes who renders
to each according to his works, who convicts and posts up the counsels and
movements of every breath; he comes who has mercy on the merciful, and
condemns the uncompassionate, He who is fearful in counsels above the sons of
men; he comes to whom every knee bows of things in heaven and on earth and
under the earth; he comes whose throne is a flame of fire, and his wheels
burning fire; he comes to save and glorify the righteous, but to punish the
sinners; he comes to bless those who thirst, but to call wretched those who
have been filled; he comes to honor those who struggle and keep vigil in
prayers and psalmody, in thanksgiving and petition and endurance; but the
slothful and careless he will send on to torments and punishments; he comes
toward the sinners fearful and hard, and with much threatening he will punish;
but toward the righteous gentle and cheerful and mild; and what does he say to
them? Come, you blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you
from the foundation of the world; not because you acquired much wealth upon
the earth, nor because you have had many menservants and maidservants, nor
because you were well-born and glorious; not because you inherited many fields
and vineyards, not because you built storehouses and splendid houses, not
because you were well supplied with many yokes of oxen and flocks of sheep,
nor because you enjoyed a rich and unstinting table, nor because you put on
splendid garments, nor because you were anointed with myrrhs and spices, nor
because you were much honored by all; not because you were glorified and
lifted up and magnified, nor because you fasted or kept vigil; nor because you
kept virginity, or because you suffered hardship; but Because I hungered, and
you gave me to eat; I thirsted, and you gave me drink; you saw me a stranger,
and you gathered me into your houses, and you greatly consoled me; you have
seen me naked, and you clothed me; you beheld me lying in sickness, and you
visited me, and you deemed me worthy of much tending; you saw me in prison,
and with much eagerness you came to me.

And the righteous say to him: Lord, when did we see you hungering, and feed
you? or thirsting, and give you drink? or a stranger, and gather you in? or
naked, and clothe you? or sick or in prison, and minister to you? And the
Judge says to them: Amen, amen I say to you, inasmuch as you did it to one of
these my brethren the least, the poor, the cast-off, the strangers, the
humble, the despised, the maimed, those in distress, those in necessity, those
in affliction, those in famine, those in thirst, the wounded, the worn down,
the sick, the ill-treated; inasmuch as you did it to one of all these, you did
it to me, you warmed my members, you ministered to me, you gave rest to my
soul; for this reason, Come, you blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom
prepared for you from the foundation of the world. Come first, you who have
kept the faith spotless and undefiled; come, you who have become poor for my
sake with the spiritual poverty; come, you who in deserts and mountains and
caves and the holes of the earth have dwelt with the wild beasts for my name;
come, you who mourn and are afflicted; come, you who have hungered and
thirsted for my sake; come, you who have been persecuted for righteousness'
sake; come, you who have been reproached and hated for my name; come, you who
are pure in heart; come, you merciful; come, you who are ready to share; come,
you compassionate; come, you who nourish orphans; come, you who receive
strangers; come, you who are patrons of widows; come, you who succor the
powerless; come, you who console the sick; come, you who visit those in
prison; come, you who pay the debts of those who owe; come, you who have
filled the souls of those hungering and thirsting for my name; come, you who
are the hottest patrons of those unjustly enslaved and in danger; come, you
compassionate liberators of those in exile and bitter slaveries; come, you who
have lived the quiet and still life for my sake; come, you who have struggled
until death in virginity and ascesis and a reverent way of life, come, you who
in temperance and purity have kept the body and the soul spotless and
undefiled; come, you who in fasts and vigils and psalmody have completed the
whole of your life; come, you who every night and =60.703= day have not stood
away from the church; come, you who adorn and care for my altars; come, you
who have despised the earthly things, receive the heavenly; come, you who have
left for my sake fathers and mothers, sons and daughters, houses and fields
and kinsmen, receive the good things of the heavenly Jerusalem; come and
behold my glory, and rejoice and exult, because your reward is great in the
heavens; come, and see of what sort of Master you happen to be servants, and
rejoice and leap, because yours is the kingdom of the heavens.

Enter into the joy of your Lord, receive those good things which eye has not
seen and ear has not heard, and which have not gone up upon a man's heart;
enter into the treasuries of my Father, where angels long to stoop and look;
receive the prizes and the crowns and the awards of your contests. You bore
the burden and the heat of the day; receive the kingdom of the heavens; you
endured hunger and thirst, sleeping on the ground and dry eating; enjoy then
glory beyond compare, and joy unspeakable, which I prepared beforehand for you
my friends; rest then in the courts of paradise, in the light-formed
dwellings, in luxury and gladness that has no end, in tents bright and
glorified. Enter into places of light, and rest from your labors, which you
endured for my sake. Rest with my holy angels, with apostles, with prophets
and the saints who from the ages have been well-pleasing to me; enter into my
palace, where there is no toil, grief, and groaning nor tear, but eternal life
and unending. Such gifts I grant to those who have kept my word; thus I honor
those who have honored me, thus I glorify those who glorify me, thus I give
rest to those who give rest to my members, the poor and powerless; thus I
bless those who care for my Churches, thus I render richly and unstintingly
and a hundredfold, the eternal life. You gave me a fragment of bread, take a
heavenly kingdom; you gave me silver, take the paradise of luxury; you gave me
an old garment, I clothe you with the unapproachable light; you gave me a cup
of water, receive the living water of rest; you brought me in under shelter;
behold I grant you to be with the holy angels; you gave me perishable things,
take imperishable; you gave me things of a season, take eternal; you deemed me
worthy of your table, take eternal luxury; you delivered me from a prison, you
visited me when I was sick, you loosed my famine and thirst, you washed my
feet, you warmed me when I was shivering; behold I grant you the bosom of
Abraham. I received clay, I give pearls; I received grass, I give gold. For
all your things, such as they are, beside my gifts are grass and clay; for
yours are earthly, but mine heavenly; all yours withering, but mine light,
life and joy and rest.

I know how many things you have done for me, I know how many things you have
cut off from your mouth, and filled my soul; I understand, and I have not
forgotten how many times you received me as a stranger; and for all these
things, as many good things as there are in heaven and as many as upon the
earth, I grant to you my friends unto age of age.

γʹ. You have seen, brethren, the gain of almsgiving, you have known its
strength, you have learned its power, you have considered its benefit. Then,
he says, the Lord says to those on the left, to the unmerciful, to those who
spend life shamefully: For I hungered, he says, and you did not give me to
eat; I thirsted, and you did not give me drink, and the rest. How many times
did I come to your door seeking a commandment, and you turned me away
empty-handed? how many times did I come into your houses, and saw a table full
of all kinds of good things, and feeding harlots and dancers and flatterers,
and you did not deem me worthy even of a fragment of bread? For was I seeking
what was yours? Even from what is mine you did not give me to eat. How many
times did I come into your houses when a violent winter was upon us, naked and
unshod, not even able to speak from the violence and the necessity; but you,
having given orders, having despised me, left me outside, and did not even
reckon me as one of the little dogs? And for this reason go from me, you
cursed, into the outer fire, where the unsleeping worm is, =60.704= and
gnashing of teeth; because not only did you yourselves not have mercy on me,
but you also often hindered those who wished to have mercy on me, and you were
persuading them saying, that The poor are thieves; why do you pity them? You
fed the dogs, you tended the horses, you cared for the cattle and looked after
asses, and you were anxious about swine and sheep; but my members, the poor
and powerless, you never pitied; thus neither shall you be pitied. Do you not
know, wretched ones, that through the poor every man has salvation? did you
not read, that I called them my brethren? did you not hear the Scripture
saying, that He who pities a poor man lends to God? did you not learn, that
Judgment is without mercy to those who have not done mercy? O how much did the
needy, my brethren, cry out, mourning, weeping and lamenting? what word did
they not cry out to bend your implacable heart, Have mercy, help, saying,
give, you who do good, give, you merciful, give, you friends of God, give, you
compassionate, and practicing countless other words and gestures able even to
soften stones, and to make wild beasts gentle; but you hardened your inward
parts, and not only do you not pity and console, but you even rather pursued
them from place to place, so that many died from famine and cold?

O how many orphans did you wrong, how many widows did you defraud, how many
powerless did you afflict, how many worn down did you make naked and captives?
o how many hired men's wages did you withhold, for which you will also be
punished eternally? Did you not learn from the five foolish virgins, that
every virtue without almsgiving profits nothing? Even if you speak of
virginity, even if of fasting, even if of prayer, even if of vigil, dry eating
and hunger and thirst, even if you speak of anything whatsoever without
almsgiving, it is nothing. What then shall I say to your inhuman choice? For
this reason Go from me, you cursed, into the eternal fire. For into my palace
an unmerciful man does not enter, for the heavenly dwellings an
uncompassionate man is not deemed worthy; into my paradise a wicked man or a
blasphemer or a knave does not come; into my bridal chamber a fornicator or an
adulterer or a man who lies with a male will not be deemed worthy; at my table
a hard man or one who does not share will not recline; in the dwellings above
a robber or a greedy man shall not walk; to my palaces a thief or a reviler
shall not draw near; into the altar above a liar or a slanderer or a drunkard
shall not enter; in my house a lover of money or a withholder shall not rest;
through the heavenly veil an unclean or a wanton or a profane man does not
pass; into eternal rest a slothful or a sleepy or a man-slayer shall not pass
through; at my supper an angry man or a murmurer or one who remembers evils
shall not touch; in my bridechamber a babbler or a teller of tales or a jester
shall not lodge at all; in eternal tents a shameless or an envious man has no
inheritance.

Let no one be led astray; no unjust man or anyone of such as these shall be
deemed worthy of the kingdom of the heavens; but those only shall enter, the
ready to share, the poor in spirit, those who mourn, the meek, those who
hunger and thirst for righteousness, the pure in heart, and the peacemakers;
such as these shall enter into the kingdom of the heavens, and they shall
rejoice and exult, because their reward is great in heaven. But you, the
inhuman and unmerciful, go into the outer fire, that prepared for the devil
and his angels. For just as you did not have mercy, so then you shall not be
shown mercy. Then depart from me, you workers of lawlessness; go into the
eternal darkness, you cursed and hated, where the unsleeping worm is, and the
fire is not quenched; there shall be the weeping and the gnashing of teeth.
And these shall go away into eternal punishment, but the righteous into
eternal life.

Having learned these things then, brethren, come, and let us turn from our
wicked way; let us repent, let us groan, let us strike the breast, let us
watch who are nodding, let us wake, let us weep over our sins; and if we have
slandered anyone or seized anything, let us give it back, lest we be
condemned; let us have mercy, that we may find mercy. And if we have anything
against anyone, whether poor or rich, let us forgive all from the heart
itself, as Christ commanded, lest somehow we too be condemned and sentenced on
the day of judgment, just as those unmerciful and misan =60.705= thropic ones.

Let us become toward one another compassionate, lovers of mankind, mild,
lovers of the good, reasonable, long-suffering, helping one another, bearing
with one another, forgiving one another, correcting one another, admonishing
one another, bearing one another's burdens; that we may both escape the
experience of the punishments spoken of before, and having completed the
present =60.706= life in forgiveness of sins, we may attain the eternal good
things, by the grace and love of mankind of our Lord Jesus Christ, to whom be
the glory and the might, together with the Father and the Holy Spirit, now and
always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
