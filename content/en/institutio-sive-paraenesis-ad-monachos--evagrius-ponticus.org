* Instruction or Exhortation to Monks
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of the same our father among the saints Nilus, Exhortation to
Monks.
=79.1236= Fasting cuts off incontinence of foods, and continence with
prayer takes away the licentiousness of fornication. Poverty contracts
love of money, the mother of idolatry, and hope toward God cuts off
bodily care. Reading of the oracles of God, and vigil with prayer,
contract a wandering mind. Long-suffering and psalmody soothe the
assault of anger. Patience and tears put down acedia, and hatred toward
pleasures silences worldly grief. Humility acquires hospitality, and
turning away from love of rule and of preeminence; and immeasurable
humility, which says, "I am earth and ashes," puts to rest pride, the
ancient evil, the abominable thing before the Lord, which God also
resists. And again: "Unless the Lord build a house, in vain has the
builder labored," and trying to keep watch. As it is not possible to
purify troubled water unless it remain unshaken, so it is not possible
to know a monk's state without stillness. As it is impossible for an
athlete to be crowned without having wrestled, so it is not possible to
become a Christian without contests. As the yoke is not even when one
part is heavy, so it is not possible to accomplish a commandment of God
when the fleshly mind prevails. Just as it is not possible to learn an
art without attending diligently, so it is not possible to acquire
prayer without persevering toward God in an upright heart. He who has
not acquired pure prayer has no weapon for war.
He who is adorned and swept of the virtues of God will be a
dwelling-place of demons. =79.1237= As it is not possible for a king to
dwell in a poor man's house, so Christ will not dwell in a soul soiled
by sin. Just as a wild place is tamed by careful industry, so a soul
made wild by sins is tamed if it lives according to the law of God.
Just as a rock being in the sea remains firm and unshaken when struck
by waves, so also he who has accomplished the virtues and been wholly
mixed with them will not be shaken by the devil. Just as a physician
devises the medicine toward the healing of the passion, so also the
knowledge of God, being kept, teaches the one who partakes of it how he
may be preserved and come to greater things. Just as a torrent makes
its own passage, so also sin once received works the destruction of the
one who has acquired it. As it is not easy for a deer that has fallen
into nets to escape, so he who fulfills the desires and lives in this
world will not flee sin; for wicked desires and pleasures are bonds
hard to loose. Just as a vine that does not produce good fruits is cut
out so that it may not make the place idle, so also everyone who does
not produce fruits of the Holy Spirit will be uprooted from the Lord.
As it is impossible that a man struck by an arrow not be harmed, so it
is impossible that a monk who has accepted an evil thought not be
wounded. Just as one who has gone out of a straight road wanders in a
foreign land, not knowing where he is going, so also a man wanders who
does not believe in the consubstantial Trinity. Just as someone divided
into many things is one by nature, so also the holy Trinity, even if it
is divided in names and hypostases, is one by nature. You would not
grasp the nature of God, not even if flying with wings; God is
incomprehensible, and therefore he is also our creator. Just as the
vessel being fashioned does not know the beginning and nature of the
one who fashioned it, so also a man will not be able to find out the
nature or beginning of God. Do not busy yourself about the Trinity, but
only believe and worship; for he who busies himself does not believe.
Acquire wisdom and not silver; make a luminous garment of Christ our
Lord, above every linen robe, because possessions will not profit in
the day of death. Just as fire consumes a thicket, so every sinner will
be consumed by the eternal fire, and he cannot come to an end; for he
will be tortured immortally. It is no great thing if we become
righteous, for what we were first created by God, very good, this we
shall become. =79.1240= He who lives contrary to nature does not keep
commandments of God. All wisdom is to fear the Lord, and all
instruction that does not teach the law of the Lord is folly and
mindlessness. Faith and baptism will not deliver from the eternal fire
without works of righteousness.
For if you have covenanted with Christ, keep his commandments, and if
you believe the things to come, strive for the eternal glory, and fear
the flaming sword; but if you do not accomplish the ordinances of God,
do not call yourself faithful. All creation obeys the ordinance of God,
but man alone disobeys, for whose sake all things came to be. We will
give nothing to God if we keep ourselves pure, but we will procure life
for ourselves, and unspeakable enjoyment of the eternal goods. Do not
say that I cannot keep the commandment of God, either because of
father, or because of mother, or because of wife, or because of
children, or because of someone else; for they will not deliver you
from the impending wrath and the unending worm. And let everyone who
opposes the accomplishment of God and of virtues be abominable and
hateful to you; it is good not even to eat with such a one.
