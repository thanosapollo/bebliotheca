Sermon on the Palm Branches. Of the <same> Athanasius, archbishop of
Alexandria, a discourse on the palms.
1.1 A fearful mystery, beloved, has also today taken place in Jerusalem.
And I call mystery that which was proclaimed by the prophets and
fulfilled by Christ. 1.2 For truly "blessed is he who comes in the name
of the Lord" according to the blessed David. And again the same: "out of
the mouth of infants and sucklings you have prepared praise." 1.3 For,
confirming the common resurrection of the race of men, the Master and
Maker of all, Jesus Christ, who ordered all things for our sake, before
his saving passion raised Lazarus from the dead. 1.4 Hence after this,
hastening, as I have already said, to take upon himself the voluntary
passion for our sake. 1.5 "He sends," says the evangelist, "two of his
own disciples, saying: Go into the village opposite you; you will find a
donkey bound and a colt with her; having loosed them, bring them to me."
2.1 For this reason the blessed Jacob, the patriarch, when he was giving
the blessings by lot to his children, coming to Judah spoke thus: 2.2
"Judah, may your brothers praise you." And after a little: "and he is
the expectation of nations." Of what nations then did Judah become the
expectation? Of Jews? By no means. But the prophet makes this clear,
because from Judah the Lord has arisen. 2.3 And again after other things
the same: "binding his donkey to the vine and to the vine-shoot the colt
of his donkey." By saying donkey and colt he signified the church of the
nations. 2.4 For the Lord truly bound the nations to the vine and to the
vine-shoot, that is, to the old and the new covenant, so that they might
bear fruit of piety through faith. 2.5 Then he adds: "he will wash his
robe in wine and his garment in the blood of the grape." He calls the
flesh the robe of Christ and the garment. 2.6 For willingly for our sake
he was nailed to the cross in the flesh, that by his own blood he might
wash out the filth of our sins. 2.7 As also it is written in Isaiah:
"Why are your garments red, and your clothes as from a trodden
winepress, full of what has been trampled?" 3.1 But let us return to the
matter at hand. For the Lord says to the disciples concerning the
donkey: "And if anyone says to you, What are you doing, or why are you
loosing the colt, say that the Lord has need of it." 3.2 Do not
hesitate, he says, in any of the things done according to my economy; no
one of you will stand against nor will contradict even one word. 3.3
"Say that the Lord has need of it." No one will hinder you doing what is
commanded by me. "For mine is the inhabited world and its fullness." 3.4
All the things written concerning me must therefore come to fulfillment.
I must enter into Jerusalem today, mounted upon a beast of burden. 3.5
"Search the scriptures:" behold, Zechariah the prophet cries out plainly
saying: "Rejoice greatly, daughter Zion; a just one and a savior is
proclaimed to you; he is meek and mounted upon a beast of burden and a
young colt of a donkey." 3.6 The present time is therefore a time of
good pleasure. I must dissolve the enmity and make peace of all things
through the cross. The present Jerusalem must come to an end and the
church from the nations must be established. 3.7 Or did not also David
the hymn-singer prophesy concerning this calling of the nations? For
what did he say? "All the nations, as many as you have made, shall come
and shall worship before you, Lord." These are the "true worshipers,"
who receive the Word through faith. 3.8 Then the evangelist says: "And
the disciples, having gone, did as he commanded them and brought the
donkey and the colt and laid their garments upon them, and they seated
Jesus." 4.1 O the great condescension of the Savior toward us. Who will
not marvel at the greatness of his love toward us? Who would not be
struck, that the one who is mounted upon the cherubim sat upon a colt
for our sake? 4.2 The one hymned by the seraphim is praised by infants.
For some, he says, were strewing the way with garments, and others,
cutting branches from the trees, were doing the same. 4.3 And those
going before and those following were crying out together with the
children inexperienced in evil, saying a certain victory hymn: "Hosanna
to the son of David, blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord,
hosanna in the highest." 4.4 Therefore we too, having put away every
filthy thought and shameful ponderings and the wickedness in cunning,
having taken up a thought inexperienced in evil, together with those
children with bright lamps let us go to meet the Savior Christ, our true
God. 4.5 And with the palms let us proclaim him victor over death, and
with the branches of olives let us entreat the merciful and good one,
saying: "Blessed is he who came and who comes in the name of the Lord,
hosanna in the highest." 4.6 Blessed is he who came according to economy
and "being rich became poor for our sake, that we too might become rich
by your poverty." And coming with glory to judge the living and the
dead. To him be the glory and the power unto the ages of ages. Amen.
