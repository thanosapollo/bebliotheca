OF ARETHAS THE DEACON
Even if a swift fate quenched for me the lamp of an age, having robbed the
light of our torch, yet the demon sufficed by the rage of his own wickedness
to complete all my hated life; he made me a widow near a breast being raised,
nor did I see a fair child playing, a sweet delight for parents in the arms;
wherefore an unbearable grief mastered me with a wasting torch of wasting;
going toward three and twenty years this tomb, host of our birth, has held;
there aged parents wove a dirge for all, plucking the gray, and a chorus of
well-born brothers groaning, remembering gladly the brightness of Anna's form.
=15.33= OF THE SAME The solemn character, the philanthropic grace, the
obedience of manner toward parents, for shame led as a great teacher being
present, the bright marks of the race and the husband-loving standings toward
nature shaking the unfixed things by force of widowhood, and being overcome by
an intertwining of better things from which the bright crowns according to
worth of a one-husband shine, the tomb will not be able to darken the boldness
of Anna; for in no way would the mouth of all forget her who bears a fair
admonition for life; and if not, a writing too will speak more than all, this
one having been set beside the tomb itself.
=15.34= OF THE SAME Febronia somehow did even among the nether spirits of men
symbols of her own sympathy, if there is even here a need of the rich or for
the poor; for not even there do the souls of the great-hearted altogether
forget their own benefaction; yet may an age of our life be sufficient to send
her upon a divine chamber of Christ, whom as a solemn daughter-in-law of
uncorruption she took, brightening a lamp with oil of love of the poor; whose
this sign of long sleep know.
