* Question 136 from the Questions to Duke Antiochus
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

52 Him who does not receive the divine scriptures we ought henceforth to
persuade concerning knowledge of God from the very nature of the things. For we
see certain essences in the creation existing with one another not according to
nature, but above nature. I say something of this sort. Nature, the essence of
the waters, is flowing and downward-bearing. How then do we see the so-called
siphons drawing water from the sea toward the clouds? And the more wonderful,
that coming up salty, these are brought down sweet upon the earth through the
rains. And how again the nature of stones and bodies, being by nature sinking,
is seen unsinkable and unsinking in the waters of Marmaric Pentapolis? And not
only, but also the nature of water and fire being contrary was at the same time
once in Lycia in the mountain called Olympus, as myriads of men have seen. And
other myriads like these and paradoxical things are and are named and are
wondered at in the creation; all of which would not have been about to come to
be thus above nature, unless some essence of God were in them mastering and
permitting these not to array against one another. And how again, let the
children of Greeks tell us, when a solid thunder comes to be, all the nature of
men trembles, both shudders and stands out, showing through the form that it is
under a master who works the thunders? These examples bring toward the simpler
of the Greeks unto knowledge of God. For toward the wise among them it is
possible to use many testimonies from ancient powerful philosophers concerning
piety. Not only did wise of certain Greeks speak piety, but they also dimly
fore-announced the economy of Christ. And at any rate one of the wise among them
many times before the sojourn of Christ says that the young woman will come
having for us a heavenly offspring. And another again spoke thus. Late sometime
God comes down upon this much-divided earth, and without a fault he will become
flesh, he will be hung as a condemned one on a wood, and he will heal incurable
sufferings.
