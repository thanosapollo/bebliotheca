* Chapter 1
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

It happened recently, a dispute was held between a Christian and a Jewish
proselyte. By alternate turns with a contentious rope both drew the day into
evening. With some also shouting from the parties of each, the truth was
overshadowed by a certain cloud. It pleased therefore that what through the
concert of disputation could be less fully made clear be inspected more
carefully and that the questions retraced by readings with the stylus be
brought to an end. For the occasion of defending divine grace even for the
nations had from this a prerogative, that a man from the nations and not a Jew
from the stock of the Israelites undertook to claim for himself the law of
God. For this is enough, that the nations can be admitted to the law of God,
lest Israel still be proud that the nations are reckoned as a drop of a bucket
or dust from a threshing-floor. Although we have God Himself as a suitable
promiser and a faithful sponsor, who promised to Abraham that in his seed all
the nations of the earth would be blessed, and that from the womb of Rebecca
two peoples and two nations were going to come forth, namely of the Jews, that
is of Israel, and of the nations, that is of ours. Each therefore was called
both people and nation, lest anyone dare to defend for himself a privilege of
grace from the appellation of the name. For God destined two peoples and two
nations to come forth from the womb of one woman, and He did not distinguish
grace in the appellation of the name, but in the bringing forth of the birth,
so that he who was going to come forth first from the womb would be subjected
to the lesser, that is to the later. For thus God spoke to Rebecca: Two
nations are in your womb, and two peoples will be divided from your belly, and
a people will overcome a people, and the greater will serve the lesser.
Therefore since the people or nation of the Jews is earlier in time and
greater through the grace of the first dignation in the law, but ours is
understood as lesser in the age of times, as having obtained knowledge of the
divine mercy in the last space of the age, without doubt by the edict of the
divine utterance the prior and greater people, that is the Jewish, must serve
the lesser, and the lesser people, that is the Christian, must overcome the
greater. For also according to the memorials of the divine scriptures the
people of the Jews, that is the more ancient, having abandoned God served
idols and, divinity having been abandoned, was given over to images, the
people saying to Aaron, Make us gods who may go before us. When gold from the
necklaces of the women and the rings of the men had been melted by fire and a
bovine head had come forth for them, to this figment all Israel, God having
been abandoned, gave honor, saying, These are the gods who cast us out of the
land of Egypt. For thus also in later times, in which kings were commanding
them and when with Jeroboam they were worshipping golden heifers and groves
and were selling themselves to Baal. Whence it is proved that they have always
been designated guilty of the crime of idolatry from the instrument of the
divine scriptures. But our lesser people, that is the later, having left the
idols which it formerly served, has been converted to the same God from whom
Israel, as we have recalled above, had departed. For thus the lesser people,
that is the later, has overcome the greater people, while it obtains the grace
of divine dignation, from which Israel has been repudiated.

* Chapter 2
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h2
:END:

Therefore let us join step, and let us determine the sum of the question
itself by sure lines. For why should God, the founder of the universe, the
governor of the whole world, the former of man, the sower of all nations, be
believed to have given a law through Moses to one people, and not be said to
have assigned it to all nations? For unless He had given it to all, He would
by no pact permit even proselytes from the nations to have access to it. But
as befits the goodness of God and His equity, as former of the human race, He
gave to all nations the same law, which He commanded to be observed at certain
and appointed times, when He willed, and through whom He willed, and as He
willed. For in the beginning of the world He gave a law to Adam himself and
Eve, that they should not eat of the fruit of the tree planted in the middle
of paradise, and that if they had done otherwise they would die by death.
Which law would have sufficed for them, if it had been kept. For in this law
given to Adam we recognize all the precepts stored which afterward sprouted,
given through Moses, that is, You shall love the Lord your God from your whole
heart and from your whole soul, and, You shall love your neighbor as yourself,
and, You shall not kill, You shall not commit adultery, You shall not steal,
You shall not speak false testimony, Honor your father and mother, and, You
shall not covet another's. For a primordial law was given to Adam and Eve in
paradise, as it were the matrix of all the precepts of God. Finally if they
had loved the Lord their God, they would not have done against His precept; if
they were loving the neighbor, that is themselves, they would not have
believed the persuasion of the serpent, and so would not have committed
homicide upon themselves, by falling from immortality, by doing against the
precept of God; they would also have abstained from theft, if they had not
tasted in secret of the fruit of the tree, nor would they have desired to hide
from the sight of the Lord their God under the tree, nor would they have been
made partners of the devil asserting a falsehood, by believing him that they
were going to be like God, and so they would not have offended God as a
father, who had formed them from the mud of the earth as from a mother's womb;
if they had not coveted another's, they would not have tasted of the illicit
fruit. Therefore in this general and primordial law of God, which God had
sanctioned to be observed in the fruit of the tree, we know that all the
precepts of the later law were specially implanted, which, issued in their
times, germinated. For it belongs to the same one afterward to superinduce a
law who had first sent a precept in advance, since it is also His afterward to
instruct who had before undertaken to form the just. For what wonder, if he
increases discipline who instituted it, if he advances who began? Finally I
contend that before the law of Moses written on stone tables there was a law
nou written, which was understood naturally and was kept by the fathers. For
whence was Noah found just, if the justice of the natural law was not going
before for him? whence was Abraham reckoned a friend of God, if not from the
equity and justice of the natural law? whence was Melchizedek named priest of
the most high God, if not because before the priesthood of the Levitical law
there were Levites who were offering sacrifices to God? For thus after the
patriarchs written above the law was given to Moses, at that time after they
went out from Egypt, after an interval and spaces of many times. Finally after
four hundred and thirty years from Abraham the law was given. Whence we
understand the law of God even before Moses, and not first only at Horeb or at
Sinai and in the desert, but more ancient, first in paradise, then to the
patriarchs, and so also reformed for the Jews at certain times, so that we may
no longer attend to the law of Moses as to a principal law, but to a
subsequent one, which at a certain time God both exhibited to the nations and,
promised again through the prophets, reformed for the better, and forewarned
that it would be that, just as at a certain time the law was given through
Moses, so it should be believed to have been observed and kept temporally. Nor
let us take away this power of God reforming the precepts of the law according
to the condition of times for the salvation of man. Finally he who contends
that both the sabbath is still to be observed as a remedy of salvation and
circumcision of the eighth day because of a threatening of death, let him
teach that in the past the just sabbatized or were circumcised, and were thus
made friends of God. For if circumcision purges a man, when God made Adam
uncircumcised, why did He not circumcise him, even after he sinned, if
circumcision purges? Certainly, placing him in paradise, He set him
uncircumcised as husbandman over paradise. Therefore since God instituted Adam
neither circumcised nor sabbatizing, consequently also his offspring, Abel,
offering sacrifices to Him, uncircumcised and not sabbatizing, He praised,
accepting what he was offering in simplicity of heart, and reproving the
sacrifice of his brother Cain, who was not dividing rightly what he was
offering. Noah also, uncircumcised, but also not sabbatizing, God freed from
the flood. For Enoch too, most just, not circumcised nor sabbatizing, He
translated from this world, who has not yet tasted death, that as a candidate
of eternity he might already show us that we too without the burden of the law
of Moses can please God. Melchizedek also, priest of the most high God,
uncircumcised and not sabbatizing, was chosen to the priesthood of God. Lot
also, brother of Abraham, proves that for the merits of justice without
observation of the law he was freed from the burning of the Sodomites.

But Abraham, you say, was circumcised. But he pleased God before he was
circumcised, nor yet did he sabbatize. For he had received circumcision, but
such as was for a sign of that time, not for a prerogative of salvation.
Finally the following patriarchs were uncircumcised, as Melchizedek, who to
Abraham himself already circumcised, returning from battle, offered bread and
wine uncircumcised. But also the son, he says, of Moses would then have been
choked by the angel, if Zipporah his mother had not circumcised the foreskin
of the infant with a pebble. Whence, he says, there is greatest danger, if
anyone has not circumcised the foreskin of the flesh. But if circumcision were
altogether to bring salvation, even Moses himself would not have omitted in
his son so as not to circumcise him on the eighth day, since it is established
that Zipporah, compelled by the angel, did it on the journey. Let us consider
therefore that the compelled circumcision of one infant could not prescribe
for the whole people and as it were found a law of this precept. For God,
providing that He was going to give this circumcision to the people of Israel
for a sign, not for salvation, therefore urges that the son of Moses, the
future leader, be circumcised, so that, when He had begun through him to give
the people the precept of circumcision, the people would not reject it, seeing
that example already celebrated in the leader's son. For circumcision was
going to be given, but for a sign, whence Israel would have to be recognized
in the last time, when according to its merits it would be forbidden to enter
into the holy city, through the words of the prophets saying: Your land
desert, your cities burned with fire, foreigners will eat your region in your
sight, and the daughter of Zion will be left desert and overthrown by foreign
peoples, as a hut in a vineyard, and as a watch-place in a cucumber-garden,
and as a city which is stormed. Why so? Because the following speech of the
prophet upbraids them saying: I have begotten sons and exalted them, but they
have rejected me. And again: And if you stretch out your hands, I will turn my
face from you; and if you multiply prayers, I will not hear you; for your
hands are full of blood. And again: Woe, sinful nation, a people full of sins,
wicked sons, you have abandoned God, and you have provoked to indignation the
Holy One of Israel. This therefore was the providence of God, of giving
circumcision to Israel for a sign whence they could be recognized, when the
time should come at which by their merits aforesaid they would be forbidden to
be admitted into Jerusalem; which both because it was going to be was
announced, and because we see it done, we recognize. For just as carnal
circumcision, which was temporal, was imbued as a sign for a contumacious
people, so a spiritual one has been given for salvation to an obedient people,
the prophet Jeremiah saying, Renew yourselves, and do not sow among thorns: be
circumcised to God, and circumcise the foreskin of your heart. And in another
place he says, For behold days will come, says the Lord, and I will arrange
for the house of Judah and the house of Jacob a new testament, not such as I
already gave to their fathers on the day on which I led them out of the land
of Egypt. Whence we understand that both the former circumcision then given
would cease, and a new law, not such as He had already given to the fathers,
is announced as going to come forth, as Isaiah preached, saying that in the
last days the mountain of the Lord and the house of God would be manifest
above the vertices of the mountains: and it will be exalted, he says, above
the hills, and all nations will come upon it, and many will walk and say:
come, let us go up into the mountain of the Lord and into the house of the God
of Jacob, not into Esau of the prior son, but into Jacob of the following,
that is of our people, whose mountain is Christ, cut out without the hands of
those who cut, filling all the earth, shown in Daniel. Finally from this house
of the God of Jacob Isaiah announces by the following words that a new law
also would come forth, saying, For from Zion will go out the law and the word
of the Lord from Jerusalem, and He will judge among the nations, that is among
us, who have been called from the nations: and they will cut, he says, their
swords into ploughs and their lances into sickles, and a nation will not take
up a sword against a nation, and they will no longer learn to fight. Who
therefore are understood other than we, who taught by the new law observe
these things, the old law having been obliterated, whose future abolition the
act itself demonstrates? For the old law was vindicating itself by vengeance
of the sword, and was digging out an eye for an eye, and was repaying
vengeance of injury, but the new law was designating clemency, and was turning
the former ferocity of swords and lances to tranquillity, and was reforming
the former execution of war against rivals of the law and enemies into
peaceful acts of ploughing and cultivating the earth. Therefore, as we have
shown above, that the old law and carnal circumcision were pronounced going to
cease, so also the observance of the new law and of spiritual circumcision has
shone out in the obediences of peace. For a people, he says, whom I had not
known, has served me, in the hearing of the ear it has obeyed me. The prophets
have announced. But what people that was ignorant of God, except ours, who
formerly did not know God? and who has heard Him in the hearing of the ear,
except we, who having left idols have been converted to God? For Israel, who
had been known to God, and who had been exalted by Him in Egypt, and was
carried across through the Erythraean sea, and who in the desert fed with
manna for XL years was reduced after the likeness of eternity, not
contaminated by human passions or fed with the foods of this age, but fed with
the breads of angels, manna, and sufficiently obligated to God by benefits,
forgot his Lord and God, saying to Aaron, Make us gods who may go before us;
for that Moses, who cast us out of the land of Egypt, has left us, and what
has happened to him, we do not know. And therefore we, who were not formerly
the people of God, have been made His people by receiving the new law,
aforesaid, and the new circumcision, predicted before.

It follows therefore that, inasmuch as the abolition of carnal circumcision
and of the old law is demonstrated as accomplished in its times, so the
observation of the sabbath also may be demonstrated to have been temporary.
For the Jews say that from the beginning God sanctified the seventh day, by
resting on it from all His works which He made, and that thence also Moses
said to the people, Remember the day of sabbaths, to sanctify it; you shall
not do any servile work on it, except what pertains to the soul. Whence we
understand that we ought rather always to sabbatize from every servile work,
and not only on every seventh day, but through all time. And through this it
must be asked by us which sabbath God would wish us to keep? For the
scriptures designate an eternal sabbath and a temporal sabbath. For Isaiah the
prophet says, My soul hates your sabbaths; and in another place he says, You
have profaned my sabbaths. Whence we distinguish that the temporal sabbath is
human, and the eternal sabbath is judged divine, of which He preaches through
Isaiah: And it will be, he says, month from month and day from day and sabbath
from sabbath, and all flesh will come to adore in Jerusalem, says the Lord.
Which we understand fulfilled in the times of Christ, when all flesh, that is
every nation, has come to adore in Jerusalem God the Father, through Jesus
Christ His Son, as it was predicted through the prophet: Behold proselytes
will go through me to you. Thus therefore before this temporal sabbath there
was also an eternal sabbath shown beforehand and predicted, just as also
before carnal circumcision there was also a spiritual circumcision shown
beforehand. Finally let them teach, as we have already spoken in advance, that
Adam sabbatized, or that Abel offering a holy victim to God pleased by
religion of the sabbath, or that Enoch translated was a worshipper of the
sabbath, or that Noah, builder of the ark, observed the sabbath because of the
immense flood, or that Abraham in observation of the sabbath offered his son
Isaac, or that Melchizedek in his priesthood received the law of the sabbath.
But the Jews are going to say that from the time this precept was given
through Moses, from then it had to be observed. It is therefore manifest that
the precept was not eternal nor spiritual, but temporal, which would at some
time cease. Finally so little is this solemnity to be celebrated in a vacation
of the sabbath, that is of the seventh day, that Joshua son of Nun, at that
time when he was warring down the city of Jericho, said that it was commanded
him by God that he should command the people that the priests should carry
around the ark of the testament of God for seven days in a circuit of the
city, and so, the circuit of the seventh day having been completed, the walls
of the city would fall of their own accord. Which was so done, and, the space
of the seventh day having been finished, as it had been predicted, the walls
of the city fell. From which it is manifestly shown that into the number of
those seven days the day of the sabbath had fallen. For seven days, from
wherever they have taken a beginning, must enclose with them a sabbath day, on
which day not only the priests worked, but also the city was made spoil at the
mouth of the sword by all the people of Israel. Nor is it doubtful that they
worked a servile work, when they were driving spoils of war from the precept
of God. For also in the times of the Maccabees by fighting on sabbaths they
did bravely, and stormed the foreign enemies, and by fighting on sabbaths they
recalled the paternal law to the former state of life. Nor would I think they
defended another law except that in which they remembered it was prescribed
concerning the day of sabbaths. Whence it is manifest that precepts of this
kind availed for a time and the necessity of a present cause, and that God had
not given them a law of this kind for observation of perpetual time.

So also we have shown the sacrifices of earthly oblations and of spiritual
sacrifices preached, and indeed that from the beginning the earthly sacrifices
of the greater son, that is of Israel, were shown beforehand in Cain, and the
diverse sacrifices of the lesser son Abel, that is of our people,
demonstrated. For Cain, greater by birth, offered gifts to God from the fruit
of the earth, but the lesser son Abel from the fruit of his sheep. God looked
upon Abel and upon his gifts, but upon Cain and upon his gifts He did not
look. And God said to Cain, Why has your face fallen? have you not sinned, if
indeed you offer rightly, but do not divide rightly; be still. For your
turning is toward you, and he will dominate you. And then Cain said to Abel
his brother, Let us go into the field, and he went with him there and killed
him. And then God said to Cain, Where is Abel your brother? And he said, I do
not know; am I the keeper of my(??) brother? To whom God said, The voice of
the blood of your brother cries to me from the earth. On account of which the
earth is cursed, which opened its mouth to receive the blood of your brother.
Groaning and trembling you will be upon the earth, and everyone who finds you
will kill you. From this act therefore we already then from the beginning
observe the double sacrifices of the two peoples shown beforehand. Finally
when through Moses in Leviticus the priestly law was being written, we find it
prescribed to the people of Israel that sacrifices should be offered to God in
no other place than in the land of promise, which the Lord God would give to
the people of Israel and their brothers, so that, Israel having been
introduced, sacrifices and holocausts both for sins and for souls should be
celebrated there, and nowhere else except in the holy land. Why therefore
afterward through the prophets does the Spirit preach that it would be that in
every place and in every land sacrifices would be offered to God, as through
Malachi the angel, one of the twelve prophets, He says: I will not receive
sacrifice from your hands, because from the rising sun even to the setting my
name has been made famous in all nations, says the Lord almighty; and in every
place they offer clean sacrifices to my name. Likewise in the psalms David
says: Bring to God, native land of the nations; without doubt because the
preaching of the apostles was going to go out into every land: bring to God
glory and honor, bring to God sacrifices of His name: Take up victims and
enter into His courts. For that God is to be appeased not with earthly
sacrifices but with spiritual, we so read, as it is written: A tribulated and
humbled heart is a victim to God. And elsewhere: Sacrifice to God a sacrifice
of praise, and render to the Most High your vows. Thus therefore spiritual
sacrifices of praise are designated, and a tribulated heart is demonstrated an
acceptable sacrifice to God. And so just as carnal sacrifices are understood
as reproved, of which Isaiah also speaks saying, To what purpose the multitude
of your sacrifices to me? says the Lord, so spiritual sacrifices are preached
as accepted, as the prophets announce. Because even if you bring, he says,
fine flour to me, a vain supplication, it is an execration to me. And still he
says, Your holocausts and sacrifices and the fat of goats and the blood of
bulls I do not want, not even if you come to be seen by me: for who has sought
these things from your hands? because from the rising sun even into the
setting my name has been made famous in all nations, says the Lord. But of
spiritual sacrifices he adds saying, And in every place they offer clean
sacrifices to my name, says the Lord.

Therefore since it is manifest that both a temporal sabbath has been shown and
an eternal sabbath predicted, carnal circumcision also predicted and spiritual
circumcision indicated beforehand, a temporal law also and an eternal law
denounced, carnal sacrifices and spiritual sacrifices shown beforehand, it
follows that, all those precepts having been given carnally to the people of
Israel in the preceding time, a time would come upon them in which the
precepts of the ancient law and of the old ceremonies would cease, and the
promise of a new law and the recognition of spiritual sacrifices and the
pledge of a new testament would come upon them, a light shining upon us from
on high, who were sitting in darkness and were being held in the shadow of
death. And so a necessity lies upon us that, since we have said in advance
that a new law was preached by the prophets, and that it was not such as had
already been given to their fathers at that time when He led them out of the
land of Egypt, we ought to show and prove both that that old law has ceased
and that the promised new law now operates. And indeed first it must be asked,
whether a bringer of a new law is awaited, and an heir of a new testament, and
a priest of new sacrifices, and a purger of a new circumcision and a
worshipper of an eternal sabbath, who may restrain the old law, and establish
a new testament, and offer new sacrifices, and repress the ancient ceremonies,
and restrain the old circumcision with its own sabbath, and announce a new
kingdom which is not corrupted. This one, I say, the bringer of a new law,
worshipper of a spiritual sabbath, high priest of eternal sacrifices, eternal
dominator of an eternal kingdom, it must be asked whether he has already come
or not, so that, if he has already come, he must be served, if he has not yet
come, he must be awaited, provided it be manifest that by his advent the
precepts of the old law are compressed, and the beginnings of a new law ought
to arise. And in the first place it must be defined that the ancient law and
the prophets could not have ceased unless he had come who through the same law
and through the same prophets was being announced as going to come.

Therefore in this let us join step, whether Christ who was being announced as
going to come has already come, or is still hoped as going to come. Which
itself, that it may be able to be proved, times also must be sought by us when
the prophets announced that Christ would come, so that, if within those times
we have recognized that He has come, we may without doubt believe that He is
the one whom the prophets were singing as going to come, in whom we, namely
the nations, were being announced as going to believe, and when it has been
established that He has come, we may without doubt also believe that a new law
has been given by Him, and we may not deny that a new testament has been
arranged for us in Him and through Him. For we know that even the Jews do not
refute that Christ is going to come, as those who stretch their hope toward
His advent. Nor must more be asked about this, since formerly all the prophets
preached of Him, as Isaiah: Thus says the Lord God to my Christ the Lord,
whose right hand I have held, that the nations may hear Him: I will break the
strengths of kings, I will open gates before Him, and cities will not be
closed to Him. Which itself we see fulfilled. For to whom does God the Father
hold the right hand, except to Christ, His Son? whom all nations have heard,
that is in whom all nations have believed, whose preachers also, the apostles,
are shown in the psalms of David: Into the whole earth, he says, their sound
has gone out, and even to the ends of the earth their words. For in whom else
have all the nations believed, except in Christ, who has already come? For in
whom have the nations believed, Parthians, Medes, Elamites, and those who
inhabit Mesopotamia, Armenia, Phrygia, Cappadocia, and those dwelling in
Pontus and Asia and Pamphylia, remaining in Egypt, and inhabiting the region
of Africa which is beyond Cyrene, Romans and sojourners, then also Jews in
Jerusalem, and the remaining nations, as already the varieties of the
Gaetulians, and many bounds of the Moors, all the limits of the Spains, and
the diverse nations of the Gauls, and the places of the Britons inaccessible
to the Romans, but subjected to Christ, and of the Sarmatians, and of the
Dacians, and of the Germans, and of the Scythians, and of many hidden nations
and provinces and of many islands unknown to us, and which we are less able to
enumerate? In all which places the name of Christ, who has already come,
reigns, as before whom the gates of all cities have been opened, and to whom
none are closed, before whom iron bars have been broken, and bronze doors
opened. Although these things also are to be understood spiritually, that the
hearts of individuals besieged in various ways by the devil have been unlocked
by the faith of Christ, yet they have been clearly fulfilled, as in all which
places a people of the name of Christ inhabits. For who could have reigned
over all nations, except Christ, the Son of God, who was being announced as
going to reign over all unto eternity? For if Solomon reigned, yet only within
the bounds of Judah; from Beersheba even to Dan the limits of his kingdom are
marked. But if Darius reigned over the Babylonians and Parthians, he did not
have power in all nations; if Pharaoh over the Egyptians, or whoever succeeded
him in the hereditary kingdom, there only he obtained the dominion of his
kingdom; if Nebuchadnezzar with his petty kings, he had the limits of his
kingdom from India even to Ethiopia; if Alexander the Macedonian, he held no
more than all Asia and the remaining regions, after he had conquered them; if
the Germans, they are still not allowed to cross their own limits; the Britons
are shut within the circuit of their ocean, the nations of the Moors and the
barbarism of the Gaetulians are besieged by the Romans, lest they exceed the
bounds of their regions. What shall I say of the Romans themselves, who
fortify their empire from the garrisons of their legions, and cannot stretch
the forces of their kingdom beyond those nations? But the name of Christ is
stretched everywhere, is believed everywhere, is worshipped by all the nations
enumerated above, reigns everywhere, is adored everywhere, is granted equally
to all everywhere; not a greater grace of a king with Him, not an inferior joy
of some barbarian, not distinguished merits of dignities or of anyone's birth;
equal to all, king to all, judge to all, God and Lord to all. Nor should you
doubt to believe what we assert, when you see it being done.

And so the times of the predicted and future nativity of Christ and of His
passion and of the extermination of the city of Jerusalem, that is of its
devastation, must be sought. For Daniel says that both the holy city and the
holy one are to be exterminated with the leader who is going to come, and the
pinnacle destroyed even to Interitum. And so the times of Christ the leader
who is going to come must be sought, which we will investigate in Daniel,
which having been computed we will prove that He has come even from the times
prescribed and from competent signs and from His operations. Which we prove

Let us therefore begin to prove that the nativity of Christ was announced by
the prophets. As Isaiah preaches: Hear, house of David, the contest with men
is not a small thing for you, because God furnishes the combat, on account of
this God Himself will give you a sign. Behold a virgin will conceive and will
bear a son, and you will call His name Emmanuel, which is interpreted God with

father or mother, He will receive the virtue of Damascus, and the spoils of
Samaria against the king of the Assyrians. And so the Jews say: Let us
challenge that preaching of Isaiah, and let us make a comparison, whether to
Christ who has already come there first belongs the name which Isaiah
preached, and His signs which he announced of Him. Indeed Isaiah preaches that
He ought to be called Emmanuel, then that He would take the virtue of Damascus
and the spoils of Samaria against the king of the Assyrians. Further, they
say, this one who has come has neither been called under such a name nor
performed a warlike business. But we on the contrary have thought they should
be admonished to recognize also the coherences of this chapter. For there has
been joined also the interpretation of Emmanuel, God with us, so that you may
look not only at the sound of the name, but also at the sense. For the Hebrew
sound, which is Emmanuel, has an interpretation, which is God with us. Ask
therefore whether that voice, God with us, which is Emmanuel, is from then
when Christ shone agitated in Christ. And I think you will not deny it. For
those who from Judaism believe in Christ, from the time they believe in Him,
when they wish to say Emmanuel, they signify that God is with us, and so it is
established that He who was being preached Emmanuel has already come, because
what Emmanuel signifies has come, that is God with us. Equally they are led by
the sound of the name, when they so take the virtue of Damascus and the spoils
of Samaria and the kingdom of the Assyrians, as if they portend Christ as a
warrior, not observing that the scripture sends in advance, Because before the
boy knows to call father or mother, He will receive the virtue of Damascus and
the spoils of Samaria against the king of the Assyrians. For it is first that
you inspect the demonstration of age, whether that age can already exhibit
Christ as a man, let alone as an emperor. Namely the infant would have been
about to call to arms with a wailing, and to give the signal of war not with a
trumpet but with a rattle, and not from a horse or from a wall, but from the
back or neck of his nurse and bearer to point out the enemy, and so to subdue
Damascus and Samaria instead of breasts. It is another thing if among you
infants break out into battle, I believe first anointed to the sun, then armed
with swaddling-bands and paid with butter, who would know how to lance before
they know how to tear. But if nature nowhere grants this, to serve as a
soldier before making a man, to take the virtue of Damascus before knowing a

also, they say, nature does not allow a virgin to bear, and yet it must be

Concerning the issue of His passion you plainly hesitate, denying that the
passion of the cross was preached in Christ, and arguing besides that it is
not to be believed that God exposed His Son to that kind of death which He
