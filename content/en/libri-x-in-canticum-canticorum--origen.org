90 Let him not betroth me through prophets, she says, but let him converse
through himself according to spiritual sense; (through which John says: "and
our hands have handled concerning the word of life". And she says these things
as =91.30= having heard the prophets saying: "the king desired your beauty",
and elsewhere: "as a bridegroom will rejoice over a bride, so the Lord will
rejoice over you".) 92 And as often as, seeking some divine doctrine, we grasp
it, let us consider ourselves "to have been kissed from the mouth" of the
bridegroom; but while we are at a loss, let us suppose that we say in prayer:
"let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth". 96 There being many wines in
Scripture, both better and worse, now the "breasts" of the bridegroom are set
beside the better; for they would not have been compared with the worse. The
bride therefore, having been gladdened by many and various wines and having
preparation to receive the breasts of the bridegroom that are better than
these, says this, preferring these to the wine "in the law and the prophets".
98 And see, how upon the evangelical way of life he spoke of a scent; upon the
legal worship he has set the spices themselves, showing of that the
superiority and the spiritual, but of this the gross. 101 Perhaps he
prophesies of the name of Christ the power that filled the world according to
his presence, so as to become according to Paul "to some a scent from death
unto death, and to others from life unto life". For if it were "unto life" for
all, the bride would have said: therefore all "have loved you", but not only
"the young women" "being renewed day by day", and "not having wrinkle or
spot". And it "was emptied", so as no longer to be kept silent, locked up in
secret things. 108 "The king brought me into his chamber". That is, she calls
"inner sanctuary" the worthy-to-be-loved soul or church or the governing
[faculty] of Christ, into which Paul having entered said: "but we have the
mind of Christ, that we may know the things granted to us by God". And what
are these? Things which "eye has not seen and 109 ear has not heard and have
not risen upon a man's heart; for these God has prepared for those who love
him". For in "the chamber" of the bridegroom "all the treasures of wisdom and
of knowledge are hidden". For she, as having gone before the young women, was
brought in and "stood as a queen at the right of the king in a gold-woven
garment" according to David; but concerning the young women: "virgins shall be
brought to the king after her" and what follows. 111 "We will love your
breasts above wine". That is, even if now through weakness "we love the wine"
above your "breasts", O bridegroom, when we have been improved, "we will love"
them "above" this. "Wine" then that gladdens "young women" is the legal
things, but the perfection of the bridegroom is "breasts". Then in apology for
not already loving she says: "uprightness has loved you". But we have not yet
made our own ways straight. 126 Me "having been darkened" by sin Christ
"looked upon" because of unbelief, whom the scriptures proclaim "sun of
righteousness". 128 And attend also to these things toward the place, that
"the sun" being white and bright seems to have the cause of "making black",
not from itself, but from the one being "blackened", as we have given. And so
perhaps also "the Lord hardens the heart of Pharaoh", the cause of this being
about him "afflicting the life of the Hebrews in the hard works, in the clay
and in the brickmaking and in all the works", not those in mountains and
hills, but "those in the plains"; for being someone material from his own
wickedness and living according to flesh in all things, being a friend to
"clay" he wishes also to make the Hebrews into clay, having the governing
[faculty] not clean of "clay", which as clay is hardened by the sun, 129 thus
by the rays of God overseeing Israel "he was hardened". And that such things
are in those according to the place and it does not lie before the servant to
write a bare history, will be clear to the


one perceiving that, when "the sons of Israel groaned", they neither "groaned
from the brickmaking" nor "from the clay" nor "from the chaff", but "from the
works"; "and their cry went up to God", not "from clay", but again "from the
works". Wherefore also "God heard their groanings"; not "hearing a groaning"
of those not "crying from works" toward him, but "from clay" and earthly
deeds. 130 "Sons of my mother fought in me; they set me as a keeper in
vineyards; my own vineyard I did not keep". That is, those teaching me,
disciples of Christ, wishing to cleanse me of the evil reasonings before these
and of the demons cooperating with these, "fought" with these "in me, being
sons of the same mother as I"; of "the free woman" I mean; they being taken up
into 131 "the remnant according to election of grace", but I having received
"the" covenant according to "the Jerusalem above" and "being born again from
promise". And they did not first "set" her "as a keeper", then "fight"; but
"having fought" first they made her fit to be set "in vineyards for keeping".
And this is Moses and the prophets, of whom =132.30= each "was a full field,
which the Lord blessed" and "a vineyard"; in which if someone comes to be and
does not keep his former habit as being "from Sodom", he uses the present word
of "vineyard". 141 And the much-talked-of saying among the Greeks has been
anticipated, having been handed down to the wise Solomon, this: "know
yourself". According to which the soul is now threatened by the lover and
bridegroom, if she does not 142 keep the beauty given to her "according to the
image of God", "shepherding" the inner casting-out and the being ranked last
"the left-hand kids". And "you will shepherd" them "upon the tents of the
shepherds", now of this one, now of that one, as some wanderer, until having
been trained by experience you turn back toward the knowledge of yourself. And
the word will also fit toward the church. 143 And someone knows himself
knowing whether he has a good or a base disposition and whether he falls far
short of the way toward virtue or "stretches forward to the things before,
forgetting the things behind"; and whether he has not yet received or has been
perfected or approaches the boundaries of beauty. And it is also a work 144 to
attend to each deed, how they have come to be, and to every reasoning and
word. And he calls "women" the souls that are not pure nor indifferent; 145
and neglecting her own knowledge she would become "tossed and carried about by
every wind of teaching in the sleight of men"; and everywhere rather she
shepherds her own "left-hand kids" than beside the "good shepherd" Word. (And
this word is gentle, even if not yet, already now "she is fair among women"
being numbered with the church of God and being fairer than the little ones in
it; for the so-called "women" of the church are incomparably better than the
gentile souls going after the lovers of the opposing powers, being called
"women" but not virgins. For how is she not better who has looked up toward
her own creator? And like is 146 "go out" "to being handed over to Satan for
destruction of the flesh" of one set apart from the church after, as he was
saying to the young women, "being brought in by the king into his chamber".)
154 But also Paul says "members" of Christ, of the "church" bride; of whom as
it were a face are those differing from the rest in beauty, thinking nothing
shameful, 155 but bearing to her as fruit the shame of temperance. Those who
perhaps formerly were not beautiful have so changed, that it is said to the
bride: "how have your cheeks been made beautiful;" "not having spot or
wrinkle". And perhaps also by knowing the one God only; for "the turtledove"
also comes together with one only. 161 And then the silver was not much nor
poured out, but few things were said in secret by prophets and the wise of
old. But when he sojourned, the things in the inaccessible 162 places of the
temple have been contemplated by the one seeing "the veil" the true one "being
torn from above to below" for the things within to be contemplated, when the
one "having lain down having slept as a lion and as a cub


is raised". -And after these things. For then "those who have become conformed
to his resurrection" will receive from him gold instead of "likenesses", they
will be multiplied with the gold and the silver. 165 For the "scent" of the
bridegroom, she says, "my nard", in which I anointed him, "has given". For 166
"having anointed the feet of Jesus with her own nard and having wiped with the
hair" she took this transformed from "the scent" of the bridegroom; perceiving
which as it came upon "the head" she says: "my nard has given me the scent of
the bridegroom". Wherefore as from the flesh of Jesus "the myrrh" of the
disciple "filled the whole house". 168 "A bundle of stacte is my kinsman to
me, he shall lodge in the midst of my breasts". Having said that "the nard" of
the bridegroom "has given me the scent" now I say "my kinsman" breathes "of
stacte", not being poured out nor breathing outside, but bound up and giving
the fragrance more densely. And the church from the nations, having had the
first people as a brother, since from him "Christ was according to the flesh",
has called the bridegroom "son of a brother" 169. And Aquila having rendered
"father's brother" one must say that the father of the church from the nations
is the people of old, since from the law among them and the prophets the birth
in God and the leading-forth from us into piety came to be. This then having
been so rendered, "brother of the father" of the bride is the savior,
similarly to those "having come under law". 174 Now the bride seems first to
have looked more clearly upon the beauty of the bridegroom and through her
"dove" "eyes" to perceive the superiority in the beauty 175 of the Word. And
perhaps she riddles as a "bed" common to herself and the bridegroom the body,
in which while still being the soul is deemed worthy of the communion of the
Word. At any rate Paul says: "your bodies are members of Christ". For through
the "your" he says the body is of the bride, and through the "members of
Christ" of the bridegroom. And she calls the bridegroom "shady" because of the
density of the contemplations in the Word and in wisdom. (And if the common
bride of both is flourishing, it is no wonder, a divine power leading, that
also upon the body every deed through the body is shown to be good.) 178 "I am
a flower of the field, a lily of the valleys". He calls "flower" that which is
on the way toward becoming fruit. Therefore in the earthly place called
"field" the bridegroom Word is a "flower", as toward the things to come; "for
when the perfect comes", it will change the "flower" into becoming fruit. And
since those on earth do not have room for more, of which he speaks as
"flower", for this reason the bridegroom has become as "a flower of the
field". For "he emptied himself taking the form of a slave" toward our being
able "to behold" his "glory" after these things 179. And perhaps to the more
well-born and level ones named "fields" he is a "flower", but to those lower
and more hollow than these a "lily". Having said these things the bridegroom
compares "the one near" him to other "daughters", the remaining souls, who are
"thorns" when compared to her. And such are the souls not "near" the
bridegroom, but "the one near" is a "lily in the midst" shining among them).
"As an apple among the trees of the wood, so is my kinsman in the midst of the
sons; under his shadow I desired and I sat, and his fruit is sweet in my
throat." It was fitting for the bride, having become wholly of the beauty of
the bridegroom 180, to say nothing about herself, but toward the things the
bridegroom said about himself, to compare him to an "apple", and the "sons"
beside him to "trees of a wood"; and by excess of longing even to desire his
"shadow" and to send on to the depth his quality. And she seems to say these
things toward "the young women" as the bridegroom the first things toward "the
companions". And she calls "sons" either "the companions", whom by comparison
of the bridegroom she likened to "unfruitful trees", or those alien to him.
181 And "under his shadow she desired" both to sit "and has sat". Jeremiah
also says in Lamentations: 182 "the breath of our face, Christ the Lord, was
taken in our corruptions, of whom we said: in his shadow


we shall live among the nations". For how was "his shadow" not going to become
a cause of life for us who have become free "of the shadow of the law" ("for
the law had a shadow of the good things to come"), being no longer "under law,
but under grace"? even if now we are "under a shadow" differing much; for
after the 183 present life no longer "through a mirror" and "in a riddle", but
"face to face" we shall "contemplate" the truth. And the bride also will say:
"until the day shine through and the shadows be moved". For "our life" is as a
"shadow" and "in the shadow of death" those from the nations "were sitting"
and those still unbelieving "sit". 184 "Bring me into the house of wine".
These things she says toward the friends of the bridegroom, the holy angels or
Apostles and prophets, all but saying: mix me with the body of Christ. 191
"Support me with myrrhs, pile me with apples, for I am wounded with love".
Symmachus has rendered thus: "lean me back upon vine-blossom", that is, upon
fragrant 192 trees that make a good fruit flowering, the base so-called trees
being unfruitful or ill-fruited. And some of the copies have: "support me with
un-myrrhed ones". Which must be understood either as faithful, yet not so as
to be styled "members" "of her who has no spot or wrinkle", or as aliens 193
to the faith of Christ. And the: "pile me with apples" Symmachus making clear
said: "roll apples around me". For the bride wishes to rest among many "apples
being rolled around" her, 194 which, I think, are fruit "of the apple among
the trees of the wood", of the bridegroom, that she may partake of their
quality. "For I have been wounded with a love-charm" she says according to
Symmachus, from "the chosen arrow" according to Isaiah. 199 "The voice of my
kinsman". This some have assigned to the preceding, but the Hebrew sets it
from its own section. And it is clear that the bride conversing "with the
daughters of Jerusalem" suddenly perceives "the voice" of the bridegroom
conversing with some, as is likely; of which she perceiving, "upon the
mountains" and "the hills" lying beside the place of the bride he leaps so as
to be in nothing out of tune with "a fawn". Then by eagerness concerning the
bride he came near the house and stood "behind" it. Then "having leaped" 200
he reaches as far as the "windows" of the house, as if amorously wishing "to
peek" into her. And near the house, where the bride is, many "nets" have been
spread for an ambush of the bride and those about her, which "having cut
through" the bridegroom as stronger "looked through" them and calls the bride
"to come near", encouraging by deed to despise "the nets" as already split and
the hard time "having run past" through them, which he calls "winter" bringing
unprofitable and violent "rain". And he also exhorts through the good things
of the present time, calling "flowers" as in spring and tending of "vines".
And he describes "the voice of a turtledove" and "unripe figs of a fig-tree"
and "vines putting forth" 201 and the place where he will rest together, where
having come to be he wished her to appear with her whole "face" to the
bridegroom and "to grant her to hear the sweet voice". Or also he calls
"mountains" the prophets complete in understanding, contemplators of the true
things. And I think also the Apostles, since according to generation wisdom
passes into souls. And "mountains", since when the sun rises they are first
shone upon, but "hills" those who have received less the manifestation of the
spirit. And you might also call "mountains" the thoughts in the law, and
"hills" that of the remaining prophets. Or also the one who has contemplated
the Word in the new covenant sees him "leaping upon the mountains", and
"bounding upon the hills" him in the old letter. And the bridegroom is likened
to "a fawn of deer", not only as consuming the serpents, but also because 202
"a child was born to us a son and was given to us" and because "he humbled
himself"; for otherwise he would have been a perfect "deer". =220.30= "Arise",
he says, from the sensible things toward the intelligible, that she may
understand through the things by which you contemplate them. 221 "For the
winter has passed, the rain has gone away". We would say


"rain" to have become the time before the sojourning "of God having commanded
the clouds to rain" the legal and prophetic word, and that this has ceased,
since "the law and the prophets until John"; but spring and summer after the
sojourning, when there is no longer need of "rains", when "the flowers"
through Christ "were seen in the earth". And from his sojourning "the
fig-tree" is not cut down, having become unfruitful in the former time; for
now "it has put forth unripe figs" and "the vines put forth". Wherefore one of
the branches says: "we are a fragrance of Christ to God". 224 "A time of
cutting" is that of the putting-away of the superfluous. "The voice of the
turtledove". Or also he says "of a turtledove" "of the unspeakable and unknown
wisdom"; for the animal is fond of solitude. Whose "voice is heard" by those
still wearing an earthly body. 226 and this (sc. time) is that of his
presence, in which the bodily things of the law and of the history in the
prophets that have grown around the spiritual must be circumcised and the
better things remain. And it is again a "time" also of the cutting-out and
forgiveness of sins "through a washing of regeneration". 230 He wishes the
soul to go beyond the sensible things; and as a wall of a city and "outwork"
he calls the sensible world. Therefore the soul that will have communion with
the Word must 231 "in the covert of the rock" come to be not only outside the
wall of the city, but also "of the outwork"; so that having come near she may
"with unveiled face behold as in a mirror the glory of the Lord" being
persuaded by the one saying: "show me your face". And he desires also to hear
"the voice" of her conversing 232, wondering at it as "sweet". And an
"outwork" 233 is what Isaiah names "surrounding-wall" saying: "he will set a
wall and a surrounding-wall". He wishes then her having gone out of the bodily
things not only to come to be in the "wall" - which I think is the word about
the world -, but also "next to the outwork", which is a last word of the
bodily things, but a beginning of the incorporeal. 235 These things the
bridegroom [says] to the friends, either angels or holy men, as many as are
teachers of the church. And the exhortation is for the salvation "of the
vineyards", that when the knavish powers are caught, who corrupt the beginning
of the sprouting of the fruit, "the vines" may be able to advance from
"putting forth" as far as the perfection of the fruit, being farmed by God
236, but through free will being able to bear fruit 237 and not. Or perhaps
also some men are "foxes", through heterodox sharpness hindering those who
have begun to run well; 240 whom while still "small" he wishes "to be caught",
before they advance unto more impiety. For having become a great "fox" he is
uncatchable by those, but is hunted by the bridegroom alone; for beginning to
work the worse 241 things they are easily caught even by the friends. And he
might also say "small vineyards"; for "foxes" cannot against the great ones.
