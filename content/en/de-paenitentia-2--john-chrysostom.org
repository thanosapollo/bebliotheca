On repentance, and on Herod and John the Baptist.

αʹ. Come then, let us prefer your own profit even to your applause, and,
having briefly reminded the Church concerning the moral way of life, let us
discourse among you from those things from which least applause is sent up to
the speaker, but most desire of the soul's salvation is born in the hearers.
For the word concerning the faith is good too, and fitting for God-loving
souls; and whenever we see your zeal awakened in the hearings concerning the
faith, we learn exactly the Master-loving quality of your soul. For a wicked
servant accuses the master, but a well-disposed servant rejoices when the
master is praised by all; therefore eagerness for the praises of the master
is a proof of a servant's goodwill. And it is good also to scour the vessel
of the soul, so that, having been first cleansed through moral teaching, it
may be found receptive of the myrrh of theology. For into an unclean soul, it
says, instruction shall not enter. And the word concerning the way of life is
for us in great need of exact examination. For if I hold out hopes of
repentance, I fear lest someone, made slack by hope of the coming repentance,
grow careless of the way of life; and if again I shut off the hopes of
repentance, I fear lest someone, having grown numb, give over the body to the
desires. For equally a prompting toward luxury is both carelessness, when the
soul is made slack by pleasures, and despair of repentance. Therefore let our
word be divided in two, toward those who are healthy, and those who are sick.
Let him who thinks he stands take heed lest he fall; let him who has already
slipped forestall the fall by repentance; let him who has even been in danger
of falling down not be dashed down; and if anyone, having been forestalled,
has even been dashed down, let him not despair of the loving-kindness of him
who sets upright those who have been dashed down. It is possible sometimes to
be shaken a little; My feet were almost shaken. It is possible also for one
who has been shaken to be helped by the Master; If I said, My foot has been
shaken, your mercy, O Lord, helped me. It is possible not only to be shaken,
but even to be thrust and to be in danger before being brought down; but God
does not overlook this one either. I was thrust, I was overturned that I might
fall, and the Lord took hold of me. Has then the one who fell perished
utterly? By no means. There is comfort for this one too. When he falls, it
says, he shall not be dashed down. What is the cause? Because the Lord
supports his hand. For it is possible also to fall gently, and not to be
dashed down more violently into the pleasure. Have then those who have been
dashed down perished? Let us see here too the Master's loving-kindness. The
Lord supports all who are falling, and sets upright all who have been dashed
down. These things, it seems to me, the psalmody also, showing well, has not
led us to despair of repentance. For speaking concerning God, it says: God is
a righteous judge. Do not despise the word; for righteousness is
distributive equally according to desert. First hear that he is righteous, and
prepare yourself beforehand for a defense, then, since it often stands against
a suspicion: If then God is righteous, how does he not at once repay those who
sin? This man is unjust, and he prospers; this man is violent, and he is rich;
this man is wanton, and he has power; how are these things of the
righteousness of God? if he is righteous, how does he not immediately bring
the punishment upon those who sin? is he perhaps righteous, but not able at
once to give back the punishment to those who sin? For sometimes a judge
judges justly, but is weak to bring his own sentence into effect. Is it then
perhaps through

weakness that the delay of the punishment? God forbid. For what does he
immediately add? God is a righteous judge and strong. Therefore as righteous
he knows what is just, and as strong he is able to bring what has been thought
into effect. What then is the cause of the delay of the sins? what is the
delay of the punishment? He is righteous, he knows the one who sins; he is
strong, he is able to bring the vote to its end. What then is the cause of the
delay? God is a righteous judge, and strong, and long-suffering. And it
interprets what Long-suffering is: Not bringing wrath every day? There is a
small hyperbaton according to the wording; Not every day bringing wrath? And
that you may know that the delay of the punishment awaits your repentance, If
you do not turn, it says, he will furbish his sword. If you do not turn.
Therefore if we turn, we do not at once receive experience of the cutting. And
do you wish to see the excess of the loving-kindness? He will furbish his
sword. He has not yet said that He will use the sword against the mouth, but,
He will furbish; so that, having seen the gleam of the sword, you may
forestall the cutting of the sword by repentance. By the furbishing he
frightens, and he does not cut. Let the fear constrain, let your repentance
outrun the cutting. And that you may know through all things the purpose of
the judge, He has stretched his bow, and made it ready. And in it he has
prepared instruments of death. Is the figure not enough to frighten? has he
not sketched the bodiless one by the word? He has stretched his bow, and made
it ready. And in it he has prepared instruments of death; he has wrought his
arrows for those who are being burned. Here, as some painter, he has brought
the judge before the eyes. Think for me of the bow stretched, the hands made
ready; the one thrusting the bow, the other drawing the string; the arrow held
by the judge with the tips of the fingers. The bow is stretched, the arrow
ready, the judge prepared. Do not despise the bow; for it is stretched; nor
despair of repentance; for the arrow has not yet been shot. But do not be made
slack through the excess of the long-suffering, nor wish always to keep
yourself in the pleasures. Do not say, For now I will enjoy the desire, for
now I will use my youth, a time of repentance will come after a little,
tomorrow I will repent, around old age I will make my life secure. Vain are
such reasonings, rotten are the counsels of your desire, =59.759= you have
used a wicked counselor, the pleasure. You say, I will do the sin today, and
tomorrow I will repent. Are you lord of tomorrow? who is your surety for
tomorrow? If you hear accurately, not even today is wholly yours; and of today
the one part has passed, the other is about to be; the one is not yours, the
other is not yet yours. You say: Today I will do the pleasure, and I will
repent tomorrow. What you have, to the pleasure, what is another's, to
repentance? Use me rather as counselor: today to repentance, and to the sin
not even tomorrow. For this reason the divine word well, calling us back
variously to a recalling from sin, says, Today if you hear his voice, do not
harden your hearts as in the provocation; and, Exhort one another, while it is
called today. For this too the devil often does with evil art. Whenever he
sees a soul beginning to perceive the sins, beginning to understand the end of
the punishment, he casts in such thoughts: I am younger, I have a time of
repentance, for now today let me keep the need, for now let me get the better
of the suit; let me avenge this man, let me accomplish the pleasure of this
desire, and I will be in time to repent. Tomorrow comes again, again he holds
you down with the same demonic reasonings; and again he shows the future, and
drags down the present; and thus every day, holding today in the desires, and
pointing out tomorrow in the hopes of repentance, he spends out your life as
you sin day by day.


βʹ. But you, campaign against the pleasure; hear the Lord crying: Be well
disposed today toward your adversary, while you are with him on the way. It is
good then also once for all even toward those who love busyness, that they not
even exercise lawsuits more contentiously. For it is better even in lawsuits
to forestall by friendly settlements the uncertain end of the suit, than,
leaning on hopes of victory, to trust oneself to an uncertain sea. You read
your documents, you are confident in the outcomes; the advocate, having
hollowed his hand with the take, guarantees the victory, so that he may be
bound tight by hired service. And sometimes even a judge already corrupted
beforehand promises you the end of the suit. You are confident in unstable
hopes. For if you, having forestalled, have already corrupted the one judging,
having opened a door to others' takes, consider that the opponent too is
practicing the same things; and often, as you sail the affairs of the suit in
a calm, they quietly persuade you to slacken the sail about the voyage. Have
you never seen a sea having a calm toward the shore, and being tossed in the
deep? How many, having put out with fair weather and calm into the midst of
the sea, having met sudden storms and waves, have been shipwrecked? Such are
the ends of the suit; sometimes the preludes with the hopes, and the end
carrying off an outcome contrary to the hope. It is good then, as I said, even
to forestall the money lawsuits with friendly settlements, so that toward the
end of the suit you may steer the friend; but as for criminal ones, may it not
even be that they be settled, nor even that they be exercised at the start;
yet even there sparing of the anger is better. Still it does not seem to me
that the word has its cause only concerning money. Be, it says, well disposed
toward your adversary quickly, while you are on the way with him. This
addition gives me something even greater to think. For if the word were only
concerning money suits, or were speaking of the charges of things without
feeling, why did he add, While you are on the way with him? for what adversary
accepts to travel with an adversary? who shares the journey without sharing
the mind? does he not fear both theft of writings, and corruption of
witnesses, and the plots along the highways? Therefore we ought to find an
adversary who always travels with us, whose companionship even when we guard
ourselves very much we cannot flee, and to think of this adversary, toward
whom it is fitting to behave with goodwill. Who then is an exact adversary? If
you examine accurately, the body of the soul; this is an adversary of the
spirit in you. And that you may not think the word is mine, let Paul confirm
what has been said: The flesh desires against the spirit, and the spirit
against the flesh; and these are opposed to one another. And I am not
introducing a word of Manichaeans, nor am I speaking of another creator of the
body; =59.760= but the flesh desires against the spirit, using pleasure as
counselor; and the spirit against the flesh, leaning on the hopes that are to
come. Desire of the world and desire of the kingdom of the heavens are opposed
to one another; this is the adversary who travels with us both willing and
unwilling; for the way is the life. The soul hastens to imitate its own image,
the flesh flows down toward the dust. And these are opposed to one another.
Toward this adversary of yours be well disposed. And when? While you are with
him on the way, while you are in this life. For if the way is finished, there
is no longer a time of repentance, Because there is not in death the one who
remembers you, and in Hades who will confess to you? Toward this adversary of
yours then be well disposed. He did not say, Gratifying, but, Being well
disposed. Do not do what is pleasant to your adversary, but as one well
disposed call him back from the pleasure to the repentance. He does not
command you to use the body as a cook, doing all the things from pleasure; but
as a physician. For a physician often both mixes a bitter thing, and cuts, and
burns, and while grieving further takes a wage. For even if he grieves the
sense a little, yet there is a purpose


of health for the one being treated. Never let the adversary hand you over to
the judge; to the coming judge; The adversary; to whom if now you grant favor,
then the judge is found handing you over to the attendant, that is to the
punishing powers, and you will be cast into prison into the outer darkness.
Fearful is the place; You shall not come out from there, until you pay the
last quadrans. If before death you dissolve the debt, it has been loosed; if
you go away owing, even the remainder of the smallest debts will be exacted of
you. What is, The last quadrans? That the thought of a man will confess to
you, and the remainder of a thought will keep feast to you. Knowing these
things, brethren, set the pleasures side by side with the coming day of
judgment, and the vote of the one judging; and then you will understand how
bitter pleasure is, when you know of how great bitterness it becomes the
sponsor. Do you wish that we briefly admonish the Church from two banquets
written in the Gospels, what the end of those who love pleasure is, and what
the fruit of those who repent? And do not marvel if Scripture writes also
often even the banquets of those who love pleasure; for it writes all things,
what you may guard against, what you may imitate. It writes the things of
those who love pleasure, that it may continuously pillory them, and that you
may flee the imitation; it writes the things of those who love God, that you
may run toward the image of repentance, and, having set side by side the lover
of pleasure and the lover of God, you may flee the one, and emulate the other.
And at the same time let it also be a school for those who come together, not
to feast your eyes too much on the public spectacles, nor to run readily to
the hippodrome, nor to occupy the theater from the nights; for hope of
unseemly spectacles is mad. For they also forestall the day by the speed of
their eagerness, occupying in night the place of darkness, so that they may
delight the pupils with unseemly sights, or may watch the defenses of beasts
and the beast-fighters. And often men sit as spectators, turning their
goodwill toward the beasts rather than toward those of the same race. And you
do not call wretched the miserable one who, that he may fill the belly,
deserts to the belly of beasts; and that he may fill the disease of gluttony,
is gladly buried in the bellies of the beasts. That one has an unbridled
belly; do not take an unbridled eye. Another, a jester, comes in, to fashion
members to his own shame, having hired out the head to laughter, being ashamed
if he is not slapped publicly, and preparing the cheeks beforehand for the
knocks of the doors, and taking away the hairs with a razor, so that not even
a hair may mediate in the insults. Another, being made effeminate with women's
dances, and imitating the things against nature, and grieving that he was not
born a woman from his parents, and setting the change of nature as a point of
honor; and doing all these things for the sake of belly and gain, and taking
honor in the shameful things; concerning whom it is good to speak the voice of
Paul: Whose god is the belly, and their glory is in their shame. Do not run
together toward these things, do not turn feet swift toward such steps; but do
not even bring the unseemly pleasures into the banquets. Sometimes you say: I
decline the theater, I do not go up the steps; there is a banquet and a time
of merriment; and =59.761= there you accomplish the unseemly sights of the
theaters. What then do you gain by abhorring the theater? For do we abhor the
stones? for have the stones of the theaters been condemned? The shamefulness
of the things done, the wickedness of the unseemly spectacles, which through
the senses of the eyes and of the hearing, as through certain windows, brings
in the robber. Whenever then you decline the theater, and beside your own
table you bring in the shames of the spectacles, you have unnoticed made your
own house a theater. For I do not hate the place of the theater, but I fear
the manner of those who come together.

γʹ. And that you may know that even beside banquets unseemly spectacles often
and harmful


being brought in upon the souls become causes of condemnation, I will remind
you of a royal banquet, or rather a tyrannical one, the one written in the
book of the Gospels. It was a royal dinner, or rather, if one must speak the
truth, a tyrannical one, the birthday of Herod, of the polluted fox, of the
bloodstained beast, of the treacherous one; whose manner the Lord too,
knowing, did not say, Tell that lion, although this beast seemed royal, but,
Tell that fox. For even if the authority is of a lion, yet the manner is of a
fox. And as the banquet was being assembled, and they had gone forward to
drinking, a dancing-girl is present at the banquet, a shameful and unapproved
spectacle, pitiable even if she had come in at the theater and on the stage,
much less in a royal banquet. Do you wish to know the shameful spectacle of
the banquet? The dancing-girl is from the kings, daughter of Herodias; the
race is of a king, and the manner of a dancing-girl. And this is Herodias who
did not even remain within the bounds of nature; who was joined to Philip, and
was living with Herod. The thing said is a riddle; for the end of wantonness
leads even into a search of riddles. For Herod, having thrust aside the memory
toward the brother, and having become wholly a slave of wantonness, was king
of the Jews, and was still more a slave of the pleasures. Perhaps such as
those who are ruled, such also are the masters. When then the cohabitation of
Herod was taking place, John the great, the one more than a prophet, with
boldness was convicting the wantonness; for truly even more than a prophet.
For every prophet, having come into the habit of age, then was counted worthy
of the prophetic grace; and some even many years later. But John alone, still
having six months of the conception in the womb, and being nourished according
to the laws of nature, leaped beforehand at the God in the womb, through the
greeting of the mother, incomplete in the members, perfect in the faith. And
perhaps the infant leaps in gladness, pressing, if possible, to forestall the
appointed time of nature. He saw the Master's mother present; he is straitened
by the prison of the womb, he leaps in gladness; and since he cannot yet
speak, not yet even having the tongue able to utter, nor the mouth serving the
eagerness, having borrowed a tongue from the mother he prophesies, and
Elizabeth utters; but John from the womb dictates the words. Is he not then
justly even more than a prophet? There is also another word, according to
which he is even more than a prophet. The prophets proclaimed the future, and
they desired to see what they foresaw with the soul. For this reason they have
cried: Show us, O Lord, your mercy, and give us your salvation. For this
reason concerning them the Lord said: Many prophets and righteous men desired
to see what you see, and to hear what you hear; and they did not see, nor
hear. The prophets then proclaim the future beforehand; but John even points
out the one prophesied. He proclaims the future from the womb, and after these
things he says: Whose sandal-strap I am not worthy to loose, he stands in the
midst of you. How does he point him out? Behold the Lamb of God, who takes
away the sin of the world. This one then, a prophet, and more than a prophet,
did not bear the unseemly cohabitation, but for the sake of chastity he speaks
with boldness. Let the good narrative admonish each, that even if there are
not open persecutions, there is always a time of martyrdom. For did someone
set before John an altar and frankincense? for sacrifices and libations? for
did he say, Deny God? for did he say, Sacrifice to the idols? But speaking
with a free mouth for the sake of chastity, he is found a martyr of chastity.
For that you may know how much =59.762= prophetic boldness can do, he says to
the king: It is not lawful for you to have your brother's wife. Do you see how
according to nature virtue has the ruling of wickedness? Herod a king, having


spear-bearers, clothed with wealth, exercising royal power; John poor,
hearthless, cityless, without property, without food, eating so much as nature
compels. The one clothed from camel's hair legislates for the one clothed with
the purple; the one going in the deserts, for the ruler of cities of many men;
the one eating locusts from plants and wild honey, with authority commands the
one setting out the bright and royal table: It is not lawful for you. And yet
it seemed that all things were lawful for a king. But All things are lawful,
but not all things are expedient. And upon these things he ought to have given
thanks, he ought to have done good, or to worship the teacher for the sake of
chastity; Herod was provoked, and Herodias was provoked still more, for they
are neighbors of the names, and of the manner still more than yoke-fellows;
and the two shall be, not into one flesh, but into one incontinence; and for
these things they cast the prophet into a prison, that they may show their own
lack of sense. Reprove a wise man, and he will love you; but a senseless one,
and he will add to hating you. He is cast into the prison, and he does not
change the boldness. Loose, he says, the members; only loose the unjust
cohabitation. And while he was enduring in the prison, that bitter banquet
comes to be, the tyrannical and cruel one; whose end of merriment is a
prophet's murder. Herodias, daughter of Herodias, comes in, a child of the
flesh, a child of the soul; she comes in to a wine-soaked banquet, the queen
dances publicly, she who ought not to have peeked out of the women's quarters,
she who ought not to have been bold before men's sights. But perhaps, just as
John a little before borrowed a tongue from the mother, so the girl too
borrowed the eyes of shamelessness from the mother. She comes in to the
banquet. This was not enough to have the excess of the shame; but also how
does she come in? as a queen? solemnly? with eunuchs, with maidservants, with
the rest of the adornment, and was she adorned with solemnity? By no
means; but she comes in dancing. Worse than a bad entrance is the subject. And
she danced
in the midst of the banquet, in the eyes of the wanton, when the wine too was
stirring the passion still more. And do you wish to know the worse? She does
not only dance, but she also dances well. An addition of the shame, even to
have practiced well the evil things. For she seems not to have accomplished
the shame as a side-work. She pleases Herod and those reclining with him. And
the fox is not covered. He sees the daughter of the brother and of the one
living with him badly, dancing in the midst of men, and he is not covered, but
is even pleased, and she pleases both him and those reclining with him. With
the holy
you will be hallowed, and with the crooked you will twist. I wished him to be
discipled a little to Paul: Dancing is wholly heard among you, and such
dancing as not even among the nations, that a king's daughter should dance.
And you are puffed up, and you have not rather mourned. But she also pleases
badly, and she receives a wage of the achievement worse, so that Herod swore:
Ask what you wish, and I will give you, up to half of my kingdom. Do you see
the end of those who love pleasure? do you see the shame of those who love
spectacles? Forestalling you swear: Whatever you ask, I will give you; and you
make a little dancing-girl mistress of the asking, and you pledge the vote
beforehand by an addition of an oath. Do you see how love of pleasure drags
the dignity of kings down to earth? do you see where he flung the kingdom?
"Whatever you ask, I will give you, and up to the half of the kingdom." Do you
judge the royal dignity at so much? Have you been so corrupted beforehand in
the eyes? The girl danced once, and you have given away the half; if she
dances a second time, the king is a private man. Where is the dignity of those
who love pleasure? where the purple of the wanton? You have flung the half of
the kingdom under the feet of the dancing-girl; pray that she not dance again,
or that she not, having danced again, succeed; and if not,


there remains for you to beg. But for her wanton =59.763= is the proposal of
the wage; and what of the dancing-girl's asking? She did not improvise the
gift, she did not ask the wage from improvisation; but having been put forward
by the mother, she goes away to use as counselor of the wage the wicked
teacher. And what wage did she ask? Give me here at once on a platter the head
of John the Baptist. Shall I not weep the excess of the shame, the shameful
thing of the achievement, the king's promise, the asking of the wage? do you
not shudder henceforth at the ends of those who dance? do you not abhor the
reasonings of the applauses? do you not spit out the twistings of the
half-naked? Do you dare to have a dancer as a friend? do you dare to set
yourself as a lover of a dancer? Do you not hate the thing, which when
neglected incurs laughter even among the spectators? It incurs laughter, but
when accomplished to the extreme, it murders a prophet. Let us hate dancing, a
discovery of demons and a practice of the ill-fated. Upon these things Herod
was grieved; and he was grieved extremely, as one might say, and he imitates
Pilate, or rather the manner of Pilate; for the narrative is earlier. That one
washes the hands, and hands over to the punishing vote; this one too is
grieved, and because of the oaths and those reclining with him he nodded. Was
not the Lord saying well today, Do not swear at all? You have seen a Master's
exhortation: Do not swear at all. For why do you swear, either that by
swearing falsely you may trap yourself, or by swearing truly you may murder a
prophet? He swore badly, he fulfilled the oath worse; still, Because of the
oaths, it says, and those reclining with him. You respected those reclining
with you, and you did not fear the Judge? And he commanded, it says, And
immediately the head of John the Baptist was brought on a platter, and was
given to a girl. See of the bitter banquet an end still more bitter; the
prophet's head is cut, it is given to the girl, and the girl receives it; she
does not shudder at the spectacle, she was not struck by the head, she did not
turn away from the drops of the bloods; truly of a savage lioness a cub more
savage. On a platter is set a prophet's head, on which the foods are brought
forward; and the same vessel of foods and of murder, and at the same hour
still of the gore dripping, the head still palpitating, perhaps even the head
having been brought into the banquet; still as the tongue was being shaken it
was crying: It is not lawful for you to have her. And having taken it she
brought it to her mother. The dancing-teacher received the impious achievement
of the wicked teaching.

δʹ. This narrative then we have necessarily searched out, that we may scour
the eyes of those who love spectacles, and persuade you to turn away from such
spectacles. But let us not dissolve the word in a gloomy narrative, nor let a
dancing-girl put down an ecclesiastical word, but let us set against a wanton
and tyrannical and bitter banquet a Master's and loving banquet, that we may
understand how great the fruit of repentance is. One of the Pharisees asked
the Lord to come in and eat bread. The Master came in, and reclined. For even
if he knew the minds of those calling, yet even being long-suffering he bore
it. For that the one who called was a hypocrite, the things that follow show;
While the Lord was reclining and the banquet was still going on, There was a
woman, it says, in the city a sinner. Having heard that Jesus is reclining
there, she comes, it says, into the house; and having brought an alabaster
flask of myrrh, she poured it upon the Master's feet, and was kissing the
tracks, and was wiping with her hairs. And that you may know that the one who
called was a hypocrite, the Pharisee reasons in himself: This man, if he were
a prophet, would have known who and what sort the woman is who is touching
him; that she is a sinner. For the Pharisees are not only hypocrites, but also
envious. Christ reclines at your house, and this while knowing your manner,
and no one begrudges; there came


a sinful woman to be saved, and you envy, O Pharisee? Perhaps he would have
been ... his kinsman, or rather a forefather of the doctrines. In order then
that you may know that he is not a prophet, but the prophet (for the addition
of the article has something), what great thing was it, if he knew that the
woman was a sinner? This even through report it was easy to learn; this even
you the Pharisee, not being a prophet, knew. He shows then the greater thing
than knowing. For her life perhaps even report was announcing, and especially
as she was exceedingly a sinner; but the Lord, that he might show the divine
power, read the reasoning of the Pharisee. For neither does =59.764= the
narrative have it that the Pharisee said to one of those reclining, but he was
saying in himself, and was reasoning in himself. In order then that you may
know that he is not only a prophet, but also the prophet: you move the thought
within, and the Master reads; you turn the notions, and the Lord finds, and
heals; and thus he brought forward the narratives of the two debtors, to the
one that he forgave five hundred, to the other fifty, saying Who will love
more? When the Pharisee answered, The one to whom he granted the more, he
compares then the Pharisee, and that woman who received forgiveness of many
sins; whom I do not praise for the former life, but I bless for the second
end. Let no one reproach her with Pharisees; She was a harlot and wanton. Let
Paul interpret, let Paul advocate for the woman; It is God who justifies. Who
is the one who condemns? And come, let us examine the narrative more finely,
so that we may not only bless that woman, but also imitate; She comes to Jesus
prepared, bringing myrrh in an alabaster flask, and a tear in the eyes; the
lifeless vessel of the myrrh, the living spring of the repentance; from the
