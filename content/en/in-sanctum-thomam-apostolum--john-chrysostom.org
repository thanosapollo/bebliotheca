On Saint Thomas the Apostle, and against the Arians, and on the one who
tyrannized in Thrace and was slain, himself also an Arian.
=59.497=
Obeying the law of the Church I have taken hold of the pulpit, as far as I
can; but overpowered by the greatness of the subject I grow dizzy, and I am
hemmed in from every side: how I shall meet the matter, what way out I shall
find for the speech. Shall I proclaim Thomas as living? But the tomb proclaims
his death. Shall I tell of him as dead? But the facts refute me. He is both
dead and immortal; he has died as a man, and he runs through the world as an
angel; he received suffering, and he wars against sufferings =59.498=; he lies
below, and he rejoices above. For nothing was strong enough to hide him; no
place made him vanish; he has illumined the whole inhabited world. He received
a tomb, yet from every side he rises like the sun; the relics of the righteous
man have conquered the earth, and he has appeared broader than all creation;
grace has sown him through the whole world. Every corner has a share of
Thomas; he has filled the whole world =59.499=, and he remains entire in his
place; his glory has gone out into all the earth, and his trophies to the ends
of the inhabited world. How then shall I name him? A sun? But night does not
refute him. A star? But day does not hide him.
At every season he lights up creation; he drives every gloom from the
inhabited world. Night is no longer with him; darkness does not dull him; a
river does not hinder him; the sea cannot hold him; ocean knows the man;
barbarians honor Thomas. All the nations keep festival today, and they offer
his voice, as a gift, to the Master: My Lord and my God; my kinsman and my
Maker, my Redeemer and my King. For these things Thomas taught; this
inheritance he left us as to children. Nature holds the word as a treasure;
the whole race is made strong through this voice; the saying has been
stretched even to the angels; angels hold this confession as an anchor; all
creation wears the dogma as a diadem. Arius alone is robbed of the word; he
alone falls from the inheritance; he alone does not confess Christ as Lord
with Thomas; he does not acknowledge him as God with him. He calls the Master
a slave, the radiance a creature, the genuine Son a bastard, the Fashioner a
fashioned thing. He does not name the Master Lord and God; he does not endure
the truth; he does not agree with Thomas; he does not follow the righteous
man; and this while he honors his day, keeps festival with the whole inhabited
world, and ranks himself among the friends of Thomas. But wickedness does not
steal virtue; the deceiver does not fit with the guide of truth. The shepherd
knows the beast; the slinger knows the wolf; the helmsman knows the wave; the
harbor knows the storm; the general knows the enemy; Thomas knows Arius, and
knowing him he drives him away. He does not receive the honor of the
blasphemer; he does not enter beside the cave of the murderers; he does not
lodge with them. He turns from an assembly of those who reject; he abhors a
voice that wars against him; he hates a mouth that speaks injustice against
God; he turns aside from a tongue that does not call Christ Lord and God; and
he cries to that man from the whole inhabited world: Arius, why do you drag me
down? Why do you anoint my head with oil? Why do you keep my day, you
lawbreaker? I do not receive your blasphemous hymn; I do not agree with your
vain faith; I do not acknowledge your lawless dogma.
Do not honor me while you dishonor the Fashioner; I do not receive honor that
is the mother of dishonor. If you insult the Master, how will you serve me,
the slave? If you do not hold my faith, how will you keep the memorial? If you
do not agree with me, how do you share my tomb? I was taught that Christ is
Lord and God; I handled him with the hand, and I found the truth; I was made
sure by my own fingers. No one else suggested the dogma to me; I do not have
the wonder from hearing; I did not believe those who said to me, We have seen
the Lord. I fought with the apostles; I opposed Peter when he tried to teach
me. I said to him: Man, why do you trouble me? Why do you grant me words in
place of facts? Why do you command me to believe from sayings? Unless I see, I
will not believe. As you also saw, let me also see; as you were taught, let me
be taught; as you learned, let me learn. What I am about to preach, let me
behold, and I preach. No one announces what he has only heard; no one puts
forward a dogma from rumor; no one accepts me when I merely repeat your
report. If I am asked how Christ rose, I do not know what I shall say. If I
say, Peter told me, who will attend as to one worthy of belief? Let me see,
and I preach; let me learn, and I teach; let me be made sure, and I make
others sure. Let me also say: Thomas, an apostle not from men, nor through
=59.500= men.
While I was saying these things, the Lord appeared, and he dissolved the fight
at once; and he says to me: What has happened to you, Thomas? Why do you fight
with Peter? Why do you contend with your companions? Come, take the wonder by
experience; learn the resurrection by the deed; bring your hand, and look at
the body. If you can receive it, take hold; if you have clean fingers, touch
the marks; if you believe, you will also attain it; but if you contend, you do
not find; if you disbelieve, you do not recognize; if you doubt, you do not
hold what suffered; if you have passion, you do not learn what is without
passion. Hearing this I cleansed the soul of unbelief; I stripped off the
doubtful mind; I took up the mind that had been persuaded. I touched the body
rejoicing and trembling; I spread out with the fingers also the eye of the
soul, and thereafter I perceived two operations. I was holding, and I was
seeing; and with the hand I was holding a body, but with the soul I was
contemplating God; and what was outside I found marvelous, and what was
within, fearful; and what appeared was great, and what was held was a wonder.
I cried out then, struck by what I had grasped: My Lord and my God; for
nowhere did I see a slave; nowhere did a lowly form appear; and the kindred
nature shone out, and the divine flashed; and what was worn was exalted, and
what wore it was glorified; and of two things conceived, one was the person
worshipped. These things I received by experience; these I held with a finger;
these I contemplated with the soul. But you, Arius, from where were you taught
the blasphemy? From where did you learn what you preach? Did you handle Christ
as I did? Did you bring the hand? Did you search the marks? Do the fingers
bear witness for you? Did you take hold of the Lord? Into which hand? The one
in which you seized the treasures? The one in which you plundered the
apostles? The one in which you received the barbarian's spoils? The one in
which you held the wage of impiety? The one in which you melted down the
vessels of the blood? Into this did you handle Christ? God forbid. The Master
is not so unjust, nor is Christ so unguarded, as to entrust you his own flesh.
You did not take hold of him whom you rejected; you did not handle him whom
you hated; you did not search him whom you belittled. For if you had searched,
you would not have humbled him; if you had inquired well, you would not have
so badly misinterpreted. Away with you; enjoy your wickedness; fall with your
own allies. For now I have thrown down one of your towers; for now I have
freed Thrace from your tyranny; yet a little, and I pull you down from every
side; yet a little, and I drive you from the inhabited world.
Just now I take off your head; just now I also free the West from your
madness. Yes, blessed Thomas, fulfill the word by the deed; follow your own
voice; confirm the good beginning also with the end. As you have freed Thrace,
free the West as well. Let me hear the robber too cut down as the tyrant was;
crown the emperor with the victory; award peace to the world; receive the
sweats of those who have gathered; receive the city that entreats you. The
whole common age has sent you an embassy.
Old men and young we fall at your tomb; virgins and youths we cling about your
body; infants, like nestlings, gaze at you with open mouths. Do not overlook
such a sight; do not send our labors away empty; give us a wage worthy of the
sweats. We ask nothing great of you; what you have, we ask; what you give, we
seek; what you wish to see, we desire: the barbarian defeated, Arius driven
out, the Emperor crowned, the world converted, Christ exalted, and hearing
from the whole inhabited world together: My Lord and my God; to whom be glory,
honor, and power unto the ages of ages. Amen.
