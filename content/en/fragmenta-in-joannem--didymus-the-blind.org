Jo 3:13 When a certain preeminence fitting to God is indicated by the name of
heaven according to "the heaven of heaven is the Lord's," the one who later
became devil, looking toward earth, being in heaven when he was holy, said: "I
will ascend into heaven," as being somewhere below in relation to that heaven.
Into the heaven so called no one has ascended except the Son of God alone,
since he also alone has descended from there, being called Son of Man because
of the economy. Even if then the angels were seen by Jacob going up into
heaven and coming down by a ladder, yet the vision is not spoken of by the
scripture concerning the heaven mentioned before. 2 Jo 3:35-36 These things
are said concerning the rational beings. And since among all these there are
also some that have become worse, consider how these will have a state when
they have come to be in the hand of the Son, namely having cast off the vice
they had and having taken up virtue; for one must not attend to the one who
has set down sophisms, that "all" are called only the things that have
holiness. And not having life is not the same as not seeing; this is made
clear from "you shall see the life before your eyes" and "you shall not
believe." If someone should inquire into these things, which is worse? Whether
not seeing the life nor believing in it, or not having life, yet seeing it?
And one will say that the one lacking both is worse than the one who has the
one. But another will say that it is a lesser evil for the one not seeing the
life to disbelieve it; for there will be pardon for the one who has such a
condition. But rather the one who does not believe in the life is to be
accused, even though he is able to see, its beauty drawing him toward assent.
3 Jo 4:24 Since God is invisible, and the incorporeal follows immediately upon
the invisible, God therefore, being incorporeal, is invisible. And if this is
so, being called spirit he would not be moved air; for it is not because among
human beings spirit indicates a body that this must also be signified in the
case of God. Just as therefore, being called light, since he does not
illuminate sight but understanding, he is not a body but intellectual light,
and being called love he is not a disposition, but an essence that loves the
things it creates and those for which it provides, so being named spirit he
presents not wind but an incorporeal and life-giving essence. And everyone who
has learned that God is spirit, worshiping him spiritually in spirit and
truth, no longer worships the God of all typically. And he says "in spirit" in
contradistinction to the letter, and "in truth" to the type; for these were
needful until a time, but when the truth came, that is the presence of Christ,
all things have ceased. 4 Jo 5:19-21 If what is said by Jesus: for whatever
that one does (and it is clear that it is the Father), these the Son also does
likewise, has reference to the paradoxical things done by him in the gospels,
one must consider how the Father did these things. To which it must be said:
since the Son, being consubstantial with the Father, has an activity
inseparable from the activity of the Father, being an unstained mirror,
whatever the Father does, at the same time and inseparably the Son also works,
and what the Son works, immediately the Father also brings into existence, so
that according to this neither the Son without the Father nor the Father
without the Son does anything. This contemplation must also be applied to the
created things; for it is not that the Father creates some things and the Son
then gives substance to others; for it would follow from this that there be
two suns and each of the things that are be double, but the creatures do not
appear to be so. Therefore the sameness of the activity of Father and Son is
contemplated in the things created. If being raised and being made alive is
said of a dead man, they differ by word alone; for a dead man being made alive
and being raised is the same, and it converts entirely, a dead man being
raised being made alive. But if ever being raised should be said not of a dead
man but of a living one, being made alive would not be the same; for someone
rising from sitting or from having fallen asleep is not made alive. It
signifies a taking of life, and the living one does not receive this; for he
has it, unless perhaps according to another signification, for the name of
life is homonymous. 5 Jo 5:23 As the one who transgresses the law, through the
transgression, dishonors God, and the one who keeps it honors the one who gave
it, so the one who honors the Son by acting according to the law and right
reason will honor the Father, and conversely. 6 Jo 5:28 "All who are in the
tombs" signifies the same as "all who have died"; for if he had not said this,
those whose bodies have been consumed by beasts or fire and are not placed in
tombs would not rise. But since there will be a resurrection of all human
beings, these too therefore will be numbered among those in the tombs. And if
those who contradict should be contentious, one must point out to them that
those who practice the good things and the base things are not the bodies laid
away in the tombs. Whence the word will not prevent them from accepting also
the bodies consumed by fire or otherwise. 7 Jo 5:28-29 Human beings being
divided according to their mind into the impious, that is unbelievers, or the
pious, that is believers, they themselves, all human beings being raised, the
unbelievers and impious are raised, yet not unto judgment, because the one who
does not believe has already been judged. And there are those who have
believed in the Savior and besides this have also done good works, who are
raised unto a resurrection of life, not going to be judged, those who have
sinned after believing being raised unto judgment. And the state of infants,
since they too are raised, God alone knows, and perhaps also those to whom the
revelation of the truth concerning this has come. 10 Col1 Jo 6:44-45 Someone
is drawn by the Father in this way, the human being having become rational
"according to image and according to likeness" of God. There have been sown in
him the notions concerning virtuous actions and the account concerning the
truth and knowledge. Therefore the one who has heard according to the common
notions and judged the good and learned from the Father comes to Christ
through faith. Or also through revelation he draws to himself those who hear
and have learned. In which manner also to Peter having confessed he says:
"because flesh and blood has not revealed it to you, but my Father who is in
heaven." "According to image and likeness" and this very thing, the human
being having become rational. There have been sown in him by God the notions
concerning praiseworthy actions, but also of knowledge, of the truth.
Therefore the one who has heard according to the common notions and learned
from the Father comes through faith to the Lord. And a little later: teaching
through revelation the things concerning the Son, the Father leads to him
those who hear and have learned; for thus also he led Peter through revelation
to believe in the Son, having revealed him. 11 Col1 Jo 6:58 He says heaven is
signified as the as-it-were lookout and state of God, which is not other than
his intelligible essence. And "bread of life" "that comes down" is the
nourishing word of God, which the one who comes to him eats by practicing
virtue. And since one must not only go to him through practice, but also
scientifically believe the things true according to contemplation, one drinks
from him from a spring "living water" and from a vine wine that gladdens "the
heart of man." Whence partaking according to practical virtue we eat, and
according to the dianoetic we drink, so as always to live having been filled
and never to thirst having drunk from him. And if "blessed are those who
hunger and thirst for righteousness" seems contrary, there is by no means a
disagreement; for if someone does not hunger nor thirst having eaten of this
bread and drunk of the immortal drink, yet nevertheless because of the lovable
and praiseworthy character of the food and the drink the one who has been
filled still hungers and drinking still thirsts, so as both always to be
filled and to hunger. Which heaven he signifies here of the three heavens, the
primordial, the firmament, the air; of these one must understand none, but the
as-it-were lookout and state of God, which is not other than his intelligible
essence. And "bread of life" "that comes down" is the nourishing word of God,
which the one who comes to him eats by practicing virtue. Or also those who
according to participation in his teaching pursue practical virtue are said to
eat, and according to the dianoetic examination of the hidden of scripture are
said to drink, as Christ says: "blessed are those who hunger and thirst for
righteousness," who pursue both the practice and the understanding of it. 12
Jo 8:51-52 The Savior having said that the one who keeps the word will not see
death, the Jews present, having erred, supposed that to see death is the same
as to taste it; for it seems to them, so far as their own words go, that that
one does not taste death who has not taken experience of the cosmic death; and
this seems to overturn the Lord's promise, even those who have kept his word
having died, and a deceit came about for them, the meaning being craftily
sophistrized from homonymy; for the one who keeps the word of Jesus will not
see the death that follows upon sin, even if he will taste the common and
bodily death. And since the prophets also said they died without
determination, not adding "all," the not having died of Enoch and Elijah does
not conflict. 13 Jo 8:56 One must inquire what day he means: the intelligible
day of the Lord. 14 Jo 9:37 He has been enlightened "the eyes of the heart,"
being illumined by the true light. The one who has the opposite to this,
having been blinded by the ignorance he has, seems not to have sin because he
has not sinned great things voluntarily. But the very being ignorant is
according to another manner a sin; for it was possible, being rational, to
have knowledge of good and evil. He is therefore accused of neglect of this,
beyond the ignorance itself. For this you will take "the servant who did not
know the will of the lord will be beaten with few." 15 Jo 10:9 The Word of God
is a door for those who enter toward God according to "enter before him with
rejoicing"; besides these things, the mind being the inner man, and the more
bodily the outer man, the one who takes care of the inner man enters through
the Word who is a door, likewise the one who puts to death "the members upon
the earth" and bears about "the dying of Jesus in his own body" goes out
"through the door," being ambitious to be well-pleasing to him even while
remaining at home. 16 Jo 10:29 The Father gives to the Son those who have been
qualified according to the good, as with unsurpassable quality; for "all that
the Father gives me will come to me," and "behold I and the children whom God
has given me"; for as the Son leads to the Father and hands over to him those
to whom he attaches himself, having furnished himself for comprehension
according to "he who has seen me has seen the Father," so the Father gives to
the Son those to whom he makes him known, as Peter; for not "flesh and blood,
but the Father revealed" the Son. All who have thus known the Son from the
Father attain the things given to the Son through exceeding perfection, these
being thus. How Judas? Being himself also one of those given by the Father to
the Son according to "those you have given me" I have kept and "none of them
has perished except the son of perdition," he fell from such greatness as to
be called "son of perdition"; for it no longer had place that Judas was evil
from constitution, as the heterodox think, so great a change being found
concerning him. 17 Jo 14:28 How has he taken "greater" here? Is it as upon
magnitudes of bodies? Or as greater in time? Or as adorned with more virtue?
But all these are within quantity and of things contingent and measured. And
nothing contingent and measured is in God. Therefore the greater and the less
has not been said of the Word of God or of the Father; for God is not of a
nature to be measured or delineated. And if you should say the Father is
greater, because he is unbegotten, and the Son begotten, we shall say that
these are modes of the essences, not of the existences. And especially in the
case of the qualityless and incorporeal they do not diminish the dignity of
those who are consubstantial, furnishing the less or the greater to this one
or that; for that which is simple in essence and knowledge is not of a nature
to be compared by measure or quantity of magnitude. Why then was it said? The
Savior in one word stretched forth a double manner of teaching; for the
meaning of the word spoken is twofold. For example through "the one who sent
me" and I go he hinted at the body contained in places, being circumscribed,
and not his uncircumscribed divinity that contains all things. And through the
comparative word of saying "greater" he indicates his own divinity being
compared with the Father, because he is consubstantial, but by the assumption
of the body, conceived as less than the Father according to his having become
man. And do not wonder if the Savior, having become man, says he is less than
the Father, when he is also said to be a little lower than the angels
according to this: "we see Jesus who was made a little lower than the angels
because of the" suffering "of death crowned with glory and honor." And he was
also said to be less than his own disciples: "but I," he says, "am in the
midst of you as the one who serves" and as the smaller. Therefore the things
compared are consubstantial, but the things simple both in knowledge and in
essence do not admit the greater and the less. And if you wish to take "the
Father who sent me is greater than I" of the divinity of the Son without
having taken flesh, you will grant him contained and shut up in places, and
sent where he was not, and departing from where he is; which is absurd. 18 Jo
17:1 The Father glorified his own Son, having subjected what is under the sun
to his scepters, and he himself was also glorified in return through this; for
the Son was glorified by the Father, being believed by all that he is
offspring and fruit of the one who is able to do all things. And the Father
was glorified in return as by his own offspring; for since the offspring was
known easily to accomplish all things as being all-powerful, the grace of the
good fame passed over to the one who begot it. Just as therefore, he says, you
have glorified and been glorified, having given him the authority and lordship
over all, clearly in the manner that has been said, so I will that none of the
things given perish; for this too is a glory that passes over to us; for it
was necessary, it was necessary that that which has come wholly under
authority and under the hand of the Word of God who is able to do all things,
having once been saved, remain also in the unending goods, so as no longer to
be tyrannized by death nor held by corruption or by sins and to be subject to
the ancient evils. 19 Jo 17:1-3 This is what he says: reveal me to those who
do not know, make manifest my glory which I have with you, being God the Word;
for "no one," he says, "can come to me unless the Father who sent me draws
him." And likewise one may hear also the Son glorifying the Father according
to revealing him to those who do not know God. In this manner the Holy Spirit
also is said to glorify the Son in revealing him to those who do not know. And
if also here: to imagine concerning him for the present. And this is the
eternal: they will cast out the Son of God. And this one must know, that he
says the cause i.e. the caused; for knowing the true one procures for us the
eternal life. Jo 17:5 The pre-cosmic glory of the Son of God, what else would
it be than himself God the Word, himself "true light," for it is not as being
other than the glory that he is glorified by another glory through another,
himself being, as I said before, the "Lord of glory" and king of glory. But
since "he emptied himself taking the form of a slave" (for "the Word," he
says, "became flesh" and we saw him "and he had no form nor beauty") and many
disbelieved the so great condescension that came to be, many disbelieved that
God became man, for this reason, wishing his divinity to be revealed to those
who do not know, he says: "Father, glorify your Son," that is, reveal me to
those who do not know, make manifest my glory which I have with you, being God
the Word. Wherefore Paul said, "when God was pleased to reveal his Son in me,
that I might preach him among the nations."
