* That Love According to God Is a Work Worthy of God
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

That love according to God is a work befitting God.
=61.681=
No speech can hymn as it deserves the love that is a ladder to heaven.
Love shares the throne of the Father; love joins earthly things to heavenly
things. Tongue will not suffice concerning it, nor will the mind find a way
to glorify it worthily; but the work itself shows that love is the leader of
the best way of life. For this love throws a perfect beauty over the
creation of God; this is the tutor of the many into unity, shaping the
multitudes of human beings, angels and men, after the likeness of the one
and holy beauty. For being one form, it shapes every rational nature into
the likeness of God; it does not rest upon those alienated from him, but
upon those who will be made by him and mingled with him. Though they are
many, all have bent toward one concord, and they display upon themselves a
most beautiful and delightful harmony. Thus the many and various operations
through the Holy Spirit, gathered into one good like-mindedness, display a
beauty most Godlike and most befitting God, most precious to God and worthy
of the holy gladness that is with the Creator.
Because of love toward human beings God came down upon the earth and dwelt
among human beings, not worshipped in a hidden and set-apart sanctuary as
before, but coming down as one who shares a tent with human beings, into
our likeness and our manner of life, that by drawing all into the kinship
of one Spirit he might make them as one body and one spirit. Divine love,
being a servant of the Lord and a minister of the Spirit, gathers what is
sundered and makes its own what has been alienated. O marvelous work of
Christ, love! O mystery of the world's salvation! Through love the Father
and God sends his only-begotten Son into the world; for Christ, it says,
loved the Church, and gave himself up for her. Love gladly bears the toils
for one's neighbor; through love we are commanded to be slaves of one
another: For through love serve one another. Love makes the private
possessions of each one common to all; love shows you your neighbor as
yourself, and bears sins as if they were its own. You will be angry in few
things, but you will be long-suffering in most. Through love the flock of
Christ is increased; love tames murderous rages, blunts the weapons of
enemies, and puts forgetfulness of all enemies, and introduces into the
world the peaceful polity of Christ. For nation shall not take up sword
against nation, and they shall not learn war any more. For this reason, In
his days, it says, righteousness shall spring up =61.682= and abundance of
peace, until the moon be taken away. And the type of the peaceful polity is
also bright. O love, leader of blessed peace, who drives out from among
human beings the enemy and hostile demon, through whom the devil groans
over peaceful human beings, while angels rejoice seeing the image of their
own peace in human beings! We are bright toward one another, and a brother
seen by a brother is greater than all cheerfulness.
Paul, then, says that he is comforted in his afflictions at the coming of
Titus; but when Titus is not present, he has no rest in his spirit. Love of
God and love of the brothers are two things inseparable. And this
commandment, says John, we have from God, that the one who loves God should
also love his brother. For this he said also to Peter: If you love me, feed
my sheep. That is, Show your love for the shepherd by taking care of the
sheep with zeal. What wall is so unbreakable, fortified by the fittings of
great stones and turning back the assaults of enemies, as a company of
saints fitted together by the bonds of love, and turning back the trials of
the devil? What beauty of plants so planted in a row, or so well fitted an
array of an army advancing in seemly order and rank? For these things
afford delight; but more delightful and sweeter than all is the beauty
according to natural harmony of those fitted to one another in love; for
this appears august and honored in the eyes of the Lord. Hear David, or
rather the grace of the Holy Spirit: Behold, he says, what is good, or what
is delightful? nothing else, nothing other of the things upon the earth,
but that brothers should dwell together; as ointment poured out upon the
head. Angels dance around our choirs and rejoice over us, because we
imitate their way of life. For if there is joy in heaven over one sinner
who repents, according to the word of the Lord, much more over so great a
multitude of those being saved. For the gladness of the age to come appears
from here: mourning to the devil, a winnowing-fan of demons, when we have
stood in choirs with one another, and in one soul and one mind toward the
hymnody of God we imitate the all-harmonious and greatly glorious melody of
the Cherubim. For as those, receiving one from another in succession, sing
the thrice-holy hymn, so also we, having formed two choirs after their
pattern and answering one another, sing the spiritual melody. A wonder to
those outside, a thing desired by those who see, and longing is born in
those who hear, and a divine, active perception is found in us, through
which we per =61.683= ceive all the causes of wickedness and division being
rooted out from us.
All will glorify God because of us, seeing a heavenly way of life upon the
earth. For this reason Paul taught us all to be of one mind and to say the
same thing; for this reason Christ, when he sends, sends the apostles two
together; even as far as women he introduces like-mindedness, that he might
not leave even the assembly of virgins without a share in this angelic
imitation. For this reason he says, I entreat Euodia and Syntyche to be of
the same mind in the Lord. He commands us to count others as surpassing
ourselves, so that we may not be dissolved by rivalry, or rather by
vainglory, but by yielding the honor to our neighbor we may be united and
joined together. The things among us, then, are august, and the things of
sacred and divine love are truly ornaments of the world. But let us see
also the teachings in the law. For these too teach what is profitable. You
shall not overlook, it says, the needy, but you shall share with the one in
distress; the poor shall enjoy the fruits that are with you, and the poor
shall have the remnants of the harvest and of the vintage for food; and in
the seventh year the slave also shall be released into freedom, and in the
fiftieth year every debt shall be loosed; and those who have sold their
possessions shall take them back, without having paid the price. You shall
not demand interest, you shall not take a mill or an upper millstone in
pledge, nor shall you take the poor man's cloak in pledge, nor shall you go
in by force to take a pledge. You shall not abandon the beast of burden of
your neighbor, even if he happens to be an enemy; but you shall bring it
back to him, even a wandering animal, becoming a servant as to a friend
=61.684=, and dissolving the enmity when an occasion has arisen. And all
these commands are for no other reason than for the sacred love that is
much desired by God; for the evangelical commands also run down to this.
For after saying many things that fulfill the law, he added: Do good to
those who hate you, and pray for those who despitefully use you. In all
things dissolving enmity, and planting love. Let loving one another be
among you a debt never fulfilled; for Paul says, Owe no one anything,
except to love one another. For just as we seek food each day, and the
previous satiety will not keep us from seeking food each day, but being
required this by nature we owe it as a debt, so also in the matter of love
let us keep the same pattern. The eye, it says, will not be filled with
seeing, nor the ear with hearing. Light is sweet to the eyes, and no one
grew weary seeing with the eye, and no one grew weary hearing with the
ears; so also we ought to perform the lovely work of love without
shrinking, and never to be sated. For much water will not be able to quench
love; For these three remain, it says, faith, hope, love. For as it was
from the beginning, so also it will be kept unto the age; to which may it
be granted that we, following it, attain the eternal goods in Christ Jesus
our Lord, to whom be the glory and the power unto the ages of the ages.
Amen.
