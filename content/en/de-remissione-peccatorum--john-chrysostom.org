* On the Remission of Sins
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

On the [saying], "Whatever you bind on earth shall be bound also in heaven;"
and concerning the parable of the hundred sheep; and on the saying of the
evangelist, "If two shall agree," and what follows; and on, "How often, if my
brother sin against me, shall I forgive him?"
=60.759=
The spring of water, having one nature, grants the healing of thirst to many
and various living things. For herds of cattle and flocks of sheep, running
down to it, receive drink, and a company of four-footed beasts and many-formed
kinds of circling wild animals, hastening toward it, draw water from there,
and numerous multitudes of birds, flying down to it and drawing the water,
there quench the burning of thirst. And the spring not only fills the need of
the irrational creatures, but indeed from its flanks it also sprouts the rich
grace of the place for rational men; and every nature of creeping things and
birds and four-footed beasts and rational men, drawing the water from one
spring, is satisfied. Such is the teaching of the divine Scriptures, beloved,
being one. For since it has its outflow from the one wisdom of God, it indeed
makes wise many and various hearts of men. And from there teachers are made
wise, disciples are trained, the Churches are made fragrant, the thirsty are
given drink, those in darkness are enlightened. And that these things are so,
it is easy to learn the matter from the Gospel just now read to us.
For what was the Lord saying? Amen I say to you, whatever you bind on earth
shall be bound also in heaven. The voice is one, and it is divided in many
ways toward the need and benefit of each; the voice is one, and it furnishes
old men and younger, and women and virgins and men, and disciples and
teachers. Whatever you bind on earth shall be bound in heaven. He has given
you the authority of binding and loosing; you bound yourself with the chain of
love of money, loose yourself by the commandment of love of the poor; you
bound yourself with the sting of pleasures, loose yourself by temperance; you
bound yourself with the evil faith of Eunomius, loose yourself by the piety of
orthodoxy. Teachers, bind the teachings of Eunomius and Arius and Marathonius,
and loose for us the meanings of Father and Son and Holy Spirit in the
Gospels; bind the demons who campaign against the faithful by your
supplications to God, and loose those who turn from the nations in baptism,
tearing apart the chains of their sins; bind by excommunication those who have
sinned after baptism, and loose them again when they repent, receiving them as
brothers; for the word is true: Whatever you loose on earth shall be loosed in
heaven. For this the apostles also used to do. Paul once bound the one who had
committed fornication in Corinth, and again loosed him when he repented from
the heart; Peter also bound the one who after baptism wished to acquire the
gift of God through money, and again loosed him when he fell before him from
the heart. And furthermore they bound many who had sinned without repentance,
and they loosed others who had repented. Peter bound Ananias together with
Sapphira when he was tempting the Spirit of the Lord, and he loosed the pangs
of death of Tabitha, having raised her from the dead; he loosed Cornelius the
centurion when he believed, and he bound Simon the magician in Rome when he
was mocking with his magics; Paul also bound Elymas the magician, having
blinded him by a word, and he loosed the servant-girl, having separated her
from the python spirit; Judas bound =60.760= himself with the rope of
betrayal, and Peter loosed his own denial through repentance; Paul also bound
himself when he was persecuting the Church, but again he loosed himself when
he recognized on the road the way of truth.
And the prophets also bound many things, and loosed them. Elijah the Tishbite
bound the rain-bearing clouds, not giving the rain to the ungodly, and he
loosed them again, when king Ahab fell before him; Elisha his disciple also
loosed Naaman the Syrian, having separated him from the leprosy, and he bound
Gehazi who had lied for money, having cursed the leprosy upon him; Daniel
loosed the dream of Nebuchadnezzar, and he bound sword-bearing hands of
Chaldeans armed against them; and he undid the temple of the idols, having
overthrown Bel and slain the dragon, and he bound the mouths of bloodthirsty
lions. The three children also bound the power of the fire, not having been
burned by it, and they undid the king's decree, not having worshipped his
image. And above all these our Lord Jesus Christ bound and loosed. He undid
the enmity that had come to be between us and God, and he bound the first-evil
serpent, through whom enmity has come to be; he bound all the demons who
campaign against men, and he loosed all the men who were troubled by them; he
bound the flaming sword that was turning against us, and he loosed the thief
bound upon the cross, bringing him into the delight of paradise; he bound Cain
the brother-slayer, wrapping him in tremblings, and he loosed the tremblings
of the paralytic, setting his members right; he loosed Isaac who had been
bound foot to foot by Abraham, and he bound instead of him a ram held in the
plant Sabek; he bound Judas who loved silver pieces, and he loosed the chief
tax-collector Zacchaeus who distributed his money to the poor; he bound the
Jewish swine, having permitted them to the demons, and he loosed the bound
donkey and the colt, having sent his disciples; he bound the bitter-bearing
fig tree, drying up its roots, and he loosed the woman bent double, having
said to her, Take courage, daughter, you have been loosed from your infirmity.
He loosed Peter bound with chains, and he bound the murderous Herod, wrapping
his members with the worms; he made firm the orthodox Church founded upon the
solid rock, and he dissolved the tent of the heterodox built upon the unstable
sand; Jews also bound Christ, but Christ undid their temple, he overthrew
their Jerusalem, he paralyzed their lawful things, he made void their
circumcisions, he dissolved their synagogues, he snatched away from them the
law and the prophets, and he winnowed them like chaff through every city and
country. The devil also bound the Lord, having brought him up upon the cross,
but Christ burst open his insatiable belly, he shattered his flesh-eating
teeth, having broken them against his solid rock, he paralyzed his sinews, he
crushed his robe, he laid low his hair, he cast upon him an eternal lament and
dark mourning, because he had brought down into mourning of sins the man who
had stood in joy. He quenched his shameless eyes, he put to shame his
dishonored face, he struck him with sharp javelins, he stabbed him with a
three-edged sword, he scattered his =60.761= hopes, he searched out his hidden
nests of the right hand, and he winnowed his stores with every wind; he dashed
to the ground the pitiless children and offspring of his demons, he burned
with unquenchable fire his handmade idols, he sank his couch into a pit, he
tore open his dark storehouses, he flung open the gates of Hades, and having
loosed all who had been bound by him and taken them with himself, he ran up
into the heavens toward the Father as a giant, as the prophet says: He shall
exult as a giant to run his course; his going forth is from the end of heaven
and his reaching is unto the end of heaven, and there is none who shall be
hidden from his heat. He shall exult as a giant to run his course.
He exulted, as was just now heard in the Gospel, because of the finding of the
one lost sheep. For he left the ninety-nine sheep that had not gone astray,
that is, the angelic powers upon the mountains, that is, upon the heavens, and
he came upon the sheep that had gone astray, which was crying and saying: I
have gone astray like a lost sheep; seek your servant, for I have not
forgotten your commandments. For having heard such a voice of the sheep that
had gone astray, he bowed the heavens and came down and came to seek the sheep
that had gone astray, and having found it he exulted. He shall exult as a
giant to run a course. He ran the course from heavens to earth exulting; then
from earth he ran to the regions under the earth; then there, having loosed
all the souls held by the devil, he ran down to earth, and again from earth to
the heavens toward the Father, as a giant he leaped. And there is none who
shall be hidden from his heat. Hearing the heat of Christ, understand him as
sun of righteousness. And there is none who shall be hidden from his heat. For
who, having been made dead by the coldest death, shall be hidden from the heat
of his Spirit, and shall not all rise from the dead? For the trumpet shall
sound, and the dead shall be raised incorruptible. Then what is next, beloved?
Even if, having spoken few things from many, on the [saying], Whatever you
bind on earth shall be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth shall
be loosed in heaven, let us cast our mind upon the next verse: Amen I say to
you, that if two shall agree on the same thing concerning every matter,
whatever they shall ask, it shall be given to them. Abraham having agreed with
Sarah his wife, he running to the cattle, and she hastening the loaves, they
received a paradoxical thing in old age, having reaped from the loins the
birth of a child. Jacob the patriarch, having been of one mind with his
mother, received the fruit of an eternal blessing. Moses and Aaron having
agreed, they laid waste all Egypt, having set free the race of the Hebrews.
Joshua and Caleb, having been of one mind, alone inherited the land of the
promise. Deborah having agreed with Barak, they conquered the most savage
tyrant. Judith having been of one mind with her own maidservant, they
conquered the foreigner Holofernes. Tobias having been of one mind with the
angel, bound the man-slaying demon of his own wife, and opened his father's
closed eyes. Hezekiah and Isaiah having prayed with one voice, laid low one
hundred eighty-five thousand of the foreigners. Paul and Silas having prayed
with one voice in the prison, caused the foundations of the jail to be shaken.
And the letter can also be understood otherwise: If two shall agree on the
same thing concerning every matter, whatever they shall ask, it shall be given
to them. If these two that are much at variance and contentious shall agree;
that is, flesh and spirit, inasmuch as the flesh lusts against the spirit, and
the spirit against the flesh; if these two shall agree, so that the one may
rule, and the other serve; the one may be temperate, and the other not
contradict; then the Lord shall be in the midst of soul and flesh, and
concerning every matter, whatever they shall ask, it shall be given to them.
For many who have practiced this were heard, as Elijah on the mountain, and
Daniel in the pit, and Jonah in the whale, and Hezekiah on the bed of his
sickness, and Peter in Joppa, and Cornelius in Caesarea. And many women also,
praying alone, were heard; as Sarah of Raguel in Ecbatana, and Judith in the
upper room, and Susanna in the judgment of the lawless. Then Peter, coming, he
says, said to the Lord: How often shall I forgive my brother who has sinned
against me? until seven times? And the Lord =60.762= what does he say to him?
I do not say to you only, Until seven times, but until seventy times seven.
For since he heard the Lord saying, Whatever you bind on earth shall be bound
in heaven; and whatever you loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven; he works
upon the promise of the Lord, wishing at once to learn also how great is such
a grace of the Lord's love of mankind. You said to us, Whatever you bind on
earth shall be bound also in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth shall be
loosed also in heaven. How often then, if my brother sin against me, shall I
forgive him? until seven times? But the Lord, having regarded the poverty of
his love of mankind toward the neighbor, said to him: I do not say to you
only, Until seven times, but until seventy times seven. For you as a poor man
asked poverty; but I as God, a great giver, have granted the grace richly. Not
only until seven times, but until seventy times seven. But the word has a
hidden mystery. If the farmer does not sink the mattock deep into the earth,
he does not find the fruits suitable in the coming season; so also we, if we
do not sink our mind deep into the letter, do not find the hidden treasure of
the Spirit. How often, if my brother sin against me, shall I forgive him?
until seven times? And why did he not say, Until eight times, or six times, or
ten times, but Until seven times? Because God made all things in six days, and
on the seventh day he rested from his works.
Peter asks the Lord a mystery, wishing to learn ecclesiastical canons; as it
were, If my brother sin against me in all the days of his life, but on the
last day of his life, as it were, if on the seventh he repent, shall I forgive
him? But the Lord turned, saying: Not only in this age shall you forgive him
the sins, but also in the age to come you shall pardon him, it being longer
than this age seventy times seven. For he is true who said to you, that
Whatever you loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven. But let us work the
thought still more deeply. How often, if my brother sin against me, not
against you the God, but against me, shall I forgive him? And the Lord to him,
Not only shall you forgive the sins against me until seven times, but indeed
also seventy times seven. For it is true what he said, that Every sin and
blasphemy shall be forgiven to men, but the blasphemy against the Holy Spirit
shall not be forgiven either in this present age, or in the one to come. But
thanks be to God, that we do not fear this sin; for we do not blaspheme the
Holy Spirit, but we worship and glorify. But let us still scrape the word. For
gold also, being melted, becomes more shining; so also Scripture, being
scraped, is found more distinct. How often, if my brother sin against me,
shall I forgive him? until seven times?
Because he knew that the law forgives men their trespasses in the week of this
age; for it did not have the eighth day of the resurrection; for Christ had
not yet come, who has the resurrection day; whence also David, since he was
under the law, praises God seven times in the day; for he says, Seven times a
day I praised you; not eight times; for Christ has not yet come to me, the
eighth day of the resurrection; I am still under the law, under the law I
shall serve you; I am still a son of the maidservant, of the synagogue; for I
have not yet been born of the free woman; therefore he says, I am your
servant, and son of your maidservant. For the mother of the Christians who
have been born again in grace is free, as the Apostle says: But the Jerusalem
above is free, which is the mother of us all. How often then shall my brother
sin against me, and I forgive him? until seven times? As if he were saying, Do
you command that the faults be forgiven to my brothers, as the law, for this
is, Until seven times, or something more? But the Lord superabounds the grace,
saying: Not only until seven times, but until seventy times seven.
For as much as the Word exceeds the law, so much also does grace exceed the
law; for the Word is not God from the law, but the law is from the Word. And
it is possible to learn, beloved, =60.763= the benefit from the word; that you
may learn how much greater the Word is than the law. The law gives ten words
to the Jews, and it did not even benefit; but God, having given us one Word,
saved all through him. But let us still hoe the letter with the Spirit. For he
who has opened a treasure does not go away taking only gold and silver, but
indeed he also seeks precious stones and those of many kinds. How often, if my
brother sin against me, shall I forgive him? until seven times? It is a
mystery, beloved, hidden in the letter, and it has escaped many. But you,
attend exactly to the thought for me; for it is exceedingly subtle, swifter
than a bird, easily able to run past the eye of the mind, even those who see
very sharply. How often if my brother sin against me, shall I forgive him?
seven times? For it is worthy of inquiry, why Peter said Until seven times,
and our Lord answered, Until seventy times seven. But Peter attended to the
charge of murder, that it is most heavy and terrible, and he wis =60.764= hes
to learn in a mystery ecclesiastical canons, whether then the Lord commands
him to forgive in baptism the charge of murder.
For having attended to this, that Cain slew his brother by deceit, and fell
under seven charges; for he was first to murder among men, first to play the
hypocrite, first to lie, first to defile the earth with blood, first to open
the gates of death; for death had not yet tasted a man, unless Cain conceived
it through envy; Peter therefore, having attended to this, that the murder of
Cain is subject to seven charges, comes to the Lord asking mysteriously, How
often, if my brother sin, shall I forgive him? until seven times? That is, Do
you command that in baptism I forgive him the charges of Cain the
brother-slayer? And the Lord, what does he say to him? Not only shall you
forgive the sevenfold of Cain in baptism, but also that of Lamech, the until
seventy times seven; in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory unto the
ages of ages. Amen.
