On Susanna.
=56.589=
We have come to you as grateful debtors of the word, not filling up as much as
we owe, but giving back as much as we have. For the eagerness is abundant and
is spread out to give more than what is owed; but the poverty of speech is
straitened; wherefore, entreating you, we give a few things in place of many.
And if any of you should be willing to stretch out the fewest of our words
with the eagerness of the heart, we shall fall short of nothing toward what is
owed, our eagerness filling up the lacking part of the words. For God, when he
sees a poor man's short offerings and a rich man's abundant ones, knows how to
receive the honors equally; and often he receives those of the poor man more
greatly; for he looks not at the bulk of the money, but at the eagerness of
the heart. And a proof of what is said is the matter of the needy widow who
cast the two mites into the treasury; for her short offering outweighed with
the Lord those of the rich who had cast in gold; since God does not need
money, but a sincere and well-disposed heart.

Come then, we too, having spread out our hearts in sincerity, as much word as
we have obtained from God, casting it as into certain living treasuries of God
=56.590=, let us pay you the debt of the word. For I remember that on the
previous day I promised you to proclaim the contest of the blessed Susanna's
chastity, for the benefit of many; when Joseph also contended against the
Egyptian woman, and showed us all the wrestling-bouts of chastity. Let Susanna
then contend also in this public theater, where God, and angels, and men, and
women look on; let her contend, and teach how younger women must contend even
unto death for chastity. This blessed Susanna then, being well-born in race,
kept from her tenderest years in a little chamber, being comely in beauty, and
trained in the manner of a solemn life, when the beauty of her youth was in
bloom, was joined to the yoke of lawful marriage. And keeping the mystery of
marriage unharmed for her own husband, she did not carry about an unordered
eye toward the sight of youthful and foreign beauty; she did not, opening her
ear, receive the fine-voiced singing of fornicating songs; she did not let her
smell creep up to the fumigations of youthful garments; nor did she simply
allow any other sense to be carried along, lest the chastity kept in her
heart, =56.591= being corrupted beforehand by the soft baits, should be
tripped toward pleasures. For most of our sins come about from the senses of
our soul.
For the soul is, as a virgin in some chamber, resting in the midst of the
heart, and escorted by the five senses, as by certain handmaids.

Of what senses? Sight, taste, smell, hearing, and touch. If then no sense,
being corrupted beforehand, should wander, the soul is kept whole and
unharmed; but if it happens that the sight, remaining ungoverned, is whirled
toward the sight of youthful beauty, and receives into the depth of the heart
the fierce wave of desire through the eyes, at once, fanned by a whirlwind of
pleasures, she is carried underwater toward sin, having been shipwrecked
concerning chastity; and thereafter she suffers that which David also suffered
and said: I came into the depths of the sea, and a storm drowned me. And
likewise the hearing also, if, being opened wide, it receives the charms of
fine-voiced singing, stretching the mind toward those things, and dragging
itself wholly with the fornicating songs, overthrows her. And the taste,
filled with gluttony and much wine, corrupts the soul toward drunkenness and
beds. And the smell, being bound to the curious perfumes of the young, bound
the soul also with itself. Thus then, as some virgin, the soul is captured by
foolish handmaids, the senses, being betrayed toward the sin of incontinence.
For sin, having stupefied all the senses, after the manner of a thief, enters
stealthily into the storehouse of the heart; then, having entered, it strips
it wholly, having plundered the store of chastity. That blessed woman, then,
made secure the glance, the bearing, the step, the hearing, the sight, the
smell, the taste, the touch; and she was truly, according to the Wisdom that
says, A garden enclosed, a fountain sealed. For the blessed woman was as a
garden, no one being able to plunder it, gushing forth the aromas of chastity,
and she was as a fountain sealed by faith, from which none of the unordered
could draw the beauty of chastity.

Two elders of the people, who seemed to govern the flock, came into desire of
her, and each of them, burning with the flame of desire, was ashamed to make
manifest to the other the hidden fire of the heart. And on one of the days
each of them went off secretly from his companion to spy on Susanna; and
finding one another, and examining, they confessed to one another the cause
for which they were present; then, making a partnership in the deed of sin,
they made a careful search of the wickedness, and they watched for a time,
that they might catch her alone. It happened then on one of the days,
according to custom, that Susanna went into the garden of her husband, to
bathe because of the heat; and when she had sent out the maidservants to fetch
soap, the elders, running upon her suddenly, held her as wolves a lamb,
wishing to tear her with the frenzy of incontinence; and Susanna was alone in
the midst of the two lions, and there was no one helping, no maidservant, no
slave, no kinsman, no friend, no neighbor, except God alone who looked on from
heaven, who was able to hinder, but allowed the wrestling to take place, that
he might convict hidden thoughts, and might publish the chastity of Susanna
and the incontinence of the elders, and at the same time might show to the
women, as a very great school, the virtuous contest of Susanna. Great was the
battle, a fierce contest was laid up for Susanna, greater than Joseph's. For
he, being a man, fought with one woman; but she, being a woman, wrestled with
two men. It was a very great and delightful theater: a woman boxed with two
men; and the wondrous thing, if you hear where the wrestling-pit was opened:
in a garden, where the serpent deceived Eve. When therefore the wrestling
became ready, the heavens were opened, the trumpet cried, the contest was
ready, the president of the games looked on the wrestling from heaven, and the
people of the angels, bending forward, watched; the serpent was in the lawless
men, but faith in the chaste woman. There was much care on both sides
concerning the victory: for the elders, not to be defeated by a woman; for
Susanna, not to lose her chastity; for those the demons prepared the wages of
victory, but for Susanna the angels prepared the prizes of chastity. The
lawless men hold Susanna; =56.592= and first they wrestled with her through
words; saying what? We are elders of the people, the law has been entrusted to
us, we have received authority to loose and to bind.

There is no spy here; Be with us; for we are in desire of you. But if you are
not willing, we will bear witness against you, that a young man was with you,
and for this reason you sent the girls away from you. And see how many things
were constraining Susanna: a slander of incontinence, fear of death,
condemnation before so great a people, hatred of a husband, hatred of
kinsfolk, mourning of household servants, and, in a word, a flood of the whole
house. But none of these things was able to conquer the reasoning of the
chaste woman; for the hope of her heart was toward God. Sighing then she said:
I am straitened on every side; for if I do it, death is for me; and
if I do not do it, I shall not escape your hands. It is preferable for me, not
having done it, to fall into your hands, than to sin before the Lord. Woe is
me, those whom I thought to be shepherds, I see as wolves; those whom I
thought to be harbors of the storm-tossed, these have become a shipwreck. You
shall not defile my chastity; I do not shame my parents; I do not dishonor my
kinship; I do not grieve my husband; I do not dissolve my yoke with him by
unordered intercourse with you; I choose a lawless death rather than an
unordered bedchamber. And the husband is present with me also, not by the
presence of the body, but by the disposition of the soul, and I carry about
with myself the marks of my parents. Fear God who sees; be ashamed of the
angels who are present; understand what you are; know the law which you read,
that says: You shall not desire the wife of your neighbor.

Having said these things she cried out, calling together witnesses of their
disorder; and the two elders also cried out; the slaves of Susanna ran in, and
the maidservants; they see the two elders with her. And when the elders spoke
the words against Susanna, the slaves were greatly ashamed, because such a
word was never spoken about Susanna. And on the morrow there was a very great
council; for the decisive wrestling was still remaining, the victory
concerning the crown. All the people, of men, women, infants, was gathered;
the theater was assembled again; the men below did not know what they were
watching; but the angels in heaven recognized the matter. The elders came,
full of lawlessness; they call Susanna, as they thought, toward shame and
death; but as it had been predetermined by the just judge, toward life and
eternal glory. And they say to the people: Send for Susanna, daughter of
Helkias; and they sent. She came, and her parents, and her husband, and her
children. And Susanna came as one who ought to die because of chastity,
sighing with much reverence, being afflicted, not because she was about to
die, but because she was leaving a bad name to her parents, because she was
about to leave a reproach to her race; for she had no witness of the thing
that had happened in the garden. Many were those weeping for her, the friends,
the kinsfolk, the parents, the neighbors; the husband mourned, her house
lamented; and she herself, in pain, downcast, weeping, afflicted, stands in
the midst in much lowliness of heart. The spectacle was of all the people, of
God, of angels, of men.

The two elders stand up, wolves in the form of shepherds; they lay their hands
upon her, and in them the slandering serpent spoke. What did he speak?
Yesterday as we walked in the garden alone, this woman came in, and she
dismissed her maidservants, and the young man who was hidden came to her, and
he lay with her. And we, being in the corner of the garden, and seeing the
lawlessness taking place, ran upon them; and we were not able to get hold of
him, because he was stronger than we, and having opened the doors he leapt
out; but taking hold of this woman we asked who the young man was, and she was
not willing to tell us. These things we witness. And the synagogue believed
them, as elders of the people and judges; and they condemned her to die. And
Susanna was led away toward death, and there was no avenger of the truth, and
witness, except God alone; and he allowed what was happening to take place,
that the deed of both might be shown complete, of the lawless men the =56.593=
wickedness of incontinence and the slander, and of the blessed Susanna the
standing-fast of chastity even unto death.

Do you see until when God forsakes? And he forsakes, testing the righteous in
temptations as gold in a furnace; as he also did in the case of Abraham. Isaac
was led away to a whole-burnt offering, the altar was built, the wood was laid
on, and nowhere the sheep. A father takes the knife, and nowhere a sheep; he
rushed to slay, and then God with the voice bridled his hand. And I said these
things, that each of you, when he is tried, even if he come near death, may
not despair of the help from God, but may wait until the end. Susanna then was
led away to die; there was no one helping. When thereafter she saw that the
help of men was not present, she runs up in her mind to the faithful witness
in heaven, to the unsleeping eye, and she says with a voice: O God
everlasting, knower of the hidden things, who knows all things before their
generation, you know that they bear false witness against me; and behold I die
having done none of the things which these men wickedly devised against me.

And he who said, While you are still speaking I am present, heard her. And as
she was being led away to perish, God raised up the spirit of a younger child,
whose name was Daniel. And he cried with a great voice: I am clean from the
blood of this woman. And all the people turned, and said: What is this word
which you have spoken? And he, standing in the midst of them, said: Are you so
foolish, sons of Israel, not having examined, nor having recognized the clear
thing, you condemned a daughter of Israel? Return to the judgment-seat; for
these men have borne false witness against her. And all the people returned
with haste. And Daniel said: Separate them from one another. And he questioned
the one, saying: You who have grown old in days of evils, now your sins have
come, which you used to do. Tell me then, under what tree did you see them
=56.594= conversing with one another? And he said: Under a mastich. And Daniel
said: Rightly you have lied against your own head; for the angel of God,
having the knife, is about to split you in the midst. And having moved this
one aside, he calls the other, and says: Seed of Canaan, and not of Judah,
beauty has deceived you, and desire of the eyes has twisted your heart. Tell
then, under what tree did you see them conversing? And he said: Under a
holm-oak. And Daniel to him: Rightly you also have lied against your own head;
for the angel of God, having the sword, is about to saw you in the midst. And
all the people cried out with a great voice, saying: Blessed is the Lord who
saves all who hope in him. And on that day righteous blood was saved. And what
was said by David from the person of Susanna was fulfilled:

But your right hand took hold of me; and they sought my soul in vain, the
lawless elders; they shall go into the lowest parts of the earth; they shall
be delivered into the hand of the sword; for the angel of God, having the
sword, is about to tear them; They shall be portions of foxes. And the king
shall be gladdened in God: the husband of Susanna. Everyone who swears in him
shall be praised, that is, he who believes in him; because the mouth of those
speaking unjust things was stopped: the unjust mouths of the lawless elders.

Then the mourning of her parents was turned into joy; the husband exulted,
glorifying God; the kinsfolk rejoiced; the neighbors were of good cheer; her
whole house was gladdened, and, in a word, there was a festival of all the
people, of God, of angels, of men. Did you see a manly soul in a woman's body?
did you see a woman's chastity, whose memory remains immortal with victory?
She was glorified by men, she was magnified by angels, she was crowned by God;
emulate her, women, that you too may be counted worthy of the same honor from
God in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory and the power unto the ages
of the ages. Amen.
