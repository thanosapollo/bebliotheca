much as is among angels and the powers above angels, and as much as is in the
heavens, stand with trembling, awaiting the fearful manifestation of the
divine counsel. When we have gone down upon these, deeds will be read, and
there will be a stripping of hidden things; so that all thus know one
another's sins, as each knows his own, reading the book of his own conscience.
For he says, "I will convict you, and I will set your sins before your face";
and again, "Their counsels will surround them." And behold, a man and his
work, and a judge who cannot be cheated, having the magnificent power of the
heavenly ranks standing by, sits in high state flashing forth all
righteousness, and to each, by a true and just recompense, assigning what is
according to desert, to those standing on the right because of good deeds

of the world; =28.592= but those who have been allotted the station on the

justly against them, that they have shown themselves such, they who have
looked upon God who is by nature and alone the lover of mankind? Who will
endure the boundless shame according to conscience that is going to be at the
revealing of hidden things? Who will be able to bear the unsilenced weeping
and that bitter tear, and the gnashing of teeth, and the wailings of those
crushed in the torments, or of the pains from negligence, lying upon the midst
of their heart and justly consuming them? Who is able to tell the distress
that arises in them from not hoping for a term of change, and an end of the
punishment laid upon them, nor expecting or looking for a change again toward
living well? For that last and only fearful tribunal is just more than it is
dreadful; and it is perhaps fearful because it is just, and because it shuts
up all the ages in itself according to the just verdict. Who, taking into mind
the wailings and groans brought up from the midst of their heart, fastening
even on the marrows themselves and bitterly biting the inward parts, will be
able to be free of tears? Who could bear the lament and the wailing even apart
from the dejection upon it, and with what words of repentance they are
brought, both of each of the punished toward himself, and of all toward all?
For then each is truly a knower of his own faults, when the deeds appear naked
on themselves, free of every hindrance of deceit. Just is your judgment, O
God; for you called us, and we did not obey you; you offered us words, and we
did not attend; therefore we even made your counsels void. Justly there came
upon us destruction and ruin and tempest and affliction and siege. And now we
call upon you, and you do not hear, and we seek from you, and we do not find
mercy. For we who hated wisdom and did not choose the word of the Lord, nor
wished to attend to the counsels of God, reap such fruits of our own seeds,
and we are filled with our own folly; =28.593= for the deceit of life blinded
us, and we did not know the mysteries of God, nor did we hope at all that
there is a reward of holiness; and because of this we were filled with paths
of lawlessness. And we traveled impassable paths, that is, apart from the
divine visitation; but the way of the Lord we did not know. And what is the
wealth that has been joined to us with arrogance? All those things have passed
like a shadow and like a message running by, and like a ship going through
surging water, which, when it has passed, there is no trace to find, nor the
path of her keel in the waves. Or as of a bird that has flown through the air,
no token of its passage is found. All those things have passed, leaving us
this fire, an inheritance remaining unto all ages. Just is your judgment, O
God; for saying these things we will never again cease; we were called, and we
did not obey; we were admonished, and we did not attend. Every day we were
taught about these terrible things, and we despised what was said; the words
about these things did not grieve us then even a little; since otherwise the
facts would not grieve us so violently; what now holds our life was thought by
us to be nonsense. This we chant continually: O the sloth! O the deceit! O the
evil counsels! O the wicked bargains! O the wonder of God's just judgment and
sentence upon these things! How for each kind of our sins he has given us back
a fitting torment! For because of a pitiable and abominable pleasure of flesh
we were justly condemned to have this fearful Gehenna; we were condemned to
dwell in this gloom and darkness. Since we loved all shameful things, and
having despised all the divine goods together, we preferred the horse-races
and the theaters and the hunts, in which companies of demons danced, and every
divine watch and knowledge of angels was absent. There the dignity of gladness
was insulted in many ways; but the evil and loathsome thing of wantonness was
held in honor. Justly we have been deprived of the light now, because we did
not look upon creation to the glory of God who made it during our temporary
life; but we defiled our eyes and ears and tongue, or rather we corrupted the
soul made according to the image of God with all the sights and sounds and
words hated by God, and with jealousies against one another, and we have been
cast into this Tartarus, worn terribly by frost and depth against them. Since
we preferred pride to the high humility that procures the high and divine
things, and we thrust away the narrow and intense way of virtue because of the
exceeding softness and disposition of the flesh. Where now is our pride, and
the boastful disposition toward all, and the sluggish and easily-dissolved
softness of the flesh? O the folly! Instead of unrestrained laughter and the
wanton noise of the cheeks, there is this heavy gnashing of teeth. Not wishing
to practice the learning of the divine and saving words, but preferring the
excess of drunkenness to the divine praise through speech, we are now terribly
punished with a fearful thirst of the tongue in vain. And we also reap a fruit
worthy =28.596= of our hatred toward our brothers, and of envy and deceit, and
of hypocrisy (from which and through which comes envy), and of railing and
slander, and of remembrance of wrongs, and of falsehood, and of perjury: this
sleepless worm, eating the depth of our soul insatiably. Since we warped the
straightness of love, having corrupted it, creating for ourselves a willing
evil worm through hatred, and digging a more wicked crookedness through
falsehood, through which we were not united to God who is straight and
upright. For he himself said through the holy prophet David: "A crooked heart
did not cleave to me." Therefore we receive things worthy of what we did; or
rather we are paid the wages worthy of our own disposition according to
purpose: for the fire of pleasure, the fire of Gehenna; and for the darkness
of ignorance and temporary deceit, eternal darkness and a punishing and
sleepless worm; of the worm that through hatred and falsehood made the heart
crooked and warped, and of the noises of wanton cheeks, the noise of seething
and the gnashing of teeth; and for the height of vain pride and of
dissipation, a fall and a yawning depth and the straitness of freezing; and to
speak simply, for each of our willing evils we justly receive back each one's
corresponding and unwilling punishment. O our pitiableness! that we wretched
ones did not quench the fire of the flesh through fasting and vigil and the
study of the divine oracles, which are good and easy extinguishers of the
fire, so as not to be fried now in this fire. Why did we not accustom
ourselves to see and hear, and the tongue to speak, according to nature, so
that we might not now have this gloom and the heaviest silence, but that we
too might enjoy the light and the word and the wisdom of God with the saints,
as having become beholders and hearers and hymn-singers of the divine glory?
Or if through love we had put to death the willing worm of hatred, and through
truth had made falsehood vanish, we would now have stood free of this burning
worm, and of the rest of the things that now hold our most terrible life. Just
is the judgment of God. We do not find what we sought; the door of the kingdom
of the heavens is not opened to us. For since we did not knock at the door of
good things through action, we do not receive the enjoyment of the eternal
goods; since we did not ask through prayer for the grace of knowledge; (for we
had no desire for any of the divine things, but, having bound the mind to
earthly things, we corrupted our whole life together with them;) and all those
things have passed, vanished like smoke; but the account of the judgment upon
them remains forever inescapable. And these things they, learning afterward
the power of the divine justice from what they suffer, will say to one
another, to take it in a figure. But I, wretched, becoming what, leaning on
what deeds, shall I hope to be delivered from the fearful condemnation? Being
barren of every virtue and every knowledge, I fear that, bound hand and foot,
I may be cast into a dark and gloomy land, =28.597= into a land of eternal
darkness, where there is no light nor seeing the life of mortals, as one who
has bound the practical virtues of the soul willingly with the passions, and
from the divine course of the angelic way of life has hindered its steps. Be
propitious, Christ, and save us from this pain. For those with whom in this
age we chose by will, according to our intent, to be together through our
wicked practices, with these we will reasonably be condemned of necessity to
be in the age to come, though we do not wish it. For there will truly be,
there will be a time, when a fearful trumpet will sound, having a strange
voice, and this whole will be dissolved, the visible order in it falling
apart; the whole apparent world will pass away, receiving its own
consummation; and the now-expected world of the things that are will appear,
bringing mysteries strange to eyes and ears and thoughts. And God will render
to each according to desert, toward what he did through the body, whether good
or worthless; giving a great and fearful and final closing to all. But may it
be that we all take up both the saving fear of Gehenna and the precious
longing for the kingdom of the heavens: the fear producing in us a complete
abstinence and inactivity from evils; and the longing also urging us
cheerfully toward the deeds of good things, that we may obtain the enjoyment
of the promised goods in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory and the
power with the unoriginate (Father) and his all-holy and good and life-giving
Spirit, now and always and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
