ORATION 37.
On the saying of the Gospel: "When Jesus had finished these sayings," and what
follows.
1. Jesus who chose the fishermen, himself also nets, and changes place from
place. For what reason? Not only that he may gain more of the god-loving
through the visitation, but it seems to me also that he may hallow more
places. He becomes to the =36.284= Jews as a Jew, that he may gain Jews; to
those under the law, as under the law, that he may redeem those under the law;
to the weak, as weak, that he may save the weak. He becomes all things to all,
that he may gain all. But why do I say, all things to all? What even Paul did
not endure to say of himself, this I find the Savior suffering. For he does
not become a Jew only, nor only take upon himself as many of the strange and
wicked names, but even that which is stranger than all these, even sin itself
and curse itself; he is not so, but he is called so. For how is he sin, who
also frees us from sin? And how a curse, who redeems us from the curse of the
law? But that he may display the lowliness even as far as these things,
forming us unto humility, which procures exaltation. As I said then, he
becomes a fisherman, he condescends to all, he nets, he bears all things, that
he may bring up from the depth the fish, the human being swimming in the
unstable and salty waves of life.
2. Therefore even now, when he finished these sayings, he removed from
Galilee, and came into the borders of Judea beyond the Jordan. He sojourns in
Galilee well, that the people sitting in darkness may see a great light. He
removes into Judea, that he may persuade those who have risen from the letter
to follow the Spirit. Now he teaches on a mountain, now he discourses on
plains, now he passes into a ship, now he rebukes storms. Perhaps he even
receives sleep, that he may bless sleep also; perhaps he even toils, that he
may hallow the place also; perhaps he even weeps, that he may make the tear
praiseworthy. He passes from place to place, who is contained by no place, the
timeless, the bodiless, the incomprehensible. The same both was and becomes;
and he was beyond time, and he comes under time; and he was invisible, and he
is seen. In the beginning he was, and he was with God, and he was God. The
"was" is third, confirmed by the number. What he was he emptied, and what he
was not he assumed; not becoming two, but deigning to become one out of the
two. =36.285= For both are God, that which assumed, and that which was
assumed; two natures running together into one, not two sons; let the blending
not be falsely accused. This one who is so great, so much; but what have I
suffered? Again I have fallen into human words. For how is the simple "so
much"? And how is the quantity-less "so great"? But grant pardon to the word;
with a small instrument I speak about the greatest things. And the great one
will bear this, the long-suffering, the formless and bodiless nature, the
words as if about a body and weaker than the truth. For if he received flesh,
he also bears such a word.
3. And great crowds followed him, and he healed them there, where the
wilderness was greater. If he had remained upon his own eminence, if he had
not condescended to the weakness, if he had remained what he was, keeping
himself unapproachable and incomprehensible, few perhaps would have followed;
and I do not know if even few, perhaps only Moses, and he so far as barely to
see the hinder parts of God. For he split the cloud, having come outside the

bodily weight, or having been contracted from the senses; but the subtlety of
God, or incorporeity, or I do not know how one would name it, how would he
have seen it, being a body, and applying sensible eyes? But since he is
emptied for us, since he comes down (and I mean by emptying a kind of lowering
and lessening of the glory), through this he becomes containable.
4. But grant me pardon in the midst, as I again suffer a certain human
passion. I am filled with wrath and grief for my Christ, and may you too
suffer with me, when I see my Christ dishonored for this, for which he was
most justly to be honored. Is he therefore without honor, tell me, because for
you he is lowly? Is he therefore a creature, because he cares for the
creature? Is he therefore under time, because he visits those under time? Yet
he bears all things, he receives all things. And what wonder? He endured
blows, he bore with spittings, he tasted gall because of my tasting. He bears
even now being stoned, not only by those who insult, but even by us ourselves
who seem to be pious. For for one discoursing about the bodiless to use bodily
names is perhaps of those who insult and stone; but pardon, again I say, to
the weakness. For we stone not willingly, but not having another way to speak,
using what we have. You are called Word, and you are above word; you are above
=36.288= light, and you are named light; you hear fire, not being perceptible,
but because you purge the light and wicked matter; a sword, because you cut
the worse from the better; a fan, because you purge the threshing-floor, and
sending away whatever is light and windy, you store whatever is heavy and full
into the storehouses above; an axe, because you cut down the unfruitful
fig-tree after being long-suffering, because you cut out the roots of
wickedness; a door, because of the bringing in; a way, because we go straight;
a sheep, because a sacrifice; a high priest, because you offer the body; Son,
because of the Father. Again I move the tongues; again some rave against
Christ, or rather against me, who have been deemed worthy to become a herald
of the Word. I become as John: A voice of one crying in the wilderness, a
wilderness once even waterless, but now even exceedingly inhabited.
5. Yet as I was saying, that I may run back to my word, for this reason great
crowds were following him, because he condescends to our weaknesses. Then
what? And the Pharisees came to him, it says, tempting him and saying: Is it
lawful for a man to put away his wife for every cause? Again Pharisees tempt;
again those who read the law do not know the law; again those who happen to be
expositors of the law need other teachers. It was not enough that Sadducees
tempting about resurrection, and lawyers inquiring about perfection, and
Herodians about tribute, and others about authority. But someone also again
asks about marriage the untempted one, the creator of the pairing, who from
the first cause constituted this whole race of humans. And answering he said
to them: Have you not read that he who made them from the beginning made them
male and female? He knows how to dissolve some of the questions, and to stop
others' mouths. When he is asked, By what authority do you do these things?
because of the extreme lack of education of those who asked, he himself asks
in return: The baptism of John, was it from heaven, or from men? And from both
sides he entangles those who ask. So that we too, imitating Christ, are able
at times to stop the mouths of those who converse with us over- =36.289=
curiously, and by more absurd counter-questions to dissolve the absurdity of
the questions. For we too are wise in the vain things at times, that I may
boast the things of folly. But when he sees a question needing reasoning, then
he does not think the questioners unworthy of intelligent answers.
6. The question which you asked seems to me to honor chastity, and to demand a
humane answer; chastity, concerning which I see the many ill-disposed, and
their law unequal and

irregular. Why ever did they punish the female, but permit the male? And a
woman having planned evil concerning a husband's bed commits adultery, and
bitter from this are the penalties of the laws; but a man whoring against a
woman is without account? I do not accept this legislation, I do not praise
the custom. Men were the lawgivers, therefore the legislation is against
women; since they have also given the children under authority to the fathers,
but left the weaker uncared for. But God is not so; but, Honor your father and
your mother, which is the first commandment, that it may be well with you,
lying in promises. And, He who speaks evil of father or mother, let him die
the death. Likewise he both honored the good and punished the evil. And, A
father's blessing strengthens children's houses; but a mother's curse uproots
foundations. You see the equality of the legislation. One maker of man and
woman, one dust both, one image, one law, one death, one resurrection.
Likewise we have come from man and woman; one debt is owed by the children to
the parents.
7. How then do you demand chastity, but do not bring it in return? How do you
ask what you do not give? How, being a body of equal honor, do you legislate
unequally? And if you look at the worse: the woman sinned, this Adam also; the
serpent deceived both. The one was not found weaker, and the other stronger.
But do you reckon the better? Christ saves both by the sufferings. Did he
become flesh for man? This also for woman. Did he die for man? The woman too
is saved by the death. He is named of the seed of David; do you think perhaps
the man is honored? But he is also born of a Virgin, this also on behalf of
women. Therefore the two, he says, =36.292= shall be one flesh; and let the
one flesh have the equal honor. And Paul also legislates chastity by the
example. How, and in what manner? This mystery is great; but I speak unto
Christ and unto the Church. It is good for the woman to reverence Christ
through the husband; it is good also for the husband not to dishonor the
Church through the wife. Let the wife, he says, fear the husband; for Christ
also. But also let the husband cherish the wife; for Christ also the Church.
Or rather let us still labor further upon the saying.
8. Milk the milk, and there will be butter; examine also and perhaps you may
find something more nourishing in it. For the word seems to me here to decline
second marriage. For if there are two Christs, two also husbands, two also
wives; but if one Christ, one head of the Church, and one flesh; and let the
second be spat out. And if he should hinder the second, what account of the
third? The first is law, the second concession, the third lawlessness. And
what is beyond this is swinish, such as does not even have many examples of
the wickedness. The law gives the divorcement for every cause; but Christ not
for every cause; but he allows only to be separated from the fornicatress, and
in all the other things he commands to philosophize. And the fornicatress,
because she adulterates the race; but in all the other things let us endure
and philosophize; or rather endure and philosophize, as many as have received
the yoke of marriage. If you see paints or under-writings, unadorn; and if a
rash tongue, make it modest; if a whorish laugh, make it downcast; if expense,
or drink without measure, contract it; if untimely goings-out, fetter them; if
a lifted eye, chastise it. But do not cut rashly, do not separate. It is
unclear what is in danger, the cutting, or the being cut. Let the fountain of
water, he says, be your own, and let no stranger share with you; and, Let the
colt of your graces, and the stag of your friendship, company with you. You
therefore do not become a strange river, nor strive to please other women
rather than your own wife. And if you are carried elsewhere, you also
legislate the wantonness for your own member. Thus the Savior. =36.293=

9. But what of the Pharisees? The word appears harsh to these. For even other
things that are well said displease, both the Pharisees then, and the
Pharisees now. For not race only, but also manner, makes a Pharisee. Thus I
know also an Assyrian, and an Egyptian, him who is ranked with these by
choice. What then do the Pharisees say? If the case is so with the wife, they
say, it is not expedient to marry. And do you now learn this, O Pharisee, the
"It is not expedient to marry"? But formerly you did not know, when you saw
widowhoods, and orphanhoods, and untimely deaths, and the mournings succeeding
the applauses, and the tombs upon the bridal chambers, and childlessness, and
evil offspring, and unfinished births and motherless ones, and all the comedy
or tragedy about these things? For to say both is most fitting. Is it
expedient to marry? I too accept it; for marriage is honorable, and the bed
undefiled; but it is expedient for the moderate, not for the insatiable, and
those who wish to honor the flesh more than is due. When marriage is this
only, marriage and pairing and desire of succession of children, marriage is
good; for it brings in more who please God. But when it kindles matter, and
surrounds with thorns, and is found as it were a way of wickedness, then I too
speak: It is not expedient to marry.
10. Marriage is good; but I cannot say that it is also loftier than virginity.
For virginity would not even be something great, if it were not fairer than a
fair thing. Do not then be angry, as many as are under a yoke. One must obey
God rather than humans. Yet be bound together with one another, both virgins
and wives, and be one in the Lord, and one another's adornment. There would be
no unmarried, if there were not marriage. For whence did a virgin come into
this life? Marriage would not be venerable, if it were not bearing virgin
fruit both to God and to the life. Honor you also your mother, from whom you
have come. Honor you also her who is from a mother, and a mother. She is not a
mother, but she is a bride of Christ. The appearing beauty is not hidden; but
the unseen is seen by God. All the glory of the daughter of the King is
within, clothed in golden fringes, embroidered, whether by deeds, or also
=36.296= by contemplations. And let her under the yoke also be something of
Christ; and the virgin wholly Christ's. Let the one not be bound altogether to
the world; and let the other not become of the world at all. For what the part
is to her under the yoke, this is complete to the virgin. Have you taken up
the citizenship of angels? Have you been ranked with the unyoked? Do not be
brought down into flesh, do not be brought down into matter; do not be married
to the matter, even if otherwise you remain unmarried. An eye committing
fornication does not guard virginity, a tongue committing fornication is mixed
with the evil one. Feet walking disorderly are accused of disease, or danger.
Let the mind also be virgin; let it not roam, let it not wander, let it not
carry in itself forms of wicked things. And the form is a part of fornication;
let it not idol-make in the soul the things that are hated.
11. And he said to them: Not all receive the saying, but those to whom it has
been given. Do you see the loftiness of the matter? It is found nearly even
uncontainable. For how is it not better than flesh, that what has come from
flesh should not beget into flesh? For how is it not angelic that she who is
bound with flesh should not live according to flesh, but be loftier than the
nature? The flesh bound to the world, but the reasoning led up to God; the
flesh weighed down, but the reasoning gave wings; the flesh bound, but the
longing loosed. Stretch wholly toward God, O virgin, with the soul; for this
very thing I legislate both to men and to women; and nothing of the other
things that are so to the many shall appear fair to you; not birth, not
wealth, not a throne, not power, not the beauty imagined in good color and
composition of members, a plaything of time and disease. If you have emptied
the whole power of affection toward God, if the things longed for should not
be two for you, both the flowing and the invisible; then you have been so
wounded

by the chosen arrow, and have so learned the beauty of the bridegroom, that
you can even say from the bridal drama and song, that You are sweetness, and
wholly desire.
12. You see the streams included in the leaden channels, that by being
exceedingly straitened, and being carried toward one, they so far go out from
the nature of water =36.297= often, that even toward the above the one pushed
from behind always goes. Thus if you compress the longing, and are wholly
joined to God, you will go above, you will not fall below, you will not be
poured out, you will remain wholly Christ's, until you even see Christ your
bridegroom. Keep yourself unapproachable, both in word, and in deed, and in
life, and in thought, and in movement. From every side the Evil One busies
himself about you, he spies all things, where he may strike, where he may
wound, lest he find something bared and ready for a blow. By how much he sees
you purer, by so much more he is contentious to stain; for the stains of a
bright garment are more conspicuous. Let not eye draw eye, nor laughter
laughter, nor familiarity night, nor night destruction. For what is drawn away
and stolen by parts has an unperceived harm for the present, but arrives at
the sum of wickedness.
13. Not all, he says, receive this saying, but those to whom it has been
given. When you hear "it has been given," do not suffer anything heretical, do
not bring in the natures, the dusty and the spiritual and the middle. For
there are some so evilly disposed as to think that some are of a nature
altogether perishing, and others of a nature being saved, and others have it
thus, however the choice may lead toward the worse, or the better. For that
one has more or less aptitude than another, I too accept; but that the
aptitude alone does not suffice for perfection; but that it is reasoning that
calls this out, that the nature may come forth into work, just as a fire-stone
struck with iron, and thus fire comes to be. When you hear, To whom it has
been given, add, it has been given to those who are called and to those who so
incline. For even when you hear, not of him who wills, nor of him who runs,
but of God who shows mercy, I counsel you to suppose the same. For since there
are some who are so high-minded over the successes, as to give the whole to
themselves, and nothing to him who made, and gave wisdom, and is the supplier
of the good things, the word teaches these, that even to wish well needs the
help from God; or rather, the very choosing the things that ought is a divine
thing and a gift of God's philanthropy; for it is necessary both that what is
in our power be, and that we be saved from God. Therefore he says: Not of him
who wills; that =36.300= is, not of him who wills only, nor of him who runs
only, but also of God who shows mercy. Then, since even the willing is from
God, he has reasonably referred the whole to God. However much you may run,
however much you may contend, you need him who gives the crown. If the Lord
should not build a house, in vain have they labored who build it. If the Lord
should not guard a city, in vain have they kept vigil who guard it. I know, he
says, that the race is not to the light, nor the war to the strong, nor the
victory of those who fight, nor the harbors of those who sail well; but it
belongs to God both to work victory, and to bring the hull safe into harbors.
14. And this being said and understood elsewhere, and perhaps it is necessary
to add also what has come upon the things said, that I may grant my wealth to
you also. The mother of the sons of Zebedee asked, having suffered something
loving of children, and not knowing the measure of the things asked, yet
pardonable through excess of affection, and goodwill owed to children. For
nothing is more tender-hearted than a mother; and I say this, that I may
legislate that mothers be honored. Therefore that mother asked Jesus, that one
sit on the right, and one on the left. But what does the Savior? He asks
first, if they are able to drink the cup which he himself was about to drink.
And when this too was confessed, and the Savior accepted it (for he knew that
they too were being perfected by the same, or rather that they would be

perfected), what does he say? They shall drink the cup, but to sit on my right
and on my left is not mine, he says, to give this, but to those to whom it has
been given. Is the ruling mind then nothing? Is the labor nothing? Is the word
nothing? Is the philosophy nothing? Is the fasting nothing? Is the keeping
vigil nothing? The lying on the ground? The dripping fountains of tears? Is
none of these, but according to some allotment both Jeremiah is sanctified,
and others are alienated from the womb?
15. I fear lest some absurd reasoning also enter, as of the soul having lived
as a citizen elsewhere, then having been bound to this body; and from the
citizenship there, some having received the prophecy, and others being
condemned, as many as have lived badly. But since to suppose this is
exceedingly absurd, and not ecclesiastical (for let others play about such
doctrines, but for us to play such things is not safe), there too to the "And
to whom it has been given," add the "to those who are worthy"; who have
received being such not only from =36.301= the Father, but have also given it
to themselves.
16. For there are eunuchs, who were eunuchized from a mother's womb, and what
follows. I very much wished to have something manly to say about the eunuchs.
Do not be high-minded, you who are eunuchs from nature. For the thing of
chastity is perhaps involuntary. For it has not come to a trial, nor has the
being chaste been tested through experience. For the good from nature is
untested; but that from choice is praiseworthy. What thanks to fire for
burning? For it has burning from nature. What thanks to water for being
carried downward? For it has this from him who crafted it. What thanks to snow
of the coldness? Or to the sun of shining? For it shines even if it does not
wish. Grant me the willing of the better things. And you grant it, if having
become flesh, you become spiritual; if being drawn by the lead of the flesh,
you are winged by the word; if you are found heavenly, having become lowly; if
having been bound with flesh, you appear above flesh.
17. Since then the minding of bodily things is not praiseworthy, I ask some
other thing of the eunuchs. Do not commit fornication about the Godhead.
Having been yoked to Christ, do not dishonor Christ. Being perfected by the
Spirit, do not make the Spirit of equal honor with yourselves. If I were still
pleasing humans, Paul says, I would not be a slave of Christ. If I were
serving a creature, I would not be named a Christian. For in what is the
Christian honorable? Is it not that Christ is God? If I do not suffer this as
a human, being mixed with him by friendship. And yet I also honor Peter, but I
am not called a Petrine; and Paul, but I have not been called a Pauline. I do
not accept being named from humans, having come to be from God. Thus if,
because you have taken him as God, for this you are called Christian, both may
you be called, and may you remain in the name, or in the thing. But if,
because you love Christ, for this you are called from him, you assign him
nothing more than the other appellations, which are set from some pursuit or
deed.
18. You see those who are eager about the horse-races. Who are named from the
colors =36.304= and from the parties with which they have stood. And you know
the names, even if I do not say them. If thus you hear "Christian," the
appellation is exceedingly small, even if you adorn yourself upon it; but if
because you have also taken him as God, show by the works that you have so
taken him. If the Son is a creature, even now also you serve the creation
beside the creator. If the Holy Spirit is a creature, you have been baptized
in vain, and in the two parts you are sound, or rather not even in those; but
in the one you are altogether in danger. Suppose the Trinity to be one pearl,
like from every side, and equally gleaming. Whatever the pearl should suffer,
the whole grace of the stone has vanished. Thus when you dishonor the Son,
that you may honor the Father, he does not receive your honor. The Father is
not glorified upon dishonor of the Son. If a wise son gladdens a father, how
much more shall the honor of the Son become honor of the

Father? And if you also accept this: Child, do not be glorified upon dishonor
of a father; thus neither is a Father glorified upon dishonor of the Son. If
you dishonor the Holy Spirit, the Son does not receive your honor. For even if
not as Son from the Father, yet at any rate from the same Father. Either honor
the whole, or dishonor the entire, that you may happen to be a mind consistent
with yourself. I do not accept your piety from a half. I wish you to be wholly
pious, but as I wish. Pardon the passion. I ache even for those who hate. You
were my member, even if you are now cut off; you will even become a member
perhaps; therefore I also speak humanely. These things because of the eunuchs,
that they may be chaste about the Godhead.
19. For not only the sin about a body is called fornication and adultery, but
also whatever you have sinned, and especially the lawlessness about the
divine. From where shall we furnish this? Perhaps you demand this: They
committed fornication, he says, in their pursuits. Do you see even a shameless
deed of fornication? And, They committed adultery, he says, in the wood. Do
you see even a certain adulterous worship? Do not then commit adultery
according to soul, being chaste in the body; do not show the being chaste
involuntarily in the flesh from the things in which you are not chaste, in
which you are able to commit fornication. Why have you made the impiety yours?
Why are you all carried toward the worse, so that it is the same henceforth
either to be called a eunuch or an impious =36.305= one? Become with the men,
at last at least think something manly, flee the women's quarters, let not the
shame of the proclamation be added to the shame of the name. Do you wish that
we still wait a little upon the word? Or do the things said have a surfeit?
Yet let the eunuchs also be honored by what follows; for the word is
praiseworthy.
20. For there are eunuchs, he says, who were born thus from a mother's womb;
and there are eunuchs who were eunuchized by humans; and there are eunuchs who
eunuchized themselves because of the kingdom of the heavens. He who is able to
receive it, let him receive it. It seems to me that the word, having stood off
from the bodies, types the loftier things through the bodies. For to stand the
word as far as the bodily eunuchs is perhaps both small and exceedingly weak
and unworthy of a word; but we must think out something worthy of the Spirit.
Therefore some seem to incline toward the good from nature. And when I say of
nature, I do not dishonor the choice, but I set both, both the aptitude toward
the fair, and that which leads into work the naturally apt. And there are
certain others, whom the word purifies, cutting out the passions. These I
consider to be those eunuchized by humans, when the teaching word, having
divided the better from the worse, and having sent away the one, and having
legislated the other (as the "Turn from evil, and do good"), crafts the
spiritual chastity. I praise this eunuchizing also, and I praise exceedingly
both the teachers likewise and the taught, because the ones cut well, and the
others were cut more fairly.
21. And there are those who eunuchized themselves because of the kingdom of
the heavens. Others have not met with teachers, but have become praiseworthy
teachers of themselves. A mother did not teach you what ought, a father did
not teach you, nor an elder, nor a bishop, nor any other of those entrusted
with the teaching. But having moved the word in you, but having kindled the
spark of the fair through the choice, you eunuchized yourself, you cut out the
root, you banished the organs of wickedness, you acquired so great a habit of
virtue, that even the carrying toward the worse became almost impossible for
you. Therefore I praise this eunuchizing also, and perhaps more than the
others. He who is able to receive it, let him receive it. Receive which
portion you wish, either follow the teacher, or become a teacher of yourself.
One thing only is shameful, that the passions not be cut. But concerning the
passions being cut, differ in nothing. Both the one teaching is a fashioning
of God, and you have come to be =36.308= from there;

and whether the teacher snatch the grace, or the fair become yours, it is fair
likewise.
22. Only let us cut ourselves from the passions, lest some root of bitterness
growing up trouble. Only let us follow the image, only let us reverence the
archetype. Cut the bodily passions, cut also the psychic. For by how much a
soul is more honorable than a body, by so much more honorable is it to purify
a soul than a body. And if the purification of the body is of the praiseworthy
things, consider for me how much that of the soul is greater and loftier. Cut
the Arian impiety, cut the evil-opinion of Sabellius. Neither join more than
is well, nor divide badly, neither contract the three into one person, nor
work three alienations of natures. Both the one is praiseworthy, well
understood, and the three well divided, when the division is of persons, but
not of Godhead.
23. These things I legislate also to laymen, these things I command also to
elders, these things also to those entrusted to rule. Help the word, all as
many as have the being able to help from God. It is a great thing to check
murder, and to punish adultery, and to chasten theft; much more to legislate
piety, and to grant a sound word. My word will not be able to do so much
fighting for the holy Trinity, as the command, if you stop the mouths of those

24. And for the rest let there be a prayer on behalf of those who have come
together. Men together and women, both those ruled and those ruling, old men
and young men with virgins, every kind of age, bear every loss, that of money,
that of bodies; but one thing only do not receive, being damaged in the
Godhead. I worship Father, I worship the Son, I worship the Holy Spirit; or
rather we worship, I both before all, who say these things, I also after all
and with all, in Christ himself our Lord, to whom the glory and the might unto
the ages. Amen.
