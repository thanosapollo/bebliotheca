1. Of the same, on the saying of the Gospel, and on virginity; an
exhortation to those who have fallen.
=64.37=
Nothing without purpose, and nothing foreign to our salvation, has the
divine word handed down in enigma. Hence it urged me to send out my
mind and to search out the dense meaning, and to see what it is that
the saying of the Master in the Gospel wishes. For he spoke many
things to many in parables, to some making clear the difficult things,
to others revealing the plain things to us, making the word of
salvation, as good and philanthropic, delivering the trial of the
enigmas clear and unconfused: in one way enabling us to unfold the
intimation from daily affairs, in another to receive the sense from
the very meaning of what is said.

The Master said that narrow and confined is the way that leads to
life, and few are those who find it; but broad and roomy is the way
that leads to destruction, and many enter through it. The word is one
of amazement, the manner one of ignorance: What, he says, broad, is
the way of destruction? Christ does not know this way; for it did not
come into being through him. The word is therefore one of sympathy;
the enigma is a healing. O the philanthropic kindness! O the Master's
providence! He sets forth together both the victory and the defeat,
together both the pity and the sentence: I know, he says, how slippery
the race of men is, how earthbound, how eye-tempting, how easily
sliding, how easily tripped, how unmanly, how easily captured, unless
it be steadied by faith. He heard of a broad way, and ran like a bird
upon a snare; he heard of a narrow one, and flees salvation, not
having searched out the usefulness of either. For =64.38= the struggle
is required of him, and he flees the arena; and above all, having
heard that Many are the afflictions of the righteous, and out of them
all the Lord will deliver them, he did not run to the helper, but
flees with flight, not knowing that the good escapes him and clings to
the worse. O the folly! Having heard Many are the afflictions of the
righteous, did you bend, O man? And having heard Many are the scourges
of the sinner, were you not terrified? Rather you were terrified at
the scourge, but did not quench the passion of sin. What, broad and
roomy is the way of destruction? Here he calls life a way, since the
broad seems to the deceived the pleasure of the world. But what among
the sensible is more wretched than this? For the narrow, thought so,
leads into the heavens, but the broad, seeming so, drags down those
who follow it into Hades. Do not suppose, beloved, that that way is
truly broad, or good, because the Master used such a word.

For as Solomon says, honey drips from the lips of a harlot woman; but
the saying is contrary to nature; for how is it possible for honey
ever to drip from the lips of a woman? But the word called honey the
seeming sweetness of deception and the delight of vain pleasure; so
here too he calls the breadth of way the varied passions of pleasures,
and all the enjoyment of life.

Hear me, you who have bowed the neck and received the good and light
and blessed yoke, monks and virgins; hear me, all you who have
dedicated yourselves and given your bodies to the Lord; hear me, the
crowns of the heavenly Bridegroom; hear me, you who are brighter than
the sun; hear me, the entrances of the kingdom; hear me, the friends
of God; =64.39= hear me, the dancers of David,

hear me, the offspring of Elizabeth; hear me, the companions of John;
hear me, the heirs of God; hear me, those who carry about in the arena
the arena and the victory and the awarder of the prize; hear me, the
sisters of Mary; hear me, the mothers and brides and handmaids of
Christ; hear not me, I beseech you, but the Apostle saying: I beseech
you therefore, present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy,
acceptable to God. Trustworthy is the one who beseeches, and he will
not refuse the gift. Behold who it is that beseeches you, and shudder
at the request: he who ascended to the third heaven, he who was
counted worthy of the voice of God, he who heard unutterable words,
beseeches: Present your bodies pure to God. Why do you beseech, Paul?
What do the virgins matter to you? Did you not say, Concerning
virgins I have no commandment of the Lord? Did you not say, Marriage
is honorable? Did you not say, Do not deprive one another of the
union? Why now do you preach chastity? Then why do you care any longer
about the virgins? From fleshly marriage and corruption you snatched
Thecla and saved her; why do you care any longer about the rest of the
virgins? Yes, he says, I care greatly for the pure and passionless
marriage. For if I mediated for the marriage of men, much more shall I
mediate for that of Christ with all zeal; I am the bridesman of
Christ; the Master is not content with me for one maiden, he does not
want one marriage; great is his bride-price, great the inheritance.

Therefore he sent me to gather many to him; whence he said: I have
betrothed you to one husband, to present a pure virgin to Christ.
Behold how much precision the Apostle spoke, lest you suppose the
marriage to be fleshly. O blindness! O folly! Who would not long for
the immortal and unwearied marriage? the pure bed, the undefiled
chamber, the unstained childbearing, the immortal union, the
passionless generation of children, the mourningless love of the
husband, the costless bride-price, the eternal enjoyment, the carefree
life, the dwelling together with Christ, the life with the angels, the
sweetness of God-befitting songs, the all-virtuous fragrance, the
all-bright lamp, the universal freedom, the journey toward God, the
dance with the angels, the refreshment of the body, the purity of the
soul? For she who is joined in marriage to a man worries how she may
please flesh that is being corrupted; she has not yet given birth, and
she laments her mourning; lament often over the husband, lament over
barrenness and childlessness, lament over much childbearing, when
these die; lament over children walking disorderly. But the virgin of
the Lord worries how she may please the Lord. Hear with precision the
Apostle crying: I have betrothed you to one husband, a pure virgin, to
present to Christ. And he did not say, You, but, You in the plural,
lest you suppose that he speaks of fleshly marriage. I beseech you
therefore, he says, present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy,
acceptable to God. Common is the command, but it applies especially to
those who practice holiness. For those who wish to approach the Holy
One holily, if they succeed here, obtain great and shining crowns; but
those who undertook and did not complete it, to them the fall becomes
extraordinary. For it is a terrible thing to fall from a height, and
the things themselves bear witness, rather than to fall from the
ground. A poor man, if he lives in poverty to the end, bears the
reproach more lightly; but for a rich man who has been driven into
poverty, the outcome becomes worse than death. Thus, I suppose, the
tear is more bitter and the mourning more lamentable for the loss of
virginity than for those who daily, like swine, roll in the mire and
sin, since for them the habit of sin has in the end become the craft
of ease; but for those who have corrupted the temple of God it is
fitting, I suppose, to mourn and lament, to beat the breast and pluck
out the hair, because such and so great a treasure has been dug
through all at once.

But what is it to you, =64.40= O virgin? When, or what warlike sight,
deceived you? What swords? What spears? What shields? What tumult of
soldiers forced you so quickly to depart from your safety? You were
both city and temple of Christ, and wall and guard of the city and of
the gate. But O the lament! How did you allow the enemy to open it
without necessity? How, beguiled in a brief hour, did you betray that
through which Christ went out and sealed? O the evil war! You broke
through the wall, you corrupted your holy temple, you transgressed the
covenants of the marriage, you wiped out the writings, you set at
naught the bride-price, you insulted the bed of Christ; but you also
lost the pledge. Do you not see the laws here, what punishments await
the adulteress? But what will you do, you who did not insult the bed
of a fellow slave, but that of our Master Jesus Christ, who lives
forever, having entangled yourself with flesh that is being corrupted
and ill-smelling? How I shall mourn you I do not know; for wounded by
no one warring, you cut yourself off without a sword. What I shall
judge you I do not know: unnatural, or conscienceless, or
soul-destroying, or self-murderer.

For so great was the sight of that madness, that it did not find the
dream worthy of mourning. Did you not remember the covenants of
Christ? Did you not remember the chastity in which Christ daily,
beside your side and your bosom, invisibly and mystically leaps and
exults? And why so? Because you too were a Mary, so long as you kept
your virginity. What wax then of wickedness melted your eyes? What
eclipse darkened you, so as to make the members of Christ members of
harlotry? O the mourning! You preferred the harlot's ill odor to the
virginal fragrance? You exchanged the mire of pleasure for the myrrh?
Did you not remember that hour full of compunction, in which angels
and men together rejoiced, holding lamps, sending you forth into the
bridal chamber of Christ, singing the songs of David, and all
proclaiming you the poor and needy bride of Christ and queen? O who
would not mourn the queen who departs, having joined herself to the
rottenness of worms, or despising him with whom she was about to live
and to reign as consort, and desiring him who was about to be cast by
the king into the fire of Gehenna? Why did you dig such a pit for
yourself? But the breadth of the way deceived you. What breadth, I
beseech you? What did the guide of destruction, the broker of Gehenna,
promise you? What enjoyment of life did the man-killer promise? Did
you not hear, O virgin, the Master saying in the Gospels: If you
should gain the whole world, but your soul be harmed, what profit is
it to you? But O blindness! O folly! O darkening! Consider, O virgin,
the destruction; consider whom you prefer, whom you set at naught, and
what he who deceives grants you: a leaning of pleasure kneaded in mud,
and a drop of mire mingled in a virginal womb by sin, leading away to
the fire of Gehenna and the unending worm. Shudder at that
unseemliness. For can this pleasure be called enjoyment of life? Is
this luxury, this breadth? What is this consent of madness? But be
sober in all things and say: for we are not ignorant of our own
thoughts. Rejoice therefore always, as it is written; Let your
moderation be known to all men; the fear of God let it be established
in your heart. Do not become a soldier who throws away the shield, do
not be a coward and slothful workman, do not flee the crown.

Life is short, but the judgment long. Looking toward it, brother,
sound out your heart together with the Holy One, and say: Be of good
courage, and let your heart be strengthened, and wait for the Lord;
imitate David, and with one throw of the stone strike down the enemy.
Angels stand as beholders of your life; for, he says, we were made a
theater to the world and to angels and to men; if they see you
conquering, they rejoice at your success; if conquered, they depart
grieved, but the demons laugh at you.

Draw therefore instead of a sword the fear of God; for the fear of God
is as a two-edged sword, cutting down every wicked desire. =64.41=
Take therefore always the fear of God, remembering continually the
last day, when the heavens being kindled shall be dissolved, when the
stars shall fall as leaves, and the sun and the moon shall be
darkened, and shall not give their light; when the Son of Man shall be
revealed, and shall come down from the heavens upon the earth, and the
powers of the heavens shall be shaken; when the swoops of angels and
the voices of the trumpets that are coming, fire burning before him
and running through shall flood the inhabited world, around him a
storm exceedingly, terrible earthquakes and lightnings, which never
were, nor are, until that day, so that even the powers of the heavens
are seized with great trembling. Must we then be rivers, my brothers?
What fear and what horror will seize us? Consider Israel in the
desert, when they could not bear the mist and the darkness and the
voice of the God who spoke from the midst of the fire, but they strove
that no word be added to them. For they did not truly bear the voice
that was distinguishing itself, although he came down not with wrath,
nor spoke to them with anger, but filling them with consolation, that
he is with them.

Hear then, my brother, if with consolation we were not able to bear
his coming, when neither were the heavens being kindled dissolved, nor
did the trumpeters sound as that trumpet is about to cry out and to
wake those who have slept from the ages, nor did fire flood the
inhabited world, nor did anything of the terrors that are to come
happen; what shall we do when he comes down with wrath and
ungovernable anger, and sits upon the throne of his glory, and the
earth is summoned from the rising of the sun to its setting, and from
all the ends of the earth, to judge his people, and to render to each
according to his works? Woe! What must we be, when we are presented
naked and stripped, about to be led into the dread tribunal? Alas,
alas! Where then the delicacy? Where then the courage of the flesh?
Where the false and unprofitable beauty? Where then the sweet voice of
men? Where then the shameless and impudent boldness? Where then the
adornment of garments? Where then the pleasure of sin, the unclean and
defiled truly? Where then those who accounted the dung of males a
pleasure? Where then those who drank wine with drums and musical
instruments? Where then the contempt of those who live without fear?
Where then luxury and prodigality? For all things passed away and were
dissolved like vain smoke. Where then avarice and the love of
possessions, and the mercilessness from them? Where then the inhuman
pride, that abominates all things, and itself reckons to be something?
Where then the new and vain human advancement and glory? Where then
the dominion and the tyranny? Where then a king, where a ruler, where
a leader? Where those set over authorities, who boast in the multitude
of wealth and despise God? Seeing him thus, they marveled; trembling
took hold of them; there were pangs as of one in travail; they shall
be crushed in a violent spirit.

Where then the wisdom of the wise? Where their vain cleverness? Woe to
them; They were troubled, they were shaken as a drunkard, and all
their wisdom was swallowed up. Where then the wise? Where the scribe?
Where the disputer of this vain age? My brother, consider what manner
of men we must be, giving an account of the things we did, whether
great or small; for even for an idle word we give apology to the
righteous Judge. What manner of men must we be then in that hour, if
we find grace before him? And what joy will succeed us, when we are
set apart at the right of the King? What manner of men must we be,
when all the saints greet us? There Abraham greets you, Isaac, Jacob,
Noah, Job, David,

and the holy prophets and apostles and martyrs, and all the righteous
who pleased God in the life of their flesh, and as many as you hear of
and admire their life, and even now wished to behold, they themselves
come there to you, and embracing they seize you, exulting at your
salvation. What manner of men must we be then? And what joy must we
have, that unutterable one, when the King says with cheerfulness to
those at the right: Come, you blessed of my Father, =64.42= inherit
the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world? Then,
my brother, you shall receive the royal robe of comeliness, and the
diadem of beauty from the hand of the Lord, and henceforth you shall
reign with Christ; then you shall inherit those good things which God
has prepared for those who love him; then henceforth you shall be
carefree, no longer shaken by any terror. Consider, my brother, what
it is to reign in heaven. For as we said before, you shall receive the
diadem from the hand of the Lord, and reign henceforth with Christ.
Consider, my brother, what it is to behold the face of God
continually, what it is to have him as luminary.

For then the sun shall not be to you as the light of day, as Isaiah
says, nor shall the rising of the moon light your night, but the Lord
shall be to you an everlasting light, and God your glory. Behold, my
brother, what joy is laid up for those who fear the Lord, and keep his
commandments. Consider then after this also the destruction of the
sinners, when they are led into the dreadful tribunal, what shame will
seize them before the righteous Judge, having no word of apology; what
disgrace will seize them, when they are set apart at the left of the
King; what darkness shall fall upon them, when he shall speak to them
in his anger, and in his wrath trouble them, saying: Depart from me,
you cursed, into the everlasting fire prepared for the devil and his
angels; alas, alas! What tribulation and anguish shall their spirit
have, when there comes the cry of all saying: Let the sinners be
turned into Hades, all the nations that forget God! Woe! What is that
place where is the weeping and the gnashing of teeth, the one called
Tartarus, which even Satan himself shudders at! Alas! What is the
Gehenna of the fire of asphalt! Alas! What is the sleepless and
poison-bearing worm! Alas! What are the angels over the punishments,
merciless and pitiless! For they revile and rebuke terribly. Then
those being punished shall cry out to the Lord, and he shall not hear
them; then they shall know that all the things of life turned out vain
for them, and that what they thought pleasant here were found more
bitter than gall and poison. Where then the falsely named pleasure of
sin? For another pleasure there is not, except to fear the Lord.
Truly this is pleasure. Truly this fills the soul.

Then they shall condemn themselves, and the works which they did; then
they shall confess, saying that righteous is the judgment of God; for
we heard these things, and we would not turn from our wicked
practices; and then they shall lift up their voice saying these
things: Woe to me who have been taken in incurable sins; above the
number of the sand of the sea I sinned, and I am bent down by them as
by many bonds of iron; for there is not to me boldness to gaze into
the height of heaven. To whom then shall I flee, except to you, O
philanthropic one, except to you, O one who remembers not evil? Have
mercy on me, O God, according to your great mercy, and according to
the multitude of your compassions blot out my transgression. Wash me
yet more from my lawlessness, and from my sin cleanse me; for my
lawlessness I know, and my sin is before me continually. Against you
only have I sinned, and I have done the evil before you. To you I flee
because of your much goodness and mercy; you I provoked, and to you I
flee because of your much forbearance; you I set at naught, and to you

I flee because of your much goodness and philanthropy, and beseeching
I cry: Turn your face from my sins, and blot out all my
lawlessnesses. Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right
spirit in my inward parts, for your name's sake, and only. For I have
nothing to offer you, neither a good deed, nor a clean heart, but
trusting in your compassions I cast myself upon you, that you may
create in me compunction, that I may not again easily fall into sin,
but that from now on I may serve you in holiness and righteousness all
the days of my life, for yours is the kingdom and the power unto the
ages. Amen. =64.43= I beseech you therefore, my brothers, while
expecting these things, to strive that you be found blameless and
unblemished before him in peace. When a wicked thought comes upon you,
draw this sword, that is, consider the fear of God, and you cut down
all the power of the enemy. Instead of a trumpet have the divine
Scriptures; for as the trumpet crying gathers the soldiers, so also
the divine Scriptures crying to us gather our thoughts into the fear
of God. For our thoughts also are like soldiers warring against the
enemies of the King. Again, as the trumpet crying in time of war stirs
up the eagerness of the young and of the combatants against the
adversaries, so also the divine Scriptures stir up your eagerness
toward the good, and embolden you against the passions.

Therefore, my brother, as much as you are able, compel yourself to
entreat them more often, that they may gather your thoughts, which the
enemy scatters by his wicked craft, whether by wicked thoughts, or by
often bringing afflictions, or by providing many good days and rooms of
ease. For these things he works by his craft and deceit, that he may
alienate man from God =64.44=. For when often he cannot assault and
cast him down through thoughts, then he brings upon him afflictions,
that he may darken the mind, and find henceforth to sow what he
wishes. And he begins then to suggest to the man such thoughts, and to
say with oaths: Since I have worked the good, I have seen evil days;
Let us do the evils, that the goods may come. Then, if one is not
found sober, it swallows him as Hades, living. But if even in these he
cannot assault him, then he brings upon him the roominess, and exalts
him, and provides him much deception, the more grievous and worse than
all the passions.

For this is what makes man proud and fearless; this drags the mind
down into the depth of pleasures; this makes man not to know God, nor
to know his own weakness, nor to think of the day of death and of
judgment; for this is the way of all evils. Whoever loves to walk this
way, the way of roominess and ease, is found arriving at the chambers
of death; this is the way of which the Lord said: Broad and roomy is
the way, that leads to destruction.
