On the dance of Herodias, and on the beheading of John the Forerunner and
Baptist.

=59.521=

1. Just as a man who loves solitude, in some wooded place shaded under trees,
being carried along by the fair-sounding cries of the birds and the
well-sounding streams of the waters, has a gentle mind, such a one is pleasing
to all men; so also we, in the preceding days, being comforted by the gentle
breeze of the Spirit, were discoursing to you concerning almsgiving. But when
the Gospel proclaimed the madness of Herod, and the wantonness of women, and
the revel of mad men, and an accursed table, and an unlawful gift, and an
unholy deed, and the burial of a most reverend body, I have become another
from another. For I become speechless, beloved, when I bring before my own
sight the deeds done by Herod; if indeed those things can be called deeds, and
not wounds, of him who has done such deeds. =59.522=.50 At that time, it says,
Herod the tetrarch heard the report of Jesus, and said to his servants, This
is John, whom I beheaded. He has been raised from the dead, and therefore the
powers work in him. He confesses the murder of the prophet, which he cannot
deny; and he knew him whom he killed, that he is a prophet, and a righteous
man.

For if he did not know, he would not have said that he had been raised from
the dead, and that therefore the powers were working in him. O evil
conscience: at whatever murder does the same man bear witness against himself?
O evil conscience that lives together with a defiled soul, and punishes even
unto death, =59.523= and after death judges inescapably. And for what cause
did he kill the prophet? Because he became a herald of the truth, and wished
through the rebuke to undo his lawless deed; and he who as king ought to have
been a guardian of the commandments of God, himself by the unmixed disorder of
pleasures was dissolving the laws. For that man is a king who issues just
laws, and first practices them, and does not counterfeit them. For how else
would he order into good order the world ruled by him, if he does not, with
just laws as with reins, bridle the hard-to-rein race of men? But Herod, being
a king not of peoples but of pleasures, or rather a slave of pleasures (For by
whatever a man is overcome, to that he has also been enslaved), not only was
dissolving the law of God, but was also unjustly contriving murder. And whom
did he murder? A righteous man, than whom none greater has arisen among those
born of women, as the Lord also bore witness; a man who dwelt in a desert, a
desert uninhabited, waterless, fruitless, pathless, treeless, without pasture,
without green growth. But John dwelt in a desert, not fleeing the kindred and
related race of men, but fleeing to see and hear the abominable misdeeds of
men. This man neither sowed, nor ever reaped; therefore he had neither bread,
nor wine, nor a table, nor a couch that is the servant of pleasures, nor the
soft goose-down that loosens and makes flabby the bodies of the effeminate; he
had no lampstand, no lamp, no table, no bench, no cup, no platter. For being
outside the world, he used none of the things of the world. And he dwelt, not
under a gold-roofed house, but under a self-roofed rock. For this man both
table and bench and couch was the ground, and meat the locusts, and cake the
wild honey; for this man a bowl was the hollow of the hand, and wine of
gladness the rock-born and most transparent water. This man Herod seized and
bound. For Herod, it says, having seized John, bound him, and put him in
prison because of Herodias, the wife of Philip his brother. O strange deed.
John wished through the rebuke to loose his soul bound with the chain of sin,
and that man was binding the one who loosed. But even if John was bound, he
spoke, and being imprisoned he rebuked; for he was fulfilling what is written:
And I spoke before kings, and was not ashamed. For John did not fear death,
but feared not speaking the truth.

This man Herod murdered; and having murdered this man, he was not committing
one murder only, but many also. For he who has killed a teacher and a
benefactor of souls does not kill one, but as many as the teacher, while
living, could give life through the word. And when did he murder this man?
Shame to say it, but shame not for the one who says it, but for the one who
has done the murder. For when Herod's birthday was being kept, the daughter of
Herodias danced in the midst, and it pleased him; whence also with an oath he
promised to give her whatever she should ask. And she, being prompted by her
mother, Give me, she says, on a platter the head of John. O most wicked
birthday, breathing the murder of a prophet, instead of which he ought to have
shown kindness to holy men at his own table. For thus his twisted soul would
have been set right. For with the holy you shall be holy, and with the crooked
you shall be perverse. Instead of which he ought to have provided for widows,
to have pitied orphans, that through many and various prayers he might keep
his own life unshaken, but now, feasting with wanton men and unseemly women,
stupefied with much wine, and mocked by unchaste women, he dissolved by murder
John, the dawn, the forerunner of the day; him who had well borne the torch,
the lamp of the faith, the lamp of the whole world, John, he shattered by
murder. And having shattered this lamp, Herod darkened himself. For even if
that man's earthen vessel was being dissolved into earth, nevertheless the
light-casting lamp of his spirit was well bearing as a torch the light of the
faith to those in Hades. For being a prophet, and knowing that he was about to
go before his Lord in Hades, he sends to him some of his disciples, saying:
Are you the one who is coming, or do we look for another? And not =59.524=
being ignorant of him, he was inquiring.

For how would he have been ignorant of the Lord, when he was pointing him out,
saying: Behold the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world? for how
would he have been ignorant who had also become a spectator of the descent of
the Spirit from heaven, and a hearer of the Father's voice saying concerning
him: This is my beloved Son, in whom I have been well pleased? But he
inquires, how? Are you the one who is coming, or another? He did not say, Are
you the one who has come; for he came upon the earth; but, Are you the one who
is coming (for it is clear that in Hades), or do we look for another?

2. For John was saying in himself, Perhaps he wishes, having empowered an
angel, to send him into Hades, accomplishing his own work? or perhaps he will
wish to raise all with one voice, as he has done in the case of Lazarus?
Therefore, not being able to judge the counsel of the Master, he inquires
saying: Are you the one who is coming, or do we look for another in Hades?
This man Herod killed. And how did he kill him? For when a birthday was being
kept, it says, the daughter of Herodias danced in the midst. A plaything
worthy of such a table. For where there is luxury and delicacy and drunkenness
and revels, nothing is firm, but all things are shaken and overturned,
foretelling the frenzy of Herod. Hear, you lovers of the spectacle of dancers,
who attune your own eyes to the much-turning feet of the loosened youths, and
who with their loosened bodies loosen your own hearts together; hear what a
wage of murder the much-wheeling dance has blossomed. And she, being prompted
by the mother, says: Give me here on a platter the head of John.

O asp-born child of an evil lioness; she did not ask for gold, nor silver, nor
precious stones, but the head of John.

And if she had asked for it through faith, the head of John is far more
precious than all gold and silver and costly stones. But she did not ask
through faith, that she might honor, but that, having killed the rebuke, she
might grant her mother impunity for the sin of her evils. And when he heard,
The king was grieved, not because of the murder of the prophet, but because of
fear of the crowd, because all held him as a prophet. For if he were grieved
through piety, he would not from the beginning have bound him and put him in
prison; but he was grieved because of the crowd. And then the soul of Herod
was as a ship caught in the sea, and listing toward either side; fearing to do
the murder because of the crowd, and not being permitted not to do the murder
because of the oaths and those reclining with him. But the wretched man,
weighed down by the many-flamed pleasure toward the woman, using the keeping
of the oath, forsooth, as the defense of the murder, Sending, he beheaded
John.

That it is an evil thing to swear, now at least remember, you who swear
gladly; hear the Lord saying, Swear not at all. For if the wretched man had
not then sworn, he would not have endured to do the murder. He ought then not
to have done it at all; but since he swore, it was expedient for him to swear
falsely; for in a choice of evils it was better to accept the lesser. Sending,
he beheaded him. What hand dared, beloved, to bring the sword to the holy neck
of John, whom wild beasts in the desert revered, whom lions trembled at, whom
horned serpents and dragons and asps shuddered at, whom wild bees, making
their vaults because of the man's holiness, nourished? For from various
flowers and plants, as in a desert gathering the gold-like drops of the dews,
weaving the honey they offered it to him. This man an executioner's hand
beheaded, not shuddering at his angel-like face, nor trembling at his face
sending forth godlike radiances. But I think that the executioner who was
sent, darkened by the drunkenness of Herod, had a sword, but did not have
eyes. And the head of John was being cut off (for he never drank wine), and
the head of John was being carried on a platter at the supper, and then crying
to Herod, It is not lawful for you to have your brother's wife. For even after
death the righteous man was living, and when his head had been cut off the
voice lives. O cruelty of a man; on the platter on which he was eating the
meats, on that he was setting a man's head. O lawless tyrant, =59.525= was not
the slaughter of the young children that took place in Bethlehem under the
Herod before you enough, when the jointly-mourning wailings of fathers and
mothers were sharper than spears? when the much-poured drops of tears welling
from the inward parts overcame springs? when fathers' breasts were being
struck, and continual gaspings with pains were being sent from the heart upon
the nostrils? when mothers were mourning the green shoot of the infants of
their own loins withered before its time? when even breasts flowing with milk
were whitening the earth, the infants who were drawing them having been
suddenly slain? when fathers' arms were becoming empty of their own offspring,
and mothers' breasts were seeking the itching sucklings from the tender teeth?
For what wild beast did not then weep, seeing the harmless infants murdered
unjustly, who possessed guileless souls? whose souls were as harmless as their
limbs also happened to be tender, as of infants? For did those also rebuke the
Herod before you, who had neither a distinct tongue, nor teeth in the mouth,
that they might, =59.526= turning the tongue, send forth a word through the
lips? That so great cruelty of those things was not enough. But you also,
imitating that man's lack of pity, beheaded John, of whom you were not worthy
of a single hair. That man slaughtered the offspring of Rachel, and you
beheaded John, the instructor of the Church. With which of these shall I be
gracious to you? says the Lord.

O city of bloods, in which Herod reigned, and this man's descendant of the
same name, and like in his ways. Woe, those who seem to be of good order,
these have become leaders of disorder. The Jews, being ruled by such men,
taught the murder by them, were driven together into the killing of Christ;
they sawed Isaiah, they killed Jeremiah, they stoned Naboth, they murdered
Zechariah, they crucified Christ. But leaving to those the mourning over the
evils, let us run up to the light of the mystagogy in Christ Jesus our Lord,
to whom be the glory and the power unto the ages of ages. Amen.
