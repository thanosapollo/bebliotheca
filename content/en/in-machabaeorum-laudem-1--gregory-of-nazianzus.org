ORATION XV.
On the Maccabees.
A. And what of the Maccabees? For of these is the present festival, not
honored among many, because the contest was not after Christ; but worthy to be
honored by all, because the endurance was concerning the ancestral things; and
those who bore witness before the sufferings of Christ, what in the world were
they going to do being persecuted after Christ, and imitating his death on our
behalf? For those who without such a pattern were so great in virtue, how
would they not have appeared more noble, running risks with the pattern? And
at the same time this word is a certain mystical and unspeakable one, and
exceedingly persuasive at least to me and to all the god-loving: that none of
those perfected before the coming of Christ attained this apart from the faith
toward Christ. For the Word was spoken with boldness later in its own times,
but was known also before to those pure in the =35.913= understanding, as is
clear from many of those honored before him.
B. Therefore they are not, because before the cross, such as to be overlooked;
but because according to the cross, to be praised, and worthy of the honor
from words; not that they might receive an addition or glory (for what of
those whose deed has the glorious?); but that those who eulogize may be
glorified, and those who hear may emulate the virtue, being stirred up toward
the same things as by a goad by the memory. Who these were, and whence, and
from what original training and education they set out, so as to have advanced
to so great virtue and glory, that they are honored even with these yearly
processions and festivals, and a glory concerning them greater than the things
seen is stored up in the souls of all, the book concerning them will make
clear to the lovers of learning and of toil, which philosophizes concerning
reason being autocrat of the passions, and master of the inclination toward
both, I mean virtue and vice. For it used not a few other testimonies, and
indeed also these men's contests; but for me it will suffice to say this much.
C. Eleazar is here, the first-fruits of those who suffered before Christ, as
Stephen of those after Christ, a man priest and elder, grey in the hair, grey
in the prudence; formerly both sacrificing for the people and praying, but now
also offering himself as a most perfect victim to God, a purification of the
whole people, a fair prelude of contest, and an exhortation both speaking and
silent; offering also the seven boys, the accomplishments of his own
education, a living sacrifice, holy, well-pleasing to God, both more splendid
and purer than every legal liturgy. For the things of the children to be
reckoned to the father is of the most lawful and most just things. There were
noble and great- =35.916= souled boys, noble offshoots of a noble mother,
ambitious contestants of the truth, higher than the times of Antiochus,
genuine disciples of the law of Moses, accurate guardians of the ancestral
customs, a number of those praised among the Hebrews, honored by the mystery
of the hebdomadal rest, breathing one thing, looking toward one thing, knowing
one way of life, the death on behalf of God, brothers no less in the souls
than in the bodies, being jealous of one another for the end (O the wonder!),
snatching beforehand as treasures the tortures, running risks in advance for
the pedagogue law, not so much fearing the tortures being applied as seeking
those remaining; fearing this one thing only, lest the tyrant punishing should
give out,

and some of them should depart uncrowned, and be separated from the brothers
unwillingly, and win the evil victory, having run the risk of not suffering.
D. There was a mother manly and noble, loving children and at the same time
loving God, and having the maternal bowels torn contrary to the likelihood of
nature. For she did not pity the children suffering, but was in agony at their
not suffering; nor did she long more for those who had departed than she
prayed that those left behind be added; and of these she had more account than
of those who had passed over. For of those still remaining the wrestling was
in doubt, but of those who had passed over the dissolution was secure; and the
ones she was
already committing to God, but of the others she was taking thought how God
might receive them. O courageous soul in a womanly body! O wondrous and great-
souled advance! O of that Abrahamic sacrifice, if one must dare something,
even greater! For he offers one readily, even if the only-begotten, and the
one from the promise, and the one unto whom the promise was; and the greater
thing, that he is established as first-fruits and root not of the race only,
but also of such sacrifices; but she consecrated a whole people of children to
God, having defeated both mothers and priests, with victims ready for
slaughter, with rational whole-burnt-offerings, with sacrificial animals
hurrying; she who displayed the breasts, and reminded of nurture, and
stretched forth the grey hair, and =35.917= put forward old age instead of a
supplication; not seeking salvation, but urging on the suffering, and
considering the delay a danger, not the death; whom nothing bent, nor
softened, nor made more cowardly; not joint-dislocators being held forth,
not wheels being set forward, not windlasses, not catapults, not edges of iron
claws, not swords being whetted, not cauldrons boiling, not fire being raised,
not a tyrant threatening, not a people, not a spear-bearer pressing, not the
race being seen, not limbs being torn apart, not flesh being carded, not
channels of blood flowing, not youth being spent, not the present terrors, not
the expected hardships; and what is heaviest to the others in such things, the
extension of the danger, this was to her the lightest; for she luxuriated in
the spectacle; for somehow she even produced a practice in the sufferings, not
only the variety of the tortures being applied, of all which they thought
nothing, as not even one of one; but also the words of the persecutor being
manifold, insulting, threatening, flattering; for what did he not move toward
obtaining the things he hoped?
E. And indeed also the answers of the children toward the tyrant, having so
much the wise and at the same time the noble, that all the good things of the
others gathered into one are small toward those men's endurance; and the
endurance is small toward those men's understanding in words; and that it
belongs to the same men only both to suffer thus and to philosophize in the
answers toward the threats of the persecutor and the fears being held forth,
by none of which were the noble boys defeated, and the more noble mother; and
having set herself above all, and having mixed the spirit with the affection,
she gives herself as a fair shroud to the children, having departed after
those who had departed before; and this how? having gone willingly upon the
dangers; and with what funeral speeches? For fair also are the words of the
children toward the tyrant, and fairest of fair; for how not? with which they
arrayed themselves, and with which they struck the tyrant; but fairer also
=35.920= are those of the mother, both hortatory before, and afterward
funeral. What then are the words of the children? For it is a fair thing to
recall these also to you, that you may have a pattern, as of contest, so also
of martyr words in such seasons. Of one man others, and as each the words of
the persecutor, or the order of the dangers, or the ambition of the soul
armed; but so as to take them in a type, they were such: "For us, Antiochus,
and all you who stand around, there is one king, God, from whom we have come
to be, and toward whom we shall return. And one lawgiver, Moses, whom we will
not betray, nor outrage; no, by the dangers of the man on behalf of virtue,
and the many

wonders, not even if another Antiochus threaten us harder than you. And one
safety, the keeping of the commandment, and that the law with which we have
been walled not be broken. And one glory, to look down on all glory in such
matters. And one wealth, the things hoped for; and nothing is fearful, except
to fear something before God. With these reasonings we have been arrayed, and
thus we have been armed; the word is toward such young men for you. Sweet also
is this world, and the paternal ground, and friends, and kinsmen, and those of
the same age, and this temple the great and renowned name, and ancestral
festivals, and mysteries, and all things in which we seem to differ from the
others. But not sweeter than God, and than the dangers on behalf of the good;
do not think this. For another world is ours, much higher than the things seen
and more abiding; and a fatherland, the Jerusalem above, which no Antiochus
will besiege, nor will expect to bring over, the strong and impregnable. And
kinship is the inbreathing, and those begotten according to virtue. And
friends are prophets and patriarchs, from whom we have also the type of piety.
And those of the same age are those running risks with us today, and of the
same time in endurance. And of a temple, heaven is more magnificent, and a
festival, a choir-standing of angels; and one mystery great, and greatest, and
hidden from the many, God, toward whom even the mysteries here look."
F. "Cease then promising us the small things, and those worthy of nothing. For
we will not be honored with the dishonorable, nor will we gain things that
bring loss; not so =35.921= wretchedly will we traffic. Cease also
threatening, or we will counter-threaten, to convict your weakness, and
besides this our own punishments; we too have fire, with which we punish the
persecutors. Do you think the contest is for you toward nations and cities,
and the most unmanly of kings, of whom some will prevail, and some perhaps
will be defeated? For neither is the danger concerning such things for them.
You array yourself against a law of God, against tablets God-engraved, against
ancestral things honored both by word and by time, against seven brothers
bound with one soul, who will pillory you with seven trophies; of whom to
prevail is no great thing, but to be defeated is even exceedingly shameful. We
are both race and disciples of those whom a pillar of fire and of cloud led,
for whom a sea was divided, and a river stood, and a sun was checked, and
bread was rained, and a stretching of hands put myriads to flight, striking
through prayers; whose beasts were defeated, and fire did not touch, and kings
went away, wondering at the nobility. Let us say something also of the things
known to you: we are initiates of Eleazar, whose courage you have known. A
father has contested first, children will contest after; the priest has
departed, the victims will follow; you frighten with many things, we have been
prepared toward more. What even will you do to us, arrogant one, with the
threats? What even will we suffer? Nothing is stronger than those ready to
suffer all things. O executioners, why do you delay? And why do you hang back?
Why do you wait for the kind command? Where are the swords? Where the bonds? I
seek the speed. Let the fire be kindled more, the beasts more active, the
racks more elaborate; let all things be kingly and more sumptuous. I am
firstborn, consecrate me first; I am last, let the order be exchanged? let
there be also one of the middle ones among the first, that we may be honored
with equal share. Do you spare? Do you perhaps expect even something of the
contrary things? Again and many times we will say the same word: We will not
eat unclean food, we will not yield. Sooner will you be made to worship our
things, than we yield to yours. Sum of the word: Either devise newer
punishments, or know the present ones despised." =35.924=
G. These things toward the tyrant. But the things they exhorted one another,
and the things they furnished to be seen, how fair and holy, and sweeter than
every other spectacle and hearing to the god-loving! I at least myself am
filled with pleasure remembering, and I am with them contesting in the mind,
and I am adorned by the narrative. They embraced one another, they clasped one
another, it was a festival, as upon contests completed: "Let us go, brothers,
toward the dangers," they cried, "let us go, let us hurry, while there boils
against us the

tyrant, lest he somehow be softened, and we be damaged of the salvation.
A banquet is set forth; let us not be left behind. It is a fair thing also for
brothers dwelling together with one another, and feasting together, and
shielding together; but fairer running risks together on behalf of virtue. If
it were possible, we would have contested even with the bodies on behalf of
the ancestral things; this death too is of the praiseworthy. But since it is
not the season of this, let us bring in the bodies themselves. For what? Even
if we do not die now, shall we not die altogether? Shall we not discharge the
things owed to generation? Let us make the necessity ambition; let us be
sophists of the dissolution; let us make the common our own; let us buy life
with death. Let none of us then be a lover of life, nor cowardly. Let the
tyrant despair even of the others, having met with us. He himself will set the
order upon the dangers, but we will set an end upon those being persecuted;
let us not differ at all concerning this by the boiling of eagerness. And the
first will be a way for the others, and the last a seal of contest. But let
this be fixed in us all alike, to be crowned household-wide, and that the
persecutor take no portion from us, that he may not boast as over all by the
one, inflamed with wickedness. Let us appear brothers of one another, both in
the generation and in the passing over; and all, as one, let us run risks, and
each in place of all. Eleazar, receive; mother, follow; Jerusalem, bury your
own dead magnificently, if anything be left to the tombs. Narrate our things,
and show also to those later, and to your lovers, the pious many-tomb of the
one womb." =35.925=
H. These men then, both having said these things and having done them,
=35.925= and having sharpened one another as teeth of swine, in order of age,
and in equality of eagerness, endured. Pleasure and wonder to those of the
same race; fear and astonishment to the persecutors, who having campaigned
against the whole nation, were so defeated by seven brothers of one soul
contesting on behalf of piety, that they no longer had even good hopes
concerning the others. But the noble mother, and truly of those so many and
such in virtue, the great and great-souled nursling of the law, was for a
while mixed with joy and fear, and in the mid-space of two passions; with joy,
because of the courage and the things seen; with fear, because of the future,
and the excess of the punishments; and as a bird her nestlings, a serpent
creeping near, or some other of the plotters, she flew around, she chirped
around, she entreated, she contested together, saying what not? and doing what
not of the things that anoint toward victory? She snatched the drops of the
blood, she received the torn pieces of the limbs, she worshipped the remains;
the one she gathered, the other she handed over, the other she prepared. She
cried out upon all: "Well done, O children, well done, my champions, well
done, almost bodiless in bodies, well done, champions of the Law, and of my
grey hair, and of the city that reared you, and that led you forth unto this
point of virtue. Yet a little, and we have conquered. The torturers have grown
weary, this I fear only. Yet a little, and I am blessed among mothers, and you
blessed among youths. But do you long for the mother? I will not be left
behind you, this I promise you; I am not so a hater of children."
I. And when she saw them perfected, and had the security from the completion,
having lifted the head very brightly, as some Olympic victor, in a high mind,
and having stretched out the hands, with a great and splendid voice: "I thank
you," she says, "holy Father, and you, pedagogue law, and you our father and
first-contestant of your children, Eleazar, that you have received the fruit
of my pangs, and that I have become a mother more priestly than all mothers. I
have left nothing to the world, all things to God =35.928= I have handed over,
my treasure, my hopes of old-age-nursing. How magnificently I have been
honored! How exceedingly I have been nursed in old age! I have received the
nurture-gifts, O children. I have seen you contesting on behalf of virtue, I
have beheld you all as crown-wearers. How I see the torturers as benefactors;
I almost even confess thanks to the tyrant for the order, that he

stored me last for the dangers; that having first made a spectacle of my
offspring, and having contested according to each of the children, I may thus
depart after with perfect security to perfect victims. I will not tear hair, I
will not rend a tunic, I will not card flesh with nails, I will not raise a
lament, I will not call the co-mourners, I will not shut myself into darkness,
that air also may mourn with me, I will not wait for comforters, I will not
set forth mourning bread. For these things belong to ignoble mothers, who are
mothers of flesh only, whose children have gone without some solemn narrative.
But you have not died for me, dearest of children, but you have been fruit-
borne; you have not failed, but you have passed over; you have not been
carded, but you have been compacted; no beast snatched you, no wave washed
over, no robber destroyed, no disease dissolved, no war consumed besides, nor
any other either small or greater of the human things. I would have lamented
even very vehemently, if some of these things had befallen you. I would have
appeared then by the tears, as now by the not weeping, a lover of children.
Yet even these things are small. Truly I would have bewailed you, if you had
been saved badly, if you had been defeated by the tortures, if they had
prevailed over some of you, as now the persecutors were defeated. But the
things now: eulogy, joy, glory, choir-standings, brightnesses for those left
behind. For I am poured as a libation upon you. We shall be ranked with
Phinehas, we shall be glorified with Anna. Except that he was one, but you so
many zealots, slayers of fornication, having stabbed not fornication of
bodies, but of souls. And she consecrated one God-given, and this newly born;
but I seven men, and these willing, to God. Let Jeremiah also complete the
funeral speech for me, not lamenting, but eulogizing a holy end. You have
shone above snow, you have been curdled above milk, your composition is above
a sapphire stone, both having been begotten and given to God. What still? Add,
O tyrant, me also to the children, if there is even some grace from enemies,
that the contest may be more solemn for you. Would that I had gone through all
the punishments, that I might mix my ichors with their =35.929= ichors, and
the aged flesh with the fleshes. I love even the punishments because of the
children; but if not this, yet at least the dust with the dust, and let one
tomb receive us. Do not grudge an end of equal honor to those of equal honor
in the virtue. Rejoice, O mothers, rejoice, O children. Thus rear those who
have come forth from you; thus be reared. We have given you a fair pattern;
contest."
J. These things she said, and she added herself to the children. In what
manner? Having run upon the pyre as upon a bridal chamber (for this she was
condemned to), and not even having waited for those leading, that not even an
unholy body might touch a holy and noble body. Thus Eleazar enjoyed the
priesthood, having been initiated and having initiated the heavenly things,
and having sanctified Israel not with the outward sprinklings, but with his
own blood, and having made the end a last mystery; thus the boys of the youth,
not having been slaves to the pleasures, but having become masters of the
passions, and having hallowed the body, and having transferred themselves
toward the passionless life. Thus the mother enjoyed the many-childed state;
thus she both adorned herself with them living, and rested together with them
departed; having presented to God those whom she bore to the world, and having
numbered her own pangs with the contests, and having made known the sequence
of the birth by the deaths. For from the first of the children until the last
were the things of the contest, and as in uprisings of waves, one upon another
displayed the virtue, and was more eager toward the suffering, being whetted
by the dangers of the one who had gone before; so that the tyrant was glad
that she had not become mother of more; for he would have departed more
ashamed and defeated. And then first he knew that he was not strong in all
things by arms, when he attacked unarmed boys, armed with one thing only,
piety, and more eager to suffer all things than he was prepared to do.

K. This is more secure than the sacrifice of Jephthah, and =35.932= more
magnificent. For it was not a heat of a promise, and a love of a victory
despaired of, that made the offering necessary, as there; but a voluntary
liturgy, and having as wage only the things hoped for. This is not more
dishonorable than the contests of Daniel, who was given over for food to
lions, and by the stretching of the hands defeated the beasts. This is not
second to the youths in Assyria, whom an angel cooled in fire, who did not
dissolve the paternal law, nor admit profane and unholy food. This is not less
unto ambition than the later victims on behalf of Christ. For the latter,
which I also said beginning the word, followed the blood of Christ; and God
was guide of such contests, having brought in so great and so paradoxical a
contribution on our behalf; but for the former, the patterns of virtue were
not many, nor such. Their endurance all Judaea wondered at, and as herself
crowned she rejoiced and was stirred up. For this too was a contest, and
greatest of contests of those that ever surrounded the city, either that the
law be dissolved on that day, or that it be glorified; and as upon a razor the
affairs then stood for the whole race of the Hebrews, the things of those
men's contest. And Antiochus also was astonished, thus having changed the
threat into wonder. For even enemies know how to wonder at the virtue of men,
whenever, the anger having ceased, the deed is tested by itself. So that he
even departed without effect, having praised much the father Seleucus for the
honor toward the nation, and for the magnificence toward the temple; and
having blamed much Simon who brought him on, as cause both of the inhumanity
and of the ill-fame.
L. Let us imitate these, both priests, and mothers, and children; the ones,
unto the honor of Eleazar the spiritual father, who showed the best both in
word and in deed; and the women, of the noble mother, having appeared truly
lovers of children, and having presented those from them to Christ, that
marriage also may be sanctified through such a sacrifice; and the children,
reverencing the holy boys, and spending the youth, not in the shameful
passions, but in the contests against the passions, and toward the daily
Antiochus nobly playing the man =35.933= who wars with all members, and
persecutes variously. For I long to have contestants, according to every
season and manner, and every race, and every age, both openly warred upon, and
secretly plotted against; and to be helped by the ancient narratives, and to
be helped also by the new, and from everywhere, as the bees, to collect the
most useful things unto the craftsmanship and sweetness of one honeycomb, that
both through Old and New God may find approval in us, the one glorified in Son
and Spirit, and knowing his own, and being known by his own, both being
confessed and confessing, both being glorified and glorifying, in Christ
himself, to whom is the glory unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
