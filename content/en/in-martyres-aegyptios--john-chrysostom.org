Encomium on the Egyptian Martyrs.
=50.693=
1. Blessed be God, that there are martyrs even from Egypt, Egypt the
God-fighting and most frantic, and from where the godless mouths, from where
the blasphemous tongues: martyrs from Egypt, not in Egypt only, nor in the
neighboring and bordering land, but everywhere in the inhabited world. And
just as in a plentiful year of market goods, when those who dwell in the
cities see the crop become more than the need of the inhabitants, they also
send it out to the cities abroad, at once displaying their own kindness, and
besides this abundance easily buying in return from those what they may need;
so also the Egyptians have done with the athletes of piety. Seeing by the
grace of God that the crop among them had become abundant, they did not shut
up this great gift of God in the city; but they sent out everywhere on earth
the treasures of the good things, displaying their own brotherly love,
glorifying the common Master of all, adorning their own city before all, and
making it appear as metropolis of the whole inhabited world. For if cold and
cheap resources, and favors that contribute only to the present life, have
been able to furnish this honor to many of the cities; how is it not just that
the one which grants none of these perishable and corruptible =50.694= things,
but men who possess much safety for the cities allotted to them even after
death, should receive from them this presidency most of all? For the bodies of
these saints wall our city more securely than any wall of adamant and
unbreakable; and like certain high reefs thrown forward on every side, they
not only beat back the assaults of these sensible and visible enemies, but
also the plots of the invisible demons, and they overturn and dissolve every
method of the devil so easily, as if some noble man were to overturn and throw
down the playthings of children. And the other devices made by men, such as
walls, and ditches, and arms, and multitudes of soldiers, and whatever has
been invented for the safety of the inhabitants, it is possible for the
enemies to beat back by other more numerous and greater excesses of devices
from their side; but when a city is walled with the bodies of saints, even if
those spend countless sums, they will not be able to set up any such device
against the cities that have them. And this possession is useful to us,
beloved, not only against the plots of men, nor against the mischiefs of
demons; but even if the common Master is angry with us because of the
multitude of sins, we shall be able, putting forward these =50.695= bodies,
quickly to make him gracious to the city. For if those who accomplished great
things in the time of our ancestors, putting forward the names of holy men,
and taking refuge in the appellation of Abraham and Isaac and Jacob, obtained
some consolation, and reaped great benefit from the memory of these names,
much more we, when putting forward not names only, but the very bodies that
contended, shall be able to have God gracious and gentle and kind.
And that what we say is not boast, many both of the locals and of those who
have come from elsewhere know how great is the power of these saints, who also
bear witness to what is said, having learned by experience itself their
boldness toward God; and very reasonably. For they did not contend for the
truth at random, but they stood so steadfastly and vigorously against the
rushing and unbearable violence of the devil, as if competing in bodies of
stone and iron, not in corruptible and mortal ones, as if they had already
passed over to the passionless and immortal nature, which does not yield to
the bitter and painful necessities of the body. For like certain wild beasts,
savage and untamed, the executioners, having encircled the bodies on every
side, dug through the ribs, tore the flesh, uncovered and laid bare the bones,
and nothing stopped them from that cruelty and inhumanity; but even laying
hold of backs and inwards, and going into the innermost parts, they did not
find the treasure of the faith stored in them so as to plunder it, but they
suffered the same as if some, besieging a most royal city, full of much wealth
and having sufficient treasures, and having pulled down its walls, and coming
to the very storehouses of the money, should overturn doors, pull down bars,
dig up the floor, search everything, and not be able to go away having
snatched its wealth.
For such are the possessions of the soul; they are not betrayed to the
sufferings of the body, when it holds them with security, but even if someone
should tear open the very breast, and taking the heart should divide it little
by little, it will not give up the treasure once entrusted to it by the faith.
And this has come from the grace of God that orders all things, and is able to
accomplish wonders even in weak bodies. And what is more marvelous: not only
did they, raging so much, steal nothing of the treasures stored in them, but
they even caused them to be kept with greater security, and made them more
splendid and more wealthy. For no longer the soul alone, but the body itself
also partook of more grace, and not only did it not lose what it had, after
being cut and chopped many times, but it even drew to itself a greater and
larger weight of grace. What could be more marvelous than this victory, that
those whom they held and had under their hands, and having bound they tore
with authority, these they were not able to conquer, but were defeated by them
pitiably and wretchedly? For they were not warring against them, but against
the God who dwelt in them; and that the one who wars against God must of all
necessity be defeated with much surplus, paying the penalty of the attempt
alone, is surely clear to everyone. =50.696= 2. Such are the victories of the
saints. And if the contests and the wrestlings are so marvelous and
paradoxical, what would we say about the prizes and crowns laid up for them
for their endurance? For they did not stop at these tortures, nor did they end
the course here, but the lists were stretched further for them, the evil demon
expecting by the addition of punishments to trip up the athletes, and the
philanthropic God permitting and not preventing; so that both the madness of
the unbelievers might be shown more clearly to all, and more splendid and more
numerous crowns might be woven for these. And just as it happened in the case
of Job and the devil: the one asked from God more punishments against him,
expecting by the addition of the terrible things to prevail over the noble
athlete of piety; and the other permitted, and granted the wicked requests of
the wicked demon, making his own athlete more conspicuous; so also it happened
here. For when more harshly than any beast he had chewed their body from every
side, and had bloodied the tongue and the mouth, even if not with the blood of
the saints, but with those inhuman and savage sentences, having been defeated
by their endurance, and having been filled enough with that inhuman feast, and
having taken his fill, he went away.
For see how great is the endurance of the saints, which sated so great a
madness with its own sufferings. He came on again, contending further in his
madness, and striving to outdo all the beasts through another cruelty. For
those come to this feast by necessity of nature, and when filled they go away,
and even if they see countless bodies, they touch none of them; but this one
both went to this feeding by wickedness of choice, and having been filled with
their flesh, weaves another device against them, handing the saints over to the
longest and harshest death; for he ordered them to work continually in the
mines. O the madness! having taken so clear a trial of their courage and
endurance, he expected by this to prevail. And so the saints dwelt with
beasts, they who are fellow-dwellers of the angels, and citizens of the
heavens, and already enrolled in the Jerusalem above, and the desert was now
holier than every city. For in the cities these lawless and tyrannical
commands were dared every day; but the desert had exemption from the inhuman
public service; and the courts were full of unholy action and unlawful
commands; but the deserts had citizens more law-abiding than all men, those
who had become angels from men, and the desert rivaled heaven, at least from
the virtue of the citizens dwelling there. And by nature the punishment was
most harsh, but by the eagerness of those who contended it became easy and
light and ready.
They thought then that they saw the light many times over, and they thought
they had already received that light spoken of by the prophet, that The moon
shall be as the sun, and the sun sevenfold.
For there is not, there is not anything more cheerful than a soul counted
worthy to suffer something for Christ of the things that =50.697= seem to us
terrible and unbearable. They thought they had already passed over to heaven
and were dancing with angels. For what more of heaven and of angels did they
need, when the Master of the angels, Jesus, was with them in the desert? For
if, where two or three are gathered in his name, there he is in the midst of
them, much more was he then in the midst of those assembled, not as gathered
into the name, but being punished continually for the name. For you know, you
know clearly, that there is no other punishment harsher than this, and that
those condemned by this sentence would have chosen to endure countless other
deaths rather than bear the pain that comes from there. They were handed over
to
mines, where they had to dig copper, they themselves often being more precious
than a mine of gold and of immaterial gold, not dug by the hands of condemned
men, but found by the eagerness of faithful men; they worked mines who were
full of countless treasures. What more bitter and more painful than that life?
They saw even coming out upon themselves the narratives about those great men,
which Paul recounts speaking about the saints: They went about in sheepskins,
in goatskins, being in want, afflicted, ill-treated, of whom the world was not
worthy [wandering in deserts and mountains and caves and the holes of the
earth. These things also happened in our generation]. These things then
knowing also we, beloved, that both now and of old, from when men have
existed, all who are friends of God have been allotted the grim and toilsome
=50.698= life full of countless terrible things, let us not pursue the
slack and dissolved life full of ease, but the toilsome, the laborious, the
one that has afflictions and hardships. For just as it is not possible for the
one who contends through sleep and sloth and luxury to obtain the crowns, nor
the soldier the trophies, nor the helmsman the harbor, nor the farmer the
filled threshing-floor; so neither can the faithful man who has led his own
life through sloth obtain the promised good things.
How then is it not absurd that in all worldly things the toils lie before the
pleasure, and the dangers before the safety, and this when a cheap and small
expectation from those toils remains; but when heaven is set forth, and honors
of angels, and a life having no end, and the sojourn with angels, and the good
things which it is not possible even to conceive or speak, to expect to attain
them through sloth and ease and a dissolved soul, and not to deem them worthy
of the same zeal as the worldly things? Do not, I beg, do not so badly counsel
concerning ourselves and our own salvation, but looking to these saints, the
noble and enduring athletes, who have been given to us in place of luminaries,
let us direct our own life toward their endurance and patience, so that by
their prayers we may be able after the departure from here both to see them
and greet them, and to be ranked toward their heavenly tents; which may it be
that we all obtain by the grace and philanthropy of our Lord Jesus Christ,
through whom and with whom to the Father and to the Holy Spirit be glory unto
the ages of ages. Amen.
