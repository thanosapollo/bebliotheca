On the apostolic saying: I would not have you ignorant, brethren, that all our
fathers were under the cloud, and all passed through the sea.

=51.241=

αʹ. Sailors especially love that part of the sea which has been broken up by
frequent harbors and islands. For a harborless sea, even if it have a calm,
furnishes much trembling to those who sail; but wherever there are moorings,
and headlands, and shores stretched out on every side, they sail with much
safety. For even if they see the sea a little stirred, having the refuge
through a short way, they could find a very swift and easy deliverance from
the evils that lie upon them. For these reasons, not only when they are
driving near a harbor, but even if they are far off, they receive much
consolation even from this sight. For it is not at random that even a
mountain-peak appearing from afar, and smoke leaping up, and flocks of sheep
grazing along the mountain-foot, are naturally able to restore their souls.
And when they come even to the very mouth of the harbor, they enjoy the
gladness whole. For then they both lay aside the oar, and wash round with
drinkable waters the bodies pickled by the brine, and going out onto the
shore, and associating with the land a little with naked bodies, they put off
all the hardship from the seafaring. As therefore those men especially welcome
this part of the sea because of these frequent and continual restings, so I
too especially love this hour, not because we have been delivered from winter,
nor because we enjoy summer, the west wind blowing shrilly, but because we
have the spiritual harbors continually receiving us in turn, I mean the
festivals of the holy martyrs. For harbors are not so naturally able to
restore sailors as the feasts of these saints are able to restore the
faithful. For harbors deliver those men from the assault of sea-waves and from
long rowing; but those who come to a festival of martyrs the memory of these
saints is accustomed to snatch away from wicked and =51.242= unclean spirits,
from unseemly thoughts, from the much tempest that arises in the soul. And if
he comes in dragging some despondency from civic or from household affairs,
having laid it all aside he so goes away, and becomes easier and lighter, not
laying aside an oar, nor rising from the tillers, but dissolving the
hard-to-bear and various burden of worldly grief, and receiving much gladness
in the soul. And of all these things you are witnesses, who yesterday feasted
on the contests of the blessed Barlaam, and with much freedom leaped into that
man's harbor, and washed off the brine of worldly cares, and withdrew home
light from his narratives.

But behold, a festival of other martyrs is driving toward us again. Until then
we arrive at their harbor, come, let us imitate the sailors; and just as
those, sailing the sea, sing, consoling the toil with the song, so too we,
until we arrive at their harbor, let us set in motion some spiritual words
toward one another, making the blessed Paul the leader of this fair converse,
and following whichever way he himself commands. What way then does he command
us to follow? The way through the wilderness, and of the wonders that happened
there. For you heard him today crying out and saying: And I would not have you
ignorant,



brethren, that all our fathers were under the cloud, and all passed through
the sea, and all were baptized into Moses, and all ate the same spiritual
food, and all drank the same =51.243= spiritual drink. For they drank of a
spiritual rock that followed, and the rock was Christ. But with most of them
God was not well pleased; for they were overthrown in the wilderness. Now
these things became types of us, that we should not be desirers of evils, as
they also desired; nor become idolaters, as some of them, as it is written:
The people sat down to eat, and rose up to play. Nor let us commit
fornication, as some of them committed fornication, and there fell in one day
twenty-three thousand. Nor let us tempt Christ, as some of them tempted, and
perished by the serpents. Nor let us murmur, as some of them murmured, and
perished by the destroyer. And the things said seem to be clear, yet they
furnish no ordinary difficulty to those who attend. For it is worth asking
first, for what reason he himself recalled the ancient histories, from what
sequence, while discoursing about things sacrificed to idols, he fell into
this narrative, bringing into the midst the things in the wilderness. For that
blessed man does not speak simply, nor as it chanced, but with much sequence
and everywhere keeping an accurate harmony of the things said by him. For what
reason then, and from where, did he fall into this history? He was rebuking
those who simply and without examination went in to the idols, and tasted of a
polluted table, and touched things sacrificed to idols; and having shown that
they suffer a double harm from the deed, both striking the weak, and
themselves becoming partners of demons, and having sufficiently put down their
high thoughts by the things said before, and having taught that the believer
must look not only to his own, but also to that of the many, wishing to
stretch the fear upon them, he reminds them of ancient narratives. For since
those men were high-minded, as being faithful, and delivered from error, and
counted worthy of knowledge, and having shared in the unspeakable mysteries,
and called to the kingdom of heaven, wishing to show that there is no profit
in these things, if a way of life agreeing with so great a grace is not
present, he teaches them from an ancient history.

βʹ. But this very thing again is full of much inquiry. For what reason does he
not discourse with them from the words of Christ that lie in the Gospel, nor
remind them of Gehenna, and outer darkness, and a venomous worm, and immortal
bonds, and the fire prepared for the devil and his angels, and the gnashing of
teeth, and the other unspeakable punishments? For if he wished to frighten, he
ought to have done this from the greater things, not from those in the
wilderness. For those men, even if they were punished, yet more mildly and for
a time, and in one day; but these are going to be punished with immortal and
harsher things. For what reason then did he frighten them from there, and did
not even recall the words of Christ? For he could have said to them: And I
would not have you ignorant, brethren, what laws Christ laid down concerning
those who have faith, but do not display an excellent life. For even men who
have done miracles, and displayed prophecy, he cast out of the kingdom of
heaven, saying: Many will say to me in that day, Lord, Lord, did we not cast
out demons in your name, and in your name =51.244= prophesy, and do many
mighty works?

And then I will confess to them, Depart from me, I do not know you, you who
work lawlessness. And the virgins too, not charging them concerning faith and
doctrines, but concerning a corrupted life and inhumanity and cruelty, he shut
out of the bridechamber; and the one who had put on filthy garments, bound for
this he



cast out, not because he did not have right doctrines, but because he had a
filthy and unclean life; and those whom he commanded to be carried away into
the fire prepared for the devil and his angels, he did not send there because
they had fallen from the faith, but because they never pitied anyone. All
these and such things he could have recalled to them, and said: And I would
not have you ignorant, brethren, that all these both obtained baptism, and
shared in the mysteries, and displayed much faith, and had completed
knowledge; but because they did not furnish a life agreeing with the faith,
they were cast out of the kingdom, and were delivered to the fire. For what
reason then did he not say these things, but having left all these, he speaks
somehow thus: And I would not have you ignorant, brethren, that all our
fathers were under the cloud, and reminding them of the things of Moses,
having for the time kept silence about the things under grace? He does not do
this simply, nor without some reason; for he was filled with much wisdom. But
why ever, and on account of what? For these two reasons: both wishing at once
to lay hold of them more, and to show that there is much kinship of the Old
Testament with the New. For since many of men, some distrust Gehenna, and
think there is not even a punishment, but that God has simply for fear and
chastening threatened these things, the unending worm, the unquenchable fire,
the outer darkness, yet they cannot distrust the things that have passed. For
how could anyone say that the things that have happened have not happened? For
of the things that have not yet appeared, nor come out into deed, there are
many who distrust; but of the things that have happened and have received an
end, even if someone be ten thousand times irresponsible and ungrateful, he
will never, even if he should wish, be able to distrust.

From the things strongly confessed, and having an end, and of which many
remnants remain, he wishes to persuade them concerning the just judgment of
God, all but saying: If you do not think there is a Gehenna, nor vengeance and
punishment, but that God has simply threatened, having reasoned over the
things that have passed, believe also the things that are to come. For if it
is the same God, who both manages the former things and the present, both the
things under the Old and the things under grace, as indeed he is the same,
what reason would it have, that he should punish and avenge those who sinned,
but leave us unpunished who fail in these things, and in things much harsher
than those? I ask then: did the Jews commit fornication, and were they
punished? Did they murmur, and were they avenged? It is entirely necessary to
confess it. How then will he who avenged those men overlook you daring the
same things? It would have no reason. But you have not paid the penalty here?
For this very reason especially believe that there is a Gehenna and a
punishment, because you have not paid the penalty here. For if some punishment
were not going to be stored up after these things, you would not, having dared
=51.245= the same things as the former men, have remained unpunished. And you
then, whenever you take someone slack, and dissolved, and displaying much
wantonness, and he says to you that these things are a myth, there is no
punishment, nor Gehenna, but God simply threatened wishing to frighten, say to
him: Man, you distrust the things to come, because they do not appear, nor
have come into the midst, nor lie under our eyes; is it possible for anyone to
distrust the things that have happened and have received an end? Consider for
me Sodom and Gomorrah. That country paid so great a penalty for no other sin,
than that those who inhabited those cities introduced lawless mixings, and
unlawful loves, and overturned the laws of nature from the foundations.

How then would it have reason, that God, being the same both then and now,
should punish those who sinned without any pardon, but leave you unpunished
who have sinned after them, who are worthy of much greater vengeance and
punishment, inasmuch as you also enjoyed grace, and were not even chastened by
their punishments?



γʹ. For these reasons Paul too, having said nothing yet about Gehenna, since
to many the things to come are untrustworthy, wishes to chasten them from the
things already happened, and of which they had sufficient faith. For even if
the things to come are more fearful, yet to the more imperfect of men the
things that have passed are more trustworthy, and these are accustomed to
frighten them more than those. For these reasons he discourses with them from
here, things which not even the one who is exceedingly shameless was able to
distrust; and at the same time he gives Marcion and Manes, and all who are
sick with the same things as those men, a mortal blow. For if it is not the
same God of the Old and of the New, who both admonished those things, and is
going to shape these, you say these things to me superfluously, O Paul, and
you put no fear into the hearers. For the one who hears can say, that if that
God is another, and this one another, this one will not altogether judge
according to that one's mind, nor is he persuaded by the same laws. For what,
if it seemed good to the God of the Old to punish and avenge all, why do you
scare and frighten?

I have another Master who is going to judge me. So that if the God of the Old
were another, and the God of the New another, Paul did the opposite of what he
wished; for he not only did not frighten the hearer, but even delivered him
from all agony and fear; which not even one of ordinary men and exceedingly
foolish would have suffered, much less Paul who was full of so much wisdom.
Whence it is clear that one and the same is God who both overthrew the Jews in
the wilderness, and is going to punish those of us who sin. For if he were not
one (for I will say the same thing again), he would not from the things
already done by that one frighten us also concerning the things to come; but
since he is the same, he introduced for them an undeniable expectation
concerning the punishment, showing that one must dread and fear. For he who
punished our fathers when they sinned will not spare us when we commit the
same faults. And it is worth coming to the very beginning of the narrative,
and examining each saying with all accuracy. And I would not have you
ignorant, brethren. He called the disciples brethren, addressing them not from
the name of dignity, but from that of love. For he knew, he knew clearly, that
nothing is equal to this, and that the greatest form of dignity is the form of
love.

This =51.246= let us too first emulate. And even if some are far inferior to
us, let us call them by names of honor, not only free men, but also slaves,
not only rich, but also poor, since Paul too honored not the rich among the
Corinthians, nor the free and distinguished, nor the notable only, but also
private men, and household slaves, and simply all, with this title. For in
Christ Jesus there is no slave, no free, no barbarian, no Scythian, no wise,
no unwise, but every inequality of worldly dignity has been taken away. And
what wonder, if Paul called his fellow-servants thus, when even his Master
called our nature thus, saying so: I will declare your name to my brethren, in
the midst of the Church I will hymn you? And he not only called us brethren,
but also wished to become our brother, and became so, putting on our flesh,
and sharing in the same nature of ours. Which indeed Paul himself also
wondering said: For God does not take hold of angels, but he takes hold of the
seed of Abraham, whence he was obliged to be made like the brethren in all
things. And again: Since therefore the children have shared in flesh and
blood, he himself likewise partook of the same. And hearing all these things,
let us banish boastfulness, and conceit, and all madness from our soul, and
with much eagerness let us accomplish this, to call those near us by names
that honor and have honor. For even if the achievement seems small and bare,
yet it is a cause of great goods; just as the contrary often made many



enmities and strifes and contentions. And not this saying only, but also the
next must be examined with much accuracy; for he has not set this down simply
either. For having said: I would not have you ignorant, brethren, he added:
That all our fathers; he did not say, That the Jews, nor those who came out of
Egypt; but what? All our fathers; at once both displaying his own humility,
that he did not disdain the kinship toward those men, having risen so far
above them according to the account of virtue, and stopping the shameless
tongue of those who slander the Old. For if he had been disposed with hatred
toward that, he would not have recalled them from the more well-spoken names,
when all the things that happened then had a slander. All. Nor has he set this
down simply either, the All, nor as a side-matter, but with much wisdom. For
he did not say it once and keep silence, but twice and thrice and many times,
that you may learn that he did not throw these things in as a side-matter. For
having said: That all our fathers were under the cloud, he added: And all
passed through the sea, and all were baptized into Moses, and all ate the same
spiritual food, and all drank the same spiritual drink.

Did you hear how many times he set down the All? He would not have done this,
if he had not wished to hint at some great and wonderful mystery. For if he
were setting it down simply, it would have sufficed to say it once and keep
silence, and to speak thus: That all our fathers were under the cloud, and
passed through the sea, and were baptized into Moses, and ate the same
spiritual food, and drank the same spiritual drink. =51.247= But now he did
not speak thus, but setting down the All at each of the things that happened,
he opened for us no small door of his mind, so as to see his wisdom. For what
reason then does he continually recall this word? He wishes to show that there
is much kinship of the Old with the New, and that these things were a type of
those, and a shadow of the things to come.

And first from this he shows the harmony. For wishing to show that just as in
the Church there is no distinction of slave and free, nor of stranger and
citizen, nor of old man and young, nor of wise and unwise, nor of private man
and ruler, nor of woman and man, but every age, and every dignity, and either
nature likewise go into that pool of the waters, and whether one be a king, or
a beggar, they enjoy the same purifications; and this especially is the
greatest token of the nobility among us, that we initiate alike both the
beggar and the one who has the purple, and this one has nothing more than that
one according to the account of the mysteries; so also in the Old he has
continually set down the All. For you cannot say that Moses passed through on
dry land, but the Jews through the sea, nor that the well-off went by one
road, and the poorer by another, nor that the women were under the air, but
the men under the cloud, but both through the sea all, and under the cloud
all, and into Moses all. For since that crossing was a type of the baptism to
come, this had to be typed first of all, that all enjoyed the same things,
just as here too all share the same things equally. And how, he says, can that
be a type of the present things? If you learn first what a type is, and what
the truth, then I will also furnish you the accounts of this.

δʹ. What then is a shadow, and what the truth? Come, let us bring the argument
to the pictures which painters paint. You have often seen a royal image
colored with dark-blue pigment, then the painter leading around white lines,
and making a king, and a royal throne, and horses standing by, and
spear-bearers, and enemies bound and lying under. Yet seeing these things



sketched in outline, you neither know the whole, nor are ignorant of the
whole, but that a man is being painted and a horse, you know dimly; but of
what sort the king is, and of what sort the enemy, you do not know very
accurately, until the truth of the colors coming makes the sight distinct and
clearer. As therefore in that picture you do not demand the whole before the
truth of the colors, but even if you take some dim knowledge of the things
being done, you think the sketch sufficiently completed; so reason with me
also concerning the Old and concerning the New, and do not demand of me all
the accuracy of the truth in the type; and we shall be able to teach you how
the Old had some kinship with the New, and that crossing with our baptism.
There water, and here water; a pool here, and there a sea; all here go into
the waters, and there all; in this the kinship. Do you wish then to learn the
truth of the colors? There they were delivered from Egypt through the sea,
here =51.248= from idolatry; and there Pharaoh was drowned, here the devil;
there Egyptians were choked, here the old man of sins is buried. And see
kinship of type with truth, and superiority of truth over type.

For the type must neither be entirely alienated from the truth, since it would
not be a type; nor again be equal to the truth, since then it too will be
truth; but it must remain in its own proportion, and neither have the whole of
the truth, nor have fallen from the whole. For if it have the whole, it is
truth again itself too; but if it fall from the whole, it can no longer be a
type; but it must have the one, and keep the other for the truth. Do not then
demand the whole of me in the Old, but if you take even some small and dim
hints, think this to be desirable. Where then is the kinship of the type with
the truth? That all there, and here all; that through water there, and here
through water; that those were delivered from slavery, and we from slavery,
but not the same; but they from that of the Egyptians, we from that of the
demons; they from that of the barbarians, we from that according to sin. Those
were led up to freedom, and we, but not to the same, but we to a much more
splendid one. And if our things are greater and superior to those, do not be
troubled. For this especially is of truth, to have much superiority over the
type, not opposition, nor conflict. And what is, All were baptized into Moses?
And perhaps what is said is unclear; therefore I will try to make it clearer.
There was a sea poured out before the eyes of those men then, and they were
commanded to cross a strange and paradoxical road, which no one of men ever
crossed. They hesitated and drew back, and were distressed. Moses crossed
first, and thereafter gave all to follow with ease. This is: They were
baptized into Moses; having believed him, they thus took courage to step upon
the waters, having taken him as leader of the journey.

This has happened also in the case of Christ; for having led us out of error,
and delivered us from idolatry, and guiding us by the hand toward the kingdom,
he himself first began this road for us, having first gone up into the
heavens. As therefore those men, taking courage in Moses, dared the journey,
so we, taking courage in Christ, dare this departure. And that this is the:
They were baptized into Moses, is clear from the history. For they were not
baptized into the name of Moses. And if we not only have Jesus as pioneer, but
are also baptized into his name, those not having been baptized into the name
of Moses, do not be troubled here either; for I said that the truth must have
some great and unspeakable superiority. Did you see in the matter of baptism
what the type is, and what the truth? Come, I will show you also the table and
the communion of the mysteries sketched there, if you do not



demand the whole of me again, but examine the things that happened as it is
reasonable to see them in a sketch and in types. For when he spoke about the
sea and the cloud and Moses, he added again: And all ate the same spiritual
food. Just as you, he says, going up from the pool of the waters, run to the
table, so those too, having gone up from the sea, came to a new and =51.249=
paradoxical table, I mean the manna. And again just as you have a paradoxical
drink, the saving blood, so those too had a paradoxical nature of the drink,
not finding springs, nor flowing rivers, but receiving from a hard and
waterless rock a great abundance of streams. For this reason he also called it
spiritual, not because it was such by nature, but because it became such by
the manner of the supplying. For it was not given to them according to the
sequence of nature, but according to the energy of God who was commanding them
as general. Which indeed he also said, correcting this very thing. For when he
said: And all drank the same spiritual drink, and it was water that was being
drunk, wishing to show that he said Spiritual for this reason, not because of
the nature of the water, but because of the manner of the supplying, he added:
For they drank of the spiritual rock that followed; and the rock was Christ.
Not the nature of the stone, he says, but the power of God who was working
sent forth those springs.

εʹ. Here he also tore up the heresy of Paul of Samosata from the root. For if
Christ was the one who worked all those things, how do they say that he is
from then, from when Mary bore him? For when the things in the wilderness
appear to have happened before Mary, and Christ had done all those things
according to the voice of Paul, it is necessary that he also be before this
birth, and before these birth-pangs; for surely he who was not was not going
to work such wonderful and paradoxical things. And just as having said that
all passed through the sea, he made clear the nobility of the Church typed
beforehand from above; so having said, They ate the same spiritual food, he
hinted at this same thing again. For just as in the Church it is not one body
of which the rich partakes, and another of the poor, nor this one of one
blood, and that one of another; so also then, the rich did not receive one
manna, and the poor another; nor did that one share in one spring, and this
one in another inferior; but just as now the same table, the same cup, the
same food is set before all who enter here; so also then the same manna, the
same spring was set before all. And what is wonderful and paradoxical, some at
that time attempted to collect more than was needed, and they profited nothing
from the greed. But as long as they honored equality, the manna remained being
manna; but when they desired to have more, the greed made the manna a worm;
and yet they did not do this with the damage of the others; for they were not
snatching the food from the neighbor, and so themselves collecting more, but
nevertheless since they desired the more, they were condemned. For even if
they wronged the neighbor in nothing, yet they harmed themselves the greatest
things, being accustomed toward greed by the manner of this collecting. So
that the same thing was at once both food and a teaching of the knowledge of
God; at once it both fed the bodies, and educated the soul; and it did not
only feed, but also delivered from toils.

For there was no need to yoke oxen, nor to drag a plow, nor to cut furrows,
nor to wait a year, but they had a table made ready, fresh and new and for the
day; and that evangelical command educated them through the deeds, not to be
anxious for the morrow; for there was no profit from this anxiety. For if
anyone collected more, it was corrupted and perished, and what happened was
only a proof of greed. Then, that they might not think that rain to be
according to the sequence of nature, on the Sabbath day nothing of the kind
=51.250= happened,



God teaching them both these things, that on the former days he himself worked
this wonderful and paradoxical rain, and that on that day he held it back for
this reason, that even unwilling they might be taught to keep idle the day of
the Sabbath. And not in the matter of food only, but also in the matter of
garments, and of sandals, and of all the other things, one could see through
the deeds themselves the commands of the apostles being fulfilled. For they
had no house, no table, no bed, no second garment, no sandals, God having so
managed it. See how much kinship of the Old with the New. For just as Christ
later shaped the apostles for the sake of the necessaries, so somehow the
manner of life was also ordered for those men, and all creation was prepared
for their service. And for what reason did these things happen, he says? He
was going to shut them into one place of the inhabited world, and command them
to serve him there continually, and neither temple nor altar to be founded
anywhere else in the inhabited world, but both offerings and sacrifices to
bring there, and to accomplish feasts, and to read the law, and to fulfill all
the other things of that holiness.

In order then that they might not think that by the defined manner of the
service his providence also was shut up there, and that he is a partial God,
he anticipated and displayed his power in a foreign land, in Egypt, in the
wilderness, where there was no one serving, where there was no one
worshipping; and creation served toward the opposites, persuading even the
ungrateful to reckon, through the things it did, his creation from the
beginning. For the sea choked some, and saved others; and the air now brought
down hail, and destroyed the barbarians, now brought down manna, and fed the
Jews. Again the earth now brought forth gnats for the punishment of the
enemies, now quail for the salvation of his own. And for those there was
darkness in day, and for these light in night. And the Egyptians, having the
Nile flowing by, were destroyed by thirst and drought; but these, encamping in
a dry and parched wilderness, enjoyed a great abundance of waters; and frogs
overcame the ones, but not even those giants were able to subdue these.

ςʹ. But for what reason did the blessed Paul remind you of these things? For
the cause which I told you from the beginning, that you may learn that neither
baptism, nor forgiveness of sins, nor knowledge, nor communion of mysteries,
nor a holy table, nor the enjoyment of the body, nor the communion of the
blood, nor any other of these things will be able to profit us, if we do not
have a right life, and a wonderful one, and delivered from all sin. For that
he reminded of these things for this reason, having listed the type of
baptism, that through the sea and the cloud, he sketched beforehand in the Old
the type of the mysteries, that through the manna and the rock, and having
said that All ate the same spiritual food, and drank the same spiritual drink,
he added saying: But with most of them God was not well pleased. After so many
and so great wonders, he says, God did not love them. But what? They were
overthrown in the wilderness. For what reason then do you say these things to
us, O Paul?

Now these things became a type for us, that we should not become desirers of
evils, as they also desired, nor become idola =51.251= ters, as some of them,
as it is written: The people sat down to eat and drink, and rose up to play.
See the understanding of Paul. He said the sin, he said the cause of the sin,
he said the punishment upon the sin, through all things teaching us to flee
the imitation of those men. The cause of the sin is gluttony; For the people
sat down to eat and drink. The sin, the playing itself. Then the vengeance,
that They were overthrown in the wilderness. Again, Nor let us commit
fornication, as some of them committed fornication. Here he did not set down
the cause of



the fornication, but the punishment. And what is this? There fell in one day
twenty-three thousand. And for what reason did he not say the pretext from
which the fornication came? Giving it to the industrious to come to the
history, and to learn the root of the evil. For this is a manner of healing,
to say from what the diseases are born, and to put drugs on the wounds. For
this reason he says: Now all these things happened as types to those men, but
they were written for our admonition. So that he who did those things, and
punished those who were ungrateful, this one admonishes us now, not through
words only, but also through the deeds themselves, which is the greatest
manner of admonition. Did you see how for those under grace he set as teacher
the one who did these things in the Old, showing that he is one and the same,
who both did those things, and speaks these through him? For if he were alien,
he would not have called those things types of these, he would not have said
that they were written for our admonition, he would not have set over us as
teacher one whom he did not think to be God, he would not have frightened us
from the things done then by that one, as being going to fall again into that
one's hands. But now showing that we are going to fall again into that one's
hands, and eith =51.252= er people, both that one and this one, are
administered by that one's laws, he recalled all those things, and said that
They were written for our admonition. Knowing these things then, let us
believe both the things that have passed, and the things that are to come. And
if there are some who do not believe the things to come, let us lead them from
the things that have passed into the care of virtue, narrating the things
concerning the Sodomites, telling the things concerning the flood, reminding
of the things concerning Egypt, that having been chastened by the punishments
of others, and having displayed an excellent life, they may also receive the
word concerning Gehenna and the resurrection. Since even now as many as
distrust the judgment suffer this from nowhere else, but from a corrupted life
and an evil conscience.

So that if we cleanse our own sins, and educate by the fear of the things that
have passed, we shall also persuade them to receive the word concerning the
things to come. For just as wicked doctrines are accustomed to introduce an
unclean life, so also a corrupted life has often begotten wickedness of
doctrines. Which, that it may not happen, both to ourselves, and to the
others, chanting these words, let us both remain having the right faith, and
display an excellent way of life, since from every side the argument has shown
us that without this there is no profit for us in right doctrines.

And may it come to pass by the prayers of the saints, and of all the
presidents, that we both keep unimpaired the rightness of the doctrines, which
we received from above and from our ancestors, and add a life agreeing with
it, that we may obtain the promised goods, by the grace and philanthropy of
our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom to the Father, together with the Holy Spirit,
be glory, power, honor, now and always, and unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
