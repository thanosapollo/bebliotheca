Rom 1:24
Or perhaps they delivered themselves over, because they desired this; for in
the desires of their hearts, he says. rom 2:5 As regards the interpretation
the "according to" is redundant. rom 2:8 The "and" is redundant. rom 2:10 "It
will be possible," he says. rom 2:29 That is, the intelligible. rom 3:4 Having
served gods and demons. rom 3:25-26 For the promises toward Abraham had to be
fulfilled, "and in your seed shall all the nations be blessed," by God not
only being righteous, but also by justifying the one who through the faith of
Jesus becomes God's own and a people. 424 rom 4:19 Well said the "about a
hundred years old"; for he was not a hundred years old, but ninety-nine.
rom 5:5-6 And well did he say that the love of God, which is the gift of the
Spirit, has been poured out, that is, has been given abundantly in our hearts;
for the indwelling of the worshipful and thrice-holy Spirit is intelligible
and graspable by mind and heart alone. That is, according to the timely and
fitting season and time. rom 5:12 Lest someone charge God with injustice
because, Adam having fallen, we die, he adds: "in that all sinned," as if he
said: he himself furnished the beginning and the cause, because we all sinned
according to his likeness. rom 5:13-14α And see the precision of the apostle:
lest we should then seem to be wronged, dying through another, he said: sin
was in the world, even if it was not reckoned. Therefore we die not only
because of Adam but also because of sin. I think that here he means by sin
that which is constituted from the transgression of the law of Moses and of
the commandments there, such as needing to be circumcised, to keep Sabbath,
and to eat these things and abstain from those, since the universal sin also
existed, known even to the nature of men, and it was and was charged, such as
murder, theft, corruption of children, and the like. And witnesses of these
are Cain, Lamech, those at the flood, the Sodomites; for upon these sins the
natural law also lay; for the Creator, bringing man into being, implanted a
certain natural knowledge of things to be done and not to be done. And some of
the fathers say, among whom is also Gregory in the work On Man, that the
commandment given 425 to Adam, not to eat from the tree of knowing good and
evil, is a law 425 restraining these universal sins; for they took the tree
that produces the knowledge of good and evil to be sin. rom 5:14β As he says,
Adam was a type of the one about to come, that is of Christ. How? Just as, he
says, by the death of Adam all were dying, so by the death of Christ we shall
all live and be raised. The type therefore has come about from the contrary.
rom 5:15α He wishes to show that the grace of Christ has operated more than
the transgression of Adam operated. And how is this? For we also see them
having operated equally, even if the things operated are contrary; for just as
the obedience of Christ sent forth life and resurrection unto all men, in the
same manner also the disobedience of Adam sent forth death unto all men. How
then does he say: "not as the transgression, so also the gift"? And though the
matter is as you said, the grace of God operated more, and I do not say
greater according to this, as one might suppose, inasmuch as life is greater
and more excellent than death, but because even in the very work and operation
the obedience of Christ accomplished the greater than the transgression of
Adam. How? Because the death that took its beginning from Adam had also the
cooperation of the sin of us all, that it might prevail against all, not being
about to prevail at all if men had remained clean of every stain. But the
grace of Christ came unto all even without our cooperation, and this is shown
by the grace of the resurrection being operated not only unto the faithful,
who as it were seemed to contribute the faith, but also unto the unbelievers,
such as Jews and Greeks. Greater then is that which did not additionally need
alliance from us than that which needed also our own cooperation in order to
prevail. 426 rom 5:15β By "abundance" he means the lavishness; and this
establishes that they shall certainly reign in life, inasmuch as the grace
also was given not sparingly nor in measured fashion, but from abundance.
rom 5:19 Or because the sin of the forefather passed in order and sequence
unto all of the race, all accomplishing it according to a certain imitation
and succession. rom 6:5 And see the goodness of God: the death of the Lord we
died as in an image, but of the resurrection we shall truly partake. rom 6:9
For how should he die again, through whom the rest of us also shall live, the
whole being sent up together with our firstfruits? rom 6:10 And thus you will
understand, just as we say concerning the man according to us: he died to the
flesh, but lives to the soul, rendering the phrase according to the dative;
so, understand for me, he lives to God, that is being God, not because he also
did not live by being soul, but he set down the more incontestable, that he is
God. rom 7:14 "Spiritual": a gift, he says, of the Holy Spirit and as it were
a dictation. rom 7:18 And perhaps also the "good does not dwell in me" shows
free will, and that man is master of the inclination toward both, both the
good and the evil; for "does not dwell" one might say is the same as: I do not
427 have the doing of good things allotted and remaining with me by some
necessity, so as even by force to drag me away from evils; for this is
dwelling, the remaining forever and as it were a settling-in. Upon the good he
did not say dwelling, but upon sin he said it; for now from sinning we have
come into a certain habit of sin, and habit becomes equal to nature. And he
did not say the sin rooted in me, the inborn evil, but the sin dwelling in me,
that no one might think sin natural, but as in the case of a lodger able to go
out as it entered. And the same is also an advocacy of the body; for the
wickedness of those dwelling is not a slander of the nature of the house; for
what is it against the house, if some robber dwells in it? rom 7:23-24 Well
did he say: "taking me captive"; for a battle being moved between sin and the
commandments of the law, that is the reasoning that contradicts sin, sin goes
away having conquered, having taken the wretched man captive. rom 8:9 For the
Spirit is common to Father and Son. rom 8:15 And perhaps, even if the Jews
were called gods and sons of God, just as all the things among them were a
type of ours, so also the adoption; for they were neither called sons properly
but typically, nor did they receive the Spirit universally. rom 8:21 "Unto the
freedom," that is through the freedom of the glory, that is through us the
creation also will be incorruptible; for just as through us it became
corruptible, so again through us it will be incorruptible. 428 Or the "unto
the freedom" shows that unto the same into which we also, the creation, being
freed from corruption, will come, that is becoming from corruptible
incorruptible. rom 8:23 For then are the fulfillments of the adoption.
rom 8:26 And likewise also the Spirit will take thought for you, but if you
are careless, you will be neglected. This now the deacon fulfills, making
supplication. rom 8:34 And thus you can understand as toward the letter of the
saying: if the Spirit, he says, helps, if the Father justified, who is the one
able to condemn? There remains, he says, the Son. The Son therefore, he says,
has power to condemn, and yet he loves so much as even to choose death for us,
and being at the right hand of the Father and having returned to his own glory
he did not cease from the love toward us, but even entreats the Father for us;
for this is what "makes intercession" shows. And understand the entreaty thus:
by the very having become man he entreats the Father for us. rom 9:17 The
longsuffering is a power of God, and a very great power; for who would not be
astonished at God having been so longsuffering? He says therefore that for
this I have permitted you to reign, that it may be shown how longsuffering I
am. 429 rom 9:22 God was not captured by a passion of wrath. Far from it. But
since he did these things which we do when we are angry, he called the matter
wrath from what is known to us. rom 9:28 The "accomplishing" shows that all
words of knowledge and faith of God have an end, and there will not be another
after this one now being preached, not as the things of the Jews were and
passed over into the things now, but at the completion of all faith and every
word the present word of faith has been given. For the Mosaic law was given
with God prefiguring and predescribing that of grace, but the evangelical he
gives accomplishing and cutting short, that is finishing all things and
bringing them to an excellent end. And "accomplishing a word" he rendered as a
cause of the things said before; for since he said: "I will call the not my
people my people," lest someone say: and what of this? For just as some from a
people became not a people, so even if from not a people a people comes to be,
nothing prevents these also again becoming not a people. Not so, he says,
"accomplishing a word"; for those things were changing toward the better, all
needing to be recast, but now no longer, because the present calling and the
spiritual legislation is perfected. Besides, since he said: "if the number of
the sons of Israel" and what follows, lest they say that many times having
heard many promises we either did not obtain them at all, or after a little
time, for they also fell from many things through their ingratitude and
thanklessness. Not so also now, he says, but if you wish, the salvation is
perfect and short upon your hands. rom 9:29 And you can also understand this
concerning Christ: thus Cyril among the saints. 430 rom 11:9-10 What was about
to happen from them of their own choice he speaks as in a part of a curse; for
since, he says, they did not wish to see the sun of righteousness, let them
not see. It shows that they were about to come under the Romans, and the
"through all" signifies that they should not even be freed at any time.
rom 11:15 Or he says this: what will their taking-back be, except that we may
say that God who takes them back makes them alive who have died by sins.
rom 11:16 Or that he calls them holy, exhorting unto the faith, as if he said:
you are fit and suitable, the willing alone is lacking to you. rom 11:28-29
Then since they have remained in unbelief and for this reason are not beloved,
speaking securely he says: "because of the fathers," as if he said: even if
they themselves because of unbelief are not beloved, yet because of the
fathers they are still loved, whence they also await salvation. And by
"calling" he means that which has come about concerning the nation. rom 12:1-2
Or that you should not change together with the times. And how might one offer
himself a living sacrifice? How "do not be conformed," that is do not stamp
yourselves according to the fashion of this world? For it has nothing sound or
lasting; wherefore he also calls it a fashion, showing that which does not
remain; and having said this he shows the transitory. 431 rom 14:5 The saying
is not to be accepted upon a dogma of faith, yet he himself also says it.
rom 14:11-12 It has been said concerning God and Father that he himself judges
no one, but has given the judgment to the Son. Clear then that concerning the
Judge it has been said by Paul the "shall confess to God" and the "shall give
account to God"; therefore Christ is God, even if the impious do not wish it.
rom 14:15 And he advises even the faithful to abstain from foods because of
condescension to those of little faith, which also in passing he says. You
teach well, but the fight becomes a cause of blasphemy. rom 14, God built
through the cross, you therefore are destroying the building of God. rom 15:8
And it is possible also to understand thus: a servant, he says, Christ became
of the Jews, that is he became a servant for the Jews to come within the
promises, and that the Father who promised these things might prove true.
rom 15:29 Or thus: I know that coming I shall come toward filling you with the
blessing from the gospel, that is I shall anoint you toward virtue and faith.
432 rom 16:25-26 That they may live thus, he says, as the gospel declares.
"Now to him who is able to establish you," "to the only wise God"; and to
establish how? Through Jesus Christ glory, he says, unto the ages.
