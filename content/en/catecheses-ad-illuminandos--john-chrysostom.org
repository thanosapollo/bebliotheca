2.3 Thus then from above and from the beginning he continued benefiting the

For at the same time he created the first-formed man and immediately and from
the outset he settled him in paradise and granted him that toil-free life,
furnishing the enjoyment of all things in paradise except one tree. But
through incontinence that man, deceived by the woman, trampled the commandment
that had been given and insulted so great an honor. 2.4 But see here too the
greatness of the loving-kindness. For though it was necessary that one who had
become so ungrateful concerning the benefactions that had gone before should
no longer be thought worthy of any pardon but should be set outside his
providence, not only did he not do this but, just as an affectionate father
who has an unruly child, moved by natural affection, does not apply the
rebukes in proportion to the fault, nor again does he let him go altogether,
but chastens him moderately so that he may not run aground into greater
wickedness. In the same way the good God also, since he displayed great
disobedience, cast him out from that way of life, and, checking his mind
thereafter so that he might not leap away still further, condemned him to toil
and hardship, all but saying to him: 2.5 The great ease and the license led
you into so great a disobedience and made you forget my commandments, and
having nothing to do prepared you to think something more than your own
nature, For idleness taught every wickedness, therefore I condemn you to toil
and hardship so that, working the earth, you may have a continual reminder
both of the disobedience and of the cheapness of your own nature. For since
you imagined great things and were not willing to remain within your own
bounds, therefore I command you to return again there from which earth you
were taken; For you are earth, he says, and to earth you shall go. 2.6 And in
order to work for him an intensification of the pain and to make him take
perception of his own fall, he did not settle him far off somewhere but near
paradise, walling off from him the entrance there, so that, seeing every hour
of what things he had deprived himself through inattention, he might enjoy
continual admonition and become more secure for the future concerning the
keeping of the commandments that are given. For when, being in the enjoyment
of goods, we do not perceive the benefaction in the way we ought, whenever we
come into deprivation of them, then taking greater perception from the taking
away we endure also a greater pain. Which indeed then happened also in the
case of the first-formed man. 2.7 But that you may learn both the plot of the
evil demon and the wise and resourceful dealing of our Master, consider what
the devil attempted through the deceit to work upon the man, and what
loving-kindness the Master and guardian displayed concerning him. For that
evil demon, envying him the way of life in paradise, by hope of a greater
promise cast him out even from the things in his hands; for having prepared
him to imagine equality with God he led him to the penalty of death. For such
are his baits, and he not only casts us out from the goods in our hands but
also attempts to throw us into a greater precipice. But the loving God did not
even so overlook the race of men, but showing both to the devil that he had
attempted things unprofitable and to the man how great a care he displays
concerning him, through death he granted immortality. For consider: that one
cast him out of paradise, the Master brought him into heaven; greater than the
loss is the gain. 2.8 But what I was saying from the beginning, which is why I
was led to say these things also, if then he thought him who had become
ungrateful toward so many benefactions worthy again of so great
loving-kindness of his own, if you the soldiers of Christ are eager to become
grateful concerning these unspeakable gifts that are being furnished to you
and keep watch concerning the guarding of the things that have been granted,
of how much munificence from him, tell me, will you obtain after the guarding?
For he himself is the one who has said, To him who has it shall be given and
he shall have abundance. For he who works himself worthy concerning the things
already given would be just even of greater



enjoyment. 2.9 All then who have been thought worthy to be enrolled in this
heavenly book, bring in a copious faith and a robust reasoning. For the things
that take place here need faith and the eyes of the soul, so as not to attend
only to the things seen but from these to imagine the things not seen. For
such are the eyes of faith; for just as those of the body are able to see only
those things that fall under sense, so also those of faith are the opposite to
those. For they see nothing of the things seen, but they see the things not
seen as lying before the eyes. For this is faith, to attend to the things not
seen as to things seen. For faith, he says, is the substance of things hoped
for, the proof of things not seen. =2.10= What then is it that I say, and
toward what has this been said by me, not to attend to the things seen but to
possess spiritual eyes? In order that when you see the pool of the waters and
the hand of the priest touching your head, you may neither think that to be
simply water only nor that the hand of the high priest alone is laid upon your
head. For it is not a man who works the things being accomplished, but it is
the grace of the Spirit that both sanctifies the nature of the waters and,
together with the hand of the priest, touches your head. Did I not say well
that we have need of the eyes of faith toward the faith of the things not
seen, so that we may suspect nothing sensible? =2.11= For baptism is both a
tomb and a resurrection; For the old man is buried together with sin and the
new man rises, the one being renewed according to the image of him who created
him. We put off and we put on; we put off the old garment that has been soiled
by the multitude of sins, and we put on the new one that has been freed from
every stain. But what am I saying? We put on Christ himself; For as many of
you, he says, as were baptized into Christ, you put on Christ. =2.12= But
since the time is already at the doors according to which you are about to
enjoy so many gifts, come, let us teach you also, as far as possible, the
causes of each of the things that take place, so that you may be able to know
and, having received greater assurance, may thus withdraw from here. You must
then know for what reason after the daily teaching we send you on to the
voices of those who exorcise you. For this does not take place simply nor at
random, but since you are about to receive the heavenly king as a dweller
within, therefore after the admonition from us those appointed for this,
taking you, just as preparing a house for the king who is about to arrive,
thus cleanse your mind through those fearful words, putting to flight every
device of the evil one, and making it worthy of the king's presence. For it is
not possible, even if some demon be wild, even if he be harsh, after those
fearful voices and the invocation of the common Master of all, that he should
not stand off from them with much speed. And together with this, the very
thing that takes place also instills much reverence in the soul and leads it
into much compunction. =2.13= And the thing that is wonderful and paradoxical
is that every inequality and difference of ranks is here put out of the way;
for even if someone happen to be in worldly rank, even if in the distinction
of wealth, even if he think great things upon nobility and upon the glory of
the present life, he too stands similarly to the beggar and to the one wrapped
in rags, and often even to one whose eyes have been cut out and whose leg has
been maimed, and he does not take it ill at the things that take place; for he
knows that in the spiritual things all these have been driven away and only
the soul's good acknowledgment is sought. =2.14= And those voices and those
invocations, fearful and wonderful, have so great a gain; but the figure of
the nakedness of the feet and of the stretching out of the hands indicates
something else to us. Just as those who endure this bodily captivity also show
through the figure the dejection of the calamity that has overtaken them, so
also these, having become captives of the devil, since they are about



to be freed from that one's tyranny and to come under the kind yoke, first
through the figure remind themselves of the former condition, so that they may
be able to know from what they are being released, and to whom they are
running, and that this itself may become for them a ground of greater
thanksgiving and good acknowledgment. =2.15= Do you want us to transfer the
discourse also toward those who receive you, so that they too may be able to
know of what rewards they are thought worthy if they display much zeal
concerning you, and what condemnation follows them if they are slothful?
Consider with me, beloved, those who receive some upon money, how they undergo
a greater contest even than the one liable and receiving the money. For if the
one who borrowed appear grateful he lightened the burden for the one who
received him, and if again he become ungrateful he prepared a greater
precipice for that one. Therefore a certain wise man also exhorts saying, If
you go surety, take thought as one who will pay. If then those who receive
some upon money make themselves wholly liable, much more those who in the
spiritual things and for the account of virtue receive some ought to display
much wakefulness, exhorting and advising, correcting, displaying fatherly
affection. =2.16= And let them not think the thing that takes place to be a
chance matter, but let them know accurately that they themselves also become
partners of the good repute if through their own admonition they will guide
them toward the road of virtue, and that if those again are slothful much
condemnation will lie upon them. For this reason it is also the custom to call
such spiritual men fathers, so that through the very works they may learn how
much affection they ought to display in the teaching of the spiritual things
concerning such persons. For if it is a fine thing to lead those who belong to
us from nowhere into the zeal of virtue, much more that one whom we receive
into the place of a spiritual child, we ought to fulfill the charge toward
him. You who receive have learned also that no small danger hangs over you if
you are slothful. =2.17= Come then, let us converse with you also concerning
the mysteries themselves and the covenants that are about to be accomplished
between you and the Master. For just as in the affairs of this life, whenever
someone wishes to entrust his own affairs to someone, documents must be
accomplished between the one entrusted and the one entrusting; in the same way
now also, since you are about to be entrusted by the Master of all not with
perishable affairs nor with things corruptible and perishing but with
spiritual and heavenly things. For this reason it is also called faith,
because it has nothing seen but all things able to be surveyed with the eyes
of the spirit. For documents must be accomplished between, not on paper nor
through ink but in God through spirit. For the words which you send forth here
are written in heaven, and the covenants which you offer through the tongue
remain inerasable with the Master. =2.18= See again here the figure of the
captivity; for the priests, bringing you in, first command you, having bent
the knees and stretched the hands up toward heaven, thus to pray and through
the figure to remind yourselves from what you are being released, and to whom
you are about to assign yourselves. Then the priest, coming to each, demands
the covenants and the confessions and prepares you to say those fearful words
and ones full of shuddering: I renounce you, Satan. =2.19= It comes upon me
now to weep and to groan greatly; for I thought of that day according to which
I myself also was thought worthy to send forth this voice, and having reckoned
the burden of the sins which from that time until now I have collected, I am
confused in mind and I am bitten in reasoning, seeing how great a shame I
scattered upon myself through the sloth after these things. Therefore I also
exhort you all to display some munificence concerning me, and whenever you are
about to meet with the king, for he will receive you with much eagerness and
will clothe you with that royal robe and will furnish gifts



as many and of such kind as you may wish, only if we seek spiritual things, to
ask grace even on our behalf so that he may not demand accounts of the things
we have sinned but, having given pardon, may think us worthy of his
inclination for the future. But that you will do this, being affectionate
toward your teachers, I do not doubt. =2.20= Come then, let us hold to the
sequence of the discourse. Then therefore the priest prepares you to say: I
renounce you, Satan, and your pomp and your worship and your works. Few are
the words but great is their power. For angels standing by and the invisible
powers, rejoicing at your turning, receive the voices from your tongue and
carry them up to the common Master of all, and these are written in the
heavenly books. =2.21= Did you see of what sort the documents of the covenants
are? For after the renunciation of the evil one and of all the affairs that
belong to the evil one he prepares you to say again: And I enlist with you,
Christ. Did you see an excess of goodness? Receiving the words from you only,
he entrusts to you so great a treasure of affairs, forgetting all the former
ingratitude and reminding you of none of the former things, but he is content
with these brief words. =2.22= Then after this covenant and the renunciation
and the enlistment, since you confessed the mastery and through the words of
the tongue enlisted with Christ, thereafter as a soldier and one enrolled also
into the spiritual stadium he anoints you upon the forehead with the spiritual
myrrh, laying on the seal and saying: So-and-so is anointed into the name of
the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. =2.23= For since he knows
thereafter that the enemy is raging and whets his teeth and goes about as a
roaring lion and, seeing those who formerly were under his tyranny suddenly
rising up against him and having renounced him, and having changed sides and
displayed the enlistment toward Christ, therefore he anoints upon the forehead
and lays on the seal in order that he may turn away that one's eyes. For he
does not dare to look straight, seeing the lightning leaping out from there
and blinding his eyes. For thereafter battle and resistance from that time
takes place toward him, and therefore just as certain athletes of Christ he
thus through the anointing leads them into the spiritual stadium. =2.24= Then
after this, at the time of night, having stripped off the garment entire and
just as being about to lead you into heaven itself through the things being
accomplished, he thus prepares the whole body to be anointed with that
spiritual oil so that all the members may be walled through the anointing and
become unconquerable to the missiles sent from the adversary. =2.25= After
this anointing then he lets you down into the sacred streams, at once both
burying the old man and raising the new man who is being renewed according to
the image of him who created him. Then thereafter through the words of the
priest and this one's hand the visitation of the Holy Spirit flies upon, and
another instead of another comes up, having wiped off all the filth of the
sins and having put off the old garment of sin and having put on the royal
clothing. =2.26= And that you may learn from this too that the substance of
Father and Son and Holy Spirit is one, thus also the tradition of the baptism
takes place. For as the priest calls out, So-and-so is baptized into the name
of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, he lets the head down
three times and raises it, preparing through this mystical rite to receive the
visitation of the Spirit. For it is not the priest alone who touches the head
but also the right hand of Christ. And this is shown also from the very words
of the one baptizing; for he does not say, I baptize so-and-so, but, So-and-so
is baptized, showing that he himself becomes only a minister of the grace and
furnishes his own hand, since he has been appointed for this by the Spirit.
But the one who fills all is the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, the
Trinity



the undivided. The faith into this then grants the remission of sins, this
confession gifts us the adoption. =2.27= And the things that take place after
these also are sufficient to teach us from what they were freed, and of what
they have obtained who have been thought worthy of this mystical rite. For
immediately as they come up from those sacred streams all who are present
embrace them, greet them, kiss them, share their pleasure, rejoice with them
that those who formerly were slaves and captives have suddenly become free and
sons and have been called to the royal table. For after the ascent from there
they are immediately led to the fearful table and one full of countless goods
and they taste of the body and of the Master's blood and become a dwelling of
the Spirit and, just as having put on Christ himself, they go about thus,
appearing everywhere as certain earthly angels and flashing back against the
rays of the sun. =2.28= All these things I taught your love beforehand not in
vain nor at random, but in order that even before the enjoyment you may reap
much pleasure, already winged by the hope, and may take up a mind worthy of
the things that take place, and, as the blessed Paul exhorted, may mind the
things above and transfer your reasoning from the earth to heaven, from the
things seen to the things not seen. And we see these things more clearly than
the sensible sights with the spiritual eyes. =2.29= But whenever you come near
the royal forecourts and are about to draw near to the very throne where the
king sits who distributes the gifts, display all munificence concerning the
requests, nothing of this life, nothing human, but making the request worthy
of the one who furnishes. Setting foot then upon those divine streams and
displaying the symbol of the resurrection through the ascent from there, ask
the alliance from him so that you may display much guarding of the things that
have been gifted to you, so as to become unconquerable to the devices of the
evil one. Entreat concerning the peace of the churches, beseech concerning
those still wandering, fall down concerning those who are in sins so that we
may be thought worthy of some sparing. For he who has imparted to you so great
a freedom of speech and has enrolled you among the first of his friends and
has led you up into the adoption, you who formerly were captives and slaves
and without freedom of speech, will not refuse your requests but will furnish
all things to you, imitating his own goodness in this also. =2.30= And by this
especially you will draw him into greater goodwill. For when he sees you
making so great a care of your own members and taking thought for the
salvation of the rest, he will think you worthy of much freedom of speech for
this also. For nothing so gladdens him as that we be sympathetic concerning
our members and display much affection concerning the brethren and make much
provision for the salvation of those near. =2.31= Knowing these things then,
beloved, with joy and spiritual gladness prepare yourselves toward the
reception of the grace, so that you too may enjoy the gift copiously and all
in common, having displayed a way of life worthy of the grace, may be thought
worthy to obtain the eternal and unspeakable goods, by the grace and
loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus Christ,

3.t DISCOURSE 3. Of the same, a homily spoken to the newly illumined. 3.1
Blessed be God; behold, even from earth stars shine, stars brighter than those
of the heavens. Stars upon earth because of the one who appeared from the
heavens upon earth; and not stars upon earth only, but also stars in day; this
is a second wonder. Stars in day brighter than those in night. For those, when
the sun has appeared,



are hidden, but these, when the sun of righteousness has appeared, shine forth
the more. Did you ever see stars appearing together with a sun? 3.2 Those,
when the consummation has appeared, vanish, but these, when the consummation
has overtaken them, shine through more greatly. And concerning those the
gospel says that the stars of heaven shall fall as leaves fall from a vine;
but concerning these, that the righteous shall shine forth as the sun in the
kingdom of the heavens. 3.3 What is as a leaf falls from a vine, thus the
stars of heaven shall fall? Just as the vine, as long as it nourishes the
clusters, has need also of the covering from the leaves, but whenever it has
put off the fruit it puts off also the foliage from the leaves, so indeed also
the whole world, as long as it has in itself the nature of men, the heaven
holds also the stars, just as the vine the leaves; for night then not being,
there is no need of stars thereafter. 3.4 Fiery is the nature of those stars,
fiery also is the substance of these stars. But there it is sensible fire, and
here intelligible fire. He himself will baptize you, he says, in Holy Spirit
and fire. Do you want to learn the names of both also? In those stars there
are these names, Orion, Arcturus, Hesperus and Phosphorus; but in these stars
there is no Hesperus, but all are light-bearers. 3.5 Blessed be God, let us
say again, who alone does wonders, who makes all things and refashions them.
Those who before yesterday were captives, now free and citizens of the church;
those formerly in the shame of sins, now in freedom of speech and
righteousness. For they are not free only but also holy, not holy only but
also righteous, not righteous only but also sons, not sons only but also
heirs, not heirs only but also brothers of Christ, not brothers of Christ only
but also fellow-heirs, not fellow-heirs only but also members, not members
only but also a temple, not a temple only but also instruments of the Spirit.
3.6 Blessed be God who alone does wonders. Did you see how many are the gifts
of baptism? And yet to many it seems that the gift has only remission of sins,
but we enumerated ten honors. For this reason at any rate we baptize the
children also although they do not have sin, in order that sanctification may
be added, righteousness, adoption, inheritance, brotherhood, the being members
of Christ, the becoming a dwelling-place of the Spirit. 3.7 But O most
longed-for brethren, if indeed it is permitted me to call you brethren, for I
shared with you the same birth-pangs, but by the sloth after these things I
lost the accurate and genuine kinship. Nevertheless grant me to call you
brethren because of the much love and to exhort you so that by how much more
honor you have enjoyed, by so much you may display a greater zeal. 3.8 For the
time before this was a wrestling-school and a gymnasium and the falls had
pardon; but from the present day the stadium has been opened, the contest has
come on, the theater sits above, not the nature of men only but also a company
of angels watches the wrestlings, and Paul cries writing to the Corinthians,
We have become a theater to the world, not to men only but also to angels.
Angels then watch, the Master of the angels is president of the games; this is
not honor only but also security. For when he who laid down his soul for us
judges the wrestlings, of what honor is this not, of how much security is it
not? 3.9 In the Olympic contests then the umpire stands in the middle of those
competing, attached neither to this one nor to that one, but waiting for the
end. Therefore he has also stood in the middle since he is also in the middle
in judgment. But in the case of us and the devil Christ has not stood in the
middle, but he is wholly ours. And that he is not in the middle but is wholly
with us, consider from this. Us entering into the contest he anointed, that
one he bound; us he anointed with oil of gladness, that one he bound with
unbreakable bonds so that he may be entangled for the wrestlings. To me, even
if it happen that I am tripped, he stretches a hand and raises me having
fallen and makes me trample him again; For trample, he says, upon



serpents and scorpions and upon all the power of the enemy. =3.10= To that one
after the victory he threatened Gehenna; I, if I conquer, am crowned; that
one, if he conquers, is punished. And that you may know that he is then
punished more when he prevails, come, I will show you from the facts. He
conquered Adam and tripped him. What then was the prize of the victory? Upon
your breast and your belly you shall go and earth you shall eat all the days
of your life. And if he thus punished the sensible serpent, with what
punishment will he punish the intelligible one? If such is the condemnation of
the instrument, it is quite clear that a much greater punishment awaits the
craftsman. For just as an affectionate father, having found the one who killed
his own son, not only punishes the murderer but also cuts the sword itself to
pieces, so indeed also Christ, having found the devil having killed the man,
not only punished the devil but also broke the sword itself. =3.11= Therefore
with confidence let us strip for the wrestlings; for he has also thrown around
us weapons more gleaming than all gold, stronger than all adamant, hotter and
more vehement than all fire, lighter than all air. For the nature of these
weapons does not weigh down the knees, but wings the members, and lifts them,
and if you should wish to fly up into heaven with these weapons, there is
nothing to hinder. For new is the nature of the weapons since new also is the
manner of this battle; for being a man I am compelled to box with demons,
wearing flesh I wrestle against bodiless powers. Therefore God made the
breastplate for me not from iron but from righteousness; therefore he
constructed the shield for me not from bronze but from faith. I have also a
sharpened sword, the word of the Spirit. That one sends missiles against me, I
have a sword; that one is an archer, I am a heavy-armed soldier. Learn also
from this that one's mind; the archer does not dare to come near but shoots
from afar. =3.12= What then? Did he construct weapons only? No, but he also
prepared a table more powerful than every weapon, so that you may not fight
while wearying, so that while feasting you may prevail over the evil one. For
if he see you only returning from the Master's dinners, just as one seeing a
lion sending forth fire from the mouth, so he flees more vehemently than every
wind; and if you show him the tongue bloodied with the precious blood, he will
not even be able to stand; if you show the mouth reddened, just as a cheap
beast he will thus run backward. =3.13= Do you want then to learn the strength
of this blood? Let us run back to its type, to the old narratives, the ones in
Egypt. God was about to bring a tenth plague upon the Egyptians; for he wished
to destroy their firstborn since they were holding his firstborn people. What
then was to happen so that the Jews might not be mixed with the Egyptians,
since one place held them? Learn the power of the type in order that you may
learn also the strength of the truth. A plague sent from God was about to be
borne from above and the destroyer was coming upon the houses. =3.14= What
then did Moses? Sacrifice, he says, a lamb without blemish and smear its blood
on the doors. What are you saying? Does blood of an irrational animal know how
to save rational men? Yes, he says, not because it is blood but because it is
a type of the Master's blood. For just as the royal statues, being soulless
and without perception, save the men who flee to them who are perceptive and
ensouled, not because they are bronze but because they are an image of the
king, so indeed also that blood, without perception and soulless, saved the
men who have a soul, not because it was blood but because it was a type of
this blood. =3.15= Then the destroyer saw the blood smeared on the doors and
did not dare to leap in; now if the devil see not the blood of the type
smeared on doors but the blood of the truth smeared on the mouth of the
faithful, on the doors of the temple that bears Christ, will he not much more
hold himself back? For if an angel seeing the type was in awe, much more will
the devil seeing the truth flee. =3.16= Do you want



to learn from another place also the strength of this blood? See from where it
flowed at the beginning and from where it had the spring, from above from the
cross, from the Master's side. For when Christ, he says, had died and was
still on the cross, the soldier coming near pierced the side with the lance,
then there came out water and blood. And the one was a symbol of the baptism,
and the other of the mysteries. Therefore he did not say, There came out blood
and water, but first there came out the water and then the blood, since first
is the baptism and then the mysteries. That soldier then pierced the side, he
dug through the wall of the holy temple and I found the treasure and took the
wealth. Thus it happened also in the case of the lamb; the Jews slaughtered
the sheep and I reaped the salvation from the sacrifice. =3.17= There came out
from the side water and blood. Do not run past the mystery simply, beloved.
For I have also another mystical word to say; I said that that blood and the
water is a symbol of the baptism and of the mysteries. From both of these the
church has been born, through a washing of regeneration and renewal of Holy
Spirit, through baptism and through mysteries. And the symbols of the baptism
and of the mysteries are from the side. From the side then Christ created the
church, just as from the side of Adam he created Eve. =3.18= Therefore Moses
also, going through concerning the first man, says, Bone from my bones and
flesh from my flesh, hinting to us at the side of the Master. For just as then
God took the side and formed a woman, so he gave us blood and water from his
side and formed the church anew. As then at that time in the trance he took
the side, Adam sleeping, so now also after the death he gave the blood and the
water, and first the water then the blood. But what then the trance was, this
now the death has become, in order that you may learn that this death is sleep
thereafter. =3.19= Did you see how Christ joined the bride to himself? Did you
see with what food he nourishes us all? From the same food we both have been
constituted and are nourished. For just as a woman nourishes the child that
has been born with her own blood and milk, so also Christ nourishes those whom
he himself generated continually with his own blood. =3.20= Since then we have
enjoyed so great a gift let us display much zeal and let us remember the

become deserted and naked of all reverence and understanding. 5.9 For the one
who is drunk does not know how to manage his words in judgment, but just as a
house opened from every side and easy of access to all who plot, so is the

drunkenness is nothing else than a betrayal of reasonings and a calamity that

your own salvation and on behalf of the things by which you were eager to heal
your member. This is the boast of the church, this the command of the savior,
not to consider our own things only but also those of the one near. =6.19= For
consider into how great a dignity he raises himself who makes much of the
salvation of the brother; according to human power such a one imitates God.
For hear through the prophet what he says, He who leads out a worthy one from
an unworthy shall be as my mouth. He who is eager, he says, to save the
neglected brother and to snatch him from the throat of the devil, according to
human power imitates me. What could become equal to this? This is greater than
all the accomplishments, this is the head of all the virtue. =6.20= And very
reasonably; for if Christ poured out his own blood because of our salvation



and Paul cries saying upon those who furnish the scandals and harm the
conscience of those who see that the weak brother will perish upon your
knowledge for whom Christ died, if then your Master poured out the blood
because of him, how is it not just that each of us at least bring in the
exhortation through the words and stretch a hand to those who from sloth have
fallen into the snares of the devil. But that you will do this, being
affectionate concerning your members and with all zeal will lead your brethren
back toward the common mother, I am wholly persuaded, knowing that being
intelligent through the grace of God you are able to admonish others also.
=6.21= And I wish thereafter to turn the discourse toward the newly illumined;
and I say newly illumined not those recently thought worthy of the spiritual
gift but those from a year before and from more time. For if they wish, these
too will be able continually to enjoy this appellation. For this youth does
not know old age, is not subject to disease, is not caught by despondency, is
not dimmed by time, yields to nothing, is defeated by nothing, except by sin
only. For the heavy old age of this is sin. =6.22= And that you may learn that
this is heavier than all, hear the prophet saying, As a heavy burden they were
made heavy upon me. And not only does it happen to be heavy, but also
ill-smelling. For he added, My bruises stank and rotted. Did you see sin not
only heavy but also ill-smelling? Learn also from where this is born through
the things added; For from the face, he says, of my folly. So then folly is a
cause to us of all the evils. It is possible then even being an old man
according to the age of the body to be young and newly illumined according to
the prime of the grace, and being young according to the body to have grown
old according to the multitude of the sins. For wherever this takes an
entrance, it works much the spot, much the wrinkle. =6.23= Therefore I exhort
both those recently thought worthy of the baptism and those who obtained the
gift before this, the ones to wipe off through confession and tears and most
exact repentance the filth that has gone before, and the others to keep the
brightness blooming and to look around at the beauty of the soul so as to
receive no drop able to work filth. Do you not see those who throw around
themselves the brilliant clothing, how much zeal they make walking upon the
marketplace lest somewhere a drop of mire shot out ruin the beauty of the
garment, and yet no harm would come to be from here to the soul; for it is a
garment and consumed by moth and spent by time, and otherwise even having been
soiled it is easily cleansed with water. But in the case of the soul if it
should ever come to be, which may it not be, and it will receive some stain
either through tongue or through reasonings born in the mind, immediately much
ruin comes in, much the weight of the burden, much the ill smell. =6.24=
Therefore I too, fearing the devices of the enemy, make the exhortation
continual so that you may keep the bridal robe unmixed and with this may
always go into this spiritual marriage. For that the things being accomplished
here are a spiritual marriage, see how just as in these human marriages the
bridal chambers are stretched as far as seven days, so we also in so many days
stretch this spiritual bridal chamber for you, setting beside you the mystical
table, the one full of countless goods. And what am I saying seven days? If
you wish to be sober and to keep watch, through all the time these bridal
chambers are stretched for you, only if you keep your bridal garment unmixed
and bright. =6.25= For thus you will both draw the bridegroom into greater
love and you yourselves as time goes forward will appear more brilliant and
brighter, the grace being increased more through the working of good actions.
And may it be that we all both display a guarding worthy of the gift already
granted and be thought worthy of the loving-kindness from above; by grace and
compassions of his only-begotten Son and Lord



of us Jesus Christ, with whom to the Father together with the Holy Spirit,
glory, power, honor; now and always and unto the ages of ages. Amen.

7.t DISCOURSE 7. Of the same, that the relics of the holy martyrs become a
ground of greatest benefit for us and that it is necessary to despise all the
things of this life and to hold to the spiritual things and how great a good
prayer is and almsgiving; and toward the newly illumined. 7.1 The loving God,
displaying much and various provision concerning the race of men, not only
created all the creation and stretched out the heaven and spread the sea and
kindled a sun and prepared a moon to shine and granted the earth toward
dwelling and furnished all the things from the earth toward nourishment and
constitution of our bodies, but also has granted us the relics of the holy
martyrs, and having himself taken the souls, For souls of righteous men, he
says, are in a hand of God, he left the bodies to us for the present as a
sufficient consolation and comfort so that, coming to be beside the tombs of
these holy ones, we may be awakened into zeal and imitation and through the
sight may take a reminder of the things accomplished by them and of the
recompenses laid up upon the accomplishments. 7.2 For a great profit comes to
our souls from here if we are sober; for neither is a word able thus to teach
and to lead into philosophy and the disdain of the present things as the
sufferings of the martyrs sending forth a voice more brilliant than a trumpet
and showing to all through the facts the greatness of the recompense of the
toils here and the excess of the repayment. For as much as a word falls short
of action, by so much the things said by us fall short of the teaching from
these holy ones. 7.3 Whenever then having come to be here, beloved, you reckon
with the mind that this whole people is eager to run together with so great a
zeal so as to embrace the dust and to reap the blessing from here, how will
you not become thereafter lifted in mind and will you not be eager to display
the equal zeal to the martyr, so that you yourself also may be thought worthy
of the same recompenses? For consider, if from us the fellow-slaves they enjoy
so great an honor here, of how much and how great a freedom of speech they
will enjoy with the Master in that fearful day, when they are about to flash
more brightly than the solar rays; for then, he says, the righteous shall
shine forth as the sun. 7.4 Knowing then the greatness of their freedom of
speech, let us always flee to them and take the alliance from them. For if men
who have freedom of speech toward the king upon earth are able to benefit
those who flee to them many and great things, much more these holy ones who
have acquired the freedom of speech toward the king of the heavens through
their own sufferings will profit us the greatest things, only if we bring in
the things from ourselves; for then especially also the protection from them
will be able to benefit us, if we are not slothful but ourselves also are
eager by the attendance and by the care concerning the life to draw the
loving-kindness of the Master upon ourselves. 7.5 As toward spiritual
physicians then let us continually flee to these. For this reason the good
Master left their bodies to us, so that, coming to be here and embracing these
with the disposition of the soul, we may take from here the greatest healing
both of the soul's and of the body's illnesses. For if we come to be with
faith, even if we have a soul's passion even if a bodily one, having received
the suitable treatment we shall thus return. 7.6 And yet in the case of the
bodily passions it is often even possible to send a long journeying so as to
obtain the hands of the physician and to put down money



and to invent many other things able to persuade that one to bring in the
knowledge from his own art and to find a consolation for the passion. But here
we need none of these, not a long traveling, not labor, not a great circuit,
not an expense of money, but it is sufficient, having brought in a genuine
faith and having poured forth warm tears with a sober soul, immediately both
to find the healing for the soul and to carry the treatment of the body. 7.7
Did you see a power of physicians? did you see munificence? did you see an art
not conquered by diseases? For even in the case of the bodily passions often
the difficulty of the disease conquered the art of the physician. But here
there is nothing such ever to suspect, but if we make the approach with faith,
immediately we reap the benefit. And do not wonder, beloved; for the loving
Master, since because of him and the confession into him they endured all
things and as far as blood they stood against, having stripped toward the sin,
wishing to show them more brilliant through this also and to increase their
glory more even in this perishable life, unto their honor he munificently
gifts the gifts to those who come forward with faith. 7.8 And that the things
said by us are not words only but the very experience of the facts also bears
witness, you too I well know will say and will bear witness. For what woman
who has the husband in a journeying and takes it ill at the separation, having
come to be here and having brought the supplication through the holy martyrs
to the Master of all, did not more quickly bring him back from the long
journeying? and another again seeing her own child besieged by a harsh illness
and being torn so to speak and cut through in the bowels, having arrived here
and having sent forth warm tears and having awakened these holy ones into the
petition on her behalf, I mean the crowned ones of Christ, did she not
immediately drive out the disease and prepare the sick one to come back toward
health? 7.9 And many also having fallen into circumstances of affairs and
seeing unbearable dangers hanging over them, having arrived here and having
made the prayer intense, fled the experience of all those terrible things. And
what am I saying bodily diseases and circumstances of affairs? Many also being
tyrannized by the devil himself and being besieged by soul's passions, having
come to these spiritual physicians and having come into memory of their own
faults and having stripped their sores so to speak with the word, received so
great a consolation from here as immediately to take perception and to work
their conscience lighter and with much assurance to return home. =7.10= For
just as spiritual springs able to pour forth the streams copiously, so the
Master has granted us the chests of the holy martyrs. And just as the springs
of the waters lie open without fear to all who wish to draw the streams from
there and as much as the size of the vessel is able to receive, so much having
taken the one who wishes goes away, in the same way it is possible to see also
in the case of these spiritual springs. For these springs too are opened to
all and there is no difference of persons here, but even if someone be rich
even if poor, even if a slave even if free, even if a man even if a woman, he
receives so great a measure from these divine streams as he is eager to bring
in a size of eagerness. =7.11= For what the vessels are there toward the
reception of the waters, this here is the mind and the boiling eagerness and
the approach with sobriety. For the one who thus comes forward immediately
carries the countless goods, the grace of God invisibly lightening his
conscience and furnishing much assurance and preparing him thereafter to be
released from the earth and to shift harbor toward heaven. For it is possible
even while wrapped with a body to have nothing in common with the earth but to
imagine all the things in the heavens and to look around at those things



continually. =7.12= Therefore Paul also writing was saying to men wrapped with
flesh and turning about in the middle and taking thought for wife and
children, Mind the things above. Then in order that we may learn what this
exhortation wishes for him and what it is to mind the things above he added,
Where Christ is sitting at the right of God. Those things, he says, I wish you
to mind which are able to transfer your reasoning there, the things that set
you off from the affairs upon the earth. For your polity exists in heaven.
Where then you were enrolled, he says, there be eager to transfer all your
mind and plan and do those things which will be able to show you worthy of the
polity there. =7.13= And in order that we may not think him to be charging
some impossible things and ones that overstep our nature, again he seconds the
exhortation and says, Mind the things above, not the things upon the earth.
What is it that he wishes to teach us? Do not, he says, mind these things that
are worthy of the earth. And what are the things worthy of the earth? The
things that have nothing lasting, the things that fly away before they appear,
the things that have nothing firm nor unmoved, the things that are dissolved
together with the present life, the things that wither before they bloom, the
things that are subject to corruption. For such are all the human things even
if you say wealth even if power even if glory even if bloom of body even if
all the good days of life. =7.14= Therefore he also used such a word having
said, Not the things upon the earth, wishing by the name of the earth to
indicate their much cheapness. Do not then mind these things, he says, but the
things above; instead of the things upon the earth, these cheap and
running-past things, mind the things above, he says, the things in the
heavens, the unmoved things, the things that stretch along with the continual
age, the things seen with the eyes of faith, the things that do not know an
end, the things that do not know a succession, the things that do not have a
limit. Around those I wish your reasoning continually to turn. For the study
in these things releases from the earth and transfers toward heaven. =7.15=
Therefore Christ also was saying, Where the treasure of the man is, there also
is his heart. For the soul, whenever it receives the thought of those
unspeakable goods, just as having been released from the bonds of the body,

not even able to take a thought of the affairs upon the earth but runs past

the exhortation of this blessed one, the wonderful teacher of the inhabited

which he himself advised; for thus we shall be able both to enjoy the present
things and to obtain the things to come. For if we seek those things first, we
shall have these also in the part of an addition; For seek, he says, the
kingdom of God and his righteousness, and all these things shall be added to
you. Do not then seek first the things which he promised to furnish us in the
part of an addition, so that we may not, accomplishing things opposite to the
Master's exhortation, fall out of both. For does the Master wait for the
reminder from us and then furnish us the gifts from himself? He knows of what
things we have need before we ask him. If then he sees us having the zeal
concerning those things, he will both grant us the enjoyment of those and will
furnish these with copiousness which he promised to gift in the part of an
addition. Let us seek then, I exhort, the spiritual things first and let us
mind the things above, not the things upon the earth, so that we may both
obtain those and enjoy these. =7.17= Since these holy martyrs also, because
they minded the things above and despised the earth and sought those things,
therefore they both obtained those with copiousness and enjoy the honor here
each day, even if they do not need this, having once despised it, yet
nevertheless because of our benefit they accept



the honor from us even while not needing it, so that we may be able to reap
the blessing from them. =7.18= And that you may learn how they despised all

depositing into the souls of the pupils the sowing of the divine lessons. 8.4
Do not then looking simply toward the figure and the conversation of the
tongue run past their virtue, but accurately learn their angelic life, the
philosophical way of living. For all luxury and gluttony has been driven away
among these; and not only these but also the remaining stupidity that is
practiced in the cities, and they eat only so many things as are able to
suffice them toward the constitution of life, and all the remaining time they
occupy their own mind in hymns and continual prayers and in this imitating the
angelic way of living. 8.5 For just as for those bodiless powers this one
thing only is a work, to hymn through all the creator of all, in the same way
these wonderful men also both console the need of the body because of having
been wrapped with flesh, and all the other time they attend to the hymns and
the prayers, having said a long farewell to the worldly imaginings and through
their best way of life they are eager to lead the subjects into imitation of
themselves. Who then would bless these according to worth because, not
partaking of the schooling from outside, they were taught the true wisdom,
showing that apostolic saying being fulfilled through the works, that the
foolish thing of God is wiser than men. 8.6 For when you see this private and
rustic person who knows nothing more than the things according to farming and
the care of the earth making no account of the present things, but having the
mind winged toward the goods laid up in the heavens and knowing how to be
philosophical concerning those unspeakable goods and knowing these things with
exactness which the philosophers who think great things upon the beard and the
staff were not even able ever to imagine, how will you not take a clear proof
of the power of God? For from where else, tell me, is there for these so great
a philosophy of virtue and the not attending to the things seen but preferring
the unclear and unseen and the things in hopes to the things appearing and
being in hands? For this is faith, whenever someone thinks the things promised
by God, even if they do not appear to these eyes of the body, more trustworthy
than the things appearing and lying before our eyes. 8.7 Thus all the
righteous were well reputed and were thought worthy of those unspeakable
goods. Thus the patriarch Abraham was proclaimed by the Master, having run
past the weakness of the human nature and having stretched all his mind toward
the power of the one who promised. Therefore he is also proclaimed by the
divine scripture; For Abraham, he says, believed God and it was reckoned to
him unto righteousness. Therefore also from the outset having heard, Go out
from your land and from your kinship and from the house of your father, and
come into a land which I will show you, with much eagerness he obeyed and led
the thing charged into work and was leaving his own land where he happened to
have encamped, and was going out not knowing where he would stand. But he
preferred the thing commanded by the Master to the clear and agreed things and
not only did he not busy himself about the command nor was he troubled in
reasoning, but looking toward the dignity of the one who commanded, having run
past all the human hindrances, he was of one thing only, to leave out nothing
of the things charged. 8.8 And these things were coming to be not because of
the righteous one only so that the greatness of his faith might be shown, but
so that we also may become zealots of the patriarch. For since he saw his
grateful soul, therefore just as a luminary that was escaping notice and
hidden he wished to transfer him into the Canaanite land so that he might
guide those wandering there and still having the mind maimed by the darkness
of ignorance into the word of piety. Which indeed also came to be and through
that one thereafter not those who dwell in Palestine only



but also those in Egypt were learning both the provision of God concerning him
and the virtue of the righteous one. For see the excess of his greatness of
soul, how having been winged by the longing concerning God he was not stopping
at the things seen nor attending only to the things promised, but was
imagining the things to come. And though God promised a land instead of a land
and said, Go out from your land and come into a land which I will show you, he
himself having left the sensible things was letting himself go of the
intelligible. 8.9 Perhaps the thing said seems to you to be a riddle? But do
not be troubled; I bring on the proof so that you may learn how the righteous
one, having received a promise of sensible things, had the desire concerning
the intelligible. From where then shall we know this accurately? Let us hear
him saying, or rather the blessed Paul, the teacher of the inhabited world,
who knows all these things with exactness, saying concerning him, and not
concerning him only but also concerning all the righteous. For wishing at some
time to remember the catalogue of the righteous, such as of Abraham, of Isaac,
of Jacob, he says, According to faith these all died not having received the
promises but having seen them from afar and having greeted them and having
confessed that they are strangers and sojourners upon the earth. =8.10= What
are you saying, O blessed Paul? Did they not receive the promises? Did they
not occupy all Palestine? Did they not become masters of the land? Yes, he
says; they have received Palestine and the occupation of the land, but with
the eyes of faith they had the desire concerning other things. He added then
saying, For those who say these things make it appear that they seek a
fatherland and if they were remembering that from which they went out, they
would have had a time to turn back; but now they reach after a better, that
is, a heavenly. Did you see their longing? Did you see their desire? Did you
see how, though God everywhere was promising sensible things and conversing
about land, these were seeking that fatherland and were reaching after that
heavenly one? For this reason he added, Of which God is craftsman and creator.
Did you see how they were desiring the intelligible things and were imagining
those things that do not appear to the bodily eyes but are thought with the
faith? =8.11= But here my mind is troubled and the reasoning is confused,
whenever I think that we come opposite to all these. For just as these
righteous, having received a promise of sensible things, had the desire
concerning the intelligible, so we, having received a promise of intelligible
things, have been fluttered about the sensible and we do not hear the blessed
Paul saying, For the things seen are for a time, but the things not seen are
eternal. And again elsewhere making clear that such are the things prepared
for those who love him he says, Things which an eye did not see and an ear did
not hear and they did not go up upon a heart of man. But we even after all
these things have gaped about the present things, I mean the wealth and the
glory of the present life, the luxury, the honors from men; for these seem to
be the brilliant things of the present life. Seem, I said, since they have
differed nothing from a shadow and dreams. =8.12= For even wealth itself often
did not remain as far as the evening with those who think to hold it, but just
as an ungrateful runaway it transfers from this one toward another, and leaves
naked and deserted those who with much zeal treat it; and that it also often
threw around unbearable dangers those who have much desire concerning it, the
very experience of the facts has become a teacher to all. And some such thing
is also the human glory; for he who today appears brilliant and distinguished
among all suddenly becomes without honor and easy to despise for all. =8.13=
What then of these would become more nothing, I mean of the things that fly
away before they appear, of the things that never stand but thus quickly run
past those fluttered about these things? For just as it is never possible to
see the wheel remaining upon the same part of the rim but continually being
turned and being led around up and down, so it is possible to see also in the
case of the affairs. For sharp-turning is the change of human affairs



and swift the falling-over and having nothing firm nor unmoved, but all things
easily overturned and having much the inclination toward the opposite. What
then would be more laughable than these who have gaped and been nailed toward
the present things, but think these more precious than the things that exist
continually and always remain? =8.14= Therefore the prophet also making a
vehement accusation of those fluttered about these things says, They reckoned
them as standing and not as fleeing. See how with one saying he wished to set
beside their nothingness, he did not say as being furnished, he did not say as
being changed, he did not say as running past, but what? as fleeing, wishing
to indicate their speed and the much and sudden change and to teach us never
to have melted toward the things seen but to have trusted and to be confident
in those things only which God may promise. =8.15= For even if countless
hindrances come to be in the middle, it is never possible that the things
promised by God fall through. For just as he himself happens to be unturned
and unalterable and always remaining and continually, so also his promises
happen to be unfailing and unmoved, except if perhaps they are hindered from
coming to a work. But in the case of human affairs the opposite happens. For
just as the nature of men is corruptible and perishable, so also the gifts
from men are corruptible and withering. And reasonably; since we all men are
also corruptible and the nature of the human gifts imitates our nature. But in
the case of the promises of God there is nothing such ever to suspect, but
those things promised alone have the firm and unmoved and stable and solid.
=8.16= Therefore, I exhort, let us seek those things that always remain and do
not admit a change. For this reason I also on purpose brought this discourse
into the middle so that I may make the exhortation common toward all both
toward those initiated of old and toward those recently thought worthy of the
gift of the baptism. Since then in the days that have passed, continually
coming to be beside the tombs of the holy martyrs, we reaped much the blessing
from there and enjoyed the teaching copiously, and thereafter the continuity
of the gatherings is about to be cut through, I necessarily remind your love
so as to have ringing in the ear the memory of so great a teaching and to make
the spiritual things more precious than all the things of this life. =8.17=
And with much zeal both the prayers under the dawn and the confessions, coming
to be here, to render to the God of all and to give thanks on behalf of the
things already granted and to entreat to be thought worthy of much alliance
toward the guarding for the future; and thus after the going-out from here
with all reverence let each take hold of the fitting affairs. And let one be
eager toward the working from the hands, and another hasten into the roll of
the army, another about the civic affairs; yet let each come forward to the
affairs with fear and agony and let him complete the time of the day thus as
owing around the time of the evening again to come to be here and to render
the account of the whole day to the Master and to ask pardon concerning the
things fallen. For it is not possible even if we secure ourselves ten thousand
times not to set ourselves down as liable to many and various falls; for
either we uttered something unseasonably, or we received a vain hearing, or we
moved an unseemly reasoning according to the mind, or we saw rashly, or we
consumed the time vainly and at random and toward nothing needful. =8.18= And
therefore it is fitting that we each evening ask the pardon on behalf of all
these from the Master and flee to the loving-kindness of God and entreat. And
having spent the time of the night with sobriety, thus again to meet upon the
confession under the dawn so that thus each of us, managing his own life, may
both be able to run through the sea of the present life without danger and to
be thought worthy of the loving-kindness from the Master.



And whenever a time of gathering call, let the spiritual things and the coming
together here be preferred to all things, so that the things in our hands also
may be managed with security. =8.19= For if these are first for us, in those
we shall have no toil, the loving God furnishing us much ease in them; but if
neglecting the spiritual things we are eager about those only and making no
account of the soul we continually turn about the things of this life, we
shall both endure the loss of these and in those we shall have nothing more.
Do not then reverse the order, I exhort, but knowing the goodness of our
Master let us yield all things to him and let us not stretch ourselves with
the worldly cares. For he who led us from the not being into the being because
of his own loving-kindness will much more furnish us all the remaining
provision; For your heavenly Father, he says, knows that you need all these
things before you ask him. =8.20= For this reason indeed he wishes us to have
been released from every such care and to have all the leisure concerning the
spiritual things. For seek you, he says, the spiritual things and I will
furnish you with copiousness all the bodily things. From here all the
righteous also were well reputed. For from the virtue of these this discourse
of ours also was led into the middle; for we were saying that those, having
received a promise of sensible affairs, were seeking the intelligible; but we
do the opposite to those and, having a promise of intelligible things, have
gaped about the sensible. =8.21= For this reason indeed, I exhort, at least
now we who are in the grace, let us imitate those who from home and before the
law, from the teaching lying in the nature, were able to reach so great a
height of virtue, and let us transfer all the zeal upon the care of the soul
and let us exchange our cares and let us divide the thought and of the soul
let us ourselves take up the care, since this is the more sovereign in us; but
of the body let us yield all the thought and the care to the common Master of
all. =8.22= And this also is a greatest token of his wisdom and of the
unspeakable loving-kindness, that of the greater in us, I mean the soul, he
entrusted the care to us, teaching us through the very facts that he worked us
self-determining and left it upon us and upon our judgment both to choose the
virtue and to desert toward the wickedness; but of all the bodily things he
himself promised to furnish the provision, wishing to shame also through this
the human nature so as not to have trusted in its own power nor to think it
able to contribute anything toward the constitution of the present life.
=8.23= For this reason indeed he urges us who have been honored with reason
and thought worthy of so great an attendance upon the imitation of the
irrational creatures and says, Look at the birds of the heaven that they do
not sow nor reap nor gather into storehouses and your heavenly Father feeds
them, as if he were saying: If of the irrational birds I make so great a
thought as to furnish them all things unsown and unplowed, much more of you
the rational I will make a greater care, if you should choose to prefer the
spiritual things to the fleshly. For if I also led these through you and all
the creation, and I make so great a provision even of these, of how much care
would I not think you worthy, because of whom all these also were led. =8.24=
Let us be confident then, I exhort, in the promise of God and let us stretch
our whole mind about the longing of the spiritual things and let us think all
things second to the enjoyment of the things to come, so that we may both
obtain the present things with copiousness and be able to be thought worthy of
the goods promised to us and to be freed from the punishment in Gehenna. Let
not again, I pray, all the time of the day be consumed upon sloth and
unprofitable spendings of time and upon corrupted gatherings and upon
drinking-bouts and daily drunkenness, let us not make the things well
collected flow away through the neglect after these things but with security



let us hold the all that has been gifted to us from the loving-kindness of
God. =8.25= And especially you who have newly put on Christ and have received
the visitation of the Spirit, each day, I exhort, look around at the
brightness of your garment so as nowhere to receive a spot or a wrinkle, not
through unseasonable words, not through a vain hearing, not through wicked
reasonings, not through eyes leaping simply and at random upon those who
happen to meet. From every side then let us all wall ourselves with the
continual memory of that fearful day so that, having remained in brightness
and having kept the garment of incorruption spotless and unstained, we may be
thought worthy of those unspeakable gifts; of which may it be that we all
obtain by the grace and loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom to
the Father together with the Holy Spirit be glory, power, honor; now and
always and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
