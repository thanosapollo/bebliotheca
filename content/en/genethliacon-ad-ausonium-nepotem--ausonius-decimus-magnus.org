** BIRTHDAY POEM TO AUSONIUS THE GRANDSON AUSONIUS THE GRANDFATHER TO AUSONIUS THE GRANDSON
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1-h1
:END:

When already in your boyish years
the care of a constant master, soothing, would hand you first songs
and would steep new ears and following senses,
so that an amended tongue might carry through voices to be answered
with a docile murmur also, from a rude palate,
we old men added nothing sad, lest the care of warning
should wound or spoil sweet tastes with bitter.
But now, since you thrive with the motion of already pubescent age
and can distinguish the strong from the tender and yourself
seem a monitor of manners and of speaking,
receive not precepts indeed, but the vows of a praying
and congratulating grandfather for the festive solemnity of his grandson.
He has nodded that I may spend as a safe man old age brought back from the end of the fates
and see the festive day and the doubted stars,
almost already lamented, with life laid down.
This, honey-sweet grandson, born at doubled interest
as your birthday arrives, and this gift of safety
is fuller for us, because already for you the honor of pubescent age
grows and I an old man now see my grandson a youth.
This sixth three-year period returns to you from the first year,
bringing back the September Ides with known birthdays.

The Ides, a kindly day, also cultivated by the genii of the gods.
Latonian Hecate claims the August Ides,
Mercury the May Ides, joined to the honor of the gods above.
Maro, once born, dedicates the October Ides:
May you often keep all the Ides in twice six months,
whoever shall have celebrated the Ides of my Ausonius.
Farewell, sweetest grandson.
