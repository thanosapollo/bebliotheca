unbearable to men. For death also had power to reign over all and against all.
But our Lord is immortal and incorruptible; and as he is immortal and
incorruptible, so is he good and a lover of men. For of a good Father the Word
is good; wherefore he did not overlook that what had come to be should perish,
nor did he endure that another should become Lord of his own works; but seeing
the unbearable thing of wickedness, and that the mortal race was not
sufficient to stand against death, nor able to pay the punishment of the
evils; for the excess of wickedness overtopped every punishment; and seeing
also the goodness of the Father, seeing also his own sufficiency and power;
"For Christ is power of God and wisdom of God;" he is moved by love of men,
and having pitied our weakness, he put this on. For he himself, as the prophet
says, took our weaknesses, and the diseases =28.205= he bore. And having had
mercy on our mortal thing, he wrapped himself in this. For Paul says: "He
humbled himself unto death, and death of a cross." And seeing also our
inability toward the payment of the punishment, he himself received this; "For
Christ has become a curse on our behalf." And so then, having wrapped himself
in the human things and clothed himself, he brought our things through himself
to the Father, in order that, as himself suffering, he might render the
suffering man unharmed, and might exchange small things for great. For he has
come down, in order that he might prepare our ascent; and he has been tried of
generation, in order that we through him might be made friends with the
Unbegotten. He was weak on our behalf, in order that we might be raised in
power, and might say, as Paul: "I can do all things in Jesus Christ who
strengthens me." He took a corruptible body, in order that the corruptible
might put on incorruption; he put on the mortal, in order that the mortal
might put on immortality. And finally he became man and died, in order that
we, who die as men, might be deified, and might no longer have death reigning.
For death does not lord it over us, and the apostolic word also proclaims; for
this reason he is also crucified, in order that he might buy out the curse,
and we might inherit the blessing. For himself suffering, he was harmed not at
all, but rather he was granting gifts; and dying, he did not remain dead, but
rather he even made the dead alive; and being sacrificed he was not destroyed,
but by the blood of the sacrifice he simply redeemed all, and himself remained
impassible. And how this has come to be, if anyone should ask, let him hear. A
meeting of two things has come to be in this; and I speak of things not equal,
but not even having comparison according to all. For mortal was being
interwoven with immortal, and a corruptible body had come together with an
incorruptible Word. And the mortal did not harm the immortal; for it was
impassible; but rather it itself also remained impassible by the dominance of
death; for the things that prevail are always masters of those prevailed over.
Through the immortal Word then who was with him, death also was being made to
vanish paradoxically in him, as stubble vanishes from fire. And the Word,
being incorruptible, rather benefits the corruptible being with it; for the
darkness is shone through by the light, and the lesser is blessed by the
better. For this reason also now he is stripped by the executioners of those
coats of skin which we put on in Adam, in order that instead of these we might
be able to put on Christ. For this reason he also endures the garments being
divided, in order that we may have the undivided Word from the Father; "For I
and the Father," =28.208= he says, "are one." For this reason they also cast
lots upon the tunic, and suffering this he overlooks it, in order that Christ
may become our lot, as also the psalmist says: "The Lord is the portion of my
inheritance and of my cup." Whence he neither suffers elsewhere, nor is
crucified in another place, than in the place of the Skull, which the teachers
of the Hebrews say is the tomb of Adam. For there him after the


curse they affirm to have been buried. Which if it is so, I marvel at the
fitness of the place. For it was necessary that the Lord, wishing to renew the
first Adam, suffer in that place, in order that, loosing that one's sin, he
might take it away from the whole race; and since Adam heard: "Earth you are,
and to earth you will go," for this reason he is again placed there, in order
that, finding Adam there, he might loose the curse, and instead of: "Earth you
are, and to earth you will go," he might say at last: "Arise, you who sleep,
and stand up from the dead, and Christ will shine upon you;" and again:
"Arise, and come, follow me," in order that you may no longer be placed upon
earth, but may go up in the heavens. For it is necessary, when the Savior is
raised, that Adam also be raised with him, and all who have come to be from
Adam. And as, when Adam died, we also remained dead through him, so when the
Lord's body is raised, it is necessary at last that all be raised with him.
This is the mind of Paul; for he writes to the Corinthians saying: "For as in
Adam all die, so in Christ all will be made alive." And how they lead him upon
the cross, it is necessary to see the things written; for these too are wholly
not unprofitable, nor have they been written simply in the Gospels. After the
Lord was judged; for he was judged, and stood before the governor. And let no
one be ashamed of these things; for he endured this also on our behalf, in
order that the plot of the Jews might not be hidden, he having been ambushed
according to what is passed over in silence; but in order that even from
judgment the calumny of those who plotted might become manifest. For if the
Savior had not been judged, the envy of the Jews against him was being
supposed unclear; but when he was judged, it was no longer able thus to escape
anyone. And Pilate himself was bearing witness to the Savior of the unaccused
and clean thing; and he was bringing out the plot of the Jews. "For he knew,"
he says, "that they delivered him through envy." And Pilate would not have
known the envy of the Jews, if the Savior had not stood being judged. A great
thing and truly paradoxical the Savior has done; being silent he was working,
and not making a defense he was making the judge confess the =28.209=
conspiracy, and that he has been persuaded rather by the crowds than by the
just one. For if he were making a defense, one might have suspected cowardice
of death; but when he was silent, to marvel at the firmness and the courage.
And that Pilate thus made a defense was nothing other than to believe the one
being judged to be God. At any rate, being judged by Pilate, he was giving an
oracle to this one's wife; in order that by the silence she might be struck at
the courage; and by the oracle she might know that he is judging not a man,
but God. The one judging therefore feared the one being judged, and he himself
was rather being judged by fear of the one being judged than, judging, he
himself was striking the Lord with terror. Wherefore he also washed his hands
saying: "I am innocent from this blood;" even if later, moved by friendship of
the Jews, he ran with them as they plotted; so as even to deliver him to a
cross. The Savior then is delivered, and having been delivered, he did not
cower at death, but rather even stripped toward it, and was pursuing the
serpent as it fled. For the Lord was not as one dying, but as pursuing death
into this. Where indeed the wretched devil, having seen the wonder according
to Pilate, that the one judging rather than the one being judged cowered; and
seeing also his present stripping with confidence, was eager to try all
things, wishing to know what he was not able before, tempting him on the
mountain, to learn if this is the Son of God. Then having been put to shame,
he was watching the season of death; thinking that in that by which he had
enslaved all, he could tempt this one also in the death. For it has been
written in Luke, that the devil, having completed every temptation, departed
from him until a season. And this is that season, in which the enemy himself
also was knowing that he would be trampled by all, if he should fail of this
too; for this only


was left to him at last as the contest, when he had been stripped of all.
Wherefore he also does all things for the sake of learning if this is the
Savior; and he was doing this, in order that, if he should truly learn
concerning him, he might not even dare to come near, knowing his
unapproachable and divine power. But the Lord, seeing his knavery, that not by
honor toward him, but for the sake of the slavery of men he was hunting this,
in order that he might tyrannize against all, was hiding his own Godhead, and
was living as a man; in order that, not having come near as to God, he might
not go away impassible, but, having despised him as a man, he might come near,
and at last begin to be mocked by men. And as if someone, seeing his
antagonist cowering, and for this reason now fleeing away, should feign
weakness =28.212= toward the invitation of that one; then that one who is
fleeing, looking toward the feigned weakness, having taken heart should come
near, and the noble wrestler, having invited him by the weakness, will conquer
by his own power; so also the Lord, having invited by human weakness, by his
own power strengthened the man against the enemy. For this reason at the
season of death he is also in distress, and began to become very sorrowful;
and he was asking that that cup pass by, and he was crying: "The spirit is
willing, but the flesh is weak;" in order that our adversary, having come near
as to a man, might be tried of divine power. Led then by these things the
wretched one was mocked and came near to the Lord. Then, seeing his courage,
he cowered; and seeing also the weakness of the body, he was daring again; and
at last one could see the devil with all his army, and his principalities, and
his authorities fleeing; and the Lord with human weapons pursuing the diabolic
phalanx. For the interval was not much, and he spoiled the enemy of all; when
having been put to shame at last he was through himself moving all things
against the Lord; Jews, in order that they might conspire, and Pilate in order
that he might condemn; and the soldiers, in order that they might play against
him; but even this he did not know he was doing against himself; for
wickedness is blind, and evil is unforeseeing; sharpening the hands against
itself it did not know. For as if someone should take hold of a serpent with
the hand, and wishing also to throw it at another, he himself was wholly
bitten; or also holding fire with the hand, and wishing to wrong the enemy, he
does not know that he himself is wholly burned; so wickedness campaigns
against those who use it, and rather wrongs those who hold it than those
toward whom they may wish to send it. Thus Pharaoh, wishing to overtake, was
overtaken; and having begun to pursue, was pursued down; and having wished to
kill, was drowned. Thus Goliath, in the things in which he was lifted up, in
these was cut off, and wishing to spoil, was spoiled. In this manner the
wretched devil also has suffered; for wishing to tempt, he was tempted, and
moving the soldiers, or rather himself being moved in the executioners, he was
working all things against himself. For just now he was being spoiled, and the
victory was invisible to men; but the wretched one brought it into the open
even unwillingly; for as the Lord stripped, and the trophy that had come to be
invisibly against the devil came to be, in order that this too might become
manifest, immediately they clothed him, as John says, with purple, and as
Matthew and Mark, a scarlet cloak, and a crown from thorns upon his head, and
they were giving a reed upon the hand; and thus coming forward, they were
kneeling to him. A new and paradoxical wonder, and a mark of a truly =28.213=
great victory! For him whom they judged as a man, this one as he was dying
they were now worshipping as God; and him whom they were setting at nought as
cheap, this one they were confessing king; and from whom they took the
ordinary garments, on this one they were putting purple; and him whom not
knowing they were insulting, this one even unwillingly they were addressing as
prophet; and him whom they were playing at, striking, to this one they were
rendering the prizes of victory, the scarlet cloak, and the crown from thorns,
and the reed. For even if they were doing these things as insulting, yet they
did not know that even unwillingly they


were being spoiled by him. For since the earth had been polluted with murder
by the devil through sin, and more than with waters it had been dyed with
bloods, having from the beginning gaped and received the blood of righteous
Abel, and later at last men mixing bloods upon bloods, according to what has
been written: "Curse and falsehood, and theft, and adultery have been poured
out upon the earth, and they mix bloods upon bloods." And for this reason the
earth was also accursed, God saying: "And now accursed is the earth, which
gaped its mouth to receive the blood of Abel your brother." And earlier also
through the transgression of Adam the Master had cursed it saying: "Accursed
is the earth in your works. In griefs you will eat it in all the days of your
life." And wholly all things were full of bloods, and thorns were growing
everywhere through the curse. And having the handwriting of these things, the
enemy was lifted up against all, and was tyrannizing as having all as
subjects. For this reason the Lord, having spoiled him of all, being now led
away to the death, was wearing these things around him; in order that he might
show the achievement of the death, that it has come to be not simply, but on
behalf of our salvation. And the bloods he had in the scarlet cloak, and the
thorns in the crown, and the handwriting in the reed, in which also of old the
devil was writing us down; in order that together with the death he might at
last make these things also vanish, and might cleanse the creation from these;
and instead of the thorns he might grant the tree of life; and instead of the
bloods of sin he might cleanse the earth and all by his own blood; and instead
of the curse he might at last bless those on the earth, saying: "Blessed are
the meek, for they will inherit the earth." For this reason, having borne our
bloods, he emptied his own into it, from which also instead of thorns good
things and life have grown into it; looking toward which holy David says: "I
believe to see the good things of the Lord in a land of living." And otherwise
he was wearing the crown of thorns, in order that he might make our cares of
life vanish. For the wretched one even unwillingly threw our cares upon him
not knowing that, the Lord bearing these, =28.216= he will render all without
care against that one; so as to have the seed of the word free from the
thorns, and in the prayers the undistracted thing. For he himself was in pain
and was caring concerning us, in order that we might become without pain. And
for this reason again the Lord was also taking the reed from them, and the
devil himself was giving this to the Lord, not knowing that he sharpens the
sword against himself. For the reed is said to be destructive of serpents, and
by this rather than by another they are struck down. Since then the Lord
Sabaoth himself, as Isaiah says, was pursuing the fleeing dragon, the serpent,
the great devil; for this reason the Lord was taking from them his destructive
thing, in order that, the devil having been weakened by his own things, men
might be strengthened by the things of the Lord. And such a thing as David has
done upon Goliath, taking the sword of the enemy, and with it cutting the head
of the enemy. This now the Savior also has done, taking the reed from them, in
order that he might not only free us from the deceit of the serpent, but also
might destroy the serpent itself with the reed, and show it dead. Thus then
the wretched one having been played, and seeing himself fallen out of these
things in which he was lifted up against us; and seeing himself cast out from
the earth, and the earth being cleansed from the bloods and the thorns, and
seeing himself having lost the handwriting and the reed, in which he was
writing us down, he dares again undareable things, striking himself more even
in this. For the evil one, having heard the Lord saying: "The spirit is
willing, but the flesh is weak," having thought that the Word was weak
together with the body, and that the body was not rather strong together by
the power of the Word, again attempts unlawful things; and he sharpens the
executioners against the Savior, and himself was holding to the thorns, and
was rolling about them, suffering a thing


contrary. For having them he lost them, and he thought not having them to be
able to take them; but he has not taken them before, unless the Savior lifted
the curse lying against us in them. For when the Lord had borne this, he again
took the thorns and the cloak; no longer having power against us, but
convicting the wickedness of those. For now the devil himself is in the thorns
as in goads, kicking against us. And it is said to him concerning the scarlet
cloak: in the manner that a garment mixed in blood is not clean, so neither
will you be clean, because you destroyed my earth, and killed my people. For
this reason also now, moving the executioners against the Savior, he was
kicking as in goads with the thorns, and he was rather being struck than
striking. For those whom he was moving against the Savior, these were being
armed against him. For taking the reed from the Lord, and thinking they were
striking him, they were going blind, cutting the =28.217= devil who moved them
in the thorns. And as the nations, having gone up against Jerusalem, were
cutting one another in the valley of Josaphat, and as those pursuing Israel,
having come beside the banks, were interwoven with one another, and were
overtaking one another; so also now those pursuing the Lord were cutting one
another not knowing, harming him nothing, but rather wronging themselves. For
as if someone should wish to cut a rock with the hand, he does not cut the
rock, but wounds the hand; in this manner also those acting against the Lord,
daring as against an incorruptible, were being destroyed, and attempting as
against an immortal, they themselves were rather dying. And the worst thing at
least, that he was suffering through his own members; which especially has
come to be for the knave toward perfect shame. For wholly being struck by the
Lord, he was able to boast; but when by those whom he was being served, by
these he is cut, this overtopped every shame. But it is not the blow of those
that destroys the serpent; for men being also executioners were not able so
much; but the insult coming to him from the Jews was grieving him; and it was
not the blow coming from these that was wounding, but the Lord who was
striking against their blow, and the power that came to be from the hand of
the Savior with the reed. For in this too the Lord having feigned, and putting
forward the human things, was receiving the serpent in the thorns, and being
struck as a man, he was permitting the executioners to strike the serpent; in
order that he who once boasted to be God, and boasted as having tempted the
Lord, being struck by executioners, might carry away the perfect shame. And
these things were turning out for him even contrary to the mind of what he
wished. For working in those who were crying out against the Savior, he did
not know that he was bringing in the silence against himself; sharpening
Pilate, he did not know that he was preparing fearlessness instead of
cowardice; moving the Lord to be struck, he was being deceived as
longsuffering was being shown. And finally reveling against the Savior, he was
not seeing that he was arming virtue against wickedness; for the enemy
rejoices in every wickedness and evil; and if anything is bad, this is a
diabolic weapon; for either by cowardice of death, or through defending
oneself at any rate, he persuades to sin. Whence the Lord endured all things;
in order that in the cowardice he might teach the courage, and in the insult
the longsuffering, and in the wrath the meekness, and upon all things that
virtue is set against wickedness. And first he was teaching to turn also the
other cheek to the one striking upon the cheek; and: "Do not fear from those
who kill the body;" and: "From the one taking your things do not demand back;"
and: "Bless those who pursue." And in order that the commandment might not be
thought impossible beside some, for this =28.220= reason the Lord showed all
things through himself, and from the things he was suffering, he persuaded all
who are insulted not to insult in return, and those struck not to strike in
return, and those mocked not to be angry, and from the one taking our things
not to demand back; and wholly not to fear death; but even to despise it by
the hope of the


things to come. For thus rather the victory against the devil and every sin
may become easy, than when we defend ourselves. For defending ourselves and
wronging in return those who wrong in return, and insulting those who insult,
we both increase and nourish the wickedness against ourselves, not rather
wronging the enemies, but ourselves, by the condemnation through the
wickedness and the judgment that remains for those who have used this; but
when, as the Lord, we endure being wronged, and set longsuffering against
those who revile, and courage against those who threaten, and against death
the hope of immortality; wholly in these things we grieve the enemy; and that
one falls in such a panoply, in which also Paul arming us says: "Put on
therefore the panoply of God, in order that you may be able to stand toward
the methods of the devil." For thus also when the Savior had endured, there
came to be the: "I was seeing Satan falling from heaven as lightning."
Immediately then after the Lord had been brave in these things, and the enemy
had fallen, they set the cross upon him, providing a mark of the victory truly
even unwillingly against themselves. For as long as the enemy was standing,
the executioners had the wood; but when he has fallen and been broken down,
then the Lord took a trophy, and bore it for himself. For it was necessary
that the victor triumphing against the devil not yield to another, but bear
the trophy for himself. And he was taking together with the trophy also the
garments from them, going away to the death; in order that he might strip
these too together with the death. For this reason, having stripped again, he
was hastening those leading him away to lead him. And as he was being led
away, according to John, he was bearing the cross for himself, and according
to the other evangelists, Simon the man from Cyrene was carrying the cross.
And it is not contrary to John; for neither does John write contrary to the
others, but both have come to be upon this; for the Lord was both bearing the
cross for himself, and again Simon the man was carrying it. As a trophy then
against the devil he was bearing it first, and voluntarily he was also
carrying the cross for himself; for he was not coming upon the death being
forced. And again Simon the man was bearing it, in order that it might be
known to all, that not his own, but that of =28.221= men the Lord dies the
death. And these things having thus come to be, he comes hastening upon the
place, where it was necessary also for it to go up upon the cross, in order
that he might nail to the cross the handwriting against us, which he took from
the enemies, and having stripped the principalities and the authorities, he
might make a show of these in boldness, triumphing against them in the wood,
and showing the man drawn out from the lion, and at last sprinkling with his
own blood and cleansing the earth he might be well-pleased. And being about to
do this, he stripped the garments. For it was necessary that he, bringing the
man into paradise, strip off the coats which Adam took being cast out from
paradise. For when he sinned, and at last was about to die, he took coats of
skin, come to be from dead animals, being a symbol of the deadness that came
to him through the sin. But the Lord, who received all our things on our
behalf, put these on, in order that he might also strip them off, and instead
of these might clothe us with the life and the incorruption; and with these he
put on also an unsewn tunic woven from above through the whole; in order that
even from these the Jews might be able to believe who he is and from where is
the one who put these on; that not from earth, but from above the Word was
coming, and that the Word of the Father is not divisible, but undivided, and
that, having become man, he did not have the body sewn from man and woman, but
from a Virgin alone, woven by the art of the Spirit. Whence the executioners
divide the other garments, for they were divisible, and they are divided into
four; for on behalf of the sin of the four-part inhabited world according to
east


and west, and north, and south, he was wearing these; which John seeing him
clothed said: "See the lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world." The
tunic alone they were not able to split for fear of the sign made known
through it. For this too is not their work, but of the Savior who was hanging.
For even hanging, as a man, he was pursuing the principalities, and was
overtaking the devil, and was frightening the executioners not to split the
tunic; in order that, this remaining, the conviction against the Jews might
also remain, which came to be not long after, that the veil was split, but the
tunic of the Savior was not even divided by executioners, but remained whole.
For the Gospel remains through all whole, the shadows being divided. Except
that having stripped the garments he was hastening toward the death, then
again the devil, seeing the eagerness of the Savior, and himself fallen from
heaven, and cast out from the earth, did not have what he would do; for all
things were full of tumult and perplexity for him. And to remain he was not
able, and to flee again he was fearing. Except that having considered that
those who die must die upon the earth according to what has been written:
"Earth you are, and to earth you will go;" having feared to remain upon earth,
lest he be caught there, at last into =28.224= the air he flew; perhaps even
thus, the Lord's body having been placed upon earth, he might have the air
free. But the Lord, being a lover of men even in this, did not die into earth,
but went up into the air through the cross, pursuing the serpent there too; in
order that from there too he might drive away the ruler of the authority of
the air, and might throw down the spiritual things of wickedness, not leaving
even thus the earth untreated. For even hanging upon the cross, he was
cleansing the air by the stretching of the hands, and he was redeeming the
earth, washing it off with the blood and the water of the side. What then was
the enemy, having suffered these things, still to do? From heaven he had
fallen, from earth he was cast out, from the air he was pursued; and besides
these he was ashamed, having promised great things, and working nothing.
Beaten then from every side, and perplexed from every side, he knew to guard
even Hades; for this region was still left to this one. Wherefore also fearing
lest the Lord, having come down there, drive him out from there, and free all
the subjects, he was stirring and moving all things together, calling all to
help, and he sharpened the Jews again to reproach the Lord, and to insult and
to laugh wholly against him; and wholly if any panoply was left to him, this
he was letting go against the Lord; perhaps even thus he might hold him being
overly liable to sin. For the Jews were mocking the Lord saying: "If you are
Son of God, come down from the cross;" but the Savior, who is in truth Son of
God, wished to be known Son of God not from fleeing the death, but from
remaining it, and remaining to trample the death, and that he himself is the
life. For he was able also to come down from the cross, who even raised the
other dead; but the coming down was showing a flight of the death; and the
waiting around was making known nothing other than that the one waiting around
is life. For this reason also it was much about to the devil that the Lord be
reproached in these things, and he wished him to come down from the cross and
not to come to be among the dead, in order that even thus he might go away
impassible, and might lord it over the dead. For he was fearing lest according
to what has been written, having gone up into height of the cross, he might
take captivity captive, and take gifts among men. For he suspected lest this
is he concerning whom these things have been written. Whence again the knave
was moving the Pharisees, and at last excessively he was making them promise
and believe, if he should only come down from the cross. And he was moving all
things, and contrary to mind he was promising, only in order that he might not
become dead, and come down into Hades. He was making then again the Pharisees
say: "He saved others, himself he cannot save. If he is king of Israel, let
him come down now from the cross, and


we will believe him." And the dragon, the teacher of unbelief, did not wish
the Pharisees to believe; but he wished =28.225= the Lord to flee the death.
Nor did he wish to learn if he can save himself; but in order that salvation
might not come to be for the others. For if he wholly knew him to have saved
the others, it was not doubtful that he who has given salvation to the others
is himself Savior and life. Therefore for this reason they were reproaching
him, in order that salvation might not come to be for the others. For the
busybody knew that the Lord does not die, but that this one's death will
become salvation and life for all. For this reason he wished him to come down
from the cross, and he was making the Pharisees promise to believe; perhaps
even by the promise of the faith he might prevail in what he wished. But the
Lord, who is in truth Savior, who seeks not his own, but our things, wished to
be known Savior not from saving himself, but from redeeming the creation. For
neither will a physician be known a physician from himself being healthy,
unless he also show the art to those who are sick; and the sun will not, if he
contract the rays into himself, be known light, unless he also stretch these
out to the inhabited world, and shine the darkness in them. Thus also the Lord

giving back to the widow, is the same as those who plotted against the Lord
and calumniated him. And finally, whatever things one may work toward the
neighbor, these are reckoned toward the Lord, who came down on our behalf and
died on our behalf. For thus also the Lord says in the Gospels: "Inasmuch as
you did to one of these little ones, you did to me." And again, which it is
necessary to know and to keep: "He who fornicates destroys his own soul, and
himself also, as Jews, insults the temple and the body of the Lord." For "he
who corrupts the temple of God is corrupted by God," as Paul writes. And he
who is being reigned by the sin in nothing falls short of the denying Jews,
and saying they do not have the Lord as king, but Caesar. And let him who is
held by the pleasures, and rather delights in them, not be ignorant that he
too, as the Jews, asks for Barabbas, and kills the Lord. For thus the Jews,
having loved the cheap manner of Barabbas, at last drove away the virtue. And
let him who is not strong in the faith also know that he is such, as those who
disbelieve and say: "If he is Son of God, let him come down now from the
cross, and we will believe him." For those too disbelieving were uttering
these things. What then must one do upon these things? Nothing other than to
believe the Christ, and to live according to him, as also Paul says: "Become
imitators of me, as I also of Christ;" and to turn aside from the friendship
of Jews, and unbelief, and mockery, and myths, and endless genealogies, which
provide inquiries rather than an economy of God that is in faith; and to hold
to the cross and to live worthily of it, and to say the same voices as Paul:
"And to me may it not be to boast, if not in the cross of our Lord Jesus
Christ." For having thus lived and =28.249= believed into the Lord, we will
know his taking up into heavens, and the sitting at the right of the majesty,
and we will see the subjection of the angels, and his coming again with glory;
which angels signify beforehand, and holy ones hymn; and all seeing exult, and
rejoice in Christ Jesus, through whom to the Father is the glory and the power
into the ages of the ages. Amen.
