* Homily II on Holy Mary the Theotokos

Of Hesychius, presbyter of Jerusalem, on holy Mary the Theotokos
1 Every commemoration of the righteous is all-praiseworthy, and every
festival of those who love God is glorious; all were champions for piety,
all received the labors for the true confession, all, for the crowns of
the truth, raved amid the dangers; but the present day of the feast is
exceedingly glorious, for it contains a festival of a virgin, who so far
surpassed all that she even received of her own will the God Word himself,
contained by her without constraint. To her, before all, Gabriel of the
archangels first cried out: "Hail, full of grace, the Lord is with you;
for behold, you shall conceive in the womb and bear a son, and you shall
call his name" Emmanuel. The good news from Gabriel was at once a
beginning of joy toward her; for since the first virgin had been shut in
by the sorrows of the sentence upon the transgression, and the multitudes
of groanings sprang up from her, and every woman through her was in pain
and every birth through her was in bitterness, the second virgin, through
the greeting, thrust away all the distress of the female sex and blocked
every spring of grief at childbearing and scattered the cloud of
despondency over the childbearing of women and flashed the lights of joy
around those of her own kind, having heard from Gabriel: "Hail, full of
grace, the Lord is with you."
2 She did not receive the greeting in quiet, but having heard such a
voice, and having seen the archangel in the house contrary to intention,
and having had her thoughts shaken by the word concerning the birth, she
put forth, as was likely, some such sayings toward the archangel: "I see,"
she says, "all things paradoxical and strange today in you," and this:
"How did you dare to enter boldly upon a virgin not betrothed and to speak
the proclamation of your unbelievable words? For you say that I am to bear
a child without seed. You said I would conceive without marriage, that the
place of the womb would bear fruit without meeting and converse with a
man. Who has seen, who has heard from the beginning of generation, before
now, an unploughed field putting forth an ear of grain, or unplanted earth
sprouting a cluster, wine without a vine, or a river going forth without a
spring? A thing which no one of the first ages either heard with the ears,
or saw with the eyes as having happened. How shall I believe you, playing
a comedy toward me?"
3 What then did Gabriel say to her when she was unbelieving? "What I
learned I spoke, what I heard I say: 'The Holy Spirit shall come upon you,
and the power of the Most High shall overshadow you; therefore also the
holy one who is born shall be called Son of God,' as existing from whom,
as toward whom all creation is, as creator and fashioner of all things, as
father of the ages, as begetter of the world, as maker of the whole, as
older than the heavens, as fashioner of the angels, as molder of humanity,
as 'savior of those who are perishing.' I am able to announce nothing more
than these things; for I was not commanded, O virgin, to say the how, but
to minister to you the matters of the good news. Marvel then with me at
the mystery and receive the good news without hesitation." And she says:
"Behold the handmaid of the Lord; be it unto me according to your word."
Let us then also be disciples of the archangelic words of Gabriel
concerning the Master's birth, let us walk behind on his paths, as he did
let us exalt without curiosity the power of the birth.
4 Let us be taught piety from the Magi, or rather let us be schooled, who,
seeking the infant, a star leading their feet, did not say to those they
asked: "How is the conception divine, how is the womb seedless, how is the
birth without corruption, how after the birth is the mother among virgins,
how is the one who exists beforehand under years, how is the one before
the ages in times, how did the nature of the womb contain the
uncontainable, how did the bodiless become flesh without being changed,
how did the God Word, emptying himself in the virginal womb, all-praiseworthy,
become incarnate from her unspeakably toward the form of a servant, how is
the perfect one an infant, how does the one who feeds suckle, how is the
one who cares for all things held in arms, how is 'the father of the age
to come' a babe, how is he above and below, how is he in the heavens and
on earth, how is the charioteer of the cherubic chariots in
swaddling-clothes, how is 'he who is in the bosom of the Father' in a
manger, how is 'he who leads out those bound in manliness' in
swaddling-clothes, and all the other things which it is even dreadful to
speak?" But having reached the cave in silence with gifts, and after the
inquiry having offered worship fitting to a king and God, they were glad
to return toward her who bore him, having been shown as firstfruits of the
knowledge of God to the nations.
5 Them the nations emulated; but the Jews, being unbending, or rather
stiff-necked, did not even, when "the sun of righteousness" appeared in
their own places, hasten toward recognition to imitate those who were led
from afar by a star. But I, beloved, seeing your eagerness for the hearing
of the divine oracles and your affection toward those who interpret, being
overcome by your love of hearing, rise toward the desire of teaching,
delighted by the applause of your love; for you continually applaud those
who speak, as well-instructed disciples, you regulate the helmsman as
passengers, you hold the rudders of the tongue by prayers. Who will be
able worthily to announce your zeal? Who will set forth your hatred
against the Hellenes? Who will unfold your boldness against heretics? Who
will be able to announce your hostility toward the Jews? For the Jews,
along with the others, are truly abominable, hungering and thirsting
always for nothing but innocent murders, rejoicing in nothing except to
throw the prophets into pits, to drag the righteous along the ground and
stone them, to pierce with swords and divide with saws the heralds of
piety, to cut off parts of the hands and feet of the pious, and with
bloodied hands to hold the instruments of vengeance, to redden the palms
with the continual slaughter of the righteous; who have driven on to such
most impious daring against our Savior that they now cry out toward him
without shame: "We know that God has spoken to Moses, but this man we do
not know where he is from."
6 O the daring deeds. O hatred toward the Jews that has no strength to
flee. For as often as I wish to run away from the abomination toward them,
so often they meet me openly, either accused by the words of the prophets,
or insulted by the syllables of the law, or wrapped in the books of the
Gospels that are worshiped, as even now the divine pages have brought them
before our eyes, putting forward the shameless voices concerning the
Master of all and saying boldly the things you have heard: "God has spoken
to Moses, but this man we do not know where he is from." O the open
shamelessness. O the uncovered babbling of robbers. O the blind
contradiction of the words. For when Herod "inquired of them where the
Christ is born," they said more freely: "In Bethlehem of Judea," and they
displayed the citation of the prophecy and brought forward the hierophant
bearing witness: "A great prophet has been raised up" in Israel. And now
with covered face they have cried out: "God has spoken to Moses, but this
man we do not know where he is from." Truly "they have not known nor
understood, they walk in darkness." Truly this is "a foolish people and
not wise." Fittingly Christ cried out toward them: "We piped to you and
you did not dance; we mourned to you and you did not beat your breasts."
Who will array himself against a mind so thankless as theirs? Who will
refute their contentious hostility? So many wonders Christ poured around
them, those paralyzed in their sinews, cleansing the lepers, healing the
afflicted, curing the demon-possessed, opening the eyelids of the eyes,
raising their dead from tombs, changing water into wine, and granting
countless other things which it is laborious to speak. And faith among
them is nowhere, not even a little.
7 Be it that you are not persuaded by us, O Jew: read the hierophants,
unfold the pages that lie with you. Do not receive Peter, nor Paul. Have
done with the evangelists. Hear then even Moses. What did he write
beforehand in the law? "The Lord God shall raise up for you a prophet from
your brothers, like me; you shall hear him." See what Isaiah cries,
prophesying: "Behold, the virgin shall have in the womb and shall bear a
son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel." Hear Jeremiah crying out
great things: "This is our God, another shall not be reckoned beside him."
And going further he says: "After these things he was seen upon the earth
and consorted with men." See again what Zechariah signified: "Behold a
man, <Rising> is his name" <and Malachi: "The sun of righteousness shall
rise upon you who fear my name> and healing in his wings." And the woman
with the flow of blood, healed after many years, with others also
confirmed and sealed the word, having received the healing by the touch of
the hem. What then? Read Ezekiel and "the man of desires" as a lover of
the word. How the one? "This is the gate" of the Lord, he said, "and the
Lord shall enter through it and shall go out and the gate shall be shut."
And Daniel: "I was watching," he says, "and behold, upon the clouds of
heaven one like a son of man coming."
8 Stand beside Jacob and learn what he says among the blessings. "A ruler
shall not fail from Judah and a leader from his thighs, until he comes for
whom it is reserved, and he is the expectation of the nations." Touch the
lyre of the Spirit and see what David sang accurately: "Mother Zion, a man
shall say, and a man was born in her, and the Most High himself founded
her." For the mother was a virgin and remained so after the birth, keeping
unshaken the seals of virginity which nature had set. Again see what David
says: "Righteousness shall rise in his days," and next: "He shall have
dominion from sea to sea and from the rivers to the ends of the inhabited
world." Did any of our teachers foretell these things? Did any of the
Savior's disciples write such things in tablets or papers? Did not all who
proclaimed concerning him beforehand, the children of circumcision,
trumpet these things around? Did not the descendants of Abraham announce
these things? Do you not rejoice to believe in these? Be put to shame by
the demons crying out and saying: "What have we to do with you, Son of
God" the living? Revere the confession of angels and men and demons and of
all creation together concerning the Master Christ. And if you refuse even
these, receive the Father's testimony upon him thundering from heaven:
"This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased"; to him be the glory
unto the ages of ages. Amen.
