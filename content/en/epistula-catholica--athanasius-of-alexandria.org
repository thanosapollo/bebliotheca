Catholic Epistle of our father among the saints Athanasius. To the brethren in
each place who lay claim to faith and salvation, and to the orthodox bishops
throughout Egypt, and Syria, and Cilicia, and Phoenicia, and Arabia,
Athanasius and the fellow-bishops, greeting in the Lord. As many of you as
perceive the impiety that is rising up against piety, both through open empty
talk and through deceitful hypocrisies, and divisions, and unseemly tumults,
and blows, and wounds, and murders, you ought to withdraw, as from some storm
into a harbor, and to save yourselves, neither mixing yourselves with the
impiety of those who reject the one and true Godhead of the Holy Trinity, and
alienate the Son from the Father, and separate the Spirit from the Son, nor
mixing yourselves with their confusions and bloodshed. For thus we also,
wishing to save ourselves and those who are persuaded by us, have already for
a long time been exposing both the open impiety of Arius and the hypocrisy of
those who by fabricated words lead the simpler astray; and we separate
ourselves from all their disorder, mourning over the things they do, because
they cause the good name to be blasphemed, and wishing that both the law of
the faith and of decent conduct be somehow preserved among us and those with
us. To these things we also exhort you, both as many as have already taken the
same stand, and as many as have not yet risen to this zeal. Or have you not
even understood that through such things Christianity is being overthrown, and
that the devil, in a deceitful guise, is warring down the Church, and sinking
it as a ship? And if you also wish to write to us and to communicate
concerning such things, we will receive with joy your like-mindedness and
concurrence, brethren, and the communion of prayers, even if we are separated
in place; making known to you that, even if for a long time already the things
of this corruption have been prevailing, still even now the good of the
apostolic faith remains both among most of the peoples and among most of the
bishops; even if some hold the people by force and with other deceitful words
=28.84=; and a multitude of bishops, some through fear of those more prominent
in the cities, others through pretension of glory; and what they think they
hide, and what they do not think they pretend. So that, if they have not been
preoccupied by these passions, even if the truth is spoken in only a few
words, they recognize that neither can a creature of God be God and Son of
God, nor is the Holy Spirit numbered among creatures. And we have received an
appearing of God, and not a sojourning of a creature; nor have we believed in
a servant's presence, and we have received of a divine Spirit, through whom we
also are deified, and are made temples of God. For these things, even when
spoken thus briefly, are evident to every Christian, as they preserve the
exact truth proclaimed from the beginning, remaining in which we await the
eternal hopes. The symbol of our faith, then, is the consubstantial Trinity,
true God, become man from Mary. And let him who does not agree be anathema.
For these things the writing of the great synod at Nicaea also intends: that
the Son is consubstantial with the Father; and that the Spirit is glorified
together with the Father and the Son; that the Son of God, true God, was
incarnate, suffered, rose, has gone up into the heavens, will come as judge of
living and dead; to whom be the glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
