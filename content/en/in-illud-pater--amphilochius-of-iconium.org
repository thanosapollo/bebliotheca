* On that saying, Father
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Discourse of our holy father Amphilochius, bishop of Iconium, against the
heretics, on that saying: Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from
me.
Again, as a soldier under law, I take up the battle against the heretics, with
Stephen, the general of piety, marshaling the ranks. For as, while Moses
prayed, Joshua put the enemies to flight, so I too, with the martyr
cooperating, vanquish the enemies of the truth. I say these things not bold in
a superfluity of words, but trusting in the martyr's prayer; therefore with
confidence I take hold of the words. For I do not cower before their pomp of
words; I do not fear the devices of their wicked artifice of deceit. For I
have learned not to fear the fear of which there was no fear. For they say
that Christ feared and cowered before death; but we, for Christ's sake, choose
to die as many times as we live days in the flesh, that with head bare we may
triumph for the truth. Therefore, as one already holding the victorious
pebble, I advance against them. For Christ encourages me through the prophet,
saying: Son of man, be not afraid before their face, for they will act
wantonly against you all around. In the midst of scorpions you dwell. For I do
not fear scorpions that sting. For I do not walk with bare foot; I have the
sandals of the gospel of peace, that even if I am bitten I may not be harmed.
And having shod the feet, he says, in the readiness of the gospel of peace.
Come then, with the sandals of peace let us trample the many-formed head of
the heretics, that by that through which we pass over them without fear we may
teach them how greatly they err who attach fear and cowardice to the nature of
the Only-begotten. For those who understand and speak all things uncritically
and without reasoning, and do not understand Father, if it be possible, let
this cup pass from me, vote cowardice against Christ and say that he is needy
and inferior, because he prays to the Father and seeks release from the
necessity laid upon him. Since, then, they slander the word of God to the
outrage of God, and run about with the letter to the rejection of the Spirit,
mocking and dragging down the economy of Christ, and with cleverness of phrase
and elegance of expressions cover their venomous bites, despoiling the simpler
of salvation, come, as unskilled in words, let us call upon the very Word of
God, that he himself, becoming interpreter of his own words, may brand their
folly and establish our understanding. Declare to us the meaning of what was
said, Master; unfold the leaf of the letter and show the fruit of the Spirit,
that we may not die by attending to the letter, but running to the Spirit may
enjoy life. For the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life. Teach us as
little children, but chasten the heretics as fools; for you are a chastener of
fools and a teacher of children. To us, as to children, bare the breast of
grace and offer the nipple of the Spirit. Let the pure milk of the evangelical
teaching be poured upon us. But make the folly of the heretics manifest. For
if they were not fools, they would not have woven a dogma of blasphemy for the
abolition of your divinity. For the fool has said in his heart, he says, There
is no God. Strip bare their pretense of impiety, that the drama of their
hypocrisy may appear. Show that it is for this reason that they carry about
your name,


that they may cover the blasphemy of their own choice. Tell us then, Master,
for what reason, having come to the passion, you pray to be spared the
suffering; and why do you fear the threat of the Jews, you who command others
not to fear those who kill the body? Or again, if you fear death, why do you
not pray to be spared death? For if it lies with you to suffer or not to
suffer, you say superfluously: If it be possible, let it pass. For it is in
your power either to accept or to decline the passion. But if it does not lie
with you, you cower with reason, as one dragged to the passion unwilling and
against your judgment. But if you undergo death for us unwillingly, how do you
say: I have power to lay down my soul, and I have power to take it again? And
how, again, in another passage, mocking the threat of the Jews, did you say:
Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up? The one, then, who
many times and in many ways prefigured death before death, and sketched the
trophy of the cross before the cross; who said that the Son of man must be
betrayed and crucified and buried and rise on the third day; why, having now
come under the cross, does he pray to be spared death? For if the Son of man
must be betrayed, how do you say: If it be possible, let this cup pass from
me? Or again, if you wished not to suffer, why did you put on a passible body?
But if, having worn a passible body, you are coming to the passion, why are
you grieved and troubled? And how, when you were sketching out death, when you
were setting up the passion, when you were prefiguring the cross, were you
indignant with Peter when he said: Mercy on you, Lord; this shall not be to
you; and were you so indignant as to say: Get behind me, Satan; you are a
scandal to me, for you do not mind the things of God, but the things of men?
If, then, to be spared death is a human frame of mind, why do you now seek
what you long ago forbade? And how do you now pray to decline death, you who
long ago rebuked the disciple for shrinking from the assault of death? Are you
not the one proclaimed beforehand by the law and the prophets? Was it not of
you that Hosea the prophet spoke: Thus says the Lord: From the hand of Hades I
will ransom them, that they may say: Where is your victory, O death? Where is
your sting, O Hades? If, then, you are the one ransomed from Hades, why do you
cower before death? But if you cower before death, through whom we hope to
conquer death, vain is our hope, profitless our expectation. Or again, if you
fear death, in what shall we henceforth have the hopes of life? And how do you
say: I am the life and the resurrection? For life does not fear death, nor
does resurrection cower before passion. But you are the life and the
resurrection. Why then do you frighten my soul? Why do you unstring the sinews
of my mind, uttering words of cowardice? Was the thing done for Lazarus a
phantom? Was the thing done for the daughter of Jairus a dream? Was the thing
done for the son of the widow a shadow? But the things that came to pass were
truth. Why then do you who despoiled death fear death? And why do you who with
authority raised the long-dead pray and cower lest you fall in with death? And
why, to speak simply, do you fear to fall in with it? For if you fall in with
it, you will set free. For he who raises others raises his own body much more.
Either declare to me the meaning, Master, or cease from the saying and do not
split my soul, or make the reasoning yield. For if you are the one who knows
all things before their coming to be, how do you now not know whether it is
possible or impossible for the cup to pass? For if you do not know, Paul lies
in saying: There is no creature invisible before him, but all things are naked
and open to his eyes. But if you know, why do you say: If it be possible?
Inquiry belongs to one who does not know, and denial to one who does not know.
Declare to us, then, Master, the meaning of what was said. For now Eunomius
rejoices, now Arius makes merry, snatching the saying as material for
blasphemy. But they rejoice in vain; for it is not for the impious to rejoice,
says the Lord. It is not, as they say, that I fear death; nor, as they
suppose, that I decline the passion.


For if I did not wish to bestow life on the race of men through death, I,
being the impassible Word, would not have entered in beneath a passible flesh.
But since I saw the race of men held by deceit and not by necessity, mastered
by the sorcery of demons and not by force, I entered into your body, that
making the opposites from the opposites I might free man from every deceit.
For because the tyrant thought greatly of himself, in that, finding sin and
introducing death through sin, he made Adam, the citizen of paradise, the
ordained master of the world, a slave and a robber and a tiller of the ground,
therefore I encamped on the earth as king, wearing the flesh as a royal purple
robe, that, cutting off the tyrant's insolence, I might undo deceit with
deceit, turn righteousness in war against sin, and bring life into contact
with death. It is not, then, fearing death that I cower; for the thing is not
unwilling, but the achievement is of free choice. For I am the good shepherd,
who of free will gives up his own soul for the sheep. Therefore, as, being in
the form of God, I emptied myself, taking the form of a servant with no one
compelling me, so too I endure death willingly, not held by force, but coming
of free choice to the passion. But why did you suffer, Master? For the
salvation of men, he says. And I give the manner. For since by the sentence of
death the race of men was cast down because of Adam, who was formed from
virgin earth, and I was he who cast out the sentence of death, but it was
impossible for man to be raised up from the punishment unless I myself
recalled my own sentence, therefore, taking from a virgin womb the form of a
man in the likeness of Adam, I endure death, that as God I may undo the
sentence, and as man accept death on behalf of men. This I do that I may free
man not by authority, but by fellow-feeling. For if, when man had sinned, God
were the one who set it right, what was set right would not be great. But now
for this reason I have become man, and for him who had undone the law I
fulfilled the law, that the race of men might glory in the kinship of the one
who set it right. Therefore now too I receive the sentence of death in the
person of Adam, that he through me may receive the grace of the resurrection.
Adam, stumbling, brought in death along with him; I, setting it right, brought
in the resurrection in exchange. And since death reigned from Adam to Moses;
and it reigned because it had the body, the coworker of sin, in its hands,
therefore I took up the same body, which seemed to be the instrument of sin,
<that> undoing sin I might show man free from sin. But otherwise: since men
lived a bad life, entangled with sin, so that their life was worse than death,
therefore, preferring the good death to the bad life, I endure death, that
those who shared in the good death may enjoy the true life. And they enjoy it
through baptism; for as many as are baptized are baptized into my death, that
as I was raised from the dead, so they too may walk in newness of life. And
let no one, doubting, say that darkness does not make light, nor does black
produce whiteness; how then did death beget life? That the thing is beyond
nature is clear. But seek from me no order of nature. For I am the master of
nature, at one time letting nature move according to nature, at another time
driving it beyond nature. For whenever I wish to work something extraordinary,
then nature too, renouncing its own order, does those things to which my nod
directs it. And this is clear from the visible ordering; let me give some
instance: the sun and the moon are fire by nature, but water is the
crystallized firmament, which I called heaven; if then you seek the course of
nature, how is the fire by


the water kept in check, or how does the crystallized firmament not melt and
flow away under the heat of the fire? And that I may not keep your soul busy
with the visible ordering, leading you by the hand to the economy itself I
persuade you that one ought not to seek the course of nature in the
paradoxical wonder-workings. Reason out, then, that, being God, you put on a
human body. And this is first of all beyond nature. For how, tell me, could
the human body, threefold in its division, contain him who cannot be
contained, the measureless one, the one who is everywhere, who encircles the
whole inhabited world, who measures heaven with a span and the earth with a
handbreadth and the sea with a cup? That it did contain him is clear, since
all the fullness of the godhead dwelt bodily in the flesh. But the how, I
know, he says. For I know what I dispense, but you are not able to hear it;
for the knowledge of the secret things so surpasses mortal hearing that even
if at some time I wish, out of the abundance of mercies, to interpret
something, I interpret it not as the nature has it, but as you are able to
learn. For this reason also, calming the pride of those who seem to be wise, I
said: If anyone seems to know anything, he does not yet know anything as he
ought to know. That, being the Word, I have become flesh, you have heard; but
the how, you do not know. For how was I in the flesh, as body in body or as
soul in body? I co-suffered, then, with the passible body, but I did not
suffer. For the nature of God does not fall into passion. Therefore, as I
became flesh without falling from being God, so too I endured passion without
falling into passion. I was born by economy; I died for the salvation of men;
or rather, I died for this reason, because I was born. Seek from me, then,
neither an order of birth nor a manner of death. For what nature does not do,
power accomplishes. Being God, then, I entered into a human body. This is
first of all beyond nature; but nature did not make it impossible, since it
lay in my authority, of him who made nature. But I allowed the body itself
also to fall in with the common death, that the life ruling over death might
show even in death itself the energy of life. That the living thing lives is
nothing marvelous; but to make the dead live, this is great and paradoxical.
Therefore I allowed it also to be dissolved, and after it was dissolved I
raised it, that I might show that, even when the body was subjected to death,
I remained so impassible that I raised not only my own body but also the
bodies of the righteous who had fallen asleep before. Willingly, then, out of
love for man I endure death; and willingly again, by a certain economy, I
cower before death. For I cower before death that I may deceive death. As in
the wilderness, if I had not hungered, the devil would not have come to me.
But I hungered for this reason, that, not supposing me to be another Adam, he
might bring the snare. So too here for this reason I utter the word of
cowardice as a bait, that I may hook death for myself. For since the devil saw
me working many signs, making the paralytic's sinews strong, giving articulate
speech to tongues that could not speak, opening the ears of the deaf, giving
eyes to the blind, changing the surge of the sea into calm, drying up the
springs of the woman with the flux by the touch of the hem, raising Lazarus by
a word, raising the daughter of Jairus, calling back the widow's son from the
dead, when he had beheld all these things and been persuaded from the deeds
themselves that I am the Son of God who appeared in the form of a man
according to the foretelling of the prophets, he fears and dreads to nail me
to the cross and give me over to death, lest, entering in in the guise of a
dead man, I set the dead free. What then do I do, that I may turn even the
devil's cowardice into recklessness and make him run upon death? I flee that
he may pursue; I cower that he may take courage; I use humble words that,
supposing me to be one of the many, he may run up and swallow me. For if he
does not swallow me, he has no way to vomit up those he has swallowed.
Therefore I utter words of cowardice, that


deceiving the deceiver I may undo the deceit. He deceived Adam with crafty
words; I deceive the crafty one with words of cowardice. But he uttered
deceitful words to destroy man; I utter words of cowardice to set the world
free. I am a fisherman, a fisher of fishermen. For as the fisherman, putting
the worm on the hook, extends the rod, and now gives slack to the hook, now
draws and pulls it in, that, showing the worm as if fleeing, he may get the
fish to dart upon it, so I too, putting my body, like a worm, on the hook of
my godhead (for I am a worm and no man), now draw death as one cowering, now
give slack to the hook as one taking courage, that the devil, darting like a
fish at the worm, that is, coming upon the flesh, may unknowingly fall in with
the hook of the godhead and, falling in, be drawn, that there may be fulfilled
upon him the word spoken by Job: And you will draw the dragon with a hook. It
was not, then, fearing death that I said: My soul is exceeding sorrowful. Nor,
cowering at suffering, did I say: Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass
from me. For I do all things that death may not escape me. I cower as a man,
that, swallowing me as a man, he may find me at work as God. I have used
humble words, that, swallowing the leaven of my body, he may find the coal of
my godhead terribly burning him. I cower, that he may chew my body as a grain
of mustard; he will find the pungency of the godhead severely tormenting him.
I cower, that he may swallow me as a man. For if he swallows me, he will find
the solid rock of the godhead crushing his teeth, that there may be fulfilled
upon him what is written: God shall crush his teeth in his mouth. For this
reason, then, I hide the authority of the godhead and put forward the
cowardice of the flesh, that he may not be caught by the power of the godhead,
but may be brought down by the weakness of the body. For that he may not think
greatly of himself as being defeated by God, therefore I deceive him through
the flesh, that by the very things he plotted he may be plotted against. I
conquer him, then, through the weakness of the body, that he may not boast as
one convicted by divine power. For to be defeated by the godhead would have
brought him glory rather than outrage. Therefore now I conquer him through
cowardly and humble and weak flesh, not tempting but tempted, not striking but
struck, not killing but dying, not crucifying but crucified, not smiting but
smitten, that the victory may not be inscribed to me, but that through the
form of a man the prizes may be awarded to the race of men. Therefore I seem
to cower, that the devil may fall upon the bait like a fish, and death be
caught on the birdlime like a sparrow. For since he deceived wickedly,
therefore he is deceived righteously. Since through bodily pleasure he
beguiled the race of men, therefore through a body he is driven out from the
race. What he did he suffers; what he did to the many he endures from one. But
what that one did was destructive to those who suffered it, while what I do to
him will be saving for the world. For I deceive through the body the deceiver,
that, being convicted, he may no longer be able to lead astray those whom he
himself led astray through the body; he brings death, and I through it provide
the resurrection. Unwillingly he benefits the race; for by the very things by
which he brings death forward, he brings forward the occasion of the
resurrection. The plotter against all has suddenly become a benefactor of the
world; and he benefited the world by being deceived, finding a deception
profitable to the world and a trophy against his own tyranny. As one who
throws a stone against a rock will not split the rock but will shatter the
stone, so too the devil, hurling death like a stone against my body, did not
corrupt my body (for you will not give your Holy One to see corruption), but
the


goads of his own he crushed, not being able to endure the solidity of the
resistance. Let no one, then, because of ignorance vote cowardice against me,
nor let him cast my humble words upon the godhead. I am a shepherd according
to the word of the godhead: Attend, he says, you who shepherd Israel, you who
lead Joseph like a sheep; and a sheep according to the economy of the flesh;
for as a sheep I was led to the slaughter, and as a lamb voiceless before the
one shearing it. Since, then, the devil lies in wait for me, wanting to snatch
my flesh as a sheep, but fearing the godhead as a shepherd and so withdrawing
and fleeing, therefore I hide my godhead as a shepherd and give up my flesh as
a sheep, that he may take courage and come near, and coming near may snatch,
and snatching my flesh as a sheep may be slain by the two-mouthed sword of the
Spirit. Cease, then, heretic, voting cowardice and ignorance against me. For I
cower in order to show that the assumption of flesh was no phantasm. I say: My
soul is troubled, that you may learn that I did not take a soulless body, as
the delusion of Apollinarius wishes. Do not then throw the passions of the
flesh to the impassible Word; for I am God and man, heretic. God, as the
wonders warrant; man, as the sufferings bear witness. Since, then, I am God
and man, tell me who is the one who suffered. If God suffered, you have spoken
the blasphemy; but if the flesh suffered, why do you attach the passion to
that to which you do not attach the cowardice? For while another suffers,
another does not cower; and while a man is crucified, God is not troubled. And
that the flesh suffered while the Word remained impassible, Isaiah is witness,
who, seeing me in the Spirit stained with blood, asked: Why are your garments
red? Your garments, he says, are red, and not you. For the body is wounded,
but the Word of the godhead is not harmed. And Jacob the patriarch is also
witness, who says of me: He will wash his robe in wine and his mantle in the
blood of the grape. What robe? The body. What blood? That which flowed from
the side. But if you do not cast out my testimony, I bear witness to myself;
for my testimony is true, even if you are unwilling; the testimony I spoke to
the Jews: Why do you seek to kill me, a man who has told you the truth? He
said man, heretic, but not therefore not God. This man, then, Jesus, the man
slain by the Jews, is himself the one who cowers. And do not trouble the Word
with your ignorance. For this reason I cower, that death may be entangled with
me as with a man and be defeated as if by God. But also, even cowering, I do
not altogether decline the passion. For if I were declining it, I would not
have said: Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass. For I knew that
nothing is impossible with the Father. But if I asked death to be set aside
and he did not set it aside, two absurdities arise. For if he did not set it
aside because unable, or handed me over unsparingly, you are found, heretic,
attaching cowardice to me and inability to the Father. But in saying that I
cower you have pardon, because of the flesh; but in imputing inability to the
Father you stumble unpardonably, saying that the power of God is weaker than
death. Moreover, you also declare me stronger than the Father. And I give the
manner. For if I, living in the flesh, raised Lazarus, the daughter of Jairus,
the son of the widow, and, dying, again in the flesh despoiled above five
hundred dead from their tombs, while the Father, my Father, who took trial
neither of flesh nor of death, could not rescue from dangers the one me whom
he loved, his son, as he himself says: This is my beloved Son; consider what
you are bringing on yourself, heretic. Because in not granting me equality in
your judgment you unwillingly grant me the more, and him whom you think you
are dragging down by weakness you declare stronger than the Father; because I,
even <not> entreated, rescued the souls held fast in Hades (for he says: Lord,
you brought up my soul from Hades), while he himself, even when entreated, did
not free my troubled soul from the evils hanging over it. But far be it! For
neither did the


Father, whether unsparing or unable, fail to free me from the passion; nor
did I, declining the passion, ask for the cup to pass. For if the passion were
unwilling, I was truly cowering; but if the death were of free choice, I
seemed to cower, that, outwitting the devil, I might set man free. Therefore
the devil, not seeing this, drew me up as about to swallow me, not knowing
that in swallowing me he had those he had swallowed to give up. For as soon as
I stood by Hades, I despoiled its tombs and stripped bare its repositories.
And I stripped them not fighting openly, but providing the resurrection
invisibly. For I loosed no one and all were being loosed; I said nothing and
liberty was being proclaimed; I called no one and all came running. For as
soon as I stood there as king, the tyrant was held in contempt; I shone as
light and the darkness was broken up. It was a sight to see: every prisoner
beholding liberty, every captive dancing before the resurrection; the very
dead wondering at me for the victory, dragging death for his defeat, and
saying: Where is your sting, O death? Where is your victory, O Hades? So much
for these things. But for what has been said let us send up glory to Christ
the king of all, for to him belongs glory, honor, and worship, with the
spotless Father and the all-holy Spirit, unto the ages of ages. Amen.
