1 Of Didymus. For just as the people of the world in the writings of their
worldly contracts wish to be styled from the dignities about them, so also the
apostles at the beginnings of their writings think it worthy to be styled
slaves of God and of Christ. (Cr 2, 8–12)
2 Exhorting them therefore to the perfection that is according to courage and
endurance, he accordingly names them brothers, but not children nor sons. (Cr
2, 28–29 Ma 183) Of Chrysostom. Having sufficiently exhorted them to undergo
the trials with joy, in order that approved work and perfect endurance may
come to be; and these are perfected when they are done for themselves, and not
being done for something else; by another exhortation he attempts to persuade
them to accomplish the matters at hand through a promise, saying that the one
who endures trial is blessed; for he who thus athletically contends the
contest will be 3 come an approved man, exercised through all things; and to
the one who has thus appeared from the grim things will be given a crown of
life prepared by God for those who love him. (Cr 4, 19-27 Ma 184) thus the one
who by enduring trial despises painful things and even death itself will
receive a crown of eternal life, in return for the temporal life he despised;
you will inquire whether the matter of the crown of life which God prepared
for those who love him is those things which God prepared for those who 4 love
him, which through greatness of divinity are not subject to sight and hearing
nor have they ascended even to a bare conception of a human being. (Cr 4,
27-32 Ma 184)
6 The saying of the Savior being true which says: "ask and it shall be given
you", which he also confirms by saying: "everyone who asks receives", someone
might inquire how some who pray are not heard. To which it must be said: the
one who comes to asking by the consistent way, having omitted nothing of the
things that contribute toward obtaining what is sought, will altogether
receive what he asked to be given him; but if someone, having gone outside the
aim of the transmitted asking, should seem to ask, not asking in the way one
ought, he does not even ask at all; therefore when he does not receive, the
saying "everyone who asks receives" is not made false. For when a teacher
says: everyone who ap 7 proaches me for the sake of lessons will have the
knowledge of them, we take "to approach the teacher" grammatically, that is
with attending closely to the things from the teacher together with practicing
and studying them; but of the one who does not do so, it must be said to him:
you did not approach him as you were exhorted. (Cr 25, 21-26, 1) Making more
manifest, the one writing the letter, who they are who seeming to ask do not
receive, has anticipated those who ask contentiously and badly for the sake of
vain pleasures. But someone will say: and yet some who ask for divine
knowledge and the taking up of virtues do not receive; but it must be said
also to them, that they did not think it worthy to receive the goods
themselves, but for the sake of being praised through them; and it belongs to
the pleasure-loving also to rejoice in praises; hence these too are not given,
since they wish to spend on pleasures the things concerning which they make
request. (Cr 26, 1-9)
8 Of Origen. Since vice procures friendship toward the world, but virtue that
toward God, virtue and vice cannot coexist. (Cr 26, 16-18) Proverbs. The one
who through sinning loves the world is shown to be an enemy of God; likewise
also the one who through piety confirms the friendship toward God is
immediately found an enemy of the world; hence it is impossible, just as to
serve God and mammon, so to befriend God and the world. At any rate then the
Lord, having come to make those who obey him friends of God, cast a sword and
a division upon the earth; for the word of his teaching divides and parts from
the earthly and material, preparing through this to have enmity toward the
world and union toward God, which is the

friendship toward him; wherefore he also gives peace not as the world offers;
for that one, through the peace he gives, makes people passionate toward
material things; but the Lord, providing peace, prepares friends of God; world
then in these things one must hear as the sympathy toward the things here. (Cr
26, 19-32 Ma 193)
9 Of Origen from the interpretation on "according to foreknowledge of God".
For God, having foreseen the faith and 10 practice of human beings, these
having been set right for them from the evangelical teaching, also chose them
in Christ before the foundation of the world, then having predestined them to
be sons by participation in the spirit of adoption; for foreknowledge is to be
considered nothing other than a contemplation of things that will be, which is
no longer foreknowledge but knowledge when the things foreseen have come into
existence; even if then they were elect according to foreknowledge, those to
whom Peter writes, yet the address does not come to them as now existing; the
understanding lying here therefore also casts out the myth concerning the
natures. (Cr 42, 28-43, 6 Ma 196) If the inheritance has been kept in the
heavens for the faithful, some grow cold thinking they will re 11 ceive it in
the Jerusalem below, thinking they will receive luxury as the prizes of the
kingdom for whole thousand years; whom one must also ask, what they have to
say concerning bodily luxury as incorruptible and unfading, especially since
they also circumscribe it when the millennial year has ceased; to these one
must join the texts that show the inheritance is in the kingdom of heavens and
that it does not fall under the sense of mortals, because it is intelligible.
(Cr 43, 13-21)
12 Since some set the promise and salvation of the saints before the sojourn
of the Savior as lesser, thinking those after it have had something more by
having seen the Lord perceptibly and the signs done by him, it must be shown
that their supposition is mistaken. (Cr 45, 16-20)
=13.10= For how is Abraham lesser than any of those after the sojourn, who saw
the day of the Lord, all who are being perfected according to the Lord
departing into his bosom? And how are Moses and Elijah lesser, who appeared in
glory together with the Lord transfigured, because they did not see the Lord
perceptibly? For one must not prefer the one who has seen the sojourn
according to flesh over those who have had the intelligible sojourn. (Cr 45,
20-26)
14 Many at any rate of the heterodox go along with this mistaken opinion,
having a desire in every way to slander the old covenant; and this too will be
said, that even if they did not see or hear perceptibly what the Lord said,
yet they had a divine love of them; "for many prophets and righteous desired
to see". (Cr 45, 2631) If someone should take the salvation sought by the
prophets as being according to the end, which happens to be the last and final
object of desire (for for its sake all the other things come to be, but it for
the sake of nothing), it is not unfitting to take the present matter
concerning the holy angels and simply all the bles 15 sed rational beings, all
having a desire to stoop to look into the things according to the end. (Cr 45,
32-46, 3 Ma 196f)
17 If the Father judges no one, but has given all the judgment to the Son,
what must one think concerning the present text? For if it is the Father who
is signified, how is this one a judge judging according to each one's work?
But if the Son judges, having received all the judgment, how is he Father? To
which it must be said: if Father and Son are one divinity, when the Son judges
the Father is the one who judges; but also the Son can be Father of the
generated, having begotten them by participation in holiness; at any rate he
often called his own disciples little children and said to others, to one:
"child, your sins are forgiven you", and to another: "daughter, your faith has
saved you". And these things convict those who cut the divinity apart. (Cr 46,
28-47, 7 Ma 197)

18 He calls the living word of God that remains unto the age the evangelical
word. (Ma 197) The gospel saying: "heaven and earth will pass away, but my
words will not pass away", says the Lord Jesus Christ. (Cr 48, 23-25 Ma 197)
19 Just as the Lord, being true light, has come into the world for judgment,
in order that he might give eyes to those who were blind before his presence,
20 and might blind those who see badly, so also being a chosen precious stone
he provides honor to those who are built upon him through faith, having been
shown a firm foundation of them, but of those who disobey through unbelief he
is not precious, but is considered a stone of stumbling and a rock of offense,
they having approached the builders who set him at nought; and these are
scribes and Pharisees. (Cr 52, 12-19) And the saying "unto which they were
also appointed" he says not as if God had forced them to this very thing, but
they suffered this from their own wicked mind and base choice. (Cr 53, 9 Ma
198)
21 According to the old arrangement of the law the royal race was one thing
and the priestly race was another; after that arrangement the gospel
succeeded, teaching one and the same priest and king; for Christ is both;
these things having been thus built, it is necessary that those sown by him,
he being at once king and priest, be a chosen race being at once a royal
priesthood; for since the one who begot them has both authorities, they too of
necessity are royal as from a king and a priesthood as from a priest;
wherefore they are also a holy nation, having been called by the one who
calls, who is holy, unto "you shall be holy". The same, toward being the holy
nation, are also a people for a possession, having come to be outside all
destruction; for possession is of those taken in, but not of those ostracized.
For since those called from different nations and 22 laws have cast off all
variety in taking up one mind, attending to one faith and teaching, through
which soul and heart become one, they are one holy nation. (Cr 53, 16-32 Ma
199)
23 Those who through piety happen to be worthy of being obeyed are reasonably
addressed as beloved, of those desired through some human thing being loved,
but not being beloved. Since then I am so disposed toward you, to whom I write
the letter, I exhort and urge you to abstain from the pleasures that grow from
flesh; and these campaign against the soul, having much enmity toward it,
since the substance of soul is one and that of flesh another. (Cr 54, 20-26)
24 He calls human creation the authorities appointed by the kings; to these
then one must be subject on account of the Lord; and if we are subject on
account of the Lord who said: 25 "render the things of Caesar to Caesar", when
they command something outside the will of the Lord, let us not obey. (Cr 55,
1-5) 27 He does not command the household servants to have the passionate fear
toward the masters (for this is irrational and exists even in the irrational
animals), but the one that comes about with knowledge and right reason; whose
proper name is reverence. For the one who through Christ and his teaching
fears a master is subject to him with reverence, whereas the one who through
vice and other tortures fears a master has the passionate fear; he has used
this signification also after other things toward the women, having said:
"observing", that is the husbands, "your chaste conduct in fear"; for this is
customary in many places of the divine scripture. (Cr 56, 27-57, 5)
28 He exhorts the women to imitate the holy women and especially Sarah,
persuading them to be subject to the husbands as she was to Abraham, of whom
he also says they have become children through doing good; for just as someone
doing the works of Abraham and having his faith becomes his child, so the
faithful women who do good

have Sarah as mother. And toward allegory consider how someone becomes a child
of Abraham and of Sarah; according to the apostle 29 Abraham had two sons, one
from the maidservant and one from the free woman; then bringing them to the
two covenants he says after other things that she has been born according to
spirit, having called the Jerusalem above free; this agrees: "look unto
Abraham your father and unto Sarah who was in travail with you"; for it is not
possible for these things to be referred to those; for how does she still
travail those at the time of the prophet, who many generations before received
the death owed to nature? Hence these things must be taken of the mother of
those born according to spirit, the Jerusalem above; which women he exhorted
through doing good to have Sarah as mother. (Cr 59, 27-60, 11) "But sanctify
the Lord God in your hearts." Just as to magnify God does not indicate
bringing an increase of greatness 30 to him, nor yet does to glorify present
bringing fame from outside, so neither does to sanctify the Lord signify
adding holiness to him; which we are also commanded to do this in our heart;
for if we should have an accurate apprehension of his holiness, we do not
sanctify him simply, but in our heart, there being added from this doing to
our understanding the holiness concerning God; likewise also the one who
magnifies him receives into himself a share of his greatness; hence it has
been said: "my soul magnifies the Lord"; and: "magnify the Lord with me";
likewise the one who knows the thing of glory into him glorifies himself; and
these things do not seem paradoxical to the one who accurately understands
that God does not receive, but furnishes the goods. (Cr 64, 17-30)
31 Of Didymus. For we must be so prepared toward knowledge of the faith, that
at every time when we are asked the account concerning it we answer readily,
and answering display meekness and fear of God; for the one who speaks the
account concerning God ought to speak thus as with God present. (Cr 65, 10-14)
for the deed is then praiseworthy, when it is accomplished with reasoning; 32
for to seem to know something without understanding and examination brings
blame; "for knowledge of the unintelligent: most inextricable words". (Cr 65,
33-66, 3)
33 When the time has come for the judgment of God to take beginning, it begins
to pass from the greater to the lesser, that is from the faithful and those
who complete the church of God; wherefore he also adds: if first from us who
are the house of God the examination of the things lived comes to be, what
must one think the end for those who disobeyed the gospel of God? He puts much
struggle and fear into the faithful, so that they may learn securely the
judge, toward whom they have the account; having stirred these toward the fear
of him he exhorts those who disobey the divine teaching, saying that
inescapable 34 punishment will meet such, which he named their end,
circumscribing the disobedience they had; stirring these still more he says:
the righteous barely having received salvation, what must one think concerning
the impious who through being self-condemned are not raised unto judgment and
concerning the sinners who do not stand in the counsel of the righteous? For
the righteous being barely saved is in agony concerning how he completed the
judgment according to him; for even if he is conscious of nothing base in
himself, yet knowing that he has not been justified in this, until the judge
will justify him, he is in agony. (Cr 79, 5-22 Ma 204f) 35 and the one who is
held by God, but otherwise despises and transgresses his laws; but if
according to the underlying subject the same should be sinner and impious, he
will have the difference according to conception; and it is possible for the
one who sins immediately also to be impious by being impious toward God
through contempt. (Cr 79, 22-28 Ma 205)
36 The saying: "the heavens now and the earth by the same word have been
stored up" is likened to the things that happened according to the flood; for
he says: as through the former

heavens and the earth the world then perished, having been flooded, so when
the heavens and the earth are resolved into fire the impious and sinners 37
will utterly perish; and this is the thing of the likeness, that they perish
together likewise with those who perished in the flood; "for as in the days of
Noah", says the Lord, "they were eating, drinking, marrying, being given in
marriage, until the day Noah entered into the ark and the flood came and took
them all, according to these things will be the presence of the son of man."
And advocating further that it will be thus, he says the day of judgment will
come unnoticed like a thief, in which swiftly and without delay the heavens
will pass away, the elements melting toward the fire brought upon them; after
which heavens have passed with a rushing sound there will also be new heavens
and a new earth, in which righteousness and the promises of God have their
dwelling. (Cr 98, 11-28 Ma 210)
38 Or he says this on account of the resurrection of Christ, inasmuch as
through Thomas it was made known to all the apostles that he raised this
flesh, which they nailed to the cross. (Cr 107, 16-18 Ma 213) Each of
generated things is said to be something, for example to be angel, to be sun
or heaven, but the Savior alone happens to be absolutely; of whom all things
participating come into existence. Someone who has first received a hearing of
this according to the introductory teaching comes to seeing him scientifically
after much exercise, which has been called a handling concerning the word of
life, of her who said: "I am the life". It will also be said thus: concerning
the one who was in the beginning we have heard through the 39 law and the
prophets, that he will come; this one having come we saw manifestly with our
eyes, not having assented as it happened to the one who appeared in flesh; for
after much handling of ours, searching the scriptures that bear witness
concerning him, we believed the word concerning the life. (Cr 107, 1-13 Ma
213)
40 He says we have seen the life that is in the Father, which has been
manifested to us, having correspondingly seen it also having heard concerning
it; bearing witness to this manifested life we announce also to you those
things from which it follows that you too also see it. (Cr 107, 29-32 Ma 213)
He writes that an announcement has come to be from the Savior himself to the
disciples, making clear that God, being light, has no darkness in him; this
announcement we also bring to you, in order that you may have the same opinion
in you concerning God as being light; and God is intelligible eternal light;
for nothing perceptible exists always; con 41 trary to this light one must
understand the darkness as being ignorance and a most wicked habit; since then
neither of the forms of darkness, neither ignorance nor vice, is in God, it
has reasonably been said that there is no darkness in him. (Cr 109, 9–17 Ma
214) But if it has been said somewhere: "darkness is his hiding-place",
darkness in these things is said the unclarity in incomprehensibility; and it
is possible for the unclarity to be our ignorance, but not of God who is
light. (Cr 109, 17-19 Ma 214)
42 For knowing in scripture especially does not always indicate understanding,
but also having had experience of something 43 and having been united to it,
as in: "who knew no sin"; and: "I never knew you, workers of lawlessness"; for
he knew no sin not as not understanding what sin is, but as not having enacted
it. (Cr 111, 15-18 Ma 216) The one then who says he has known God, that is has
been mixed with him and partakes of him, necessarily has following it the
keeping of his commandments; wherefore it also converts; both of the
significations of knowing are made 44 manifest from the present text; for he
says: "in this we know", that is we understand, "that we have known him"
having been united to him and keeping his commandments; for the one who does
not keep them is manifest as being outside the knowledge of God. (Cr 111,
20-27 Ma 216) Just as then our Lord Jesus, not having known sin nor the
workers of lawlessness, is not deprived

of fullness, being wisdom and truth of God, so not having known the day of
judgment he is not lessened, since neither, when it speaks concerning God as
having forgetfulness and repentance and wrath, is the divinity lessened; for
by the same account by which those things are made clear, by this also
concerning the being ignorant of the day it would be resolved; for as of God
who alone is wise and has knowledge of all things neither forgetfulness
according to passion nor repentance nor any such thing exists, being said
economically concerning 45 him, so of the wisdom and truth of God not
admitting ignorance it has been said for some useful reason that he is
ignorant of the hour and the day of judgment. (Cr 111, 27-112, 3) And truly he
speaks of perfect love, from which nothing of the evils or deceitful things
can separate the one who is held by it. (Cr 112, 18-20) 46 Perhaps someone
will say, how is it possible to understand the commandment written concerning
love as being from the beginning and as having been heard by these to whom the
letter is written; for they were not Jews, as the end of the letter presents;
"little children", he says, "keep yourselves from the idols". Perhaps then an
old commandment existing from the beginning and heard by all human beings is
the friendly disposition according to the natural conceptions; for all, being
by nature tame and social animals, love the neighbors; hence the Savior,
stirring this natural disposition, says: "all things then, as many as you wish
that human beings do to you, you also likewise do to them". (Cr 112, 9-18 Ma
216f) 47 Lest someone think the system from heaven and earth and the things in
them is presented here from the word "world", he interpreted what "the things
in it" happen to be, in order that we too may correspondingly take them; "and
the things in it" then is a passionate and material disposi 48 tion, signified
through desire of flesh that campaigns against the soul and appetite of eyes.
(Cr 116, 11-16 Ma 218) And it is possible from the present texts to take also
concerning the visible world no longer being loved by those who have gone
above, by looking no longer at the temporary things, but the eternal; and
since those who prefer to spend time in it by love of life handle the
perceptible things passionately, reasonably boastfulness of life and desire of
flesh and of eyes have been said to be in it; of all which the one who thinks
above will be supercosmic, doing 49 the will of God by remaining always and
loving God. (Cr 116, 16-23 Ma 218) He says this, since having become disciples
they departed from the truth, and invented their own devices of blasphemies.
(Cr 118, 27-28)
50 The one who has scientifically distinguished concerning God as being just
according to essence will immediately have knowledge, that from this is
begotten the one who does his righteousness; just as the one who has known him
as being sanctification gives himself over to being sanctified, understanding
51 accurately: "you shall be holy, because I am holy"; and well also the
saying "doing", but not having done or going to do righteousness; virtue,
being practical, presents as righteous the one who is enacting; no one then
before the doing of it is righteous, nor the one who has ceased from acting
according to it. And that God is just, Moses says: "holy and just is the
Lord"; this one is Father of the Savior; "righteous Father, and the world did
not know you"; being his Son, as God from God and holy from holy, so also he
is just from just; it does not fight with these that he is said to be
righteousness; for that which is such according to essence would be called
both righteousness and just. (Cr 120, 30-121, 11 Ma 221) 52 now we are
children of God, that is according to the present life. (Ma 221)
57 Of Chrysostom. Since the devil, having turned before all, came to be in
sinning, from him is styled everyone who acts sinfully; for he takes the lead
in the one who sins through suggestion of wicked thoughts as in the case of
Judas. (Cr 123, 29-32 Ma 221) But someone will say, that the devil comes to be
in those who sin of those who have sinned before him in giving him place; to
which it must be said that doing the sin is the same as sinning in giving
place to the devil; for he gives 58

him place, having been led by desire, after receiving him accomplishing the
sin practically; for this is what doing it signifies; and well also the saying
"doing", but not having done, the one who has repented no longer being of the
devil, but only the one still enacting it; for thus also a slave of sin is the
one who does it, but not the one who has done it; "for everyone who does the
sin", he says, "is a slave of it". (Cr 123, 32-124, 10 Ma 222)
59 Of Severus; and he adds also the cause of not being able to sin, saying,
that his seed remains in him; and what is the seed of God that remains upon
the faithful? or the visitation of the Holy Spirit, through which we were born
again, 60 which does not depart from us, when according to Paul we do not
grieve through the most shameful deeds the Holy Spirit of God, in whom we were
sealed unto a day of revelation. (Cr 124, 15-21 Ma 222) For also when we say
that the one who has righteousness cannot do injustice we do not indicate a
natural inability, according to which we say the irrational animal cannot have
knowledge or some other such thing. (Cr 126, 25-28 Ma 222) If everyone who
does the sin is of the devil, from so acting he is shown to be a child of the
devil, just as also Elymas the magician from being "full of all deceit" was
known as a son of the devil; and since everyone who has been begotten of God
does not do sin by having in himself seed of God, the one who thus does not
sin one can find clearly being a son of God; and I added "thus" to the one who
does not sin, lest someone be clever saying that we set as children of God all
who do not sin; for infants not doing sin are not to be called children of
God; for they are prevented from sinning from age, but not from virtue, that
we may not say some other thing, asserting that it is not 61 soundly said
concerning infants that they do not do sin; for not sinning is not the same as
not doing sin; for the one seems a negation, but the other exists from a
disposition. (Cr 126, 13-25 Ma 222) Since the one who does righteousness has
been begotten of God, and it follows for the one so begotten to love the
brothers; the one who does not have righteousness by not doing it, but hates
his brother, is not of God; not in another way than not having been begotten
of him; for one must not attend to those who say these are not of God through
an earthy nature (Cr 127, 14-19)

=66.10= For there came to be some who said 66 that the Lord had come down from

him be except the Word himself? If then someone has the love, he has Christ in
himself; or rather the one who said: "God is love", this one again knows love
from God, which is not to be thought other than the Only-begotten, being as
God from God, so also love from love. (Cr 129, 26-31 Ma 225f) 73 Those who are
being begotten of God from succeeding according to virtue happen to be these

who begot him. (Cr 138, 22-33 Ma 228)
75 He says the subsistence and, that I may so speak, the matter of the love
one must have toward God is the keeping of the divine commandments; which
