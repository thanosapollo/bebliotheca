* On the Resurrection of the Lord
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

A discourse of our father among the saints John Chrysostom on the holy
Pascha.
It is always possible to rejoice in the Church of Christ among us, but much
more so in the present feast of the day with its myriad-lighted
torch-bearing, when Christ himself, the sun of righteousness, having risen
from the tomb's bridal chambers by the resurrection, shining first of all
upon the race of women, says to them: Rejoice. The first voice of the
resurrection is "Rejoice"; fear was split and grace was given. The first
voice of the resurrection is "Rejoice"; and there he met first of all with
women, that where sin abounded, grace might superabound. The first voice of
the resurrection is "Rejoice"; and this met with women, and the Lord said to
them: "Go quickly and tell my disciples that I go before you into Galilee;
there you will see me."
O the Lord's wise dealing. He did not send an angel to the disciples, nor an
archangel, but he sent women; and not simply women, but faithful women, his
own soldier-women. He sent them to the disciples, both gladdening and
rebuking: gladdening because he rose from the dead, rebuking because the
men, seized by cowardice, were hiding, and women, made bright by boldness,
were announcing the good news of the resurrection. Therefore we keep
festival today of a spiritual feast, the mother of every feast, which opened
from every side the entrance of paradise, which prepared a way into heaven
that men can tread, which turned one harmony of angels and men. Every tongue
is small for the praise of the present feast; for this stopped the dragon's
hissings, this plucked out the thorns of Adam's curse, this taught Eve to
give birth without pain.
The present feast is many-working; it removed the flaming sword and drew in
a breeze of the Holy Spirit, it plucked up the death-dealing tree and rooted
the life-giving Cross, it dried the fig leaves and made the lilies of the
newly illumined grow. No longer do we mourn Adam the first-formed, but we
glorify the second Adam, Christ; no longer do we rebuke Eve who
transgressed, but we bless the Virgin Theotokos; no longer do we turn away
from the tree, but we bear the Cross; no longer do we fear the serpent, but
we entreat the Holy Spirit; no longer do we go down into the earth, but we
go up into the heavens; no longer are we outside paradise, but we lodge in
Abraham's bosom; no longer do we hear in Jewish fashion: "I likened your day
to night," but we chant spiritually: "This is the day which the Lord has
made; let us rejoice and be glad in it." Why? Because the sun is no longer
darkened but all things are illumined, the veil is no longer torn but the
Church is made known, no longer do we bear the palm branches, but we carry
about the newly illumined, saying: "The righteous shall flourish as a palm
tree." "This is the day which the Lord has made; let us rejoice and be glad
in it"; let us be glad both with the senses and with the mind. This is the
day, this and no other, for there is one queen and not many tyrannies.
"This is the day which the Lord has made; let us rejoice and be glad in
it"; not running into taverns but hastening to martyrs' shrines, not
honoring drunkenness but loving due measure, not leaping in Jewish fashion
but feasting in apostolic fashion, not playing in the marketplaces but
chanting in the houses: "Is anyone cheerful?" says James, "let him sing
psalms." The present day is of resurrection, not of outrage; no one dancing
goes up into the heavens, no one drunk stands before a king. "This is the
day which the Lord has made; let us rejoice and be glad in it." The prophet
David, having long ago perceived this feast, says also in another psalm:
"All nations, clap your hands; shout to God with a voice of gladness." Why?
Because Judaism was thrust away and Christianity was added. "All nations,
clap your hands," because we who were far off have become near. "All
nations, clap your hands," because you were bought with the Master's blood,
because you trampled the power of death, because being men by nature we are
allotted gods by grace. It is fitting for the Church to keep feast, she who
was planted after the image of the paradise in Eden; here the tree of life
is rooted in the altar, here is one Christ-flowing spring of four
evangelical rivers, here the unfading rose of virginity, here the
ever-living violet of well-doing, here the grape-bearing vine-shoot of
lawful marriage, here the white-shining lily of the newly illumined,
according to the saying: "Let the desert rejoice and blossom as a lily." The
Church from the nations was once a desert before the Master's resurrection,
but the resurrection of the Lord's flesh showed the desert many-childed,
changed the barren into a tree of fruitfulness that does not shed its
leaves, made the untrodden passable.
And the prophet Isaiah, having foreseen these things in the Spirit, said:
"Rejoice, barren one who does not bear; break forth and cry, you who do not
travail, for many are the children of the desolate more than of her who has
the husband." The circuit of the newly illumined shines everywhere, and we
all keep feast. We rejoice, we trumpet the Lord's Pascha, obeying Paul who
cries: "For Christ our Pascha has been sacrificed for us." The shadow has
passed, the truth has shone; the law has ceased, grace has been exalted; the
wound of circumcision has been buried, the seal of baptism has been made
alive. No longer the brine of the Red Sea, but the sweetness of the holy
font shines through; no longer does Moses' sister shake the timbrel, but the
Church sets forth the Gospel; no longer the night of ignorance, but the
light of the knowledge of God, as the divine Paul says: "The night is far
spent, the day is at hand." What day? The Christ-appearing day, which showed
the dawn of the resurrection, which raised the sun of righteousness from the
tombs, which brought to birth the twelve-hour light of the apostles, of
which David said: "This is the day which the Lord has made; let us rejoice
and be glad in it." I rejoice at the gathering of the Christ-loving
multitude, I rejoice at all men's feasting at the spiritual festivals, I
rejoice seeing you all striking the spiritual lyres of David and with all
eagerness melodiously singing the psalmic oracle. And just as on a
well-sounding lyre the high notes joined to the low, and again the middle to
the others, produce one well-rhythmed sound when struck by the plectrum, in
the same way I see also in you; for the mixed notes of men and women,
children and old men, give back one concordant and well-sounding voice, and
the word of blessed David is cried aloud, which in the present season has
received its fulfillment.
"Let them say," he says, "those who have been redeemed by the Lord, whom he
redeemed from the hand of the enemy, and gathered them from the lands, from
the east and the west and the north and the sea." The prophet's words stand
unlying, for from every nation and tribe and land the net of the font has
caught these rational fishes through the fishermen apostles. For such is the
craft of Christ's fishermen: they do not set nets, but they take alive by
faith. Let them say, those who have been redeemed by the Lord, whom he
redeemed from the hand of the enemy, the one who sowed the tares, who begot
the curse, who brought forth the transgression, the begetter of the Jews, as
the prophet says: "Vain shall be the strength of your father." The word is
true: that one carpentered the Cross as a tree of curse, but Christ
transferred it into blessing; that one hewed a tomb, but Christ worked it
into an altar; that one tore Judas away from the disciples, but Christ
established all the newly illumined. For the brightness of the newly
illumined has become darkness of the Jews, and these men's feast those men's
misery, and these men's salvation those men's and the devil's drowning. For
these are in church and those in a wilderness; these with angels, that one
with swine.
From where that one fell, these were tossed back up; the captives are in
freedom and the tyrant in slavery. Let them say, those redeemed by the Lord:
what should they say? "Blessed are they whose iniquities have been forgiven
and whose sins have been covered." What should they say? "Christ has
redeemed us from the curse of the law." What should they say? "The snare was
broken and we were delivered." What should they say? "We will sing your
mercies, Lord, forever." Let them say; let them not be silent, nor send the
benefaction into forgetfulness. What should they say? "One Lord, one faith,
one baptism, one God who is over all and through all and in all," according
to the apostle. Let them not say one thing in the font and think another,
but as you were taught, O newly illumined, worship; as you were baptized,
glorify; as you were born again, believe; keep what you were entrusted with;
do not sell what you acquired; as an eagle you were born again, seek the
things above; you came out of Sodom, do not turn back; be saved on the
mountain, do not look around behind, lest you become a pillar of salt like
Lot's wife; you were called a child, do not imitate Absalom; you became a
disciple, do not meet with Judas; you entered the ark, do not draw near the
raven; you were freed from Egypt, do not deify a bull-head; you were torn
from the nations, do not worship the sun; you were delivered from idolatry,
do not busy yourself with the moon's shapes; you were redeemed from demons,
do not suppose stars to be holy; you were freed from the polytheism of men,
do not deify the passions; you have been counted worthy of freedom, do not
become a slave of pleasures, for by whatever a man is overcome, to that he
is also enslaved. You have put off the old man and put on the new, who is
Christ. Keep your soul and body safely from pollutions.
Let them say, those redeemed by the Lord: what should they say? "I have put
off my tunic; how shall I put it on?" "Let my soul rejoice in the Lord, for
he has clothed me with a garment of salvation and put a tunic of gladness
around me." What garment? The clothing of faith. And a tunic of gladness.
Which? "As many of you as were baptized into Christ have put on Christ,"
that is, the confession of the undivided Trinity. To him be the glory, to
the Father and to the Son and to the Holy Spirit, now and always and unto
the ages of ages. Amen.
