* Homily I on Saint Longinus the Centurion
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

The martyrdom of the holy Longinus the centurion, who was at the cross of our
Lord Jesus Christ, and of the two soldiers with him, written by Hesychius,
presbyter of Jerusalem.

1 Many are the writings of the martyrs. For as the pictures of painters,
imitating the sights of certain cities, and the heights of towers, and the
forms of men and women, sometimes young, sometimes aged, sometimes royal,
sometimes bridal, sometimes grim, sometimes rejoicing, display them, so also
those who have written out the words of the contests of the martyrs show to
the hearings the firmness of the soul, and the manliness of their ways, and
the fearless and bold and unshaken standing fast in the dangers, and the
alliance from God descending upon them, attested sometimes by the multitude of
signs, sometimes by the richness of the grace in their words. How then shall I
worthily write out the story of Longinus, wise and brave, and above all of his
witness for Christ, which he began by confessing him?

2 For when the whole chorus of the apostles, confounded by fear, had denied
him, while the lame whom he had raised up, and the blind whom he had
illumined, and the lepers together with these whom he had freed from the
many-scaled skin and the flesh of their blemishes, and others whose
misfortunes he had stilled and whose diseases he had driven away and whose
nature he had refashioned toward the better, all these, astounded at the drama
of the cross, endured a shipwreck full of winter; the confession of the
God-fearing centurion Longinus was sounding in the hearings of the twilight
synagogue of the Jews, when he said: "Truly this was the Son of God." For he
was not ashamed of Pilate hired out against Christ, nor did he shrink from the
fear of so many demons, nor was he overborne by the multitude of those
countless myriads. Let therefore the fairest contest of his be told and
written, which those who saw from the beginning narrated and handed down to
the generation after them; and from that one to the next, and so descending
even to us, the words of the testimony of Longinus have been kept like certain
pearls, flawless, adorning the holy bridal chamber of Christ.

3 When therefore Pilate, being asked, gave a guard for the keeping of the tomb
of the only-begotten Son of God, Longinus was the chief over them; for he it
was also who guarded the cross of Christ with the soldiers under him. But when
the resurrection of Christ had taken place, as the writings of the divine
Gospels have handed down to us, and the soldiers reported to the chief priests
and presbyters of the Jewish assembly the wonders that had happened, those
men, shamed before the people and wishing to quench the wonder, gave the
soldiers silver enough that they should slander the resurrection of Christ;
and in the same way they persuaded Pilate, with much money, to accept the
slander. Yet Longinus did not prefer deceit to the truth, nor did he take gold
against Christ, nor did he endure to set mammon above God, nor, enslaved to
greed, did he slide into its abyss of many deaths, nor did he put up with the
falsehood's slander against Christ, who raised up Adam and slew death and made
the mortal alive.

4 At these things Pilate and the magnates of the Jews, stung with rage, sought
an occasion against the blessed Longinus, but had none; for Longinus was the
most distinguished of the whole military phalanx. But because he chose wholly
to become Christ's, he takes off the belt of Caesar; and thereafter he sits at
home with the two soldiers, who with him also became joint heirs of the
blessed contest, having become heralds of what they saw and heard. And leaving
Jerusalem he returned with them, and becomes a herald in the land of the
Cappadocians, as Thomas among the Indians, and Peter among the Romans, and
John among the Asians, and Paul "from Jerusalem and as far as Illyricum," and
each to others, evangelizing Christ and the mysteries done by him and upon
him.

5 But the Jews, burning with zeal and stung with wrath, could no longer endure
the testimony of Longinus, which had now traveled through much of the earth.
Then they persuade Pilate to write a false letter to Caesar against the
blessed Longinus, that he is estranged in mind from the kingdom of the Romans
and, having neglected his arms, preaches Christ as king to the nations; and
that he has already filled his own fatherland and country with talk of this.
Along with these letters they send money also, and buy the death of Longinus,
having snatched away the ears of Caesar. And royal letters come to Pilate in
Judea against Longinus and those with him; and these, as having neglected
their arms, are commanded to lay their heads to the sword. And with much haste
Pilate entrusts the letters of Caesar to the servants of the murders. And
they, crossing with speed the land of the Cappadocians, arrive; and learning
that Longinus was philosophizing in his ancestral field and practicing quiet,
having freed himself from all the affairs of public life, they seize the field
at once in silence, wishing to carry out the order of Pilate and Caesar, so
that Longinus should not turn to flight. Hence they fell upon the man all at
once, as if crossing over upon some other man, and they took no heed of
anything of what their hands carried. Then, entering and falling in with
Longinus, not knowing him to be the very man sought by them and by Pilate,
they begin to ask where Longinus might be.

6 But he, the divine Spirit having made plain to him the drama, says: "Follow,
and I point this man out to you." Then the blessed one, rejoicing with
himself, as is fitting, and hastening on the sacrifice of the martyrdom, said:
"How beautiful are the feet of those who evangelize peace, of those who
evangelize the good things. Now I shall see the heavens opened; now I shall
narrate the glory of the Father; now I shall behold the ineffable lightning of
the Spirit. Now I shall say, 'Lord Jesus Christ, receive my spirit,' as
Stephen the first of the martyrs, whose most radiant voice I heard when he let
it go. Now Jerusalem, the golden-towered height, at once the fatherland of the
angels and the metropolis of the whole chorus of the saints, I shall tread
with drums and trophies of victory and royal triumphs. Now I take off the
clay tunic and lay aside the many-groaning bonds of the flesh and am parted
from corruption; for I am drawn toward incorruption, going out from this
temporary life into a life for me freed of winter, whose waves are many and
its shipwrecks hard. That life alone is an immortal harbor, where the saints
dwell. Rejoice, O soul, as you depart to your maker; show a bright face, as
the good season calls, and let us graciously entertain, rejoicing, those who
have procured for you such good things, and let us set a costly table for
those who call us to the royal supper."

7 Having said these things to himself, Longinus received into his own house
those who had come upon him; and having entertained them splendidly, with a
bright face and rejoicing exceedingly, and having eaten with them at the table
he had set, he begins after supper to ask what they need of Longinus. But
they, requiring oaths from him that whatever they say he will not publish the
matter nor strip bare the mystery entrusted to them, then tell what had been
written to Pilate from Caesar, and that they had come to cut off the head of
Longinus together with two others. But he, having learned who they were who
were with Longinus, knew upon learning that these were the very ones who had
preferred Christ to the Jewish hire; and with much haste he writes to them to
come quickly to him, as about to share with him in great good things. And
Longinus, having entertained them thus one day and a second, on the third,
taking them out to the field, waited there for the men sent from him. And
learning that they were near, he announces to them that he himself is
Longinus, the one sought by them.

8 But these at first did not know it, and after this, being persuaded, they
wept, plucking their own hair and uttering piteous cries toward Longinus:
"What thought, friend, seemed this venture to you? Why did you receive into
your house those coming upon you for murder? For what reason did you make a
supper for those who came to you for slaughter and set a table, not one day
only, but a second and a third? Did you not see your wine being mixed in your
blood, which is sought of us? What now is there to say? And what shall we be
able to do or contrive concerning you? Go, taking as the reward of your
hospitality from us the forgiveness of death; for we dare not lay the sword
upon you; our hands are benumbed, we blush at the table, we fear God, the
overseer of hospitality. Better for us to hazard the head of Longinus with
Pilate than that our whole life should melt in the conscience and we should
see at our table those called blood-shedding demons. For how shall our supper
be good if we lay hands upon you? For not only our hands are benumbed, but
also our feet and our whole body, if we do this, if we become murderers of our
benefactor and host."

9 These things they were saying to Longinus. Yet they did not persuade the
eager and brave-hearted athlete to decline the contest for the Lord and Savior
Jesus Christ, but he answers them boldly, saying: "Do not wish to see me a
traitor of my life, nor make me out a tempter of the grace. Let me not become
a wolf stripping the election of the sheep; by no means may I deny him whom I
once confessed. Let me not be robbed of that glory which I saw the elements
shaken of. Let not the assembly of creation become my accuser, that I passed
over him for whom heaven veiled its own sights and the sun put off the chariot
of the lamps, the days declined the stadium of the light, night was dragged
away by force; for the weight of the cross it did not stand, and in the Jewish
fight against God the charge was refused. These things I witnessed with my own
eyes and my own sight. How then shall I scatter the wealth of so many good
things bestowed on me? Not even the reproaches of angels shall I escape."

10 While Longinus was yet speaking these things, the company of the soldiers
with him, the one that had drawn the vote for death from Caesar, arrived. And
Longinus met them with a bright face and rejoicing exceedingly, and, kissing
both their eyes and their neck, he said to them: "Rejoice, O fellow-soldiers
of Christ and victors in the divine contests and heirs of the kingdom of the
heavens; rejoice that the gate has been opened for us into heaven, and the
angels are about to receive our souls and offer them to the only-begotten Son
of God. Behold, I see the lamps and I behold the crowns and I discern the
prizes with which we shall stand by the judgment seat of the Bridegroom." And
having said these things, he said to the executioners: "If then you have been
commanded anything, make haste." And he bade the steward of his house bring
him a pure robe, as one summoned to a wedding and a bridal chamber. And having
put it on, and pointing with his hand to the ridge on which he ordered his
blessed body to be laid, then bending the knee rejoicing exceedingly, and
having disposed himself together with the companions with him who had
completed the victory-bearing contest, he has his head cut off and is enrolled
among the choruses of apostles and martyrs; for they showed forth that
manliness, and these the good zeal.

11 But the executioners, taking the head of the holy Longinus, offer it to
Pilate; for he did this too for the favor of the Jews and on account of the
money he had received. And when the executioners brought the head of the saint
to Jerusalem, Pilate orders it to be shown to the Jews, who thirsted for the
death of Longinus, having sold the drama for much gold to those accustomed to
buy with money the blood of the righteous. Then Pilate orders the precious
head, precious to God and terrible to the demons, desired by the angels and
marveled at by the elements, to be cast outside before the gate of the city.
It lay therefore before the gate like some treasure beyond seizure; and when a
great heap of dung lay there, the priceless pearl of Christ was preserved
unharmed, and the dung was for it a tomb, or rather a chest, the man-befriending
God being long-suffering and in this working out a dispensation.

12 For a certain widow of that same land of the Cappadocians, mother of an
only child, having lost the eyes of her body and having conquered the
affliction by faith, arrived at Jerusalem with her only child, hoping here to
receive back her eyes in the veneration of the holy places. Therefore, guided
by the hand of her own child, she gathered the dust of the earth and looked to
plaster it upon her own eyes, perhaps hoping to find in it the salve of light
so as to see again; but suddenly a sickness came upon her only son, and he was
taken from her motherly hands; then a double darkness of the eyes came upon
her, and was put out by the sharpest affliction of her calamity. And the
wretched woman, crying out cries to God, said: "How long, Lord, will you
forget me utterly?" and what follows. "Am I alone a sinner," she said, "that I
am thus sharply exacted of the penalties? Am I worse than the harlot whom you
received as she brought you myrrh? But me you have bereft even of light, and
you have taken my only child; and there is no longer for me hope of salvation
from anywhere."

13 But as she was in great tribulation and inconsolable mourning, the blessed
Longinus appears to her in sleep and told her all the things concerning
himself, saying: "I am Longinus the centurion, who confessed upon the cross
our Lord Jesus Christ, that 'Truly this was the Son of God.' Hence I reveal to
you also how Pilate, having uttered false speeches against me, stirs up Caesar
and cuts off my head and brings it here and casts it outside the city; for the
children of the Jews buy it with money, and with him they cried agreement.
And a great heap of dung is piled upon it, so that this treasure ought to be
kept by you for healing; for as soon as you uncover it, you shall receive your
eyes; and then I shall present to you also the child with glory, and he will
comfort your mourning."

14 But the woman, encouraged by these words and at the same time nerved up, as
brave and faithful, hastened to the deed, guided by the hand to the place
where she learned the dung had been heaped, by those leading her, and they
being faithful themselves; and when they had come to the aforesaid place, she
begins to dig with her own hands; and by the providence of God, having more
quickly found the precious head of God, at once she beheld the rays of the
sun. And having folded it about and washed it with all diligence and anointed
it with costly myrrhs, she returns bearing the pearl to the place where she
was lodging. But again on the following night the blessed Longinus stands by
her, having her son at his own side, radiant and smiling and wearing as it
were the robe of a bridegroom, and says: "Behold him whom you longed for, O
woman. This is he whom you thought to be snatched from your hands. But God,
having freed him, has enlisted him in the kingdom above. And now I have him
with me; for I received him from the Savior, and he shall never depart from my
right hand. Therefore, having taken my head and the body of your child, lay
them in one chest, and weep for your only-begotten no more; for great glory
has been given him with me, and a holy altar and mystical sacrifices and
much-hymned dances and public festivals, and many other things besides these
which are impossible to be accomplished on earth, but in heaven 'what eye has
not seen and ear has not heard, and has not come up upon the heart of man,'
which God, having shown forth by deed and word his love toward us, has
prepared."

15 These words the mother of the young man received as the commands of a holy
prophet, and rising at once she hastened to her fatherland. And she took the
all-precious and holy head and restored it to her own fatherland, and laid her
own child beside the holy relic of Saint Longinus brightly and rejoicing, as
Saint Longinus commanded her, in a village called Sandralis, for from there
was the holy Longinus; and crying out to herself and saying in her heart, and
preaching with her tongue: "We know that to those who love God all things work
together for good. I sought eyes of flesh, and with them I received also those
of the spirit. The loss of my child grieved me, but behold I have the heir
standing before God with royal glory. I see the youth clad in purple, I behold
the young man exceeding fair. The child is star-faced, smiling at his mother
and flashing beside the side of Longinus like the morning star beside the ray
of the sun, appearing many times by night and many times by day, and not in
dream only, but openly he shows himself to me after the manner of the martyrs.
And his lot is with the prophets, though he did not live the life of prophets,
and he bears the cross with Longinus, and the solemn trophy of the kingdom of
the heavens is borne before him, and he preaches among the angels, and in the
midst of the people of the archangels he has cried out, as a disciple of
Longinus, as many things as the seraphim yield to Longinus to glorify, and he
does not clash with the cherubim as they hymn; he too utters a holy voice,
hymning, trophy-bearing, mystical, a song of victory: 'Truly this was the Son
of God,' and is and shall be; which he has before the ages and shall have unto
ages. He makes the ages old, and he himself remains unaging; for his kingdom
is an everlasting kingdom, and his dominion unto the age shall not be
corrupted; to him the glory and the might unto the ages of ages. Amen."

16 I, Hesychius, presbyter of Jerusalem, having searched much with much toil,
was able to find the martyrdom of the holy Longinus the centurion, who said
upon the cross of Christ, "Truly this was the Son of God"; and I found it in a
book-cabinet, in the library of the Holy Resurrection, and I compiled his
confession together with the encomium. Henceforth let those who fear the Lord
be assured that this is truly the holy Longinus the centurion, who confessed
him upon the cross of Christ; whose intercession and entreaty may we all be
kept safe, both those who read it and those who hear it, in Christ Jesus our
Lord, to whom be the glory and the might and the kingdom of the Father and of
the Son and of the Holy Spirit, now and always and unto the ages of ages.
Amen.
