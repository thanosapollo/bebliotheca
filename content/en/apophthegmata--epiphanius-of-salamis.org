Concerning Saint Epiphanius, bishop of Cyprus.
αʹ. Saint Epiphanius the bishop related that in the time of the blessed
Athanasius the Great, crows flying around the temple of Serapis were crying
uncea =65.164= singly, Cras, Cras. And the Hellenes, standing before the
blessed Athanasius, cried out: Wicked old man, tell us what the crows are
crying. And answering he said: The crows cry, Cras, Cras; and Cras in the
Ausonian tongue is tomorrow; and he added that Tomorrow you will see the
glory of God. And next the death of the emperor Julian was announced. And
when this had happened, they ran together and cried out against Serapis,
saying: If you did not want him, why were you receiving his things?
βʹ. The same related that there was a certain charioteer among the
Alexandrians, who was the son of a mother Mary. This man, when the horse-race
was being held, fell; then rising he overtook the one who had thrown him
down, and won. And the crowd cried out: The Son of Mary has fallen and has
been raised and has conquered. While this cry was still being spoken, the
report about the temple of Serapis fell upon the crowd, that the great
Theophilus, having gone up, had overthrown the idol of Serapis, and had
become master of the temple.
γʹ. It was made known to the blessed Epiphanius, bishop of Cyprus, by the
abba of the monastery which he had in Palestine, that By your prayers we have
not neglected our canon, but with diligence we perform both the third and the
sixth and the ninth. But he, blaming them, made known to them saying that You
are shown to be neglecting the other hours of the day, being idle from
prayer. For the true monk must have prayer and psalmody unceasingly in his
heart.
δʹ. Saint Epiphanius once sent to abba Hilarion, entreating him and saying:
Come, let us see one another before we go out from the body; and when he had
arrived, they rejoiced with one another. And while they were eating, a fowl
was brought. And the bishop, taking it, gave it to abba Hilarion. And the old
man says to him: Forgive me, for from the time I received the habit, I have
not eaten a sacrificed thing. And the bishop says to him: But I, from the
time I received the habit, have not allowed anyone to go to sleep having
something against me, nor have I gone to sleep having something against
anyone. And the old man says to him: Forgive me, for your way of life is
greater than mine.
εʹ. The same used to say that the image of Christ, Melchizedek, blessed
Abraham, the root of the Jews; much more does the truth itself, Christ, bless
and sanctify all who believe in him.
ς ʹ. The same used to say: The Canaanite woman cries out and is heard; and
the woman with the issue of blood is silent and is blessed; but the Pharisee
cries out and is condemned; the publican does not even open his mouth and is
heard.
ζʹ. The same used to say that David the prophet prayed at an untimely hour,
rose at midnight, made supplication before dawn, stood present at dawn,
begged in the morning, entreated at evening and at midday, and for this
reason he said: Seven times a day I have praised you.
ηʹ. He said again that the possession of the books of Christians is necessary
for those who have them. For even the sight of the books by itself makes us
more sluggish toward sin, and urges us rather to rise up toward
righteousness.
θʹ. He said again that a great safeguard against sinning is the reading of
the Scriptures.
ιʹ. He said again that a great cliff and a deep pit is ignorance of the
Scriptures.
ιαʹ. He said again that a great betrayal of salvation is to know none of the
divine laws.
ιβʹ. The same used to say that the sins of the righteous are about the lips,
but those of the ungodly, from the whole body. Whence David sings: Set a
watch, O Lord, upon my mouth, and a door of enclosure about my lips; And: I
said, I will guard my ways, that I may not sin with my tongue.
ιγʹ. The same was asked, Why are the legal commandments ten, but the
beatitudes nine? and he said: The Decalogue is equal in number to the
Egyptian plagues; but the numbering of the beatitudes is an image of the
triple Trinity.
ιδʹ. The same was asked whether one righteous man is enough to prevail upon
God, and he said: Yes, for he himself said: Search out one who does judgment
and righteousness, and I will be gracious to all the people.
ιεʹ. The same said that God yields even the principal to sinners when they
repent, as to the harlot and the publican; but from the righteous he
dema =65.168= nds interest as well. And this is what he was saying to the
apostles, that Unless your righteousness abounds more than that of the
scribes and Pharisees, you shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven.
ις ʹ. And he used to say this also, that God sells the acts of righteousness
very cheaply to those who are eager to buy: for a small fragment of bread, a
cheap garment, a cup of cold water, a single obol.
ιζʹ. And he added this also, that a man borrowing from a man because of
poverty, or need of plenty, and when he pays back, confesses thanks, but pays
back secretly being ashamed. But the Master God does the reverse: being
borrowed from in secret, he pays back before angels and archangels and the
righteous.
