FIFTH THEOLOGICAL ORATION ON THE HOLY SPIRIT

1. Such then is the discourse concerning the Son; and thus he has escaped
those who were stoning, having passed through the midst of them. For the Word
is not stoned, but he stones, when he wills, and slings at beasts, words
climbing the mountain badly. But what would you say, they ask, concerning the
Holy Spirit? From where do you introduce to us a strange God and one
unwritten? This already even those who are moderate concerning the Son. For
what is found on the roads and the rivers, that they both split from one
another and are gathered into one another: this happens here too because of
the wealth of impiety, and those who differ in the other things agree in
others, so that one cannot even know clearly what agrees or what fights.

2. The discourse concerning the Spirit has something difficult too, not only
because men, worn out in the discourses concerning the Son, wrestle more hotly
with the Spirit; for they must altogether be impious in something, or life is
not even livable for them; but also because we ourselves, worn down by the
multitude of questions, suffer the same as those with a bad stomach, who when
they have taken a disgust at some of the foods are similarly displeased at
every discourse, as those at food. Nevertheless let the Spirit give, and the
word will run, and God will be glorified. The careful examining and
distinguishing, in how many ways either spirit or holy is understood and said
in the divine Scripture, with the testimonies fitting to the contemplation,
and what besides these is in its own way the thing joined from both, I mean
the Holy Spirit, we will leave to others, who have philosophized these things
both for themselves and for us, since we too have these things from them. And
we ourselves will turn to what follows of the discourse.

3. Those then who are displeased, as though we were introducing the Holy
Spirit as some strange and interpolated God, and who fight fiercely for the
letter, let them know that they fear a fear where there is no fear, and let
them know clearly that the friendship of the letter is a garment of impiety
for them, as will be shown a little later, when we have refuted their
objections as far as we can. But we are so confident in the Godhead of the
Spirit, which we champion, that we will even begin the theology from here,
applying the same voices to the Trinity, even if it seem more daring to some.
There was the true Light, which lights every man coming into the world, the
Father. There was the true Light, which lights every man coming into the
world, the Son. There was the true Light, which lights every man coming into
the world, the other Paraclete. There was, and there was, and there was; but
there was one. Light, and light, and light, but one light, one God. This is
what David also imagined before, saying: "In your light we shall see light."
And now we have both seen and we proclaim, from the light of the Father
grasping the Son as light in the light the Spirit, a concise and unadorned
theology of the Trinity. Let him who sets it aside set it aside, let him who
does lawlessness do lawlessness; we proclaim what we have understood. We will
go up upon a high mountain and cry out, if we are not heard from below. We
will exalt the Spirit, we will not fear. And if we do fear, it will be by
being silent, not by proclaiming.

4. If there was a time when the Father was not, there was a time when the Son
was not. If there was a time when the Son was not, there was a time when the
Holy Spirit was not either. If the one was from the beginning, the three also.
If you throw the one down, I dare, and I say, do not set the two up either.
For what is the profit of an incomplete Godhead? Or rather what Godhead, if it
is not perfect? And how is it perfect, if something is lacking toward
perfection? And it lacks somehow, if it does not have the Holy; and how would
it have it, not having this? For if there is some other holiness besides

this; and what this is understood to be, let someone say; or if it is the
same, how is it not from the beginning? As though it were better for God to be
incomplete at some time, and without the Spirit. If he was not from the
beginning, he is ranked with me, even if a little before me. For we are cut
from God by time. If he is ranked with me, how does he make me God, or how
does he join me to Godhead?

5. Rather I will philosophize for you about him a little from above. For we
have distinguished concerning the Trinity also before. The Holy Spirit the
Sadducees thought did not even exist at all; nor angels, nor resurrection; I
do not know from where, having spat out the so many testimonies concerning him
in the Old. And of the Greeks the more theological, and those who have come
nearer to us, imagined him, as it seems to me; but they differed about the
name, calling him Mind of the all, and the mind from without, and such things.
And of the wise among us some supposed this an energy, some a creature, some
God, and some did not know which of these, from reverence of Scripture, as
they say, as having declared neither clearly. And for this reason they neither
worship nor dishonor, being disposed somehow in the middle concerning him, or
rather even exceedingly miserably. And of those who have supposed him God,
some are pious as far as thought, and some dare to be pious with the lips
also. And I have heard of others measuring Godhead more wisely, who confess
that the things understood are three according to us, but they have stood so
far from one another as to make the one unbounded both in essence and in
power; and the other in power, but not in essence; and the other circumscribed
in both; imitating in another way those who name Creator, and fellow-worker,
and minister, and think the order in the names to be also a sequence of the
things.

6. And we have no discourse toward those who have supposed he does not even
exist, or those who talk nonsense among the Greeks. For let us not be fattened
with the oil of sinners into the discourse. But toward the others we will
converse thus. The Holy Spirit must altogether be supposed either among the
things that subsist by themselves, or among the things contemplated in
another; of which the experts in these things call the one essence, and the
other accident. If then he is accident, this would be an energy of God. For
what else, or of whom? For this somehow also more flees composition. And if
energy, he will clearly be energized, not energize, and will cease together
with being energized. For such is energy. How then does he energize, and say
these things, and divide, and is grieved, and is provoked, and as many things
as clearly belong to one moved, not to motion? And if he is some essence, not
of the things about the essence, he will be supposed either a creature, or
God. For some middle of these, either sharing in neither, or composed from
both, not even those who fashion goat-stags would think of. But if a creature,
how do we believe into him, or are we perfected in him? For it is not the same
to believe into something, and to believe concerning it. For the one belongs
to Godhead, the other to every thing. And if God, then not a creature, nor a
thing made, nor a fellow-slave, nor at all any of the low names.

7. Here the discourse is yours; let the slings be sent, let the syllogisms be
woven. Either unbegotten altogether, or begotten. And if unbegotten, two
things without beginning. And if begotten, subdivide again: either this from
the Father, or from the Son. And if from the Father, two sons and brothers.
And you, fashion even twins for me, if you will, or the one older, and the
other younger; since you are exceedingly fond of body. And if from the Son,
there has appeared, he says, even a grandson God for us; than which what could
be more paradoxical? These things then the wise at doing evil, not willing to
write the good things. But I, if I saw the division necessary, would have
accepted the facts, not fearing the names. For it is not, because the Son is
Son according to some higher relation, we not having been able otherwise than
thus to indicate the being from God and consubstantial, that one must already
think it necessary to transfer all the lower names also, and those of our
kinship, onto the divine. Or perhaps you would even suppose God male for us,
according

to this argument, because he is named God, and Father? And the Godhead
something female, as far as the names go? And the Spirit neither, because not
generative? And if this too should be played for you, God having intercourse
with his own will, according to the ancient nonsense and myths, and having
begotten the Son, some male-female God of Marcion and of Valentinus has been
introduced to us as well, of the one who molded the new aeons.

8. But since we do not accept your first division, which supposes nothing
between unbegotten and begotten, immediately the brothers and the grandsons
will be gone for you together with the solemn division, dissolved together as
when some many-folded bond of the first principle has been loosed, and they
have withdrawn from the theology. For where will you set the processible, tell
me, which has appeared in the middle of your division, and is introduced by a
theologian better than according to you, our Savior? Unless you have taken
that voice out of your Gospels, because of your third testament: "The Holy
Spirit, who proceeds from the Father"; who, inasmuch as he proceeds from
there, is not a creature; and inasmuch as he is not begotten, is not Son; and
inasmuch as he is between unbegotten and begotten, is God. And thus, having
escaped the nets of your syllogisms, he has appeared God, stronger than your
divisions. What then is the procession? You tell the unbegottenness of the
Father, and I will give the physiology of the generation of the Son, and the
procession of the Spirit, and we will both be beside ourselves, peering into
the mysteries of God; and who are we that do this? Those who cannot even know
the things at our feet, nor number the sand of seas, and drops of rain, and
days of an age, much less to walk into the depths of God, and to give an
account of the nature so unspeakable and above reason.

9. What then is it, he says, that is lacking to the Spirit toward being Son?
For if something were not lacking, he would be Son. We say he does not lack;
for God is not deficient; but the difference of the manifestation, that I may
speak thus, or of the relation toward one another, has made their name
different also. For neither is something lacking to the Son toward being
Father, for sonship is not a deficiency, but he is not Father for this reason.
Or thus something will be lacking to the Father also toward being Son; for the
Father is not Son. But these things are not from some deficiency, nor from a
lowering according to essence; but the very not having been begotten, and the
having been begotten, and the proceeding, has named the one Father, the other
Son, and the other this which is called Holy Spirit, in order that the
unconfusedness of the three hypostases may be preserved in the one nature and
dignity of the Godhead. For neither is the Son Father, for there is one
Father, but he is what the Father is; nor is the Spirit Son because he is from
God, for there is one Only-begotten, but he is what the Son is; the three are
one in the Godhead, and the one is three in the properties; in order that
neither the one may be Sabellian, nor the three of the wicked division now.

10. What then? Is the Spirit God? Entirely so. What then, consubstantial? If
indeed God. Give me then, he says, from the same the one a son, and the other
not a son, then consubstantial, and I accept God and God. You too give me
another God, and a nature of God, and I will give you the same Trinity with
the same names and facts. But if God is one, and the highest nature also one,
from where shall I present the likeness to you? Or do you seek again from the
things below and those about you? It is exceedingly shameful, and not shameful
only, but also vain enough, to take the comparison of the things above from
the things below, and of the unmoved from the flowing nature, and, as Isaiah
says, to seek the living among the dead; yet I will try, for your sake, to
give some help to the discourse from here too. The rest I think I will pass
by, though I could say many things from the history of animals, some known to
us, some to the few, as many as nature has crafted about the generations of
animals. For they are said to be generated not only the same from the same,
nor others from others, but also the same from others, and others from the
same. And if the account is credible to anyone, there is also another mode of
generation, some thing itself consumed by itself and

brought to birth. And there are some that also somehow stand out of
themselves, being transferred and remade from other animals into others, by
nature's ambition. And already even of the same, the one is not an offspring,
and the other is an offspring, yet consubstantial; which also somehow more
resembles the present matter. And having said one of our own things, which is
known to all also, I will pass to another discourse.

11. What then was Adam? A fashioning of God. And what was Eve? A section of
the fashioning. And what was Seth? An offspring of both. Does then fashioning,
and section, and offspring seem the same to you? How not? And are these
consubstantial, or what? And how not? It has been agreed then that even the
things that have come to subsist differently can be of the same essence. And I
say these things, not carrying the fashioning, or the cutting, or any of as
many things as belong to body, onto the Godhead, lest some one of the
word-fighters fasten on me again, but contemplating on these, as on a stage,
the things understood. For it is not possible for any of the things compared
to reach purely the whole truth. And what is this, they say? For it is not of
the one the one an offspring, and the other something else. What then? Are Eve
and Seth not of the same Adam? For of whom else? Or are both offspring? Not at
all. But what? The one a section, and the other an offspring. And yet both are
the same as one another; for they are human beings; no one will contradict.
Will you then cease fighting against the Spirit, as though he is either an
offspring altogether, or not consubstantial, nor God, having taken from the
human things the possibility of our supposition? I at least think it is well
for you, if you have not known how to be exceedingly contentious, and to fight
against the clear things.

12. But who has worshiped the Spirit, he says? Who either of the ancients, or
of the new? And who has prayed? And where is it written that one must worship
or pray? And from where do you have this? The more perfect cause we will give
later, when we converse concerning the unwritten. But now it will suffice to
say this much: it is the Spirit in whom we worship, and through whom we pray.
"For God is Spirit," it says, "and those who worship him must worship in
spirit and truth." And again: "For what we should pray as we ought, we do not
know, but the Spirit himself intercedes for us with groanings unutterable."
And, "I will pray with the spirit, and I will pray with the mind also," that
is, in mind and spirit. To worship the Spirit, then, or to pray, seems to me
to be nothing else than that he brings the prayer and the worship to himself.
Which of those inspired, and of those who know well that the worship of the
one is also the worship of the three, because of the equal honor of the
dignity and of the Godhead in the three, would not praise this? And I will not
even fear that saying, that all things have come to be through the Son, as
though the Holy Spirit also were one of the all. For as many as have come to
be have been said, not simply all things; for neither the Father, nor as many
as have not come to be. Show then that he has come to be, and then give him to
the Son, and number him with the creatures. But so long as you do not show
this, you are helped in nothing toward impiety by the comprehensive word. For
if he has come to be, through Christ altogether; I myself will not deny it.
And if he has not come to be, how is he either one of the all, or through
Christ? Stop then both honoring the Father badly against the Only-begotten
(and it is a bad honor, giving a creature, to deprive the Son of what is more
honorable), and the Son against the Spirit. For he is not craftsman of a
fellow-slave, but is glorified together with one equally honored. Set nothing
of the Trinity with yourself, lest you fall out of the Trinity. Cut off from
none the one nature and equally to be revered, since whatever of the three you
pull down, you will have pulled down the whole, or rather you will have fallen
out of the whole. It is better to take a small imagination of the union, than
to dare a complete impiety.

13. And the discourse has come for us upon the very head; and I groan that a
question long dead, and having yielded to the faith, is now being renewed; yet
it is necessary all the same to stand against the chatterers, and not to be
condemned in default, having a discourse, and advocating for the Spirit. If
God, they say, and God, and God, how not three Gods? or

how is what is glorified not polyarchy? Who are these? The more perfect in
impiety, or also those of the second portion, I mean those somehow
well-disposed concerning the Son? For the one discourse is common for me
toward both, and the other is proper toward these. The one toward these then
is of this kind. What do you say to us tritheists, you who worship the Son,
even if you have stood off from the Spirit? And are you not ditheists? For if
you deny the worship of the Only-begotten also, you have clearly been ranked
with the opponents; and why are we being humane toward you as not wholly dead?
And if you worship, and are disposed savingly as far as this, we will ask you:
what is the account of your ditheism, if this is charged against you? If there
is an account of understanding, answer, give us also a way of answer. For with
whatever words you beat off the ditheism, these will suffice for us also
against the tritheism. And thus we win, using you the accusers as advocates;
than which what is more noble?

14. And what is the common contest and discourse for us toward both? For us
there is one God, because there is one Godhead; and the things from him have
the reference toward one, even if three are believed. For it is not that the
one is more God, and the other less; nor that the one is earlier, and the
other later; nor is it cut by will, nor divided by power, nor is any of as
many things as belong to the divisible things to be taken here; but the
Godhead is undivided in things divided, if one must speak concisely; and as in
three suns holding to one another, one blending of the light. Whenever then we
look toward the Godhead, and the first cause, and the monarchy, one is what is
imagined for us; and whenever toward the things in which the Godhead is, and
the things from the first cause being from there timelessly and with equal
glory, three are the things worshiped.

15. What then, would they say, is there not also among the Greeks one Godhead,
as those who philosophize the more perfect things among them, and among us one
humanity, the whole race? And yet there are many gods, and not one, as indeed
also human beings? But there the commonality has the one only contemplated in
thought; and the things according to each are divided from one another very
much both by time and by the passions and by the power. For we are not only
composite, but also opposed both to one another and to ourselves, not
remaining purely the same even for one day, much less the whole life, but
always flowing and changing both in bodies and in souls. And I do not know if
not also angels and every nature above after the Trinity, even if they are
some simple, and more firmly set toward the fair by nearness to the extreme
fair.

16. And the gods and daemons worshiped among the Greeks, as they themselves
say, have no need of us as accusers, but they are caught by their own
theologians, as passionate, and as factious, and as full of as many evils and
changes, and standing opposed not only toward one another but also toward the
first causes, whom they name Oceans, and Tethyses, and Phanetes, and I do not
know whom; and last some god who hates children because of love of rule,
swallowing all the others from greed, in order that he may become father of
all men and gods, who are unhappily eaten and vomited. And if these are myths
and some under-meanings, as they themselves say, fleeing the shame of the
account, what will they say to this, "And all things have been divided in
three," and that one is set over one of the things that are, being divided
both by the materials and by the dignities? And ours is not of this kind; nor
is this the portion of Jacob, says my theologian; but each one of them has
toward the compound not less than toward itself, by the sameness of the
essence and of the power. And this is the account of the union, as far as
concerns what we have grasped. If then this account is strong, thanks to God
for the contemplation; and if not, let us seek the stronger.

17. And your words I do not know whether we shall say belong to one playing,
or being serious, by which you destroy our union. For what then is even the
account? The consubstantial are co-numbered, you say; calling co-numbering the
gathering into one number; the things not consubstantial

are not co-numbered; so that you will not escape saying three Gods according
to this account; and for us there is not even one danger; for we do not say
consubstantial. You then have released yourself from troubles with one voice,
and have won the wicked victory; having done something like those who hang
themselves through fear of death. For in order that you may not toil standing
up for the monarchy, you have denied Godhead, and have betrayed to the enemies
what was sought. But I, even if there is need to toil some, will not give up
what is worshiped. And here I do not even see what the labor is.

18. The consubstantial are co-numbered, you say; and the things not so having
have a monadic indication. From where do you have this, and from what
dogmatists and myth-tellers? Or do you not know that every number is
indicative of the quantity of the underlying things, not of the nature of the
facts? And I am so old-fashioned, or rather ignorant, that I name three the
things so many in number, even if they have stood apart in nature; and one,
and one, and one, otherwise the so many monads, even if they are joined in
essence, looking not more toward the facts than toward the how-many of the
facts, upon which the numbering is. And since you cling exceedingly to the
letter, though indeed fighting against the letter, take the proofs for me from
there. There are three in the Proverbs that go well on their way, a lion, and
a goat, and a cock; and a king speaking in a nation the fourth; not to mention
the other tetrads numbered there, but divided in nature. And I find two
cherubim for Moses numbered monadically. How then are those three, according
to your technology, so torn from one another in their natures; or these
monadic, so of like nature to one another and compounded? For if I should say
God and mammon two lords numbered into one, being so far from one another, I
would perhaps be laughed at even more for the co-numbering.

19. But for me, he says, those things are said to be co-numbered, and of the
same essence, with which the names also are pronounced correspondingly
together; as, three men, and three gods, not three these and these. For what
is the exchange? This belongs to one legislating for the names, not speaking
truly. Since for me too Peter, and Paul, and John are not three, nor
consubstantial, so long as three Peters, and three Pauls, and so many Johns
are not said. For what you have observed upon the more generic names, this we
too will demand upon the more specific according to your invention. Or will
you do injustice, not giving what you have received? And what of John, saying
in the catholic epistles that those who bear witness are three, the spirit,
the water, the blood? Does he seem to you to talk nonsense, first because he
has dared to co-number the things not consubstantial, which you give to the
consubstantial (for who would say these are of one essence?), and second
because he has not met it correspondingly, but having set forward three in the
masculine, he brought on the three in the neuter, against your bounds and laws
and those of your grammar? And yet what is the difference, either having set
forward three to bring on one and one and one, or saying one and one and one
to name not three but three things? Which you yourself refuse upon the
Godhead. And what is the crab to you, both the animal, and the instrument, and
the star? And what the dog, both the land one, and the water one, and the
heavenly? Do they not seem to you to be called three crabs and dogs? Entirely
so. Are they then consubstantial besides this? Who of those who have mind will
say it? Do you see how the account concerning co-numbering has fallen apart
for you, having been refuted by so many things? For if neither the
consubstantial are co-numbered altogether, and the things not consubstantial
are co-numbered, and the joint pronunciation of the names is upon both, what
more do you have from what you dogmatized?

20. And I consider this too, and perhaps not outside reason. Is not the one
and the one compounded into two? And are the two not resolved into one and
one? Clearly so. If then the things compounded are consubstantial according to
your account, and the things cut are of other essence, what happens? That the
same things are both consubstantial and of other essence. I laugh also at your
pre-numberings, and the

sub-numberings, in which you think greatly, as though the facts lay in the
order of the names. For if this is so, what prevents, according to the same
account, since the same things are both pre-numbered and sub-numbered in the
divine Scripture because of the equal honor of the nature, that they are both
more honorable than themselves and more dishonored? And the same account for
me also concerning the voice God and Lord; and still of the prepositions, of
from whom, and through whom, and in whom, with which you technologically treat
the divine for us, giving the one to the Father, and the other to the Son, and
the other to the Holy Spirit. For what would you have done, if each of these
had been firmly assigned to each, when, all having been arranged with all, as
is clear to those who labor, you introduce through these so great an
inequality both of the dignity and of the nature? These things too suffice for
those not exceedingly ungrateful. But since it is of the hard things for you,
once you have leapt upon the Spirit, to be held from the rush, and not, just
as the bolder of the swine, to contend to the end, and to push against the
sword, until you take the whole blow inside, come, let us consider what
account is still left for you.

21. Again and many times you cycle round for us the unwritten. That this is
not strange, nor brought in from beside, but is recognized and laid bare both
to those of old and to those now, has already been shown by many of those who
have distinguished concerning this, as many as have not met the divine
Scriptures lazily nor as a side-work, but having split the letter and peered
inside, have been counted worthy to see the stored beauty, and have been lit
by the illumination of the knowledge. And let us too show in passing, as far
as is possible, so as not to seem to be some superfluous people, nor more
ambitious than what is needed, building upon another's foundation. And if the
not having been written very clearly God, nor often by name, as the Father
first and the Son later, becomes a cause of blasphemy for you, and of this
superfluous tongue-ache and impiety, we will loose this harm for you, having
philosophized a little concerning facts and names and especially in the custom
of Scripture.

22. Of the facts some are not, but are said; and some that are are not said;
and some neither are, nor are said; and some both, both are, and are said. Do
you demand the proofs of these from me? I am ready to furnish them. God sleeps
in Scripture, and watches, and is angry, and walks, and has the cherubim as a
throne; and yet when has he become passionate? And when have you heard that
God is a body? This not being was fashioned. For we have named, as far as is
reachable for us, the things of God from our own. His being still from us, and
as it were neglecting, for causes which he himself knows, sleeping. For our
sleeping is of this kind, both without energy and without deed. And his doing
good suddenly from a change, watching. For the loosing of sleep is waking, as
visitation of turning away. And we have made punishing to be being angry; for
thus for us punishment is from anger. And his energizing now here, now there,
walking. For the passage from one thing toward another is walking. And his
resting among the holy powers, and as it were being fond of the place, sitting
and being enthroned. And this too is ours. For the divine rests in none so as
in the saints. And the swift-moving, flight; and the oversight, face; and the
giving and receiving, hand; and some other of the powers or energies of God
altogether has drawn for us some other of the bodily things.

23. Again from where do you have the unbegotten, or the unoriginate, your
citadels, or we too the immortal? Show these by name, or we will set them
aside, since they have not been written, or we will strike them out. And you
have died from your own hypotheses, your names having been pulled down for
you, and the wall of refuge upon which you had trusted. Or is it clear that
from the things that gather these, even if these are not said? From which of
these? "I am first and I after these." And, "Before me there is not another
God and after me there will not be." For the whole "is" is mine; neither
having been begun, nor going to cease. Having taken these, the not being
anything

before him, nor having an older cause, you named unoriginate, and unbegotten;
and the not going to stand from being, immortal and indestructible. The first
pairs then are of this kind, and so having. And what things neither are, nor
are said? That the divine is wicked, that the sphere is square, that the past
stands present, that man is not composite. For whom have you known as having
come at some time to so great a madness, as to dare either to think something
of this kind, or to declare it? It remains to show what things both are and
are said: God, man, angel, judgment; such syllogisms are vanity, and an
overturning of the faith, and an emptying of the mystery.

24. Since then there is so great a difference in the names and the facts, how
do you slave so exceedingly to the letter, and come to be with the Jewish
wisdom, and follow syllables, having left the facts? And if when you said
twice five, or twice seven, I gathered the ten, or fourteen from the things
said; or from living being rational, mortal, the human being, would I have
been thought by you to talk nonsense? And how, saying your own things? For the
words belong not more to the one saying them, than to the one compelling to
say. As then here I would not have looked more at the things said than at the
things understood; so neither if I found some other of the things not said, or
not clearly, understood from Scripture, would I have fled the pronunciation,
fearing you the sycophant of the names. Thus then we will stand toward those
well-disposed from a half. For you are not even permitted to say this. For he
who denies the titles of the Son that are so clear and so many would clearly
not have revered these either, even if you knew them much clearer and more.
And now I will also show the cause of the whole concealment, though you are
wise, having brought the discourse up a little.

25. Two conspicuous changes of lives have come to be from the whole age, which
are also called two covenants, and earthquakes of earth, because of the fame
of the matter; the one from the idols to the law, and the other from the law
toward the gospel. And we proclaim a third earthquake, the translation from
here upon the things there, the things no longer moved, nor shaken. And the
two covenants have suffered the same. What is this? They were not moved over
all at once, nor together with the first motion of the undertaking. For the
sake of what? For it is necessary to know. In order that we may not be forced,
but persuaded. For the involuntary is not even lasting; as what of the streams
or of the plants is held down by force; and the voluntary is both more lasting
and more safe. And the one belongs to the one who forced, and the other is
ours; and the one to the fairness of God, and the other to tyrannical
authority. He did not then think he must do good to the unwilling, but benefit
the willing. For this reason both pedagogically and medically he takes away
some of the ancestral things, and concedes some, yielding a little of the
things toward pleasure; as the physicians to those who are ill, in order that
the drugging may be received, being drugged by the art with the more useful
things. For the change of the things honored in custom and by the long time is
not very easy. And what do I say? The first, having cut round the idols,
conceded the sacrifices; and the second, having taken away the sacrifices, did
not prevent the circumcision; then once they received the taking away, they
also conceded what had been conceded; the ones the sacrifices, and the others
the circumcision; and they have become, instead of nations, Jews; and instead
of these, Christians, having been stolen by the changes according to part upon
the gospel. Let Paul persuade you of this, having gone forward from
circumcising and purifying upon saying: "But I, brothers, if I proclaim
circumcision, why am I still persecuted?" That was of the economy, this of the
perfection.

26. To this I can liken the matter of the theology, except inasmuch as from
the opposites. For there the change is from the takings away; and here the
perfection is through the additions. For it is thus. The old proclaimed the
Father openly, the Son more dimly. The new made the Son manifest, indicated
the Godhead of the Spirit.

The Spirit now dwells as a citizen, furnishing us a clearer indication of
himself. For it was not safe, the Godhead of the Father not yet having been
confessed, for the Son to be proclaimed openly; nor, that of the Son not
having been received, for the Holy Spirit, that I may say something even more
daring, to be loaded on; lest, just as having been weighed down by food beyond
power, and having struck a still weaker sight against the sun's light, they
should be in danger even as to what is according to power; but by the
additions according to part, and, as David said, by goings up, and by advances
and progresses from glory into glory, the light of the Trinity may shine out
for the more brilliant. For this cause, I think, he also visits the disciples
according to part, being measured to the power of those receiving, at the
beginning of the gospel, after the passion, after the ascent, accomplishing
the powers, being breathed out, appearing in fiery tongues. And he is made
manifest by Jesus little by little, as you yourself will also attend, meeting
it more carefully: "I will ask the Father," he says, "and he will send you
another Paraclete, the Spirit of truth"; in order that he may not seem to be
some one against God, and to make the words as from some other authority.
Then, "He will send," but "in my name." Having passed over the "I will ask,"
he has kept the "He will send." Then, "I will send," the proper dignity; then,
"He will come," the authority of the Spirit.

27. You see illuminations lighting us according to part, and an order of
theology, which it is better for us also to keep, neither making manifest all
at once, nor hiding to the end. For the one is artless, and the other godless;
and the one is able to strike those who are others, and the other to make our
own other. And what perhaps has already come upon the mind of some of the
others also, but I suppose a fruit of my own thought, I will add to the things
said. There were some things for the Savior, even if they were being filled
with many teachings, which were said not to be able then to be borne by the
disciples, for the causes I perhaps said, and for this reason it was veiled;
and again that we would be taught all things by the Spirit having come to
dwell. Of these I think one is also the Godhead itself of the Spirit, being
made distinct later, as the knowledge then happening to be already ripe and
containable, after the restoration of the Savior, no longer being disbelieved
because of the wonder. For what greater than this would that one have
promised, or the Spirit have taught? If indeed one must think something great,
and worthy of the magnificence of God, that which is promised, or that which
is taught.

28. I am thus concerning these things, and may I be, and whoever is a friend
to me: to worship God the Father, God the Son, God the Holy Spirit, three
properties, one Godhead, not divided in glory, and honor, and essence, and
kingdom, as one of the God-bearing men a little before philosophized; or may
he not see the morning star rising, as the Scripture says, nor the glory of
the brightness from there, whoever is not thus, or is carried with the times,
becoming another at another time, and counseling unsoundly concerning the
greatest things. For if he is not even to be worshiped, how does he make me
God through the baptism? And if to be worshiped, how not to be revered? And if
to be revered, how not God? One hangs from the one, a golden chain indeed and
saving. And from the Spirit we have the regeneration; and from the
regeneration the refashioning; and from the refashioning the recognition of
the dignity of the one who refashioned.

29. These things then one would say having set down the unwritten; and now the
swarm of the testimonies will come to you also, from which, that it is even
exceedingly written, the Godhead of the Spirit will be shown to those not
exceedingly clumsy, nor alien from the Spirit. And consider thus: Christ is
born, he goes before; he is baptized, he bears witness; he is tempted, he
leads up; he accomplishes powers, he accompanies; he goes up, he succeeds. For
what of the great things can he not do, and of the things of which God can?
And what is he not named of the things of which God is, except unbegottenness
and generation? For the properties had to remain for Father and Son, in order
that there may not be confusion in the Godhead, which also leads the other
things into order and good arrangement. I at least shudder at the wealth

when I think of the names, and upon as many names as those who oppose the
Spirit are shameless. He is called Spirit of God, Spirit of Christ, mind of
Christ, Spirit of the Lord, himself Lord; Spirit of adoption, of truth, of
freedom; Spirit of wisdom, of understanding, of counsel, of strength, of
knowledge, of piety, of fear of God; for he is also productive of all these;
filling all things by the essence, holding all things together; filling of the
world according to the essence, uncontainable by the world according to the
power; good, straight, leading, by nature not by position; sanctifying, not
being sanctified, measuring, not being measured, being shared, not sharing,
filling, not being filled, holding together, not being held together; being
inherited, being glorified, being co-numbered, being threatened; finger of
God, fire as God, for indication, I think, of the consubstantial; the Spirit
that made, that recreates through baptism, through resurrection; the Spirit
that knows all things, that teaches, that blows where he wills and as much,
leading, speaking, sending, dividing, being provoked, being tempted;
revealing, lighting, making live, or rather self-light and life; making a
temple, making God, perfecting, so as both to go before the baptism, and to be
sought after the baptism; energizing as many things as God, being divided in
fiery tongues, distributing gifts, making apostles, prophets, evangelists,
shepherds, and teachers; intellectual, many-parted, clear, distinct,
unhindered, undefiled; which is equal in power to the most wise and
many-turned in the energies, and making all things clear, and making distinct,
and self-authoritative, and unchangeable; all-powerful, all-overseeing, going
through all intellectual spirits, pure, most fine, of angelic powers I think,
as also of prophetic and apostolic, at the same time, and not in the same
places. And others having been assigned elsewhere, by which the
uncircumscribed is shown.

30. Those who say and teach these things, and besides naming another
Paraclete, as another God, those who have known the blasphemy against him
alone as unforgivable, those who so fearfully posted Ananias and Sapphira,
since they lied to the Holy Spirit, as having lied to God, not to a human
being: what do these seem to you, whether to proclaim the Spirit God, or
something else? How exceedingly thick you are indeed, and far from the Spirit,
if you are at a loss about this, and need the one who will teach. The names
then are so many and so living. For why must I set beside you the testimonies
upon the sayings? And as many things as here too are said more lowly, the
being given, the being sent, the being divided, the gift, the present, the
inbreathing, the promise, the intercession, or some other such thing, in order
that I may not speak according to each, must be referred to the first cause,
in order that the from whom may be shown, and three principles divided may not
be received in a polytheistic way. For it is equal toward impiety both to join
Sabellianly, and to stand apart Arianly, the one by the person, and the other
by the natures.

31. For I at least, having considered many things with myself by the busyness
of the mind, and having straightened the account from every side, and seeking
some image of so great a matter, have not had with which of the things below
one must compare the divine nature. For even if some small likeness is found,
the more flees me, having left below with the example. I thought of some eye,
and spring, and river, and others too, lest the Father may analogously have to
the one, and the Son to the other, and the Holy Spirit to the other. For these
have neither stood apart in time, nor been torn from one another by the
continuity; even if they seem somehow to be cut by three properties. But I
feared, first to receive some flow of Godhead that does not have a standing;
and second lest the one in number be introduced through this comparison. For
eye, and spring, and river are one in number, being shaped differently.

32. Again I thought of sun, and ray, and light. But here too there is fear,
first lest some composition be thought of the uncompounded nature, as of sun
and of the things

in the sun; and second lest we make the Father substantial, and not give the
others subsistence, but make them powers of God existing in, not subsisting.
For neither ray, nor light, is another sun, but some solar outflows, and
essential qualities. And at the same time we would give to God in these both
the being and the not being, as far as from the example, which is even more
out of place than the things said. And I have heard of someone also sketching
such an account. Some solar gleam flashing upon a wall, and trembling round
from motion of waters, which the ray having taken through the air in the
middle, then having been held by the resistant, became a pulse and a paradox.
For it darts with many and dense motions, being not one more than many, nor
many more than one, by the speed of the coming together and of the standing
apart, fleeing before it is held by sight.

33. But not even this is possible for me to set down, for one thing, because
that which moved it is even very clear; and nothing is older than God, in
order that there may be something that has moved this one. For he himself is
cause of all, and he has no older cause. And second, because here too there is
the suspicion of the same things, of composition, of pouring, of an unstable
and not firm nature, of which nothing is to be thought concerning Godhead. And
altogether there is nothing that stops my thought as I contemplate what is
imagined upon the examples, unless someone, having taken one thing of the
image, from good disposition throws away the rest. In the end then it seemed
to me to be strongest to let the images farewell, and the shadows, as
deceptive and falling very far short of the truth, and myself holding to the
more pious thought, standing upon a few sayings, using the Spirit as a guide,
keeping to the end this illumination which I received from here, as a genuine
partner and companion, to go through this age cutting a way, and to persuade
the others as far as power to worship Father, and Son, and Holy Spirit, the
one Godhead and power; because to him is all glory, honor, might, unto the
ages of the ages; Amen.
