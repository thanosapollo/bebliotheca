** AUSONIUS TO THE READER GREETING
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1-h1
:END:

Ausonius is a father to us, I of the same name:
who I am, of what sect, stock, hearth and fatherland,
I have written, that you might know, good man, whoever you were,
and knowing me cultivate me with a remembering mind.
Vasates is the fatherland to the father, an Aeduan race to the mother
from the father, but the mother from Aquae Tarbellicae,
I myself born at Bordeaux: divided through four
ancient cities is the origin of my stock.
from here kinship is widely poured; names to many
from our house, as it has pleased, may come drawn:
derived to others, to us from the first stem
and not cognate, but native, please,
but I return to the series. The begetter studied medicine,
which one of the disciplines gave a god.

We turned study to grammar and soon
rhetoric too, which is enough, we touched.
nor were the forums not celebrated by me, but the care of teaching
more cultivated, and I earned the name of a grammarian
not indeed so great, by which our glory would go under
Aemilius or Scaurus or Berytian Probus,
but by which our countrymen, Aquitanian names, many
compared, but not subjected, I would look at.
And the fasti having been completed from there through three decades
I as doctor deserted the municipal work,
and ordered to go to the golden palaces of Augustus
I as grammarian taught the Augustan offspring,
soon also a rhetor, for we have not a vain confidence
or a glory of a judgment not solid,
yet I yield that there have been teachers of better fame,
while no one has had a better disciple.
Alcides of Atlas and Aeacides of Chiron,
almost this one sprung from Jove, that one a son of Jove,
had Thessaly and Thebes as their household gods:
but this mine of mine reigns in his whole world.
whose companion I and quaestor and, peak of honors,
prefect to the Gauls and Libya and Latium

and, having first obtained the fasces and the Latin curule,
I was consul, with a later colleague.
This then is Ausonius: but do you not despise, that of myself
I prepare you as a patron for our songs.

** AUSONIUS TO SYAGRIUS
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h2-h1
:END:

As you cultivate the seat of our breast, kindly Syagrius,
and inhabit a common Ausonius, another I:
so also you will be held as prefaced in our book,
that it differ by nothing, whether it be yours or mine.

** LETTER OF THEODOSIUS AUGUSTUS
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3-h1
:END:

**
Theodosius Augustus to Ausonius the parent greeting.

My love which is in you and admiration of your talent and erudition, which are
much the greatest, has made, most pleasant parent, that I set aside a custom
usual to other princes and transmit a familiar autograph speech to you, asking
by a right not indeed royal, but of that private affection among us, that you
not suffer me to be cheated of the reading of your writings, which once known to
me and now through time forgotten I desire again, not only that what are known
be recollected, but also that I receive those which are remembered as added by
celebrated fame, which you from the store of your scrinia, you who love me, will
willingly impart, having followed the examples of the best authors, to whom you
have merited to be equal: who to Octavian Augustus possessing things eagerly
handed over their works, composing many with no end in his honor. Who I hardly
know whether he admired them equally as I you, certainly he did not love more,
farewell parent.

** TO MY LORD AND OF ALL THEODOSIUS AUGUSTUS YOUR AUSONIUS
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4-h1
:END:

If yellow Ceres should order a farmer to give seeds to the earth,
if Gradivus should order a leader to take arms,
Neptune to loose an unarmed fleet from port:
it is as lawful to trust as it is unlawful to doubt,
although the sea winter insanely and the earth be raw
for seeds, and the hand not enough apt for war,
doubt nothing with a good author. Mortal things seek
counsel, take certain the commands of a god.
Augustus orders me to write and demands my songs
almost asking: a bland force lies in the command.
I have not talent, but Caesar has ordered: I will have.
why should I deny I can, what he thinks I can?
He himself stirs invalid forces and the same helps,
who orders: it suffices that my obedience be.

It is not safe to have refused a god. Often a delay of shame
has been praised, as often as you doubt against an equal.
Yes even songs not ordered prepare to burst forth already,
who would not wish a book to be of Caesar,
lest he bear an unworthy poet and a hundred erasures,
always to be changed with a worse mark?
Only remember, Roman father, that you have ordered
and in my faults give yourself pardon.
