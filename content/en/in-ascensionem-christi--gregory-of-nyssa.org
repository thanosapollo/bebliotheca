* On the Ascension of Christ
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

OF GREGORY, BISHOP OF NYSSA, ON THAT WHICH IS CALLED, BY THE LOCAL
CUSTOM OF THE CAPPADOCIANS, THE EPISOZOMENE, WHICH IS THE ASCENSION OF
OUR LORD JESUS CHRIST
How sweet a fellow-traveler of human life is the prophet David, found in all
the ways of life and fittingly mixed with every spiritual age, and laying hold
together with every rank of those who advance: he plays with those who are
infants according to God, he contends with the men, he trains the youth, he
supports old age, he becomes all things to all: a weapon of soldiers, a
trainer of athletes, a wrestling-ground of those who exercise, a crown of
those who conquer, gladness at the table, consolation at the funeral. There is
nothing in our life that does not share in this grace. What power of prayer is
there in which David does not take part? What gladness of a feast is there
which the prophet does not brighten? Which indeed one may see even now, that
the feast which was already great for us the prophet has made greater through
himself, bringing in from the psalms a gladness fitted to the subject. For in
the one psalm he bids you become a sheep shepherded by God and lacking none of
the good things, for whom grass of pasture and water of rest and food and a
tent and =9.324= a path and guidance and all things the good Shepherd becomes,
distributing his own grace fittingly toward every need. Through all of which
he trains the Church, that you must first become a sheep of the good Shepherd,
being led through good catechesis toward the divine pastures and springs of
the teachings, so as to be buried with him through baptism into death, and not
to fear such a death: for this is not death, but a shadow and imprint of
death.
For if I walk, he says, in the midst of the shadow of death, I will not fear
what happens as an evil, because you are with me. After these things, having
comforted with the staff of the Spirit (for the Paraclete is the Spirit), he
sets forth the mystical table prepared over against the table of the demons:
for those were they who through idolatry were crushing the life of men; over
against whom is the table of the Spirit. Then he anoints the head with the oil
of the Spirit, and having set before him the wine that gladdens the heart, he
produces in the soul that sober drunkenness, having set the thoughts from the
temporal toward the eternal: for he who has tasted such drunkenness exchanges
the short-lived for the unending, stretching out his sojourn in the house of
God unto length of days. Having granted us such things in the one of the
psalms, he raises the soul toward a greater and more perfect gladness in the
next psalm, and, if it seems good, let us also set before you the sense of
this one, cutting it short in a few words: The earth is the Lord's and the
=9.325= fullness thereof.
Why then are you astonished, O man, if our God appeared upon the earth and
consorted with men? For the earth is his creature, because it is also his
work. Therefore it is nothing new nor outside what is fitting that the Master
should come into his own: for he does not come to be in an alien world, but in
the one which he himself established, who founded the earth upon the seas and
prepared it to be suitable for the passage of the rivers. For what then is the
presence? That, having cast you out of the pits of sin, he may lead you upon
the mountain of the kingdom, using as a vehicle toward the ascent a way of
life according to virtue: for it is not possible to go up upon that mountain,
unless one travel together with the virtues, having become innocent in the
hands and unsoiled by any wicked deed, and pure in heart, bearing his own soul
toward nothing vain, nor preparing any deceit for his neighbor. The prize of
this ascent is the blessing; to this one the Lord gives the mercy laid up.
This is the generation of those who seek him, of those who through virtue go
up toward height and Seek the face of the God of Jacob. But what follows of
the psalmody is perhaps even higher than the evangelical teaching itself: for
the Gospel related the Lord's sojourn and converse upon earth, but this lofty
prophet, having gone out from himself, so that he might not be weighed down by
the trailing-boat of the body, and having mixed himself with the supermundane
powers, recounts to us their voices, when, escorting the Master at the
descent, they command the entrances of the earthly angels entrusted with human
life to be lifted up, saying: =9.326= Lift up gates, you rulers of yours, and
be lifted up, everlasting gates, and the King of glory shall enter.
And since he who contains the all in himself makes himself commensurate with
whatever he comes to be in (for not only among men does he become man, but,
following this throughout, even when he comes to be among angels he
condescends himself toward their nature), for this reason the gatekeepers need
one who shows: Who is this King of glory? For which cause they point out to
them the strong and mighty in war, him who is about to wrestle with the one
who took human nature captive and to destroy him who has the power of death,
so that when the last enemy has been made to vanish, the human race may be
called back into freedom and peace. Again he goes through the like voices (for
the mystery of death has already been fulfilled, and the victory against the
enemies has been accomplished, and the trophy against them, the cross, has
been raised, and again He went up on high leading captivity captive, he who
gave life and the kingdom, these good gifts to men) and the gates that lie
above must again be opened to him. Our guardians take over the escort in turn,
and they bid the gates that lie above to be opened to him, so that he may
again be glorified in them; but he is unrecognized who has put on the filthy
garment of our life, whose redness of garments is from the winepress of human
evils.
For this reason this questioning voice comes from those toward those
escorting: Who is this King of glory? Then the answer of these is no longer:
the Strong and mighty in war, but: The Lord =9.327= of hosts, who has laid
hold of the power of the all, who has recapitulated all things in himself, who
holds first place in all things, who has restored all things to the first
creation: He is the King of glory. You see how David has made the feast
sweeter for us, mixing his own grace into the brightness of the Church.
Therefore let us also imitate the prophet, in those things in which it is
possible to accomplish the imitation, in love toward God, in meekness of life,
in long-suffering toward those who hate, so that the prophet's teaching may
become a guiding of the way of life according to God, in Christ Jesus our
Lord, to whom be the glory unto the ages of ages, amen.
