OF THE SAME SAINT CYRIL ARCHBISHOP OF ALEXANDRIA
SOME FEW FRAGMENTS ON THE SECOND EPISTLE TO THE CORINTHIANS
VOLUME I
DISCOURSE I.
Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ through the will of God, and Timothy the
brother. The most wise apostle Paul, having been appointed to the ministry
that is upon Christ through the will of God, says that he has been brought
forward, thrusting aside as impious the glory of the false prophets and false
apostles, speaking in the Spirit, and having Jesus himself dwelling in his own
soul; so as to say in one place "And I think that I also have the Spirit of
God," and in another "If you seek a proof of Christ speaking in me." He says
that he is his servant, even though God is the one who has authority over all
things and is the only ruler; for it has been said to him "That all things are
your servants"; how then does he not clearly assign to him a glory of lordship
both natural and truly most God-befitting? 321 From which, calling himself an
apostle of Jesus, he proclaims Jesus God by nature and truly; for no one would
be an apostle of men but rather of God who is according to nature; and he
shows that he was not called to the apostleship contrary to the mind of the
Father, nor does he speak from the heart as the false seers do, but he
ministers the oracles of God. And he makes Timothy a partner of the epistle,
as approved and following in his footsteps, in order that he may persuade more
swiftly through the agreement, since the law says "At the mouth of two
witnesses and three shall every word be established." To the church of God
that is in Corinth. They write to the church of God that is in Corinth,
although the Church is said to be of Christ; for he presented her to himself a
pure virgin; if then the Church is of God, and Christ is the one who presented
her to himself, then God by nature is he who is from the holy and theotokos
Mary. Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ. If
he knew the Son not to be God by nature, how was it not rather necessary to
say Grace to you and peace from God and Father alone? But now, receiving
Christ in together and setting him as co-giver of whatever the divine nature
would grant, he would clearly not be ignorant that he is God by nature; 322
necessarily therefore we shall receive the Son in together with God and Father
into the account of identity and consubstantiality, in order that one God also
may be understood as the supplier of the spiritual goods. And naming Christ
Jesus, he indicates the Word from God who has appeared in flesh; and if he is
supplier of whatever even the Father himself would supply, how is Christ not
God by nature, understood according to an economic union with his own flesh?
Thus also the most wise Paul says somewhere concerning those from Israel
"Whose are the fathers, and from whom is the Christ according to the flesh,
who is over all God blessed unto the ages, amen."

DISCOURSE II.


God is faithful, that our word toward you was not yes and no. No one says
"Lord Jesus except in the Holy Spirit"; and the spirit is truth, according to
what is written; having then their mind full of truth, that is of the Holy
Spirit, the heralds of the evangelical oracles would not be understood as
speaking falsehood; and whatever they would say concerning Christ, these
things are altogether right and firmly established; and Christ himself will
confirm it saying "For it is not you who speak, but the spirit of your Father
that speaks in you." Therefore he does not say this as toward those who
attempt to slander him as saying things contrary to himself, because at one
time he proclaims Jesus as God by nature, and at another also as man.
Necessarily therefore, leaping as it were upon their unfairnesses, that his
word has not become twofold and unclear, but rather simple and true, he fully
as 323 sures saying Our word toward you was not yes and no; that is, not
fighting against itself and sick with inconsistency, at one time praising
this, at another blaming, but speaking truth in the preaching; God then is
faithful, that is, without falsehood and true. The notables of the Greeks will
hold various opinions concerning the gods supposed among them, saying yes and
no; for among them there is not the truth but rather the falsehood; wherefore
they are also tossed by various opinions; but the Son who is truly and by
nature of the God who is able to do all things, how would he become yes and
no? .... And necessarily also the Son from him by nature who was preached
among you through us, through me and Silvanus and Timothy, was not yes and no;
for every word concerning him has become true through the voice of those who
speak of God. And as many as are the promises of God, he says, in him is the
yes, that is, we have received in him from God and Father every munificence
and promise; and this in the Yes, that is in truth; for these things have come
to fulfillment through Christ; "for death was swallowed up unto victory," and
the power of corruption was shaken down, and the feebleness will cease and
besides this the dishonor; as also the most wise Paul said even in the former
epistle; and we have been shown to be heirs and partakers of the very kingdom
of the saints; for he himself in whom is the yes will say to us at the divine
tribunal "Come, you blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you
from the foundation of the world." For we are household servants as far as it
pertains to nature; but from slaves we were called to a free dignity through
him, from corruption to incorruption, we were delivered from serving the
devil. Every promise 324 of God then through Christ alone has the yes; and the
no is altogether unacceptable; which is the denial; for he himself is the
truth. And he further shows this also in the singular that he is God by
nature; for if he would not prove false the promises, but rather will bring
them to completion for the saints, although God and Father is the one who
distributes the goods, how would he not be by nature and truly Son, and as God
from God, he in whom "every good giving and every perfect gift" is not
falsely? And that the word is without falsehood, he further confirms saying
Wherefore also through him the amen to God for glory through us; for since he
himself is the mediator, through whom and through whom alone the Father is
accessible, he in whom is every spiritual gift, through him altogether also
every end of prayer will be; and this is the amen; and toward this the Son
himself has trained us saying to the holy apostles "And in that day you will
ask me nothing; amen amen I say to you, if you ask anything of the Father, he
will give it to you in my name; until now you have asked nothing in my name;
ask and you will receive, that your joy may be fulfilled." Through him then
the amen to God the Father for


glory through us. For it is our custom to close the end of every prayer in the
name of Christ. And that he himself would not be without a share of the glory
that is fitting, we shall easily see from the blessed Jacob prophesying
"Judah, let your brothers praise you"; for Christ sprang up from the tribe of
Judah according to the flesh; Isaiah the prophet indeed saying "I saw the Lord
sabaoth sitting upon a throne high and 325 lifted up," and the seraphim
standing around, calling him holy, and naming him lord sabaoth, and saying
that heaven and earth are full of his glory. And that he has seen the Son
himself, the wise John will show saying "These things Isaiah said because he
saw his glory and spoke concerning him." Therefore even if the Father would be
glorified through him, and since he has been set "a propitiation through
faith," and has become also "a minister of the saints" because of the human,
he offers as a spiritual sacrifice the prayer of each of the saints; for
through him is our amen; and he would not be understood as outside of needing
also to be worshipped and glorified together with the one who begot him by us
ourselves and by the upper multitude of the holy spirits. And the divine Paul
said somewhere "And when he again brings the firstborn into the world", he
says And let all angels of God worship him; and yet to whom of those that are
is it not clear that he is Only-Begotten according as he would be understood
as God? For he alone has been begotten from the Father alone; but since having
let himself down among the things that are ours he has become among many
brothers, then he himself has been set as first; and besides being
Only-Begotten as God, he has also been called firstborn because of the human.
And he who confirms us with you unto Christ etc. God and Father confirms us
unto Christ, having established a right and unshaken faith in the souls of
all, persuading them to think that he is God by nature and in truth, even if
he should be seen in the form that is ours, having been born according to the
flesh from a woman, he who is above all creation; and indeed our Lord Jesus
Christ himself, when Peter confessed the faith, and said clearly "You are the
Christ the Son of the living God," answered saying "Blessed are you, Simon 326
Bar Jonah, for flesh and blood has not revealed it to you, but my Father who
is in the heavens." For since the mystery is exceedingly great, it would
reasonably need a mystagogy from above and from the Father; therefore he
himself confirms us unto Christ, who also sealed us and anointed us, God, and
gave the earnest of the Spirit. And it would not be unclear even through these
things to us, that the Son has not become "yes and no"; but rather true God,
and upon all the goods "in him is the yes"; for when God is said to have
sealed us and anointed us, and to have given the earnest of the Spirit, Christ
would again be the one who fulfills these things in us, not ministerially, nor
indeed anointing and sealing us with an alien Spirit, but with his own and
that of the Father; for in both is the Holy Spirit through the identity of the
substance, not dividedly, but as proceeding from the Father through the Son
upon the creation; for he breathed upon the holy apostles saying "Receive the
Holy Spirit"; and through him and in him we have been sealed unto the divine
and intelligible image; for the divine apostle himself said writing to the
Galatians "My little children, of whom I am again in travail, until Christ be
formed in you"; and if being formed unto Christ, we are rich in the divine
imaging in ourselves, he himself then is the image of God and Father and the
accurate likeness, and not as we who have been called to likeness according to
a participation of sanctification, but rather by nature and essentially; for
God who is by nature toward the one who is by nature and truly God and who has
been begotten from his substance, would have nothing unlike; and that he
himself also has been sealed by God and Father, John the most wise says; "For
he who has received his witness has sealed that God is 327 true"; and he has
been sealed again not in the equal manner according


to which we also, but the Father as it were writing his whole self in the
nature of the Son and as it were marking himself essentially; wherefore he
said "He who has seen me has seen the Father." But thanks be to God who always
leads us in triumph in Chri st and manifests the odor of his knowledge through
us in every place. The apostles give thanks when they are tested and toil and
they boast in the contests that are through Christ; for just as Christ has
been led in triumph for us having endured the death upon the wood, and having
been perfected through sufferings, so they also say they are led in triumph
for him, that is, to be made known to those everywhere and to be made
illustrious through trials and to conquer the world, through being ready to
suffer all things and very gladly for the name of Christ; for they are, they
are partakers of his sufferings, as he says, "and of the glory that is going
to be revealed" they are sharers; and they say that it is God who leads them
in triumph, not as putting them into suffering nor indeed as heaping
afflictions, but that according to what seems good to him they who proclaim
Jesus throughout all that is under heaven fall into the trials for him; for
without trials it is impossible to be well-approved; and the fruit of these is
that they suffer in Christ, that is because of Christ, which is glorious and
full of good hopes. And what sort of thing then is the odor of the knowledge
of God and Father, which is manifested to the world through the holy apostles
and in 328 every place, as they themselves say, the divine Paul himself will
teach again saying that "For we do not preach ourselves, but Christ Jesus the
Lord, and ourselves your servants through Jesus Christ"; and again "For I
decided to know nothing among you, except Jesus Christ, and him crucified."
Then in what manner will he who was born from a woman, who endured a cross and
went down under death, even if he lived again, be an odor of the knowledge of
God and Father, if as some think Christ is understood as a mere man like us,
or God-bearing, and not himself being God by nature, even if the Word from God
should be understood as having taken on the human beforehand economically? For
it is not in being as we are and beyond nothing that he will make fragrant the
nature of God and Father, nor would he who endured death become an odor of him
who does not know death; how then is Christ the odor of the knowledge of the
Father? Or is it clear that insofar as he is understood and is God, even if he
appeared in flesh for us? Since in what manner do those who proclaim Christ
announce to the world him who is God by nature and truly? Or how would they
know Jesus? And in what manner is God and Father said by the holy mystagogues
to be reconciling the world to himself in Christ, unless one should receive in
together with the Word sprung from God the human unto union, the wise economy
requiring this? For the divine disciples announce speaking in spirit, not as
the Word of God having dwelt in a man, but as having become flesh, that is
having been united to flesh having a rational soul; for thus the cruci 329
fied one would also be lord of the glory; therefore even if he should be
understood with flesh, even if not this, but as it were alone and not yet
having become as we are, the Word from God is an odor of the knowledge of God
and Father, as making fragrant unto us in his own nature him from whom he is.
And if what I say is true, how would he be generated and numbered with those
who have obtained generation from things that are not? For there would not
become an odor of that which is not created, nor of that which exists
generatedly; for we shall not see the generated in the ungenerated, nor the
ungenerated in the generated. But indeed the Son has shown the Father in
himself; he is then God by nature through the sameness of the substance; let
the Son then hear also from us "Your name is ointment poured out"; for we have
known through him and in him the Father and God from whom he has been
begotten. That we are a fragrance of Christ to God among those who are being
saved and among those who are perishing.


They make fragrant to God and Father our Lord Jesus Christ, first as having
him through the Spirit dwelling and lodging within, then that proclaiming and
carrying about everywhere the word concerning him, as some of the most
sweet-smelling incenses, they offered to God the zeal in this; and in a third
manner, that having also become conformed to him, they have been perfected
through equal sufferings. They say indeed that they themselves are a fragrance
of Christ among those who are being saved and among those who are perishing,
to some an odor from 330 death unto death, to others an odor from life unto
life, it is consistent to see. Therefore those being saved and those perishing
would reasonably be understood by us, according to the aim fitting to the
contemplations, as those who believe and those who thrust aside the faith, and
those who have not yet known God who is by nature and truly, but have rather
worshipped the creation rather than the creator. These, not receiving the word
of the divine preaching, but rather mocking and having thought the mystery of
Christ to be foolishness, receive "the odor of the knowledge" of the Father,
from death unto death; for being in death because of the ignorance and
hardness that is in them, they would be caught as it were stumbling also upon
a second death; those indeed who through the legal instruction have been
brought to the beginnings of the knowledge of God, have become somehow in
life, yet if they should believe they will receive the odor of the knowledge
of the Father from life unto life; for Christ says somewhere to the Father and
God in the heavens " This is eternal life, that they may know you the only
true God, and Jesus Christ whom you sent." But who would be sufficient for
these things, he says? And very reasonably; for truly hard to find is he in
whom would exist the power to speak rightly and without error the mystery of
Christ, of which sort were the divine disciples, also correcting the
perversities of those of other mind, with whom it is custom and it has been
set through very great zeal as it were to hawk the word of God, and always to
love to make the rendering of the faith mixed with deceit and falsehood; for
it is not possible among them to find the truth unmixed as it were and
unadulterated; but the divine mystagogues speaking in the Spirit, as from
sincerity before God do and say all things. 331 You are our epistle written in
our hearts. We do not need the witness that is through words, for you the
disciples, being well-approved in the very deeds and having been shown
faithful of Christ, crown us your teachers and have shown us well-approved.
Therefore you are our epistle written in our hearts, that is always and
continually lying in the memory that is with us; for we are always proud, he
says, in you. Not on tablets of stone, but on tablets of hearts of flesh. He
seals also with prophetic witness the Not on tablets of stone but on tablets
of hearts of flesh; for God said somewhere, he says, through the voice of a
prophet "Behold days are coming, says the Lord, and I will make with the house
of Israel and with the house of Judah a new covenant, not according to the
covenant which I made with their fathers in the day when I took hold of their
hand to bring them out of the land of Egypt, because they did not remain in my
covenant, and I neglected them, says the Lord; giving my laws into their mind,
and upon their hearts I will write them, because this is the covenant which I
will make with the house of Israel after those days, says the Lord"; for of
old the divine Moses received a law that was the pedagogue on tablets of
stone, as it is written; and we say that this has become a riddle of the
hard-heartedness of the Jews; for rather he shows the law as it were
unacceptable to their own minds, and as it were in stones having settled
briefly and scarcely, yet altogether easy to throw off and very readily spat
332 out. And indeed God said somewhere


"This people draws near to me with their mouth, and they honor me with their
lips, but their heart is far from me; and they worship me in vain teaching
teachings, commandments of men." But our Lord Jesus Christ on tablets of
hearts of flesh, that is sensitive, writes in us the sacred and divine laws;
therefore he also said through the voice of Ezekiel the prophet "And I will
pluck out their heart of stone from their flesh and I will give them a heart
of flesh, that they may walk in my commandments." If then anyone has not
leaned upon the shadows through Moses, nor indeed has the law written with ink
as on tablets of stone, but into the inner mind through the Spirit, this one
is an epistle of Christ known by all men.

VOLUME II.
DISCOURSE I.
And we have such confidence through Christ toward God etc. KNOWING, Paul does
not rather ascribe the dignity of the apostleship to human powers but to the
God who is able to do all things and who stretches it out to those worthy of
receiving through the mediator Christ. Therefore he says all the goods to the
saints are in Christ; for "every perfect gift is from above and from the
father of lights"; but nevertheless the one through whom are all things and in
whom are all things is Christ, mediating us and God because of the economy,
yet working all things from God and Father, inasmuch as he also is his very
wisdom and power; and what is the confidence that is through Christ toward
God, hear him adding and saying Not that we are sufficient of ourselves to
reckon anything as from ourselves; "for all wisdom is from the Lord,"
according to what is written; but our sufficiency is from God who also made us
sufficient as ministers of a new covenant, not of letter but of spirit; for
the letter kills, but the spirit makes alive. And what is the letter? The 334
type in the law, the things still fulfilled as in shadows; and spirit, the
power of the worship in Christ, showing those who approach it through faith to
be spiritual; and by how much the type is less than the truth, by so much I
think those who minister it are behind those who perform the truth; for Christ
sprinkles us with his own blood, and not unto "the purity of the flesh" alone,
as indeed also the things in the law, but rather he cleanses also "our
conscience from dead works," and sets us outside of transgression; and besides
this he also grants a hope of life; for it is true that "he who believes in
the Son has eternal life"; for he has also promised the resurrection of the
bodies themselves and the ascent unto incorruption and unto a long-lasting
life. For even that which has been glorified has not been glorified in this
part, on account of the surpassing glory. He refers again to the incomparably
superior dignity of the divine apostleship and having made a comparison of the
ministries and having brought forward very well the property of each he shows
the one in Christ through spirit more transparent. And the aim of the thought
comes to him through these things, yet the saying has been phrased not very
clearly; for it seems somehow not very easy to see nor ready for those who
wish to see. What 335 then is the It has not been glorified that which has
been glorified and the rest? And it is something of this sort that he wishes
to indicate. For Moses has been glorified, he says, as minister of a law that
is being done away, but the glory of the new preaching and that in Christ,
conquering the first, has shown it such

that it seems not even to have been glorified at all. For in the same manner
as while night still remains and the darkness according to it blackens this
whole, the circle of the moon flashes as it were, and the choir of the stars
has its own glory; yet when the sun rises and unfolds the glory of its own
radiance, the light of the moon is worsted by that which is superior, and it
seems somehow not even to have been illuminated; according to the same manner
I think you will receive the sense of the things set forth. For that which has
been glorified, he says, that is the ministry of the law, although it has
obtained the glory from God, seems somehow not to have been glorified at all
on account of the surpassing glory, that clearly given to those who minister
the divine and evangelical preaching; for the lesser somehow always yields to
that which is superior. For if that which is being done away is through glory,
much more that which remains, in glory. What is that which is being done away?
The shadow and the law or the type, and what is that which remains? The
oracles of God. Therefore the shadow also was in glory, but the things of
Christ in a more transparent glory; for it remains if it will prevail over
that under heaven. Having then such a hope we use much boldness. What hope?
Being made sufficient by God and being equipped through the grace unto being
"ministers of a new covenant not of letter." And it befits such to speak in
much boldness, neither being ashamed of the feebleness of the types, 336 nor
of the unwisdom of the hearers; for the Holy Spirit having been established in
the souls of the faithful, makes them understand easily even the deep words.
And not as Moses used to put a veil upon his own face. In this again the
disciples of Christ are more transparent in glory, because the word of the
preaching has become for them clear and unconcealed and having nothing
difficult as that through Moses. For since the sons of Israel were unapproved,
having made a calf after so many wonders, Moses used to put a mask upon
himself and speaking the divine law to them used coverings, teaching through
riddles that they would not be able to see the true face of the law, that is
Christ, but they strike as it were upon a second face scarcely, the shadow
having no glory, which also is done away, the shadow and the type. For it has
ceased when a new covenant has been brought in, and the end of the law being
done away is Christ. Therefore the hidden glory of Moses, Christ, was the end
of the law being done away; for if one should take away the shadow and as it
were strip off the apparent and second face that is as in letter, he will see
Emmanuel being formed in many ways upon each, and enduring the slaughter for
the world and saving that under heaven, in order that he may also be glorified
by us and worshipped by the holy angels. Inaccessible then to the minds of the
Jews is the light of the glory of the Savior. For since every spiritual
contemplation looks toward the mystery of Christ, and the wretched Jews do not
receive the faith unto him, the veil has remained upon them, and it is done
away in Christ. 337 But whenever it shall turn to the Lord, the veil is taken
away. The Lord himself is the light and he reveals "the deep and hidden
things," and he shows the true face of the law that has been glorified to the
wise who have been sanctified in spirit and have been made illustrious through
the faith unto Christ. For the law trains unto Christ, and he who has been
appointed to train unto some other, does not set those being trained upon
themselves, but rather carries them off unto something of the better and
greater things than according to himself. Whence also the divine Paul says he
has considered the things in the law dung "because of the surpassing of the
knowledge of Christ." And the Lord is the Spirit, and where the Spirit of the
Lord is, there is freedom. It is necessary then to become spiritual, for it is
not otherwise possible to contemplate Christ, who is "the end of that which is
being done away," I mean of the law. Then altogether


and in every way "the veil is taken away," and with a bare and unveiled face
they will see the sacred Moses not having the mask from the letter or the
overshadowing from the apparent history. For among these are those who in
Christ have richly received the free revelation of the Spirit. And if wherever
the Spirit is, there altogether also is the freedom, how is it not free by
nature? And if this, it has also been removed from the measures of slavery,
and is no longer generated, and would reasonably be ranked with the highest
and free nature, to which belongs the power over all; for it is Spirit of the
Father proceeding from him upon the creation in Christ. 338 But we all with
unveiled face beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord are transformed
into the same image from glory to glory, as from the Lord spirit. For Moses
used to put a veil upon himself because the sons of Israel were not able to
behold "the glory of his face"; for they were soulish and fleshly and
incomplete unto faith; but we the faithful having the heart illuminated
through the torch-bearing of the Spirit and the eye of the mind knowing how to
look purely have beheld the Lord with unveiled face; for he has spoken to us
and has been seen in glory and in a most God-befitting superiority; for we
have known him as God, the impress of the hypostasis of the Father. As in a
mirror then we contemplate the divine and ineffable nature, for the mind of
those who believe is as it were recast from legal learning unto evangelical
instruction, and is transformed in a certain manner unto the spiritual
knowledge, not enduring shadow or types, but having chosen to pass from the
glory according to the law unto another glory that is in Christ through
Spirit. Therefore we are transformed into the same image; for transferring the
types unto truth, and from the shadow according to the law forming the mystery
of Christ by the thinness of the thoughts, we shall find as in a form the same
glory; for if glory has been given to those who minister the law, greater and
incomparably superior to the first is that of the holy priests, who also have
been made sufficient by God as ministers "of a new covenant not of letter but
of spirit." It is quite clear then and not unclear to any of those that are,
that those who are transferred from legal instruction unto evangelical
accuracy, 339 going as it were from the first glory unto another glory would
be understood, and as it were being recast toward the better things. And that
which transfers them unto this is the Spirit, which frees from the slavery
according to the law; for in it "we cry Abba the father" as sons and free. And
it is not otherwise possible to see the depth of the thin speech according to
the law, except through the Holy Spirit alone; for it itself is also that
which searches "even the depths of God." And depths of God, we say the
knowledge stored up in the sacred letters has been named; wherefore also the
divine David used to make his prayers to God saying thus "Uncover my eyes and
I shall perceive your wonders from your law"; and the Savior himself also made
not unadmirable him who rises from legal knowledge unto spiritual, that is the
evangelical; for he said that "For this reason I say to you that every scribe
who has been discipled to the kingdom of the heavens, is like a rich man who
brings forth from his treasure new and old." For old, the things in the law;
new indeed, the things in Christ. And if someone should wish the
transformation of our same image from glory to glory to be understood also
according to another manner, he will receive something of this sort into mind,
that as many as have known Christ, and have also been numbered among children
of God, we are altogether and in every way in glory; and at the time of the
resurrection we are transformed into the same image from glory to glory as
from lord spirit; for Christ himself, who has granted us "the earnest of the


Spirit," will add what is lacking at that time, and he will also transform
"the body of our humiliation conformed to the body of his glory." Since the
mystagogue says As from lord spirit, and "And where the spirit of the Lord is,
freedom," we shall not endure the slander of the unholy heretics, to make the
Spirit numbered with the generated; for if wherever the Spirit is, there
altogether also is the freedom, how is it not free by nature? And if this, it
also lies above the measures of slavery; for it is Spirit of the Father
proceeding from him upon the creation in Christ. Unto their not shining the
illumination of the gospel of the glory of Christ, who is the image of God.
Since the sacred preaching shines forth like a sun and every faithful with
unveiled face beholds as in a mirror "the glory of the Lord," no one of those
who think rightly would ascribe the ignorance of some to the divine preaching.
For what if, it being possible to know, some having a mind hardened from
diabolic deceit shake off the knowledge? But those better than their
perversity, wise and more sensible, have become rich in the knowledge. For if
