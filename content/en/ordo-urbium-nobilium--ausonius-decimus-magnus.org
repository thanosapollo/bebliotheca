FIRST AMONG CITIES, HOME OF THE GODS, GOLDEN ROME.

To CONSTANTINOPLE, which is prior, Carthage rises,
not about to yield by a whole step, because she scorns to be
called third, not having dared to hope for the second place,
which belonged to both. Ancient wealth prefers this one,
recent fortune this other; this one was, that one comes up, and
excelling in new merits she dims the old honor
and forces Elissa to yield to Constantine.
Carthage accuses the gods, already full of shame,
even now if she should yield, having scarcely endured Rome as
prior.
Let ancient fortune compose your swellings.
Go as equals, at last remembering that by the will of the gods
you changed narrow resources and names: you, when
you were Byzantine Lygos, you were Punic Byrsa.

THIRD is Antioch, house of Phoebus' laurel,
if Alexander's colony would wish to be set as fourth:
the place of both is one. And the frenzy of ambition
drives these too into a contest of vices: both turbulent in the
crowd and unsound with the tumult of a mad people.
This one boasts because she is fortified by the Nile and nested
in places set far within, fertile and safe;
that one, because she is set as rival against the faithless
Persians.
And you too go as equals and raise high the Macedonian name.
Great Alexander founded you; that one names Seleucus,
whose birth-token was an anchor,
such as is wont to be branded, a sure mark of the race; for
through the whole line of offspring the native image ran.

WARLIKE Gaul has long been eager to be celebrated,
and the throne of the TREVERAN city, which, nearest the Rhine,
rests secure as in the lap of midmost peace,
because she feeds, clothes, and arms the forces of the empire.
Broad walls run forth along the stretched-out hill:
the generous Moselle glides past in a tranquil stream,
bearing the far-off commerce of every kind of land.

AND AT MILAN all things are wonderful, abundance of goods,
countless and cultivated houses, eloquent minds of men
and cheerful manners; then with a double wall
the appearance of the place is enlarged, and the people's
pleasure, the circus and the wedged mass of the enclosed
theatre;
temples and Palatine citadels and a rich mint
and a quarter famous under the honor of Hercules' baths;
and all the peristyles adorned with marble statues
and walls girt about with a rim in the form of a rampart:
all which things excel as if rivals of great forms of works:
nor does the joined neighborhood of Rome press her down.

NOR shall I be silent of CAPUA, pol, powerful in tillage of
the field and in stores,
in delights, wealth, and former fame,
fortune changing the turns, who, relying on successes,
did not know how to keep measure, now subject to Rome
¦rival, now mindful of faith; before unfaithful, doubting
whether she should scorn the senate or cultivate it, having
dared to hope for curule offices under Campanian auspices and
with one consul of her own, that she might raise the empire of
a divided world.

Nay even she sought in war the mistress of things and mother of
Latium, not relying on toga'd leaders.
Sworn to Hannibal's arms and deceived, mad, she passed into the
slavery of an enemy under the appearance of a mistress.
Soon, as, driven toward their fall by common vices,
the Poeni collapsed in luxury, Campania in pride
(alas, pride that never obtained a stable seat!),
that one, powerful and strong in wealth, once another Rome,
who could dress her peaks with equal cones,
thrown back, scarcely almost holds the eighth place.

THIS was not that place: yet, increased by recent desert,
ninth among famous cities you shall be celebrated, Aquileia,
an Italian colony thrown against the Illyrian mountains,
most famous for walls and harbor, but more that stands out,
that in the last time he chose you, for whom, the lustrum
completed, Maximus would pay late atonements, once a sutler
under the name of an armor-bearer.
Happy you, who as a glad spectator of so great a triumph
punished the Rutupine robber with Ausonian Mars.

OPEN, double Arles, your harbors, gracious hostess,
Gallic Rome, Arles, whom Martian Narbo and whom
wealthy Vienne with Alpine colonists neighbors,
so cut through by the rushing streams of the Rhone
that you make a mid street with a ship-bridge,
through which you receive the commerce of the Roman world
and do not keep it back, and you enrich other peoples and
walls, which Gaul enjoys and Aquitaine in her broad lap.

DEAR to me, after these you shall be recalled, Iberian name,
Hispalis, whom a sea-like river glides past,
to whom all Spain submits her fasces.
Corduba does not, nor Tarraco powerful in her citadel, contend
with you, nor wealthy BRACARA which flaunts herself in a fold
of the sea.

NOW let us also recall Athens of earth-born fathers,
once the citadel of the contest of Pallas and Consus,

to whom first the peace-bringing olive tree fell,
whose is the unmixed glory of the eloquent Attic tongue,
whence through the peoples of Ionia and the Achaean name
the Greek band, turned, poured itself into a hundred cities.

WHO would pass over CATINA in silence? Who fourfold Syracuse?
This one famous for the piety of the burned brothers,
that one embracing the wonders of a spring and a river,
where, making their way beneath the salt shallows of the Ionian
sea, sweet waters join in a seat that pleased them,
mingling the kisses of uncorrupted waters.

NEVER shall I keep silent our nurse Toulouse,
whom a huge circuit surrounds with baked-brick walls
and along her side the Garonne glides past in a fair stream,
cultivated by countless peoples, because of the neighboring
snowy Pyrenees and the piney Cevennes,
between the Aquitanian nations and the Iberian name,
who, though she has just poured forth fourfold cities from
herself, feels no losses of an exhausted people,
having embraced in her lap all the colonists she has begotten.

NOR shall you, Martian Narbo, be passed in silence, by whose
name the Province, once spread through an immense realm,
held many colonists by right of ruling,
where the Allobroges insinuate themselves into the Greek shores
and Alpine peaks shut out the Italian borders,
where the Iberians are parted by Pyrenean snows,
where the Rhone is swept headlong from father Leman
and farther in the Cevennes press the Aquitanian fields,
even to the Teutosagi and the rustic names of the Belcae,
all was Narbo: you, first of Gaul of the toga'd
name, raise the fasces with a Latin proconsul.
Why should I recall your harbors and mountains and lakes?
Why peoples distinguished by variety of dress and face?
And that a temple of Parian marble once was yours
of such mass as Tarquin would not once have scorned,
and Catulus again, and last that one
who set the golden roofs of the Capitol, Caesar?
The merchandise of the Eastern sea and the Iberian waters
enrich you, the fleets of the Libyan and Sicilian deep,
and whatever is brought by various course through rivers,
through straits: a cargo-fleet sails to you from the whole
world.

IMPIOUS silences I have long condemned, that you,
O fatherland, famous for Bacchus and rivers and men,
for the manners and talents of men and the senate of nobles,
I do not recall among the first, as if conscious of a small
city I should hesitate to touch unmerited praises.
No shame to us from this; for I have neither the barbarian
shores of the Rhine nor a glacial home in arctic Haemus:
BURDIGALA is my native soil; where the mildness of the sky
is gentle and the generous kindness of a well-watered land,
a long spring and winters warming with the new sun
and summer-flowing rivers, whose vine-clad ridges below
seethe, imitating the currents of sea-streams,
a fourfold aspect of walls, so high with tall towers
that the roofs enter the airy clouds,
you would marvel at the streets marked out within, the
arrangement of houses and that they keep the name of broad
streets,
then gates answering to the straight crossroads;
and through the middle of the city the channel of a
fountain-river, which when father Ocean has filled with
returning tide, you will watch the whole sea glide in with
fleets.
Why should I recall a spring covered with Parian marble
seething with a Euripus-strait? How great a wave of the deep!
How great a swelling in the stream! With how great a column it
rushes headlong

in its course through twice six mouths of the stretched bank,
never exhausted for the countless uses of the people!
This you would wish, Median king, to touch with your camps,
when the rivers failed, their course consumed,
to carry the waters of this spring as foreign through cities,
you who were wont to drink only Choaspes through them all.
Hail, spring of unknown source, sacred, kindly, perennial,
glassy, blue-green, deep, sounding, without mud, shaded.
Hail, genius of the city, drinkable in a healing draught,
Divona in the tongue of the Celts, a spring added to the gods.
Aponus is not purer in drink, nor Nemausus in glassy light,
nor Timavus fuller with a sea-like stream.
This last labor shall have gathered the famous cities,
and as renowned Rome is the head of the number, so with that
head let BURDIGALA confirm her seat with a two-fold crown.
This is my fatherland: but Rome surpasses all fatherlands.
I love Burdigala, I worship Rome; I am a citizen in this one,
consul in both; here the cradle, there the curule chair.
