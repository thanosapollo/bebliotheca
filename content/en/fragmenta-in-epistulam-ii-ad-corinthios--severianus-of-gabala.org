2 Cor 1:1 And when formerly charging them he does not charge alone, and now
being reconciled he does not write alone, showing the matter trustworthy by
the inclusion of holy Timothy. And here too he showed that the "through whom"
belongs also to the Father, saying "through the will of God." 2 Cor 1:1-2
Since in the former epistle he did not charge alone, now also consoling he
does not write alone, but by the inclusion of holy Timothy he shows the matter
trustworthy. And since he says "from God our Father," it must be known that
upon the generated both God and Father is said, but upon the Son only Father;
for "my God and your God" Christ was saying while being in the flesh.
2 Cor 1:3 This glory with which he glorifies and by appellations names God
already pre-announce the reconciliation. "Father of mercies and God of all
consolation"; for upon the generated both God and Father is said, but upon the
Son only Father is confessed. Fittingly for those afflicted he begins from the
mercies of God and consolation, hinting good hopes to them; for if God is
Father of mercies, he would console those persecuted and afflicted for him.
2 Cor 1:4 God consoling and comforting does not make the comfort upon a small
thing. These things he pre-proclaims, since he was about to recall through
consolation the one condemned for sin. 2 Cor 1:6 Our affliction becomes a
comfort to you; for if the apostles are afflicted, much more the rest. Or he
also says this: even if we are afflicted by men for your salvation, we suffer
this, because we preach Christ to you. If we obtain comfort from God for your
sake, that we may suffice for your consolation, so as to exhort you to suffer
the same sufferings which we also endure. 279 2 Cor 1:7 I hope that those
enduring the same things as I will likewise obtain also the consolation from
God. 2 Cor 1:8 Not displaying himself he narrates the afflictions that befell
him in Asia, but anointing them toward endurance of the sufferings. Instead of
"so that we despaired even of living." 2 Cor 1:9 "Sentence" is called the
verdict from the judgment. So far as it was in themselves: for we have had the
verdict of death, through which we are disciplined not to trust in ourselves
but in the God who saves even those who despair of themselves. 2 Cor 1:11 Not
as one who loves the body does he pray to escape those plotting, but that he
may serve the gifts given to him, so that from many faces of those now and
through many of those to come the preaching that saves them may be thanked;
for by saying "through many" he showed the teaching of those to come. And "may
be thanked on your behalf" instead of "on behalf of the gift of which we
ourselves also have obtained." 2 Cor 1:12a This is our boast, that our
conscience testifies to us that we preach rightly. 2 Cor 1:12b And indeed he
wants the wisdom of those outside to be soulish, but now he hints that he did
not use speech according to those outside; as to receiving something from
them, he did not have a fleshly cause of the preaching. 2 Cor 1:13-14 We did
not teach you other things while being with you than what we now write, but
these things which you also read, being unadorned; whence you also recognize
our speech as being that of a layman, but the power of God working unto faith.
280 Paul calls the Corinthians his own boast in the things he accomplished by
preaching; and the boast of the Corinthians themselves in the day of Christ,
as having glory in the things for which they received the preaching. I hope
that you will recognize, since already also in part you have recognized, that
in the day of the Lord we are your boast just as you also are ours; for the
ones are proud in the things they accomplished by preaching, and the others
are ambitious in the things they believed in the preaching. 2 Cor 1:17 Not for
some fleshly thing was I planning to come but for God; and to plan to come for
God is not of lightness. Planning to come to you I promised this to you not
from rashness and lightness; for neither do I plan what I plan according to
flesh but spiritual things. And it befits the one planning about spiritual
things nowhere to prove false, so that with him the yes may be yes and the no
no. 2 Cor 1:18 To come or not is not in me, but to purpose is in me. If then
the preaching had yes and no, justly my plan of coming to you would have yes
and no; but if the word is true, throughout also that of the plan is true. I
believe God that nothing of what we preached is a lie. 2 Cor 1:19 For neither
did the Son of God our Lord Jesus become a liar. 2 Cor 1:20-22 Through Christ
we send up the Amen to God and Father. And the confirmation unto Christ we
received through God. And through "having sealed us" he made clear our kinship
toward God 281 and the alienation from the evil one; for also shepherds put a
seal on the flocks, distinguishing their own from the alien. And by saying
"earnest" he indicated that we have received a small pledge from the
knowledge, but the perfect is owed to us; for the earnest is a part from the
whole. 2 Cor 1:23-24 Since the charges against them were many, he did not
wish, having gone, to condemn them. The word has been said elliptically; for
he wants to say that I wished to come to you, but not as one who is lord and
teacher of your faith. To stumble is in you, but to correct those stumbling is
in us, that we may become fellow-workers of the joy according to God. In you
is the believing, in us the being-corrected of those who stumble, that we may
become fellow-workers of the grace of God. And he says the joy according to
God; for the apostle would not have worked with them unto worldly joy. By
saying "in the faith," he indicated that they fall short in the works.
Therefore, as they have the faith fruitless and still incomplete, he says he
is a fellow-worker to them. 2 Cor 2:1 For this reason I did not come, not that
I might not grieve you but myself, and that I might not condemn some for what
they do. 2 Cor 2:2 You do not grieve me and I you from like things; for you
grieve me from the things you sin, but I grieve you from the things I rebuke.
But the one being grieved gives joy (for he seems, having perceived, to be
grieved); for the one not grieved is clear as not even having taken thought
when charged. For even if I grieve you, I do a fitting thing, tending toward
my gladness upon your benefit. 2 Cor 2:3 This I wrote, that sparing you I did
not come, at the end of this epistle where it lies "lest perhaps when I come
282 God humble me." And for this I wrote, that I may find you in pleasure,
having been corrected, so that you may not be grieved. And I said this not
looking to my own but to yours; for I know that if you see me rejoicing you
rejoice, and if you know me in pain you are in pain. These things I wrote, so
that if you wish me to come to you, you may first correct the faults.
2 Cor 2:4 Having shown that he makes the same faults his own, he indicated by
mournings and tears the love he has toward them. By saying "I wrote to you,"
not that you might simply be grieved, but that the joy according to God might
succeed the grief. 2 Cor 2:5 If anyone has grieved even me who love, he has
not grieved me alone but also you in part, that I may not weigh heavily by
saying also Christ; and he was silent about saying "and Christ," that he might
not, even by saying it, weigh them down. 2 Cor 2:6 He calls "punishment" the
condemnation from the majority. 2 Cor 2:7 To grant him this. 2 Cor 2:8-9 You
obeyed me unto condemning; obey me also unto recalling him who was condemned.
2 Cor 2:10 Grant him this, he says; for you know that what you grant this one
I also wish to grant. And through this he shows that from the disposition
concerning them he wishes to think the same things as they. 2 Cor 2:12-13 He
appears to have been grieved toward those in Troas, because they did not make
much of Titus nor hold him until his own arrival, but as slack concerning the
faith they let him go. Therefore disciplining 283 them as unworthy he passed
them by, even though a door was opened and no one shutting out the preaching.
Or also for this he passed by, as being able easily to correct the things
there later when visiting, since there was no one hindering. 2 Cor 2:14 That
is, to the one who publishes our insults and the circumstances through Christ.
And he confesses greater grace for the things in which he is counted worthy to
be led in triumph through Christ. And he says these things also because of the
Corinthians; for he feared lest they perhaps give way because of the
afflictions they were enduring and fall from salvation. 2 Cor 2:15-16 The
idolaters, being in the intelligible death through the ignorance and hardness
that is in them, also fall into a second death by not receiving the
evangelical preaching; but those who through the legal training have come to
the beginnings of the knowledge of God have become somehow in life, yet if
they believe in Christ, they receive the fragrance of the knowledge of the
Father from life unto life. After a little he interprets this passage
otherwise. To accomplish such things is to be a fragrance of Christ, not of
chance people but of those who genuinely serve God. And as burdensome he does
not say these things about himself, but from what he adds, saying "for we are
not as the many," he displayed himself. 2 Cor 2:17 He says "to peddle" the
word, either mixed with circumcision and observance of sabbaths, or because
some consider piety a means of gain. And I say that I speak from sincerity,
and this very thing he says he has from God, and not from his own power. And
he says also another sense, that for this reason from sincerity, since we
speak from God, not playing with the matters but preaching a revelation of
God. He says this, since there were also false apostles considering the word
about piety a means of gain. Then he adds "from sincerity," instead of "we do
not play with the matters," but we preach the revelation from God, not
peddling the word of God. And the word of God is peddled when mixed with
circumcision and sabbath and in considering piety to be a means of gain. 284
2 Cor 3:1 From here he wishes to show how far the law falls short of grace,
lest some of those from circumcision, overawing those from the nations with
the dignity of Moses and the prophets, transfer them to the observance of the
law. 2 Cor 3:2-3 That is, this faith of yours and the life becomes a letter to
all and a mark of our preaching. And "written by the Spirit," he says, by the
energy of the Holy Spirit. And by saying "on tablets of stone and hearts of
flesh" he shows the superiority of grace toward the law, by how much a heart
is better than a lifeless tablet. And he also shows how much the grace of the
Spirit is better than the law and the preaching of the apostles higher than
the ministry of the prophets. 2 Cor 3:5 "From ourselves" instead of "from one
another." 2 Cor 3:6 He calls "letter" the law, and "spirit" the grace; for the
law, commanding indeed, but not helping, burdens and puts to death through
punishment those who were not able to fulfill it, but the Spirit, making clean
from sin and providing the gifts, clearly leads unto life. 2 Cor 3:7-9 And yet
the glory did not come to be in tablets but in the face of Moses as he himself
says next: "because of the glory of his face which is being done away." But
not wishing to speak against the prophet Moses, he calls the law Moses, and
toward this he makes the word, showing the grace given to Moses lesser than
the apostolic grace. "But if the ministry of death." He calls Moses a ministry
of dea285th, calling the law death, since when disobeyed it caused death. But
the law is not slandered from this; for the apostles also are called a
fragrance of death unto death. And he calls the apostles a ministry of spirit,
and Moses a ministry of death. But sparing Moses he mentions tablets; Moses
brought down tablets from a mountain, apostles sent being dispatched brought
Spirit to the world. 284,col2 And yet the glory did not come to be in tablets
but in the face of Moses. And wishing here also to speak even toward Moses,
through the law he indicates Moses. And he calls the law itself a minister of
death, since the law was putting to death those not fulfilling it. And he does
not say this as slandering it, for he also called the apostles a fragrance of
death because of those not obeying the preaching. And fragrance of
dea285,col2th is the threat about Gehenna bringing eternal death upon the
unbelievers, and fragrance of life the preaching of the kingdom sending those
who follow the teaching unto eternal life. And the fragrance is a forerunner
of the matter. He calls Moses a minister of condemnation and death, for in the
law he condemned those not obeying, and the apostles a ministry of spirit; for
Moses brought down the law, but the apostles conveyed the Spirit to the world.
2 Cor 3:10 The glorified letter of the law, when not examined toward the
surpassing glory of the Spirit, has not been glorified. Nor would anyone say
it has been glorified in this word of the comparison toward that which excels.
2 Cor 3:11 He showed that the law must also cease and be idle, but the grace
of the Spirit, having been introduced in its place, remain. 2 Cor 3:12-14
"Having therefore such a hope." The hope that is seen is not hope; and he made
clear that the grace remains and after the resurrection receives the
additions, toward "not being able for the sons of Israel to gaze at the end of
that which is being done away"; for as they were not 286 able to attend to the
glory ..., so they were not able even to the end of the law, that is, that the
law will be stopped. Straightway he says: "until today they have a veil." The
end of the law, when the veil is taken away and they behold the glory of the
Spirit, that it is done away in Christ, that is through faith in him. 285,col2
What hope? That the grace of the Spirit is not done away as the law is, but
remains even after the resurrection. We, because of the blessed hope of the
remaining of the grace of the Spirit, teach with boldness, and we do not
imitate the commands of Moses, who through 286,col2 the veil upon the face
signified that Jews cannot gaze and perceive that the law will take an end and
be idle. The veil remains, not even showing a face, that when Christ has come
the law is done away. 2 Cor 3:15-16 Just as Moses when speaking to Jews had
the veil, but turning back to the Lord has it taken away, so also now Jews
after reading the law and giving back the book carry away the veil as of Moses
having turned back to the Lord. If then having stood away you come to the
Spirit, your veil is taken away just as also of Moses turning back to the
Lord. 2 Cor 3:17 The one counted worthy of the Spirit of the Lord, this one
has been freed from the condemnation of the law; for through the Spirit the
gifts are energized. And the gift is toward the worthy. 2 Cor 3:18 We, he
says, the apostles, with unveiled face are seen by men; and by saying "we all"
he shows the benefaction of the Spirit common. And the glory of Moses was
unapproachable to Israelites, but our glory of God is approachable; for having
established ourselves by faith and having been cleansed we mirror the glory of
God, that is we both see and imprint as in a mirror on the souls the image of
the glory of the Spirit. We are transformed from legal knowledge into
spiritual grace. Or thus it must be understood: from the glory of the Spirit
being energized in us we come 287,col2 clearly into the glory of adoption. And
"just as" makes clear inasmuch as from the Lord Spirit. And it must be
observed that he called the Holy Spirit Lord, not the Son of God. And just as
one sees that mirror, and another receives the image of the one seeing, so
also upon the Spirit; for those mirroring the glory of Christ themselves,
carrying away a certain likeness from the glory into glory, that is they draw
and receive glory of the spiritual image. =286.30= We are transformed from
legal knowledge into spiritual grace, and from glory not 287 of the law but of
the Spirit, into the glory of adoption; for having obtained the bath we come
into adoption. 2 Cor 4:1 He says that of grace, not that of the law; for
through the excess of grace we trample the afflictions. 2 Cor 4:2 Hidden
things of shame are the base and the shameful which those who practice them
are eager to hide because of the shame; for the base things do not partake of
boldness, but escape notice in darkness. 2 Cor 4:3 Even if the gospel is
veiled, it has been veiled to the unbelievers, not as in the law the glory of
the face of Moses was veiled from all Israel. 2 Cor 4:4 If "the god of this
age" is said with the reading joined, then "he blinded" also has sequence. But
if "God blinded," according to 288 hyperbaton, as "God gave them a spirit of
stupor, eyes not to see," and "I hardened the heart of Pharaoh," according to
the account given. 287,col2 And just as "ruler of the age" has been said, not
being ruler by nature, but because those deceived appointed him ruler of
themselves by obeying him, so also he has been named the 288,col2 god of this
age, not as being truly God, but named God from what the nations say and
think, as also of the gluttons the belly is called god. 2 Cor 4:5 If we
preached ourselves, he says, it was possible to suspect that we adulterate the
word; but if Jesus Christ and his glory, not even one deceit is in the
preaching, according to what is said: "he who seeks his own glory is not true,
but he who seeks the glory of the one who sent him, this one is true." If we
were preaching ourselves, it was possible to suspect that we adulterate the
word, but if we preach Christ and his glory, not even one deceit is in the
preaching. 2 Cor 4:6 "In the face of Jesus Christ," instead of "through Jesus
Christ," through that one. "Unto illumination of the knowledge," that the
knowledge was not found by reasonings, but was set forth through revelation.
Instead of "through Jesus Christ it shone in our hearts," and the knowledge
was not found by reasonings, but was made known through revelation and
illumination; for never would a man clothed in flesh have been able to reach
this. 2 Cor 4:7-9 He shows through these and the following, that God does not
permit us to be tempted beyond what we can. For even if we are afflicted by
the persecutors, yet we are not so straitened as nowhere to have escape and
turning aside; for God widens for those afflicted for piety, whence also David
says: "in affliction you widened me." 2 Cor 4:10 We always carry about the
dying, by always being in danger through Christ, being sharers of the
sufferings of Christ. 289 2 Cor 4:11 The Lord was delivered unto death for us,
but we for ourselves through Jesus, that we may become sharers of his life.
2 Cor 4:12 Through the preaching the apostles were in danger, but they
preached that those who believe might live, and this is "but the life in you."
2 Cor 4:13 Since, he says, a spirit of slavery is in the law, but a spirit of
adoption in grace. And he said that we have the same Spirit which David also
had, he added "of faith" the same Spirit, not of the legal slavery. Having, he
says, the same Spirit as David who spoke according to faith, as he said: "I
believed, therefore I spoke." Then having said "Spirit" he added "of faith";
for this alone is common to new and old, since in the law there was a spirit
of slavery, but in grace a spirit of adoption. 2 Cor 4:17-18 If deaths and
dangers and temptations followed, how is the affliction lighter? How
"momentary"? For indeed the temptations remained with him and were being
driven even into old age. How then is the affliction of the saints light? Or
clearly as being exchanged against the glory of the age to come and against
the goods laid up for the just; for toward these even the heaviest of trial
appear light to me. And not simply are the afflictions light but according to
excess, which afflictions fall far short of the magnitude of the glory; for
the great body is also heavy. Or he also said "weight" according to a metaphor
of the weights; for the glory being weighed against the afflictions is found
very heaviest. And he says "momentary" the temporary; for even if the
afflicting and grieving be long, yet it appears temporary as toward the
boundless of the glory. 2 Cor 5:1-2 "House of a tent" is not the tent but the
house; for the dwelling here ceases, but the 290 tent is raised. And he called
"house" both the mortal sojourn and the expected one. Next however he shows
that he calls "dwelling-place" the corruption and the incorruption. 289,col2
He calls the body "house of a tent," which, he says, even if it is corrupted
in trials and dangers 290,col2 through Christ, we shall put it off as a
corruptible house and at last of the handmade. But we shall find in the
heavens another house which God provides incorruptible and not handmade. And
through these he indicates both the mortal sojourn and the immortal. Through
"to be clothed upon" he shows that the corruptible flesh is raised and is
clothed upon with the immortal; for the clothing-upon is said toward another
garment. And he says two houses, the mortal and the expected; and two
dwelling-places, the corruption and the incorruption, that we put off the one,
and are clothed upon with the other. And he calls "dwelling-place from heaven"
the incorruption from the heavens, inasmuch as it is not handmade but a gift
of God. 2 Cor 5:3 All, both just and unjust, put on the incorruption. If
however someone is delivered to punishment, he differs in nothing from a naked
one. 2 Cor 5:4 He wants the following: upon which we wish to be clothed upon
with the incorruption, but not to put off the body itself into corruption.
Therefore he also added: "that the mortal may be swallowed up by life."
2 Cor 5:5 "Unto this very thing," clearly unto becoming incorruptible.
2 Cor 5:6 Instead of "being persuaded that being at home in the body," that is
still sojourning in flesh in this world. 291 2 Cor 5:7 Through faith we hope
unto God; for his form is not visible to us; and this we believe, that we
shall go abroad toward him, when we shall see him as far as it is possible for
a man to see God. For Moses also saw bodily, and angels see as it is possible
for them to see; for no one has ever seen God and every creature falls short
of the vision of God. 2 Cor 5:7-9 If you are well-pleasing here, uncontestedly
you are more well-pleasing there, no longer struggling but having the security
of well-pleasing. And it is possible that "we walk through faith not through
form" has been said also about the economy of the Word of God, since he
himself was not of those who had seen the Lord in flesh before the passion.
2 Cor 5:12 Those who please men and boast not through the reverence itself
clearly boast through the face of men; for the heart is watched by God.
2 Cor 5:13 He calls the boast "ecstasy." We boast that that one may be
glorified, we keep silent the boasting (which he calls being sober) that we
may teach you not to boast. And he calls "ecstasy" the being forced to boast
of the one who ought to flee boasting. He says "ecstasy" the boast upon the
manner of life; for to boast is of those beside themselves, to be moderate of
those being sober. Often then we are forced to be beside ourselves and boast,
that God may be glorified through us. But we are eager to be sober and
moderate, that we may also teach you not to boast. And he says "ecstasy" the
being forced to come to this of the one who ought to speak the boasting. 292
2 Cor 5:14-15 Wishing to be silent we are forced to boast, being constrained
by the love of Christ, that that one may be glorified. "All died": he means
the faithful, both through baptism and through the life being put to death to
the world. "And no longer live to themselves"; for I too, if I were living to
myself, would not boast, but since I live to Christ, through that one I also
keep silent if it is needed toward your advantage, through that one I also
boast, that that one may be glorified. Whether we are beside ourselves, he
says, or we are sober for you, we do all things through the love of Christ.
Since then the one dying for some releases those already condemned of death,
and so far as it was upon us we were dead, if one died for all. Therefore all
died so far as the condemnation. And he means the faithful who through baptism
were put to death to the world, no longer living to themselves but to Christ;
for Christ also, having died for all and paid the common debt, made the world
a debtor to himself. 2 Cor 5:16 "So that we from now know no one according to
flesh." Since of flesh is the circumcision and "eat this and do not eat this"
and the sabbath, he says that we of the faithful know no one according to
flesh. But since it was striking that the Lord was circumcised and kept
sabbath, he says this timely, that he may benefit those in the law. But now
not yet this ... we have known; for he does not wish us to live according to
law. This has been said not only toward putting to death the desires, but not
even toward still obeying the law; for even if Christ was circumcised and came
under the observances of the law, which is even if we have known Christ
according to flesh, yet now we no longer know. For when he was among men, he
lived as a man, fulfilling the law by his life; but having died and risen
incorruptible he did away the things of the law, and showed the way of the
heavenly citizenship. Therefore also those being baptized put to death the
bodily citizenships as having died, 293,col2 and imitating the pure sojourn in
the heavens they join themselves according to the heart to God. 2 Cor 5:17 If
anyone lives in Christ and is baptized, this one is a new creation, no longer
living according to law but according to the new grace of the Spirit. For
whether you say law or grace, all things are from God; for we do not say that
the law is not from God, even if it received an end. 2 Cor 5:18 He says the
preaching is a ministry of reconciliation, through which believing we shall be
reconciled to God. 2 Cor 5:19 Thus it must be understood: that God was
reconciling a world to himself in Christ. Thus then it must be punctuated:
that God was in Christ reconciling a world to himself, that is through Christ.
2 Cor 5: Now we in place of Christ are ambassadors, God exhorting you through
us. Then he also shows that he is grieved because of those keeping the law;
for "on behalf of Christ," he says, not on behalf of the law. And God exhorts
through us as through Christ, whose ministers and servants we are. 2 Cor 5:21
From here he showed how he is grieved because of those contentious to keep the
law; for keeping law, he says, we have become sinners. Christ became sin, that
he might release us from sin. And God provided these things; receive the
reconciliation, standing away from keeping the legal things. 2 Cor 6:1 He was
not only permitting the apostles to exhort but also to work with and toil with
them toward salvation, that they may genuinely receive the grace from God. 294
2 Cor 6:4 As an unbreakable foundation he lays down beforehand endurance,
which being present it is easy to set every commandment upon it. 2 Cor 6:7
Weapons of righteousness on the right: to hold to good works, and on the left:
to abstain from evil works, and through both of these righteousness is
accomplished; for it is clear that if one is absent the other is rendered
idle. 2 Cor 6:8 Through glory of those receiving the word and marveling at it,
and dishonor of those insulting the believers; and the same word also upon the
following. 2 Cor 6:11-13 Having said "you are not straitened in us" he teaches
this instead of "we do not straiten you by the saving teaching nor by lack
from us"; for our heart has been widened and our mouth has opened toward
teaching. You too then as by exchange widen your mind and receive the
teaching; for your heart has not been so spread as to receive the teachings
from me. So that you are straitened through yourselves, not being receptive of
more charges. 2 Cor 7:9 Receive us saying: that in nothing you may be damaged
from us. For if I had been silent, he says, and had not rebuked, you would not
have been grieved; but if you had not been grieved, you would have been
damaged by not repenting. I rejoice, he says, upon your afflictions, because
they are not worldly but spiritual. And next he remembers the things that
befell him through Christ in Asia. 2 Cor 7:12 And yet for this he wrote. But
as it seems and as the outcome showed, I was not writing for this, but for
your zeal and the obedience to be shown; so that by the pretext of one thing
another was perceived. 295 2 Cor 7:13 In this he has been refreshed, that they
were obeying his teaching. 2 Cor 8:2 In that, he says, in which they are
greatly afflicted being persecuted, in this they rejoice more, and their deep
poverty abounded unto the wealth of their simplicity. And the apostle
testifies to the Macedonians that even being poor they became lavish unto
well-doing, that the Corinthians also may be stirred toward fruit-bearing.
2 Cor 8:6 The eagerness of Macedonians unto well-doing urged us to exhort
Titus to come to you, that he may also urge you unto fruit-bearing. And for
this he said "also this," since they had from him the grace of the teaching.
2 Cor 8:8 "Through that of others" he says of the Macedonians. 2 Cor 8:18-
This must be joined to "we sent with him the brother whose praise is in the
gospel," that they may suffice to serve the well-doing from the nations being
much and abundant, being wrapped and guarded, lest anyone blame us as
hindering the well-doing unto the saints. Before the Lord it is good to serve
the well-doing, but before men not to be blamed and slandered in the abundance
of the ministry. 2 Cor 10:2-6 Not as the soldiers of the world receive wages,

2 Cor 11:2 You died, he says, to the law, you have become to another husband,
to the one risen from the dead. How then a pure virgin formerly being married?
But 297 having been regenerated through the bath of regeneration and having
passed into another life, she has justly been named a pure virgin. 2 Cor 11:4
Christianity is confirmed by the faith unto Christ and the illumination of the
Spirit. If then you did not receive these among you, you were seeking well
those still wishing to live according to law. 2 Cor 11:10-12 He does not say
this reproaching Corinthians, but he refutes those using the preaching as
trade. Therefore he says that I do not cease boasting, that I have not made
the preaching a trade as those have; for I neither received anything from you
nor do I receive, not as not loving you, for I love, but that I may cut off
the pretext of the accusers, that whether they boast falsely or truly, they
may be manifested as we also have been manifested. 2 Cor 12:2-5 This is strong
to show the soul immortal; for if he knew that it was mortal, he would not
have doubted whether he saw the vision outside the body or in the body. And
"as far as the third heaven" he said of the interval the third; for if he had
gone up as far as three heavens, he would altogether have heard the
unspeakable words there and not in paradise. And let no one think paradise is
above the heaven; for it has been planted toward the east upon the earth in
the place Eden, whence also Adam was formed as Moses narrates. And he says
"weaknesses" the persecutions, not a bodily passion as some think. 2 Cor 12:7
He is suspected by many as having a pain about the head, but the true is he
means the persecutions, since the temptations were coming from diabolic
energy. And the temptation serving the devil he named an angel of Satan from a
metaphor of the angels serving God. And "it was given" must be understood
according to permission of God as also upon Job; for God does not give evils.
