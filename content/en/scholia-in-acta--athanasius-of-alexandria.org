Of the holy Athanasius from the treatise against the Novatianists.
One must understand here also that it was the beginning of the preaching, and
these indeed dared a great thing, having attempted to tempt the Holy Spirit;
but great benefit has come to the other brothers from their punishment; for
they were educated and taught that one must not dare against the Spirit, nor
attempt to tempt it. Just as a man being baptized by a human priest is
enlightened by the grace of the Holy Spirit, so also the one confessing in
repentance receives forgiveness through the priest by the grace of Christ.
CHAP. VIII, v. 24. Pray you for me to the Lord. OF ATHANASIUS
From the third discourse against those called Cathari.
Toward then his underlying bitterness, and this his daring and attempt against
the gift of God, Peter was justly moved, and having been moved he said to him:
May your silver be with you unto destruction. Even if one must not, yet
because of this daring of yours, that you thought to acquire the gift of God
through money, there is for you no portion, nor =26.1317= lot in this word.
For your heart is not straight before God. And having said so many things
against him rightly and justly, considering none of these incurable by the
medicine of repentance, but judging these things to be therapeutic, he added:
Repent therefore from your wickedness, and pray the Lord, if perhaps the
thought of your heart will be forgiven you. For this too is great for you. And
do not already hasten after the gift thus uncleanly and filthily through
money. But for the time being, having ceased from your bitterness and this
evil thought, repent, if perhaps the thought of your heart will be forgiven
you, not as if doubting, but because they are hard-to-heal passions, not
incurable indeed, but hard to heal. For if it were incurable, it would have
been superfluous, the: Repent from this wickedness of yours, and pray the
Lord. But that repentance indeed has power through these things, and that
those who have been weighed down into wickedness have need of much repentance
and care, showing this he added: If perhaps the thought of your heart will be
forgiven you. For I see you being in a gall of bitterness and a bond of
unrighteousness. And so much did the exhortation unto repentance of this great
apostle have power even toward so great a magician, and toward one so full of
gall and bitterness, and filled with so many evils, that already he gave
himself to care, and that Scripture bears witness to him saying these things:
Pray you for me to the Lord; for I have no boldness of speech, that nothing of
the things you have said may come upon me. Nor indeed did the divine Apostle
forbid these things to him, nor did he refuse. For how would he who both
proposed and said? And so much was this very man, so great a magician,
benefited from the hope unto repentance, that already having been so
much changed from wickedness and bitterness to say: I indeed am not sufficient
to pray for myself; but you pray for me to the Lord.
