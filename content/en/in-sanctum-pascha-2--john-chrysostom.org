* On Holy Pascha
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

A discourse of the same on the holy Pascha. Bless, father.
Every day is bright and distinguished, but the day of the resurrection
stands brighter than all. On it the insatiable mouth of death has been
bridled, and mortals laugh at death as now powerless and passing, or
rather those who live well even find him a benefactor. For having cut us
off from this painful and passing life, he sends us on to a course
without toil and a blessed state, where "sorrow and grief and sighing
have fled away," unto unspeakable enjoyment and incomprehensible
delight, "what eye has not seen, and ear has not heard, and what has not
entered into the heart of man." Death, then, is an unwilling benefactor
of men and a serviceable enemy, useful to us but at war with himself;
for in the very thing by which he increases what is ours, he undoes what
is his own.
For the undoing of death is this: that those who die pass into unending
immortality.
And who has turned him to this reverse craft, so that he now benefits
and, even while he harms, grants a gift? He who proclaimed the promise
of the resurrection to the world: "Destroy this temple, and in three
days I will raise it up." "Destroy this temple," not the one your hands
raised, but the one I built and put around myself; for this one, when it
falls, will rise, but yours, though it stands, will fall: "For amen I
say to you, there shall not remain a stone upon a stone." "Destroy this
temple, and in three days I will raise it up." He did not say, The
Father will raise it, lest Arius leap upon the word, but, I will raise
it: I have authority of myself, not as one commanded. But why not at
once, but after three days? You ask why he does not rise at once, but
after three days: that he might not break the type of the prophet Jonah.
"For as Jonah was in the belly of the whale three days and three nights,
so shall the Son of man be in the heart of the earth three days and
three nights." For the sun scatters gloom, and the light-bearing day
drives night away; and the white-robed day of the resurrection has
covered the night of ignorance. All things are full of joy and
brightness. And while all creation is made bright, I see the devil alone
in mourning. For our joy is the demons' disaster. The devil, then,
mourns. Why? Because he has lost Adam and did not hold the thief,
because the handwriting against us was snatched away, and the trophy for
us was planted: the cross, a victory-bearing trophy against demons. The
cross is an ever-green tree, withering the devil every day.
For as often as he sees where he pierced Life through, so often he
withers, struck hard by the spear of grief, because he did not kill Life
and through Life he put death to death. For death, having seized Life,
was itself put to death, and the dead, seeing Life, rose. "The old
things have passed away; all things have become new." The first Adam
fell; the second shatters him. For from below he shatters the one who
threw man down from above. He tripped Eve; by Mary he is tripped. He
gives thanks for the tree of transgression; he will wail, punished by
the cross. In paradise he held them; in Hades he is held. In paradise,
though he did not hold by right, he tyrannized; but in Hades, though
holding, he is stripped. Him he did not have before, he took; but later,
him he held, he lost. He ran rejoicing, and he withdrew wailing. "The
old things have passed away; all things have become new." Paradise no
longer shuts Adam out, since the thief opened the doors. Mary avenges
Eve. For him whose commandments she did not keep, this one received in
her womb; and him whom heaven did not contain, the womb of the virgin
did not straiten.
And he who hung the heaven upon the world was hung upon a cheap wood,
that by wood he might deceive the one who through wood deceived Adam.
Let us then take refuge in the Master who loves mankind, for him who
suffered for us. Let us not flee the sufferings: "For we suffer with
him, that we may also be glorified with him." The resurrection has
arisen, and creation is filled with joy. For the herald of the
resurrection first showed the resurrection in himself.
For if he had proclaimed it in word but not shown it in deed, those who
heard the word but did not see the deed would not have believed. What
then did the herald of the resurrection devise? Like an excellent
physician he first tastes the medicine, so that those in need of healing
may then receive the proclamation without doubt. First, then, the Master
is "from the dead: Christ the firstfruits of those who have fallen
asleep." And let no one say this: Yet we find others who rose before
him. Elijah the Tishbite raised the son of the woman of Sarepta before
the Savior's becoming man. Elisha likewise raised the son of the
Shunammite. How then is "Christ the firstfruits of those who have fallen
asleep"? I say more: the Master Christ himself, before his own rising,
raised Lazarus four days dead. How is "Christ the firstfruits of those
who have fallen asleep"? What we say of Lazarus, we say of all who rose
before him. Lazarus did rise before the Master Christ; but Lazarus rose,
that he might die again. He takes up living for a little, that he might
serve the miracle, and again come under the sentence of death. But if
"Christ is the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep," he no
longer went on to a second death, but having once for all set himself
apart as to the body, he remained immortal, as he was from the
beginning.
Bear witness, O Paul, to what is said: "For Christ, being raised from
the dead, dies no more; death no longer has dominion over him. For the
death he died, he died to sin once for all; but the life he lives, he
lives to God." Christ therefore first displayed the perfect
resurrection. For this reason Christ is "the firstfruits of those who
have fallen asleep," that what he promised in words he might fulfill in
deeds. For unless death first exists, there is no resurrection; unless
the passion comes first, there is no healing. But Christ, having endured
death through the cross, healed our diseases of the soul and drove out
the soul-destroying demons. Let someone say this: If he is God, how was
he crucified? How did a bodiless nature endure bodily pain? How did the
almighty power receive the experience of death? What then shall we say
to this? It was not the Godhead that was crucified, but the mantle of
the Godhead. Yet when the body was crucified, the Godhead was
dishonored. For he who tears the garment insults the one who wears it;
he who cuts up the royal purple provokes the king to anger. And then the
Jews crucified him; but now the first-throned among the Christians
crucify him again. For he who re-anointed Christ crucified him again.
"For there is one Lord, one faith, one baptism." For the chrism is upon
the baptism. How then was he who had been anointed until now re-anointed
as if unanointed? For the superfluous chrism made the Christian
unanointed, and you have become a traitor of yourself like Judas,
denying Christ for gold.
Therefore the rope of Judas awaits you also. For you who think his
thoughts must fulfill his end. But gathering from every side the word
concerning the resurrection, let us also display a life worthy of the
doctrines, that we may obtain better and unstained goods, which surpass
both mind and speech; which may we all obtain by the grace and love for
mankind of our Lord Jesus Christ, to whom be the glory and the power,
now and always and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
