* Disputation of a Christian and a Saracen
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Dialogue of a Saracen and a Christian.
1 The Christian, being asked by a Saracen: Whom do you say is the cause of
good and of evil? The Christian: Of all good things we say no one is the cause
except God, but of evils, no. And answering, the Saracen said: Whom do you say
is the cause of the evils? The Christian: Clearly the devil, who is of his own
will, and we human beings. The Saracen: For what reason? The
Christian: Because of free will. The Saracen: What then? Are you endowed with
free will, and whatever you will, are you able to do, and do you do it? The
Christian: In two things only have I been fashioned by God as endowed with
free will. The Saracen: What are these? The Christian: To do evil and to do
good, that is, good and evil. For this reason, when I do evils I am punished
by the law of God, but when I do good things I do not fear the law, but I am
even honored and shown mercy by God. Likewise also the devil, before men, was
fashioned by God as endowed with free will and sinned, and God thrust him out
of his own rank. But perhaps you will also say to me in objection: What are
the things you call good and evil? Behold, the sun and the moon and the stars
are good: make one of these. I do not tell you this for that reason. I work
good and evil among men. Goods, such as glorification of God and prayer and
almsgiving and things like these; evils, fornication, theft, and the like.
Since, as you say, good and evil are from God, God will be found according to
you unjust, which he is not. For if God commanded, as you say, the fornicator
to fornicate and the thief to steal and the murderer to murder, they are
worthy of honor; for they have done the will of God. Your lawgivers also will
be found false, and your books falsely titled, since they command the
fornicator and the thief to be flogged though they have done the will of God,
and the murderer to be killed, who ought to have been honored, since he did
the will of God. And the Saracen: Who, he says, fashions the infants in the
wombs of women?
For this the Saracens put forward against us as a most terrible problem,
wanting to prove God the cause of the evils. For if answering I say that God
fashions the infants in the wombs of women, the Saracen will say: Behold, God
is a fellow-worker with the fornicator and the adulterer. The Christian
answers to these things: We nowhere find after the first week of the making of
the world the scripture saying that God fashions or creates anything. But if
you doubt about this, show any creature or fashioned thing whatsoever
that came to be by God after the first week; but you can in no way prove this;
for all the visible creatures came to be in the first week. For God fashioned
man in the first week and commanded him to beget and to be begotten, saying:
"Increase and multiply and fill the earth." And since man was ensouled, having
ensouled seed, a sowing sprang up in his own wife. So that man begets man, as
the divine scripture says: "For Adam begot Seth, and Seth begot Enos, and Enos
begot Cainan, and Cainan begot Mahalalel, and Mahalalel begot Jared, and Jared
begot Enoch." And it did not say: God fashioned Seth or Enos or anyone else;
and from this we know that Adam alone was fashioned by God, but those after
him are begotten and beget until the present. And thus by the grace of God the
world is constituted, since also every herb and every plant by the command of
God from then on begets and is begotten. For God said: "Let the earth bring
forth herb of grass," and by his command each tree sprouted, <and every> kind
of herb and plant has in itself a seminal power. And the seed of every plant
and herb is ensouled, which, falling again of itself in the earth or also
being sown by another, sprouts up, not being fashioned by anyone, but obeying
the first command of God. Behold, I also, as I said before, being endowed with
free will in those things only which I said beforehand, wherever I sow,
whether into my own wife or into another's, using my authority, it sprouts and
comes to be, obeying the first command of God, not because even now God
fashions and works each day, since in the first week "God made the heaven and
the earth and the whole world in six days and on the seventh he rested from
all his works which he began to make," as also the scripture testifies for me.
And the Saracen: And how does God say to Jeremiah: "Before I fashioned you in
the belly I knew you, and from the womb I sanctified you"? The Christian: In
the belly of every man God fashioned the ensouled and seminal power from Adam
and thereafter. For Adam, having Seth in the belly, begot him, as I said
before, and Seth Enos, and each man already having a son in his belly, and
the son begot and begets until the present. And the "from the womb I
sanctified you" understand as the one who truly begets the children of God
according to the testimony of the holy Gospel: "for as many as received him,
it says, he gave them authority to become children of God, to those who
believe in his name, who were born not of bloods nor of the will of man nor of
the will of flesh, but of God" through baptism.
2 And the opponent: And was there baptism before Christ? For Jeremiah is born
before Christ. The Christian: There was, according to the testimony of the
holy apostle saying that some were baptized through the cloud, others through
the sea. And the Lord in the Gospels says: "Unless one is born through water
and spirit, he shall not enter into the kingdom of the heavens." So that
Abraham and Isaac and Jacob and the remaining saints before Christ, entering
into the kingdom of the heavens, were baptized beforehand, since according to
the testimony of Christ, if they were not baptized, they would not have been
saved. And the Holy Spirit testifies saying: "The sinners were alienated from
the womb," that is, from that of baptism. For this reason we confess that all
who have been saved and who are being saved were saved and are saved through
baptism by the grace of God.
3 The Saracen: The one who does the will of God himself, do you say he is good
or evil? And knowing his cunning, the Christian said: What you want to say, I
understand. The Saracen: Make it plain to me. The Christian: You want to say
that Christ suffered willing or not willing? And if I say to you: He suffered
willing, so that you may say to me: Go then, worship the Jews, because they
did the will of your God. The Saracen: Thus, he says, I wanted to say to you;
if you have a word, answer me. The Christian: What you say is will, I call
forbearance and long-suffering. The Saracen: From where can you establish
this? The Christian: Through a fact: therefore, while I and you are sitting or
standing, can any of us without the authority and lordship of God stand up or
move? And the Saracen: No. The Christian: God having said: "Do not steal, do
not fornicate, do not murder," does he will that we steal and fornicate and
murder? The Saracen: No; for if he willed it, he would not have said: "Do not
steal, do not fornicate, do not murder." The Christian: Glory to God, that you
have confessed, saying the things I want to say. Behold, you have agreed with
me that none of us without God can stand up or move, and that God does not
will that we steal or fornicate. If just now I stand up and go and steal or
fornicate, do you call this the will of God, or permission and forbearance and
long-suffering?
4 The Saracen, understanding and wondering, said: Truly, so it is. The
Christian: Understand this also, that though God was able to strike, he held
back for the present, that is, he was long-suffering toward the sin. But when
he wills, if I do not repent, he renders to me, just as he also did to the
Jews. For after a few years he raised up against them Titus and Vespasian and
the Greeks and brought down their boastings.
5 If you are asked by a Saracen saying: What do you say Christ is? say to him:
Word of God, thinking that you sin in nothing in this, since he is also called
Word in the scripture and Wisdom and Arm and Power of God and many other such
things; for he is many-named. And you also ask him in turn, saying: What is
Christ called in your scripture? And perhaps that one will want to ask you
something else, wanting to escape you; do not answer him until he resolves
your question. By all necessity he will answer you saying: In my scripture
Christ is called Spirit and Word of God. And then say to him again: The Spirit
of God and the Word, in your scripture, are they called uncreated or created?
And if he says that they are uncreated, say to him: Behold, you agree with me;
for that which has not been created by anyone, but creates, is God. But if he
dares at all to say that they are created, say to him: And who created the
Spirit and the Word of God? And if from perplexity he says that God created
them, say to him: A little before you said they are uncreated, and just now
you say that God created them. Behold, if I were saying such a thing to you,
you would say to me: You have destroyed your testimony, and henceforth I do
not believe you, whatever you say. Nevertheless then I also ask you this:
Before God created the Spirit and the Word, did he not have Spirit nor Word?
And he will flee from you, having nothing to answer you (for such people are
heretics according to the Saracens and very abominable and rejected), and, if
you wish to make him public to the remaining Saracens, he will fear you
greatly.
6 And if the Saracen asks you saying: The oracles of God, are they created or
uncreated? For this they put forward against us as a most terrible question,
wanting to prove that the Word of God is created, which is not so. For if you
say: They are created, he says to you that behold you say the Word of God is
created. But if you say: Uncreated, he says that behold all the oracles of
God, being uncreated, are not gods. Behold you have confessed that Christ,
being Word of God, is not God. Therefore answer him neither created nor
uncreated, but answer him thus: I confess one Word of God only, enhypostatic,
being uncreated, just as you also confessed, but I do not call my whole
scripture oracles, but utterances of God. And if the Saracen says: And how
does David say: "The oracles of the Lord are pure oracles," and not: The
utterances of the Lord are pure utterances? say to him that the prophet spoke
tropologically and not in the proper sense. And if he says to you: What is
tropology and proper speech? say to him: Proper speech is a sure demonstration
of a thing, but tropology is an unsure demonstration. And if the Saracen says
to you: Is it possible for a prophet to speak an unsure demonstration? say to
him: It is a custom for the prophets to personify the soulless things, and
they put eyes and mouths on them, as "the sea saw and fled." Behold, the sea
also does not have eyes; for it is not ensouled. And again the same prophet
addresses it as ensouled: "What is it to you, sea, that you fled?" and the
rest. "And my sword shall eat flesh," says the scripture; for eating is said
of a mouth that gnaws and swallows, but the sword cuts, yet does not swallow.
Thus also, having tropologized the utterances, he spoke oracles, which in the
proper sense are not oracles, but utterances.
7 And if the Saracen says to you: How did God come down into a woman's belly?
say to him: Let us use your scripture and my scripture; your scripture says
that God purified beforehand the virgin Mary above all flesh of woman, and the
Spirit of God and the Word came down into her, and my Gospel says: "The Holy
Spirit shall come upon you, and the power of the Most High shall overshadow
you." Behold, one voice of both sayings and one meaning. And know this also,
that scripture, in a manner suited to our own condition, speaks of the
descent of God and ascent tropologically and not in the proper sense; for in
the proper sense descent and ascent are said of bodies according to the
philosophers, but God contains all things and is not contained by any place.
For one of the prophets said: "Who measured with his hand the water of the
sea and the heaven with a span and all the earth with a handful?" And in a
word, all the waters are under the hands of God and all the heaven a span and
all the earth a handful.
How is it possible that he himself, in his own hand that holds all things,
should come down and go up?
8 If the Saracen asks you saying: And if Christ was God, how did he eat and
drink and sleep and the rest? say to him that the pre-eternal Word of God who
created the universe, as my scripture and your scripture testify, himself
created from the flesh of the holy virgin Mary a perfect man, ensouled and
intelligent; that one ate and drank and slept, but the Word of God did not eat
nor drink nor sleep nor was crucified nor died, but the holy flesh which he
took from the holy virgin, that was crucified. And know that Christ is called
double in the natures, but one in the hypostasis. For the pre-eternal Word of
God is one even after the assumption of the flesh, hypostatically, that is
personally, and not naturally; for a fourth person was not added to the
Trinity after the ineffable union of the flesh.
9 If the Saracen asks you, that she whom you call Theotokos, did she die or
does she live? say to him: She did not die, taking courage from the scriptural
demonstration. For the scripture says concerning this: "The natural death of
men came upon her also, yet not imprisoning or overpowering as among us (God
forbid!), but as one might say: The first man slept and his side was taken
away."
10 If the Saracen says to you: Behold I have been struck in some place of my
flesh, and the flesh being struck produced a bruise, and in the bruise a worm
came to be. Who then fashioned it? say to him, as we said before, that after
the first week of the making of the world we do not find God having fashioned
or fashioning any thing whatsoever, but by the command of God which he
commanded in the first week the things that come to be come to be. For after
the transgression the earth was condemned to bring up thorns and thistles. And
until now even without being sown it brings up thorns and thistles. Then also
our flesh, having been judged, until today brings up lice and worms.
11 The Saracen then asked the Christian: Who is greater among you, the one who
sanctifies or the one who is sanctified? And the Christian, knowing his armed
question, said: What you want to say, I know. The Saracen: And if you know,
tell it to me. The Christian said: If I say to you: The one who sanctifies is
greater than the one who is sanctified, you will say to me: Go, worship John
the Baptist as having baptized and sanctified your Christ. And the Saracen:
Thus, he says, I wanted to say to you. Enigmatically the Christian said to the
Saracen: When you go with your slave to the bath and are washed by him and
cleansed, whom do you have to say is greater, that pitiable slave bought with
silver, or you who were cleansed by him, as being also his master? I call
myself greater, the one who acquired him, than that one who was acquired for
me, said the Saracen to the Christian. And the Christian answered: I thank
God. Understand John thus also as a slave and household servant who ministered
to Christ in the Jordan, in which my Savior, being baptized, crushed the heads
of the wicked demons lurking there. And the Saracen, greatly wondering and at
a loss and having nothing to answer the Christian, withdrew, no longer
attacking him.
