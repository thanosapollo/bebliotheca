8 of the same, when he was in Antioch, of a discourse on the mystical supper
whose beginning is:
today we feast a firstfruit of an unspeakable sacrifice.
concerning divine and human energies. The same one then as man worked the
obedience for us and brought to nothing the disobedience of the forefather;
according to will having entered under the condemnation of the death defined
against us through it, that we might live; and as God he worked the
resurrection, having put death to death by an almighty will, and having
created incorruption, that through the firstfruit from us we may partake of
the same nature by divine grace. Having two natural energies, being shown
divine and human as God and man, the same one; for to say one and the same
energy of the both together is in no way possible, lest we make the same one
passible according to both natures, or impassible, or on the contrary bring
both to one another. 9 of the same from the discourse on the passion of the
Lord, whose beginning is: no one circumscribes in a word the mystery above
nature and word. concerning natural energy. For even the Lord's having been
able to come to the wood of the cross is of a natural energy of the nature
which he took on, which is naturally receptive of all the sufferings according
to us except sin alone.
