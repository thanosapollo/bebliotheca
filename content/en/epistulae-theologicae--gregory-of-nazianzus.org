Of the same, first letter to Cledonius the presbyter

101.1 To the most honored and most God-beloved brother and fellow-presbyter
Cledonius, Gregory, greeting in the Lord. 101.2 We wish to learn what the
innovation concerning the Church is, that it should be permitted to everyone
who wishes and who "passes by," according to what is written, to tear apart
the flock that has been well led and to plunder it with stealthy assaults, or
rather with robber-like and unreasonable teachings. 101.3 For if those who
have now come upon us had something to find fault with in us concerning the
faith, not even so ought they to have dared such things without a reminder of
us. 101.4 For they ought first to have persuaded, or to have been willing to
be persuaded (if indeed there is even some account of us as fearing God and
having toiled for the word and having benefited the Church), and then, if at
all, to innovate; yet even so there was perhaps some defense for the insolent.
101.5 But since both in writing and without writing, both here and farther
off, both with dangers and without danger, our faith has been proclaimed, how
do some attempt such things, and others keep quiet? 101.6 And this is not yet
the terrible thing, though it is terrible, if men deposit their own evil
opinion in the more simple souls through the more knavish; but that they also
lie against us as being of the same doctrine and of the same mind, 101.7
wrapping the bait around the bronze and through this pretext wickedly
fulfilling their own will, and making our simplicity, from which we looked on
them brotherly and not as strangers, a provision for malice; 101.8 and not
only this, but that they also say they have been received by the Western
synod, as I hear, by which they had formerly been condemned, as is quite clear
to all. 101.9 If then those who think the things of Apollinarius have been
received, either now or formerly, let them show this and we will acquiesce.
For it is clear that they have agreed with the right word; for it is not
possible otherwise, if they have obtained this.

=101.10= And they will show it altogether either through a synodical tome or
through letters of communion; for this is the law of synods. =101.11= But if
this is a story and a fiction invented for the sake of seemliness and of
persuasiveness toward the many, because of the credibility of the persons,
teach them to be quiet and refute them. For we suppose this to be fitting to
your manner of life and orthodoxy. =101.12= Let not the men deceive, nor let
them be deceived, receiving as a mindless man the Lordly one, as they
themselves say, or rather our Lord and God. =101.13= For we do not separate
the man from the Godhead, but we dogmatize one and the same, formerly not man,
but God and Son only and before the ages, unmixed with body and with whatever
belongs to body, =101.14= but at the last also man, assumed for the sake of
our salvation, passible in flesh, impassible in Godhead, circumscribed in
body, uncircumscribed in spirit, =101.15= the same earthly and heavenly, seen
and intellected, contained and uncontained, that by the whole man, the same
also God, the whole man who fell under sin may be refashioned. =101.16= If
anyone does not take holy Mary as Mother of God, he is apart from the Godhead.
If anyone should say that he ran through the Virgin as through a pipe, but was
not formed in her both divinely and humanly (divinely, because without man;
humanly, because by the law of pregnancy), he is likewise godless.

=101.17= If anyone should say that the man was formed, and then God slipped
under, he is condemned. For this is not a generation of God, but a flight from
generation. =101.18= If anyone introduces two Sons, one from God and the
Father, and a second from the


mother, but not one and the same, he would fall away also from the adoption
promised to those who believe rightly. =101.19= For there are two natures, God
and man, since also soul and body; but not two sons, nor Gods. For neither
here are there two men, even if Paul named in this way the inner of the man
and the outer.

=101.20= And if one must speak briefly, one thing and another are the things
from which the Savior is (if indeed the invisible is not the same as the
visible, and the timeless as that under time), but not one person and another;
God forbid. =101.21= For both are one by the mixture, God having been made
man, and man having been deified, or however one might name it. And I say one
thing and another, the reverse of how it is in the Trinity. For there it is
one person and another, that we may not confuse the hypostases; but not one
thing and another, for the three are one and the same in Godhead.

=101.22= If anyone should say that he has worked as in a prophet according to
grace, but has not been joined and is not joined according to essence, let him
be empty of the better energy, or rather full of the contrary. If anyone does
not worship the crucified, "let him be anathema" and let him be ranked with
the God-killers. =101.23= If anyone should say that he has been perfected from
works, or after the baptism, or after the resurrection from the dead, has been
deemed worthy of adoption, just as those whom the Greeks introduce as enrolled
later, "let him be anathema." =101.24= For that which has a beginning, or
progresses, or is being perfected, is not God, even if it is so called because
of the gradual manifestation. =101.25= If anyone should say that he has now
put off the flesh and that the Godhead is bare of the body, but is not and
will not come with that which was assumed, let him not see the glory of the
coming. =101.26= For where is the body now, if not with the one who assumed
it? For it has not, according to the nonsense of the Manichaeans, been
deposited in the sun, that it may be honored through dishonor; =101.27= or
poured out into the air and dissolved, as the nature of a voice and the flow
of a smell and the course of a lightning that does not stand. =101.28= And
where also is his being handled after the resurrection, or his being seen at
some time by those who pierced him? For Godhead by itself is invisible.
=101.29= But he will come with the body, as my word has it, such as he was
seen by the disciples on the mountain, or was shown, the Godhead overcoming
the little flesh. And just as we say these things, putting away the suspicion,
so we also write those, correcting the innovation.

=101.30= If anyone should say that the flesh has come down from heaven, but is
not from here and from us, "let him be anathema." For "The second man from
heaven," and "As is the heavenly, such also are the heavenly," and "No one has
gone up into heaven, except he who came down from heaven, the Son of man," and
if there is anything else of this kind, it must be thought to be said because
of the union toward the heavenly, =101.31= just as also "that all things have
come to be through Christ" and "that Christ dwells in our hearts," not
according to the appearing of God, but according to what is intellected, the
natures being mixed, as it were, and so also the names, and circulating into
one another by the word of the co-naturality. =101.32= If anyone has hoped in
a mindless man, he is truly mindless and not worthy to be saved at all. For
what has not been assumed is not healed; but what has been united to God, this
also is saved. =101.33= If Adam fell in half, then also what was assumed and
what is saved is half. But if the whole, he has been united to the whole of
the one who was born and is wholly saved. Let them not then begrudge us the
complete salvation, nor let them put around the Savior only bones and sinews
and a painting of a man. =101.34= For if the man is without soul, this the
Arians also say, that they may bring the passion upon the Godhead, as of the
one who moves the body, this one also suffering. But if with soul, if not
intelligent, how is he even a man? for man is not a mindless living thing.
=101.35= And of necessity the shape would be human and the tent, but the soul
of some horse or ox or another of the mindless. This then will also be what is
saved; and I have been deceived by the truth, another having been honored,
another boasting. But if man is intelligent and not


mindless, let them stop truly being mindless. =101.36= But the Godhead
sufficed, he says, in place of the mind. What then is this to me? For Godhead
with flesh only is not man, nor even with soul only, nor with both without the
mind, which is even more man. Keep then the whole man and mix the Godhead,
that you may benefit me perfectly. =101.37= But two perfect things did not
have room, he says. Nor indeed, if you look bodily. For a vessel of a medimnus
will not hold two medimni, nor the place of one body two or more bodies;
=101.38= but if as intelligible and bodiless, consider that I myself have
contained both soul and word and mind and Holy Spirit, and before me this
world, I mean the system from things seen and unseen, contained the Father and
the Son and the Holy Spirit.

=101.39= For such is the nature of the intelligibles, to be mixed both with
one another and with bodies bodilessly and without partition. Since also
several voices are containable by one hearing, and sights of several by the
same visibles, and smell by things that can be smelled, neither the senses
being cramped by one another or squeezed out, nor the sensibles being
diminished by the multitude of the apprehension.

=101.40= And where is even a mind of man or even of an angel =101.40= perfect
in comparison with Godhead, that the one should be squeezed out by the
presence of the greater? For neither is some gleam toward the sun, nor a
little moisture toward a river, that we may first take away the small things,
the gleam of a house, and the moisture of earth, and so the greater and more
perfect may have room. =101.41= For how will two perfect things have room, a
house gleam and sun, and earth moisture and river, let us consider this; for
the matter is truly worthy of much thought. =101.42= Or do they not know that
what is perfect toward one thing is imperfect toward another, as a hill toward
a mountain and a grain of mustard toward a bean, or some other of the greater
seeds, even if it is called greater than those of the same kind; and if you
will, an angel toward God, and toward an angel a man? =101.43= Our mind then
is perfect and governing, but of soul and body, not simply perfect, and a
slave of God and subject, but not co-governing nor of equal honor. =101.44=
Since Moses also is a god to Pharaoh, but a servant of God, as it has been
written; and stars shine around the night, but are hidden by the sun, so that
by day they are not even known to exist; =101.45= and a little torch, having
drawn near a great fire, neither perishes nor appears nor is separated, but
the whole is fire, the superior having prevailed. =101.46= But our mind is
condemned, he says. And what of the flesh? Is it not condemned? Either put
this away too because of the sin, or bring that also because of the salvation.
If the worse has been assumed, that it may be sanctified through the
incarnation, will the better not be assumed, that it may be sanctified through
the becoming-man? If the clay has been leavened and has become a "new lump," O
wise ones, will the image not be leavened and mixed toward God, having been
deified through the Godhead? =101.47= And we will add this too: if the mind
has been wholly spat upon as sinful and condemned, and for this reason body
has been assumed, but mind has been left aside, there is pardon for those who
fail concerning mind. For a testimony of God has clearly shown the
impossibility of the healing.

=101.48= Shall I say the greater thing? You, O best one, dishonor my mind for
this reason, as a flesh-worshipper, if I am a man-worshipper, that you may
bind God to flesh, as not able to be bound otherwise, and for this reason you
take out the middle wall. =101.49= And what is my word, of the unphilosophical
and uneducated? Mind is mixed with mind, as nearer and more kindred, and
through this mediating for flesh between Godhead and thickness.

=101.50= And let us see also what is their word of the becoming-man, or rather
incarnation, as they themselves say. If it is that God may be contained, being
otherwise uncontained, and that he may converse with men as under a veil, the
flesh, the mask is clever for them and the drama of the acting; not to say
that it was possible to converse with us also otherwise, as formerly in a bush
of fire and in human form.


=101.51= But if it is that he may loose the condemnation of sin, having
sanctified the like by the like, just as there was need of flesh because of
the flesh that was condemned and of soul because of the soul, so also of mind
because of the mind, which not only sinned in Adam, but also first suffered,
which the physicians say of the illnesses. =101.52= For what received the
commandment, this also did not keep the commandment; and what did not keep it,
this also dared the transgression; and what transgressed, this also especially
needed the salvation; and what needed the salvation, this also was assumed;
the mind then has been assumed. =101.53= This has now been demonstrated, even
if they do not wish it, by geometric, as they themselves say, necessities and
proofs. But you do something similar, as if, a man's eye having failed and a
foot having stumbled, you healed the foot, but left the eye unhealed; or a
painter having painted something not well, you altered what was painted, but
passed by the painter as succeeding. =101.54= But if, being shut out by these
reasonings, they flee to this, that it is possible for God to save the man
even without mind, it is possible surely also without flesh by willing alone,
just as he also works and has worked all the other things bodilessly. Take
away then with the mind the flesh also, that the matter of your madness may be
perfect for you. =101.55= But they are deceived by the letter and for this
reason they run to the flesh, being ignorant of the custom of Scripture. We
will teach them this too. =101.56= For that everywhere in Scripture he is
named man and Son of man, what need is there even to say to those who know?
But if they insist on "The Word became flesh and tabernacled among us," and
for this reason they scrape off the fairest of the man, as cobblers the
thicker of the hides, that they may glue God to flesh, =101.57= it is time for
them to say also that God is God of fleshes only, but not also of souls,
because it has been written: "As you have given him authority of all flesh,"
and "To you all flesh will come," and "Let all flesh bless his holy name,"
that is, every man; =101.58= or again that our fathers went down into Egypt
bodiless and invisible, and that the soul of Joseph alone was bound by
Pharaoh, because it has been written: "In seventy-five souls they went down
into Egypt," and "His soul passed through iron," a thing not able to be bound.
=101.59= For those who say these things are ignorant that such things are
named synecdochically, the whole being shown from a part, just as also: "The
young of ravens call upon God," that the winged nature may be shown, and
Pleiades and Hesperus and Arcturus are mentioned instead of all stars and of
the economy concerning them.

=101.60= And at the same time it was not otherwise possible for the love of
God concerning us to be shown, except from the flesh being mentioned and that
he came down for us even as far as the worse. For that flesh is cheaper than
soul, everyone of those who think well would confess. =101.61= Then "The Word
became flesh" seems to me to have the same force as his being said to have
become both sin and a curse; not that the Lord was transformed into these, how
so? but as, by receiving these, having taken up our lawlessnesses and having
borne the diseases. =101.62= These things then are enough for the present,
because of the clear and easy for the many. For we write these things not
wishing to write a treatise but to check the deceit, and the more perfect word
about these, if it seems good, we will also render through longer discourse.
=101.63= But what is heavier than these, it is necessary not to pass over even
this itself. "I would that those who unsettle you would even cut themselves
off," introducing a second Judaism and a second circumcision and second
sacrifices; =101.64= for if this, what hinders Christ from being born again
for a setting-aside of the same things, and being betrayed again by Judas and
being crucified and buried and rising, that all the things of the same
sequence may be fulfilled, according to the


Hellenic recirculation, the same motion of the stars carrying around the same
things?

=101.65= For what is the arbitrary choice, that one of the things that
happened then should come to be, and another be let go? Let the wise and those
who pride themselves on the multitude of books show this.

=101.66= And since, being puffed up with the word about the Trinity, they lie
against us, as not being sound concerning the faith, and they bait the many,
it is necessary to know this, that Apollinarius, though giving the name of
Godhead to the Holy Spirit, did not keep the power of the Godhead. =101.67=
For to compose the Trinity from great and greater and greatest =101.67=, as
from gleam and ray and sun, of the Spirit and of the Son and of the Father
(which has been clearly written in that man's words), is a ladder of Godhead,
not leading up into heaven, but leading down from heaven.

=101.68= But we know as God the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, and
these not bare names cutting inequalities of dignities or of powers, but one
and the same, as appellation, so also nature and essence and power of Godhead.
=101.69= And if anyone thinks these things are said rightly, but accuses the
communion of the heretics, let someone of ours show this, and we will either
persuade, or we will withdraw. But before judgment it is not safe to innovate
even any other thing, much less a matter so great and concerning such things.

=101.70= We then have testified these things to God and men and we testify.
And not even now, know well, would we have written these things, if we did not
see the Church being torn apart and cut and by the other marvel-mongerings and
by the present "council of vanity." =101.71= And if anyone, while we say and
testify these things, either through some needs or through human fear or
through absurd smallness of soul or through the being as yet unshepherded and
ungoverned or through the love of strangers and readiness toward innovations,
=101.72= spits on us as worthy of no account, and runs to such people and
divides the fair body of the Church, he himself will bear the judgment,
whoever he may be, and will give account to God on the day of judgment.
=101.73= And if the long words and the new psalters and those sounding against
David and the charm of the meters is thought a third Testament, we too will
psalm and write many things and measure. Since we too seem to have the Spirit
of God; if indeed this is a grace of Spirit, and not a human innovation.
=101.74= These things I wish you to testify to the many, that we may not have
a burden as overlooking such an evil, and a wicked doctrine taking pasture and
strength through our negligence.

102.t Second letter to Cledonius the presbyter

102.1 Since many coming to your reverence seek assurance concerning faith, and
for this reason you have asked us lovingly for some short definition and canon
of our mind, for this reason we have written to your reverence (which you knew
even before the letters) that we have never preferred anything to the faith
according to Nicaea, of the holy Fathers who gathered there for the
overthrowing of the Arian heresy, nor are we able to prefer, 102.2 but we are
of that faith with God and will be, additionally articulating what was said
deficiently by them concerning the Holy Spirit (because this question had not
even been moved at that time), that one must know the Father and the Son and
the Holy Spirit of one Godhead, knowing the Spirit also as God. 102.3 Those
then who think and teach thus, hold as in communion, since we also do; but
those who are otherwise, turn away from and consider as strangers both to God
and to the catholic Church. 102.4 And since also concerning the divine


becoming-man, or rather incarnation, some question is being moved, define this
too to all concerning us, that we bring into one the Son of God, who was
begotten from the Father and after this from the holy Virgin Mary, and we do
not name two sons, but we worship one and the same in undivided Godhead and
honor. And if anyone does not agree with these things, either now or later, he
himself will give account to God on the day of judgment. 102.5 Our stand then
and opposition toward their mindless opinion about the mind, as in brief, is
such and so; for they almost alone both dogmatize and suffer what they
dogmatize, cutting off the mind by mindlessness. 102.6 And that they may not
accuse us as formerly receiving the faith of the beloved Vitalius, which being
asked by the blessed Damasus the bishop of Rome he gave in writing, but now
denying it, we will make clear a few things about this too. 102.7 For these,
whenever they theologize to their genuine disciples and initiates of the
secret things, as the Manichaeans to those called elect, uncovering their
whole disease, barely even give the flesh to the Savior. 102.8 But when they
are refuted and pressed by the common conceptions about the becoming-man which
Scripture presents, they confess the pious words, but they play knavishly
about the mind, 102.9 not confessing the man as without soul nor without
reason nor without mind nor imperfect, but introducing as soul and word and
mind the Godhead itself, as of it alone having been mixed with the flesh, but
not also of our and human things, even if the sinlessness was better than
according to us and a cleansing of our passions.

=102.10= Thus at any rate they also badly and very simply misinterpret "But we
have the mind of Christ," saying that the mind of Christ is the Godhead, not
taking, as we do, that those who have purified their own mind by imitation of
that mind which the Savior received for us, and who tune themselves toward
him, as far as attainable, these are said to have the mind of Christ; =102.11=
as those also would be witnessed to have the flesh of Christ who have
disciplined the flesh and have become of one body and fellow-partakers of
Christ according to this, and "As we have worn the image of the earthy, so, he
says, we shall wear the image of the heavenly." And thus also a perfect man
for them is not the one who has been tried in all our things without sin, but
the mixture from God and flesh is dogmatized. For what, they say, is more
perfect than this? =102.12= And the same they also play knavishly about the
voice of the becoming-man, interpreting "he became man" not as "he came to be
in a man," which he framed around himself, according to what has been said:
"For he himself knew what was in the man," but saying that he conversed with
men and lived among them and taught, and fleeing to that voice which says
"After this he was seen upon the earth and associated with men."

=102.13= And why should one contend further? Those who put away the man and
the inner image cleanse only our outer through the new mask and the seen,
=102.14= fighting so much against themselves that at one time they interpret
thickly and fleshly because of the flesh and the other things (for from here
their second Judaism has started, and the thousand-year and nonsensical luxury
in paradise, and almost that we take up the same things again upon the same),
and at another time they introduce an appearance rather than a truth of the
flesh as having suffered none of our things, not even as many as are free of
sin, =102.15= and they use for this the apostolic voice, not apostolically
understood or said, that our Savior has come to be in the likeness of men and
has been found in shape as a man, as if the human form is not shown in these,
but some deceitful fantasy and appearance. =102.16= Since then these voices,
well understood, are with piety, but badly interpreted have the impious, what
wonder if we also received the words of


Vitalius toward the more pious, the wishing so persuading, but others grow
savage toward the mind of the things written? =102.17= which I think Damasus
himself also, having been taught otherwise and at the same time =102.17=
having learned that they remain upon the former interpretations, has made them
outcasts and has overturned the document of the faith with anathema, being
displeased also at the very deceit of them, which he suffered from simplicity.

=102.18= These things then having been clearly convicted, let them not be
harsh toward us, but let them be ashamed, nor let them lie, but let them
shrink, and let them wipe from the gateways that great and wondrous
proclamation of theirs and preaching of orthodoxy, meeting those who enter at
once with a question and a distinction, that one must worship not a
God-bearing man, but a flesh-bearing God. =102.19= Than which what could be
more unreasonable, even if the new heralds of the truth think big at this
phrase? For it has a certain sophistic charm by the speed of the inversion and
a pebble-counting marvel-mongering that delights the uneducated; but it is
more laughable than the laughable and more senseless than the senseless.
=102.20= For if someone, changing the "man" voice and the "flesh" into God (of
which the one pleases us, the other them), should then use this wondrous and
God-knowing inversion, what will be concluded? that one must worship not a
God-bearing flesh, but a man-bearing God. =102.21= O the absurdity; they
announce to us today the wisdom hidden after Christ, at which it is even
worthy to weep. =102.22= For if the faith began thirty years before these,
nearly four hundred years having come to be from when Christ has been
manifested, our Gospel is empty in so much time, and our faith empty, =102.23=
and in vain those who were martyred were martyred, and in vain also such and
so great leaders stood before the people, and the charm of the meters, but not
of the faith.

=102.24= And who would not admire them for their education, who themselves
clearly divide the things of Christ, and assign the being born and being
tempted and hungering and thirsting, and having toiled and slept, to the
human, =102.25= but the being glorified by angels, and having conquered and
turned the tempter, and so also having walked upon the sea, they reckon to the
Godhead; =102.26= and saying that "Where have you laid Lazarus" is ours, but
to cry "Lazarus, come out" and to raise the four-day dead, above us; =102.27=
and the having been in agony and having been crucified and buried, of the
covering, but the having been of good courage and having risen and having gone
up, of the treasure?

=102.28= Then they accuse us as introducing two natures detached or fighting
and dividing the supernatural and wondrous union; =102.29= whereas they ought
either not to do what they accuse, or not to accuse what they do, if indeed
they have resolved at least to follow themselves, and not both to say their
own things and the things of the opponents. =102.30= Such is unreason: to
fight both against itself and against the truth, so that they do not even,
falling upon themselves, either understand or feel shame. =102.31= And if
anyone thinks that we write or say these things willingly, and not being
tyrannized, and that we are eager to put away the union, and not even very
zealous for it, let him know that he thinks badly and has not aimed at our
desire, =102.32= for whom nothing is or has been more precious than peace, as
the facts themselves persuade, even if what they do and swagger against us
shuts out concord altogether.

202.t To Nectarius bishop of Constantinople

202.1 It seems that the care of God, which in the times before us was guarding
the Churches, has altogether left the present life. 202.2 And my


soul has been so baptized by misfortunes, that I reckon the private painful
things of my life not even among evils (being so many and so great that,
having happened to any other, they would not be thought bearable), and look
only toward the common sufferings of the Churches, 202.3 of which if there
should not come to be some zeal toward correction in the present season, it
will little by little go forward into complete hopelessness. 202.4 Those of
the heresy of Arius or rather of Eudoxius, I do not know what has stirred them
to madness, as having laid hold of some boldness, make a show of their own
disease, gathering churches, as doing this from commission.

202.5 And those who contend according to Macedonius have advanced to such
madness as even styling themselves with the name of bishops to overflow in our
places, babbling Eleusius over their ordinations. 202.6 And our bosom evil,
Eunomius, is no longer content to be somehow; but if he should not drag all
with his own destruction, he judges it a loss. 202.7 And these things are
bearable; but the most grievous of all in the ecclesiastical misfortunes is
the boldness of the Apollinarists, whom I do not know how your holiness has
overlooked, having procured for themselves authority to gather on equal honor
with us. 202.8 Now altogether having been trained through all things according
to the grace of God in the divine mysteries, you know not only the advocacy of
the right word, but also those things which have been invented by the heretics
against the healthy faith; 202.9 yet perhaps it is not untimely for your
solemnity to hear also from our smallness that a booklet of Apollinarius has
come into my hands, in which the things constructed surpass every heretical
evil.

=202.10= For he affirms that the flesh is not acquired, assumed by the
only-begotten Son according to economy for a change of our nature, but that
from the beginning that fleshly nature is in the Son. =202.11= And badly
taking some evangelical saying he puts it forward as a witness of such
absurdity, saying that "No one has gone up into heaven, except he who came
down from heaven, the Son of man," =202.12= as even before he came down he was
son of man, and came down bringing that flesh of his own which he happened to
have in the heavens, some pre-eternal and co-essential.

=202.13= For he says again some apostolic saying, having nipped it from the
whole body of the context, that "the second man from heaven." =202.14= Then he
constructs that that man who has come from above does not have the mind, but
that the Godhead of the Only-begotten, having filled up the nature of the
mind, has become the third part of the human mixture, soul and body being
about him according to the human, but mind not being, but the Word of God
filling up that place of it. =202.15= And this is not yet the terrible thing;
but the most grievous of all, that he constructs the only-begotten God
himself, the judge of all, the prince of life, the overthrower of death, to be
mortal, =202.16= and to have received passion in his own Godhead, and in that
three-day deadness of the body the Godhead also to have been deadened together
with the body, and so again to have been raised from death by the Father.
=202.17= And the rest of as many things as he adds to such absurdities it
would be long to go through. =202.18= If then those who think such things come
to be in authority to gather, let your thought well-reputed in Christ consider
that, we not agreeing with what they think, their taking authority of assembly
is nothing other than to be thought more true than our doctrine. =202.19= For
if as being pious they are permitted to teach as they think and to proclaim in
boldness the things according to themselves, it is clear that the word of the
Church has been condemned, as the truth being with them. =202.20= For two
contrary


words about the same matter do not have it in nature to be true. =202.21= How
then has your great-natured and high mind endured not to use the accustomed
boldness toward correction of so great an evil? =202.22= But if this has not
happened formerly, let your inimitable perfection in virtue at least now rise
up and teach the most pious emperor that there will be no gain of the rest of
his zeal concerning the Churches, if such an evil, for the overthrowing of the
healthy faith, prevails through their boldness.
