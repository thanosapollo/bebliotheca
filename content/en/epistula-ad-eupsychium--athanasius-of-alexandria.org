=26.1245=.50 Athanasius indeed in the aforementioned letter to Eupsychius,
whose beginning is:
Upon the things you thought us, most pious, after other things he says: Common
is the fruit of the sheep; and this is the back harvest, the wool set forth
=26.1248= for common use to all. But when it has associated with the dye from
the sea, it is called purple, both the appellation being exchanged, and the
use having obtained to fit according to an exception to kings alone. And it is
wools, and it is not; in nature that which it was before, in use no longer.
For it flees the commonness because of the dignity of the one using; so then
also the flesh, taken from the common nature, since it has become a wrapping
of a king, has been deemed worthy of the same glory with the one using, even
if it did not become the nature. So that he is reasonably called Lord of glory
even insofar as man; the nature according to itself admitting the suffering,
and the despite going over upon him who used the flesh as a garment. For as he
who has torn the purple pays the punishment as having attacked the king
himself; and yet the king himself has suffered nothing terrible, but the
suffering of the purple has been brought up upon him; so also the suffering of
the flesh is said to have come upon him in the impassibility of the Word
through the despite. And for this reason Paul hands down the Master Christ,
insofar as man, as Son of God, and before this Gabriel the archangel,
announcing the paradoxical birth to Mary, Hail, he says, you who have been
graced, the Lord is with you. Behold you will have in the womb, and you will
bear a son, and they will call him Jesus; this one will be great, and will be
called Son of the Highest. So that Jesus is called Son of God, not the flesh
having been changed into a divine nature, but having taken the homonymous
dignity by the union toward the God Word.
