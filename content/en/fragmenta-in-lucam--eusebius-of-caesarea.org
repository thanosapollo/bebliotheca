=24.529= OF EUSEBIUS OF CAESAREA ON THE GOSPEL ACCORDING TO LUKE. "In the days
of Herod the king of Judaea." For the tribe of Judah was from of old the
ruling tribe, and it led all until they were autonomous, according to the
prophecy. And they were free and autonomous until the times of Augustus, in
whose time, when Christ appeared, the whole nation was enslaved; when also
Herod, a foreigner, first ruled them, and king Augustus, who also entrusted
the rule to him together with the senate, held the sceptres of the inhabited
world. And Herod was born of Antipater, of the race of an Ascalonite, a
temple-servant of Apollo, and of Cyprine a mother from the Arabs. For this
man, having slain Hyrcanus who last ruled from the succession of the high
priests, is proclaimed king of the Jews. Of this man Luke also now makes
mention, in order to show that Christ truly came, when the rulers among the
Jews had failed according to the prophecy.
And Zacharias said to the angel: Whereby shall I know this? What sign do you
show me, he says, that I may be persuaded of the things prophesied by you, as
about to come to pass? For the thing said is almost contrary to nature; for
those advanced in years do not beget. But he ought not to have looked to his
own old age and that of his wife, but to the power of the God who was
promising. But great things are hard of access for men, and God pardons; for
not even Abraham himself believed as he ought, but at first both laughed and
said: "If to one a hun =24.532= dred years old shall a son be born?" and Sarah
also, having laughed, was then blamed. For sometimes God blames by word alone,
and sometimes he also rebukes by deed. For this reason then he also rebuked
Zacharias by deed, and brought him round into deafness and speechlessness; for
he ought to have been instructed through the former things, and to have made
the blame against the fathers his own instruction.
I am Gabriel. What then does the angel say to him? Do you disbelieve an angel,
he says, who stands before God? and you do not believe the one who sent him,
but look to the impossibility of the thing? Therefore because of this
unbelieving voice you shall be deprived of voice until the thing is fulfilled,
knowing both your unbelief and the power of God.
The Lord God will give him the throne of David his father. He calls throne the
one promised to David, not indeed also given: "For once," he says, "I swore in
my holy one, if I shall lie to David; his seed remains forever, and his throne
as the sun before me"; and again: "I swore to David my servant: Forever I will
prepare your seed, and I will build up your throne unto generation and
generation." And these things he says not concerning fleshly seed, nor
concerning a sensible throne, but concerning that of which the angel also
speaks to the Virgin, that which has nothing temporary, that which shines out
like light over the whole inhabited world, and intellectually illumines souls
through the inspired teaching. And house of Jacob, do not think only the
people of the Jews, but all who through the calling of the Saviour from all
the nations are adopted into the sonship of the saints. The people of God is
sometimes called Jacob, sometimes Israel; for the same one was both Israel and
Jacob; and by nature the old people was Jacob, as from Jacob according to
kinship of blood; but by adoption, the new people according to kinship from
virtue; for the new was brought in in place of the old. For the rest then upon
this naton having sinned they mourned, with repentance and confession and a
temperate life gon having recognized him, and voluntarily subjected to him;
for Christ was king also as God: "For my kingdom," he says, "is not of this
world"; and he was also as man; for he had the works of a king, to legislate
for his subjects, to order, to tend, to die for them; which things especially
characterize


to order, to tend, to die for them; which things especially characterize the
true king.
The Holy Spirit will come upon you. God, he says, is the one who works; do not
doubt; he who both strengthens nature that has grown old and weak, and makes
nature new, when he wills. Mary said: Behold the handmaid of the Lord, etc. I
am the handmaid of the Lord; I am a tablet being written; let the writer write
=24.533= what he wills; let the Lord of all do what he wishes.
A light for revelation of the Gentiles. See how he already speaks also of the
salvation of the Gentiles that is to be; for the nations, he says, await his
coming.
The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he anointed me. The Saviour,
beginning the beatitudes, evangelized to the poor the heavenly wealth and the
kingdom of the heavens; and Isaiah the prophet, taking these things in
advance, made mention of them, prophesying the future thus from the very
person of the Saviour: "The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he anointed
me, he has sent me to evangelize to the poor." He signified outright, then, a
gospel of good things to the poor; therefore as soon as he appeared to men who
had been anointed with the Holy Spirit according to the human, he also from
that point began the teaching, evangelizing a heavenly kingdom to those who
are poor through him.
Who came to hear him, and to be healed of their diseases. See then at present,
having begun from above, going through the sayings of the Saviour, and
receiving from him the teachings as certain laws, through which one may obtain
the heavenly kingdom; and not stopping at learning, but also advancing through
works, so that they may not fall away from the promises; because the kingdom
of God is not in word, but in power.
Blessed are the poor, for yours is the kingdom of God. Except that the kingdom
of the heavens is conceived in many ranks of good things, according to "Many
mansions with the Father," the first dignity, as one might say, and the first
step of those going up, is that of those who are poor for God's sake. And
these would be those who have made light of all the honours of the present
life, who are poor in moderation of mind, who have also driven to the extreme
of possessionlessness through the philosophy according to God, they will
obtain the saving beatitude. And such he made those first disciples of his;
wherefore also it was fitted to their person according to one of the
evangelists, when he said: "Blessed are the poor, for yours is the kingdom of
God"; for "yours" was said demonstratively toward those present. And upon them
he also lifted his eyes, and before the word drawing them to the hearing by a
cheerful look; and they readily offered themselves as hearers of what was
said. This then was the first step of the saving beatitudes, the one that
presents as reigning with him those who have taken up poverty for his sake,
and the life that is extremely without possessions, both philosophical and
dear to God, or rather the humble-minded. Second then and third beatitudes are
listed next =24.536= by him, and fourth and so on. For he taught that it is
possible to be ambitious to be counted worthy of the things promised according
to each one of the named ways of life; so that it is possible, having come to
be in the kingdom of God, even if not to reign with Christ according to those
poor in spirit, yet at least to be comforted for those things unto death for
which they sinned and mourned, having given themselves over by repentance and
confession and a temperate life; or to obtain the promised land, and to become
inhabitants and citizens of the city of God above, having been shown meek and
patient of evil; or having hungered in the present life, and thirsted, to
obtain righteousness itself, and to be filled according to the promised age,
whenever they enjoy the essential virtue itself pure, seeing as substantial
the righteousness of God, and seeing as substantial the righteousness of God,
and the very wisdom of God, and the eternal life, and that which illumines
souls; all which things are conceived according to the


powers existing in the only-begotten Word of God.
But if some have otherwise become lovers of men and merciful, showing
compassion and fellowship toward their neighbours, they will suffer the same
as they have done, being shown mercy according to the mercy and love of men of
God. And another rank of those blessed according to the promise would be that
of those purified in heart, that is in understanding; and another again of the
peacemakers; of whom the former will receive as a prize of purity the vision
of God, inasmuch as they will see him with purified eyes of soul; and the
second will become sons of God, as having accomplished a like work; such as
also the Son of God made peace of all things through himself, both the things
in heaven and the things on earth. But these, upon different achievements,
will obtain different honours in the announced kingdom of the heavens. Those
at any rate who will reign with the Son of God himself were first the poor in
spirit who have been indicated; and second after those the noble martyrs of
God, concerning whom he taught next saying: "Blessed are those who have been
persecuted for the sake of righteousness"; for through these he blessed those
who were going to contend for the kingdom as noble soldiers of God;
necessarily promising also to them as brothers of the previously blessed poor
the prizes of the contests, the kingdom of the heavens. And with much order
and sequence he imparted to these of his own kingdom after those poor in
spirit; for one must first live in a manner dear to God, having spent the
whole time of life in a possessionless and philosophical way, and then thus
come to the end dear to God. The first then pursued the extreme possessionless
conduct of life, competing through all their life in the contests of virtue;
and the second for the sake of the witness to him =24.537= were driven, being
warred upon and hated, and being slandered with false accusations for no other
reason than for the confession of Christ.
But we shall see those who hunger and weep and are reproached for Christ also
in Luke; and Matthew sets before us for examination the meek and the merciful
and the pure in heart and the peacemakers. And he said these things arming and
fencing the disciples for the war against the adversaries, which they were
going to draw upon themselves as they proclaimed to all the world, and bore
witness to the kingdom of the one God over all. For since they were going,
because of the new proclamation, to be driven and warred upon by all, he
secured them in advance and fenced them and counted them worthy of beatitudes;
because indeed, putting forward an unassailable life, so as not even to
furnish occasions of slander to enemies hunting pretexts against them;
nevertheless because of the confession of him alone, and for the sake of his
commandments, they would be persecuted and warred upon, being ill-spoken of
and enduring all railings and insults and reproaches, not through their own
faults, but through their virtuous and extreme life in righteousness; which
thing also, having been said according to the divine providence, obtained a
true end in the time after these things. Yet he heals the grievous part of the
word, showing them equal in honour to the prophets of God of old. Wherefore he
also exhorted them to rejoice and be glad in all that was coming upon them, as
being about to suffer the like things as the prophets, and clearly to be
counted worthy likewise with them of the honour with God. Hence reasonably
Paul, taking heart from these things, said: "If indeed we suffer with him,
that we may also be glorified with him"; and, "The sufferings of the present
time are not worthy to be compared with the glory about to be revealed to us."
And he did not say these things in words, yet fall behind the things said in
works, but also by the very deeds he showed the contests on behalf of piety,
which he also enumerated saying: "Therefore I take pleasure in weaknesses, in
insults," and the rest. And these things were still the preludes of the
contests; but the conclusion of the whole struggle was completed for him in
the city of the Romans, where by martyrdom his head was cut off. And Peter
counted the death through the cross a prize through


the saving hopes; and James likewise has his head cut off with the sword in
Jerusalem; and John was condemned to dwell on the island of Patmos; and
another in another way, having shown the purity and unfeignedness of their
obedience to his words, endured a different end of life. And they suffered
these things not against their will, counting the hope of the saving promises
sweeter than all pleasure; since at any rate once the rulers of the nation of
the Jews, having seized them, subjected them to the outrages of scourges, as
the history concerning them testifies, how they went out from the council
rejoicing, because they had been counted worthy to be dishonoured for the
=24.540= name of Christ. And they did this, fulfilling his commandment, since
he taught them to rejoice when they suffer for him. And he showed them equal
in honour to the prophets, saying according to Matthew: "For so they
persecuted those before you"; as these too being prophets, falling short in
time, yet being equalled to those through the likeness of suffering. Since of
those also one was slain with the sword, another with stones, another was
thrown to lions, another was cast in a pit of mire; and another had his body
cut with a saw; and others were shut in a furnace of fire. Wherefore the
Saviour exhorted his acquaintances to take heart in the endurance of terrible
things, having reminded them in season of what the prophets of God have
suffered; so that through the comparison of those they too might sketch for
themselves the same hopes: "For when," he says, "they slander you, rejoice;
since the prophets also underwent the same."
And all the people having heard, and the tax-collectors, justified God, having
been baptized with the baptism of John. But the Pharisees and the lawyers set
aside the counsel of God for themselves, not having been baptized by him. And
he alludes to both these also when he says in a parable: "What do you think? A
certain man had two children; and coming to the first he said: Child, go, work
today in my vineyard. And he answering said, I will not; but later, having
changed his mind, he went. And coming to the other he said likewise; and he
answering said, I, lord; and he did not go. Which of the two did the will of
the father? They say to him, The first." For here, having presented two ranks
by the word, one the Israelite, and the other that of the foreign nations
indicated through the tax-collectors, he named both two children of one
father, since from one God are all, both Jews and Greeks. And he called first
those from the nations, and second those from the circumcision; since before
Israel the nations existed, and to the nations first, when there was not yet
even an Israelite name among men, the oracles of God and the theophanies were
supplied. For Enoch was a Gentile, being uncircumcised, who received as a
prize of well-pleasing the translation from among men; and Noah, having become
righteous in his generation, was counted worthy of oracles of God, being
uncircumcised himself also; and Melchizedek, having been older than the people
from the circumcision, was declared priest of God most high; and Abraham and
Isaac and Jacob before Israel were counted worthy of oracles of God; and Job
outright, being an Idumaean of the sons of Esau, shone with deeds of
godliness. The remaining rank then of the nations, being commanded by natural
laws to work in the vineyard, and this was the polity of godliness, refused
and spoke against the paternal command, idol-worshipping the whole =24.541=
time; yet at the end it showed the obedience; but the nation of the Jews,
which was the second child, called after the refusal of the former to the same
work through Moses and the prophets, showed the eagerness in word; for they
said, "We will do all things and we will hear"; but in deed the very opposite.
Wherefore the Saviour inquires of the high priests: Which then of these two
did the will of the father? And when those confessed that it was the first, he
makes clear who this first was, saying: The tax-collectors and the harlots and
all the rank of the unbelieving nations go before you into the love of God.
And you


whom, if not the high priests and elders, and all their nation, which indeed
in word promises to be godly, but in deeds acts against the promise? Wherefore
those from the nations, having turned from their former wickedness, and having
shown fruits worthy of the kingdom, will obtain it; but you, who say that you
yourselves are children of God, will be shut out of the kingdom; because you
did not believe him, but the tax-collectors and the harlots believed.
Wherefore they also justified God; for he appeared just in all that he did,
thrusting you away as disobedient and ungrateful, and choosing those as
obedient and well-disposed. And you have no longer to accuse him, which the
prophet says: "That you may be justified in your words."
To go away into the abyss. Because divine words call the so-called tartarus an
abyss. And a woman being in a flow of blood for twelve years, etc. But I do
not think it worthy to pass by a narrative worthy of being remembered also by
those after us. For they said that the woman with the issue of blood was from
Paneas, and that her house is shown in the city, and that wondrous trophies of
the benefit done to her by the Saviour remain. For there stands on a high
stone toward the gates of her house a bronze figure of a woman, kneeling and
with hands stretched forward, like one making supplication; and opposite this
another upright figure of a man of the same material, decently wrapped in a
double cloak, and stretching out the hand to the woman; at whose feet on the
stele a strange kind of plant has grown, which, going up as far as the hem of
the bronze double cloak, is an antidote of all kinds of diseases. This statue
they said bears the image of Jesus, which Maximinus of =24.544= his own
impiety made a side-work; he made. And so much for this. But let us go on to
the next of the word.
And having called together his twelve disciples, he gave them power and
authority, etc. For these reasons the Lord armed his own disciples with
inspired powers, so that first they might overthrow the demonic companies and
the hostile and warlike arrays of men; then heal those worn down by diseases
and all kinds of sufferings; furnishing tokens of the kingdom being proclaimed
for all men to see. For it was necessary, as they announced a paradoxical
proclamation, to furnish clear signs of strange and new good things to those
being discipled. And they were truly proofs of the kingdom of God, the
portentous powers, both the wonders and the saving healings of souls and
bodies; because every word, empty of works, would never have strength to
implant firm persuasion in the hearers. Wherefore by the deeds accomplished
through works they furnished clear faith to the proclamation. If then with
bare little words and voices brought forth at random they cried a kingdom of
God to all, and a dissolution of the ancestral gods, who would have given them
his mind? and whom would they have persuaded that Jesus is God as they
announced, bringing no faith to the testimony? Wherefore the Saviour,
anticipating every word with divine deeds, first chose his own disciples,
unpractised in manner, inexperienced in words and mortal wisdom, for the
setting right of the whole inhabited world; and then, having prophesied that
he would make them fishers of human souls, he himself became the maker of the
promise; whence he made them workers of paradoxical wonders, handing over to
them these saving hooks of souls, so that through them they might net and draw
up the mortal race from the depth of evil, putting to flight wicked spirits,
and healing every disease and every infirmity at his command; for the Spirit
was not yet given.
Take nothing for the way, neither staffs, nor a wallet. According to Matthew
then he forbade them the possession of gold and silver and bronze, by
foreknowledge of the future; for he saw together that those who would be
healed of diseases by them and would be delivered from incurable sufferings
would wish even to give over to them of all their belongings


wherefore having taken these things in advance he charged them, exhorting them
not to traffic the grace nor to sell the gifts from God; and according to the
rest and the same Matthew not permitting them to bring either a staff, or a
wallet, or sandals, or two tunics, nor even bread, nor silver, as they were
about to be sent into a foreign land, he took a test of their faith and
eagerness, whether, having been commanded to pursue the extremely
possessionless life, so as not even to make provision of the daily food, nor
to bring a second tunic, nor to use sandals, being about to go about the whole
inhabited world, they would not flee the commandments; but also wishing them
to be un =24.545= avaricious and without care of life. He testified these
things, so that the gifts from God would in this way be given to them, if they
too from themselves should prepare the corresponding life worthy of the
acquisition of the divine gifts; for it is fitting to make some exchange and
to give in return, receiving from God the saving and beneficial powers for
men, and giving back to him obedience of his commandments, and eagerness and a
purpose free of sympathy toward the things of life. But also, receiving
heavenly wealth into their souls, and being supplied with earnests of the
kingdom of God, he thought they ought to despise the things on earth; and to
consider neither gold, nor money, nor any of the things honoured among men
worthy of the heavenly wealth provided to them. But also, making them soldiers
of the kingdom of God, he prepared them well-girt for the war against those
who oppose. For these reasons he exhorted his soldiers to be without
possessions; since "No one serving as a soldier entangles himself in the
affairs of life, that he may please the one who enlisted him."
And into whatever house you enter, remain there. The Lord, however, since he
forbade the disciples the possession of money and gold and silver and a second
tunic, and of every substance, next in sequence according to Matthew
necessarily made clear how the things for food would be provided for them;
wherefore he added saying: "The labourer is worthy of his food." And he said
this permitting them to share only food with those who wish to give them a
share; and he allowed them to carry away so much as was sufficient for a man
who has hired himself out for daily work to partake of food. Thus then also it
was permitted them not to exact wages of the benefits, "For freely," he says,
"you received, freely give"; but in return for the leisure and labour about
the proclamation, and in return for the toils of the body, which they were
likely to endure as they were sent on so great a journey, they ought to
partake only of necessary food; and not to share even this with chance people,
nor to take untested from those willing to provide, but to honour only the
worthy with the taking; to whom also, taking in advance a very great
recompense of the food, they give in return, evangelizing peace to them and
the reconciliations of God. And these things he exhorted in sequence to the
soldiers of his kingdom; testifying in advance not to desire money, nor any
possession at all small or great, but to drive to the extreme of
possessionlessness; and of the daily food not to share with all, or with the
worthy alone; and not even from these to make the partaking gratis, but having
first lent and first benefited with the blessings from themselves.
Having fenced the disciples with inspired powers and with the philosophical
exhortations, having made them well-girt as soldiers of the kingdom of God, he
sent them out, teachers and physicians to the Jews, who would be procurers and
heralds of countless good things for them. And they did according to these
things, and went about everywhere, as teachers =24.548= evangelizing, and as
physicians healing, and confirming the words through the works and the signs.
And Herod the


tetrarch heard all the things being done by him. At the presence of the
Saviour, the things of the kingdom from the ancestors had already been
dissolved for the Jews; and the things of autonomy and freedom had been
dissolved, and those of the lawful priesthood. And the rulers according to the
laws were no longer theirs; but Herod and his children Agrippa and Archelaus,
foreigners in race, had been entrusted by the Romans with the rule over them;
and their country had been divided into a tetrarchy; and the whole nation had
been made tributary to the Romans, procurators collecting the census head by
head at times with a military hand. Wherefore, having commanded his disciples
to announce the proclamation of the kingdom of God first to them, the Saviour
named them lost sheep of the house of Israel.
Whoever is ashamed of me and my words, of this one the Son of man will be
ashamed. Having spoken the things said concerning the need for them too to
become imitators of him, and not to flee death, but to be unsparing even of
their own soul and life for the sake of the hope in him, he joins on in season
the things concerning his second theophany, handing mysteries over to them.
And very sequentially and necessarily he said these things to those who were
going to go through tortures for him, and to contend even to death, presenting
to them what the fruit of so great a struggle will be. For those who were
going to follow him and go behind him, and to take up their own cross
according to his imitation, needed to know accurately what this one is for
whom they are going to suffer so many things. And in season he named the one
coming in these things Son of man, for a reminder of the prophecy from Daniel,
which said: "And behold, one like a Son of man was coming upon the clouds; and
he came as far as the Ancient of days. And to him was given the rule and the
honour and the kingdom." But when Peter confessed him to be Christ, and Son of
the living God, from then he began to show them that he must suffer many
things and be killed; and when he himself exhorted them to hold death in
contempt, and taught that he must suffer many things, then in season he handed
over the words concerning his second and glorious coming; so that they might
be able to know who it was who was going to suffer so many things from the
Jews, and what the fruit of the struggle for him would be for them.
And having discoursed these things to the disciples, and having spoken out to
them the great and hidden mystery of his second theophany, lest he seem to
entrust it only in words and bare phrases, he necessarily proceeded to the
works, showing them with their own eyes the image of his divine =24.549=
kingdom. Wherefore having said that he will be ashamed of the one who was
ashamed of him, when he comes in the glory of the Father, he next joins on
saying: "But I say to you truly, there are some of those standing here," etc.
Having taken Peter and John and James. And in the transfiguration three only
were counted worthy to behold the kingdom of the heavens that appeared to them
in power; but at the consummation of the age, whenever the Lord arrives with
the paternal glory, no longer will Moses only and Elijah escort him, nor will
three only of the disciples be with him, but all prophets and patriarchs and
righteous; and not onto a high mountain, but into heaven he will lead up those
worthy of his godhead. Then his godhead will shine not as the sun, but beyond
every conceivable generated light both among sensibles and among
intelligibles; since he himself is the light that enlightens every man coming
into the world; when he will also show his face; for he will not then do as of
old he once said to Moses, that "You shall see my back parts, but my face
shall not be seen by you"; but he will so present himself to the saints that
all can say: "But we, with unveiled face beholding as in a mirror the glory of
the Lord, are transformed into the same image from glory to glory." And then


a cloud will not cry out, nor through a cloud will the Father bear witness to
the Son, but he himself through himself apart from every overshadowing, and
apart from every interpreter, will by the very deed glorify his only-begotten
Son upon all his saints, having shown him fellow-throned with himself and
fellow-king, and having set him above every rule; nor any longer as then the
three disciples only on the mountain, having heard the voice, fell on their
face and were afraid, but every knee will bow of things heavenly and earthly
and under the earth.
A voice came from the cloud saying, etc. A paternal voice through a cloud, for
thus God appears, bore witness to Christ of the sonship; for it was necessary
that it not be known from Peter only that he himself is the Christ the Son of
the living God; nor that he himself only had borne witness to Peter as having
received the knowledge concerning him from the Father in the heavens; but that
the paternal voice itself also seal the truth of the word, bearing witness
that he is Son of God, and exhorting that one must hear him.
If the son of peace is there, your peace will rest upon him. You see how he
exhorted them to examine in advance and not to give themselves out to all
without restraint, but also to begin first the benefit from themselves, and
first to give of the things they have? And this peace was a voice fitting for
soldiers of God, who have taken victory-tokens against enemies. Pursuit of
enemies, then, and overthrow of foes, and friendly reconciliations of God, the
name of pea =24.552= ce procured for the worthy. This peace therefore, having
first lent it to the souls of those who received them, they were to partake of
the food from them; wherefore he necessarily charged in advance not to enter
every house, nor to judge every man worthy of their reception; but to ask
first whether there is anyone in the city or the country attested by good
deeds and a reverent life, as able to receive the royal soldiers with
guest-gifts of virtue. And whenever such a one is found, going in under his
roof, they are to lay down first the proper arms of the campaign; and these
were the symbols of peace; wherefore they are also to count the host himself,
and all his house, worthy of peaceful greetings; then to remain there, and not
to move, nor to change houses from houses. For this is a sign of an
examination made accurate; for the easy and undiscriminating is not firm nor
of a steadfast manner. Wherefore he exhorted them not first to lodge, then to
ask and inquire, then having insulted the guest to pass to another. And if
this one is found unworthy of the reception, again to move from there, drawing
on a willing enmity, together with casting their peace upon the unworthy. But
first to search out the worthy accurately; then having thus entered, to give a
share of the goods of peace, and to provide ungrudgingly not only to the one
attested, but also to all the house because of him. But if, when some worthy
man has been found and has received the soldier of the kingdom, it should
happen at times, as often is accustomed to happen in the house of a godly man,
that parents or brothers or others belonging by race oppose and make war, not
only on the one girt, but also on the one who has honoured him with the
reception, necessarily the Saviour did not pass by the mention of these

Do not be anxious for your soul what you shall eat, etc. And these things as
our Lord encourages us, that one must not be anxious about food nor for the
needs of the body, and not exhorting irrationally, but comforting the teaching
with a rational presentation; for he exhorted saying: "Do not be anxious what
you shall eat or what you shall put on. Is not the soul more than the food,
and the body than the clothing?" And this is the first syllogism, concluding
that one ought not to be anxious about himself. For if the soul of a man,
being a rational and intellectual substance, is greater and better and more
honourable than food that grows from the earth, and God brought this, which
was not, into being, and imparted it to us who were anxious about nothing,
having made us living and rational, what need is there to be concerned about
the worse, which is the food? For he who has given the better will not be
unable also to provide what is needful for it. And upon the covering of the
body the same argument. For if it is confessed that the body of the living
being is by an unplaced comparison better than the clothing lying around it
from without, and the Creator himself constituted the whole body partaking of
life and soul; what need is there to be distressed lest he be unable to
provide the worse, and this was the clothing, he who established the better?
This then is the first demonstrative syllogism. And he uses a second example
saying: "Look at the birds of heaven, that they do not sow, and your heavenly
Father feeds them. Do you not differ from them more?" For if for these, of
whom he is not father, and these were the birds, God the guardian and provider
of all provided the sufficient food, how will he not make every care of life
for you who have been made according to his image, being father because of the
intellectual and rational substance in you? and he will feed you without
anxiety, as a father providing from the ready the things for need to sons more
than to the birds of heaven, for whom =24.557= without toil unsown and
untilled the things for food are procured, your Father both granting them life
and food together.
And very accurately, for the exhortation of his disciples, he did not use a
simple example of the irrational animals; he did not then mention reptiles nor
cattle nor even fishes, nor did he simply say birds, since of these too some
eat flesh, some feed on earth, some eat one another. Wherefore having set
apart from the scavengers and flesh-eaters the seed-eating of the birds, and
having indicated these through saying: "They do not sow nor reap nor gather
into storehouses, and your heavenly Father feeds them"; and having shown of
these themselves the high-flying through addressing them as birds of heaven,
he urged his disciples to the likeness of the food of these; together also
sending them to the proof of the providence that oversees all, so that they
might be able to infer from the example that their heavenly Father will take
thought of their food much more than of the birds of heaven. And calling
Father heavenly, he taught a very great dogma. For since there is a father of
bodies, the one who sowed the flesh for each, and there is also a heavenly
Father who established in us the rational and intellectual power, what need is
there to be anxious? Is it not to be accurately persuaded that the father who
is by nature and better will provide for us much sooner than for the birds of
heaven, which, through his providence extending even as far as them, have such
abundance of food from the ready that they are well-fed and strong and
vigorous? And according to Luke the Lord, saying: "Consider the ravens,"
indicates something more. For for the seed-picking of the winged the food is
readier, but for the flesh-eaters, as the ravens, more difficult; yet
nevertheless not even


such as these lack food through the Providence extended everywhere. And he
used a third syllogism to the same saying: "And which of you by being anxious
is able to add to his stature one cubit? If then you are not able to do even a
very little, why are you anxious about the rest?" But if no one by his own
care ever devised an addition of the body for himself, nor even to the
appointed time of the span of life would anyone, having become anxious, be
able, having come to the end of life, to invent for himself an interval of one
day or a very short season of an hour; what need is there to be anxious
superfluously, when one ought, having entrusted it to God, to await his will?
For if he wills us to grow, we grow, having been anxious about nothing; and if
he wills us to live and be long-lived, we shall live and become many-yeared,
having been anxious about nothing; but if he does not will one of lives to
remain, all care of man is vain. =24.560=
And these things he ordered concerning food; and likewise he also exhorted
concerning clothing saying: "Consider the lilies how they grow," etc. Again
here too he concludes with a syllogism, persuading them to expect that
clothing will altogether be given them by God; for if anyone among men is
ambitious in costly dress and love of beauty, behold, he says, with the eyes,
how God the maker of all, having extended his many-coloured wisdom even as far
as the flowers that grow from the earth, adorned these with all kinds of
colours and ornaments, having transformed the earth, and the lifeless dust
itself, and having changed its form, with dyes far better than purple and
gold, having set in it the adornment from himself in thin and well-flowered
membranes; so that no luxurious king, not even Solomon himself who was cried
among the ancients for wisdom and wealth and luxury, was found to have such
art-loving skill as to acquire a covering most like to one of the fair flowers
from the earth. Why then must one wonder, if he who clothed in this way the
grass of the field that is useful for nothing, whose end is destruction
through fire, will also provide for you yourselves sensible garments in the
present life?

For the powers of the heavens will be shaken. The powers that served the
former age in the constitution of the former world, and that oversaw the
sensible parts of the all, then will be shaken, so that they may obtain a
better station. For until now they serve, ministering to corruptible bodies,
but according to the new age they too will be freed from the slavery of
corruption into the freedom of the glory of the children of God. For upon the
present the creation groans, serving the corruption of the sensible bodies,
and keeping its own station, in which the Lord of all established it; but
then, according to the saving theophany having been freed from the present
station, it will pass over to the better things.
And when these things begin to come about, look up and lift your heads. And he
says these things toward the disciples, not as they themselves being about to
live, and to remain in life until the consummation, but as their body and ours
being one, and of those after these things until the consummation who have
believed in him, and because of him have been humbled according to the present
life. Then then, having received the promises for which we hoped, we who then
were bent down will look up, and we who of old were humbled will lift our
heads. For our expected redemption will be that very one, which also all the
creation expects according to the apostolic teaching, which also Paul awaiting
groans serving with the saving voices. And when the redemption draws near, he
taught accurately saying, that when these things begin to come about. For if
not beginning, but the things said already proceeding to works, it is no
longer likely that the redemption draws near, but is completed in works. And
the divine Apostle teaches that then there will be redemption and freedom also
of the whole creation itself, because of the transformation of it too that is
going to come about to the better. Wherefore he says: "And the creation itself
will be freed from the slavery of corruption into the freedom of the glory of
the children of God." For when all the children of God, incorruptible and
immortal, have inhabited the new age, it was necessary surely also for the
creation itself that now serves the corruptible nature to change into an
incorruptible substance, having been freed from the present condition.

And of what sort signs, Luke yielded to Matthew to speak out. For it was
necessary, as the great luminary, the God Word himself, was about to make his
second presence with power and much glory, that the sun and the moon and the
stars should withdraw and recede, so that instead of all the Son of man
himself, having shone, might enlighten the new age, the former fashion of the
world having passed according to the apostolic witness, which says: "For the
fashion of this world passes"; whenever then this fashion should pass, the sun
and the moon and the stars will pass with it. And the sun will then be
darkened, not so as to be deprived of its own light, but being dimmed by
comparison of the better; he himself then will obtain an exchange of his
labours, becoming sevenfold; and the moon herself too will be as the sun; and
the stars also will obtain a like change to the better. And the sun and the
moon will then be idle; nor will there then be any need of stars, night not
being; and they too will rest having been freed with the heaven; and thus the
new age will be sunless and moonless, and starless, a day of our light
illumining the holy souls.
And what the things coming upon the inhabited world after the luminaries are
darkened, and why, and from where the distress of the nations will be, he
presents next saying: "In perplexity of a roaring sea and surge, men
fainting." And it seems to me to teach that the beginning of the then coming
about turning of the all will be from a failure of the moist substance. For
this first having been drunk up or frozen, so that a sound of sea will no
longer be heard, nor the surge of its waves come about through exceeding
drought, the remaining parts of the world, no longer partaking of the
customary exhalation sent up from the moist substance, will suffer a turning
and change; so that from this there will be signs in sun and moon, and stars.
And a recalling of the all having come about, and a consummation of the world,
and this appearing fashion passing, for the first things will pass so that the
second may stand, it was sequential that there be also some turning of the

announced to the Lord." Wherefore demonstratively looking to his apostles, and

and through them throughout the inhabited world, he said: "This generation

Take heed to yourselves lest your hearts be weighed down in surfeit. He raises
the disciples to being always ready, preparing themselves for his presence,
toward which he himself, urging us, made all the words of the teaching; and he
exhorts to be sober and to watch, and to be sleepless through all the night of
mortal life, because the time of the consummation is known to no one. But for
you at least, he says, the weight from here must be guarded against, so that
you may be able to be sober, lest that day stand suddenly upon you; concerning
which also the holy Apostle exhorted writing: "Brothers, you know accurately,
that the day of the Lord, as a thief in the night, so comes. And when they say
peace and safety, then sudden destruction stands upon them. But you are not in
darkness, that the day should overtake you as a thief; for you all are sons of
light and sons of day." We must then watch not only because of the sudden and
unexpected arrival of the Master, but also because of the thief who lies in
wait. And the thief is the time of the end of each; or also the angel who is
going to receive the soul of each. For as a snare the presence of the Lord
will come upon all those on the earth; those then who take heed to themselves
are saved, as a gazelle from snares, and as a bird from a trap; but those
weighed down with drunkenness, and sleeping unto death, fall out into
unexpected danger.
Watch then at every time praying, etc. The Apostle, agreeing with these
things, said: "For this reason take up the full armour of God, that you may be
able to withstand in the evil day, and having worked all things to stand.
Stand then having girded your loin in truth." That you may eat and drink at my
table. Of such good things will be full a table, of which those will partake
for whom he appointed the Covenant because they remained with him in the
trials; that they may eat and drink at his table; being fed with heavenly
bread nutritively of holy souls, and partaking of wine of the new produce of
the true vine; which God of all himself, farming, will provide the new produce
from it to those then worthy.

Two men stood by them in garments flashing. They furnish to see signs of joy
and laughter and tokens of the saving resurrection also through the white
wrapping, so that from the figure too the women may infer the brightness of
the raising, and begin the feast of Pascha with the white-clad angels. For
Moses, being about to minister plagues of Egyptians, correspondingly saw in a
flame of fire the angel upon the bush; and the same, legislating for wicked
men, said that God is a consuming fire, hinting that he would be punitive and
a chastiser for them; wherefore also in a pillar of fire God led them. Thus he
taught that for the impious the angels become a flame of fire saying: "He who
makes his ministers a flame of fire." But not such did the women see those
within the tomb, but some calm and bright, such as it was necessary to appear
upon a Master's joy and kingdom. For upon the passion of the Saviour the sun
failed, wrapping darkness around the all, showing symbols of wailings and
mourning to those who crucified the Son of God; and none of the angels
appeared at that time as was likely to the disciples, they themselves
similarly having been scattered here and there. And if indeed someone ought
also to have appeared, perhaps somewhere he showed the dress opposite to the
white-clad men; at once then upon the passion not only did the sun darken, but
also the Saviour having cried with a great voice gave up the spirit, the veil
of the temple was torn in two, all but the angels themselves too who of old
inhabited the temple having rent their garments, and having rent the reverend
fabric, so that, left naked and desolate of their guard, the place of the
untrodden and inmost things might be made ready for enemies and foes. But
these things happened upon the passion; but upon the saving resurrection, as
angels of life and heralds of the resurrection, they tended the place where
the body lay, having partaken as from the very touch of the place of a
holiness better than according to themselves, and sharing in his resurrection.
Wherefore they showed bright and smiling faces, and a figure of a saving
feast, being white-clad. For a divine descent is said to have come about upon
the mountain at the time when he legislated, so that all might behold the
energy of God; for this is a clear descent, not a local one. The things
growing upon the mountain then being burned vehemently, the fire consumed
nothing; and voices of trumpets were heard together more vehemently, such
instruments not being set forth, nor of the one who voiced, but all things
coming about by a divine construction. So that it is clear through these
things that the descent came about as divine, and that God without anyone
shows his own magnificence through all.
