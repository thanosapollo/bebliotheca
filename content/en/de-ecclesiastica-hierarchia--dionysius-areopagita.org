<3>

Every hierarchy is, according to the august character of our sacred tradition,
the whole account of the sacred things subject to it, the most catholic
summing-up of the sacred things of this hierarchy or of that. The hierarchy
according to us, then, is said to be and is the comprehensive treatment of all
the sacred things according to it, according to which the divine hierarch,
being perfected, will have the participation of all the most sacred things
according to him as being named from the hierarchy. For as he who said
hierarchy spoke in a lump of the arrangement of all the sacred things
together, so he who says hierarch makes clear the inspired and divine man who
is skilled in all sacred knowledge, in whom also the whole hierarchy according
to him is purely perfected and known. The origin of this hierarchy is the
fountain of life, the essence of goodness, the one cause of beings, Trinity,
from which both being and well-being come to beings through goodness. And to
this most thearchic blessedness beyond all things, the threefold, the monad
that truly is, according to the will unattainable for us but known to itself,
the will is the rational salvation of the essences according to us and above
us; and this cannot

come to be otherwise unless those who are saved are being deified; and
deification is the assimilation to God as far as attainable, and union. And
this is the common end of every hierarchy: the close love toward God and the
divine things, sacredly worked in an inspired and unified way, and before this
the complete and unreturning departure from the opposites, the knowledge of
beings as they are beings, the sight and science of the sacred truth, the
inspired participation of the uniform perfection, the feast of the looking-on
of the One itself as far as attainable, feeding intellectually and deifying
everyone who stretches up to it.

<4>

Let us say then that the thearchic blessedness, the deity by nature, the
origin of deification, from which the being-deified comes to those who are
being deified, by divine goodness gifted the hierarchy for the salvation and
deification of all the rational and intellectual essences, and to the
supermundane and blessed lots more immaterially and more intellectually (for
God does not move them to the divine things from without, but they are
illuminated intellectually and from within in a pure and immaterial gleam
toward the most divine will), but to us that which was gifted to those in a
unified and folded-up way has been given from the God-traditioned oracles, as
far as attainable for us, in variety and multitude of distinguishable symbols.
For the essence of the hierarchy according to us is the God-traditioned
oracles. And we call most august oracles these, as many as have been gifted to
us by our inspired sacred-initiators in holy-written and theological tablets,
and indeed as many as from the same sacred men, by a more immaterial
initiation and already somehow neighboring the heavenly hierarchy, from mind
to mind through a middle speech that is bodily yet more immaterial, our
leaders were initiated outside writing, the inspired hierarchs having handed
down not even these to the common of the sacred-working

in unveiled intellections but in sacred symbols. For not everyone is sacred,
nor is the knowledge of all, as the oracles say.

and the folded-up in variety and multitude and the divine in human things and
the immaterial in material things and the superessential in the things
according to us, not of the

symbolic analogously to ourselves, needing the sensibles for our more divine
leading-up from them to the intelligibles. Yet the accounts of the symbols are
unveiled to the divine sacred-initiators, whom it is not lawful to lead out to
those still being perfected, knowing that the lawgivers of the sacred things
in a God-traditioned way arranged the hierarchy by the stable and unmixed
orders of the arrangements and by the analogous and sacred distributions of
what belongs» to each according to worth. Wherefore also, persuaded by your

<II.>

It has then been said by us sacredly that this is the aim of the hierarchy
according to us: our assimilation to God as far as attainable, and union. And
this, as the divine oracles teach, we will attain only by the loves and
sacred-workings of the most august commandments. For "he who loves me," it
says, "will keep my word, and my Father will love him, and we will come to him
and make an abode with him." What then is the beginning of the sacred-working
of the most august commandments, that which most fitly shapes our psychic

habits toward the reception of the other sacred-speakings and sacred-workings,
the road-making of our leading-up toward the superheavenly lot? The tradition
of our sacred and most divine regeneration. For as our renowned leader said,
the first movement according to mind toward the divine things is the love of
God, and of the sacred love the most originating procession toward the
sacred-working of the divine commandments is the most unspeakable creation of
our being divinely. For if to be divinely is the divine generation, never
would anyone know any of the God-traditioned things nor indeed operate who has
not even had inspired existing. Or must we not also (we speak humanly) first

exist, then operate the things according to us, since that which in no way is
has not even movement [nor even existence], but that which somehow is operates
or suffers only those things in which it is naturally to be? But this, as I
think, is clear.

Next let us look upon the divine symbols of the god-begetting. And let no one
unperfected come to the sight. For it is not even without danger to look
against the sun-wrought gleams with weak pupils, nor is it harmless to attempt
the things above us, if the hierarchy according to law is true, turning away
Uzziah because he attempted the sacred things, and Korah because he attempted
the sacred things above him, and Nadab because he used his own unholyly.

Mystery of illumination.

<1>

The hierarch, wishing at each time by the assimilation to God "all men to be
saved and to come to knowledge of truth," proclaims to all the things that are
truly good tidings: that God is gracious to those on earth, being from his own
and natural goodness, having deigned to come as far as us through love of man,
and by the union toward himself to assimilate those united, after the manner
of fire, according to their fitness toward deification. "For as many as
received him, he gave them authority

to become children of God, to those who believe in his name, who were born not
of bloods nor of the will of flesh, but of God."

bringing-near and of his whole life toward what follows. And he, loving
sacredly that man's salvation, but measuring the human against the height of

finishing he confessed in a good-like way that he would do what was asked, and

Contemplation.

<1>

This then, as in symbols, is the rite of the sacred god-begetting, having
nothing unfitting or unholy even of the sensible images, but riddles of
God-worthy contemplation imaged in natural and human-fitting mirrors. For what
would it appear to miss, even if the more divine account of the things being
perfected is kept silent, when by persuasion and God-speaking it sacredly
works out the good-life of the one approaching, and more bodily announces to
him the cleansing of all evil together through a virtuous and divine life by
the natural cleansing through water? In this way then,

even if the symbolic tradition of the things being perfected had nothing more
divine, it was not, as I think, an unholy teaching, introducing a teaching of
a well-set life, wholly riddling the complete


cleansing of the way of life in evil by the body being wholly cleansed
naturally through water.

<2>

But let this be for the unperfected an introductory soul-leading,
distinguishing as is lawful the hierarchic and uniform things from the
multitude and measuring to the orders according to part the harmonious
leading-up; but we, having looked up by sacred ascents to the origins of the
things being perfected and having been sacredly initiated in these, will
recognize of what characters the impressions are and of what unseen things the
images. For

it is, as has been clearly declared in the treatise On intelligibles and
sensibles, that the things sacredly sensible are likenesses of the
intelligibles and a hand-leading and road upon them, and the intelligibles are
origin and science of the hierarchic things according to sense.

<3>

Let us say then that the goodness of the divine blessedness, always being
according to the same and in the same way, unfolds ungrudgingly upon all the
intellectual sights the good-working rays of its own light. If then the
self-chosen self-authority of the intellectual ones should stand off from the
intelligible light, having shut by love of evil the powers naturally sown in
it toward being illuminated, it has been hung off from the light present to
it, the light not having stood off but shining upon it as it is dim-sighted
and turned away,

running toward it in a good-like way; or if it should dare to leap over the
bounds of the visible given to it moderately and to look against the gleams
above the sight according to it, the light will operate nothing besides the
things of light, but it, attacking the perfect things imperfectly, would not
attain the unfitting things, and unorderly overthinking the moderate it will
miss through itself. Yet, as I said, the divine light is always unfolded
good-workingly to the intellectual sights, and it is in them to take hold of
it as present and always being most ready toward a God-fitting giving of its
own. Toward this the divine hierarch is stamped in imitation, unfolding
ungrudgingly the light-formed gleams of

his inspired teaching upon all, and being most ready to illuminate the one
approaching in a God-imitating way, not using envy nor an unholy wrath of the
former

standing-off or lack of measure, but always inspiredly illuminating those who
approach with his light-leadings, hierarchically, in good order and order and

analogy of each one's measure toward the sacred things.

<4>

But if the divine is origin of sacred good-order, according to which the
sacred minds are knowers of themselves, he who runs up toward the visible
belonging to the nature of himself, at the beginning, whoever he is, will
himself see, and this he will take first as a sacred gift from the looking-up
toward the light. And he who has well looked upon his own things with
unpassionate eyes will depart from the unlit recesses of ignorance, and being
himself unperfected he will not at once desire the most perfect union and
participation of God, but by little through his first things upon the still
former things and through those upon the very first

and having been perfected he will be led up in order and sacredly upon the
thearchic summit. Of this well-ordered and sacred order an image is the awe of
the one approaching and the self-knowledge of the things according to himself,
having the sponsor as leader of the road to the hierarch. And the divine
blessedness receives the one so led up into the participation of itself and
gives a share of its own light as of some sign to him, making him inspired and
a partner of the allotment and sacred ranking of the inspired, of which a
sacred symbol is the seal gifted by the hierarch to the one approaching and
the saving register of the priests, enrolling him among the saved and

setting him in sacred memorials, and with him the sponsor also, as of the
life-giving journey toward truth, the one a true lover and fellow-follower of
an inspired leader, the other an unerring hand-leader of the follower by
God-traditioned leadings.

<5>

But it is not possible to participate at once in the things extremely
opposite, nor for him who has had some communion toward the One to have
divided lives, if he holds to the firm participation of the One, but to be
unheld and unranked in all the divisions of the uniform. Which the


tradition of the symbols sacredly riddles, having stripped the one approaching
of the former life as it were and having unbound him even as far as the last
relations according to that, it stands him naked and unshod looking toward the
west and by the thrusting-away of the hands refusing the communions of unlit
evil and as it were breathing out the habit of unlikeness that has come to be
in him and having wholly confes

sed the renunciations toward the opposite of the godlike. Thus having become
wholly unheld and unsharing it transfers him toward the dawn, announcing that
the standing and looking-up in the divine light will be purely in the complete
departure from evil, and receiving in a truth-loving way his sacred
confessions of the whole bending-together toward the One, when he has become
uniform.

But it is somewhere clear, as I think, to those skilled in the hierarchic
things, that by the continuous stretchings-up in tension toward the One and by
the whole deaths and non-existences of the opposites the intellectual things
of the godlike habit have the unchangeable. For one must not only withdraw
from all evil, but also be manfully unsoftened and unfearing always toward the
ruinous sinking upon it, nor ever come to a ceasing of the sacred love of the
truth, but closely and

eternally be stretched up upon it with as much power as one can, always
sacredly working out the leading-up upon the more perfect things of the
thearchy.

Next they throw light-formed garments upon the one being perfected. For by

<III.>

But well that I remembered this, which it is not lawful for me, running past,
to hymn any other of the hierarchic things before this; for it is according to
our renowned leader the rite of rites, and one must, having set it out
sacred-graphically before the others, be led up from the inspired and
hierarchic science according to the oracles to its sacred contemplation by the
thearchic spirit.

And first let us look upon this sacredly, for what reason the common

even to the other hierarchic rites has been exceptionally assigned to it
besides the rest and has been named in a unified way communion and synaxis, of
each sacred-initiating treatment, gathering our divided lives into uniform
deification and by the godlike folding-together of the distinguishable things
gifting the communion and union toward the One. And we say that the perfection
in the participations of the other hierarchic symbols is from the thearchic
and perfective gifts of this. For it is not possible for almost any hierarchic
rite to be perfected unless the most divine eucharist, in the summing-up of
the things being perfected according to each, sacred-works the gathering

of the one who has been perfected upon the One and by the God-traditioned gift
of the perfecting mysteries perfection-works his communion toward God. If then
each of the hierarchic rites, being unperfected, will not perfection-work our
communion and synaxis toward the One and, having been deprived of being a rite
through the unperfected, and the end for every and the summing-up is the
giving of the thearchic mysteries to the one being perfected, reasonably the
hierarchic understanding found for it a proper naming from the truth of the
things. Thus also the sacred rite of the god-begetting, since it gives a share
of first light and is origin of all

the divine light-leadings, we hymn from what is perfected the true naming of
illumination. For even if it is common to all the hierarchic things to give a
share of sacred light to those being perfected, yet this gifted me the first
seeing, and through this most originating light I am light-led toward the
looking-on of the other sacred things. And having said these things let us
look back and see hierarchically the accurate sacred-working and contemplation
according to each of the most holy rite.

Mystery of synaxis or then of communion.

The hierarch, having perfected a sacred prayer upon the divine altar,
beginning from it to incense, comes upon the whole surrounding of the sacred
place; and having returned again upon the divine altar he begins the sacred
melody of the psalms, the whole ecclesiastical arrangement singing with him
the psalmic sacred-speaking. Next through the ministers the reading of the
holy-written tablets comes to be in sequence, and after these the catechumens
come to be outside the sacred surrounding and besides them those being
operated and those being in repentance, but there remain those worthy of the
looking-on and communion of the divine things. And of the ministers some stand
beside the gates of the sacred place having been shut, and others operate some
other of the things of their own order,

and the select of the ministering arrangement together with the priests set
forth upon the divine altar the sacred bread and "the cup of blessing," the
catholic hymnology having been professed beforehand by all the fullness of the
church. After which the divine hierarch perfects a sacred prayer and announces
the holy peace to all, and when all have kissed one another the mystical
recitation of the sacred folds is perfected; and when the hierarch and the
priests have washed the hands with water, the hierarch is set in the middle of
the divine altar, and around him stand only, with the priests, the select of
the ministers. And the hierarch, having hymned the sacred theurgies,
sacred-works the most divine things and brings under sight the things hymned
through the symbols sacredly set forth, and having shown the gifts of the
theurgies he himself comes to their sacred communion and turns the others
forward. And having shared and given a share of the thearchic communion he
ends in sacred thanksgiving, the many having stooped only to the divine
symbols,

but he always being led up by the thearchic spirit toward the holy origins of
the things being perfected in blessed and intelligible sights


hierarchically in purity of the godlike habit.

Contemplation.

<1>

Come then, O fair child, after the images in order and sacredly upon

the godlike truth of the archetypes, having said that to those still being
perfected for their harmonious soul-leading, that not even the varied and
sacred composition of the symbols is unprofitable for them even appearing only
as far as the outside. For the most sacred songs and readings of the oracles
sketch for them a teaching of virtuous life and before this the complete
cleansing of ruin-making evil, and the most divine common and peaceful giving
of the one and the same both bread and cup legislates for them an inspired
sameness of manner as being fed alike, and leads to sacred memory of the most
divine supper and first-symbol of the things being perfected, according to
which also

the creator of the symbols himself most justly allots him who unholily and not
in the same manner suppered the sacred things together with him, teaching
holily and at once in a God-fitting way that the true approach according to
habit upon the divine things gifts to those approaching the communion toward
the like of them.

<2>

These things then, as I said, well

drawn upon the foregates of the inner places, having left as sufficient for
those still unperfected toward contemplation, let us enter from the caused
things into the causes according to our sacred synaxis, and we will see the
well-fitting contemplation of the intelligibles, Jesus light-leading, flashing
out manifestly the blessed beauty of the archetypes. But O most divine and
sacred rite, having unveiled the garments of riddles lying around you
symbolically, be shown to us far-shining and fill our intellectual sights with
unified and unveiled light.

<3>

We must then (for I think we are within the all-holy things), having stripped
the intelligible

of the first of the images, gaze into its godlike beauty and see the hierarch
inspiredly going from the divine altar as far as the last things of the sacred
place with good fragrance and again being restoratively set upon it. For the
thearchic blessedness above all things, even if by divine goodness it proceeds
into the sacred communion of those who participate in it,

yet does not come to be outside the unmoved standing and founding according to
essence, and analogously illuminates all the godlike, being truly around
itself and wholly not moved aside from its own identity.

Likewise the divine rite of the synaxis, even if having a unified and simple
and

folded-up origin it is multiplied in a man-loving way into the sacred variety
of the symbols and goes as far as all the hierarchic image-drawing, yet
uniformly from these it is gathered again into its own monad and unifies those
being sacredly led up upon it. According to the same godlike manner the divine
hierarch, even if he leads down in a good-like way the unified science of his
hierarchy to those who have come under, using the multitudes of the sacred
riddles, yet again as free and unheld by the lesser he is restoratively set
undiminished into his own origin, and having made

the intellectual entrance into his own One he sees purely the uniform accounts
of the things being perfected, making the more divine turning into the first
things the end of the man-loving procession upon the second things.

<4>

And the sacred-speaking of the psalms, being co-essential with almost all the
hierarchic mysteries, was not going to be hung off from the most hierarchic of
all. For every sacred and holy-written tablet either the generated existence
and arrangement of beings from God, or the legal hierarchy and way of life, or
distributions and holdings of the inheritances of the divine people, or the
understanding of sacred judges or wise kings or inspired priests, or the
unshaken philosophy in endurance of ancient men in variety and multitude of
the distressing things, or wise suggestions of things to be done, or songs of
divine loves and inspired images, or the prophetic pre-announcings of things
that will be, or the manly theurgies of Jesus, or the God-traditioned and
God-imitating
