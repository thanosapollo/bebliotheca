Of our father among the saints Athanasius, On Sabbaths and Circumcision, from
Exodus.
«And the sons of Israel shall keep the Sabbaths, to observe them throughout
their generations. An everlasting covenant between me and the sons of Israel,
it is an everlasting sign with me, that in six days the Lord made the heaven
and the earth, and on the seventh day he rested.» It is a sign of knowing the
day of creation, and its end. It was fitting, as long as the things of
creation were being worked, at Jerusalem until then to keep the Sabbaths. But
when the other generation and the people being created have come, according to
what is said: «Let this be written for another generation, and a people that
is being created shall praise the Lord;» it is necessary that this people no
longer keep the end of the former creation, but seek the beginning of the
second creation. And what is that, if not that according to which the Lord
rose? For from then the new creation had its beginning, concerning which Paul
said: «So that if anyone is in Christ, a new creation.» God, then, making the
former creation, rested; for this reason also those of that generation kept
the Sabbath on the seventh day; but the second creation has no end; for this
reason he has not rested, but even until now he works. Whence we do not keep
a day as Sabbath as in the first, but we hope for the coming Sabbaths of
Sabbaths, which the new creation does not take as an end, but is made manifest
and keeps festival throughout. For this reason the Sabbath was given to the
former people, that they might know both the end and the beginning of
creation. But to the new creation he did not command to keep a Sabbath; that
it might know the beginning on the Lord's Day, and know that its grace is
without end. And for this reason he also made known the beginning, that is the
Lord's Day, that you might know the end of the one that has passed. The
Apostle therefore, writing concerning the former, says: «But when the fullness
of time came, God sent forth his Son;» and again: «At the consummation of the
ages.» Therefore the first has been fulfilled and completed, not at any other
time than as the =28.136= prophets said: «In the last days I will pour out of
my Spirit.» And the first days having an end, others come to be, as Jeremiah
says: «Behold, days are coming, says the Lord.» And the first having an end,
accordingly a beginning of another has come about; for this reason after the
Sabbath the Lord rose; for God did not give the Sabbath primarily for the sake
of men being idle, he who says: «The ways of the idle are strewn with thorns,»
and who commands in Numbers: «And on the day of the Sabbaths you shall offer
two yearling lambs without blemish, and two tenths of fine flour mixed with
oil for a sacrifice and a drink-offering, and a whole-burnt-offering of
Sabbaths on the Sabbaths upon the continual whole-burnt-offering.» And again a
little before he says in the things above: «And you shall put upon the
sacrifice pure frankincense and salt, and they shall be for loaves for a
memorial, set forth before the Lord. On the day of the Sabbaths it shall be
set forth before the Lord continually before the sons of Israel, an
everlasting covenant.» For if idleness were his concern, he would not have
commanded the Levites to set forth, and to offer, and to slaughter. For if
idleness sanctifies, clearly work defiles; but the Levites working are not
defiled; therefore idleness does not cleanse. Or why are the Levites alone
deprived of sanctification, offering, and slaughtering, and setting forth on
the Sabbath? For they are as far from transgression as they also make
atonement, on behalf of those who offer. Therefore the Sabbath does not
signify idleness, but knowledge of the Maker, and a pause of the fashion of
this creation, as also in Ezekiel God testifies saying: «And my Sabbaths I
gave them to be a sign between me and them, that they might know that I am the
Lord who sanctifies them.» Therefore the Sabbath was given for the sake of
knowledge and not of idleness; so that knowledge is rather more necessary than
idleness. For this reason the Lord said to those who accused the disciples for
plucking ears of grain: «If you had known what this is, I desire mercy and not
sacrifice.» For he accused them of being idle on the Sabbath, that you do not
have what is proper to the Sabbath, which is knowledge toward the truth. For
this reason also around Jericho those who received the law of the Sabbath are
commanded to trumpet for seven days, in which =28.137= seven the Sabbath also
falls. If therefore the Sabbath was given for the sake of being idle, why are
they commanded to work by trumpeting? But he knew that by trumpeting they kept
Sabbath, not by being idle, but by knowing the Lord who helped them; just as
also in the case of the eight-day-old being circumcised; for there is
necessity to circumcise even on the Sabbath, and yet he keeps Sabbath, working
indeed, but knowing God. Indeed even the days that are not days of the
Sabbaths, nevertheless because of the remission of sins and the grace of the
knowledge of God, the law is accustomed to name Sabbaths, or rather the Lord
of the law, as in Leviticus: and the Lord spoke to Moses saying: «And on the
tenth of this seventh month there shall be a holy convocation, a day of
atonement, for you; and you shall humble your souls and you shall offer
whole-burnt-offerings to the Lord. You shall do no work on this same day;» and
a little after: «And every soul that shall do work on this same day, that soul
shall be destroyed from its people. You shall do no work, an everlasting
ordinance throughout your generations. It shall be Sabbaths of Sabbaths for
you, and you shall humble your souls from the ninth of the month, from evening
until evening, and you shall keep Sabbath your Sabbaths.» Therefore the
Sabbath is not the seventh, but the remission of sins, when someone rests from
them. Nor is the Sabbath idleness, but confession and the humbling of the
soul. For he has not spoken specifically of the Sabbath, but simply of the
tenth, which happens to fall not on a Sabbath. And yet the day that falls,
because of the atonement, he names not simply Sabbath, but Sabbaths of
Sabbaths. And if he names this a Sabbath, and on a Sabbath he commands to
work: therefore the Sabbath is not primarily a law of idleness, but of
knowledge and atonement, and of idleness from all wickedness. He did not
therefore give the Sabbath because of idleness, but that they might know the
rest of creation. For God commanded saying: «That you may know that on this
day the Lord your God rested from the works which he began to make.» And he
wanted them, knowing the end of this one, to seek the beginning of another.
The Sabbath then was the end of the former creation, and the beginning of the
second was the Lord's Day, on which he renewed and made new the old. Just as
therefore he commanded first to keep the day of the Sabbath, being a memorial
of the end of the former things, =28.140= so we honor the Lord's Day, being a
memorial of the beginning of a second re-creation. For he did not create
another in addition, but he made new the old, and that which he began to make
he completed. For this reason on the sixth the things that had come to be were
finished, and on the seventh he rested from all; but in the Gospel the Word
coming says: «I came to complete the work.» For he who rests from all things
signifies that some things needed an end, which coming he completed. For he
says in the Gospel according to John: «The works which the Father has given
me, that I should complete them; the very works which I do, bear witness
concerning me.» For the work was incomplete if, Adam having sinned, man was
dying; but it has become complete, he having been made alive. For this reason,
having renewed the creation in six days, he sets a day for the renewal, which
the Spirit proclaims beforehand through the psalm saying: «This is the day
which the Lord has made.» For instead of a sun God rises, shining upon the
soul of each; for this reason also in the saving passion itself the sun did
not shine, signifying the end of the former creation, and the beginning of
another, that which rises from the Savior; which the prophet having seen says:
«Behold a man, East is his name;» and again: «But for you who fear him the sun
of righteousness shall rise.» For this is not a day of all, but of those who
have died to sin, and live to the Lord. For this reason also the law commanded
to be circumcised on the eighth day; and the commandment was inviolable,
signifying the regeneration of all after the seventh. For circumcision showed
nothing else than the putting off of generation; for we put off the one who
died on the sixth, and we are renewed on the Lord's Day, when the old man,
having been put off, was regenerated by the resurrection. For this Paul also
says in the letter to the Colossians: «In whom also you were circumcised with
a circumcision not made with hands, in the putting off of the body of the
flesh, in the circumcision of Christ, having been buried with him in baptism
into Hades, in which also you were raised together.» For circumcision was a
type of the putting off through baptism; which he commanded to take place in
those members through which the body also is generated, that those being
circumcised might know that it is a sign of the putting off of oldness. For
Abraham having believed received circumcision, being a sign of the
regeneration through baptism. For this reason, the thing signified having
come, the sign has ceased. For circumcision was a sign, but the washing of
=28.141= regeneration was the thing signified. For the whole of the old being
put off, the signification through the part is superfluous. And just as the
Lord's Day is the beginning of creation, and puts a stop to the Sabbath; so
the same, having regenerated man, stopped circumcision. For both have been
accomplished on the eighth, both the beginning of creation, and the
regeneration of man. For this reason the eighth loosed the Sabbath, and not
the Sabbath the eighth. For on a Sabbath man was circumcised; and on the
Sabbath circumcision was not idle. For the eighth, being the beginning of the
re-creation, put to rest the first. And I think circumcision was given for
this reason also. For Adam having heard: «You are earth, and to earth you
shall go,» was dying, and the body was being corrupted; whence they were
commanded to pay out a certain part of the body, and this the cause of the
generation of the body, being taught beforehand the putting off of the whole,
that, as it were paying back the thing condemned, they might be able to be
saved. And just as the sacrifices were shadows of the things to come, so also
the circumcision from a part was a shadow of the whole. And this the word also
signifies clearly in Joshua the son of Nun; for Joshua according to the
command of God circumcising the people at Gilgal beyond the Jordan, the Lord
said to Joshua son of Nun: «On this present day I have taken away the reproach
of Egypt from you. And I called, he says, the name of that place Gilgal.» For
if circumcision is a taking away of the generation in Egypt, clearly
uncircumcision is a mark of Egyptian condition and generation. And God took
this away, that they might no longer be as those born in Egypt, but be
reckoned children of the promise, into which the Lord led them. These things
being so, circumcision appears to have been given altogether, that the
reproach of earthly generation might be taken away from the children of
Abraham, and that we might no longer be liable to: «You are earth, and to
earth you shall go.» For the reproach of the transgression was taken away. And
this happened then as a type of the baptism through Christ. For then it
happened in part, as in a shadow, but now, as the Apostle said, we put off the
whole earthly generation, being regenerated through the washing, that we might
no longer die according to the first generation, but according to the
circumcision of the putting off of the body, which we put off through the
washing, we might live. And just as the Lord said to Joshua: «I have taken
away the reproach of Egypt on this present day from you;» so much more might
it be said to each of those now being baptized: On this day I have taken away
the reproach of earthly generation, and the reproach of the corruption of
death from you, on this present day.
