Velenus, king of the Cadusii, wrote thus:
/I have received with gratitude the auxiliary forces sent back to me
whole and unharmed. but I do not congratulate enough on Valerian,
prince of princes, having been captured; I would congratulate more if
he were given back. For the Romans

are more formidable then, when they are defeated. act therefore as befits a
prudent man, and do not let fortune inflame you, which has deceived
many. Valerian has both a son who is emperor and a grandson who is
Caesar, and what of that whole Roman world, which will rise as one
against you?

give back Valerian therefore and make peace with the Romans, which
will be of use even to us on account of the nations of Pontus./

* Chapter 4
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4
:END:

The Bactrians and the Hiberians and the Albanians and the Tauroscythians
did not receive Sapor's letters but wrote to the Roman generals,
promising auxiliaries for freeing Valerian from captivity.

But while Valerian was growing old among the Persians, Odaenathus the
Palmyrene, having gathered an army, restored the Roman state

almost to its former condition. he captured the king's treasures, he
captured also those whom Parthian kings hold dearer than treasures,

the concubines, wherefore Sapor, fearing the Roman generals more,
withdrew more swiftly into his own kingdom out of fear of Ballista and
Odaenathus, and this meanwhile was the end of the Persian war.

These things are worthy of being known about Valerian, whose
praiseworthy life through sixty years had risen to that glory that
after all honours and magistracies outstandingly held he became
emperor, not, as is usual, by a tumultuary gathering of the people,
not by the shouting of soldiers, but by the right of his merits and as
if by one judgement of the whole world.

in short, if power had been given to all of putting forth their choice
whom they wished as emperor, another would not have been chosen.

* Chapter 8
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h8
:END:

Valerian the younger, born of another mother than Gallienus,
conspicuous in form, approved in modesty, famous in learning for his
age, most pleasant in character and set apart from his brother's
dissoluteness, was named Caesar by his father while absent, by his
brother, as Caelestinus says,

Augustus. he has nothing praiseworthy in his life, except that he was
nobly born, reared most well, and pitiably slain.

And since I know that most err, who, reading the title of the emperor
Valerian on a tomb, think that the body of that Valerian has been
given back who was captured by the Persians, lest any error creep in,
I have thought it should be set down in writing that this Valerian was
buried near Milan with a title added by order of Claudius:
/Valerian the emperor./

I do not think that anything more should be asked either concerning
the elder Valerian or

concerning the younger. and since I fear that I may pass the measure
of a volume, if I should publish, joined to this book, Gallienus, son
of Valerian, concerning whom already much and perhaps too much speech
has been ours in that man's life, or even Saloninus, son also of
Gallienus, who is called both Saloninus and Gallienus in the history
of his time, let us now pass to another volume, as we are bidden. for
we have always given ourselves to you and to fame, to whom we can deny
nothing.
