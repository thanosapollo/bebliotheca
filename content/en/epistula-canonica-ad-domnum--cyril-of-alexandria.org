OF SAINT CYRIL ARCHBISHOP OF ALEXANDRIA Cyril
to Domnus I Concerning a bishop deposed and ceasing from liturgizing.
Each of the affairs among us, whenever they are borne straight of
canonical good order, begets no tumult for us, and also delivers us
from the ill-speaking of some, and rather even procures for us the
good reports of those who think well. For who would not accept an
unswerving vote, which 277 ever might come about concerning some, or
how shall judging rightly and lawfully not be without rebuke, rather
even full of every praise? And these things I write now, your piety in
its own letters, those sent both to me and to our most holy and most
God-beloved brother and fellow-bishop Proclus, naming as bishop the
most devout and most pious Peter, while he himself weeps and says that
he has been moved unreasonably from the church allotted to him.
It was consistent either that he should have the name of the
priesthood together with the thing itself, or at any rate, if he was
worthy not to preside at the divine altar, that he should not be
honoured even with the very title of the episcopate. But perhaps my
word would seem to your piety to be some harsh and unbrotherly thing;
yet it is not so according to the truth. For we think perhaps
somewhere to pity the old man, leaving him the title only; but it was
far 278 better to consider the other point also. For he says that he
is able to establish his own reputation, but that he did not receive
an occasion of defence, nor indeed was a canonical hearing granted
him. And if any such thing had taken place, the very composition of
the minutes would have convicted him, either as caught by the charges,
shown guilty and having nothing further to say as one wronged, or at
any rate, declaring him free, would have given again the presidency of
the church, which had also come under his hand. But since nothing of
the kind has been done, he cries out against the affair; he says he
has suffered unbearable injustice, and has been unlawfully cast out,
adding also that all the money belonging to him has been seized.
Let your holiness therefore, considering both what seems good to the
divine canons and what is becoming to the church and to those
appointed to the sacred liturgy, and further besides these being
entreated also by our letters, stay 279 the old man's tear. And if he
should choose to go to law with those bringing the charges against
him, let him be judged according to custom before your piety, the most
pious bishops under its hand being present, of course, unless he
should decline some as suspected. For we believe that none of the most
pious bishops thinks hostilely toward a brother; but that this may not
become a pretext, dissolving the judgment that shall be upon him,
toward its seeming not to have been done in justice, there is nothing
grieving that some of those under suspicion be absent from the
council.
II Concerning the bishop having the authority of all the
ecclesiastical things.
And that the money taken from him unjustly be restored is just
according to two ways. First, because no such thing ought to have
happened at all, and because it grieves exceedingly and brings down
into extreme listlessness the most pious bishops being every 280 where
on earth, that they should be required to give accounts of the
administration of the expenses falling to them, whether from
ecclesiastical revenues, or even from the fruit-bearing of some. For
each of us will give account of his own times to the Judge of all. For
treasures and immovable possessions ought to be preserved inalienably
for the churches, but those who from time to time administer the
divine priesthood should be trusted with the administration of the
expenses that fall to them.
III Concerning not giving a resignation as a priest.
And the libels of resignation he says he gave not according to his own
choice, but as from necessity and fear and the threat of certain
persons. And otherwise it is a thing not pleasing to the ordinances of
the church that some of the ministers should present libels of
resignation. For if they are 281 worthy to liturgize, let them remain
in this; but if unworthy, let them not go out from resignation, but
rather having been condemned upon facts; of which one might make much
outcry, as running outside of all sequence. Greet the brotherhood with
you; that with us greets you in the Lord.
