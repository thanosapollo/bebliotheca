The Gnostic, or To the One Deemed Worthy of Knowledge
1 Practitioners will understand words of practice, but gnostics will see
things of knowledge.
2 The practitioner is the one who has acquired impassibility of the passionate
part of the soul only.
3 The gnostic is the one who holds the place of salt for the impure, and of
light for the pure.
4 The knowledge that comes to us from outside tries, through words, to point
out the subject-matters. The knowledge that arises from the grace of God
presents the things themselves to the mind by direct vision, and looking
toward them the intellect receives their reasons. Opposed to the former is
error; to the latter, anger and wrath, and the things that follow upon these.
5 All the virtues make a way for the gnostic; but above them all is freedom
from anger. For the one who has laid hold of knowledge and is easily moved to
anger is like one who pricks his own eyes with an iron pin.
6 Let the gnostic take care in his pastoral condescensions, lest the
condescension become a habit without his noticing, and let him try always to
practice all the virtues equally, so that they may follow one another in him
as well, because the intellect is by nature betrayed by the virtue that is
diminished.
8 It is shameful for a gnostic to go to law, both when he is wronged and when
he wrongs: when wronged, because he did not endure; when wronging, because he
did injustice.
9 Knowledge, when it is kept, teaches the one who shares in it how he may be
preserved and how he may go on to greater things.
13 It is right to discuss with monks and with people in the world concerning a
right way of life, and to clarify in part those doctrines of natural or
theological knowledge without which no one will see the Lord. 15 Know the
reasons and the laws of seasons and of lives and of occupations, so that you
may easily say to each what is beneficial.
21 You will not allegorize the words of blameworthy persons, nor will you seek
anything spiritual in them, unless God has acted through them by economy, as
in Balaam and Caiaphas, so that the one might foretell concerning the birth,
and the other concerning the death, of our Savior.
22 The gnostic must not be gloomy nor hard to approach: for the former belongs
to one who does not know the reasons of the things that come to be; the latter
to one who does not wish that all human beings be saved and come to the
knowledge of the truth.
24 Take heed to yourself, lest for the sake of gain or of living at ease, or
for passing glory, you say something of the secret things and be cast outside
the sacred precincts, as one who himself sells the young of the dove in the
temple.
27 Do not theologize rashly, and never define the divine: for definitions
belong to things that have come to be and are composite.
28 .............................................. ........ that the one who
has been tried by vice hates vice, and trial is the offspring of abandonment
.............................
29 Let those who are being taught always say to you: Friend, go up higher. For
it is shameful that, after you have gone up, you should again be brought down
by those who hear you.
30 The lover of money is not the one who has money, but the one who longs for
it: for they say that the steward is a rational purse.
31 Exhort the old to master anger, and the young to master the belly: for the
former are fought by the demons of the soul, the latter for the most part by
the bodily ones.
32 Stop the mouths of those who slander in your hearing; and do not be
surprised when you are accused by many: for this temptation is from demons.
For the gnostic must be free of hatred and of remembrance of wrongs, even
toward those who do not wish it.
33 Perhaps the one who heals human beings for the Lord's sake has escaped his
own notice that he is healing himself: for the medicine which the gnostic
applies heals the neighbor as it may, but himself of necessity.
36 Let the higher discourse concerning judgment escape worldlings and the
young, for it easily begets contempt: for they do not know the pain of a
rational soul condemned to ignorance.
37 Saint Paul, bruising his body, brought it into subjection. Do not therefore
neglect diet in your life, and do not insult impassibility by humbling it with
a fat body.
38 Do not be anxious about foods or clothing, but remember Abener the Levite,
who, having received the ark of the Lord, became rich from being poor and
glorious from being without honor.
41 Every proposition has as its predicate either a genus, or a difference, or
a species, or a property, or an accident, or what is composed of these; but
none of the things said can be taken of the Holy Trinity. Let the ineffable be
worshipped in silence.
42 A temptation of the gnostic is a false opinion standing about the
intellect, of what exists as not existing, of what does not exist as existing,
and of what exists, yet not existing in the manner in which it has come to be.
44 We have learned from the righteous Gregory that there are four virtues even
of contemplation itself: prudence, courage, temperance, and justice. And he
said that the work of prudence is to contemplate the intelligible and holy
powers apart from their reasons: for he handed down that these are made known
by wisdom alone. Of courage, to hold fast to the true things even when warred
against, and not to enter into the things that are not. To receive the seeds
from the first farmer and to drive away the one who sows over them, he
answered, is proper to temperance. And of justice again, to render the words
to each according to worth, announcing some things darkly, signifying some
through riddles, and revealing some for the benefit of the simpler.
45 The pillar of the truth, Basil the Cappadocian: the knowledge that comes
upon us from human beings, he says, is strengthened by close study and
exercise; but that which arises from the grace of God, by justice and freedom
from anger and mercy. And the former even those who are still under the
passions can receive; but of the latter only the impassible are receptive: who
also, at the time of prayer, behold the proper light of the intellect shining
around them.
46 The holy luminary of the Egyptians, Athanasius: Moses, he says, is
commanded to set the table toward the north; let the gnostics know who it is
that blows against them, and let them endure every temptation nobly, and with
eagerness feed those who come to them.
47 Serapion, the angel of the church of the Thmuites, used to say that the
intellect, having drunk spiritual knowledge, is perfectly purified, while love
heals the inflamed parts of irascibility; and self-control stops wicked
desires as they flow in.
48 Always exercise by yourself the discourses concerning providence and
judgment, says the great and gnostic teacher Didymus, and try to carry their
subject-matters in memory: for nearly all stumble in these. And you will find
the discourses concerning judgment in the difference of bodies and of worlds;
and those concerning providence in the ways that lead us back from vice and
ignorance to virtue or to knowledge.
50 Always looking toward the archetype, try to write the images, omitting
nothing of the things that contribute toward recovering the one that has
fallen away.
